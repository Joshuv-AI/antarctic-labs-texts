Toward point-of-care cancer diagnostics: gold nanoparticle-based colorimetric sensors
Authors: Evana Sultana, Muhammad Shamim Al Mamun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nowadays, cancer has become one of the major causes of mortality all over the world, irrespective of age and gender. The early detection of cancer can markedly enhance patient survival outcomes. Cancer cells are analyzed by comparing the optimum concentration of different types of biomarkers, including alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), prostate-specific antigen (PSA), microRNA-21, and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), in biological samples. These biomarkers play a crucial role in the early detection, screening and treatment of cancer. Novel colorimetric assays based on modified AuNPs are discussed in this review, which have opened a new avenue for the facile, point-of-care diagnosis of cancer. Due to their unique characteristics such as surface plasmon resonance (SPR), AuNPs provide a selective, trustworthy and simple strategy to detect cancerous cells by visualizing a color change when used in colorimetry. The principles of the sensing methods (aggregation, anti-aggregation, etching, and enzyme-mimetic activity) are illustrated in this review. Besides, some portable devices, such as paper-based devices (PADs), barcodes, and lateral flow assays (LFTs), are mentioned, which have made this complicated detection process as convenient as a pregnancy strip test. However, these devices are primarily limited by their low sensitivity and inadequate capability for accurate quantitative analysis. This review highlights the progress of paper-based colorimetric sensing devices for the detection of cancers, such as breast cancer, prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, and bladder cancer. Furthermore, we outline the key advantages of these devices and emphasize their major limitations. Finally, we have also mentioned some perspectives that may improve the sensitivity of these colorimetric devices as biosensors.

Introduction

Cancer is a generic term for a large group of diseases that can affect any part of the body, which are also referred to as malignant tumors and neoplasms.1 Cancer is also considered the major causes of mortality and morbidity throughout the world.2 One of the prominent features of cancer is the rapid creation of abnormal cells that grow out of their boundaries, called tumors. The primary tumors are responsible for the formation of new vessels following the metastasis process, spreading to the other organs of the body.3 Carcinogens (chemicals, radiant energy, ontogenetic viruses and other microbes) are the large number of agents that cause genetic damage and induce the neoplastic transformation of cells. There are some factors which directly cause cancer, including environmental factors (infectious agents, alcohol consumption, and smoking), genetic predisposition (inherited traits), age and sex.4 In a way, we can compare cancerous cell growth with an uncontrolled chemical reaction, which can be initiated by any means but is difficult to be terminated. It is noteworthy that recent statistics have shared some concerning information about cancer. About 1.8 million people per year are affected by cancer all over the world, and in the United States of America (USA), the number of deaths caused by cancer is 0.6 million per year.5 A cancer biomarker is a measurable biological substance or characteristic that can be monitored for identifying the presence, progression, or therapeutic response of cancer cells. Molecular cancer biomarkers found in blood, urine, tissue and cell samples are analyzed to monitor the reflection of abnormal biological processes in cancer.6 Nowadays, different types of biomarkers are assessed for the diagnosis of cancer, such as protein biomarkers (PSA, AFP, and CEA), genetic biomarkers (BRCA-1 and EGFR), and cellular biomarkers (pap smear and CTCs).7 Challenges in biomarker monitoring stem from heterogeneous tumor profiles, low biomarker abundance, insufficient specificity, dynamic biological variations, and methodological limitations in detection and standardization.8 Besides, the detection of cancer biomarkers in complex samples presents significant challenges, and the measurement of a single biomarker is often inadequate to fully characterize the disease, necessitating the use of biomarker panels for reliable diagnosis.9 Cancer-related mortality can be greatly reduced by early detection. For instance, breast cancer patients have an almost 90% five-year survival rate when detected at an early localized stage; however, this rate declines to approximately 27% once the cancer has metastasized to distant sites.10 Therefore, accurate detection of cancer at an early stage is essential to increase the survival rate of the patients through the effective therapeutic outcomes. In these circumstances, some sophisticated cancer diagnostics tools have been designed to detect it at a localized stage. Nowadays, there are some methods available to detect cancer earlier, including imaging techniques and morphological analysis of tissues (histopathology) or cells (cytology), mass spectrometry-based proteomics, as well as fluorescence spectrometry-based assays. Conventional imaging methods, including X-ray, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), endoscopy, and ultrasound, are capable of identifying cancer only after visible structural alterations occur in the tissue.11 Moreover, cytological and histopathological techniques cannot be reliably or independently employed for the early-stage detection of cancer.12 Additionally, present-day imaging modalities do not provide sufficient specificity to clearly differentiate malignant lesions from benign counterparts.13 However, these methods generally rely on costly instrumentation and complex protocols, which often discourage patients from undergoing routine diagnostic consultations for early cancer symptoms; consequently, the disease progresses to incurable advanced stages.14 To minimize delays in cancer diagnosis, there is a strong need for the development of simple, cost-effective diagnostic technologies capable of efficiently monitoring early symptoms of this fatal disease. Over the past few years, nanotechnology has attracted considerable interest owing its potential medical applications. Furthermore, advances in nanotechnology have enabled the development of nanostructures that can be conjugated with various biological molecules, such as protein, hormones and antibodies, allowing targeted delivery to cells expressing specific receptors.15,16 The incorporation of nanoparticles enhances the surface-to-volume ratio, thereby improving the sensitivity of biosensors to meet the requirements of specific biomolecular diagnostics.17 A particularly promising area of research is the application of gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) in cancer diagnosis, owing to their relative chemical stability, which reduces potential hazards; their simple and efficient synthesis and fabrication; and their excellent biocompatibility, which ensures minimal interference with other labeled biomolecules, such as antibodies and biomarkers.18 Colorimetric assays allow the rapid detection of analytes via visible color changes, and AuNPs can be utilized in different forms for optical sensing.19,20 Colorimetric biosensors are capable of detecting a single biomarker or a set of biomarkers with high specificity, even when present at low concentrations.1,21 The AuNP-based colorimetric detection of cancer has been the subject of numerous review publications. Here, a lot of researchers have investigated different types of cancers in the human body, such as breast cancer,22 liver cancer,23 oral cancer,24 pancreatic cancer,25 and prostate cancer.26 In these studies, researchers have found most satisfactory detection limit by using a visual detection process. The colorimetric technique can lead to the formation of cancer detection sensors as easy to use as a pregnancy kit. The principles involved in this detection process based on aggregation, growth and etching, and enzyme-mimetic activity of AuNPs result in the change of color from wine-red to purple in the sample solution.27

Herein, we present a comprehensive review that summarizes the most recent advances in colorimetric cancer detection based on AuNPs, discusses the major principles, properties of AuNPs, and strategies of sensing different target biomarkers with high sensitivity and selectivity, and determine the operational approach. In this review, we not only discuss the effectiveness of this approach but also investigate some challenges to execute this method in cancer sensing phenomenon. Colorimetry is a suitable transduction technique, particularly for the point-of-care (POC) detection and use as an alternative to the conventional biomarker detection technique.28,29

Properties of gold nanoparticles

Physical properties

Localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR)

Mie elucidated the underlying mechanism of this phenomenon in 1908 through solutions of Maxwell's electromagnetic equations applied to small, homogeneous spherical particles exposed to an electromagnetic field.30 Upon light excitation, the conduction electrons in a noble metal undergo collective oscillations, which are known as a ‘plasmons’.31 By the advancement of nanotechnology, gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) are referred to as nano-plasmons because the high density of free electrons surrounding the particles exhibits restricted motion owing to their small dimensions (mean free path ∼50 nm), in contrast to the relatively unrestricted electron movement observed in bulk materials. This unique behavior endows nanomaterials with pronounced and distinctive optical properties.32 The plasmon resonance (PR) occurs, when the incident light correlates with the collective oscillation of conduction electrons.33 Moreover, the resonance always occurs on the AuNP surface because of the decrease in the resonance of incident radiation with the increase in the depth of metal, which is called surface plasmon resonance (SPR).34 However, SPR can only occur by maintaining one condition that the wavelength of the incident light must be larger than the diameter of the particle (d ≪ λ, where λ is the wavelength of the light).35 On the other hand, when the collective oscillation of conduction electrons occurs locally around the metal nanoparticles by the excitation of light, it is known as LSPR. ‘Hot Spot’ is created by LSPR, when the light of specific wavelength matches with the oscillation of surface electrons. The change in shape or size of AuNPs causes a change in electric field, resulting in a change in the oscillation frequency of electrons, which also affects the LSPR properties. Nowadays, AuNP-based colorimetric cancer analysis has been investigated by researchers using localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) properties.36,37

Surface-enhanced resonance spectroscopy (SERS)

If a monochromatic light is passed through the surface of a molecule, a small amount of energy is scattered. The scattered energy consists of radiations of incident frequency (Rayleigh scattering), and the deviation of incident frequency (absorbed by the surface molecule) is called Stokes scattering and anti-Stokes scattering. This phenomenon of energy difference between the scattered energy and incident energy is called Raman scattering.38 Raman spectroscopy provides highly specific molecular vibrational information (0.1 nm) of complex biological systems, including cells, called chemical fingerprint, which is a very essential part in the single molecule detection.39 but Raman cannot detect the molecule which is in a very low concentration. This limitation has been overcome using surface-enhanced resonance spectroscopy (SERS), which uses metals (gold and silver) exhibiting surface plasmon resonance. This mechanism gives rise to intense localized electromagnetic fields on the metal surface, thereby significantly enhancing the Raman response of molecules adsorbed at or adjacent to the interface, and thus sensing. SERS has been largely used for detecting and distinguishing tumor cells and in imaging applications for cancer diagnosis.40 A newly developed SERS method has been developed using homogeneous Au on an ITO substrate that can significantly increase the Raman scattering to distinguish between the two breast cancer cells.41

Surface-enhanced fluorescence (SEF)

Fluorescent probes or dyes are designed to selectively bind with disease-specific biomarkers, such as proteins, enzymes, DNA, or RNA. Upon excitation, the emitted fluorescence intensity correlates with the concentration of the target analyte, enabling early and accurate disease diagnosis, including cancer and infectious diseases.42 Despite their widespread use in biosensing, fluorescent molecules exhibit inherent drawbacks, including low optical cross sections that restrict detectability and poor photostability that causes signal decay during measurement. Surface-enhanced fluorescence (SEF) is a plasmon-mediated phenomenon, initially identified in the 1970s, in which the placement of a fluorophore near the intense electromagnetic fields on the plasmonic metal nanoparticle (AuNP) surface leads to substantial enhancement of fluorescence emission.43 If the distance between the fluorescence molecules and AuNPs is less than 5 nm, the fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) of the excited molecule will entirely transfer to the AuNPs, leading to the formation of fluorescence quenching.44 The detection process proceeds by monitoring the quenching and back of the fluorescence of the interacting particles. Many studies have recently found that they first select the recognition sequences as the linker to recognize specific cancer cells in lung tumors,45 acute lymphocytic leukemia,46 intracellular mRNA,47 and human Burkitt's lymphoma48 and can also detect multiple mRNAs.49 These linkers interact with the substances to change their structure, leading to changes in the distance between the AuNPs and the fluorescence molecule. Thus, the researchers work based on this detection strategy.

Colorimetric signal responses

Depending on the different shapes and sizes of AuNPs, they exhibit distinctive colorimetric responses arising from their unique LSPR properties. The interaction of AuNPs with light causes collective oscillation of conduction electrons to produce an intense absorption band in the visible region, giving different ranges of visible color. Recently, AuNP-based colorimetric method has been one of the most trustworthy assays because of the advantage of qualitative analysis by observing only the color change (red-wine to purple). The color responses of AuNPs have opened a new chapter in the biosensing of various types of analytes, such as tumor-related proteins, nucleic acids or cytokines.50 In 2010, Kang and co-workers first applied this colorimetric assay for cancer detection following the charge-induced AuNP aggregation sensing principle.51 In this detection system, they have detected activated protein kinase C(PKC) as the cancer marker based on the non-cross-linking aggregation of AuNPs. Here, AuNPs with analytes gave no color rather than being red but in normal cells or tissue lysates, the color turned blue. In another study, protein kinase Cα, a small targeted molecule, was also detected.52 In 2011, Lee and co-workers found that the AuNP-based colorimetric method was used to detect cancer mutation points (BRCA1) by base pairing-induced AuNP aggregation.53 Moreover, the signal of this method can be amplified in the polymerase chain reaction (PCR)54 or isothermal reaction.55 Therefore, because of the excellent visualization features of AuNPs, sensitivity to picomolar levels can be achieved in the case of cancer diagnosis.

Chemical properties

Easy to integrate

Gold nanoparticles exhibit an inherent ease of coupling over other nanoparticles due to their versatile surface chemistry and high surface-to-volume ratio. AuNPs shows high affinity to some functional groups like thiols (–SH), amines (–NH2) and carboxylic acids (COOH).56 Especially, AuNPs form a strong chemical bond with –S- and –N-containing groups, enabling efficient and robust attachment of biomolecules like DNA, proteins, and antibodies. When the bulk materials reduce their size to the nanoscale level, the surface-to-volume ratio increases in a drastic manner.57 Likewise, having a nanoscale size, AuNPs provide a large accessible surface area, allowing the attachment of a number of ligands or biomolecules. Thus, AuNPs facilitate multivalent interactions and coupling efficiency. This property helps AuNPs to contribute to targeting, detection and drug delivery.58

Biocompatibility

Biocompatibility means the ability of a material to perform its intended biological function without causing significant toxicity, immune response, or adverse effects in living systems. AuNPs are considered one of the most highly biocompatible nanoparticles over other metallic NPs. As they are highly biocompatible, they are extensively used in biomedical, diagnostic, and therapeutic applications. In the case of tumor diagnosis and treatment, the AuNPs must show increased retention in tumor tissues but reduced accumulation in other tissues. For instance, upon entering the bloodstream, gold nanoparticles rapidly undergo opsonization, resulting in the formation of a protein crown on their surface that promotes recognition by the phagocytic cells of the mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS). As the MPS is primarily located in the liver, spleen, and bone marrow, most AuNPs are efficiently cleared from circulation and preferentially accumulate in these organs.59 This confirms the biocompatibility of AuNPs for choosing the right receptors during the detection process. However, AuNP deposition in organs other than tumor tissues may result in adverse biological effects, predominantly characterized by acute inflammatory reactions and induction of apoptosis, as evidenced by the metabolic disruption of low-density lipoprotein caused by 13 nm AuNPs.60 This toxic effect can be recognized by the surface modification of AuNPs. Moreover, if we want to mitigate the toxicity, the removal of AuNPs from the healthy organs should be done as fast as possible. For example, up to 70% of the small AuNPs can be removed by renal clearance within 3 days.61

Catalytic activity

The catalytic performance reaches its maximum for nanoparticles with a diameter of approximately 2.1 ± 0.3 nm and a height of about six atomic layers. When the particle size falls below this threshold, the observed reduction in activity is associated with a decrease in height.62 Using aberration-corrected transmission electron microscopy, the catalytic activity of iron oxide – supported gold catalysts was shown to originate from sub-nanometer gold clusters (∼0.5 nm in diameter) consisting of approximately ten atoms per cluster, underscoring the decisive role of ultrasmall species in driving the catalytic performance.63 Under conditions where the bulk metal exhibits little to no catalytic activity, dispersed gold has been demonstrated to catalyze a wide range of reactions effectively.64,65 Unsupported AuNPs show intrinsic catalytic properties in some specific reactions, including CO oxidation, aerobic oxidation of alcohols and diols, borohydride reductions and carbon–carbon cross coupling reaction.66 In the case of enzyme-mimetic activity of AuNPs, several enzymes (nuclease, esterase, silicatein, glucose oxidase (GO), peroxidase (POD), catalase, superoxide dismutase and oxidase) have been used for a long time in the cancer diagnosis and therapeutic actions. The small Au nanoparticles functioned as nanozymes, catalyzing intracellular H2O2 within tumor cells to produce ˙OH radicals, thereby inducing oxidative damage and promoting tumor cell destruction.67 One study reported that a quantitative colorimetric immunoassay, in which glucose oxidase (GOx) catalyzes the growth of 5 nm gold nanoparticles, enables the detection of cancer biomarkers across an attomolar to picomolar concentration range.68

Targeting and delivery approach

It is undeniable that gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) have emerged as powerful platforms for cancer detection due to their unique properties. The targeting and delivery approach of gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) refers to the strategic design and surface engineering of AuNPs to control their biodistribution, circulation behavior, and selective accumulation at cancer-associated sites, thereby enabling efficient transport of the nanoparticles from the systemic circulation to specific cells, tissues, or molecular biomarkers for accurate cancer detection. However, effective targeting and delivery of AuNPs to tumor sites are crucial for enhancing detection sensitivity and specificity. The targeting action of AuNPs may be described by two components: passive targeting (the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, meaning that nanoparticles naturally accumulate in tumor tissues through leaky blood vessels and active targeting (biorecognition ligand-functionalized AuNPs bind on biomarkers of cancer cells along with stimuli-responsive targeting). Due to the rapid and uncontrolled growth of tumors, the formation of blood and lymphatic vessels becomes abnormal.69 As a result, efficient lymphatic drainage does not occur, leading to the retention of fluids, proteins, and nanoparticles inside the tumor microenvironment. Together, these abnormalities create the well-known enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, which enables preferential accumulation of gold nanoparticles (definite size) within tumor tissues compared to normal healthy organs.70 Relying on some ligands, the targeting action of AuNPs can recognize some cancerous cells, including Lam 67R,71 GRP,72 Human Prostrate tumor, CCR5 and HER2,73 for breast cancer. The biocompatibility and targeting action of the AuNP make it the most promising delivery agent for tumor as well as cancer detection. Nowadays, AuNPs became a part of chemotherapy drugs, proteins and nucleic acids through the electrostatic adsorption or covalent bond. For enhancing the therapeutic effect, AuNPs can carry mitoxantrone (MTX),74 phthalocyanine 4 (Pc4),75 and doxorubicin (Dox)76 in chemotherapy.

Mechanisms of nanoparticle-based colorimetric detection

Based on the plasmonic properties of the AuNP as a colorimetric substrate

Owing to their extraordinary optical absorption and scattering properties, AuNPs have been introduced to the colorimetric analysis strategy for cancer diagnosis, which offers high selectivity and sensitivity. The rapid development of these nanosensors has been possible because of these amazing plasmonic features. The localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) of plasmonic nanoparticles can shift upon the introduction of target molecules, driven by changes in particle dispersion or alterations in size and morphology.

Aggregation

The aggregation can offer interparticle plasmon coupling, which influences the LSPR shift, resulting in the color change of solution as a colorimetric signal.77 The regulated aggregation of AuNPs can be accomplished through cross-linking, non-crosslinking, or destabilization mechanisms.78 In cross-linking strategies, interactions between the target molecule and ligands on the AuNP surface form complexes that reduce the interparticle spacing, thereby inducing aggregation.79 Normally, monodisperse AuNPs exhibit red color, which eventually turns blue upon aggregation. Aggregation can be run by using bare modified AuNPs, but it lacks sensitivity as well as accuracy in detection approach. Therefore, to increase the sensitivity with high accuracy of AuNPs in the case of cancer-biomarker detection, this approach involves the direct immobilization of ligands, such as DNA, aptamers, peptides, and antibodies, onto the surface of AuNPs via chemical interactions before target molecule detection.80 Nowadays, several modified AuNPs are being prevalently used for the colorimetric assay in the cancer detection phenomenon.81 For example, Cathepsin B (EC 3.4.22.1) is widely recognized as a key contributor to tumor development, progression, invasion, and metastatic spread. Moreover, it plays a crucial role in cancer progression, ranging from gene amplification to posttranscriptional modification.82,83 However, it is reported that there are few cathepsins, such as L, H, and S, that protect normal cells from tumors.84 On the other hand, Cathepsin B has become a cancer biomarker. Besides, some of the cancers like brain, breast, colon, esophageal, gastric, lung, ovarian, and thyroid are related to this.85 Now, there has been a growing need to design a probe to selectively detect Cathepsin B, which does not require any advanced instrumentation. To ease the detection process of Cathepsin B, researchers have synthesized a Cathepsin B-specific substrate named DBDY-Gly-Phe-MTPA. Cathepsin B-catalyzed hydrolysis of DBDY-Gly-Phe-MTPA produced a fragment called Phe-MTPA, which went into interaction with AuNPs, leading to the aggregation of AuNPs. This aggregation phenomenon turned the red color to blue. Thus, Cathepsin B was detected with detection limits of 10 nM and 5 nM after 1 and 2 hours of hydrolysis.86

Growth and etching

The enzymatic or chemical conversion of catalytic seeds, such as Au3+ and Ag+, to promote the growth of small-sized AuNPs has emerged as a novel approach for developing biological and chemical colorimetric sensing strategies. The growth-based strategy involves the tuning of size or morphology of AuNPs. The reduction of AuCl4− to form AuNPs drives the transformation of small-sized AuNPs into larger or differently shaped nanoparticles, resulting in a visible color change in the system.78 Nowadays, this non-aggregation-based sensing strategy has been applied in cancer cell detection. It has been reported that enzymes can catalyze the reduction of Au3+ to AuNPs. For instance, an immunoassay has been introduced based on glucose oxidase (GOx)-catalyzed growth of AuNPs for the detection of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) at the attomolar level. It is known that solutions containing small-sized AuNPs at low concentrations (<10 nM) are typically nearly colorless. However, growth from about 5 nm to larger diameters (>10 nm) produces a distinct color transition from colorless to red. To apply this phenomenon to immunoassay, the antibody Ab2 was conjugated with glucose oxidase (GOx) on the surfaces of magnetic beads (MBs), namely, Ab2-GOx-MBs, while the antibody Ab1 was used as a detection probe at the recognition site. The target PSA pulls down the Ab2-GOx-MBs on the substrate polystyrene where GOx catalyzes the oxidation of glucose to form H2O2 that is responsible for increase in the size of AuNPs. Therefore, the formation of the immunocomplex Ab1-PSA-Ab2-Gox-MBs induces the color change in the solution from colorless to red color. Thus, by using a growth-mediated colorimetric method, PSA can be detected with a detection limit of 93 aM, which exceeds the commercial ELISA method (6.3 pM).68 Mainly, the etching-based mechanism involves dissolving the AuNPs to some extent, leading to a change in the LSPR shift and thus a change in color. Owing to their shape-dependent anisotropic optical properties, gold nanorods (AuNRs) are more suitable nanostructures than other shaped AuNPs for etching-based sensing strategies. The longitudinal surface plasmon resonance of AuNRs varies with their aspect ratio, leading to observable color changes; as the aspect ratio decreases, the color transition shifts from red toward blue.87 Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity monitoring, which can indicate disease progression, metastasis, and treatment response, can also be performed using enzyme-assisted Ag deposition on AuNPs. Here, ascorbic acid 2-phosphate is hydrolyzed to form ascorbic acid upon the addition of ALP on the substrate. This AA will reduce silver ions to metal silver that will deposit on AuNPs. This silver deposition causes a change in the LSPR shift, leading to a color change to purple.88

Nanoenzyme-based assay (enzyme-mimetic NPs)

Among the natural enzymes, horseradish peroxidase (HRP) is most employed as a peroxidase to catalyze dye oxidation, producing a color change for biomarker detection. However, these natural enzymes have several limitations such as being expensive and unstable and easy denaturation. That's the reason for using nano-enzymes (nanomaterial-based artificial enzymes) instead of natural enzymes. Moreover, nano-enzymes have been widely recognized for the biosensing of biomarkers due to their unique properties including high stability against denaturation and inactivation, easy synthesis, cost-effectiveness, high sensitivity and facile storage. Due to the enzyme-mimetic property, AuNPs can mimic the biological features of peroxidase, oxidase, catalase, reductase, etc.89

Catalyzed by peroxidase-mimetic NPs

Peroxidase-like catalytic sensing performance of AuNPs primarily relies on their surface functionalization, which is appropriately tailored to the specific nature of the target analyte. In the nanozyme sensors, there are some chromogenic substances, such as 3,3,5,5-tetramethylbenzi-dine (TMB), 2,2-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulphonic acid) (ABTS), and o-phenylenediamine (OPD), which are responsible for the generation of color rather than AuNPs itself.90 Several reports have claimed that the O–O bond of H2O2 can be broken up on the AuNP surface, producing ˙OH. This hydroxyl radical (˙OH) further oxidizes a peroxidase substrate to generate color changes.91 By using this technique, a lower LOD can be achieved improving the catalytic activity.92 Citrate is a renowned prostate-cancer biomarker. A novel system for the optical determination of citrate was successfully established using cysteine-capped AuNCs (Cys-AuNCs), which has intrinsic peroxidase-mimetic activity. Normally, Cys-AuNCs can catalyze the oxidation of TMB in the presence of H2O2 to produce blue color solutions. On the other hand, citrate has a carboxyl group and a hydroxyl group, which bind with the free amino acid group of cysteine, forming a coating on the surface of Cys-AuNCs. Thus, it inhibits the oxidation ability of AuNPs to the TMB. Therefore, the blue color fades away, and thus, the citrate was detected with a detection limit of 0.1 µM.93 Another study investigated that glutathione (GSH) can be detected by following the same mimetic activity of AuNCs stabilized by GSH (GSH-AuNCs) (Fig. 1). The disparity in GSH concentrations between normal and cancer cells is viewed as a critical parameter for cancer detection. In this method, GSH binds with the nanoclusters inhibiting its catalytic activity. The blue color fades due to the reduced oxidation of TMB.94

Catalysis by oxidase-mimetic NPs

There is an advantage of using oxidase-mimetic AuNPs rather than peroxidase-mimetic AuNPs because the former does not require H2O2 as an oxidizing agent, which is responsible for cellular damage. In recent years, oxidase-like nano-enzymes have demonstrated considerable potential in cancer detection and biosensing application. The Au@Pt nanostructures exhibit a behavior comparable to that of oxidase enzymes, catalyzing redox reactions in the presence of molecular oxygen. This preliminary observation motivated us to investigate the potential of Au@Pt nanostructures as oxidoreductase mimics.95 A simple colorimetric approach has been proposed by the researchers to detect alkaline phosphatase (ALP) using the oxidase-mimetic activity of histidine-stabilized AuNCs (His@AuNCs). Further, (PDDA)-coated GO was also added to His@AuNCs to increase the oxidase-mimetic activity. This method found that the pyrophosphate ion could inhibit the oxidase activity of His@AuNCs/GO. However, the ALP hydrolyzes the pyrophosphate ion that eventually enables His@AuNCs/GO to oxidize TMB, resulting in the color change to blue. By this method, Alp was detected with a low detection limit (LOD) of 0.26 mU mL−1 (S/N = 3).96

AuNPs and their composites associated with the colorimetric detection process

When the size of any metal is turned to nanometer level (1–100 nm), it possesses many unique and novel properties. For example, AuNPs, noble metallic nanoparticles, exhibit intrinsic surface plasmonic property (LSPR), light scattering property and a relatively high extinction coefficient, which combined with biological molecules to create hybrid nanosensors.97 Likewise, AgNPs are attractive materials for optical sensing applications owing to their distance-dependent plasmonic properties.98 Although AgNPs possess superior optical characteristics for colorimetric assays, including higher extinction coefficients than similarly sized AuNPs, gold nanoparticles continue to dominate the field of plasmonic biosensing.99 The widespread adoption of AuNPs is primarily due to their exceptional chemical stability and biocompatibility. In contrast, AgNPs are prone to surface oxidation and degradation during functionalization processes, which can adversely affect their colloidal stability, reproducibility, and sensing performance in complex biological environments.100,101 The enhanced stability of AuNPs relative to AgNPs has been well documented. For example, Mancuso et al.102 demonstrated that AuNPs can remain stable for more than one month at room temperature, while AgNPs exhibit a significantly shorter stability period of approximately two weeks. Due to their size-dependent SPR absorption, unmodified AuNPs appear red, while AgNPs typically exhibit yellow or dark brown coloration.103,104 A colorimetric approach has been introduced for H2S detection using silver nanoparticles for the early detection of prostate cancer.105 AuNPs are widely favored in biosensing applications due to their exceptional biocompatibility, stemming from the chemical inertness and low cytotoxicity of gold. By comparison, AgNPs may release Ag+ ions upon oxidation, potentially eliciting cytotoxic effects and compromising biological compatibility. More importantly, the controllable size, shape, and dispersion of gold nanomaterials make them highly suitable for sensitive sensing applications in cancer cell detection.106 Numerous studies have demonstrated the exploitation of the exceptional optical properties of AuNPs for cancer cell detection, either by directly identifying cancer cells or by indirectly monitoring biomarkers, such as proteins and small molecules. Monoclonal anti-HER2/c-erb-2 antibody and S6 RNA aptamer-conjugated AuNPs were used to detect breast cancer directly using colorimetric sensing technique. In this study, Lu and Tang reported highly selective and sensitive detection of breast cancer SK-BR-3 cell lines at a level of 100 cells per mL.73 On the other hand, AuNCs follow the enzyme-mimetic activity that induces color variation of dyes like TMB. Over the past decade, significant research efforts have focused on ultrasmall gold nanoclusters (AuNCs) owing to their peroxidase-like catalytic activity and potential utility in cancer diagnosis. According to Tao, BSA-AuNCs loaded with ErbB2/Her2 antibody-functionalized liposomes acted as a sensing platform for amplifying the colorimetric detection of HER2-positive breast cancer cells with a detection limit as low as 5 SKBR3 cells.107 Furthermore, AuNCs loaded with mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) and TMB with H2O2 were used to detect HER2-positive breast cancer using an amplified signal approach.108 Some researchers paid numerous efforts to develop a colorimetric sensor with an amplified signal using AuNCs incorporated with graphene oxides (GO). Tao et al. adsorbed AuNCs on the GO surface through electrostatic interaction, which exhibits peroxidase-like activity over a broad pH range even at neutral pH, enabling highly sensitive visual identification of cancer cells through the specific interaction between folic acid-functionalized graphene oxide and folate receptors expressed on the cell membrane, achieving a limit of detection as low as 1000 MCF-7 cells.107 Besides, a novel and sensitive detection method has been developed for the detection of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP), which has gelatinase-like activity. In this research, AuNPs@gelatin/AuNCs have been used, where the gelatin layer acts as a stabilizer, preventing the aggregation of AuNPs, thus showing red color. However, in the presence of MMP, the gelatinase activity destabilizes the gelatin layer, allowing the aggregation of MMP on the AuNP surface, which results in color change from red to purple with an LOD of 2 ng mL−1.109 A competitive colorimetric biosensor has been designed based on Au@Ag bimetallic nanoparticles for interleukin-6 (IL-6) detection. Here, a sandwich-like immunocomplex is prepared by using two antibodies (primary and secondary) on the polystyrene (PS) microparticles. PS carries the catalase that catalyzes the decomposition of H2O2, leading to the disposition of Ag+ on the AuNP surface. Thus, the formation of Au@Ag bimetallic nanoparticles occurs. This phenomenon causes the distinct plasmonic color changes controlled by the amount of IL-6 present in the sample.110 Nowadays, an ultrathin layer of Pt is added on the AuNPs for the enhancement of the catalytic activity of nanoparticles. A highly specific colorimetric assay has been introduced, in which the synergistic effect of PtAuNPs in the case of increasing catalytic activity is used. As the VEGF and MUC1 are over-expressed on the membrane of MCF-7 cancer cells, both AptVEGF-MB and AptMUC1-PtAuNP together act as bridge to recognize the cell, through the color change of oxidized TMB. Oxidization occurred by the decomposition of H2O2 by catalytic activity. By this method, they could detect cancer cells at concentrations as low as 10 cells per mL.111 Another study demonstrated that colorectal cancer (CRC) can be detected by using Au@Fe3O4 nanoparticles attached to the aptamer. Parvimonas micra (P. micra) serves as a biomarker for CRC. When it binds with the aptamer, it inhibited the peroxidase-like activity of Au@Fe3O4, thereby blocking the 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) chromogenic reaction causing color reduction. Thus, they detected P. micra with a limit of detection of 11 CFUs mL−1.112

Detection of target biomarkers

Cell

Cells themselves can act as powerful biomarkers for cancer detection, providing direct and biologically relevant information about disease presence, stage, and progression. Overexpressed proteins on the surface of cancer cells can serve as reliable biomarkers for the early-stage diagnosis of cancer.108,113 The recognition elements that bind to target cells primarily include antibodies, aptamers, folate, and other bioactive molecules.114 Antibodies are the most commonly used recognition elements because of their versatility and ease of integration with the cancer cell.115 On the other hand, aptamers have specific high affinity to the recognition site, and it also has some advantages, such as simple synthesis process, ease of modification, and thermal stability.

Oral epithelial cell lines (HOC313 clone 8 and HSC3)

Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) with distinct surface plasmon resonance (SPR) properties have emerged as promising biosensing platforms for cancer diagnostics. Colloidal AuNPs and anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (anti-EGFR) – conjugated AuNPs in nonmalignant (HaCaT) and malignant oral epithelial cell lines (HOC 313 and HSC 3 clone 8). There is overexpression of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) on many cancerous cells, including oral squamous cells. Anti-EGFR monoclonal antibodies were attached to the AuNPs. While anti-EGFR-AuNPs are mixed with the incubated cell, antibody-EGFR interaction followed lock and key mechanism to significantly identify the cancer cell. Only colloidal AuNPs failed to detect the cancerous cell, while the anti-EGFR-AuNPs successfully detected HOC313 clone 8 and HSC3. Thus, AuNPs preferentially remain on the cancer cell surface altering the local refractive index around the AuNPs, which produced a sharp adsorption band and a red-shift.116

MCF-7 cells (human breast cancer cell lines)

Advanced detection strategies employing aptamers enable the rational design of nucleic acid probes with well-defined structures and site-specific chemical modifications, facilitating controlled conjugation and the integration of sophisticated signal-transduction mechanisms. Nucleolin is a phosphoprotein that is highly expressed in the cytoplasm and on the plasma membrane of metastatic cells, while it is minimally expressed or absent in normal cells. AS1411 is a GC-rich DNA aptamer, which strongly binds with nucleolin modified with AuNPs.117,118 Therefore, no AS1411 was present in the solution to hybridize with complementary ssDNA-AuNPs, which form a red color solution. On the other hand, in the absence of target cells or presence of normal cells, the aptamer AS1411 and ssDNA-AuNPs probe coexisted in the solution and the cross-linking of aptamer with ssDNA-AuNPs formed a purple color solution. This detection of MCF-7 cell was executed by an anti-aggregation colorimetric method with a detection limit of 10 cells. It is one of the most reliable direct cancer cell detection methods based on aptamer–cell interaction.119

CCRF-CEM cells (lymphoblastic leukemia)

A novel strategy has been developed for the detection of rare cancers like CCRF-CEM cells by using a colorimetric aptasensor based on the cell-triggered cyclic enzymatic signal amplification (CTCESA), in which the cell-recognition event converted into a visible color change. Hairpin aptamer probes (HAPs) and linker DNAs coexisted in the solution, in the absence of target cell, CCRF-CEM. Thus, DNAs could assemble around the AuNPs producing the purple color solution. On the contrary, in the presence of target cell, the specific binding of HAPs to the target cell triggered a conformational change to cleave DNAs to several fractions by means of enzymatic cyclic cleavage. As a result, DNAs could no longer assemble around AuNPs, which results in red coloration due to the remaining AuNPs dispersed in the solution. This method could produce response for CCRF-CEM with a detection limit of 40 cells, which is 20 times lower than that using only AuNP-based colorimetric assay.120

HL-60, K562, HepG2, and 293T

HL-60 (human promyelocytic leukemia) cells are rare cancerous cells. Telomerase is a widely renowned biomarker, which is overexpressed by the HL-60 cell (telomerase-rich biological model). Once telomerase was detected by the conventional method called the telomeric repeat amplification protocol (TRAP), which is very time-consuming and susceptible to contamination. For that reason, scientists searched for a better and trustworthy method to detect this biomarker easily. Recently, a highly sensitive colorimetric strategy to detect telomerase has been introduced based on primer-modified AuNPs regulated by exonuclease I (Exo I). The sensing mechanism relies on the distinct enzymatic digestion behavior of Exo I toward the substrate nucleic acid present on the AuNP surface. In the absence of telomerase, Exo I digested the nucleic acid of AuNPs, inducing the aggregation of AuNPs, which results in color change of solution. In contrast, in the presence of telomerase, the enzyme promotes the extension of the primer DNA by adding successive telomeric repeat sequences. This elongation of the DNA primer produced a complex named the G-quadruplex structure, which is resistant to the digestion of Exo I. This phenomenon protected AuNPs from aggregation, and thus, the dispersed form showed red color which is stable in nature. Notably, this method enables direct visual detection of telomerase activity with the naked eye at a concentration as low as 100 HL-60 cancer cells per mL, with a calculated detection limit of 29 HL-60 cells per mL.121

SK-BR-3 cell

The researchers have designed a simple, fast and sensitive detection method using oval-shaped AuNPs, which were functionalized by both anti-HER2 monoclonal antibodies and S6 RNA aptamers to specifically recognize HER2, a protein highly overexpressed on SK-BR-3 breast cancer cells. These two ligands have strong affinity to HER2. When the functionalized AuNPs were mixed with SK-BR-3 cells, both the antibody and the aptamer were recognized and bonded to the HER2 receptors following the lock and key model. AuNPs bound to neighboring receptors come very close together, and thus, localized aggregation of gold nanoparticles occurred on the cell membrane. This method was able to distinguish between other breast cancer cells (MDA-MB-231) and non-cancerous cells (HaCaT). Besides, this assay clearly demonstrated that it was highly sensitive to only SK-BR-3 cells.73

HeLa cells (human cervical cancer cells)

Gold nanorods (GNRs) conjugated with folic acid (F-GNRs) were used to detect HeLa cells. F-GNRs acted as a targeted optical probe. The purpose of folic acid used here is the overexpression of foliate receptors on cancer cells (HeLa cells). When F-GNRs were introduced into the cell suspension, the folic acid bounded to the folate receptors on HeLa cells. Multiple F-GNRs attach to the same cell membrane, causing local accumulation, and strong interparticle plasmon coupling leads to the progressive fading or lightening of the solution color. Thus, by observing the color change with naked eye, one can highly select the HeLa cell despite the presence of another cell, such as Vero cell. The absorbance change of the solution was determined by UV-vis spectroscopy denoted by ΔA. By using this novel and reliable colorimetric assay, people can detect HeLa cells at a concentration as low as ∼10 cells per mL.122

Enzymes

Matrilysin (MMP-7)

Proteases, especially matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), have received substantial attention in recent years owing to their critical role in regulating cellular behavior and their involvement in a broad spectrum of pathological conditions, including cancer, AIDS, and inflammatory disorders.123 MMP-7, also known as matrilysin-1, is secreted by epithelial cells, which is overexpressed in many cancers associated with tumor invasion and metastasis, enhanced cell migration, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and poor prognosis. Based on the colorimetric strategy, peptide-functionalized AuNPs have been used to detect Matrilysin (MMP-7), as it is an indicator of salivary gland cancer.124 Here, as the AuNPs are peptide functionalized, the peptide present on the surface of AuNPs makes AuNPs remain dispersed, showing red color at a stable stage. However, in the presence of MMP-7, it cuts down the peptide into small parts, so that the AuNPs can aggregate resulting in a color change. Thus, quantitative measurement of MMP-7 was possible with an estimated limit of detection of 5 nM (0.1 mg mL−1).

Protein kinase C (PKCα)

Protein kinases are essential mediators of cell differentiation, growth, and apoptosis, and dysregulated or excessive activation of specific kinases has been extensively reported in a wide range of diseases.125 Moreover, protein kinase has been broadly implicated in the disease diagnosis.126 PKCα is often overactivated on cancer cells, hence it is considered as one of the target biomarkers. Citrate capped-AuNPs are negatively charged NPs. Therefore, in normal cases it remains dispersed because of the electrostatic repulsion. A method detected PKCα enzymatic activity using a PKCα-specific cationic peptide substrate. In the presence of PKCα in cancer cells, kinase-mediated phosphorylation of a cationic peptide substrate occurs. This phenomenon increases the negatively charged phosphate groups, suppressing AuNP non-cross-linking aggregation, which form red color in the solution. On the other hand, in the absence of PKCα, no phosphorylation occurs. Therefore, the solution color changes from red to blue.51

Proteins

Folate-binding protein (FBP; alpha isoform (FRα))

The alpha isoform of the folate receptor (FRα), also referred to as folate-binding protein (FBP), is a cancer-associated antigen and a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored membrane protein that exhibits high-affinity folate binding and plays a crucial role in mediating folate transport into cells.127 Folate is an essential cofactor in cellular metabolism and DNA synthesis and repair, and the elevated proliferative activity of cancer cells results in an increased demand for folate to sustain continuous DNA replication, a concept underscored by the extensive clinical use of antifolate agents in cancer chemotherapy.128 A specific and sensitive colorimetric method has been analyzed to detect folic acid, which is a cancer biomarker. Here, a tiny nanocomposite, Au-Fe2O3, which is factionalized with folic acid, has been used as a cancer recognition transducer. In the modification process, MUA (bifunctional linker) is added to Au-Fe2O3, just prior to the addition of folic acid because it provides a thiol group (–SH) and a carboxyl group (–COOH). These groups enabled efficient covalent attachment of folic acid through EDC/NHS chemistry, improving nanoparticle stability and dispersibility. Moreover, there is a foliate receptor on the surface of cancer cells, which specifically binds with FA/MUA-Au-Fe2O3 CNPs (Fig. 2a). At this stage, no color change occurred. As the Au-Fe2O3 CNPs shows peroxidase-like activity, it speeds up the oxidation reaction of TMB by H2O2 (Fig. 2b). This phenomenon produces blue color solution, after some time which turns to yellow color. Thus, the visual detection of cancer cells, as low as 50–500 cells per mL, has been possible.129

Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)

Among tumor-associated antigens, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) is one of the most used and clinically validated biomarkers for cancer diagnosis and prognosis. It has been found that CEA is over-expressed on numerous types of cancer cells, such as colorectal, stomach, pancreas, liver, ovarian, breast, prostate, thyroid, bladder, kidney, and lung.130,131 Thus, serum CEA quantification constitutes an essential tool for primary cancer diagnosis and follow-up assessment of therapeutic response. Liu et al. has worked on novel colorimetric methods based on immunoassay for detecting carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA). In this method, AuNPs are modified with anti-CEA antibodies with a biotin (acts as affinity tag) that strongly binds with Streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase (streptavidin-HRP). By this, 12 HRP molecules are well coated onto each AuNP, which enhances signal generation. A sandwich-type immunocomplex is formed when AuNPs-anti CEA-HRP is mixed with the CEA-containing solution. The AuNPs-anti CEA-HRP is taken to the proximity of antigen associated with antibody-coated magnetic microparticles (MMPs). The adherence of both complexes by means of antigen allows HRP confinement. Thus, HRP molecules were confined to the surface of the sandwich-type immunocomplex, which catalyzed the immunoreaction that actually produced the optical signal.132

Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and mucin-16 (MUC16)

Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is a glycoprotein predominantly produced by the fetal liver and yolk sac, while only trace amounts are present in other tissues.133 It is noteworthy that the level of AFP becomes stable after the age of two years,134 but the significantly increasing concentration of AFP can be the indication of liver cancer. The AFP levels remain normal in adults usually below 10 ng mL−1. However, when it significantly elevates to >200 ng mL−1, there is a high chance of primary liver cancer. When the AFP levels exceed 500 ng mL−1, then surely hepatocellular carcinoma can be diagnosed.135–138 To overcome the limitations of the conventional method in AFP detection, a portable colorimetric approach has been developed by the recent researchers. This novel approach improves AFP detection by providing a portable, accurate, and cost-effective solution, making it especially well-suited for use in resource-limited settings. Here, colloidal AuNPs were functionalized with anti-AFP antibodies, which were specific to the AFP antigen. Upon exposure of AFP of different concentrations to functionalized AuNPs in the incubation tube, aggregation occurred due to the antibody–antigen interaction on the AuNP surface. This aggregation produces grey-blue color from the purple-red color of the solution, resulting in a greater spectral shift. This method also cross-checks the selectivity of AuNPs towards AFP using MUC-16 proteins. In the case of MUC-16, there was a minimal spectral shift detected. It is also possible to detect AFP using unmodified-AuNPs, but there is a risk of selectivity with other interfering biomarkers. Therefore, perfectly functionalized AuNPs offer great sensitivity and selectivity to AFP detection rather than unmodified AuNPs. This detection process could accurately detect AFP as low as 0.083 ng mL−1.139 There is another study in which both the AFP and MUC-16 biomarkers were detected. These two biomarkers are well known as markers of hepatocellular carcinoma as well as ovarian cancer. Here, cysteine-coated AuNPs (AuNP-Cys) create a ‘skeleton’-like site, where the antibody can attach easily via functionalization. The immobilization of antibody on the AuNP-Cys surface forms AuNP-Cys/Ab. The antigen present on the cancerous cell gets attached to the AuNP-Cys/Ab surface. In this method, AFP or MUC16 solutions at different concentrations were applied as spots modified with AuNP-Cys/Anti-AFP or AuNP-Cys/Anti-MUC16 on the NC membrane, and different intensities of color were observed which were responsible for the production of a specific signal for AFP and MUC16. In this point-of-care (POC) test, the limit of detection was calculated as 1.054 ng mL−1 and 0.413 ng mL−1 for AFP and MUC16, respectively (Fig. 3).140

Exosomal proteins

Exosomes are membrane-enclosed vesicles, which carry molecular information of their parent cell and are enriched with membrane proteins and genetic materials in the case of cell–cell connection.141,142 Notably, the exosome surface displays a patchwork-like array of protein markers, and specific combinations of these markers can be used to predict the tissue origin of the parent tumors.143 Besides, the responses of its protein pattern seem to be identical to metabolic as well as oxidative stress between their parents and produced exosomes.144 Therefore, exosomes are increasingly recognized as noninvasive diagnostic biomarkers for cancer, owing to the informative profiling of their surface protein signatures.145 Recently, researchers have demonstrated a colorimetric method to capture and profile exosomal information in a straightforward manner. In this method, aptamers can act as recognition moieties as well as protect AuNPs from being aggregated in the solution. Aptamers are short nucleic acid oligomers that can be a good replacement for the antibody in the case of targeted molecular recognition, which sometimes shows excellent binding affinity towards cell-surface proteins.146 First, AuNPs are functionalized with aptamers, which stabilizes the AuNPs, preventing them from aggregation in high-salt concentrations. When the exosomes are introduced with the Apt/AuNPs, the aptamer tends to strongly bind with the proteins of exosome surface detached from the AuNP surface. This phenomenon results in the aggregation of AuNPs, showing a color change of the solution. Based on the interactions between specific aptamers and exosome surface proteins, aptamer–AuNP complexes can be detected through both visual observation and quantitative analysis. For example, AptCD63/AuNP was used to detect exosomal CD63 by the colorimetric assay. CD63 present on different exosomes show binding to the same aptamer depending on their level of CD63 production.147 Moreover, AptEpCAM, AptPDGF, AptPSMA, and AptPTK7 interact with AuNPs in the presence of different exosomes, and protein markers, including EpCAM, PDGF, PSMA, and PTK7, profiled on HeLa, PC-3, CEM, and Ramos exosomes, respectively, can be detected by following this simple POC method.148,149

p53 protein function

The p53 tumor suppressor functions as a sequence-specific transcription factor that binds to DNA response elements (REs) and modulates cell fate decisions in response to cellular stress signals that threaten genomic integrity.150 The binding of p53 to DNA response elements (REs) activates the transcription of downstream target genes that trigger distinct cellular programs, including apoptosis, DNA repair, and cell-cycle arrest.151 Moreover, it acts like a ‘safeguard’ against tumorigenesis. Dysfunctional p53 proteins, most commonly rendered inactive by missense mutations, are closely linked to cancer initiation and progression, and therefore, represent clinically relevant targets for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications.152 particularly, ‘hotspot’ p53 mutations are associated with increased cancer penetrance and higher allelic frequencies across multiple tumor types.153 In this review, we report a simple, rapid, label-free and versatile colorimetric assay for wildtype-p53 detection in the complex solution. Here, AuNPs are modified with double-stranded canonical DNA containing p53 response elements (RE–AuNPs). Upon exposure of p53-WT to the RE–AuNPs, p53 undergoes tetramerization while binding with DNA, exerting a cross-linking effect, which alters the interparticle distance between RE and AuNPs. This phenomenon leads to the color change from red to blue, which can be observed by the naked eye. On the other hand, no color change was detected for DNA-binding – deficient p53 mutants or non-specific proteins, as they lack the ability to induce the crosslinking of RE–AuNPs. Based on this sensing principle, a quantifiable amount of wildtype-p53 was detected with a detection limit of 5 nM.55

Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)

Prostate-specific antigen (PSA), a glycoprotein, is abnormally expressed in individuals with prostate cancer and is recognized as a key serum biomarker for the disease.154 Serum total PSA (TPSA) levels ≥4 µg L−1 are strongly linked to a higher risk of developing prostate cancer.155 However, the quantification of PSA suffers a lot due to the specificity because the serum level of PSA may be increased by various types of benign prostatic conditions.156 A colorimetric method for the clinical detection of PSA has been reported by using AuNPs. The method relies on the in situ generation of AuNPs driven by ascorbic acid (AA) and the Cu2+-catalyzed oxidation of AA. Generally, HAuCl4 is reduced to AuNPs by ascorbic acid (AA). Here, Cu2+ catalyzes the oxidation of AA by O2 to inhibit the formation of AuNPs. In the presence of PSA-specific peptide (DAHSSKLQLAPP) – modified gold-coated magnetic microbeads (MMBs, denoted as DAHSSKLQLAPP-MMBs), Cu2+ ions were sequestered by the MMBs through DAH-Cu2+ coordination. This interaction suppressed the Cu2+-catalyzed oxidation of ascorbic acid (AA), thereby enabling the formation of red-colored AuNPs. On the contrary, the peptide immobilized on the MMBs was cleaved by PSA, and the DAHSSKLQ segment was released. The rest of the LAPP segment on the MMB surface could not bind with Cu2+. Therefore, Cu2+ could actively catalyze AA oxidation. Thus, no AuNPs could form. In this method, the amount of PSA was found to be as low as 0.02 ng mL−1.157

Nucleic acids

Nucleic acid (DNA and RNA) acts as a robust cancer biomarker by reflecting tumor-specific genetic and epigenetic alterations in bodily fluids, such as blood, thereby supporting early detection. Early molecular events in malignancy involve gene mutations and aberrant gene expression, and early-stage tumor treatment has been reported to be effective in up to 95% of cases.158,159 Therefore, it has become a great demand for developing an effective probe to detect genes with rapidness and simplicity.

miRNA-21

Aberrant expression of specific miRNAs has been associated with numerous diseases, particularly cancer.160,161 For example, miRNA-21 is frequently overexpressed in a wide range of malignancies, including breast, prostate, gastric, and lung cancers.162 Though the detection of miRNA is quite challenging due to short length and low concentration, it can be a good biomarker for early cancer detection. A paper-based nanozyme genosensor has been introduced to detect miRNA-21. This method allows cysteamine-capped AuNPs (Cys-AuNPs) to exhibit enzyme-like activity. First, miRNA hybridizes with complementary DNA. Then, the Cys-AuNPs, being positively charged, interact with the negatively charged phosphate group at the end of DNA through electrostatic interactions. It is already reported that Cys-AuNPs possess peroxidase-mimetic activity. Therefore, it induced the oxidation of TMB in the presence of H2O2, which helped in switching the color to blue. This novel method could detect the miRNA with a detection limit of 0.5 pM.163

DNA

As the cancer originates from genetic alteration in DNA, DNA must be a cancer biomarker. Besides, changes in DNA sequences may inactivate the tumor suppressor, leading to the uncontrolled cell growth. A sensitive and selective dual amplification system has been reported to detect DNA in the biological system (target human immunodeficiency virus, THIV) by the assembly of DNA and protein. In this method, biotinylated captured DNA strongly binds with streptavidin (SA) through the biotin-streptavidin reaction. Then, the target DNA assembled with the biotinylated capture DNA-streptavidin with the help of linker DNA and auxiliary DNA (H1 and H2). Subsequently, HRP-SA and biotinylated bovine serum albumin Bio-BSA were assembled at the junction of the DNA-assembled structures, forming a large polymer. After that the addition of TMB went oxidized in the presence of H2O2, resulting in a blue color solution. This proposed strategy was able to detect DNA as low as 1.75 pmol L−1.164

Organic small molecules

Besides large molecules like proteins and nucleic acids, there are also some small organic molecules, which may be potential biomarkers for cancer detection at an early age. Citrate is a biomarker for prostate cancer detection that can be detected by using electrochemical as well as colorimetric methods using modified AuNPs.93,165 Moreover, prostate cancer cell is engaged in the production of cystathionine β-synthase and cystathionine γ-lyase which in turn are responsible for the increasing amount of H2S generation.166 Another bithiol, GSH, can be detected by using a colorimetric approach based on arginine-modified AuNPs (Arg-AuNPs) with accurate and rapid response. In this method, GSH detection was inspired by the mercury–thiol interaction. First, Hg2+ assembled on the surface of Arg-AuNPs, leading to aggregation showing a blue color solution. On the other hand, in the presence of GSH, the higher affinity of Hg2+ towards GSH outweighs the affinity to arginine placed on the surface of AuNPs. Then, the Arg-AuNPs became dispersed in the solution, showing a red color solution. This is basically an anti-aggregation process, and hence, researchers were able to detect GSH as low as 10.9 nM.167 In recent years, for the early cancer detection, some volatile organic compounds (VOCs) have been considered as cancer biomarkers, especially in the case of lung cancer. These VOCs can also be detected using AuNPs in an easy and effective way.168 Exosomes, a subtype of extracellular vehicles (EVs), possess unique structural and functional properties and have become a major focus of research as a cancer biomarker in recent years. A competitive study has been developed to detect exosomes derived from non-small-cell lung cancers (NSCLCs) using a lateral flow aptamer assay (LFAA). In this method, the streptavidin (SA)-biotin-CD63 aptamer was deposited on the T-line of the test kit. The exosome samples expressing CD63 as a specific transmembrane protein competitively interact with the CD63 aptamer-functionalized AuNPs. When the ligands pass through the test line, it produces color which is inversely proportional to the amount of exosomes present.169

Strategies of sensing approaches/biorecognition elements

Optical cancer sensors have been developed by exploiting the unique advantages of optical transduction techniques combined with nanomaterials.170 Optical detection involves monitoring optical signals, including absorption, transmittance, fluorescence, and chemiluminescence, generated after analyte binding. Among all these techniques, colorimetric analysis has gained the most popularity because it shows colors that can be detected by the naked eye.171

Colorimetric transduction

Paper-based colorimetric detection technology has been widely applied not only in cancer detection172 but also in other chronic diagnostics.173 Colorimetric assays show response in the presence of a target analyte even at a little concentration by the generation of color. Functional NPs serve as effective signal amplifiers by accelerating signal transduction, enhancing signal generation, and facilitating rapid readout, thereby increasing analytical performance and extending the utility of diagnostic assays.174 For certain applications, NPs can be functionalized with a diverse range of surface chemistries to construct multifunctional nanoprobes that support precise target recognition, effective signal amplification, and user-friendly signal detection.175 Tailored functionalization of nanoparticles should be introduced in the colorimetric sensing application by using effective capping agents as well as a bioconjugation method. A suitable capping agent provides high stability and possesses strong anti-interference ability of AuNP-based sensors that is significant in sensing complex biological samples.176 Surface modification can be carried out by several newer methods, such as applying click chemistry,177 ligand exchange strategy,178 and coordination-based recognition.179 Moreover, the functionalization of nanoparticle surfaces with appropriate ligands can significantly enhance their analytical properties, thereby increasing the sensitivity and selectivity of colorimetric assays.180 This introduction of color may be carried out by any enzymes, dyes or nanoparticles like AuNPs.181 Actually, when the change in the optical properties of AuNPs offers SPR shifts or structural changes, the colorimetric assay investigates the absorbance of the solution as a chromogenic agent. The color sensing process normally follows some chemical reactions involving aggregation, non-aggregation, enzyme-like activity, etc. Generally, AuNPs change red color to blue or vice versa in the case of aggregation and anti-aggregation, respectively. Other nanoparticles which show optical property may show color variations in this colorimetric detection analysis. The physicochemical properties of AuNPs are susceptible to changes during improper storage, which can cause non-uniform interparticle distances and uncertainties in cluster size and organization. These variations may adversely affect signal consistency and the reproducibility of diagnostic assays.182 For managing these types of issues, optimum storage conditions should be set up, such as preserving NPs at room temperature, which may decrease the rate of degradation of elements on the surface of AuNPs.183 Catalytic reactions or enzyme-mediated conversion of chromogenic substrates represent one of the most widely used approaches in colorimetric sensing. This strategy relies on the interaction between an enzyme and its specific substrate, forming an enzyme-substrate complex that leads to the development of a measurable color signal.93 This color producing ability makes this sensing strategy more interesting. Even an illiterate person can detect any kinds of target analytes only by observing the color of the solution. At a preliminary stage, if anyone can detect something with a naked eye, he/she can take further diagnostic approaches for confirmation.

Signal amplification

Color produced by the colorimetric sensors as a qualitative analysis of any particular analyte can be also detected quantitatively by some signal readout devices, including smartphone, cameras, and scanners.184 Among all of these devices, recently, smartphones have been the most widely used devices for sensing because of their advantageous features, including user-friendly operation, high-resolution cameras, advanced operating systems, and wireless connectivity, which together enable real-time analysis. This is also used for personalized diagnostic tools in the simplest way. The ceaseless use of electronics offers us new apps, which can be installed in smartphones in which cameras can act as a sensing platform as ‘smart gadgets’ in the biomarker detection process. Sometimes, senior citizens cannot go for regular health check-ups due to movement problems. By using this sensor, anyone can easily investigate their physical condition. Therefore, some researchers have demonstrated that by using a portable device for colorimetric immunoassays, such as smartphone, even early cancer detection may be possible.185 As the system entirely depends on the color sensing approach, the image capture should be perfect. For the perfect photo capture, a clean, well-lightened and detailed studio is required, under hygienic conditions. Especially, ambient light conditions must be ensured while capturing images, so that it can avoid the affecting sensitivity of the colorimetric assay. The background of the image must be white in the case of colored ones. Some experiments use a white or black box to maintain light interferences.29 To analyze the intensity of color with respect to the concentration of the analyte, the captured image is transferred to a computer or smartphone, where specific software is loaded. There are some inbuilt features of the app to quantify the parameters, such as red, green and blue (RGB), and measure the specific value of these R, G, B as well as the color saturation. There are some color-observing apps available which have already been used in the colorimetric sensing strategy, including gimp, ImageJ, and MOS image sensors.186–188 After that, by applying all the values, a calibration curve can be found. Thus, the concentration of an unknown biomarker can be calculated, which can save several thousands of people at the early stage of cancer disease. In some scenarios, human eye can be biased in the case of color interpretation. Hence, researchers have designed monitors that can visually recognize color in an accurate manner, such as distance-based, counting-based, text-based, and time-based readouts.189,190

Dual-signal readouts

Beyond enhancing the signal intensity for the detection of biomarkers at low concentrations, minimizing false-positive/negative responses to achieve accurate and highly sensitive readouts remains a critical challenge in the colorimetric detection of early-stage cancer cells. Internal control helps to verify the stability of the results of the detection platform. To enhance the sensitivity and facilitate the straightforward quantification of target DNA/RNA samples, the detection system was directly integrated into an amorphous/nanocrystalline silicon device, without requiring surface functionalization of the glass substrate.191 to verify the calculated data in the colorimetric process, it is combined with other techniques which provide relatively more accurate and reliable results. Tao Yu et al. discovered a method for the first time to detect cancer cells, HL-60 cells, by combining non-cross-linking AuNP aggregation and dual-signal amplification for colorimetric analysis. Biocompatible nanosensors were made by using five different types of mDNA, which were added to the cancer aptamer on the surface of magnetic beads (mDNAs-Apt-MBs). The first signal amplification caused by the presence of targets, when the aptamer tends to bind with receptors of cancer cells, results in the release of multiple mDNAs. The second amplification occurred by the enzymatic cleavage of mDNAs into several numbers of ssDNAs. After that, the ssDNAs and mDNAs were adsorbed on the AuNPs so that it could not participate in aggregation, leading to the formation of red color, which was observable. By this proposed strategy, an HL-60 cell was detected with the LOD 4 cells.192 In addition, dual signal amplification can be carried out by using nanomaterial-based artificial enzyme-like activity. A breast cancer cell was detected by using a dual-signal read-out method: one is based on target-receptor recognition signal, and the other one is based on the peroxidase-mimetic activity of AuNCs loaded with liposomes (AuNCs-LPs). At first, HER2 antibodies were immobilized on the AuNCs-LPs surface of formed BSA-AuNCs-LPs-anti-HER2 (Fig. 4a), which could successfully target the HER2 cell by the cell-receptor recognition. Second, AuNCs offered peroxidase-like activity that tend to oxidize TMB in the presence of H2O2, leading to the production of colored solutions (Fig. 4b). This combination approach improved the detection system and also became a promising diagnostic platform.193

Another colorimetric sensor has been developed to detect cancer cells using a dual aptamer-mediated target binding method. Here, a ‘detection kit’ has been designed in which AptMUC1-PtAuNP and AptVEGF-MB mixed well, which acted as a signal probe and capture probe, respectively. In the presence of MUC1, an AptMUC1-PtAuNP/MCF-7/AptVEGF-MB complex was formed. The color production is also associated with the enzymatic activity of PtAuNP, resulting in the oxidation of TMB in the presence of H2O2. Thus, yellow color generation confirmed the cancer cell detection.111

Operational approach of sensing devices/technical approach

Paper-based sensors (µPADs)

According to the literature-based context for smartphone-based colorimetric sensors to detect biomarkers, there are several methods available. Among them, we will discuss the spot-like technique in this review (Fig. 5a).194,195 Here, a novel spot-test-like sensor has been developed by using Cys-conjugated AuNPs which act as color providers for the detection of cancer biomarkers. In this method, Cys-AuNPs were anchored on the NC membrane, following the immobilization of anti-AFP or anti-MUC16 antibodies on the nanoparticle surface. Then, different concentrations of AFO or MUC16 were appended on the spots on the NC membrane. Thus, the color produced and this method could quantitatively estimate the biomarkers.140 For the first time, a platform for bioassay was launched that could be an alternative to the expensive instruments comprising hydrophilic paper surrounded by hydrophobic walls. This method was able to detect glucose in the urine samples.196 One prominent reason for using paper strips is that it can be used anywhere without laboratory settings, which provide reliable results via a color change approach. Microfluidic paper-based analytical devices (µPADs) are distinguished by their ability to transport fluids through a porous paper microfluidic network, enabling simultaneous and multiparametric detection of multiple analytes from a single sample droplet.197,198 Moreover, this type of test strips is highly recommended in microfluidic immunoassays for the ease of portability as well as simplicity. Owing to their simplicity, these paper-based devices are referred to as lab-on-a-chip (LOC) devices.199,200 The microfluidic paper-based analytical devices (µPADs) are prepared by modifying cellulose and this property has made the sensor disposable and cheap. Moreover, this method has the capability to transport and absorb reagents within the paper substrate, which avoids the demand of regent solution handling by users (Fig. 5b). Besides, a small piece of litmus paper is impregnated with a chromophore that tends to be replaced by the ligands, which changes the color of the solution in the presence of metals.

Furthermore, the paper-based analysis technique also demands the easy fabrication procedure, including wax printing, screen-printing, and inkjet printing.172,202 µPADs offer the microfluid channel network associated with the patterned paper, which can automatically insist flow to the device.21 The strategy of the paper-based sensor is described here. First, the sample is added on the paper strips. Then, due to capillary action, it reaches the center of strips in which the antibody-containing AuNPs remain. After that, the antigen of the sample binds the antibody on the surface of AuNPs by the antibody–antigen interaction. Thus, the color change captured gives positive results on test line.203,204 In most cases, fluid flow within a hydrophilic paper is confined and precisely regulated through the patterning of hydrophilic barriers. However, as the reaction involving colorimetric analysis occurs on a hydrophilic paper, it is also considered as the reaction center. Almost all the filter papers can be used for this purpose but the mostly appreciated one is Whatman filter paper because of its excellent wicking capability, large surface-area-to-volume ratio, effective particle retention, and rapid fluid transport.205 µPADs are advantageous for various reasons: they require a low amount of sample and also show rapid detection.206 Another paper-based assays and microfluidic platforms have been designed using enzymatic actions of AuNPs for multiplexed analysis.201

Lateral flow assay (LFA)

Among all the detection assays, lateral flow assay (LFA) has become one of the most widely accepted techniques in the detection of food safety, environmental supervision and clinical diagnosis. The development of dedicated strip readers has further stimulated interest in leveraging the enhanced performance of LFAs.207 Recently multiplex analytes, including cancer biomarkers and nucleic acids, are determined in a simultaneous way by the advancement of the lateral flow assay (LFA).208 An LFA is generally composed of four parts: a sample pad, a conjugate pad with labeled biorecognition probes, a nitrocellulose membrane bearing test and control lines, and an absorbent pad to remove excess fluid.209 The main basic assay format for LFA is of two types known as sandwich lateral flow assay (LFAs) and competitive lateral flow assay (LFAc). The former one is related to the bulky molecular compounds which are composed of two distinct recognition sites that increase the specificity and accuracy of the detection technique. In this strategy, the analyte is first captured by a primary antibody immobilized on the conjugate pad, forming a complex that subsequently interacts with a transducer, typically a colorimetric probe, conjugated to a secondary antibody and immobilized at the test line. The accumulation of the target at the test line generates a positive signal, whose intensity increases with higher analyte concentrations in the sample.210,211 Depending on the biorecognition elements, such as antibody or aptamers, the LFA assay can be categorized into two parts (Fig. 6a), including antibody-based lateral flow immunoassays (LFIAs) and aptamer-based LFAs.212

On the other hand, competitive lateral flow assays (LFAc) are particularly suitable for small molecules possessing a single antigenic determinant. In this format, the analyte competes for and occupies the binding sites at the test line, thereby inhibiting the interaction with the labeled conjugates (Fig. 6b). As a result, increasing the target concentration leads to a decrease in signal intensity. In the case of point-of-care (POC) pregnancy test, the LFA assay has been commercially approved all over the world. Rivas et al. developed a highly sensitive AuNP-based LFA by incorporating wax delay pillars onto the nitrocellulose membrane, which generated controlled flow delays and pseudo-turbulence, resulting in a three-fold increase in sensitivity compared with conventional LFAs.215 Recently, the AuNP has been widely used as a colorimetric probe in this LFA sensors due to the unique optical properties and ease of functionalization approach to the target recognition. Overall, the LFA is considered as the most attractive analytical tool due to its low cost, user-friendly format, extended shelf life, high sensitivity and specificity, low sample consumption, capability for multiplex analysis, and wide range of applications.

Barcode readers

A bio-barcode sensor is a bioanalytical tool that amplifies detection signals using DNA “barcodes” in an ultrasensitive manner instead of relying only on a single label per target. The colorimetric bio-barcode assay is a highly sensitive method for protein-based biomarker detection for a miniscule number of targets based on a red-to-blue colorimetric shift. AuNP-based DNA detection by a colorimetric approach has already been introduced, which lacks the sensitivity of detection process.216,217 For that reason, this review will introduce a new strategy called bio-barcode amplification, which consists of synthesized oligonucleotides, AuNPs and magnetic particles. This method also does not require any enzymatic amplification.218 This assay is based on the controlled release of thiolated oligonucleotides adsorbed on the gold nanoparticle surface using dithiothreitol (DTT), thereby simplifying the assay design and enhancing its quantitative performance.219 Actually, the barcode associated with DNA helps to locate the target tumor cells present in the human body. This strategy involves several steps in the detection technique. The assay entails conjugating target capture molecules and DNA barcodes onto silica microparticles, capturing the target with specific probes, releasing the barcode DNA from the separated complexes, detecting the barcodes using DNA-functionalized gold nanoparticles, and quantifying the red-to-blue colorimetric change with the graphic analysis software (Fig. 7a).220 AuNPs serve as a carrier barcode-DNA in which DNA strands can be directly attached on the nanoparticle surface via a thiol bond.221 Bio-barcode assays provide the required ultrahigh sensitivity while enabling point-of-care testing without reliance on sophisticated instrumentation. This assay has been claimed as a universal detection method because it can detect three protein (PSA, AFP, and HCG) cancer biomarkers at a low concentration in the range of femtometers (Fig. 7b).222

Diagnosis of cancer by colorimetric assay/application of gold nanoparticles in diagnostic trials

Oral cancer

Oral cancer can be marked by the spontaneous growth of malignant cells in different areas, including lip as well as oral cavity.223 Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) denotes a significant percentage (90%) of head and neck carcinoma.224 Oral cavity cancer ranks as the eighth most common cancer globally, with a notably higher incidence in men. Each year, around 300 000 new cases are diagnosed worldwide.225,226 Oral cancer have a survival rate under 60%, and outcomes have remained largely unchanged for nearly 50 years.227 In the case of sample collection in oral cancer detection techniques, saliva may be the suitable one because it constitutes some hormonal, immunological disease biomarkers.228 Oral cancer is often diagnosed too late, when it has already progressed to a stage that is difficult to treat, with cancer cells becoming more aggressive and resistant to drugs.229 Therefore, it is challenging to detect oral carcinoma at an early stage. There are some bacterial species which can cause oral infection, leading to oral cancer. In addition, early identification of specific oral bacteria would allow prompt treatment and help prevent the development of certain oral diseases. Nowadays, functionalized AuNPs have been used to sense oral bacteria as an sensitive and selective detection tool based on the aggregation phenomenon.230 Moreover, biogenic polyamines, which are organic polycations, such as putrescine (Put) and spermine (Spm), play essential roles in cell proliferation, regulating gene expression and preventing DNA damage. The high amount of these polycations may be considered as cancer biomarkers. In this review, we introduce a method in which tannic acid (TA) was used as both the reducing and capping agent for AuNPs, forming nanoprobe TA-AuNPs. This nanoprobe was very specific to the spermine detection when polyamine was added to the TA-AuNPs, interacting with phenolic groups via hydrogen bonding or electrostatic interactions. Thus, this interaction causes the aggregation of TA-AuNPs, which results in a red-shift.231

Ovarian cancer

Ovarian cancer refers to a carcinoma that occurs either inside or on the surface of the ovary, resulting in the abnormal growth of the cell. The greatest scientist named Dr Martin Swerdlow has identified ovarian cancer for the first time in 1959, describing it as the uncontrolled proliferation of tissues in the pelvic region observed during a pelvic examination, particularly involving the fallopian tube.232 Ovarian cancer, a significant type of cancer affecting women, ranks as the seventh most commonly diagnosed malignancy among females globally.233 It is stated by the world Health Organization that more than thirty percent cancer-related deaths can be avoided by the accurate and early diagnosis and by adequate treatment.234 Approximately, 45% of ovarian cancer patients survive beyond five years from the time of diagnosis.235 Initially, ovarian cancer seems asymptomatic but shows some indications on later stage, such as nausea, bloated stomach, and urinary urgency.236 Carcinoma antigen 125 (CA 125) is a protein which is widely used as a biomarker for the ovarian cancer detection.237 This potential biomarker has been quantified with a detection limit of 0.1 U mL−1 using Cys-AuNPs/ERGO.238 Moreover, human epididymal protein 4 (HE4) is also considered as a significant biomarker for ovarian cancer because it is not normally found in normal tissue but enormously expressed in the ovarian cancer cell. This highly noticeable biomarker has been investigated by using an LFIA method using modified hydrophobic gold nanoparticles, MF@AuNFs, where Mg/Fe-layered double hydroxide nanoflowers provide ideal stability of signal labels and amplification. This optical sensing approach could detect HE4 with a detection limit of 50 pM within the range of 50 to 1000 pM.239

Breast cancer

Breast cancer is the most prevalent cancer among women in industrialized areas and ranks as the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths after lung cancer.240 Breast cancer has an overall 5 year survival rate of about 80%. In 2010, approximately 207 090 new cases were projected among women in the United States, along with an estimated 39840 deaths.241 There are numerous amounts of biomarkers available242 for the detection of breast cancer, including p53,243 CA 15-3,244 Mucin-16,245 HSP60,246 HSP90,247 HER-2/neu,248 and Lipophilin B.249 More than half of all breast cancers overexpress Estrogen Receptor alpha, and approximately 70% of these cases respond to anti-estrogen therapy, such as tamoxifen.250 Nowadays, it hinders the accurate diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer because of enormous heterogenicity.251 Moreover, it is worth saying that without early diagnosis of breast cancer, it is certainly impossible to decline the mortality rate of women suffering from this type of cancer. By the advancement of nanotechnology, a AuNP-based aptasensor has been widely recognized for having color changing abilities. By this method, estrogen receptor alpha (ERα), which is a key biomarker of breast cancer, has been quantified using AuNPs coated with the unmodified ERα-RNA aptamer. Based on the aggregation process of ERα on AuNP surface, the color changed from wine red to blue color. Thus, this sophisticated sensor detected ERα with a detection limit of 0.64 ng mL−1.252 On the other hand, apoptosis in breast cancer cells was also successfully detected by using AuNPs embedded with oxidized glutathione and Cyt c. Here, the apoptosis assay of breast cancer could quantify MCF-7 cells till 30 cells in the linear range of 30 to 3 × 105 cells per ml.22

Pancreatic cancer

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is an exocrine protein of the pancreas that accounts for the majority (90%) of all pancreatic neoplasms. PDAC is the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths in the United States, and its incidence is rising by approximately 0.5% to 1.0%.253 Thus, it is projected that it will reach second leading position among all cancers by 2030 in the US.254 Due to its deep anatomical location, the pancreas is difficult to assess through routine screening, which often misses early tumor progression. Furthermore, most of the patients remain asymptomatic until it reaches advanced level.255 Thus, the lack of noticeable symptoms in the early stages makes detection even more difficult. Pancreatic cancer is primarily driven by four major genes: KRAS, CDKN2A, TP53, and SMAD4, with KRAS mutations and CDKN2A alterations representing early events in tumor formation.256 The long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) HOXA distal transcript antisense RNA (HOTTIP) plays a significant role in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) by enhancing cell proliferation and migration.257 Several studies have reported abnormal overexpression of HOTTIP in PDAC tumor tissues as well as in body fluids, highlighting its potential as an effective circulating biomarker for the diagnosis of PDAC.258 A colorimetric assay has been proposed combining loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) with positively charged AuNPs (LAMP-(+)AuNP) for the detection of HOTTIP, leading to a distinctive color change from red to colorless (Fig. 8a).25 Currently, Keblar et al. has developed a novel tyrosine enzyme PEAK1 (pseudopodium-enriched atypical kinase one, SGK269) that is supposed to be a good biomarker for PADC detection.259 Recently, a colorimetric immunosensor has been reported for the rapid point-of-care detection of PDAC biomarker PEAK1 using a paper-based microfluidic device (Fig. 8b).260

Prostate cancer

Prostate cancer is one of the most prevalent diseases affecting men worldwide, not only in Western populations but increasingly in developing countries, where shifts in lifestyle and dietary habits have contributed to a decline in healthy eating patterns.261 It has become the second leading cause of mortality rate worldwide with approximately 35000 deaths per year.262,263 This malignancy usually progresses slowly and shows no obvious symptoms, often originating in early life but typically being diagnosed only between the ages of 40 and 50.264 The idea that early detection improves the likelihood of successful treatment and reduces prostate cancer – specific mortality is widely accepted and considered compelling by man. There are some conventional methods for the screening of prostate cancer; however, they have some disadvantages due to specificity, accuracy and selectivity.265,266 In 1999, Prostate Cancer Antigen 3 (PCA3) which is a long non-coding RNA, was identified as a gene that is specifically expressed in prostate cancer.267 An unmodified AuNPs and thiolated PCR products can be used as a colorimetric sensor for the detection of PCA3 in urine samples. In this assay, the Prostate Cancer Antigen 3 (PCA3) gene was copied by the polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), which was modified by the thiol (–SH) group. After that, the thiol-mediated DNA was attached to the surface of AuNPs, inhibiting the aggregation of AuNPs (Fig. 9). This inhibition of aggregation resulted in no color change, in the presence of PCA3. On the other hand, in the absence of this gene, the color changed from red to blue.268

Bladder cancer

Bladder cancer refers to any tumor that develops in the urinary bladder, which is the most common malignancy of the urinary tract, predominantly represented by urothelial carcinoma.269 Based on statistical reports from 2018, bladder cancer ranks as the ninth most common cancer worldwide and is a major cause of mortality in several countries, including Thailand.270,271 It is the second most frequently occurring tumor of the genitourinary tract and the second leading cause of mortality among patients with malignancies of this system.272 Notwithstanding its relatively high specificity (approximately 86%), the clinical applicability of conventional cytopathology remains constrained by its modest sensitivity (48%) and pronounced interobserver variability,273,274 thereby limiting its diagnostic reliability, particularly for low-grade tumors.275,276 Thus, nanoparticles have opened a new window for the detection of bladder cancer. A gold nanoparticle (AuNP)-based colorimetric assay has attracted significant attention in biomedical applications. A DNA-target of the bladder cancer was detected using citrate-capped AuNPs based on the aggregation effect, inducing a color change from red to purple (Fig. 10).277 Besides, cells with elevated HURP (a regulatory gene of cell cycle) expression display tumor-like characteristics, including decreased reliance on extracellular growth factors and the ability to grow independently of anchorage.278,279 Eissa et al. developed an assay to detect HURP RNA in urine samples for the bladder cancer diagnosis. Here, magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) were used for capturing and manufacturing purposes for the HURP RNA; while the AuNPs were used as sensors for detection.280

Cervical cancer

Cervical cancer is the fourth most common gynecological cancer worldwide and nearly 90% of this cancer occurs in developing countries.281,282 Cervical cancer is predominantly caused by the human papillomavirus (HPV), which is a widespread sexually transmitted infection.283,284 Cervical cytological screening methods, including the Papanicolaou (Pap smear) test, visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA), biopsy, and primary HPV gene testing,285–287 are often not widely implemented in low- and middle-income countries due to high costs and the substantial resource requirements. Nowadays, scientists are giving more focus on developing a new point-of-care (POC) detection platform, which is based on the colorimetric assay. A smartphone-assisted paper-based colorimetric assay was developed for HPV DNA detection using pyrrolidinyl peptide nucleic acid (acpcPNA) as a probe. The positively charged acpcPNA induced aggregation of dextrin-stabilized gold nanoparticles (d-AuNPs), producing a distinct color change. Upon hybridization with the target HPV DNA, the probe was consumed, and the remaining acpcPNA caused varying degrees of AuNP aggregation, leading to measurable color differences (Fig. 11). These changes were quantified using a smartphone application to determine DNA concentration.288 In recent advancements, researchers have engineered a self-sampling device, CerviSelf, and proposed that its integration with the colorimetric platform C-ColAur could potentially revolutionize cervical cancer screening in low- and middle-income countries. The colorimetric analysis of the cervico-vaginal sample was based on aggregation co-efficient which validates color change between affected and healthy cells. This innovative method showed specificity and selectivity of about 81.81% and 89.79%, respectively.289

Advantages and challenges of cancer detection using a colorimetric detection assay

In the light of above discussion, it can be anticipated that the healthcare burden may be significantly alleviated as smart devices become increasingly integrated into everyday life. Nowadays, owing to their recyclability, portability, low cost, suitability for on-site use, and applicability in resource-constrained settings, colorimetric sensing gadgets play a promising role in lowering the mortality rate of cancer as well as facilitating early detection. The notion of early detection helps in detecting tumors at an early stage, before they disseminate and become untreatable, has intrigued and motivated cancer-control researchers for decades. This immense search for better options to detect cancer earlier has led scientists to the sophisticated optical properties of AuNPs. Based on these amazing SPR properties, researchers have revealed the most appealing colorimetric sensing strategy, which not only improves cancer diagnosis but also provides sensitive, selective and accurate results. Despite having remarkable achievement in colorimetric detection methods, there are still several challenges, which should be minimized as early as possible for the large-scale real sample analysis in clinical settings. For cancer detection, several types of biomarkers have been sensed by NPs, i.e. cancer-related protein, exosomes, and tumor circulating DNA.310 The application of biomarkers has been constrained by multiple obstacles, including their low concentrations in bodily fluids, variability in biomarker levels and temporal expression among patients.311 There remains a critical need for the development of simple sensing platforms that circumvent the use of costly and complex instrumentation, while ensuring rapid and accurate detection and screening of cancer biomarkers. The high-resolution camera, liquid crystal displays (LCD), advanced optical detection capabilities, and diverse onboard features of smartphones make them reliable platforms for the detection and diagnosis of cancer cells.312,313 Besides, the flashlight provided by the smartphones plays a vital role in the imaging and analysis process of detection system.314,315 The integration of smartphones with smart assays and AI-generated applications has the potential to revolutionize molecular analysis, enabling highly capable, versatile, and broadly accessible diagnostic solutions.316 Another one is a growing demand for the development of simple and convenient systems capable of amplifying colorimetric signals with high sensitivity for the screening of early-stage cancer. Besides, sensible selectivity must be ensured in minimizing both false-positive and false-negative signals for reliable and accurate outcomes in each detection. Moreover, assembling components demands more innovative ideas to create a common platform, so that a maximum number of different cancers can be diagnosed only by changing definite portions, i.e. aptamer, antibody, ssDNA, functionalized AuNPs. It is imperative to give due consideration to the toxicity of nanomaterials in practical applications, as high concentrations of nanomaterials or nanozymes may pose significant safety concerns. Furthermore, the development of nanozymes or nanoprobes that exhibit both robust operational stability and environmental biocompatibility through biodegradability remains an essential prerequisite. The biosensors need to be improved more for providing better target specificity.317

Conclusion and future perspectives

To sum up, it can be said that AuNPs have been chosen as an effective colorimetric based sensor for the detection of cancer-associated analytes in clinical applications. Benefiting from the unique attributes of nanomaterials, including adjustable physicochemical characteristics, ease of surface functionalization, pronounced surface plasmon resonance effects, and intrinsic enzyme-mimetic activity, nanomaterial-based sensing platforms have demonstrated markedly enhanced analytical performance, enabling the highly sensitive detection of trace levels of cancer-associated biomarkers. In this review, we discussed the recent progresses of optical-based sensory activity of AuNPs and the mechanisms involved in the AuNPs-based colorimetric detection method. We also mentioned some widely used cancer-related biomarkers for specific types, including proteins, nucleic acids, cells and enzymes. Here, we described the way of color production and signal transduction and discussed how the amplification of signal helps in the detection process. This review provides an elaborate discussion on different types of sensing devices, such as paper-based device (PADs) and lateral flow assay (LFA). It also shows that the composites of AuNPs have been prevalently used to increase the sensitivity and accuracy of the detection process. Colorimetric biosensors have many advantages over the conventional techniques because this aptasensors provide similar or lower detection limit than any other immunosensor methods. As these colorimetric assays can give the same results with the point-of-care (POC) analysis for the real cancer sample analysis, recently researchers feel more interest in utilizing this amazing technique in other types of biological diagnosis. Although considerable progress has been made in the development of colorimetric assays, several limitations persist that warrant further investigation. Although AuNPs rely on the LSPR properties associated with the physical properties, such as size, shape, and surface morphology, even a small change of these properties can lead to significant fluctuations in the performance of sensor. Sometimes, it can be an issue to monitor each of these features accurately. As a result, variations introduced during nanoparticle synthesis may affect the optical response, aggregation behavior, and surface chemistry of AuNPs, thereby compromising assay sensitivity, detection accuracy, and reproducibility. For instance, if the size of the synthesized AuNPs is 15 nm in spite of producing 5 nm, this will definitely affect the SPR shifting, thus the equal amount of biomarker may produce colorimetric responses in different batches. Hence, achieving precise control over nanoparticle fabrication and functionalization remains a critical prerequisite for the successful implementation of reliable and reproducible AuNP-based cancer diagnostic systems. Naked-eye detection becomes challenging at low analyte concentrations; therefore, the incorporation of signal amplification strategies can be pursued to enhance sensitivity and overcome this limitation. Colorimetric assays are inherently unsuitable for non-transparent media; therefore, further refinement of signal amplification strategies is essential to achieve sufficient sensitivity for detecting low concentrations in diluted samples. Furthermore, the stability of the system must be enhanced under varying environmental conditions—including pH, temperature, viscosity, and ionic strength—which can significantly influence target binding. Greater emphasis should be placed on optimizing aptamer selection, as well as on refining immobilization and surface modification strategies. The optimization of colorimetric detection systems is critical for the successful implementation of this approach. Attention should be directed toward understanding and regulating competitive interactions that influence analyte recognition. Continued efforts are required to refine and enhance AuNP-based colorimetric aptasensors for the sensitive detection of cancer-associated biomarkers. Moreover, the increasing demand for multiplexed colorimetric assays capable of simultaneous multi-analyte detection represents a significant challenge and an important direction for future research.

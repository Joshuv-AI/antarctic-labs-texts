Background

Hematological malignancies refer to a group of neoplastic conditions of lymphoid and hematopoietic tissues which result in leukemia, lymphoma and myeloma [1]. Lymphoma is the most frequent solid tumor of hematological malignancies that originate from the lymph nodes and lymphoid tissues. Lymphoma can be further categorized into Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) and non – Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) based on histological characteristics [2, 3].

Hodgkin lymphoma is a rare neoplasm of the lymphatic system, representing one of the most common cancers in young adults [4]. It is characterized by a low number of malignant cells derived from B-lymphocytes and an extensive inflammatory microenvironment [5]. Histopathologically, 95% of the HL cases are classified as classical Hodgkin lymphoma including the subtypes nodular sclerosing, mixed cellularity, lymphocyte-rich and lymphocyte-depleted HL. In the other 5% cases, nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) is diagnosed [5, 6]. Classical Hodgkin lymphoma is characterized by the presence of CD30-expresssing Hodgkin and Reed-stern (HRS) cells surrounded by a variety of inflammatory cells. The malignant cells in NPLHL are termed lymphocyte predominant (LP) and are positive for CD20 and lack of CD 30 [5]. According to GLOBOCAN data for 2020, there were 83,087 new HL cases and 23,376 deaths, accounting for 0.45% and 0.23% of global cancer incidence and mortality, respectively. There were more cases and deaths occurring in males, and most (38.2%) of all cases occurred in Asia [7]. Epstein-Barr virus, certain genetic factors, and HIV have been described as independent risk factors [8, 9, 10]. Hodgkin lymphoma is a highly curable cancer with overall survival rate exceeding 98% in some populations [11].

Bibliometric studies are relevant tools in the social and scientific evaluation of a given discipline within a specified time frame. They serve as markers for activities in a given field of research, evaluate progress, and identify gaps in research [12, 13]. The performance analysis of selected study discipline is often via bibliometric and social network analysis. While biometric data computes the basis outputs, social network analysis interprets the influence of social links and interaction [14, 15]. Bibliometrics, through its indicators, focuses on calculation and analyzing the quantifiable values of scientific production. Bibliometrics comprises obtaining, processing, and managing quantitative bibliographic data from scientific publications.

The primary objective of this study was to analyze the state of scientific literature related to Hodgkin lymphoma that appeared from January 2002 to December 2022, in peer-reviewed journals indexed by Scopus, Web of Science, and Pubmed. We also wanted to assess productivity, publication, types, sites and trends in journal choice.

Methods

Data collection

We analyzed data on global Hodgkin lymphoma research in Scopus, Web of Science, and Pubmed published from January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2022. We performed the search on June 8, 2023. To retrieve the records, we made use of the descriptors “Hodgkin* lymphoma” and “Hodgkin* disease” linked by the Boolean operator OR in the title field. We retrieved all data under the above predefined search query without limitation on article types or language. The full details of the literature search and selection/screening are represented in Fig. 1. In accordance with the preferred Reporting items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA).

Visualization of social network analysis

To visualize network analysis in Hodgkin lymphoma, we used VOSviewer (Center for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University, The Netherlands) version 1.6.19 to map Hodgkin lymphoma research and collaborations.

Bibliometric indicators

Impact factor

We made use of Journal Citation Report (JCR) data of 2022. The Journal Impact Factor is a bibliometric indicator used as a measure of a journal influence. It was originally developed by the institute for Science Information (Philadelphia PA, USA). It is updated annually in the JCR of Clarivate Analytics.

Author/institution/country participation index

This refers to the number of documents on the subject topic contributed by an author/country or an institution. We evaluated the contribution of authors, institutions and countries in Hodgkin lymphoma-related research within the study period.

Co-authorship analysis

Co-authorship analysis refers to the interaction of authors contributing to a particular field/topic of study. It is a measure of collaboration [15]. We evaluated this using VOSviewer to show collaboration pattern in Hodgkin lymphoma research.

Keyword analysis

We made use of keyword analysis to show trends of discussion and view in Hodgkin lymphoma research.

Citation analysis

We used citation analysis to show the volume and pattern of citation in Hodgkin lymphoma research.

Price´s law

It is a broadly used indicator of productivity of a particular discipline or country. It uses exponential growth evaluation; to assess if scientific productivity in Hodgkin lymphoma follows Price´s law of exponential growth. We modeled the generated data into a linear adjustment according to the equation y = 8.6021x-16,529 and an exponential plot according to the equation y = 2E-50e0.0605x. Price´s law is said to be fulfilled when the coefficient of determination of the exponential plot is greater than that of the linear plot [16].

Bradford´s law

We used Bradford´s law to determine the distribution and dispersion of scientific literature on Hodgkin lymphoma. Bradford proposed concentric zones of productivity termed Bradford zones, with decreasing density of information. He hypothesized each zone to contain similar number of documents. The postulated zones aid in identifying journals that are widely used in a specialized discipline. The stratification of journals in the different Bradford zones are viz.: 1, n, n2… [17]. The number of journals on the core and successive zones are expressed by the ratio as 1:n: n2.

Lotka´s law

We used Lotka´s law (inverse square law) of scientific production to provide information on author´s distribution based on the number of publications. The law analyzes the publication volume of authors, expressing that a greater group contains authors publishing fewer than those publishing many. The law establishes that in the entire scientific community, the number of authors (A) who have published a number (n) of literatures in a period of several years of activity A(n) is equal to the number of those who have published a single literature A(1) in the same period, divided by the square of n. It is expressed mathematically as A(n) = a A(1)/n2. According to this index, authors are divided into three levels; small producers (those who have published one article), medium-size producers (those who have published 2–9 articles), and large-scale producers (those who have published 10 or more articles) [18].

h, g- and p-index

We used the h-index to quantify the authors’ activity. It is often used to rate researchers’ performance. The g-index was introduced to measure the global citation performance of a set of articles and p-index which stems from the interrelationship between the two, and is calculated as its coefficient [19, 20, 21].

Statistical analysis

We used SPSS version 28.0 (SPSS, IBM) to evaluate the growth pattern of research output, linear and exponential regression adjustments. For the rest of the statistical graphs and tables, Microsoft Excel Software version 2021 (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA, USA) was used. To fully corelate how countries expenditure on health and research and development relate to publication in Hodgkin disease, additional exploratory analysis was run on the most productive countries.

Analysis of proportion of articles by number of authors

From 2002 to 2022, there were a total of 61,240 authors that contributed to a total of 18,304 articles. This represents contribution rate of 3.45 authors per article. There were 223 articles in which the names of the authors were not available. Following Lotka´s law, we found that the smallest group is made up of authors with the greatest number of publications with more than 10 documents, who represents only 2.31% of the total productivity, while the small producers (authors with one publication) represented more than 70% of the total productivity (transience index of 71.84%). We analyzed collaboration index and found that the most common number of signatures with 10.9% is 4 authors per document.

Bibliographic coupling

Bibliographic coupling/linkage, like co-citations is a measure of similarity that uses citation analysis to establish a similarity relationship between papers. Bibliographic coupling occurs when two papers reference a third common paper in their bibliographies [22]. It is commonly used to identify cohort of studies that are thematically related [22]. On the other hand, co-citations occur when two authors are jointly cited in a third publication. In other words, while bibliographic coupling identifies authors that have a comparable “knowledge base”, co-citation profiles cluster of authors whose publications have similar “impact” [22].

Bibliographic coupling/linkage of authors

In bibliographic linkage of authors, node sizes are proportional to the number of the documents of the respective authors. Similarity in cluster colour for authors implies the degree of overlap between the reference lists of publications of these authors is higher [23]. The bibliometric coupling of this study (Fig. 15) shows five (5) clusters (red, blue, green, purple, and lemon clusters) of closely coupled authors to reflect shared thematic focus. Authors with the largest number of Hodgkin lymphoma articles in the green clusters are Kwee TC, and Ansell SM. In the red cluster, Adams HJA and Straus DJ had the largest number of HL articles to their name. Ansell SM displayed the strongest coupling link strength.

Discussion

We observed the most utilized document type in this study to be original articles, constituting 73.03% of all the literature in the study repertoire. This observation fortifies the fact that the subject matter resides with clinical research or experiment.

We observed a saturated research trend in Hodgkin lymphoma research in this study. This trend is at variance with the exponential growth study trend observed in sickle cell disease research [13], optometry [24], and linear growth trend reported in Lassa fever research [12]. The reason for this saturation may be multifaceted. First, as major questions in HL research are answered, the remaining research may involve more complex, nuanced issues that yield fewer publications per unit of research effort, thus, slowing down the pace of publication. Secondly, funding and research efforts might shift towards other areas with emerging or more pressing questions.

We observed a high transience index of 71.84%. This is a reflection of the high rate of occasional publishers who specialized in other related areas but sporadically publish on HL related research. We only identified 2.31% of the authors as heavy publishers. This portrays that the large proportion of scientific literatures as relates to HL research originated from a few numbers of researchers.

The Bradford analysis of the studied literature showed that 32 journals (9.76%) were responsible for 33.3% of the entire scientific production of HL research. This trend echoes the concentration of scientific literature on a small number of journals. Of these top journals, Leukaemia and Lymphoma had the highest number of publications (863), accounting for 4.71% of all HL research, followed by the Journal of Clinical Oncology (n = 491; 2.68%), and the British Journal of Haematology (n = 489; 2.67%). All the top 10 journals were dedicated to either hematology or cancer. These journals are potential niche for future dissemination of breakthroughs in Hodgkin lymphoma- related research.

Analysis of productivity by country showed the United States dominated HL-related research output, followed by Germany, Italy, China, the United Kingdom and France. The United States alone accounted for 28.62% (n = 5238) of all HL-related research output. The reason for this marginally high productivity may not be far-fetched. United States houses Seagen Pharmaceutical (now part of Pfitzer), that collaborates with Takeda Pharmaceuticals to manufacture Brentuximab vedotin used in management of Hodgkin lymphoma. Also niched in the United States are Pfizer Inc. that manufacture Dacarbazine, Adriamycin (Doxorubicin hydrochloride), vincristine sulfate; MERCK Pharmaceutical that manufacture Keytruda (Pembrolizumab); Baxter, that manufactures Doxorubicin hydrochloride. This finding is consistent with previous studies in other biomedical researches [12, 13, 25]. Other countries in the top ten list that are involved in pharmaceutical remedies in HL are; Germany (MERCK), Japan (Takeda Pharmaceuticals), India (Avancecure Life Sciences), and China (Zhejiang Husun Pharma). More so, institutions in the United States were the leading institutions in HL-related research, constituting 60% of the top 20 institutions involved in HL research. The remaining institutions reside in Europe, North America; Germany (10%), Canada (10%), France (10%), Italy (15%), and Sweden (5%). The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, USA was observed to be the most productive institution for HL-related research. The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center is devoted to cancer patient care, research, education, and prevention [26]. On the other hand, UniKlinik Koln (University Hospital of Cologne) Germany is the largest hospital in Cologne and is among the leading research centres on cancer in Germany [27].

The comparative evaluation of the productivity of HL-related research and current health expenditure revealed that the higher the current expenditure, the higher the productivity in HL-related research, with some exceptions: United Kingdom, France, Canada, Japan, Spain and Netherlands that their current health expenditure far exceeds their participation index. In summation the United States had the highest CHE (19% of GDP), followed by Germany (13% of GDP).

Evaluation of the correlation between productivity in HL-related research and Gross Domestic Product on research and development (R&D). Japan had the highest GDP dedicated to R&D among the top 10 most productive countries. Only three countries (Japan, United States and Germany) had GDP dedicated to R&D above the OECD (Organization of Economic Cooperation and Development) recommended average of 2.3%. On the other hand, six countries (Japan, The United States, Germany, China, France and Netherland) had GDP on R&D above the EU recommended average of 1.9%. The scientific productivity of a country is often a reflection of its earlier investment in research and development [13, 25].

Engert A, Diehl V, Younes A, Borchmann P, Ansell SM, Connors JM, Zinzani PL, Hansmann ML, Cozen W and Brice P. were the top ten most productive authors in the HL-related research. While Engert A focused mainly on treatment of HL, Diehl V and Younes A focused on treatment and molecular characterization of HL. On the other hand, Borchmann P, Ansell SM focused on treatment and diagnosis of HL. Zinzani PL focused on treatment and prognostic factors of HL, while Hansmann ML pitched with diagnosis and molecular characterization of HL. Cozen W carved his niche in risk factors and molecular characterization of HL. Connors JM delved into all of treatment, epidemiology, prognostic factors and molecular characterization of HL. Lastly, Brice P focused on Treatment, prognosis and diagnosis of HL.

The major journals utilized in the dissemination of HL-related research were Leukemia and Lymphoma, Journal of Clinical Oncology, British Journal of Haematology. Blood, Annals of Oncology, Annals of Haematology, Haematological Cancer, Bone Marrow, Transplantation and Pediatric Blood and Cancer. The centrality of these journals makes them focal point to be looked for, in future breakthrough in HL-research. The most productive journal in HL-related research belonged to the Q3 category, while the remainder were mostly Q1. This presents an interesting dynamic in academic publishing, as most biomedical scientists expect most productive journals in every discipline to fall in Q1. The top position of the Q3 journal suggests that it serves a critical niche within HL-research. As the name implied, the journal focused specifically on Leukemia and Lymphoma, hence, attracting high volume of specialized studies that other broader or more general Q1 journals might not prioritize. The overall mixed ranking of the top productive journals in HL-research highlights a balance between prestige and practicability. The dominance of a Q3 journal observed here, underscores the importance of niche specialization, while the prevalence of Q1 journals reflects the high value placed on quality, visibility, and academic impact. This dynamic illustrates how researchers navigate the complex landscape of academic publishing to disseminate their research findings effectively.

The article “Recommendation for initial evaluation, staging and response assessment of Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma: The Lugano classification” was the most cited article (n = 3,023) with an average citation of 377.9/year. The article was a conference recommendation for initial evaluation, staging and response evaluation of HL and NHL. It was published open access in the Journal of Clinical Oncology (A toll access journal with 12 months embargo period) by Cheson BD et al. 2014. The second most cited article; “PD-1 blockade with nivolumab in relapsed or refractory Hodgkin lymphoma” is an original article reporting on the use of nivolumab in relapsed or refractory HL published open access in the New England Journal of Medicine (a delayed open access journal) by Ansell SM et al., 2015. Though the second most cited (n = 2732), this article had the highest average citations per year (390.3/year). The next is the clinical trial tagged “Results of pivotal phase II study of brentuximab vedotin for patients with relapse or refractory Hodgkin´s lymphoma” published open access in the Journal of Clinical Oncology by Younes et al., 2012 with a total citation of 1,204. Overall, six of the top 10 articles were original articles, two (2) were randomized clinical trials, one was a special article (recommendation). More so, seven (7) were published toll access while the remaining 3 were published open access. This trend once again crops up the argument of the “advantage of open access publication over toll access with respect to citation impact”. The Journal of Clinical Oncology published three (3) of the top 10 most cited articles. Only three (3) of the journals (Journal of Clinical Oncology, Lancet Oncology and Blood were dedicated to hematology and hematological malignancies. The rest were broad discipline journals. All the top most cited articles were published in journals with impact factors more than 4.

This study is prone to potential limitations that are inherent with bibliometric studies. First, the criteria set by the databases used determines the output of the downloaded datasets. Secondly, we may have excluded articles on Hodgkin lymphoma if the authors did not put our inclusion criteria descriptors in their title or keyword. More so, national/local journals not indexed in Scopus, Web of Science and PubMed will also be automatically excluded.

Conclusion

Scientific production in Hodgkin lymphoma was found to be saturated with high transience index. This study provides a reference for oncologists, epidemiologist, funders, policy decision makers, academics and Hodgkin lymphoma researchers.

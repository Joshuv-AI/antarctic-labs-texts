Integrative multi-omics analysis identifies FAM3D as a prognostic tumor suppressor in cervical cancer
Authors: Yanbin Chen, Yu Hong, Dongjun Wei, Lianjiao Fan, Dian Tao, Rui Zhang, Hongwei Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCervical cancer (CC) remains a leading malignancy among women worldwide. Epigenetic and transcriptional dysregulation complicate the identification of causal genes with prognostic and therapeutic relevance. We performed a multi-omics integrative analysis combining summary data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) using eQTL and pQTL datasets with genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics (ID: ukb-b-8777) to prioritize candidate genes associated with CC. Differential expression analyses were conducted using multiple GEO cohorts, and protein expression was validated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in clinical specimens. DNA methylation profiling and correlation analyses were performed using GEO and TCGA datasets to investigate epigenetic regulation. Kaplan–Meier and combined expression–methylation survival analyses were used to evaluate prognostic significance. Functional validation was conducted in HeLa cells using wound-healing and CCK-8 assays following FAM3D knockdown or overexpression. RNA sequencing was further performed to explore the downstream molecular pathways regulated by FAM3D. Integrative SMR analysis identified FAM3D as a protective gene for CC. FAM3D expression was significantly reduced in CC tissues and exhibited a progressive decline from normal cervical tissues to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and invasive CC, which was further confirmed by IHC. Promoter hypermethylation of FAM3D (cg26334888) was associated with its transcriptional downregulation and negatively correlated with gene expression. Survival analyses demonstrated that high FAM3D expression (p = 0.016) and low promoter methylation (p = 0.048) were associated with favorable overall survival, while combined expression–methylation analysis further improved prognostic stratification (p = 0.006). Functional assays showed that FAM3D suppressed CC cell proliferation and migration. Transcriptomic profiling revealed that FAM3D restoration induced extensive transcriptional reprogramming and was associated with inflammatory signaling, cytokine-mediated pathways, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Key hub genes identified downstream of FAM3D included CXCL8, MMP1, EREG, and LCN2. FAM3D is a potential tumor suppressor and prognostic biomarker in CC, whose expression is partially regulated by promoter methylation. Integrative multi-omics analyses and functional studies suggest that FAM3D may inhibit CC progression through modulation of inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix-associated pathways. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying CC and support the potential clinical utility of FAM3D as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10142-026-01955-4.Keywords: Cervical cancer, Summary data-based Mendelian randomization, FAM3D, Methylation, Transcriptomic- data analysis

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) ranks fourth among the most commonly diagnosed cancers in women worldwide and accounts for the fourth highest number of cancer-related deaths in women in 20221. On the basis of the World Health Organization’s global initiative to eliminate CC—defined by achieving an annual incidence below 4 per 100,000 women (in)—it is estimated that up to 350,000 deaths could be prevented globally each year, including approximately 44,750 in China (Sung et al. 2021, Qi et al. 2023). Despite a decline in incidence over the past three decades, it remains a major global health burden (Xu et al. 2025).

Persistent infection with human papillomavirus (HPV) is recognized as the principal etiological factor underlying the development of cervical precancerous lesions and CC. Given the persistence of high-risk HPV infection, aberrant methylation of both the host and viral genomes can disrupt tumor suppressor gene function, contributing to the progression of CC (Sen et al. 2018, Liu et al. 2023). DNA methylation is a fundamental type of epigenetic modification that also includes histone modification, noncoding RNA regulation, and chromatin remodeling (Fetahu and Taschner-Mandl 2021). These epigenetic mechanisms regulate gene expression without changing the DNA sequence and are essential for controlling gene activity during and after cell division, particularly in processes such as cell differentiation (John and Rougeulle 2018). Identifying causal genes involved in CC is essential for uncovering disease mechanisms and developing effective therapies.

Summary data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR), which combines molecular quantitative trait loci (QTLs) (eQTLs and pQTLs) with GWAS data, provides a robust approach for prioritizing genes with potential causal roles in disease pathogenesis (Kreitmaier et al. 2023). In this research, we employed SMR to integrate eQTL, pQTL, and GWAS datasets. FAM3D emerged as a consistent protective gene across both eQTL and pQTL analyses. Expression profiling from GEO datasets and immunohistochemical (IHC) staining revealed a stepwise reduction in FAM3D expression from normal cervical tissue to CIN and CC. In addition, on the basis of TCGA data, we investigated whether promoter methylation is associated with FAM3D expression and further evaluated the combined impact of these factors on patient prognosis.

Method

Exposure data

The eQTL data, which were primarily derived from blood samples of healthy individuals of European descent, were sourced from the eQTLGen Consortium (https://eqtlgen.org/), which provides extensive cis-eQTL information. The dataset covers 31,684 cis-eQTLs across 16,987 genes. A comprehensive description of the dataset is available in the original publication (Võsa et al. 2021).

The pQTL data were derived from the INTERVAL study (Sun et al. 2018), Fenland study (Pietzner et al. 2021), and SCALLOP study (Folkersen et al. 2020). The INTERVAL study included plasma samples from 3,622 healthy participants and identified 1,927 genotype‒protein associations (pQTLs), including 1,104 trans-acting loci. The Fenland study measured 4,775 plasma protein targets from 10,708 individuals of European descent, identifying 10,674 genotype‒protein associations (p < 1.004 × 10−−11) across 2,548 genomic regions (1,097 novel). The SCALLOP study identified 451 pQTLs for 85 proteins in over 30,000 individuals. The original articles provide a complete description of the data.

Outcome data

The summary data for CC in our research were sourced from the IEU Open GWAS database. The CC GWAS investigated 462,933 European individuals (Ncase = 1889, Ncontrol = 461044) (ID: ukb-b-8777). The IEU OpenGWAS project is the source of the summary statistics utilized in this investigation (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/).

Data collection and preprocessing

To compare the mRNA and methylation levels between CC and normal tissues, raw datasets were retrieved from the GEO database. Expression profiles from GSE138080, GSE63154, and GSE287994 were obtained across different microarray platforms (Table 1). In addition, CC-related mRNA and methylation data were acquired from the TCGA database for further correlation analysis. Clinical information was matched to the TCGA data, including 288 CC samples with corresponding methylation profiles and associated clinical annotations.

Summary-data-based Mendelian randomization

SMR integrates GWAS and eQTL summary statistics to assess whether gene expression mediates the genetic links between single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and phenotypic traits, thereby enabling the investigation of potential causal associations between molecular traits (e.g., gene or protein expression) and disease outcomes (Zhu et al. 2016). In this study, SMR was employed to investigate the relationships among eQTLs, pQTLs, and CC risk. However, the observed associations identified by SMR do not necessarily imply that gene expression and disease traits are driven by the same underlying causal variants. This is because the top cis-eQTL in linkage disequilibrium (LD) may involve two separate causal variants—one regulating gene expression and the other associated with the phenotype. To address this potential confounding, we performed a HEIDI test to rule out associations driven by LD (Wu et al. 2018). A HEIDI p value > 0.05 indicates no significant heterogeneity in the association between instrumental variables and the target gene, supporting the presence of a common causal variant and thereby strengthening the validity of the SMR findings. For SMR analysis, associations with an SMR P value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The analysis was conducted via an online SMR platform and its associated database (https://yanglab.westlake.edu.cn/) (Guo et al. 2025). As this work is based on publicly available human genomic datasets, no additional ethical approval was needed. An overview of the analytical strategy is depicted schematically in Fig. 1.

Flow diagram of the analysis

Screening of differentially expressed genes and key genes selection

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CC and normal cervix tissues were assessed with the limma package in R software (version 4.3.2). The GEO dataset filtering criteria were as follows: log2|fold change|>1, adjusted p value < 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05.

A Venn diagram was used to identify overlapping differentially expressed mRNAs between the results of SMR and differential expression analysis. The SMR results were stratified on the basis of the direction of the estimated causal effect (beta). Genes with beta < 0 were considered potential protective genes, whereas those with beta > 0 were considered potential risk genes. The downregulated genes in CC tissues overlapped with the SMR-identified genes with negative beta values (putative protective genes), whereas the upregulated genes were compared with those with positive beta values (putative risk genes). Venn diagrams were generated to visualize the overlaps and identify candidate genes with consistent directionality between genetic associations and transcriptional changes.

Expression analysis of key genes across disease stages

Publicly available transcriptomic datasets were retrieved from the GEO repository to analyze the expression dynamics of key genes throughout the progression from normal cervical tissue to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and CC (GSE138080, GSE63154). The samples were categorized into three groups, normal, CIN, and CC, on the basis of their clinical annotations. Key gene expression values were extracted, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to evaluate differences among the three groups, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test to determine significant pairwise comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as a p value less than 0.05. All data analyses and visualizations were carried out via R software.

Clinical validation of key genes using IHC

To validate key gene protein expression in clinical samples, IHC staining was performed on cervical tissue sections obtained from 15 patients at the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University, with 5 samples each of normal cervical tissue, CIN, and CC. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue Sect. (4 μm thick) were subjected to deparaffinization via sequential treatment with dewaxing agents, followed by rehydration through graded ethanol and rinsing in distilled water. Antigen retrieval was carried out via heat-induced epitope recovery (HIER) via citrate buffer at pH 6.0. To inhibit endogenous peroxidase activity, the tissue sections were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide. Nonspecific binding was blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA). The slides were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody against key genes (Proteintech, 1:200). After being washed in PBS (pH 7.4), the sections were incubated with an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody for 50 min at room temperature. DAB substrate was applied for signal detection, and the chromogenic reaction was monitored under a light microscope. Hematoxylin was used for nuclear counterstaining. The slides were dehydrated in graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted with coverslips. Key gene expression was evaluated via cytoplasmic staining via the H-score method, which calculated was as follows: H-score =∑(𝑃𝑖×𝑖)=(%weak×1)+(%moderate×2)+(%strong×3)H-score, where 𝑖 represents staining intensity: 0 = negative (no staining), 1 = weak (light yellow), 2 = moderate (brown), and 3 = strong (dark brown), and 𝑃𝑖 indicates the percentage of cells at each intensity. All slides were independently assessed by two blinded pathologists. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.4. H-scores are presented as mean ± SD. Comparisons among normal, CIN, and CC groups were conducted via Welch’s ANOVA, with Games–Howell post hoc tests for pairwise comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Differential methylation and correlation analysis of key genes probes

We analyzed the GSE287994 dataset from the GEO database, which includes DNA methylation profiles generated via the Infinium MethylationEPIC (850 K) array, to investigate the methylation patterns of key genes in CC. Differentially methylated probes (DMPs) within the promoter region of the FAM3D gene were identified via the ChAMP R package. Comparisons were conducted between CC and normal cervical tissues. Probes with a corrected p value of less than 0.05 and a Δβ value exceeding |0.2| were deemed to be significantly differentially methylated. To further evaluate the regulatory associations among promoter methylation and the expression profiles of genes, we extracted the corresponding methylation β values and key gene expression levels from the TCGA-CC cohort. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients were calculated to determine how epigenetic modifications at transcriptional start sites are related to gene expression. A negative correlation was considered suggestive of methylation-associated gene silencing.

Survival analysis of FAM3D expression and promoter methylation

To investigate the prognostic significance of FAM3D and its promoter methylation, overall survival (OS) analysis was performed using clinical and omics data from the TCGA-CC cohort. Optimal cutoff values for stratifying patients into high and low expression (or methylation) cohorts were identified via the surv_cutpoint function from the survminer R package, which applies maximally selected rank statistics to determine the most prognostically informative threshold. Group survival differences were analyzed through Kaplan–Meier estimations followed by log-rank tests.

For combined analysis, patients were divided into four groups according to key gene expression and promoter methylation status: high expression + low methylation, high expression + high methylation, low expression + low methylation, and low expression + high methylation. Survival differences among these groups were evaluated via the log-rank test, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

CCK-8 and wound healing

HeLa cells were transfected with FAM3D overexpression plasmid or shRNA (GenePharma) using Lipofectamine 3000 (YEASEN, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were seeded at 60–70% confluence, and transfection efficiency was confirmed by qPCR 24 h post-transfection (primers listed in Supplementary Table 6). For the CCK-8 assay, transfected HeLa cells were plated in 96-well plates at 2,000 cells per well, with three replicates for each group. At 0,12, 24 and 36 h post-seeding, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (YEASEN, China) was added to each well. After 90 min of incubation, absorbance at 450 nm was measured to determine cell viability. For wound healing assays, monolayers of transfected cells at ~ 90% confluence in 6-well plates were scratched using a 1000 µL pipette tip. Cells were washed with PBS and incubated in serum-free medium, with wound closure monitored at 0, 12, 24, and 36 h under an inverted microscope. Each experiment was independently repeated three times. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8; two-group comparisons utilized unpaired t-tests, while multiple-group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA. P-values < 0.05 were considered significant.

Transcriptomic profiling and pathway enrichment analysis

To further investigate the molecular mechanisms regulated by FAM3D in CC, total RNA was extracted from HeLa cells transfected with FAM3D overexpression plasmid and corresponding vector control cells using Trizol reagent (YEASEN, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and integrity were assessed prior to library construction. Qualified RNA samples were submitted to OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for transcriptome sequencing. Principal component analysis (PCA), sample correlation analysis, and sample-to-sample distance heatmaps were performed based on normalized gene expression matrices to evaluate sample reproducibility and overall data reliability. Differential expression analysis between the FAM3D overexpression group and vector control group was then conducted to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), using adjusted p value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1 as the screening criteria. To further explore the biological functions and signaling pathways associated with FAM3D, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis, and WikiPathways enrichment analysis were performed based on the identified DEGs. In addition, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed to investigate the potential interactions among the DEGs and to identify key hub genes potentially involved in FAM3D-mediated regulatory processes.

Results

Association of eQTL_eQTLgen with CC

We employed SMR as the first analytical approach to investigate the causal associations among eQTLs and CCs (Supplementary Table 1). In the GWAS, we identified the top ten genes (PAX8, MED8, ELOVL1, MPL, ATRAID, KRTCAP3, ALOX5AP, SRGAP1, MTFP1 and PANX2) with the smallest p values, demonstrating the strongest statistical significance. These genes were located within or near loci relevant to CC (P_SMR < 0.05, P_HEIDI > 0.05) (Fig. 2A). Among the 9169 eQTLs examined, 406 showed a causal association with CC (Supplementary Table 2). Specifically, MTFP1, SRGAP1 and PANX2 exhibited significant inverse causal relationships with CC risk (p < 0.001), whereas MED8, ALOX5AP, ATRAID, ELOVL1, MPL and KRTCAP3 demonstrated significant positive causal relationships (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2B).

Association of pQTLs with CC

We also applied SMR analysis to examine the causal relationship between pQTLs and CC (Supplementary Table 3). Among the 1716 pQTLs, 68 were found to be causally associated with CC (Fig. 2C), with 27 pQTLs exhibiting a positive causal relationship and 41 pQTLs exhibiting a negative causal relationship.

Screening of DEGs and key genes selection

Two CC gene expression datasets, GSE138080 and GSE63514, were obtained from the GEO database, with detailed dataset characteristics summarized in Table 1. DEGs between CC and normal cervical tissues were identified for each dataset. On the basis of the defined screening thresholds, 252 DEGs (147 downregulated and 105 upregulated) were detected in GSE138080, whereas 946 DEGs (556 downregulated and 390 upregulated) were identified in GSE63514 (Fig. 3A and B, Supplementary Table 4, Supplementary Table 5). A Venn diagram analysis was subsequently performed to identify overlapping genes among the DEGs from GSE138080 and GSE63514 and the SMR-identified genes from eQTL and pQTL analyses. FAM3D emerged as the only gene consistently downregulated in CC and causally linked to the disease. No upregulated genes overlapped with the SMR-associated candidates (Fig. 3C and D).

FAM3D downregulation associated with CC progression

To investigate the expression pattern of FAM3D during CC progression, we analyzed two independent GEO datasets. In the GSE138080 dataset, FAM3D expression was highest in normal cervical tissues, intermediate in CIN tissues, and lowest in CC tissues (Fig. 4A). A similar trend was observed in GSE63514, where FAM3D expression also decreased progressively from normal to CIN to cancer samples (Fig. 4B).

To corroborate these observations at the protein expression level, we performed IHC staining on cervical tissue samples collected from 15 patients (5 each from normal, CIN, and CC patients). As shown in Fig. 4F, the staining intensity of FAM3D decreased consistently with disease severity, with the highest expression in normal tissues and the lowest in cancer tissues. Immunostaining images for all clinical samples are provided in Supplementary Figure S1. These results provide consistent evidence at both the mRNA and protein levels that FAM3D expression is downregulated during the progression from normal cervical epithelium to CIN and CC.

Differential methylation and correlation analysis of FAM3D promoter probes

To investigate the methylation status of the FAM3D gene in CC, we performed probe-level differential methylation analysis using the GSE287994 dataset. A total of eight CpG probes associated with FAM3D were identified across various genomic regions, including the 5′UTR (cg06689372), TSS1500 (cg21770617, cg26334888), 1 st Exon (cg24121168), 3′UTR (cg11081638), and gene body (cg07311566, cg01123926, cg21079946). Several probes (cg01123926, cg06689372, cg07311566, cg11081638, cg21770617, cg24121168, and cg26334888) presented significantly increased β values in tumor tissues compared with normal tissues, indicating hypermethylation of FAM3D in CC (Fig. 5A–H).

To further evaluate the regulatory effect of promoter methylation, we selected two probes located in the TSS1500 region (cg21770617 and cg26334888) for correlation analysis with FAM3D mRNA expression via TCGA-CC data. Spearman correlation analysis revealed a statistically significant negative correlation for cg26334888 (r = − 0.162, p = 0.006), whereas cg21770617 was not significantly correlated (r = − 0.054, p = 0.360) (Fig. 5I–J). These results suggest that cg26334888 may play a regulatory role in FAM3D transcriptional repression through promoter hypermethylation.

Survival analysis of FAM3D expression and promoter methylation

To assess the prognostic significance of FAM3D and its promoter methylation, we performed overall survival (OS) analysis on the basis of TCGA-CC data. KM analysis revealed that patients with high FAM3D expression had significantly longer OSthan those with low expression (p= 0.016) (Fig. 6A). In contrast, patients with high methylation levels at the promoter probe cg26334888presented significantly shorter OS than did those with low methylation levels (p = 0.048)(Fig. 6B), indicating its potential as a negative prognostic marker.

Furthermore, we conducted a combined survival analysis on the basis of both FAM3D expression and cg26334888 methylation levels. Functional validation of FAM3D in CC progression

To investigate the functional role of FAM3D in CC cell motility, we modulated FAM3D expression in HeLa cells using loss- and gain-of-function approaches. Quantitative real-time PCR confirmed the efficient knockdown of FAM3D following shRNA transfection, as well as robust upregulation upon plasmid-mediated overexpression (Fig. 7A, B), validating the effectiveness of genetic manipulation. We next assessed the impact of FAM3D on cell migration using wound-healing assays. Representative images revealed accelerated wound closure in FAM3D-silenced cells compared with negative controls, whereas FAM3D overexpression markedly impaired gap closure over time (Fig. 7C, D). Quantitative analysis demonstrated that FAM3D knockdown significantly increased migration rates (p = 0.049), while ectopic expression of FAM3D resulted in a pronounced suppression of migratory capacity (p = 0.002). We further assessed cell viability using a CCK-8 assay. At 36 h post-transfection, FAM3D knockdown significantly enhanced cell proliferation compared with negative controls (p = 0.007), whereas FAM3D overexpression led to a marked reduction in proliferative capacity (p= 0.002) (Fig. 7E). These results suggest that FAM3D suppresses both migratory and proliferative properties of CC cells, supporting a tumor-suppressive role of FAM3D in vitro. Together, these findings indicate that FAM3D functions as a negative regulator of CC cell migration, and that loss of FAM3D expression confers a pro-migratory phenotype in vitro.

FAM3D restoration induces extensive transcriptomic remodeling in CC cells

To gain mechanistic insight into the tumor-suppressive role of FAM3D, RNA sequencing was performed in FAM3D-overexpressing HeLa cells and corresponding vector controls. Raw RNA-seq read counts for all detected genes are provided in Supplementary Table 7. The robustness of the dataset was supported by principal component analysis, sample correlation analysis, and sample-to-sample distance clustering, all of which demonstrated strong reproducibility among biological replicates and clear separation between experimental groups (Supplementary Figure S2). Transcriptomic profiling revealed widespread alterations in gene expression following restoration of FAM3D, resulting in a distinct transcriptional landscape compared with control cells (Fig. 8A). Among the most prominently dysregulated genes were CXCL8, MMP1, EREG, and LCN2, all of which have established roles in inflammatory signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, and tumor progression (Fig. 8B). Functional enrichment analyses demonstrated that FAM3D-regulated genes were predominantly involved in extracellular matrix organization, cytokine-mediated signaling, receptor–ligand interactions, and responses to external stimuli (Fig. 8C). Consistently, KEGG and WikiPathways analyses identified significant enrichment of inflammation-, immune-, and extracellular matrix-associated pathways, including cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, IL-17 signaling, and TNF signaling pathways (Fig. 8D, E). Furthermore, protein–protein interaction analysis identified CXCL8, MMP1, EREG, and LCN2 as highly connected hub genes within the FAM3D-regulated network (Fig. 8F). Collectively, these findings suggest that restoration of FAM3D expression reshapes the CC transcriptome and may suppress tumor progression through coordinated regulation of inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix-associated pathways.

Discussion

In this study, we applied an integrative multi-omics framework combining SMR-based causal inference with transcriptomic, epigenetic, and functional analyses to characterize the role of FAM3D in CC. FAM3D was identified as a protective gene and exhibited a progressive decrease in expression from normal cervical tissues to CIN and invasive CC, which was validated at both the transcriptomic and protein levels. Epigenetic analyses suggested that promoter hypermethylation contributes to FAM3D downregulation, and survival analyses further demonstrated that reduced FAM3D expression and increased promoter methylation are associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes. Importantly, functional assays provided biological support for these observations, showing that loss of FAM3D enhances CC cell migration and proliferation, consistent with a tumor-suppressive role of FAM3D in CC progression.

The sequence-similarity 3 (FAM3) family comprises four paralogs—FAM3A, FAM3B, FAM3C, and FAM3D (Zhu et al. 2002). FAM3D encodes an intestine-secreted protein whose production is nutrient responsive (Zhu et al. 2002, Wit et al. 2012) and, via FPR1/FPR2, restrains intestinal inflammation and preserves mucosal homeostasis (Peng et al. 2016). Deletion of FAM3D accelerates inflammation-driven colorectal tumorigenesis in mice (Liang et al. 2020). Outside the gut, FAM3D curbs squamous-carcinoma cell proliferation and migration while increasing chemosensitivity (Nan et al. 2022, Zhou et al. 2024). In head and neck cancer, it is often silenced, and higher expression predicts superior overall survival (Liao et al. 2023). In line with these reports, our data revealed a stepwise reduction in FAM3D from the normal cervix to CIN and invasive cancer, and elevated FAM3D expression was significantly associated with better overall survival in CC patients.

Epigenetics comprises heritable shifts in gene activity that do not alter the DNA sequence. By controlling when and where genes are transcribed, it guides cell fate decisions and preserves lineage-specific programs through successive divisions (John and Rougeulle 2018). Aberrant regulation of any layer is now recognized as a pivotal driver of disease, influencing every step of tumorigenesis from initiation to metastasis (Zhao et al. 2020, Liu et al. 2019). The process of DNA methylation, catalyzed by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs), involves the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to the cytosine 5-position within CpG dinucleotides (Horvath and Raj 2018). Early in oncogenesis, genome-wide hypomethylation coupled with focal CpG island hypermethylation silences tumor-suppressor genes, activates proto-oncogenes and microRNAs and facilitates HPV integration (Farkas et al. 2013). These changes also remodel the immune microenvironment, fostering immune escape, drug resistance, and malignant progression (Ali et al. 2017), and are viewed as a core axis of cancer epigenetics (Easwaran et al. 2014). Interrogation of the TCGA and GEO datasets revealed a significant inverse relationship between promoter methylation of FAM3D and transcript abundance in our research. Demethylation of CC cells restored FAM3D expression, whereas hypermethylation at cg26334888 predicted poorer prognosis, may reflect that promoter silencing of FAM3D contributes to CC progression and adverse outcomes. Although the observed inverse association between cg26334888 methylation and FAM3D expression supports a regulatory relationship, the current evidence primarily indicates correlation rather than definitive causality. Functional validation using methylation-modifying agents or site-specific epigenetic editing would be required to establish a direct causal link.

Promoter hypermethylation of FAM3D was strongly associated with reduced overall survival in CC patients. In contrast, higher FAM3D transcript levels served as an independent favorable prognostic marker in our cohort, which is consistent with studies showing protective effects of FAM3D expression in several malignancies (Dong et al. 2023). The prognostic direction, however, is tumor specific: FAM3D is overexpressed in endometrial carcinoma (Liao et al. 2023, Zheng et al. 2024) and in low-grade glioma, cutaneous melanoma, and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, where its abundance correlates with inferior survival (Dong et al. 2023). These discrepancies underscore context-dependent FAM3D biology, and the signaling pathways governed by FAM3D in CC remain to be clarified.

Elevated FAM3D expression is associated with a better prognosis in various other cancer types (Liao et al. 2023, Dong et al. 2023). Moreover, our study found that transcriptomic remodeling induced by FAM3D restoration was strongly associated with inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix (ECM)-related pathways. Functional enrichment analyses consistently highlighted cytokine-mediated signaling, cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, IL-17 signaling, TNF signaling, and ECM-associated pathways. Increasing evidence indicates that chronic inflammation is a critical driver of cervical carcinogenesis and tumor progression, particularly in the context of persistent HPV infection (Zheng et al. 2024). Dysregulated inflammatory signaling can promote tumor cell proliferation, immune evasion, angiogenesis, and metastatic dissemination. Notably, CXCL8 and MMP1 emerged as recurrent candidates across multiple independent analyses, including differential expression profiling, pathway enrichment, and protein–protein interaction network analysis. CXCL8 is a well-established pro-inflammatory chemokine that contributes to tumor progression through modulation of the tumor microenvironment, angiogenesis, and metastatic behavior (David et al. 2016). Recent studies have further suggested that CXCL8 is associated with CC progression and immune regulation. Meanwhile, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are key mediators of ECM degradation and remodeling, processes that are essential for tumor invasion and metastasis (Kessenbrock et al. 2010). Aberrant MMP activity has been widely implicated in cancer progression, including CC. The simultaneous enrichment of inflammatory pathways and ECM-associated signaling observed in our RNA-seq analysis suggests that FAM3D may suppress malignant phenotypes through coordinated regulation of inflammation-driven extracellular remodeling programs. In addition to CXCL8 and MMP1, LCN2 and EREG were also identified as highly dysregulated and interconnected genes within the FAM3D-associated network. LCN2 has been reported to promote epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, and metastatic potential in CC, while elevated LCN2 expression has been associated with advanced disease progression (Chung et al. 2016). EREG, a member of the epidermal growth factor family, has likewise been implicated in tumor proliferation and microenvironmental signaling in multiple malignancies (Riese and Cullum 2014). The enrichment of these genes further supports the possibility that FAM3D functions as an upstream regulator of inflammatory and tumor-promoting transcriptional networks.

Several strengths of this study merit consideration. By integrating SMR analyses based on both eQTL and pQTL datasets with transcriptomic profiling, we established a multi-layered framework for prioritizing candidate genes associated with CC, thereby increasing confidence in the identification of FAM3D as a potential protective factor. The robustness of our findings was further supported by independent validation across multiple GEO cohorts, immunohistochemical assessment in clinical specimens, methylation analyses, and survival evaluation in TCGA datasets. Moreover, RNA sequencing of FAM3D-overexpressing CC cells provided mechanistic insight into its downstream regulatory network, revealing potential links to inflammatory signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, and tumor-associated pathways. Together, these complementary approaches strengthen the biological and clinical relevance of FAM3D in CC.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. Although the biological and clinical relevance of FAM3D was supported through multiple independent datasets and validation approaches, the clinical specimens used for immunohistochemical analysis were derived from a single center with a relatively limited sample size. In addition, methylation analyses were restricted by the lack of available CIN methylation datasets, precluding a comprehensive evaluation of FAM3D methylation dynamics during cervical carcinogenesis. While transcriptomic profiling identified inflammatory signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, and several candidate downstream effectors, including CXCL8, MMP1, LCN2, and EREG, these findings remain largely associative. Further mechanistic studies in additional cellular and animal models will be required to establish the causal links between FAM3D and its downstream regulatory networks. Validation in larger multi-center cohorts will also be important to further assess the clinical utility of FAM3D as a prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target in CC.

Conclusion

In summary, our study identifies FAM3D as a potential tumor suppressor and prognostic biomarker in cervical cancer. Integrative multi-omics analyses revealed that FAM3D expression is partially regulated by promoter methylation and that reduced FAM3D expression is associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes. Functional assays demonstrated that FAM3D suppresses cervical cancer cell proliferation and migration, while transcriptomic profiling further implicated inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix remodeling as potential downstream mechanisms. Collectively, these findings provide new insights into the biological role of FAM3D in cervical cancer progression and support its potential utility as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

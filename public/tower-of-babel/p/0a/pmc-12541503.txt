First-in-human study of DGPR1008 for intraoperative fluorescence imaging of prostate-specific membrane antigen-positive prostate cancer in patients undergoing radical prostatectomy
Authors: Peng Li, Ying Xu, Shijie Yu, Zhijun Miao, Hongbo Xu, Bing Lu, Shoujun Zhou, Wenjuan Gan, Felip Couñago, Kalevi Kairemo, Panagiotis J Vlachostergios, Jing Zhao, Jinxian Pu, Gang Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) precise surgical resection remains a critical challenge due to positive margins compromising outcomes. This study sought to explore the safety and efficacy of DGPR1008, a novel near-infrared (NIR) prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted fluorescent contrast agent, in fluorescence-guided surgery (FGS) of PCa.MethodsThis single-center, open-label, prospective, single-arm, exploratory study was carried out in The Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University, China. Patients newly diagnosed with PCa (a Gleason score ≥7) were eligible for enrollment. All patients underwent laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) with pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND) using DGPR1008. Four dose cohorts were studied: dose cohort A, which received 0.06 mg/kg of DGPR1008 24 h preoperatively; dose cohort B, which received 0.03 mg/kg of DGPR1008 24 h preoperatively; dose cohort C, which received 0.03 mg/kg of DGPR1008 12 h preoperatively, and dose cohort D, which received 0.045 mg/kg of DGPR1008 24 h preoperatively. Safety and efficacy were assessed.ResultsFrom July, 2023 to January, 2024, 14 eligible patients were included in the study. In total, 32 adverse events (AEs) were observed. One patient (7.1%) presented with four serious adverse events (SAEs), including urinary fistula, infected lymphocele, wound complications, and acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. None of the AEs or SAEs was found to be associated with the administration of DGPR1008. Compared with the other dose cohorts, DGPR1008 showed the highest sensitivity (80.6%) for detecting PCa in vivo in dose cohort B. Further, dose cohort B provided the highest target-to-background ratio (TBR) through all phases of tumor imaging, both in vivo and ex vivo, on the gross and fixed tissue. DGPR1008 enabled the real-time visualization of tumor-positive surgical margins, and had a sensitivity of 90% in vivo and 100% ex vivo.ConclusionsDGPR1008 is safe and well tolerated, and may be used in the intraoperative identification of primary PCa and positive surgical margins. Dose cohort B (0.03 mg/kg, 24 h preoperatively) is optimal for efficacy and TBR.Keywords: Fluorescence-guided surgery (FGS), prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA), prostate cancer (PCa), intraoperative imaging, radical prostatectomy

Highlight box

Key findings

• DGPR1008, a novel near-infrared prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted tracer, demonstrated high sensitivity and optimal tumor-to-background contrast for intraoperative prostate cancer (PCa) detection. Administered at 0.03 mg/kg 24 hours preoperatively, it achieved 80.6% sensitivity for in vivo tumor identification and a sensitivity of 90% in vivo and 100% ex vivo for surgical margin detection. All adverse events were unrelated to the agent.

What is known and what is new?

• PSMA is an established target for PCa imaging and surgical guidance. Multiple PSMA-targeted tracers are under clinical evaluation.

• Our study is the first to investigate DGPR1008 in humans. It showed higher tumor detection sensitivity and better intraoperative imaging compared to previously reported agents.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• DGPR1008 enables real-time visualization of prostate tumors and margins, potentially improving surgical precision. Future randomized, multicenter trials are warranted to confirm its clinical benefit and support standardization of PSMA-based fluorescence-guided surgery in PCa.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is among the most frequently diagnosed malignancies in men worldwide and remains a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality (1). For patients with localized disease, radical prostatectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND) is the standard of care (2). However, two major surgical challenges persist: achieving complete tumor resection with negative surgical margins and accurately identifying metastatic lymph nodes intraoperatively (3). Failure to overcome these issues can result in residual disease and early recurrence, ultimately compromising oncologic outcomes.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA), a type II transmembrane glycoprotein, is overexpressed in more than 90% of primary PCa lesions and their nodal and distant metastases (4,5). Owing to its high specificity, PSMA is a well-validated molecular target for both imaging and therapy in PCa and has demonstrated superior accuracy in nodal staging compared to conventional imaging modalities, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) (6-8) .

In recent years, PSMA-targeted intraoperative imaging has emerged as a promising approach to improve surgical precision. Two main strategies have been developed: PSMA-radioguided surgery (PSMA-RGS) and PSMA-fluorescence-guided surgery (PSMA-FGS) (9,10). While PSMA-RGS has shown utility in identifying metastatic lymph nodes using tracers such as 99mTc-PSMA-I&S, 111In-PSMA, and 68Ga-labeled agents (11-19), its reliance on radioactivity and limited spatial resolution constrain its application for real-time tumor delineation during surgery.

PSMA-FGS offers a radiation-free alternative that enables real-time visual identification of tumor tissue and surgical margins. Several PSMA-targeted tracers—such as OTL78 (20), IS-002 (21), and ICG-PSMA-D5 (22) —have entered early-phase clinical trials, demonstrating encouraging feasibility and performance. However, challenges remain regarding optimal dosing, timing, and tumor-to-background discrimination.

DGPR1008, a short peptide, is a novel near-infrared (NIR) fluorescent probe targeting PSMA, developed to enhance intraoperative detection of PCa. Preclinical study has shown favorable pharmacokinetics and the ability to clearly distinguish tumor from adjacent normal tissue (unpublished data). Initial safety assessments in healthy volunteers confirmed its tolerability (unpublished data).

This first-in-human study aims to evaluate the safety, feasibility, and imaging performance of DGPR1008 in patients undergoing laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP). Specifically, we sought to determine the optimal dose and timing for intraoperative administration and to assess its sensitivity for detecting PCa and tumor-positive surgical margins. We present this article in accordance with the TREND reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-537/rc).

Methods

Ethics

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (approval No. 242018). All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/), with the registration number: ChiCTR2500104844.

Study design

This single-center, open-label, prospective, single-arm exploratory trial was carried out in The Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University. A total of 14 patients with newly diagnosed PCa were enrolled between July 21, 2023 and January 12, 2024. The study workflow is summarized in Figure S1.

Intervention and imaging procedure

DGPR1008, a novel PSMA-targeted tracer, was supplied by SIGNDO Biotechnology (Suzhou, China) (Design of DGPR1008 is shown in Appendix 1). Based on preclinical data (see Appendix 2), an initial dose of 0.06 mg/kg was selected. Patients received a single intravenous injection of DGPR1008 preoperatively, allocated into four dose cohorts:

❖ Cohort A: 0.06 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort A: 0.06 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort B: 0.03 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort B: 0.03 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort C: 0.03 mg/kg, 12 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort C: 0.03 mg/kg, 12 h before surgery;

❖ Cohort D: 0.045 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery.

❖ Cohort D: 0.045 mg/kg, 24 h before surgery.

Vital signs, ECG, and laboratory values were monitored before, during, and after drug administration. Based on safety and imaging findings from the first three patients, dosing adjustments were made to reduce background signal and optimize imaging contrast. Adverse events were recorded up to 14 days postoperatively. All data were recorded using case report form (CRF) forms.

LRP and PLND were performed under white light using standard techniques. Intraoperative imaging was conducted using a NIR fluorescence laparoscope (wavelength 785–805 nm; H3800, DPM, China). Imaging was performed before, during, and after prostate and lymph node dissection (the workflow for DGPR1008 in FGS is shown in Appendix 3).

Following resection, the prostate was sectioned into nine standard regions (see Appendix 4) (20). Fluorescence imaging was repeated on ex vivo specimens, including the prostate, lymph nodes, and residual fluorescent tissue from the surgical bed. All specimens were formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned for histological analysis.

Histopathology and fluorescence evaluation

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was used to confirm tumor presence according to ISUP criteria. PSMA expression was assessed via immunohistochemistry using the total immunostaining score (TIS) (see Appendix 5) (23).

Fluorescence microscopy (Odyssey CLx, LI-COR, USA) was used to evaluate DGPR1008 signal in relation to tumor presence and PSMA expression on microscopic slides.

Image analysis

Fluorescence images (in vivo and ex vivo) were analyzed using ImageJ (v1.48, NIH). The target-to-background ratio (TBR) was calculated by dividing the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of the lesion by that of the surrounding normal tissue (see Appendix 6). A TBR ≥1.5 was considered indicative of clear lesion contrast (24). For residual disease assessment, TBR was calculated between the resection bed and adjacent background.

Statistical analysis

As a first-in-human, exploratory study focusing on safety and tolerability exploration rather than power calculations, no formal sample size calculation was performed. Continuous variables were summarized using means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages.

Sensitivity, specificity, false-positive (FP), and false-negative (FN) rates were calculated using 2×2 contingency tables, using final histopathology as the reference standard. Between-group comparisons were assessed using Student’s t-test. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM, USA) and GraphPad Prism 6.0 (GraphPad Software, USA).

Results

Patient characteristics

Of 20 patients screened between July 21, 2023 and January 12, 2024, 14 met all eligibility criteria. The median age at enrollment was 69 years (IQR, 69–70 years), with a median baseline PSA of 24.0 ng/mL (IQR, 23.3–25.8 ng/mL) and a median prostate volume of 34 mL (IQR, 28.6–56.4 mL). Most patients (57.1%) had tumors classified as ISUP grade group ≥3. All underwent LRP and PLND (Table 1).

Efficacy

A total of 135 regions were evaluated across all patients, including 126 prostate sections and 9 residual fluorescent foci. Among these, 80 regions (59.3%) were confirmed to harbor PCa on histology.

In vivo imaging

DGPR1008 identified 58 of 80 tumor-positive regions in vivo, resulting in a sensitivity of 72.5% and specificity of 89.1% (Figure 1A, Table 3). Sensitivity increased with higher ISUP grade groups and specificity improved with stronger PSMA expression (TIS ≥2).

Surgical margin and residual tumor analysis

A total of 10 tumor-positive surgical margins were confirmed histologically in six patients. In vivo fluorescence imaging detected 9 of these (sensitivity: 90%), while ex vivo imaging identified all 10 (100%) (Figure S2 and Table S1).

In addition, 9 residual fluorescent foci were excised from the prostate bed or bladder neck after prostatectomy. Of these, 7 (77.8%) were confirmed as tumor-positive. All apex and anterior rectal wall foci were true positives, whereas 2 of 3 bladder neck specimens were false positives, likely due to DGPR1008 excretion in urine contaminating the surgical field.

Discussion

PSMA is overexpressed in more than 90% of primary PCa lesions and their nodal and distant metastases. Owing to its high specificity, PSMA is a well-validated molecular target for both imaging and therapy in PCa. In terms of diagnosis, PSMA PET/CT could spare 80% scheduled biopsies showing a lower false positive rate in comparison with mpMRI (20% vs. 43.3%) in men enrolled in Active surveillance protocols (25). In addition, fluoride 18 (18F) PSMA PET/CT can also detect prostatic ductal carcinoma (DAC) that is difficult to identify by conventional imaging modalities (26). With respect to treatment, 177Lu-PSMA-RLT is recommended as a second-line treatment option for PSMA-positive metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) patients (27).

This first-in-human study demonstrates that DGPR1008, a novel NIR PSMA-targeted fluorescent agent, is safe, well tolerated, and has the potential for real-time intraoperative detection of PCa and tumor-positive surgical margins. Unlike PSMA-radioguided surgery (PSMA-RGS), which relies on ionizing radiation, PSMA-fluorescence-guided surgery (PSMA-FGS) offers a radiation-free approach with improved spatial resolution and visual guidance. Our findings support DGPR1008 as a promising addition to the growing field of PSMA-FGS agents, alongside OTL78, IS-002, and ICG-PSMA-D5 (20-22).

DGPR1008 achieved an in vivo tumor detection sensitivity of 72.5%, with the optimal performance observed in cohort B (0.03 mg/kg, 24 hours preoperatively), which yielded 80.6% sensitivity and the highest TBRs across all imaging phases. This performance is superior to the sensitivity reported for OTL78 (65.3%) in comparable studies (20,28,29), highlighting the potential of DGPR1008 to improve intraoperative tumor delineation.

The 24-hour preoperative administration window was found to be optimal for both logistical and biological reasons. Preclinical data showed that DGPR1008 maintains high tumor-specific signal up to 72 hours post-injection. Moreover, a 24-hour interval allows sufficient clearance from non-target tissues and minimizes bladder contamination, which can be problematic with shorter intervals. Importantly, this timeframe aligns with standard hospital workflows, facilitating practical integration into surgical planning.

DGPR1008 also demonstrated high accuracy in identifying positive surgical margins, with a sensitivity of 90% in vivo and 100% ex vivo. The only missed margin was located dorsally, an anatomically challenging region during laparoscopic procedures. In addition, 77.8% of suspicious residual fluorescent foci excised from the prostate bed were confirmed to be tumor-positive, emphasizing the agent’s value in identifying occult disease that would otherwise remain untreated. These findings are consistent with prior reports using PSMA-FGS agents (20-22) and suggest a potential role for DGPR1008 in improving surgical completeness.

Nevertheless, this study has several limitations. The single-arm, single-center design and small sample size limit the generalizability of the results. Without a control group, formal comparisons with standard methods or other tracers cannot be made, which restricts the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, the study did not assess long-term oncologic outcomes such as recurrence or survival. Lastly, false-positive signals near the bladder neck—likely due to urinary excretion of the agent—represent a potential pitfall in intraoperative interpretation, warranting further optimization in probe design, surgical timing or surgical procedures, such as: irrigating the bladder with warm saline before opening the bladder neck.

To address these limitations, a multicenter phase 2 trial is currently underway to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy, nodal staging performance, and potential oncologic impact of DGPR1008-guided surgery in a broader patient population.

Conclusions

DGPR1008 is a safe and feasible PSMA-targeted tracer with the potential for intraoperative identification of primary PCa, positive margins, and residual disease during LRP, with particular utility in patients harboring high-grade tumors. A single intravenous dose of 0.03 mg/kg administered 24 hours prior to surgery provides optimal imaging performance. These findings support the continued clinical development of DGPR1008, and larger randomized studies are now warranted to validate its clinical utility and impact on surgical outcomes.

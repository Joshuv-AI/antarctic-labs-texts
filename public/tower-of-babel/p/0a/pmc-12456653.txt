Evaluation of correlation between etiology of end stage liver disease and intraoperative utilization of blood products during liver transplantation
Authors: Amer Majeed, Basel A. Jobeir, Muhammad Shabbir, Mohammed Ibrahim, Mohamad S. Nagy, Bilal Tufail, Dimitri A. Raptis, Luluah Altukhaifi, Areej A. G. AlFattani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Liver transplantation is frequently associated with massive blood loss and utilization of blood products to optimize coagulation; this study aimed to evaluate their possible correlation with etiological and perioperative factors.Methods:A retrospective analysis of adult liver transplant (LT) recipients (excluding re-do transplants) operated upon at our center between 2011 and 2021 was conducted using R package with rBiostatistics.com graphical user interface.Results:Of the 947 cases, 70.70% had cirrhosis, and 28.6% hepatocellular carcinoma, as secondary diagnoses; the most common primary diagnosis was viral hepatitis (B = 20.9% and C = 20.0%). The mean blood loss volume was 3393.2 ml. Living donor liver transplantation (LDLT) recipients (n = 740, 78.2%) had lesser blood loss (mean difference 738 mL, P = 0.037) and reduced requirement for fresh frozen plasma (FFP, OR = 0.734, P = 0.001) and platelets (OR = 0.809, P < 0.001). Presence of hepatocellular carcinoma (n = 273, 28.6%) was significantly protective for blood loss (mean difference 717 ml, P = 0.037) and the need for FFP (OR = 0.991, P < 0.001), cryoprecipitate (OR = 0.568, P = 0.001), and platelets (OR = 0.602, P < 0.001). Schistosomiasis (n = 23, 2.4%) was accompanied by increased blood loss (mean difference 2328 ml (P = 0.012)). A body mass index (BMI) >35 kg/m2 increased the hazard of cryoprecipitate requirement (OR = 1.203, P = 0.008).Conclusion:The blood loss and the blood products transfusion requirements in LT are influenced by the etiology, graft type, and other perioperative factors such as BMI.

Introduction

End stage liver disease (ESLD) often requires liver transplantation (LT) to improve survival and quality of life.[1] The procedure is highly complex and frequently complicated by significant intraoperative blood loss requiring transfusion of blood and blood products.[2] Excessive blood loss and transfusion during orthotopic liver transplant can diminish graft survival, increase morbidity, and prolong ICU stay.[3] Effective utilization of blood products during LT can improve these outcomes,[4] yet it carries inherent risks, such as transfusion reactions and the transmission of infectious diseases.[5]

Current literature does not provide a reliable tool to predict the anticipated blood loss or the need for transfusion of blood and blood products during liver transplantation surgery; this in turn is linked with the lack of a comprehensive overview of the factors contributing to coagulopathy and blood loss in these patients. Yet, the need for transfusions is crucial to improve patient care.[6]

The patient and graft morbidity and mortality data from King Faisal specialist Hospital (KFSH) have been published separately and are comparable with other high-volume centers globally.[78] A wide variation in blood management practices in LT is reported internationally[9]; the local protocol evolved here over the past 2 decades directs judicious use of packed red blood cells (and salvaged red blood cells), fresh frozen plasma (FFP), cryoprecipitate, and platelets to target an intraoperative hemoglobin level between 70 and 80 g/L, INR between 2 and 2.5, and thromboelastographic (TEG/ROTEM) values approximately 20% undercorrected.

This study aimed to find associations between the etiology of ESLD and graft type, with intraoperative blood loss and blood product utilization. Identifying these relationships may potentially guide the clinicians to develop targeted strategies to minimize blood loss and transfusion requirements.

Material and Methods

A retrospective electronic chart review was carried out at a high-volume LT center on all patients who underwent liver transplantation between 2011 and 2021. Pediatric patients (<18 years of age) or those undergoing redo-transplantation were excluded; absolute blood or blood product volumes are smaller in the former, and complexity of the surgery causes more than usual blood loss in the later, making the comparisons difficult.

Data collected included demographics, etiology of liver disease (ICD-10 nomenclature),[10] the latest preoperative laboratory results (complete blood counts, coagulation, liver and renal functions, etc.), type of donation (living or deceased), type of liver graft (whole liver, right, left, or left-lateral partial graft), estimated blood loss volume (eBLV), transfused blood volume (TxV) including cell salvaged blood, and utilization of blood products, including FFP, platelets, and cryoprecipitate.

Statistical analysis

The characteristics of the study population, including mean, standard deviation (SD), range, and interquartile range (IQR), were reported for continuous variables and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Box plots were employed for data visualization. The associations between etiology of liver disease, graft type, and other perioperative factors with eBLV and blood product utilization were analyzed using linear regression and binary Cox regression models. The BMI cutoffs used in the analysis included obese (BMI > 30), severely obese (BMI > 35), and morbid obesity (BMI > 40). While for age, the cutoff was set at 65 for univariate analyses.

Binary Cox regression was used to identify factors having higher risk of blood products transfusion, while linear regression was used to find the variables associated with the highest amount of blood loss. All significant univariate analyses (P < 0.05) and BMI with the highest significance were included in the multivariate analysis. Cases with missing data and factors with very low frequency (<5) were omitted in the linear regression analysis to improve reliability. The significance level was set at P < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using R version 3.3.2 (R Core Team, GNU GPL v2 License), R Studio version 1.0.44 (RStudio, Inc. GNU Affero General Public License v3, Boston, MA, 2016) with the graphical user interface (GUI) rBiostatistics.com alpha version (rBiostatistics.com, London, UK, 2017).

Ethical considerations

The Research Ethics Committee at the King Faisal Specialist Hospital and Research Center, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia provided ethics approval for this study on August 16, 2021 via REC # 2211161, including waiver of informed consent for retrospective examination of the routinely collected clinical data during the patient care episodes. The study was conducted in line with international ethical standards of Declaration of Helsinki and ICH GCP guidelines following local regulations of Saudi Arabia. Patient confidentiality was maintained through anonymization. The data were securely stored on password-protected hospital computers and accessed only by the research team when needed.

Discussion

Several key factors influence blood product utilization during liver transplantation. Our study highlighted the independent effects of HCC, parasitic or abscess etiologies, LDLT, and preoperative hemoglobin on intraoperative blood loss; although the effect of viral etiology was significant in univariate analyses, it lost significance in the multivariate model. Similarly, the independent role of HCC in reducing the usage of FFP, cryoprecipitate, and platelets was elucidated; LD was associated with lesser usage of platelets. Again, BMI > 30 kg/m² and viral etiologies showed significant effect for blood products utilization with univariate analyses but lost significance with multivariate models suggesting confounding by other factors. The favorable effect of the presence of HCC on reducing blood loss and blood products consumption [Figure 4] is possibly attributable to the accompanied higher coagulable status.[1112]

Blood products requirements in the presence of HCC

Patient health status and the source of the liver graft may influence the necessity for and management of blood products during surgery; for example, a BMI greater than 35 kg/m² was observed to increase the cryoprecipitate requirements [Figure 5]. An LD graft significantly lowered platelet transfusion needs; possible explanations perhaps include reduced immunogenicity through improved donor–recipient matching, or better graft preservation through shorter ischemia times.

Blood products requirements according to BMI categories

In contrast, the elevated blood loss in patients with parasitic abscesses may be explained by the inflammatory response characteristic of infectious processes which may produce tissue fragility and hypervascularity complicating the surgical dissection and hemostasis.[13]

These findings suggest that personalized perioperative strategies, tailored to the patient’s specific conditions and type of liver received, such as preoperative optimization of coagulopathy, or institution-specific blood management protocols, may help optimizing blood product use and improving surgical outcomes.

These findings also align with the previous studies that suggest the inflammatory liver diseases tend to present more complex intraoperative challenges, reduced intraoperative blood loss in patients receiving organs from living donors,[14] and higher blood loss in those with parasitic infections[15] or elevated BMI.[16] Advocating for refined criteria in the utilization of blood products and the adoption of surgical technologies such as intraoperative blood salvage and normothermic regional perfusion could possibly further minimize the need for transfusions.[1718]

A single-center study and a large cohort offer the advantage of standardization of the anesthetic and surgical practices, yet it potentially has a limitation in extrapolation of results across other centers or settings. A multicenter prospective cohort study design may validate and possibly generalize the findings, enhancing their applicability to diverse clinical settings. We recommend that future research should incorporate coagulation and ESLD score profiling for various etiologies and explore the physiological and molecular mechanisms underlying the observed differences in blood loss and blood product utilization among various etiologies of liver disease.

Conclusion

The findings of this study highlight the need for etiology-specific blood management strategies, particularly in patients with HCC, LDLT, and parasitic liver disease, to enhance patient outcomes and surgical efficiency through tailored perioperative transfusion management.

Key Message

The blood loss and the blood products transfusion requirements in Liver Transplant (LT) are influenced by the etiology, graft type, and other perioperative factors; parasitic and abscess etiology is associated with higher blood loss, whereas presence of hepatocellular carcinoma and living donor graft have a protective effect against blood loss.

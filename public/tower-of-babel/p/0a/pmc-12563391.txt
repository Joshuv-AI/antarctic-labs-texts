Comparative Evaluation of Apically Extruded Debris Using Two Rotary and Two Reciprocating File Systems: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Arjun B Ravi, V. P. Prabath Singh, T. R. Razdan, Shince Mathew, R. Kavitha, Kaushik Haridas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:The research aimed to quantify, evaluate, and compare the amount of apically extruded debris in 80 extracted mandibular premolars using two rotary and reciprocating file systems.Materials and Method:Rotary and reciprocating single files were employed to instrument root canals. Debris was collected using Eppendorf tubes, and an analytical microbalance was used to ascertain the average weight of debris.Results:While there was no discernible difference among the two groups, data demonstrated that reciprocating file systems generated significantly higher quantities of debris in contrast to continuous rotation group file systems.Conclusion:As per data from the study, all four file systems generated measurable amounts of apically extruded debris; however, reciprocating file systems produced noticeably greater cell debris than continuous rotation file systems.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontics mainly involves debridement, disinfection, and obturation to assist periradicular healing. Chemomechanical preparation of the root canal is pivotal in modern treatment.[1] Debridement leads to extrusion of infected debris through the apical foramen, triggering postoperative flare-ups and peripheral inflammation. This incidence ranges between 1.4% and 16%, resulting in pain, swelling, and unscheduled emergency treatment visits.[23] Minimizing apically extruded debris can mitigate postoperative discomfort, based on preparation methods and file system design.[4]

ProTaper Universal (PTU) and ProTaper Next (PTN) rotary systems are popularly used for root canal preparation, with varying tapers for progressive canal preparation.[5] WaveOne (WO) and Reciproc (RP) NiTi systems revolutionized root canal preparation by operating in unequal reciprocal motion.[6] Using two rotary and reciprocating file systems, this investigation sought to assess and contrast the total amount of apically extruded debris from extracted human mandibular premolars by both systems.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

80 extracted human mandibular premolars with mature apices, straight single canals, and similar root lengths were selected and randomly divided into four groups (N=20). Teeth with defects or curvature >10° were excluded. Teeth were disinfected in 0.5% chloramine-T for 48h and stored in distilled water. Apical patency confirmed with size 10 K-file (Mani, Japan) & glide path prepared to size 20 K-file (Mani, Japan).

The Myers and Montgomery (1991) model[7] was used. Each tooth was mounted in an Eppendorf tube through a rubber stopper with CEJ 1–2 mm above it. A 27-gaugeneedle balanced air pressure. Tubes were placed in glass vials [Figure 1].

Experimental setup according to Myers and Montgomery. (Source – Original)

Each empty Eppendorf tube was weighed three times using analytical balance (Sartorius, Germany), and the average was recorded.

All groups were instrumented using X-Smart Plus motor with manufacturer settings:

Group I: ProTaper Universal (SX to 2/3 Working length (WL), S1/S2 to WL–1 mm, F1–F4 to WL in continuous rotation).

Group II: ProTaper Next (X1–X4 to WL in continuous rotation).

Group III: WaveOne (Size 40 and a taper of 0.08 to WL using reciprocating motion).

Group IV: Reciproc (R40 file to WL using reciprocating motion).

After every three strokes, files were cleaned, canal patency was confirmed using a size 10 K-file. A total of 5 mL distilled water was used for irrigation. After instrumentation, the root was rinsed with 1 mL distilled water into the same tube to collect debris. Tubes were dried using a lyophilizer (LYOMAC, Switzerland) and reweighed thrice, final debris weight was calculated by subtracting initial from post-instrumentation weight.

RESULTS

One-way ANOVA test identified a significant difference (P < 0.001) in mean values of debris extrusion among four groups. [Table 1]. Post-hoc Bonferroni test confirmed the difference between rotary and reciprocating file systems (P < 0.05) [Table 2]. Reciprocating single-file systems extruded more debris than continuous rotation group file systems. The greatest amount of debris extrusion was observed in the Recipro single-file system, followed by WaveOne and ProTaper Universal.

DISCUSSION

Postoperative pain is primarily caused by extrusion of infected debris during chemomechanical preparation, disrupting the balance between aggression and defense leading to inflammatory reaction, causing pain. Instrumentation techniques vary in extrusion amount, with crown-down techniques being more effective for infected root canals. Instrumentation method, instrument type, size and taper, length and width of root canal, preparation end point, irrigating needle, and solution were among the factors that affected the extrusion of debris.[8] To reduce operator bias, a standardized procedure was used, with ISO Size 40 as the standard apical diameter for master apical files. Eppendorf tube debris collecting technique proposed by Myers and Montgomery[8] was employed.

The current investigation demonstrated that every file system studied resulted in apical extrusion of debris. Significant differences were found between reciprocating group (WO and RP) and rotary group (PTU and PTN) file systems. Because of its in-and-out and reciprocating filing action, the Reciproc and WaveOne files may function as a piston, extruding more debris and irrigant than file systems with continuous rotation. The variation in the apical expulsion of debris is also significantly influenced by the instrument design.[9] WaveOne files, despite having a greater taper, demonstrated lower cutting efficiency[10] and less debris extrusion compared to Reciproc files. Although they are unable to displace debris coronally, these files may remove a significant quantity of dentin in a shorter length of time. The ProTaper Universal file systems have balanced pitches, active cutting blades, a convex triangular cross-section design, a progressive taper, and a revamped guiding tip, resulting in greater cutting efficiency and less debris extrusion in PTU compared to reciprocating groups.[11] The ProTaper Next file system’s offset design improves waste removal, reduces interaction between the file and dentin, and improves file flexibility, potentially leading to less debris extrusion in PTN file systems.[12]

The study emphasizes the need for standardized experimental groups and procedures for clinical applications. It acknowledges the influence of chemomechanical instrumentation on apical extrusion, with vital teeth having a pulp stump as a barrier, while necrotic teeth lack such resistance. The data suggest developing study designs that better reflect in vivo conditions and include other parameters like apical pressure.

CONCLUSION

The study found that all file systems produced measurable debris, with full-sequence rotary file systems having less apically extruded debris than reciprocating single-file systems. There was no discernible difference between reciprocating and continuous rotation files. Debris extrusion was the highest by the Reciproc single file system and the lowest by the ProTaper Next file system.

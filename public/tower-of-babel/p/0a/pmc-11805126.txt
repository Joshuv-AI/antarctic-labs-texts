Role of TM7 in the Oral Microbiome and its Implications for Periodontal Disease: A Systematic Review
Authors: J. Bhuvaneswarri, Julius Amaldas, Vidhya Rekha Umapathy, V Ramya
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPeriodontal disease, a prevalent oral health condition, involves a complex microbial community. Advanced sequencing technologies have enabled in-depth studies of the oral microbiome, focusing on specific bacterial taxa like TM7. This systematic review analyzes the role of TM7 in the oral microbiome and its implications for periodontal disease. This review underscores TM7’s importance in the oral microbiome and its potential as a target for future research on periodontal disease pathogenesis.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal disease is a prevalent oral health condition characterized by inflammation and destruction of the supporting tissues surrounding the teeth. The oral microbiome is a diverse ecosystem of microorganisms inhabiting the oral cavity, and its dysbiosis has been implicated in the pathogenesis of periodontal disease.[12]

TM7 is a candidate phylum, initially identified through metagenomic studies, and its taxonomy and characteristics are still being elucidated (Bor et al., 2020).[34]

The objectives of this review are:

To provide an overview of the current understanding of periodontal disease and the oral microbiome.

To introduce TM7 as a less well-known bacterial group in the oral microbiome and describe its taxonomy and characteristics.

To explore the existing literature on the potential role of TM7 in periodontal health and disease.[56]

To discuss the implications of TM7 in the pathogenesis and progression of periodontal disease.

To identify gaps in the current knowledge and highlight the need for further research on TM7 in the context of periodontal health.[78]

Research Question: What is the current evidence regarding the role of TM7 in the oral microbiome and its implications for the development and progression of periodontal disease?

Population (P): Patients diagnosed with periodontal disease

Intervention (I): Presence or abundance of TM7 in the oral microbiome comparison

(C): Comparisons may include different levels of TM7 abundance or comparisons between periodontal disease severity groups

Outcome (O): Impact on periodontal health parameters (e.g., clinical attachment loss, pocket depth, gingival inflammation)

A study repoted[13] a positive correlation between the abundance of TM7 and periodontal clinical parameters, further supporting the involvement of TM7 in periodontal disease.[1] A study reported[9] identified specific TM7 subtypes that were significantly associated with periodontitis severity, suggesting a potential role of specific TM7 strains in disease progression.[12]

A study reported[10] a higher prevalence of TM7 in patients with aggressive periodontitis compared to healthy controls, indicating a potential association between TM7 and the aggressive form of the disease.[13] Nguyen et al.[14] (2022) investigated the presence of TM7 in peri-implantitis lesions and found a higher prevalence of TM7-associated bacteria in diseased sites compared to healthy implant sites, suggesting a potential role of TM7 in implant-related diseases. A study reported[1516] conducted a longitudinal study and observed a significant increase in TM7 levels in patients who experienced periodontal disease progression, suggesting that TM7 may serve as a biomarker for disease monitoring.[15]

DISCUSSION

The findings consistently suggest a significant association between TM7 abundance and periodontal disease severity. Higher levels of TM7 were consistently observed in patients with periodontal disease compared to healthy individuals, aligning with existing literature and indicating its potential role in disease pathogenesis.[1516]

Additionally, the co-aggregation of TM7 with other oral bacteria emphasizes its role in forming microbial biofilms linked to periodontal disease.[12131415]

CONCLUSION

This systematic review provides a comprehensive analysis of the current evidence on the role of TM7 in the oral microbiome and its implications for periodontal disease. Further high-quality studies are needed to establish a clearer understanding of TM7’s contribution to periodontal disease pathogenesis and its potential as a therapeutic target.

The research landscape and trends of dental pulp stem cell studies (2010–2025): A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Yu Zheng, Zhe Tang, Lu Li, Zheng Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) have emerged as a promising therapeutic modality in regenerative medicine, yet systematic bibliometric evaluation of global research landscape has been lacking.MethodsPublications from 2010 to 2025 were retrieved from Web of Science Core Collection. After screening, 4599 English-language articles and reviews were analyzed using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Bibliometrix for publication trends, geographic distribution, author collaboration, journal characteristics, citation networks, and keyword co-occurrence.ResultsGlobal DPSC research demonstrated sustained growth, with annual publications increasing from 97 in 2010 to 464 in 2024. China (32.6 %), United States (15.9 %), and Japan (9.9 %) contributed over half the global output, though U.S. publications achieved higher citation impact. Sichuan University, Sun Yat-sen University, and Capital Medical University ranked highest in productivity. Author collaboration revealed interconnected clusters led by pioneering researchers including Shi Songtao, Nakashima Misako, and Nor Jacques E. Journal of Endodontics was the leading publishing platform, while co-citation analysis highlighted intellectual influence of multidisciplinary journals including Stem Cells, Biomaterials, and PNAS. Keyword analyses demonstrated thematic evolution from cellular characterization toward translational applications, with emerging frontiers focusing on extracellular vesicles, exosomes, and cell-free therapeutic strategies.ConclusionsDPSC research has evolved into a mature, internationally collaborative scientific discipline successfully bridging fundamental biology and clinical application. A pivotal paradigm shift from cell-centric to cell-free therapies is currently underway. Future breakthroughs will likely depend on harnessing the DPSC secretome and developing intelligent, clinically viable regenerative strategies.Keywords: Dental pulp stem cells, Bibliometric, Regenerative medicine, VOSviewer, CiteSpace

Introduction

Over the past two decades, stem cells have been proposed for regenerative medicine applications and have demonstrated significant roles in tissue repair and disease treatment. As an important component of this field, the discovery of dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) has fundamentally transformed the landscape of regenerative dentistry, establishing new paradigms for tissue engineering and clinical therapeutics in oral and maxillofacial medicine. In 2000, Gronthos et al. were the first to successfully isolate and characterize DPSCs, which represented the first mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) to be identified from oral and maxillofacial tissues [1]. Following this landmark work, Miura et al. isolated stem cells from human exfoliated deciduous teeth (SHED) in 2003 [2]. Subsequent research has identified additional dental-derived stem cell populations [3], including periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) [4], stem cells from the apical papilla (SCAP) [5], gingival mesenchymal stem cells (GMSCs) and dental follicle stem cells (DFSCs) [6]. Despite their diverse origins, these cell populations are collectively referred to as dental stem cells (DSCs). The rapid development in this field has profoundly changed our understanding of dental tissue regeneration and expanded the therapeutic scope beyond traditional endodontic treatments. Dental pulp stem cells represent a distinct subset of mesenchymal stem cells derived from the neural crest. They possess the fundamental characteristics of stem cells, including self-renewal capacity, an adherent growth pattern, clonogenic potential, and multi-lineage differentiation capabilities [3,7,8]. Research has demonstrated that compared to classical bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMMSCs), DPSCs exhibit biological properties, multipotent differentiation potential, and immunomodulatory functions similar to BMMSCs [9,10]. However, DPSCs, particularly SHED, offer distinct advantages, including ease of accessibility and enhanced clonogenic and proliferative capacities in vitro. DPSCs can modulate growth factors, transcription factors, extracellular matrix proteins, and receptor molecules [11,12], enabling their unique capacity for direct differentiation into odontoblast-like cells and their ability to regenerate dentin-pulp complex structures following in vivo transplantation [13]. Their distinctive neural crest origin endows them with powerful neurogenic and angiogenic potential, a characteristic not commonly observed in MSCs from other sources, thereby establishing a unique biological foundation for their applications in tissue regeneration, particularly in neural injury repair [10].

The field of DPSC research has evolved significantly, progressing from initial proof-of-concept studies demonstrating DPSC-mediated dentin formation and SHED-mediated pulp-like tissue generation to sophisticated tissue engineering approaches integrating biomaterials, scaffolds, growth factors, and advanced cell delivery systems. Early investigations concentrated on their multipotent differentiation capacity, confirming that DPSCs can give rise to osteogenic, chondrogenic, adipogenic, neurogenic and myogenic lineages [8,14,15]. As research advanced, subsequent studies elucidated their unique cell surface markers (e.g., CD146, PD-1, Gli1) [2,16,17], their specific perivascular niche [18], and their pivotal role in immunomodulation. In recent years, research focus appears to have expanded from traditional biomaterial scaffold-based tissue engineering strategies to utilizing scaffold-free cell aggregates formed through cellular self-assembly for functional tissue regeneration [19]. Particularly encouraging is that DPSC-mediated pulp regeneration has progressed from animal models to clinical trials, successfully achieving functional pulp tissue regeneration containing vasculature and innervation in patients, and even promoting further root development [20,21]. Furthermore, the therapeutic applications of DPSCs and SHED have extended beyond orofacial contexts, demonstrating efficacy in preclinical models of systemic diseases, including ischemic brain injury, spinal cord injury, kidney injury, and diabetes [10,22].

This explosive growth in research breadth and depth has generated a vast body of academic literature, expanding from cellular and molecular biology to translational and clinical applications. However, despite the rapid increase in publications, the overall knowledge structure, major contributors, collaboration patterns, and thematic evolution of this field remain inadequately and insufficiently characterized. Bibliometrics, as a powerful quantitative analytical tool, can objectively reveal disciplinary development trends, collaboration patterns, and intellectual structures through visualization mapping[[23], [24], [25]]. Therefore, this study aims to conduct the first systematic quantitative and qualitative analysis of global research literature on DPSCs using bibliometric methods. The objective is to delineate the current research status and development trends in this field, providing researchers with a clear macroscopic knowledge map and offering data support for senior scholars to identify future directions and seek interdisciplinary collaborations, ultimately facilitating more efficient translation of basic DPSC research into clinical applications.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategies

The data for this study were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). The literature search was conducted on July 2, 2025, covering the period from January 1, 2010, to May 1, 2025. In present study, the search strategy was set as the following: TS=((pulp OR "dental pulp" OR "tooth pulp" OR "pulp tissue" OR pulpal) AND ("stem cell∗" OR "progenitor cell∗" OR "mesenchymal stem cell∗" OR "MSC∗" OR "DPSC∗" OR "SHED" OR "SCAP" OR "dental pulp stem cell∗" OR "stem cells from human exfoliated deciduous teeth" OR "stem cells from apical papilla")), Only “Article” and “Review” were selected as article types, and the language was limited to English, 6565 records were obtained. A two-stage screening process was implemented by two independent reviewers from our team. Initially, all publications were screened based on their titles and abstracts to exclude irrelevant articles. In cases of uncertainty, the full text was reviewed to determine eligibility. Following this rigorous screening, a final corpus of 4599 articles was included in the analysis. The full records of all included publications, encompassing bibliographic information such as titles, authors, affiliations, publication sources, years, countries/regions, journal impact factors, and cited references, were exported as plain text (.txt) files for subsequent analysis.

Data analysis and visualization

The prepared.txt files were imported into the online analysis platform Literature Metrology (http://bibliometric.com/) for visualization analysis of country collaboration relationships and publication volume information. VOSviewer (version 1.6.18), developed by Leiden University in the Netherlands, is one of the most widely used tools in bibliometrics, offering robust functionalities for collaboration, thematic, and citation-based analyses. In this study, we utilized VOSviewer to generate network maps that intuitively illustrate the collaborative relationships among authors, institutions, and countries, as well as the co-occurrence of keywords. Nodes represent entities such as time, country/region, institution, author, keyword or cited reference; node size denotes an element's weight (e.g., number of publications, citations or occurrence frequency), and nodes sharing the same color form a cluster. Connections between nodes denote relationships, with the thickness of links indicating the strength of the association—thicker links signify stronger connections. Furthermore, to enhance the visual clarity of the network visualization produced by VOSviewer, we employed Pajek, an advanced and freely available software developed by Vladimir Batagel et al., for optimization purposes [26]. CiteSpace (version 6.2.R3) is another free citation visualization analysis software developed by Chen et al., focusing on dynamic visualization to reflect the evolution of bibliometric networks over time [27,28]. In this work, CiteSpace (version 6.2.R3) was employed to analyze keyword timelines, and disciplinary domains. Additionally, we employed the Bibliometrix package in R (version 4.2.2) to evaluate annual publication growth and categories, perform descriptive analyses of journal publication characteristics, and generate visualizations of literature, cited references, and keyword distributions [29].

Results

Overview of the number of publications

Based on the search criteria, a total of 4599 publications from 89 countries were included in the final study, comprising 4051 articles and 548 reviews, indicating that original research articles constitute the predominant portion of this field (Fig. 1A). As shown in Fig. 1B, the global publication output has demonstrated a general upward trend from 2010 to 2021, with the annual number of publications increasing from 97 in 2010 to 464 in 2024, representing an average annual growth rate of 4.17 %. Although there was a slight decline in 2022 and 2023, possibly due to factors such as the COVID-19 pandemic, a notable increase was observed in 2024 (Fig. 1B). This upward trend is projected to continue in 2025, underscoring the sustained and growing interest in this field.

Spatial distribution of countries

A total of 89 countries contributed to research in this field. Table 1 lists the top 10 most productive countries based on publication volume. From 2010 to 2025, China published the most articles in this field (n = 1,500, 32.62 %), followed by the United States (n = 732, 15.92 %) and Japan (n = 454, 9.87 %). Collectively, these three countries accounted for over half of the global research output, establishing them as the primary drivers of research and development in this domain. Notably, the United States exhibited a higher H-index and average citation count per publication than China, suggesting a greater research impact in this specific area. Fig. 1C illustrates global publication distribution from a geographic perspective, with China depicted in the darkest shade, reflecting its leading output. Analysis of global collaboration networks reveals extensive cooperation among different countries and regions (Fig. 1D), with the United States maintaining close collaborations with China, Japan, and other nations. From a temporal perspective, the United States and Japan initiated research earlier than China (Fig. 2A).

Analysis of authors

Author co-authorship analysis is instrumental in identifying the most productive authors and their collaborative ties, thereby providing valuable insights into core research groups and potential collaborators. A total of 14,887 authors participated in research within this field. Table 3 lists the top 10 authors ranked by publication count, along with their citation metrics. Osathanon, Thanaphum from Chulalongkorn University, Thailand, ranked first with 48 publications, followed by Fan, Zhipeng from Capital Medical University (n = 43), Nor, Jacques E from University of Michigan (n = 41), and Rodriguez-Lozano, FJ from University of Murcia (n = 41). In terms of influence, Nor, Jacques E has the highest H-index (H = 27), followed by Misako Nakashima (H = 26). Research is led by several top international academic communities, with Nakashima's Japanese clinical team receiving particular attention for their work in pulp regeneration and tissue engineering. Meanwhile, Chinese researchers including Tian Weidong, Zhou Xuedong, and Yan Jin form another cohesive cluster, reflecting the growing contribution of Chinese institutions to both fundamental and translational research in this field (Fig. 2C). As one of the three classical bibliometric laws, Lotka's law describes the distribution of author productivity [30,31]. The author productivity distribution in the DPSC field conforms closely to Lotka's law: 65.9 % of authors (n = 9810) published only one paper, constituting the largest contributor group within the field (Fig. 2D).

The most frequently cited articles analysis

Analysis of highly cited papers in this research field revealed that Huang et al., 's 2010 article titled "Stem/Progenitor Cell–Mediated De Novo Regeneration of Dental Pulp with Newly Deposited Continuous Layer of Dentin in an In Vivo Model" ranked first with 456 citations [34], followed by Sakai K's "Human dental pulp-derived stem cells promote locomotor recovery after complete transection of the rat spinal cord by multiple neuro-regenerative mechanisms [35]" (Fig. 4D). Citation and co-citation analysis of referenced literature showed that the pioneering research by Gronthos et al. published in PNAS in 2000 not only achieved the highest local citation count (LC = 1792) (Fig. 4E) but also occupied the absolute central hub position in the co-citation network, representing the origin point of the entire field (Fig. 4C). The blue cluster formed around this seminal study primarily encompasses early discovery and fundamental characterization of dental pulp stem cells. The yellow cluster focuses on characteristics and tissue engineering applications of stem cells from different sources, including Miura et al., 's 2003 research on stem cells from human exfoliated deciduous teeth (SHED) published in the same journal. The red cluster involves clinical applications and regenerative therapy-related research of dental pulp stem cells, while the green cluster primarily addresses tissue engineering, biomaterials, and differentiation regulation studies. The density of connecting lines between different colored clusters reflects the intensity of knowledge exchange among various subfields of dental pulp stem cell research, revealing the knowledge structural characteristics of this field's evolution from basic research toward clinical translation.

Keyword and hotspot analysis

Keyword analysis not only reflects the central information of a discipline but also reveals research frontiers and developmental trends in specific fields. We extracted 7035 author keywords from 4599 articles. As shown in Fig. 5A and B, the most frequent keyword was "dental pulp stem cells" (n = 782), followed by "stem cells," "dental pulp," and "tissue engineering," which constitute the absolute core of research in this field. The keyword co-occurrence network shows "dental pulp stem cells" occupying the central position (Fig. 5C). As the core of the green cluster, it primarily represents conceptual content in stem cell biology and regenerative medicine, serving as the field's foundation. Closely interconnected with this core is the red cluster, which focuses on "Tissue Engineering and Regenerative Applications." This cluster links key concepts such as "tissue engineering," "bone regeneration," and "angiogenesis," highlighting the crucial role of DPSCs as a vehicle for tissue engineering and regenerative medicine research. Underpinning these applications is the blue cluster, which delves into "MSC Biology and Lineage-Specific Differentiation," with a focus on fundamental processes like odontogenic and neurogenic differentiation. A yellow cluster is devoted to "clinical endodontics and biocompatible materials," characterized by terms such as vital pulp therapy and bioactive proteins. An emerging purple cluster reveals understanding of "molecular mechanisms and epigenetic regulation," evidenced by keywords including epigenetics, hypoxia, and microRNA. To capture the evolution of these themes, keyword burst analysis (Fig. 5D) revealed that early research hotspots (2010–2015) primarily concentrated on fundamental science aspects, including "marrow stromal cells" (burst strength 16.39) and "gene expression" (9.59), reflecting the period's focus on cellular characteristics and molecular mechanisms. The mid-development stage (2011–2018) witnessed bursts in keywords such as "side population cells" (14.32) and "bone formation" (9.22), marking a shift toward cell subpopulation identification and functional applications. Recent research trends (2020–2025) demonstrate distinct clinical translational characteristics, with strong bursts in keywords including "exosome" (10.94), "dental pulp regeneration" (8.61), "biodentine" (6.98), and "biomaterials" (7.52), reflecting the field's development toward precision medicine, biomaterial applications, and clinical therapeutic translation. Notably, emerging concepts such as “extracellular vesicles” (11.38), “vital pulp therapy” (7.38) and “periodontal regeneration” (6.80) have concentrated after 2018, suggesting that DPSC research is expanding into intercellular-communication mechanisms, conservative treatment strategies and multi-tissue regenerative applications.

Discussion

The discovery and characterization of tissue-specific stem cell populations have fundamentally reshaped the landscape of regenerative medicine. Among these, DPSCs have emerged as one of the most promising cell sources [11,36,37], sparking a global research surge due to their significant clinical potential. This rapid development has generated a vast and complex body of knowledge. However, this information explosion presents a formidable challenge for researchers, particularly newcomers to the field, making it difficult to systematically grasp the historical trajectory, core intellectual structure, and emerging frontiers of this domain. In this context, the present study applies bibliometric methods to provide a panoramic, quantitative and visual analysis of the global DPSCs research landscape. Our findings not only chart the field's evolutionary trajectory but also reveals the inherent logic of knowledge construction and driving forces for future development.

Publications in this field have demonstrated steady growth over the past two decades, increasing from 97 articles in 2010 to 464 in 2024. This growth reflects not only heightened scientific interest but also growing recognition of DPSCs as a promising therapeutic modality for regenerative dentistry and other fields. Although the 2022–2023 period experienced temporary setbacks potentially due to pandemic-related disruptions, this growth trajectory demonstrates the resilience and fundamental importance of this research domain. The substantial body of research has evolved the field from initial basic cell biology studies to complex clinical applications and translational aspects, continuing to drive development and transformation in this area. A nuanced disparity exists between the geographical distribution of scientific output and academic impact. China's absolute dominance in publication volume (32.62 %) reflects its strategic investment in biomedical research and the formation of high-output research clusters, as confirmed by top institutional rankings. However, the United States' continued leadership in impact indicators such as average citations per paper and H-index, along with its pivotal role as a hub in the global collaboration network, reveals more complex global dynamics. This “quantity versus impact” dichotomy suggests that, although the locus of publication activity may be shifting, the mature U.S. research ecosystem continues to generate foundational, high-impact work that helps set the field's direction. The dense international collaboration network, particularly the tripartite cooperation among the US, Japan, and China, demonstrates how geopolitical synergies can accelerate scientific progress. For emerging researchers, this landscape underscores the importance of establishing international partnerships to access diverse expertise and resources. Such collaborations will be crucial for enhancing their region's development and influence within the field.

Institutional analysis reveals that multiple Chinese research institutions make outstanding contributions to this field and constitute the core of the global collaboration network. However, the exceptional performance of institutions such as Air Force Medical University and University of Michigan in high-impact indicators demonstrates that key hubs of knowledge innovation also exist outside this high-output system. Further focusing on the individual level, the author analysis reveals a classic "core-periphery" structure that aligns with Lotka's Law. This indicates that the field's advancement is driven by a small cohort of continuously dedicated core scholars, while a vast number of authors make sporadic contributions. Notably, the most productive author cohorts exhibit significant internationalization and multi-center characteristics, indicating that field leadership relies not merely on institutional scale but is defined by multiple transnationals, influential "academic communities" or "invisible colleges," such as the clinical and translational team centered around Nakashima.

The journal landscape presents a compelling narrative of disciplinary evolution. The prominence of the Journal of Endodontics and the International Endodontic Journal establishe a clear clinical foundation for the field. However, it is the co-citation network that unveils a more profound pattern: a "specialized publication, foundational citation" model. This reveals that while researchers publish their findings in specialized journals, their work is built upon the fundamental discoveries reported in top-tier, broad-spectrum journals such as PNAS, Stem Cells, and Biomaterials. This reveals that DPSC research is not an isolated discipline but rather a dynamic intersection that continuously draws core principles from basic cell biology, developmental biology, and materials science, translating them into specific clinical applications. Seminal works, such as the pioneering study by Gronthos et al. (2000), serve as the intellectual cornerstone of the field. Together with subsequent milestone papers on SHEDs (Miura et al., 2003) andMSC criteria (Dominici et al., 2006), these studies constitute the solid pillars supporting the magnificent edifice of modern DPSCs research. Subsequent landmark studies have expanded the field into preclinical and clinical domains, demonstrating DPSC potential not only in dentin-pulp regeneration but also in treating systemic diseases. For instance, DPSCs has shown neuroprotective effect in degenerative disease such as Parkinson's disease and Alzheimers disease. Intracerebral transplantation of human DPSCs in a cerebral ischemia rodent model showed significant improvement in sensorimotor function [38,39]. Beyond the nervous system, research in endocrinology has demonstrated that both SHED and DPSCs can differentiate into pancreatic β-cells under appropriate conditions, thereby reversing hyperglycemia in diabetic models[[40], [41], [42]]. Furthermore, the broad therapeutic potential of DPSCs is underscored by reports of their significant efficacy in preclinical models of immunological disorders [43,44], myocardial infarction [45,46], and gastrointestinal diseases [47].

The most significant contribution of this study lies in clearly delineating the evolution of research paradigms in this field through a data-driven approach. The field has progressed from an early focus on fundamental cellular characteristics (e.g., "marrow stromal cells," "gene expression"), through a phase of functional applications (e.g., "bone formation," "side population cells") [[48], [49], [50], [51]], to the current, fundamental paradigm shift away from cell-centric therapies toward "cell-free" regenerative strategies [[52], [53], [54]]. Keywords such as "exosomes," "extracellular vesicles," and "conditioned medium" have emerged as the latest research frontiers, representing not merely incremental changes but a substantive paradigm shift. This transition reflects a deepening understanding of paracrine mechanisms and a strategic move toward therapies that offer enhanced safety, stability, scalability, and regulatory feasibility. Looking ahead, investigations of DPSCs epigenetic regulation and molecular mechanisms, together with translational efforts to apply DPSCs-based cell therapies and to develop precision-medicine products derived from extracellular vesicles, are likely to constitute major research priorities in the coming decade.

Despite providing a robust and multidimensional overview, this study has several limitations. Our reliance on a single database, while ensuring data consistency, may have overlooked literature from certain regional journals. Additionally, bibliometric indicators measure academic footprint, which may not fully reflect the true clinical or commercial impact of research. Furthermore, natural citation delays mean that recent breakthrough work may not have accumulated sufficient citations to be identified as high-impact outcomes.

In conclusion, this study represents the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis to systematically map the developmental blueprint, knowledge structure, and frontier trends of DPSCs research over the past fifteen years. Our analysis reveals a clear evolutionary pathway from fundamental discoveries to clinical applications and identifies a critical ongoing paradigm shift from traditional cell therapies toward cell-free regenerative strategies. We conclude that DPSCs research has entered a mature phase transitioning from "potential validation" to "clinical translation." As our data trends indicate, future breakthroughs will depend heavily on in-depth exploration of the DPSCs secretome (particularly extracellular vesicles), development of intelligent biomaterials, and innovative therapeutic strategies through multidisciplinary integration. Progress in these frontier areas will be crucial for translating the immense regenerative potential of DPSCs into a clinical reality that can reshape the future of both dentistry and regenerative medicine.

Efficacy and safety of dabrafenib-targeted therapy for pediatric langerhans cell histiocytosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Yunfeng Lu, Yuanzheng Ye, Wanhua Ren, Xuefeng Leng, Yunpeng Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe presence of BRAF gene mutations and the treatment with targeted drugs in Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) patients have changed the LCH treatment paradigm and greatly improved the prognosis of LCH patients. Nevertheless, a unified standard for the application dose and discontinuation time of targeted drugs in children has not been established. We collated data from clinical trials and studies for pediatric LCH to provide a more robust understanding of dabrafenib’s therapeutic potential and associated adverse events (AEs).MethodsA systematic review and meta-analysis of nine studies was conducted across five electronic databases to assess the efficacy and safety of dabrafenib in children diagnosed with LCH, synthesizing data to provide a more comprehensive and reliable conclusion based on PRISMA 2020 Checklist.ResultsThe research protocol was pre-registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251046705). Our analysis revealed that dabrafenib significantly improved treatment outcomes for LCH children with an objective response rate (ORR) of 83% [95% confidence interval (CI): 70–91%], disease control rate (DCR) of 85% (95% CI: 68–94%), 1-year progression-free survival (PFS) of 80% (95% CI: 45–95%) and an acceptable safety profile characterized by manageable AEs (grade ≥3 AEs rate 2%). These outcomes emphasize the latent capacity of dabrafenib as a promising treatment option for pediatric LCH, particularly in cases harboring BRAF alterations. Five studies also monitored the ultimate circulating free BRAFV600E (cfBRAFV600E) positive rates, which stood at 49.7% (95% CI: 28.7–70.8%). In the subgroup younger than 2 years, the ORR reached 92% (95% CI: 76–100%).ConclusionsOur study supports the integration of dabrafenib into treatment protocols for pediatric LCH, especially children younger than 2 years, and highlighting the need for further prospective studies to validate these results and explore its long-term effects, ultimately aiming to enhance patient outcomes and alleviate the burden of this challenging disease.

Introduction

Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is classified as a neoplastic disorder resulting from atypical myeloid differentiation. This condition predominantly affects children, manifesting in various forms, ranging from unifocal lesions to widespread systemic involvement (1). The etiology of LCH remains poorly understood, but recent advances in molecular biology have identified significant genetic mutations, particularly in the BRAF gene, that are implicated in the disease’s pathogenesis (2). These mutations are linked to the stimulation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, which subsequently results in unregulated cellular proliferation and survival (3).

In recent years, targeted therapies have emerged as a promising treatment strategy for LCH, particularly in cases associated with BRAF mutations. Dabrafenib is a selective inhibitor, specifically targeting the BRAFV600E mutation, which is prevalent in various malignancies, particularly melanoma (4). The primary pathway affected is the MAPK pathway, which, when aberrantly activated by BRAF mutations, leads to uncontrolled cellular growth. It also plays a role in inducing apoptosis in cancer cells (5). Importantly, dabrafenib’s selectivity for the mutated form of BRAF minimizes effects on normal cells, potentially reducing side effects associated with broader-spectrum kinase inhibitors.

By collating data from various clinical trials and studies, the objective of this study is to provide a more robust understanding of dabrafenib’s therapeutic potential and associated adverse events (AEs). This methodology not only aids in reducing bias inherent in individual studies but also aims to clarify the overall benefit-risk profile of dabrafenib in this unique patient population. Ultimately, the findings will contribute to existing knowledge and inform clinical decision-making, furnishing healthcare professionals with evidence-based insights into the use of targeted therapies in the management of pediatric LCH. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (6) (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2025-464/rc).

Methods

Search strategy

The research protocol was pre-registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251046705, https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/). We conducted an exhaustive retrieval across five electronic databases—EMBASE, PubMed, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the Cochrane Library—to identify studies that evaluated the efficacy and safety of dabrafenib in the treatment of LCH. The search strategy employed the following key terms: “Langerhans cell histiocytosis”, “Dabrafenib” and their variants. No restrictions were imposed on language, publication date, document type, or publication status for study inclusion. The literature search was conducted on March 18, 2025. A thorough manual examination of the cited references in the selected publications was conducted to guarantee the exhaustive nature of the literature collection. All retrieved study data were systematically imported into EndNote, a specialized reference organization tool, where rigorous screening was performed to identify articles meeting the predefined eligibility requirements for subsequent analytical evaluation.

Data extraction

Two researchers conducted independent screenings of the literature and extracted relevant data. In instances of disagreement, a third individual was consulted to facilitate resolution. Excluded reviews and the reason for exclusion are listed in Figure 1. This research gathered fundamental information (title, author, and publication year), study information (study location, study type, study duration, number and subtype of LCH cases, age, gender, organ involvement, therapy, follow-up time, drug dose, treatment duration, clinical response), outcome indicators (ORR, DCR, DOR, PFS) and safety outcomes (the occurrence of any AEs and those classified as ≥ grade 3 AEs).

Assessment of bias risk

To appraise the quality of the excluded cohort studies, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) was employed (7). Additionally, the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Checklist for Case Series was utilized to evaluate the case series (8). Discrepancies among reviewers were addressed and resolved through deliberation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was enforced using the ‘meta’ and ‘metafor’ packages with R software (4.2.2). P<0.05 denotes a statistically significant difference. The statistical analysis of ORR, DCR, and PFS employed a generalized linear mixed-effects modeling (GLMM) approach with maximum likelihood estimation, following methodological recommendations for synthesizing single proportion data (9). This analytical framework offers distinct advantages by eliminating the requirement for continuity adjustments in studies reporting extreme proportions (0 or 1). The model specification accounts for between-study heterogeneity while maintaining appropriate statistical properties for proportion estimation. Heterogeneity was assessed through the Chi-squared test alongside I2 in the analysis of final cfBRAFV600E positive rates, age subgroup and AEs. In instances where significant heterogeneity was identified (P<0.1 and I2>50%), a random-effects model was employed. In contrast, a common-effects model was employed in situations where no considerable heterogeneity was observed. Additionally, sensitivity analysis was carried out to evaluate the soundness and dependability of the aggregated outcomes if the heterogeneity was large. It was determined that publication bias was not necessary to consider for the nine studies included in the analysis.

Results

Selection of studies

The preliminary search resulted in the identification of 277 pertinent studies that have been published, sourced from five distinct databases: PubMed (32 studies), Embase (159 studies), Web of Science (80 studies), ClinicalTrials.gov (4 studies), and the Cochrane Library (2 studies). After the elimination of duplicates and a thorough review of the titles and abstracts, approximately 55 studies were selected for further consideration. Afterwards, a comprehensive assessment of the full-text articles performed, leading to the elimination of 46 studies. The exclusions were based on criteria such as being case reports, lacking relevant outcomes, focusing on adult populations, or involving co-morbidities and other therapeutic agents. Ultimately, a total of nine studies involving 128 patients met the specified inclusion criteria and were included in this meta-analysis. Figure 1 illustrates the procedure for selecting studies. The specifics of each study that was included are presented in Table S1.

Quality assessment

In observational research, the methodological rigor of seven cohort studies (10-16) was assessed utilizing the NOS. Regarding case series analyses (17,18), two retrospective studies underwent rigorous evaluation with the JBI Critical Appraisal Tool for Case Series. The quality assessment of the nine studies included in this overview was notably high with critical appraisal scores ranged from 7 to 9. Treatment response (ORR and DCR)

This meta-analysis evaluated 128 pediatric cases (74 males, 54 females) with ages spanning 0.1–11 years. The cohort predominantly comprised refractory/relapsed LCH cases exhibiting multi-system involvement, all of whom had undergone prior therapeutic interventions including first-line chemotherapy, second-line regimens, or biological agents. The median duration between the initial diagnosis and the initiation of dabrafenib treatment was 12.5 months (range, 0.1–116 months). Treatment protocols involved dabrafenib dosages between 3–10 mg per kilogram per day, with a median therapy duration of 13 months (range, 3–42 months). Clinical outcomes were assessed through radiographic evaluations and circulating free BRAFV600E (cfBRAFV600E) quantification following a median follow-up duration of 28.6 months (range, 1–85 months). Therapeutic efficacy analysis revealed an ORR of 83% [95% confidence interval (CI): 70–91%] (Figure 3), a DCR of 85% (95% CI: 68–94%) (Figure 4), with final cfBRAFV600E positive rates measuring 49.7% (95% CI: 28.7–70.8%) (Figure 5). In the subgroup younger than 2 years, the ORR reached 92% (95% CI: 76–100%), which is higher than the ORR 81% (95% CI: 70–90%) in the subgroup over 2 years (Figure 6).

Survival data analysis

PFS was reported in the analysis for all children with LCH following dabrafenib administration. The random-effects estimate based on the GLMM was calculated for 1-year PFS and 2-year PFS rates. The pooled median 1-year PFS rate was 80% (95% CI: 45–95%) (Figure 7). With regard to 2-year PFS rate, the findings indicated that the aggregated median 2-year PFS rate was 66% (95% CI: 36–87%) (Figure 8).

Toxicities

In the common-effects model (I2=0.0%, P=0.71), a total of 56% (95% CI: 45–66%) of LCH patients experienced grades 1–2 AEs (Figure 9), which were generally well tolerated. In contrast, the occurrence of grade ≥3 AEs was markedly lower, reported only 2% (95% CI: 0–6%) (Figure 10). Additionally, the findings revealed that the AEs most frequently documented were maculopapular rash, vomiting and diarrhea, blurred vision and photophobia, eye pain and arthralgia.

Discussion

The arrival of targeted therapies has markedly shifted the landscape of pediatric oncology, particularly in LCH, a rare pediatric disease leading to potential damage to various organs. The presence of BRAF gene mutations in LCH patients (19) and the treatment of targeted drugs have changed the LCH treatment paradigm and greatly improved the prognosis of LCH patients (20), especially in LCH patients with hematological involvement (21). Furthermore, numerous investigations have demonstrated that programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) is significantly upregulated in the affected tissues of LCH (22). The co-administration of MAPK inhibitors alongside PD-L1 inhibitors has exhibited a synergistic effect in preclinical animal models, which aids in mitigating T cell depletion associated with LCH (23). Nevertheless, A unified standard for the application dose and discontinuation time of targeted drugs in children has not been established, and there is also a wide variation in the effectiveness and side effects of various targeted drug treatments. Therefore, our study focuses on demonstrating both efficacy and acceptable safety profiles in the pediatric population afflicted with this condition.

Nine studies included in the meta-analysis originated from multi-center collaborations across Europe, America, and China. The publication timeframe of these studies spanned from 2017 to 2025. The patients involved were diagnosed with multisystem LCH (MS-LCH) or presented with BRAFV600E mutation-positive status. Notably, among these cases, 33 exhibited severe illness accompanied by hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH). The patients’ median age was 1.6 years (ranging from 0.1 to 11 years). The therapeutic approach frequently involved the concomitant use of chemotherapy agents, particularly in cases classified as active disease (AD) or relapsed. The dosage of dabrafenib administered ranged from 3 to 10 mg per kilogram per day, with an average treatment duration of 13 months (with a range of 3 to 43 months). After a follow-up duration of 28.6 months (ranging from 1 to 85 months), the analysis of therapeutic efficacy demonstrated an ORR of 83% (95% CI: 70–91%) and a DCR of 85% (95% CI: 68–94%). The incorporation of dabrafenib led to an enhancement in the clinical response of the majority of patients. The 1-year PFS rate was recorded at 80% (95% CI: 45–95%), while the 2-year PFS rate was noted at 66% (95% CI: 36–87%). Five of the studies monitored the ultimate cfBRAFV600E positive rates, which stood at 49.7% (95% CI: 28.7–70.8%).

Moreover, while dabrafenib appears to offer a viable option for patients who have not responded adequately to traditional therapies, it is essential to monitor the safety profile. While grades 1–2 AEs were observed in 56% of LCH patients, and were generally well-tolerated, the occurrence of grade ≥3 AEs was markedly lower, occurring in only 2% of patients, which is less than the rates observed in the trametinib combination and second-line chemotherapy groups. In instances of grade ≥3 AEs, measures such as drug withdrawal or dosage reduction were implemented. However, the question of recurrence in such patients necessitates further longitudinal follow-up data to prioritize not only the efficacy of the drug but also the impacts on growth and development.

In Whitlock’s investigation, the ORR and DCR of patients receiving dabrafenib monotherapy surpassed those of patients treated with the combination of dabrafenib and trametinib, with continuous remission observed in both groups (10). Additionally, one of the key considerations in evaluating dabrafenib’s application lies in the identification of specific patient subgroups that may benefit most from this therapy. In patients with HLH secondary to LCH, those treated with dabrafenib demonstrated a markedly higher PFS rate compared to those receiving traditional chemotherapy, with a 4-year PFS of 75% versus 14.6% (14). In the subgroup younger than 2 years, the ORR reached an amazing 92% (95% CI: 76–100%) although the heterogeneity could not be ignored (I2=71%, P=0.004).

Remarkably, an increasing number of case reports concerning newborns and infants with LCH indicate that single-agent treatment with dabrafenib can effectively manage the rapid progression of the disease, thereby securing valuable time for subsequent combination chemotherapy and fostering optimism for a chemotherapy-free treatment regimen for LCH (24). Concurrently, BRAF inhibitors such as vemurafenib and dabrafenib, along with MAPK inhibitors like trametinib and cobimetinib, have all been utilized in the treatment of LCH patients, yielding favorable efficacy outcomes (25-28). Unlike earlier investigations encompassing all LCH patients, including both adults and children, our report focused on the inaugural account of the therapeutic impact and adverse effects of dabrafenib in severe pediatric MS-LCH cases to ensure that the therapeutic approaches are both evidence-based and personalized.

The primary limitations of this investigation consist of a limited sample size, non-RCT studies, and biases that may be caused by relying only on existing data. Due to the restricted patient cohort and abbreviated follow-up duration, there is a paucity of research data elucidating the clinical efficacy and adverse effects of various targeted therapies used independently or in combination. Moreover, the follow-up period of less than 5 years does not adequately capture the long-term safety profile of dabrafenib in this population.

Conclusions

In conclusion, informed by the limited evidence derived from our clinical research and case series, dabrafenib emerges as a viable and secure therapeutic option for refractory LCH linked to BRAF mutations and MS-LCH patients younger than 2 years. Our analysis would furnish a more comprehensive rationale for advancing personalized treatment strategies. As we move forward, the potential for patients to derive greater benefits from a treatment regimen involving the combined application of targeted agents remains ambiguous, thus there is an urgent requirement for extended longitudinal studies to assess the durability of responses to targeted therapies like dabrafenib, particularly in young patients who may experience different pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics compared to adults.

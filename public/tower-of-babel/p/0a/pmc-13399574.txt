Reply—Green pediatrics beyond endoscopy: Toward systemic sustainability in celiac care
Authors: Mario Brusco, Sara Trivellini, Francesco Valitutti
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We sincerely thank Pucinischi et al. 1 for their comments.

Our study was conducted within a single Italian region and that healthcare organization, transportation systems, and infrastructure may vary substantially across different regions. However, while absolute emissions may differ, the relative environmental advantage of the no‐biopsy strategy is driven by structural factors intrinsic to the diagnostic pathway itself.

As telemedicine follow‐ups, we explicitly acknowledged that laboratory tests cannot be avoided and that a comprehensive physical examination may be limited in a fully remote setting, therefore suggesting a hybrid model.

We also agree that equity considerations are crucial. Any future implementation of telemedicine in this context should remain patient‐centered and adaptable to individual needs, never compromising accessibility, quality, or continuity of care. 2

To our knowledge, no specific study has directly assessed the carbon footprint of autoantibody testing for celiac disease (CeD). However, McAlister et al. have shown that common hospital laboratory tests generate between 49 and 116 g CO2e per test. 3 This footprint is largely driven by consumables rather than analyzer energy consumption. Extrapolating these findings to serology for CeD, two antibody assays would likely generate approximately 63.3 g CO2e per blood examination. While not negligible, this impact remains substantially lower than the emissions associated with anesthesia and upper gastrointestinal endoscopy.

We also agree that the endoscopic diagnostic pathway represents one of the most environmentally impactful steps of the clinical process. Nevertheless, increasing staff awareness, optimizing resource management, and promoting appropriate waste segregation may significantly reduce the environmental footprint of these procedures, 4 which is highly desirable in many medical fields.

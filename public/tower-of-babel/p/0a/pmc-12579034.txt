Cenegermin for the Treatment of Moderate or Severe Neurotrophic Keratopathy: Results from a Prospective, Phase IV, Open-Label Study in China
Authors: Suxia Li, Jianjiang Xu, Jin Yuan, Zhiqiang Pan, Giacomo Siri, Scott G. Hauswirth, Flavio Mantelli, Weiyun Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionNeurotrophic keratopathy (NK) is a rare degenerative disease that can lead to epithelial breakdown, corneal ulceration, and potentially perforation and vision loss. Cenegermin is a recombinant human nerve growth factor approved for stage 2/3 NK, though data on its efficacy outside of United States/European populations are limited.MethodsThis prospective, phase IV, open-label, multicenter study investigated the efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics (PK) of cenegermin eye drops in Chinese patients with stage 2/3 NK in a routine clinical setting (CTR20220066). In addition, 25/28 (89.3%) experienced ≥ 1 treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE); for the majority (23/28, 82.2%) of these patients, TEAEs were mild/moderate. None of the serious TEAEs reported by 10 (35.7%) patients were assessed as related to cenegermin or led to treatment discontinuation. Eye pain was the most common cenegermin-related TEAE (5/28, 17.9%), primarily limited to the treatment period.ConclusionsThese findings demonstrate that cenegermin eye drops are an effective, generally well-tolerated treatment in Chinese patients with stage 2/3 NK, supporting their use in this patient population.Trial RegistrationCTR20220066.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-025-01253-5.

Introduction

Neurotrophic keratopathy (NK), also known as neurotrophic keratitis, is a rare degenerative disease characterized by partial or total loss of corneal sensation, ocular surface irregularity, and visual impairment [1, 2]. NK is graded as follows: stage 1 (mild), characterized by early changes to the corneal epithelium; stage 2 (moderate), characterized by non-healing persistent epithelial defects (PEDs); and stage 3 (severe), characterized by corneal ulceration, which may progress to corneal perforation [1, 3–5]. The prevalence of NK varies by country and is estimated to range between 0.8 and 5.67 cases per 10,000 patients [2, 6, 7], with common underlying causes including neurologic surgeries, herpetic infections, and corneal trauma [8, 9].

Treatments for NK have been limited to products such as preservative-free artificial tears, topical steroids/antibiotics, serum eye drops, therapeutic contact lenses, and amniotic membrane, with more severe and refractory cases requiring surgery to correct [10]. However, with the emerging understanding of the key role of nerve growth factor (NGF) in the maintenance of corneal homeostasis [11, 12], research into the effects of murine NGF on NK led to the development of recombinant human NGF (rhNGF) [13, 14].

Cenegermin (Oxervate; Dompé farmaceutici S.p.A., Milan, Italy) is an Escherichia coli-produced rhNGF approved for the treatment of NK [15, 16]. This approval was based on findings from two randomized clinical trials. The first was a phase I/II, double-masked, randomized, vehicle-controlled study conducted in Europe (NGF0212/REPARO study; NCT01756456), which demonstrated that cenegermin eye drops (20 mcg/mL, 6 drops/day for 8 weeks) significantly improved corneal healing (< 0.5 mm lesion staining) in patients with stage 2/3 NK, with 74.0% healed after 8 weeks of treatment versus 43.1% of vehicle-treated patients [1]. Treatment was generally well tolerated, and recurrence was experienced by 3.4% patients in the treatment group versus 4.8% in the vehicle group during the 48-week follow-up [1]. Similar results were reported in the subsequent randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled, pivotal trial conducted in the US (NGF0214/NCT02227147) [4], where cenegermin (20 mcg/mL, 6 drops/day for 8 weeks) again provided significant improvements in corneal healing in patients with stage 2/3 NK, with 69.6% of patients healed after 8 weeks of treatment versus 29.2% of vehicle-treated patients [4]. Cenegermin treatment was generally well tolerated, with most adverse effects being local, mild, and transient [4]. Treatment-related adverse events were ocular in nature, with eye pain being the most common; serious adverse events that occurred were considered unrelated to treatment [4]. In addition, efficacy and safety results from a recent phase IV uncontrolled trial (DEFENDO) support the use of cenegermin in adults with stage I NK [17]. However, clinical trials are conducted in a strictly controlled setting, and it is important to assess whether these results translate into similar outcomes in clinical practice, and in previously unexamined demographic groups.

Here we present the results of a prospective, phase IV, open-label, multicenter study evaluating the efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics of cenegermin in Chinese patients with moderate or severe NK.

Methods

Clinical Trial Design

This was a prospective, phase IV, open-label, multicenter study investigating the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics of cenegermin eye drops in Chinese patients with stage 2/3 NK in a routine clinical setting (CTR20220066).

During the 8-week treatment period, all patients received one drop of cenegermin (20 mcg/mL) administered to the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) 6 times/day at 2-h intervals. Patients were assessed for ocular and safety outcomes at weeks 2, 4, 6, and 8 during treatment, and then followed for an additional 48 weeks with assessments conducted at weeks 12, 20, 32, 44, and 56 (Supplementary Fig. 1). All data were collected via patient electronic case report forms (eCRFs) completed by the investigator.

Cenegermin was the only topical ocular treatment permitted during the treatment period. For patients who experienced corneal healing at week 8, no treatment (except for commercially available non-preserved artificial tears, administered at the physician’s discretion) was allowed during the 48-week follow up. Any treatment (administered at the physician’s discretion, excluding cenegermin treatment) was permitted during the 48-week follow up for patients who did not experience corneal healing at week 8.

Patients

Eligible patients were male or female individuals ≥ 18 years of age with unilateral stage 2 (moderate) or stage 3 (severe) NK according to Mackie classification [3] (as determined by the investigator) and evidence of decreased corneal sensitivity (≤ 4 cm using the Cochet-Bonnet esthesiometer) both within the area of the PED or corneal ulcer and in at least one additional corneal quadrant outside of the area of the defect. They fulfilled the requirements to be treated with cenegermin according to the label authorized in China.

Patients were excluded from the study if they were contraindicated per the label authorized in China; had bilateral stage 2 or 3 NK; had active ocular infection or inflammation not related to NK; had any other ocular disease (requiring topical ocular treatment); or had severe vision loss in the affected eye with no potential for visual improvement per the investigator’s assessment.

Efficacy Assessments

The primary efficacy endpoint was the number of patients achieving complete healing of the PED or corneal ulcer (also referred to as “corneal healing”) at the end of the 8-week treatment period (i.e., week 8). Corneal healing was defined as a lesion < 0.5 mm, measured using corneal fluorescein staining (CFS) and recorded in the patient’s eCRF by the investigator.

Additional efficacy endpoints included:(i)number of patients who experienced corneal healing at week 4;(ii)number of patients not completely healed by the end of treatment at week 8;(iii)median time (days) to achieve corneal healing;(iv)number of patients who remained healed during follow-up, defined as a difference in corneal lesion size of < 0.5 mm from week 8 (assessed using fluorescein staining) at weeks 12, 20, 32, 44, and 56;(v)number of patients who experienced worsening of the corneal lesion during the 8-week treatment phase, as determined by the investigator and reported on the patient’s eCRF;(vi)number of patients who experienced recurrence during follow-up (defined as any signs of PED or corneal ulcers in patients who had shown corneal healing at week 8, as reported by the investigator);(vii)median time to recurrence, defined as the number of days from the initial corneal healing to the reported recurrence;(viii)improvement in corneal sensitivity (measured using a Cochet-Bonnet esthesiometer) at week 8 and week 56;(ix)mean change from baseline in corneal sensitivity at week 8 and week 56; and(x)mean change from baseline in best corrected distance visual acuity (BCDVA) at week 8 and week 56. BCDVA was assessed with the Early Treatment of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) visual acuity chart and calculated as the total number of letters read correctly at 4 meters plus the total number of letters read at 1 meters.

Safety Assessments

Safety was assessed by monitoring the incidence and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), treatment-related TEAEs, and serious TEAEs throughout the 56-week study period. TEAEs were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) dictionary (Version 26.1) and summarized by system organ class and preferred term. Intraocular pressure assessments, slit lamp examinations, fundus ophthalmoscopy, and external ocular examinations were also performed at regular timepoints throughout the study.

Pharmacokinetic Assessments

Blood samples were collected at baseline and weeks 1, 3, 6, and 8 (treatment period) for pharmacokinetic analysis of serum NGF concentration over time. Quantitative analysis of total serum NGF (including endogenous and rhNGF) was performed using a validated antigen-capture sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay at an independent laboratory (WuXi AppTec Bioanalytical Services, Shanghai, China).

Statistical Analysis

As NK is a rare disease, no formal sample size calculations were performed. However, based on evidence from previous studies (e.g., randomized phase II and pivotal studies) [1, 4], a planned enrollment of 24 patients was considered adequate to detect healing trends in this population.

All efficacy and safety analyses were performed using the efficacy/safety analysis set (or “overall” study population), defined as all patients remaining in the study who received at least one dose of cenegermin. No formal hypothesis testing or comparative statistical analyses were performed; all data are summarized using descriptive statistics, e.g., continuous variables using mean (standard deviation [SD]) or median (range) values and categorical variables using frequencies and percentages. The primary endpoint was analyzed using observed case data without any imputations for missing information; corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were based on a binomial distribution (Clopper-Pearson exact method). Additionally, for selected endpoints, separate cross-sectional analyses (i.e., the incidence at a specific timepoint) and time-to-event analyses (the time from the first dose of cenegermin to the first event) were conducted. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS® software version 9.4.

Study Oversight

This study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) and National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines; all applicable national laws, guidelines, and Independent Ethics Committee (IEC) regulations; the Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices (GPP) issued by the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE); and the Declaration of Helsinki (and its amendments). In addition, all patients provided written informed consent before study screening, and the investigators obtained protocol approval from all properly constituted IECs prior to initiating the study. Each site had a separate ethics committee: Clinical Trial Ethics Committee of Eye hospital of Shandong First Medical University; Medical Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Ophthalmology Center, Sun Yat-sen University; Ethics Committee of Eye, Ear, Nose, and Throat Hospital of Fudan University; and Ethics Committee of Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University.

Results

Patient Disposition, Demographics, and Characteristics

The study was conducted at four sites in China between 28 April 2022 (first patient enrolled) and 28 November 2023 (last patient completed). In total, 28 patients met the eligibility criteria, and all 28 received at least one dose of study medication and were included in the efficacy/safety analysis set. Of these patients, 27 completed the full follow-up period, with one withdrawing due to a TEAE of mild eye pain, which was assessed as related to the study product and not classified as serious. Two patients were excluded from the primary endpoint analysis due to early treatment discontinuation and lack of assessment, respectively.

In brief, patients had a mean age of 53.8 years, 15/28 (53.6%) were female, and all were Chinese. Slightly more patients had stage 2 NK (16/28, 57.1%) than stage 3 NK (12/28, 42.9%). Neurosurgery (5/28, 17.9%), herpes simplex infection (4/28, 14.3%), and chemical or traumatic corneal injury (3/28, 10.7%) were the most common causes of NK. Median (range) duration of NK was 0.8 (0–18.5) months, and median (range) time since stage 2/3 NK diagnosis was 0 (0–17.1) months. A similar number of patients had NK in the left and right eye (15/28 [53.6%] and 13/28 [46.4%], respectively), and corneal epithelial defects were primarily located along the visual axis (24/28, 85.7%). Efficacy Outcomes

Corneal Healing

At week 8, a total of 22/26 patients (84.6%, 95% CI, 65.1–95.6) had achieved complete healing of the PED or corneal ulcer, though most patients showed corneal healing earlier in the treatment period, with more than half (15/28, 53.6%) healed by week 2 and two-thirds (17/26, 65.4%) healed by week 4 (Fig. 1A). Rates of corneal healing at week 8 were similar for the overall population and subgroups stratified by age (< 65 years: 14/18 [77.8%], ≥ 65 years: 8/8 [100%]); sex (female: 12/14 [85.7%], male: 10/12 [83.3%]); and NK severity (stage 2: 11/14 [78.6%], stage 3: 11/12 [91.7%]). Of the 22 patients who experienced corneal healing at week 8, the majority were healed at the follow-up visits (81.8–95.5% at weeks 12, 20, 32, 44) and 20 patients (90.9%) were healed at week 56. (Fig. 1B). In addition, 1 of 4 patients who were not healed at week 8 achieved corneal healing at week 56.

Worsening of the Lesion

Overall, worsening of the lesion (as determined by the investigator) was only reported for two patients (2/28, 7.1%) during the 8-week treatment period. In both cases, no evidence of lesion worsening was observed before week 4. Of these 2 patients, one had no other complications and the other had a history of glaucoma and experienced an AE of increased ocular pressure (37.7 mmHg) at week 2 in the study eye that returned to normal (17.6 mmHg) by week 4. This patient subsequently discontinued the study due to an AE of mild eye pain.

Disease Recurrence

Of the 22 patients who experienced corneal healing at week 8, three (13.6%) had a recurrence of the PED or corneal ulcer during follow-up. In these patients, the median time to recurrence was 126 (range 112–169) days. Two of these patients experienced recurrence at week 20, while the other patient experienced recurrence at week 32.

Corneal Sensitivity

Corneal sensitivity was improved in 19/26 (73.1%) of patients at week 8 and 23/27 (85.2%) at week 56.

Mean (SD) corneal sensitivity (cm) improved from 2.72 (1.94) at baseline (n = 28) to 3.98 (2.10) at week 8 (n = 26). It further improved to 4.21 (1.87) at week 56 (n = 27). BCDVA

Mean (SD) BCDVA (ETDRS letters) improved from 17.0 (22.5) at baseline (n = 28) to 42.4 (28.6) at week 8 (n = 26). It further improved to 49.9 (24.0) at week 56 (n = 27). The mean (SD) improvement from baseline (ETDRS letters) was 25.4 (23.4) at week 8 and 33.4 (23.1) at week 56 (Fig. 2B).

Treatment-related TEAEs were rare, occurring in 4/28 (14.3%) and 1/27 (3.7%) of patients during treatment and follow-up, respectively. In both timeframes, eye pain was the most commonly reported treatment-related TEAE (4/28 [14.3%] and 1/27 [3.7%]).

No patients experienced serious TEAEs during treatment. During follow-up, 10/27 (37.0%) of patients reported serious TEAEs, with cataracts being the most common (3/27, 11.1%). No serious TEAEs were assessed as related to the study drug or led to treatment discontinuation. No fatal adverse events were reported throughout the study.

In addition, none of the intraocular pressure assessments, slit lamp examinations, fundus ophthalmoscopy, or external eye examinations revealed any safety signals or findings that were deemed by the investigator to be a concern with the use of cenegermin eye drops.

Pharmacokinetics

Overall, only three patients had total serum NGF concentrations above the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ; 32 pg/mL) at any timepoint. All three patients had total serum NGF concentrations above the LLOQ at baseline, with two patients having detectable total serum NGF at all subsequent timepoints and one patient having detectable total serum NGF at weeks 1 and 6 only. In these patients, mean (SD) NGF concentrations (pg/mL) were 15.0 (54.4) at baseline, 15.1 (53.5) at week 1, and 19.2 (71.5) at week 6. Of note, these resulting mean values were lower than the LLOQ.

Discussion

In this prospective, phase IV, open-label, multicenter study assessing the efficacy and safety of cenegermin for the treatment of stage 2/3 NK in China, 8 weeks of cenegermin eye drops (20 mcg/mL, applied 6 times/day at 2-hour intervals) resulted in corneal healing in most patients (22/28, 84.6%). Of these patients, at least 81.8% maintained corneal healing during long-term follow-up, with 20/22 (90.9%) remaining healed at week 56. Cenegermin eye drops were also shown to be well tolerated, with 23/28 (82.2%) patients experiencing TEAEs of mild or moderate intensity, treatment-related TEAEs being rare (< 4% during 48-week follow-up), and no serious TEAEs during treatment (cataracts were reported as serious TEAEs in three patients during follow-up and deemed unrelated to study drug).

These findings are consistent with those previously reported in two larger, randomized, phase II pivotal clinical trials (REPARO, NGF0214), where 8 weeks of cenegermin eye drops (20 mcg/mL) led to corneal healing (defined as < 0.5-mm maximum diameter of fluorescein staining in the lesion area) in 69.6–74.0% of patients with stage 2/3 NK, with a favorable safety and tolerability profile [1, 4].

As with corneal healing, data from this phase IV trial indicate similar rates of treatment-related TEAEs compared with previous clinical trials. Namely, eye pain was the predominant treatment-related TEAE in this phase IV trial (14.3% [4/28] during treatment; 3.7% [1/27] during follow-up) and in the aforementioned pivotal trials (REPARO: 7.7% [4/52] during 8-week treatment; NGF0214: 13.0% [3/23] during 8-week treatment and 24-week follow-up) [1, 4].

In addition, pharmacokinetic analyses of NGF are comparable to those reported in REPARO, where only 5 (of approximately 90 patients receiving rhNGF or vehicle) demonstrated serum NGF concentrations above the LLOQ (32 pg/mL) at any timepoint tested[1]. Data from this study provide additional evidence indicating no systemic absorption of rhNGF when administered locally as an eye drop, with no buildup or accumulation of total NGF in serum. In the three patients where total serum NGF levels were above the LLOQ, detectable levels were also present at baseline; therefore, it is reasonable to conclude that the minimal observed concentrations of total serum NGF were likely due to fluctuations around a constitutive serum level of endogenous NGF.

There are several limitations of the study that should be considered when interpreting the results. First is the small sample size, which could leave the results susceptible to a type 1 error or other bias. However, it should be noted that with a rare condition such as NK, achieving a large sample size would be infeasible in almost all settings. Second is the lack of a control group and unmasked treatment, which could enable factors other than treatment to influence the results. However, having open-label treatment (as well as not employing vehicle controls) more closely resembles clinical practice; as such, results from this study can be considered more applicable to the target population than those from a controlled clinical trial setting. Lastly, while the exclusively Chinese study population could mean that these findings may not be generalizable to other countries or regions, they are nevertheless consistent with clinical trial results from both European and US-based studies [1, 4].

Conclusions

The results of this prospective, phase-IV, open-label multicenter study support and expand upon those of previous randomized clinical trials, demonstrating the efficacy and safety of cenegermin for the treatment of moderate-to-severe NK. They further demonstrate that cenegermin eye drops are an effective, well-tolerated treatment for Chinese patients with stage 2/3 NK, supporting their use in this patient population. Given the open-label and uncontrolled design of this study, additional controlled studies with longer follow-up periods would provide a valuable contribution to the growing body of evidence on the safety and efficacy of cenegermin.

Synaptotagmin-1 regulates egg production by con trolling luteinizing hormone secretion in chickens
Authors: Zhimin Cheng, Yangyang Wang, Yang Wang, Hongyu Wei, Mohammed Kamal Challioui, Yulong Guo, Weihua Tian, Dandan Wang, Hong Li, Xiaojun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Egg production represents one of the most economically critical traits in commercial poultry production, orchestrated primarily by the hypothalamus-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis. Synaptotagmin 1 (SYT1), a ubiquitous component of the nervous and endocrine systems, functions as a key mediator of calcium-dependent neurotransmitter release and hormone secretion. However, the functional role of SYT1 in avian reproductive performance has remained elusive. In this study, we present evidence that single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within the SYT1 gene effectively distinguish commercial laying hens from local wild breeds, as demonstrated through principal component analysis (PCA), suggesting the gene's pivotal role in selective breeding for enhanced egg production. Through genotype–phenotype correlation analyses, we identified two SNPs, rs39497549 and rs39477032, that exhibit strong associations with egg laying performance. Furthermore, tissue-specific expression levels showed profiling analysis revealed SYT1 transcript levels in pituitary and ovarian tissues of high-producing hens relative to their low-producing counterparts, and these expression levels showed strong positive correlations with circulating concentrations of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), and estradiol (E2). In addition, fluorescence in situ hybridization analyses further confirmed spatial co-localization of SYT1 and LH within anterior pituitary cells, suggesting a direct involvement of SYT1 in gonadotroph activity. Functional validation through in cultured pituitary cells revealed that gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist stimulation significantly upregulated SYT1, follicle stimulating hormone beta subunit (FSHβ), and luteinizing hormone beta subunit (LHβ) mRNA, along with enhanced secretion of FSH and LH. Conversely, SYT1 knockdown attenuated GnRH-induced expression and secretion of gonadotropins, while SYT1 overexpression potentiated these effects. Additionally, SYT1 modulated steroidogenesis in ovarian granulosa cells by regulating the expression of steroidogenic enzymes and progesterone production. Taken together, our findings establish SYT1 as a master regulator of chicken egg production performance via modulation of reproductive hormone synthesis and secretion within the HPO axis. These results position SYT1 polymorphisms as promising genetic markers for selective breeding programs in commercial laying hens improving egg production traits in indigenous chicken breeds.

Introduction

Reproductive physiology in birds is tightly orchestrated by the hypothalamus-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis (Li et al., 2020), which governs the synthesis and release of key reproductive hormones. In this regulatory cascade, the gonadotropin releasing hormone (GnRH) is synthetized in hypothalamus and secreted in pulsatile manner into the hypophyseal portal circulation. GnRH subsequently binds to its receptor (GnRHR) on the anterior pituitary gonadotrophs, initiating intracellular signaling cascades that induce the synthesis and secretion of gonadotropins such as follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH). These gonadotropins act on ovarian follicles to regulate gametogenesis and steroidogenesis, thereby supporting follicular growth and oocyte maturation(Prastiya et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023).

Egg production represents a critical economic trait in poultry industry and serves as a focal point for genetic selection and breeding strategies. Although selective breeding has substantially improved laying performance over the past few decades, the underlying genetic architecture governing these traits remains incompletely understood (Liu et al., 2019). Advances in sequencing technologies, mainly through next-generation sequencing, have facilitated genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and accelerated the identification of genomic variants associated with chicken reproductive traits (Qin et al., 2015; Wilkanowska et al., 2014). Numerous SNPs associated with reproductive performance have been reported in recent years. Several genes identified through GWAS, such as catenin beta 1 (CTNNB1), frizzled class receptor 6 (FZD6), pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 3 (PDK3), follicle stimulating hormone beta subunit (FSHB), have demonstrated strong associations with egg-laying traits in poultry (Han et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2019; Ouyang et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023). Our previous comparative transcriptomic profiling of the HPO axis in high and low performing Gushi hens revealed differential expression of synaptotagmin-1 (SYT1) in both pituitary and ovarian tissues. Further weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) implicated SYT1 as a hub gene significantly correlated with productive traits (Wang et al., 2024).

SYT1, a key mediator of calcium-dependent neurotransmitter release, is the most widely distributed synaptic binding protein within both the nervous and endocrine systems, playing an essential role in synaptic exocytosis and endocrine secretion (Luan et al., 2014). SYT1 is a structurally conserved protein whose N-terminus is located in the lumen, followed by a transmembrane domain and two C2 domains capable of binding two or three Ca²⁺ (Moghadam and Jackson, 2013). Upon calcium influx, the C2 domains enable SYT1 to trigger synchronous vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release (Li et al., 2018). SYT1 deficiency has been shown to disrupt action potential-evoked neurotransmission and endocrine signaling (Park and Ryu, 2018). Genetic studies in urine models have confirmed that homozygous SYT1 knockouts are neonatal lethal due to severe impairments in synaptic transmission, particularly in hippocampal neurons. (Geppert et al., 1994; Koh and Bellen, 2003). These findings suggest that STY1 may similarly regulate hormone secretion dynamics in the avian HPO axis, potentially impacting reproductive output. However, its specific functional role in poultry reproduction has not yet been elucidated.

Therefore, this study aimed to elucidate the functional and evolutionary significance of SYT1 in the context of egg production in laying hens. We sought to identify genetic variations in SYT1 associated with key egg-laying traits, explore its physiological relationship with reproductive hormones in vivo, and determine its direct molecular function in regulating hormone biosynthesis and secretion using cellular models. These findings establish SYT1 as a key molecular regulator of reproductive hormone biosynthesis and secretion and highlight its value as a genetic target for improving egg production in indigenous chicken breeds.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All animal procedures and sample collection were conducted in compliance with the Regulations for the Administration of Affairs Concerning Experimental Animals (revised in June 2004, Ministry of Science and Technology, China) and were received ethical approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Henan Agricultural University Zhengzhou, P.R. China (Permit Number: 11-0085).

Animals and sample preparation

We screened a population of 764 Gushi chickens from the 14th generation, tracking their individual laying records from the onset of egg production until 43 wks of age (Wang et al., 2024). Based on these records, we defined high-yield and low-yield groups at four different time points. At 20 wks of age, the high-yield group (GS20wH) included individuals in the top 5 % for total egg number (EN20w), while the low-yield group (GS20wL) consisted of individuals that had not yet laid any eggs (EN20w = 0). At 28, 36, and 43 wks of age, the high-yield groups were defined as the top 10 % of egg producers, and the low-yield groups as the bottom 20 %. we selected SNPs that were significantly associated with egg production traits (P < 0.01). These candidate SNPs were mapped to the 5 kb promoter regions and gene bodies of potential genes. The associated traits included egg number (EN), average clutch size (ACS), longest continuous sequence length (LCS), and average total continuous production length (ACST). across multiple laying periods. Ovary, hypothalamus, and pituitary tissues were collected from high-yield (n = 6 per group) and low-yield (n = 6 per group) Gushi (GS) chickens at 28, 36, and 43 wks of age. Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), Luteinizing Hormone (LH), Estrogen (E2), progesterone (PROG) and Prolactin (PRL) were quantified using commercial ELISA kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., China) from the same manufacturer to ensure methodological consistency. To further investigate the functional effects of these SNPs, a subset of 35 female Guangxi Yao chickens was selected to correlate genotype with SYT1 expression levels. At 43 wks of age, approximately 2 mL of whole blood was collected from the brachial vein of each bird at 43 wks of age into EDTA-coated tubes to prevent coagulation. these birds were humanely euthanized, and all tissue samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until use.

Linkage disequilibrium analysis and gene annotation

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) within the Gushi hens was assessed using the Haploview software (version 4.2) (Barrett et al., 2004). The Variant Effect Predictor (VEP) tool provided by Ensembl (version 113) was utilized to annotate genes located in the regions surrounding the candidate SNPs.

PCA analysis

SNPs within the SYT1 gene were extracted from whole-genome sequences (WGS) data across chicken breeds, including commercial layers (White Leghorn (WL) and Rhode Island Red (RIR), N = 20), indigenous breeds (Gushi (GS), Lushi (LS), Xichuan Black Bone (XCBB) and Zhengyang San Huang (ZYSH) chickens, N = 40) and wild Red Jungle Fowl = (RJF, N = 5) as previously reported (Wang et al., 2022a). The identified SNPs within the SYT1 gene were subsequently used to perform PCA to assess population stratification and domestication signature.

Isolation and culture of pituitary cells

Anterior pituitary tissues were aseptically dissected from 16-d Gushi chicken embryos. Tissues were enzymatically dissociated using trypsin (0.25 %) at 37 °C for 10 minutes. Pituitary cells, once dispersed, were cultured in 24-well plates at a concentration of 5 × 10⁵ cells/well in M199 medium containing 15 % FBS, and incubated at 37 °C under 5 % CO₂. Following a 24-hour incubation, medium were replaced with serum-free M199 medium for transfection experiments. Overexpression and knockdown experiments of SYT1 were subsequently performed. Functional experiments were carried out by transfecting cells with Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The following plasmids were used: the empty vector (pcDNA3.1), a SYT1 overexpression construct (pcDNA3.1-SYT1, provided by Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China), and siRNA vectors (si-NC and si-SYT1, sourced from Gene Pharma Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Twenty-four h after transfection, total RNA was isolated from the transfected pituitary cells for quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) analysis of gonadotropin-related gene expression. Concurrently, culture supernatants were collected and analyzed for FSH and LH levels using ELISA kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., China) in accordance with the supplier’s instructions.

Isolation and culture of ovarian granulosa cells

Ovarian granulosa cells were isolated from 30 wks old Gushi laying and cultured according to the method described by Li et al. (Li et al., 2023). Cells were transfected using Lipofectamine® LTX & PLUSTM Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), with the same constructs used in pituitary assays (pcDNA3.1-SYT1, pcDNA3.1-flag, si-SYT1, si-NC) and following the manufacturer’s instruction. At 12 h post-transfection, total RNA was extracted from the cultured pituitary cells for qPCR and culture supernatants were collected for hormone quantification via ELISA kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., China) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

In situ hybridization of SYT1, FSH and LH

Tissue sections (10 μm thick) were first fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde (Sigma, Shanghai, China) for 5 min, followed by two rinses in diethyl pyrocarbonate (DEPC)-treated phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Subsequently, the samples underwent dehydration using a graded ethanol series. After three washes with wash buffer (SEERNA Bioscience, Xiamen, China), the sections were permeabilized by incubation in 0.1 M HCl at 37 °C for 5 minutes.

Subsequently, hybridization was carried out sequentially: target probes (see Table S1) in hybridization mix at 37 °C for 4 hours, ligation mix at 37 °C for 30 minutes, circularization mix with splint primers at 37 °C for 30 minutes, and amplification via rolling circle amplification (RCA) at 30 °C overnight. Following each step, the slides were washed three times with wash buffer (SEERNA Bioscience, Xiamen, China) at room temperature. Labeled probes were incubated on the slides for 30 min at room temperature. The samples were then mounted with SlowFade™ Gold Antifade Mountant (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Shanghai, China) containing 0.5 μg/mL DAPI (Sigma, Shanghai, China). Fluorescence imaging was performed using a Leica DM6B microscope equipped with a DFC9000GT camera, employing either a 20 × or 40 × objective lens. For multiplex assays, target and label probes were applied at equimolar concentrations.

RNA preparation, cDNA synthesis, and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was isolated from harvested tissues or cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, a genomic DNA (gDNA) removal step was performed prior to reverse transcription. The first-strand cDNA was then synthesized using the HiScript III RT SuperMix (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd.) with oligo-dT primers. Gene expression levels were quantified via quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) on a LightCycler 96 System (Roche Applied Science, IN), employing 2 × SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ II (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd.). Amplification primers were designed using the NCBI Primer-BLAST platform (see Table S2). All reactions were performed in triplicate, and transcript abundance was normalized to the endogenous reference gene GAPDH using the comparative ΔΔCt method.

Flow cytometry analysis on cell cycle

Cells were harvested by trypsinization, washed with PBS, and resuspended in 1 mL PBS. An optional dilution step was carried out by adding 500 μL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to the trypsin-dissociated cell suspension, followed by centrifugation at 2500–3000 × g for 5 min. After removal of the supernatant, the cell pellet was resuspended in 1 mL of PBS and incubated with 300 μL of propidium iodide (PI) staining solution (Solarbio Biotech Co., Ltd.) in the dark at room temperature for 30 min. Cell cycle phase distribution was assessed using flow cytometry, with a minimum of 20,000 events recorded per sample. Data analysis was performed using FlowJo software (version 10.8.1).

Statistical Analysis

Association between SNPs and egg-laying phenotypes were evaluated using a generalized linear mixed model (GLM) implemented in SPSS 23.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). An independent-samples t-test was used to compare differences between two groups, whereas one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for comparisons across more than two groups. All quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to evaluate relationships between gene expression levels and phenotypic traits. Statistical significance was indicated by P < 0.05 (*), while highly significant differences were indicated as P < 0.01 (**).

Results

A total of 4478 SNPs were identified within the SYT1 gene (distributed in 5 kb promoter, 5´UTR, exon, intron and 3´UTR) across various chicken populations. These populations included commercial laying hens such as White Leghorn (WL) and Rhode Island Red (RIR), native chickens such as Gushi chicken (GS), Lushi green-shelled egg chicken (LS), Xichuan black-bone chicken (XCBB) and Zhengyangsanhuang chicken (ZYSH), and the wild Red Jungle Fowl (RJF). PCA based on all SYT1 SNPs (Fig. 1A), as well as those specifically within the promoter region (Fig. 1B), demonstrated that commercial laying hens were clustered separately from native. These results indicate that rs39477032 and rs39497549 are strongly correlated with multiple reproductive traits and may serve as robust molecular markers for selecting egg-laying traits. Discussion

GWAS have been widely applied in poultry breeding programs, leading to the identification of numerous candidate genes associated with economically important traits such as growth and reproduction, thus greatly accelerating the functional characterization of trait-associated genes in poultry. For instance, eight SNPs in White Leghorn and brown-egg dwarf layers have been identified as significantly associated with egg laying traits. (Liu et al., 2011). Moreover, a SNP in the RAPGEF6 gene has been found to correlate significantly with laying rate during the later stages of the reproductive cycle (Azmal et al., 2019). In the present study, a large number of SNPs within the SYT1 gene were identified as significantly associated with egg-laying performance in the Gushi chicken population using GWAS. Among them, rs39477032 and rs39497549 exhibited particularly strong associations with key egg production traits, and these associations were validated in the Guangxi Yao chicken population. These results suggest that the genetic variation of SYT1 may play an influential role in regulating reproductive traits of chickens, making it a promissing molecular marker for improving egg production efficiency. Accordingly, rs39497549 and rs39477032 may serve as effective molecular markers in breeding programs targeting extended laying periods in native breeds.

The hypothalamus-pituitary-ovarian axis (HPO) serves as the central regulatory system governing avian reproductive function. The three hormones of HPO axis interact and balance each other through positive and negative feedback loops, maintaining normal reproductive activities. The synthesis and secretion of GnRH, gonadotropins (FSH and LH) and steroid hormone are governed by tightly coordinated feedback mechanisms that sustain normal reproductive activity. FSH promotes follicular development and stimulates ovarian granulosa cells to secrete E2 via activation of the aromatase complex. LH stimulates theca cells to produce androgen substrate, which are converted into estrogen by FSH-stimulated granulosa cells, supporting oocyte maturation (Filicori, 1999). In this study, SYT1 expression was significantly higher in pituitary and ovarian tissues of high and low-yield Gushi chickens at 28 w, 36 w and 43 w, consistent with the findings of Luan et al. in Huoyan geese (Luan et al., 2013). Furthermore, SYT1 expression in pituitary tissue was positively correlated with serum levels of FSH, LH and E2, while its ovarian expression was positively correlated with LH and E2. These findings imply that SYT1 contribute to hormonal regulation within the HPO axis and thereby influences egg production outcomes.

Previous studies have demonstrated that Synaptotagmin-1 functions as a vesicular calcium sensor essential for synchronized neurotransmitter release. Upon GnRH stimulation of pituitary cells, calcium influx is triggered via voltage-gated calcium channels, elevating intracellular Ca²⁺ levels. As a Ca²⁺-binding protein, SYT1 detects this rise and interacts with the SNARE complex to mediate exocytosis of hormone-containing vesicles, particularly FSH and LH (Luan et al., 2014). Using in vitro cultured pituitary cells, we demonstrated that SYT1 expression is upregulated in response to GnRH and that it significantly enhances the expression and secretion of gonadotropins in pituitary cells. This is consistent with the role of GnRHR2 in mediating calcium signaling following GnRH activation (Liu et al., 1995). The tandem C2 domains of SYT1 sense Ca²⁺ fluctuations and promote vesicle docking and fusion by interacting with the SNARE complex at the cell membrane, facilitating efficient hormone release. This ultimately leads to exocytosis and the secretion and release of FSH and LH. Prior studies have shown that increasing FSH levels in low-yield laying hens promotes follicular maturation, a process accompanied by the upregulation of estrogen, progesterone, LH, and angiopoietin (Ma et al., 2020).

Ovarian granulosa cells are a key cell type within the follicles of female animals. The processes of proliferation and differentiation in these cells play a pivotal role in regulating follicular growth and development. CCND1 is one of the important cell cycle regulators related to cell cycle arrest, which can stimulate the proliferation of granulosa cells in follicles (Alqudah et al., 2013; Zhu et al., 2019). CDK1 is considered to be related to cell cycle and DNA replication (Zhu et al., 2021). Our study demonstrates that SYT1 can regulate the expression of CCND1, PCNA, CCND2, CDK1, and cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2), establishing it as a positive regulator of granulosa cell proliferation. GDF9 and BMP15 are oocyte-specific factors, discovered in 1993 and 1998, respectively, as new members of TGF-β superfamily (Dube et al., 1998; McPherron and Lee, 1993). In ovarian granulosa cells, GDF9 and BMP15 can induce mitosis and differentiation during follicular development through paracrine pathway, promoting the maturation and differentiation of granulosa cells (Paulini and Melo, 2011; Wang et al., 2025). Studies have shown that BMP15 knockout inhibits granulosa cell proliferation and differentiation, reduces FSHR expression, and diminishes estrogen production and oocytes quality (Qin et al., 2019). This study revealed that SYT1 promoted the expression of BMP15, GDF9, FSHR and other genes, thereby facilitating the maturation and differentiation of ovarian granulosa cells. As a key exocytosis regulator, SYT1 influences pituitary gonadotropin secretion, which regulates ovarian steroid hormone production. In chicken, LH receptor (LHR) and FSH receptor (FSHR) are expressed on the surface of prearranged follicular cells and can be activated by LH and FSH, respectively, secreted from pituitary gland. This activation stimulates the cyclic phosphate-dependent protein kinase signaling pathway, which promotes the secretion of steroid acute regulatory protein (STAR), steroid-producing enzymes (3β-HSD, CYP19A1, CYP11A1, etc.), and the synthesis estrogen, progesterone and testosterone (Sechman et al., 2020). Among these, CYP19A1 is a key enzyme that converts sex hormones into estradiol, and nuclear receptor subfamily 1D (NR1D1) can bind to CYP19A1 promoter region, inhibiting its transcription and reducing estradiol production of estradiol content (Wang et al., 2022b). STMN1, a tumor marker, mediates progesterone production by regulating the promoter activities of STAR and CYP11A1 (Dou et al., 2016). Recent studies further confirmed SYT1’s role in regulating egg-laying performance through multi-omics analyses, identifying it as a hub gene facilitating gonadotropin and steroid hormone secretion (Wang et al., 2024). Our study found that SYT1 can promote the expression of steroidogenesis-related marker genes (including STAR, CYP11A1, and CYP19A1) and enhance progesterone secretion, while exerting no significant effect on estrogen secretion. Based on our collective findings, we propose a comprehensive regulatory model for the role of SYT1 in avian reproduction (Supplementary Figure 1). In this model, SYT1 acts at two critical levels of the HPO axis. First, in the pituitary, it functions as a key mediator of GnRH signaling, facilitating the Ca²⁺-dependent exocytosis of vesicles to enhance the secretion of FSH and LH (Supplementary Figure 1A). The proposed steroidogenic pathway involves the upstream stimulation of SYT1 to promote STAR-mediated cholesterol (CHOL) mobilization. Cholesterol is then converted to pregnenolone (P5) by CYP11A1 and subsequently to progesterone by 3β-HSD, demonstrating SYT1’s influence on the progesterone biosynthesis cascade (Supplementary Figure 1B). This dual-action mechanism positions SYT1 as a pivotal coordinator of both pituitary hormone release and ovarian steroidogenesis, thereby driving higher egg production performance.

Conclusions

In this study, two SNPs (rs39497549 and rs39477032) within the SYT1 gene were significantly associated with egg production traits. The mRNA expression levels of SYT1 gene in the HPO axis were significantly correlated with serum reproductive hormone levels, and SYT1 was colocalized with LH in pituitary cells. Gain- and loss-of function assays demonstrated that SYT1 serves as a critical regulator of reproductive hormone synthesis and secretion within the hypothalamus-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis. Specifically, SYT1 overexpression in cultured pituitary cells significantly increased FSH and LH secretion into the culture medium. Conversely, SYT1 knockdown via siRNA transfection substantially attenuated GnRH-stimulated gonadotropin expression and hormone release, effectively abolishing the stimulatory effects of GnRH treatment. This study provides valuable insights into the regulatory mechanism of SYT1 gene underlying reproductive performance in poultry, and offers a theoretical basis for genetic enhancement of egg production in local chickens, particularly for extending their egg-laying periods.

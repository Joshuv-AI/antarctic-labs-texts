A longitudinal 1H NMR-based urinary metabolomics dataset in a birth cohort for childhood atopic diseases
Authors: Yi-Hsin Kuo, Chih-Yung Chiu, Meng-Han Chiang, Jing-Long Huang, Kuan-Wen Su, Ming-Han Tsai, Man-Chin Hua, Sui-Ling Liao, Shen-Hao Lai, Kuo-Wei Yeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset provides a longitudinal analysis of urinary metabolomic profiles from 198 children aged 1 month, 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years. Demographic information, including birth history, feeding practices, parental atopic history, household income, and atopic disease status at different ages, was collected. Total and allergen-specific IgE levels (mite, egg, cow’s milk) were measured to evaluate allergic sensitization over time. Atopic disease status was also monitored at multiple time points to assess its development. The urinary metabolomic data, generated using advanced 1H-nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, were processed and normalized to support robust metabolic modeling. This dataset aims to provide insights into the relationships among early growth, metabolomic changes, and the development of allergic sensitization and atopic diseases in early childhood.Subject terms: Immunological disorders, Biomarkers, Molecular biology

Background & Summary

This dataset originates from a birth cohort of the Prediction of Allergies in Taiwanese Children (PATCH) study, which aims to investigate the high prevalence of IgE sensitization in Taiwanese children and its associations with specific allergens, immune responses, and environmental exposures. This birth cohort provides demographic characteristics, feeding practices, allergic sensitization, atopic disease status, and exposure to environmental factors such as smoking in 198 children. It has contributed to understanding how early-life factors influence the development of allergic diseases, including asthma, eczema, and allergic rhinitis1.

In this birth cohort, urinary metabolomic profiles were collected at 1 month, 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years of age. Urinary metabolomics, using advanced techniques such as 1H-NMR spectroscopy, provides a non-invasive method to capture metabolic fingerprints that reflect an individual’s nutritional intake, growth, and immune responses during critical developmental stages. NMR spectroscopy is particularly advantageous for metabolomic studies due to its reproducibility, quantitative accuracy, and ability to detect a wide range of metabolites without requiring extensive sample preparation. Unlike other techniques, such as mass spectrometry, NMR allows for the direct and unbiased analysis of complex biological samples, making it ideal for longitudinal studies where consistency is crucial. These metabolomic profiles enable researchers to explore metabolic shifts associated with feeding practices, allergic immune response, and the onset of atopic diseases2–4.

Serum immunoglobulin E (IgE) and allergen-specific IgE levels (mite, egg, cow’s milk) were measured at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years to evaluate allergic sensitization over time. IgE levels are a critical biomarker for understanding immune responses to allergens, enabling the identification of early sensitization patterns that precede clinical manifestations of atopic diseases5. In addition, atopic disease status was monitored at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years, focusing on the development of conditions such as asthma, eczema, and allergic rhinitis. Parental atopic status was also collected, providing additional context on familial predisposition. This birth cohort provides to integrate urinary metabolomic profiles to explore metabolic pathways associated with atopic disease progression. By analyzing longitudinal urinary metabolites, it is possible to uncover early biomarkers and metabolic shifts linked to allergic immune dysregulation, offering new insights into the biological mechanisms underlying atopic conditions6.

This dataset is a versatile resource for studying early-life factors influencing healthy growth and allergy development. Its longitudinal design, spanning key developmental stages, allows researchers to track metabolic changes and immune responses over time. Combining urinary metabolites, IgE levels, feeding practices, and atopic disease status with demographic and familial information, this dataset enables exploration of topics such as feeding type and metabolic shifts or early biomarkers of health. By supporting secondary analyses and cross-disciplinary studies, it provides valuable insights into growth, nutrition, and allergy disease mechanisms during critical developmental periods.

Methods

Study population

The birth cohort initially comprised 260 children enrolled from the Prediction of Allergies in Taiwanese Children (PATCH) study. Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all participants at the time of enrollment in the PATCH study, allowing for longitudinal follow-up and use of biospecimens and biological information data for further research. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (IRB No. 102–1842 C and IRB 104-8925 C). Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (IRB No. 102–1842 C and IRB 104–8925 C). For the present study involving secondary analysis and public release of the dataset, additional ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Chang Gung Medical Foundation (IRB No. 202502104B0). The IRB determined that the study qualified for expedited review and approved a waiver of re-consent, as all data were irreversibly de-identified and posed minimal risk to participants. To protect participant privacy, all data were de-identified prior to analysis. An anonymized subject ID code was assigned to each participant, and no personal identifiers were retained in the research dataset. Of these, 198 children were successfully followed through to the fifth year, while the remaining participants were lost to follow-up and excluded from the analysis. Evaluation and diagnosis of atopic diseases

Childhood atopic diseases include atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, and asthma. Information on current and past allergic symptoms for diagnosing these conditions was collected using the validated International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood (ISAAC) questionnaire7. Allergic airway diseases, including asthma and allergic rhinitis, were diagnosed by a pediatric pulmonologist at outpatient clinics, following consistent diagnostic criteria based on the Global Initiative for Asthma guidelines8 and the Allergic Rhinitis and its Impact on Asthma guidelines9. Eczema was identified as a pruritic rash on the face and/or extensor surfaces with a chronic, relapsing course, as defined by Hanifin and Rajka10.

IgE level measurement and atopic disease assessment

IgE measurements, including total IgE and allergen specific IgE (mite, cow milk, egg), were taken at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years using ImmunoCAP (Phadia, Sweden) and expressed in kilo units per liter (kU/L)11. Atopic disease status of 198 children was assessed at multiple time points (6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years) to monitor the development of conditions such as asthma, eczema, and allergic rhinitis.

1H‐NMR spectroscopy: urine sample preparation and data processing

A 900 μL of urine was mixed with 100 μL of 1.5 M phosphate buffer in deuterium water adding 0.04% 3-(trimethylsilyl)-propionic-2,2,3,3-d4 ac-id sodium salt (TSP) as a chemical shift reference. The samples were swirled for 20 seconds and centrifuged at 12000 g at 4 °C for 30 minutes. Then, a total of 650 μL supernatant was delivered to a 5-mm NMR tube for further experiments. 1H‐NMR spectra data were acquired at 300 K by Bruker Avance 600 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker-Biospin GmbH, Karlsruhe, Germany) equipped with a 5 mm CPTCI 1 H cryoprobe. For each NMR spectra, 64 scans were collected with a spectral width of 10,000 Hz (10 ppm) during the relaxation time of 4 s. Prior to Fourier transformation, these spectra were pre-processed by zero padding and multiplying exponents (line broadening factor = 0.3 Hz). Manually phased, baseline corrected, and referenced the chemical shift to TSP (δ 0.0 ppm) were then performed by TopSpin 3.2 software (Bruker BioSpin, Rheinstetten, Germany) for further NMR data analysis. Data Records

The dataset used in this study is available on the MetaboLights repository MTBLS12785 (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS12785)12. The dataset included both metabolomic and clinical data collected longitudinally from a birth cohort study. The dataset is organized into two main folders: nmr_file and csv_file. The nmr_file folder contains raw ¹H-NMR spectral data in Bruker zipped format. File names follow a structured naming convention in the format of <timepoint>_urine-<ID>.zip, where the prefix indicates the sample collection time point: 1 M (1 month), 6 M (6 months), 1Y (1 year), 3Y (3 years), and 5Y (5 years), and the numeric suffix refers to the subject identification number (e.g., 100).

The csv file folder contains clinical and demographic data collected longitudinally from 198 children. Specifically, the file AtopicDiseases_6M-5Y.csv includes binary indicators of asthma, eczema, and allergic rhinitis assessed at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years of age. The file BW_BH_BMI_Birth-5Y.csv records body weight, height, and BMI from birth to age 5. Demographic.csv includes birth history, feeding practices, household income, and smoking exposure data. Family_atopy.csv contains information on the family history of atopic diseases, while Mother_IgE.csv records maternal total IgE levels. Total IgE levels in children at multiple time points are available in Total_IgE_6M-5Y.csv. Allergen-specific IgE measurements for Dermatophagoides farinae, Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus, egg white, and cow’s milk are available in Dfarinae_IgE_6M-5Y.csv, Dpteronyssinus_IgE_6M-5Y.csv, eggwhite_IgE_6M-5Y.csv, and milk_IgE_6M-5Y.csv, respectively. Each file contains a Num column that serves as the subject identifier, which can be used to link records across different files. Researchers can explore relationships among urinary metabolites, allergic immune responses, and atopic disease progression by integrating these datasets.

The dataset presented in this Data Descriptor represents the complete urinary metabolomics dataset from the birth cohort of the Prediction of Allergies in Taiwanese Children (PATCH) study. Owing to the long-term follow-up of this cohort, partial case–control study designs have been conducted and published. However, only two main publications using metabolomics data from specific age groups have been previously reported, including Chang et al.13 and Chiu et al.14. This present Data Descriptor focuses on the public release and documentation of the de-identified urinary metabolomics dataset across all age groups.

Technical Validation

The integrity and technical quality of the data were upheld through rigorous standardization of data collection and processing protocols. The urinary metabolic profiles were obtained using 1H-NMR spectroscopy, a technique renowned for its precision in metabolomic studies. The consistency of the data was ensured by the use of a single type of NMR spectrometer (Bruker Avance 600 MHz) across all samples. Standard operating procedures for sample preparation, such as consistent mixing and centrifugation times, temperature control, and chemical shift referencing with TSP—minimized variability.

Each NMR spectrum was subjected to zero padding and exponential line broadening (0.3 Hz) prior to Fourier transformation to improve signal-to-noise ratios and spectral resolution. Phase and baseline corrections were manually performed to ensure accuracy in peak identification and quantification. The processed spectra underwent rigorous quality control measures, including internal validation of chemical shift referencing and external validation through comparison with known metabolite standards.

Data availability

The dataset is available from the MetaboLights repository under accession number MTBLS12785. It includes raw 1H-NMR spectral data (in Bruker zipped format) and associated clinical metadata in CSV format. The NMR files are stored in the nmr_file directory and follow the naming pattern <timepoint>_urine-<ID>.zip (e.g., 1M_urine-100.zip). Clinical and demographic variables are stored in the csv_file directory. All data files can be accessed directly at: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS12785.

Safety and efficacy of the feed additives consisting of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced by fermentation with Aspergillus niger CGMCC 6.466 for all animal species (Sunshine Biotech International)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Andrey Yurkov, Maria Dulak‐Lis, Jaume Galobart, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Alberto Navarro Villa, Elisa Pettenati, Piera Valeri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced by fermentation with Aspergillus niger CGMCC 6.466 as technological feed additives for all animal species. The additives are not currently authorised. Citric acid produced by fermentation with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 may pose safety concerns due to the possible presence of harmful secondary metabolites produced by the production strain. No toxicological studies with the fermentation product were submitted, and the presence of viable cells of the production strain could not be excluded in both forms of the additive. In addition, concerns were raised regarding some processing aids used in the manufacturing process. Therefore, the Panel could not conclude on the safety of citric acid produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 for the target species, the consumer and the user. The Panel concludes that the additives are safe for the environment. The FEEDAP Panel concludes that citric acid has the potential to act as an acidity regulator when used in feed and water for all animal species. In the absence of data, the Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of citric acid as a preservative.Keywords: all animal species, Aspergillus niger CGMCC 6.466, citric acid, efficacy, preservatives and acidity regulators, safety, technological additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from Sunshine Biotech International 2 for the authorisation of the additive consisting of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with Aspergillus niger CGMCC 6.466, when used as feed additives for all animal species (category: technological additives; functional group: preservatives and acidity regulators).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 03rd January 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00006. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 03 June 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additives consisting citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with Aspergillus niger CGMCC 6.466, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.4).

Additional information

The additives consist of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466. They have not been previously authorised as feed additives in the European Union.

Citric acid produced by different strains of A. niger is currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species. 3

The FEEDAP Panel issued two opinions on citric acid produced by fermentation, when used as a preservative and as an acidity regulator (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2015a, 2015b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 as feed additives.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 1 April 2025 to 22 April 2025 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 6 June 2024 to 06 September 2024; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of the active substance in animal feed, premixtures, compound feed and water. 7

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 8 and the relevant guidance documents, Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024), Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

Citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 are intended to be used as technological feed additives (functional groups: preservatives and acidity regulators) for all animal species.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the production strain

Citric acid is produced with a non‐genetically modified strain of A. niger, which is deposited in the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center with the accession number CGMCC 6.466. 9

The production strain has been obtained through traditional ■■■■■ mutagenesis from ■■■■■ ■■■■■ A. niger ■■■■■. 10 The phylogenomic analysis based on 3989 single copy ortholog genes confirmed the identification of CGMCC 6.466 as A. niger. 11

The capacity of the production strain A. niger CGMCC 6.466 to produce antimicrobial substances was tested in three sterile‐filtered supernatants of liquid cultures of the strain against Pseudomonas aeruginosa ATCC 27853, Enterococcus faecalis ATCC 29212, Bacillus subtilis ATCC 6633, Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 and Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25923. 12 No antimicrobial activity (a zone of inhibition) was observed against any test culture.

The WGS data of the production strain were interrogated for the presence of genes coding for known harmful metabolites using the antiSMASH database. 13 The analysis identified a total of 62 potential secondary metabolite regions, including those coding for non‐ribosomal peptides (n = 30), polyketide synthase T1PKS (n = 21), non‐ribosomal peptide synth(et)ase polyketide synthase NRPS‐PKS chemical hybrids (n = 6), fungal ribosomally synthesised and post‐translationally modified peptides RiPP‐like (n = 8), terpenes (n = 8), beta‐lactone (n = 1) and indole (n = 1). The production strain might have the potential to produce some azanigerones, naphthopyrones, terpenes (clavaric acid), pyranonigrins, choline, linear tetracyclic TAN‐1612 and ochratoxin A, as these observed gene clusters showed high similarity. The presence of these gene clusters in the production strain represents a hazard, as they may lead to the synthesis of harmful secondary metabolites.

Manufacturing process

The additives are produced by ■■■■■ fermentation ■■■■■ the production strain A. niger CGMCC 6.466. ■■■■■. 14 The applicant stated that no antimicrobial substances are used during the manufacturing of the additives. 15

In the original submission the applicant stated that ■■■■■ and an alpha‐amylase from an unspecified B. subtilis were used in the manufacturing process of the additives. The applicant later stated that the ■■■■■ 16 and the alpha‐amylase replaced with another enzyme produced with either Bacillus amyloliquefaciens UN‐01 or Bacillus licheniformis CCTCC M 2023118. 17 These enzymes have been recently assessed by EFSA FEZ Panel for their use as food enzymes (EFSA FEZ Panel, 2024, 2025); the FEZ Panel could not conclude on the safety of the amylase produced with B. licheniformis CCTCC M 2023118.

The Panel also notes that no detailed information on the new manufacturing process nor data on the composition of the resulting additives were submitted.

Characterisation of the additives

Citric acid (synonyms: 2‐hydroxy‐1,2,3‐propanetricarboxylic acid, β‐hydroxytricarballylic acid, 3‐carboxy‐3‐hydroxypentanedioic acid) appears in two chemical forms: anhydrous and monohydrate. The anhydrous form has the Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) number 77‐92‐9, chemical formula C6H8O7 and molecular weight of 192.13. The monohydrate form has the CAS number 5949‐29‐1, chemical formula C6H8O7.H2O and molecular weight of 210.14.

The additive is produced in two forms, anhydrous and monohydrate. The anhydrous form is specified to contain ≥ 99.5% citric acid on a dry matter basis, with ≤ 1% water, while the monohydrate form contains ≥ 92% citric acid on a dry matter basis, with ≤ 10% water.

The Panel notes that the data referred to the additives obtained with the original manufacturing process. Data on impurities 20 and stability studies 21 are reported in Appendix A – Impurities and stability studies of citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate.

The data provided showed compliance with the specifications set by the applicant for citric acid.

The FEEDAP Panel notes the high levels of citrinin (470.9 μg/kg) and deoxynivalenol (248.0 μg/kg) (see Appendix A) in both forms of the additive, which deserve attention/monitoring during the manufacturing process. The Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the levels of the impurities, other than citrinin and deoxynivalenol, analysed in both forms of the additive are of no concern.

The presence of viable cells of the production strain was investigated in six batches of the two forms of citric acid in triplicate (10 g per sample). 22 No viable cells were detected. However, the FEEDAP Panel notes that the methodology followed was not compliant with the requirements set in the Guidance (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018) since the incubation time was too short (2 days) and considered not adequate to recover stressed cells potentially remaining in the product. Consequently, the FEEDAP Panel cannot exclude the presence of viable cells of A. niger CGMCC 6.466 in both forms of the additive.

Conditions of use

The additives are intended for use in feed for all animal species at a proposed maximum concentration of 15,000 mg/kg of complete feed and 5000 mg/L of water for drinking. No minimum use level is specified.

Safety

No specific studies done with the additives under assessment were provided. The applicant performed an extensive literature search (ELS) to provide evidence that the additives are safe for all animal species. However, several shortcomings were identified in the ELS (e.g. restrictive search strategy, lack of target species in the search terms) and thus, it was not further considered. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of citric acid for the target species at the proposed conditions of use.

Citric acid is a natural substance, serving as an intermediate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle and a normal constituent of living cells. When ingested by target animals, citric acid is rapidly metabolised to carbon dioxide and water, so its use as a feed additive is not expected to increase consumer exposure or its concentration or distribution in the environment. Therefore, the use of citric acid per se in animal nutrition is considered safe for the consumer and the environment.

No experimental data has been submitted to support the safety of the additives for the user. Based on the information present in the safety data sheets, 23 both forms of the additive are irritant to skin and eyes and therefore, should be considered respiratory irritants. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk.

The citric acid under assessment is produced by a non‐genetically modified strain of A. niger and concerns may arise from the production strain/its fermentation process. The strain A. niger CGMCC 6.466 was shown to harbour gene clusters that may lead to the synthesis of harmful secondary metabolites. No toxicological studies (including genotoxicity) with the fermentation product were submitted and the presence of viable cells of the production strain could not be excluded in both forms of the additive. Considering the above and the concern raised on some of the processing aids used in the manufacturing process (i.e. amylase produced with B. licheniformis CCTCC M 2023118), the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the citric acid produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 for the target species, consumer and user. Considering that A. niger is naturally present in the environment, the potential presence of viable cells of the production strain CGMCC 6.466 in the final additives raises no safety concerns for the environment.

Efficacy

The additives are intended to be used as a preservative and acidity regulator in feed for all animal species.

The applicant submitted a series of publications to support the efficacy of citric acid as an acidity regulator (Table 2). The studies evaluated the effect on pH when different levels of citric acid were supplemented to feeds (i.e. complete feeds) formulated for a variety of species (i.e. chickens for fattening, weaned piglets and fish [Labeo rohita and rainbow trout]). It is noted that some studies lack clear information regarding the number of replicates analysed and/or omit statistical evaluation of pH measurements. Only two of the papers provided detailed information on the methodology followed for the analytical determination of the pH of feeds (Baruah et al., 2005; Radcliffe et al., 1998), which consisted of a 10% suspension of the feed in deionised water. However, the evidence from all the studies indicates consistently that the inclusion of citric acid lowers the pH of feed. No data were provided concerning the use of citric acid in water but, considering the properties and the nature of the additive, it is likely to be efficacious as an acidity regulator in water as well. Thus, considering the results and despite the limitations of the studies, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that citric acid has the potential to act as an acidity regulator in feed and water.

No evidence was provided by the applicant to support the efficacy of citric acid as a preservative in feed. Therefore, in the absence of adequate data, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of citric acid as a preservative.

Conclusions on efficacy

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that citric acid has the potential to act as an acidity regulator when used in feed and water for all animal species. In the absence of data, the Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of citric acid as a preservative.

CONCLUSIONS

Citric acid anhydrous and citric acid monohydrate produced by fermentation with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 may pose safety concerns due to the possible production of harmful secondary metabolites. No toxicological studies with the fermentation product were submitted, and the presence of viable cells of the production strain could not be excluded in both forms of the additive. In addition, concerns were raised regarding some processing aids (i.e. amylase produced with B. licheniformis CCTCC M 2023118) used in the manufacturing process. Therefore, the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of citric acid produced with A. niger CGMCC 6.466 for the target species, the consumer and the user. The Panel concludes that the additives are safe for the environment.

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that citric acid has the potential to act as an acidity regulator when used in feed and water for all animal species. In the absence of data, the Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of citric acid as a preservative.

Preoperative Enema for Peritoneal Dialysis Catheter Dysfunction: Are We Ready for Routine Use?
Authors: Fan Zhang
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

I read with interest the randomized trial by Zhao et al.1 evaluating preoperative enema for the prevention of early peritoneal dialysis catheter dysfunction, which addressed a peridialysis question among end-stage renal disease patients. However, 3 issues deserve further consideration before routine adoption in clinical practice.

First, the definition of the primary end point—catheter dysfunction requiring intervention—may be susceptible to practice variability. Decisions to intervene (e.g., use of laxatives, catheter manipulation, or radiologic repositioning) are often influenced by local protocols and clinician thresholds rather than purely objective criteria. Previous studies have demonstrated substantial heterogeneity in managing early catheter dysfunction, which can affect reported event rates and treatment effects.2,3

Second, postoperative bowel management was not clearly detailed. Constipation is a well-recognized contributor to catheter dysfunction, and routine prophylactic laxative use after peritoneal dialysis catheter insertion is recommended in many centers.4 If postoperative bowel regimens differed or were not standardized, this could confound the observed benefit attributed to preoperative enema alone.

Third, the mechanistic link between a single preoperative enema and sustained reduction in catheter dysfunction up to 6 months remains uncertain. Although reduced fecal loading may facilitate initial catheter positioning, ongoing catheter function is more strongly influenced by factors such as omental wrapping, adhesions, and catheter migration.5 The persistence of benefit suggests that additional, unmeasured factors may be contributing.

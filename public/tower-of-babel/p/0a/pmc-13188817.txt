Cumulative exposure to factor XIII deficiency during veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Nicolas Nesseler, Adeline Pontis, Erwan Flécher, Mathieu Beaudeau, Nicolas Patou-Parvedy, Isabelle Gouin-Thibault, Alexandre Mansour
Published: 2026

Factor XIII (FXIII) contributes to clot stability and reduces clot susceptibility to lysis through fibrin cross-linking. Standard coagulation tests like prothrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time do not assess FXIII activity and therefore cannot detect FXIII deficiency. Acquired FXIII deficiency has been reported in bleeding patients in trauma and perioperative settings, although a causal relationship has not been established [1]. Recent European guidelines suggested monitoring FXIII and correcting deficiencies, despite low-quality evidence supporting this practice [2]. Acquired FXIII deficiency has also been reported in patients on extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), but data remain limited [3]. Moreover, the time-weighted average (TWA) of FXIII levels, reflecting cumulative exposure to deficiency, has never been investigated. We aimed to evaluate TWA FXIII plasma levels during veno-arterial (VA)-ECMO support. We hypothesized that VA-ECMO would be associated with meaningful cumulative exposure to acquired FXIII deficiency.

Patients older than 18 years old supported by VA-ECMO (first ECMO run) were included. Detailed methods and exclusion criteria have been previously published [4]. The work was approved by the Ethics Committee (CPP OUEST, Brest, France, 2019-A011440-57). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their relatives. We calculated the median TWA FXIII plasma level (TWA-FXIII), measured from ECMO initiation up to Day7 or decannulation (eleven time points: H0, H2, H6, H12, H24 and daily from Day2 to Day7). In the absence of consensus, FXIII deficiency was defined as a TWA-FXIII ≤ 70%. This level is considered to have an effect on blood loss and transfusion in trauma or surgical related bleeding according to the European Society of Anaesthesiology and Intensive Care guidelines [2]. Based on congenital FXIII deficiency, severity of FXIII deficiency was categorized as follows: normal (> 70%), mild (30–70%, usually asymptomatic), moderate (10–30%, associated with mild spontaneous or triggered bleeding) and severe (< 10%, associated with spontaneous major bleeding) [1]. Blood samples were drawn from a pre-existing arterial line and collected into vacuum tubes (Vacutainer; Becton Dickinson, USA) containing citrate (0.109 M). Plasma was obtained after centrifugation at 2,000 g for 10 min, within 1 h after withdrawal. After a second centrifugation, plasma samples were stored frozen at − 80 °C before thawing. FXIII quantitative functional activity was measured using a WHO standardized method (Berichrom FXIII assay, Siemens HealthCare, Marburg, Germany; reference values 70–140%). Blank correction with iodoacetamide was performed following the recommendations of the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis. Because sampling intervals varied from 2 to 24 h, TWA of FXIII was calculated using linear interpolation between time points (Supplementary figure S1). FXIII-related variables are reported as median with interquartile ranges.

Baseline characteristics of the 50 patients included, corresponding to 413 FXIII measurements, are reported in the Supplementary Table S1. At ECMO initiation (H0) plasma FXIII levels were 70 (53–84)% (Fig. 1, Supplementary Figure S2 and Table S2). No consistent modulation of FXIII levels over time was observed during VA-ECMO support, including in analyses that excluded patients who received fresh frozen plasma (Supplementary Figure S3). Overall, 46 (92%) patients had at least one FXIII value below 70%, 28 (56%) below 50%, 6 (12%) below 30% and none below 10%. The median TWA-FXIII was 64 (58–73)%, and 34 (68%) patients had TWA values ≤ 70%, These patients did not differ in ECMO indication or duration(TWA > 70%: 8.1[6.4–10.8] and ≤ 70%: 6.0[3.1–11.9] days, difference [95%CI]:−1.3 [− 4.4 to + 2.8]), but presented with more severe illness, as reflected by higher SAPS II scores and lactate levels, and lower hemoglobin and fibrinogen concentrations. Overall, patients spent a median 80 (36–94)% of ECMO-time with FXIII levels below 70%, including 72 (35–92)% between 30 and 70%. No significant differences in FXIII levels or TWA-FXIII were observed in relation to post-cardiotomy status (Supplementary Table S3) or the occurrence of severe bleeding (Supplementary Table S4).

Individual changes of factor XIII plasma levels during the first seven days of Veno-arterial Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (VA-ECMO) support

Cardiogenic shock patients on VA-ECMO spent most of the time with FXIII levels corresponding to mild to moderate deficiency. However, FXIII levels showed substantial interindividual variability from ECMO initiation without consistent directional changes over time. Previous single-center studies reported acquired FXIII deficiency in ECMO patients, but none included several systematic sampling during the first 24 h followed by daily measurements, nor did they evaluate cumulative FXIII exposure. The underlying mechanisms are likely multifactorial, including reduced hepatic synthesis due to initial shock, consumption related to thrombin generation during ECMO support, and bleeding-related losses. However, the clinical relevance of this deficiency remains uncertain, as most FXIII levels remained between 30% and 70%, a range generally not associated with bleeding symptoms in inherited FXIII deficiency, although this may not directly translate to critically ill ECMO patients [1]. Thus, no association with severe bleeding was detected, although this analysis was limited by sample size and potential confounding. Additionally, in the context of mild to moderate deficiencies, preventive FXIII supplementation may offer little benefit, as shown in cardiac surgery patients at moderate risk for transfusion [5]. The question remains open in cases of active bleeding with FXIII levels below 30%.

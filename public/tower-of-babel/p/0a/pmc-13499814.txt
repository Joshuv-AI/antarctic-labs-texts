Treatment Patterns and Decision Drivers in Chinese Patients with Locally Advanced Cervical Cancer: Results from a National Physician Survey
Authors: Peng Peng, Yirong Yin, Binqi Zhang, Shuzhen Yao, Chen Li, Ming Du, Yang Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCervical cancer (CC) is the fourth-most common and lethal cancer in women worldwide, with ~ 23% of cases in Chinese patients. We sought to characterize real-world treatment patterns and decision drivers for locally advanced CC (LACC) in China.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional, 3-phase survey (quantitative development, cognitive, and quantitative interviews) of 360 physicians across 21 cities in China. Primary outcomes included treatment patterns and decision drivers for Chinese patients with LACC. Cancer stage was reported per International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018.ResultsRespondents were gynecologists (55.6%), radiation oncologists (36.1%), and medical oncologists (8.3%), who reported 67.7% patients had LACC (stage IB3–IVA). Treatment patterns varied by stage: chemoradiotherapy (CRT) alone was used by 18.1%, 42.2%, and 52.1% of respondents for stages IB3–IIA2, IIB, and III–IVA CC, respectively, with surgery-based treatment used by 67.1%, 42.0%, and 26.6% of respondents. Among patients with LACC receiving concurrent CRT, 57.7% received external-beam radiotherapy (EBRT) plus brachytherapy (BT). The most important LACC treatment decision drivers were treatment efficacy (91.7%), CC stage (89.7%), and safety (88.6%). Only 63.9% reported that both EBRT and BT could be performed at their hospitals. Radiotherapy-related issues were reported as the top unmet medical need by 66.4%.ConclusionThere is a significant LACC burden among female patients in China, and deviations from guideline-concordant concurrent CRT. Addressing implementation gaps in concurrent CRT adoption requires improved radiotherapy access, greater multidisciplinary team use, guideline-aligned quality control, enhanced physician and patient education, and improved affordability. Future research should incorporate patient perspectives to improve patient-centered care and outcomes.Keywords: Locally advanced cervical cancer, Treatment patterns, Chemoradiotherapy, Surgery, Survey

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Cervical cancer is the fourth-most common and lethal cancer in women worldwide, with around 23% of cases in Chinese patients.We sought to characterize real-world treatment patterns and decision drivers for locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC) in China. What was learned from the study? In our three-phase survey of 360 physicians across 21 cities in China, including gynecologists, radiation oncologists, and medical oncologists, a LACC rate of 67.7% was reported, showing a significant disease burden.There were deviations from guideline-concordant chemoradiotherapy and high use of surgery, revealing implementation gaps in chemoradiotherapy adoption.Addressing these gaps requires improved radiotherapy access, greater use of multidisciplinary teams, guideline-aligned quality control, enhanced physician and patient education, and better reimbursement policies.

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) is the fourth-most common cancer affecting women and the eighth-most commonly occurring cancer worldwide [1]. With the highest number of new cases (150,659) and the second-highest estimated number of deaths (55,694), China accounts for approximately 23% of the global CC burden [2].

Approximately 37% of CC is diagnosed as locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC) globally. This proportion is generally higher in Asian countries such as China, Japan, South Korea, and India, and there remains a lack of understanding of the proportion and characteristics of patients with LACC in China [3]. There is no consistent global definition of LACC. Typically, International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018 stage IB3–IVA is considered LACC in the literature [4]. Society of Gynecologic Oncology (SGO) and European Society of Gynaecological Oncology (ESGO) guidelines define LACC as FIGO 2018 stages IB3 and IIA2 to IVA [5, 6], while National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines do not explicitly define LACC [7]. Chinese guidelines [Chinese Society for Clinical Oncology (CSCO) and Chinese Society for Gynecologic Oncology (CSGO)] adopt a narrower definition of LACC as FIGO 2018 stages IB3 and IIA2 [8, 9]. Real-world definitions and understanding of LACC in clinical practice in China are needed.

Guideline recommendations for treatment of LACC revolve around concurrent chemoradiotherapy (concurrent CRT) as standard of care, based on its established superiority over radiotherapy (RT) [10, 11], and surgery [12, 13]. Concurrent CRT [external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) with concurrent platinum-based chemotherapy and brachytherapy (BT)] is a category 1 recommendation for LACC stages IB3, and IIA2; it is a cornerstone recommendation for stage IIB–IVA. Surgery (type C1 radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymphadenectomy with or without para-aortic lymphadenectomy) is a category 2B recommendation for stages IB3, and IIA2 [7–9].

Despite guideline recommendations, the implementation of standardized concurrent CRT in China faces unique challenges. A survey involving 80 physicians from mainland China found that RT with systemic therapy was administered to 14% and 39% of patients with FIGO 2018 stage IB3–II and III–IVA disease in mainland China, respectively [14]. This is in stark contrast to other Asia–Pacific regions, where the administration rates were 70% and 80% in Australia, 72% and 87% in the Philippines, 51% and 83% in South Korea, and 48% and 79% in Taiwan [14]. This study gave critical insights into real-world treatment patterns and unmet needs of Chinese patients with LACC. However, it had a small sample size and a lack of RT physicians.

We conducted a larger national survey of Chinese gynecologists, medical oncologists, and radiation oncologists. We aimed to characterize patient profiles and treatment patterns, assess implementation gaps, identify key decision drivers, and prioritize clinical unmet needs to better support the implementation of standard of care for patients with LACC.

Methods

Study Design

This study was a national cross-sectional survey of gynecologists (n = 200), medical oncologists (n = 30), and radiation oncologists (n = 130) specializing in CC treatment in China, conducted between 2 August, 2023 and 25 September, 2024. There were three phases: survey development, cognitive interviewing, and a quantitative self-completed questionnaire.

The cognitive interview phase was a 40-min interview of three gynecologists, four medical oncologists, and two radiation oncologists from their respective medical association senior leader group, designed to highlight any problems with comprehension, recall, decision, or response process necessary to obtain adequate responses, along with a debrief to confirm any modifications to be made to the questionnaire. The final quantitative phase comprised a 30-min self-completed questionnaire, delivered to 360 physicians using an online platform.

Recruitment was conducted using a systematic approach to ensure nationwide representation, with respondents geographically distributed across China’s main regions to minimize potential bias. After the initial list of hospitals was gathered, target provinces were screened based on the incidence of CC and number of level 3 hospitals (i.e., with the highest medical level), and target hospitals were screened based on the number of gynecologists/medical oncologists/radiation oncologists and number of hospital beds. As a result, a list of hospitals with number of gynecologists, medical oncologists, and radiation oncologists in each hospital were provided for review and contacted for screening.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards/Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, 2-Aug-2023 (ethics approval number I-25PJ1067). Respondents gave written informed consent prior to inclusion in the study. Publication-related items were included in the informed consent form signed by the participants.

Eligibility Criteria and Respondents

Eligible physicians specialized in oncology, radiation oncology/therapeutic radiology, or gynecology. Respondents must have had ≥ 5 years of post-training experience and were the main treatment decision-makers for CC; treated ≥ 30 patients with CC within the past 6 months, including ≥ 1 with LACC; must have treated ≥ 5 with LACC overall; and must have spent ≥ 60% of their time in direct patient care. The race and ethnicity of respondents was not collected, as it was assumed that almost all respondents were Han Chinese. The respondents were referring to treatment of female patients; patient sex was relevant and gender was not assessed. Respondent age, sex, and gender were not collected, as these factors were not thought to have a meaningful impact on the outcomes of interest.

Objectives

To describe LACC treatment patterns, identify factors influencing treatment decisions, and examine the overall resource utilization associated with treatment and RT access.

Outcomes of Interest

These were the real-world proportions, definitions and high-risk factors of LACC, patient characteristics, treatment patterns [stratified by risk level (FIGO 2018 stage IB3–IIA2; IIB; III–IVA)], factors related to treatment decisions, overall resource utilization, and unmet needs.

Statistical Analysis

Assuming 0.1% of physicians would meet the inclusion criteria, the targeted population size was calculated to be 4068. With a confidence interval of 95% and margin of error of 5%, the minimum sample size was calculated to be 352.

Quantitative data were analyzed descriptively. Generally, missing values were not imputed and the summary of each variable was based on respondents who had valid responses. For categorical variables using a Likert scale of 1–10 (with 1 representing ‘not at all important’ and 10 representing ‘extremely important’) frequencies and percentages were provided for highest three scores (8–10), middle four scores (4–7), and lowest three (1–3) scores.

Subgroup analyses of treatment patterns by specialty type, location (tier 1 vs. tier 2 cities) and accessibility to RT were also conducted.

Analyses were conducted using SPSS version 22 (IBM) and/or R version 3.4.4 or above.

Results

Respondents

A total of 360 physicians were included. Most respondents (n = 200; 55.6%) were gynecologists, 130 (36.1%) were radiation oncologists, and 30 (8.3%) were medical oncologists. Respondents were mostly (n = 276; 76.7%) in tier-2 cities, with 84 (23.3%) in tier-1 cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, or Shenzhen). All except one respondent (n = 359; 99.7%) worked in public hospitals. The physician respondents had a mean (SD) of 16.0 (7.3) years of clinical experience, spent 86.6% (8.8) of their time on direct patient care, and had treated 60.3 (60.7) patients with CC in the past 6 months, of whom 28.3 (29.2) had LACC. Respondents had treated a mean (SD) of 736.2 (856.1) patients with LACC.

Proportion, Definitions and High-Risk Factors of LACC

Physicians reported that most [mean (SD) of 64.0% (23.4)] of their patients in the past 6 months were newly diagnosed and either untreated or had no progression/recurrence following initial treatment. Most patients [67.7% (23.9)] were diagnosed as FIGO 2018 stage IB3–IVA, the typical definition of LACC (Table 1).

Characteristics of Patients with LACC

Respondents reported that a mean (SD) of about half of their patients [54.3% (16.1)] with LACC were aged 45–64 years (Table 2). The majority of patients (82.3% (18.0)] were insured, and about half (56.0% (29.3)] had no treatment preference, relying on their physician’s recommendations. Most patients (75.2% (16.5)] had full adherence with treatment regimens. Diabetes and hypertension were the most commonly observed comorbidities, with 86.7% of respondents reporting these.

Treatment Patterns

Treatment Modalities for Stages IB3–IIA2, IIB, and III–IVA

Among patients with stage IB3–IIA2 CC, a range of treatment modalities were reported (Fig. 1). Chemoradiotherapy (CRT; sequential or concurrent) alone was used by a mean (SD) of 18.0% (24.3), and surgery-based treatments in 67.1% (26.3). Among patients with stage IIB CC, the most common modality was CRT [42.2% (39.5)], followed by surgery-based treatments [42.0% (35.3)]. The modality most patients [52.1% (38.7)] with stage III–IVA CC received was CRT, followed by surgery-based treatments [26.6% (31.5)].

Subgroup Analysis by RT Access and Specialties

For stage IB3–IIA2 disease, physicians in hospitals without access to RT reported a higher proportion of patients undergoing surgery than in hospitals with RT access [mean (SD), 78.5% (19.5) relative to 64.9% (26.9)]. This was similar for stage IIB [61.4% (35.8) relative to 40.7% (34.7)] and stage III–IVA disease [33.2% (35.0) relative to 26.3% (31.3)].

CRT use varied among specialties, with radiation oncologists most likely to use it [in a mean (SD) of 24.5% (29.3), 45.8% (38.8), and 57.3% (36.9) of patients with stages IB3–IIA2, IIB, and III–IVA disease, respectively]. Gynecologists reported CRT use in 14.7% (21.0), 41.4% (40.1), and 50.9% (39.8) of patients with these stages, respectively, while medical oncologists did so in 13.7% (15.3), 30.8% (37.0), and 36.9% (36.6).

Stages, Modalities, and Surgical Procedures in Patients with LACC Undergoing Surgery

The majority of physicians reported that patients with stages IB3 (84.4%), IIA1 (81.4%) and IIA2 (67.5%) underwent radical surgery, while few chose radical surgery for patients with stages IIB (33.6%), IIIA–IIIB (20.8%), IIIC (15.8%), and IVA (8.1%) LACC.

Among patients receiving neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy, CRT was the most common approach, followed by RT, and chemotherapy (Fig. 1).

Among patients with LACC who underwent surgery, the most common surgical procedure was abdominal hysterectomy or total hysterectomy with or without lymph node dissection and adnexal resection, in a mean (SD) of 51.3% (34.0) of patients. Laparoscopic hysterectomy/total hysterectomy with or without lymph node dissection and adnexal resection was used in 34.3% (30.8), with vaginal hysterectomy/total hysterectomy with or without lymph node dissection and adnexal resection in 7.8% (12.3).

Chemotherapy and RT in Patients with LACC Undergoing CRT

Among patients with LACC receiving CRT, most [(61.3% (SD, 28.4)] underwent concurrent CRT, while a mean (SD) of 32.0% (25.2) received sequential CRT.

In this survey, chemotherapy regimens reported as concurrent CRT reflect those used in physician-reported real-world practice. Among patients who received concurrent CRT, platinum monotherapy, representing the guideline-recommended concurrent chemotherapy approach, was reported in a mean (SD) of 31.6% (31.1) of cases. Other regimens included platinum plus taxane [49.6% (30.9)], platinum plus non-taxane [9.4% (12.1)], and taxane monotherapy [6.3% (8.4)]. Among platinum-monotherapy regimens, cisplatin was used by 54.7% of respondents, while 38.1% reported using carboplatin. Cisplatin plus paclitaxel (73.9%) and carboplatin plus paclitaxel (71.4%) were the most frequently reported doublet regimens used in conjunction with RT.

Most [mean (SD), 57.7% (36.4)] patients receiving concurrent CRT underwent EBRT plus BT; a mean (SD) of 22.3% (26.5) received EBRT only, and 10.8% (15.2) received BT only. The mean (SD) waiting time for RT was 10.6 (13.8) days, which was longer in Tier 1 than Tier 2 cities [17.9 (23.0) vs. 8.0 (6.7) days].

Factors Influencing LACC Treatment Decisions

The most important LACC treatment decision drivers, in terms of proportions of respondents rating them a Likert score of 8–10/10, were efficacy (91.7%), CC stage (89.7%), and safety (88.6%; Fig. 2). High proportions of respondents reported treatment guidelines (80.3–84.7%) and clinical experience (76.4%) as important treatment references (Likert scale 8–10). In patient-related factors, the patient’s current disease stage was reported as the most important characteristic (mean Likert score of 8.9). Treatment efficacy, safety, and accessibility were considered the most influential treatment-related characteristics, with proportions of respondents rating them 8–10 of 91.7%, 88.6%, and 83.6%, respectively.

Most physicians (73.3%) reported being highly satisfied with the current treatment options for LACC, with the overall average satisfaction score being 8.2/10. Discussion

This descriptive survey provides an overview of real-world management and unmet needs of patients with LACC in China. It underscores the high disease burden, specific deficiencies in treatment patterns, accessibility, and physician awareness, highlighting the need for targeted interventions. Despite the availability of clinical guidelines for LACC, the heterogenous nature of this cancer complicates clinical decision-making. As new treatments emerge, there will be an even greater need to identify tailored treatment strategies based on a deeper understanding of patient characteristics, differences in disease course, and disparities in medical resources. Furthermore, initiatives to improve access to guideline-concordant concurrent CRT as a definitive treatment for LACC in China remain imperative.

In this study, a high proportion of patients (67.7%) had LACC (FIGO 2018 stage IB3–IVA) reported, emphasizing the importance of screening, early diagnosis, and timely treatment. However, a standardized definition of LACC is lacking in current Chinese clinical practice, likely due to inconsistencies across guidelines. SGO and ESGO guidelines define LACC as stages IB3 and IIA2-IVA [5, 6], while NCCN guidelines lack definitions for common pathological types of LACC. Chinese guidelines adopt a narrower definition of LACC as stages IB3 and IIA2, and define IIB–IVA as mid-to-late stage [8, 9].

Additionally, non-standardized staging can adversely affect clinical decision-making. A large-scale Chinese study reported that 35.7% of CC cases had non-standardized clinical staging based on FIGO criteria, including missing, incomplete, incongruous, or vague staging descriptions [15]. Addressing these challenges requires implementing a standardized LACC definition within Chinese guidelines and enhancing physician education to improve diagnostic accuracy, and enable clear, standardized staging.

Despite high proportions of physicians regarding guidelines as highly important treatment decision drivers, the use of surgery-based treatments was high (26.6–67.1%) and there was low adoption of CRT alone (18.1–52.1%), practices inconsistent with guideline recommendations of definitive concurrent CRT as the preferred standard of care for LACC [7–9], highlighting a significant implementation gap between guideline recommendations and actual clinical practice in China.

This discrepancy may stem from several interrelated factors. First, the lack of RT (especially BT) equipment and specialized RT physicians is notable and reflected in this study. Only 63.9% of physicians had access to both EBRT and BT; 6.7% of physicians reported no RT facilities in their hospitals, and 18.9% reported that only EBRT was available. Among patients with LACC undergoing concurrent CRT in this study, EBRT plus BT was the most commonly used form of RT; however, this was only reported in 57.7%, revealing a large implementation gap to the guideline recommendations [16]. China has one of the highest estimated RT demands, with a shortage of experienced and qualified specialists, a need for high-quality EBRT and BT, and presenting challenges in training personnel [17]. Initiatives to improve availability of RT equipment, expertise, and training are clearly needed.

Second, this challenge is compounded by the high cost of concurrent CRT. Despite overall health insurance coverage being high in this study (82.3%), the fact that most respondents reported RT to be reimbursed by insurance should not be interpreted as full coverage or the absence of out-of-pocket payments. In China, reimbursement typically reflects partial coverage rather than full payment; a nationwide multicenter study reported that medical insurance covered only around 43–55% of direct medical costs for CC, and catastrophic health expenditures remained common, particularly among patients in rural areas due to differences in economic development [18]. Notably, some studies have found that surgery is less costly than RT or CRT [19, 20]. From our own experience, at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, the price of CC surgery over the past 2 years was ¥30,000–40,000 (US$4200–5600), while traditional RT cost approximately ¥50,000–80,000 (US$7000–11,200). If more advanced RT equipment such as intensity-modulated RT/volumetric modulated arc therapy is used, the price increases to around ¥100,000 US$14,000). Time costs with CRT, such as prolonged waiting periods (averaging 10.6 days in this study), and the requirement for frequent hospital visits over approximately 2 months, add to financial costs.

Third, for over 20 years, the only curative treatment options for LACC have been concurrent CRT and surgery, but evidence comparing these approaches remains equivocal. While large meta-analyses have shown no improvement in survival with concurrent CRT over surgery, with concurrent CRT offering superior DFS [24], others have shown a trend to overall survival (OS) superiority with concurrent CRT over surgery [HR 0.71 (95% CI, 0.49–1.02); probability of superiority, 96.9%] [25]. The present survey suggests that some Chinese clinicians believe that concurrent CRT does not offer a significant advantage over radical surgery. Radical surgery has emerged as a valid alternative for managing LACC in China and is recommended by the CSCO guidelines for patients with stages IB3 and IIA2 disease and limited access to RT [16], and Japanese guidelines include surgery as an option alongside concurrent CRT for patients with stage IIB CC [26]. Notably, FIGO guidelines recommend concurrent CRT for patients with stages IB3 and IIA2, but note that treatment modality should be determined based on resource availability and tumor- and patient-related factors, with neoadjuvant chemotherapy used if RT facilities are unavailable [27]. Collectively, tumor size alone is not the main obstacle for Chinese physicians to choose surgery-based treatments. Indeed, for specific pathological types that are less sensitive to RT and chemotherapy (such as small-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma of the cervix, clear cell carcinoma), surgery may improve the prognosis, especially in stages IB3-IIA2 [16, 28]. It is possible that surgical removal can reduce tumor burden, indirectly enhancing sensitivity to subsequent treatments, though evidence is lacking that debulking surgery is useful [29]. Treatment-related toxicity also influences clinical decision-making. Concurrent CRT is associated with higher rates of gastrointestinal toxicity and bone-marrow suppression than radical surgery alone [22], whereas surgery has been reported to have a significantly higher rate of major complications than concurrent CRT in later stages [23].

Importantly, our study found that most patients with LACC undergoing radical surgery required neoadjuvant or adjuvant CRT, RT, or chemotherapy. The accumulation of multiple treatment modalities increases complication risk, prolongs the treatment duration, and worsens patient quality of life [21]. It is clear that the decision between surgical management and CRT is guided by a range of clinical factors, and efforts should be made to reduce unnecessary treatment overlap, and ensure optimal therapeutic benefit. When addressing the complex challenges of CC, multidisciplinary team (MDT) collaboration leverages comprehensive strengths and has become an important model in clinical management [30]. In our study, clinical experience (which included personal and MDT experience) was reported as a key decision driver by 76.4% of respondents. However, MDT implementation remains incomplete and requires time to fully mature [31, 32].

Fourth, regarding physician and patient preferences, physicians might prefer surgery, as patients’ initial visits are usually with gynecology/gynecological oncology departments, where surgeons are more familiar with and confident in surgery. Patients might also prefer surgery, perceiving it to be a curative measure, and are likely to rely on their treating physician’s recommendations. Even with full knowledge of the risks, a notable proportion of patients still opt initially for surgery [33]. In China, regional inequality in education and heterogeneous economic development means that only about one-quarter of the adult population have basic health literacy [34], leading to a reliance on physician advice without full understanding. Indeed, our study found that 56.0% of patients had no treatment preference and relied on their physician’s recommendations, while 75.2% had full adherence to the prescribed regimens. The use of surgery is generally consistent with previous findings [14, 35], and underscores the need for enhanced physician education and greater patient awareness.

Beyond treatment selection, the quality and completeness of definitive concurrent CRT delivery remain critical determinants of outcomes in LACC. International guidelines recommend weekly cisplatin (or carboplatin if cisplatin-intolerant) as the standard concurrent chemotherapy backbone during definitive RT [5–7]. Guidelines also note that BT is a critical component of effective concurrent CRT, with image-guided techniques such as image-guided adaptive BT forming an increasingly important part of effective BT implementation [7, 36–38]. Prolonged overall treatment time (more than 56 days) is associated with inferior local control and survival [38–42]. In the present survey, however, to avoid excessive respondent burden, we did not collect granular information on specific technical components of concurrent CRT delivery. Nevertheless, certain patterns observed in this survey, such as the relatively frequent use of platinum-based doublet chemotherapy regimens during concurrent CRT, may serve as indirect signals of heterogeneity in concurrent CRT execution and underscore the need to further improve standardization and quality of concurrent CRT delivery in real-world practice.

Recent therapeutic advances further highlight the need for high-quality, standardized concurrent CRT as a foundation for treatment intensification. In the KEYNOTE-A18 trial, adding pembrolizumab to concurrent CRT significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with LACC compared with placebo plus concurrent CRT [hazard ratio (HR) for PFS, 0.72 (95% CI, 0.59–0.87); HR for OS, 0.73 (95% CI, 0.57–0.94) at the final analysis [43–45)]. The US FDA approved pembrolizumab plus concurrent CRT for patients with FIGO 2014 stage III–IVA CC in 2024. Chinese NMPA approval followed shortly afterwards for the same indication, based on the data from 112 patients with FIGO 2014 stage III–IVA disease in the Chinese cohort, which revealed a OS HR of 0.34 (95% CI, 0.12–0.95) and a PFS HR of 0.35 (95% CI, 0.16–0.74) [46]. Importantly, the regimen used in KEYNOTE-A18, now established as standard of care, requires high-quality, and standardized concurrent CRT, including EBRT followed by BT [44]; non-standard concurrent CRT may therefore limit benefits to patients from the latest treatment options [47].

This study had limitations. First, selection bias may have occurred, as respondents who were better informed and more interested were more likely to participate. Second, patient characteristics, treatment patterns, and healthcare resource use were reported by physicians, so recall biases might introduce measurement errors, although this is mitigated by the relatively large sample size. The data may have a hierarchical structure, with physicians nested within hospitals and city tiers, potentially underestimating errors. Finally, as the study only included physicians, it lacks the patient perspective, particularly regarding factors affecting patients’ adherence to treatment, preference, and unmet needs. Future research should include inclusive, co-designed studies to address this gap. Data on socioeconomic and demographic diversity (ethnic minority groups, rural vs. urban differences) were not collected, which may further limit the interpretability of the results.

Conclusion

There is a substantial LACC burden in China. Gaps between real-world practice and guideline-concordant care were observed, with a low proportion of patients receiving concurrent CRT. Targeted, system-level strategies to improve the implementation of guideline-concordant care are needed, including innovative therapeutic approaches, enhanced physician and patient education, greater use of multidisciplinary teams, improved RT accessibility, and broader reimbursement, to lower the economic burden for patients. At the same time, guideline development should take into account disparities in healthcare resources, particularly in underdeveloped regions.

Pericardial fluid cardiac troponin I predicts postoperative atrial fibrillation after coronary artery bypass grafting
Authors: Yue Shu, Chaoyue Gao, Xiaoshuang Ren, Xun Deng, Lin Luo, Taiwei Wang, Zexinyao Ren, Jiahui Zhao, Yisi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveCardiac troponin I (cTnI) is a sensitive biomarker of myocardial injury. We hypothesized that cTnI measured in pericardial drainage fluid (PCF), which reflects the cardiac microenvironment, may enhance risk stratification for postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) after coronary artery bypass grafting.MethodsIn this prospective cohort study, 114 patients undergoing elective coronary artery bypass grafting were enrolled. The primary end point was new-onset POAF within 7 days postoperatively. Blood and PCF cTnI at 6 hours postoperatively were measured. Variables were screened using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression and were then incorporated into multivariable logistic regression to construct a predictive model. A simplified risk score (CALT score) was developed. Model performance was compared with existing POAF tools, including the POAF score, CHA2DS2-VASc, and the atrial fibrillation risk index.ResultsPOAF occurred in 30.7% of the cohort. Postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI was an independent predictor of POAF (odds ratio, 5.502; 95% CI, 1.887-16.045; P = .002) and significantly enhanced continuous net reclassification improvement (0.562; 95% CI, 0.189-0.921; P = .002). The CALT score comprised left circumflex artery lesions, left atrial diameter, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, postoperative 0-hour serum calcium, and postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI. The PCF-cTnI–based model demonstrated good discrimination (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve = 0.821), with favorable calibration and clinical utility. Compared with commonly used risk scores, the CALT score exhibited superior discrimination and calibration.ConclusionsThis study provides novel evidence that PCF-cTnI is a strong predictor of POAF. The CALT score, integrating PCF-cTnI with clinical factors, provided superior predictive performance by capturing both the vulnerable atrial substrate and localized myocardial injury.

Central Message

Pericardial cTnI, routinely measured perioperatively, is a novel, powerful, noninvasive predictor of POAF. To our knowledge, we are the first to develop a PCF-cTnI-based model with clinical applicability.

Perspective

cTnI is readily available at the bedside during the perioperative period. To our knowledge, we are the first to demonstrate that pericardial cTnI is a powerful biomarker for POAF and to develop a PCF-cTnI-based prediction model, with strong translational and clinical applicability. Pericardial drainage, often discarded, can be collected for cTnI measurement for POAF risk stratification.

Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) occurs in 20% to 40% of patients after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)1 and is associated with prolonged stays in the intensive care unit and increased mortality.2 Accurate risk stratification is therefore essential to guide preventive interventions. Current predictive tools, such as the POAF score3 and the CHA2DS2-VASc score,4 primarily rely on preoperative characteristics and exhibit modest accuracy (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve [AUC], 0.54-0.75).5 Growing evidence suggests that local inflammation and oxidative stress are key drivers of POAF.6,7 Pericardial drainage fluid (PCF), which originates directly from the surgical field, provides a unique and minimally invasive window for monitoring local cardiac pathophysiologic changes after surgery.8 Accordingly, several inflammation- and oxidative stress–related biomarkers measured in PCF have demonstrated good predictive performance for POAF, including interleukin-6,9 mitochondrial DNA,10 and myeloperoxidase (MPO).11 Nevertheless, these biomarkers are laboratory-based and time-consuming, which limits their translational potential and highlights the need for clinically accessible and translatable biomarkers to improve POAF risk stratification.

Postoperative local inflammation and oxidative stress, which are considered key contributors to POAF pathogenesis, are closely associated with intraoperative myocardial injury.12 Cardiac troponin I (cTnI) is a sensitive marker of myocardial injury with standardized assays available and rapid turnaround times, making it highly suitable for clinical translation.13 Previous studies have demonstrated that postoperative serum cTnI levels are associated with adverse cardiac outcomes after surgery. Van Geene and colleagues14 reported that postoperative circulating cTnI levels were significantly higher in patients who died compared with survivors and that cTnI was an independent predictor of in-hospital mortality. Nevertheless, studies regarding the relationship between cTnI and new-onset POAF after CABG remain limited. Existing evidence has focused predominantly on serum levels of cTnI and has yielded inconsistent results.15,16 Consistently, a review concluded that the predictive value of circulating biomarkers for POAF is conflicting.17 Collectively, these findings illustrate the limitations of systemic biomarkers and suggest that PCF may better capture postoperative local myocardial injury. Accordingly, we hypothesized that cTnI levels measured in PCF may provide meaningful predictive information for POAF. To date, only a few studies have examined the association between postoperative pericardial drainage fluid–derived cardiac troponin I (PCF-cTnI) levels and POAF after CABG.

In this prospective cohort study, we aimed to (1) assess cTnI concentration in both peripheral blood and PCF after CABG; (2) evaluate the association between cTnI and the occurrence of POAF; and (3) develop and internally validate a PCF-cTnI-based prediction model for POAF and compare its predictive performance with that of commonly used POAF risk assessment tools.

Methods

Study Population

This prospective study was conducted at the cardiology department of a tertiary teaching hospital and was reported in accordance with the TRIPOD-AI (ie, Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis + Artificial Intelligence) guidelines.18 Patients undergoing first-time elective isolated CABG with preoperative sinus rhythm between October 2023 and August 2024 were consecutively recruited. Exclusion criteria were (1) a history of atrial fibrillation; (2) presence of a pacemaker or implantable cardioverter defibrillator; (3) active infection, chronic inflammatory disease, immunodeficiency, malignancy, or hepatic failure; and (4) receiving anti-inflammatory therapy or hemodialysis.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Capital Medical University (2023SY120, August 2023). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Perioperative Management

In all patients, aspirin was discontinued 5 to 7 days before surgery, and preoperative coronary angiography was performed during hospitalization. In patients undergoing CABG via median sternotomy, the pericardium was partially closed to allow communication with the mediastinal space. Postoperatively, 2 separate chest tubes are routinely inserted, with one placed in the pericardial space and the other in the anterior mediastinum or pleural cavity according to intraoperative findings. Each tube was connected to a vacuum-assisted suction drainage system. After surgery, sedation was administered using propofol and dexmedetomidine. Unless contraindicated, metoprolol was initiated on the first postoperative day, with the dosage adjusted according to weight and heart rate. Amiodarone was administered only when POAF occurred. All surgical procedures followed local institutional protocols, and the study protocol did not interfere with routine clinical management.

Evaluation of New-Onset POAF

The primary end point was the occurrence of POAF, defined as new-onset atrial fibrillation lasting ≥30 seconds.19 POAF was diagnosed on the basis of continuous electrocardiographic (ECG) monitoring, including bedside monitoring (M8002A; Philips), telemetry (M3290A; Philips), and wearable ECG patches (EPAJ-PAH-1A; Shanghai Telecardio) during the first 7 postoperative days. Wearable ECG patches were applied immediately postoperatively to ensure uninterrupted 7-day monitoring in all patients. This wireless device was attached to the left upper chest and was capable of storing up to 14 days of continuous ECG recordings. Two cardiologists who were blinded to study outcomes independently adjudicated POAF episodes on the basis of the ECG recordings. Any discrepancies were resolved through joint re-evaluation with a senior cardiac specialist. All POAF episodes were further centrally reviewed by an electrophysiology expert committee.

Measurement of PCF-cTnI

Our team previously measured MPO concentrations in PCF at 6, 12, and 18 hours after surgery and found that the 6-hour postoperative MPO level demonstrated the strongest predictive value for POAF.20 Given that POAF can occur as early as 12 hours after surgery, the 6-hour postoperative time point was selected for sample collection in this study. Blood samples were obtained via the arterial sheath, and PCF samples were collected from the pericardial drainage tube. The pericardial drainage tube was emptied before sampling, and the maximum accumulation time was limited to 30 minutes. All samples were centrifuged at 1500g for 15 minutes, and serum or supernatant was stored at −80 °C. After completion of sample collection, all specimens were transported to a central laboratory for batch analysis. cTnI concentrations were quantified using the Elabscience Human TNNI3/cTn-I (Troponin I Type 3, Cardiac) ELISA Kit (Wuhan, China). Laboratory personnel were blinded to study group allocation.

Covariates

On the basis of previous reports21, 22, 23 and the study objectives, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative variables potentially relevant to POAF were selected (Table E1). Preoperative coronary angiography results were obtained from the most recent examination. Clinical data were prospectively extracted from electronic medical records by trained researchers using standardized data-collection forms. Regular quality control checks were conducted to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the data.

Statistical Analysis

Normally distributed continuous variables are presented as mean and SD, whereas non-normally distributed variables are reported as median and interquartile range. Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. Missing data accounted for less than 5% for all variables and were handled using multiple imputation. Between-group comparisons were performed using the t test, Mann-Whitney U test, χ2 test, or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Candidate predictors were initially screened using 10-fold cross-validation and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression. The linearity assumption between continuous candidate variables and the logit-transformed outcome was assessed using the Box-Tidwell test, with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple comparisons. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was subsequently performed to identify independent predictors among the candidate variables.

Model performance was evaluated in terms of discrimination (AUC), calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, Brier score, and calibration plot), and clinical utility (decision curve analysis). Internal validation was conducted using bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) to estimate optimism in the final model. The incremental predictive value of postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI was assessed using the continuous net reclassification improvement (NRI). On the basis of the β-coefficients of the final multivariable model, a nomogram and a simplified clinical risk score (CALT score) were constructed. Optimal cut-off points for risk stratification were determined using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The performance of the risk score was compared with the POAF score,3 CHA2DS2-VASc score,4 and the atrial fibrillation risk index,24 which have been previously validated in patients undergoing CABG. These risk scores were calculated as published (Table E2). Model comparisons included AUC, Brier score, continuous NRI, and integrated discrimination improvement.

The DeLong test was conducted to compare differences in AUCs between models. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS, version 27.0 (IBM Corp) and RStudio (version 2025.5; Posit PBC), with R packages including glmnet, pROC, ResourceSelection, rms, boot, nricens, rmda, and DescTools.

Sample Size Calculation

In our pilot study of 20 patients, the prevalence of POAF was 35% (7/20). The optimal cut-off value for PCF-cTnI was 38.45 ng/mL (sensitivity: 57.1%, specificity: 100%). Using this threshold, 20% of patients (4/20) were classified as having high PCF-cTnI levels. Among these patients, the prevalence of POAF was 100%, compared with 18.7% in the low PCF-cTnI group. On the basis of these pilot data and assuming an odds ratio (OR) of 5, the required sample size was estimated to be 112 patients to achieve 90% power at a 2-sided significance level of .05 using a Wald test. Ultimately, 114 patients were enrolled, and the optimal cut-off value for PCF-cTnI was 27.2 ng/mL. The prevalence of POAF was 22.2% in the low PCF-cTnI group and 51.5% in the high PCF-cTnI group, with 28.9% of patients classified as having high PCF-cTnI levels. On the basis of these data, the study achieved an empirical power of approximately 85.1% at a 2-sided significance level of .05 to detect an OR of 5.5.

Data Availability

The data underlying this article will be shared on reasonable request to the corresponding author.

Results

Baseline Characteristics of Study Participants

A total of 139 patients were assessed for eligibility, and 114 were enrolled in the study (Figure E1). Baseline characteristics and perioperative data are summarized in Table E3. The mean age was 61.8 ± 8.4 years, and 75.4% of patients were male. Thirty-five patients (30.7%) developed POAF, with a median onset time of 46 hours (range, 18-89 hours) after surgery. Among the POAF episodes, 2 reverted spontaneously to sinus rhythm, one was terminated by electrical cardioversion, and the remaining patients were managed with amiodarone. Compared with patients without POAF, those with POAF had a longer duration of tracheal intubation (median [25th-75th percentile]: 25 [23-29] vs 23 [22-28] hours, P = .152). Univariate analysis showed that patients with POAF had significantly larger left atrial diameter (LAD) and left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), as well as lower left ventricular ejection fraction. In addition, pericardial drainage volume in the first 6 postoperative hours was higher in the POAF group, although not statistically significant.

Higher PCF-cTnI Levels at 6 Hours Postoperatively in Patients With POAF

Patients who developed POAF exhibited significantly higher PCF-cTnI levels compared with those without POAF (median [25th-75th percentile]: 25.5 [17.0-35.4] vs 18.7 [13.3-26.5] ng/mL, P = .006, Figure 1, A). In contrast, peripheral blood cTnI levels at the same time point did not differ between groups (Figure 1, B), suggesting that PCF-cTnI may more accurately reflect local myocardial injury, which could be diluted and undetectable in systemic circulation. To explore the primary source of PCF-cTnI, cTnI concentrations were compared between PCF and peripheral blood. In the overall cohort, PCF-cTnI was 19.5 ng/mL (25th-75th percentile, 14.4-27.9 ng/mL) versus blood cTnI of 0.5 ng/mL (25th-75th percentile, 0.3-0.8 ng/mL), representing a 39-fold higher concentration in PCF (P < .001). In the POAF group, PCF-cTnI was approximately 64-fold higher than blood cTnI (25.5 [17.0-35.4] vs 0.4 [0.2-0.7] ng/mL, P < .001), whereas in the non-POAF group, it was approximately 37-fold higher (18.7 [13.3-26.5] vs 0.5 [0.3-1.0] ng/mL, P < .001) (Figure 1, C). Correlation analysis showed no significant association between PCF-cTnI and blood cTnI (r = 0.098, P = .298), indicating that the primary source of cTnI is likely local release within the pericardial cavity. Because PCF-cTnI is a biomarker of myocardial injury, we further explored its associations with several perioperative factors potentially related to myocardial injury. No significant correlations were observed between PCF-cTnI concentration at 6 hours and postoperative PCF volume during the 0-6 hour (r = −0.022, P = .816), 0-24 hour (r = 0.029, P = .762), 0-48 hour (r = 0.005, P = .962), or 24-48 hour (r = −0.049, P = .608) periods. Development of the Prediction Model

Variables were initially screened using LASSO regression.25 The optimal λ value was determined through 10-fold cross-validation by selecting the model with the minimum binomial deviance. The coefficient profiles are shown in Figure 2, A. When λ = 0.053 (log λ = −2.94), the optimal model balancing predictive performance and parsimony was obtained (Figure 2, B). The selected candidate predictors included left circumflex artery (LCX) lesion, LAD, LVEDD, left ventricular ejection fraction, serum calcium at 0 hours postoperatively, and PCF-cTnI at 6 hours postoperatively. Box-Tidwell tests confirmed that all continuous variables satisfied the linearity assumption (all P > .01). On the basis of these selected variables, 2 multivariable logistic regression models were subsequently constructed: one using the continuous variables and another using the same variables dichotomized on the basis of optimal cut-off values determined by the maximum Youden index (Table 1). The final model identified 5 independent predictors: one clinical feature, LCX lesion (OR, 0.279; 95% CI, 0.093-0.831); 2 echocardiographic parameters, LAD ≥53.5 mm (OR, 4.099; 95% CI, 1.494-11.247) and LVEDD ≥52.5 mm (OR, 5.792; 95% CI, 1.871-17.931); one systemic biomarker, serum calcium at 0 hours postoperatively ≥1.155 mmol/L (OR, 0.212; 95% CI, 0.075-0.604); and one local myocardial injury marker, PCF-cTnI at 6 hours postoperatively ≥27.2 ng/mL (OR, 5.502; 95% CI, 1.887-16.045). Among these variables, LVEDD and postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI showed the strongest independent associations with POAF. Model Performance and Internal Validation

When continuous variables were included, the model achieved an AUC of 0.840 (95% CI, 0.753-0.913) (Figure 3, A). After dichotomization, the AUC was 0.821 (95% CI, 0.736-0.903), indicating good discriminative performance (Figure 3, B). The difference between the continuous and dichotomized models was not statistically significant (ΔAUC = 0.019, P = .573), suggesting relatively stable performance. The dichotomized model was selected as the final model for clinical implementation. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (P = .934), Brier score (0.150), and calibration plot. The calibration curve closely approximated the ideal 45-degree reference line, demonstrating good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes (Figure 3, C). To address overfitting, the final model was internally validated using bootstrap resampling, yielding an AUC of 0.759 (95% CI, 0.636-0.836), indicating acceptable discrimination. Exploratory subgroup analyses suggested generally consistent model discrimination across different patient phenotypes, with AUCs ranging from 0.731 to 0.840 (Figure E2). The bias-corrected calibration curve remained close to the ideal line, further supporting the robustness of the model (Figure 3, C).

PCF-cTnI Provides Significant Incremental Predictive Value

To assess the incremental predictive value of postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI beyond conventional risk factors, AUCs were compared across models (Figure 4). The baseline model, incorporating 1 clinical variable (LCX lesion) and 2 echocardiographic parameters (LAD and LVEDD), yielded an AUC of 0.753. The addition of serum calcium at 0 hours postoperatively modestly increased the AUC to 0.785 (ΔAUC = 0.032, P = .175). The greatest discriminative performance was observed after further inclusion of postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI, resulting in an AUC of 0.821 (ΔAUC = 0.036, P = .337). Although the improvement in AUC did not reach statistical significance, the addition of PCF-cTnI led to a significant improvement in risk reclassification (NRI = 0.562; 95% CI, 0.189-0.921; P = .002). Overall, 56.2% of patients were reclassified into more appropriate risk strata. Specifically, among patients without POAF, 79.8% were reclassified to a lower predicted risk, whereas among patients who developed POAF, 48.6% were reclassified to a higher predicted risk. Comparison of Different Models

A nomogram was constructed on the basis of the β coefficients derived from the predictive model to estimate the probability of POAF (Figure E3). By summing the points assigned to each predictor, a total point can be calculated, providing an estimated risk of POAF. To facilitate rapid clinical application, the nomogram was further simplified into a risk scoring system—the CALT score (Table 2). The optimal cutoff value of the CALT score was 5 points, yielding an overall accuracy of 71.9%, with a sensitivity of 82.9% and a specificity of 67.1%. In clinical practice, points corresponding to each predictor can be summed, and patients with a total score exceeding 5 are classified as being at high risk for POAF. When compared with commonly used risk tools, including the POAF score,3 CHA2DS2-VASc,4 and the atrial fibrillation risk index,24 the CALT score demonstrated superior discriminative ability and better calibration. In addition, the CALT score showed significant improvements in both continuous NRI and integrated discrimination improvement, indicating enhanced predictive performance over existing models (Table 3). In addition, a sensitivity analysis was performed using clinically relevant thresholds for predictors with established reference limits (LAD >51 mm, LVEDD >55 mm in men or >50 mm in women, and serum calcium at 0 h postoperatively <1.10 mmol/L). PCF-cTnI retained its original classification because no universally accepted clinical threshold is currently available. After reconstruction of the score, the optimal CALT cutoff changed modestly from 5 to 6 points. Discussion

This prospective cohort study is the first to evaluate the predictive value of postoperative PCF-cTnI for new-onset POAF after CABG from a clinical translational perspective. We found that cTnI levels in PCF were approximately 39-fold higher than peripheral blood without significant correlation. Postoperative 6-hour PCF-cTnI emerged as a strong independent predictor of POAF, significantly improving risk reclassification. The PCF-cTnI–based model demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility, and the derived CALT score outperformed commonly used clinical risk tools. Importantly, PCF-cTnI measurement is rapid and readily implementable in clinical practice, facilitating timely risk stratification.

POAF is closely linked to local myocardial injury,26 and cTnI is a sensitive biomarker of myocardial damage.13 In the present study, serum cTnI measured at 6 hours postoperatively did not differ significantly between patients with and without POAF, whereas PCF-cTnI levels were markedly elevated in those who developed POAF and remained a strong independent predictor after adjustment for covariates. Previous studies investigating perioperative serum cTnI and POAF after CABG have been limited and yielded inconsistent results. Leal and colleagues27 reported greater serum cTnI levels at admission to the intensive care unit among patients who developed POAF, whereas Xu and colleagues28 found that peak serum cTnI was not an independent predictor for POAF. Similarly, Izadi-Moud and colleagues29 measured serum cTnI levels before, immediately after, and 72 hours after cardiopulmonary bypass and observed no significant differences between patients with and without POAF, despite a greater postoperative increase in patients with POAF. Sezai and colleagues15 also reported no association between postoperative serum cTnI levels and POAF. Consistent with these findings, a systematic review suggested that the association between circulating biomarkers and POAF remains debatable.7 This discrepancy may be explained by plasma reflecting the overall systemic state and lacking specificity, whereas pericardial fluid, because of its proximity to the heart, provides a direct and sensitive window into the local cardiac microenvironment. Several studies have reported that biomarkers in PCF are strongly predictive of POAF, whereas their circulating levels are less informative, further supporting this plausible explanation. Manghelli and colleagues10 observed a significant association between elevated mitochondrial DNA levels in PCF and POAF, with no corresponding association in circulation. Liu and colleagues11 demonstrated that intraoperative PCF-MPO levels were significantly greater in patients who developed POAF, whereas blood MPO levels did not differ between groups. Moreover, our previous prospective clinical studies showed that interleukin-6 and MPO concentrations in postoperative PCF were among the strongest predictors of POAF.9,20 Collectively, these findings indicate that biomarkers in PCF more accurately reflect local cardiac pathophysiology than systemic measurements.30

We observed an interesting phenomenon: postoperative PCF-cTnI levels were markedly greater than peripheral blood levels—approximately 39-fold—and did not show a significant correlation with systemic cTnI. This difference was even more pronounced in the POAF group, with PCF-cTnI levels approximately 64-fold greater. These results are consistent with previous studies reporting substantially greater concentrations of cardiac injury biomarkers in PCF compared with serum after cardiac surgery.31,32 These observations suggest that local myocardial release of cTnI into the pericardial space may represent a major source of PCF-cTnI. A potential mechanistic explanation is that myocardial injury during CABG may lead to the release of intracellular proteins, including troponin, into the pericardial space and may activate pattern-recognition receptors on resident immune cells, thereby initiating a localized inflammatory cascade and generating a pericardial inflammatory milieu characterized by elevated cytokine levels, increased reactive oxygen species production, and enhanced matrix metalloproteinases activation.33 Given its direct contact with the atrial epicardial surface, this inflammatory milieu may contribute to electrophysiological alterations, including impaired gap-junction integrity, shortening of the atrial effective refractory period, and increased conduction heterogeneity. These changes may create a substrate favorable for reentry and triggered activity, thereby facilitating the development of POAF.6 Supporting this hypothesis, posterior pericardiotomy has been reported to reduce the incidence of POAF after cardiac surgery, potentially by facilitating pericardial drainage and reducing the accumulation of inflammatory, oxidative stress-related, or injury-associated mediators within the pericardial cavity.19 However, these mechanisms remain speculative and require further validation.

Beyond PCF-cTnI, the CALT score integrates structural and clinical factors. The associations of enlarged LAD and LVEDD align with existing literature, reinforcing the role of structural remodeling in providing a proarrhythmia substrate.34,35 The inclusion of LCX lesions is intriguing. In our cohort, patients with LCX lesions had a lower prevalence of 3-vessel disease (6.1% vs 12.5%) and a lower incidence of POAF (25.6% vs 43.7%) than those without LCX lesions. This observation may reflect differences in overall coronary artery disease burden between groups and is consistent with previous evidence suggesting an association between coronary disease severity and POAF risk.36 Serum calcium measured at 0 hours postoperatively was also independently associated with POAF, suggesting that early postoperative calcium homeostasis may influence atrial electrophysiological stability and modulate susceptibility to atrial fibrillation.37 The specific mechanisms underlying these associations warrant further investigation. In addition, postoperative PCF volume, CPB duration, and operative time were not significantly associated with PCF-cTnI levels. This is likely attributable to the fact that these variables are not direct markers of myocardial injury and are influenced by multiple perioperative factors, including fluid retention, surgical characteristics,38 thereby limiting their ability to reflect the extent of myocardial injury.

Clinical Translation and Strengths

A main strength of this study is that it is the first to evaluate the predictive value of PCF-cTnI for POAF, as well as the translational potential of a PCF-cTnI–based prediction model. Unlike other PCF-biomarkers (eg, mitochondrial DNA,10 MPO20), cTnI can be measured using standardized, rapid, and widely available assays. By using PCF—routinely collected via existing drainage systems—clinicians can achieve superior risk stratification without additional invasive procedures. The CALT score facilitates identification of patients who may benefit from targeted prophylaxis. In addition, the prospective design with consecutive patient enrollment closely reflects real-world CABG populations. Continuous ECG monitoring for 7 days using bedside monitors, telemetry, and wearable patches minimized outcome misclassification.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this single-center study had a relatively small sample size. To mitigate overfitting risk, we used LASSO regression for variable selection and performed internal validation using 1000 bootstrap resamples. The observed optimism (AUC drop from 0.82 to 0.76) reflects the challenges of model stability in small cohorts. Consequently, the CALT score should currently be considered exploratory research tools. External validation in multicenter cohorts is needed before clinical implementation. Second, residual confounding from unmeasured variables cannot be excluded, including the potential influence of residual blood contamination, because hemoglobin or hematocrit measurements in PCF were not available. Nevertheless, we found no significant correlation between PCF cTnI and blood cTnI levels, suggesting that the primary source of PCF cTnI is likely local myocardial release within the pericardial cavity rather than systemic circulation. Third, inflammatory biomarkers were not measured, and their incremental predictive value warrants investigation. Fourth, preoperative cTnI was not included because it is not routinely measured and has shown inconsistent association with POAF. Fifth, the study population mainly consisted of patients who underwent off-pump CABG, which improved homogeneity but may have limited detection of associations between CPB duration with POAF or PCF-cTnI levels, and may reduce generalizability to other cardiac surgical populations. Finally, the observational nature of this study precludes causal inference. Further experimental studies and randomized controlled trials are needed to establish a causal relationship.

Conclusions

Postoperative PCF-cTnI is a novel and effective predictor of new-onset POAF after CABG. The PCF-cTnI–based model exhibited superior discrimination and calibration compared with commonly used clinical scores, offering a tool with translational potential for risk stratification.

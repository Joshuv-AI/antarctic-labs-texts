ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIntracranial atherosclerosis is the main cause of stroke globally, with acute large vessel occlusive (LVO) stroke being a predominant contributor to stroke-related mortality. In recent years, aspiration thrombectomy (AT) has emerged as a novel therapeutic method for treating acute LVO stroke. The purpose of this study aims to investigate the safety and efficacy of AT alone or combined with stent retriever thrombectomy (SRT) in the treatment of acute LVO strokeMethods and analysisThis is a multicentre and observational real-world study involving patients diagnosed with acute LVO stroke. Participants will be treated with AT alone or combined with SRT. According to the actual annual number of embolectomy in the sub-centre and the research years, the sample size of this study is estimated to be 400 patients, of which 300 patients of anterior circulation lesions and 100 patients of posterior circulation lesions are planned to be recruited, being considered that the incidence of posterior circulation is about 20–25%. Clinical data, including baseline characteristics, intraoperative details, postoperative outcomes and follow-up results, will be systematically collected using an Electronic Data Capture system over a follow-up period of 3 months. The primary efficacy endpoint is the rate of excellent functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score range 0–3) after 90 days, and the successful recanalisation confirmed by digital subtraction angiography. The primary safety outcome is symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage within 48 hours (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score increase ≥4). This study will provide us with powerful guidance for the treatment of acute LVO stroke with different aetiologies.Ethics and disseminationThis study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee on Human Experimentation at Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University (approval number: SWYX:2022–1025). All the participating sites have received the ethics approval. The outcomes will be disseminated through national and international presentations and peer-reviewed publications.Trial registration numberChiCTR2200065172.

Introduction

Stroke has emerged as the leading cause of mortality in China, contributing significantly to the healthcare burden of the society due to its high disability rate.1 According to statistics from 2020, among people over 40 years old in China, the prevalence of stroke was 2.61%, with an incidence rate of 505.23 per 100 000 persons, and a mortality rate of 343.40 per 100 000 persons. Notably, ischaemic stroke accounts for 60–80% of stroke patients.2 The prevalence of intracranial atherosclerosis (ICAS) in China is markedly higher than that in Europe and America. Among stroke patients in hospitals, the proportion of ICAS is as high as 60–70%.3 Furthermore, the proportion of ischaemic stroke caused by ICAS is substantially higher than that in Europe and America, with ICAS responsible for approximately 30% of acute large vessel occlusive (LVO) stroke.4

Endovascular treatment has been increasingly recognised as the most effective intervention for acute LVO stroke, supported by a growing body of evidence.4 In the initial stages of endovascular treatment, stent retriever thrombectomy (SRT) was predominantly used to remove thrombus. However, with the maturity of technology, aspiration thrombectomy (AT) has gradually been used as a viable alternative. Previous studies haveshownd that AT is comparable with SRT in terms of safety and efficacy.58 Given its relatively straightforward procedure, AT may offer a more practical approach for thrombus removal. Nonetheless, several large-scale clinical trials failed to confirm a definitive advantage of AT, potentially due to the lack of comprehensive subgroup analysis,9 especially for diffeaetiologiesogies. So far, no real-world study (RWS) has been conducted in this population.

Real world study refers to the systematic collection and analysis of data from real-world settings, such as medical institutions, families and communities, rather than controlled environments with strict criteria. Compared with randomised controlled trials (RCTs), RWS is more heterogeneous and less restricted on real-world patients, enhancing their relevance to everyday clinical practice.10 The flexible and adaptable design of RWS aligns well with routine medical practice, thereby increasing their practical applicability.10 Additionally, RWS can better reflect the current medical situation, which is conducive to the popularisation of study evidence.11

Therefore, this study will carry out a RWS to evaluate the safety and efficacy of AT alone, or in combination with SRT, for the treatment of acute LVO stroke with various aetiologies.

Methods

Study design

SUCTION (aSpiration based thrombectomy in acUte large vessel oCclusive sTroke with dIfferent etiOlogies: a real-world multiceNter) study is a multicentre, observational RWS involving multiple medical institutions across various provinces in China. This study design is shown in figure 1. Enrolment started in March 2023, and the study is planned to be completed in June 2026.

Study design. CTA, CT angiography; MRA, magnetic resonance angiography.

Inclusion criteria for this study participants include (1) age >18 years old; (2) acute ischaemic stroke; (3) modified Rankin scale (mRS) score range 0–1 before onset; (4) occlusion of internal carotid artery (ICA), M1 segment of middle cerebral artery (MCA), M2 segment of MCA, vertebral artery or basilar artery confirmed by brain CT angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA); (5) Alberta Stroke Programme Early CT Score (ASPECTS) ≥3 confirmed by brain CT/DWI; and (6) within 6 hours of onset or suitable for thrombectomy evaluated by multi-modality imaging with 6–24 hours of onset. For anterior circulation artery occlusion, if the onset occurs within 6–16 hours, multi-modality imaging should meet the DAWN or DEFUSE-3 criteria12 13; if the onset occurs within 16–24 hours, multi-modality imaging should meet the DAWN criteria.12 For posterior circulation artery occlusion, the inclusion window can be extended to within 24 hours of onset.14 15

Exclusion criteria for this study participants include (1) intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) confirmed by brain CT/MRI; (2) chronic occlusion or dissection of offending vessels; (3) women in pregnancy or lactation; (4) iodine contrast medium allergy; (5) high risk of bleeding (platelet concentration <40 000/µl, activated partial thromboplastin time >50 s or prothrombin time/international normalised ratio >3.0) or history of coagulation disorders, systemic bleeding, thrombocytopaenia or neutropaenia; (6) not suitable for thrombectomy because of severe or fatal comorbidity (hepatorenal insufficiency, cardiopulmonary insufficiency, life expectancy of less than 3 months, etc); (7) mental illness, cognitive or emotional disorder; and (8) midline shift confirmed by brain CT/MRI.

Standard of shedding: the participants quit the study at any time for various reasons, such as (1) patients who could not complete thrombectomy for various reasons and (2) patients with incomplete clinical data or imaging data, which affect the judgement of curative effect, are defined as shedding cases. For shedding cases, the investigators contact them by phone or visit and record the reasons for shedding. If the shedding case falls off due to adverse reactions and other reasons, he/she should be treated accordingly. Shedding cases also need to be recorded as completely as possible.

Clinical data collection

The participating medical institutions include Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University, Liaocheng Third People's Hospital, Yongchuan Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, Shengli Oilfield Central Hospital, Chongqing University Three Gorges Hospital, Qingdao Central Hospital of University of Health and Rehabilitation Sciences, Heze Municipal Hospital, Affiliated Hospital of Jining Medical University, Handan First Hospital, Wuxi Xishan People’s Hospital, Shanghai Seventh People’s Hospital, Rizhao Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, The First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong Medical University, The First People’s Hospital of Lianyungang City, Zaozhuang Municipal Hospital and Xuanwu Hospital Capital Medical University. The permissions were obtained from all institutions.

In this study, the electronic data capture (EDC) system is used to collect and input data. All the contents required by the test scheme in the system must be provided. Any missing data must be accompanied by an explanation, with reasons documented in the remarks section. The investigators or coordinators should fill in the study-related data into the EDC system, ensuring that all entries are accurate, complete and timely.

The following clinical parameters will be collected at baseline: basic demographic information (age, sex, smoking and drinking history, family history), medical history (history of present illness, history of past illness, medication history), laboratory examination (blood routine, blood coagulation analysis, blood lipid, liver function, kidney function and blood electrolyte), vital signs, physical examination and neurological examination score (National Institutional Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), mRS and ASPECTS) (table 1). The participants should complete brain CT and CTA/MRA as soon as possible in emergency and complete MR multi-modality imaging if possible or necessary.

The investigators should record the administration and timing of intravenous thrombolysis, thrombectomy method, thrombectomy instruments, operation process, thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (TICI) grade in digital subtraction angiography (DSA), therapeutic drugs, perioperative and postoperative management. Other elements, such as catheter diameter, infarct location and infarct volume, should also be recorded.

The participants should complete a brain CT at 24±12 days after operation and CTA/MRA at 7±2 days after operation to judge the recanalisation of blood vessels. The neurological examinations (NIHSS and Glasgow Coma Score) should be evaluated at 2 hours, 24±12 hours and 7±2 days after operation, and the scores should be recorded in the original medical records or recorded and saved with an interview form. The investigators should record new cerebrovascular events, adverse events (AEs) and deaths since enrolment.

This study includes a follow-up protocol conducted via video or telephone at 90±21 days post-treatment. During the follow-up, assessments will be completed using the NIHSS, mRS, EuroQol Five-Dimension Questionnaire, etc, and to record the condition changes, haemorrhagic transformation events, new cerebrovascular events, deaths and other AEs in time nodes and record drug compliance.

Treatment regimens

Etiological classification of acute LVO stroke is mainly divided into three categories: (1) intracranial atherosclerosis occlusion, (2) artery-to-artery embolism occlusion and (3) cardiac embolism occlusion. Patients with acute LVO stroke with different aetiologies in each centre will be enrolled after clinical and imaging evaluation meets the inclusion criteria and informed consent is obtained. Endovascular treatment will be performed by experienced neurointerventional physicians, and Penumbra ACE68 aspiration catheter (Genesis MedTech) will be used for AT, either alone or in combination with SRT. Further angioplasty may be performed at the discretion of the operator, except in cases where contraindications are present.

The interventional products included Penumbra ACE68 thrombus aspiration catheter and RECO flow restoration stent (Minitech Medical) or other flow restoration stents are used. According to the inclusion criteria, patients will be excluded even if they consent to participate but do not undergo initial treatment with the aspiration catheter Penumbra ACE68. However, patients will be eligible for inclusion if the Penumbra ACE68 catheter can be effectively used for thrombectomy in the M2 segment of MCA. In addition, for patients who meet the indications of recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours after hospital arrival, intravenous thrombolysis can be performed after obtaining informed consent.

Postoperative medical treatment will be carried out according to the treatment methods recommended by the guidelines, mainly including antiplatelet or anticoagulation, management of vascular risk factors, management of complications and other supportive treatments. If the obvious brain oedema causes space-occupying effect and cerebral hernia after operation, decompressive craniotomy can be performed if dehydration drugs are not effective.

Study objectives and endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoint is the rate of excellent functional outcome after 90 days (mRS score range 0–3). The primary imaging outcome is successful recanalisation confirmed by DSA. The secondary endpoints are as follows: mRS score range 0–2 after 90 days; mRS score range 0–1 after 90 days; mRS score changed by 1; NIHSS score decreased ≥8 within 48 hours; NIHSS examination after 90 days; ED-5Q examination after 90 days; proportion of reperfusion in TICI 2b-3 grade for vascular recanalisation; proportion of reperfusion in TICI 2b-3 grade for the first time of AT; average operation time; and average time from successful arterial puncture to recanalisation. The safety outcomes include symptomatic ICH within 48 hours (NIHSS score increase≥4), any ICH within 48 hours; death within 90 days (mRS score=6), recurrent ischaemic stroke within 90 days, re-occlusion of blood vessels within 7 days and complications related to other operations.

Quality control

Data Safety and Monitoring Committee (DSMC) will perform data checking exactly in this study. The primary purpose of the termination is to protect the rights and interests of the patients and to avoid unnecessary economic losses. If one therapy shows a statistically significant difference of efficacy and/or safety over the other, the DSMC has the right to terminate the study unconditionally.

AEs monitoring

AEs are any adverse medical events that occur during the study. All information about AEs should be recorded on the AEs page of the case report. Whether AE is associated with endovascular treatment will be further adjudicated by the principal investigator.

Patient and public involvement

All aspects of this study (development of the research question, study design and conduction of the present study, interpretation of results and editing of the final manuscript for publication) are taking place independent of patient and public involvement. The results will be disseminated to participants by their physicians.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data are presented as frequencies and percentages, while continuous data are described using mean±SD, along with maximum and minimum values. On the basis of descriptive analysis, the likelihood ratio χ2 test is used to compare the counting data between groups; if more than 25% of expected frequencies are below 5, Fisher’s precision probability test is used. Group t test is used to compare the measurement data of normal distribution between groups, whereas the Wilcoxon rank sum tes is used for non-normal distribution data. The effects of baseline characteristics and operation-related factors on the main results are analysed using univariate or multivariate logistic regression models.

The results of this study will be shared through open-access peer-reviewed journals. Additionally, to reach a broader audience, findings will also be shared through social media platforms.

Sample size

According to the actual number of thrombectomy in each sub-centre and the research years, the estimated sample size of this study is 400 patients. Considering that the incidence of posterior circulation lesions is about 20–25%, it is planned to include 300 patients of anterior circulation lesions and 100 patients of posterior circulation lesions. The sample size estimation (n=400) was based on (1) statistical assumptions targeting a 15% absolute difference in the primary outcome (80% power, α=0.05) and (2) adjustment for a 10% dropout rate.

Interim analysis

An interim analysis will be performed on all the enrolled patients who have completed 1 month of follow-up (visit 3). This interim analysis will include safety, effectiveness and treatment patterns of AT or SRT and patients’ characteristics at enrolment, while other variables might also be analysed as applicable.

Ethics and dissemination

The study will be performed in accordance with ethical principles that are consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Conference on harmonisation’s Good Clinical Practice, Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices and the applicable legislation on non-interventional studies and/or observational studies. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee on Human Experimentation at Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University (approval number: SWYX:2022–1025), and all the participating sites have received the ethics approval. Informed consent will be obtained from all the included patients before study initiation. The participant informed consent form can be seen in online supplemental material. The present study is registered with the clinical trials website: https://www.chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2200065172). The outcomes will be disseminated through national and international presentations and peer-reviewed publications.

Discussion

Endovascular treatment has been widely used for acute LVO stroke with different aetiologies. Current guidelines recommend treating LVO stroke in anterior circulation with ASPECTS ≥6 within 6 hours of onset or within 6–24 hours if there is imaging evidence of a mismatch in perfusion.16 For acute LVO stroke in anterior circulation with ASPECTS <6, endovascular treatment still has better curative effect than medical treatment.1719 The latest results of the Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion and Basilar-Artery Occlusion studies also showed that endovascular treatment is obviously superior to medical treatment for acute basilar vessel occlusive stroke.14 15

Several innovative endovascular treatment techniques have recently been developed, including first-line aspiration5 20 and various combination strategies, such as continuous aspiration prior to intracranial vascular embolectomy, stent retriever assisted vacuum-locked extraction, BADDASS BAlloon guide with large bore Distal access catheter with Dual Aspiration with Stent-retriever as Standard approach and Solitaire FR With Intracranial Support Catheter for Mechanical Thrombectomy.2125 AT, a form of endovascular treatment, offers several advantages over SRT.26 Although AT did not increase successful revascularisation rate compared with SRT,5 it has higher rates of successful recanalisation in less operative time and higher additional therapy rates.27 28

The curative effect of endovascular treatment is different in acute LVO stroke with different aetiologies. Several factors, such as the location of vascular occlusion, infarct volume and selection of surgical methods, can significantly influence the outcomes of endovascular interventions. Compared with intracranial arterial embolisation, intracranial in situ thrombus exhibits stronger adhesion to the vessel walls, often requiring more time to remove the thrombus.29 30 In this study, RWS can integrate various influencing factors and comprehensively analyse the safety and efficacy of AT on acute LVO stroke across different aetiologies, which is helpful to accurately screen the beneficiaries and improve the prognosis of patients.

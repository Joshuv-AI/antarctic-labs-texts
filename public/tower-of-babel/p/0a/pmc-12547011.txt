Vocal features based Parkinson’s detection: An ensemble learning approach
Authors: Megha Chakole, Sanjay Dorle, Rahul Agrawal, Priya Dasarwar, Uma Yadav, Rashmi Sharma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractParkinson’s disease (PD) primarily affects the central nervous system. In 2019, over 8.5 million cases were reported, with numbers continuing to rise. This growing prevalence emphasizes the urgent need for early detection and preventive strategies. To resolve this, numerous methods have been introduced, one of them being machine learning technique. By employing deep learning methods on the large-scale datasets, the early prediction and detection of PD is possible. These methods should be precisely evaluated on the basis of vocal features and the best method to predict this neurodegenerative ailment is disclosed. The core objective of this research is to facilitate the medical centers by providing an optimal machine learning technique to early detect PD. In order to decide an ideal method, the renowned machine learning algorithms like Random Forest, K Nearest Neighbor, Naïve Bayes, Gradient Boosting and XGBoost are evaluated according to their performance. Gradient Boosting outperforms earlier results with high recall, low log loss, and overfitting resistance.Vocal features proved to be valuable indicators for early-stage Parkinson’s detection.The Gradient Boosting model has scored the highest in terms of all the mentioned parameters, showing a promising result for predicting the occurrence of PD.Machine learning can play a significant role in supporting clinical diagnosis and decision-making.Keywords: AUC-ROC curve, Machine learning classifier algorithm, Metric analysis, Parkinson disease, Vocal features

Background

The precarious disorder of PD is a deteriorating condition, the root cause for which is unidentified till date. Honoring James Parkinson, this disease reflects a unique name and symptoms as well. This condition affects human progressively slow, which if left unattended, can cause the body to lose the motor control, lose sensory functions, affect the sleep and inter subjective behavior. Parkinson’s disease can be a result of weakened production of dopamine in the body and any tiniest injuries or deaths of the brain cells. Two of the human neurotransmitter enzymes namely dopamine and norepinephrine are the most vital chemicals in regulating the functions in human body [1]. To assess and early detect the Parkinson’s disease, one is expected to work with enormous and complex dataset. Various machine learning algorithms can be utilized to generalize the dataset. These algorithms are optimized in order to provide better solutions for advanced queries [2].

Parkinson's disease originates from a composition of genetic and environmental factors making it a threatening and a neurodegenerative disorder. Due to such factors, there can be anomalous collection of protein cells near the neurons which can further eliminate the brain cells. Parkinsonism is clinically simple to diagnose but to comprehend the other cause for this disease, one is expected to acknowledge a higher suspicion index. Parkinson’s disease can be addressed, and treated at an early stage with the help of adequate medical facilities at present. Future expects extensive medical advancements and the development in pharmaceuticals that offer neuroprotection for Parkinson’s disease [3,4].

The phonetic feature of the speech signal acquired from the data is a useful tool to determine the presence of PD in an individual. The investigators deployed various techniques namely SVM, RF, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and KNN. The highest accuracy in the context of prediction was acquired by MLP with the percentage value of 98.31 %. Being second in rank, SVM achieved an accuracy value of 95 % [5]. The authors in [6] highlighted the importance of boosting of machine learning algorithms for training the classifiers. The latest Boosted Logistic Regression method can be utilized in prediction tasks. The boosting technique enhances the performance of these ML algorithms. PPMI (Parkinson’s Progression Markers Initiative) is source for dataset. The dataset included the information about cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) tests and dopamine transporter imaging, etc. [7].

The researchers highlighted the symptoms such as speech problems, hand or leg tremors, facial rigidity, and postural changes [8]. The study shows the deployment of different feature extraction techniques, employing various classifiers. Using such methods, binary classification was accomplished. To gain a higher accuracy, the data from various motor functions was merged and the important features from both the sides were unified [9]. The authors adapted a deep learning technique for the screening of PD at an initiation phase. This research involved the evaluation of twelve various algorithms for the prediction purpose. They also figured out that introduction of Boosting method in machine learning models enhances the performance [10].

An enormous speech dataset was refined and was utilized by separating the two groups. The two groups were Parkinson’s disease group (PD) and Non-Parkinson’s disease (NPD) groups. This research utilized ML algorithms like RF, KNN and Support Vector Machine. These methods were assessed against Principal Component Analysis (PCA). PCA helped in transfiguring the 26 primary phonetic features into two principal components. This facilitated a clear differentiation between individuals with PD and without PD. The ML method prominently enhanced the F1 score and AUC, and the curve values for ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic) reached upto 1. Among the tested models, the ML method with highest accuracy is Random Forest [11]. The core objective of the research is to evaluate the various ML as well as deep learning models and to assess their performance according to the accuracy and effectiveness of the model to early detect PD [12]. A computerized solution for the early detection of PD is implemented by using the vocal functions isolated from recorded voice data [13,14]. This research highlights the assessment of the importance of Machine Learning in the processing of auditory diagnostics and feature engineering. Previous studies were also refined in the process by introducing an ML approach [15]. Various ML models were trained, compared and evaluated extensively for detecting Parkinson’s disease [16]. The basic objective of the research in [17] was to expose the ML model which is highly precise for PD detection, which will further facilitate the medical experts to prescribe timely solutions beforehand. This research highlights the key factors causing this disorder and the use of various ML models to accurately detect the presence of this disease with an in-depth assessment. This manuscript addresses three critical research gaps in Parkinson's disease (PD) detection using vocal biomarkers: (1) Previous works have focused primarily on single-model approaches (e.g., SVM, RF) without comprehensive comparison of modern ensemble methods, particularly gradient boosting variants. This research paper demonstrates GB's superiority (94.07 % accuracy, 0.419 log loss) through rigorous benchmarking against five algorithms; (2) Existing studies often prioritize technical metrics over clinical utility. Authors bridge this by optimizing for both sensitivity (89 %) and specificity while incorporating clinician-validated feature interpretations; (3) Most of the prior models lack methodological transparency – this research provides complete preprocessing documentation, prevent data leakage through strict train-test isolation. The author’s work uniquely advances the field by developing not just a more accurate model, but one with demonstrated clinical adoption potential through real-time capability. The detailed description of the type and features of the given dataset is discussed in the next section.

Pitch period entropy (PPE)

PPE short for Pitch Period Entropy is a fundamental feature of speech signal which utilized for detection of dysphonia. It plays a vital role in differentiating the patients from two categories: Individuals with PD and non-PD. To detect the level of degradation of fundamental frequency control, PPE deploys a log-based scale. When deployed against the disorienting factors like natural fluctuations in speech signal frequency and background sounds, PPE proves robust and strong in analysis of PD individuals.

Detrended fluctuation analysis (DFA)

It is a crucial feature for the study of speech signals. It prominently investigates broad-range temporal correlations in data dependent on time-series dynamics. DFA is extensively used in electrophysiological recordings. The function of DFA converts the given data into an equally detrended data. It is then dismantled into small fragments of length L. The deviations in the detrended data are then assessed relative to L [19].

Recurrence period density entropy (RPDE)

It is a potent fundamental characteristic utilized to differentiate signal periodicity. For individuals with dysphonia, the given restraints are predicted to be more flexible and permissive because of the generation turbulent airflow in voice channel. RPDE has been in use for detection of fluctuations in voice signals, giving a solid proof to the valuable contribution in identifying various speed disorders in human [20]. Feature preprocessing

This research implemented a rigorously documented preprocessing pipeline to ensure transparency and reproducibility in Parkinson's disease detection from vocal features. The pipeline began with comprehensive data quality checks, including missing value analysis and signal-to-noise ratio verification, followed by feature-specific normalization - using Robust Scaler for entropy-based features (PPE, DFA, RPDE) to mitigate outlier effects while preserving clinical relevance, and Min Max scaling for pulse-related features to maintain proportional relationships. Crucially, all transformations were applied after dataset splitting to prevent data leakage, with separate scaler objects maintained for training (n = 452) and test (n = 112) sets. The collected data was extensive, encompassing medical and personal information of the individuals with the PD and NPD. The training of the ML models was carried out on this gathered data. The models were assessed based on their performances. In order to organize the given set of data for model training, data-preprocessing is carried out. The data was suitably structured throughout the preparation process and the ML algorithms were trained. In preprocessing process, the data is initially fragmented, while further the feature scaling was done. A partition of the present dataset was done and two sets were created, one being evaluation set and another being training set. This facilitates the accurate assessment of performance of the models. This split ensured that the model could recognize patterns from smaller portions of data so that a general conclusion could be reached and a new, unseen data can be unfolded. Additionally, the introduction of feature scaling of dataset was carried out to customize the numerical features of the dataset. This proved to be an important step for comparing all of the features, while any of the feature was not allowed to take over the control due to magnitude differences. To enhance explainability, authors generated summary plots at each processing stage and consulted neurologists to validate clinically meaningful value ranges for all vocal metrics.

Standardization and Normalization, these two are essential tools used for feature scaling. The selection of method of scaling depends upon the feature’s characteristics. The specific requirements of the ML algorithm were taken into account when these methods are studied.

Model training

This research investigated the different ML algorithms namely KNN, NB, RF, GB, and XGBoost. Each one of the algorithms was trained using the processed dataset. For the training phase, only a small and decided portion of data is used.

K-nearest neighbors (KNN)

KNN is a unique method as it utilizes the previous examples for making general predictions. It includes a voting mechanism to decide the nearest data point whenever a new data point is introduced to it. Through voting, it decides the category. A specific and reserved dataset is required for the training of this method. KNN shows the best results when the preprocessed data is clear and has appropriate parameters [5].

Naive Bayes (NB)

It is used to construct various class labels or feature vectors using some finite data. The features in the dataset are assumed as independent, which makes classification of text as one of its applications. The probability of features is estimated by naive bayes by knowing the characteristics of each class beforehand. Conditional probability can also be estimated for a specific class. Naïve Bayes possesses a straightforward nature, but despite of that, NB generates comparable results and can contribute to ML advancements [33].

Gradient boosting (GB)

GB is prominently used for regression tasks and is renowned for its superior predictive analysis. It relies on the principle which aims at decision trees (typically weak learners) to turn them into strong learners. The strong learners in this case are the predictive models. This ML model is highly skilled in training the weak models, focusing primarily on the mistakes done by previous models. To achieve this, GB optimizes the models by training them repeatedly with the help of datasets. The performance of the model is enhanced by the correction in the previously misclassified data points and generating a better efficiency Loss function is reduced in the case of GB, which further leads to the enhancement with training. As an illustration, the numerical values of a regression task are predicted by the mean squared error. The data points are classified into different classes with the help of log loss which is predominantly utilized in classification tasks. GB utilizes boosting method to gain higher accuracy of prediction [25].

XG boost

eXtreme Gradient Boosting is a highly potent ML algorithm designed for regression as well as classification tasks. It works on traditional gradient boosted decision trees for improving the speed and accuracy aspects with the help of advanced techniques. XGBoost prominently trains the weak algorithms to change them into stronger ones by implementing a training method to correct the errors from previous models. It’s core working principle is similar to gradient boosting. The difference between the GB and XGBoost techniques is that XGBoost introduces an additional regularization feature to ease with the overfitting. Also, a parallelization technique is present to enhance speed, and a loss function to increase accuracy [25].

Random forest

Random Forest collects the weak decision trees and brings them together to work on the prediction tasks. Random subset of features is selected and training is done for each tree for the given data. The important role of this random selection process is it facilitates the prevention of overfitting. This makes the model to generalize unseen data. Random Forest algorithm allows each decision tree to independently learn the patterns. The prediction is then made by the models accordingly. Majority voting among the decision tress takes place when the task is regarding classification tasks, and the final prediction is made. While in the case of regression tasks, RF decides the final predictions by averaging. To find out the disorder in the dataset, a new metric is used which is known as The Gini Index. Handling significantly bigger data has become easier with Random Forest. It also gives importance to feature selection [11,24].

Limitations

While this study demonstrates promising results with Gradient Boosting achieving 94.07 % accuracy in Parkinson's disease detection, we acknowledge two key methodological constraints: First, our evaluation relied on a single train-test split rather than k-fold cross-validation. Although this approach is computationally efficient, it may inflate performance metrics due to data partitioning bias. Second, the generalizability of our findings to diverse clinical populations requires further validation, as the dataset—while substantial—represents a specific demographic distribution. These limitations do not invalidate our conclusions but highlight opportunities to strengthen reliability in future work through repeated cross-validation and multi-center data collection.

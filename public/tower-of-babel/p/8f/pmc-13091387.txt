Comment on “Relevance of cerebral microbleeds with leaky blood brain barrier in traumatic brain injury and protective role of interferon-gamma: A cohort study”
Authors: Sangameshwar Manikya, Varshini Vadhithala, Rajnish Kumar, Pankaj Nainwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Semi-quantitative Ktrans modeling may influence leaky CMB classification.•Absolute T1 mapping could improve BBB permeability accuracy.•Lesion location may outweigh lesion count for prognostic value.•Peripheral IFN-γ levels require longitudinal mechanistic validation.•Continuous BBB modeling may refine TBI biomarker stratification.

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the study by To et al. examining the relevance of cerebral microbleeds with blood–brain barrier disruption in traumatic brain injury and the potential protective association of interferon gamma [1]. The integration of susceptibility-weighted imaging with dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging represents an important step toward refining radiologic phenotyping beyond conventional structural lesion burden.

The central inference that leaky cerebral microbleeds, defined by elevated volume transfer constant values derived from Patlak–Gjedde modelling, are more closely aligned with injury severity warrants further scrutiny at the level of quantitative modelling. Because permeability estimates were generated without individualized T1 mapping and relied on tissue-assigned baseline relaxation values, the resulting Ktrans values are semi-quantitative and susceptible to regional hematoma susceptibility artifacts, partial volume effects, and altered hematocrit in the subacute phase. In small foci such as microbleeds, voxel contamination from adjacent normal-appearing brain may influence permeability estimation [2]. This modelling uncertainty carries clinical implications, as severity stratification based on relative permeability thresholds could misclassify lesions that are biologically stable yet technically influenced by acquisition variability [3]. Incorporation of absolute T1 mapping, hematocrit-adjusted vascular input functions, and reproducibility testing would strengthen confidence that permeability differences reflect vascular pathobiology rather than protocol-related variance.

A second consideration concerns lesion-level versus patient-level inference. The analysis emphasizes the number of leaky microbleeds, yet cerebral microbleeds are spatially heterogeneous and frequently involve shear-vulnerable regions including the corpus callosum, brainstem, grey–white matter junction, corona radiata, basal ganglia, thalamus, and periventricular white matter, with the grey–white junction representing a particularly consistent site across TBI severities [4]. The prognostic significance of a microbleed adjacent to eloquent white matter tracts is unlikely to equal that of a peripheral cortical lesion. Emerging TBI-focused imaging studies suggest that lesion topography and involvement of critical white matter pathways are more closely aligned with cognitive and functional outcomes than lesion count alone, indicating that spatial distribution contributes materially to prognostic interpretation. While advanced approaches such as diffusion tensor or connectome-based analyses may further refine this relationship, their acquisition in acute or unstable TBI settings remains operationally challenging and should be interpreted as an extension rather than a prerequisite for current imaging workflows.

The reported association between higher interferon gamma levels and fewer microbleeds in the subacute phase also merits mechanistic refinement. Interferon gamma exerts pleiotropic effects, including modulation of endothelial integrity and microglial activation. Peripheral cytokine concentrations do not necessarily reflect intracerebral signaling, particularly when blood–brain barrier permeability is regionally heterogeneous [4]. Studies in traumatic brain injury have demonstrated discordance between circulating and cerebrospinal fluid cytokine profiles, reflecting compartmentalized immune responses rather than direct systemic-to-central correspondence [5,6], reinforcing caution in extrapolating peripheral IFN-γ as a surrogate of intracerebral activity. Moreover, correlation within the day 8+ subgroup does not establish temporal directionality; higher interferon gamma may represent a compensatory immune phenotype in patients with less microvascular injury rather than a direct protective mediator as seen in animal models [7]. Serial cytokine measurements analyzed with longitudinal modelling and parallel cerebrospinal fluid assessment would help determine whether interferon gamma trajectories precede stabilization of permeability metrics or simply accompany recovery.

Finally, the dichotomization of microbleeds into leaky and non-leaky categories assumes a binary vascular state, whereas blood–brain barrier dysfunction after traumatic brain injury likely exists along a continuum influenced by endothelial, pericytic, and extracellular matrix alterations [8]. While such dichotomization may provide operational stability in small and temporally heterogeneous cohorts, alternative approaches such as modelling Ktrans as a continuous variable, stratification into quantiles, or incorporation of lesion volume–permeability composites may offer greater biological resolution without substantially increasing analytical instability [9,10].

By advancing multiparametric imaging of microvascular injury, this study contributes meaningfully to biologically informed stratification in traumatic brain injury. Further refinement of quantitative permeability modelling, spatial network contextualization, and longitudinal immune profiling may enhance the clinical applicability of leaky microbleeds as biomarkers for prognosis and therapeutic targeting.

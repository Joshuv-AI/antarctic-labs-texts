Knowledge Mapping of Alport Syndrome: A Bibliometric Analysis From 2000 to 2025
Authors: Xiujuan Cao, Wei Zhou, Yong Wang, Lvxian Wu, Jianhua Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAlport syndrome (AS) is a multisystem hereditary disorder characterized by persistent hematuria, progressive renal insufficiency, sensorineural hearing loss, and ocular abnormalities. Its pathogenesis is mainly due to mutations in COL4A3, COL4A4, or COL4A5 genes encoding type IV collagen α chains, leading to glomerular dysfunction and end‐stage renal disease. A systematic evaluation of its global research landscape is lacking, and bibliometric analysis can fill this gap.MethodsA comprehensive bibliometric analysis was conducted using Biblioshiny, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace. Introduction

Alport syndrome (AS) is a progressive, inherited renal disorder characterized by a gradual decline in kidney function, sensorineural hearing loss, and a range of ocular abnormalities (Gregorio et al. 2023; Warady et al. 2020). The condition arises from pathogenic variants in the COL4A3, COL4A4, and COL4A5 genes, which encode the α3, α4, and α5 chains of type IV collagen, respectively (Gross et al. 2017; Nakanishi et al. 1994; Kashtan 1999). Approximately 85% of AS cases follow an X‐linked dominant inheritance pattern due to mutations in the COL4A5 gene, while the remaining 15% are attributed to autosomal recessive or autosomal dominant forms associated with mutations in either COL4A3 or COL4A4 (Bruni et al. 2019). The prevalence of AS has been reported to range from 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 5000 people. It accounts for approximately 0.5% of new adult cases and 12.9% of pediatric cases of end‐stage renal disease (ESRD) (Hattori et al. 2015; Nozu et al. 2019). However, a recent analysis of a sequencing database of individuals with unknown kidney disease predicted that pathogenic COL4A5 variants were at least 1 in 2320 in the population, and heterozygous predicted pathogenic COL4A3 or COL4A4 variants were at least 1 in 106 in the population, which led to an underestimation of the prevalence of AS (Gibson et al. 2021; Torra et al. 2025). AS is recognized as the second most prevalent monogenic cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD), following autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (Martínez‐Pulleiro et al. 2021).

Type IV collagen is a critical structural scaffold in all basement membranes, including those of the glomerulus, lens, and cochlea. The six α chains (α1–α6) give rise to three distinct triple‐helical heterotrimers—α1α1α2, α3α4α5, and α5α5α6—via trimeric polymerization, a highly specific molecular assembly process (Abrahamson et al. 2009; Khoshnoodi et al. 2008). In the glomerular basement membrane (GBM), the α3α4α5 network is uniquely produced by podocytes, establishing these cells as central contributors to GBM integrity and key determinants in the pathogenesis of AS (Wiggins 2007). As terminally differentiated cells with minimal proliferative potential, podocytes are particularly vulnerable to irreversible depletion. Once substantial podocyte loss occurs, the structural and functional continuity of the renal filtration barrier is disrupted, triggering progressive glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis. This cascade of pathological changes commonly culminates in end‐stage renal disease during adolescence or early adulthood, often requiring lifelong dialysis or kidney transplantation (Kruegel et al. 2013; Daga et al. 2018; Ding et al. 2017; Zheng et al. 2024). Given the irreversible nature of podocyte loss and the subsequent decline in renal function, early diagnosis and prompt therapeutic intervention are essential to delay disease progression and improve long‐term clinical outcomes in individuals with AS.

Bibliometric analysis serves as a powerful method for evaluating the development trends and scholarly impact of scientific publications within a specific discipline, utilizing quantitative and statistical techniques to identify core research themes, prolific authors, influential journals, and patterns of international collaboration (Luo et al. 2022; Lunny et al. 2022; Royle et al. 2013; Agarwal et al. 2016). To date, no dedicated bibliometric study has been conducted in the field of AS. This paper fills this gap by performing a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of relevant publications and existing research in the AS domain, offering an in‐depth overview of academic productivity and the current research landscape. By mapping key research areas, tracing the evolution of domain‐specific knowledge, and identifying emerging trends, this study provides a foundational reference for future scholarly endeavors.

Materials and Methods

Data Origin and Search Process

Ethical approval was not required for the bibliometric analysis of previously published literature. All relevant publications on AS were retrieved from the Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection database, a comprehensive and multidisciplinary research platform widely recognized for its extensive coverage of high‐impact scholarly journals across the natural sciences, social sciences, arts, and humanities (Sevinc 2004). Due to its rigorous selection of data sources, robust citation indexing capabilities, and advanced analytical tools, WoS is regarded as a premier resource for conducting bibliometric studies. It serves as an essential instrument for in‐depth scientific inquiry, offering researchers a reliable framework for navigating the vast landscape of academic knowledge.

To ensure the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the search results, the following retrieval strategy was employed: TS = (“Alport syndrome” OR “Hereditary nephritis” OR “Hereditary progressive nephritis” OR “Familial nephropathy” OR “Oculo‐Oto‐Renal Syndrome” OR “Thin basement membrane nephropathy”). The search was limited to the “SCI‐EXPANDED” and “SSCI” indexes, with a publication period ranging from January 1, 2000, to December 31, 2025. Only English‐language publications classified as “Articles” or “Reviews” were included (see Table 1). All searches were conducted on the same date—March 15, 2025—to minimize potential variations in citation counts due to differing update times. From an initial pool of 2149 publications, a more focused set was selected based on relevance and scholarly significance. Publications were required to clearly report original research or review articles specifically related to AS. Following screening, 46 proceeding papers, 13 early access articles, 439 meeting abstracts, 55 letters, 3 news items, and 19 corrections were excluded. Additionally, 100 editorial materials, 11 book chapters, and 258 articles deemed unrelated to the topic were removed. After this systematic exclusion process, a final set of 1205 publications was retained, comprising 1016 original research articles and 189 review articles.

Data Analysis and Research Methods

The data in this study were analyzed using the online tool “Results Analysis” on WOS, along with Biblioshiny, VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1). For literature indexed in WOS, multidimensional analyses of thousands of search results were conducted using WOS's “Results Analysis” function, which enables rapid generation of visualizations containing key information such as research domain, publication year, document type, institutional affiliation, funding agencies, authors, countries, languages, journals, publishers, and open access status. Additionally, the “Citation Report” feature of WOS was utilized to collect bibliometric indicators, including the total number of publications, cumulative citations, average citations per publication, h‐index, and annual trends in publication and citation counts.

Biblioshiny (version 4.1.3), a dynamic and interactive bibliometric analysis platform built on the R programming language and Shiny framework, was employed to visualize and analyze bibliographic data (Lunny et al. 2022; Royle et al. 2013). The raw data files of the retrieved publications were imported into Biblioshiny to extract detailed information, including the number of sources, documents, reference keywords, and authorship details. This tool facilitated comprehensive analyses such as author productivity over time, identification of the most influential and frequently cited sources, and thematic mapping of keywords. Sankey diagrams illustrating relationships among authors, countries, and institutions were generated. Furthermore, citation metrics—including citation frequency, h‐index, and co‐citation networks—were extracted to evaluate the scholarly impact of individual articles, authors, and journals.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), developed by Nees Jan van Eck and Ludo Waltman at the Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University, Netherlands, is a robust software tool designed for constructing and visualizing bibliometric networks (van Eck and Waltman 2010; Arruda et al. 2022). Equipped with an advanced visualization engine and an intuitive user interface, VOSviewer produces clear and aesthetically refined network maps suitable for large‐scale data analysis. It supports various analytical functions, including co‐occurrence, collaboration, co‐citation, and bibliographic coupling analyses, with clustering capabilities and the calculation of Total Link Strength (TLS), a critical metric reflecting the intensity of connections within networks.

CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1) is a well‐established scientific knowledge mapping tool widely used in bibliometric and scientometric research (Synnestvedt et al. 2005). It enables researchers to uncover the intellectual structure, foundational theories, and emerging trends within a given field. By generating visual knowledge graphs, CiteSpace was used to examine collaborative patterns among countries, institutions, and authors in the study of AS, identify pivotal references with strong citation bursts, detect potential emerging topics, and support interdisciplinary research initiatives.

As illustrated in Figure 1, a systematic methodology was adopted for literature retrieval and result evaluation. Given that this study relied solely on publicly accessible data, ethical approval from an institutional review board was not required.

From January 1, 2000 to December 31, 2025, a total of 1205 articles on AS were retrieved and screened from the Web of Science database, comprising 1016 original research articles and 189 review articles. Between 2000 and 2025, the annual publication output on AS reached its peak in 2025 with 102 articles (accounting for 8.46% of the total). From 2000 to 2016, annual publications consistently remained below 50, indicating that research in this area was primarily in an exploratory phase. Between 2017 and 2019, the number of annual publications fluctuated around 50. However, from 2020 to 2022 and again from 2024 to 2025, annual outputs surpassed 70, demonstrating a stepwise increase despite a decline observed in 2023. Overall, these trends suggest sustained and expanding scholarly interest in AS‐related research (see Figure 2B).

Citation analysis serves as a valuable method for assessing the influence and impact of academic publications within a given field. In academia, citation counts are widely regarded as indicators of research quality and scholarly impact (Sevinc 2004; Kulkarni et al. 2009). According to the Web of Science citation report, a total of 15,193 citations were recorded, including 1090 self‐citations and 14,103 non‐self‐citations, with the latter accounting for 92.82% of the total. The retrieved articles were cited 34,314 times overall, of which 12,224 (35.62%) were self‐citations and 22,090 (64.38%) were external citations. On average, each article received 28.45 citations. As illustrated in Figure 3, the average annual citation count generally increased from 2000 to 2025. This upward trend in annual average citations further underscores the growing prominence of AS research. In Figure 3, the annual citation count is represented by a purple line, showing a steady rise over the study period. Collectively, although research activity on AS was relatively limited before 2015, cumulative publication numbers, average annual citations, and yearly citation counts indicate substantial growth in this field over the past 10 years.

Judy Savige and Oliver Gross shared the top position with an h‐index of 29, while Jeffrey H. Miner ranked third (h‐index = 26), and Clifford E. Kashtan placed fourth (h‐index = 22). Among all authors, 37 had an h‐index exceeding 10. The m‐index serves as an indicator of the sustainability of a researcher's academic influence over time (Hirsch 2005). In the m‐index ranking, Tomohiko Yamamura ranked first (m‐index = 1.273), followed by Naoya Morisada (m‐index = 1.154). Constantinos Deltas achieved the third‐highest score (m‐index = 1.105), and Tomoko Horinouchi ranked fourth (m‐index = 1.100). The g‐index is another important measure of scholarly influence, placing greater emphasis on highly cited publications (Ali 2021). Co‐citation analysis is a method used to assess the strength of relationships between academic documents. Rather than focusing on individual publications, it aims to uncover the macro‐level knowledge structure of a research domain by examining patterns of co‐citation among large sets of documents (González‐Alcaide et al. 2016). Author co‐citation analysis, in particular, helps identify collaboration networks, research trends, and academic influence based on citation patterns. Using VOSviewer, Figure 3B visualizes the associations among cited authors. With a minimum threshold of 70 citations per author, a total of 60 authors were included and grouped into three clusters. The analysis revealed that Clifford E. Kashtan was the most influential and collaborative author, receiving 19,074 citations and achieving a TLS of 14,729. Judy Savige, with 913 citations, ranked second in citation count and was prominently positioned in Cluster 2 with a TLS of 11,611. Oliver Gross ranked third in citations (820) with a TLS of 10,994. Additionally, other frequently cited authors (with over 200 citations) include Jean‐Philippe Jais (TLS = 8728), Jeffrey H. Miner (TLS = 5374), David F. Barker (TLS = 5218), Billy G. Hudson (TLS = 4801), Dominic Cosgrove (TLS = 4740), Laurence Heidet (TLS = 4768), Henny H. Lemmink (TLS = 4164), and Raghu Kalluri (TLS = 3508).

The co‐cited journals were grouped into five clusters: Cluster 1 (red) comprised 22 journals, Cluster 2 (green) included 21 journals, Cluster 3 (blue) contained 12 journals, Cluster 4 (yellow) consisted of 9 journals, and Cluster 5 (purple) encompassed 8 journals. The most frequently cited journals included Kidney International and Journal of the American Society of Nephrology in Cluster 2, and Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation and Journal of Biological Chemistry in Cluster 1.

Keywords Plus refers to supplementary indexing terms automatically generated by citation databases to enhance search accuracy and retrieve additional relevant literature. These terms encompass synonyms, related concepts, and associated topics, thereby broadening the scope of information retrieval (Wei et al. 2024). The thematic map generated via Biblioshiny comprises four quadrants: the first quadrant represents niche themes (highly developed but low centrality); the second, motor themes (high development and high centrality); the third, emerging or declining themes (low development and low centrality); and the fourth, basic themes (low development but high centrality). As illustrated in Figure 8B, the research hotspots in the field of Alport syndrome are concentrated in the motor themes (Alport syndrome, proteinuria), which are characterized by high centrality and density and serve as the core engine driving the development of the discipline. Meanwhile, basic themes such as mutations and type IV collagen are closely related to the field but require further maturation, offering potential for innovations in molecular mechanism research. Niche themes (e.g., natural history, genotype–phenotype correlations) and peripheral themes (e.g., renal failure, hematuria) constitute the supplementary and extended components of the research landscape.

CiteSpace was employed to identify the top 20 most cited keywords over the past 25 years (Figure 8C). Through burst detection analysis, the duration and intensity of keyword citation bursts were extracted. Most citation bursts initiated in 2000 (7 out of 20 events, 35%), followed by 2022 (3 events, 15%), while 2013 saw 2 bursts (10%). The strongest burst occurred for the keyword “hereditary nephritis” (strength = 18.98), active from 2000 to 2011, underscoring its foundational role in early Alport syndrome (AS) research. Meanwhile, the keyword “identification” (strength = 17.11, 2000–2005) also exhibited a high‐intensity early burst, highlighting its fundamental importance in disease and gene detection studies. Since 2012, keywords such as “basement membrane nephropathy” (strength = 14.25, 2012–2019) and “focal segmental glomerulosclerosis” (strength = 12.83, 2013–2019) have experienced strong citation bursts, indicating growing research attention to these clinical phenotypes. More recently, bursts for “digenic inheritance,” “variants,” and “guidelines” have emerged, reflecting a shift toward precision genetics and standardized clinical practice in the field.

Discussion

AS, caused by genetic variations affecting type IV collagen coding, is characterized primarily by renal symptoms, including hematuria and progressive renal failure. This disorder can also manifest with hearing loss from inner ear dysfunction and ocular issues such as anterior lenticonus and retinal changes, including pigmentation alterations and thinning (Ahmed et al. 2013; Kashtan et al. 2018; Noël 2000). It can be divided into three types according to different inheritance patterns: X‐linked Alport syndrome (XLAS), autosomal recessive Alport syndrome (ARAS), and autosomal dominant Alport syndrome (ADAS) (Bullich et al. 2018). Patients with XLAS, particularly males, are associated with a grave renal prognosis, as nearly 90% will progress to ESRD before the age of 30, while 18% of female carriers experience ESRD after age 40. For ARAS patients, the median age of onset of renal failure is 22.5 years (Jais et al. 2000, 2003; Wang et al. 2012). It is widely recognized that, currently, there is no radical or etiologically targeted therapy for AS; existing treatment strategies primarily focus on slowing disease progression and managing complications (Pedrosa et al. 2021). Therefore, early diagnosis and effective intervention are of great significance, which can help delay the progression to renal failure and preserve kidney function.

This study aimed to conduct a systematic bibliometric analysis of AS using VOSviewer, Biblioshiny, and CiteSpace software. A comprehensive collection of articles published between 2000 and 2025 was compiled for scientometric evaluation. After screening for relevance and eligibility, a final dataset comprising 1205 publications was analyzed. Annual publication output remained relatively stable from 2000 to 2016, followed by a gradual increase starting in 2017. From 2020 onward, a marked surge in research productivity was observed, culminating in a peak in 2025. Citation data extracted from the WOS were utilized to assess the academic impact and significance of scholarly contributions within this domain. The total number of citations across all included articles reached 34,314, with an average of 28.45 citations per article. Over the past 25 years, citation counts have exhibited a general upward trajectory, despite a minor decline observed in 2015.

The evolution of AS research over the past quarter‐century not only reflects growing clinical and scientific interest in this genetic disorder but also underscores the substantial academic and industrial value generated by advancements in the field. This body of work has established a robust knowledge foundation that supports ongoing investigations into disease mechanisms, diagnostic innovations, therapeutic development, and clinical translation. Although citation trends show occasional fluctuations, the overall increasing pattern confirms the sustained academic recognition of research outcomes in AS. The continuous growth in scholarly engagement and knowledge dissemination highlights the long‐term vitality and enduring intellectual contribution of this research domain.

Our study included 5950 authors and encompassed 1205 published papers. The top five most productive authors were Judy Savige (58 articles), Oliver Gross (56 articles), Kandai Nozu (48 articles), Jie Ding (39 articles), and Jeffrey H. Miner (39 articles). Among them, Judy Savige and Oliver Gross are the only authors with an h‐index of 29 and a publication start year (PY‐start) of 2000, which positions them as recognized core leaders in the field. Judy Savige's research primarily focuses on diagnostic criteria and phenotypic heterogeneity. In 2013, she led a global initiative involving experts from 12 countries to develop the first consensus statement on the management of AS and thin basement membrane nephropathy, published in the Journal of the American Society of Nephrology (Savige et al. 2013). Additionally, her work has highlighted the unique clinical risks faced by female patients (Savige et al. 2016). Oliver Gross's research centers on the relationship between therapeutic interventions and genotype–phenotype correlations. In 2000, he coordinated a consortium of 19 European countries to analyze 195 families with XLAS (COL4A5 mutations), establishing a model linking mutation types to disease prognosis (Jais et al. 2000, 2003). In 2012, as first author, he led a multicenter cohort study titled “angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI) Early Intervention,” evaluating the safety and efficacy of Ramipril in early treatment for pediatric AS patients (Gross, Friede, et al. 2012). As a Chinese researcher, Jie Ding likely specializes in the genotypic characteristics of AS among Chinese patients, positioning him as a regional leader (Wang et al. 2002). Furthermore, Kandai Nozu and Oliver Gross exhibited the highest link strength among co‐authors, indicating a close collaborative relationship with the other members of the research team.

A total of 66 countries are actively engaged in research related to AS. The United States, Germany, England, Australia, and France constitute the top five contributing nations. Notably, the United States ranks first across all four dimensions—publication output, influence, quality, and international collaboration—and serves as both a “research hub” and a “knowledge center” in the field of AS. Among the top 10 institutions globally, five are located in North America: four in the United States (the University of Minnesota, the University of Washington, Vanderbilt University, and Harvard University) and one in Canada (the University of Toronto). The University of Melbourne and the University of Minnesota ranked the top two in terms of the number of articles published, with more than 60 articles, and the total citation and cooperation intensity were at the forefront, which were the core institutions with the largest scale and most active cooperation in this field. Although Harvard University produced only 23 articles, it stands out as the “quality benchmark,” with an average citation rate of 97.83 per article—the highest among all institutions—indicating that its individual article impact is two to three times greater than that of other leading institutions. Peking University was the sole Chinese institution represented in the top 10, ranking fifth with 35 publications. However, its total citations (1285) and average citations per article (36.71) placed it in the middle range within this group, suggesting that while a solid foundation in research output has been established, further breakthroughs and enhanced international recognition remain areas for improvement.

The thematic focus of the top 10 most cited papers centers predominantly on the “etiology, clinical features, and treatment and management” of AS. Among these, four studies are grounded in research on type IV collagen and basement membrane biology, elucidating novel functions of type IV collagen and establishing a foundation for interdisciplinary investigations into AS. Another four articles concentrate on three pivotal clinical aspects—natural history, therapeutic strategies, and standardization—providing direct guidance for clinical practice. The most frequently cited publication is a 2007 article by R. C. Wiggins, which introduced a paradigm shift by situating AS within the broader “podocyte disease spectrum” and integrating it into a unified framework of glomerular disorders. This contribution represents a landmark advancement in the field. R. C. Wiggins proposed that podocyte depletion may contribute to progressive renal function decline, suggesting that early interventions aimed at preserving podocytes could extend renal survival in patients with AS (Wickman et al. 2016). Oliver Gross (Germany) co‐authored papers ranked 5th, 7th, 9th, and 10th, covering key areas including the natural history of AS, clinical guidelines, and ACEI therapy, positioning him as a leading figure in clinical research. His work demonstrated that early initiation of ACE inhibitors in AS patients delays the onset of renal failure and prolongs life expectancy in a time‐dependent manner (Gross, Licht, et al. 2012). Furthermore, recent studies led by Oliver Gross indicate that sodium‐glucose cotransporter‐2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), when used as adjunctive therapy to renin‐angiotensin system inhibition (RASi), may reduce albuminuria in AS patients (Boeckhaus et al. 2024). Additionally, Jin‐Ju Kim et al. reported that ezetimibe may serve as a promising therapeutic agent targeting mitochondrial dysfunction and dyslipidemia associated with Alport syndrome (Kim et al. 2024). In recent years, emerging therapies such as gene replacement approaches are under investigation, offering the potential to address the underlying basement membrane defects caused by COL4A5 gene mutations and heralding new prospects for curative treatment (Chavez et al. 2022; Boudko et al. 2022; Tavakolidakhrabadi et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2024).

A total of 371 sources were included in the 1205 articles analyzed. Among these, Kidney International (Impact Factor: 12.6, JCR Q1) emerged as the most frequently cited journal, with a primary focus on the research category of Urology & Nephrology. It published 55 articles, primarily addressing clinical translation, innovative mechanisms, and prognosis prediction, indicating its leading role and significant influence in advancing AS research and its prominence among academic scholars. Pediatric Nephrology (Impact Factor: 2.6; JCR rankings: Pediatrics Q1, Urology & Nephrology Q2) ranked highest in terms of total publications, with research emphasis on the clinical characteristics of pediatric AS, drug safety in children, and impacts on growth and development. This reflects growing global scholarly interest in childhood‐onset AS.

A total of 93 keywords related to AS research were retrieved from literature published between 2000 and 2025, with inclusion criteria requiring a minimum occurrence frequency of 20. Through co‐occurrence network analysis and keyword clustering, this study identified four distinct thematic clusters within the AS research domain, among which Cluster 1 contained the largest number of keywords (n = 38). Cluster 1 primarily focused on the pathological mechanisms and therapeutic interventions associated with type IV collagen abnormalities. For instance, Dominic Cosgrove and Jacob Madison elucidated the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying the initiation and progression of glomerular lesions in AS, highlighting that podocyte injury and disruption of the GBM structure result from alterations in basement membrane composition (Cosgrove and Madison 2022). Additionally, Damien Noone and Christoph Licht investigated the pathogenic transition from “collagen deficiency” to “multi‐cell, multi‐pathway synergistic induction of kidney injury,” as well as the evolution of treatment strategies from symptomatic management to precision‐targeted therapies (Noone and Licht 2013). In summary, the core pathogenesis of AS involves GBM collapse due to mutations in type IV collagen genes; however, certain mechanistic details remain incompletely understood and continue to be a major focus of current research. Cluster 2 centered on the COL4A gene family, associated nephropathies, pediatric populations, and potential intervention targets, encompassing 27 keywords. For example, Catherine Quinlan et al. examined the association between COL4A gene variants and various basement membrane‐related nephropathies, characterizing the genotype–phenotype correlations across different COL4A mutations (Quinlan and Rheault 2021). Selasie Goka et al. conducted the first single‐center, long‐term follow‐up study focusing on female patients, revealing the disease progression pattern in females from childhood through adulthood (Goka et al. 2021). This work prompted experts such as Judy Savige to develop standardized management protocols and clinical guidelines for female carriers (Savige et al. 2022). Clusters 3 and 4 focused on natural history, gene identification, and genotype–phenotype associations, comprising 20 and 8 keywords, respectively. To date, the clinical phenotype of AS has been recognized as being determined by the underlying pathogenic mechanism—including the presence of mutations in COL4A3, COL4A4, or COL4A5 and their specific types—leading to increasing research interest in the natural history and genotype–phenotype correlations, particularly in patients with XLAS. In 2003, the European Community Alport Syndrome Concerted Action initiated a comprehensive investigation into the natural history and genotype–phenotype relationships in female XLAS carriers (Jais et al. 2003). Similarly, Tomohiko Yamamura et al. analyzed the natural history and genotype–phenotype correlations in 275 genetically confirmed women with XLAS and 61 family members of symptomatic women presumed to carry mutations (Yamamura et al. 2017). Tomoko Horinouchi et al. further explored abnormal splicing detection methods for the COL4A5 gene in XLAS and assessed the clinical implications of splice‐site mutations (Horinouchi et al. 2018).

Combined with keyword co‐occurrence and citation burst analysis, the research trajectory of AS from 2000 to 2025 showed a clear phased evolution. In the early stage (2000–2006), the research centered on the analysis of basic genetic and molecular characteristics, focusing on the identification of pathogenic genes and the elaboration of glomerular basement membrane structural abnormalities, which laid a molecular foundation for subsequent studies. During the middle stage (2007–2014), the research focus shifted to clinical pathological features and renal disease progression, concentrating on phenotypic heterogeneity, renal injury progression, and complications, thus building a bridge between basic research and clinical practice. Since 2015, the field has entered the stage of precision genetics, standardized clinical management, and translational intervention. The discovery of the digenic inheritance model has broken through the traditional monogenic inheritance cognition, the improvement of clinical guidelines has promoted the standardized management of patients, and the application of renin‐angiotensin‐aldosterone system inhibitors such as Ramipril has become a key research focus for delaying disease progression. Despite the remarkable progress achieved in AS research, there are still obvious deficiencies. First, the innovation of therapeutic methods is insufficient: Ramipril is the only major drug‐related research hotspot, and studies on cutting‐edge directions such as new targeted therapies and gene therapy are scarce. Second, research on long‐term prognosis and patients' quality of life is inadequate; existing studies mostly focus on short‐term renal function indicators, while long‐term follow‐up and assessment of psychosocial outcomes are neglected. Third, precision and individualized research is lacking, and the genotype–phenotype correlation and subtype‐specific management strategies still need to be improved. Fourth, research on extra‐renal manifestations is insufficient, and common complications such as hearing impairment and ocular lesions have not become research hotspots. Fifth, epidemiological and health economic studies are scarce, which restricts the formulation and optimization of public health prevention and control strategies for the disease.

To date, no bibliometric analysis of AS has been conducted. This study employs three distinct visualization tools to examine research trends and collaborative patterns among authors, organizations, and countries within the field. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the data were sourced exclusively from the Science Citation Index Expanded and the Social Sciences Citation Index, both components of the WOS database. While WOS is widely regarded as the most frequently used database in scientometric research due to its robust analytical functionalities, it does not comprehensively index all relevant journals. Consequently, the absence of certain publications in WOS may compromise the completeness of the analysis. Moreover, since only English‐language journals were included, the generalizability of the findings to non‐English scholarly contexts is limited. Second, potential selection bias may affect the results. The process of data selection in scientometric studies inherently involves subjective judgments—such as decisions regarding the inclusion or exclusion of specific records—which may introduce bias and influence the validity of the outcomes. Therefore, it is crucial to establish rigorous screening criteria and, where possible, involve multiple experts during the data curation phase. Furthermore, despite the use of three advanced analytical tools, scientometric approaches often rely on simplified models to represent complex academic landscapes, which may lead to oversimplification or misinterpretation of nuanced phenomena. In conclusion, as scientometric methodologies continue to evolve, the field of AS is expected to benefit from increasingly accurate and comprehensive analyses.

Conclusion

Biblioshiny, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace were used in this study to conduct a bibliometric analysis of the literature published in the AS field from 2000 to 2025 in the WOS database. We analyzed and visualized the literature in the field of AS from multiple perspectives, such as cited reports, authors, references, sources, keywords, etc., which was helpful to summarize the current hotspots in this field and gain insight into possible future research directions. Future studies of AS should focus on genetic pathogenesis, especially on prognostic differences between different inheritance patterns. The simultaneous establishment of standardized international guidelines for the classification and management of AS is essential to ensure a consistent treatment approach. In addition, great attention should be paid to early and effective drug interventions, such as Ramipril, to delay the progression of renal failure, maintain the stability of renal function, and improve the long‐term quality of life of patients. In summary, this study provides support for scholars seeking academic collaboration, provides new insights for clinicians and researchers, and provides new directions for the development of the field.

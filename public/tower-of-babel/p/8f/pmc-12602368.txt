Dear Editor,

This letter is in response to the letter to the editor submitted titled “A Prospective Study to Assess the Profile and Outcome of Acute Paraquat Poisoning in a Tertiary Care Hospital of West Bengal”. We thank Dr Mohan Kumar Hanumanthappa and Dr Saurabh Chandrabhan Sharda for their interest in this article and highly appreciate the valuable comments. We would like to address the concerns raised in the given paper.

Firstly, as rightly pointed out in the letter, we classified our key outcomes, i.e., AKI, ALI, and ARDS as per KDIGO, O'Grady, and Berlin Definition respectively.1–3 We did not mention it separately as these are already accepted worldwide.

Secondly, there was no missing data regarding the amount of poison consumption; we recorded information obtained from either patients or their legally acceptable representatives. We dichotomized the amount of paraquat consumption at >10 mL as the literature source and previous studies have shown that the lethal dose is about 10–15 mL.4–6

Thirdly, we only stated that a positive urine dithionite test in all tested cases corroborated the clinical presentation. We did not claim about its prognostic significance. Rather, we mentioned as limitation in the article, our inability to comment on its utility as mortality predictor as we could not perform serial urine testing due to logistic issues.

Finally, we already mentioned in our article that being a single tertiary-care center study, treatment outcomes have limited generalizability for primary and secondary healthcare settings.

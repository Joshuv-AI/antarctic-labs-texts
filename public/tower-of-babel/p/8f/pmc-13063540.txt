Impact of centralized volume-based procurement of high-value medical consumables on patients’ hospitalization expenses in Gansu, China: an interrupted time series analysis
Authors: Yajie Zhang, Feihan Shen, Qiyin Luo, Zixuan Gan, Zhenjie Lian, Xuping Song, Kehu Yang, Howard White
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHigh prices of medical consumables pose a major barrier for patients. To address those inflated prices, the National Healthcare Security Administration of China implemented the Centralized Volume-Based Procurement policy (CVBP) in 2021. However, evidence assessing the effect of CVBP in Gansu is insufficient. This study aims to estimate the impact of CVBP for high-value medical consumables in Gansu on the hospitalization expenses.MethodsData from 8076 inpatients in Gansu who underwent surgeries involving coronary stents, artificial joints, or intraocular lenses between April 2019 and October 2024 were collected. An interrupted time series analysis (ITSA) was conducted to examine the effect of CVBP on patient hospitalization expenses. An autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model was used to assess the difference between the predicted and actual expenses after the implementation of CVBP.Results(i) After implementation, the material expense per patient who underwent surgeries involving artificial joints was reduced by 387.782 Chinese yuan (CNY) (95%CI: −592.583 to −182.981, p < 0.001) per capita per month. The material expense of patients decreased immediately by 18,668.870 CNY (95%CI: −24,241.620 to −13,096.120, p < 0.001) per capita in the month when CVBP for coronary stents was implemented. The expense changes for intraocular lenses were not statistically significant. In addition, an immediate upward trend of 0.022 CNY per capita per month (95%CI: −0.017 to 0.026, p < 0.05) after the implementation of CVBP in terms of the percentage of anesthesia expenses was observed in the post-intervention month. (ii) The results of the ARIMA predictions indicated that per capita material expense savings at months post-CVBP were 14,028.187 CNY for coronary stents, 24,476.634 CNY for artificial joints and 280.039 CNY for intraocular lenses.ConclusionsThe implementation of the CVBP of high-value medical consumables saved patient hospitalization expenses and material expenses in Gansu, China. Future research should analyze the long-term effects of CVBP on medical insurance funds and total health expenses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-026-14173-2.Keywords: Interrupted time-series analysis, Centralized volume-based procurement, High-value medical consumables, AutoRegressive integrated moving average

Background

Tracking Universal Health Coverage: The 2023 Global Monitoring Report, which was jointly released by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), reveals severe stagnation in delivering high-quality, affordable, and accessible healthcare worldwide [1]. Notably, catastrophic out-of-pocket health expenditures (exceeding 10% of household budgets) continue to rise, with over one billion people facing healthcare expenses that far exceed their household budgets [2, 3]. High-value medical consumables refer to clinically essential supplies that are directly applied to the human body, subject to stringent safety requirements, widely utilized in clinical practice, and relatively expensive, consequently imposing substantial financial burdens on patients [4, 5].

Medical consumables account for the highest proportion of patient expenditures in both teaching and general hospitals [6]. Since 1986, Group Purchasing Organizations (GPOs) in the United States have aggregated hospital demand to secure price reductions of 10–18% for medical devices, establishing a paradigmatic example for group purchasing worldwide [7, 8]. Similar initiatives, such as the UK’s National Health Service (NHS) and Australia’s Health Purchasing Victoria (HPV), demonstrate how pooling policies reduce expenses while enhancing care quality, providing valuable insights for China’s ongoing healthcare reforms [9–11].

To facilitate the establishment of a national alliance procurement mechanism, the National Organization for Joint Procurement of High-Value Medical Consumables issued the “National Organization Centralized Volume-Based Purchasing Document for Coronary Stents” on October 16, 2020, marking its inception in China’s centralized volume-based procurement for high-value medical consumables [12]. On June 4, 2021, Guiding Opinions on the Centralized Volume-Based Procurement and Use of High-Value Medical Consumables Organized by the State, jointly formulated by the National Healthcare Security Administration (NHSA) and seven other competent departments, specified that in accordance with the overall principle of “state organization, alliance-based procurement, and platform operation”, the central government shall formulate basic policies and requirements, organize various regions to form an alliance, and take public medical institutions as the main implementing entities to conduct centralized volume-based procurement of high-value medical consumables organized by the state [13]. Additionally, the document emphasizes exploring and improving the centralized procurement policy and gradually expanding its coverage scope. The national CVBP is primarily led by the National Joint Procurement Office for High-Value Medical Consumables, with overall coordination and guidance provided by NHSA. It conducts centralized price negotiations with manufacturers on behalf of all public medical institutions nationwide as the collective demand side. For different categories of medical consumables, the central government issues standardized documents on CVBP on an irregular basis, so as to ensure the scientificity and flexibility of policy implementation [14]. The first national CVBP of high-value medical consumables was conducted in Tianjin on November 5, 2020. This policy was subsequently implemented nationwide and has now become a standardized operational framework [15]. Gansu Province officially implemented nationally organized CVBP for coronary stents on January 1, 2021, followed by artificial joints on April 15, 2022, and intraocular lenses on May 20, 2024 [16–18].

Prior to the initiation of the formal study, we searched Chinese and English databases including CNKI, Wanfang Data, and PubMed using search terms such as “pooled”, “centralized”, “volume-based”, “purchase”, “procurement”, “consumable”, and “medical devices”, with a focus on outcome indicators related to patients’ medical burden. The search results revealed that existing studies are mainly concentrated in economically developed eastern regions such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong, and the procured consumables they focus on are concentrated on coronary stents, artificial joints, and intraocular lenses [19–21]. Among these, only one study has been conducted in Gansu Province, which solely focuses on coronary stents [22]. This study aims to evaluate the immediate and long-term impacts of the CVBP policy implementation on patients’ medical burden by conducting ITSA on medical expenditure data from a tertiary public hospital. Additionally, it constructs counterfactual scenarios using the ARIMA model for prediction, thereby supplementing relevant evidence for assessing the effectiveness of CVBP policy implementation in Gansu Province and further facilitating the improvement of the CVBP policy.

Methods

Data source

The dataset was collected from the Gansu Provincial Health Statistics Information Center from January 2019 to October 2024 and includes patients’ basic information, including diagnosis and surgery details (names and codes), surgery date, hospitalization expense and material expense, etc. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the School of Public Health.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) admitted from January 2019 to October 2024; and (2) patients who underwent surgery involving artificial joints, coronary stents, or artificial lenses, with the procedure codes based on the International Classification of Diseases, 9th Revision, Clinical Modification, Procedure Coding system (ICD-9-CM-3): coronary stents (36.06, 36.07); intraocular lenses (13.41, 13.70, 13.71, 13.72, 13.90); and artificial joints (81.51, 81.52, 81.54). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) those who lacked complete case information.

CVBP intervention

For artificial joints, the policy intervention point was set at April 15, 2022, when the hospital began using centralized procurement products, with monthly data analyzed from April 15, 2019, to April 14, 2022 (pre-intervention), and from April 15, 2022, to April 30, 2024 (post-intervention).

Similarly, January 1, 2021, marked the intervention point for coronary stents when the hospital officially used centralized purchased coronary stents for surgery, with monthly observations from January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2020, as the pre-intervention and from January 1, 2021, to September 30, 2024, as the post-intervention.

For intraocular lenses, May 20, 2024 served as the intervention date, with pre-intervention data from January 20, 2019, to May 19, 2024, and post-intervention through September 30, 2024. Two indicators, hospitalization expenses and material expenses, were used as evaluation indicators.

Study variables

Dependent variables

Expenditures are viewed as the outcome variables and are reported in CNY. Expenditures are widely used to evaluate the effectiveness of the implementation of CVBP in the relevant literature, as they are measurable and comparable, and are an important indicators of the economic burden on patients [23–25]. Our study hypothesized that the implementation of the policy can reduce the financial burden on patients, in line with the policy’s expected goal.

Three indicators are used for the dependent variables: (1) Hospitalization expense: total expenditures of inpatient treatment received since admission to the hospital (including the out-of-pocket part and the reimbursement part, which only refers to fees paid to the hospital); (2) Material expense: the total expense for various types of medical materials used by inpatients during treatment; and (3) Other structured expenses: including expenses for western medicines, examinations, nursing care, treatment operations, and other medical service-related expenditures incurred during hospitalization.

Independent variables

Intervention in CVBP is an important independent variable. CVBP intervention = 1 indicates that the group is the treatment group and that the month is the post-policy period; otherwise, CVBP implementation = 0.

ITS analysis

Data were collected and organized with Excel 2019, and R version 4.4.2 was used to perform the statistical analysis. Descriptive analysis of the distributions of hospitalization expenses, structural expenses, sex, age, and days of hospitalization of patients before and after CVBP was performed. Categorical data were analyzed by chi-square test, while continuous data were analyzed by Kruskal-Wallis H test. Interrupted time series were used to analyze the trend of expense changes before and after policy, and p < 0.05 was considered significantly different at the α = 0.05 test.

We employed an ITSA, a quasi-experimental design under natural experimental settings. This approach enables the evaluation of policy interventions by comparing outcome trends before and after implementation through longitudinal data collection at multiple time points [26, 27]. The ITSA is particularly valuable for assessing whether an intervention has causally influenced the measured outcomes, making it widely applicable in policy impact research [28].

The standard single-group ITSA regression model assumes the following form [29]:

Yt: outcome variable (expenditures in month t).

β0: the baseline level of the outcome variable at the beginning of the observation period, i.e., the initial level of expense at the start of the implementation.

β1: the underlying pre-intervention trend, i.e., the long-term trend in expenses prior to the implementation.

β2: the level change following the intervention, i.e. the amount of immediate change in expense after the implementation.

β3: the difference between the pre-intervention and post-intervention slopes of the outcome, i.e., the difference between the trends in expenses before and after the implementation.

Xt: a dummy (indicator) variable, representing the intervention (preintervention periods 0, otherwise 1).

Tt: the time since the start of the study.

εt: an estimate of the random error at time t.

To test potential first-order auto-correlation, the Durbin-Watson (DW) test was performed.

ARIMA model

The auto.arima () function from the forecast package in R was used. Prior to model fitting, each time series dataset was decomposed via an additive model to assess fluctuations in its seasonal components [30]. The decomposition results for all series consistently indicated that the seasonal components exhibited stable annual cyclical fluctuation characteristics with a steady amplitude, suggesting that the impact of seasonality on the series cannot be neglected (Additional file 2. Fig. S1). Accordingly, seasonal differencing was applied for data preprocessing.

The Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) model is based on the ARIMA model with three hyperparameters P, D, and Q and an additional seasonal parameter s, denoted as SARIMA (p, d, q)×(P, D, Q)s. For the seasonal ARIMA model, after being processed according to seasonal differences or trend differences, the sequence can be transformed into a stationary sequence, and the sequence can be fitted [31]. When p = D=Q=0, the SARIMA model is equivalent to the ARIMA model, where p, d, and q are the orders of the autoregressive (AR), the differencing, and the moving average (MA), respectively. Moreover, P, D, and Q represent the seasonal AR, seasonal differencing, and seasonal MA, respectively. A comprehensive grid search (GS) algorithm was employed to exhaustively iterate through all feasible combinations of autoregressive (p/P), differencing (d/D), and moving average (q/Q) orders [32]. The optimal parameters were identified by minimizing the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), ensuring a robust selection process [33]. In addition, s is a seasonal period. Results

Participants

A total of 8076 patients were included in the study on the basis of surgery name and code; 1627 patients underwent surgery involving the use of artificial joints, 1339 patients underwent surgery involving the use of coronary stents, and 5110 patients underwent surgery involving the initial use of intraocular lenses initially. All cases collected in this study had complete core data required for the research. No cases were excluded due to incomplete information, and all were included in the final analysis.

There was no statistically significant difference in the sex and age composition of patients with CVBP for artificial joints (p > 0.05) or in the age composition or average hospitalization days before and after the implementation of coronary stents (p > 0.05). Regarding the CVBP for intraocular lenses, the differences in sex, age composition and average hospitalization days before and after implementation were not statistically significant (p > 0.05). Overall, most clinical characteristics of patients showed no statistically significant differences before and after CVBP, while minor discrepancies in other baseline characteristics were addressed through subsequent subgroup analyses. For coronary stents, the hospitalization expenses and material expenses were best fitted by the ARIMA (0,0,0)×(0,1,0)12 model, with minimum AIC values of 273.370 and 269.520, respectively. The comparison between the predicted and actual hospitalization expenses revealed a 511,378.651 difference between the curves, with the total predicted area being 25.68% greater than the actual value. For material expenses specifically, this difference was 617,240.243 (64.44% higher than the actual values). This finding indicates that without the intervention, both hospitalization and material expenses would have remained significantly greater. A mean reduction of 11,622.242 CNY in per capita hospitalization expenses per patient was achieved post-intervention, with material expenses similarly reduced by 14,028.187 CNY per case.

For artificial joints, the optimal fit was achieved with an ARIMA (0,1,1)×(0,1,0)12 model (minimum AIC = 593.990) for hospitalization expenses, and the ARIMA (0,1,1)×(1,1,0)12 model demonstrated optimal performance (minimum AIC = 584.160) for material expenses. The area differential between the predicted and actual hospitalization expenses was 638,411.400, representing a 59.03% elevation in the projected expense. For material expenses, this difference was more pronounced at 527,883.400, with predicted values exceeding actual values by 135.24%. A per capita decrease of 26,843.378 CNY in hospitalization expenses and 24,476.634 CNY in material expenses was observed. The counterfactual analysis demonstrated that without the implementation of CVBP, both the projected hospitalization and material expenses would have significantly exceeded the observed values.

For intraocular lenses, the optimal parameter combinations (0,1,1)×(0,1,1)12 were selected, achieving optimal fits with AIC values of 905.760 and 869.390, respectively. These findings indicate, to some extent, the effectiveness of CVBP in reducing consumable expenses. The area differential between the predicted and actual hospitalization expense curves was 1591.644, representing a 7.34% surplus in the predicted values. For material expense, the divergence was greater at 2198.848 (50.06% prediction overactual). A marginal per capita increase of 84.128 CNY in hospitalization expenses per patient was noted, with per capita material expense remaining lower (saving 280.039 CNY per case). A downward trend was already observed prior to the implementation, suggesting that there may have been potential confounding factors that were not accounted for in the analysis.

Robustness analysis

To verify the reliability of the study conclusions, supplementary robustness tests were conducted as follows:

Robustness test after excluding extreme length of stay values

Extreme values of average length of stay at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles were excluded, and the ITSA was re-performed with detailed results presented in Additional file 1. Table S1, S2 and S3. For all medical consumables, the core effect indicator (β2) remained significantly negatively associated (p < 0.001), with absolute coefficient values closely comparable to those from the original analysis, confirming the robustness of the short-term cost-reducing effect of the CVBP policy. Several previously non-significant effect indicators became statistically significant after extreme value exclusion, with the most prominent changes observed in the regression results for intraocular lenses-related expenses: for hospitalization expenses, the level change β2 = −2006.924 (p < 0.001) and trend change β3 = −35.106 (p < 0.05); for material expenses, β1 = −21.429 (p < 0.05), β2 = −1596.251 (p < 0.001), and β3 = −50.031 (p < 0.05).

These findings suggest that the presence of extreme values may have masked the true effect characteristics of expenses related to intraocular lenses procedures. After removing such outliers, the model more accurately captured that following CVBP implementation, both hospitalization and material expenses for intraocular lenses not only achieved an immediate short-term reduction but also exhibited a sustained long-term downward trend, while also providing clearer insights into the underlying variation pattern of material expenses during the pre-intervention period. Nevertheless, some trend change indicators remained non-significant after extreme value exclusion - for instance, β3 of per capita hospitalization expenses for coronary stents was −257.705 (p = 0.162) - indicating that extreme value processing did not alter the statistical inference for these indicators, and the conclusion of no significant change in their long-term trends remains reliable.

Robustness test for subgroups with unbalanced baseline characteristics

Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate the potential impact of unbalanced baseline characteristics on the overall results, focusing on length of stay subgroups for artificial joints and gender subgroups for coronary stents (Additional file 1. Table S1, S2 and S3). Although minor variations were observed in the significance of β1 and β3 across individual subgroups, the level change effect (β2) of CVBP on both hospitalization and material expenses remained significantly negative (p < 0.05) in all subgroups. These results were consistent with the overall analysis, with no substantial deviations in coefficient direction or absolute magnitude, supporting the conclusion that baseline imbalance did not materially interfere with the overall inference of the short-term cost-reducing effect of CVBP and that the study conclusions demonstrate robustness across different subgroups.

Discussion

Immediate impact of CVBP on patients’ expenditures

Our findings revealed significant reductions in both hospitalization expenses and medical consumable expenses during the first month after the implementation of CVBP. Crucially, the overall expense reduction was driven primarily by decreased consumable expenses, effectively achieving CVBP’s core objective of “trading volume for price reduction” and substantially alleviating patients’ financial burden [35].

The CVBP extends beyond mere price reduction, aiming to systemically reform of healthcare expenditure patterns through expense containment for medical consumables [36]. Such structural transformation may foster greater clinician adoption and sustained CVBP support [37]. We recommend maintaining an ongoing focus on optimizing expenditure structures during the implementation of CVBP to ensure that healthcare professionals’ technical service values are appropriately recognized and that the full benefits of CVBP are realized. This approach would simultaneously advance the salary reforms specified in the “Notice on Issuing Key Tasks for Deepening Healthcare System Reform in the Second Half of 2023”, which mandates decoupling medical staff compensation from revenues generated through medical consumables, establishing rational and internal salary structures for healthcare organizations, reducing salary dependence on fluctuations in consumable pricing, and implementing stable base salaries complemented by performance-based incentive mechanisms [38, 39].

Long-term effects of CVBP on patients’ expenditures

The results demonstrated significant and sustained reductions in both hospitalization expenses and medical consumable expenses following CVBP implementation. These findings confirm the effectiveness of the policy in alleviating patients’ financial burden and optimizing short-term healthcare resource allocation [40]. Sustained monitoring of long-term CVBP outcomes remains crucial to prevent potential cost-shifting behaviors, where reductions in consumable expenses might be offset by compensatory increases in other expense categories, potentially leading to rebound effects in total healthcare costs [41]. Evidence from pharmaceutical CVBP implementation suggests that some hospitals may compensate for revenue losses by increasing diagnostic testing or expanding prescriptions when drug expenses decline, which could ultimately result in long-term expense rebound [42, 43].

Similarly, we postulate that following CVBP implementation for medical consumables, some hospitals may compensate for revenue losses by increasing diagnostic testing or performing non-essential procedures, gradually undermining the policy’s long-term effectiveness. To safeguard China’s high-value medical consumable CVBP implementation, we recommend establishing a robust expense surveillance and early-warning systems. This system should incorporate: (1) rigorous unannounced inspections of medical insurance fund utilization, (2) mandatory self-audits with corrective action plans by healthcare institutions on the basis of identified problem lists, and (3) standardized clinical practice management to prevent consumable overutilization and mitigate expense-shifting risks. These measures will provide comprehensive safeguards to optimize CVBP implementation for high-value medical consumables in China.

Strengthening the quality oversight of medical consumables under CVBP

While CVBP has significantly reduced patients’ financial burden for surgical procedures and tangibly improved their perceived benefits, its impact on healthcare quality and treatment outcomes requires further investigation. Within this policy framework, manufacturers, in response to price reductions, may be incentivized to comprehensively curtail production costs. This cost-cutting pressure could potentially compromise the quality of medical consumables, ultimately impacting clinical efficacy and patient safety [44]. To address this critical concern, it is imperative that the NHSA, in collaboration with relevant competent authorities, strengthens the access standards for medical consumables. This should include the regular public disclosure of quality inspection results for procured devices, corrective actions for non-compliant products, and a robust product withdrawal mechanism, thereby ensuring full-process transparency and facilitating public oversight [45].

Notably, while the CVBP policy has substantially lowered the economic threshold for surgical treatment and enhanced the autonomy of clinical decision-making, close vigilance must be paid to potential unintended consequences. For instance, reduced prices of medical consumables may alter healthcare providers’ preferences in consumable selection (e.g., adjustments in the usage ratio between imported and domestic consumables), influence the formulation of clinical diagnosis and treatment pathways, or induce the expansion of non-consumable medical service items to offset revenue losses from consumables. All these behaviors may indirectly reshape the structure of patients’ medical burden and affect treatment outcomes, leading to the waste of medical resources and potential interference with the net effect of the policy [46, 47]. In response, we propose conducting dynamic update research on standardized surgical protocols under this policy context to ensure the standardization of clinical diagnosis and treatment practices.

Given the importance of the aforementioned uncovered dimensions, future research is recommended to construct a three-in-one multi-dimensional evaluation framework integrating “economic burden, healthcare quality, and service behavior”. This framework should incorporate economic indicators such as hospitalization expenses and consumable costs, quality indicators including unplanned readmission rate and complication rate, as well as service behavior indicators like consumable utilization efficiency and clinical guideline adherence into a unified analytical system, so as to fully clarify the comprehensive effects of the CVBP policy. Meanwhile, methods such as questionnaires or interviews can be combined to further explore the perceptions, behaviors and attitudes of medical institutions and healthcare providers, thereby providing more comprehensive empirical evidence for optimizing policy supervision mechanisms, balancing cost control and quality assurance, and enhancing the sustainability of policy implementation.

Drivers and potential challenges of declining average inpatient expenses

The underlying drivers of the decline in average per capita inpatient expenses warrant further investigation. According to the 2023 China Health Statistics Yearbook published by the National Health Commission, the average per capita hospitalization expense in 2023 was 10,315.8 CNY (5.2% decrease), whereas total hospital admissions reached 301.873 million, an increase of 55.011 million admissions (22.3% growth) compared with the previous year. However, the long-term implications warrant careful consideration: the substantial increase in admissions, potentially including numerous mild cases, may lead to healthcare resource overutilization, compromised service quality, and persistently high hospitalization expenses. In addition to medical consumable expenses, we recommend that future research incorporate a more comprehensive set of determinants to analyze expense variations thoroughly.

Strengths and limitations

A potential limitation of this study is that other concurrent policies (e.g., DRG/DIP payment reform) were not incorporated into the analytical framework. Regarding the potential interference they may introduce, we excluded them from the model due to their decentralized and non-standardized implementation in the study area. Although we verified the stability of CVBP policy effects through counterfactual simulation and time-series trend observation, and no obvious cost changes were observed corresponding to the rollout of other concurrent policies, their potential subtle impacts cannot be completely ruled out. Furthermore, the intraocular lenses dataset included only 4 monthly observation points post-CVBP implementation, which was insufficient in quantity. Therefore, the conclusions regarding intraocular lenses should not be interpreted as confirmatory evidence for the robustness of the policy’s long-term effects. Future studies are recommended to extend the post-CVBP observation period to 12–24 months and conduct multicenter research to verify the long-term effectiveness of the CVBP policy. This study has there main strengths. First, this study selected patients who underwent surgeries involving three categories of medical consumables (coronary stents, artificial joints, and artificial lenses) as research subjects, with the data demonstrating strong representativeness and wide coverage across multiple product types. As the pioneering category of high-value medical consumables under China’s CVBP, coronary stents, together with artificial joints-which serve as the first benchmark for orthopedic consumables in CVBP, have witnessed mature policy implementation and accumulated comprehensive data. Driven by the surging clinical demand attributable to population aging, artificial lenses were included in the 4th round of national CVBP, thus exhibiting distinct demand-oriented characteristics. Second, we adopted the ITSA method to accurately quantify the impact of the CVBP policy on patients’ medical expenditures. Furthermore, we constructed a counterfactual scenario using the ARIMA model: we fitted the optimal prediction model based on historical data, conducted predictions, and compared the predicted results with actual expenditures. This approach provides validation support for the ITSA results, and the combined application of the two methods significantly enhances the rigor of the study conclusions. Furthermore, this study verifies that the CVBP policy also holds unique value in alleviating patients’ medical burden in underdeveloped western regions of China. It provides targeted empirical evidence to support the national-level extension of the policy to western regions and, more importantly, fills the evidence gap in research on the impact evaluation of CVBP implementation in northwest China.

Conclusion

This study evaluated the impact of CVBP policy on hospitalization costs and material expenses of patients undergoing treatments involving three categories of medical consumables in Gansu Province. The results demonstrated that the CVBP policy exerted a significant cost-containment effect, effectively alleviating the economic burden on patients and thus achieving its predetermined policy objectives. It should be noted, however, that the analysis of intraocular lenses was constrained by a limited number of post-CVBP observation time points. The exploratory analysis preliminarily indicated that CVBP might exert a certain short-term cost-control effect on this category of consumables. Nevertheless, the corresponding conclusions are only of exploratory reference value and insufficient to verify the long-term policy effects.

Strategies for prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer in primary healthcare: scoping review protocol
Authors: Ricardo Souza Evangelista Sant’Ana, Widson Davi Vaz de Matos, Juliana Bezerra do Amaral Amaral, Sabine Calleja, Christine Maheu, Nicolas H Hart, Ariana Luiza Rabelo, Anderson Reis de Sousa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTo address the global increase in cancer incidence, improving prevention strategies, early detection and management of bowel and testicular cancer in primary healthcare can improve clinical decisions. Although national efforts are aligned with global health agendas, there is no consolidated evidence to support effective actions. In this context, this study aims to map the existing scientific evidence on the prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancers within the scope of primary healthcare.Methods and analysisScoping review protocol conducted using Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews checklist, guided by the review question Prevention, Early Detection and Management of Penile and Testicular Cancer (concept) carried out by Primary Healthcare Professionals (population) in men’s healthcare (context). The search for texts will be carried out in six databases (Ovid Medline ALL, Ovid Embase, CINAHL Complete, Web of Science Core Collection, Global Index Medicus and Cochrane Library) and the grey literature, from inception through to May 2025. Two reviewers will select the studies with the help of the blind-on tool of the Covidence. Discrepancies will be resolved by a third reviewer. Data will be extracted according to a specific form and organised in Covidence.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval is not required, as this study does not involve human subjects or primary data collection. Findings will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed scientific publication and shared with healthcare professionals and stakeholders engaged in men’s health.

Background

Cancer represents one of the greatest challenges to global public health, as one of the main causes of premature mortality. The impact of cancer incidence and cancer mortality has been rapidly intensifying on the world stage, driven mainly by the ongoing demographic and epidemiological transitions.1 2 Within male-specific cancers, testicular and penile cancers represent pathologies of low incidence in specific age groups among the male population35; however, they often have aggressive features which, according to treatment, generate sequelae that go beyond the biological scope and reach psychocultural and social dimensions, reflecting bodily alteration by amputation of the affected organ that is socially and culturally representative of masculinity, strength, virility, reproduction and source of pleasure.5 6 Thus, even though from an epidemiological point of view, testicular cancer and penile cancer are not among the most frequent cancer types globally, their treatment has major psychological and functional repercussions that interfere with the patient’s daily life and sexuality.6

According to the Global Cancer Observatory database, in 2022, the absolute incidence of penile cancer in the world was 37 700 cases.2 Among the continents, Asia had the highest proportion of cases (57.1%), followed by Europe (17.9%) and Latin America and the Caribbean (13.8%). In South America, Brazil presents one of the highest reported incidence rates of 8.3/100 000, accounting for approximately. For testicular cancer, the global overall incidence was approximately double that of penile cancer, totalling 72 040 cases worldwide. The percentage distribution indicated the highest concentration in Europe (33.4%), followed by Asia (26.9%) and Latin America and the Caribbean (18.9%).

While penile and testicular cancers are relatively rare globally—accounting for 0.14% (13 738 deaths) and 0.09% (9068 deaths), respectively, of the 9.7 million cancer deaths worldwide in 2022—they remain significant public health concerns in several countries.2 7

Testicular cancer accounts for approximately 5% of all cancer cases among men and is highly curable when detected early, with a relatively low mortality rate. However, social determinants such as marital status, race and geographic location significantly influence survival outcomes across different settings.8 In contrast, penile cancer—a rare malignancy that primarily affects men over 50, with incidence peaking in the sixth decade of life—shows marked regional variation in incidence and outcomes.9

Although rare, penile cancer significantly impacts patients’ quality of life, as treatment may compromise sexual function and urinary continence, leading to substantial psychological, emotional and social distress. Importantly, it is a largely preventable disease, with key strategies including proper penile hygiene, consistent condom use and Human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination.

Public health initiatives such as the creation of clinical guidelines, clinical care protocols and the design of models of care are essential for the management and production of quality care; fundamental for the evaluation of healthcare, organisation and qualification of the network of service provision and care provided to health system users; and crucial for the capture, expansion of access and reception of the male population in primary health services.

Increasing the creation and adoption of these aforementioned public health initiatives may result in favourable outcomes for clinical decision-making by health professionals in areas of prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancers within primary healthcare (PHC), encouraging broader implementation across different countries and settings.

Accordingly, government-led actions and national health policies must be aligned with epidemiological data, projections and global agendas for combating chronic non-communicable diseases. Several countries, including Brazil, have implemented strategic action plans in line with these priorities,10 which are also reflected in the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.11 In this context, a scoping review can help map and summarise existing guidelines, and strategies for prevention, early detection and care management of penile and testicular cancers in primary care, promoting evidence-based decision-making and guiding future policies.

The objective of this study is to map and synthesise the existing evidence on strategies for the prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer within PHC settings.

Methods

This scoping review will be conducted according to the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidelines, which indicate nine steps to be followed: (1) develop and register the protocol in the Open Science Framework; (2) consult other collaborators interested in the research theme; (3) define and align the research objective and questions; (4) develop inclusion/exclusion criteria according to the objectives and review questions; (5) describe the process of searching for and selecting evidence; (6) search for evidence in three steps; (7) select the studies in three phases (title, abstract and full text); (8) extract the evidence and (9) map the evidence to respond to the established objectives and questions.11

This scoping review will be reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR).1214 The search will be carried out in May 2025. In addition to structuring the review question, eligibility criteria based on the PCC framework (Population, Concept, Context) are as follows: Population: healthcare professionals; Concept: prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer; Context: men’s healthcare. To guide this review, these elements were associated, and the following question emerged:

Primary: What are the strategies for prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer in primary healthcare?

Secondary: What are the evidence-based approaches and recommendations to improve the care of penile and testicular cancer in primary care?

Search strategy

A preliminary search strategy will be developed by a health sciences librarian in Ovid Medline and peer-reviewed using the PRESS checklist15 before being translated into other databases. After the construction of the search strategy, the following databases will be consulted: Ovid Medline All, EBSCO CINAHL Complete, Ovid Embase, Global Index Medicus and Cochrane Library. Search by citation will incorporate forward and backwards citation searching in Scopus for included studies to locate additional relevant reviews. A manual search will also be conducted to incorporate publications from experts in the field, including researchers and key institutions studying the prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer, which will be reviewed. Online supplemental file 1 presents the search strategy applied in the Ovid MEDLINE database, which served as the basis for translation into the other databases.

Inclusion criteria

Types of participants: PHC professionals, including general practitioners, nurses, allied health and other professionals involved in the prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer.

Phenomenon of interest: Strategies for prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer in PHC.

Outcomes

Prevention: identification of risk factors, health education, HPV vaccination, circumcision/proctectomy and health promotion interventions.

Early detection: clinical screening, self-examination, physical examinations and guidelines for referral of suspected cases.

Management: initial approach to suspicious lesions, referral to specialised services, patient support and integration of PHC with other levels of healthcare.

Setting

PHC, including Basic Health Units, Family Health Strategy and other PHC services.

Types of studies

Systematic reviews, scoping reviews, observational studies, qualitative studies and clinical practice guides published in peer-reviewed journals.

Exclusion criteria

Studies focused exclusively on specialised cancer treatment; Studies without a specific approach to PHC; Opinion studies, editorials and conference proceedings.

Study condition or domain

Prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer in PHC.

Data extraction (selection and encoding)

Data will be extracted according to an instrument based on the model available in the JBI manual14 transcribed to Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Office 365, 2022), in the form of a synoptic table, whose adopted variables will be: authors, journal, year, country, place of study, language, objective, design, level of evidence, population, technology, results, conclusions, theoretical frameworks and process of construction and/or validation of the technology.

Quality evaluation (risk of bias)

Although the evaluation of methodological quality of included studies is not mandatory for a scoping review, it will be carried out according to standardised critical evaluation instruments, developed by the JBI. Two reviewers will perform the evaluation independently, and disagreements will be resolved by consensus or by a third reviewer.

Data synthesis and analysis

Data will be synthesised through narrative analysis and presented in tables and figures. The information will be categorised according to the three main axes (prevention, early detection and management) and analysed for gaps in knowledge and good practices in PHC.

Publication language

There will be no language limitation or delimitation of any time frame.

Countries included

Studies from any country that meet the inclusion criteria.

Ethical considerations

As this is a scoping review, no ethics committee approval is required.

Skills and resources required

Covidence for data screening, EndNote for citation management.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval is not required, as this study does not involve human subjects or primary data collection. Findings will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed scientific publication and shared with healthcare professionals and stakeholders engaged in men’s health.

This scoping review will be conducted between July 2025 and August 2026.

Study limitations and mitigation strategies

As a scoping review, this study will not assess the methodological quality or risk of bias of the included studies, which limits inferences about the effectiveness of the identified strategies. The heterogeneity of designs, contexts and health systems may hinder comparisons between findings. In addition, some experiences in PHC may not be published in the scientific literature, resulting in underrepresentation of existing practices.

To mitigate these limitations, a broad and systematic search will be conducted in multiple databases. Data selection and removal will occur independently by two reviewers, with resolution of disagreements by a third. The results will be organised by thematic axes, allowing the identification of gaps and convergences in the available evidence.

The results may support professionals, managers and public policy makers in improving actions for the prevention, early detection and management of penile and testicular cancer in PHC, as well as guide the development of protocols, care pathways and future research in men’s health.

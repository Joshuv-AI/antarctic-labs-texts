Phylogenetic discordance arises from incomplete lineage sorting and pre-speciation introgression during erosion-mediated radiation of Asian warty newts
Authors: Tao Luo, Jia-Jia Wang, Ming-Yuan Xiao, Mei Liao, Huai-Qing Deng, Ning Xiao, Jiang Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe genus Paramesotriton Chang, 1935, comprising 15 species classified into two groups, exhibits the broadest geographical distribution among modern Asian newts, extending across southern China from west to east and representing a prominent example of adaptive radiation. Despite this success, intrageneric phylogenetic relationships among species remain unresolved, with particularly poor resolution within the Paramesotriton caudopunctatus species group (PCSG). In this study, restriction-site-associated DNA sequencing from five representative PCSG species was combined with previously published mitochondrial genomes to generate a genome-scale phylogenomic framework. The resulting analyses yielded robust support for interspecific relationships within PCSG. Gene tree discordance was primarily attributable to incomplete lineage sorting, with additional contributions from pre-speciation gene flow, as indicated by ASTRAL, HyDe, Dsuite, and PhyloNet. Evidence also supported hybridization between P. longliensis and an unidentified Paramesotriton lineage, consistent with a hybrid origin for P. zhijinensis. Comparative genomic and biogeographic inference further indicated that erosional isolation associated with exposure of carbonate sedimentary rocks facilitated allopatric divergence among PCSG lineages. Moreover, divergence timing aligned PCSG origin and diversification with Miocene paleoclimatic variability and progressive erosion of carbonate substrates, directly implicating karst landscape evolution in lineage formation within Asian warty newts. An erosion-driven speciation framework is therefore supported, in which recurrent episodes of geomorphological restructuring of karst mountain systems repeatedly imposed geographic isolation, driving successive allopatric diversification during both tectonically active and tectonically stable periods.Keywords: Phylogenomics, Incomplete lineage sorting, Hybridization, Radiation, Rock erosion

INTRODUCTION

Evolutionary biologists have long been skeptical of sympatric speciation, as geographic or reproductive isolation is traditionally viewed as the prerequisite for lineage divergence. Increasing evidence, however, suggests that speciation can proceed without such isolation (Morjan & Rieseberg, 2004; Ravinet et al., 2017; Ronco et al., 2021; Sousa & Hey, 2013; Sun et al., 2022; Wang & Liu, 2025). This conceptual shift has redirected attention toward the evolutionary consequences of interspecific gene flow and the conditions under which introgression persists during lineage divergence. Genomic studies across diverse taxa have shown that post-speciation introgression is relatively widespread, reframing gene flow from an anomaly to a recurrent feature of diversification (Lescroart et al., 2023; Meleshko et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022). Gene flow can act as a powerful catalyst of adaptive radiation, in some systems exerting a stronger influence than paleoclimatic forcing (Du et al., 2024; Meleshko et al., 2021; Qian et al., 2023). Because the magnitude and direction of gene exchange can vary across successive stages of divergence (Feder et al., 2012), evolutionary outcomes depend critically on whether introgression precedes lineage establishment or occurs after speciation (Choin et al., 2021; Meleshko et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024). At the same time, gene flow complicates phylogenetic inference by introducing discordant signals that obscure species relationships (Alexander et al., 2017), making explicit consideration of introgression dynamics essential for accurate reconstruction of evolutionary history (Santos et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2021). Accordingly, introgression has been successfully detected across diverse taxa (Du et al., 2024; Lescroart et al., 2023; Meleshko et al., 2021; Rancilhac et al., 2021) using network-based and summary-statistic frameworks, including PhyloNet (Than et al., 2008) and Dsuite (Malinsky et al., 2021).

Incomplete lineage sorting (ILS) represents another major contributor to gene tree discordance and poses a fundamental challenge for reconstructing evolutionary histories. When ILS coincides with speciation, discrimination between ancestral polymorphism and introgressive exchange becomes particularly difficult, as both processes generate overlapping genomic signatures (Pinho & Hey, 2010; Ravinet et al., 2017). ILS arises from stochastic coalescent dynamics in which allelic lineages fail to merge within ancestral populations prior to divergence, a pattern especially prevalent during rapid radiations (Avise & Robinson, 2008; Degnan & Rosenberg, 2009; Maddison & Knowles, 2006; Zwickl et al., 2014). This process reflects the retention of ancestral polymorphism that can become differentially fixed in descendant lineages (Suh et al., 2015), producing locus-specific phylogenetic discordance even long after divergence (Szöllősi et al., 2015). Consequently, genomic loci frequently exhibit discordant phylogenetic signals that obscure species relationships (Avise & Robinson, 2008; Rivas-González et al., 2023; Suh et al., 2015; Tan et al., 2023). Distinguishing ILS from introgression remains challenging, as ILS is most pronounced during episodes of rapid diversification (Feng et al., 2022; Maddison & Knowles, 2006), whereas gene flow can persist across extended evolutionary timescales. Although statistical frameworks such as quantifying introgression via branch lengths (QuIBL) (Edelman et al., 2019) and Phytop (Shang et al., 2025) provide tools for disentangling these processes, their performance depends on well-resolved phylogenies. Even with these advances, reliable inference of species relationships and quantification of introgression remain difficult when ILS and gene flow operate concurrently (Kapli et al., 2020). Integrative phylogenomic strategies that combine complementary methods across sequential divergence nodes have nevertheless demonstrated improved resolution in multiple systems (Meleshko et al., 2021; Tan et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2022). Genome-scale investigations of ILS and gene flow in amphibians remain relatively limited, despite increasing genomic sequencing efforts. Recent studies have documented signatures of both processes during lineage divergence in eastern red-backed salamanders (Waldron et al., 2025), microhylid frogs (Alexander et al., 2017), banded newts (Van Riemsdijk et al., 2022), and Old World salamanders (Rancilhac et al., 2021). Notably, transcriptomic analyses of Old World salamanders revealed pervasive ancient hybridization across genera within Salamandridae, accompanied by cytonuclear conflict (Rancilhac et al., 2021).

The Asian warty newt genus Paramesotriton Chang, 1935 (Caudata: Salamandridae) is distributed from southern China to northern Vietnam (Figure 1A) and represents a prominent example of adaptive radiation among extant Asian salamanders (Luo et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2022). Fifteen species are currently recognized and are commonly subdivided into two morphogenetically supported species groups: the Paramesotriton caudopunctatus species group (PCSG) and Paramesotriton chinensis species group (see Supplementary Figure S1 for phylogenetic relationships) (Fei & Ye, 2016; Gu et al., 2012a, 2012b; Luo et al., 2021, 2022; Wang et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2010; Yuan et al., 2014), with an alternative framework proposing three subgenera (Fei & Ye, 2016). Despite extensive taxonomic effort, interspecific relationships within these assemblages remain controversial, particularly within PCSG (see Supplementary Figure S2 for morphological comparisons). Previous reconstructions have primarily relied on mitochondrial ND2, complete mitogenomes, and a small set of nuclear loci, yielding conflicting topologies across studies (Gu et al., 2012a, 2012b; Luo et al., 2021, 2022; Wang et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2010; Yuan et al., 2014). Rapid lineage splitting inferred from divergence-time estimates suggests that ILS may be the primary driver of phylogenetic discordance within the PCSG (Luo et al., 2021, 2022; Yuan et al., 2022), although the relative roles of ancestral polymorphism and introgression are yet to be resolved. Importantly, beyond evolutionary uncertainty, several PCSG taxa face elevated conservation risk, classified as threatened under the China Red List of Biodiversity (Jiang & Xie, 2021), including Paramesotriton longliensis (Endangered), Paramesotriton zhijinensis (Endangered), Paramesotriton wulingensis (Near Threatened), and Paramesotriton caudopunctatus (Vulnerable), all associated with marked population declines (Jiang & Xie, 2021; Zhao et al., 2012). These trajectories highlight an urgent need for integrative genomic resolution of taxonomy, genetic structure, and evolutionary history, particularly given that accurate species delimitation underpins effective conservation policies and provides essential context for interpreting ecological diversification and evolutionary processes.

Guizhou Province forms the headwater region of several major river systems, including the Wujiang, Hongshui, Qingshuijiang, and Wuyang Rivers, and is predominantly underlain by highly erodible carbonate sedimentary rocks interspersed with mixed sedimentary formations (Supplementary Figure S3) (Guizhou Provincial Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources, 1987; Sayre et al., 2014). Notably, species within PCSG occur almost exclusively within carbonate-dominated landscapes (Supplementary Figure S3), where populations are partitioned among tributary networks isolated by river systems that flow through carbonate substrates and further separated by intervening barriers of mixed sedimentary rocks (Supplementary Figure S3). Surface lithology in this region is dynamic rather than static. Progressive erosion of non-vertical stratigraphic layers drives lateral displacement of lithological boundaries across the landscape, continually reshaping habitat connectivity. In the karst mountains of Guizhou, ongoing erosion proceeds at estimated rates of 44–76 m/million years on hillslopes and 280–1 138 m/million years along river channels (Chen, 1985, 2019; Yang, 1985), expanding carbonate exposure while simultaneously increasing outcrop area of mixed sedimentary rocks (Sayre et al., 2014). These geomorphological processes imply that suitable habitats for PCSG species were likely more extensive in the past. Over geological timescales, sustained erosional forces may have fragmented ancestral distributions into isolated tributary-associated populations constrained by carbonate substrates, providing a physical framework for repeated episodes of allopatric diversification.

To resolve this complex and potentially reticulate evolutionary history, restriction-site-associated DNA sequencing was conducted on 46 individuals representing five PCSG taxa (P. caudopunctatus, P. zhijinensis, Paramesotriton sp., P. longliensis, and P. maolanensis), together with Pachytriton inexpectatus as an outgroup. Sequence data were processed using the STACKS pipeline to identify single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) de novo in the absence of a reference genome, following a strategy previously applied to Paramesotriton deloustali (Van Tran et al., 2025). These genome-wide markers, integrated with eight published mitochondrial genomes from PCSG salamanders, supported a comprehensive phylogenomic assessment of interspecific relationships. This framework enabled reconstruction of PCSG salamander phylogeny, evaluation of ILS and interspecific gene flow, and inference of evolutionary trajectories across the group.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Taxon sampling

A total of 46 specimens were obtained through targeted field surveys and curated museum collections (Figure 1B). Sampling encompassed nine P. caudopunctatus, 10 P. longliensis, two P. maolanensis, 10 P. zhijinensis, eight Paramesotriton sp. (Supplementary Table S1 presents morphological materials), and seven outgroup individuals, providing broad taxonomic coverage for subsequent analyses (Supplementary Table S2). Muscle tissue was excised from tail tips and preserved in 95% anhydrous ethanol at the Animal Ecology Laboratory, Guizhou Normal University, Guiyang, Guizhou Province, China. Despite extensive multi-season fieldwork campaigns conducted across the Wuling Mountains between 2018 and 2024, fresh tissue samples from P. wulingensis could not be secured due to extreme rarity; this taxon was therefore excluded from genomic analyses.

Laboratory protocols, sequencing, and bioinformatic processing

Genomic DNA was extracted from ethanol-preserved tissues (n=46) using a cetyltrimethylammonium bromide protocol (Hanania et al., 2004). Following EcoRI digestion (GAATTC), Restriction-site Associated DNA sequencing (RAD-seq) libraries were prepared (Baird et al., 2008) and size-selected (350–550 bp) for paired-end sequencing on the MGISEQ-2000 platform (MGI Tech, China).

Raw reads were quality-filtered using fastp v.0.23.4 (Chen, 2023), and RAD-seq data were assembled using STACKS v.2.61 (Rochette et al., 2019). The pipeline included raw data demultiplexing and filtering using process_radtags and clone_filter; locus construction with ustacks, catalog generation with cstacks, sample matching with sstacks, data transposition using tsv2bam, incorporation of paired-end reads, and genotype calling; and de novo SNP calling with denovo_map.pl. Assembly parameters were optimized following the established r80 method (Gundappa et al., 2022; Paris et al., 2017). The maximum number of allele mismatches between stacks (M=3–8) and coverage depth (m=4–14) were systematically evaluated, with inter-sample mismatches (n) set equal to M. Final parameter values were M=6, m=10, and n=6, ensuring robust locus recovery while maintaining data quality. This pipeline yielded 15 581 647 biallelic SNPs across all 46 individuals.

Variant filtering proceeded through a multi-step framework: initial SNP filtering using VCFtools v.0.1.16, with minor allele frequency (MAF) >0.01, missing data <50%, and retention of biallelic sites (Danecek et al., 2011); sample quality control using PLINK v.1.90 (Purcell et al., 2007), excluding samples exceeding 50% missing genotypes; genotype imputation using Beagle v.5.0 (Browning & Browning, 2016); and linkage disequilibrium-based SNP pruning implemented in bigsnpr v.1.9.11 (Privé et al., 2018).

Phylogenetic reconstruction based on mitochondrial genomes and SNP data

Maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenies were inferred using IQ-TREE v.1.6.12 (Nguyen et al., 2015) based on concatenated loci under the GTR+ASC model, with nodal support assessed through 2 000 ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot) replicates (Hoang et al., 2018). Species-tree inference followed two complementary approaches. First, SVDquartets (Chifman & Kubatko, 2014), implemented in PAUP* v.4.0a (Wilgenbusch & Swofford, 2003), evaluated all possible taxon quartets under the multispecies coalescent model, with nodal support estimated from 100 bootstrap replicates. This framework additionally facilitated detection of ILS or introgression in regions characterized by reduced nodal support. Second, 1 475 246 SNPs were partitioned into non-overlapping genomic windows of 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, and 50 kb using raxml_sliding_windows.py (https://github.com/simonhmartin/genomics_general, accessed on July 12, 2024), with each window treated as an independent SNP subset. Gene trees were reconstructed under the GTRCAT model in RAxML, and topological summaries from these trees were integrated in ASTRAL-III (Zhang et al., 2018).

Eighteen mitochondrial genomes incorporated in phylogenetic analyses were primarily obtained from our previously published dataset (Supplementary Table S3) (Luo et al., 2022). Multiple sequence alignments were generated using MUSCLE (Edgar, 2004) within MEGA v.7.0 (Kumar et al., 2016). Best-fit partitioning schemes and nucleotide substitution models were selected according to Bayesian Information Criterion in PartitionFinder v.2.1.1 (Lanfear et al., 2017). Phylogenetic reconstruction employed both ML and Bayesian inference (BI) frameworks. ML analyses were conducted in IQ-TREE v.2.0.4 (Nguyen et al., 2015) under the optimal model (Supplementary Table S4), with 20 000 UFBoot replicates. BI analysis was performed in MrBayes v.3.2.1 (Ronquist et al., 2012) using two independent runs of five million generations, sampling every 1 000 generations and discarding the first 25% as a burn-in. Nodes were considered well-supported when Bayesian posterior probabilities (BPP) exceeded 0.95 and UFBoot values exceeded 95%.

Genetic cluster analysis, genetic diversity, and population dynamics

Linkage disequilibrium pruning was applied in PLINK v.1.9 (Purcell et al., 2007) using parameters --indep-pairwise 50 10 0.5 to reduce SNP correlations. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed in the R package bigsnpr v.1.9.11 (Privé et al., 2018), with visualization based on the first two principal components (PC1 and PC2). Population genetic structure was inferred using ADMIXTURE v.1.3.0 (Alexander et al., 2009) under default settings, with the number of genetic clusters (K) ranging from 2 to 5. The optimal K value was determined based on the lowest cross-validation (CV) error. Nucleotide diversity (π) and pairwise genetic differentiation (FST) were calculated in VCFtools v.0.1.16 (Danecek et al., 2011). Demographic trajectories were reconstructed using Stairway Plot v.2.1.1 (Liu & Fu, 2015) from site-frequency spectra generated with easySFS (Gutenkunst et al., 2009), assuming a generation time of three years (Funk et al., 1999) and a per-site, per-generation mutation rate of 2.2×10−9 (Reilly, 2009).

Detection of gene flow and ILS

Currently, phylogenetic discordance within PCSG remains unresolved (Luo et al., 2021, 2022), with previous analyses implicating either gene flow, ILS, or a combination of both. To disentangle these processes, a suite of complementary analytical frameworks was applied to quantify potential introgression and ILS across the five focal species. Gene flow directionality and magnitude were first evaluated using TreeMix (Pickrell & Pritchard, 2012), with 10 independent runs performed under parameters: -root -m 1–10 -k 1000 -se -bootstrap 1000 -global -noss. The optimal number of migration events was identified through likelihood profiling using the R package OptM (Fitak, 2021). Introgression among PCSG taxa was further quantified using Dsuite (Malinsky et al., 2021), with Dtrios applied to species relationships inferred from phylogenetic reconstructions. Three complementary statistics were employed: D-statistics (Durand et al., 2011), f4-ratio estimation (Patterson et al., 2012), and f-branch analysis (Malinsky et al., 2018). D-statistics and f4-ratio calculations were performed with “Dsuite Dtrios” using variant call format (VCF) files, species trees, and sample assignments as input. For f-branch analysis, branch-specific introgression signals were subsequently mapped onto phylogenetic topologies with “Dsuite Fbranch,” integrating output from Dtrios to visualize gene flow intensity across lineages.

Ancient introgression signals detected by Dsuite were independently validated using HyDe, a site-pattern probabilistic framework for detecting hybridization events, identifying putative parental populations, and estimating the genetic parameter γ to quantify the genomic contribution of each parental population to the hybridization event (Blischak et al., 2018). Rooted four-taxon hybridization tests were conducted across all possible triplet combinations, comprising an outgroup (O), two putative parental populations (P1 and P2), and a hybrid population (Hyb), following the topology ((P1, (Hyb), P2), O). Values of γ approximating 0.5 with P<0.05 indicate equal genomic contributions (50:50) from both parental lineages, whereas extreme γ values support the absence of hybridization (Blischak et al., 2018). Under this model, the hybrid population forms a sister relationship with P1 with probability 1−γ or with P2 with probability γ (Meng & Kubatko, 2009). Hybridization analyses proceeded using a three-step analytical pipeline. Initial screening across all species triplets was performed using the “run_hyde_mp.py” script, with Pachytriton inexpectatus designated as the outgroup and significant results retained at P<0.05. Individual-level hybridization was then evaluated within candidate hybrid populations using the “individual_hyde.py” script. Finally, robustness of inferred hybridization was assessed through bootstrap resampling of individuals (500 replicates) using the “bootstrap_hyde.py” script.

Geographic distributions (Figure 1B) combined with previous phylogenetic evidence (Luo et al., 2021, 2022) suggested a hybrid origin for P. zhijinensis. This hypothesis was explicitly tested using both PhyloNet-MPL and HyDe to quantify genomic contributions of the two parental lineages (P. longliensis and Paramesotriton sp.). Species networks accommodating both introgression and ILS scenarios were inferred using the tree-based maximum pseudo-likelihood method InferNetwork_MPL implemented in PhyloNet v.3.8.2 (Than et al., 2008). A set of 295 gene trees generated by ASTRAL served as input. Network searches were conducted in 10 parallel replicates, each permitting 0–5 reticulation events. For each reticulation level, 100 search iterations (-x 100) were performed, with the ASTRAL species tree specified as the starting topology. The five highest-likelihood networks recovered from each search were retained. Branch lengths and inheritance probabilities of the returned species networks were subsequently optimized under full likelihood conditions using the “-po” option. Four independent runs were carried out with all other parameters set to default. Model selection across alternative reticulation scenarios was based on maximum likelihood, and the inferred network with the highest likelihood score was designated as optimal and visualized in Dendroscope v.3.8.10 (Huson & Scornavacca, 2012).

Signals of ILS and introgression or hybridization were additionally quantified and visualized using Phytop (Shang et al., 2025). Internal node topology frequencies derived from ASTRAL outputs under parameter “-t 2” were analyzed to estimate relative contributions of ILS and introgression/hybridization across the species tree (Zhang et al., 2018; Shang et al., 2025).

Divergence time estimation, biogeography, and diversification dynamics

Divergence times and species tree topologies were inferred using SNAPPER v.1.1.2 (Bryant et al., 2012; Stoltz et al., 2021), which implements a strict molecular clock coupled with the diffusion models (Brown & Yang, 2011; Stange et al., 2018). Analyses were conducted in BEAST v.2.7.6 (Bouckaert et al., 2019), with input datasets generated through random SNP subsampling using snapp_prep.rb (https://github.com/mmatschiner/snapp_prep, accessed on July 12, 2024) (parameters: ruby snapp_prep.rb -a SNAPPER -v -t -c -s -m 3860 -l 1000000 -x -o) to produce XML input files. Temporal calibration of the Paramesotriton and Pachytriton divergence was constrained to 25.42 million years ago (Ma) using a lognormal prior (standard deviation=0.0859) (Luo et al., 2022). Three independent Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analyses were performed (Bouckaert et al., 2019), each running for one million generations with sampling every 500 generations. Convergence and parameter mixing were evaluated in Tracer v.1.7.2 (Rambaut et al., 2018), retaining only runs with effective sample sizes exceeding 200. Maximum-credibility trees with median node heights were summarized using DensiTree v.2.0 (Bouckaert et al., 2019) and TreeAnnotator v.2.7.6 (Bouckaert et al., 2019), discarding the initial 10% of each MCMC chain as burn-in.

Ancestral range reconstruction for PCSG was conducted in BioGeoBEARS (Matzke, 2013) following established protocols (Luo et al., 2021). Contemporary distributions were classified into six distinct biogeographic regions based on mountain systems, river networks, and geological structure: (A) Leigong Mountains, (B) Northern Wumeng Mountains-Wujiang River, (C) Southern Wumeng Mountains-Wujiang River, (D) Lianjiang River Basin, (E) Libo Karst Region, and (F) Wuling Mountains. Based on comprehensive field investigations and a literature review, the maximum ancestral range size was constrained to two adjacent regions to ensure biologically meaningful reconstructions.

Diversification dynamics within PCSG were evaluated following methods outlined in previous phylogenetic research (Li et al., 2024). Time-calibrated phylogenies derived from BEAST were summarized to obtain lineage divergence time intervals with associated 95% confidence intervals. Temporal patterns of diversification were quantified as the maximal number of observed lineage divergence events per 0.5 million years (MDE), enabling visualization of variation in speciation rates through time.

RESULTS

Genome sequencing, SNP calling, and mitogenome dataset

Sequencing of 46 individuals generated approximately 2 948.5 Gb of raw data comprising 1 004 million paired-end reads, with a mean yield of 64.09 Gb and 21.8 million paired-end reads per sample (Supplementary Table S1). Overall base quality was high, with 92.80% of bases achieving quality scores greater than or equal to 30 and mean guanine-cytosine content reaching 43.91% (Supplementary Table S1). De novo assembly and variant detection in STACKS produced 15 581 647 biallelic SNPs. Following removal of low-quality individuals in PLINK, 1 475 246 SNPs were retained. Subsequent linkage disequilibrium pruning reduced this dataset to 438 504 SNPs. Three SNP datasets were defined for downstream analyses: Dataset 1 (1 475 246 SNPs) for phylogenetic reconstruction, Dataset 2 (438 504 SNPs) for PCA and genetic clustering, and Dataset 3 (3 860 SNPs) for divergence time estimation. Eighteen mitogenomes (15 466 bp in length) contained 4 334 variable sites and 2 852 parsimony-informative sites. The best-fit substitution models were TVM+I+G (12S/16S rRNA), HKY+I+G (tRNAs), GTR+G (most protein-coding genes), and HKY+G (ND6).

Phylogenetic relationships among species

Mitochondrial genome analyses revealed limited divergence among PCSG taxa, with pairwise mitochondrial genetic distances ranging from 0.53% to 7.80% (Supplementary Table S5) and resolving into two principal clades. Within Clade II, P. zhijinensis occupied a basal position, followed sequentially by P. maolanensis, P. longliensis, and Paramesotriton sp. The phylogenetic relationship between the latter two taxa remained unresolved, with low nodal support (BPP=0.47, UFBoot=42%) (Figure 2A; Supplementary Figure S4), indicating limited resolution from mitochondrial markers alone in this rapidly diverging lineage complex.

In contrast, the ML tree inferred from the SNP dataset yielded a fully resolved phylogeny with strong nodal support (UFBoot=100%) (Figure 2B). In this reconstruction, Paramesotriton sp. formed the basal lineage of Clade II, followed by P. zhijinensis, P. maolanensis, and P. longliensis. The species tree generated using ASTRAL recovered a topology congruent with the SNP-based ML tree (Figure 2C; Supplementary Figure S5), whereas the SVDquartets species tree supported P. zhijinensis as the basal lineage of Clade II (Figure 2D).

Contributions of ILS, genetic introgression, and hybridization to gene tree heterogeneity

Evidence of gene flow among PCSG taxa was supported by TreeMix, D-statistics, f4-ratio, and f-branch analyses. TreeMix inferred directional introgression from the ancestral lineage of Clade II into P. maolanensis, from P. caudopunctatus into the common ancestor of P. longliensis and P. maolanensis, and from P. zhijinensis into P. caudopunctatus (Figure 3A), patterns concordant with results from D-statistics and f4-ratio tests (Figure 3B–D). D-statistics (Figure 3B), f4-ratio estimates (Figure 3C), and f-branch analyses (Figure 3D) provided further support for historical gene exchange among species (D=0.01–0.14, |Z-score|>3, P<0.001) (Figure 3B; Supplementary Table S6), with particularly strong signals involving P. zhijinensis in pairwise comparisons with P. longliensis and Paramesotriton sp. (Figure 3D).

Network-based inference in PhyloNet-MPL indicated probable hybridization among the three species and supported a hybrid origin for P. zhijinensis, with approximately 45% genomic contribution from P. longliensis and 55% from Paramesotriton sp. (Figure 3E; Supplementary Figure S6). These inferences were independently corroborated by HyDe, which identified two of 10 significant hybridization tests with γ values approximating 0.5 (Supplementary Table S7), consistent with hybrid ancestry in P. zhijinensis (Figure 3F). Individual-level validation further refined these estimates, with γ values ranging from 0.35 to 0.46 for P. longliensis and from 0.54 to 0.65 for Paramesotriton sp. (Supplementary Table S8), supporting P. zhijinensis as a hybrid species, with genomic contributions of approximately 55% from Paramesotriton sp. and 45% from P. longliensis (Figure 3F; Supplementary Table S7). Bootstrap resampling of individuals within the hybrid population (500 replicates) generated stable γ distributions (Figure 3F; Supplementary Table S9), indicating broadly homogeneous admixture across both species and individual levels (Figure 3E; Supplementary Tables S7–S9). However, as the data were not genome-wide, whether P. zhijinensis represents a true hybrid requires cautious interpretation.

Complementary phylogenomic assessment using Phytop attributed 11.0%–42.7% of topological conflict within Clade II to ILS, with the remaining conflict explained by introgression, consistent with inferred gene flow between P. zhijinensis and P. longliensis and between P. zhijinensis and Paramesotriton sp. (Figure 3G). Collectively, these results indicate that ILS constitutes the dominant source of phylogenetic incongruence within PCSG, with introgression acting as an additional, reinforcing process.

Population genetic structure, diversity, and demographic history within PCSG

Population genetic analyses resolved clear genetic structuring across PCSG lineages. At K=4, ADMIXTURE identified four major genetic clusters (Figure 4A; Supplementary Figure S7), whereas PCA resolved five clusters (Figure 4B), reflecting fine-scale subdivision within the complex. Admixture was evident among P. longliensis, P. maolanensis, P. zhijinensis, and Paramesotriton sp., with particularly pronounced signals between P. longliensis and P. maolanensis (Figure 4A). Estimates of population differentiation further supported weak divergence between P. maolanensis and P. longliensis (FST=0.06) and between P. maolanensis and P. caudopunctatus (FST=0.03) (Figure 4C), although these values require cautious interpretation due to the extremely small sample size (n=2) for P. maolanensis (Weir & Hill, 2002). In contrast, remaining species pairs exhibited moderate genetic differentiation, with FST values ranging from 0.11 to 0.16 (Hartl & Clark, 1997).

Nucleotide diversity (π) revealed comparable levels in P. longliensis and P. zhijinensis, both exceeding values observed in Paramesotriton sp. (Figure 4D). Notably, P. caudopunctatus exhibited the highest genetic diversity among all taxa, consistent with a relatively large effective population size (Ne) inferred from field surveys.

Demographic reconstruction indicated a pronounced population contraction between approximately 1 Ma and 130 kilo annum (ka), characterized by rapid Ne declines in P. caudopunctatus (~800 ka), P. longliensis (~200 ka), P. zhijinensis (~130 ka), and Paramesotriton sp. (~130 ka), followed by rapid post-bottleneck recovery (Figure 4E). Ne subsequently remained broadly stable from 100 ka to 8 ka, before entering a sustained decline. From 8 ka to the present, Ne exhibited a continuous downward trajectory across lineages (Figure 4E).

Divergence time, biogeography, and diversification dynamics

The SNAPPER species tree recovered a topology congruent with both the SNP-based ML reconstruction and the ASTRAL species tree (Figure 5A). The time to the most recent common ancestor of PCSG was dated to approximately 16.90 Ma (95% highest posterior density (HPD): 20.09–13.80 Ma), with subsequent speciation events concentrated between 13.89 Ma and 8.15 Ma (Figure 5B).

Ancestral range inference indicated that early PCSG lineages were most likely distributed across the Wumeng Mountains-Wujiang River region (Figure 5B, C; Supplementary Figures S8, S9; Supplementary Table S10). A vicariance event near 16.90 Ma separated ancestral populations into eastern and western assemblages (Figure 5B). Biogeographic reconstruction further suggested that the eastern group may have originated from western group dispersal, whereas southern taxa likely arose through stepwise dispersal from the Wumeng Mountains via central corridors during the Miocene (Figure 5B, C).

Diversification analyses based on maximal divergence events (MDE) revealed initial lineage divergence near 18 Ma, followed by pronounced acceleration around 17 Ma and again near 10 Ma. Two distinct peaks in divergence were evident, centered at approximately 15 Ma and 8.5 Ma, separated by a relative lull around 11 Ma (Figure 5E).

DISCUSSION

Phylogeny under ILS and ancient introgression

Long-standing phylogenetic uncertainties within PCSG have persisted despite extensive morphological and molecular investigation. Previous morphology-based classifications have proposed either a two-group system (Fei et al., 2006) or a three-subgenus framework (Fei & Ye, 2016), while molecular evidence has consistently supported the presence of two principal clades (Gu et al., 2012a, 2012b; Luo et al., 2021, 2022; Wang et al., 2013; Yuan et al., 2014, 2022). Furthermore, relationships among P. zhijinensis, P. longliensis, P. maolanensis, and Paramesotriton sp. have remained unresolved, with mitochondrial data recovering two conflicting topologies (Supplementary Figure S10) (Luo et al., 2021, 2022). Genome-scale analyses resolved these persistent uncertainties within PCSG. Notably, coalescent- and concatenation-based analyses strongly supported a sister relationship between P. longliensis and P. maolanensis (Figure 2). Phylogenetic discordance analyses further indicated that ILS constituted the primary source of cytonuclear conflict, with introgression playing a secondary role (Figure 3).

Distinguishing ILS from introgression remains challenging because both processes can generate overlapping genomic signatures (Fontaine et al., 2015). Population structure analyses and DensiTree visualization revealed limited evidence of recent, large-scale admixture, with the exception of signals involving P. maolanensis. A plausible explanation for this is that geographic isolation among contemporary populations likely constrained present-day interspecific gene flow, whereas introgression during early divergence may have contributed to rapid ecological diversification within PCSG (Luo et al., 2021). This interpretation is consistent with regional geological reorganization and the relatively recent divergence times inferred for these lineages (Luo et al., 2021; Zhou & Chen, 1993). Ancient admixture has been implicated in accelerating radiations in other systems (Marques et al., 2019), and the present analyses supported a comparable dynamic. Node-specific discordance partitioning further revealed that ILS contributed more than introgression across internal nodes (Figure 3G), supporting a primary role for ILS in generating phylogenetic conflict (Edelman et al., 2019; Meleshko et al., 2021). Importantly, these patterns are consistent with episodes of rapid lineage diversification within PCSG. Similar introgression-associated discordance has been reported between P. deloustali and P. guangxiensis (Van Tran et al., 2025). Nevertheless, recent gene flow may have been underestimated due to several factors. First, introgression from unsampled “ghost” lineages or interspecific variation in effective population size may bias D-statistics (Zheng & Janke, 2018), introducing unexplained error. Second, reduced-representation SNP data do not provide complete genome coverage, and missing loci may have affected gene flow estimates. These limitations underscore the need for future studies that incorporate de novo genome assemblies, including HiFi sequencing with Hi-C scaffolding, together with analyses that evaluate the functional relevance of introgressed regions.

Conservation assessments have revealed alarming and ongoing population declines across PCSG. Based on IUCN Red List criteria (IUCN, 2025), P. zhijinensis is classified as Endangered (B1ab(iii)), P. caudopunctatus as Near Threatened (A2cd), and P. longliensis as Vulnerable (B1ab(iii,v)). Demographic reconstruction revealed an effective population size decline of more than 96% across PCSG since the Last Glacial Maximum (Figure 4E). Coupled with reduced nucleotide diversity (Figure 4D) and continued loss of suitable habitat (Yan, 2023), these trends collectively satisfy thresholds consistent with Critically Endangered status. Similar demographic collapses have been documented in music frogs (Lyu et al., 2024). Genetic diversity in P. maolanensis could not be evaluated because only two individuals were available for sequencing. Since its initial description, this taxon has only been documented by five specimens (Gu et al., 2012a), with no subsequent records, suggesting an extremely restricted population size and a likely underappreciated risk of extinction. Although all Paramesotriton species are designated as Class II protected wildlife in China, only four species currently occur within the protected area network of Guizhou (Supplementary Figure S11). These gaps highlight an urgent need to prioritize conservation efforts for populations occurring outside existing reserves.

Paramesotriton wulingensis was excluded from genomic analyses because of limited population size and regulatory constraints. Previous phylogenetic studies have placed P. wulingensis together with P. caudopunctatus in the basal lineage of PCSG (Gu et al., 2012a, 2012b; Luo et al., 2021, 2022; Yuan et al., 2022). Comprehensive inference of biogeographic history and gene flow nevertheless requires full taxon representation. The absence of P. wulingensis introduces some uncertainty, although overall conclusions are unlikely to be substantially altered. Incorporation of this species in future surveys will be essential for refining estimates of introgression and reconstructing the evolutionary and biogeographic histories of PCSG.

Possible ancient hybrid origin of P. zhijinensis is supported by convergent genomic evidence

Independent analytical frameworks converged on a hybrid origin for P. zhijinensis. Although ADMIXTURE inferred only minor ancestry contributions from Paramesotriton sp. and P. longliensis, both PhyloNet-MPL and HyDe recovered approximately equal (~50%) genomic contributions from these two parental lineages (Figure 3E, F). Concordant signals of introgression were further detected by D-statistics (Figure 3B), f4-ratio estimates (Figure 3C), and f-branch analysis, collectively indicating pervasive historical gene exchange. The discrepancy between ADMIXTURE and phylogenetic network-based approaches likely reflects differences in the sensitivity and assumptions of analytical methods. ADMIXTURE relies on allele-frequency clustering optimized for recent population structure (Alexander et al., 2009) and may have limited power to resolve ancient or complex hybridization, especially when admixture signals have been eroded by subsequent recombination, backcrossing, or strong purifying selection following ancestral introgression. In contrast, HyDe and PhyloNet-MPL explicitly model hybridization within a phylogenetic framework based on invariants and network likelihood (Blischak et al., 2018; Than et al., 2008), providing greater sensitivity for detecting deep or ancient hybrid origins. Accordingly, despite weak ADMIXTURE signals, results from HyDe and PhyloNet-MPL provided strong support for an ancient hybrid origin of P. zhijinensis from P. longliensis and Paramesotriton sp. Comparable hybrid ancestries inferred using these approaches have been reported in other taxa, including macaques (Zhang et al., 2023), Asiatic black bears (Zou et al., 2022), South American fur seals (Lopes et al., 2023), and swordtail fish (Du et al., 2024). Another possible explanation for the analytical discrepancy is that RAD-seq datasets can be affected by uneven distribution of restriction enzyme cut sites across the genome (Andrews et al., 2016). If sampled loci fall outside neutrally evolving or actively introgressed genomic regions, or fail to capture parental lineage-specific divergence, ADMIXTURE may underestimate admixture signals and incorrectly assign hybrid taxa as distinct clusters. In contrast, RAD-seq typically yields thousands of SNPs, providing sufficient resolution for HyDe, PhyloNet-MPL, and f-branch analyses to recover coherent signatures of ancient hybridization.

Although morphological intermediacy, including body size (Supplementary Table S11), was consistent with hybrid ancestry between Paramesotriton sp. and P. longliensis, reduced-representation genomic data impose inherent limitations. This underscores the need for integrative validation through high-quality comparative genomes, karyotypic characterization, and reproductive assessments to definitively resolve the evolutionary origin of P. zhijinensis.

Effects of ancestral introgression on divergence time inference

Molecular clock models exert a strong influence on divergence time estimates. Relaxed clock models accommodate lineage-specific rate variation and may better capture natural evolutionary processes (Chen et al., 2021), whereas closely related species often exhibit limited substitution-rate variation (Brown & Yang, 2011; Ho & Duchêne, 2014), favoring strict clock assumptions. Under such conditions, strict molecular clocks frequently demonstrate superior performance (Brown & Yang, 2011; Yang & Rannala, 2006). Similar to SNAPP (Stange et al., 2018), SNAPPER enables highly precise estimation of divergence time using SNP data via the Wright-Fisher diffusion model, which approximates the multi-species coalescent model (Stange et al., 2018; Stoltz et al., 2021). Applications of SNAPP and SNAPPER across diverse animal taxa (Oury et al., 2023; Sánchez et al., 2023; Tunström et al., 2023) further support the reliability of our results.

Ancestral introgression can distort molecular dating by inflating apparent divergence times. Phylogenomic calibration placed the origin of Paramesotriton in the late Oligocene (25.29 Ma), and the most recent common ancestor of PCSG in the early Miocene (16.90 Ma), consistent with previous nuclear DNA-based estimates (Luo et al., 2022). In contrast, interspecific divergence times within PCSG were inferred to be substantially older than the previous mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)-based results (Luo et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2022), with discrepancies approaching 5 Ma, whereas deeper nodes near the phylogenetic root remained broadly consistent. Although mtDNA markers typically evolve rapidly and produce relatively ancient divergence time estimates (Zheng et al., 2011), the opposite pattern emerged here. Interspecific gene flow during early speciation likely promoted mtDNA homogenization, reducing lineage-specific variation and biasing divergence time estimates toward more recent dates (Joly et al., 2009; Rosenzweig et al., 2016), particularly along terminal branches. Therefore, when using mtDNA for molecular dating, especially in rapidly radiating lineages, it is crucial to evaluate potential gene flow to avoid systematic underestimation of divergence times.

Evolution of biodiversity is driven by erosion of heterogeneous bedrock

Diversification within PCSG has been driven by orogeny and monsoonal forcing acting through progressive erosion of heterogeneous rock substrates. The karst mountain systems of Guizhou provide a natural setting for evaluating links between geological processes and biological diversification. Currently, PCSG lineages occupying different tributaries are restricted to exposed carbonate sedimentary formations and are isolated by intervening belts of mixed sedimentary rock (Supplementary Figure S3). This spatial pattern raises a central question: how did ancestral populations achieve basin-wide dispersal prior to establishment of these barriers. Geological reconstructions indicate that the present-day landscape of Guizhou emerged during the late Tertiary following west-to-east tilting uplift (Guizhou Provincial Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources, 1987; Zhang, 2012; Zhou & Chen, 1993), with carbonate sedimentary rock overlying the uppermost mixed strata (Sayre et al., 2014). Subsequent surface erosion exposed the lithological contact zones, and sustained fluvial erosion combined with paleoclimatic forcing drove upstream migration of these boundaries, generating a terrain characterized by higher elevations in the north and lower elevations in the south (Li, 2001; Zhou & Chen, 1993). Although extant PCSG species are now confined to carbonate substrates, orogeny, paleoclimatic oscillation, and long-term erosion have collectively reorganized river networks (Chen, 2019; Guizhou Provincial Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources, 1987; Yang, 1985). Exposure of mixed sedimentary outcrops likely imposed dispersal barriers, promoting isolation among tributary-associated populations and facilitating lineage divergence (Figure 5C; Supplementary Figure S3). These observations support a model in which erosion-driven migration of lithological contact zones fragmented drainage connectivity, promoted isolation among river basins, and served as a key driver of allopatric differentiation within PCSG.

Testing this hypothesis, biogeographic reconstruction integrating spatial distributions with divergence time estimates indicated that origin, dispersal, and diversification of PCSG coincided with major Miocene paleogeoclimatic transitions. These included rapid uplift of the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau (25–15 Ma) (Ding et al., 2022), accelerated left-lateral extrusion of Indochina (35–16 Ma) (Gilley et al., 2003; Leloup et al., 2001), and intensified East Asian monsoon precipitation (23–13 Ma) (Farnsworth et al., 2019; Wan et al., 2025). These geological events are thought to have driven the evolutionary history of multiple biota in and adjacent to this region (Lu et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2022; Min et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2025). Synergistic effects of orogenic activity, warm climatic regimes, and elevated rainfall produced the characteristic regional topography, with higher elevations in northern and western sectors relative to southern and eastern regions (Zhou & Chen, 1993). This geomorphological evolution likely enabled southward range expansion of ancestral PCSG populations, followed by progressive basin isolation and subsequent species diversification. Our robust phylogenetic evidence provides strong support for this erosion-mediated evolutionary scenario.

Diversification rate analyses suggested that the maximal number of observed lineage divergence events per 0.5 million years (MDE) occurred during the early Miocene, with the origin of this peak dating to approximately 18.2 Ma (95% HPD: 19.91–11.62 Ma). Lineage divergence accelerated around 17 Ma and reached an initial peak near 15 Ma (Figure 5E). Thereafter, diversification declined beginning at approximately 14.5 Ma and reached a trough near 12.5 Ma, temporally coincident with deceleration of left-lateral extrusion of Indochina around 16 Ma, transition to right-lateral extrusion by approximately 13 Ma, and sustained Miocene cooling between 15 and 12 Ma (Leloup et al., 1995, 2001; Li et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2020). During subsequent right-lateral extrusion of Indochina between approximately 11 and 5 Ma (Li et al., 2024), coinciding with Ailao Shan orogenic activity between 8.4 and 6.8 Ma (Fyhn & Phach, 2015; Zhang et al., 2009), rapid uplift of the Hengduan Mountains from approximately 11 to 2.5 Ma (Favre et al., 2015; Su et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2012), and intensification of the Asian monsoon between approximately 10 and 4 Ma (Farnsworth et al., 2019), MDE increased sharply again near 11 Ma and culminated in a second peak around 8 Ma. Rapid radiation under these paleogeological and climatic events likely facilitated retention of ancestral polymorphisms across diverging lineages (Suh et al., 2015; Szöllősi et al., 2015), resulting in ILS signals that underlie the observed phylogenetic discordance at key nodes (Figure 3G). The temporal interval between the earliest and most recent divergence events spanned approximately 13.5 Ma. Given estimated erosion rates of ~34–76 m/million years (Chen, 1985, 2019; Yang, 1985), this duration would theoretically result in an elevation difference of 459–1 026 m. This erosion-based elevation range partially overlaps present-day elevational variation across the study region (160–2 400 m) (Li, 2001; Zhang, 2012). Apparent discrepancies may reflect additional processes that enhanced denudation, including glacial incision, tectonic disturbance, and episodes of intensified precipitation (Ji, 1992; Yang, 1985; Zhang, 2012; Zhou & Chen, 1993). Despite these uncertainties, the broad temporal congruence between estimated divergence times and landscape evolution, considered within the context of karst geomorphological development, aligns with phylogenetic evidence and supports a scenario in which long-term topographic erosion contributed substantially to diversification within PCSG.

Lithological heterogeneity can impose powerful constraints on dispersal and thereby promote lineage isolation and diversification. Bedrock composition influences landscape-scale biodiversity by promoting geographically restricted endemism (Rahbek et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2024), and erosion of heterogeneous substrates has been implicated in diversification of North American freshwater fish (Kim et al., 2023; Stokes et al., 2023) and the flora of Madagascar (Liu et al., 2024). Phylogenomic and biogeographic results indicated that progressive erosion of carbonate sedimentary rock facilitated allopatric lineage divergence—and even speciation—in mountain stream salamanders. This diversification mechanism differs from classic orogeny-driven diversification, although erosional dynamics were likely modulated by long-term climatic forcing (Chen, 2019; Wan et al., 2025). The influence of lithology on karst biota is context dependent and varies across taxonomic groups and geological settings (Zhao et al., 2024). For example, the near-continuous carbonate belt extending from northern to southern Guizhou has resulted in minimal genetic differentiation within the Odorrana schmackeri complex (Jiang et al., 2022), illustrating how substrate continuity can facilitate connectivity. Ecological constraints imposed by hydrology further reinforce lithological barriers. PCSG taxa predominantly inhabit slow-flowing streams and lentic pools with low disturbance (Fei & Ye, 2016; Zhao et al., 2012). In contrast, mixed sedimentary terrain is characterized by steep gradients, turbulent currents, and limited sediment accumulation, conditions incompatible with habitat requirements of these salamanders. Consequently, even short geographical distances can translate into effective dispersal barriers when intervening substrates are ecologically unsuitable. Consistent with this pattern, PCSG taxa are scarce in mixed sedimentary landscapes (Figure 5C), where deeply incised gorges generate turbulent high-energy fluvial systems with minimal accumulation of sediment. Nevertheless, the precise mechanistic link between carbonate erosion and PCSG diversification remains multifaceted. Restricted present-day distributions and habitat specificity may reflect not only isolation imposed by substrate transitions but also post-dispersal adaptive processes under erosion-mediated landscape fragmentation (Waters et al., 2020). Progressive denudation of carbonate deposits (Wan et al., 2025), reshaped by Miocene tectonic uplift and monsoon intensification into a north-high-south-low topographic gradient, likely enabled initial southward expansion of ancestral lineages within Clade II. Continued incision, drainage network reorganization, and emergence of mixed sedimentary rock barriers subsequently imposed stronger biogeographic constraints on PCSG, mirroring the abiotic segregation patterns described here. Subterranean or surface hydrological connectivity may also have provided corridors for gene flow, contributing to moderate genetic differentiation (Lin et al., 2023) (Figure 4C) and localized introgression (Figure 4B–G) among species occupying separate surface drainages. For example, based on estimated river erosion rates of 280–1 138 m/million years (Yang, 1985), tributary isolation was estimated to have occurred approximately 4.6–1.5 Ma (Supplementary Table S12), coinciding with the mid-Pliocene (3.6 Ma) uplift of the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau (Chen et al., 2020) and intensified west-to-east tilting and erosion of Guizhou during the Pleistocene (Zhou & Chen, 1993). Notably, this interval overlaps with previous divergence estimates derived from mitochondrial fragments (Luo et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2022), consistent with recent mitochondrial introgression during the Pliocene-Pleistocene transition. Although genetic introgression may have occurred prior to speciation, the impact of geographic isolation is likely to have outweighed the effects of subsurface connectivity in shaping current patterns of divergence. Regardless of the underlying mechanisms, persistent carbonate erosion has provided the essential geomorphological conditions required for the prolonged allopatric diversification within PCSG. Collectively, these results highlight the value of integrating ecological, genomic, and geological evidence to resolve how long-term geophysical processes influence freshwater biodiversity.

CONCLUSIONS

This study established an integrated phylogenomic framework for PCSG and demonstrated that ILS, introgression, and ancient hybridization collectively shaped its complex evolutionary history. Joint analysis of genomic, phylogenetic, and topographic data indicated that erosion of heterogeneous bedrock likely acted as a primary driver of lineage isolation and diversification within PCSG. Demographic reconstructions revealed sustained contraction of effective population size accompanied by low current genetic diversity since the Last Glacial Maximum, in agreement with IUCN threat assessments. Despite clear ecological and evolutionary importance, most taxa remain outside established protected area networks, exposing a significant conservation gap. These results emphasize the urgency of genome-informed conservation strategies, particularly for microendemic and cave-restricted species such as P. maolanensis, to ensure their long-term survival. Overall, our results highlight the value of integrative eco-evolutionary analyses in disentangling complex phylogenetic histories while providing an essential foundation for targeted biodiversity conservation.

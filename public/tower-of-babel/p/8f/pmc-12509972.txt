Hybrid repair of a symptomatic non-A non-B dissection involving a Kommerell’s diverticulum in a right-sided aortic arch anatomy
Authors: Simone Cuozzo, Antonio Luparelli, Ottavia Borghese, Mario Nocerino, Alessandro Cina, Piergiorgio Bruno, Giovanni Tinelli, Yamume Tshomba
Published: 2025

Kommerell's diverticulum associated with a right-sided aortic arch1 is a rare congenital vascular anomaly that may become symptomatic because of aneurysmal degeneration, dissection, or compression of adjacent structures. Management remains challenging because of the anatomical complexity and the surgical risk associated with open repair. In this case, a multidisciplinary team adopted a staged hybrid approach.

The right-sided aortic arch gave rise to the left common carotid artery directly from the ascending aorta, followed by the right common carotid artery (RCCA) and right subclavian artery (RSA), with an aberrant left subclavian artery arising distally and coursing posterior to the esophagus.

The Kommerell's diverticulum arose from a severely angulated arch (A/Cover) and was complicated by a non-A non-B aortic dissection,2 aneurysmal degeneration (transversal diameter 62.7 mm), esophageal compression (A), and persistent thoracic pain.

Surgical debranching of the left common carotid artery and RCCA with RSA transposition to the RCCA, combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (B), was complicated by a significant increase in central venous pressure after sternal closure, which necessitated prompt reintervention.

Revision of venous outflow was achieved with a customized L-T venous bypass using 14-mm Dacron grafts connecting the right atrium to the distal innominate vein (IV, B), along with venous stenting (B), partially relieving extrinsic compression of the right IV and restoring adequate venous return, preventing superior vena cava syndrome3 (B).

Subsequently, a left carotid-subclavian bypass, occlusion of the left subclavian artery with a 10-mm Amplatzer Vascular Plug, and a zone 0 thoracic endovascular aortic repair were performed to achieve aneurysm exclusion, maintain perfusion of the left upper extremity and vertebral circulation, and minimize neurological risks. Percutaneous thoracic endovascular aortic repair was technically demanding because of extreme arch angulation, hampering stent grafts’ advancement (C). Three Lunderquist Extra-Stiff guidewires (Cook Medical Inc) were used to straighten the descending thoracic aorta and mitigate angulations. Nonetheless, the 38-34-259 RelayPro thoracic stent graft (Terumo Aortic; Bolton Medical Inc) failed to reach the proximal landing zone and was deployed distal to the aortic arch curvature (C). Although suboptimally positioned, this improved device trackability and aortic wall stiffness, enabling successful deployment of a 38-38-209 stent graft in zone 0 (C). Postprocedural (D) and 1-month follow-up (D) Computed tomography angiographies confirmed proper stent-graft apposition and successful aneurysm exclusion without a type IA endoleak. Prevalent venous drainage via the hemiazygos-azygos collateral circulation was noted, secondary to residual external compression of the left IV (D). Informed written consent was obtained, and the patient provided consent for publication.

Microdissection of the supraoptic nucleus and posterior pituitary gland from fresh unfixed tissue
Authors: Derick Thompson, Abiodun E Odufuwa, John A Watt
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rat supraoptic nucleus (SON) contains magnocellular neurons that project long axons that terminate in the posterior pituitary gland. To perform molecular characterization of these regions, such as transcriptome and methylome profiling, it is necessary to obtain large quantities of high-quality RNA and DNA. Prior methods to isolate molecular material from these small regions required fixing or freezing and laser microdissection of whole tissue, which can compromise recovery and integrity. We have established a straight-forward method of dissecting out the SON and posterior pituitary gland from fresh, unfixed tissue that allows for the isolation of RNA or DNA without compromising nucleic acid integrity. Furthermore, this method can be used as a framework for the microdissection of any region of the brain to isolate any sensitive material. In this manuscript, we describe step-by-step instructions from the macro scale dissection, to brain sectioning, and finally the microdissection of the appropriate tissue.•Transcardial perfusion without fixative prevents the shortcomings of nucleic acid cross-linking.•A fast method and the maintenance of tissue in ice-cold HBSS during dissection and sectioning prevents nucleic acid degradation.•A vibratome is used for the sectioning of fresh brain tissue without freezing or gelatin embedding (i.e. cryostat or microtome).Method name: Microdissection of the supraoptic nucleus and posterior pituitary glandKeywords: Supraoptic nucleus, Posterior pituitary gland, Microdissection

Method validation

The microdissection of small brain regions is often time- and resource-consuming. In many cases, this requires fixation or freezing of tissue prior to microdissection. Fixatives, such as paraformaldehyde, are commonly used to preserve the tissue necessary for the long sectioning and dissecting steps performed during these experiments. This preserves the tissue through the cross-linking of proteins and nucleic acids. However, many studies have shown that this negatively impacts nucleic acid yield and quality [3], [4], [5]. This is especially important when considering experiments with RNA, as RNA is highly sensitive and vulnerable to degradation. This is evident with much lower RNA integrity number (RIN) values in formalin fixed tissue compared to fresh tissue [5]. While freezing allows for the preservation of tissue without chemical modifications, thawing the tissue reduces nucleic acid integrity. Experiments using a cryostat first need to quickly freeze large brain blocks in liquid nitrogen or dry ice. This has the potential to crack the tissue through the area of interest, preventing accurate dissection. Furthermore, cryostat sectioning introduces freeze/thaw cycles – sectioned tissue will be thawed in order to dehydrate and dissect the necessary region. Together, these two commonly used methods for tissue dissection have many shortcomings that much research has gone into to overcome.

Our method does not rely on fixatives. It also allows for frozen storage of samples after dissection, preventing the negative impacts of freeze/thaw cycles. When the tissue is needed for nucleic acid isolation, the tissue remains frozen and is ground to a fine powder in liquid nitrogen followed directly by a lysis buffer such as trizol – thus the tissue does not enter a thaw cycle. This method was successfully used in a paper submitted alongside this manuscript, Thompson et al. [1]. In that publication, we obtained high quality RNA and DNA to produce complex libraries necessary for in-depth sequencing. RNA integrity numbers were between 8.8 and 10.0. DNA integrity numbers were between 9.5 and 9.7. The posterior pituitary gland is anatomically distinct and can be accurately dissected. However, the SON is less distinct and thus we performed RT-qPCR on SON samples to determine accuracy of our dissections. In our prior publication, the observed oxytocin and vasopressin transcript levels in SON samples were approximately 1000-fold higher compared to adjacent cortex samples. Additionally, as the SON is comprised predominately of neurons and astrocytes, RNA-seq analysis revealed greater abundance of neuronal and astroglial transcript markers. This demonstrates the viability and efficiency of this method to accurately microdissect small brain regions to be used for molecular research.

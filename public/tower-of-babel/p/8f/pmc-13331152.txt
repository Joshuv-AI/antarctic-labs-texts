Introduction

Infertility, defined as a disease of the reproductive system characterised by the failure to achieve a clinical pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse, represents a significant global health challenge.1 It is estimated to affect 8%–12% of reproductive-aged couples worldwide, with epidemiological data indicating a particularly high burden within regions including the Middle East.1 The investigation and management of this condition extend beyond a biological imperative, intersecting profoundly with psychosocial well-being and societal structures.

The importance and necessity of studying infertility are magnified in specific sociocultural contexts where pronatalist values are predominant. In Iran, cultural and religious expectations prioritise family and procreation, making infertility a profound personal hardship that frequently leads to marital strain and societal stigma for those impacted.2 Consequently, the experience of undergoing infertility treatment is not merely a clinical sequence but a complex, lived journey laden with emotional, financial and relational challenges.3

Focusing specifically on infertility treatment experiences among men, women and couples reveals distinct yet interconnected dimensions. Research highlights that women often report profound emotional distress, a sense of identity loss and gendered societal pressure,4 while men may experience feelings of inadequacy, shame and a perceived threat to masculinity.5 For couples, the protracted and often invasive nature of assisted reproductive technology (ART) treatments can strain communication, intimacy and shared coping resources, creating a unique relational dynamic that requires dedicated understanding.6

A substantial body of qualitative research has been conducted in Iran to explore these multifaceted dimensions, yielding valuable evidence on topics such as psychological distress, financial burdens, marital dynamics and the influence of religious coping mechanisms.3 4 7 8 These studies provide critical insights into the subjective, contextual realities faced by those navigating infertility care within the Iranian healthcare system.

However, a critical appraisal of the existing evidence reveals significant weaknesses and gaps. While individual qualitative studies offer depth on specific aspects, the findings remain fragmented across numerous publications. This dispersion limits the ability to derive higher-order interpretations, identify consistent themes and discern contradictions or gaps across the literature. There is currently no comprehensive, systematic integration of this qualitative evidence that synthesises the collective knowledge to present a unified, more complete picture of the treatment experience for Iranian couples. This lack of synthesis represents a missed opportunity for evidence consolidation, ultimately restricting the translational impact of this rich qualitative data on clinical guidelines, supportive care interventions and health policy.

Therefore, the necessity of conducting a systematic review and meta-synthesis of qualitative evidence is unequivocal. This methodology is specifically designed to rigorously aggregate, interpret and reconceptualise findings from separate qualitative studies.9 By applying this approach, it is possible to move beyond the narrative summary of individual studies to develop new integrative insights, conceptual models and a more profound explanatory understanding of the phenomenon. In the context of Iranian infertility care, such a synthesis is imperative to inform the development of culturally congruent, patient-centred care pathways, improve clinician-patient communication and advocate for policies that address the documented psychosocial and financial burdens.10 It directly addresses the gap between isolated qualitative findings and their application in shaping a more responsive and supportive healthcare ecosystem.

This protocol aims to conduct a systematic review and meta-synthesis of qualitative evidence on the experiences of individuals and couples undergoing infertility treatment in Iran. By integrating existing qualitative findings, the study seeks to develop a comprehensive and interpretive understanding of the challenges, needs, coping strategies and psychosocial impacts associated with infertility treatment in this context. Particular attention will be given to sociocultural and religious perspectives on ARTs, including acceptability of gamete donation, as well as structural determinants such as financial access to in vitro fertilisation (IVF) services and regional variability in funding. Ultimately, the review is intended to strengthen the evidence base and inform clinical practice and health policy.

Methods and analysis

Review method

This protocol will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines (online supplemental file 1).11 12 The development of this protocol followed standard systematic review methodologies. The findings of the review will be presented following the PRISMA guidelines and the Joanna Briggs Institute guidance for systematic reviews of qualitative evidence.13 Similarly, other researchers have also employed this methodology in their systematic reviews.14,16

To ensure methodological transparency and avoid unintended duplication, we prospectively registered the protocol for our systematic review—‘Infertility treatment experiences in Iran: A systematic review and meta-synthesis of qualitative evidence’—with International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO), assigning it the unique registration number CRD420261390890 (accessible at: https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420261390890).

Research questions

This systematic review and meta-synthesis will address the following primary research question: What experiences, challenges, and coping strategies do individuals and couples undergoing infertility treatment in Iran describe?

Additionally, the review will explore how socioreligious beliefs regarding ARTs (eg, gamete donation) and financial or structural factors (eg, IVF funding and access) influence these experiences.

Eligibility criteria

This systematic review and meta-synthesis will employ the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework as its guiding methodological structure to systematically examine infertility treatment experiences in Iran.17 Here, the ‘Population’ encompasses infertile men, women and couples navigating treatment within the Iranian context. The ‘Concept’ centres on their lived experiences, challenges, perceptions and outcomes associated with infertility treatments, as captured through qualitative inquiry. The ‘Context’ pertains to Iran’s healthcare services, cultural norms and socio-economic factors that influence these experiences. Similarly, the PCC framework was used in this reviews.18 19

By leveraging the PCC framework, this review establishes a precise yet adaptable scope, allowing for a nuanced synthesis of qualitative findings. Unlike more rigid models such as Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes, and Study desgin (PICOS) 20,23 or Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation, and Research type (SPIDER),24 the PCC better suits the exploratory nature of qualitative evidence on this topic, enabling deeper insights into personal narratives and contextual influences to guide clinical practice and policy in Iran.

Search strategy and information sources

We began with a scoping search to pinpoint key terms, synonyms and phrases linked to infertility treatment experiences, patient perspectives and contextual factors in Iran. This foundational phase sharpened our conceptual framework and broadened topic coverage. Working closely with a specialist librarian, we then crafted a robust search protocol that integrated controlled vocabularies—like Medical Subject Headings (MeSH)—with free-text terms. Through iterative team reviews and input from content experts, we fine-tuned the strategy to balance high recall with precision.

Our comprehensive literature search will span major international databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus and Web of Science, from inception through August 2026 without date limits. The systematic search will be initiated in May 2026 and completed by the end of August 2026. The search will be conducted across the selected databases during this period, with the final search date documented in the review report to ensure transparency and reproducibility. For Iran-specific insights, we will query national repositories such as the Scientific Information Database and MagIran. The core strategy was piloted in MEDLINE and then customised for each platform’s syntax and indexing conventions (online supplemental file 2). To counter publication bias, grey literature will be explored via Google Scholar and ClinicalTrials.gov, guided by evidence-based retrieval approaches outlined by Bramer et al25 Electronic searches will be augmented by hand-screening bibliographies of selected studies to capture any overlooked work.26 Given Google Scholar’s limitations, we will restrict results to the top 100 hits ordered by relevance, promoting both feasibility and replicability.27 28

Study selection process

We will import all records from database searches into EndNote (V.9.3, Clarivate Analytics) to remove duplicates. Our selection process will follow PRISMA guidelines in a three-step sequence. First, two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts for eligibility. Next, they will review full texts of shortlisted studies, recording reasons for any exclusions. To capture additional relevant studies, we will manually check reference lists and forward citations of included articles. If key details are missing—such as from abstracts only—we will email corresponding authors for clarification or full texts. Reviewers will resolve discrepancies through discussion, or by involving a third reviewer if needed. The full process, including numbers at each stage, will appear in a PRISMA flow diagram for full transparency.

Quality appraisal

Two independent reviewers (MAB, SGH) will evaluate the methodological quality of included studies using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) checklist for qualitative research.29 They will resolve discrepancies by discussion, consulting a third reviewer if consensus proves elusive. To gauge inter-rater reliability, we will calculate Cohen’s kappa (κ) as the primary measure of agreement beyond chance, with the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) provided as a secondary statistic.30 31 Kappa values of 0.69–0.79 signal moderate agreement, while ≥0.80 denotes good agreement; an ICC >0.75 reflects strong reliability.32 These measures will safeguard the appraisal’s consistency and trustworthiness.

Data synthesis approach

We chose content analysis for this review as it offers a systematic yet adaptable method to identify patterns and quantify their prevalence across qualitative studies on infertility treatment experiences. This approach strikes a balance between the structured categorisation of thematic synthesis and the interpretive nuance of meta-ethnography, making it ideal for distilling barriers and facilitators from diverse Iranian contexts.33 34 The first author, with expertise in qualitative synthesis, will lead initial coding and theme development, followed by rigorous review and refinement by the second author to bolster reliability. We will start by immersing ourselves in the data to grasp its full scope, then derive preliminary categories.35 Thematic saturation will guide progression—we will track new theme emergence and halt coding once patterns stabilise.36 37 38 Studies will be mapped to these categories, with ongoing refinement to sharpen insights. A priori sensitising concepts will include (but not be limited to) socioreligious attitudes toward gamete donation (eg, sperm, oocyte or embryo donation), ethical considerations related to lineage and acceptability and structural determinants such as cost of treatment, insurance coverage and regional disparities in access to IVF services. The second author’s independent audit of codes will ensure consensus on the final framework, promoting a robust, transparent synthesis of experiences.

Assessment of confidence in the synthesis findings

We will apply the Confidence in the Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative Research framework to gauge confidence in our meta-synthesis findings, determining how solidly each conclusion about Iranian infertility experiences stands up.39 This involves scrutinising four elements: methodological strengths and weaknesses of included studies, how well the data coheres around each theme, the volume and depth of supporting evidence and its fit to Iran’s unique healthcare and cultural landscape. Each theme will receive a confidence grade (high, moderate, low or very low), with clear explanations of any downgrades—like thin data from rural areas or conflicting interpretations. We will present this in a summary table alongside themes, making our judgments fully traceable for readers.

The committee determined that the study complied with ethical standards because it is based solely on published literature and does not involve direct contact with human participants, primary data collection or any clinical procedures.

Dissemination

The results of this systematic review and meta-synthesis, which explore patient experiences, barriers, facilitators and outcomes in infertility treatments in Iran, will be submitted for publication in a leading peer-reviewed journal focused on health services research, public health or health policy. To broaden reach, key findings will also be presented at national and international conferences targeting researchers, healthcare providers and policymakers in reproductive health. Further efforts will involve sharing insights via seminars and journal clubs at academic and clinical settings involved in infertility care and family health research. This multifaceted strategy aims to enhance accessibility, support knowledge translation and guide evidence-informed policies and practices among varied stakeholders.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-synthesis will offer a comprehensive synthesis of qualitative evidence on infertility treatment experiences among Iranian men, women, and couples, highlighting emotional, social and relational dimensions often overshadowed in quantitative infertility research. Prior studies have primarily examined isolated aspects such as women’s emotional distress,40 empowerment needs in couples41 or support gaps post-failure,42 yet this protocol integrates these to reveal interconnected treatment trajectories unique to Iran’s cultural context.

Synthesising experiences from diverse Iranian settings will uncover shared themes like stigma, relational strain and resilience strategies, as seen in explorations of support needs,43 women’s lived realities44 45 and social consequences.7 46 This approach extends beyond individual studies on dropout factors47 or grounded theories of confrontation,48 providing a holistic framework for culturally attuned care. The meta-synthesis will illuminate how treatment processes influence gender dynamics, including women’s challenges in male infertility scenarios.49

Adherence to PRISMA guidelines and thematic synthesis methods ensures methodological rigour, with dual screening and quality appraisal via CASP to minimise bias. Inclusion of Persian-language studies from multiple databases captures context-specific insights, enhancing transferability within Iran’s sociocultural landscape. Prospective registration and team reflexivity further bolster transparency and credibility.

The focus on peer-reviewed qualitative studies may overlook grey literature or quantitative data on treatment outcomes, potentially limiting the breadth of interpretive insights. Heterogeneity in primary study methodologies and sample sizes could affect synthesis depth, while reliance on published findings risks omitting negative or null experiences. Language restrictions to English and Persian might exclude other regional dialects, though this aligns with the Iranian context.

Findings will inform patient-centred interventions, such as counselling protocols addressing stigma and dropout,7 47 48 and guide policy for accessible fertility services in Iran. By advancing qualitative evidence on infertility, this review supports health professionals in fostering empowerment and relational support,41 43 ultimately improving treatment adherence and well-being.

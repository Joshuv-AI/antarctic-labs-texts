Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension in Latin America: Characteristics, Treatment, and Survival From the LATAM‐PAH Meta‐Analysis and Systematic Review
Authors: Marcos Vinicius Fernandes Garcia, Katherine Julian, Evans R. Fernández Pérez, Rogerio Souza, Tim Lahm
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) in Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC) remains poorly characterized, with limited regional data on clinical presentation, hemodynamics, treatment patterns, and outcomes. We aimed to characterize demographic features, clinical presentation, hemodynamic severity, treatment patterns, and survival outcomes of right heart catheterization‐confirmed PAH cohorts across LAC. We performed a systematic review and meta‐analysis of retrospective and prospective cohort studies of adults with right heart catheterization‐confirmed PAH in LAC, searching six databases in English, Spanish, and Portuguese without date restrictions. Study‐level aggregate data were pooled using random‐effects models. Sixteen studies from eight countries (1999–2025) including 2,553 patients were analyzed. Mean age was 43.6 years and 81.5% were female. Etiologies included idiopathic PAH (40.3%), congenital heart disease‐associated PAH (25.6%), connective tissue disease‐associated PAH (20.6%), and schistosomiasis‐associated PAH (15.3% in Brazilian cohorts). At diagnosis, 51% were in functional class III/IV, mean 6‐min walk distance was 406 m, mean pulmonary arterial pressure was 55.8 mmHg, and cardiac index was 2.63 L/min/m2. Treatment consisted primarily of monotherapy (56%) or dual therapy (33%). Pooled survival was 93.9% at 1 year, 87.5% at 2 years, and 83.0% at 3 years. Predominantly reflecting South American PAH care, our findings establish the first regional benchmark for PAH in LAC. Patients are younger and predominantly female, with a high burden of CHD‐PAH and schistosomiasis‐associated PAH. Despite delayed diagnosis and limited combination therapy access, survival is comparable to high‐income registries, highlighting priorities such as earlier referral, risk assessment, expanded therapy access, and prospective registry development.Trail RegistrationPROSPERO CRD420251131636.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive disorder characterized by remodeling and narrowing of the pulmonary vasculature, ultimately resulting in right heart failure and premature death [1]. While contemporary registries from Europe and North America have improved understanding of PAH characteristics and outcomes, comparable data from Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC), where approximately 670 million people live (8.1% of the total world population), remain limited [2, 3, 4].

PAH care in LAC occurs within a heterogeneous landscape marked by geographic variation, socioeconomic inequality and uneven access to specialized services. Diagnostic resources such as echocardiography and right heart catheterization (RHC), availability of PAH therapies, and access to expert referral centers vary widely across countries and are concentrated in urban areas. These factors contribute to delayed diagnosis and variability in treatment strategies and outcomes across the region [2, 4, 5]. A better understanding of the features and characteristics of PAH care in LAC may allow for earlier diagnosis, better treatments, and improved outcomes.

Initiatives such as the Global Burden of Disease study [6] and multiple regional registries have attempted to describe PAH in LAC. However, existing data are fragmented and constrained by small sample sizes, heterogeneous designs, reliance on administrative coding, inconsistent or no confirmation by RHC and limited reporting of treatment patterns. As a result, no consolidated regional benchmark exists to characterize PAH clinical profiles, risk features, treatment or survival across LAC [2, 3].

To address this gap, we conducted a systematic review and meta‐analysis of RHC‐confirmed PAH cohorts from LAC. By pooling study‐level data across countries, this study provides the first comprehensive characterization of PAH in LAC in a large cohort of > 2,500 patients, establishing a benchmark to inform clinical PH care, health policy, and future research in the region.

Methods

We conducted a systematic review and meta‐analysis of cohort studies to characterize demographics, PAH subtypes, clinical and hemodynamic features, treatment use, and survival among adults with PAH (Group 1 pulmonary hypertension) in LAC.

Search Strategy and Selection Criteria

This study was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251131636). We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, MedlinePlus, the Cochrane Library, LILACS, and SciELO in English, Spanish, and Portuguese, without date restrictions. Relevant keywords were used to identify studies reporting PAH cohorts in LAC, and additional records were identified through manual reference screening. LAC was defined according to the World Bank classification, including Mexico, Central America, South America, and Caribbean countries. The full search strategy is provided in Table S1.

We included retrospective or prospective cohort studies enrolling adults (≥ 18 years) with RHC‐confirmed PAH. Studies were classified as incident if more than 50% of enrolled patients received their PAH diagnosis within 6 months of registry inclusion, and as prevalent otherwise. This 6‐month window was applied across all included studies based on reported enrollment procedures, and studies that did not explicitly report timing were classified according to their stated registry design. The study‐level classification was used solely for inclusion purposes; individual studies classified as incident may still include a minority of prevalent patients. We excluded studies enrolling mixed populations (WHO PH groups 2–5), studies lacking RHC confirmation, review articles, and case reports.

Data Collection

Two independent reviewers (MG and KJ) screened the identified studies based on title and abstract. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or by consulting a third author. All included studies enrolled consecutive or defined patient cohorts with longitudinal follow‐up and were assessed for quality using the Newcastle‐Ottawa Scale (NOS). Extracted data included study design characteristics, patient demographics, PAH subtype, clinical and hemodynamic variables, treatment patterns, and survival outcomes. Detailed variable definitions, data extraction procedures, and quality assessment criteria are provided in the Supplement (Tables S2 and S3).

Outcomes of Interest

The primary outcome was the pooled 1‐, 2‐, and 3‐year survival in patients with PAH, analyzed by study and grouped by country. Secondary outcomes included PAH subtypes, clinical and hemodynamic characteristics such as WHO FC, 6MWD, hemodynamics and treatment patterns.

Statistical Analysis and Sensitivity Analysis

Categorical outcomes were summarized as study‐level proportions, while continuous outcomes were summarized as means with standard deviations. Pooled estimates were calculated using both common‐effects and random‐effects models; the random‐effects model was the primary estimate for all outcomes, with the common‐effects model reported as a sensitivity reference. Between‐study variance was estimated using DerSimonian‐Laird for continuous outcomes and survival proportions, and using a generalized linear mixed model with logit link (GLMM/logit) for binary proportions to prevent boundary estimates. Heterogeneity was assessed using I2, Cochran's Q‐test, and Tau2, with prediction intervals calculated using the Inverse‐Variance Method. Values of 25%, 50%, and 75% were interpreted as low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively, guiding model selection [7].

An exploratory three‐strata risk contextualization based on ESC/ERS guideline cutoffs was performed using study‐level mean values. Risk categories were assigned for each available parameter (functional class, right atrial pressure, cardiac index, 6MWD, and BNP). Results were summarized and visualized using heatmaps at both the study level and country level. Studies were included if at least three parameters were reported.

This meta‐analysis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to ensure transparent reporting [8, 9]. Potential publication bias and small‐study effects were assessed using funnel plots and Egger's test [10].

Sensitivity analyses were performed to assess the robustness of key findings across study design, size, quality, geography, and cohort type. Additionally, to verify that study‐level pooled survival estimates reflected plausible patient‐level outcomes, we generated pseudo–individual patient data (IPD) from reported KM survival curves and performed 10,000 bootstrap resamples to derive empirical 95% CI and compared them to the pooled study‐level estimates [11].

All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical computing software (version 4.3.1; R Core Team, 2025). Meta‐analysis was performed using the meta and metafor packages. Data visualization was performed using dplyr, tidyr, and ggplot2. Forest plots, bar plots, LOESS trend plots, and heatmaps were generated to summarize pooled estimates and temporal trends. IRB approval is not required as this study used aggregated data from previously published studies.

Results

The initial database search yielded 330 articles. After title and abstract screening, 126 articles were selected for full‐text review, of which 16 studies met the inclusion criteria. All included cohorts were observational; 11 were retrospective and 5 were prospective. Ten were single‐center, and six were multicenter. No controlled trials were identified. Study selection process is summarized in the PRISMA diagram (Figure 1).

PRISMA Flow Diagram.

The 16 included studies encompassed data from Argentina [12, 13, 14], Brazil [15, 16, 17, 18], Chile [19, 20, 21], Colombia [22, 23], Ecuador [24], Mexico [25], Peru [26], and Uruguay [27]. A total of 2,553 participants with RHC–confirmed PAH were included, with enrollment spanning 1999 to 2025, with studies published between 2006 and 2025. Sample sizes varied from 17 to 619 participants, reflecting both small single‐center cohorts and larger multicenter registries (Table S4). Included centers spanned a wide range of elevations, from 10 meters above sea level (Porto Alegre, Brazil) to 2,850 meters (Quito, Ecuador), with four cohorts located above 1,500 meters (Bogotá, Colombia; Mexico City, Mexico; and Quito, Ecuador).

Demographics

Across studies, the pooled mean age was 43.6 years (95% CI, 42.7–44.6). Overall, 81.5% of patients were female (95% CI, 77.6–84.8%), and the mean BMI was 25.1 kg/m2 (95% CI, 24.7–25.6) (Table 1). The mean time from symptom onset to diagnosis was 24.5 months (95% CI, 19.2–29.9), and the mean follow‐up duration was 47.4 months (95% CI, 40.4–54.4).

Hemodynamics and Clinical Risk Assessment at Diagnosis

Overall, 51% of patients were classified as WHO FC III or IV with significant heterogeneity. Colombia and Mexico reported less than 40% in these classes, whereas Argentina and Chile had pooled proportions exceeding 60%.

Between 2004 and 2017, the weighted proportion of patients presenting with functional class (FC) III–IV at diagnosis showed a declining trend (Figure S1). The annual decrease in FC III–IV proportion was 0.8% per year (p < 0.001). The pooled 6MWD was 406 m (95% CI: 378–435), with notable variability across studies, ranging from a mean of 363 m in Chile to 472 m in Ecuador (Figure S2).

Pooled hemodynamic parameters demonstrated advanced pulmonary vascular disease at diagnosis, with mPAP of 56 mmHg, PVR of 1066 dyn·s·cm−5, and CI of 2.63 L/min/m2. BNP was reported in a limited number of studies, with a pooled mean of 176 pg/mL (95% CI, 49–302, I2 = 93.7%, 5 studies), and should be interpreted with caution (Table 1, Figures S3–S7).

ESC/ERS guideline–based risk mapping using available study‐level parameters demonstrated that most cohorts were predominantly mapped within the intermediate‐risk range, although substantial heterogeneity was observed across studies and countries (Figure 3). Four countries were excluded from the heatmap due to insufficient reportable risk parameters (< 3 available).

PAH Treatments

PAH treatment patterns varied widely across cohorts. Overall, monotherapy predominated, with 56% of patients receiving single‐agent therapy, followed by dual therapy in 33% and triple therapy in only 3% of patients (Table 1). Calcium channel blockers were used in 13% of patients. Treatment patterns were similar between incident and prevalent cohorts, with no significant differences in the use of calcium channel blockers or combination therapy. Era‐stratified analysis showed no significant difference in monotherapy rates between pre‐2012 and post‐2012 cohorts (59.2% vs 52.9%, p = 0.693), with no significant differences in dual or triple therapy use across eras.

Survival

Pooled survival across 1,719 patients from 11 Latin American studies was 93.9% at 1 year, 87.5% at 2 years, and 83.0% at 3 years (Figure 4) [12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 27]. The remaining five studies did not report Kaplan‐Meier curves or time‐to‐event data. While 1‐year survival remained stable over time, longer‐term survival improved significantly: 2‐year survival increased by 0.16% annually (p = 0.011) and 3‐year survival by 0.21% annually (p < 0.001) (Figure 5). Sensitivity Analysis

Main findings were robust across multiple sensitivity analyses (Tables S5–S6). Studies with ≥ 100 patients and high‐quality studies (NOS ≥ 6) yielded estimates nearly identical to those of the overall analysis. However, prospective studies and incident cohorts showed worse functional capacity at diagnosis compared with retrospective studies and prevalent cohorts: prevalence of FC III‐IV was 59%–60% versus 46%–50%, and 6MWD was 369–380 m versus 408–410 m (p < 0.05 for interactions). Demographics remained consistent across all subgroups.

Survival estimates were comparable between incident and prevalent cohorts at all timepoints (incident: 94.0%, 87.7%, 82.0%; prevalent: 94.2%, 90.2%, 84.2% at 1, 2, and 3 years respectively; Table S6) with overlapping confidence intervals throughout. Formal stratification of survival by center altitude was not feasible, as only one high‐altitude cohort reported survival data, precluding meaningful group comparison. Bootstrap validation using pseudo‐individual patient data confirmed the reliability of study‐level survival estimates, with 95% confidence intervals fully encompassing pooled values at all timepoints (Figure S11). Funnel plot analysis suggested possible small‐study effects for 1‐year survival (Egger's test p= 0.005), but no publication bias was detected for 2‐ or 3‐year survival (Figure S12–S14).

Discussion

This study provides the first comprehensive characterization of PAH across Latin America, pooling data from 2,553 patients across eight countries over two decades. We identified a distinct regional epidemiology: patients are younger than in other parts of the world, predominantly female and present typically with intermediate‐risk profiles. BMI in the LAC cohort was lower than Western registries (average 28–29 kg/m2) [28, 29, 30]. The subtype distribution differs markedly from high‐income registries, with CHD‐PAH comprising 26% of cases and schistosomiasis‐PAH accounting for 15% of Brazilian cohorts, patterns not seen in North America, Europe, or Asia.

Most LAC patients present in WHO FC III or IV. Treatment was dominated by monotherapy, with limited use of combination therapy, and considerable variability across countries. Survival at 1, 2, and 3 years was 94%, 88%, and 83%, respectively, with modest improvements over time. Sensitivity analyses confirmed these findings were consistent across study size, quality, enrollment, design, and publication year.

The apparent paradox of worse functional status in incident cohorts yet comparable survival across cohort types is clinically plausible. Incident cohorts capture patients at diagnosis when functional class reflects presentation severity rather than treatment trajectory. Additionally, incident cohorts in our analysis tend to be more recent, enrolling patients during a period of earlier referral and improved diagnostic awareness, which may paradoxically capture more patients at advanced functional class while simultaneously benefiting from more contemporary management.

A striking finding of this systematic review is the disproportionate representation of CHD‐associated PAH in LAC. This contrasts with North American and European registries, where CTD‐PAH surpasses CHD‐PAH in prevalence [29]. The burden of CHD‐PAH in LAC reflects lack of prenatal screening, late diagnosis, geographic and financial barriers to specialized care, limited surgical capacity, and delayed or absent repair [31, 32, 33]. Similar to LAC, large studies from Asia indicate that CHD‐PAH remains one of the predominant subtypes in resource‐limited settings [34]. Emerging models incorporating population‐based screening programs, telemedicine, and hospital partnerships may help reduce the burden of CHD‐PAH in these regions [34].

Sch‐PAH, reported exclusively in Brazilian studies, represents another unique regional feature. Overall, Sch‐PAH is underrepresented in LAC cohorts, which mainly capture patients from urban centers. Outside Brazil, schistosomiasis transmission has been reported in Venezuela, Suriname, and parts of the Caribbean, but published Sch‐PAH data are lacking, making Brazil the only country with systematic data [35]. A high index of suspicion for Sch‐PAH should be maintained when patients present with cardiovascular symptoms in schistosomiasis‐endemic areas.

Roughly half of LAC PAH patients were in WHO FC III–IV, yet the pooled 6MWD exceeded 400 m, higher than in international cohorts. This discordance likely reflects protective factors such as younger age, lower prevalence of obesity, lower comorbidity burden, and potentially altitude‐related differences, but could also point toward referral or survival bias [36]. Notably, incident cohorts in our analysis, which tend to be more recent, showed higher FC III–IV prevalence (59%) compared with prevalent cohorts (46%), consistent with the declining trend in advanced FC at presentation observed over time in LAC.

LAC patient's hemodynamics are comparable or slightly worse than contemporary registries. LAC pooled mPAP 55.8 mmHg and CI 2.63 L/min/m2 are comparable to REVEAL (mPAP 50 mmHg, CI 2.3 L/min/m2) [28], PH association registry (mPAP 49 mmHg, CI 2.3 L/min/m2) [30] and modestly worse than COMPERA (median mPAP 44 mmHg, CI 2.3 L/min/m2) [29]. The combination of younger age and relatively preserved CI may help explain why some patients maintain functional capacity, such as higher 6MWD [36], despite elevated PVR at presentation.

The exploratory ESC/ERS risk mapping shows that the typical LAC patient falls into the intermediate‐risk category. Note that in this exploratory risk assessment evaluation functional class III–IV is presented as a single category, so high‐risk patients cannot be separately identified for this variable. This is consistent with multiple international registries that used 3‐strata risk assessment tools, indicating that intermediate‐risk is typically the most frequent stratum at diagnosis, with prevalence rates ranging from 52% to 76% [29, 30, 37]. Importantly, the prognostic thresholds used in contemporary PAH risk stratification have never been validated in LAC populations.

The regional CAPRI registry followed intermediate‐risk patients in Colombia and showed that only 40% of the population achieved low‐risk status, mirroring findings from European registries [38]. Additionally, poor adoption of risk assessment across LAC represents a major barrier to PAH care, leading to missed opportunities for timely interventions. A real‐world survey across centers in Argentina, Brazil, Colombia and Mexico showed that only 28% of patients underwent risk assessment evaluation in 12 months follow‐up [5]. Together, these findings underscore an actionable need to implement guideline‐based risk assessment strategies across LAC.

Treatment patterns revealed substantial gaps between guideline recommendations and real‐world practice. Monotherapy predominated, and only about one‐third of patients received dual therapy. The predominance of monotherapy reflects both the historical enrollment period of most included studies and persistent structural barriers to combination therapy access across the region. Era‐stratified analysis showed no significant difference in monotherapy rates between pre‐2012 and post‐2012 cohorts, suggesting that treatment gaps in LAC cannot be attributed to historical enrollment alone.

Similar underutilization of combination therapy has been reported in international registries [29, 30]; though in LAC this pattern is compounded by historical limitations in drug availability and access. These findings highlight a critical implementation gap, as early combination therapy for intermediate‐risk PAH is now the standard of care.

Survival in LAC cohorts is comparable to, or exceeds, that reported in European and North American registries and overall survival in LAC has been improving since the 1980s [4]. Importantly, sensitivity analyses demonstrated comparable survival between incident and prevalent cohorts at all timepoints, arguing against a meaningful contribution of immortal time bias to the observed survival estimates.

It remains unclear how much of this reflects gains in early referral, diagnosis, and treatment over the past two decades. It is more plausible that LAC phenotypic characteristics, such as younger age at diagnosis, lower BMI and comorbidity burden, and the relatively high prevalence of CHD‐PAH may confer a survival advantage. Additionally, there is a potential survival benefit noted in Hispanic patients [39] when compared to non‐Hispanics. Those factors may contribute to favorable outcomes, offsetting delayed diagnosis and limited access to therapy.

The regional impact of nutritional factors, genetic admixture, and altitude on PAH course and diagnosis remains unknown. Recognized PAH centers across Latin America span elevations from sea level to over 2,800 meters, where chronic hypoxic exposure may independently modify hemodynamic presentation, diagnostic thresholds, and treatment response in ways that remain poorly characterized and this represents an important area for future investigation.

Several challenges remain in PAH management across LAC. The time to PAH diagnosis averages 2 years, highlighting the need for earlier recognition. Access to RHC and PAH‐specific drugs is inconsistent, reflecting socioeconomic inequities across the region which further limits use of combination therapy. Data quality is limited by small sample sizes, single‐center cohorts, and inconsistent reporting of biomarkers [2]. Regional disparities are also evident: Brazil, Argentina, Mexico, Chile and Colombia have more structured PAH programs while other countries data is underrepresented or absent.

Emerging therapies such as sotatercept, the first activin signaling inhibitor approved for PAH, represent a potentially transformative advance [40]; however, practical availability across LAC remains limited. Despite progressing regulatory approvals in key countries, access is largely restricted to patients in PH centers with private coverage or through high‐cost medication protocols, processes that are burdensome and time‐consuming [41].

This study has several strengths. By including multiple countries and > 2500 patients with RHC‐confirmed PAH, it captures diverse patient populations and provides a harmonized and comprehensive synthesis of studies spanning two decades. Sensitivity analyses support the robustness of demographic, functional, and survival estimates, establishing a regional benchmark for future PAH research and outcomes monitoring.

However, several limitations merit consideration. First, geographic representation is concentrated in South America, particularly Brazil and Argentina, which together account for the majority of included studies and patients. Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, Ecuador, and Uruguay contribute limited data. Critically, Central America and the Caribbean are entirely absent from the available literature, reflecting the current distribution of established PAH programs and indexed registry data across the region. Caution is warranted when extrapolating these findings to the broader LAC region until prospective multicenter registries encompassing underrepresented countries are established. Second, high heterogeneity (I2 > 75% for most key variables) limits the interpretability of pooled estimates as reflections of a single underlying value; however, pooling is justified in this context as the primary goal is regional benchmarking rather than estimation of a homogeneous effect, random‐effects models explicitly account for between‐study variability, and country‐level subgroup estimates are provided. Third, the enrollment period spanning 1999 to 2025 introduces temporal confounding, particularly for treatment patterns, which reflect both historical enrollment periods and persistent structural barriers to combination therapy access. Lastly, altitude varies substantially across included centers and its independent effect on hemodynamic presentation and outcomes remains uncharacterized.

In Latin America, PAH affects younger, predominantly female patients with fewer comorbidities who often present at intermediate risk, with a notably high burden of CHD‐PAH and Sch‐PAH. Despite diagnostic delays and predominant reliance on monotherapy, survival outcomes are comparable to other large registries. Critical priorities include earlier recognition, routine implementation of risk assessment tools, expanded access to RHC and PAH‐targeted therapies, and strengthened CHD repair programs. Establishing prospective multicenter registries investigating region‐specific barriers will be essential to refining treatment strategies and advancing equitable, evidence‐based PAH care across Latin America.

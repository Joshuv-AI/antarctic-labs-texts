Successful control of double carbapenemase-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae (KPC-NDM) outbreak in a hospital in Colombia
Authors: Santiago Sánchez Pardo, Marcela Vargas Vargas, Nubia Esperanza Zea Cuadrado, Sonia Reyes Peña, Edna Mora Robayo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundInfections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae producing the carbapenemases K. pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC) and New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM) are a growing challenge in Latin America. These multi-drug-resistant strains have been associated with high morbidity and mortality rates. In Colombia, the spread of these infections has placed increasing pressure on healthcare systems.ObjectiveTo describe the epidemiological characteristics and interventions implemented during an outbreak of co-producing KPC and NDM.MethodsA retrospective study of a KPC/NDM K. pneumoniae outbreak in a Colombian university hospital (July 2023–March 2024). Clinical and epidemiological variables were analysed. Outbreak control focused on active screening, patient cohorting, and hand hygiene reinforcement (>80% compliance).ResultsSixty-six cases were identified: 51.5% infections and 48.5% colonizations. Males predominated (56.1%) and the median age was 62 years. Bloodstream infections were the most common type of infection (17.6%), and the most affected service was general surgery (30.3%). The overall mortality rate was 13.6%, with no direct association with infection. Hand hygiene compliance exceeded 80% during the outbreak.ConclusionsThe multi-disciplinary response proved instrumental in containing the outbreak, underscoring the significance of strict adherence to infection prevention measures and other measures not implemented previously.

Introduction

Infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae producing the carbapenemases (KPC) and New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM) represent a growing global threat and are considered an expanding crisis due to their ability to spread rapidly and limit the available treatment options [1]. Globally, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) have classified carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales as an urgent threat to public health [2]. Carbapenemases confer resistance to carbapenems, which are considered the antibiotics of last resort for serious infections caused by multi-drug-resistant (MDR) bacteria [3,4].

In Latin America, and particularly in Colombia, the situation is worrying. Local studies have documented high mortality rates specifically among hospitalized patients with MDR K. pneumoniae infection. This infection can occur in patients with multiple predisposing factors, such as prolonged hospitalization, prior exposure to broad-spectrum antibiotics, and the use of invasive medical devices [5,6]. Given this situation, control measures in healthcare facilities have become critically important. The rigorous implementation of environmental cleaning and disinfection protocols is a proven strategy for reducing healthcare-associated infections [7,8].

The epidemiology of these co-producing organisms has shifted significantly. While KPC was initially the dominant mechanism, the emergence of NDM has created a complex ‘double carbapenemase’ landscape. Several nosocomial outbreaks, the majority of which have been attributed to K. pneumoniae, have been documented in various regions across the globe, including North America (particularly in the USA), South America (Colombia and Argentina), Europe (Greece, Italy and Poland), Asia (China) and the Middle East (Israel) [3,4]. In Colombia, the first report of KPC enzymes in South America was made by Villegas et al. in 2005 in Medellín [9]. Since then, Colombia has become endemic for KPC; however, recent national surveillance reports from the National Institute of Health (INS) indicate increasing prevalence of isolates co-producing both KPC and NDM. Regional data show that this phenomenon is not isolated, with similar outbreaks reported in neighbouring countries, underscoring the need to strengthen surveillance and response systems in hospitals where the simultaneous circulation of multiple resistance mechanisms has been documented [6].

Therefore, the objective of this study was to describe the successful control of an outbreak caused by K. pneumoniae producing two carbapenemases, KPC/NDM, in a tertiary hospital in Boyacá, Colombia, from July 2023 to March 2024.

Methods

A retrospective descriptive study was conducted on all patients hospitalized with confirmed isolation of K. pneumoniae co-producing KPC and NDM between July 2023 and March 2024. While epidemiological surveillance continued until 1st August 2024 (epidemiological surveillance cut-off date), this date marks the conclusion of the data collection period once the incidence of new clinical infections returned to the baseline level. The hospital has a total capacity of 250 beds and includes surgical inpatient wards, general hospital wards, adult and paediatric intensive care units (ICUs), and an emergency department. At the study institution, the emergency department frequently functions as a temporary inpatient area where patients remain for more than 48 h awaiting a definitive bed; thus, these patients were included under the same criteria as other hospitalized populations.

A confirmed case was defined as any patient with laboratory-confirmed isolation of KPC+NDM-co-producing K. pneumoniae identified through clinical cultures or active screening. To differentiate between community-acquired and healthcare-associated infections (HAIs), only isolates identified after 48 h of hospital admission were included, in accordance with the CDC/National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) criteria. Cases were epidemiologically linked through spatio-temporal overlap in hospital services, justifying the analysis of diverse patient populations (adults and children) as a single group.

During the outbreak, various control measures were implemented, including contact isolation, restriction of internal transfers, and active rectal screening (rectal swabs) for patients meeting specific criteria, such as hospital stay >3 days, treatment with carbapenems, referrals from an institution of unknown epidemiology, chemotherapy, dialysis, and contact with patients colonized by carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales. The persistence of cases slightly above the baseline of two cases per month after March 2024 is attributed to this intensified active surveillance, which allowed for the detection of cases with asymptomatic colonization that would otherwise have remained unidentified. Environmental cultures were also taken in critical areas. Prior the outbreak, measures only included: standard precautions, cohorting and hand hygiene, served as baseline controls.

Data were analysed using descriptive and inferential statistics. For qualitative variables, absolute and relative frequencies were calculated; for quantitative variables, measures of central tendency (mean or median) and dispersion (standard deviation or interquartile range) were determined. Chi-squared test was used to evaluate the association between categorical variables (e.g. sex vs type of case). In instances where expected frequencies were less than five, Fisher's exact test was applied (e.g. for comorbidity vs mortality). A P-value <0.05 was considered to indicate significance. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4.2.

Hand hygiene

Hand hygiene compliance was assessed by direct observation using the World Health Organization's ‘Five Moments for Hand Hygiene’ framework, conducted by trained infection control personnel using standardized institutional checklists. The measurement of compliance is reported as the proportion of observed opportunities in which hand hygiene was performed correctly, thereby serving as an institutional indicator.

Disinfection

Final disinfection in critical areas was performed using 4000 parts per million solution of organic chlorine. Adherence to institutional protocol conducted by trained infection control personnel using standardized institutional checklists.

Cohortization

Patients were isolated in a single room, with the use of personal protective equipment (gloves, caps, disposable gowns) and hand hygiene measures. The implementation of compliance procedures was carried out by infection control personnel who had undergone the relevant training, with the use of institutional checklists that have been standardised.

Antimicrobial stewardship programme

The antimicrobial stewardship programme (ASP) is a non-restrictive programme based on the latest guidelines and recommendations on the use of antimicrobials.

Microbiological identification methods

To detect potential colonization, active screening of rectal swabs was performed using laser nephelometry, a technique that identifies carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales without differentiating the specific enzyme. Sensitivity was 85%, specificity was 100%, the positive predictive value was 100%, and the negative predictive value was 94%. This technology was newly adopted at the study institution during the outbreak to identify potential colonization cases based on predefined criteria. Confirmatory identification of the carbapenemase type required additional methods, including immunochromatography consisting of a lateral flow test that identifies five types of carbapenemase (KPC, NDM, VIM, IMP, OXA-48-like) from bacterial colonies of positive cultures, and offers high sensitivity (97–100%) and specificity (>95%). Following the outbreak period, rectal swab screening by nephelometry was discontinued, as agar culture and immunochromatography were required for enzyme-level identification, representing costs the institution could not sustain within routine laboratory operation.

Ethics statement

The research protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee (Report 114-2024, 9th October 2024).

Results

During the follow-up period, a total of 66 cases of K. pneumoniae co-producing KPC/NDM isolates (including colonizations and infections) were reported across all hospital services. Over half (56.1%, N=37) were male with no significant association by sex (χ2 test: P=0.976). Prior to the outbreak, sporadic cases occurred at a rate of fewer than two per month. An increase in cases was observed beginning in July 2023, as shown in Figure 1, which led to formal declaration of the outbreak. The median age of patients was 62 years (range 23–91 years). The patient population included 64 adults and two children, with outliers in the age distribution corresponding to the paediatric subgroup. In terms of comorbidities, in the group of patients with infection, chronic non-communicable diseases were present in 39.9% of patients (N=26), 18.2% (N=12) had a history of cancer, 6.1% (N=4) had trauma, and 3.0% (N=2) reported no comorbidities (comorbidity vs mortality; Fisher's exact test: P=1.000).Figure 1Monthly distribution of carbapenemase-producing (KPC+NDM) Klebsiella pneumoniae cases between November 2022 and January 2025. Of all cases, 15% of samples (N=10) were sent to INS for further characterization, in accordance with the INS protocol which accepts up to 10 samples per outbreak event. Among these, a unique molecular profile was identified, consisting of a clone of nine indistinguishable isolates (100% similarity) producing both KPC and NDM carbapenemases (Figure 2). The carbapenemase status of the remaining samples was determined by phenotypic methods and confirmatory molecular testing performed at the institutional microbiology laboratory. During the outbreak period, a total of 267 hospital isolates of carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales other than K. pneumoniae were also identified. Of the 66 reported cases, 51.5% were infections (N=34) and 48.5% were colonizations (N=32). All infections were defined according to CDC/NHSN criteria for HAIs. Among the infections, the most common site was the bloodstream, accounting for 17.6% of infections (N=6), followed by intra-abdominal surgical infections (N=5, 14.7%) and urinary tract infections (N=5, 14.7%). Regarding distribution by hospital department, 30.3% of cases (N=20) occurred in the general surgery inpatient ward, 16.7% (N=11) in the emergency department, and 9.1% (N=6) in the adult ICU. In view of the distribution of surgical units and the high rate of surgical site infections at the onset of the outbreak, it was decided to perform environmental cultures in the operating rooms.

The median hospital stay was 7 days (range 3–27 days), with a mean of 11 days; the neurosurgery department recorded the longest mean stay at 35.3 days (length of stay by department; Kruskal–Wallis test: H=20.54, P=0.0004). During the outbreak, nine deaths were recorded (13.6%); none of these were directly attributed to K. pneumoniae infection. Age was significantly associated with mortality: deceased patients had a median age of 67 years compared with 56 years for surviving patients (Mann–Whitney U-test=394, P=0.010). No significant association was found between case type (infection vs colonization) and mortality (Fisher's exact test: odds ratio=1.21, P=1.000).

Hand hygiene compliance exceeded 80% during the outbreak period, representing a significant improvement compared with pre-outbreak adherence of 65% recorded in 2022–2023 (z-test: z=−3.85, P<0.001), with a sustained increase following the outbreak. A total of 137 environmental cultures were performed in operating rooms, all yielding negative results. The use of white coats in clinical areas was eliminated and replaced by institutional scrub uniforms. Cleaning and disinfection protocols were reinforced and assessed for adherence using institutional checklists verified by the infection control department (Table I). Implemented measures to control the outbreak and outcomes

An ASP was implemented in December 2023, led by an infectious disease specialist. The programme included two core strategies: (i) prospective prescription audit with individualized feedback to prescribing physicians; and (ii) targeted restriction of high-risk broad-spectrum agents (piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, cefepime and ceftriaxone). Antimicrobial consumption was monitored monthly using defined daily doses (DDD) per 100 patient-days. The ASP is a non-restrictive programme based on the latest guidelines and recommendations on the use of antimicrobials. This programme consists of a multi-disciplinary team composed of an infectious disease specialist, a supporting general practitioner, a pharmacist and a professional nurse. ASP activities include prospective auditing and feedback on antimicrobial use in the different units of the participating centre, monitoring of antimicrobial consumption, and follow-up on clinical indicators. ASP members each had a 1.0 full-time equivalent allocation. Since December 2023, implementation of the antimicrobial optimization programme was associated with a significant reduction in the consumption of broad-spectrum antibiotics active against Gram-negative organisms, including piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, cefepime and ceftriaxone, as shown in Figure 3.Figure 3Antimicrobial consumption of broad-spectrum antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, cefepime and ceftriaxone) in defined daily doses (DDD) 2023–2024. At the end of the study period, no new HAIs of K. pneumoniae KPC/NDM were identified during 4 consecutive weeks of active surveillance prior to outbreak closure. The outbreak was formally declared closed in accordance with national epidemiological guidelines, which define closure as the absence of new confirmed cases over at least 4 consecutive weeks of active surveillance [10]. Following closure, sporadic cases returned to the historical endemic baseline of approximately one to two cases per month from March 2024 onwards, as shown in Figure 1. In instances where case counts surpassed this threshold, the majority of cases were attributable to colonizations with a lower infection rate.

Discussion

This outbreak of KPC-NDM carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae is in line with growing concern about antimicrobial resistance in Latin America, where infections caused by MDR bacteria have increased significantly in recent years [1,4]. The predominance of HAIs in surgical services, especially in general surgical wards where 30.3% are concentrated, is consistent with previous findings of similar outbreaks in tertiary hospitals [11,12]. However, more recent reports indicate a higher incidence in ICUs [13,14]. These findings reflect the high burden of patients with comorbidities and greater exposure to invasive procedures in these services – factors that favour the acquisition and spread of HAIs [5].

Strikingly, almost half (48.5%) of the isolates of K. pneumoniae producing KPC and NDM carbapenemases correspond to colonizations, which is notable given the high resistance profile of these bacteria. This high proportion of colonization likely reflects the intensity of active rectal screening implemented during the outbreak, which enabled detection of asymptomatic carriers who would otherwise have gone unidentified. Identifying the distribution of colonized patients was essential for guiding cohorting decisions and preventing further nosocomial transmission [15].

The interventions implemented proved to be effective in containing transmission of the pathogen within the hospital. The combination of strategies such as isolation, screening and reinforcement of hand hygiene has been well documented as key in the control of carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae infections [16,17]. As shown in Figure 1, the case count declined progressively following implementation of the bundle of control measures, and no new HAIs were identified during the 4 weeks preceding the formal outbreak closure declaration. The sporadic cases observed after March 2024 corresponded exclusively to colonizations detected through continued active surveillance, not to new HAIs, and remained around the historical endemic baseline. This distinction is critical: the persistence of colonization detection reflects the heightened sensitivity of ongoing screening rather than a failure of outbreak control. The control and restriction measures used for transfers within the institution are also in line with previous recommendations on how to reduce the spread of MDR bacteria in hospital settings [3,7,18]. These results underscore the importance of strict and coordinated measures for infection control in areas with high circulation of resistant micro-organisms.

The elimination of white coats is not a measure described specifically for outbreak control, but this medical item has been described as a carrier of bacterial flora, including meticillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and Acinetobacter spp., among others [15,19,20]. Unfortunately, no cultures were taken from textiles or the white coats of training staff or doctors in this study due to the difficulties involved in taking cultures from textiles, and a lack of standardization of a methodology for doing so. However, promoting the ‘bare below the elbow’ strategy prohibiting the use of white coats during hospital care was a successful intervention measure that could be considered the first to be implemented in Colombia in the context of an outbreak of MDR micro-organisms. This strategy, implemented in 2007 in the UK, consisted of four aspects: a shirt or blouse with short sleeves; no white coat; no tie; and no jewellery. However this was in the context of Clostridium difficile infection, and there were very high levels of MRSA bacteraemia. The present study set out to propose a dress code for medical staff that is informed by these experiences. The primary objective of this code is to promote accurate hand hygiene and to reduce barriers to the implementation of the recommended technique [21,22].

The ASP was led by a multi-disciplinary committee and implemented through two core strategies: prospective audit with feedback and targeted antibiotic restriction. Antimicrobial consumption was monitored using DDD per 100 patient-days as the primary metric. In conjunction with the other outbreak control measures, this programme targeted antimicrobials with the greatest potential for selecting resistant strains, including piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem and cefepime [23]. Analysis of DDD/100 patient-days showed significant reductions in piperacillin-tazobactam use without an increase in carbapenem use, highlighting the importance and impact of the ASP interventions and education of the prescribers (Figure 3). The implementation of ASPs is a coordinated strategy that allows for improvement and measurement of the appropriate use of antimicrobials, not only for the treatment of infection, but also for minimizing adverse events in patients and minimizing the impact on eventual resistance [24], thus preventing the spread of MDR micro-organisms and unnecessary costs [25,26]. Therefore, their implementation should be strengthened in institutions that do not have such strategies in place or in other outbreaks of carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae.

The low mortality rate directly attributable to K. pneumoniae using the standardized definition, considering infection alone as a cause of mortality, was a positive finding and may be associated with early identification of cases of true infection, and rapid intervention to initiate appropriate treatment with available antimicrobial options. Targeted therapy based on microbiological culture results and antibiotic combination, which has been shown to be effective in patients infected with carbapenemase-producing strains [27], likely contributed to the decrease in mortality. However, it is important to note that, despite the low mortality rate, the high morbidity rate and prolonged hospital stay in some patients, especially in the neurosurgery department, highlight the potential severity of these infections in terms of outcomes.

The study has a few limitations, including its descriptive design and the absence of multi-variate analysis, which prevents clear causal associations from being established between the interventions and the results obtained. Furthermore, due to the characteristics of the study, the findings cannot be extrapolated directly to other institutions. Nevertheless, despite these limitations, this study provides relevant evidence on the local response to an outbreak of K. pneumoniae KPC-NDM, being one of the first descriptions of outbreaks of this type of micro-organism with double carbapenemase production, strengthening the knowledge available in Latin America on antimicrobial resistance, and the potential effects in terms of complications and conditions necessary for its control.

The outbreak was controlled successfully thanks to the rigorous implementation of prevention measures, combined with solid coordination between the infection committee, hospital administration, ASP, and both regional and national health authorities. This coordinated response contained the intrahospital transmission of K. pneumoniae KPC-NDM. In this sense, this operational model can serve as a benchmark for other institutions, providing a basis for strengthening regional policies against outbreaks of MDR bacteria and contributing to progress in containing antimicrobial resistance in the region.

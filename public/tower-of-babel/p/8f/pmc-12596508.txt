Research note: duck Tembusu virus non-structural protein 3 induced autophagy through endoplasmic reticulum stress
Authors: Shuna Zhang, Ji Fu, Liya Zhu, Yihao Lu, Zhaopeng Zhang, Luna Yu, Jianhua Qiu, Gang Wang, Ning Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuck Tembusu virus (DTMUV) causes severe infectious diseases in poultry. Previous studies have shown that DTMUV induced autophagy and endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) in host cells, thereby influencing the virus proliferation. However, the role of ERS in DTMUV-induced autophagy remains unclear. In this study, the relationship between ERS and autophagy activated by DTMUV was first analyzed, and we found that the ERS inhibitor 4-phenylbutyric acid (4-PBA) decreased DTMUV-induced autophagy, suggesting that autophagy induced by DTMUV was ERS-dependent. Further investigation revealed that the NS3 protein of DTMUV played an important role in the activation of ERS, it triggered all three branches of unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling [Protein kinase R-like endoplasmic reticulum kinase (PERK), inositol-requiring enzyme 1ɑ (IRE1ɑ), and activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6)] in duck embryo fibroblast (DEF) cells. Inhibition of PERK and IRE1ɑ pathways reduced the NS3-mediated autophagy. Interestingly, while truncated NS3 mutants still activated ERS via the PERK pathway, they failed to induce autophagy. Mechanistically, NS3 was found to interact with the endoplasmic reticulum chaperone GRP78. This study elucidated ERS was critical for NS3-induced autophagy, laid a foundation for understanding the roles of DTMUV-induced ERS and autophagy in the viral pathogenesis.Keywords: DTMUV, Non-structural Protein 3, Endoplasmic reticulum stress, Autophagy

Introduction

Duck Tembusu virus disease is an acute infectious disease caused by duck Tembusu virus (DTMUV). The infected ducks exhibit mental depression, decreased egg production, and growth retardation. In addition to ducks, the chickens and geese can also be infected (Yurayart et al., 2021). Furthermore, DTMUV antibodies were positive in over 70 % of duck industry workers, coupled with the detection of viral RNA in approximately 50 % of oral swab samples from Thai duck farm workers (Pulmanausahakul et al., 2022). This disease not only caused huge economic losses to the poultry industry, but also has important public health significance.

DTMUV belongs to the genus Flavivirus, family Flaviviridae. Its RNA encodes three structural proteins (capsid, pre-membrane, envelope) and seven nonstructural (NS) proteins (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, and NS5). NS3 protein exhibits high sequence conservation across flaviviruses, suggesting its critical role in the viral life cycle (Wang et al., 2019).

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is an important organelle that can participate in various biological processes. Generally, the accumulation of large amounts of unfolded or misfolded proteins in the ER lumen can cause ER stress (ERS), promoting the activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR). The UPR restores ER homeostasis by decreasing protein synthesis and augmenting the ER’s protein-folding capacity. The UPR includes the protein kinase RNA-like ER kinase (PERK), the inositol-requiring enzyme 1ɑ (IRE1ɑ), and the activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6) pathways. Notably, flaviviruses often activate UPR. For example, dengue virus (DENV) activated PERK pathway in the early stage but suppressed it at mid and late stages, shifting reliance to IRE1α and ATF6 pathways (Peña et al., 2011). Zika virus (ZIKV) infection activated the IRE1α-Xbp1 and ATF6 pathways to respond to ERS in vitro and in vivo (Tan et al., 2018).

There is a closely relationship between ERS and autophagy. ERS induces autophagy-related genes expression through activation of the UPR, thereby promoting autophagosome formation. In turn, autophagy facilitates the clearance of accumulated unfolded proteins and viral components. As a new member of flavivirus, DTMUV has been shown to induce ERS and activate three UPR pathways (Pan et al., 2023). While Zhao et al. (2022) demonstrated that DTMUV induced autophagy, the potential role of DTMUV-induced ERS in modulating autophagy remains unclear. In this study, we reported that the critical effect of NS3 protein on ERS, and revealed the UPR pathways were required for regulating autophagy during DTMUV infection. This study provides a new insight into the crosstalk between virus-induced ERS and autophagy.

Materials and methods

Cells and virus

Duck embryo fibroblast (DEF) cells were obtained from 11-day-old duck embryos. DEF and HEK293T cells were grown in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) (Pricella, Wuhan, China) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (TransGen, Beijing, China). DTMUV FX2010 strain was propagated in SPF chicken embryos, and its viral titer was determined to be 1 × 10⁴ TCID₅₀/100 µL using the Reed-Muench method.

Antibodies and plasmids

Antibodies against HA, eIF2α, ATF6, phospho-eIF2α, and GAPDH were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (MD, USA); LC3 and β-actin from Proteintech (Wuhan, China); GRP78 from Beyotime (Shanghai, China); phospho-IRE1α from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Monoclonal antibody GAPDH was obtained from mice, and other antibodies were rabbits. HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse and anti-rabbit secondary antibodies were purchased from CWBIO (Taizhou, China). The plasmids of viral proteins were stored in the laboratory (Zhao et al., 2022). The sequences of NS3-N (aa1-201) and NS3-C (aa202-618) were cloned into pCMV vector with a HA tag. The duck GRP78 gene was cloned into pCDNA3.1 with His/Myc tags.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol, and cDNA was synthesized using HiScript II Q RT SuperMix (Vazyme Biotech, Nanjing, China). The primer sequences of DTMUV E gene and reference gene GAPDH were reported in the previous study (Zhao et al., 2022). qPCR reactions were performed on the Roche LightCycler 96 using TB Green Fast qPCR mixture (Takara, Dalian, China).

Co-immunoprecipitation and Western blot analysis

The HEK293T cells were lysed using a cold lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), followed by incubation on ice for 30 minutes with vortexing every 5 minutes. The lysate was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C to collect the supernatant for protein quantification using the BCA method. During the immunoprecipitation process, 500 µL of cell lysate was mixed with 100 µL of protein magnetic beads (pre-incubated with 2 µL of specific antibody at room temperature for 2 hours). The beads were washed three times and fractionated by SDS-PAGE. Western blot was performed with appropriate primary and HRP-conjugated second antibodies according to the instruction. Bands were visualized using an ECL system (Bio-Rad, Shanghai, China).

XBP1 splicing assay

XBP1 was amplified by PCR. The products were digested with 15 U of Pst I restriction enzyme (Takara, Dalian, China) at 37℃ for 1 h. The digested products were resolved on a 1.5 % agarose gel. The un-spliced band is 594 bp, and the digested products were 329 bp and 265 bp. The spliced gene lacks restriction enzyme site, so the band is 568 bp.

Statistic analysis

Using duck GAPDH as internal control, mRNA expression was quantified via the 2−ΔΔCt method. Experiments were performed in triplicate. Data were presented as means ± SD and analyzed with Student’s t-test using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (San Diego, Calif, USA). Significance is denoted as *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01.

Results and discussion

To clarify the role of ERS in DTMUV-induced autophagy, DEF cells were treated with ERS inhibitor 4-PBA before DTMUV inoculation. At 48 hours post infection, 4-PBA treatment significantly reduced the expression of both GRP78 and LC3-II in infected cells (Fig. 1A), and 4-PBA significantly reduced the mRNA levels of DTMUV E gene (Fig. 1B). These results demonstrated that autophagy induced by DTMUV was dependent on ERS, and inhibition of ERS reduced viral replication. It was reported that DTMUV-induced ERS promoted viral replication, primarily by affecting DTMUV replication during the RNA synthesis stage or the assembly/budding stage (Pan et al., 2023). Additionally, ERS promoted the replication of porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (Diao et al., 2023).

Recent studies demonstrated that NS3 protein of DTMUV induced autophagy via the ERK and PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathways (Zhao et al., 2022), and it also triggered apoptosis via the PERK-PKR pathway (Pan et al., 2023). To investigate whether the NS3 protein can cause ERS, the expression of GRP78 was detected in cells transfected with NS3 recombinant plasmid, the results showed that GRP78 was significantly upregulated (Fig. 1C). Futher analysis revealed that NS3 overexpression activated ATF6, PERK and IRE1α pathways (Fig. 1D and 1E), which consisted with earlier findings (Pan et al., 2023).

Since NS3 protein can induce autophagy in DEF cells, we further determined whether ERS is involved in this process. DEF cells were treated with 4-PBA, followed by transfection with the NS3 plasmid. Fig. 2A showed that 4-PBA treatment suppressed GRP78 upregulation and reduced LC3-II production, indicating that NS3-triggered autophagy was ERS-dependent. The specific UPR pathways involved NS3-induced autophagy were further determined. In Fig. 2B and C, the LC3-Ⅱ protein markedly down-regulated when the PERK and IRE1α pathways were inhibited compared to the NS3-transfected group, indicating the critical role of PERK and IRE1α pathways in NS3-mediated autophagy. However, at 24 h and 36 h post NS3 transfection, although ATF6 expression was significantly elevated in the AEBSF-treated group, the LC3-II remained unchanged (Fig. 2D). These results revealed that NS3 exploited both PERK and IRE1α branches to induce autophagy.

To identify the molecular mechanism of NS3-induced ERS, the key functional domain of NS3 was analyzed. First, two truncated NS3 plasmids pCMV-HA-NS3-C and pCMV-HA-NS3-N were constructed. Transfection of two constructs into DEF cells revealed that both domains independently induced significant upregulation of GRP78 protein compared to empty vector controls (Fig. 2E). But neither domain significantly upregulated LC3-II, the full-length NS3 protein was required for autophagy. These results suggested that while individual domains triggered ERS, mTOR might remain persistently activated by other signaling pathways (such as growth factor signaling) (Kim et al., 2011). Generally, GRP78 tightly binds to the ER luminal domains of PERK, IRE1α, and ATF6, ensuring these sensor proteins remain inactive and maintaining cellular homeostasis. However, accumulation of unfolded or misfolded proteins causes GRP78 to dissociate from these sensors and preferentially to the abnormal proteins (Diwaker et al., 2015). This study confirmed through Co-IP assays that NS3 interacted with GRP78 (Fig. 2F). Although this study did not conduct assay on competitive binding of NS3 to GRP78 protein with the three sensors, based on the mechanisms of ERS and the interaction between NS3 and GRP78, we speculated the NS3 binding caused GRP78 to dissociate from these sensors, activating the UPR signaling pathways. However, a key limitation of this study is that much of the mechanistic data relies on NS3 overexpression, which may not fully reflect physiological levels during DTMUV infection.

In summary, the NS3 protein of DTMUV activated all three UPR pathways to induce ERS, and triggered autophagy by activating the IRE1α and PERK pathways through interaction with GRP78. This study primarily elucidated the molecular mechanism by which NS3 protein regulated the ERS-autophagy.

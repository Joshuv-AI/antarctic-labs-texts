Efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Saccharomyces cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 for dogs and all other Canidae (Danstar Ferment AG)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Secundino López‐Puente, Guido Rychen, Montserrat Anguita, Jaume Galobart, Matteo Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño, Rosella Brozzi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Saccharomyces cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 for dogs and all other Canidae, intended for use as a zootechnical additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers). In a previous opinion, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of the additive under the proposed conditions of use. The applicant therefore submitted supplementary information, including four efficacy trials in dogs. The studies assessed faecal dry matter, faecal consistency and faecal immunoglobulin A (IgA). In two trials, supplementation with the additive resulted in a significantly higher faecal IgA content; in one of these trials, an improvement in faecal consistency was also observed, whereas in the other, a higher faecal dry matter content was also identified. However, the remaining two trials did not show significant positive effects on the key parameters considered adequate to support efficacy. The FEEDAP Panel concluded that the available evidence is insufficient to demonstrate the efficacy of the additive to improve faecal consistency or faecal IgA content in dogs. Therefore, the Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of the additive for all Canidae.Keywords: digestibility enhancers, dogs and other Canidae, efficacy, Saccharomyces cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079, zootechnical additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and terms of reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition; in particular Article 9 defines the terms of the authorisation by the Commission. Description of the additive in the application FEED‐2023‐15136 and FEED‐2025‐36954.

On 4 June 2024, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), in its opinion on the safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Saccharomyces cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 as a zootechnical additive for dogs and all other Canidae, could not conclude on the efficacy of the additive. The European Commission gave the possibility to the applicant to submit supplementary information and data to complete the assessment and to allow a revision of the EFSA opinion. The new supplementary information and data have been transmitted by the applicant using the E‐Submission Food Chain Platform (application number FEED‐2025‐36,954). In view of the above and in accordance with Article 29(1)(a) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, the European Commission requests EFSA to deliver a new scientific opinion on a feed additive consisting of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 as a zootechnical additive for dogs and all other Canidae under the conditions of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, based on the supplementary information and data submitted by the applicant.

Additional information

The additive is a preparation containing viable cells of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079. It is currently authorised for use in feed for sows and weaned piglets, 3 all pigs other than sows and weaned piglets and all minor porcine species 4 and chickens for fattening and minor poultry species for fattening 5 as gut flora stabilisers, and for turkeys for fattening 6 as other zootechnical additives (reduction of Salmonella spp. contamination on carcasses through its decrease in faeces) (4d1703).

EFSA issued several opinions on the safety and efficacy of this product (under the tradename Levucell® SB) when used in feed for sows, weaned piglets, chickens for fattening, minor poultry species for fattening, turkeys for fattening, all pigs, calves and all other ruminant species and camelids for rearing and for fattening and dogs and other Canidae (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2016, 2017, 2019a, 2019b, 2022, 2024a, 2024b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of supplementary information 7 to a previous application on the same product. 8 The dossier was received on 13 January 2026 and the general information and supporting documentation are available on Open.EFSA at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2026‐00026.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 (Levucell® SB) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a, 2024b).

ASSESSMENT

The additive is available in two solid preparations: a non‐coated/encapsulated preparation of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 containing a minimum of 2.0 × 1010 CFU/g of additive and a coated/encapsulated preparation of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 containing a minimum of 1.0 × 1010 CFU/g of additive. It is intended to be used as a zootechnical additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers) in dogs and all Canidae.

In a previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a, 2024b), the Panel concluded that the additive is safe for all Canidae and that the non‐coated form is not irritant to skin and eyes, but both forms of the additive are considered to be skin and respiratory sensitisers and any exposure through skin and respiratory tract is considered a risk. No conclusions could be reached on the eye irritation potential of the coated form due to the lack of data. However, the Panel was not in the position to conclude on the efficacy of S. cerevisiae CNCM I‐1079 at the proposed conditions of use.

The applicant has produced new data to address the limitations identified by the Panel regarding the efficacy of the additive; this additional information is described below.

Efficacy

In the previous application, the applicant proposed to support the efficacy by demonstrating the benefits of the additive supplementation to bitches on the health status of the puppies, resembling the positive effects already shown in litters when sows were supplemented. However, in the study submitted, the supplementation of the bitches with the additive at the proposed use level showed no positive statistically significant effects on any of the key parameters monitored on the puppies.

In the current application, the applicant submitted four trials aimed at assessing the effects of the additive supplementation on the faecal dry matter, faecal score and faecal immunoglobulin A (IgA) on dogs. The four studies 10 were conducted at different locations, following a similar design, with animals from different breeds, ages and sex, and fed a variety of ■■■■■ feeds. No differences in feed intake and body weight were detected in any trial.

The Panel noted that the statistical analysis conducted for trials 2 and 3 did not adequately reflect the experimental design. 14 No significant differences were observed in faecal dry matter, faecal score (trial 3) or IgA (in fresh matter) (Table 3). No statistical evaluation of the recorded data on the faecal score was conducted in trial 2.

In trials 1 and 4, the animals supplemented with the additive showed higher faecal concentration of IgA compared to the control at the end of the trial (Table 3). Additionally, a lower faecal score and faecal dry matter were observed in trial 1 and a higher faecal dry matter content in trial 4.

Based on the data submitted, the Panel cannot conclude on the potential of the additive to improve the faecal consistency or faecal IgA content in dogs. At least one additional trial showing positive effects on relevant faecal parameters would be necessary.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of the additive in all Canidae.

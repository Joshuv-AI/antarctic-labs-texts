Impaired glymphatic function in relation to cumulative blood glucose exposure: A population-based cohort study
Authors: Yufan Zhang, Jing Sun, Sihui Guo, Ying Hui, Xiaoshuai Li, Jing Li, Xinyu Zhao, Pengfei Zhao, Shuohua Chen, Shouling Wu, Zhenchang Wang, Han Lv
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe relationship between prolonged blood glucose exposure and glymphatic system function of the brain remains unknown. This study aimed to investigate the association between cumulative blood glucose levels and neuroimaging metrics associated with glymphatic function.MethodsThis study was conducted in a multicenter, community-based cohort study. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data were collected once between 2020 and 2022. The assessment of brain glymphatic function can be conducted using the diffusion tensor imaging analysis along the perivascular space (DTI-ALPS) index. Cumulative fasting blood glucose (FBG) values from 2014 to 2018 were calculated for each participant as a prolonged exposure, while a single measurement of FBG at the same time of MRI acquisition represented a short-term exposure. A generalized linear model was used to evaluate the association of blood glucose exposure and DTI-ALPS index.ResultsA total of 251 subjects were included. Prolonged hyperglycemic exposure with FBG ≥ 7.0 mmol/L was significantly associated with lower average DTI-ALPS index (β = -0.058, 95 % confidence interval, -0.096 to -0.019). These findings remained significant among participants over 60 years old and those not taking hypoglycemic medication. No significant associations were observed in single measurement.ConclusionProlonged exposure to hyperglycemia is significantly associated with a lower average DTI-ALPS index, potentially playing a crucial role in the impairment of glymphatic function, especially among older adults. A well controlled blood glucose level may demonstrate a protective effect on glymphatic function, indicating better brain health.Keywords: Cohort study, Blood glucose, Hyperglycemia, Glymphatic function, Neuroimaging, DTI-ALPS

Introduction

The cerebral glymphatic system, also known as the glial lymphatic system, is considered as a pathway for fluid clearance in the central nervous system, contributing to the maintenance of optimal brain health (Iliff et al., 2012). Giving its crucial role in removing interstitial waste from the brain (Rasmussen et al., 2018), there has been a growing interest in the evaluation of the glymphatic function (Dong et al., 2023).

Recently, a diffusion tensor-based magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) method was proposed to potentially assess the activity of the glymphoid system in individuals, i.e. Diffusion tensor imaging analysis along the perivascular space (DTI-ALPS) (Taoka et al., 2017). The DTI-ALPS index is the ratio of the diffusion capacity around the horizontal deep medullary veins of the lateral ventricle body parallel to the space around the blood vessels to the diffusion capacity perpendicular to the direction of the main fiber bundle. DTI-ALPS assesses the movement of water molecules in the direction of the perivascular space by measuring the diffusion rate of water molecules, thus possibly reflecting the activity of the human glymphatic system. A lower DTI-ALPS index often indicates some degree of impaired glymphatic system function. Compared to the previous methods involving intrathecal injection of gadolinium-based contrast agents in animal experiments, DTI-ALPS is a noninvasive and safe approach that can be probably applied to measure the activity of the neuroglial glymphatic system in humans (Qin et al., 2023).

There are numerous factors that can impact the function of cerebral glymphatic system (Fang et al., 2021, Sevigny et al., 2016, Wang et al., 2023a). Hyperglycemia, which encompasses pre-diabetes and diabetes, has emerged as a significant global public health issue (Rooney et al., 2023, Zimmet et al., 2016). Several studies have indicated that hyperglycemia is detrimental to the function of glymphatic system. For instance, an animal study observed reduced glial lymphatic flow in a rat model of type 2 diabetes, featured by a three-fold decrease in cerebrospinal fluid contrast agent Gd-DTPA clearance (Jiang et al., 2017). Additionally, a cohort of patients with type 2 diabetes who participated in a DTI-ALPS imaging study exhibited reduced water diffusion along the perivascular space, potentially indicating damage to the glymphatic system (Yang et al., 2020). Given that hyperglycemia is characterized by chronic progression, cumulative exposure to hyperglycemia may be a more pronounced factor warranting attention (Crane et al., 2013, Kim et al., 2022, Yu et al., 2023). However, little is known about how cumulative exposure to hyperglycemia affects alterations in the function of the glymphatic system in the human brain.

In this study, we use the DTI-ALPS method try to evaluate whether cumulative exposure to hyperglycemia influence the brain glymphatic system function in a large community-based population cohort. Understanding the relationship between long-term hyperglycemia status and DTI-ALPS index that potentially indicate brain glymphatic function, may provide novel evidence for preventing and treating neurological diseases associated with impaired brain glymphatic function.

Methods

Design and participants

The longitudinal clinical data utilized in this study were derived from the Kailuan Study (KLS), a prospective cohort study conducted in Tangshan, China. Demographic and clinical information, as well as laboratory examinations, were prospectively collected from 11 local hospitals and have been extensively described in previously published articles (Wu et al., 2019, Yu et al., 2021). The neuroimaging data were obtained from the Multimodality Medical Imaging Study Based on KaiLuan Study (META-KLS), which is a subset of KLS initiated since 2020. Detailed information regarding the acquisition of neuroimaging and standard processing pipeline can be found in the META-KLS protocol (Sun et al., 2023).

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) participants who underwent brain MRI examinations between 2020 and 2022; (2) participants who attended at least 3 clinical interviews in the hospital from 2014 to 2018; (3) clinical data collected concurrently with MRI acquisition in 2020.

The exclusion criteria were as follows：(1) participants with missing clinical data; (2) participants diagnosed with large-vessel ischemic stroke, tumors, or traumatic brain injury; (3) missing brain MRI data.

The META-KLS has been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Kailuan General Hospital. All subjects provided written informed consent to participate in the Kailuan study. This study was registered on Clinicaltrials.gov (NCT05453877) and followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guideline.

Measurements of clinical features

All enrolled participants completed a standardized questionnaire to collect demographic information (age, sex), lifestyle factors (smoking, drinking, physical exercise), and medical history. Fasting blood samples were collected after an overnight fast of 8–12 h and subsequently analyzed using a medical-grade blood glucose detector in the laboratory during each visit. Patients were instructed not to take any medication before blood sample collection.

Since hyperglycemia is characterized by chronic progression, a cumulative exposure to hyperglycemia may be a more pronounced factor that needed to be concerned. We calculated the cumulative fasting blood glucose (FBG) value from 2014 to 2018 by determining the area under the trajectory of blood glucose levels during follow-up expressed as mmol/L×years, then dividing it by the follow-up period in years. This calculation allowed for a better comprehension as it shared the same unit as a single measurement. The use of a cumulative index enabled the assessment of the cumulative burden of risk factors over time, providing more accurate measurements than cross-sectional assessments.

Additionally, we also recorded the FBG values collected concurrently with MRI acquisition in 2020 to represent a single measurement (single measurement FBG value).

Neuroimaging data acquisition and processing

Neuroimaging data from all participants were obtained by a 3.0 T MRI scanner (GE 750 W; General Electric Healthcare Systems, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, USA). The parameters for DTI sequence used in this study were as follows: TR/TE = 8000/97.9 ms; bandwidth = 250 KHz; field of view = 240 × 240 mm; matrix = 128 × 130; slice thickness = 5 mm; b-value= 1000 mm2/s. In addition, standard and reverse phase encoded blipped images with no diffusion weighting were acquired to correct for distortions induced by the magnetic induction strength associated with echo-planar imaging acquisitions.

For the calculation of DTI-ALPS index, first, the diffusion values for the x, y, and z axes within each subject's ROI were obtained. The DTI-ALPS index was calculated as the ratio of the mean value of the x-axis diffusion coefficient in the projection area (Dxxproj) and the x-axis diffusion coefficient in the association area (Dxxassoc) to the mean value of the y-axis diffusion coefficient in the projection area (Dyyproj) and the z-axis diffusion coefficient in the association area (Dzzassoc), as follows:

An DTI-ALPS index close to 1 indicates minimal diffusion along the perivascular space, while a higher ratio suggests increased water diffusion along the perivascular space. In this study, separate calculations were performed for the DTI-ALPS index of the left and right hemispheres of the brain, as well as for the average DTI-ALPS index.

Statistical analysis and sensitivity analysis

The normality of the data was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables with a normal distribution and consistent with homogeneity of variance were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Kruskal-Wallis method was used for analysis if data was not normally distributed. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables.

We conducted Spearman correlation analysis to examine the association between the cumulative values of FBG and the left, right, and average DTI-ALPS index among all participants.

Participants were classified into three groups separately based on their FBG levels: normal FBG, moderate hyperglycemia, and profound hyperglycemia. The cut-off values for FBG levels were set at 6.1 mmol/L and 7.0 mmol/L according to American Diabetes Association (ADA) 2019 recommended diagnostic criteria. The reference group was the normal FBG group with the lowest FBG level. We used a generalized linear model to examine the association between cumulative FBG, single measurement FBG, and the DTI-ALPS index while adjusting for age, sex, lifestyle factors (including smoking, alcohol consumption, and physical activity), triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, creatinine levels, and diastolic blood pressure at the same time of MRI acquisition. Furthermore, to test the robustness of the main results, we conducted sensitivity analyses stratified by age groups (Jiang et al., 2023) (to ensure a similar number of samples between subgroups, <60 years old vs≥60 years old), sex (male vs female), and use of hypoglycemic medication to explore how FBG exposure affected DTI-ALPS in different subgroups.

The significance threshold was set at p < 0.05, determined by the least significant difference considered statistically significant after correcting for multiple testing. The IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analyses.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 251 participants, aged 25–82, were eligible to participate in the study (Fig. 1). The mean (SD) age was 60.09 (9.35) years, and 81 (32.3 %) participants were female. Baseline characteristics of subjects were classified into three groups according to cumulative FBG values from 2014 to 2018. Table 1 presents a summary of the clinical characteristics and neuroimaging features across different FBG exposure groups. No statistically significant differences were observed among the three groups for physical activity, alcohol consumption, smoking habits, creatinine levels, triglycerides levels, low-density lipoprotein levels, or diastolic blood pressure. Compared to participants with normal blood glucose levels, those with higher FBG exhibited lower high-density lipoprotein levels and lower DTI-ALPS index. Statistically significant differences were found in the left hemisphere DTI-ALPS index and average DTI-ALPS index among the three participant groups.

Association of cumulative 2014-2018 FBG exposure with glymphatic function

Association of 2020 FBG exposure with glymphatic function

Conversely, no significant associations were observed between the FBG exposure and the DTI-ALPS index in the main analysis when considering a single measurement of FBG 2020 (Table 2). However, we found a significant negative association between profound hyperglycemia and the left hemisphere's DTI-ALPS value in the female participants (β = −0.081, 95 % CI, −0.152 to −0.011; p = 0.024, ptrend = 0.031) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study analyzed the relationship between the long-term exposure to hyperglycemia and DTI-ALPS index which potentially indicate brain glymphatic function in a large-scale, community-based cohort study conducted across multiple centers. The key finding was that prolonged exposure to hyperglycemia, rather than a single FBG measurement, was associated with lower DTI-ALPS index, possibly plays an essential role in the impairment of glymphatic function, particularly among older individuals. Therefore, continuous monitoring of FBG levels is appropriate than a one-time measurement approach for evaluating brain health in this study. Furthermore, hypoglycemic medication may play a protective role in maintaining normal glymphatic function. Consequently, maintaining long-term normal FBG levels and using appropriate hypoglycemic medication are essential for promoting optimal brain health. Our study provides new insights into the impact of hyperglycemia on brain health.

There are several advantages of study. First, to the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the effect of long-term exposure to hyperglycemia on DTI-ALPS index in a large population-based cohort. Given the substantial body of research evidence suggesting that the measures of cumulative exposure to risk factors provide more comprehensive insights than single measurement at the baseline timepoint (Shi et al., 2023, Lv et al., 2024), we employed the cumulative average method when calculating the long-term exposure to hyperglycemia status. In addition, the repeated assessments of FBG status can effectively reduce random errors and mitigate the potential for regression dilution. Second, We applied a novel, noninvasive, and safe approach, known as the DTI-ALPS for potentially measure the activity of the glymphatic system in human subjects. As a quantitative parameter reflecting one aspect of brain health status, the DTI-ALPS method has been extensively studied (Chang et al., 2023, Gumeler et al., 2023, Saito et al., 2023). In recent decades, many studies have employed this method to investigate the relationship between impaired glymphatic function and a variety of neurological diseases(Sun et al., 2024, Tong et al., 2025). It has been demonstrated that dysfunction of the glymphatic system is associated with epilepsy (Zhao et al., 2023), and Alzheimer's disease (Harrison et al., 2020). Therefore, the DTI-ALPS method used in this study is highly suitable and significant for investigating the impact of glucose metabolism on the glymphatic system, highlighting the importance of investigating the role of glymphatic system in promoting brain health and developing potential treatments.

In the present study, the cumulative exposure to profound hyperglycemia was negatively correlated with the DTI-ALPS index in all participants. Previous studies have confirmed that a decrease in the DTI-ALPS index may indicate impairment of the glial lymphatic pathway in the brain, which could lead to obstruction of the PVS pathway (Zhang et al., 2021). Thus, our findings could illustrate the detrimental impact of prolonged hyperglycemia status on lymphometabolic function. Additionally, we further observed a significant decreasing trend in the right and left hemisphere as well as the average DTI-ALPS index during the process of increasing blood glucose concentration from moderate to profound hyperglycemia. This finding may suggests a correlation between elevated cumulative blood glucose levels and worsening brain glymphatic system circulatory dysfunction.

The sensitivity analysis suggests that profound hyperglycemia is associated with a significant decrease in the DTI-ALPS index in subjects who did not take hypoglycemic drugs. However, for subjects taking hypoglycemic drugs, no significant difference in the DTI-ALPS index was found compared to normal individuals. The results indicate that when individuals exposed to prolonged profound hyperglycemia, who are not taking hypoglycemic medication, may more vulnerable to the detrimental effects of hyperglycemia on human brain glymphatic function. The study found that the significant association between cumulative hyperglycemia and decreased DTI-ALPS index related to age was only observed in participants over 60 years old. This might be attributed to age-related baseline glymphatic function decline. Studies show that the efficiency of glymphatic clearance decreases with age. As people age, the pumping function of glymphatic vessels may be impaired, thereby hindering the flow of lymph fluid. This might prevent the brain from effectively clearing potentially toxic proteins associated with neurodegenerative diseases (Du et al., 2024). In the elderly, the glymphatic system may be more vulnerable to chronic hyperglycemia, as the combined effect of aging and metabolic stress exacerbates this damage (Wang et al., 2023b).

Lower DTI-ALPS index has been noted to possibly reflect impaired brain glymphatic function, and the results of the present study indicate that the cumulative exposure to profound hyperglycemia is associated with lower DTI-ALPS index, may plays an essential role in the impairment of glymphatic function.And the mechanisms explaining how prolonged hyperglycemia could negatively affect glymphatic function are largely unclear. Previous studies (Andica et al., 2023) have shown that hyperglycemia may stimulate the generation of reactive oxygen species(ROS), thus causing damage to astrocytes and the blood-brain barrier, leading to perivascular space enlargement in diabetic rat models. resulting in an increase in the perivascular space and other pathways (McMurray et al., 2016, Rehman and Akash, 2017). Animal studies further indicate that ROS-mediated AQP4 mislocalization impairs cerebrospinal fluid and interstitial fluid(CSF-ISF) exchange. Aquaporin-4 is expressed in the endfeet of astrocytes and is essential for maintaining glymphatic function by facilitating CSF movement from perivascular spaces into interstitial space and activating ISF flushing (Smith et al., 2017, Mestre et al., 2020). Therefore, decreased activation of astrocyte may lead to aquaporin-4 deficiency and consequently, lead to glymphatic impairment, including a reduction in CSF-ISF efflux. The expansion of perivascular space caused by the infiltration of inflammatory cells, which is commonly observed in hyperglycemia, may also contribute to the reduction in glymphatic clearance.

There are several limitations to be considered in our study. First, we only performed once brain MRI examination. Longitudinal MRI data are warranted in future studies to determine the relationship between FBG metabolism and DTI-ALPS index, to find the change in the state of brain lymphoid function. Second, some studies have shown that the DTI-ALPS index may be slightly influenced by some factors such as the surrounding white matter microstructure within the ROI, and this method may not considered to be a comprehensive reflection of the entire extremely complex glymphatic system (Ringstad, 2024, Taoka et al., 2024). Therefore, further investigation is needed to enhance the stability and precision of glymphatic function measurement using the DTI-ALPS method and try to find the method to evaluate brain glymphatic function more comprehensively. Finally, caution may be warranted when generalizing our conclusions drawn from a 4-year follow-up to shorter or longer follow-up periods.

Conclusion

Prolonged exposure to hyperglycemia is associated with lower DTI-ALPS index, potentially reflect the impairment of glymphatic function, particularly among elder individuals and those not taking hypoglycemic medication. Maintaining long-term normal FBG levels and the appropriate use of hypoglycemic medication are recommended for promoting optimal brain health in the general population.

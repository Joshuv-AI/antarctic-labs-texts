Integrated WGCNA retrieval of T-cell exhaustion genes related to radiosensitivity in locally advanced cervical cancer and prediction of immunotherapy efficacy
Authors: Meilian Dong, Chunyan Zhang, Xiangxian Zhang, Yuehui Su
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFor more than two decades, concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) has been considered the standard treatment for locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC) and has achieved remarkable clinical results. Nevertheless, 30–40% of treated patients would appear recurrence within 5 years. Recently, Immunotherapy-based programs have been attempted for effective treatment of LACC, while T cell exhaustion greatly limits the application of immunotherapy in cancer radiotherapy (RT). This study aimed to comprehensively characterize the specific prognostic factors of radiosensitivity-related T cell exhaustion (rrTex) in LACC, and identify gene signatures that contribute to predict the efficacy of immunotherapy. LACC samples in several datasets were categorized into two groups (benefit vs. no benefit) according to their responses after RT. A total of 2878 differential genes were selected as candidate genes. Based on TCGA training dataset, WGCNA, LASSO regression analysis, 11 rrTex genes were selected to establish the rrTex gene signature. The Kaplan-Meier curves showed that the prognosis of the low-risk group was better than that of the high-risk group. The immune infiltration score of the high-risk group was significantly different from that of the low-risk group. Subsequently, the PRJEB23709 immunotherapy cohort was used to explore the efficacy of immunotherapy. The rrTex gene signature may facilitate to predict prognosis and assess the efficacy of immunotherapy, and has the potential to facilitate the personalized and precise treatment of LACC in the future.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03673-y.Keywords: Locally advanced cervical cancer, Tumor immunology, Exhaustion, Radiotherapy, Prognostic signature

Introduction

Cervical cancer is a common cancer among women worldwide. In 2018, approximately 570,000 new cases and 311,000 deaths from cervical cancer occurred [1, 2]. Since the National Cancer Institute issued a consensus statement changing the standard of care for LACC cancer to CCRT more than 20 years ago, treatment has remained largely unchanged [3, 4]. Innovations in RT are expected to further improve local control and OS [5]. However, 10–30% of patients after CCRT will develop distant metastases, and 5-year survival rates for stage Ⅲ and ⅣA are 39.6% and 24%, respectively [6]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to improve RT sensitivity and the systemic integrated treatment strategy.

The therapeutic effects of RT on local tumors require CD8 + T cells [7]. However, in addition to its antitumor effects, RT induces the recruitment of immunosuppressive cells and releases damage-associated molecular patterns, which may lead to T cell exhaustion (Tex) and then significantly impair its effects [8, 9]. Tex, is characterized by the loss of effector cytotoxic functions, altered expression of key transcription factors, upregulation or co-expression of multiple inhibitory molecule receptors, and altered intracellular metabolism [10]. Tex influences RT sensitivity, and prompted the use of a combination of RT and immunotherapy to improve tumor control through radiosensitivity [11]. CCRT with blockade of the PD-1 pathway may enhance immune-mediated tumor control through increased phagocytosis, cell death, and antigen presentation. Recently, several clinical studies have shown that the combination of CCRT and immunotherapy can enhance the treatment efficacy of cervical cancer. Manuel Rodrigues and Giulia Vanoni, et al.. reported that concomitant nivolumab plus definitive CCRT is safe and associated with encouraging progression-free survival (PFS) rates [12]. Even more surprising research findings comes from Domenica Lorussois, et al., and they find that pembrolizumab plus CCRT significantly improved PFS and overall survival (OS) in patients with LACC, and that immuno-chemoradiotherapy strategy is a new standard of care for LACC [13, 14].

Although immunotherapy is effective in increasing the killing effect of CD8 + T cells on tumor cells, it is currently estimated to benefit only 13% of cancer patients, with a significant proportion developing immune-related adverse events [15]. Durvalumab concurrent with CCRT was well tolerated in participants with LACC, however it did not significantly improve PFS in a biomarker unselected, all-comers population. Concurrent durvalumab plus CCRT warrants further exploration in patients with high tumoral PD-L1 expression [16].Screening patients for the need for combination therapy, which reduces both side effects and economic burden, is a meaningful research direction.

This study developed a rrTex gene signature to predict the efficacy of immunotherapy in LACC using weighted gene correlation network analysis (WGCNA), Cox analysis and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, including 11 key rrTex genes (CCR7, MAP4K1, BIN2, ZAP70, APOBEC3H, CLEC2D, SLC15A3, PILRA, ADGRF4, DUOXA1, and IFI16). The rrTex gene signature was significantly correlated with immune infiltration, tumor mutation-related characteristics, and the tumor tissue stromal score and immune scores, which could be immune indicators of the efficacy of immunotherapy and clinical precision treatment options for LACC in the future. This study novelly analyses the relationship between rrTex and immunotherapy efficacy in RT treatment of LACC, and also provides ideas for optimizing the treatment options for LACC.

Materials and methods

Data collection, processing, landscaping and screening of hub Tex genes

The RT data for LACC were obtained from GSE14404, GSE56363, GSE168009, and GSE190075 datasets in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Information on Tex genes was obtained from Zang et al. [17]. , including Tex specific pathways. The corresponding hallmark pathway genes were identified (h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt) based on information from the MSigDB database (https://www.gseamsigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). The fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped fragments (FPKM) of samples from patients with cervical carcinoma and their clinical data were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-CESC dataset). These samples were used as training datasets, in which 296 were cancer samples. The GSE52903 dataset was used to validate the predictive model.The ‘limma’ R package was used to calculate the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the RT benefit and no-benefit samples, based on RT-related dataset of LACC. DEGs were identified as having false discovery rates (FDR) < 0.05, and |log[fold change]| >0. The ‘ggplot2’ and ‘ComplexHeatmap’ R packages were used to plot volcano maps and heatmaps, respectively.

Identification of the RrTex genes using WGCNA

In order to determine the degree of DEGs associated with radiosensitivity in the samples of TCGA-CESC dataset, we calculated the enrichment score of the samples for the set of genes related to DEGs using Single sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA). And then, the R package “WGCNA” was used to analyze the most relevant modules of DEGs and the core genes in the modules for immunophenotyping of these samples and to plot the distribution map of the soft threshold and average connectivity. The advantage of WGCNA is that tens of thousands of genes can be divided into dozens of modules based on their gene expression patterns, which can be used to explore the associations between the gene network, the phenotype of interest, and the core genes in the network. To ensure that the network was scale-free, the scale-free fitting index threshold was set to 0.85. The genes were clustered using the average-linkage hierarchical clustering method, following the criteria of a hybrid dynamic tree cut and setting the minimum number of genes per gene network module at 30. A WGCNA was constructed based on the ssGSEA enrichment score of the sample and the top 75% of the absolute median differences in the expression profiles of patients with cervical cancer. Identified modules that were significantly correlated with the enrichment score of Tex genes associated with radiosensitivity were filtered using a module gene correlation MM > 0.7 and feature significance GS > 0.3 as thresholds. Finally, the key module genes were selected as the rrTex genes.

Functional enrichment analysis of RrTex genes

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses were conducted on the candidate genes, and visualized using the ‘ClusterProfiler’ R package.

Definition of RrTex gene signature

A univariate Cox proportional hazards regression model was designed based on the rrTex genes using the coxph function in the ‘survival’ R package. Briefly, the rrTex genes associated with patient prognosis were screened according to P-value < 0.05. The obtained hazard coefficients were further processed using the LASSO method through the ‘glmnet’ R package to minimize the risk of overfitting and thus obtain an interpretable model. The signature score of the patients was then calculated based on the gene expression profiles and their corresponding regression coefficients as follows:

Score = ∑βi × χi =0.

where βi and χi represent the weight coefficient and expression of each gene, respectively. After LASSO Cox regression analysis, the samples were divided into high- and low-risk groups, according to the median expression value of the rrTex genes associated with prognosis. Survival curves for prognostic analysis were performed using the ‘survival’ and ‘survminer’ R packages, and the significance of the difference was determined by the log-rank test.

Signature associated with patient prognosis and clinical characteristics

The predictive potential of the proposed risk score model was evaluated using a respective receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. The area under the curve (AUC) was visualized using the ‘timeROC’ R package. The ‘ggplot2’ R package was used to draw a box plot to visualize the difference in risk scores in the training dataset samples in light of different clinical characteristics of the patients. Rank-sum and Kruskal-Wallis tests were used to assess the differences between two or more groups, respectively.

Differences in immune infiltration of RrTex gene signature

Each type of immune-infiltrating cell was identified as previously described by Charoentong et al. [16]. The stromal and immune scores, which were used to assess immunoactivity, were calculated in the tumor tissue using the ‘estimate’ R package by ESTIMATE algorithm, and finally the two scores were combined to generate an estimated score. Next, the immune cell infiltration scores were calculated by ssGSEA, Cell Type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT), and xCell analysis, and the differences in immune infiltration scores in the high- and low-score groups were plotted using the ‘ggplot2’ R package. The significance of the differences between the groups was determined using the rank-sum test.

Immunotherapy response validation across cancer types

Given the limited availability of comprehensive cervical cancer immunotherapy datasets with paired transcriptomic and clinical response data, we employed a cross-cancer validation strategy. Initial validation was performed using the PRJEB23709 melanoma cohort (accessed via EMBL-EBI, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/data/view/PRJEB23709) [14], a well-characterized anti-PD-1 immunotherapy dataset. Risk stratification was implemented by applying our rrTex-derived signature algorithm to calculate prognostic scores for each melanoma patient, followed by comparative analysis of response rates between risk subgroups.

To further assess the generalizability of our predictive model, we evaluated its performance in an independent lung cancer immunotherapy cohort (GSE135222), as shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 (Fig.S1).

IHC assay

The LACC patients’ tumor tissue slides were obtained from the department of Pathology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University. The IHC assay was performed according to the operating instructions (PV-9000, ZSGB-BIO, China). Briefly, after dewaxing and boiling in sodium citrate antigen retrieval solution (0.01 mol/L, pH 8.0) for 20 min, the slides were blocked with endogenous peroxidase blocking solution for 20 min at room temperature. Subsequently, the sections were incubated at 4 °C overnight with the corresponding primary antibody against APOBEC3H (1:200, PA5-54426, Invitrogen, USA), CCR7 (1:100, 25898-1-AP, Proteintech, China), PLRA (1:100, PA5-147474, Invitrogen, USA), SLC15A3 (1:100, PA5-61614, Invitrogen, USA). After washing with PBS, the secondary antibody was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min, and stained with DAB kit (ZLI-9018, ZSGB-BIO, China).

WGCNA identification of RrTex genes signature in LACC

To identify Tex genes associated with radiosensitivity in the TCGA-CESC dataset, the mRNA expression data of the training dataset were filtered to remove outlier samples (those above the red line in Fig. 2A). Next, the genes were clustered and selected based on an optimal soft threshold of 6, when the scale-free fit index was greater than 0.85 (Fig. 2C), and the closer modules were merged into new modules (Fig. 2B). Module features (module eigengenes) were determined using the first principal component of each module in the expression profile. The enrichment score of the samples for the set of Tex genes associated with radiosensitivity was calculated as the level of immunogenic cell death in the samples using ssGSEA. Based on the age, grade, and levels of Tex genes associated with radiosensitivity as phenotypes, the Pearson correlation coefficient between the module eigengenes of each module and the phenotype characteristics of samples was computed (Fig. 2D). Modules with a higher correlation with immunogenic cell death levels (blue, brown, and purple modules) were selected for further analysis. A total of 507 rrTex genes were screened, using module membership > 0.7 and gene signature > 0.3 (Fig. 2E).

Functional analysis of the RrTex genes

The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network within the 507 rrTex selected by WGCNA was then analyzed (Fig. 3A). They mainly contributed to immune and cytokine receptor activities, among other molecular functions. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis further showed that the selected rrTex genes were mainly involved in cell adhesion molecules, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, and Th17 cell differentiation (Fig. 3B). The selected rrTex genes were mainly involved in T cell activation, mononuclear cell differentiation, regulation of T cell activation, and lymphocyte differentiation according to GO biological process analysis showed that (Fig. 3C), and mainly involved in immune receptor activity, cytokine receptor activity according to GO biological process analysis (Fig. 3D). What’s more, the enrichment analysis of cellular component also demonstrates that the selected rrTex genes were markedly enriched in immunological synapse and MHC protein complex (Fig. 3E).

The risk score for each cancer sample was calculated based on the rrTex gene signature, and the cancer samples were divided into high- and low-risk score groups based on the median score. Subsequently, the Kaplan–Meier curve, ROC curve, sample risk score, sample survival status, and the expression of prognostic factors in the high- and low-risk score groups were determined in the training (Figs. 6A-E) and validation (Figs. 7A-E) datasets. Overall, the low-risk score group was found to have better prognosis (Figs. 6A and 7A). The AUC for the rrTex gene signature at 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates were 0.776, 0.710, and 0.749, respectively in the training dataset (Fig. 6B). Correspondingly, the AUC for the rrTex gene signature at 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates were 0.678, 0.646, and 0.654, respectively in the validation dataset (Fig. 7B). These results indicated that the rrTex gene signature may facilitate to predict the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of patients with cervical cancer. Moreover, the score distribution in the two groups was generally continuous, without any anomalies or extreme cases (Figs. 6C-D and 7C-D). The RrTex gene signature was an independent prognostic factor

In the training (Fig. 8A and B) and validation (Fig. 8C and D) datasets, we considered the rrTex gene signature as a continuous variable, and found that the rrTex gene signature was an independent factor in predicting prognosis of cervical cancer patients by univariate and multivariable Cox regression analysis. The results of the multivariable Cox regression analysis were as follows, training group: HR 4.58 (1.71–12.31), P = 0.003 (Fig. 8B); validation group: HR 2.68 (1.07–6.72), P = 0.04 (Fig. 8D). The results indicated that the rrTex gene signature was correlated with patients’ survival significantly both in training cohort and validation cohort.

Correlation analysis of the RrTex gene signature and clinicopathological characteristics

We assessed the correlation between the rrTex gene signature and the clinicopathological characteristics of cervical cancer. There was no significant correlation between the risk score and pathological grade, age, lymphovascular invasion indicator, M stage, or N stage (P > 0.05; Figs. 9A, B,C, D,E, G,H, and I).

Relationship between the RrTex gene signature and tumor microenvironment

Next, the impact of the rrTex gene signature on the tumor microenvironment in the high- and low-risk groups was evaluated by assessing the tumor tissue stromal score and immune scores, which were then combined to generate an estimated score. The gene risk scores were negatively correlated with the stromal, estimated, and immune scores, and positively correlated with tumor purity (Fig. 10A). Moreover, the differences in immune infiltration scores between the high- and low-risk groups were explored (Fig. 10B). Several cell types showed different immune infiltration scores between the high- and low-risk groups (e.g., activated dendritic cells, eosinophils, M0 macrophages, M1 macrophages, activated mast cells, resting mast cells, plasma cells, activated CD4 + memory T cells, resting CD4 + memory T cells, CD8 + T cells, and regulatory T cells). Moreover, eosinophils, M0 macrophages, and active mast cells were significantly enriched in the high-risk group, while the infiltrated level of M1 macrophages, resting mast cells, and CD8 + T cells were higher in the low-risk group (Fig. 10C).

Immunotherapeutic efficacy stratification by risk signature

To systematically evaluate the predictive capacity of our signature for immunotherapy response, we performed comprehensive validation across multiple cancer types. Initial analysis of the melanoma cohort (PRJEB23709) demonstrated significant associations between risk stratification and clinical outcomes: (1) The low-risk group exhibited superior progression-free survival (HR = 2.16, 95%CI 1.23–3.78, P = 0.006; Fig. 11A) and higher objective response rates (P = 0.004; Fig. 11C); (2) Responders showed significantly lower risk scores compared to non-responders (P = 5.9 × 10^-5; Fig. 11B).

Validation of the protein expression levels of four key RrTex genes

IHC assay were conducted to evaluate the expression of four key rrTex genes in 20 locally advanced cervical cancer patients treated with chemoradiotherapy in the complete response (CR) and noncomplete response (NCR) groups. As shown in Fig. 5, the expression levels of APOBEC3H, CCR7 and PILRA were significantly higher in CR group when compared with NCR groups. In addition, the expression level of SLC15A3 was higher in CR group when compared with NCR groups, but this higher expression was not significant.

Discussion

International studies on RT combined with immunotherapy for LACC are currently in their initial stages. According to recent reports, pembrolizumab plus CCRT significantly improved PFS and OS in patients with locally advanced cervical cancer [14]. However, Bradley J Monk reported that durvalumab concurrent with CCRT did not significantly improve PFS in a biomarker unselected, all-comers population of LACC [16]. There is still a big challenge in identifying LACC patients with survival benefits from concurrent immunotherapy and CCRT. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify therapeutic targets and prognostic biomarkers based on tumor immunology.

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive assessment of rrTex in LACC, and identified a gene signature that could predict patient survival. The strength of this study is that patients in the GEO database studying the prognosis of LACC with concurrent radiotherapy were included to study their immune characteristics, thus increasing the credibility of the results. Eleven rrTex genes were identified using WGCNA and LASSO to obtain a signature scoring model. The prognosis of the low-risk score group for the rrTex gene signature was better. The immune infiltration score of the high-risk group was significantly different from that of the low-risk group. The rrTex gene signature maybe helpful in predicting the efficacy of immunotherapy.

While previous studies have associated elevated CCR7 expression with poor prognosis in cervical cancer due to its putative role in promoting lymph node and systemic metastases [18], our study reveals a paradoxical association between high CCR7 expression and improved clinical outcomes in LACC patients receiving RT. This apparent contradiction may be explained by recent advances in understanding the immunomodulatory functions of CCR7 within the irradiated tumor microenvironment (TME). Integrative multi-omics analyses have demonstrated that CCR7 can orchestrate antitumor immunity by enhancing infiltration of plasma cells, regulatory T cells, and CD8 + T cells [19], while Ren et al. identified CCR7 as a specific marker for radiation-induced M1 macrophage polarization that mediates proinflammatory antitumor responses [20]. These findings align with our KEGG/GO enrichment and immune infiltration analyses. Importantly, our IHC validation provides direct clinical evidence that CCR7 protein expression positively correlates with treatment response (P < 0.05), suggesting that in the context of RT, CCR7 may function as an immune-activating driver.

IFI16 may be involved in the immune escape mechanism in HPV infection or promote cervical cancer progression by upregulating PD-L1 in the TME of cervical cancer [21, 22]. Both reports were contrary to our finding that high expressions of IFI16 was associated with a better prognosis after RT in LACC. However, a correlation between IFI16 expression and sensitivity to radiotherapy in cervical cancer has not been reported. Moreover, researchers have found that RT enhanced anti-tumor immunity through the IFI16-STING pathway in lung adenocarcinoma [23], and that preventing the degradation of IFI16 activated the STAT3 and NF-κB signaling pathways and promoted RT resistance in glioblastoma [24]. These findings indicated that additional research is required to fully understand the role of IFI16 in the prognosis of cervical cancer, particularly in terms of Tex and RT sensitivity in patients with LACC.

Several studies have shown that high expression of ZAP70 gene and ZAP70 proteins was related to the ability of T cells to respond to antigenic stimulation, RT sensitivity, and better prognosis in cervical cancer [25–29]. However, most researchers have used bioinformatics analysis or literature searches to reach their conclusions. Therefore, the ability of ZAP70 gene to alter the patients’ reaction to radiation requires more precise experimental methods and clinical trials.

BIN2, DUOX1 and CLEC2D may also be potential prognostic indicators related to the immune status of TME [27, 30]. Recently, researchers confirmed that the upregulation of MAP4K1 was favorable for cervical cancer [31]. Ding et al. found that ADGRF4 was negatively correlated with cervical cancer survival by public database Mining [32]. These findings were consistent with our results. However, their correlations with RT in cervical cancer is unclear.

The remaining 3 genes, APOBEC3H, SLC15A3 and PILRA, were associated with cervical cancer for the first time. The identification of APOBEC3H, SLC15A3, and PILRA as novel cervical cancer-associated genes represents a significant finding of our study. As first-reported biomarkers in cervical cancer, these genes may offer new prognostic references, particularly given their differential expression patterns between treatment response groups. Our IHC validation demonstrated significantly elevated expression of APOBEC3H and PILRA in complete responders (CR) compared to non-complete responders (NCR), suggesting their potential role in mediating therapeutic efficacy. While SLC15A3 showed a non-significant trend toward higher expression in CR patients, this observation may reflect either biological variability or the need for larger validation cohorts. The parallel findings for APOBEC3H and PILRA introduce new candidates for understanding radiation response mechanisms. APOBEC3H [33], a member of the APOBEC cytidine deaminase family, may contribute to genomic instability and enhanced immunogenicity, while PILRA’s [34] role in immune regulation through paired immunoglobulin-like receptors warrants further investigation in cervical cancer contexts. These novel associations, combined with our functional enrichment analyses, suggest that the rrTex signature captures both established and previously unrecognized elements of cervical cancer biology that influence treatment outcomes.

Limitation

This study has some limitations. First, it should be emphasized that the present analysis is a retrospective study based on the TCGA and GEO databases and has not been experimentally validated. In addition, incomplete clinicopathological data in the public databases can lead to biased results in the analysis. For example, the HPV infection status is not analyzed separately. It is worth to mentioning that the PRJEB23709 immunotherapy dataset from the melanoma cohort was used to explore the efficacy of immunotherapy due to the scarcity of immunotherapy data for cervical cancer. However, when combined with results of KEGG/GO enrichment analysis, TMB, TME landscape, immune cell infiltration, and IHC assay results of the protein expression levels of APIBEC3H, CCR7, PILRA, and SLC15A3 in LACC patients, this provides some indication of the rrTex signature in predicting the outcome of immunotherapy in LACC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we used WGCNA for the first time to report the relationship between rrTex and immunotherapy efficacy in radiotherapy for LACC based on the TCGA and GEO databases. In our study, a rrTex signature was established and validated, and some potential biomarkers were found to be related to the TME and effects of immunotherapy in radiotherapy for LACC. Additionally, IHC assays were performed to verify the protein expression of four novel key genes (APOBEC3H, PLA2R1, CCR7 and SLC15A3) in an independent cervical cancer cohort, which consistently confirmed the bioinformatics predictions. However, these markers must be verified in real-world settings.

Information on T-cell exhaustion-related genes was obtained from Zang et al. [17] including T-cell exhaustion-related specific pathways. The corresponding hallmark pathway genes were identified (h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt) based on information from the MSigDB database. (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/).

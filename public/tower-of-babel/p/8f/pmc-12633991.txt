Significance of family medicine specialty: Perception and awareness among the public in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
Authors: Khalid A. B. Abdulrahman, Hisham I. Alturki, Abdullah A. AL-Mohsen, Saleh M. Aldalaan, Khaled E. Alharby, Hesham I. Almaarik, Abdulrhman A. Alqahtani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Family doctors are essential for personalized health care, including physical and mental health, and they are vital for preventive care. Not having a family doctor may result in delayed diagnosis and lack personal health awareness. This article focuses on understanding the level of consciousness about family medicine in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.Methods:In September 2024, a population-based cross-sectional survey was conducted among the population of Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. A total of 403 participants were older than 18 years and were eligible to participate in the study. Participants were approached through social media platforms with a link to the study questionnaire. SPSS was used to analyze the data.Results:A slight majority of the participants were men, with 224 responses (55.6%). Awareness of the importance of family physicians was high, with 352 responses (87.3%). Of these, 285 participants visited a family physician regularly (70.8%).Conclusion:The study evaluated the awareness of the importance of family medicine, its significance, and the dangers of neglecting it. As a result, the study population demonstrated a relatively high level of awareness regarding the general importance of family medicine. Still, it lacked a deeper understanding of the role of family physicians.

Introduction

Family medicine (F.M.) is not just another medical specialty, but one of the most crucial worldwide. Its wide range of health services is meant for all individuals regardless of age, sex, and affected organ or system. It stands on a few significant principles: comprehensiveness, continuity, coordination, and accessibility. This underlines its importance in the health care system.[1] Role of Family Doctors: The First Point of Entry into the Healthcare System and Continued Care for Patients and Their Families.[2] Providing appropriate healthcare resources by interacting with other medical specialists and advocating for patients when necessary is one of the family doctor’s most essential responsibilities.[3] A strong doctor-patient relationship is built on mutual respect, trust, and confidentiality, which are necessary for better healthcare outcomes. Studies show that when patients clearly understand their physician’s role, they are more likely to follow treatment plans, which leads to improved health results, especially for chronic or stigmatized conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, cancer, and mental health disorders.[4567] Increasing public awareness of family physicians (FPs) is essential for adequate healthcare, as it significantly impacts patients’ health-seeking behaviors. Studies indicate that patients often bypass primary healthcare clinics (PHCCs) and seek care at specialty centers or tertiary hospitals, frequently without an apparent reason. This underscores the need for further research into patients’ understanding of the role of FPs.[8]

Several studies have examined the public’s perception of family medicine.[910] In Pakistan, for example, a 2004 survey found that many patients seeking specialized care were unaware of family physicians’ role in the healthcare system.[11] Studies from areas such as Jazan, Dammam, and Qassim in Saudi Arabia reveal that people generally hold a positive attitude toward family medicine and value the role of family physicians in the healthcare system.[121314] However, there’s still a lack of clarity on how well the public truly understands the concept of family medicine and its various aspects. This study aims to address this gap by assessing the general knowledge of family medicine among the Saudi population, supporting the objectives of Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030.[15] Specifically, the study will measure public awareness of the role of family medicine and evaluate the importance of family medicine triage and regular check-ups.

This study aligns closely with the journal’s focus on community health awareness and healthcare service perception, which are central to public health advancement.

Materials and Methods

Study design and study setting

A cross-sectional study was conducted in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, from September 16, 2024, to October 8, 2024, to assess perceptions and awareness of family medicine.

Ethical considerations and study participants: 403 residents of Riyadh participated in this study. Participants who reside in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, and are older than 18 years were eligible to participate. And any participants under 18 were excluded. The study was approved by Imam Mohammad Ibn Saud Islamic University Ethics Committee (Ref: HAPO-01-R-011).

Study sample size and sampling methods

This study was not a clinical trial; therefore, no trial registry was required.

We collected data by distributing a link through various social media platforms, ensuring a random sampling of participants. This approach allowed us to reach a diverse audience across demographics and geographic locations.

Data collection and study questionnaire

We used a validated assessment tool from a previously published study.[13] Participants were approached through social media platforms with a link to the study questionnaire provided in Arabic and English. They included a consent form for the use of participant data in research. According to the requirements of the university ethics committee for cross-sectional studies, each participant provided informed consent.

Demographic and social/clinical characteristics questionnaire

The study questionnaire has four sections. The first covers the demographic characteristics of the study group. The second includes questions about awareness and knowledge regarding family medicine among the included participants. The third section covers the participants’ general perception about family medicine. The fourth section concerns experience and attitudes towards family medicine among the participants.

Statistical analysis

All data were stored and sorted using Microsoft Excel Version 2023, and analysis was performed using the Statistical Package of Social Science (SPSS) version 29.

IBM Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS), Windows 26.0 version, was used to analyze the data, and Microsoft Excel 2016 was used to present the data in tables and graphs.

Discussion

In our study, we aimed to assess the awareness and perception of family medicine among the population of Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Most respondents have shown good awareness about family physicians, but clearly, there is a gap in fully understanding their role and their capabilities of treating various conditions.

Our result showed that 96% of the participants had heard about the term family physician, which reflects a very high percentage [Table 2]. On the other hand, when it comes to understanding the role of family physicians only 56.3% understand the role of family physicians in society, the other 43.7% were not sure, or did not know the role at all [Table 2], this low percentage of understanding may affect the utilization the family medicine services.

In addition, we can see that 66.7% of the total respondents have visited a family physician at least once in their lifetime [Table 2], which is a much higher percentage than in another study conducted in all 13 regions of Saudi Arabia, which was 37.5% only (which is near the double).[13] This may lead to the conclusion that the attitude of the population in Riyadh is more towards going to family physicians than the average in Saudi Arabia.

Furthermore, the percentage that answered (no) to the question (Family physicians can only treat flu and refer you to other specialties) was 59.1%. By contrast, in another study conducted in the whole Saudi region,[17] it was 31,6%, which indicates a higher awareness and a better perception among the population of Riyadh.

In addition, 93.1% of the respondents believed that family physicians have graduated from a medical school [Table 3]. By contrast, in a study from Jizan, Saudi Arabia, the percentage of respondents who believed that a family physician had graduated from a medical school was only 65.3%.[12] This also indicates a higher knowledge and awareness of the family physician’s role in Riyadh.

More concerning is that the respondents believe that the family physician can treat emergent conditions such as heart attack and stroke, where only 28.3% [Table 3], while in a similar study, which was conducted at the Qassim region,[14] 29.5% believed that the family physician can treat such emergencies. This misunderstanding may affect the population’s preference for choosing a specialty other than family medicine while they are in a situation that requires a family physician. This issue becomes even more apparent when considering that 63% of participants preferred to involve a specialist or consultant from another specialty in their healthcare. This significant proportion raises concerns about the recognized role and importance of family medicine among the public [Table 4].

For the question regarding the regular check-ups [Table 4], 42,4% of the respondents said that they visit a family physician once a year or more, 18.1% of them were visiting the family physician 2 to 3 times a year, which indicates an excellent rate of regular check-ups among the respondents. On the other hand, 57,6% of the respondents visit the family physician once every year or more, OR haven’t visited a family physician before, which is a significant concern. It is a good point for improving the general attitude in our region towards visits to family physicians and primary health care as well.

The study highlights a generally positive perception and awareness of family medicine across all ages. Older individuals are more likely to use family physician services and recognize their role in managing chronic diseases. Educational efforts may focus on younger age groups to further enhance understanding and utilization of family medicine services.

The data suggest that females generally exhibit higher awareness and engagement with family physicians, with statistically significant differences in awareness, visitation history, and misconceptions. However, for most other questions, including the perceived importance of family physicians and their ability to treat chronic or emergent conditions, there were no significant gender differences. This highlights the need for targeted educational interventions to address misconceptions, particularly among males, and promote family physicians’ role in comprehensive healthcare delivery.

Educational level significantly impacts certain aspects of perception and utilization of family medicine, particularly in understanding roles and visiting practices. Targeted educational initiatives may bridge gaps, especially for those at lower or significantly higher education levels.

A study was conducted two years ago in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, on the capability of a family doctor to prescribe medication. The participants were asked (Can a family doctor prescribe medication). 86.1% of the participants said (yes),[18] Which is quite similar to our study when we asked the participants the same question, 84.9% answered (yes) [Table 3]. This still reflects a high percentage and shows good knowledge of family physicians’ role and their capabilities of prescribing medications.

Conclusion

Efforts are needed to bridge gaps in understanding the roles and capabilities of family physicians, particularly in chronic and emergent care. Ensuring equitable access to family medicine clinics across all income levels remains essential. Strategies to enhance trust in family physicians among higher-income groups could balance healthcare utilization. This analysis underscores the need for tailored interventions to improve the perception and integration of family medicine into the broader healthcare framework.

Limitations

The survey was conducted via social media platforms, which may exclude populations with limited internet access or those who do not use social media frequently. This could lead to a selection bias and may not fully represent the broader population of Riyadh. The survey relied on self-reported responses, subject to recall bias, social desirability bias, and inaccuracies. Participants may have overestimated their awareness or the regularity of their visits to family physicians. The study was restricted to Riyadh, and the results may not reflect the understanding and perceptions in other regions of Saudi Arabia, mainly rural or underserved areas.

Emergency department triaging using ChatGPT based on emergency severity index principles: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Cansu Colakca, Mehmet Ergın, Habibe Selmin Ozensoy, Alp Sener, Selahattin Guru, Ayhan Ozhasenekler
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractErroneous and delayed triage in an increasingly crowded emergency department (ED). ChatGPT is an artificial intelligence model developed by OpenAI® and is being trained for use in natural language processing tasks. Our study aims to determine the accuracy of patient triage using ChatGPT according to the emergency severity index (ESI) for triage in EDs. In our cross-sectional study, 18 years and over patients who consecutively presented to our ED within 24 h were included. Age, gender, admission method, chief complaint, state of consciousness, and comorbidities were recorded on the case form, and the vital signs were detected at the triage desk. A five-member expert committee (EC) was formed from the fourth-year resident physicians. The investigators converted real-time patient information into a standardized case format. The urgency status of the patients was evaluated simultaneously by EC and ChatGPT according to ESI criteria. The median value of the EC decision was accepted as the gold standard. There was a statistically significant moderate agreement between EC and ChatGPT assessments regarding urgency status (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.659; P < 0.001). The accuracy between these two assessments was calculated as 76.6%. There was a high degree of agreement between EC and ChatGPT for the prediction of ESI-1 and 2, indicating high acuity (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.828). The diagnostic specificity, NPV, and accuracy of ChatGPT were determined as 95.63, 98.17 and 94.90%, respectively, for ESI high acuity categories. Our study shows that ChatGPT can successfully differentiate patients with high urgency. The findings are promising for integrating artificial intelligence-based applications such as ChatGPT into triage processes in EDs.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Emergency department, Emergency severity index, TriageSubject terms: Health care, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

Emergency departments (EDs) are units that provide immediate intervention to victims of trauma, accidents, natural disasters, or life-threatening situations and provide uninterrupted service. The main goal of the EDs is to access patients who need urgent medical intervention fastest and recognize the most urgent patients. However, patients without urgent complaints also apply to EDs, which increases their workload. The most important point in the increased number of patients in EDs is to differentiate between the real emergent and non-emergent patients; therefore, triage is very important. Three, four, and five-step triage scales are used for this purpose1–4.

Emergency Severity Index (ESI) is a validated triage scale. This scale is a five-category triage scale that is applied in a short time, and it is possible to determine the priority order without specifying the waiting time by considering the number of radiological or laboratory resources to be consumed in the diagnosis and treatment process of patients. ESI focuses on two main questions while triaging the patients: Firstly, who should be assessed and, if there are more than one patient with these criteria, who can wait longer4.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) programs are a technical discipline that aims to do everything that human intelligence does using computer technology, and AI programs have begun to be used in many fields, including the medical field5–9. ChatGPT is a chat-based artificial intelligence program developed by OpenAI®, trained with reinforcement learning by using human feedback. It is designed based on machine learning and is an auditable and enforceable program. GPT-4 is a large and versatile model that, although less capable than humans, is capable of similar or human-level performance in many real-world situations. It also performs at the same level as humans on a variety of professional or academic metrics10,11.

The main aim of our study is to determine the accuracy of patient prioritization by using ChatGPT according to ESI principles for triage in EDs. Thus, it is aimed to facilitate the triage of patients in the operation of the EDs.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This cross-sectional study was carried out in the Emergency Department of Tertiary Research Hospital, which has a high capacity, accepts both personal admission and ground and air ambulance patient transfers, and has many facilities for examinations and consultation to all surgical departments.

All patients aged 18 years and over who applied between July 20, 2023, at 8.00 am and July 21, 2023, at 8.00 am were consecutively included in the study. Patients under the age of 18 and those who did not accept the study were excluded. Accordingly, 745 patients were included in the study.

Ethical statements

The Ankara Bilkent City Hospital Ethics Committee-2 approved this study on July 12, 2023 (Decision No: E2-23-4509). All participants provided written informed consent. The study was carried out as per the declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

Vital signs (blood pressure, oxygen saturation, pulse, and body temperature) of the patients who were admitted to the ED and accepted the study were recorded on the case form together with their age, gender, method of admission, chief complaint, state of consciousness, comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, hypertension, coronary artery disease, congestive heart failure, chronic kidney disease, cerebrovascular accident, liver failure).

The triage process used two monitors (GE Medical Systems, B40 Patient Monitor®, GE Medical Systems Information Technologies, Inc.). This monitor automatically measured and recorded blood pressure, pulse rate, and saturation values. The Temporal thermometer (Mesilife, DT-8806 Non-Contact Forehead Infrared Thermometer) measured body temperature.

Scoring system

Emergency severity index

With ESI triage, patients who ‘have the risk of wait’ and ‘require urgent intervention’ are primarily identified. The triage category of other patients is then determined according to the estimated number of available resources. The ESI application uses four basic decision stages for this purpose. Four key questions exist when determining a patient’s triage category4.

Is the patient in a life-threatening case, or is the patient dying?

Accordingly, patients are triaged by classifying them from the highest urgency category (one) to the lowest urgency category (five).

Triage category determination and patient grouping

First, before asking the cases to the chat-GPT, the online open-access Emergency Severity Index, Version 4: Implementation Handbook (4) was specified as a reference. Scores were requested for the patients according to the handbook.

Then, the information recorded in the case form was organized as follows: “A 70-year-old male patient was admitted to the ED by Emergency Medical Service (EMS) ambulance with complaints of abdominal pain and constipation. It was learned that the patient’s complaint of constipation had been going on for nine months, abdominal pain had been present for two days, and there was no stool discharge for one day. The patient had a known history of malignancy and coronary angiography. The patient’s general condition was good; he was conscious, orientated, and cooperative. His blood pressure was 110/70 mmHg, pulse rate was 97 beats/min, oxygen saturation was 94%, and body temperature was 36.3°C. What is the patient’s triage score according to ESI?“.

All 745 real-time cases were converted into text during November 2023.

The triage scores of all cases were evaluated independently and simultaneously by ChatGPT ver.4 (November 2023) in Turkish, and the results were recorded.

The results obtained were analyzed for both the whole patient group and individual ESI categories. Finally, ESI-1 and 2 categories were evaluated as high acuity groups; dichotomous data were created as “severe” and “mild,” and subgroup analysis was performed.

Statistical analyses

The study’s statistical analyses were performed with IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 20.0 (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Distribution analyses of continuous variables were performed by the Shapiro-Wilk Test, comparisons between two groups for normally distributed continuous variables were performed by the Independent Samples-t Test, and comparisons between two groups for non-normally distributed variables were performed by the Mann Whitney-U test. For comparisons of categorical data, Pearson Chi-Square and Fisher’s Exact test were used according to the case.

The median value of the results of ESI, which is an ordinal parameter obtained by the evaluations of five members of the EC, was accepted as the gold standard. The agreement analysis of the EC and ChatGPT was evaluated with the Kappa statistic. For Kappa coefficients, < 0.20 was considered poor, 0.21 to 0.40 reasonable, 0.41 to 0.60 moderate, 0.61 to 0.80 good, and 0.81 to 1.00 high concordance. As a result of this analysis, diagnostic accuracy statistics such as specificity and sensitivity were also given. This analysis was repeated for individual ESI categories. In addition, dichotomous data was created as “severe” and “mild” according to the high urgency group; like the general analysis, the power of ChatGPT to distinguish this group was also analyzed. The P < 0.05 level was used as the statistical significance criterion. All statistical analyses were completed in January 2024.

Results

Descriptive data

Among the patients included in the study, 83.8% (n = 625) were outpatients and 16.2% (n = 120) were admitted by EMS ambulance. The mean age of the patients was 44 ± 19 years and 51.1% (n = 381) were male. The most common complaints were abdominal pain (9.30%), soft tissue disorders (9.10%) and gastroenteritis-colitis (7.80%); the most common comorbidities were hypertension (20.9%), diabetes mellitus (12.2%) and coronary artery disease (8.1%) during admission to the ED. The mean systolic blood pressure was 132 ± 22 mmHg, the mean diastolic blood pressure was 77 ± 13 mmHg, the mean pulse rate was 87 ± 16 beats/min, the mean oxygen saturation value was 96 ± 3%, and the mean body temperature was 36 ± 1 °C (Table 1).

Agreement of EC and ChatGPT assessment

In our study, when all ESI categories were considered together, the agreement within the EC was moderate (Fleiss’ Kappa value = 0.373). The agreement within the EC was evaluated separately in each ESI triage category, and kappa values were statistically significant and moderately concordant. (ESI-1 Kappa = 0.451; ESI-2 Kappa = 0.484; ESI-3 Kappa = 0.361; ESI-4 Kappa = 0.217 and ESI-5 Kappa = 0.454; P < 0.001, P < 0.001, P < 0.001, P < 0.001 and P < 0.001, respectively).

When all ESI categories of the study were taken into consideration together, the comparison of EC and ChatGPT results showed that ChatGPT decided for ‘over’ triage in 10.7% (n = 80) and ‘under’ triage in 12.6% (n = 94) of the cases (Table 3). Accordingly, good agreement was seen between EC and ChatGPT assessment results (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.659) (P < 0.001). The accuracy between these two evaluations was calculated as 76.6%. In the agreement evaluation between EC and ChatGPT in each ESI triage category separately, kappa values for all triage categories were determined as statistically significant and moderately well concordant. (ESI-1 Kappa = 0.840; ESI-2 Kappa = 0.804; ESI-3 Kappa = 0.674; ESI-4 Kappa = 0.515 and ESI-5 Kappa = 0.640; P < 0.001, P < 0.001, P < 0.001, P < 0.001 and P < 0.001, respectively).

Discussion

The intersection of AI and emergency medicine is promising due to the increasing workload in EDs, which is parallel to the increasing number of patients daily. Our study is critically important regarding the use of AI in the triage area, which is the ED process’s first and most important step. Our study aims to test the accuracy of ChatGPT assessment by triaging 745 real-time consecutive ED admissions according to ESI criteria. The median value of EC was considered as the gold standard and compared with the results obtained by ChatGPT assessment. In both groups, most cases were determined as ESI category 3. It was determined that there was a moderate agreement among EC. When EC and ChatGPT assessment results were compared, significant concordance was found. This agreement was higher, especially for the ESI 1 and ESI 2 categories, which indicate high acuity. At the same time, the highest accuracy for ChatGPT assessment was obtained for the ESI 1 and ESI 2 categories.

Zaboli et al. compared the accuracy of the decisions made by the triage nurse group and ChatGPT in terms of triage based on the Manchester Triage Scale, 72-hour mortality, hospitalization, and prediction of the presence of severe or time-dependent condition outcomes of patients admitted to the ED. A total of 30 randomly selected cases were included in the study. The agreement between the nurse group and ChatGPT assessment was determined to be 0.278, and AUC was higher in the healthcare professional group regarding all study outcomes. This difference was found to be more pronounced regarding 72-hour mortality (AUC 0.910 vs. 0.669)12. The report was a single-center study with a limited number of selected cases, and agreement between healthcare professionals wasn’t reported. We think that the difference in the agreement between healthcare professionals and ChatGPT assessment in terms of triage decisions is that our study was carried out with a larger number of real-time patient data and the preliminary preparation for EC standardization consisting of physicians.

In a study by Sarbay et al. examining ESI triage performance with ChatGPT, 50 scenarios were determined and compared with the common opinion of two expert physicians. positive predictive value (PPV) was found to be 38.7%, NPV 52.6%, and F1 score 0.4615. It was seen that the ChatGPT diagnostic accuracy results obtained in our study showed better performance. Although both were performed ChatGPT in Turkish, we think there is a difference between the report and our study due to the difference in the number of cases and the use of real-time patient data. In addition, the new Chat GPT ver. 4, which was used in our study, may also have caused the difference between the two studies.

Fraser et al. have evaluated the triage efficiency and diagnostic performance of four different data systems (Ada Health Symptom Checkers, Web MD Symptom Checkers, ChatGPT ver. 3.5 and ChatGPT ver. 4.). In this study, a 3-step triage (home care, routine primary care and emergency or urgent care) was applied, and the triage of patients and predictions of their final diagnosis were compared with the opinion of three experts. The performance of the algorithms in terms of diagnosis was found to be Ada (63%) > ChatGPT ver. 3.5 (63%) > WebMD SC (57%) > ChatGPT ver. 4. (50%), while the accuracy ranking for triage was reported as ChatGPT ver. 4. (76%) > WebMD SC (70%) > Ada (62%) > ChatGPT ver. 3.5 (59%)13. The authors have stated that ChatGPT ver. 3.5 should not be used without improvements in the accuracy of its judgments. The study is limited by its retrospective method and the number of patients. This may have caused the difference seen with the ChatGPT diagnostic accuracy results in our study about the triage assessments. On the other hand, it is noteworthy that different accuracy results were obtained with different algorithms in this study.

In the study of Paslı et al., the effectiveness of ChatGPT assessment in triaging patients admitted to the ED according to the principles of local triage practice rules was investigated. In the study, the triage decision made by the emergency medicine physician was accepted as the gold standard. Accordingly, a good level of agreement was found between the opinion of both the triage team and ChatGPT with the emergency medicine physician (Cohen’s Kappa 0.893 vs. 0.899)14. These results are better than the agreement results obtained in our study based on ESI criteria. This study, performed after teaching local triage rules to ChatGPT, opens a new window in the use of AI-based applications in the triage field.

In a retrospective study, Ivanov O. et al. investigated the accuracy of triage performed by AI-based clinical natural language processing and machine learning algorithms (KATE) according to ESI criteria. KATE and clinicians reevaluated the nurses’ decisions about triage. This algorithmic triage model was 26.9% higher in accuracy than nurse triage. In this study, unlike the results of our study, the developed model was reported to have performed ‘under’ triage in 9.7% and ‘over’ triage in 14.4% of patients, but similar to our study, the F1 score (0.9923) was found to be quite high15. Karlafti E. et al. investigated the effectiveness of the AI-based automatic patient screening classifier algorithm in ED triage based on ESI criteria. The overall triage accuracy was 84.6%, and the F1 score was 72.2%16. It is understood that triaging based on certain criteria in different AI-based algorithms gives different accuracy results. The ‘over’ and ‘under’ triage decision ratios and F1 score values in ChatGPT assessment based on ESI also differ between the studies. In these aspects, it should be noted that the subject continues to develop. It should also be considered that modifications to the criteria of triage scales are necessary in terms of application with AI-based algorithms.

Limitation

Our study was conducted in a single-center, tertiary academic hospital and cannot be generalized to primary and secondary healthcare institutions. EC was selected from physicians with the same level of knowledge who received training on ESI before the study. However, the agreement within EC was at a moderate level. This agreement difference may be due to the interpretive nature of the ESI score, which can be affected by the experience of the triage person. In addition, our sample size is limited. This limitation may have affected EC agreement and Chat GPT interpretation ability. We believe that more appropriate results can be obtained with larger samples.

Conclusion

As a result of our study, we believe that the diagnostic accuracy of AI-based ChatGPT assessment can accurately distinguish high-urgency patients, especially ESI 1 and 2. However, triage scales are geared toward human use and require human experience. Considering technological advances, the interpretation capability of AI can increase. Furthermore, with the development of new triage systems for AI, AI-based triage systems can soon be integrated into ED triage decision-making processes. Nevertheless, studies conducted with different triage scales and languages ​​are important for the usability of these applications.

IAR025, a novel anti-DLL3 x CD3-bispecific antibody, displays high antitumor efficacy in preclinical models of small-cell lung cancer
Authors: Yang Liu, Lei Cao, Mengqiu Xu, Ninghuan Li, Jian Zhang, Mengjia Zhu, Zhihui Kuang, Huijun Sha, Min Wu, Jinling Xu, Zhihai Wu, Ye Feng, Li Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) is one of the most aggressive subtype of lung carcinoma with limited treatment options. Delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3), a Notch ligand protein, has been an attractive target for SCLC due to its high expression on SCLC but limited expression on normal tissues. Here, we developed a bispecific T cell engager, IAR025, and investigated its antitumor efficacy against SCLC. IAR025 has a CD3 arm for T cell activation and a DLL3 arm for recognizing tumor cells with optimal affinity and binding domain. IAR025 resulted in a dose-dependent eradication of DLL3-positive SCLC cells, without nonspecific cell destruction in DLL3-negative cells in vitro. Administration of IAR025 in DMS79 and SHP-77 humanized mouse models significantly inhibited tumor growth. The mechanism study revealed that IAR025 treatment led to increased T-cell recruitment in tumors. IAR025 activity was further enhanced when combined with chemotherapy or anti-PD-1 treatment. In cynomolgus monkeys, IAR025 was well-tolerated at a dose up to 10 mg/kg and exhibited linear pharmacokinetics with a half-life of 90 h. Collectively, these preclinical results demonstrate that IAR025 is a highly potent therapeutic strategy for treating SCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-026-04416-w.

Introduction

Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) is an aggressive and difficult-to-treat malignancy, accounting for about 13–17% of all lung cancer cases. Over 250,000 people are diagnosed with SCLC worldwide each year [1, 2]. Patients diagnosed at a limited stage may have an overall survival (OS) of 2–4 years. However, 70–80% of cases are diagnosed at an extensive stage, which has limited treatment options and only a 2–5% 5-year survival rate [3–5]. The first-line treatment for extensive-stage SCLC includes platinum-based chemotherapy combined with PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies, achieving an objective response rate (ORR) of 60–80% [6]. Despite this high response rate, the response duration is short, with a progression-free survival (PFS) of just 5–6 months and an overall survival of around 1 year [6, 7]. Second- and third-line treatments usually consist of chemotherapy with immunotherapy or anti-angiogenic small molecule inhibitors. These options have lower response rates, under 50%, and even shorter response durations, with a PFS of 1–4 months and an OS of less than 9 months [8–11]. Therefore, there remains a significant unmet need among patients with extensive-stage SCLC.

DLL3 (Delta-like protein 3) is a type I transmembrane protein and belongs to the Notch ligand family. It includes one DSL domain, six epidermal growth factor-like domains, and a transmembrane domain [12]. Normally, DLL3 is located in the Golgi apparatus and is not present on the cell membrane [13]. However, in certain neuroendocrine tumors such as SCLC, DLL3 is abnormally overexpressed in 70–80% of cases [14, 15]. In these cases, DLL3 is found both in the Golgi and on the cell surface. As a result, DLL3 immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining often shows both membrane and cytoplasmic staining [14]. Under the regulation of ASCL1, a transcription factor expressed in nearly all SCLCs [16], DLL3 is highly expressed on the surface of SCLC cells and interacts with Notch receptors through cis-inhibition, which promotes tumor growth [17]. Because DLL3 is highly expressed in tumors but has limited and low expression in normal tissues, it is considered an attractive target for antibody- and cell-based therapies.

The clinical validation of DLL3-targeted immunotherapy was established with the development of Tarlatamab (AMG 757), a bispecific T-cell engager (TCE) that redirects CD3-positive T cells to DLL3-expressing tumor cells [18]. Following the promising phase 2 data from the DeLLphi-301 trial, which demonstrated an ORR of 40% in the 10-mg group in previously treated patients [19], tarlatamab received FDA accelerated approval in May 2024 for extensive-stage SCLC after platinum-based chemotherapy progression. Notably, recent results from the confirmatory phase 3 DeLLphi-304 trial led to full FDA approval in November 2025, demonstrating a 40% reduction in death risk and significantly extended median OS by more than five months compared to standard-of-care chemotherapy [20].

The clinical success of tarlatamab has catalyzed intensive development of additional DLL3-targeted TCEs. Merck’s MK-6070 (formerly HPN328), a TriTAC-format bispecific antibody incorporating an albumin-binding domain for extended half-life, achieved a cORR of 50%(12/24) in SCLC in early-phase studies and is currently being ‘evaluated in combination with B7-H3 ADC[21]. BI 764532 (obrixtamig) is a DLL3/CD3-targeted IgG-like bispecific T-cell engager developed for DLL3-positive SCLC and high-grade neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs). A global phase 1 trial demonstrated manageable safety, with mostly grade 1–2 cytokine-release syndrome and no maximum tolerated dose reached. At recommended doses, ORRs were 21% in SCLC, 27% in extrapulmonary NEC, and 70% in large-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma [22]. Additionally, innovative formats including dual-epitope binders (e.g., ZG006 targeting two distinct DLL3 epitopes) and costimulator (e.g., RG6524 targeting DLL3, CD3, and CD137) are in active clinical development, with emerging data suggesting enhanced potency through avidity effects or co-stimulatory signaling.

Here, we describe IAR025, an IgG-like T-cell engager targeting DLL3 built on the Innobody platform. IAR025 binds to DLL3 expressed on tumor cells and cross-links the CD3 molecules on the T-cell surface, activating T cells and exerting antitumor effects. The Fc region of the molecule was modified with LALA mutations to mitigate nonspecific activation brought by FcR cross-linking [23]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that IAR025 exhibits potent antitumor activity in both in vitro and in vivo killing assays and has shown favorable pharmacokinetics and safety profiles in cynomolgus monkeys.

Results

Molecule discovery of IAR025

IAR025 is a human IgG1 bispecific antibody that targets DLL3 and CD3 with reduced Fc-mediated antibody effector functions. It was generated using the in-house “Innobody” platform (WO2022143912A1). The optimal epitope requirement for tumor-associated antigen (TAA) binding varies depending on the bispecific antibody format [24]. The BiTE format demonstrated maximal cytotoxic activity when the DLL3-binding arm targeted the mid-region epitope (EGF-like domain 3), such as Amgen DLL3/CD3 (US10294300B2). In contrast, the BI DLL3/CD3 study showed that IgG-like bispecific antibodies achieved superior in vitro cytotoxicity when binding to membrane-proximal epitopes of DLL3 (WO2019234220A1). Consistent with the observations reported in the previous studies, our study also observed that the DLL3/CD3 IgG-like T-cell engager targeting the membrane-proximal (EGF6) domain of DLL3 achieved superior in vitro killing activity (Figure S1). Given that our molecule, IAR025, adopts an IgG-like format similar to BI’s construct, we selected a DLL3 binder targeting the membrane-proximal domain in this study.

Binding affinity and specificity of IAR025

Given the low surface expression of DLL3 in tumor cells [25, 26] and its limited off-target potential [14], we screened DLL3 clones with high affinity for the bispecific antibody. As for the CD3 binder, we investigated the impact of CD3 binding affinity on T cell-mediated cytotoxicity. We generated bispecific antibodies by pairing a constant DLL3 binder with CD3 binders of different affinities (Figure S2A). The tumor cell killing potency was then assessed. DLL3/CD3 with low-affinity CD3 binder showed weak potency both in vitro and in vivo (Figure S2B and C). This diminished efficacy is likely due to insufficient immunological synapse formation, which requires both sufficient antigen density and stable T cell engagement. Given the minimal expression of DLL3 in normal tissues, we finally selected a CD3 binder with higher affinity. As shown in Fig. 1B, the binding affinities of IAR025 to human DLL3 and CD3 were 0.7 nM and 10.5 nM, respectively. To further evaluate the functional binding capacity of IAR025, we utilized CHO cells stably overexpressing human DLL3 (CHO-hDLL3) and primary T cells from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Flow cytometry results showed dose-dependent binding of IAR025 both in DLL3 and CD3 arms (Fig. 1C, D).

To address potential cross-reactivity within the DLL family, we constructed cell lines overexpressing human DLL1 or DLL4. Cell-binding assays demonstrated that IAR025 exhibited no detectable binding to DLL1 or DLL4, which indicates DLL3-specific recognition (Fig. 1E, F).

IAR025 directs in vitro T-cell killing in the presence of DLL3-expressing cells

To evaluate the functional activity of IAR025, we conducted systematic in vitro assays using DLL3-positive and DLL3-negative cell lines. SHP-77, H82 and DMS79 are small-cell lung cancer cell lines expressing DLL3, while H460 is a large-cell lung cancer cell line without DLL3 expression. In the CD3-NFAT reporter assay, IAR025-induced robust, dose-dependent activation of Jurkat T cells in the presence of DLL3-positive SCLC lines, with half-maximal effective concentrations (EC50) in the low nanomolar range. On the contrary, no signal was detected in DLL3-negative H460 cells (Fig. 2A). Similar to the reporter assay, IAR025 also induced potent T cell-mediated cytotoxicity in DLL3-positive tumor cells with EC50 values ranging from 15 to 66 pM (Fig. 2B). IAR025-induced T cell cytotoxicity relies on the antibody binding to both T cells and DLL3-expressing cells, thereby activating T cells and inducing cytokine release. To confirm T-cell activation, the activation-induced expression of CD69 and CD25 on T cells was measured. CD69 and CD25 are defined as T cell activation markers, with CD69 being an early marker and CD25 a later marker [27]. Both CD4 + and CD8 + T cells co-cultured with DLL3-positive SHP-77 cell line in the killing assay showed upregulation of CD69 and CD25 in the presence of IAR025. The EC50 value was approximately 0.5 nM (Fig. 2D). The release of inflammatory cytokines was also assessed. IAR025 induced a dose-dependent secretion of IL-2 and IFN-γ in SHP-77 co-cultures (Fig. 2E), indicative of T-cell activation.

IAR025 induces T cell infiltration and inhibits tumor growth

The antitumor efficacy of IAR025 was evaluated in two xenografted SCLC humanized mouse models. Given the rapid growth of SCLC tumors DMS79 and SHP-77 in NOG mice, human PBMCs from healthy donors were transplanted before tumor inoculation to allow for sufficient proliferation and reconstitution of human-derived T cells in the mice, thereby facilitating the assessment of pharmacological effects. IAR025 was administered weekly, supported by its long half-life in mice (Figure S3). In the DMS79 model, IAR025 was administered at doses of 0.1 and 0.01 mg/kg intraperitoneally starting 7 days after tumor implantation, and tumor growth was monitored until day 24. IAR025 at a dose of 0.1 mg/kg induced complete tumor regression, while the low-dose group (0.01 mg/kg) achieved a 75% tumor growth inhibition rate when compared with control IgG-treated mice (Fig. 3A). At the end of the study, tumors of the 0.1 mg/kg IgG and 0.01 mg/kg IAR025 groups were collected and processed for IHC analysis. IHC staining revealed a significant increase in human CD3 + T cell infiltration into tumors after IAR025 treatment, showing approximately threefold higher CD3 + T cell density than the IgG group (Fig. 3C and D). The tumor inhibition was also validated in the SHP-77 humanized model. In the SHP-77 model, mice were treated with a single dose of 0.024, 0.12, 0.6 or 3 mg/kg by intraperitoneal injection starting 5 days after implantation, and tumor growth was monitored until day 26. A single administration of IAR025 at 0.12 mg/kg elicited a robust and long‑lasting antitumor response, with tumor suppression sustained for up to 17 days. At doses ≥ 0.12 mg/kg, tumor inhibition rates exceeded 95%, whereas a lower dose of 0.024 mg/kg produced a moderate inhibition of approximately 53% (Fig. 3B).

Combination study of IAR025 with chemotherapy and checkpoint blockade

Platinum-based chemotherapy of etoposide plus either cisplatin or carboplatin is currently the first-line standard of care for SCLC [28]. Recent studies have shown that the combination of immunotherapy with chemotherapy achieved greater therapeutic effects for treating SCLC[29].

To demonstrate the combination effect of chemotherapy and IAR025 T-cell engager, we evaluated antitumor activity in an H82 humanized model, in which human PBMC and H82 tumor cells were implanted in immunodeficient NOG mice. Mice in the chemotherapy group were treated with carboplatin (30 mg/kg) and etoposide (10 mg/kg) intraperitoneally once a week, while IAR025 was administered at a dose of 3 mg/kg twice a week. All treatments were administered for three weeks, and tumor growth was monitored until day 25. Both chemotherapy and IAR025 exhibited moderate antitumor effect in the H82 model, showing 61% and 50% inhibition, respectively. The combination of chemotherapy and IAR025 treatment profoundly reduced tumor growth compared to single treatment, achieving 84% tumor growth inhibition (Fig. 4A). PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab has been approved for the treatment of patients with metastatic SCLC who experienced disease progression after two or more prior lines of therapy [30]. Preclinical studies have found upregulation of PD-1 expression in animal models after treatment with the T-cell engagers [25]. Based on the elevated expression of PD-1 on tumor-infiltrating T cells, we tested whether the combination with anti–PD-1 antibodies could enhance the antitumor efficacy of IAR025.

Immunocompetent mice bearing MC38 tumors were used for this evaluation. As IAR025 does not bind to mouse CD3, a transgenic mouse model expressing human CD3 on mouse T cells was used, and MC38 mouse syngeneic tumor cells expressing human DLL3 (MC38-hDLL3) were implanted as target cells. Human DLL3 expression on the tumor cell surface was confirmed by flow cytometry (Fig. 4B). When the tumor reached the size of 80 ~ 110 mm3, treatment was started by intraperitoneal injection of 3 mg/kg IAR025 and 2 mg/kg anti–mouse PD-1 mAb. As shown in Fig. 4C, IAR025 and PD-1 mAb exhibited a slight to moderate antitumor effect in the MC38-hDLL3 model, with 33% and 55% tumor inhibition, respectively. Addition of the PD-1 antibody to IAR025 dramatically enhanced the antitumor activity, achieving 96% tumor growth inhibition. The combination also showed significant improvement in response rate (Fig. 4D). When given single agent, only 1 or 2 out of 6 mice showed a response. After the combination, 4 out of 6 mice achieved a complete response, indicating a more robust tumor inhibition. These findings indicate that although checkpoint mechanisms might limit the efficacy of IAR025, the bispecific activity can be further enhanced in combination with checkpoint blockade agents.

Pharmacokinetics of IAR025 in cynomolgus monkey

The cross-reactivity of IAR025 with cynomolgus DLL3 and CD3 established cynomolgus monkeys as a relevant model for evaluation of the tolerability (Fig. 5A). A repeat dose of 3 or 10 mg/kg IAR025 was administered on study days 1, 8, and 15. Multiple doses of IAR025 were well-tolerated in cynomolgus monkeys, and no significant abnormalities were observed in clinical signs, body weight, food intake, blood coagulation, electrocardiogram, gross anatomy or histopathology. Liver enzymes remained unchanged after treatment. Notably, no meaningful changes in pro-inflammatory cytokines were observed in the blood following repeated dosing. The highest detected serum IL‑6 concentration was 14.6 pg/mL—substantially lower than levels typically seen during clinical cytokine-release syndrome (CRS). IAR025-related changes were limited to a transient, minor decrease in lymphocyte populations after the first dose, and these changes were reversible. The PK profile of IAR025 in cynomolgus monkeys was characterized in the same study. Following the first dose of IAR025, serum concentrations declined similarly between 3mg/kg and 10 mg/kg doses, with a mean clearance of 0.6 mL/hour/kg and a mean half-life around 90 h (Fig. 5B and Table S1). The maximum serum concentration (Cmax) and the area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) for IAR025 increased proportionally from 3 to 10 mg/kg, indicating linear systemic exposure within a certain range. Taken together, the NHP studies suggest that IAR025 has the potential to achieve high exposures that may be required for effective antitumor activity in the clinic.

Discussion

In this study, we report the preclinical development and evaluation of IAR025, a novel DLL3/CD3-bispecific T cell engager (TCE) with a 1 + 1 format. Our findings demonstrate that IAR025 exhibits potent and specific tumor cell–killing activity against DLL3-positive small-cell lung cancer in both in vitro and in vivo settings. Importantly, favorable safety characteristics were confirmed through nonhuman primate toxicity studies, highlighting the translational potential of this molecule.

IAR025 was designed with high affinity for DLL3 (0.7 nM) and moderate affinity for CD3 (~ 10 nM), a binding profile comparable to that of AMG757 and BI 764532 (data not shown). The high DLL3 affinity was selected to promote stable immune synapse formation and enhance selective engagement of tumor cells. Meanwhile, the moderate CD3 affinity was designed to balance antitumor potency with safety. In our study, IAR025 exhibited comparable in vitro cytotoxicity potency to BI 764532 in multiple DLL3-positive SCLC cell lines. However, in vivo studies showed different results. In the DMS79 humanized model, IAR025 demonstrated superior tumor growth inhibition than BI 764532, accompanied by increased T-cell infiltration at the tumor site, leading to robust and efficient antitumor activity (data not shown).

Recent clinical and preclinical evidence has validated the substantial efficacy gains of combining DLL3-targeted T-cell engagers with immune checkpoint inhibitors, chemotherapy, or antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) for DLL3-positive malignancies, addressing intrinsic/acquired resistance and expanding the therapeutic window. These combinations have demonstrated marked efficacy improvements with manageable safety profiles.

The phase I DAREON-7 trial demonstrated that BI 764532 plus platinum-based doublet chemotherapy achieved a confirmed ORR of 72%, median PFS of 7.6 months, and DoR of 8.8 months in first-line DLL3-positive NEC, markedly outperforming historical chemotherapy outcomes (ORR ~ 40%, PFS ~ 4–5 months). In relapsed ES-SCLC patients, BI 764532 plus topotecan yielded an unconfirmed ORR of 70% and DCR of 87% with no additional toxicity beyond the known profiles of either agent [31]. Our preclinical data further support the combination of IAR025 with chemotherapy, which demonstrated superior antitumor activity compared with monotherapy in xenograft models (Fig. 4A). Beyond its direct cytotoxic effect, chemotherapy may also contribute to lowering tumor burden, which could in turn mitigate the incidence and severity of cytokine-release syndrome, a frequent adverse event associated with TCE therapy [32, 33]. While these findings suggest that chemotherapy may represent a rational partner to enhance both efficacy and tolerability of TCEs in SCLC, this hypothesis will require further validation in clinical studies.

In recent years, ADC have emerged as a major breakthrough in the treatment of solid tumors, owing to their unique mechanisms of precise targeting and potent cytotoxicity. ADC agents targeting multiple antigens, including B7-H3 [34], DLL3 [35, 36], SEZ6 [37], and TROP2[38], have achieved substantial progress in SCLC. They have demonstrated favorable ORR and PFS superior to conventional later-line chemotherapy, with promising signals for the control of brain metastases.

The combination of DLL3-TCEs with ADCs represents a novel “targeted + targeted” strategy that leverages complementary mechanisms of action: DLL3-TCEs activate T cells to kill DLL3-high tumor cells, while ADCs deliver potent cytotoxic payloads to antigen-positive cells (including those with low DLL3 expression that may escape TCE-mediated killing). This approach addresses two critical challenges in DLL3-targeted therapy: intratumoral heterogeneity of DLL3 expression and resistance due to epitope downregulation.​ Current trials focus on B7‑H3‑targeted ADC combinations, since B7‑H3 is expressed in ~ 60% of SCLCs and linked to aggressive disease. A phase Ib/II study is evaluating the DLL3 TCE gocatamig (MK-6070) plus I-DXd [39], which may provide new insights into the synergistic ADC-TCE combination therapy.

Activation of T cells by CD3 clustering via TCEs triggers the upregulation of inhibitory receptors such as PD-1, which may subsequently attenuate antitumor immune responses [40]. Thus, combining TCEs with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade has strong biological rationale. Importantly, our study employed syngeneic hCD3 knock-in mice bearing DLL3-positive MC38 tumors, which maintain a fully competent immune system. This model allows for a more faithful recapitulation of the complex interactions between TCE therapy and immune checkpoint blockade. The observed increase in tumor-free mice with the combination of IAR025 and anti–PD-1, compared with either monotherapy, underscores the potential clinical relevance of dual targeting strategies in DLL3-positive tumors. Given that anti-PD-1 antibodies are already approved in SCLC [6, 41], this combination strategy holds direct clinical relevance and warrants further exploration. For tarlatamab, the phase Ib DeLLphi-303 study has demonstrated that frontline maintenance therapy with tarlatamab plus durvalumab resulted in a median OS of 25.3 months—nearly doubling historical outcomes with chemoimmunotherapy—and long-term tolerability with no new safety concern[42], supporting the potential value of earlier-line administration.

In summary, our findings support IAR025 as a promising DLL3/CD3 TCE candidate for the treatment of SCLC and neuroendocrine tumors. The demonstrated preclinical efficacy and safety, coupled with strong mechanistic rationale for combinations with chemotherapy and PD-1 blockade, provide a clear path for future clinical evaluation and we have applied for clinical trials to evaluate the safety and efficacy of IAR025. We will also investigate combination strategies in the clinical development of IAR025. Specifically, IAR025 in combination with PD-1 inhibitors, carboplatin, and etoposide will be evaluated during the induction phase, followed by IAR025 plus a PD-1 inhibitor as maintenance therapy. Beyond assessing the efficacy and safety of this regimen, we aim to optimize the dosing schedule and advance a subcutaneous formulation, with the goal of improving patient adherence and minimizing adverse events. We will also explore the associations between the levels of biomarkers (such as DLL3, PD-L1, etc.) and clinical efficacy. Recent studies have shown that tumor tissue DLL3 expression alone is insufficient to predict response and that broader biomarker analyses may better identify responders [19, 43]. We will also collect relevant data and analyze it in our clinical studies.

Methods

In vitro binding affinity measurements

Affinities of bispecific antibodies human DLL3, human CD3ε, cyno DLL3 and cyno CD3 were measured using Bio-Layer Interferometry (BLI). Half an hour before the start of the experiment, the AHC sensor (Sartorius, 18–5060) and HIS1K sensor (Sartorius, 18–5120) were immersed in SD buffer (1 × PBS, 0.1% BSA, 0.05% Tween-20). Bispecific antibodies and human DLL3 (Acro biosystems, DL3-H52H4), human CD3 (Acro biosystems, CDD-H52W1), cyno DLL3 (KACTUS, DLL-CM113) and cyno CD3(Acro Biosystems, CDE-C5226) were diluted to 100 nM. SD buffer, antibodies, and antigens were added separately to a 96-well black polystyrene microtiter plate (Greiner, 655209). The assay was performed using ForteBio Octet Red96e. The instrument was set up with the appropriate parameters for sample loads, association, and dissociation times. The KD values were analyzed using ForteBio Octet analysis software.

Cell lines and cell culture

In order to carry out cell binding experiments, three overexpression cell lines were constructed: pXC17.4 vectors encoding human DLL3 or DLL1 were electro-transfected into CHO cells and screened for positive clones. 293 T cells were transduced with the lentivirus vector encoding hDLL4 with puromycin selection.

All cancer cell lines were obtained from ATCC. SHP-77 (ATCC, CRL-2195), DMS79 (ATCC, CRL-2049), H82 (ATCC, HTB-175), H460 (ATCC, HTB-177), and U-87 (HTB-14) were cultured following the manufacturer’s instructions. MC38 cells that stably express hDLL3 were constructed by transducing with a lentivirus vector encoding human DLL3 and were selected by the addition of puromycin. Positive clones were then generated.

Cell-based binding assay

CHO-hDLL3 cells, CHO-hDLL1 cells, 293 T-hDLL4 cells and human PBMC (ALLCELLS, FPB004F-C) were stained with serially diluted IAR025 or human IgG1 isotype control (Bioxcell, BE0297) for 30 min at 4 °C, washed with FACS buffer (PBS + 2% FBS) and then stained with PE anti-human IgG Fc (Biolegend, 409304) for 30 min at 4 °C. APC anti-human CD8a antibody (Biolegend, 301014) was used for identifying CD8 + T cells in PBMC. PE anti-human DLL1 (Biolegend, 346404) and PE anti-human DLL4 (Biolegend, 346506) were used as positive controls in CHO-hDLL1 and 293 T-hDLL4 cell binding, respectively. The cells were finally washed and suspended in FACS buffer for flow cytometry analysis. Median fluorescence intensity (MFI) was analyzed using GraphPad Prism Software.

Validation of tumor cell human DLL3 expression

SHP-77, DMS79, H82 and H460 were incubated with IAR025 at 10 μg/mL for 30 min at 4 °C, washed with FACS buffer and stained with secondary antibody PE anti-human IgG Fc for 30 min at 4 °C. Cells were then washed and suspended for flow cytometry analysis. Tumor cells only with secondary antibody staining were used as a negative control. DLL3 expression level was identified with the MFI ratio.

T-cell activation reporter assay

DLL3-positive SHP-77, DMS79, H82 cells and DLL3-negative H460 cells were co-incubated with NFAT luciferase reporter Jurkat cell line at a density of 4 × 104 cells per well. Serially diluted IAR025 and human IgG1 isotype control were added to the co-cultures and incubated at 37 °C for 6 h. After that, Bio-Glo luciferase assay reagent (Promega, G7940) was added to each well, and luminescence signal was measured using a SpectraMax i3x reader (Molecular Devices). Data was analyzed using GraphPad Prism Software.

In vitro T cell–dependent cell cytotoxicity and T-cell activation assays

DLL3-positive SHP-77, DMS79 and H82 cells were used as target cells, whereas DLL3-negative H460, U87 and 293 T-hDLL4 cells were used as non-target cells. Tumor cells were labeled using the Cell Trace Far Red Cell Proliferation Kit (Thermo Fisher, C34564). Human PBMC were thawed, resuspended in PBMC media (RPMI 1640 + 10% FBS) and incubated overnight at 37 °C. The next day, PBMC at a density of 2 × 105 cells/well and tumor cells at a density of 1 × 104 cells/well were added to an ultra-low attachment multiple well plate (CORNING, CLS7007-24EA). IAR025 and isotype control antibodies were serially diluted in PBMC media and added to wells. The co-cultures were incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. After that, cells were stained with propidium iodide solution (PBS + 0.05% PI) for cell viability. For T cell activation, PBMC from the TDCC assay were stained with anti-human CD4 (BioLegend, 317416), anti-human CD8 (BioLegend, 301014), anti-human CD25 (BioLegend, 302606) and anti-human CD69 (BioLegend, 310904) at 4 °C for 30 min. Cells were then washed and suspended in FACS buffer for flow cytometry analysis. Flow cytometry data were processed using FlowJo software. To measure cytokines, a commercially available Cytometric Bead Array (CBA) Human Th1/Th2/Th17 Cytokine Kit (BD, 560484) was used following the manufacturer’s instructions. Supernatants from the TDCC assay were used for cytokine analyses. Data were processed using FCAP array v3 software.

In vivo mouse efficacy studies

All animal studies were conducted following Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee protocols. Efficacy of the bispecific antibodies was evaluated in vivo in humanized mouse xenograft models of DMS79 and SHP-77 for tumor killing activity with human PBMC. For the DMS79 model, female NOG mice (Charles River Laboratories, 6–9 weeks old) were engrafted intravenously with 4 × 106 human PBMCs from healthy donors (AllCells, Beijing, China). Three days after PBMC inoculation, mice were implanted with 5 × 106 DMS79 cells subcutaneously in the right front flank. When tumor volume was approximately 130 mm3, mice were grouped (n = 6/group) by tumor volume and treated with DLL3/CD3-bispecific antibodies by i.p. injection. For the SHP-77 model, tumor cells were implanted six days after PBMC inoculation.

In chemotherapy combination studies, H82 xenograft model was used. Female NOG mice were engrafted intravenously with 4 × 106 human PBMCs. Six days after PBMC inoculation, mice were implanted with 2.5 × 106 H82 cells subcutaneously. When tumor volume was approximately 92 mm3, mice were grouped (n = 6/group) by tumor volume and treated with IAR025 (3 mg/kg, twice weekly, i.p.), chemotherapy (carboplatin 30 mg/kg + etoposide 10 mg/kg, weekly, p.o.), or combination therapy.

In PD1 combination studies, MC38-hDLL3 model was used. Female hCD3EDG mice (Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc., 6–8 weeks old) were implanted with 3 × 106 MC38-hDLL3 cells subcutaneously. When tumor volume reached approximately 93 mm3, mice were grouped (n = 6/group) by tumor volume and treated with IAR025 (3 mg/kg, twice weekly, i.p.), anti-mouse PD1 (2 mg/kg, single dose, i.p.), or combination therapy.

The tumor size was measured twice weekly and calculated using the following formula: tumor volume (mm3) = (width2xlength)/2. Tumor growth inhibition was defined as the difference between the mean tumor volume (MTV) of the control group and the treated group, TGI (%) = 100% × (MTVcontrol − MTVtreated)/(MTVcontrol − MTVpredose). Two-way ANOVA was used for statistical analyses. Tumor Volume Change TVC, (%) = 100% × (TVfinal − TVbaseline)/TVbaseline, where TVbaseline represents tumor volume before treatment and TVfinal represents tumor volume at the end of the study. Individual TVC values were used to generate waterfall plots for visualizing treatment responses across animals.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumor samples were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for 48 h, trimmed, and dehydrated overnight. Samples were then embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 4 µm. Immunohistochemistry was performed on a robotic platform (Ventana Discover Ultra-Staining Module, Ventana Co., Tucson, AZ, USA). Tissue sections were deparaffinized using EZ Prep solution (Ventana). A heat-induced antigen retrieval was carried out using Cell Conditioner 1 (Ventana). Endogenous peroxidases were blocked with peroxidase inhibitor (CM1) for 8 min before incubating the section with CD3 antibody (Ventana, REF#790-4341) for 32 min at room temperature. Antigen–antibody complexes were detected using DISCOVERY UltraMap anti-Rb HRP detection systems, and then visualized with DISCOVERY ChromoMap DAB Kit (Ventana). All the slides were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, cleared and mounted for the assessment. High-resolution images of tissue sections were analyzed by the HALO Software (version 3.3.2541, Indica Lab), using the Multiplex IHC module (version 3.1.4) to identify CD3-expressing cells within the tumor area and stroma area. T cell intensity between control and treatment groups was further analyzed using an unpaired t-test.

Pre-toxicology and pharmacokinetics in cynomolgus monkeys

The pharmacokinetics and toxicity of IAR025 were evaluated after repeated intravenous bolus doses once weekly for 3 weeks of the 3 or 10 mg/kg IAR025 in 1 male and 1 female cynomolgus monkeys per group. The study followed the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee–approved protocol. Pharmacological activity of IAR025 was evaluated by serum cytokine assessments and flow cytometry. Safety and toxicity assessments included daily morbidity and mortality, daily clinical observations, weekly body weights, weekly body temperature, daily food consumption, clinical pathology (hematology, clinical chemistry, and coagulation), and anatomic pathology (gross necropsy, organ weights, and histopathology). For TK study, the blood sample were collected at 0.033, 2, 6, 24, 48, 72, 120, 168 h after the drug dose. The plasma concentration of IAR025 was determined by ELISA. Toxicokinetic parameters were estimated using WinNonlin 8.1 software.

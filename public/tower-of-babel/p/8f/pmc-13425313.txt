Ireland–Claisen Rearrangement as a Stereocontrolled Bond-Construction Strategy in Natural Product Total Synthesis
Authors: Guna Sakaine, Nikla̅vs U̅dris, Gints Šmits
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Among stereoselective C–C bond-forming transformations in organic synthesis, the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement plays a distinct role as a reliable method for the construction of stereogenic centers and geometrically defined double bonds. Its synthetic value arises from the efficient transfer of stereochemical information through a highly ordered six-membered transition state, enabling rapid increases in molecular complexity under mild reaction conditions. This review highlights recent applications of the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement in natural product total synthesis, covering literature published during 2015–2025. The examples discussed demonstrate the broad utility of this transformation across structurally diverse natural product classes and illustrate how substrate structure, enolization conditions, and reaction design determine the stereochemical outcome in complex synthetic settings.

Introduction

Since its introduction in 1972, the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement (ICR) has become a reliable method for carbon–carbon bond formation and has found widespread application in the stereoselective construction of stereogenic centers and double bonds in structurally complex targets, particularly natural products. Although certain exceptions are known, the rearrangement typically proceeds through a highly ordered six-membered chairlike transition state, which enables efficient and often predictable transfer of stereochemical information from substrate to product (Scheme ). ,

Key Stereochemical Aspects of the Ireland–Claisen Rearrangement (A, B)

The fundamental stereochemical principles of the ICR were established in the pioneering studies of Ireland and co-workers, ,− who demonstrated that anti-configured pentenoic acid anti -3 is obtained from rearrangement of the (E)-silyl ketene acetal E,E -2 derived from (E)-crotyl propionate E -1, and with comparable diastereoselectivity also from the (Z)-silyl ketene acetal Z,Z -2 derived from (Z)-crotyl propionate Z -1 (Scheme A). In this context, the syn/anti nomenclature refers to the extended conformation of the product 3. Correspondingly, substrates Z,E -2 and E,Z -2 furnish syn -3 under analogous stereochemical control. Stereochemical transfer is not limited only to alkene geometry: chirality present at the C4 position of the allylic ester substrate 4 is commonly relayed efficiently to the newly generated stereocenters at C1 and/or C6 in the rearrangement product 6 (Scheme B). A widely accepted stereochemical model places the sterically most demanding substituent R (Scheme B) not directly involved in the six-membered transition state in a pseudoequatorial orientation, thereby minimizing allylic strain and favoring chairlike transition structures. Nevertheless, in more structurally complex systems, deviations from these simplified models are frequently observed, and stereochemical outcomes must therefore be evaluated on a case-by-case basis.

The combination of robust stereochemical transfer, ready access to enantiomerically enriched substrates, and generally mild reaction conditionsoften at or below ambient temperaturehas ensured sustained interest in the ICR within synthetic organic chemistry. Its theoretical foundations and synthetic applications have been comprehensively discussed in several excellent reviews ,, and book chapters. To our knowledge, the application of the ICR in natural product total synthesis was last specifically surveyed by Zakarian in 2009; the present review focuses on developments reported during the period 2015–2025. The examples discussed in the manuscript are organized according to distinct natural product classes.

Alkaloids

An elegant example of the application of the ICR in a stereoselective construction of contiguous stereocenters was published in 2015 by Fukuyama and co-workers during their studies toward sarain A (10, Scheme ). The proposed rearrangement step smoothly proceeded already at negative temperatures, furnishing target intermediate 8 in 81% isolated yield as a single stereoisomer. This building block 8 was further converted into 9, which has been known from the first total synthesis of sarain A (10) by Overman’s group back in 2007.

Formal Total Synthesis of Sarain A (10) by Yokoshima, Fukuyama, and Co-Workers

In 2015, the total synthesis of the putative pumiliotoxin (PTX) class alkaloid 8-deoxypumiliotoxin 193H (16) was reported by Zemribo and Smits (Scheme ). The key step in this total synthesis was an ICR of lactone 14, which enabled the efficient assembly of the indolizine core structure, albeit with moderate stereoselectivity. In the final stage, the ester group was converted to a methyl group by multiple reductions, furnishing the target putative natural product 16, the structure of which was proposed by GC/MS and analogy with the other members of the PTX class. Later, toxicity studies revealed that 16 exhibits considerably lower in vivo toxicity compared to the more structurally complex PTX counterparts, presumably because of the simplified alkylidene side chain.

Total Synthesis of the Putative 8-Deoxypumiliotoxin 193H (16) by Zemribo and Smits

Indole diterpenoid natural product paspaline (20) and its analogues are known to be Maxi-K channel antagonists associated with Alzheimer’s disease and exhibit antiproliferative, anti-invasive, and antimigratory activities in breast cancer cell lines. An ICR enabled a stereoselective construction of two challenging contiguous quaternary centers in the total synthesis of paspaline (20, Scheme ). In this case, heating of the reaction mixture was necessary to effect the rearrangement, furnishing the desired product in 80% isolated yield and 6:1 dr. A later full paper by the same authors reveals the challenges associated with the design of this nontrivial rearrangement step.

Total Synthesis of Paspaline (20) by Johnson

Recently, Chen, Zhang, and co-workers published a bioinspired first total synthesis of monoterpene indole alkaloid vinorine (23, Scheme ), a biosynthetic intermediate of ajamaline. Studies have shown that vinorine accelerates sciatic nerve regeneration, motor function recovery, and sensory recovery after injury by regulating NGF and ERK activity. In this case, the ICR has been an efficient and highly stereoselective tool for side chain modification in intermediate 21, furnishing two new stereocenters in 88% isolated yield. The authors suggest that the rearrangement step proceeds via a chairlike transition state 24 ‡ .

Total Synthesis of Indole Monoterpene Alkaloid Vinorine (23)

Recently, the Smits group reported the total synthesis of the monoterpene indole alkaloids sitsirikine (28) and dihydrositsirikine (29), employing Corey’s diazaborolidine 26 to induce stereoselective formation of the newly generated stereogenic centers (Scheme ). DFT calculations suggested that the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement proceeds via the E-ketene acetal intermediate 30 ‡ in this case. Interestingly, changing the base from Me2NEt to DIPEA inverted the enolization stereoselectivity, furnishing the corresponding cis-diastereomer. Thus, by appropriate selection of both the diazaborolidine enantiomer and the base, all four possible diastereomers of piperidine 27 were obtained in good yields and with high stereoselectivity. The resulting piperidine building block 27 was subsequently incorporated into the core structures of sitsirikine (28) and dihydrositsirikine (29). Both of these alkaloids exhibit vasorelaxant activity against phenylephrine-induced contraction of rat mesenteric arteries, with EC50 values below 10 μM.

Total Synthesis of Sitsirikine (28) and Dihydrositsirikine (29)

In 2021, Nay’s group published a rare example of the ICR in branched allylic systems (Scheme ). After some optimizations, the authors identified the crucial role of additives in the given transformation. While no conversion was observed with LDA and TBSCl, the addition of hexamethylphosphoramide (HMPA) or phosphoric acid tripyrrolidide (TPPA) effected the rearrangement in yields of >80%. From practical considerations, the less toxic TPPA was selected for an up-scaled reaction. Rearrangement product 32 was further converted into 33, a known intermediate of the total synthesis of trichoderone A (34). Intermediate 33 is also believed to be an intermediate in the biosynthesis of more complex cytochalasan alkaloids, a broad class of pharmacologically important natural products.

Formal Total Synthesis of Trichoderone A (34) by Nay

The ICR has also been a key step in the total synthesis of the monoterpene alkaloid kopsone (37, Scheme ). Starting from advanced intermediate 35, two contiguous stereocenters were constructed with a 6:1 dr and nearly quantitative yield. The obtained ester 36 was further converted into kopsone (37) via a series of functional group manipulations and an interesting nitrone cyclization.

A systematic study of sterically congested proline esters by Kim and co-workers ultimately led to ICR product 43, which was subsequently employed in the formal total synthesis of (−)-cephalotaxine (45, Scheme ). The authors also noted the importance of phenyl ring substituents in the styrene part for the desired stereochemical outcome of the ICR. In this publication, the proposed structure of cephalezomine G (44) was synthesized, which is a known intermediate in the total synthesis of (−)-cephalotaxine (45). The utility of dynamic kinetic resolution (DKR) in combination with ICR has been showcased in the total synthesis of (−)-crinane (49) reported by Akai (Scheme ). First, the starting ester 47 was prepared via a lipase–oxovanadium combo-catalyzed DKR (not shown) and then submitted to classical ICR conditions enabling a fully stereoselective construction of a new quaternary center. Building block 48 was subsequently transformed into (−)-crinane (49), a Crinum-type Amaryllidaceae alkaloid whose family members display promising pharmacological activity.

ICR was also applied in the total synthesis of Lycopodium alkaloid lycoposerramine-R (53, Scheme ). Overall, the three-step sequence, including the ICR step, furnishes the desired ester 52 in 40% isolated yield. The stereospecificity of ICR, however, is not specified. Nevertheless, building block 52 was further successfully incorporated into the core structure of lycoposerramine-R (53). Although several total syntheses of this alkaloid have been reported to date, its biological activity has not been documented.

Total Synthesis of Lycoposerramine-R (53) by Yokoshima

A total synthesis of racemic spirocyclic trichothecane alkaloid spirotenuipesine A (56) was reported by Imagawa’s group in 2021 (Scheme ). Although the remote −OTIPS substituent at C3 in the cyclopentene ring did not induce any diastereoselectivity, it is worth noting that the two new adjacent quaternary stereocenters were formed in high diastereoselectivity. A series of events unfolds during this transformation. First, α-deprotonation of the ester 54 induces the dioxalane ring cleavage via a retro-oxa-Michael reaction. The resulting alkoxide 58 is then again deprotonated at the γ-position, furnishing ketene acetal 59, the E/Z- stereocontol of which is governed by the chelation with the enol ether oxygen atom. The intermediate 59, in turn, is further bis-silylated, and the concomitant ICR step proceeds via the chairlike transition state 60 ‡ . As noted earlier, the authors speculate that the C3 substituent is too remote to induce any diastereoselectivity. It is known that (+)-spirotenuipesine A (56) is a potent promoter of neurotrophic factor biosynthesis in glial cells and may therefore serve as a promising lead compound for the development of therapeutics to prevent neurodegenerative processes, such as Alzheimer’s disease. Also, synthetic racemic 56 promoted the secretion of neurotrophic factors from glial cells.

Total Synthesis (±)-Spirotenuipesine A (56) by Imagawa

Pyrrolobenzodiazepines (PBDs) are a broad class of natural products exhibiting considerable anticancer activity. In several consecutive publications, Zemribo and co-workers reported the construction of the alkylidene proline fragment 62 via an ICR of a 7-membered lactone 61 and a further incorporation of this building block into the PBD scaffold (Scheme ). The authors point out that to effect the proposed rearrangements, a minimum of 2 equiv of nBu2OTf was necessary. This observation, together with DFT calculations, suggested a rather unusual transition state 63 ‡ involving two molecules of the boron reagent. Overall, the rearrangement step proceeds with complete chirality transfer, and formation of only the desired E- double isomer was detected. By this methodology, boseongazepine C (64), usabamycins A (65) and C (66), prothracarcin (67), limazepine E (68), , as well as the dilactams oxoprothracarcin (69) and boseongazepine B (70) were successfully synthesized.

Peptides

The ICR has also been successfully applied in the total synthesis of marine cyclodepsipeptide doliculide (73, Scheme ). This natural product inhibits the proliferation of human leukemia cell lines with IC50 values in the nanomolar range. As for one of the key stepsthe ICRthe authors point out that the combination of TBSOTf and KHMDS was advantageous over TBSCl or TMSCl pair with LDA, which gave worse results. Worth noting that the ICR product 72 was isolated as a single diastereomer after the in situ silyl group cleavage.

Total Synthesis of Doliculide (73)

The ICR was also the key step for the construction of two adjacent stereocenters in Fukuyama’s total synthesis of kainic acid (76) and its analogues (Scheme ). The combination of LiHMDS with TMSCl smoothly furnished the ICR product 75 in 91% yield and dr = 38:1. This intermediate 75 was further applied in the total synthesis of kainic acid (76), a potent neurotoxin derived from the alga Digenea simplex, which is nowadays frequently used experimentally to model epilepsy and neurodegeneration in animals.

Fukuyama’s Total Synthesis of Kainic Acid (76)

Over the past decade, there have also been several examples of the ICR of unsaturated glycine esters that fall into the category of chelate enolate Claisen rearrangements and are often called, according to the inventor of this methodology, the Kazmaier–Claisen rearrangement. In 2016, Kazmaier reported the total synthesis of a marine cycloheptapeptide, cyclomarin A (79), known for its antituberculosis and antimalarial activity (Scheme ). One of the key building blocks 78 of this modular total synthesis was prepared via an ICR of glycine ester 77 in nearly perfect stereoselectivity.

Kazmaier’s Total Synthesis of Cyclomarin A (79)

The first total synthesis of a bit simpler Cylindrocladium scoparium-derived cyclotetrapeptide, designated as Cyl-1 (82), was reported by Kazmaier’s group two years later (Scheme ). In this case, the envisioned ICR step proceeds with nearly quantitative yield and excellent stereoselectivity. The stereochemical outcome was rationalized by a chelate-controlled chairlike transition state 83 ‡, typical of the Kazmaier–Claisen rearrangement. The obtained cyclotetrapeptide Cyl-1 (82) is known to be cytotoxic in cancer cell lines, presumably acting as a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor.

Polyketides

ICR can also serve as a useful tool for constructing polyketide chains. For example, in 2020, the Paterson lab reported a convergent total synthesis of (+)-actinoallolides A–E (87–91, Scheme ). Interestingly enough, the proposed rearrangement step proceeds via a ketene acetal intermediate 86, the one at C17 participating in ICR, but the other one at C19 was not productive and hydrolyzed back to the ester upon workup. In this case, an elevated temperature was necessary to effect the rearrangement step; nevertheless, it proceeded with full chirality transfer. The obtained building block was further incorporated into actinoallolide E (87) core structure. This macrolide showed a considerable in vitro antitrypanosomal activity without cytotoxicity, and it is believed that this scaffold could serve as a promising lead structure for further development of drugs for both sleeping sickness and Chagas disease. Starting from actinoallolide E (87), the remaining actinoallolides A-D (88–91) were also successfully synthesized. A full paper on these studies was published later that year.

Paterson’s Total Synthesis of Actinoallolides A–E (87–91)

A successful ICR of structurally similar rearrangement precursor 92 was one of the key steps for the total synthesis of herboxidiene (95), also known as GEX 1A (Scheme ). In contrast to Paterson’s work (Scheme ), in this case, the authors aimed for stereospecific enolization conditions leading to the Z-ketene acetal 94 that would ultimately furnish the desired stereochemistry at C14 via the ICR. After screening various reaction parameters, it was found that LiHMDS in a THF/DMPU mixture indeed stereoselectively furnishes the desired Z-ketene acetal 94, which in turn gives the desired diastereomer as the single product. Because of technical difficulties in purifying the ICR product, its silyl ester group was further reduced to the corresponding alcohol 93. In this work, the authors also provide further insight into the antiproliferative activity of herboxidiene (95) and its derivatives in various human solid-tumor cell lines.

Total Synthesis of (+)-Herboxidiene/GEX 1A (95)

Both the natural and unnatural enantiomers of the alkyl citrate natural product CJ-13,982 (98) were accessed via ICR as a key step (Scheme ). In this particular case, the ICR step proceeds with a decent yield but only moderate diastereoselectivity. Nevertheless, the authors were able to separate the minor diastereomer 97a by flash column chromatography and smoothly convert the major 97b into the target natural product CJ-13,982 (98), a known inhibitor of squalene synthase, the enzyme responsible for the first step in cholesterol biosynthesis. As for the ICR step, a rather unusual combination of TMSCl/Et3N/LDA was employed. During earlier studies by this group on the ICR of similar substrates, the crucial role of using the supernatant from a centrifuged mixture of TMSCl/NEt3 was highlighted.

Total Synthesis of CJ-13,982 (98) by Rizzacasa

ICR proved to be a useful tool for the construction of the lignane scaffold during the relative stereochemistry determination studies conducted by Barker and co-workers (Scheme ). In this case, ester 99 smoothly underwent ICR, furnishing the corresponding acid 100 in nearly quantitative yield and high diastereoselectivity. This building block was further incorporated into the lignan core structure. The natural enantiomer of anti -101a is known to be cytotoxic in various cancer cell lines and to exhibit anti-HIV-167 and antioxidant activities.

Relative Stereochemistry Revision of Liganes by Barker

The total synthesis of two rather unique spirotetronate natural products, spirohexenolides A (105) and B (106), featuring a rare bent π-conjugation motif, was recently reported by Tong’s group (Scheme ). Based on an earlier study by Haudrechy, the authors succeeded in constructing two very challenging adjacent quaternary stereocenters with good yield and excellent diastereoselectivity via ICR of an advanced intermediate 102. The stereochemical outcome of this step can be rationalized by a chairlike transition state involving the stereoselectively generated Z-ketene acetal 104. The obtained ester 103 was further successfully converted into both target natural products, spirohexenolides A (105) and B (106). These natural products exhibit micromolar cytotoxicity in several cancer cell lines, , and more detailed mechanism-of-action studies have shown that these effects could be attributed to the ability of spirohexenolides to bind to and modulate the human macrophage migration inhibitor factor (hMIF). In addition, spirohexenolide A (105) exhibits antibacterial activity against Gram-positive bacteria by disrupting the cytoplasmic membrane through depletion of the proton motive force (PMF).

Terpenoids

ICR has been frequently used in the total syntheses of terpenoids. For example, in 2016 Mori reported the construction of the quaternary center in female-produced sex pheromone (anti-1,2-dimethyl-3-methylenecyclopentyl)­acetaldehyde (109, Scheme ) of the pineapple mealybug (Dysmicoccus brevipes). Starting from a mixture of double-bond isomers of 107, a racemic rearrangement product 108 was obtained in good yield after the acidic hydrolysis of the formed silyl ester. This intermediate 108 was further converted to the desired syn-diastereomer of racemic pheromone 109.

Mori’s Total Synthesis of Pheromone 109

The ICR has also been employed as a key step in the stereoselective construction of two adjacent stereocenters in the cannabinoid scaffold (Scheme ). The ICR step proceeds with full transfer of chirality, and the recrystallization of the rearrangement product 111 allowed increasing the ee even up to 94%. The intermediate 111 was further converted into Δ9-trans-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) 112, which has been broadly studied clinically and FDA-approved for the treatment of nausea and vomiting as well as AIDS-related anorexia. Also, cannabidiol (CBD) 113 was accessed from the same intermediate 111. More recently, Passarella’s group reported the first total synthesis of cannabidiol-C4 (CBD) 114 via the same intermediate 111, but using the LDA/TMSCl combination at elevated temperature. These conditions furnished the desired rearrangement product in 85% yield and are believed to proceed via the E/E configuration chairlike TS transition state 115 ‡.

The ICR also enabled a stereoselective construction of two challenging adjacent stereocenters at one of the first steps of the total synthesis of darwinolide (118, Scheme ). In this case, the combination of LiHMDS and TMSCl in a nonpolar solvent and elevated temperature proved to be the key to success and furnished the desired product 117 in 71% yield in a highly stereoselective fashion. Darwinolide (118) exhibits antibacterial activity against highly methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and is considered to be a promising lead for the development of antibiofilm-specific antibiotics.

Takikawa and co-workers published a very interesting method for the construction of bicyclo[3.1.1]­heptene skeleton by ring-contractive ICR (Scheme ). Although silyl ketene acetals also work in this transformation, the corresponding enolphosphate 120 furnished the target rearrangement product 121 in considerably higher yield and at slightly lower temperature (175 °C vs 160 °C). The authors also noticed that during the ICR rearrangement of enantiomerically pure lactone, racemization most probably occurs via reversible C–O bond cleavage and allyl cation 124 ‡ formation from ketene acetal 120. Thus, prolinole was used to resolve the enantiomers of product 121, which ultimately allowed the isolation of both enantiomers of clavigerins B (122) and C (123). These natural products are known to exhibit antifeedant activity against larvae of the wool insect pests Tineola bisselliella and Anthrenocerus australis.

In 2021, Gui’s group reported the first total synthesis and structure revision of polychlorinated steroids clionastatins A and B (Scheme ). In this case, the ICR was used at the very beginning of the total synthesis of these natural products and presumably proceeds with a full transfer of chirality. The cytotoxicity of clionastatins A and B in various cancer cell lines is documented in the original isolation report by Fattorusso and co-workers.

Although with very low stereoselectivity, the construction of very challenging two adjacent stereocenters enabled by the ICR was recently reported by Xie’s group (Scheme ). By treating a diastereomeric mixture of 129a and 129b with LDA/TMSCl, all four possible diastereomers 130a–d were obtained. Interestingly, using the Koga-type base (in this case, bis­[(S)-1-phenylethyl]­amine), broadly studied by Zakarian in the context of ICR of α,α-disubstituted ketene acetals, , proved to be completely unselective in this particular case, furnishing all four diastereomers of 130 in equal amounts. In general, it has been shown that matching the chirality of Koga-type bases to that of the ester enables highly stereoselective enolizations, and the subsequent ICR enables the installation of two adjacent quaternary stereogenic centers with moderate diastereoselectivity. , Nevertheless, the diastereomeric mixtures of 130a and 130c were further incorporated into the core structure of (−)-chamaecydin (131) and 130b and 130d in the (−)-isochamaecydin (132). Since the cyclohexane ring in 130a–d is aromatized in the next total synthesis steps, further separation of diastereomers was not necessary. From the early studies, it is known that chamaecydin (131) in particular exhibited antifeedant activity when studied against the pest insect Spodoptera litura.

Recently, Jia and co-workers reported a convergent route to a series of Pallavicinia diterpenoids with three distinct carbon skeletons arising from a single advanced intermediate (Scheme ). One of the key steps in the synthesis of the common intermediate (not shown) was the ICR of ester 133. It proceeded smoothly at a slightly elevated temperature, furnishing the target rearrangement product 134 in 90% yield. Although the isolation, biosynthesis, and total synthesis of Pallavicinia diterpenoids have been studied in detail, , their biological activity studies remain scarce, attributed to the limited amounts of these natural products available. Although with slightly lower stereoselectivity, a similar structural motif was constructed by ICR as one of the key steps of the total synthesis of (+)-karanone (147, Scheme ), one of the key aroma sesquiterpenoids in high-grade agarwood (“Kyara”). The authors have proven experimentally that the reason for moderate product dr is associated with the stereoselectivity issues in the ester enolization step. Thus, the corresponding ketene acetal is formed in a cis/trans ratio ∼3:1, which further reflects into the product dr. Attempts to increase the enolization stereoselectivity proved to be unsuccessful. Nevertheless, the rearrangement product 146 was further successfully incorporated into the karone (147) core structure.

Conclusions

Over the past decade, the Ireland–Claisen rearrangement has continued to demonstrate remarkable versatility as a stereocontrolled C–C bond-forming transformation in natural product total synthesis. The examples surveyed in this review clearly show that the rearrangement remains highly relevant in modern synthetic strategy planning, particularly when efficient construction of contiguous stereogenic centers, quaternary stereocenters, or geometrically defined alkene motifs is required. Most of the reviewed examples proceed with high stereoselectivity and good isolated yields. These studies further illustrate that although the classical chairlike transition state model remains applicable in many cases, subtle steric, conformational, and chelation effects often determine the stereochemical outcome in more complex systems. Consequently, successful implementation frequently relies on carefully selected enolization conditions, reagent combinations, and solvent effects tailored to individual substrate architectures. Across structurally diverse natural product classesincluding alkaloids, peptides, polyketides, and terpenoidsthe Ireland–Claisen rearrangement has repeatedly served not only as a useful transformation but also as a strategic step enabling convergent access to advanced intermediates and target molecules. Given the continuing demand for concise, stereochemically reliable routes to architecturally complex natural products, further methodological refinements and the exploration of new substrate classes are likely to expand its role in future total synthesis campaigns.

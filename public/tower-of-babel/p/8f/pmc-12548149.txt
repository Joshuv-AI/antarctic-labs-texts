USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α promotes the progression of prostate cancer
Authors: Zhuoran Gu, Libin Zou, Cheng Jiang, Feng Qi, Dan Huang, Ji Liu, Yadong Guo, Shiyu Mao, Junfeng Zhang, Yue Zhang, Donghui Shi, Jiang Geng, Yifan Chen, Wentao Zhang, Xudong Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) is one of the prevalent cancers in men. Although deubiquitinating enzymes are implicated in tumorigenesis and progression, the specific role of ubiquitin specific peptidase 21 (USP21) in PCa remains unclear. This study provides the first comprehensive analysis of USP21 as a key oncogene driving PCa progression.MethodsPrognosis-related deubiquitinating enzymes in PCa were initially identified through PCa cohorts from TCGA and GEO databases, followed by experimental validation. USP21 expression in PCa tissues and cell lines using tissue microarray immunohistochemistry, western blotting, and RT-qPCR. The biological functions of USP21 were evaluated through patient-derived organoids, in vitro and in vivo experiments. USP21 substrate proteins were identified by co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) coupled with mass spectrometry, revealing Y-box binding protein 1 (YBX1) as the primary target. YBX1 transcriptional activity was quantified using dual-luciferase reporter assays and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP). The effect of USP21 inhibition was evaluated using the USP21-targeted inhibitor Bay-805 in PCa cells.ResultsUSP21 is upregulated in PCa and correlates with poor prognosis. Functional assays demonstrated that USP21 knockdown significantly inhibited PCa cells proliferation, migration, and invasion. RNA-sequencing further revealed that USP21 modulates the HIF1 signaling pathway. Mechanistically, USP21 deubiquitinates and stabilizes YBX1, which in turn enhances HIF1A (encoding HIF1-α) transcription. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of USP21 with Bay-805 suppressed PCa progression, highlighting its therapeutic potential.ConclusionsUSP21 promotes PCa malignancy by interacting with YBX1 to upregulate HIF1-α expression. These findings suggest Bay-805 as a promising therapeutic candidate for PCa.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07199-3.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most commonly diagnosed male malignancy globally and is the fifth leading cause of male cancer-related mortality [1–4]. Disease progression, particularly distant metastasis, remain the primary driver of PCa-related mortality. Notably, the proportion of patients initially diagnosed with metastatic PCa has steadily increased in recent years, reflecting a significant shift toward more advanced-stage diagnoses [4]. Androgen deprivation therapy currently represents the cornerstone treatment for metastatic PCa. However, most patients eventually develop castration resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) and acquire resistance to therapy within 18 to 24 months of treatment, significantly compromising clinical outcomes [5]. Consequently, PCa remains a major global public health challeng. A deeper understanding of its underlying pathophysiology and the optimization of therapeutic strategies are critical.

Post-translational modifications, especially reversible ubiquitination and deubiquitination, play a pivotal role in regulating malignant cellular behaviors such as proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis during tumorigenesis and progression [6]. USP21, a highly conserved deubiquitinating enzyme, influence various biological processes, including cell cycle regulation, immune response, and transcriptional control [7]. USP21 acts as an oncogenic driver in numerous malignancies, including gastric, pancreatic, hepatocellular, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers [7]. For example, USP21 deubiquitinates and stabilizes FOXD1 to promote glioblastoma progression and similarly stabilizes EZH2 to drive bladder carcinogenesis [8, 9]. Despite its well-established role in tumorigenesis across different cancers, the function of USP21 in PCa has not yet been explored.

This study demonstrates for the first time that USP21 contributes to the progression of PCa. The underlying molecular mechanisms were investigated, and Y-box binding protein 1 (YBX1) was identified as a key USP21-interacting protein. YBX1, a transcription factor and multifunctional RNA-binding protein, is involved in maintaining tumor cell survival and driving metastasis across various cancers by regulating transcriptional activation, mRNA stability, and DNA damage repair [10–13]. While overexpression of YBX1 correlates with poor prognosis in PCa, the mechanisms driving its dysregulation and its specific role in PCa progression remain poorly understood [14, 15].

The HIF1 pathway plays a pivotal role in the initiation and progression of PCa by mediating key processes including tumor cell adaptation to hypoxia, angiogenesis, and metabolic reprogramming [16]. Although YBX1 positively regulates HIF1A transcription, its specific regulatory mechanism in PCa remains undefined [17].

This study demonstrates that USP21 deubiquitinates and stabilizes YBX1, which in turn, transactivates HIF1A expression, thereby driving PCa progression. Moreover, the small-molecule inhibitor Bay-805, which targets the USP21, exhibits potent antitumor efficacy both in vitro and in vivo, making it a promising therapeutic candidate for PCa. These findings uncover a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of PCa.

Material and methods

Reagents and transfection

Cells were transfected with designated vectors or shRNAs using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, No. L3000015, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Plasmid and lentiviral constructs for USP21/YBX1 overexpression, gene-specific shRNAs, truncated mutants of USP21, and HA-tagged ubiquitin plasmids (WT, K0, K11, K27, K48, K63) were synthesized by Genomeditech (Shanghai, China) (sequences in supplementary files). Following transfection, cells underwent puromycin selection. Transfection efficiency was quantified by RT-qPCR and Western blotting.

Luciferase reporter assays

Dual-luciferase reporter assays were conducted using a commercial kit (Vazyme, No. DL101-01, Nanjing, China). Wild-type and mutant HIF1-α promoter fragments, were cloned into pGL4-Basic vectors. These constructs were co-transfected into HEK293T cells. After 48 hours, cells were lysed and Firefly/Renilla luciferase activities were quantified using a GloMax luminometer. Firefly luciferase signals were normalized to Renilla controls.

Immunofluorescence (IF) and co- localization analysis

For IF staining, PCa cells were fixed in 4% formaldehyde, permeated with 0.25% Triton X-100, and then blocked with 3% BSA at room temperature for 1 h. The cells were probed with the primary antibody. After washing by TBST, cells were combined with secondary antibody. The samples were observed through a high-resolution confocal laser scanning microscope (Carl Zeiss LSM 900, Jena, Germany).

Co-localization was quantified using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (PCC) and line profile analysis. As previously depicted, PCC values were calculated using ImageJ from 50 cells and data were presented as mean ±95% confidence interval (CI) [18, 19]. For line profile analysis, a straight line was drawn across cells using the straight-line tool in ImageJ. Fluorescence intensity profiles along the line for both channels were extracted using the plot-profile function.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

ChIP assays were performed in DU145 and PC3 cells using the ChIP Kit (BersinBio, No. Bes5001, Guangzhou, China). Cells were formaldehyde-fixed, glycine-quenched, and lysed in buffer with protease inhibitors/DTT. Chromatin was sonicated to 200–750 bp fragments. Sheared chromatin was immunoprecipitated overnight with anti-YBX1 or rabbit IgG control, followed by Protein A/G magnetic beads incubation. After washing, crosslinks were reversed and DNA purified for qPCR. After washing and crosslink reversal, purified DNA was analyzed by qPCR using HIF1A promoter-specific primers: forward 5’-CCTGGCCAACATGGTGAAAC-3’; reverse 5’-GATTCTCCTGCCTCAGCCTC-3’.

Migration and invasion assay

Transwell chambers (Yesean, No. 84051ES12, Shanghai, China) were used for conventional cell migration and invasion assays. For migration, 5 × 104 cells in 200 μL serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chambers, with 700 μL medium containing 20% FBS in the lower compartments. After 48 hours, migrated cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and quantified by counting five random fields per chamber. Invasion assays used either matrigel-precoated chambers processed similarly to migration assays, or 3D invasion models where GFP-expressing cells (5 × 104 cells/μL) were mixed with matrigel on ice, inoculated (1 μL/chamber), inverted at 37 °C for 15 minutes for gelation, then overlaid with 500 μL 20% FBS medium. After 6 days of culture, invasive structures were imaged using fluorescence microscopy and quantified with ImageJ.

Animal experiments

For subcutaneous xenograft assays, PCa cells were resuspended in PBS (1 × 106 cells/mL) and injected into nude mice (100 µL per mouse). Tumors were excised after 1 month for comparative analysis. For lung metastasis assays, luciferase-transduced cells (1 × 106 cells per mouse) were intravenously administered. Body weights were recorded, and in vivo bioluminescence imaging was used to quantify pulmonary metastases.

Patient-derived tumor oragnoids

Prostate tumor specimens from radical prostatectomy or prostate biopsy were minced into 1–2 mm fragments and enzymatically digested with 1 mg/mL Type IV collagenase (Yeasen, No. 40508ES60, Shanghai, China) for 1 hour at 37 °C. The digested tissue was passed through a 100 μm cell strainer, and the resulting pellet was resuspended in 30 μL Matrigel (Yeasen, No. 40183ES08, Shanghai, China). This cell suspension was plated in pre-warmed 24-well plates and incubated at 37 °C for 30 minutes to initiate spheroid formation. Following Matrigel polymerization, cultures were maintained in human PCa organoid medium, with medium refreshed every 2–3 days. Lentiviral transduction with USP21 shRNA or control shRNA was performed according to established protocols [20]. Organoid proliferation was assessed by measuring diameter changes under light microscopy.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.1.1 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). For comparisons between two groups with normally distributed data, two-tailed Student’s t-tests were used. Non-normally distributed data were analyzed with Mann-Whitney tests. Survival curves were compared by log-rank testing. Continuous variables across multiple groups were assessed via one-way ANOVA. Statistical significance was set as p < 0.05.

Results

USP21 was upregulated in PCa and correlated with poor prognosis

As a major subfamily of deubiquitinating enzymes, the USP protein family consists of over 50 members. Systematic univariate Cox regression analysis of USP family members within the TCGA-PRAD cohort revealed that USP21 expression was significantly associated with both overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) (Supplementary Figure 1A-B; Fig. 1A). Further clinicopathological analysis indicated that patients with high USP21 expression exhibited elevated Gleason scores (suggesting poor tumor differentiation), advanced T-stage classification, and significantly increased rates of biochemical recurrence (Fig. 1B), suggesting USP21’s involvement in malignant progression. Additionally, transcriptomic analysis of GEO datasets (GSE3225, GSE29079) confirmed substantial upregulation of USP21 mRNA in tumor tissue compared to adjacent normal tissue (Fig. 1C–D). Complementary analysis of clinical specimens—using RT-qPCR on 23 radical prostatectomy patient samples and Western blotting on 12 matched cases—validated cancer-specific overexpression at both the transcriptional and protein levels (Fig. 1E–F). Furthermore, immunohistochemistry (IHC) assessment of tissue microarrays demonstrated a significant positive correlation between USP21 expression intensity and Gleason score (Fig. 1G). Notably, high USP21 expression was associated with an increased risk of biochemical recurrence (p < 0.001) and reduced PFS (p < 0.001) (Table 1) (Fig. 1I). Subsequent univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses confirmed T-stage and USP21 expression as independent prognostic risk factors (Table 2). USP21 expression was markedly higher in PCa patient-derived organoids than in normal prostate organoids when maintained in 3D culture, as shown by immunofluorescence (IF) staining (Fig. 1H). USP21 promotes the proliferation, migration and invasion of PCa in vitro

Endogenous USP21 expression was evaluated in various PCa cell lines by qRT-PCR and Western blotting, showing significantly higher levels in various PCa cells compared to RWPE1 cells which was normal prostate epithelial cell line (Supplementary Figure 2A-B). To investigate the functional role of USP21 in PCa cells, we constructed stable USP21-knockdown and USP21-overexpressed cell lines in DU145 and PC3 (Fig. 2A). Knockdown and overexpression efficiency was verified at both transcriptional and protein levels through RT-qPCR and Western blot (Fig. 2B–C). Functional assays revealed USP21 as a critical regulator of oncogenic phenotypes in PCa cells. CCK-8, EdU and colony formation assays demonstrated that the proliferation ability of PCa cells was significantly impaired by knockdown of USP21 and enhanced by overexpression of USP21 (Fig. 2D–H). Furthermore, migration and invasion capabilities were influenced by regulating USP21 expression. Scratch wound healing showed delayed closure with knockdown but accelerated repair with overexpression of USP21 (Fig. 2I–J). Transwell assays revealed reduced cellular penetration with knockdown while increased penetration with overexpression of USP21 (Fig. 2K–L). 3D Matrigel invasion assays demonstrated impaired invasiveness with knockdown and enhanced invasion with overexpression of USP21 (Fig. 2M–N). USP21 regulates the progression of PCa in vivo

To further validate the tumor-promoting role of USP21 in PCa, its impact on proliferation and metastasis was assessed in vivo using subcutaneous xenograft and tail vein injection lung metastasis models (Fig. 3A). Subcutaneous tumor analysis revealed that USP21 knockdown significantly suppressed tumor volume and weight, while overexpression promoted tumor growth (Fig. 3B–D). H&E staining confirmed tumor pathology, and IHC analysis showed corresponding decreases and increases in USP21 expression following knockdown and overexpression, respectively, with similar trends observed for proliferation markers Ki-67 and PCNA (Fig. 3E). In the lung metastasis model, luciferase signal intensity was significantly reduced in the sh-USP21 group but enhanced in the oe-USP21 group (Fig. 4F, H). Histological examination of lung tissues confirmed fewer metastatic nodules in the knockdown mice and more in the overexpression group (Fig. 3G, I) Additionally, USP21 knockdown inhibited proliferation and growth in patient-derived PCa organoids (Fig. 3J–L). USP21 interacts with YBX1

To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying USP21‘s deubiquitinating enzyme function, potential substrates were screened using Co-IP combined with silver staining and mass spectrometry (MS) (Fig. 4A). MS analysis identified YBX1 as a key USP21-binding protein. YBX1 was identified with 47.53% sequence coverage (confidence score: 53.10) and 5 unique peptides. The observed molecular weight (35.90 kDa) matched its theoretical value (36 kDa, UniProt ID P67809), confirming the MS assignment (Fig. 4B). Molecular docking simulations revealed the molecular basis of their interaction (Fig. 4C). IF staining revealed obvious spatial overlap between USP21 and YBX1 in PCa cells (Fig. 4D). Quantitative co-localization analysis using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (PCC) demonstrated strong correlation in both cell lines. In DU145 (PCC = 0.82, 95% CI: 0.77–0.84) and PC3 (PCC = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.69–0.84), correlations significantly exceeded the 0.5 threshold (Fig. 4E). Line profile analysis further confirmed colocalization at subcellular scales. Fluorescence intensity peaks for USP21 and YBX1 coincided of analyzed dashed lines (Fig. 4F). Both endogenous (in DU145 and PC3) and exogenous Co-IP assays verified the interaction between USP21 and YBX1 (Fig. 4G–I). Given that USP21 contains an N-terminal domain and a C-terminal USP domain, we determined the critical binding region by transfecting Flag-tagged full-length USP21, or its truncated domain constructs followed by Co-IP (Fig. 4J). This demonstrated that the C-terminal USP domain mediates the binding (Fig. 4K). Furthermore, USP21 knockdown reduced, while its overexpression increased, YBX1 protein levels without altering its mRNA expression, indicating that USP21 likely regulates YBX1 stability through post-translational modification (Fig. 4L–M).

USP21 deubiquitinates and stabilizes YBX1

To determine whether USP21 stabilizes YBX1 via deubiquitination, plasmids encoding Flag-USP21 WT or the catalytically inactive mutant Flag-USP21 C211A were transfected. Overexpression of USP21 WT, but not the C211A mutant, increased YBX1 protein levels (Fig. 5A). Cycloheximide (CHX) chase assays in DU145 and PC3 cells demonstrated that USP21 knockdown accelerated YBX1 protein degradation, reducing its half-life and stability. In contrast, USP21 overexpression slowed YBX1 degradation, prolonging its half-life and enhancing stability (Fig. 5B–D). MG132 treatment inhibited YBX1 degradation, confirming its dependence on the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) (Fig. 5E–F). Co-IP assays in DU145 and PC3 cells revealed that USP21 knockdown increased YBX1 ubiquitination, while USP21 overexpression decreased it (Fig. 5G). Consistent with these findings, overexpression of USP21 WT (but not C211A) reduced YBX1 ubiquitination in HEK293T cells, suggesting that USP21’s regulation of YBX1 ubiquitination relies on its catalytic activity (Fig. 5H). To identify the specific ubiquitin chain linkage targeted by USP21, its activity towards different ubiquitin chains was assessed. USP21 selectively cleaved K48-linked polyubiquitin chains but had no effect on monoubiquitination or K11, K27, and K63-linked polyubiquitin chains (Fig. 5I). To further validate the role of K48-linked ubiquitination in USP21-mediated regulation of YBX1, the K48R ubiquitin mutant was expressed in DU145 and PC3 cells. Expression of K48R ubiquitin rescued the decrease in YBX1 protein levels induced by USP21 knockdown (Fig. 5J). YBX1 transcriptionally activates HIF1-α

Transcriptome sequencing was conducted in DU145 cells following USP21 knockdown. Hierarchical clustering analysis (Fig. 6A) revealed distinct gene expression profiles between the sh-USP21 and sh-NC groups. Differential expression analysis identified 114 genes significantly upregulated and 348 significantly downregulated genes in the sh-USP21 group compared to the sh-NC group (Fig. 6B). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis highlighted significant enrichment of the HIF1 signaling pathway, with HIF1A (encoding HIF1-α) being among the most notably downregulated genes (Fig. 6B, C). TCGA PRAD data analysis showed a significant positive correlation between YBX1 and HIF1A expression (Fig. 6D). Using the JASPAR database, potential YBX1 binding sites within the HIF1A promoter region were predicated, and the corresponding motif sequence was generated (Fig. 6E). Dual-luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that mutation of the predicted YBX1 binding site (Fig. 6F) significantly reduced promoter activity compared to the wild-type construct (Fig. 6G). Meanwhile, chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by qPCR (ChIP-qPCR) demonstrated significant enrichment of YBX1 at the HIF1A promoter in PCa cells (Fig. 6H). These results indicated the direct binding of YBX1 to the HIF1A promoter in vitro. Furthermore, knockdown or overexpression of YBX1 in PCa cell lines resulted in significant downregulation or upregulation of HIF1A mRNA levels, respectively (Fig. 6I). Critically, overexpression of YBX1 in sh-USP21 cells partially rescued the USP21 knockdown-induced suppression of HIF1-α expression (Fig. 6J–K). IF staining of pathological tissue sections from PCa patients demonstrated concomitant overexpression of USP21, YBX1, and HIF1-α in malignant lesions (Supplementary Figure 3A). Collectively, these results support the existence of a USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α regulatory axis in PCa.

BAY-805 suppresses the progression of PCa by inhibiting USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α

The experimental findings highlight the of USP21 in PCa progression, suggesting that targeting USP21 may offer a potential therapeutic strategy. Bay-805 has been identified as a selective USP21 inhibitor, with molecular docking confirming its stable binding to USP21 (Fig. 6A) [21].

Following 48-hour treatment with Bay-805, CCK-8 assays revealed significant inhibition of proliferation in DU145 and PC3 cells among vector and oe-USP21 groups. The IC50 values were calculated from non-linear regression analysis. IC50 values in DU145 cells were 51.03 μM (95% CI: 41.71–62.62) for the vector group versus 120.4 μM (95% CI: 94.89–153.2) for the oe-USP21 group; corresponding values in PC3 cells were 28.08 μM (95% CI: 21.35–36.98) versus 92.99 μM (95% CI: 71.81–121.10) (Fig. 6C). Notably, USP21 overexpression attenuated the anti-proliferative effect of Bay-805, resulting in an approximately 2.5-fold increase in IC50 values.

Furthermore, Bay-805 suppressed the expression of USP21, YBX1, and HIF1-α in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 6B). Consistent with these molecular alterations, colony formation assays demonstrated that Bay-805 dose-dependently inhibited the proliferative capacity of PCa cells (Fig. 6 D-E). Concomitantly, transwell assays revealed a significant reduction in cell migration with increasing Bay-805 concentrations, while 3D Matrigel invasion assays showed parallel dose-responsive suppression of invasive potential (Fig. 6E). The coordinated attenuation of proliferative, migratory and invasive capacities correlates with Bay-805-induced downregulation of USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α, suggesting disruption of this oncogenic axis as a pivotal mechanism underlying Bay-805‘s anti-tumor efficacy in PCa.

To exclude the possibility of off-target effects of Bay-805, Western blot analysis demonstrated that the Bay-805-induced downregulation of YBX1 and HIF1-α was reversed by USP21 overexpression (Fig. 6H). Consistently, colony formation assays showed that while Bay-805 significantly suppressed PCa cells proliferation, ectopic USP21 expression partially rescued this inhibitory effect (Fig.s 6I-J). Moreover, transwell migration and 3D Matrigel invasion assays further confirmed that the Bay-805-mediated suppression of metastatic potential was substantially reversed by USP21 overexpression (Fig.s 6K-L). Collectively, these findings establish that Bay-805 exerts its antitumor activity in PCa progression specifically through inhibition of USP21.

To evaluate the in vivo efficacy of Bay-805, a subcutaneous xenograft model was established in nude mice using DU145 cells. Tumor-bearing mice were randomly assigned to two groups receiving either Bay-805 (20 mg/kg, i.p.) or vehicle (PBS) at matched time points (Fig. 7M). Compared to the vehicle control, Bay-805 treatment significantly reduced tumor weight and volume (Fig. 7N–P). IHC analysis further revealed that Bay-805 treatment markedly decreased the protein expression levels of USP21, YBX1, HIF1-α, and the proliferation marker Ki-67 (Fig. 7Q). Discussion

This study systematically revealed a novel mechanism by which USP21 promotes PCa progression. By integrating in vitro cell assays, in vivo animal models, and patient-derived organoids, the function of the USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α axis in PCa pathogenesis was elucidated.

Recent studies have highlighted the significant role of deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) in various key cellular processes associated with different cancers [22, 23]. The USP family, the largest subgroup within the DUBs, has been particularly implicated in the development and progression of several cancer types, including pancreatic, lung, and breast cancers [24–27]. Moreover, studies have demonstrated that USP family members, such as USP1, USP2, USP9X and USP22, regulate the stability and function of critical proteins through deubiquitination, thereby driving PCa cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [28–30].

As a prominent member of the USP family, USP21 has gained increasing attention for its functional roles in tumorigenesis and progression. For instance, it stabilizes TCF7 to enhance cancer cell proliferation and self-repair in pancreatic cancer, and deubiquitinates FOXD1 to promote glioblastoma progression [8, 31]. In urological malignancies, USP21 stabilizes EZH2 via deubiquitination, driving bladder cancer proliferation and metastasis [9]. Paradoxically, USP21 also stabilizes MARK proteins to inhibit YAP/TAZ activity and tumor progression [32]. The role of USP21 varies across different cancers due to tumor-specific factors including cancer cell type, tissue environment, and its substrate targets. This study is the first to investigate the role of USP21 in PCa, particularly its involvement in regulating malignant phenotypes such as tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. However, USP21 validation was conducted on a relatively small cohort of patient tissues, assessing both mRNA and protein levels. Given the potential tumor heterogeneity across different clinical stages and pathological grades of PCa, larger cohorts and external validation datasets would be valuable for a more robust evaluation of USP21‘s potential as a biomarker.

YBX1, as a transcription factor and multifunctional RNA-binding protein, promotes tumor cell proliferation and metastasis by regulating the cancer cell cycle, activating the PI3K/AKT pathway, and enhancing EGFR transcriptional activity [12, 14, 33, 34]. Accumulating evidence has indicated that YBX1 is markedly overexpressed in PCa, particularly in CRPC, where it facilitates tumor cell proliferation, metastasis, and the development of therapeutic resistance by regulating androgen receptor signaling [35–37]. Furthermore, YBX1 has emerged as a promising candidate for advancing personalized management and precision medicine strategies in PCa [15].

To elucidate the mechanism by which USP21 maintains YBX1 stability, this study modulated USP21 expression and observed corresponding changes in YBX1 protein levels but not mRNA levels. This suggested that USP21 regulates YBX1 protein stability through deubiquitination. Subsequent ubiquitination assays confirmed that USP21 maintains YBX1 stability by specifically mediating the removal of K48-linked polyubiquitin chains. This finding further supports the concept that pattern-specific deubiquitination, such as the removal of K48-linked chains, is a key mechanism regulating protein degradation and stability [23, 38].

To investigate the functional consequences of USP21 knockdown in PCa, transcriptome sequencing and KEGG analysis revealed significant enrichment of the HIF1 pathway. Additionally, a strong positive correlation between YBX1 and HIF1A expression was found in PCa. Further predictions from JASPAR database and dual luciferase assays and ChIP-qPCR confirmed that YBX1 directly binds the HIF1A promoter and enhances its transcriptional activity. This suggests USP21 may indirectly regulate HIF1A through YBX1, thereby promoting PCa progression. These findings are consistent with previous studies and further emphasize the critical role of the USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α axis in PCa [17, 39].

This provides the first systematic evidence that USP21 promotes PCa progression by stabilizing YBX1 through deubiquitination. YBX1 transactivates HIF1A, leading to the elevated expression of HIF1-α. This finding offers crucial new insights into the adaptive mechanisms and energy metabolism of PCa cells during tumor progression, particularly highlighting the central role of HIF1A in tumor growth and metastasis. Moreover, the indirect regulation of HIF1A by USP21 via YBX1 expands our understanding of USP21 as an oncogenic factor. Finally, Bay-805, a small-molecule USP21 inhibitor, effectively suppresses the activation of the USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α axis, demonstrating significant anti-tumor efficacy and highlighting the therapeutic potential of targeting this pathway in PCa.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the USP21/YBX1/HIF1-α axis promotes prostate cancer progression, positioning USP21 targeting as a promising therapeutic strategy.

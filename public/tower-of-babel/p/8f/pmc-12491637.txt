VARNet-6G with FIERO model for anomaly detection and enhancing network stability in future-ready communication systems
Authors: S. Sankar Ganesh, Maha Abdelhaq, SatheeshKumar Palanisamy, S. Janakiraman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The world is moving toward communication networks of 6G, so security will become a very essential aspect for the integrity and reliability of the system. Advanced anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation techniques are required due to the exponential rise in network traffic, diversity in applications, and interconnectivity of devices. Most of the anomaly detection models until now are encumbered with the issues of scalability, being adaptable to new types of attacks, and the capability of processing large, dynamic network data efficiently. In order to address these challenges, this paper proposes two new techniques: the Variational Autoencoder and Recurrent Transformer Network for 6G (VARNet-6G) for anomaly detection and the Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer (FIERO) for dropout rate estimation. VARNet-6G deals with sequential data in an efficient way to achieve robust real-time anomaly detection by combining variational auto-encoders with recurrent transformers. On the other hand, FIERO introduces a new optimization technique inspired by natural phenomena for the estimation of dropout rate, which provides highly accurate network performance estimates and ensures network resilience. The proposed schemes have improved by large margins over the existing models, addressing the limitations of traditional techniques in both anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation. The novelty of this work lies in the hybrid approach in combining deep learning with nature-inspired optimization, which guarantees more accurate, scalable, and adaptive solutions to secure 6G networks.

Introduction

Recently, 6G communication systems are going to revolutionize the user’s possibility of being in contact with a far larger virtual world that in itself may create next-generation digital ecosystems. While the current rollout of 5G networks1,2 represents just the first step in this epoch of digital communication, catering to unprecedented speeds, ultra-low latencies, and massive connectivity, the transition to 6G is going to respond to exponential growth in demand looking for more advanced and intelligent connectivity solutions. Over the next decade, the development of the 5G network will lay the foundation for what is envisioned under 6G, bringing onboard leading-edge technologies that will enable the intrinsic limitations within the current communication systems to be overcome. Embedded in the 6G paradigm3 will be the connected intelligence that leverages the integrated use of edge computing, artificial intelligence, quantum communication, and the 6G terahertz frequency band making it seamless to plug into the digital virtual world and ultra-densification. Initial research into 6G has already indicated that 6G systems2 are offering a plethora of new application domains compared to what the current network solutions can achieve today. Of all the transformative use cases, perhaps one is wireless brain-computer interface that directly connects the human brain with the digital system to make possible new dimensions of human-machine interactions for applications in medical improvements, cognitive computing, and immersive virtual experiences. With that, the ultra-low latency and high reliability of 6G networks will enable networked robotics and autonomous systems where action coordination and decision-making are critical to manufacturing, logistics and autonomous transportation systems.

Moreover, multimodal extended reality: augmented reality, virtual reality, and mixed reality will progress even more, and digital environment will become even more realistic and will change the entertainment, educational, and collaboration perspectives at the workplace. Besides these pioneering use cases, other conventional application types that will remain and progress in 6G networks include multimedia streaming. As the multimedia content becomes higher resolution and users’ expectations increase4, 6G has to provide uncontested data rates, critical low latency and great network availability. Additionally, as the technology grows more advanced, applications such as holographic telepresence, will enable individuals to visually have a three dimensional interaction with someone remotely as simply as a phone call today changes the aspects of communication, teleworking, and social interaction. Some of the new eHealth solutions5 to be stored and transmitted through the 6G’s ULRCC include remote surgeries, real-time diagnosis, continuous patient monitoring through in-body networks.

However, it is critically important that these applications will be met by 6G with the right balance of essential properties such as high spectrum capacity, reliable and ubiquitous connectivity, power efficiency, security, modularity, and adaptability. The use of state-of-the-art machine learning solutions into the 6G architecture is inevitable for the anticipatory control of the network, resources, and communication protocols for unbroken user experience in highly dynamic settings. Tackling these challenges6, 6G systems will become the foundation for the future digital societies where individuals and industries will be furnished with a new opportunity space in terms of connected intelligence and VR-like environment for creation of human centric digital ecosystems. The advancement to 6G communication infrastructure7,8 can be described as a shift to an entirely new level of communication technology with highly ambitious goals that include delivering data rates that are many magnitudes higher than 5G new radio, achieving virtually zero latency and supporting at least billions of connected devices. People have predicted that such improvements will bring the necessary changes in the spheres of healthcare, transport, manufacturing, and entertainment. Advanced applications such as autonomous systems, extended reality environment, holographic communication, wireless BCI and many others; will be enabled by the ultra-dense connectivity offered by the subsequently incremental generations of Networks; 6G in particular.

Nevertheless, as many countries start the process of 6G networks implementation, their protection from various threats became one of the pressing issues. 6G’s improved functionality and features as proposed above entail even more gains and risks of more complex cyber threats and abnormalities. These threats that are much more challenging and diverse compared to the previous generations of networks owe to the extremely high interconnectivity, multiple types of data, and innovative solutions such as AI-based decision-making and terahertz communication. Most of the anomaly detection methods widely implemented in 4G and even 5G networks are not sufficient for effectively solving the constraints that come with 6G environments. Such older methods are in many cases based on certain predetermined rules or pattern-matching patterns that are often unable to respond to new challenges coming with the constant evolution of cyber threats. For 6G applications9, the scale of the operations, the range of devices, and the forms of information exchange make such approaches insufficient. Limitations include low/high detection accuracy, inability to learn in real time to adapt to new threats, and high non-scalability to address the expected traffic loads in 6G environments. For instance, it will be expected that 6G network will require real-time processing and analysis of more than a billion sensors and devices detecting even slight anomalies in their activity associated with improved attacks.

Speaking of the major drawbacks entailed by the methods, the computational requirements are extremely high; thus, under 6G conditions, involving real-time processing and decision-making, these may just become prohibitive. Most of the current deep learning algorithms generalize poorly over such heterogeneous datasets, therefore leading to a loss of effectiveness and possible biases in the detection process. In addition, the architecture of the 6G network, having distributed edge computing nodes that require decentralized processing, implies lightweight and efficient anomaly detection techniques that cannot be performed by traditional deep learning frameworks. The unique challenges presented here call for equally innovative solutions to meet the challenge that advanced machine learning and artificial intelligence can handle while dealing with 6G network peculiarities in terms of scalability, efficiency, and accuracy. Overcoming these barriers, future anomaly detection systems will be able to guarantee the security and reliability of 6G infrastructures, hence promoting their wide adoption and realizing their transformative potential.

However, increased complexity in cybersecurity challenges comes along with such technological advance. Advanced and evolving cyber threats10 are waiting in the wings as these networks grow in scale and sophistication; hence, the strong anomaly detection framework that could protect critical infrastructures is really needed. In this paper, we propose a new hybrid deep learning-based model designed particularly for anomaly detection in 6G networks. The hybrid approach combines multiple deep learning architectures by fusing them in an optimization layer for better detection precision and a reduction in the problem of false alarms. This allows the model to dynamically adapt to high-dimensional heterogeneous data streams, typical in 6G environments, to ensure accurate and timely identification of malicious activities. By its very nature, the core of the proposed solution balances computational efficiency with scalability by embedding optimization techniques, hence it will be applicable in real-world high-throughput 6G networks.

For this reason, one significant success criterion is accurate dropout estimation for maintaining a resilient, fault-tolerant network; the existing techniques for estimation either oversimplify the network behaviors or are computationally expensive and not capable of real-time assessments. Another area is the largely unexplored aspect brought up by the large number of anomalies in dropout estimation models; hardly any framework caters for the two aspects together, brilliant as they are intertwined in network security and stability. Likewise, most nature-inspired optimizers suffer from premature convergence; therefore, fairly insignificant diversity in their search mechanisms reduces their effectiveness in solving complex optimization landscapes such as dropout rate prediction in dynamic networks. Thus, it leaves a big research gap in scalable, flexible, and integrated models that can tackle real-time anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation together, drawing the benefits of both deep learning and advanced optimization paradigms oriented toward 6G network environments.

The motivation behind this model is the unsuitability of existing anomaly detection techniques, which usually fail to handle the distinct challenges coming with 6G networks11,12. This work, therefore, serves not only as a contribution to securing next-generation wireless communication but also devises a scalable and efficient framework toward bridging the gap between enhanced network security and overall performance. This work therefore lays the foundation for secure and resilient communications in this era of connectivity, previously unprecedented, by tackling the imperative cybersecurity challenges of 6G networks.

Related works

In this regard, a great deal of research has been put into how to use machine learning and deep learning approaches to deal with such challenges. Previous solutions, over the past few years, have been successful for anomaly detection, intrusion prevention, and critical data protection in 4G and 5G. However, their applicability to 6G networks14 is challenged by new difficulties related to the unparalleled scale and complexity of these environments. Previous works on machine learning for 6G security investigated a few models and frameworks of anomaly detection and system resilience. Researchers develop signature-based methods, anomaly-based models, and hybrid techniques to find malicious activities sailing within network traffic. Where SVMs and random forests proved to be largely used in machine learning algorithms traditionally, their high-dimensional and dynamic datasets still give lower performance results for classical machine learning methods15. Still, these usually come at a high computational overhead cost, non-scalability, and poor adaptability to new and evolving threats, which are among the critical requirements of 6G networks.

Alsubai, et al.16 propose a robust framework with advanced optimization and deep learning techniques that are integrated toward increasing the accuracy scale of anomaly detection in 6G networks. The framework starts with data collection from various sources available within the 6G network. It assures a holistic assessment of all possible threats and has been carefully designed at its pre-processing phase to prepare data for further use with respect to its relevance and quality. The major strength of this method lies in using the TSO Algorithm for feature selection, which ensures that the most relevant features for anomaly indication will be identified, and thus reduces the computational overhead to result in better detection precision. The Multi-Scale Convolutional Auto Encoder can dramatically deepen the framework and hereby enable an effective extraction and classification of features. The MSCAE’s prowess in analyzing multi-scale features never fails to guarantee subtle understanding of the data, which is quite vital for finding subtle or complex anomalies inherent in the 6G environment. Further solidifying the applicability of the framework, the design of IDS in monitoring and classifying nodes producing anomalies guarantees real-time detection and response to threats. Altogether, the authors offer a comprehensive, creative integration of TSO with MSCAE to overcome the high dimensionality and dynamic characteristics of 6G networks.

Rzym, et al.17 introduce a well-crafted solution tailor-made for security in 6G software-defined networks (SDNs). In this solution, there are deep neural networks where anomaly detection is applied in network traffic; hence, the realization of a much stronger security framework. By harnessing the power of deep learning, it is capable of detecting and responding to new, unseen anomalies something very much needed in dynamic and complex 6G SDN environments. Hence, it can be realized that the authors focus on the development of security by intelligent and adaptive technologies that are able to scale with modern network infrastructures. This article addresses the automation of network monitoring with dynamic telemetry, a critical element in facing the unique challenges that SDNs pose. That is visionary; the approach is automation, realizing that it needs to go hand in hand with the rapid developments in 6G network architectures and the increasing volume of network traffic. In the final analysis, the authors present a comprehensive and innovative strategy for improving the security and responsiveness of 6G mobile networks. Jiao, et al.18 provide a well-argued and visionary overview of the critical role that deep learning will undoubtedly play in shaping the future of 6G networks. Starting with a perfectly framed vision for DL in 6G, this paper had clearly outlined the key areas in which DL can drive advancement in several major research directions, including adaptive resource allocation, intelligent network management, robust signal processing, ubiquitous edge intelligence, and endogenous security. The way this holistic approach demonstrates the potential of DL in dealing with the evolving demands and complexities of 6G networks establishes it as one of the cornerstones for future network operations. Given this, discussing how these different DL models, the Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), the Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), Graph Neural Networks (GNN), Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), transformer models, Federated Learning (FL), and Meta-Learning contribute to meeting complex 6G service demands is therefore very timely. The paper does an excellent job in explaining how such models, thanks to special learning abilities, are contributing to an increase in the functionality of networks with respect to resource allocation and enabling more intelligent decision-making within real-time network management.

The paper further elaborates the issues each of these models has to contend with in the 6G context, including questions of scalability, data privacy, computation overhead, and the requirement of real-time adaptation to changing conditions of a network. Although these issues have been outlined effectively by the authors, this section would be much deeper in showing how the presented challenges can, in reality, be softened with hybrid approaches and innovative ones under real-world deployment scenarios. Tarish, et al.19 provide a nice review of the challenges in advancing terahertz (THz) technologies of wireless sensor networks (WSNs), which is helpful in pointing out the big obstacles that truly impede this area’s progress. The paper will also discuss other challenges in implementing THz-based communication systems, since energy efficiency and loss minimization are critical for optimum WSN operation. The review focuses on the identification of these obstacles henceforth painting a clear picture of the complicated issues that need to be resolved for the successful integration of THz communication in WSNs. The authors also present ML strategies as the promising solutions to improve efficiency in WSNs, specifically in energy-loss reduction and optimization of energy consumption. The most important contribution, therefore, will be the ML models for increasing energy efficiency in WSNs, since it paves the path toward new ways that can be adapted considering the peculiarities of THz communication. Advanced techniques for the optimization of energy consumption while keeping network performance in mind are proposed, and that should yield extraordinary results.

Yin, et al.20 present a new anomaly detection scheme for 6G Internet of Everything (IoE) environments based on deep auto-encoders, capsule graph convolution networks, and the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA). The present scheme starts with capsule graph networks to generate pseudo-abnormal data by utilizing bottleneck features in input samples. The other core part of the method involves using a deep auto-encoder that tries to reconstruct, as much as possible, bottleneck characteristics into normal data, including those derived from the pseudo-abnormal data. The classification process of the network is further optimized using the Sparrow Search Algorithm for the optimal values of functions to ensure better anomaly classification performance. Integration by authors of multiple advanced techniques, such as capsule networks, deep auto-encoders, and SSA, hence manifesting a very sophisticated approach toward anomaly detection in the context of 6G IoE. In all, the approach is a new, multi-faceted solution for anomaly detection, which can potentially improve security in the fast-upcoming 6G IoE environments.

Shen, et al.21 find an interesting approach to anomaly detection in a 6G wireless cyber-physical system by integrating the LogFiT method with a Deep Convolutional Neural Network. It is in this regard that one of the very critical steps in the use of LogFiT for log data feature extraction is to extract such features, as this will allow the pointing out of which ones might have abnormal behaviors. Feature extraction by LogFiT is one rather giant step, considering a CPS in light of huge data volume and complexities. The integrated LogFiT-DCNM approach is then deployed for the detection and classification of anomalies in log entries after training the model on data. It puts a clear difference between the normal and abnormal data, which are necessary to secure the stability and security of the system. The presented method is creative; however, it requires more detailed discussion on false positives and model accuracy within real-time applications. Moreover, it would be recommendable to investigate the model’s scalability in large-scale, high-traffic 6G environments, especially because of the increased complexity of CPS systems. All in all, the authors have come up with a well-thought-out approach for anomaly detection in 6G wireless CPS that really is promising and could solve many security challenges arising in future networked environments. Nazar, et al.22 present a novel approach to identifying malicious behavior on Software-Defined Networking (SDN) switches that disrupts network performance. The proposed system will be responsible for the identification of misbehaving switches, which could be dropping packets or exchanging them as a result of a malicious intention and not because of some legitimate link failure. This distinction is of importance for anomaly detection accuracy because traditional methods generally fail to discriminate between network failures and attacks. The authors have been successful in bringing forth a signature-based detection model that makes use of predefined attack signatures to identify malicious behavior based on known patterns and abnormal activities.

This scheme provides a mechanism that is robust to detect such attacks, which may severely affect the stability and security of SDN-based networks. In this paper, the authors focus on exact attack signatures of malicious switches to improve the accuracy of the detection and assure a secure network environment. While this new signature-based approach does a good job in the detection of known attacks, this approach still requires more discussion of how it copes with novel or evolving attack strategies that do not yet have well-established signatures. Investigation into how the system fares in real network environments regarding scalability and adaptability to move to larger deployments of SDN would also be an interesting research task. The authors have succeeded in illustrating the critical need for identifying malicious switches in SDN systems, and they have proposed a very promising method to make the network even more secure. However, analysis of limitations and possible adaptation of the system against new threats would only strengthen the work proposed. In the paper23, a new pilot contamination signal detection approach for large massive MIMO is proposed with deep learning through the Generative Adversarial Network. This could bring promising results in that GANs had proved effective at the generation of valid-looking data samples from synthetic and the discrimination between genuine and synthetic signals, a fact that becomes essentially important to find out pilot contamination, a significant phenomenon ruining massive MIMO system performance. Applying GAN in such a context provides a much more sophisticated and dynamic approach for detecting contamination signals by reaping the benefits of the generator-discriminator architecture in terms of improved capability of identifying such harmful signals.

In the proposed scheme, GAN consists of two models: a generator which synthesizes the generated samples and a discriminator in charge of discriminating the difference between the actual signals and the generated ones by the model. In this paper, authors use residual block-based CNNs namely ResNet and reverse ResNet for discriminator models in GAN. The authors try to improve the detection accuracy of pilot-contaminated signals, otherwise hard to detect due to their similarity to legitimate signals, using this architecture. This performance is further improved by the integration of the reverse ResNet, which makes the network converge on minute details within the signal that could mean a better detection and classification of the contamination. This would therefore support the comparison between the results from the proposed GAN-based framework with already existing pilot contamination detection methods. The authors have come up with a very comprehensive and innovative solution for the problem of pilot contamination in massive MIMO systems, which may lead to enhancing the reliability and efficiency of such networks.

Prakash and Anitha24 propose a novel Robust Deep Regressive Neural Learning-based Intrusion Detection and Prevention model, RDRNL-IDP, to improve the accuracy of intrusion detection in cellular networks. The model is contrived in trying to get through the accurate discriminant between the normal and the attack behaviors by using dynamic, high-level features derived from the cellular node’s data under network environment. The input layer in this proposed model will act as a preliminary stage in processing the cellular node information coming into the network. The hidden layers of the model are designed to act as high-level feature extractors, which process the input data with a view toward predicting key node characteristics: energy consumption, packet loss, and trustworthiness. Based on these dynamic features, the model tries to improve its performances in attack nodes detection and classification with improved accuracy. The output layer carries out a robust regressive analysis contributing to the recognition and learning of the interrelations between the normal behaviors of nodes and possible attacks like Denial of Service (DoS), grayhole, or blackhole attacks.

The literature on anomaly detection systems for 6G networks outlines several outstanding challenges that are still prevalent today, despite rapid advances in technologies of deep learning, machine learning, and network security. A first line of research gaps lies at the inherent and inevitable complexity and scale of 6G environments, which promises to be several orders of magnitude more diverse and dynamic than previous generations like 4G and 5G. Most of the current models have failed to represent high-dimensional data streams, real-time adaptability, and scalability, which are very important for good detection and mitigation of anomalies in such complex settings. While most of the deep-learning-based models are powerful in some aspects, the computational requirements and demands of huge data processing in large numbers actually create roadblocks toward their real-life, practical applications, especially in the real-time realm. The literature on anomaly detection and intrusion prevention systems (IDS) for 6G networks underlines a few important challenges that still remain open despite recent progress in deep learning, machine learning, and network security technologies.

The most significant research gap in 6G environments is their inherent complexity and scale, which will be at least several times more diverse and dynamic than the previous generations, such as 4G and 5G. Existing models are often not able to handle the high-dimensional data streams, real-time adaptability, and scalability needed for the effective detection and mitigation of anomalies in such complex settings. In particular, such an explosion in connectivity, coupled with diverse network configurations, has really become a challenging task for the traditional anomaly detection techniques. Anomaly Detection Systems (AIDS) and the traditional Signature-Based Intrusion Detection Systems (SIDS) still suffer from high false alarm rates, slow response times, and a limited ability to generalize novel attack vectors. Furthermore, most of the deep learning-based models, powerful as they are in some contexts, usually suffer from high computational requirements and a demand for large-scale data processing, which seriously impede their practical application in real-time applications.

In 6G systems, where ultra-low latency and high throughput are indispensable for critical services such as holographic telepresence, eHealth, and autonomous systems, this is even more critical. Another gap of importance is the challenge in coming up with models that would fix not only the traditional security issues, such as DDoS attacks or traffic diversion, but also new and unprecedented threats created by integrating advanced technologies such as wireless brain-computer interfaces and networked robotics into the system25,26. Additionally, a lot of existing works tend to focus on some particular component of the anomaly detection system, like feature extraction, classification, or the detection algorithm without providing a complete holistic solution, which integrates all of these elements into a robust and scalable framework. One of the drawbacks from many models comes through the continuous adaption issue, which the 6G systems will encounter due to rapid changes in new threats.

While transition from 4th and 5th generation communication technology towards a more advanced sixth generation arrangement, the challenges in terms of anomaly traffic detection face fundamentally different and much more complex scenarios. While in the patterns of traffic in networks operating within 4G and 5G, these patterns are somewhat predictable and network architectures are moderately centralized, when it comes to the highly decentralized, ultra-dense, and intelligent architectures of the 6G networks involving thousands of heterogeneous devices-from nano-sensors to fully autonomous systems. Moreover, the emergence of the AI-native 6G systems, intelligent reflecting surfaces, and a new 3D networking scheme combined with the non-terrestrial platforms such as drones and satellites will grossly enlarge the attack surface and generate highly complicated multi-dimensional data distributions difficult to model. 6G will also have tighter latency and reliability requirements, meaning that anomaly detection must henceforth be real-time-compromise on accuracy, a lesser demand in 5G and almost negligible in 4G scenarios. Besides, self-evolving and self-optimizing capacities distinguish future 6G networks, hence anomaly detection systems must possess the capability of self-learning and generalizing for unknown threats even without frequent human intervention. This is sharply in contrast with 5G and 4G systems, where periodic model retraining and manual updates were often sufficient. Furthermore, privacy-preserving mechanisms-also called federated learning-which are about to become native parts of 6G, add layers of complexity to anomaly detection because they limit direct access to raw traffic data, thereby necessitating detection models to operate under constraints absent in former generations. Thus, contrary to 4G and 5G, anomaly detection in 6G requires much greater scalability, adaptability, computational efficiency, and learning incrementally from highly distributed privacy-constrained ultra-high-speed network environments.

In view of such pioneering set of issues, this paper exactly tries to solve the quest for a solution of high accuracy and real-time anomaly detection with dropout rate estimation in 6G networks using two innovative techniques designed to work synergically with each other. The conventional models of anomaly detection do not meet the demands posed by sequentiality high dimensionality and dynamicity of data produced by 6G environments, inability to adopt new attacks without retraining, being unable to scale, and also a lagging response time in cases critical for network resilience. At the same time, standard methodologies of dropout rate estimation, which are vital in doing prediction and mitigation of failures of node and links, still depend on simple statistical models or classical optimization algorithms that are not able to examine the tremendously complex non-linear behaviors embedded into 6G networks. Thus, this work introduces the Variational Autoencoder and Recurrent Transformer Network for 6G (VARNet-6G) for effective and on-the-fly anomaly detection and the Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer (FIERO) for adaptive dropout rate estimation. VARNet-6G is unique in that it brings variational autoencoders together as recurrent transformers in a way that they will properly model sequential dependencies and learn compact yet informative latent representations of traffic data to boost detection effectiveness, even for unseen sophisticated attack patterns. Also, FIERO is a new nature-inspired optimization framework to dynamically estimate dropout rates with an adjustment of high accuracy while improving robustness without provoking an excessive computational drain. Together, the hybridization of these two methods comes to a deep learning and optimized approach for deep-networks-specific high scalability, adaptability, and real-time responsiveness-the main challenges in the securing of 6G networks against the evolving anomaly and operational failure environments.

Proposed methodology

This paper proposes a framework for combining VARNet-6G with Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer (FIERO) to make improvements on the anomaly detection challenge in the Sixth-Generation networks, which will be very complex and dynamic. Another most critical concern is how the proposed system design assures to be resilient and effective while detecting subtle, rare, or even sophisticated anomalies; meanwhile, the solution is scalable and adaptive with regard to the 6G communication networks that will operate dynamically. The approach proposed guarantees optimal anomaly detection accuracy and reliability by considering innovative feature extraction, sequential pattern recognition, and optimized dropout rate estimation. At the core in this framework is the VARNet-6G, a framework that uses the strengths of both Variational Autoencoders and Recurrent Transformer Networks. Application of VAE performs dimension reduction because it holds a latent representation of high-dimensional network data, particularly important in 6G networks that boast incredibly high volume and complexity, which might choke conventional models. The Recurrent Transformer Network is a powerful model to extract long-term dependencies and temporal patterns from network traffic data. The combination of its mechanisms for self-attention with the abilities for sequential modeling makes it capable of finding anomalies that occur in time distributed denial-of-service attacks or advanced persistent threats, for instance. In VARNet-6G, the integration of VAE with Recurrent Transformer results in a robust anomaly detection model with an efficient feature extraction mechanism combined with subtle sequential analysis. Overview of the proposed 6G communication network security framework.

The complete flowchart shown in Fig. 1 provides a description of the overall framework set out for ensuring security of 6G communication networks with an integrated anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation scheme. The operation starts with Network Data Collection, where traffic logs, signal patterns, and device states have been accumulated from the 6G environment to constitute a raw input for the subsequent stages. This data, thus collected, would be subjected to Data Preprocessing using methods like normalization to handle variable input scales and feature extraction to obtain only the relevant patterns. The data thereafter passes on for the Features Extraction and Compression phase: First, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses the high-dimensional network features into a lower-dimensional latent space that preserves its essential structure and variability, thus offloading huge computational burdens. The compressed features are plugged into the Temporal Dependencies Analysis block, where a Recurrent Transformer Network (RTN) deciphers the sequential and temporal dependencies that correspond to the network behavior across time. In the secondary stage, the outputs occlude from the RTN after refining through the Attention Mechanism. Thus the input system has been simulated to selectively attend to the most salients of the temporal sequences in filtering out all irrelevant or even noisy information for improvement in detection accuracy. On the other hand, the FIERO Model executes Dropout Rate Optimization, most of which incorporates a hybrid nature-inspired optimization scheme to dynamic estimations of optimum dropout rates geared towards improving the generalization feature of the model and robustifying it. In anomaly detection, the output with compressed features integrating temporal dependencies and optimized dropout value is forwarded into the module, whereby anomalies are detected based on the reconstruction errors and extracted sequential features. Lastly, they are classed and treated within the Threat Identification and Adaptive Response module for enabling real-time intelligent countermeasures against advanced anomalous activities. Such a clear modular approach guarantees that every stage contributes to enhancing the security, scalability, and robustness of 6G communication networks.

This work, therefore, introduces the Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer (FIERO) for the estimation of the dropout rate, hence making the anomaly detection model more robust and generalized. This helps the model avoid being overfitted with certain patterns coming up in the training data. Fiero is a method that combines the ideas of the water cycle algorithm and flamingo swarm optimization to adapt the dropout rate in a way that it may become dynamic based on the characteristics of the network traffic data. Hybrid optimization ensures that a balance between exploration and exploitation is reached, resulting in an optimal dropout strategy able to improve generalizability for both normal and anomalous data. This is the first attempt to integrate the above advanced techniques VAE for dimension reduction, Recurrent Transformer Network for temporal anomaly detection, and FIERO for efficient dropout rate estimation into a coherent framework specifically tailored for 6G networks. The proposed VARNet-6G with FIERO is designed explicitly to handle the features of high data volume, velocity, and variability that characterize 6G communications, while traditional approaches usually fail to meet either scalability or adaptability when dealing with dynamic network conditions. The new combination will cause the model to detect anomalies correctly, in addition to adopting a mechanism of flexibility toward the rapidly evolving threats thus rendering full future-proof solutions for anomaly detection in 6G networks.

The framework proposed here addresses challenges that, in a nutshell, will be critical to solve with traditional methods in 6G communication networks where not only vast data flows are envisioned but also highly diverse and dynamic. This way, VAE reduces the dimensionality of these complex data streams to have the model focused on those features that most characterize anomalies. This is complemented with the Recurrent Transformer Network to analyze temporal dependencies and patterns that might lead to anomaly detection that gradually emerges over time. These are complemented by FIERO, thus making sure that the model’s dropout rate is dynamically adjusted in such a way as to maximize its generalization capability and therefore be robust against both overfitting and underfitting. Hyperparameter settings are the crucial propellers of the performance, stability, and generalization of the proposed VARNet-6G and FIERO models. In the VARNet-6G framework, hyperparameters such as latent space dimensionality of the variational autoencoder, number of transformer layers, size of attention heads, learning rate, dropout rate, and batch size are significant in determining how well the model captures the sequential dependencies and hidden patterns in the area of complex network traffic. Likewise, nontrivial balancing needs to be performed for both the number of transformer layers and heads; deeper architecture coupled with more heads is good for capturing more complex temporal relations but suffers from high training complexity and the vanishing gradient effect if regularization is neglected. The learning rate is of utmost importance for convergence; too high a value can make training unstable, while too low a value will lead to prolonged convergence and the likelihood of getting stuck in a locally optimal minimum. In FIERO, hyperparameters impacting optimally escaping local minima and accurately inferring dropout rates across a range of network conditions include uptake coefficient, flamingo movement dynamics, and search-space adaptation rate. Should the hyperparameter setting be inappropriately tuned, then there is a chance that FIERO will either cease operation before it properly optimizes the dropout rates or, conversely, abandon excellent dropout rates. In both cases, the network will become increasingly untenable to anomalies. Therefore, achieving precise hyperparameter settings along with adaptive fine-tuning strategies is considered a requisite for ensuring reliable and effective performances of both VARNet-6G and FIERO in this fast-changing and complex 6G environment.

The paper aims to present a fresh paradigm in addressing the urgent needs of anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation in 6G networks through various intermediate novelty and unique contributions represented by VARNet-6G and the FIERO model. While the classical approaches have treated anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation as independent problems, this work attempts to bring those together into an integrated framework in enhancing both security and resilience of the network. Architecturally, VARNet-6G is itself quite interesting in that it combines the probabilistic learning power of variational autoencoders with the sequence-modeling power of recurrent transformers so as to allow the system to learn compact yet highly informative latent representations of network traffic while still preserving the temporal dependencies needed for real-time anomaly detection. Such a hybrid design ensures that conventional deep learning models are not hemmed-in by their limitations, which collapse when subjected to the high speed and high variable conditions typical of the 6G. On the contrary, with respect to optimization, using the Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer (FIERO) significantly challenges the path of standard optimization schemes inspired from nature, and instead takes a novel path to modeling flamingos’ movement dynamics and evaporation-based adaptation strategies to offer an intelligent dynamic mechanism with extremely high precision and adaptability for dropout rates estimation. Moreover, new in this paper is the proposal of feedback on the dropout rates optimized by FIERO possibly participating in the regularization of the VARNet-6G model, enabling it to be tuned adaptively to the network conditions without the need for any kind of external intervention.

For both VARNet-6G and FIERO, a careful and systematic process was used to select and tune hyperparameters to optimize the trade-off between model regularization, convergence speed, and accuracy. Hyperparameters in VARNet-6G- learning rate, batch size, dropout rate, and number of layers-were initially gleaned from previously shown effective values for a typical sequence model, then extensively fine-tuned using the grid search and random search strategies. The values for the batch sizes were experimented using: {32, 64, 128} which finally settled at 64 in a compromise between hardware memory capacity and stable gradient updates. Critically varied dropout rates were within 0.1 to 0.5, with an optimal value of 0.3 settled for preventing overfitting without retarding model learning. For the architecture, the VAE part was designed with 3 hiddens in both encoder and decoder, while the recurrent transformer is designed with 4 attention heads and 2 stacked layers, which are sufficient to capture the temporal dependency without introducing that much complexity. FIERO optimizer tuning involved tuning of significant parameters such as the evaporation coefficient and flamingo swarm movement radius. The evaporation rate has been varied between and included 0.05 and 0.25. The value of 0.1 was selected as an ideal compromise between exploration and speed of convergence. The flamingo swarm exploration radius setting has been finally adjusted to an extent of 1.5 to make sure that it is covering the optimal solution space adequately but does not shatter. Tuning of hyperparameter optimization was mainly guided with cross-validation runs and early stopping to avoid overfitting.

As shown in Fig. 3, another innovation of VARNet-6G is that it is not really a pre-trained module treatment of VAE and RTN, but it is modeled for joint optimization of both. During backpropagation, the gradients are propagated through both VAE and RTN simultaneously, enabling the whole architecture to learn coherent latent representations that would be generative and discriminative for anomaly detection. Enabling the VAEs’ optimized latent space to be good at reconstruction would make it very useful for temporal anomaly classification problems poised by the RTN, creating a seamless but highly efficient model capable of achieving superior detection accuracies but in less time for response. Such a holistic training process would help VARNet-6G to outshine other traditional modular pipelines, where a VAE and recurrent networks are optimized and trained in an isolated manner.

The Transformer-based adaptation mechanism in VARNet-6G makes selective decisions as to which most pertinent aspects of network behavior may be given attention while shielding away irrelevant noise, ultimately to improve the model in predicting anomalies. Flamingo-Infused Evaporation Rate Optimizer, in short, FIERO, is the new hybrid optimization algorithm designed particularly for the purpose of optimizing the estimation of dropout rates in machine learning models. Dropout rate refers to one of the regularization techniques that is relevant in deep learning for preventing overfitting and increasing the generalizability of models. However, finding the optimal dropout rate is very difficult because an optimal tradeoff between underfitting and overfitting has to be found. The main aim of FIERO is to compute this rate effectively by harnessing the strengths of two powerful optimization methods: the Evaporation Rate Water Cycle Optimization (ER-WCO) and Flamingo Swarm Optimization (FSO). Combining these techniques, FIERO brings a robust and efficient solution in optimizing dropout rate for deep learning models, mainly when the traditional tuning methods do not work well. ER-WCO, by modeling the evaporation process of the water cycle, provides a basis for the enhancement of solution accuracy over time, and FSO mimics the social behavior of flamingos searching for food in an efficient manner to find and exploit the search space. Such synergism empowers FIERO to harvest both exploration and exploitation to get the optimal dropout rate at which the model generalizes while maintaining high performance.

What is new in FIERO is that it hybridizes two very different optimization approaches, each bringing in complementary strengths. ER-WCO is used because it inherently simulates gradual evaporation and therefore fine-tunes the optimization process in a way that properly balances exploration of new solutions with exploitation of promising regions in the search space. This evaporation-inspired process is very efficient for the task of continuous optimization, in which solution needs to be gradually refined. On the other hand, Flamingo Swarm Optimization comes with its own biological inspiration it models the social behavior of flamingos while they forage. Such an evaporation mechanism in ER-WCO will allow the dropout rate to fine-tune this incremental approach, while flamingo-inspired exploration in FSO avoids getting the optimizer stuck into suboptimal local optima. Balancing these two mechanisms, FIERO is capable of searching over a wide range of dropout rates to choose the best one that balances regularization and model accuracy, ensuring the model is generalized enough to not be able to learn the key patterns in the data. The workflow of the proposed FIERO model is given in Fig. 6.

The FIERO model is a hybrid optimization model that uniquely combines Evaporation Rate-based Water Cycle Optimization with Flamingo Swarm Optimization for dropout rate estimation in 6G Network Models for network resilience and adaptive learning in dynamic traffic environments. ER-WCO is based on a natural water cycle process where streams (candidate solutions) flow into rivers and then converge into the sea (optimal solution) through evaporation and precipitation cycles. The exploration phase in FIERO is modelled using ER-WCO: Initially, candidate dropout rates are uniformly spread across the search space; based on fitness, evaporation rates for each candidate are assessed. Highly performative candidates are to evaporate less, thus retaining a relative strong influence in their guidance of such search. Good streams have less fitness and evaporate faster, creating conditions under which new solutions may be born in well-performing areas of the search space. This has the advantage of diversity at the beginning stages of the search coupled with gradual convergence to optimal dropout rate regions. FSO serves to depict candidate solutions as flamingos whose bodies change positioning according to the collective best and well-specified location-specific best solutions.

Benefits brought by FIERO are manifold and explicitly contribute to better performance and robustness of the deep learning models. By itself, a hybrid algorithm makes possible a much deeper search of the solution space than what is possible by traditional single-method optimization approaches. Combining ER-WCO with FSO would provide better exploration and exploitation capacities important capabilities in escaping local optima for finding a globally optimal dropout rate. Second, by letting the dropout rate be iteratively tuned, FIERO enhances the quality of estimation because it can make this rate adjustable in a gradual and nuanced way. Thirdly, the self-tuning nature of the Fiero Algorithm, by using adaptive capabilities brought together from evaporation and the flamingo swarm optimization components, makes it very suitable for a wide range of datasets and any model architectures; hence, it would prove to exhibit good adaptability within the dynamics that machine-learning models portray in themselves, hence quickly able to change the drop-out rate over most of the tasks. Fourth, FIERO does not use any presetting labeled data but optimizes based on model performance; thus, they are very flexible under different applications. This unsupervised nature of the algorithm enhances its generality even further, especially in the real world when labeled data used for hyperparameter tuning are in short supply.

Among the important challenges within the 6G networks is how to deal with vast dynamic, high-dimensional data prone to anomalies, including unauthorized access, DoS/DDoS attacks, and many other network threats. Efficient detection of such anomalies calls for highly adaptable models, which can address not only the volume but also the temporal and sequential nature of the data. That is the major contribution of FIERO to anomaly detection in 6G networks: it optimizes precisely the dropout rate in deep learning classifiers that are central to ensuring good generalization over normal and anomalous data distributions. Using FIERO, the classifier easily achieves a balance between learning from past network data and being flexible enough to generalize well to new, previously unseen anomalous behaviors. This balance is of the highest importance in dealing with a dynamic and unpredictable nature of network threats, which could be of entirely different types, magnitudes, and frequencies. Moreover, FIERO contributes to the classifier’s ability in real-time adaptation toward dynamic changes that are emblematic in 6G network traffic. Applications envisaged for 6G range from ultra-reliable low-latency communications to massive machine-type communications, each with their peculiar traffic patterns. Optimization of the dropout rate via FIERO will keep the classifier sensitive to this kind of change by dynamically adapting to new conditions in the traffic.

Moreover, the classifier also becomes robust to noise and irrelevant features in network data with FIERO. The huge, heterogeneous streams of data generated by 6G networks are always prone to enormous noise and irrelevant information that could pollute the process of anomaly detection. This problem is somewhat sidetracked by optimization of the dropout rate in FIERO, where selective learning is encouraged: the model is thus forced to concentrate on the most relevant and important features, while less important or noisy features are given less attention. Hence, the classifier will become more able to detect anomalies with high precision, even on very irregular or uncertain data, and hence the whole anomaly detection system becomes much more effective.

It is a very rich real-world IoT traffic dataset that will be used to test the robustness and adaptability of the IDS designed for 5G networks. On the other side, UNSW-NB15 is one of the most commonly used datasets in the cybersecurity domain and can be more precisely defined for network traffic analysis in the search for security threats in IoT and 5G environments. Diversified attack scenarios have equipped this dataset with backdoor, DoS, and reconnaissance attacks and benign traffic. Especially in the evaluation of performance, this can be very vital in machine learning-based models tackling attack classification, anomaly detection, and security event prediction in IoT networks. The 5G-NIDD dataset contains about 2 million network traffic samples, with about 1.2 million samples labeled as benign traffic, while 800,000 samples are related to different attack types such as DDoS, infiltration, data exfiltration, and botnet behaviors. Feature selection was based on an empirical study where the most significant attributes, which could be used for the classification between benign and malignant traffic, were identified through mutual information scores and correlation analysis to filter out redundant features.

By this selection, the models are trained and validated on real-world heterogeneous traffic data, which leads to robust and generalizable performance across future communication infrastructure.

In VARNet-6G, each path consisting of three dense layers with ReLU activations, a latent space dimension of 64 for compressing high-dimensional traffic features while still retaining essential information. The RTN component integrated two layers of GRU units, having 128 hidden units each, followed by multi-head self-attention layers with 8 heads and key/query dimension 64. The learning rate in VARNet-6G was tuned empirically and set at 5E-4 with the Adam optimizer, while batch size was cross-validated and set at 128 for better convergence speed against memory occupation. The dropout rate for transformer layers was optimized to 0.3 by means of ablation studies providing good regularization without compromising accuracy. The combination of loss functions was used for training: a reconstruction loss (Mean Squared Error) and Kullback-Leibler divergence loss with equal weighting. This ensured the maximization of feature learning and latent space regularization at the same time.

For FIERO, this optimization method combines Evaporation Rate Water Cycle Optimization (ER-WCO) and Flamingo Swarm Optimization (FSO) algorithms, whereby ER-WCO, with evaporation constant equal to 0.02, initially guided the global exploration process, and water content maintained diversity, while FSO exploits a perfectly fine-tuned group behavior (flocking coefficients set at 1.2) engaging the random walk phase (random walk could be defined in this case by the standard deviation of 0.05). The number of optimization agents (droplets or flamingos) was set to 30 to ensure reasonable computation time convergence. The maximum runtime for optimization was set to be equal to 100 iterations, for it is justified after convergence analysis that a trade-off was to be made between speed and quality of optimization. The hyperparameter values for both algorithms were determined through grid searching experiments and sensitivity analysis so as to maximize precision and minimize error rates in dropout rate prediction. The achieved settings guarantee a robust convergence behavior without overfitting on attack scenarios and network traffic patterns. In all, none of the configurations was randomly selected or imperfectly tuned; rather, they were all systematically and reasonably tuned based on empirical validation through grid search and ablation experiments in order to achieve optimal and robust performances for anomaly detection and dropout estimation under 6G network conditions.

This was a study comparison trial of the efficacy of the hybrid deep learning appropriated by the proposed VARNet-6G along with the FIERO optimization framework for a variety of deep learning methods. It turned out that virtually all other methods were less efficient in training time, inference time, and resource consumption. Specifically, the time taken for training the VARNet-6G is reduced due to the lightweight nature of the VAE compression phase, coupled with the optimized recurrent-transformer hybrid architecture, taking about 7 s for complete convergence of the model under the given dataset conditions-well, this is very short compared to conventional LSTM, GRU only, or CNN based models, taking anywhere from 15 to 25 s on similar hardware. Inference time for anomaly detection with VARNet-6G is also low, as it detects anomalies quite well within milliseconds of input-wise response latencies, in fierce contrast to traditional deep recurrent models that have had speedbreak complications with sequential processing delays. On the resource front, VARNet-6G has moderate memory consumption (averaging GPU footprint of 2.1GB during training), while the larger transformer-only or stacked deep RNN models overshoot boundaries of 3-4GB. The CPU efficiently utilizes computation because VAE compressions reduce the lengths of the sequences and the attention mechanisms keep resource usage depressed for real-time deployment on midrange server architectures. With these comparative advantages, the model is supposed to offer a reasonable compromise between performance accuracy and computational efficiency at a high level of practicality in this 6G security context, where rapid detection and minimal overhead are critically needed.

Moderate sensitivity has been observed in VARNet-6G with reference to hyperparameters especially for learning rate, batch size, number of transformer heads, and VAE latent dimensions. The learning-rate settings can make the model either very convergent or extremely overshooting away from optimal weights while large excessive batch sizes wash out temporal dependencies captured by the Recurrent Transformer Network. To tackle and control this sensitivity, it is a structured hyperparameter tuning approach, a comprehensive mix of grid search and Bayesian optimization across an intended search space. An optimal learning rate is empirically taken as 0.0003 between convergence stability and training speed. A batch size of 64 was configured for retention of sequence granularity and not crowded into GPU memory. An irreducible dimension in the latent space of the VAE was kept at 128-the dimension that offered compression without the loss of features clearly capturing the anomaly-related features. The RTN used 2 layers of GRUs with multi-head attention (4 heads) to yield the model outcome capturing complex temporal behaviors. The dropout was tuned to 0.2 not to lose important signal features but still regularize the network. As more practical advice for implementation, it is best to start off with these baseline values and refashion according to dataset scale and system constraints, where validation loss and f1 score would be the main selection metrics.

While normalization, feature extraction, and noise filtering were some of the preprocessing steps employed to the greatest extent possible on both the 5G-NIDD and UNSW-NB15 IoT 5G Security datasets, small amounts of noise and inaccuracies found in real-world network data could still remain. Such minor imperfections, including mislabeled samples or slight changes to signal patterns, could inadvertently impose their micro-skew on the model’s learning. Nonetheless, the overall robustness of the VARNet-6G framework, especially with respect to the VAE-based feature compression and attention-driven anomaly detection, helped counter that effect and ensured that overall model performance was, of course, very reliable and generalizable.

In the context of anomaly detection in 6G networks, the evaluation metric selection, namely accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and error rate, is determined by two important motives-the measurement of the effectiveness of detection as well as the dependability of the system in a highly dynamic and heterogeneous traffic condition. Accuracy is a general measure of how many total predictions are right, which is important to give a sense of the model’s overall effectiveness, especially under the load with many of network data. However, here accuracy would be misleading since, anomaly detection is often performed over highly imbalanced datasets with a very much larger proportion of benign traffic compared to malicious traffic. This is where precision comes in, since it states what proportion of positive predictions were actually true positives, ensuring that anomalies with respect to these predictions are actual threats, thus directly reducing false alarm and contributing to the trustworthiness of real-time decision systems. Define recall, and on the other hand, it helps measure how many of the actual anomalies are identified by the model which is crucial for security applications wherein it would be disastrous if a real attack were not detected. As precision and recall are independent of each other and not necessarily directly dependent on a single threshold value, the F1-score can be understood as their harmonic mean and is particularly valuable in cases where the use of either metric alone could be biased. Last is the error rate, which every bit amply matters since that directly is the model’s failure regarding misclassification of attack and benign data, which lets the developer know how many times the system might go wrong in reality. All these metrics cover the range of performance requirements for 6G anomaly detection-faultless classification, reduced false alarms, robust attack detection, balanced performance, and minimized misclassification-which will ensure the real-time, scalable, and secure structure of next-gen communication frameworks in the security event network intrusion detection system.

The approaches that have been proposed combine deep generative modeling with a nature-inspired optimization technique; consequently, such methodologies become more accurate, scalable, and adaptable than any others. Results of quantitative optimization further testify to the efficiency with which the proposed methods perform: a VARNet-6G model yielded a very minimal error rate of only 0.0032, demonstrating strong generalizing capacity and robustness to both known and unseen forms of anomalies. It took only 7 s to execute the proposed model for performance purposes; therefore, the proposed model can be utilized for real-time or almost near real-time deployment purposes, which is a major requirement within 6G environments. The model attained the highest 0.9993 precision score, far away surpassing existing deep learning and anomaly detection techniques. Even with highly competitive evaluation settings, the proposed model consistently got a second highest ranking in other critical performance metrics of value 0.9993, signifying its overall balance on the three areas of precision, recall, and F1-score. Collectively, these results testify to the fact that the VARNet-6G and FIERO models tackle existing research gaps on adaptivity, scalability, and speed, and present a viable high-performance solution for protecting the integrity and reliability of future 6G communication networks.

Discussions

There are many potential future application considerations of the proposed VARNet-6G and FIERO framework, beyond their shown promise for efficient anomaly detection and dropout rate estimation in 6G networks; the observational analysis of such aspects should duly consider existing opportunities and present technological limitations. By whichever projection these new plots are expected to enlarge their domain of usage for autonomous vehicular networks, smart healthcare systems, industrial IoT infrastructures, and next-gen UAV communications, these domains will call for time-sensitive anomaly detection for safe and efficient operation. Furthermore, with further edge computing and federated learning improvements, moving sparse instances of VARNet-6G and FIERO to the network edge would allow decentralized near-real-time security monitoring without causing too much strain on central servers. Nonetheless, one must appreciate the existing technological challenges involving computational demands for training deep recurrent-transformer hybrids, generalizing the models across the wide dimension of diverse 6G use cases, and lack of large-scale publicly available high-fidelity datasets from 6G networks, where classical benchmarking of such systems would occur. Furthermore, since the 6G environment is also characterized by unpredictability and hostility—for instance, ultra-low latency requirements, device heterogeneity, and ever-evolving unknown types of attacks—they are to be continuously adapted and retrained to not cripple the already-resource-constrained environment. Thus, while well above avenues sufficiently lay a foundation for securing next-generation 6G ecosystems, further need to set the path for lightweight model design, continual learning, cross-domain generalization, and robust evaluation methodologies toward achieving real application viability in practical large-scale deployments.

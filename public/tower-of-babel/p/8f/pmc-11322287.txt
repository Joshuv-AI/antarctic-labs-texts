Phytochemical screening, antioxidant, anti-diabetic, and anti-obesity activities, formulation, and characterization of a self-nanoemulsion system loaded with pomegranate (Punica granatum) seed oil
Authors: Ahmad M. Eid, Linda Issa, Khalid Arar, Ahmad Abu-Zant, Mohammad Makhloof, Yazan Masarweh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Pomegranate (Punica granatum) is a tree of the Punicaceae family that is widespread all over the world and has several types and therapeutic uses. The current study aimed to investigate the phytochemical compounds by GC analysis and carried out physical characterization of the pomegranate seed oil and its self-nanoemulsifying system. Then antioxidant, anti-diabetic, and anti-lipase activities were investigated for both.The pomegranate seed oil was extracted, and its self-nanoemulsifying system was then prepared. Phytochemical compounds were analyzed by GC, and physical characterization was established of the pomegranate seed oil and its self-nanoemulsifying system. Then antioxidant, anti-diabetic, and anti-lipase activities were investigated for both.The GC–MS analysis revealed that punicic acid, β-eleosteric acid, catalpic acid, α-eleosteric acid, and oleic acid were the most predominant compounds in pomegranate seed oil. Other active compounds like linoleic acid, palmitic acid, stearic acid, and α-linolenic acid were detected in trace percentages. The self-nanoemulsifying system was prepared using various concentrations of surfactant (Tween 80), co-surfactant (Span 80), and pomegranate seed oil. The selected formulation had a PDI of 0.229 ± 0.09 and a droplet size of 189.44 ± 2.1 nm. The free radical scavenging activity of pomegranate seed oil, the self-emulsifying system, and Trolox was conducted using DPPH. The oil-self-nanoemulsifying system showed potent antioxidant activity compared to Trolox. Also, pomegranate oil inhibited α-amylase with a weak IC50 value of 354.81 ± 2.3 µg/ml. The oil self-nanoemulsifying system showed potent activity compared to acarbose and had a weaker IC50 value (616.59 ± 2.1 µg/ml) and a potent IC50 value (43.65 ± 1.9 µg/ml) compared to orlistat.Pomegranate seed oil self-nanoemulsifying system could be applied in the future for the preparation of possible oral medications for the prevention and treatment of oxidative stress, diabetes, and obesity due to its high activity against free radical, amylase, and lipase enzymes compared to pomegranate seed oil itself and the references used. This study reveals that self-nanoemulsion systems can enhance oil drug formulations by improving pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, acting as drug reservoirs, and facilitating efficient oil release.

Introduction

Knowledge of medicinal properties was passed down through generations through word of mouth, with many plants recorded in the early years being used to treat various pathological conditions. Palestine offers a wealth of ancient herbal treatments that have been used for generations to address a wide range of health conditions. Although these therapies are often used, their safety and effectiveness have not been comprehensively assessed. Conducting a biological assessment of traditional herbal treatments is crucial in order to ascertain their potential as valuable sources of new medicinal substances1–3. Secondary plant metabolites, which are natural products, have been re-established as a significant source of drug discovery as a result of recent advancements in screening technologies4,5. Recently, it has been stated that natural products are considered a significant source of leads for drug research. More than 100 new natural pharmaceuticals, including medicines for fighting infections and cancer, are now being developed in clinical trials6. Novel natural compounds are becoming more accessible due to molecular biology techniques, and screening libraries that resemble drugs are being produced via combinatorial chemical methods. To enhance the drug discovery process, a range of screening methodologies, including data mining and virtual screening techniques, are being used on natural product databases7.

Pomegranate seed oil is derived from the seeds of Punica granatum fruits in the Punicaceae family. It is a highly sought-after and luxurious product in skin care and cosmetics. It is a rare source of punicic acid, a powerful plant-based antioxidant, and is the only known botanical form of conjugated linolenic acid. Its high resistance to oxidation makes it a desirable component in the personal care and cosmetic industries8,9.

After investigation, the composition of pomegranate seed oil revealed a high quantity of phytosterols, tocopherols, and a distinct fatty acid composition, with punicic acid making up the majority (55%). Referred to as trichosanic acid, punicic acid is a polyunsaturated fatty acid with an omega-5 chain. It is an isomer of conjugated α-linolenic acid10,11.

Also Pomegranate fruit (Punica granatum) seed oil was described as a thin, skin-moisturizing oil that is emollient. As a humectant, it helps dry, parched, and lifeless skin by holding onto moisture and nourishing it. Because of its high vitamin E content, it keeps skin cells safe from oxidative damage, giving them a youthful appearance. Pomegranate seed oil's anti-aging properties are attributed to its abundance of vitamins, minerals, and essential fatty acids. These qualities lessen visible aging signs, shield against free radicals, and treat acne-prone skin12–14.

Diabetes, particularly Type 2 diabetes mellitus, is a global health concern. Nutraceuticals like punicic acid, found in pomegranate seed oil, have been investigated for potential therapeutic effects in diabetes. Punicic acid has no side effects and can reduce inflammatory cytokines, modulate glucose homeostasis, and possess antioxidant properties15. Obesity is a global issue caused by an imbalance between energy intake and expenditure, leading to excessive adipose tissue expansion and metabolic alterations. This, along with low-grade inflammation, can cause insulin resistance, causing b-cell dysfunction. Obesity is the main factor contributing to metabolic syndrome, increasing the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases16,17.

Recent studies have shown that Punica granatum seed oil consumption can reduce weight gain and type 2 diabetes risks in CD-1 (common docile) mice with diet-induced obesity, improve glucose tolerance, and suppress obesity-related inflammation. It has been found to inhibit TNF-α (Tumor necrosis factor alpha)-induced neutrophil hyperactivation, protect against colonic inflammation, and ameliorate inflammation-induced colorectal cancer18. A previous study found that pomegranate seed oil has potential for photoprotection due to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. The oil's topical distribution via nanoemulsions proved to be a promising delivery method19. Another study examined the gelling ability of a mixture prepared from pomegranate seed oil, propolis wax, and beeswax. Samples were prepared with varying wax concentrations. The chemical characterization, thermal, and structural properties of the wax-based organogels were examined20.

Nanotechnology focuses on designing, analysing, and implementing structures, devices, and systems by controlling their shapes and sizes at the nanometer scale, incorporating nanostructures, nanocomposites, and nanoparticle aggregates21,22. Natural bioactive materials and novel technologies like self-nanoemulsifying systems are increasingly being used as therapeutic agents in the pharmaceutical industry23,24.

Self-emulsifying formulations have become the preferred method for formulating nutraceuticals due to several advantages. These include their ease of large-scale production, higher loading capacity, improved oral bioavailability, better dissolution behavior of poorly water-soluble nutraceuticals, and their commercial potential25. Self-emulsifying technology are employed to overcome the issue of limited oral bioavailability of medications that exhibit poor solubility in water. The size of droplets has a critical role in influencing the release and absorption of drugs. Pharmaceutical formulations depend on the process of spontaneous emulsification, which is triggered by a favorable shift in entropy that promotes dispersion. The process of creating self-emulsifying formulations include the blending of oils and surfactants, which in turn impacts the concentration of surfactants and the hydrophilic/lipophilic balance26,27.

The objective of this study is to analyze the chemical components of P. granatum seed oil and assess its antioxidant, antidiabetic, and anti-obesity properties. These properties will be compared to those of the novel P. granatum seed oil self-nanoemulsifying system. Consequently, it will offer knowledge for further work. By doing more study and investigation into P. granatum and its possible medicinal characteristics, there is an expectation that this plant might contribute to the creation of formulations that have practical use in treating various diseases, finally resulting in future clinical advantages.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was purchased from Riedel De Haen, Germany. Also from the Al-Shams Company we purchased Tween 80 and Span 80. From Sigma Aldrich, Denmark, we received trolox ((s)-(-)-6 hydroxy-2, 5, 7, 8-tetramethychroman-2-carboxylic acid), and from Sigma Aldrich, Germany, we obtained (DPPH) 2,2-Diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl.

Plant material extraction was done using HPLC-grade solvents. MP Bio chemicals (Illkirch, France) supplied pig pancreatic amylase, whereas Sigma-Aldrich (USA) supplied acarbose, orlistat, starch, PNPP (p-nitrophenyl palmitate), and porcine pancreatic lipase. The experiments used only materials of analytical quality.

Plant material preparation for pomegranate seeds oil extraction

Following a previous scientific protocol28. To extract the seeds, the pomegranate fruits were collected from different regions in the West Bank of Palestine. For approximately 20 days, the seeds were dried in the shade at a temperature of 25 ± 3 °C and a humidity of 55 ± 4 RH and grind into a fine powder. Then 50 g of the grinded seeds were weighed before being macerated in a solution of 250 mL pure hexane, a nonpolar organic solvent, and 250 mL 70% ethanol solution, a polar solvent. The macerated fruit was left for 72 h in a shaking bath at room temperature. A separatory funnel was then used to divide the extract into two layers, and the solvent was then removed under air pressure to concentrate the organic layer.

Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC–MS) for the chemical analysis of pomegranate seed oil.

The GC–MS technique was used to analyze the fatty acid methyl ester (FAME) of pomegranate seed oil, which was prepared by weighing 100 mg of pomegranate seed oil with 10 ml of a 0.5 M methanolic sodium hydroxide solution. The mixture was then heated under reflux for 10 min on a heating mantel for complete hydrolysis, followed by the addition of 10 ml of 14% boron-trifluoride (BF3) solution in methanol and then refluxing for 10 min. After the esterification, 50 ml of heptane was added, and the boiling was continued for 10 min more. The reaction mixture was cooled.

The FAME of pomegranate seed oil in the heptan layer was examined by a Perkin Elmer Clarus 500 gas chromatograph with a Perkin Elmer Clarus 560 mass spectrometer. SLB TM-5 ms fused-silica capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm, film thickness 0.25 µm). Ultra-high-purity helium (99.999%) from air products was used as carrier gas at a constant flow of 1 ml/min, and the injection port temperature was kept at 290 °C. The injected sample volume was 1 µl with a split ratio of 50:1 and a gas flow of 1 ml/min. The oven temperature program was 100 °C (hold for 4 min) and accelerated at a rate of 3 °C/min to 240 °C (hold for 15 min). Solvent Delay: 0–8 min. All data were obtained by collecting the full-scan mass spectra within the scan range of 50–500 atomic mass units (amu). The ionizing energy was 70 eV. Electron multiplier (EM) voltage was obtained from autotune. The mass spectra of the chemical components of the P. granatum seed oil were compared to the reference spectra from the National Institute of Standards and Technology's MS Data Center. Additionally, their Kovats and retention indices were matched to values published in the literature29–31.

Preparation of pomegranate seed oil self-nanoemulsifying system

The self-nanoemulsifying pomegranate seed oil system was established by screening different self-nanoemulsifying formulations containing different concentrations of surfactant (Tween 80), co-surfactant (Span 80), and pomegranate oil in order to find the optimum formulation. Each formulation was prepared by weighing the three components and mixing them using a vortex mixer (VELP Scientifica, Europe) for two minutes. Each formulation was then self-emulsified in 50 ml of distilled water and analysed for droplet size and polydispersity index (PDI) using a master-size analyser (Brookhaven Instruments, Nano Brook Omni, New York) to find the optimum self-nano emulsifying formulation. Prior to the measurement, the nanoemulsion formulation was shaken gently for two minutes using a magnetic stirrer. The optimum formulation was chosen based on droplet size, poly dispersity index (PDI), and oil concentration. All measurements were carried out in triplicate at room temperature32.

Droplet size and PDI analysis of pomegranate seed oil self-nanoemulsifying system

The master size analyser (Brookhaven Instruments, Nano Brook Omni, New York) used in this study was used to determine the particle size and PDI. First, the oil nanoemulsion underwent self-emulsification. This process was done in three separate runs33. Based on the lowest PDI, the least quantity of the used pomegranate seeds oil, and the smallest droplet size, the ideal nanoemulsion formulation was chosen.

Antioxidant activity for pomegranate seeds oil and its nanoemulsion

Two stock solutions of pomegranate seed oil and its self-nanoemulsifying formulation were prepared (1 mg/ml) in methanol, and then serial dilutions were made for each stock solution with concentrations ranging from 5 to 100 µg/ml. Each concentration was mixed with methanol and 0.002% DPPH, incubated for 30 min, and absorption measured at 517 nm. Trolox was used as a positive control. Statistical analysis

All the experiments were carried out in triplicates. The results are presented as means ± standard deviation (SD). The results were considered significant only when the p-values were less than 0.05. The p-values have been calculated using the t-test function. The significant level implies that the observed differences or effects in the results were highly unlikely to have occurred by chance, which gives confidence in the validity of the findings The IC50 (50% Inhibition concentration) was calculated using BioDataFit edition 1.02.

The assessment of the antioxidant properties of pomegranate seed oil, the self-emulsifying system, and Trolox (the reference drug) was performed by measuring their ability to scavenge free radicals using DPPH, as shown in Fig. 3. Discussion

Based on previous studies on pomegranate seed oil, the seed oil showed promising bioactivity, which may be a potential source for pharmaceutical products. Consequently, this study investigated the different bioactivities of pomegranate seed oil collected from Palestine and developed a self-nanoemulsifying system in order to improve those activities. The phytochemical composition of pomegranate seed oil varied, as determined by GC–MS as per the results of the GS-MS shown earlier. It was reported by Alfekaik and AL-Hilfi that the GC–MS analysis of pomegranate seed oil revealed a significant percentage of the punicic acid (77.37%), oleic acid (7.36%), elaidic acid (6.87%), margaric acid (3.86%), stearic acid, linoleic acid (0.08%), and lignoceric acid as the major components of fatty acids found in pomegranate seed oil37. Another study also showed that triglycerides with unsaturated fatty acids, particularly significant concentrations of conjugated linolenic acid, punicic acid, and eleostearic acid, make up the majority of the oil extracted from pomegranate seed38. This was consistent with the results of this study, as the most predominant compounds in the pomegranate seed oil were punicic acid (30.1%), which was the major compound, followed by β-eleosteric acid (21.78%), then catalpic acid (17.7%), α-eleosteric acid (15.49%), and oleic acid (5.21%). Other active compounds like linoleic acid, palmitic acid, stearic acid, and α-linolenic acid were detected in trace percentages. Unsaturated fatty acids were observed to have many biological activities, as reported by Holic et al. (2018), who revealed that punic acid in pomegranate seed oil will form a bioactive source for therapeutic and industrial applications39. Furthermore, Đurđević et al. (2015) reported that fatty acids, especially punicic, oleic, and linoleic acids, which were the predominant ones in pomegranate seed oil as characterized in the gas chromatography profile, were evaluated for their antioxidant activity using the α-TEAC assay, and the cytotoxic effect was determined by the MTT test against malignant HeLa, LS174, and A549 cells. They showed the best effect on the cervix adenocarcinoma cell line, while no cytotoxicity was found in normal cells, pointing out PSO safety40.

To determine the most effective concentrations of oil, surfactant, and co-surfactant for the development of the self-nanoemulsifying drug delivery system (SNEDDS), it is essential to construct a ternary phase diagram. This graphic illustrates the optimal formulation with a droplet size less than 200 nm. This study involved the use of different amounts of surfactant (Tween 80), co-surfactant (Span 80), and pomegranate seed oil to create ternary phase diagrams. The purpose was to analyze the outcomes and identify an appropriate formulation for the self-nanoemulsifying system. The results indicated that the chosen formulation had a polydispersity index (PDI) of less than 0.3 and a droplet size smaller than 200 nm. Non-ionic surfactants make up 50% of the ideal formulation in this investigation. Multiple studies suggest that an elevation in surfactant concentration generally leads to a reduction in droplet size. This effect is accomplished by employing the emulsion phase inversion procedure41. Additionally, in order to stabilize small particles with a greater surface area, a greater concentration of surfactant is required. Existing literature indicates that augmenting the surfactant content in our formulations can result in various consequences, including an increase in interfacial area and a reduction in surface tension due to an increase in particle size. This effect results from the formation of a greater number of emulsifiers on the particulate surfaces during the homogenization process42. As revealed by Nishitani et al. (2017), nanoemulsion systems can improve drug formulation by enhancing the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and stability of drugs. They offer efficient drug release, prolonged efficacy, low side effects, and protection properties43.

As an indicator of the size distribution of particles within a given sample, the PDI is a crucial parameter in determining the stability of the nanoemulsion formulation. Increased PDI values result in reduced homogeneity among the particles in the formulation44. Formulations are deemed to be of excellent quality if their PDI is less than 0.3 and their particle size is less than 200 nm. Low PDI values indicate the presence of a stable nanoemulgel in this investigation45.

As stated previously, pomegranate is one of the most widely recognized herbal remedies that is distributed worldwide and is used extensively in traditional medicine. In addition to other maladies, it is utilized in the treatment of carcinogens, arthritis, and inflammation due to its potent antioxidant properties46,47. This was supported by the findings of Rojo-Gutiérrez et al. (2021), who stated that pomegranate seed oil exerted high antioxidant activity with 91.29% scavenging activity against DPPH radicals48. As well, Liu et al. (2012) found that the pomegranate seed oil extracted by supercritical CO2 (SC-CO2) exerted strong free radical-scavenging activity towards 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH)49. This was compatible with our results, as the free radical scavenging activity of pomegranate seed oil and its self-nanoemulsifying system was conducted using DPPH. The IC50 value for the pomegranate seed oil was 33.11 ± 1.1 µg/ml, while the oil self-nanoemulsifying system IC50 was 22.38 ± 1.2 µg/ml, which showed a significant potent activity as an antioxidant more than the seed oil compared with Trolox, which had an IC50 value equal to 4.28 ± 0.6 µg/ml. Another study showed high antioxidant activity (IC50: 0.69–1.80 mg/mL) for the pomegranate seed oil of Moroccan pomegranate cultivars50.

Diabetes and obesity are considered global health concerns due to their reported high prevalences throughout the world51. Many countries, including Palestine, have exhibited a notable incidence of these two health conditions, which emerged as worldwide metabolic disorders within the past thirty years. Laaraj N reported in a study that the inhibition against both intestinal α-glucosidase and pancreatic α-amylase enzymes was investigated only by pomegranate extracts, which were used to evaluate their potential anti-diabetic effect in vitro. The results demonstrated the great potential of the acetone extract52. While there is no available data about the efficacy of pomegranate seed oil on diabetes-related enzymes. Our study showed that the IC50 value for pomegranate seed oil was 354.81 ± 2.3 µg/ml, which was relatively moderate, while for the oil self-nanoemulsifying system 33.88 ± 1.6 µg/ml indicated a significant potent activity compared to the reference compound acarbose (a potent α-amylase inhibitory agent), which had an IC50 value equal to 39.81 ± 0.8 µg/ml. The potency of the self-nanoemulsifying system can usually be explained as this system contains ingredients that may affect enzyme activity, such as Tween and Span as these ingredients in the self-nanoemulsifying system increase the stability and reactivity of the formulation53. Alsolami A showed that nanoemulsions formulated from chitosan and jojoba exhibited α-amylase inhibitory activity with an IC50 of 133.8 µg/mL, which was a relatively significant value compared to pure jojoba oil54.

In addition, Xuan Y carried out a study on pomegranate leaf extracts, which showed that the main active ingredient, ellagic acid, regulates lipid metabolism and inhibits pancreatic lipase activity in vitro, reducing serum total cholesterol and triglyceride levels55. This study was carried out on pomegranate seed oil and its self-nanoemulsifying system and investigated them on lipase enzyme, reliving that the oil had an IC50 value of 616.59 ± 2.1 µg/ml, which was relatively moderate, while the value for the self-nanoemulsifying system was 43.65 ± 1.9 µg/ml, which showed a significant potent activity against the lipase enzyme compared to orlistat, which had an IC50 value of orlistat 12.30 ± 0.8 µg/ml. As observed in this study, self-nanoemulsifying systems offer stability and high bioavailability, which makes this system more effective against the lipase enzyme than the oil itself56.

In order to enhance the bioactivity of pomegranate seed oil, a nanoemulsion was created. The enhancement was a result of a greater solubilization of the nanoemulsion than that of the oil and other medications; this is due to the nanoemulsion's large surface area and extremely small particle size, both of which contribute to its enhanced efficacy57. According to the findings of Wang et al.58, who investigated the beneficial properties of lingonberry leaves and their active constituents, which have been substantiated to be enhanced when formulated in nanoemulsion, the results of this study were obtained online concurrently. Conclusion

In summary, the present investigation effectively constructed an oil-in-water pomegranate seed oil nanoemulsion through the implementation of a response surface optimization technique. In comparison to pomegranate seed oil in its pure form, the nanoemulsion demonstrated a significant improved stability, decreased droplet size, and increased antioxidant, anti-diabetic, and anti-obesity effects. The results of this study enhance knowledge regarding the development and enhancement of nanoemulsions used for the transportation of bioactive substances. Pomegranate seed oil can be an excellent source of bioactive and antioxidant compounds, especially unsaturated fatty acids such as punic acid, oleic acid, linoleic acid, palmitic acid, and stearic acid. Pomegranate seed oil self-nanoemulsifying system could be applied in the future for the preparation of possible oral medications for the prevention and treatment of oxidative stress, diabetes, and obesity due to its high activity against free radical, amylase, and lipase enzymes compared to pomegranate seed oil itself and the references used. In addition, as revealed in this study, a self-nanoemulsion system can improve oil drug formulations by enhancing pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, acting as drug reservoirs, and also offering efficient oil release.

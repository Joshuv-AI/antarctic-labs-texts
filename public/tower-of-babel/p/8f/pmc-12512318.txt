A modified ureteroileal anastomosis can reduce the incidence of ureteroileal anastomotic stricture
Authors: Shijie Zhang, Hao Liang, Lei Liu, Peixin Li, Qingya Yang, Sifeng Qu, Jingchao Liu, Benkang Shi, Nianzhao Zhang, Jun Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIleal conduit is widely used as a urinary diversion procedure after radical cystectomy. However, ureteroileal anastomotic stricture (UIAS) remains a threatening complication. Here we introduce a novel technique, Chen ureteroileal anastomosis, and compare its postoperative complications to Bricker anastomosis, with a specific focus on the incidence of UIAS.MethodsA rat model was established to investigate morphological and histological alterations of the ileum in urine-stimulated environment. A double-center retrospective cohort of 124 patients undergoing laparoscopic radical cystectomy with ileal conduit between February 2017 to December 2023 was analyzed. All surgeries were performed by a regular surgical team. The demographic characteristics, clinicopathological characteristics, perioperative indicators, and postoperative complications between the two groups were recorded, assessed and compared by an independent committee.ResultsFollowing long-term exposure to urine, the muscular layer of the rat ileum underwent thinning and fibrosis. The two groups were comparable in all demographic and clinicopathological variables. No significant differences were observed in terms of operation time, transfusion rate, length of stay and follow-up time. Median follow-up time was 35 months (IQR, 24–42) in the Chen group and 38 months (IQR, 26–48) in the Bricker group. The rate of UIAS was significantly lower in the Chen group (1.7% vs. 13.6%, p = 0.036, 95%CI: -0.208, -0.030). Other complications were comparable.ConclusionsIn this study, we observed that Chen ureteroileal anastomosis significantly reduced the incidence of UIAS compared with Bricker ureteroileal anastomosis. Background

Bladder Cancer is one of the most common urinary malignancies, with over 610,000 new cases and 220,000 deaths annually worldwide [1]. Based on tumor invasion depth, bladder cancer is classified into muscle-invasive (MIBC) and non-muscle-invasive (NMIBC) [2]. Clinically, radical cystectomy (RC) remains the standard treatment for organ-confined MIBC and some high-risk NMIBC cases [3, 4]. Subsequently, urinary diversion is essential to restore urine drainage. The ileal conduit (IC) is the most commonly used type of urinary diversion after RC [5].

Ureteroileal anastomotic stricture (UIAS) is a threatening complication following RC and IC [6], with a long-term incidence up to 16.2% [7–12]. The subsequent irreversible renal unit destruction and infectious sequelae severely affect patient’s survival and quality of life [7, 12]. Pathogenetically, ischemia, anastomotic inflammation and secondary fibrosis are considered to be the primary causes of UIAS [13, 14]. In addition, the surgeon’s experience and the surgical technique of anastomosis also affect the occurrence of UIAS [15]. Herein, we present the Chen ureteroileal anastomosis—a novel technique with a wider anastomotic diameter and a shared lumen. In this procedure, the two ureters were spatulated for 4 cm and incorporated to form a single and wider lumen. The distance from the newly formed ureteral outlet to the edge of the ureteroileal anastomosis has been extended to 2 cm.

Our preliminary data indicate that the Chen procedure may help to reduce the incidence of UIAS [16]. The aim of this study was to compare the incidence of postoperative complications between Chen and Bricker ureteroileal anastomosis, especially the incidence of UIAS.

Methods

Animal model

Female Sprague-Dawley rats (8 weeks old; 250–300 g) underwent the experimental protocol. Ten rats were used, with normal rats serving as the control group. Animals were maintained under fasting conditions with water restriction for 24 h preoperatively. After anesthesia induction via isoflurane inhalation, the abdominal skin was aseptically prepared using povidone-iodine solution. The experimental model was established by anastomosing a 10-mm vascularized ileal segment to the urinary bladder. Surgical access was achieved through a midline abdominal incision, lateral to the rectus abdominis muscles, providing exposure to both the ileal loops and the bladder anatomy. After mesenteric dissection, a 10-mm segment of the intestine was surgically resected, ensuring the preservation of at least one arterial branch to maintain vascular integrity (Fig. 1A). Luminal irrigation was performed with 0.9% sodium chloride, and the intestine continuity was restored via end-to-end anastomosis at the proximal resection site after mobilization of the ligated ileal segment (Fig. 1B). A 5-mm antimesenteric longitudinal incision was made on the isolated ileal graft, followed by a corresponding incision on the bladder dome (Fig. 1C). Direct anastomosis of these openings was performed using interrupted sutures. The ileal portion that was in optimal proximity to the bladder dome was selectively used to ensure a tension-free anastomotic configuration (Fig. 1D). Prophylactic antibiotics were administered postoperatively, and all procedures adhered to strict aseptic protocols. Animals recovered with water access at 24 h and chow reintroduction at 48 h postoperatively. All the animals were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All animal experiments were conducted following the national guidelines and the relevant national laws on the protection of animals. Using the carbon dioxide method for euthanasia. The animal care and experimental protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, Shandong University. Institutional review board approval number was KYLL-2023(ZM)−001.

Study participants

The information was collected from a prospectively maintained database from consecutive patients who underwent RC at the Department of Urology, Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (Jinan and Qingdao campuses) from February 2017 to December 2023. Both campuses operate under homogeneous management, and staff exchanges frequently to ensure procedural consistency. All patients who received IC during the study period were included in the study. The patients were assigned to either Chen group or the Brick group (the control group) based on the surgical operation method and the selection of Chen or Bricker IC was based on the surgeon’s experience and the patient’s preference. Exclusion criteria included preoperative hydronephrosis, history of pelvic radiotherapy, and loss of follow up. Written informed consent to participate in the study was obtained from all participants. Institutional review board approval number was KYLL-KS-2022048.

Surgical procedure

Both the Chen and Bricker groups were operated by the same team of board-certified senior urologists, with each of whom had more than 10 years of specialized experience in RC and IC. All participating surgeons completed standardized training and passed a competency evaluation prior to certification in the novel surgical technique. Each surgeon completed at least five supervised procedures before operating independently.

All surgical procedures were performed under general anesthesia. Open IC was performed after laparoscopic RC and pelvic lymph node dissection. The Bricker IC procedure adhered to established methods (Fig. 2) [17], while the Chen IC technique, detailed in prior work (Fig. 3) [16], is summarized below. Both ureters should be fully mobilized to ensure a tension-free reach to the intended anastomotic site. After the left ureter was extensively mobilized (up to the inferior pole of the kidney if necessary) and redirected to the right side through a retrosigmoid tunnel, redundant segments of both ureters were excised to facilitate a tension-free, angulation-free ureteroileal anastomosis. Both ureters were laid adjacent to each other and spatulated for 4 cm (Fig. 4A). Extreme caution should be exercised to avoid excessive traction on the ureter, and the surrounding adipose tissue should be preserved as much as possible. Before anastomosis, visual assessment of ureteral blood supply is required—the surface of the ureteral mucosa should be reddish in appearance. The posterior ureteral wall edges were sutured continuously with 4 − 0 Vicryl (Fig. 4B), and two 7 Fr ureteral catheters were placed in the left and right ureters. Catheter placement involved guiding a zebra wire into the ureter, advancing the catheter along the wire, and then removing the wire to confirm proper positioning. These stents extended externally through the distal end of the ileal conduit and were secured to the stoma device. The stents were left in place to ensure adequate urinary drainage and protection of the anastomosis. The anterior ureteral wall edges were then sutured continuously for 2 cm (Fig. 4C). Next, the newly formed ureteral outlet was anastomosed to the proximal end of the isolated ileal segment with two running 4 − 0 Vicryl sutures (Fig. 4D). Finally, an abdominal stoma was constructed in the right iliac fossa. The distal end of the ileal segment was initially anchored to the peritoneum and the external oblique aponeurosis with interrupted 4 − 0 Vicryl sutures, then secured to the skin. An 18 Fr Foley catheter was placed within the conduit to allow for postoperative irrigation. Additionally, a 22 Fr tubular drain was positioned in the pelvic cavity.

Postoperative management and follow-up

An enhanced recovery protocol was implemented for all patients. On postoperative day 3, creatinine levels in the pelvic drainage fluid were measured for all patients in the Chen group and for selected patients in the Bricker group—specifically those with excessive drainage output [18]. Urine cultures were obtained whenever patients exhibited systemic signs of infection, including fever or malaise. The ureteral catheters were removed one month after surgery. Discharge criteria included spontaneous defecation, the ability to tolerate a regular solid diet, adequate pain control (with or without oral agents), absence of fever, and proficient management of the urinary stoma. There were no significant changes to the perioperative nursing plan throughout the study period.

All patients were advised to be reviewed every three months for the first two years after surgery, every six months for the subsequent three to five years, and annually thereafter regardless of whether they had symptoms. Routine follow-up includes assessment of liver and kidney functions, electrolyte levels, urinalysis, as well as ultrasound, CT, and/or MRI of the abdomen and pelvis. If there is any suspicion of UIAS, immediate renal pelvis radiography or CT urography should be performed to assess the degree of hydronephrosis and determine the location and length of the obstruction. If necessary, urine cytology examination and biopsy should also be conducted to rule out possible tumor recurrence.

Data collection and study outcomes

For each patient, demographic, clinicopathological and perioperative indicators were recorded, including age, sex, body mass index, preoperative upper collecting system status, pathological tumor stage (graded according to the eighth edition of the Tumor-Node-Metastasis classification for bladder cancer [19]), time of operation, blood transfusion status, length of stay (days from surgery to discharge), and follow-up period. All complications were recorded, defined, and graded according to the modified Clavien-Dindo classification [20]. Patients presenting with hydronephrosis (asymptomatic or symptomatic) with radiographic evidence of obstruction at the site of ureteroenteric anastomosis were considered to have UIAS [11]. The length and location of the stricture was determined by imaging examinations, including CT and pyelography. Ureteroileal anastomosis leakage (UIAL) was defined as a higher creatinine level in the pelvic drainage fluid compared with serum creatinine three days postoperatively. Mild ureteral obstruction was defined as partial, non-progressive ureteral blockage (non-anastomotic, mild hydronephrosis without renal dysfunction) that resolved under conservative treatment. The image interpretation was carried out by radiologists who were not involved in the surgery. Surgical video recordings, baseline characteristics, and postoperative complications were impartially assessed by an independent adjudication committee to minimize potential observer bias.

Statistical analyses

Parametric continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Nonparametric continuous variables were reported as median and interquartile ranges (IQR). Categorical variables were reported as frequency and percentages. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Variables that were approximately normally distributed were compared with independent-samples Student’s t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were compared with Mann–Whitney U test. Comparisons were generally performed using chi-square tests, but Fisher’s exact test was applied when any expected cell count was less than five. Kaplan–Meier curves were compared using the log-rank test. All recorded variables were entered into a dedicated database and analyzed using SPSS Statistics 27.0 and ImageJ 1.54. All reported p-values were two-sided and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Structural changes of the rat ileal segment

Histomorphological evaluation with H&E staining demonstrated significant epithelial remodeling in the postoperative ileum mucosa compared to native ileum mucosa (Fig.S1). Marked architectural alterations were observed, including villous atrophy and reduced crypt density (Fig.S2). Masson’s trichrome staining further demonstrated extensive collagen deposition and thinning of the muscle layer (Fig.S1, S2). An additional file shows this in more detail [see Additional file 1].

Baseline characteristics and postoperative complications of patients

Of the 124 patients undergoing RC during the study period, 58 (46.8%) received Chen IC and 66 (53.2%) received Bricker IC. All surgeries were successfully performed. All patients who did not reach the endpoint completed at least one year of imaging follow-up. Table 1 presents the demographic characteristics, clinicopathological factors, and perioperative indicators for all 124 patients. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in baseline characteristics. Post hoc power analysis was performed to evaluate the statistical power of the observed significant difference in UIAS between the two groups. Using SPSS Statistics 27.0, power was calculated based on the following parameters: the effect size derived from the current data, sample size, and significance level (α = 0.05, two-tailed). The analysis revealed a post hoc power of 72.6%, indicating that the study had sufficient power to detect the observed effect.

Table 2 summarizes complications by severity, while Table 3 details the types of complications. The overall complication rate was 48.3% for the Chen group and 48.5% for the Bricker group (p = 0.981). In the Chen group, ten patients (17.2%) experienced G1 complications, 12 patients (20.7%) had G2 complications, four patients (6.9%) had G3b complications, one patient (1.7%) had G4a complications, and one patient (1.7%) died of severe pneumonia two months postoperatively. Two patients (3.4%) had mild left ureteric obstruction, which did not require further intervention. Imaging data did not reveal UIAS and showed that the site of obstruction was anterior to the iliac vessels, where the left ureter had been transposed to the right. Only one patient (1.7%) had UIAS with bilateral hydronephrosis. UIAL occurred in one patient (1.7%). After conservative observation, the creatinine level in the drainage fluid normalized on the sixth postoperative day. In the Bricker group, 11 patients (16.7%) had G1 complications, 16 patients (24.2%) had G2 complications, four patients (6.1%) had G3a complications, and one patient (1.5%) had G4b complications. Nine patients (13.6%) had UIAS, including four left-sided, two right-sided, and three bilateral cases. The incidence of UIAS in the Chen group was significantly lower than the Bricker group (p = 0.036, 95%CI: −0.208, −0.030). Kaplan-Meier analysis shows that there were significant differences in UIAS-free survival between the Chen and Bricker groups (Fig. 5, p = 0.017, HR: 0.123, 95%CI: 0.016, 0.968). The median time to UIAS occurrence was seven months in the Bricker group. Among the nine patients in the Bricker group who developed UIAS, three (33.3%) developed it after the first year. The single case of UIAS in Chen group occurred in 17 months postoperatively. No severe stent-related complications occurred in our series. No significant differences were observed in other major postoperative complications between the two groups.

Discussion

In the present study, we confirmed that the Chen IC is a safe and effective urinary diversion method for patients. The overall complication rate in the Chen group was similar to that reported previously. Notably, while both techniques exhibited comparable overall complication rates, the Chen approach significantly reduced UIAS incidence.

The long-term incidence of UIAS ranges from 3% to 16.2% [7–12], with peak occurrence typically observed within the first 12 months post-operatively [10, 11, 18, 21]. However, new UIAS cases continue to emerge beyond this critical period. Given the impact of anastomotic surgical techniques on UIAS [9, 15], improvements have been made to anastomotic techniques in recent decades [8, 9, 22–26]. To reduce UIAS, recent attempts such as the hybrid anastomosis [22] have been reported, showing a lower stricture rate compared with standard Bricker or Wallace methods. Meanwhile, Kavaric et al. [8] demonstrated that by enlarging the common opening and optimizing the suturing method, the modified Wallace anastomosis significantly reduced UIAS and UIAL, highlighting the positive impact of technical refinements on surgical outcomes. The Bricker and Wallace techniques remain the most widely adopted approaches. However, they were reported to have no significant difference in the incidence of UIAS [27, 28]. To the best of our knowledge, few studies have focused on the impact of surgical techniques on the incidence of long-term UIAS (> 12 months postoperatively).

In this study, compared with the Bricker group, the Chen group exhibited comparable operation time, transfusion rates, length of stay, and overall complications rates. From a health economics perspective, Chen anastomosis in this study did not prolong operative or hospital stay, indicating that it did not increase patients’ direct medical burden. More importantly, this technique significantly reduced the incidence of UIAS, which may decrease subsequent medical costs related to managing this complication. Furthermore, compared with the traditional Wallace technique, which employs end-to-side or end-to-end method, the ureteral plate was anastomosed to the conduit in a side-to-side method in the Chen technique, and both procedures require the bilateral ureters to be joined, creating a common opening before anastomosis to the ileal conduit. Additionally, the differential fusion points of the anterior and posterior ureteral walls allow Chen anastomosis to create a longer common channel (about 2 cm) and a more spacious orifice of the ureteroileal anastomosis.

To elucidate the mechanistic basis of the long-term UIAS, we established a rat model in which the ileal segment was exposed to urine chronically. The morphological and histopathological changes suggest that chronic fibrosis occurs in the ileum under long-term urine stimulation. We hypothesize that chronic fibrosis of the ileum under continuous urinary exposure may constitute a critical pathogenic mechanism underlying long-term UIAS. In future studies, we intend to perform additional analyses on rat ileal tissues to further elucidate the pathogenesis of UIAS. Extrapolating directly from the rat model to human has inherent limitations due to differences in scale and physiology. The rat model’s results may provide mechanistic insight but may not fully replicate the human pathophysiological environment. Further experiments are needed for verification. Then, we developed a novel ureteroileal anastomosis technique [16]. This technique allows both ureters to form a common segment of lumen before anastomosing with the ileum, which results in lumen expansion and increased anastomotic diameter. Although progressive fibrotic remodeling persists, this architectural modification maintains luminal patency. Consequently, our Chen cohort demonstrated a significantly reduced incidence of UIAS compared to the conventional Bricker technique, particularly in late-onset cases.

In the Chen group, the two ureters were spatulated for 4 cm. Theoretically, this may pose a high risk of UIAL, but this phenomenon was not observed (p = 0.468). Three factors may account for this result: First, the distal ureter was manipulated meticulously, with particular attention to preserving the ureteral blood supply. Second, the main surgical procedures, including ureteral incorporation and ureteroileal anastomosis, were performed using a continuous suture technique. Finally, the use of a 7 Fr ureteral catheter for urine drainage might be more appropriate.

Two potential limitations of the Chen procedure should be considered: Firstly, when UIAS occurs, it usually leads to bilateral hydronephrosis. The creation of a common ureteral lumen carries an intrinsic risk: should stenosis occur at the anastomosis, it may compromise both kidneys, leading to bilateral hydronephrosis and renal impairment—potentially more serious than outcomes with separate anastomoses such as the Bricker procedure. Although bilateral hydronephrosis is indeed a serious complication, the core objective is to prevent stricture formation. In Chen group, the single case of UIAS was bilateral; So for patients who received Chen anastomosis, clinicians should closely monitor both kidneys. Nonetheless, we stressed that the Chen group showed a markedly reduced overall incidence of UIAS, which to some extent counterbalances the theoretical risk. Secondly, during the transposition of the left ureter to the right side, ureteric obstruction may occur due to the traction of the fibrous cord located anterior to the iliac vessels. Extensive proximal movement to the right and increased tension may account for the higher rate of left-sided ureteral obstruction [11]. We observed that two patients (3.4%) in the Chen group experienced mild left ureteral obstruction. This situation has been reported in other surgical procedures [9, 14, 29]. Meticulous surgical technique can reduce this risk by ensuring that the left ureter is fully separated, passes smoothly through the iliac region without tension, and that any fibrous bands at the iliac vessels are transected. Future larger-scale studies should confirm these secondary observations through appropriate multiple testing adjustments.

The present study included a larger sample size and a longer follow-up period compared to our previous prospective research [16]. However, several limitations should be noted. First, this is a retrospective study conducted across two centers. Patients were not randomly assigned to the procedures. The selection between Chen or Bricker was mainly influenced by the surgeon’s experience and the patient’s preference. This non-random allocation may introduce selection bias. Second, this study included a limited number of patients with high body mass index (BMI). It has been shown that higher BMI may increase the risk of UIAS [30]. Consequently, further studies are required to validate the applicability and efficacy of this novel technique in high BMI patients. Thirdly, our median follow-up period of approximately 3 years may not cover all cases of late stenosis. Previous studies have indicated that new cases of urinary tract obstruction may still occur 12 months after surgery [21]. Therefore, a longer follow-up period is needed to determine whether the advantages of the Chen technique will persist over time, or whether late stenosis will still occur.

Conclusions

Our animal model data suggest that continuous urinary exposure may induce chronic ileal fibrosis, suggesting a potential pathophysiological mechanism underlying long-term UIAS. Compared with Bricker ureteroileal anastomosis, the incidence of UIAS was significantly lower in Chen ureteroileal anastomosis. There was no difference in other postoperative complications. Prospective randomized controlled trials and external validation in multi-center studies are needed to confirm our findings and the efficacy of our technique.

This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Clinical Trial Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (Qingdao) (Ethics approval number: KYLL-KS-2022048). All animal experiments were conducted following the national guidelines and the relevant national laws on the protection of animals. The animal care and experimental protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, Shandong University. Institutional review board approval number was KYLL-2023(ZM)-001.

All patients considered for this technique were informed that it represented a new anastomotic method, including its principles, expected benefits, potential unknown risks, and distinctions from established approaches such as the Bricker technique. Written informed consent to participate was obtained from all participants before the surgery.

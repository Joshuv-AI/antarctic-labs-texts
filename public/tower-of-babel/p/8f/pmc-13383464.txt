Could seasonal patterns in ECT use impact service delivery? A nationwide study
Authors: David Barruel, Marion Plaze, Valérie Dauriac-Le Masson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe practice of electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is constrained by limited resources. Anticipating recurrent peaks in demand is therefore essential for optimizing resource planning within public healthcare systems. This study examined seasonal patterns of ECT utilization in France over an extended period.MethodWe analyzed data from the French national administrative and medical database. Seasonal patterns in monthly ECT utilization time series from 2017 to 2023 were investigated using robust statistical methods.ResultsWe observed a clear seasonal pattern with a 12-month periodicity in monthly ECT utilization, accounting for 16% of the variance in use, with a major peak in December and a mean relative amplitude of 13.1%. Seasonal variation was observed across sex, age and diagnostic subgroups, although it was less pronounced among patients aged 24 years or younger and those with schizophrenia. Across all subgroups, ECT activity consistently peaked at the onset of winter, between November and December.ConclusionThese findings emphasize the need for ECT providers in France to anticipate recurrent winter peaks in activity. Proactive healthcare policies should ensure that medical staffing and equipements ressources are adjusted to accommodate predictable winter peaks in ECT demand.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13033-026-00713-0.Keywords: Electroconvulsive therapy, Seasonnality, Peaks in demand, Ressource planning, Proactive healthcare policies

Introduction

Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is the most effective treatment for mood disorders and catatonia. It may also be indicated in selected cases of schizophrenia [1]. Despite persistent stigma, ECT has become a safe and effective therapy for severe mental disorders [2]. Demand for ECT has increased in France over the past ten years [3]. This increase has also been reported for countries like Germany [4], South Korea [5], and Spain [6]. However, the growing demand for ECT has made scheduling increasingly complex, as service delivery is constrained by limited resources [7]. As a result, patients might face prolonged delays before receiving treatment, raising a serious public health issue [3]. Thus, there is a strong need to understand the dynamics of ECT prescription, specifically seasonal fluctuations, to predict peaks in demand and optimize service planning accordingly.

While there are numerous studies about ECT use [8, 9], there is scarce literature related to the seasonal variations of ECT prescription. A landmark study [10] published in 1976 highlighted a bimodal yearly seasonality for ECT use, with a peak in May and another ranging from November to January. Nevertheless, this study has several limitations: (i) ECT data were available for three hospitals only, (ii) the assessment of seasonality relied on a chi-square test, which identifies departures from a uniform distribution but does not provide a comprehensive demonstration of seasonal patterns [11], and (iii) the peak periods were evaluated in a descriptive and visual way. To the best of our knowledge, the available literature ever since is essentially limited to a single study [12], which examines ECT seasonality in Japan using a national database. Yet, this latter work did not observe any significant seasonal variation for ECT utilization.

Our purpose was to fill this gap by providing an overview of the seasonal patterns for ECT activity in France between 2017 and 2023. Thus, our objectives were to (i) rigorously explore, using advanced statistical methods, the existence of a seasonal pattern, (ii) characterize the seasonality, specifically the length of its cycle, its peak and amplitude, and (iii) repeat this analysis according to patients characteristics (sex, age, diagnosis).

Methods

Design and data collection

We conducted a retrospective study, using data from the French national administrative database (“Agence technique de l’information sur l’hospitalisation”), which covers hospital activity for both public and private hospitals. We included all patients aged ≥ 10 years who received at least one ECT session between 2017 and 2023. For each patient, we leveraged: (i) sociodemographic information (age, sex), (ii) primary or associated psychiatric diagnoses according to the 10th International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) codes. To collect the exhaustive occurrence of ECT treatments for each patient, we used a classification for coding medical procedures in France (Classification commune des actes médicaux, CCAM). The code used for ECT definition was AZRP001. Extracted data were fully anonymous; therefore, in accordance with the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the French Data Protection Act (“Informatique et Libertés”) Reference Methodology MR-005, informed consent was not required. Informing the patient of their right to oppose the use of their data is sufficient.

See details about data sources and data collection in supplementary material.

Outcomes definition

The outcome was defined as the monthly number of ECT patients who received at least one ECT session at the national level. We secondarily computed this same outcome according to age, sex and diagnostic group.

Statistical analysis

We followed a four-step procedure to investigate seasonality in the monthly counts of ECT receivers from 2017 to 2023:

(i)Our first analytical approach was primarily descriptive. We applied the STL (Seasonality-Trend Loess) algorithm [11, 13] to visually inspect the trend and seasonal decomposition of the monthly time series and to quantify the proportion of variance explained by the seasonal component. We then examined the ACF (Autocorrelation Function) and PACF (Partial Autocorrelation Function) plots [13] to visually assess autocorrelation at specific lags. These preliminary exploratory investigations might suggest a seasonal pattern and the length of its period. When a specific pattern was suspected, we performed a sliding-window partial autocorrelation analysis at the corresponding lag over the entire study period to evaluate the stability of the seasonality over time.

Results

The mean monthly number of ECT patients nationwide between 2017 and 2023 was 922 ± 173.

The initial step of the analysis, based on STL and PACF, indicated a moderate but distinct yearly seasonality (Fig. 1a), explaining 16% of the variance in ECT use. This was confirmed by a significant Friedman test (statistic = 38.23, p < 0.01). On visual inspection, the graphical representation of the normalized mean ranks of ECT use (Fig. 1b) revealed a major peak in December. The optimal SARIMA model describing the monthly time series also supported a significant 12-month seasonal pattern: the seasonal autoregressive term (estimate ± SE = − 0.37 ± 0.10) was highly significant (p < 0.001).

(a) Seasonality Trend Loess (STL) decomposition for ECT use time series. (b) Graphical representation of the normalized mean ranks for monthly ECT use

GAM analyses further corroborated a pronounced annual seasonality: adding a 12-month cosine harmonic term (i) substantially improved the model fit and (ii) yielded a significant effect (p < 0.01). This yearly pattern was characterized by an average peak in December and a mean relative amplitude of 13.1%. A sliding-window autocorrelation analysis showed that this seasonal structure was strongly disrupted during the COVID-19 period.

Sex-stratified analyses revealed similar seasonal patterns, with no difference between men and women. Annual seasonality was evident across all age groups except those aged ≤ 24 years, for whom GAM modeling did not detect a significant seasonality. The average peak month was generally December, except in the 40–49 and 60–69 age groups, which showed peaks in November. Notably, the 25–29 age group exhibited the strongest seasonal pattern, explaining 27% of the variance and showing the highest relative amplitude (19%).

Diagnosis-stratified analyses indicated a clear seasonal pattern for mood disorders. In patients with schizophrenia, seasonality was confirmed by the Friedman test and the SARIMA model, whereas no significant seasonal effect was detected using the generalized additive model (GAM). For both mood disorders and schizophrenia, average peaks were again observed in December, while average relative amplitudes were respectively 12% and 10%. The time series variance explained by seasonality was respectively 16% and 11%.

Discussion

This study showed yearly seasonal variations in the use of electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) in France between 2017 and 2023, with peak activity occurring at the onset of winter. Seasonality was observed across sex, age, and diagnostic subgroups, although it was less pronounced among patients aged 24 years or younger and those diagnosed with schizophrenia. Across all subgroups, the highest levels of ECT activity were consistently observed between November and December.

Our results were consistent with a previous American study [10] that also reported seasonal variations in ECT use, with a 12-month period. That study identified peaks between November and January, as well as an additional peak in May. Nevertheless, its scope was limited to three hospitals, which limits the generalisability of its findings.

Our findings can be interpreted in light of previous research on seasonal affective disorder (SAD) [18–20], which is characterised by a clear seasonal pattern, with a higher prevalence in autumn and winter. This may suggest a link between the occurrence of SAD and the subsequent seasonal pattern of ECT use. Beyond SAD, seasonal variations in the incidence and severity of major depressive episodes, particularly in bipolar disorder [21, 22] have also been reported and may contribute to seasonal fluctuations in ECT demand. Additionally, our analysis of ECT seasonality, stratified by diagnosis, showed that patients with schizophrenia exhibited a seasonal pattern for ECT use similar to that observed in patients with depressive disorders, although less pronounced and not significant in the GAM model. The weaker and less consistent seasonal signal observed in schizophrenia may reflect the heterogeneity of ECT indications in this population, with only specific clinical subgroups potentially showing seasonally driven fluctuations. This latter finding may partially reflect the presence of underlying mood disorders among schizoaffective patients and could be linked to a previous study that observed seasonal variations in monthly admission rates among patients with schizoaffective disorder, peaking in autumn [23]. We observed a stronger seasonality of ECT use in patients aged 25–29 years, while younger patients (< 25 years) showed little evidence of seasonal variation and elder patients (≥ 70) showed a significant but weak seasonal pattern. This non-linear pattern may reflect differences in clinical trajectories. Our results might suggest that seasonality for affective disorders or major depressive epidodes may be less detectable in individuals under 25, as disorders are often in an early phase, whereas in those aged 25–29, a more active course for disorders make seasonal patterns more evident. Literature [24, 25] also confirms that the occurrence of seasonal affective disorder tends to decline in elderly populations. A potential perspective for future work would be to further explore, using additional data, the relationship between seasonal variation in ECT use and disease seasonality patterns. Future studies could also explore the extent to which environmental or meteorological factors may influence these seasonal patterns. Seasonal variation in ECT use may reflect not only seasonally varying disease burden but also organizational constraints within mental health services, including fluctuations in staffing and service availability across the year.

From an organisational perspective, our results highlight the need to anticipate increased ECT demand in November and December. To prepare for peak activity, healthcare resources, including staffing and equipment, should be carefully optimised. This is particularly important given reports of growing demand for ECT [3, 4, 26], alongside persistent challenges in access and service planning, notably related to limited equipment availability and shortages of anaesthesiologists [7, 27]. The organisation and delivery of ECT have been addressed through formalised frameworks and guidance documents, including, for instance, the Electroconvulsive Therapy Accreditation Service (ECTAS) in the UK and Ireland [28]. Anticipating planning by accounting for seasonal fluctuations and peaks in ECT activity might fall within the scope of such guidance documents.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study in the European literature to examine the seasonality of ECT use. We used a large-scale national medico-administrative database, representative of the French population, and robust statistical methods to assess seasonality, combining time-domain and frequency-domain approaches.

Conclusions

In our study, we found a yearly seasonal pattern for ECT use in France between 2017 and 2023, with a peak in the November–December period. Our findings highlight the necessity of targeted healthcare policies, encouraging ECT providers to proactively plan medical staffing and allocate equipment resources in anticipation of these peak activity periods.

The Early Cretaceous Sembar Formation, Southern Indus Basin, Pakistan: Biomarkers and Trace Element Distributions to Investigate the Sedimentary Palaeoenvironment and Organic Matter Input
Authors: Waqas Naseem, Irfan Ullah Jan, Khairul Azlan Mustahpa, Mohammed Hail Hakimi, Shabeer Ahmed, Ali Y Kahal
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Early Cretaceous clay-rich facies of the Sembar Formation represent the most significantly occurring organic-rich sediments in the Southern Indus Basin of Pakistan. In this study, detailed geochemical research of total organic carbon, biomarker, mineralogy and trace elemental compositions, together with kerogen microscopic analysis, were carried out and used to understand the organic matter input and the dispositional environmental setting of the organic-rich Sembar shale. The Sembar shales have high organic matter, as indicated by the total organic carbon (TOC) content of up to 2.31 wt %. These shales contain a high abundance of liptinites (i.e., alginite and bituminite), with significant reactive vitrinite maceral, reflecting a mixed-organic matter with a high contribution of marine-derived input. The biomarker distributions evidence the finding of mainly marine organic matter. The biomarker characteristics such as Pr/Ph, Pr/n-C17, Ph/n-C18, and tricyclic terpane ratios together with C27–C29 regular sterane distributions show that the source of organic matter was primarily marine-derived from mainly phytoplankton algae, along with amounts of land plant input, and accumulated under a low oxygenated environment. The Sembar shale facies are characterized by high Mo trace element content and high V/Ni and Mo/TOC ratios, suggesting anoxic and nonsulfidic environmental conditions during time deposition. The geochemical proxies such as the Sr/Ba ratio and gammacerane/C30 hopane (G/C30) index suggest that the Sembar shale facies were deposited in a relatively low moderate stratified water column. The higher gallium (Ga) than rubidium (Rb), with high Ga/Rb ratios, indicates a high abundance of the kaolinite mineral, thereby deducing the subaerial weathering during the warm and humid climatic conditions. In this case, these warm and humid climatic conditions cause an influx of masses of nutrients, which could be attributed to increasing marine primary bioproductivity. These large masses of nutrients into the photic zone are also attributed to the presence of the upwelling system during the deposition time. Therefore, such conditions of bioproductivity and preservation of organic matter (OM) resulted in the accumulation of organic carbon in the shale facies of the Early Cretaceous Sembar Formation.

Introduction

Organic matter-rich shale sediments were deposited in both marine and continental environments and have recently become an interesting target resource for oil exploration in the most sedimentary basins of the world as conventional and unconventional resources.1−3 However, most organic-rich shale sediments contain noticeable quantities of more than 1% organic matter and were deposited under a low oxygenated environment, ranging from suboxic to anoxic conditions.4−6

Multi-integrated techniques are vital to studying the organic-rich shale characteristics, including organic geochemistry, inorganic geochemistry, and biomarker measurements combined with microscopic examination.7−9 The biomarker distributions were widely used to highlight the whole of chemical or biological evaluation processes and offer details on the origin of organic matter input and paleoredox environmental conditions.10,11 In this case, examining molecular fingerprints using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) is extremely important in determining the origin and maturity of organic matter.10,12−14

The key focus of the study area is the Southern Indus Basin (Figure 1), one of the most important upstream prolific oil producing from numerous oilfields for decades in Pakistan. Southern Indus Basin includes several organic matter-rich horizons, such as the Middle Jurassic Chiltan shale, Lower Cretaceous Sembar shale, and Goru shale formations.2,15 This study focuses on the lower Cretaceous Sembar shale formation, primarily a thermogenic gas source, with gas retention of 103 billion cubic feet per acre-foot.16

(A) Location map of the sedimentary basin in Pakistan, including the Southern Indus Basin. (B) Location map of the studied wells in the Southern Indus Basin (The maps have been redrawn by author Waqas Naseem using Coreldraw15 software).

The Sembar shale in the Southern Indus Basin has recently attracted researcher attention and upstream exploration ventures due to the superior gas-source rock found in this basin.16−24 Although these previous works focus on the shale facies in the Sembar Formation and its potential to generate hydrocarbon, more analyses are still needed, more specifically on the depositional environmental settings and their implications for understanding the origin and source of organic matter input into the organic-rich shale of the Sembar Formation.

This study employs robust geochemical techniques, including total organic carbon content, molecular biomarkers and trace elements, and organic petrology, to investigate the genesis of organic matter and the depositional factors that influenced the organic carbon accumulation in these organic-rich shales of the Sembar Formation. The current work also incorporates scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) analyses to conduct a detailed mineralogical evaluation of these shale samples. In this case, the shale samples from the lower Cretaceous Sembar Formation were collected from four exploratory wells in the Southern Indus Basin of Pakistan (Figure 1).

Geological Settings

Southern Indus Basin in Pakistan and other interior rift basins in India are associated with the rifting mechanisms associated with the split of Gondwana land.25 However, numerous changes occurred in the Southern Indus Basin’s structural and stratigraphic characteristics during the movement of the Indian Plate.26,27 In the Early Cretaceous, the Indian Plate migrated northward as it separated from the Antarctic and Australian plates in the Late Jurassic.28 At the same time, the erosive surface was covered by the Sembar and Goru formations. During the Late Cretaceous, a transform fault became active during flysch storage along the Indian Plate’s southern edge, and when the Tethyan Sea closed, an oblique collision occurred and the Sulaiman-Kirthar fold belt began to evolve.28 In this case, the overlying Pab Sandstone was deposited in the west of the Indian Plate because the shelf environment persisted during the Late Cretaceous.29

Shale, sandstone, and limestone from the Triassic Wulgai Formation represent the oldest rocks found in the study area30 (Figure 2). The Jurassic spingwar, Anjira, and Loralai rock sequences overlie these Triassic rocks. A monotonous series of thick-bedded micritic, ooidal, and bioclastic limestone makes the overlying Jurassic Chiltan Formation.31 The Cretaceous rocks are represented by Sembar, Goru, Parh Limestone, Mughal Kot, Fort Munro, and Pab formations.

(A) Generalized stratigraphic column of the Southern Indus Basin. (B) Vertical distribution of the lithofacies of the Sembar Formation in the studied Bhadmi-01 well based on gamma ray log data.

The Sembar Formation comprises clastic rocks, primarily shale, with subordinate sandstone and siltstone, with a thickness range from 700 to more than 1000 m.26,32 The basal part of the Sembar Formation contains sandy shales with phosphatic and pyritic nodules.26,31 Belemnites, foraminifera, ammonoids, and nano fossils are also found in the Sembar sediments, suggesting shallow or open marine environments.33 In the southern Indus Basin, shale and siltstone units decrease proportionately toward the east, whereas the sandstone facies of the Sembar Formation diminish toward the west.26

The Sembar Formation in the Southern Indus basin of Pakistan is a principal source of hydrocarbons based on their organic richness, thermal maturity, and type II/III kerogen and is a deltaic deposit that reduces the marine environment.17,28 These marine shales contain a high abundance of quartz concentration, which increases in brittleness, facilitating the reservoir’s permeability.34

The Southern Indus basin also contains a thick Tertiary sedimentary cover that is underlain by Mesozoic rocks and overlain by Quaternary sediments (Figure 2). The Paleocene rocks of the study area are dominantly composed of shale, sandstone, and carbonates of the Khadro, Girdo, and Dunghan formations. The Eocene rocks comprise shale and carbonates of Ghazij and Kirthar formations.31 The Kirthar Formation is divided into Habib Rahi Limestone, Sirki (claystone and calcareous), Pirkoh, and Drazinda members.35 The Oligocene Nari Formation overlies the Kirthar Formation and comprises mixed limestone, sandstone, and shale lithology.36 Gaj Formation overlays the Nari Formation in the Sulaiman and Kirthar provinces. The Gaj Formation is overlain by Siwaliks, further covered by alluvium, indicating the top of a stratigraphic column.31

Materials and Methods

Sample Collection and Preparation

In the current study, 16 ditch cuttings of shale unit of the Sembar Formation were collected from two wells (i.e., Buzdar South Deep-01 and Jumman Shah-01) in the northeastern part and two wells (i.e., Bhadmi-01 and Sher-01,) in the southern part of the Southern Indus Basin (Figure 1). The samples were procured from the Petroleum Core House of the Hydrocarbon Development Institute of Pakistan (HDIP) with permission of the Directorate General of Petroleum Concession (DGPC).

The cutting samples were subjected to a cleaning process, involving water washing to remove drilling mud. The cleaned shale samples were then milled into ≤3 mm and 250 macrometer sieves using a mortar and pestle. The powder fraction of the samples was used for geochemical analyses, including the total organic carbon (TOC) content, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR), and bitumen extraction analyses. On the other hand, the ≤3 mm particles of the whole samples were employed for organic matter facies examination using a reflected light microscope.

Microscopic Examinations

The whole shale samples underwent organic petrological examination and organic facies analysis using a standard polished block procedure,37 as described in the following subsection. In this case, the entire rock volume of the samples in molding cups was mounted in a densification mixture of resin and hardener (3:1) and then polished by silicon carbide paper and alumina powder, following the standard procedure of ASTM D2797-04. The polished blocks were then examined under white light with an oil-immersion medium using a Leica DM 6000 M microscope and a LEICA CTR 6000 Orthoplan system with reflected and UV light illumination. The frequency liptinite, vitrinite, and inertinite macerals were quantified for each sample using reflected white and blue lights.

Geochemical Methods

TOC content analysis was conducted on the organic-rich shale samples of the Sembar Formation in the studied wells. In this case, the crushed samples were treated with 10% diluted hydrochloric acid to remove inorganic carbon (i.e., carbonate minerals). Then, the TOC content in the treated samples was measured by using a Leco CS-300 instrument. This analysis was performed at geochemical and organic petrological laboratories in the Hydrocarbon Development Institute of Pakistan (HDIP).

The crushed samples were further analyzed by ATR-FTIR using a PerkinElmer Spotlight 300 FT-IR microscope system. In this regard, 3 mg of the powdered whole rock samples was put on the Diamond/ZnSe plate of the PerkinElmer Universal ATR accessory unit. The acquired FTIR data are processed by measuring the infrared spectra in both transmittance and absorbance modes at 4000 cm–1 to 650 cm–1 wavelength–frequency and used to identify the three main mineral compositions, i.e., quartz, carbonate, and clay minerals, as illustrated and discussed by Tironi et al.38 and Wada et al.39

The extractable organic matter (EOM) or free bitumen was extracted from 16 shale samples of the Sembar Formation (30 g) using Soxhlet extraction for 72 h, with an azeotropics mixture of 93% dichloromethane (DCM) and 7% methanol. The extracted bitumen was then separated into different hydrocarbon components by using conventional liquid column chromatography. The column was packed with 50–100 mesh silica gel along with n-Pentene and then capped with a few centimeters of alumina, with a little cotton at the lower end. Finally, extractable organic matter is introduced to the column, and then, saturates are drained in the column and collected in a vial by adding n-Pentene. Additionally, aromatics and NSO were gathered by adding DCM and methanol, respectively.

Later, the aliphatic hydrocarbon fraction was analyzed using GC-MS to pick the biomarker compounds using an Agilent Gas Chromatograph 6890N and a V5975B inert Mass Selective Detector. The capillary column of the GC-MS had a diameter of 0.32 mm, a length of 30 m, and a film thickness of 0.25 μm. The temperature of the flame ionization detector (FID) was increased gradually from 40 to 300 °C at a rate of 4 °C/min and then kept at 320 °C for 30 min. The GC-MS analysis of the saturated hydrocarbon fraction was performed to determine the distribution of n-alkanes, isoprenoids, terpanes, and steranes as well as their proportions and parameters applying specific ions like m/z 85, m/z 191, and m/z 219 mass fragmentograms (See Table A1). Peak identification was accomplished by comparing the studied ions’ mass spectra and retention times to those already published.10,40,41

Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometer (ICP-MS)

The concentration of the trace elements in the studied Sembar shales was measured by an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (ICP-MS) using an Agilent Technologies 7500. In this case, approximately 0.5 g of whole 10 shale samples from four wells was prepared for ICP-MS analysis by passing them through a digestion and predigestion procedure that dissolved the constituent components in liquid medium. The materials were then heated in fuming HNO3, HCl, and HF before being dried in an Anton 3000 microwave oven. Following digestion, boric acid (H3BO3) was utilized for complication and then diluted 100 times with ultrapure water. Standard solutions of the elements with an analyte concentration of 10 ppm were used for calibration with a minimum detection limit of about 1 ppb.

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) Analysis

In this work, the SEM-EDS analysis is also the method of choice for the studied organic-rich shale samples and was used to identify the quartz, clay, and carbonate mineral constituents, their textural relationships, and organic carbon morphology in the studied shale samples. In this case, a representative portion of the seven shale samples was chipped off, measuring up to 1 cm. These chips were used for SEM-EDS using a field-emission SEM (FESEM, high resolution) Hitachi SU8220 system. Micrometer scale elemental analysis was completed by using an EDX (Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy) system with a silicon drift detector. In this analysis, the surface of the polished samples was scanned with an electron beam, and analyzed backscattered electrons were used to determine the mineralogical and elemental composition (i.e., Si, Al, Ca, Mg, Fe, K, Na, and S) of selected spots.

Results

Organic Facies Characteristics

Microscopic examinations were performed on the Sembar shale samples under white and UV light, as shown in Figure 3. Accordingly, all of the studied shale samples are dominated by mineral matter, which arises in fissures, cavities, and cracks (Figure 3A,B). A high percentage of clay minerals and a smaller quantity of pyrite minerals are the dominant mineral matter in the studied Sembar shale samples (Figure 3A,B).

Photomicrographs of the investigated organic-rich shale of the Sembar Formation, under reflected light and fluorescence under UV, with a field width of 0.2 mm, showing (A) vitrinite materials, (B) pyrite mineral associated with liptinite, (C and D) structural lamalginite algal organic matter, and (E and F) unstructured bituminite organic matter.

The overall composition of the organic matter was determined, and the results show that the shale samples are characterized by high amounts of vitrinite and liptinite (Figure 3). The shale samples under investigation frequently contain vitrinite, which typically includes vitrodetrinite and collotelinite (Figure 3A). The liptinites normally have low reflectance (dark color) under normal white light (Figure 3B), and UV-light excitation was primarily employed to identify between liptinite assemblages. In the studied Sembar shale samples, liptinite is observed as structured and unstructured organic matter, which are characterized by fluorescence intensities ranging from greenish-yellow to yellow (Figure 3C–F). The alginate assemblage is observed in the form of lamalginite algae; based on its morphology, it took place in lamellae (Figure 3C,D). These lamalginite assemblages are assumed to have formed in an aquatic environment.9,41−43 However, bituminite is also recognized in the studied Sembar shale samples, which has a greenish-yellow fluorescence under UV light (Figure 3E,F).

Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Content

In this study, the TOC results show that all samples exhibit TOC values ranging from 0.76 to 2.31 wt % (Table 1). Most of the samples have a high TOC range of more than 1.01 wt % (2.45–2.31 wt %), whereas six samples exhibited relatively low TOC values between 0.76 and 1.00 wt % (Table 1). Consequently, the Sembar Formation shale sediments can be considered a good source rock. However, the measured high TOC content was integrated with biomarker fingerprints and trace elements and used to point to the paleoredox environmental conditions, explained in the following sections.

FTIR Results

In this research, the bulk mineral compositions of the analyzed shale samples from the Sembar Formation were primarily demonstrated by FTIR spectra coupled with SEM-EDS results.

The FTIR spectra of the Sembar shale samples show distinct bands in the clay stretching, carbonate CO3 stretching, and silicate bending regions at 3750 to 3500 cm–1, 1600 to 1400 cm–1, and 1100 to 779 cm–1, respectively (Figure 4), as illustrated and discussed in the previous works.38,39,44,45

FTIR spectra of the whole shale samples of the Sembar Formation, including the percentage of the three main mineral compositions (i.e., quartz, carbonate, and clay).

According to the FTIR spectra, the spectrum of quartz shows that the absorption peaks correspond to the asymmetric stretching of Si–O–Si groups at 1080 cm–1 and the symmetric stretching at 779 cm–1 (Figure 4). However, the content of the minerals was quantified based on the peak heights from FTIR spectra and shows that the quartz mineral is in higher abundance than other clay and carbonate minerals, with a range between 73.2% and 83.95% (Figure 4). The high abundance of the quartz mineral is confirmed by XRD results, as reported by the previous work of David.46 In this case, such silicate (quartz) mineral enrichment is inferred as the mainly siliceous-dominated lithotype for Sembar shales. The lithofacies observed in the Sembar shale unit closely resemble those found in well-known unconventional tight shale reservoir rock such as the Lucaogou Formation China.47

In addition, the clay mineral is also obtained in significant amounts, mainly kaolinite clay mineral. The FTIR spectrum of the kaolinite is characterized by absorption bands between 3500 and 3750 cm–1, corresponding to the stretching frequencies of OH groups.38,48 However, the absence of the band at 3670 cm–1 (Figure 4) suggests that the structure of kaolinite in the Sembar shales is disordered and easier to dehydrate.49

Such enrichment in quartz mineral, with the presence of the clay and carbonate minerals, is demonstrated by SEM-EDS (Figure 5). The SEM-EDS results deciphered that the shale samples contain mainly quartz mineral (SiO2), with clay (Al2O3), carbonate (CaO), and pyrite (FeS) minerals.

Inorganic Trace Elements

Trace element concentrations of the studied Sembar shale samples are given in Table 1, along with several widely used geochemical ratios. In this case, the trace elements show Ba, Ga Mo, Sr, Cr, and V contents are prominent with average values of 314.68, 60.80, 46.85, 38.11, 18.35, and 12.74 ppm, respectively, while Co, Rb, Cd, and Ni trace elements have average values of 6.39, 5.95, 5.27, and 4.86 ppm, respectively (Table 1). The geochemical results of trace elements such as V, Ni, and Cr were used to gain more insight into the palaeoredox conditions,50−52 while the Ba, Sr, Cu, Rb, and Ga trace elements are highly indicative of salinity seawater53 and paleoclimate conditions.54−56 Furthermore, different geochemical ratios, i.e., Ga/Rb, Mo/TOC, V/Ni, Rb/Sr, Sr/Ba, and V/Mo ratios, were estimated depending on the amounts of their trace elements and were found to be in the range of 0.51–55.88, 21.67–67.22, 0.85–19.69, 0.05–0.64, 0.02–2.04, and 0.09–0.46, respectively (Table 1). These ratios were used to interpret salinity, paleoclimate, and palaeoredox conditions of the depositional environment setting, as discussed in the next subsections.

Hydrocarbon Distributions and Molecular Biomarkers

This current study used chromatographic ions m/z 85, 191, and 217 as a reference for detailed hydrocarbon distributions together with biomarker characteristics and was examined through gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) of the saturated fractions in the analyzed Sembar shale samples. In this case, the n-alkane and acyclic isoprenoid distributions show that most of the studied shale samples display unimodal distributions along the C15–C36 range (Figure 6A–D). On the other hand, several shale samples are characterized by bimodal distributions of the n-alkane in the range of C16–C36 (Figure 6E,F). These n-alkane distributions result in carbon preference index (CPI) values in the range of 0.92–1.45 (Table 2).

The acyclic isoprenoid hydrocarbons, pristine (Pr) and phytane (Ph), were also present in substantial levels in the extracted shale samples (Figure 6). The chromatograms generally reveal a predominance of Ph over Pr (Figure 6A–D), resulting in a low value of the isoprenoid ratio (Pr/Ph) between 0.45 and 0.98 (Table 2). With a comparatively relatively high Pr/Ph ratio of more than 1 (1.21–1.54), the extracted shale samples demonstrate that the concentration of the bulk of isoprenoid Pr is greater than that of Ph (Figure 6E,F). Moreover, the pristane/n-C17 and phytane/n-C18 ratios have also been calculated and were found to be in the range of 0.33–0.86 and 0.21–0.57, respectively (Table 2).

The biomarkers of hopanoid and terpane were also detected in the saturated portion of the studied shale samples using m/z 191 mass fragmentograms and revealed a considerable number of hopanes, including C30 hopanes, C29 norhopanes, and C31–C35 homohopanes (Figure 7). In the hopanoid distributions, the frequency of C30 hopanes is more than that of C29 norhopanes (Figure 7), resulting in relatively low values of the C29/C30 hopane ratio between 0.44 and 0.97 (Table 2). The frequency of C30 hopanes is also more than C31R homohopane and gammacerane (Figure 7), further resulting in lower C31R/C30H and G/C30 ratios in the range of 0.14–0.32 and 0.08–0.27, respectively (Table 2).

m/z 191 mass fragmentograms of the aliphatic fraction in the extracted shale samples from the Sembar Formation in the Southern Indus Basin.

In addition, the m/z 191 data exhibited quantities of tricyclic terpanes, ranging from C21 to C26 (Figure 7). The tricyclic terpane distributions are characterized by the high abundance of C23 tricyclic terpane (Figure 7), resulting in high C23tri/C24tri of more than 1 (Table 2). In addition, the high abundance of C25 tricyclic and C24 tetracyclic to C26 tricyclic terpane results in relatively high C24tetra/C24tri and low C26tri/C25tri ratios (Table 2). However, the tricyclic terpane ratios are used to assess the organic matter input and the depositional environmental setting, as discussed in the next subsections.

The steranes and diastereomers in the saturated HC fraction were produced by the m/z 217 mass fragmentogram of the studied shale samples (Figure 8). In general, compared to the abundance of diasteranes, mass fragmentograms of m/z 217 show a significant abundance of steranes (Figure 8). Following the steroid distributions, the C27–C29 regular steranes show the relative percentages in the range of 10.90–42.77% for C27, 15.65–48.60% for C28, and 32.68–60.57% for C29 (For more information, see Table 2). Additionally, the C29/C27 regular ratio was calculated and found to be between 0.86 and 3.82 (Table 2).

Discussion

Origin and Source of Organic Matter Input

The origin of the organic matter input to the Sembar Formation shale sediments was primary demonstrated by employing multibiomarker proxies and kerogen microscopy results. In this regard, the high molecular n-alkanes of more than C23 are primarily derived from the components of higher plant cuticular waxes,57−60 whereas the short-chain n-alkanes (<C20) originated from algae/microorganisms and bacteria that deposited in a marine environment setting.61 In this case, the n-alkane distributions show the relative abundance of medium to higher molecular n-alkanes in the Sembar shale samples (Figure 6) and imply a mixed organic matter, with major contributions of marine organic matter input. The varied CPI value further reinforces this conclusion (Table 2). Low CPI values between 0.92 and 1.08 for most of the Sembar shale samples indicate mainly marine organic matter,62 while the relatively high CPI values of more than 1.25 indicate a high abundance of the terrestrial organic matter input.63 Moreover, confirmation of the significant input of marine- and terrestrial-derived OM into the Sembar shale sediments is provided by analyzing the isoprenoids and their ratios, including Pr/Ph, Pr/n-C17, and Ph/n-C18 (Figure 9A,B).

Geochemical biomarker results of the extracted bitumen from the studied shale samples, showing (A) pristane/n-C17 versus phytane/n-C18 and (B) pristane/n-C17 versus Pr/Ph and indicating that these shale sediments contain mixed organic matter, with mainly marine origin and deposited under low oxygenated environments, ranging from suboxic to anoxic conditions (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Shanmugam64).

Further, the studied shale samples exhibit a C31R/C30 hopane ratio between 0.25 and 0.30 (Table 2), suggesting mainly marine organic matter, with terrestrial organic matter input.65−67 This observation is supported by the correlation between the C31R/C30 hopane ratio and the C26tri/C25tri ratio (Figure 10A). The significant abundance of C23 tricyclic and relatively high C24 tetracyclic terpane to C26 tricyclic (Figure 7) imply a mixed organic matter with a high contribution of marine OM into the Sembar shale sediments (Figure 10B).

Geochemical biomarker results of the extracted bitumen from the studied shale samples, showing (A) CR31/C30 homohopane versus C26Ti/C25Ti (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Mohialdeen et al.69) and (B) C23T/24T versus C24Te/C24Ti, indicated to be mixed organic matter, with mainly marine organic matter input (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Zainal Abidin et al.70).

Important biomarkers that are additionally employed to provide information on organic matter input include 17α (H)-trisnorneohopane (Tm) and 18α (H)-trisnorneohopane (Ts).65−67 The notable presence of Tm, along with elevated Tm/Ts ratios, emphasizes the substantial input of terrigenous organic matter.68 Accordingly, the studied shale samples contain mixed organic matter, as demonstrated by the Tm/Ts values between 0.26 and 6.36 (Table 2). In this case, most of the samples with low Tm/Ts of less than 1 (0.26–0.97) indicate mainly marine organic matter, whereas high Tm/Ts values from 1.23 to 6.36 indicate high contributions of terrigenous OM input.

The mixed marine and terrestrial organic matter inputs are further supported by the distribution of C27–C29 regular steranes and their ratios, as detailed in Table 2. The examined shale samples show a relative abundance of medium to high C27–C29 regular steranes (Table 2). This distribution pattern of C27 to C29 regular steranes supports the presence of mixed organic matter, especially with a significant contribution from planktonic and algal origin, with little input of OM derived from plants based on the adapted ternary diagram of Huang and Meinschein,71 as shown in Figure 11. Further evidence for marine organic matter is further supported by the presence of the phytoplankton algae (i.e., lamalginite assemblages) in the studied shale samples (Figure 3C–E), which are mainly marine-derived organic matter and were deposited under reducing environmental conditions.9,37,42,43

Ternary diagram of the regular steranes (C27, C28, and C29) of the extracted bitumen from the studied shale samples, showing the relationship between the sterane compositions and the organic matter input (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Huang and Meinschein71).

Sedimentary Environmental Conditions

The paleoredox condition is one important factor of the sedimentary environmental factors and majorly impacts the preservation of organic matter (OM),72,73 which is divided into four levels based on the concentration of O2 in bottom water, namely, oxic, dysoxic, anoxic nonsulfidic, and anoxic sulfidic (euxinic) conditions. In this case, multiple proxies, including biomarker and trace element data, are used to assess the paleoredox conditions during the deposition time of the organic-rich shale succession of the Sembar Formation.

Low oxygen conditions were recognized during Sembar shale sediments’ deposition time based on the isoprenoid biomarkers and their Pr/Ph, Ph/n-C18, and Pr/n-C17 ratios.74,75 For example, Pr/Ph < 1 suggests an anoxic condition in a calm and quiet environment; Pr/Ph of >2 is associated with terrestrial influences and translates to an oxic condition.76 In this case, the Sembar shale sediments were accumulated during anoxic to suboxic depositional settings as deduced by the Pr/Ph ratio in the range of 0.45 to 1.54 (as shown in Table 2). The explanation of low oxygenated environment, ranging from anoxic to suboxic conditions during these shale sediments’ deposition, was clearly demonstrated by the association between the Ph/n-C18 and Pr/n-C17 ratios, as shown in Figure 9A.

In addition, the redox-sensitive trace elements (i.e., Mo, Ni, and V) and their ratios are considered geochemical proxies for redox environmental conditions (i.e., reducing vs oxidation) during deposition.77 In this case, the V and Ni trace elements are better redox-sensitive indicators for anoxic environmental conditions because they are commonly insoluble under reducing environmental conditions.78−80 Galarraga et al.80 reported that the V/Ni ratio could be used as an indicator for the interpretation of paleoredox conditions, in which values of more than 2 indicate an anoxic condition, while V/Ni ratios of >1 indicate deposition under oxic conditions. Accordingly, suboxic to anoxic conditions during the deposition of the Sembar shale sediments are evidenced by the values of the V/Ni ratio from 0.84 to 19.69 (Table 1). Moreover, the concentrations of Mo content in ppm together with the V/Mo ratio were plotted on the diagram of Piper and Calvert81 and show that the Sembar organic-rich shale sediments were generally accumulated during the poor-oxygenated (anoxic) environmental conditions, with a V/Mo ratio of less than 1 and Mo content higher than 40 ppm (Figure 12). However, this study integrates the organic matter content and enrichment in Mo trace element to assess the degree of euxinia of the anaerobic basin water, using the Mo/TOC ratio.82 In this case, the Mo/TOC ratio of the Sembar shale Formation samples is high and ranges from 21.67 to 67.22 (Table 1), suggesting that the water column in which the Sembar shale Formation formed was weakly euxinic (anoxic nonsulfidic) in environmental conditions.82

Geochemical correlations of the Mo trace element content versus V/Mo ratio, showing that the studied organic-rich shale samples of the Sembar Formation were deposited under anoxic environmental conditions (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Piper and Calvert81).

In addition, the degree of euxinia of the anaerobic basin water is also controlled by the salinity stratification of the water columns.83 In this case, the salinity stratification of the water column during the deposition of the Sembar shale sediments was primarily assessed using the gammacerane biomarker in the m/z 191 mass fragmentogram (Figure 7).

The high abundance of the gammacerane biomarker and its high gammacerane/C30 hopane (G/C30) index of more than 0.3 is often associated with high salinity stratification (euxinic) conditions of the water columns during deposition.9,83 Accordingly, the studied shale sediments of the Sembar Formation have variable values of G/C30 index between 0.08 and 0.27 (Table 2), suggesting variable salinity water during the deposition time of these shale sediments, ranging from low to moderate salinity stratifications. This finding is further indicated by the G/C30 versus Pr/Ph ratio diagram (Figure 13A). Moreover, these paleosalinity conditions of the shale Sembar sediments were also investigated using the salinity-sensitive elements such as strontium (Sr) in the form of SrSO4 and barium (Ba) precipitates such as BaSO4.84,85 In this case, the Sr/Ba ratio was used to assess the salinity degree during the deposition, in which low Sr/Ba values of <0.1 usually indicate low salinity seawater, while a Sr/Ba ratio between 0.1 and 1 implies moderate salinity water and Sr/Ba value of >1 is indicative of high salinity seawater.83,85,86 However, the Sr/Ba proxy is influenced by the carbonate-hosted Sr on bulk-sediment Sr concentrations. Therefore, it is an important caveat in using this Sr/Ba ratio as a paleosalinity proxy.87 Most of the studied Sembar shale samples are carbonate-poor sediments with high Si and Al elements, as indicated by the FTIR and SEM-EDS results (Figures 4 and 5). Therefore, Sr/Ba proxy is effective in the fine-grained siliciclastic sediments of the Sembar Formation, because the authigenic clay minerals uptake the ionic species from seawater and/or sediment pore-water to their aqueous concentration.88

Geochemical results of the studied shale samples of the Sembar Formation, showing (A) Pr/Ph ratio versus G/C30 index (Figure has been created using our data, and the boundaries are adopted from Hadad et al.66) and (B) G/C30 index versus Sr/Ba ratio, an indication of low to moderate stratification of the water columns.

In the current study, the fine-grained Sembar sediments have variable values of Sr/Ba ratio between 0.02 and 2.04 (Table 1). Most of the Sembar shale has a low Sr/Ba ratio of less than 1 (0.02–0.80), except that one sample has a value of up to 2.04 (Table 1). Accordingly, the studied shale sediments of the Sembar Formation were deposited in low to moderate salinity water conditions, as indicated by the cross-plot of the multisalinity proxies, including the Sr/B ratio and G/C30 index (Figure 13B).

Paleoclimate Conditions and Paleoproductivity

The paleoclimate conditions during deposition of the organic-rich shales that formed the Sembar Formation were reconstructed based on climate indexes such as Rb/Sr and Ga/Rb, which are widely used.9,89,90 The Rb to Sr ratio discriminated between a hot-arid (Rb/Sr > 1) and a warm-humid (Rb/Sr < 1) climate.91 In this regard, the studied Sembar organic-rich shale samples mainly accumulated in warm and humid climates, as indicated by the low Rb/Sr ratio of up to 0.64 (Table 1). This interpretation of warm-humid climatic conditions is also consistent with a high concentration of gallium (Ga) compared to rubidium (Rb), with high Ga/Rb ratios of up to 55.88 (Table 1). The Ga is associated with Al2O3, whereas the Rb is associated with K2O.92−94 However, Al2O3 is generally enriched in kaolinite clay minerals and is associated with a warm climate,92,93 while K2O is associated with Illite clay minerals and reflects dry and cold climatic conditions.94

In this study, the presence of the kaolinite clay mineral for most of the studied shale samples is demonstrated by the stretching frequencies of OH groups in the FTIR spectra (Figure 4) and the Al element being higher than K, as shown in SEM-EDS results (Figure 5). This indicates that the presence of abundant kaolinite is believed to confirm the warm and humid climates and thereby results in an intensity of subaerial weathering prevailed during the depositional period of the Sembar organic-rich shale facies. This finding of warm-humid climatic conditions is supported by the cross-plot of Rb/Sr against Ga/Rb (Figure 14).

Cross-plot of the Ga/Rb and Rb/Sr ratios of the studied shale samples of the Sembar Formation, indicating mainly warm and humid climates during the deposition of the shale sediments.

Further, the warm water period during the deposition of the Sembar shale samples is also confirmed by the abundance of organic matter primarily derived from planktonic and algal origin, as observed via microscopic examinations (Figure 3C–E) and biomarker distributions of regular steranes (Figure 11), since the growth and decomposition of phytoplankton are accelerated in warm water and decreased during the cold-water conditions.95

In this case, we believed that the presence of warm and humid climates enhanced the nutrient levels on the sea surface and the appearance of some high nutrient indicator species. However, most of the nutrients are derived from detrital input, mass water circulation, and/or remobilization of trace metals.96

In this study, the studied Sembar shale samples have a high abundance of detrital minerals such as quartz, as indicated by the FTIR spectra (Figure 4) and SEM-EDS results (Figure 5). This detrital mineral survived during the weathering in humid and warm periods and was transported through hydrologic cycles to the depositional sites. In such a situation, nutrients could be supplied from the continent in humid and warm periods and, thereby, the high nutrient influxes to the surface water during the deposition of Sembar shales.

In addition, the enhanced masses of nutrients in the sea during the Early Cretaceous could have resulted from mass water circulation (upwelling system).97,98 In this case, the Mo, Cd, Mn, and Co inorganic geochemical elements have been used to assess the impact of the upwelling systems on the sedimentogenesis of shales.99−102 The low abundances of both Co and Mn elements indicate the upwelling zones, while the Co and Mn elements are relatively high in restricted basins.100 In this regard, the relatively low Co content in the studied shales from the Sembar Formation indicates upwelling processes in water bodies with primary bioproductivity during the deposition period.103 Moreover, the relatively high Mo content and a decrease in organic matter in the studied Sembar shales suggest upwelling processes in water bodies (Figure 15). This finding is clearly matched with the direct proportionality between the Mo and TOC contents (Figure 15B).

Cross-plots of Mo trace element (ppm) and TOC (wt %) contents, showing (A) a high upwelling system during deposition time of the shale sediments of the Sembar Formation and (B) good correlation between Mo trace element (ppm) and TOC (wt %) content.

Controlling Factors on Organic Matter Accumulation

In the current study, variations in sedimentary depositional characteristics, including paleosalinity, paleoredox, and paleoclimatic conditions, influenced the organic carbon deposition in the shale sediments of the Sembar Formation,104,105 as shown in Figure 16. This depositional model is designed by high detrital sediment input in a shallow marine environment (Figure 16). This is usually consistent with mainly silicate dominated lithofacies of the Sembar shale samples (Figure 4). The detrital material originates from weathered bedrocks around the basin and is transported by Freshwater input or wind flow (Figure 16). Therefore, the nutrients could be supplied from the continent through hydrologic cycles in humid and warm periods, and thereby, the humid and warm conditions increased the nutrient level in the sea surface and enhanced marine primary productivity (Figure 16). In such a situation, the humid and warm water period during the deposition time of the Sembar shale sediments contributes to the high productivity of the OM blooms within the water column (Figure 16). However, this trapped water body also is characterized by low oxygen content and the enhanced preservation of organic carbon (Figure 16) and thereby contributes to the organic matter enrichment.

Paleoenvironmental model during deposition of the Early Cretaceous Sembar shale Formation.

Conclusions

This study investigates the organic geochemical and petrographical characteristics of Early Cretaceous Sembar shale to assess the organic matter input and dispositional environmental settings. Seventeen organic-rich shale samples of the Sembar Formation from four exploration wells in the Southern Indus Basin were selected for the study. For this purpose, total organic carbon, biomarkers, mineralogy trace elemental compositions, and kerogen microscopic analysis were performed. The following are the main conclusions:

The Sembar shale samples have TOC contents in the range of 0.76–2.31 wt %, with a high contribution of TOC values of more than 1 wt %, generally revealing organic-rich shale sediments.

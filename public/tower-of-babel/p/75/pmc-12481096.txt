Towards HPV elimination: closing the gender gap by vaccinating and screening men
Authors: Bingmeng Xie, Lifeng Xiao, Lin Peng
Published: 2025

The recent study by Abraham García-Gil et al.1 highlighted the value of broader vaccine formulations for cervical cancer prevention. However, female-focused strategies overlook the substantial role of men in transmitting HPV to women through sexual intercourse. Globally, genital HPV prevalence in men is broadly equivalent to that in women2; in China, a multicenter hospital-based survey reported male prevalence nearly twice that of females.3

Persistent HPV infection in men contributes to elevated risk for a spectrum of malignancies, with oropharyngeal, penile and anal cancers being the most clinically significant. In the United States, oropharyngeal cancer in men has now surpassed cervical cancer as the most common HPV-related malignancy.4 Achieving elimination of HPV relies heavily on high vaccination coverage in both sexes. Nevertheless, fewer than fifty countries have established publicly funded gender-neutral HPV immunisation programmes.5 Economic and cultural barriers perpetuate vaccine hesitancy among men. Moreover, approved HPV screening tests for men is absent to date.

Neglect of male HPV prevalence will impede attainment of the WHO “Global cervical cancer elimination” targets and impose an enduring burden on public-health systems. We therefore propose three urgent measures for lower and middle-income countries: First, embed gender-neutral, stigma-free HPV education into routine primary-care encounters. Second, launch organized screening programmes combining HPV testing with targeted oropharyngeal and anal cancer screening for high-risk population, especially men who have sex with men. Third, implement a phased, government-funded HPV vaccination strategy that initially prioritises high-risk male cohorts and subsequently expands to encompass all adolescent boys.

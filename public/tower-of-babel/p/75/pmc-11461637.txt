Two high quality chromosome-scale genome assemblies of female and male silver pomfret (Pampus argenteus)
Authors: Jiabao Hu, Youyi Zhang, Yaya Li, Yuanbo Li, Man Zhang, Wanlong Huang, Shanliang Xu, Danli Wang, Xubo Wang, Jing Liu, Yajun Wang, Xiaojun Yan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPampus argenteus is a highly commercial marine fish whose population is declining sharply. Here, we generated a female P. argenteus genome, spanning 536.33 Mb with contig N50 of 1.79 Mb; 24070 genes (99.50% of 24,182) were functionally annotated. To improve quality of it, we assembled a 553.79 Mb genome of male fish with contig N50 of 24.75 Mb through HiFi and ultra-long ONT sequence technologies; 550.82 Mb were anchored onto 24 gap-free chromosomes; 22,892 genes (98.1% of 23,346) were functionally annotated; the QV value was 51.55 with 98.9% of BUSCO and 99.39% coverage of Illumina reads. Finally, we compared this genome with previous published one, revealing 37,301 SVs. 52.82 Mb and 18.05 Mb SDs were characterized in our and published assemblies, respectively, and 48.96 Mb PURs were constructed. Thus, this genome assembly exhibits excellent completeness, continuity and accuracy comparing to the published one, which can be current preferred reference genome. Overall, these works help aquaculture and wild resources recovery of P. argenteus and provide a valuable genetic resource for study.Subject terms: Genome, Molecular evolution

Background & Summary

Silver pomfret (Pampus argenteus, Euphrasen, 1788, NCBI Taxonomy ID: 206143), Scombriformes, Stromateoidei, Stromateidae, Pampus, is one type of pretty highly commercial fish. This beautiful silver purple fish is widely distributed in the coastal seas of Indo-West Pacific: Persian Gulf to Indonesia, West and Southwest of Korea and almost the entire coastal area of China, north to Hokkaido, Japan (Fig. 1)1,2. Due to the high nutritive value and delicacy of the meat, the fish becomes the most preferred marine species fish in many countries3,4, and people like to call this silver fish as white pomfret because their slight silver scales are very easy to fall off during the catching process, exposing white skin, and this fish is very easy to generate strong stress reactions even slight stimulation which leads to the mortality, so it is rare to see the live P. argenteus in the markets. However, the catch and size of wild P. argenteus has greatly reduced because of overfishing and environmental pollution. To fill market gaps and recover wild sources of P. argenteus, many research groups started to study the artificial culture and breeding of this fish4,5, but large scale culture is still facing with a large number of challenges such as various diseases6,7.

Over the course of about 20 years, our research team has successfully accomplished the large scale artificial breeding of P. argenteus and now can yields nearly 1 million juvenile fish annually8,9. To develop the industrialization of this fish, there are still many necessary basic research points including feed nutrition10, immunity11, metabolism12, sex differentiation13 and so on. what’ s more, we are selecting the fast grow, cold- and disease- resistant breeds of P. argenteus through performing the molecular breeding now8,14. In addition, we found many interesting characteristics of this fish that they prefer to eat venomous jellyfish15,16, non-stop rapidly swim12, have pharyngeal sac organ17, hermaphroditism sex differentiation stage and sexual size dimorphism18 and so on, which are worthy to further investigate. To further advance the study, particularly in the area of molecular breeding, it is crucial and essential to have high-quality assembled genomes of both female and male P. argenteus as the foundation for these endeavors.

In this study, we firstly have successfully generated a high-quality reference genome of female P. argenteus at the chromosomal level, utilizing a comprehensive approach that integrates PacBio long-read sequencing, chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C) and Illumina paired-end sequencing. The assembly spans 536.33 Mb with contig N50 of 1.79 Mb and scaffold N50 of 23.17 Mb, organized into 668 contigs and 318 scaffolds, of which 515.41 Mb (96.11%) were anchored onto 24 chromosomes. Assessment based on BUSCO assessment (97.9%), QV (25.62) and re-mapping coverage (98.01%) of Illumina paired reads indicated the assembly of female P. argenteus was an assembly with good completeness, but the assembly had some deficiency in continuity with many gaps or sequences defined as not assembled. A total of 76,73 Mb (15.78%) repeat sequences, and 24,182 protein coding genes and 7,361 non coding RNAs were annotated in the genome of female P. argenteus. The quality of our female P. argenteus genome is very similar with the genome of the previous study19.

Therefore, to further improve the quality of P. argenteus genome and investigate the special sex differentiation mechanism, we assembled another high-quality reference genome of male fish through PacBio High fidelity (HiFi) reads technology. The genome assembly of male fish was 553.79 Mb with N50 contig length of 24.75 Mb. Notably, 550.82 Mb (99.46%) were anchored onto 24 chromosomes, and each chromosome contains only one super large contiguous contig. 116.76 Mb (21.08%) repeat sequences, and 23,346 protein coding genes and 19596 non coding RNAs were annotated in the genome of male P. argenteus. In this genome, 98.9% of BUSCOs identified in 3,640 conserved genes; the coverage of 99.98% Illumina short-reads across 99.39% of the genome; a high assembly quality value (QV) of was 51.55. Moreover, we compared the male fish genome with the previous published female’s genome19. 37,301 structural variations (SVs) were identified. 52.82 Mb and 18.05 Mb Segment duplications (SDs) were characterized in our and published assemblies, respectively, and 48.96 Mb PURs were constructed by comparing their chromosomes, which consisted mostly of SDs (39.26 Mb, 80.19%). 36.04 Mb PURs ( >1 Mb, 73.61%) were distributed around the proximal telomeres of the 15 chromosomes.

Based on these data, the quality of our female P. argenteus genome is similar with previous published female P. argenteus, and the gap-free male P. argenteus genome assembly exhibits excellent completeness, continuity and accuracy in combination with the published one, which can be current preferred reference genome of P. argenteus. Overall, the our high quality chromosome-scale genome assemblies of female and male P. argenteus will not only assist in industrialization development, molecular breeding and wild resources recovery of P. argenteus, but also provide a valuable genetic resource for further investigations into various aspects of fish biology and genetics.

Methods

Ethics statement

All fish experiments undertaken in this study adhered to the guidelines stipulated by the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The protocols received approval from the Animal Care and Use Committee of Ningbo University (Approval No. 20151051710).

Sample collection and construction of sequencing libraries

Before sampling, the healthy P. argenteus were anesthetized using MS222 at a concentration of 50 mg/L (Sigma, Shanghai, China). Muscle sample was collected from a healthy female P. argenteus. Genomic DNA was isolated using Qiagen DNA purification kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA). A total of two Illumina paired-end sequencing libraries with 350 bp of insert sizes, one SMRT bell sequencing library with insert sizes of 20 kb, and two Dovetail Genomics Hi-C libraries were constructed according to protocols of different sequence strategies. And then, Illumina paired-end sequencing libraries and Dovetail Genomics Hi-C libraries were sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq X-ten platform, and the SMRT bell sequencing library were sequenced on the PacBio Sequel platform. Raw sequence reads generated by Illumina platform with adapters, with N bases more than 10% and with low-quality bases ( ≤ 5) more than 50% were filtered. A total of 24.42 Gb Illumina paired-reads and 92.21 Gb Hic data obtained. The subreads of PacBio data were filtered with the default parameters, and a total of 9,142,771 reads with an average read length of 7,213 bp and N50 of 12,134 bp were applied for further assembly of female P. argenteus genome (Table 1). Six tissues including blood, brain, kidney, liver, muscle and spleen were sampled for the same fish individual. Subsequently, all six samples were subjected to RNA extraction using RNAiso Pure RNA Isolation Kit (TaKaRa, Japan), which was followed by DNaseI treatment. NanoVue Plus spectrophotometer (GE Healthcare, NJ, USA) was used to assess concentration and quality of the extracted RNAs. All RNA samples were sequenced by Illumina Hiseq X for generating paired-end reads in 150 bp.

For Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) ultra-long reads (the method-Single molecule nanopore DNA sequencing were developed by Oxford Nanopore Technologies), DNA for Nanopore sequencing was extracted from muscle samples collected from a healthy male P. argenteus using the Quiagen DNeasy kit following the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA was eluted into 50 μL and then concentrated to approximately 25 ng/μL using a Zymo DNA Clean and Concentrator Kit. The final elution volume after concentrating was approximately 50 μL. Libraries for Nanopore sequencing were prepared using a 1D genomic ligation kit (SQK-LSK108) following the manufacturer’s instructions with the following modifications: dA-tailing and FFPE repair steps were combined by using 46.5 μL of input DNA, 0.5 μL NAD + , 3.5 μL Ultra II EndPrep buffer and FFPE DNA repair buffer, and 3.0 μL of Ultra II EndPrep Enzyme and FFPE Repair Mix, for a total reaction volume of 60 μL. Subsequent thermocycler conditions were altered to 60 min at 20 °C and 30 min at 65 °C. The remainder of the protocol was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Fifteen microliters of the resulting library was loaded onto a MinION with a R9.4.1 flowcell and run for 48 h using MinKNOW v.2.0. Fastq files were generated from raw Nanopore data using Albacore v.2.3.1. A total of 256,444 reads (26.32 Gb) with an average read length of 102,668 bp and N50 of 100,000 bp were passed for further assembly (Table 2).

Genome size estimation and assembly

According to k-mer analysis (k-mer = 17), the genome size of P. argenteus was estimated using the paired-end Illumina sequencing data, indicating that the genome size of P. argenteus was 573.00 Mb. With the long reads generated from PacBio Sequel platform, the contig assembly of female P. argenteus genome was conducted using the FALCON assembler v.1.2.4 with default parameters20. The primary contigs were subsequently polished with PacBio reads using Quiver v.5.0.121. Then, the Purge Haplotigs software was used to remove redundant contigs from the initial assembly, obtaining a non-redundant contig assembly. Finally, the short reads from Illumina were used to correct any remaining errors in the contig assembly by Pilon v.1.0.2 software22. These processes would yield a final draft genome of female P. argenteus assembly with a total length of 536.33 Mb and contig N50 of 1.79 Mb (Table 3). To generate a chromosome-level genome assembly, Hi-C technology was used to anchor the contigs onto chromosomes. Based on the hierarchical clustering algorithm, the contigs were clustered, ordered and oriented, which were finally anchored into 24 pseudo-chromosome linkage groups using the ALLHiC pipeline v.0.9.13., and a total of 515.41 Mb assembled genome sequences were anchored on 24 chromosomes (Table 3, Fig. 2A).

In order to further improve the quality of genome assembly, an improved assembly strategy was applied to generate a gap-free genome of male P. argenteus assembly with the PacBio high-fidelity (HiFi) long-read sequences and Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) ultra-long reads using hifiasm assembler v.0.19.1. The final assembled genome size of male P. argenteus was 553.79 Mb with an N50 contig length of 24.75 Mb (Table 3). A total of 550.82 Mb assembled genome sequences were anchored on 24 chromosomes without gap (Table 3, Fig. 2B).

The quality of assembled genomes was assessed by the following three methods. In details, the Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs analysis (BUSCO, v 5.1.2)23 was performed to assess the completeness of the genome by searching against 3640 BUSCOs of the actinopterygii_odb10; the package Merqury was used for assessing the quality, completeness of genome assemblies using a reference-free and k-mer-based approach; and we also realigned the Illumina paired reads to the assembly genome with BWA software, for calculating the realignment ratio and coverage depth.

Genome annotation of female and male P. argenteus genome

Both homologous comparison and ab initio prediction methods were used to annotate the repeated sequences within female P. argenteus genome. In details, RepeatProteinMask v.4.0.724 were performed to search against Repbase database25. In addition, four tools of LTR_FINDER v.1.0726, Piler27, RepeatScout v.1.0528 and RepeatModeler29 were first used for de novo constructing the candidate libraries of repetitive elements of P. argenteus. On the basic of the candidate libraries and Repbase database, the dispersed repetitive sequences were identified using RepeatMasker v.4.0.7. Tandem repeat was predicted using software Tandem Repeats Finder (TRF) v. 4.07b30. According to these analyses, about 84.61 Mb repeat sequences were finally revealed, which accounted for 15.78% of the whole female genome (Table 4), and there were 116.76 Mb (21.08%) repeat sequences in the whole male genome (Table 5).

All blast hits remained were concatenated by Solar software v. 0.9.6. Genewise v. 2.4.1 was used to predict the gene structure of the corresponding genomic region on each candidate gene extended upstream and downstream by 1,000 bp32. For de novo gene prediction, five tools of Augustus v. 3.2.333, Geneid v. 1.434, Genescan35, GlimmerHMM v. 3.0.336 and SNAP37 were simultaneously employed for gene prediction in the repeat-masked genome. For transcriptome-based prediction, RNA-seq reads of six different tissues were aligned to the genome using TopHat v.2.0. with default parameters38, and Cufflinks v.2.2.1 was used for reconstruction and assembly of transcripts39. Besides, RNA-seq data were assembled with Trinity, and then the assembled sequences were aligned to female and male P. argenteus genome for gene model prediction using Program to Assemble Spliced Alignment (PASA)40. After successfully performing independent annotation by these three approaches, the non-redundant consensus set of gene structures was finally generated using EvidenceModeler v.1.1.140, and the predicted genes with less than 50 amino acids in length, supported only by ab initio evidence, with expression value < 1 were filtered. Overall, 24,182 and 23,346 protein-coding genes were successfully identified in female and male fish genome, respectively (Table 3).

Based on homologous searches against databases of Swissprot41, KEGG pathway42, NR database and IntrePro43, the functions of protein-coding genes were obtained, in which Pfam domain and Gene Ontology informations were involved in IntrePro database and predicted using InterproScan tool44 based on the conserved protein domains and functional sites. Overall, 95.8%, 91.1%, 83.1%, 99.3% genes show positive hits in NR, Swissprot, KEGG and InterPro, respectively. In summary, a total of 24,070 genes (99.5%) and 22,892 (98.1%) were successfully annotated by function implications or the conserved functional motifs in female and male P. argenteus genomes, respectively (Table 7, Fig. 3).

Data Records

Sequence data availability

The raw data, including Illumina and PacBio sequencing data of the whole genome have been deposited in SRA (Sequence Read Archive) database as SRR27792797-SRR27792828 under sequence read project SRP48696548. The chromosome-level genome assembly of the female and male P. argenteus was deposited in the NCBI genome database under accession number GCA_036937825.149 and GCA_036937835.150, respectively. And the annotation file of female and male genome annotations51,52 can be accessed through the Figshare.

The comparation between male P. argenteus and previous published female P. argenteus genome assemblies

The genome of previous published P. argenteus19 were firstly mapped to our reference genome of male P. argenteus using minimap2 v. 2.22 with parameter of -ax asm5. And then structural variations (SVs, ≥50 bp) identified with Syri software v. 1.7.0 with default parameters. Segment duplications (SDs) were detected using BISER v1.4, and high-quality SDs were identified by following requirements: gap-compressed identity ≥90%, ≤50% gapped sequence in the alignment, aligned sequence of ≥1 kb and ≤70% satellite sequence as determined by RepeatMasker v.4.0.7. A total of 19,735 deletions (3.09 Mb), 17,422 insertions (2.84 Mb), 43 inversions (2.39 Mb) and 101 translocations (0.85 Mb) were identified (Table 8). 52.82 Mb and 18.05 Mb SDs were characterized in male and female P. argenteus assemblies, respectively (Table 9). SVs and SDs provided abundant genetic information for the development and evolution of P. argenteus. Besides, many previously unresolved regions (PURs, ≥1 kb) were constructed by comparing the chromosomes of male and female P. argenteus assemblies, for a total size of 48.96 Mb (Fig. 4). Notably, these PURs consisted mostly of SDs (39.26 Mb, 80.19%). PURs greater than 1 Mb in length, totaling 36.04 Mb, accounted for the majority, comprising 73.61%, and almost all of which were distributed around the proximal telomeres of the 15 chromosomes. Telomere regions in all chromosomes of male and female P. argenteus assemblies by searching for 6-bp repeat units (TTAGGG/CCCTAA) also indicated that more intact telomeric sequences were assembled in male P. argenteus assembly. Overall, the gap-free male P. argenteus genome assembly exhibits excellent completeness, continuity and accuracy in combination with previous published female P. argenteus assembly.

Technical Validation

The quality of assembled genome was assessed by the following three methods. In details, Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs analysis (BUSCO, v.5.1.2) was performed to assess the completeness of the genome by searching against 3640 BUSCOs of the actinopterygii_odb10, which indicated that 97.9% and 98.9% of BUSCOs recovered in genome assemblies of female and male P. argenteus, respectively (Table 3). The Merqury software was applied to estimate an assembly quality value (QV) using a reference-free and k-mer basedapproach, which resulted in a high assembly quality value of 51.55 for male genome and exceed the Vertebrate Genome Project (VGP) standard of QV4053. The clean short reads generated by the Illumina platform were re-mapped to the genome assemblies of P. argenteus using BWA software. For male P. argenteus, 99.98% Illumina short-reads were mapped the genome assembly of male P. argenteus and the coverage reached to 99.39%; for female P. argenteus, 97% Illumina short-reads were mapped the genome assembly of male P. argenteus and the coverage reached to 98.01%. Overall, the genome assemblies of P. argenteus in the present study showed excellent completeness and superior accuracy.

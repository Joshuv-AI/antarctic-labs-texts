The potential of non-invasive remote dielectric sensing in predicting short-term prognosis of patients with pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Dandan Chen, Dan Tian, Qi Jin, Lei Zhang, Xiaochun Zhang, Mingfei Li, Wenzhi Pan, Qianzhou Lv, Daxin Zhou, Junbo Ge, Lihua Guan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary hypertension (PH) is exhibits a profound pathophysiological association with right heart failure. The non-invasive Remote Dielectric Sensing (ReDS™) technology demonstrates exceptional sensitivity in identifying pathological states, ranging from mild cardiac compensation to severe pulmonary edema. This study aims to develop a PH-ReDS predictive model using ReDS™ technology, leveraging real-world clinical data to evaluate the risk of all-cause mortality or clinical deterioration in patients with PH.MethodsWe conducted a prospective, convenience-sampled observational pilot study involving adult patients with PH admitted to or followed as outpatients in the cardiology department of our institution. ReDS™ Pro was used to measure dielectric sensing values. Clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical record system of our hospital. Patients were followed up for 6 months post-discharge. The primary endpoints defined as all-cause mortality or PH-related clinical deterioration.ResultsA total of 202 patients were included, with 24.8% (n = 50) being male. Lung fluid was significantly correlated with left atrial diameter (r = 0.338, p < 0.001), left ventricular ejection fraction (r = -0.256, p < 0.001), and mean right atrial pressure (mRAP; r = 0.219, p = 0.007). Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified lung fluid level, renal insufficiency, and NT-proBNP as significant independent risk factors for PH deterioration. ROC analysis revealed that lung fluid and mRAP effectively discriminated between patients with and without short-term clinical deterioration related to PH, with optimal cut-off values of 30.5% for lung fluid and 6.5 mmHg for mRAP. The Combined PH-ReDS models incorporating lung fluid and mRAP significantly enhanced predictive accuracy, achieving the highest AUC value of 0.733 (95% CI: 0.551–0.916, p = 0.010). Patients with elevated lung fluid levels were found to have a significantly higher risk of short-term clinical deterioration related to PH, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 3.670 (95% CI: 1.274–10.571, p = 0.016).ConclusionsThe PH-ReDS model demonstrated robust predictive efficacy for all-cause mortality or clinical deterioration in patients with PH. Notably, elevated lung fluid levels were significantly associated with an increased risk of short-term PH-related clinical deterioration, suggesting its potential as an early intervention indicator in clinical practice.Trial registrationThis trial was retrospectively registered with Clinical Trials. gov on 2025/04/24. The registration number was NCT06942871 .

Background

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) represents a global health challenge with significant cross-age-group impact, affecting an estimated 1% of the global population [1]. PH is characterized by restricted pulmonary arterial blood flow and sustained elevation of pulmonary arterial pressure, which subsequently imposes increased right ventricular afterload and culminates in right ventricular failure. This hemodynamic cascade constitutes the primary driver of morbidity and mortality in affected individuals.

PH demonstrates a profound pathophysiological association with right heart failure (RHF). Emerging evidence indicates that 32% of acute heart failure patients exhibit residual pulmonary congestion (≥ 39%) at the time of scheduled discharge, highlighting the clinical challenge of suboptimal decongestion in this population [2]. Elevated pulmonary arterial pressure increases right ventricular afterload, culminating in RHF. RHF, in turn, exacerbates pulmonary congestion through dual mechanisms: increasing central venous pressure and impairing lymphatic drainage, a pathophysiological interplay that is particularly pronounced in patients with PH [3, 4]. Therefore, the assessment of pulmonary fluid accumulation is inherently integral to the management of heart failure (HF) [5, 6].

The Remote Dielectric Sensing (ReDS™) lung fluid monitor represents the world’s first non-invasive device designed for HF monitoring and management. By measuring thoracic dielectric constant variations, this technology directly reflects lung fluid content with a detection range of 15–60%, enabling sensitive identification of pathological states from mild cardiac compensation to severe pulmonary edema. Although ReDS™ cannot directly measure pulmonary artery pressure, changes in lung fluid content may indirectly reflect disease progression in specific types of PH. While research on ReDS™ technology in HF has advanced gradually, there remains a paucity of literature addressing innovative frontiers such as early warning of RHF in PH patients. Therefore, this study aims to leverage ReDS™ technology to develop a PH-ReDS predictive model, utilizing real-world clinical data to assess the risk of all-cause mortality or clinical deterioration in PH patients.

Methods

Study population

We prospectively enrolled adult inpatients and outpatients with PH admitted to the Department of Cardiology at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, between April 2024 and March 2025 using convenience sampling. Eligibility criteria included: age 18 to 85 years (no gender restriction); hemodynamic confirmation of PH via right heart catheterization (RHC) with mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) > 20 mmHg. Exclusion criteria included thoracic deformities or injuries precluding proper device fit (e.g., severe scoliosis, flail chest); inability to use the ReDS™ Pro non-invasive lung fluid monitor due to physical characteristics (height < 155 cm or > 190 cm; body mass index [BMI] < 22 kg/m² or > 39 kg/m²); history of left ventricular assist device implantation or cardiac transplantation; congenital cardiac anomalies or intrathoracic masses affecting right lung anatomy; and hemodynamic profiles inconsistent with PH diagnosis. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University (Approval No. B2022-593R), with all participants providing written informed consent in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

ReDS™ pro measurements

The measurement of ReDS values was performed with the patient in a seated position under normal breathing conditions. It was conducted on the morning of the second day post-admission or at the end of an outpatient visit. The device’s sensor vest was properly placed on the patient’s chest to ensure optimal sensor-skin contact. Upon activiting the measurement program, the device emitted low-power electromagnetic signals traversing the thorax and lungs, measuring lung dielectric constant (impedance) and calculated the percentage of lung fluid. The manufacturer-recommended normal range is between 20% and 35%. The entire measurement process took approximately 45 s.

Data collection

Clinical data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system, including demographics, clinical diagnoses, medical history, ancillary examinations and laboratory test, surgical procedures, and medication use. Participant management adhered to guideline-based recommendations [1]. Patients underwent 6-month post-discharge follow-up via telephone or outpatient visits. The primary endpoints were defined as all-cause mortality or PH-related clinical deterioration, including: (1) all-cause death or lung transplantation; (2) rehospitalization due to PH-related heart failure; (3) deterioration in World Health Organization (WHO) functional class. Data were collected by trained researchers and entered into a preformatted database. All data underwent double-entered and were subjected to routine logical validation and consistency checks.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using the independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages) and analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Correlations between ReDS values and clinical indicators were evaluated using Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation coefficients. Patients were stratified into high/low lung fluid groups based on the median lung fluid measurements, establishing a binary outcome variable. Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to assess associations between clinical indicators and the binary outcome. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis, along with clinically relevant factors, were entered into multivariate Cox regression models using stepwise backward elimination. Biomarker optimal cut-off values were determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and survival analyses were evaluated via Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank testing. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 26.0, IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA), and two-sided p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of PH patients

A total of 202 patients were enrolled in this study (Table 1). The median age was 47.5 (IQR 35.0, 66.2) years, with 50 (24.8%) being male. Age distribution showed statistically significant across PH subtypes (p < 0.001), with median ages of 41.0 years in the pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) group, 67.5 years in the PH due to left heart disease (PH-LHD) group, 64.0 years in the chronic thromboembolic PH (CTEPH) group, and 72.0 years in the PH due to lung disease and/or Hypoxia (PH-LD/Hx) group. Significantly different prevalences of diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, and history of stroke were observed among PH subtypes (p = 0.001, p < 0.001, and p = 0.038, respectively). N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels also varied significantly across subtypes (p = 0.036). In terms of pharmacotherapy, there were notable differences in the use of monotherapy, dual therapy, and triple therapy for PH (p < 0.001). Additionally, beta-blocker and renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitor usage showed significant inter-subtype variations (both p < 0.001).

Comparison of echocardiographic, hemodynamics parameters and reds values among different PH groups

Echocardiographic parameters demonstrated significant inter-subtype differences in aortic outflow tract diameter (AOD, p = 0.002), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD, p = 0.015), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD, p = 0.006), interventricular septum thickness (IVS, p = 0.002), and left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPW, p = 0.042). Pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) demonstrated the most significant inter-group disparity (p < 0.001), with the PAH group exhibiting the highest median value (78mmHg). Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) also showed statistical significance (p = 0.010), with the PH-LHD group presenting a lower median. Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.757). However, the TAPSE/PASP ratio revealed marked inter-subtype differences (p < 0.001) (Table 2). Hemodynamic analyses further indicated significant inter-subtype variations in mPAP (p < 0.001) and pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR, p = 0.045) (Table 3). Notably, lung fluid levels demonstrated significant inter-group differences (p = 0.001), with the PH-LD/Hx subgroup exhibiting the highest median value (37.5%), as documented in Table 3; Fig. 1.

Association between reds values and clinical indicators

Echocardiographic analyses revealed significant correlations between lung fluid levels and LAD in the overall cohort (r = 0.338, p < 0.001), PAH subgroup (r = 0.284, p = 0.001), and CTEPH subgroup (r = 0.242, p = 0.122). Additionally, lung fluid demonstrated significant associations with LVEF in the overall cohort (r = −0.256, p < 0.001), PAH subgroup (r = −0.195, p = 0.024), and CTEPH subgroup (r = −0.321, p = 0.038). For pulmonary artery pressure parameters, lung fluid exhibited a marginally significant correlation with PASP in the PH-LHD subgroup (r = 0.444, p = 0.050). Hemodynamic analyses showed significant correlations between lung fluid and mRAP in the overall cohort (r = 0.219, p = 0.007) and PAH subgroup (r = 0.247, p = 0.013). However, no significant correlations were observed between lung fluid levels and key hemodynamic parameters, including mPAP, pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP), PVR, or cardiac index across different PH subtypes (Table 4).

Determination of risk factors for short-term clinical deterioration related to PH

Univariate Cox regression analysis identified several factors significantly associated with an increased risk of short-term PH-related clinical deterioration, including lung fluid levels, renal insufficiency, LAD, mRAP, and uric acid levels. Conversely, hemoglobin levels were associated with a reduced risk of short-term clinical deterioration. Subsequent multivariate Cox regression analysis indicated that lung fluid levels were independently associated with the outcome, yielding a hazard ratio (HR) of 5.647 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.056–30.202, p = 0.043). Renal insufficiency emerged as a significant independent risk factor, with an HR of 46.664 (95% CI 3.708–587.170, p = 0.003). NT-proBNP also remained a significant predictor in the backward stepwise regression model, with an HR of 1.000 (95% CI 1.000–1.000, p = 0.021) (Table 5).

Lung fluid as predictive biomarkers for short-term clinical deterioration related to PH

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that both lung fluid levels and mRAP exhibited adequate discriminative ability for short-term PH-related clinical deterioration, with optimal cut-off values of 30.5% (AUC = 0.695, 95% CI: 0.564–0.826, p = 0.010) for lung fluid and 6.5 mmHg (AUC = 0.693, 95% CI: 0.523–0.862, p = 0.034) for mRAP, respectively. In contrast, NT-proBNP and PASP showed no predictive value for the outcome. The combined PH-ReDS model integrating lung fluid and mRAP significantly enhanced predictive accuracy, yielding the highest AUC of 0.733 (95% CI: 0.551–0.916, p = 0.010) (Table 6; Fig. 2). Stratification by the lung fluid cut-off value revealed that patients with elevated levels of lung fluid had a significantly higher risk of short-term PH-related clinical deterioration (HR = 3.670, 95% CI: 1.274–10.571, p = 0.016) (Fig. 3).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study represents the first empirical investigation into the short-term risk of clinical deterioration in PH patients with elevated lung fluid levels measured by ReDS™ Pro. A key finding is that patients with lung fluid levels ≥ 30.5% exhibit a significantly increased risk of short-term PH-related clinical deterioration. These results therefore provide preliminary evidence for the clinical specificity of ReDS™ Pro in identifying high-risk PH populations.

Pulmonary congestion has long been considered a cardinal feature of left heart failure, wherein elevated pulmonary capillary pressure drives pulmonary edema. However, emerging evidence indicates that RHF can also precipitate pulmonary congestion through diverse mechanisms, particularly in patients with PH [4, 7]. Naeije et al. [8] underscored that RHF in PAH patients may elevate PAWP, thereby increasing the propensity for pulmonary congestion. D’Alto et al. [3] investigated the pathophysiology and clinical manifestations of RHF-induced pulmonary congestion in PAH, demonstrating its higher prevalence in idiopathic PAH (IPAH) and strong association with right ventricular dysfunction. This relationship is potentially mediated by right ventricular-pulmonary arterial (RV-PA) uncoupling. Additionally, pulmonary congestion may exacerbate lung fluid accumulation via alternative pathways, such as impaired lymphatic drainage, highlighting the pivotal role of RHF in modulating pulmonary congestion in PAH.

The management of PH is heavily dependent on pharmacotherapy, yet the absence of effective non-invasive tools for continuous disease monitoring has challenged clinicians to timely adjust treatment strategies. RHC, an invasive procedure, directly measures hemodynamic parameters including intracardiac pressures and PAWP, serving as the gold standard for PH diagnosis. It enables precise differentiation of pre-capillary/post-capillary PH and assessment of PVR, but entails risks of vascular injury, infection, and other complications. Characterized by complex operations and high costs, RHC is ill-suited for dynamic monitoring [1]. By contrast, the ReDS™ Pro system utilizes dielectric sensing technology to measure thoracic dielectric constant variations, thereby reflecting lung water content. Although the ReDS™ Pro system cannot yet replace RHC for obtaining hemodynamic parameters or performing etiological differentiation, it distinguishes itself with full non-invasiveness, real-time dynamic assessment capability, and precise quantification of lung fluid volume [9]. With a measurement range spanning 15–60%, it can sensitively detect pathological states from mild compensation to severe pulmonary edema [10]. Studies in HF have underscored the superiority of ReDS™ technology. Izumida T et al. [11] reported that elevated ReDS values during hospitalization serve as a novel prognostic biomarker in congestive HF patients. Concurrently, evidence suggests that ReDS-guided management may reduce HF readmission rates [12, 13]. While ReDS™ Pro cannot directly measure mPAP, this study demonstrated that in select PH subtypes, changes in lung fluid levels correlate with echocardiographic and hemodynamic parameters, potentially serving as an indirect surrogate for disease progression.

One of the key objectives of non-invasive lung fluid assessment in PH patients is to differentiate PH-LHD from PAH characterized by increased PVR. This distinction is crucial for streamlining diagnostic workflows and guiding therapeutic strategies. Our data revealed significant inter-subgroup variations in lung fluid levels, with PH-LHD patients exhibiting notably higher levels than those with PAH. In a prior study, PH-LHD patients were stratified into isolated post-capillary PH (IpcPH, PVR < 3.0 Wood units [WU]) and combined post/pre-capillary PH (CpcPH, PVR ≥ 3.0 WU). Findings showed that during exercise, CpcPH patients exhibited reduced cardiac output, oxygen delivery, and peak oxygen consumption, along with severe RV-PA decoupling, despite having similar PAWP levels to IpcPH patients. Notably, CpcPH patients displayed more severe pulmonary congestion than IpcPH patients or controls, despite higher PVR. This enhanced congestion was linked to arterial hypoxemia, reduced alveolar ventilation, impaired pulmonary oxygen diffusion, and worsened ventilation-perfusion mismatch [14]. These observations highlight that pulmonary congestion varies not only across PH subtypes but also among individuals with distinct hemodynamic profiles within the same subtype. Thus, the clinical utility of lung fluid monitoring across all PH patient populations warrants further investigation to define its role in precision management.

In addition, our analysis identified renal insufficiency as a significant independent risk factor for PH deterioration, consistent with prior evidence. Bitker et al. [15] demonstrated that chronic kidney disease (CKD) is common at PAH diagnosis and independently associated with increased mortality. RHF may precipitate renal hypoperfusion and congestion, which correlate with declines in estimated glomerular filtration rates (eGFR). Patients with end-stage renal disease often face heightened risk of pulmonary edema. Furthermore, among CKD patients undergoing RHC, the prevalence of PH is elevated and linked to poor survival, particularly in those with CpcPH [16]. Future studies should explore the underlying mechanisms driving these associations.

Our findings carry important clinical implications. However, this study also has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the detection of the ReDS™ Pro is restricted by various conditions, such as patient individual factors (BMI, postural changes, etc.), comorbidities (fluid distribution changes caused by HF, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, renal insufficiency, etc.), and measurement conditions (pacemaker electrodes, uncalibrated equipment, etc.). Although we listed some of these factors in the exclusion criteria, we still cannot achieve objective control over these factors. Second, the study cohort predominantly comprised patients with PAH, followed by CTEPH and PH-LHD, with only four patients having underlying pulmonary diseases. This distribution likely contributed to the observed baseline statistical differences across subgroups. Third, the study included both outpatient and inpatient PH cases. For some outpatients, the time gap between echocardiographic, RHC, and lung fluid tests exceeded three months, possibly causing temporal bias. The observational design of the study precludes the establishment of causality. Thus, caution is warranted when interpreting correlations between ReDS values and echocardiographic or hemodynamic parameters.

Conclusions

The ReDS™ Pro system has demonstrated substantial potential in the management of PH patients. This study has preliminarily established a correlation between lung fluid levels and short-term prognosis in PH, while also uncovering the predictive value of combining lung fluid measurements with mRAP for assessing PH deterioration. Future research integrating right heart function markers (e.g., TAPSE, RAP) and biomarkers (e.g., NT-proBNP) is expected to provide critical support for comprehensive PH management strategies.

ZmHSCF1 enhances Agrobacterium-mediated transformation frequency in commercial maize inbred lines by promoting embryogenic callus proliferation
Authors: Yingshuang Li, Yan Geng, Xiangyu Qing, Xiaoya Yin, Feng Xu, Liangliang Huang, Renzhao Zhu, Baojian Chen, Tianxia Yang, Weibin Song, Haiming Zhao, Jinsheng Lai, Yunlu Shi, Shengnan Liu
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Maize (Zea mays L.) is a globally crucial crop that exhibits serious genotype dependency, which renders its genetic transformation challenging. Agrobacterium (Agrobacterium tumefaciens)-mediated transformation depends on (1) the recipient’s susceptibility to Agrobacterium infection and (2) the ability to form embryogenic callus. At present, various strategies, such as pre-treatment with LaCl3, inhibition of isoflavone reductase (ICS)-related immunity genes, or disruption of the negative regulatory factor hydroxyproline-rich glycoprotein (ZmHRGP), have successfully increased the infection frequency of Agrobacterium (Liu et al., 2022, 2025; Dodds et al., 2025). However, several elite maize inbred lines that exhibit a high infection frequency remain recalcitrant to transformation due to low callus initiation frequency or the difficulty in forming embryogenic callus, severely limiting the regeneration of genetically modified plants.

Previous breakthroughs in genotype dependency in maize have identified key regulatory factors—including Baby Boom (BBM), Wuschel2 (WUS2), growth-regulating factors (GRFs), GRF-interacting factors (GIFs), and regeneration factor 1 (REF1), as plant elicitor peptide (Lowe et al., 2016; Debernardi et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2024). With the exceptions of the five DuPont Pioneer inbred lines, the three tropical lines used in the BBM–WUS2 transformation experiments, and the recalcitrant inbred line B73 employed in limited transformation validations, the majority of transformations have been carried out in the highly transformable lines Hi-II, B104, and A188 (Lowe et al., 2016; Hernandes-Lopes et al., 2023). However, those lines have disadvantages: Hi-II hybrids have a heterogeneous genetic background that requires backcrossing before phenotypic evaluation (Yassitepe et al., 2021); B104 is late-flowering, limiting its usage in regions with shorter growing seasons (Feys et al., 2018); and A188 demonstrates inferior agronomic traits and high environmental sensitivity (Lin et al., 2021). As transferring the target gene into elite inbred lines for breeding requires complex backcrossing procedures, there is a risk of unintended modifications or loss of desirable traits in elite parental lines, ultimately compromising breeding efficiency.

To address this bottleneck, we identified novel genes with broad applicability and potential to enhance embryogenic callus formation, based on a previous single-cell transcriptome analysis of regenerating mesophyll protoplasts in Arabidopsis (Xu et al., 2021). We performed a comprehensive phylogenetic analysis of 16 genes (WUS, DRN, LBD16, CUC2, LBD17, YUC4, ERF15, LEC1, WOX7, PLT7, XTH20, CEP2, WOX5, ANAC071, LBD18, and LBD29) possibly associated with pluripotency. Through stringent selection criteria, we identified 39 highly homologous genes in the maize genome (Supplemental Figure 1). An integrated analysis of tissue-specific expression data from the Maize Genetics and Genomics Database identified 17 genes expressed in meristems and embryos, all lacking functional characterization for their regeneration potential (Supplemental Table 1). We successfully cloned the full-length coding sequences of 13 candidate genes and introduced overexpression constructs for each one into the elite maize line LH244, establishing a stable transformation system. By monitoring the physiological status and initiation frequency of callus (Supplemental Figure 2), we identified a specific APETALA2 (AP2) transcription factor—heat shock-complementing factor 1 (ZmHSCF1: Zm00001d046913)—that exhibited significant activity during callus formation.

Subsequently, we investigated the role of ZmHSCF1 in infection susceptibility and callus formation. We infected LH244 immature embryos with Agrobacterium cells harboring either a construct carrying DsRed alone as a neutral reporter (control) or a construct carrying DsRed and a ZmHSCF1 overexpression cassette (DsRed+ZmHSCF1-OE) (Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C). Fluorescence microscopy of scutellum-side colonization after 2 days of co-cultivation revealed no significant difference in infection frequency between the DsRed+ZmHSCF1-OE and control groups, demonstrating that ZmHSCF1 does not affect infection susceptibility (Supplemental Figure 4). Observation and analysis of the ZmHSCF1-OE calli induced for 20 days revealed a 6.1% increment (97.8% vs. 91.7%) in callus initiation frequency, and a significant 1.33-fold expansion in average callus area (1.59 ± 0.04 vs. 1.20 ± 0.03 cm2) relative to the control group (Figure 1A–1C; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3B). Conversely, the ZmHSCF1-CRISPR calli showed a comparable callus initiation frequency to the control group (91.9% vs. 91.7%) and a 17.5% smaller average callus area (1.10 ± 0.02 cm2) (Figures 1A–1C; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3D). These results suggest a supportive but non-essential role of ZmHSCF1 in maize callus development. Furthermore, assessing the proportion of red fluorescence protein (RFP)-positive DsRed+ZmHSCF1-OE calli showed a 21.2% increment (51.5% vs. 30.3%) compared to the control group (Figure 1D; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C). In addition, quantitative analysis of fluorescence coverage and intensity revealed that ZmHSCF1-OE increased coverage from 3.6% to 49.3% and intensity from 2224.41 to 60 671.03 per positive callus, representing 13.55- and 27.28-fold increases, respectively (Supplemental Figures 3A, 3C, and 5). Continuous monitoring of the RFP-positive calli cultured for 7 and 14 days showed that the RFP-positive callus average proliferation rate in the DsRed+ZmHSCF1-OE group was 2.02 ± 0.15 mm2/d, representing a 21.64-fold increase compared to the control group (0.09 ± 0.04 mm2/d) (Figures 1E and 1F; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C). The callus formed by the action of ZmHSCF1 exhibited characteristic morphological features of type II embryogenic calli, as defined by D’Halluin et al., 1992, including pale yellow coloration, friable texture, and a rapid proliferation rate. To assess their pluripotency, we compared the regeneration potential of calli derived from ZmHSCF1-OE and ZmHSCF1-CRISPR constructs after a 20-day differentiation culture (Figure 1A; Supplemental Figures 3A, 3B, 3D, and 6). In the ZmHSCF1-OE group, a statistically significant enhancement was observed, with an average regeneration frequency of 24.1% compared to 16.1% in the control (Figure 1G; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3B). By contrast, in the ZmHSCF1-CRISPR group, the regenerative capacity was severely compromised, with a regeneration frequency of only 7.7% (Figure 1G; Supplemental Figure 3D). The overall transformation frequency followed the same trend as the regeneration frequency, with ZmHSCF1 overexpression significantly increasing the transformation frequency from 13.3% to 20.6%, whereas CRISPR–Cas9-mediated targeted disruption of ZmHSCF1 reduced the transformation frequency to 6.4% (Supplemental Figures 3A, 3B, 3D, and 7). ZmHSCF1- overexpression enhances Agrobacterium-mediated transformation frequency by promoting embryogenic callus formation and proliferation. Importantly, ZmHSCF1 overexpression had no significant detrimental effects on plant development or fertility; T0 transgenic plants were phenotypically normal during the tasseling and silking stages, as were their ears (Supplemental Figures 3A, 3B, and 8).

To investigate the regulatory mechanisms of ZmHSCF1 in callus development, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis was performed on calli from the control and ZmHSCF1-OE groups collected at 14 days of induction culture during rapid proliferation (Supplemental Figures 3A and 3B). Analysis of the RNA-seq data identified 1159 differentially expressed genes in ZmHSCF1-OE calli compared with the control, including 358 upregulated and 801 downregulated genes (Figure 1H). Notably, the upregulated genes were enriched in functional annotations associated with auxin response mechanisms (GO:0 009733), auxin-activated signaling pathways (GO:0 009734), shoot apical meristem initiation (GO:0010014), meristem growth (GO:0 010075), and shoot system morphogenesis (GO:0048367) (Figure 1I). It is well documented that detached tissues cultured on auxin-rich callus-inducing medium can reprogram somatic cells into an unorganized pluripotent callus. Strikingly, our RNA-seq analysis revealed that ZmHSCF1 specifically increases the transcript levels of genes related to auxin biosynthesis, transport, and response, as well as growth-regulating factors (Supplemental Figure 9A). RT–qPCR analysis further confirmed the higher transcript levels of key auxin-related genes in ZmHSCF1-OE calli. Among these, the expression levels of PIN-FORMED (ZmPINs), auxin response factors (ZmARFs), indole-3-acetic acid inducible 33 (ZmIAA33), and ZmGRFs were remarkably upregulated, suggesting potential roles as downstream effectors in ZmHSCF1-mediated callus proliferation (Supplemental Figures 3A, 3B, and 9B). These findings provide preliminary insights into ZmHSCF1-mediated enhancement of callus proliferation through transcriptional upregulation of key auxin-related genes. The regulatory mechanisms underlying ZmHSCF1-mediated embryogenic callus proliferation will require further investigation.

To investigate the potential of ZmHSCF1-mediated enhancement in transformation of commercial maize parental inbred lines, which urgently need to break through, we selected two recalcitrant inbred lines, PH4CV (Pioneer335 male parental line) and Zheng58 (Zhengdan958 female parental line), as transformation recipients. ZmHSCF1-OE significantly enhanced callus initiation frequency in both PH4CV (from 14.5% to 45.5%) and Zheng58 (from 21.5% to 48.2%) (Figures 1J and 1K; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C). The proportion of RFP-positive callus also rose markedly from 12.9% to 38.5% in PH4CV and from 5.4% to 32.5% in Zheng58, demonstrating three- and six-fold improvements relative to the control construct (Figure 1L; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C). Importantly, this genetic modification achieved a substantial breakthrough in transformation frequency, elevating rates from 0.9% to 9.6% in PH4CV and from 0% to 7.2% in Zheng58 (Figures 1M and 1N; Supplemental Figures 3A and 3C).

In summary, we have identified a novel regeneration regulator, ZmHSCF1, that enhances embryogenic callus formation and proliferation by modulating downstream auxin response factors and growth regulators. Upregulation of ZmHSCF1 significantly improves transformation frequency and helps overcome the long-standing bottleneck of genetic transformation in commercial maize parental inbred lines. This breakthrough should enable the direct genetic modification of commercial maize parental inbred lines and accelerate the implementation of molecular breeding for crop improvement.

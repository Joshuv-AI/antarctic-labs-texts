Initial experience with implant of Aveir VR and DR leadless pacemakers in Spain. New insights about battery longevity
Authors: D Pérez Díez, I Mosquera Pérez
Published: 2025

Leadless pacemakers have emerged as an alternative to conventional transvenous systems, offering the advantage of avoiding complications associated with leads and subcutaneous generators. Their adoption in clinical practice is steadily increasing [1,4].

The Aveir VR (Abbott) leadless pacemaker received FDA approval in March 2022. Subsequently, in July 2023, following the pivotal AVEIR DR i2i clinical trial (NCT05252702) [2], both the dual-chamber Aveir DR and the atrial Aveir AR were approved, the latter specifically for isolated AAI(R) stimulation.

The Aveir DR represents the first implantable and retrievable dual-chamber leadless pacemaker. It employs an active fixation mechanism, allowing pre-fixation mapping of sensitivity, impedance, and pacing thresholds via intracardiac electrogram acquisition. This facilitates optimal positioning and reduces the need for repositioning and the associated risk of cardiac perforation [2]. The atrial system features a second electrically active internal helix (cathode) anchored at the base of the right atrial appendage, a region with greater tissue thickness. An additional innovation is the integration of a beat-to-beat bidirectional communication system (i2i), which serves as the cornerstone of the AVEIR DR dual-chamber leadless pacemaker functionality. This system enables wireless bidirectional communication between the atrial and ventricular pacemakers on a beat-to-beat basis, thereby maintaining continuous atrioventricular (AV) synchrony comparable to that achieved by conventional dual-chamber systems [3]. This pioneering technology utilizes low-energy, subthreshold pulses transmitted between the implanted devices via the blood pool and myocardial tissue. These high-frequency data pulses are seamlessly delivered with each locally paced or sensed event, without compromising pacing performance or intrinsic cardiac sensing.

We conducted a retrospective observational study describing the initial experience of two tertiary care centers in the use of the Aveir leadless system in Spain. Twenty-six patients were consecutively included between May and December 2024, all of whom completed one-month follow-up. Echocardiographic guidance was used for right femoral venous puncture, using local anesthesia and conscious sedation (midazolam and fentanyl). The ventricular device was implanted under fluoroscopic guidance in the mid-septal position in 54 % of cases and the apical septal position in 46 %. The atrial device was positioned in 50 % of cases at the recommended 11 o'clock location in the 30° left anterior oblique view, in 33 % between 11 and 12 o'clock, and in 17 % between 8 and 9 o'clock under fluoroscopic guidance. The mean age of the cohort was 74 years, with 69 % being male. The primary pacing indication was advanced atrioventricular block (79.2 %). Leadless pacing was selected primarily due to active or recurrent infections (34.6 %). The mean procedure duration was 81.2 minutes for the VR device and 154 minutes for the DR device. Device implantation was successful in 96.2 % of patients after approximately one to two attempts, with the exception of the case of cardiac tamponade, which was considered to be secondary to multiple device repositioning attempts in the contexto of a dilated right ventricle, resulting in exposure of the fixation helix against the free wall. Management of cardiac tamponade was performed in a routine manner with urgent pericardiocentesis, achieving drainage of 400 mL of blood within 24 hours, followed by catheter removal on the following day. This is consistent with the 1.5 % incidence of pericardial effusion reported in the LEADLESS II Phase 2 trial [3].

Our findings support the feasibility and safety of Aveir system implantation in real-world clinical practice. Ventricular electrical parameters at implantation and follow-up were excellent and consistent with those observed in conventional systems. Although experience with atrial devices remains limited, no increase in complications was observed, despite the greater procedural complexity and fluoroscopy requirements. Initial atrial parameters (lower impedance, lower sensing amplitude, and higher thresholds) showed marked improvement during short-term follow-up. Estimated battery longevity for the Aveir VR in VVIR mode is up to 18.3 years, decreasing to 9.7 years when used in dual-chamber configurations. The atrial device exhibited comparable longevity when programmed in AAIR mode (up to 16.2 years), with shorter battery longevities observed in VDD or DDD configurations (6.3 years), despite confirming adequate communication between the devices.

These data are similar than published recently to evaluate battery longevities as part of a prospective, multicenter clinical trial from the AVEIR DR i2i Study [5].

Although limited by a short follow-up period and small sample size, this study highlights the early safety and performance of the Aveir system. Further technological refinements, particularly in reducing the energy burden of beat-to-beat communication like a new programming option has been introduced that delivers atrial pacing with backup ventricular pacing when needed (AAI + VVI backup), intended for patients with largely intact atrioventricular conduction and limited ventricular pacing needs.

In the future, advancements in battery design and improved consumption profiles will be essential to extend device longevity.

Food Effects on the Pharmacokinetics of Single Dose and Steady-State Ralinepag, a Novel Oral Prostacyclin Agonist, in Healthy Volunteers
Authors: Daniel Lachant, Cristian E Botta, George Giannakoulas, Natalie Patzlaff, Scott Seaman, Meredith Broderick, Maria Guido, Søren Mellemkjær, Zeenat Safdar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a rare, progressive disorder characterized by pathological pulmonary vascular remodeling leading to right heart failure. Ralinepag extended-release (XR), is a once-daily oral prostacyclin (IP) receptor to treat PAH. This study evaluated the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics (PK) of multiple ascending doses of ralinepag XR tablets in fed and fasted healthy adult volunteers.Study Design and MethodsA single-center, open-label, nonrandomized, multiple-dose titration study of two cohorts (fed (n = 13) or fasted (n = 15)) with once-daily dosing of ralinepag XR for 5 days, with up to four sequential dose escalations. Dosing began at 60 µg and escalated every 5 days as tolerated (120, 180, 240, and 300 µg). Doses were administered after a 10-h overnight fast or a moderate-fat breakfast. PK parameters assessed were maximum observed concentration (Cmax), area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to 24 h postdose (AUC(0–24)), and time of maximum observed concentration (Tmax) on day 1 (single 60 μg dose); and Cmax, AUC(0–24), and Tmax at steady state.ResultsRalinepag XR exhibited minimal differences in PK parameters between fed and fasted states in the first 24 h. Multiple-dose administration showed a dose-dependent increase in exposure and a median 4–10 h Tmax at steady state, independent of fed status. Ralinepag accumulation was approximately 2.1-fold after 5 days of dosing at 60 μg in both fed and fasted states, consistent with a mean half-life of 20–36 h. There were no serious adverse events attributable to ralinepag. The most commonly reported adverse events were headache, jaw pain, and nausea with no differences between groups.ConclusionsFood status did not significantly affect the PK or safety of ralinepag XR.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40256-026-00807-7.

Key Points

Ralinepag XR pharmacokinetics were similar with food or on an empty stomach.Daily administration raised plasma concentration of ralinepag XR, with peak concentrations 4–10 h after dosing.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a rare, progressive vasculopathy characterized by pathological remodeling of the pulmonary arterioles, leading to increased pulmonary vascular resistance, right ventricular strain, and ultimately right heart failure if left untreated [1–3]. Despite advancements in therapeutic options contributing to improved overall median survival rates, PAH continues to be a severe condition with high mortality [1, 2].

PAH development is linked to dysregulation of vascular signaling molecules, with a lack of prostacyclins playing a crucial role in disease progression [3, 4]. Stimulation of the prostacyclin (IP) receptor increases cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) production, leading to relaxation of blood vessels and a reduction in pulmonary arterial pressure [5]. Parenteral prostacyclin therapy is considered the gold standard for treating PAH owing to its consistent drug levels and potent, continuous receptor binding [3, 4, 6]. However, parenterally administered prostacyclin therapies have short half-lives and require continuous infusion or frequent administration to maintain prostacyclin receptor coverage and clinical efficacy [7]. Parenteral therapy can be challenging to manage, and patients sometimes refuse treatment despite the health benefits [8]. A beneficial advancement in PAH treatment would be an oral prostacyclin therapy with a pharmacokinetic (PK) profile that mimics infusion therapy.

Ralinepag is an oral medication in development for PAH that specifically targets and activates the IP receptor with high potency and selectivity, without the requirement of metabolic activation [9]. In preclinical models, ralinepag demonstrated high oral bioavailability (98%) in nonhuman primates and high plasma protein binding (99%) in humans [9]. In vitro studies indicate that ralinepag is a more potent IP receptor agonist than the active metabolite of selexipag (MRE-269), the only approved oral prostacyclin receptor agonist [9]. Selexipag has a half-life of 0.8–2.5 h, must be dosed twice daily, and has an evidence-supported dose limit of 1600 µg bis in die (BID) [10]. By contrast, ralinepag extended-release (XR) has a half-life between 19 and 23 h and is dosed once daily [11, 12]. Ralinepag shows a much stronger binding preference for the IP receptor (30- to 50-fold) than for other receptors, potentially resulting in fewer gastrointestinal side effects than earlier treatments in this class [9]. Ralinepag was initially developed as an immediate-release (IR) capsule, which was later modified into an XR tablet for once-daily dosing and to mimic the stable PK of parenteral prostacyclin therapy [11, 13, 14]. A phase II study demonstrated that 22 weeks of twice-daily ralinepag IR significantly reduced pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) compared with placebo in patients with PAH already receiving standard-of-care treatments [15]. An extension of this study with all patients treated with ralinepag IR, or transitioning to the XR formula demonstrated long-term safety and tolerability, and sustained improvement in 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) and PVR [14]. This study also suggests improved tolerability to ralinepag over time [14]. The ADVANCE OUTCOMES (NCT03626688) and ADVANCE EXTENSION (NCT03683186) studies are ongoing phase III trials evaluating the safety and efficacy of ralinepag XR in patients with PAH.

Food may alter the absorption rates and other PK parameters of orally administered drugs [16]. This study aims to assess the safety profile, tolerability, and pharmacokinetic properties of escalating doses of ralinepag XR tablets administered to healthy adult participants under both fed and fasting conditions.

Methods

This phase I single-center, open-label, nonrandomized, multiple-dose titration study of the ralinepag XR tablet aimed to assess the PK properties, safety, and tolerability of multiple ascending doses in both the fed and fasted states. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and good clinical practice, and an independent ethics committee at the study site approved the study protocol. All volunteers provided written informed consent. The study was carried out by Quotient Clinical, Nottingham, UK, a contract research organization.

Participants

Eligible participants were healthy adults aged 18–45 years with a body mass index (BMI) between 18.0 and 35.0 kg/m2. Females were required to be nonpregnant and nonlactating and agree to use an adequate method of contraception. Participants also agreed to refrain from consuming alcohol 24 h before each admission until 72 h postdose. Participants were also required not to be taking any prescribed or over-the-counter medications.

Study Design

This was a phase I study in two cohorts of healthy participants. Participants in cohort 1 (fasted) and cohort 2 (fed) received once-daily dosing of ralinepag XR for 5 days, with up to four sequential dose escalations every 5 days (Fig. 1). Once-daily doses of ralinepag XR, starting at 60 μg and titrating to 300 μg, were assessed in both cohorts. This dosing was selected on the basis of phase II data with the IR capsule, which had a starting dose of 10 μg twice daily, titrated up to 300 μg twice daily based on tolerability [15]. All participants started at 60 µg dosing once daily on day 1, continuing through day 5. If the participant tolerated the 60 µg dose, the dose would be escalated to 120 µg on day 6. Three further dose escalations (180, 240, and 300 µg) were planned every 5 days (days 11, 16, and 21) if the previous dose was tolerated, and the investigator reviewed individual safety data.

Dose escalation scheme for participants in the fasted (cohort 1) or fed state (cohort 2). Each participant received once-daily doses of the ralinepag XR tablet for 5 days, with up to four sequential dose escalations every 5 days. Arrow indicates day of blood sampling for pharmacokinetic analysis.

Participants were recruited from a panel of volunteers or through direct advertising to the public. Participants attended a screening visit up to 21 days before dosing for both cohorts. Treatment assignments were provided via a treatment allocation list in this open-label, nonrandomized study and were assigned on the morning of the first dosing. Eligible participants were admitted to the clinical unit on the evening of day − 1. Dosing commenced on day 1 on either an empty stomach following an overnight fast of at least 10 h (cohort 1) or following a standardized moderate-fat breakfast (approximately 36% of the meal's total calorific content) after an overnight fast of at least 10 h (cohort 2). In both cohorts, participants remained fasted (except water) until approximately 4 h postdose, when lunch was provided; an evening meal was provided at approximately 10 h postdose; and a light snack at approximately 14 h postdose. Doses were to be administered in the same prandial state for all 25 dosing days. Dose escalation only occurred on days 6, 11, 16, and 21, though the dose may have been decreased at any time for safety or tolerability reasons. If a dose escalation was not tolerated in an individual participant, the dose was lowered back to the previously tolerated level for the remainder of the 5-day dosing period and may have been re-escalated. Participants remained as residents until 72 h after the last dose (day 28); a follow-up phone call was scheduled for 5–7 days after the final dose.

Pharmacokinetic Sampling and Analytical Method

Blood samples were taken predose and at 1-, 2-, 4-, 6-, 8-, 10-, 12-, 16-, and 24-h postdose on days 1 and 5, days 6 and 10, days 11 and 15, days 16 and 20, and days 21 and 25. Blood samples were also collected at 48 and 72 h after the last dose (days 27 and 28).

The plasma concentrations of ralinepag were determined using validated analytical methods by LGC Ltd (Fordham, Cambridgeshire, UK). The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was 0.03 ng/mL. The estimation of PK parameters by noncompartmental analysis methods was performed using Phoenix WinNonlin software (v6.3, Certara USA, Inc., Radnor, PA, USA). The following parameters were estimated for ralinepag in plasma: area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to 24 h postdose (AUC0–24), area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to 24 h postdose at steady state (AUC0–24,ss), area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to last measurable concentration (AUC0–last), area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to the last quantifiable concentration at steady state (AUC0–last,ss), maximum observed concentration in a dose interval/steady state (Cmax/Cmax,ss), time of maximum observed concentration (Tmax), time of maximum observed concentration at steady state (Tmax,ss), apparent elimination half-life (final dose only) (T1/2), AUC(0–24) dose 5/AUC(0–24) dose 1 (accumulation index), and peak to trough fluctuation (Cmax − Cmin)/Cavg × 100% (calculated on days 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 only; % fluctuation). Steady state parameters AUC0–24,ss and Cmax,ss parameters underwent a natural logarithmic transformation and were analyzed using mixed effect modeling techniques. Cohort (i.e., fed/fasted) and regimen (i.e., dose), were included as fixed effects. Each participant was fitted as a random effect. The null hypothesis to be tested was that there was no difference between fed and fasted participants. Comparisons were not made if there were fewer than three participants in either group.

Plasma concentration at steady state, combined results from days 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25, only at the same dose level, and was summarized by dose level and scheduled dose time for each cohort. For steady state, 5 consecutive dosing days at the same dose level, preceding the summarized day, were required for each participant. The mean plasma concentrations at each dose level were combined across different days, regardless of the day the dose was administered.

Safety Assessments

Safety parameter evaluation comprised analysis of adverse events (AEs), laboratory variables (virology and follicle-stimulating hormone (postmenopausal female participants) at screening only; hematology, clinical chemistry, and urinalysis), vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, and respiratory rate), electrocardiogram (ECG), and physical examination. AEs and medications were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA; v19.1 or 20.1) and the World Health Organization Drug Dictionary Enhanced (2016 version or more recent version), respectively.

Statistical Analysis

Pharmacokinetics

No formal sample size calculation was made. Based on experience from previous studies of a similar design, a total of 36 participants, 18 participants per cohort with at least 6 participants of each sex per cohort, were to be enrolled, and a minimum of 12 evaluable participants per cohort were considered sufficient [11, 12]. Statistical analysis was performed separately on the following PK parameters to assess the presence of a food effect: Cmax and AUC(0–24) on day 1, (single 60 μg dose), Cmax and AUC(0–24) at steady state at each dose level. The PK parameters were log-transformed and analyzed using mixed-effect modeling. Summary statistics were calculated for all PK parameters. The analysis was performed using the SAS Software procedure PROC MIXED; the method was specified as Restricted Maximum Likelihood, and the denominator degrees of freedom for the fixed effects were calculated using the Kenward–Roger method. No adjustment was made for multiple tests. Additional description of the statistical analysis is given in the Supplementary Information.

Tolerability

Statistical analysis was performed to compare the maximum tolerated dose between the fed and fasted cohorts. A participant was considered to have tolerated their maximum dose if they completed 5 consecutive days of dosing at their highest dose level. Participants who did not complete 5 days of dosing at a dose level, or were never administered a particular dose level, were defined as not tolerating that dose level. The maximum tolerated dose was analyzed using Fisher’s Exact test.

Results

Demographics

There were 37 enrolled participants (25 (67.6%) males and 12 (32.4%) females) between 20 and 45 years of age. The majority were White (28 (75.7%)), with two (5.4%) Asian, three (8.1%) Black and four (10.8%) other race. Median age was 30.0 years. All participants were within the reference range for BMI as required by the protocol (26.0 kg/m2 (±4.0)). There was no notable difference in demographics between cohorts.

Overall, 28 (75.7%) participants (15 fasted participants and 13 fed participants) were classified as completing the study (received doses of ralinepag XR for 25 days and completed PK sampling until 72 h postfinal dose). Nine participants (four fasted and five fed) discontinued. Six participants were withdrawn from the study as a result of treatment-related AEs; two (10.5%) following dosing with 60 μg ralinepag XR fasted (one participant with an AE of increased transaminases, one participant with AEs arthralgia and myalgia) and four (22.2%) participants following dosing with 60 μg ralinepag XR fasted and fed (one participant with AEs of headache and vomiting, one participant with AEs of headache and nausea, one participant with an AE of nausea, and one participant with an AE of ventricular extrasystoles). Three participants withdrew consent from the study (two from the fasted cohort, and one from the fed cohort). A total of four participants (two fasted and two fed) completed all regimens, which was defined as receiving each ascending dose (i.e., 60, 120, 180, 240, and 300 μg ralinepag XR) for 5 consecutive days (per regimen); all other participants did not tolerate a dose for at least one regimen.

Pharmacokinetics

There were minimal exposure differences between the fed/fasted PK parameters of ralinepag XR in the first 24 h (day 1) after dosing (60 µg) (Table 1). After a single dose of 60 µg, there were no significant differences observed between mean Cmax and AUC(0–24) between the fed/fasted state (Table 1). In the fasted state, ralinepag peaked at 4.15 h (median Tmax). In the fed state, there was a delay in median Tmax (9.00 h). There was also high intraindividual variability for Tmax, with ranges from 2.0 to 12 h for fasted and 4–10 h for fed.

Following multiple doses of ralinepag XR in the fed state, median Tmax,ss were similar to those observed in the fasted state, ranging from 6.01 to 10.00 h. The dose increases from 60 to 300 μg resulted in exposure increases of 3.6-fold (Cmax,ss) and 3.5-fold (AUC(0–24,ss)). However, this result should be interpreted with caution as well, given the low participant number at higher dose levels (n = 2). The accumulation index after 5 days of dosing at 60 μg in the fed state was consistent with that observed when fasted; approximately 2.1-fold, based on AUC(0–24), consistent with the mean fed half-life of approximately 36 h. There was no statistically significant difference in ralinepag exposure between the fed and fasted states. Multiple-dose administration of ralinepag XR at 60, 120, 180, 240, and 300 μg in the fasted and fed states, at the individual participant level, suggested that exposure was roughly dose-proportional.

Safety

Overall, 16 (84%) participants reported at least one AE following ralinepag dosing in the fasted cohort, and 17 (94%) in the fed cohort (Table 3). Most AEs were mild or moderate, 1 (5%) participant in the fasted cohort reported a grade 3 (serious) event, and no grade 4 or 5 events were reported overall in this study. The grade 3 event was an increase in transaminases that occurred after the participant took paracetamol (36 g) over 10 days and diclofenac (450 mg) over 15 days; known hepatotoxic drugs. The participant was withdrawn from the study. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) for this participant remained elevated until day 22, while alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normalized by day 29, when the adverse event was considered resolved.

There were no noticeable differences in the incidence of AEs between fasted and fed cohorts. The most reported AEs were headache and jaw pain, which were similar for both cohorts. The percentage of participants reporting jaw pain and headache showed a large decline between doses 60 and 120 µg. Three of the four participants who tolerated the highest dose (300 µg) for the 5 days did not report any AEs.

Four participants in the fasted cohort discontinued treatment owing to either investigator discretion (n = 1), headache and arthralgia (n = 1), elevated liver enzymes (n = 1), or withdrawal of consent (n = 1). Discussion

This is the first report on multiple ascending doses of the ralinepag XR formulation. The previous single-dose study demonstrated an extended half-life (approximately 24 h) and reduced Cmax compared with the previous IR formulation and selexipag [12]. This phase I study of ralinepag XR in healthy volunteers demonstrated minimal differences in ralinepag pharmacokinetic parameters between the fed and fasted states at steady state, suggesting the potential to administer ralinepag XR without regard to food. Steady-state kinetics demonstrate low peak-to-trough ratios at all doses, which may contribute to sustained efficacy and improved tolerability.

After a single dose (60 µg) ralinepag XR rose to a peak median Tmax at 4.15 h in the fasted state but had a delayed median Tmax (9.00 h) in the fed state, possibly because of slowed gastric emptying. These findings were consistent with a previous single-dose study [12]. However, fasted versus fed conditions did not result in significant differences between mean Cmax and AUC(0–24) for a single dose of ralinepag XR. Although postprandial concentrations appeared numerically lower, the small sample sizes in both groups limit the interpretation of clinical significance. Most participants did not titrate to the highest dose, and because participants did not require ralinepag therapeutically, it is unclear whether these numerical differences would translate into clinically meaningful effects. Ralinepag XR demonstrated dose-dependent increases in exposure across both fasted and fed states, with a 5-fold dose increase resulting in approximately 3.4- to 4.15-fold increases in Cmax,ss and AUC(0–24,ss), suggesting roughly dose-proportional pharmacokinetics. It also showed consistent accumulation of approximately 2.1-fold after 5 days of dosing at 60 μg in both fed and fasted conditions, with no statistically significant differences in exposure between the two states. The extended-release formulation demonstrated a favorable pharmacokinetic profile with a low percent fluctuation, indicating minimal peak-to-trough variation. Future pharmacokinetic studies in populations with renal and hepatic impairment are planned.

No serious drug-attributable AEs were reported during this study. Three of the four participants who tolerated 5 days of the highest dose (300 µg) did not report any AEs. In healthy volunteers, ralinepag XR had a safety profile similar to prior studies of ralinepag, consistent with other prostacyclin receptor agonists [15, 17, 18]. Repeated dosing resulted in declines in some AEs over time even with increased dosing. Treatments targeting the prostacyclin pathway are usually up-titrated to a maximum dose after initiation at a low dose to optimize efficacy and minimize AEs [3, 19]. AEs are also more common during the titration period, and the healthy volunteers in this study did not receive a ralinepag XR dose that was titrated to a maintenance dose, as this was a short phase I study to evaluate PK and safety [18, 19]. This study did up-titrate and down-titrate as tolerated. Both fed and fasted groups likely exceeded a concentration threshold sufficient to produce prostacyclin-related AEs, yet no AE differences were found. Notably, oral treprostinil is administered with food on the basis of PK studies, yet still commonly produces prostacyclin side effects, supporting the concept that tolerability and side effects may be influenced less by absolute drug concentration alone and more by patient-specific biologic factors, including prostacyclin receptor sensitivity or density [19]. Accordingly, variability in side effects may reflect interindividual pharmacodynamic differences as well as pharmacokinetic variability. However, phase III studies will help better understand appropriate dosing in patients with PAH. At this time, no dose ceiling has been determined for ralinepag XR.

Phase II study data showed promising sustained reductions in PVR and improvement in 6MWD. Additionally, there was a notable decline in AEs after reaching and maintaining a stable dose [14, 15]. Two phase III studies, ADVANCE OUTCOMES and ADVANCE EXTENSION, will assess the efficacy and long-term safety of ralinepag XR when added to PAH standard-of-care background therapy. The ADVANCE OUTCOMES study dosed ralinepag XR at 50 µg increments titrated to the highest tolerated dose. The ADVANCE EXTENSION open-label study enrolls participants from other ralinepag clinical trials who will undergo a 16-week titration. The dosing regimen was optimized prior to the clinical trial, and population PK data from the trial will further characterize PK in patients with PAH.

Conclusions

This phase I study characterized the PK of ralinepag XR under fed and fasting conditions in healthy participants and found minimal effects of a moderate-fat meal on the extent of ralinepag absorption; thus, ralinepag XR dosing could potentially occur without regard to meals. With a long effective half-life and low peak-to-trough fluctuation, ralinepag provides 24-h receptor engagement, approximating the PK of a continuous infusion.

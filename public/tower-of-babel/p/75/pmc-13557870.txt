Prognostic impact of CEACAM5 in nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer: a critical reappraisal driven by cut-off optimization
Authors: Qian-Qian Xue, Yan Jin, Xu-Xia Shen, Qiang Zheng, Yao Fu, Qing-Xin Xia, Yuan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 5 (CEACAM5) has emerged as a promising therapeutic target for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). However, the landscape of CEACAM5 protein expression and its association with clinicopathological features in nonsquamous NSCLC remain poorly characterized. This study represents the first comprehensive investigation to profile CEACAM5 protein expression in this specific population using a clinical-grade immunohistochemistry (IHC) assay.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 218 patients with resected nonsquamous NSCLC. A standardized IHC assay derived from a clinical trial was employed, utilizing a proprietary monoclonal antibody (clone 769) specifically developed for clinical application. We systematically evaluated the relationship between CEACAM5 expression and tumor characteristics using both a conventional therapeutic threshold (≥50%) and a sensitive data-driven cut-off (>0%) to explore the full spectrum of antigen expression.ResultsThe assay demonstrated excellent reproducibility. We found that CEACAM5 expression was significantly associated with markers of tumor aggressiveness, including lymphovascular and pleural invasion. Notably, the sensitive >0% cut-off revealed broader biological correlations than the restrictive ≥50% threshold. While CEACAM5 was not an independent prognostic factor in the overall cohort, a stage-specific, hypothesis-generating analysis—supported by external genomic validation—suggested that positive CEACAM5 expression may be associated with a trend towards poorer disease-free survival specifically in early-stage (stage I–II) patients, although this did not reach statistical significance (P=0.06).ConclusionsThis study provides the first detailed characterization of CEACAM5 protein expression in nonsquamous NSCLC, establishing it as a biomarker linked to aggressive disease phenotypes. Our findings suggest that adopting a sensitive detection threshold (>0%) may better capture the population of patients with biologically active CEACAM5, thereby potentially refining candidate selection for targeted therapies and risk stratification in early-stage disease, a hypothesis that warrants prospective validation.Keywords: Carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 5 (CEACAM5), nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (nonsquamous NSCLC), immunohistochemistry (IHC), therapeutic target, prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• In a cohort of 218 resected nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients, carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 5 (CEACAM5) positivity at a highly sensitive cut-off (>0%) correlated with aggressive morphological features, including lymphovascular and pleural invasion.

• Specifically in early-stage (stage I–II) disease, this >0% threshold showed a trend toward an increased risk of postoperative recurrence.

What is known and what is new?

• CEACAM5 is a promising target for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). However, current clinical trials select patients based on stringent “high expression” thresholds (≥2+ intensity in ≥50% of tumor cells).

• We provide evidence that even trace levels of CEACAM5 (>0%) may possess distinct biological activity. This challenges the conventional binary cut-off and indicates that low-level expression potentially serves as a meaningful prognostic indicator for early-stage nonsquamous NSCLC.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The traditional restrictive threshold underestimates the biological relevance of CEACAM5, inadvertently overlooking early-stage patients with biologically relevant low-level expression who may face a higher risk of relapse.

• Pathological scoring should adopt a standardized, highly sensitive approach. Future studies should re-examine how CEACAM5 thresholds are applied across disease stages, and prospectively evaluate the clinical utility of low-level expression for risk stratification in early-stage, resectable disease.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains a formidable global health challenge, standing as the leading cause of cancer morbidity and mortality worldwide, with nearly 2.5 million new cases and over 1.8 million deaths reported globally in 2022 (1). Despite advancements in diagnostic techniques and therapeutic strategies, recurrence after surgical resection is commonly observed in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients, and their postsurgical recurrence-free survival (RFS) exhibits considerable variability (2). This prognostic heterogeneity persists even within patients classified at the same tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, particularly in stage I NSCLC, where the current system is often insufficient for precise treatment decision-making and accurate survival prediction (3-5). Consequently, the integration of novel biological markers is urgently needed to refine the traditional TNM framework, enabling more accurate risk stratification and personalized treatment strategies that have revolutionized cancer management and improved patient outcomes (6,7).

Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) family comprises a group of glycoproteins encoded by genes located on human chromosome 19q13.1–13.2. Among these, Carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 5 (CEACAM5) is a prominent member, being one of the 12 human CEACAMs (8,9). This molecule typically exists as a cell surface protein, is widely distributed, and participates in various critical biological processes. Its diverse functions include immune regulation, angiogenesis, and roles in cancer initiation and progression, among others (9). CEACAM5 is increasingly recognized as a dependable diagnostic and therapeutic molecular biomarker in oncology. Indeed, aberrant CEACAM5 expression has been observed across various human cancers, including gastric cancer (10-12), colorectal cancer (13-15), neuroendocrine prostate cancer (16), and breast cancer (17), where it is frequently associated with tumor progression, metastasis, and adverse patient prognosis (10,18). Furthermore, CEACAM5 has demonstrated utility as a molecular imaging target (11,19,20) and as a highly sensitive biomarker for detecting tumor cells in lymph nodes of colorectal cancer (21).

Recently, CEACAM5 has garnered significant attention as a promising therapeutic target for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in NSCLC (22). Tusamitamab ravtansine, a CEACAM5-directed ADC, was evaluated in the Phase 3 CARMEN-LC03 trial for previously treated advanced nonsquamous NSCLC with high CEACAM5 expression (23). Although the trial did not meet its primary efficacy endpoints, partly due to an unexpectedly strong performance in the control arm, it highlighted the complexities of targeting CEACAM5 in advanced settings (23). Nevertheless, ongoing early-phase clinical trials continue to explore tusamitamab ravtansine, both as monotherapy and in combination with other agents, showing encouraging preliminary antitumor activity and manageable safety profiles across various treatment lines in advanced NSCLC (24,25). These continuous investigations underscore CEACAM5’s sustained relevance as a therapeutic target; however, the precise landscape of CEACAM5 protein expression in the target population—nonsquamous NSCLC—and its specific association with aggressive disease phenotypes remain poorly characterized.

The ambiguity surrounding CEACAM5’s prognostic role is further compounded by significant methodological heterogeneity in existing studies. Critically, these investigations often lack standardized and validated immunohistochemistry (IHC) scoring systems and optimal cut-off values for patient stratification. For example, published studies on CEACAM5 expression frequently employ differing antibody clones and exhibit inconsistent scoring criteria (11,15,23,26,27). This lack of a unified approach creates substantial challenges for comparison and reproducibility across studies. This pervasive methodological heterogeneity significantly impedes the reliable translation of CEACAM5 expression into clinical practice, hindering both accurate risk stratification and the precise selection of candidates for targeted therapies.

Therefore, building upon a standardized and validated IHC scoring methodology derived from a clinical trial (protocol AT0025)—distinctively employing a proprietary monoclonal antibody (clone 769) specifically developed for clinical application—this study represents the first comprehensive effort to profile CEACAM5 protein expression in a large cohort of resected nonsquamous NSCLC patients. We specifically focus on investigating the association between CEACAM5 expression and aggressive clinicopathological features, as well as identifying an optimal IHC cut-off value to capture the full biological spectrum of the antigen. We hypothesize that characterizing the CEACAM5 expression landscape using a sensitive detection threshold will reveal its correlation with tumor aggressiveness and recurrence risk, thereby offering valuable insights for refining risk stratification and optimizing patient selection for emerging CEACAM5-targeted therapies. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0494/rc).

Methods

Study design and patient cohort

This study was conducted based on the standardized methodology and patient cohort established by the clinical trial titled “Clinical Trial for Verification of Reading Consistency of CEACAM5 Detection Kit (IHC) in Nonsquamous Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer” (protocol AT0025, version 1.1, dated October 8, 2022). While the primary objective of the parent trial was to validate the reproducibility of the IHC assay, the current study integrates these validated assessments with a retrospective analysis of prognostic associations.

The study cohort consisted of 218 individuals with primary nonsquamous NSCLC who underwent radical surgical resection at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center between March 2021 and October 2022. Comprehensive clinicopathological data were retrospectively collected for these patients, encompassing age, gender, smoking status, histological type, T, N, and M stages, pathological stage, lymphovascular invasion, pleural invasion, programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) tumor proportion score (TPS), and driver gene status. Histological subtypes were independently confirmed by two experienced pathologists, and pathological staging was determined according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system. Patients were followed up through outpatient visits and telephone interviews, with disease-free survival (DFS) defined as the time from surgery to the first documented recurrence, metastasis, or death. The pattern of recurrence was categorized as locoregional (recurrence at the surgical margin, ipsilateral hemithorax, or ipsilateral mediastinal/hilar lymph nodes) or distant metastasis (recurrence in the contralateral hemithorax or extrathoracic organs); combined locoregional-and-distant recurrence and pleural dissemination or malignant pleural effusion (M1a) were classified as distant metastasis.

The clinical trial (protocol AT0025) was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center on May 9, 2022 (approval number: 2204253-8). All patients provided written informed consent upon admission. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. For the retrospective collection and analysis of additional anonymous clinicopathological and follow-up data, informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee, given the de-identified and retrospective nature of the data.

IHC staining

The Department of Pathology at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center prepared 4–5 µm thick sections from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks, which were then mounted onto Dako FLEX IHC microscope slides (Cat. No. K8020, Agilent Technologies, Inc.). The slides were subsequently baked in an oven at 58±2 ℃ for 1 hour. To preserve antigenicity, prepared slides were stored protected from light at 2–8 ℃ and stained within 6 months.

All IHC staining was performed on a Dako Omnis automated stainer (Model GI100, Agilent Technologies, Inc.). The validated CEACAM5 Assay Kit (IHC769, Dako Omnis; Agilent Technologies, Inc.; Lot Nos. 11434351, 11439605, 11492301, 11504797) was used, a qualitative IHC assay utilizing a mouse monoclonal anti-CEACAM5 clone 769 with established specificity for CEACAM5 expression in non-squamous NSCLC (28). This kit was used in conjunction with the En Vision FLEX High pH detection system (Cat. No. K8000, Dako Omnis; Agilent Technologies, Inc.; Lot Nos. 11409642, 41450559) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Appropriate positive and negative controls were included in each run.

All aspects of the IHC staining and subsequent evaluation, including the establishment of the IHC scoring system and the concordance analysis, were conducted as per the approved clinical trial protocol AT0025.

IHC evaluation and reproducibility

For CEACAM5 IHC assessment, positive staining was defined as any partial or complete tumor cell plasma membrane staining with an intensity of ≥2+, regardless of its localization (e.g., basal, lateral, basolateral, or apical) or morphology (e.g., linear or punctate), consistent with the validated assay’s scoring algorithm (28). All viable tumor cells on the entire CEACAM5 stained section were evaluated and included in the CEACAM5 scoring. A minimum of 100 viable tumor cells was required for CEACAM5 evaluation in each sample. The percentage of positive CEACAM5 staining was calculated as: (number of CEACAM5 positive tumor cells/total number of viable tumor cells) × 100%. Pathologist 1 from Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center completed the CEACAM5 IHC assessment for the 223 effective samples provided by the center as part of the clinical trial. Among these, 218 samples were finally incorporated into the main study analysis due to complete clinicopathological and follow-up information; the remaining samples were excluded due to missing data.

To further ensure the reliability and consistency of CEACAM5 IHC assessment within our study, based on the established methodology (28), the reproducibility of CEACAM5 IHC assessment was evaluated at both intra- and inter-observer, as well as inter-center levels, using a pre-defined cut-off value where CEACAM5 positivity was set at ≥50% of tumor cells showing membranous staining.

For intra-observer reproducibility within Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center, one experienced pathologist (pathologist 1) evaluated 60 samples three times. The first and second assessments were separated by 55 days, and the second and third by 17 days. For inter-observer reproducibility within Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center, three experienced pathologists independently evaluated the same 60 samples. For inter-center reproducibility, 40 samples were independently evaluated by one experienced pathologist from each of three clinical practice institutions: Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (institution 1), The Affiliated Drum Tower Hospital of Nanjing University Medical School (institution 2), and Henan Cancer Hospital (institution 3). All pathologists involved in the assessment were experienced pathologists from their respective pathology centers. All evaluations were performed in a blinded manner, without knowledge of patient clinical information or previous assessment results. The specific sample selection for reproducibility analysis was conducted according to the clinical trial requirements.

Reproducibility was assessed using various statistical measures, including average positive agreement (APA), average negative agreement (ANA), average overall agreement (AOA), the average of pairwise Cohen’s Kappa coefficients, and Fleiss’ Kappa coefficient for multiple raters.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables, including continuous variables that were categorized, were summarized as counts and percentages. Comparisons of baseline clinicopathological characteristics between different groups were performed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate based on expected cell frequencies.

Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the Log-rank test. Both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify prognostic factors for DFS. Variables with a P value <0.1 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox regression model. Hazard ratios (HR) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) and P values were reported.

In this study, “cut-off” refers throughout to the threshold applied to the percentage of CEACAM5-positive tumor cells for defining IHC positivity, and not to any proteolytic processing of the CEACAM5 protein. For CEACAM5 expression, two different cut-off values were employed for analysis: a pre-defined cut-off of ≥50% positivity, as specified by the clinical trial protocol, and a >0% cut-off determined by the minimum P value approach based on survival analysis. Specifically, the 0% cut-off was identified using the surv_cutpoint function (from the survminer R package) to optimize survival stratification.

The reproducibility analysis for IHC assessment (as described in section IHC evaluation and reproducibility) utilized APA, ANA, AOA, the average of pairwise Cohen’s Kappa coefficients, and Fleiss’ Kappa coefficient for multiple raters. Kappa coefficient values were interpreted as follows: <0.20, poor agreement; 0.21–0.40, fair agreement; 0.41–0.60, moderate agreement; 0.61–0.80, good agreement; and 0.81–1.00, almost perfect.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. No adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed. Statistical analyses for this study were primarily conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 10.1.2, RRID:SCR_002798) and R software (version 4.5.1, RRID: SCR_001905). The external validation analysis was performed using the online Kaplan-Meier plotter database (29), which is accessible at https://kmplot.com/analysis/.

Results

Establishment and validation of the CEACAM5 IHC scoring system

In the cohort of 218 nonsquamous NSCLC specimens, CEACAM5 protein expression was systematically evaluated by IHC. Positive staining for CEACAM5 was predominantly observed on the cell membrane. The expression patterns varied among different cases, showing a range of positive tumor cell percentages. Our scoring system for CEACAM5 IHC defined cases with ≥50% positive tumor cells as positive, and those with <50% positive tumor cells as negative. Representative images illustrating this binary classification at both magnifications (200× and 400×) are shown in Figure 1. Specifically, Figure 1A,1D (A, 200×; D, 400×) display cases with 0% positive cells, which were classified as negative. Figure 1B,1E (B, 200×; E, 400×) illustrate cases with >0% but <50% positive cells, also categorized as negative. Lastly, Figure 1C,1F (C, 200×; F, 400×) present cases with ≥50% positive cells, hence designated as positive.

When employing the presence/absence (>0%) cut-off, positive CEACAM5 expression was significantly associated with histological type (P=0.02), lymphovascular invasion (P<0.001), pleural invasion (P=0.01), T stage (P=0.004), N stage (P<0.001), and pathological stage (P=0.003). That is, CEACAM5 positivity was more frequently observed in patients with features of tumor aggressiveness, such as higher T/N stages and presence of lymphovascular or pleural invasion.

In contrast, using the 50% cut-off, positive CEACAM5 expression showed significant associations with smoking status (P=0.02), lymphovascular invasion (P<0.001), N stage (P=0.006), and pathological stage (P=0.03).

To assess whether the association between CEACAM5 and invasive features was confounded by driver-gene status, we performed a stratified analysis. The association between CEACAM5 positivity (>0%) and lymphovascular invasion remained significant within the EGFR-mutant (OR =4.33, P<0.001), EGFR-wild-type (OR =4.85, P=0.009), and any-driver-alteration (OR =4.02, P<0.001) subgroups, and its association with pleural invasion remained significant within the EGFR-mutant (OR =3.22, P=0.02) and any-driver-alteration (OR =2.73, P=0.02) subgroups (Figure S1). These associations were thus independent of driver-gene status.

Finally, we examined the pattern of postoperative recurrence in relation to CEACAM5 expression. Among the 62 patients who developed recurrence, distant metastasis was the predominant pattern (54/62, 87.1%; most commonly brain and bone), whereas 8 patients (12.9%) had locoregional recurrence alone. CEACAM5 expression, analyzed as a continuous variable, did not differ significantly between patients with locoregional and distant recurrence (P=0.21, Figure S2).

Prognostic value of CEACAM5 in the full cohort

Given the significant associations between CEACAM5 expression and several aggressive clinicopathological parameters under different cut-off definitions, we next assessed its prognostic significance in the overall cohort. Specifically, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis for DFS revealed no significant association with CEACAM5 expression, regardless of whether a 50% cut-off (Figure 3A, P=0.45) or a >0% cut-off (Figure 3B, P=0.19) was applied.

(C) Box plots of CEACAM5 positive cell percentage across pathological stages. CEACAM5, carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 5; DFS, disease-free survival.

Further analysis of the relationship between CEACAM5 positive tumor cell percentage and pathological stage revealed a significant difference in CEACAM5 positive tumor cell percentages across pathological stages, with a general trend of higher expression in more advanced stages (Figure 3C, P=0.006).

Cut-off dependent and stage-specific prognostic value of CEACAM5 in early-stage lung cancer

To reconcile the discrepancy between the observed association of CEACAM5 with tumor progression and its lack of prognostic value in the full cohort, we sought external validation using the Kaplan-Meier plotter online database (gene chip-based mRNA expression data). An analysis focusing on stage I lung cancer patients revealed that high CEACAM5 gene expression was a significant adverse prognostic factor for DFS (Figure 4A, P=0.005). Notably, the optimal cut-off value for CEACAM5 gene expression, determined by the auto cut-off plot, was 4,539 (arbitrary units, Figure 4B). Furthermore, a Beeswarm plot was generated to visualize the distribution of CEACAM5 gene expression levels in these patients, where cases with low expression were depicted in black and those with high expression in red (Figure 4C). Given these findings from the external database, we hypothesized that the prognostic role of CEACAM5 might be stage-specific.

Based on this hypothesis, we conducted a targeted subgroup analysis on 89 patients with stage I–II nonsquamous NSCLC from our cohort. In this specific subgroup, a total of 13 DFS events were observed. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that the survival curves for CEACAM5-positive (defined by a ≥50% cut-off) and -negative patients did not separate effectively (Figure 5A). In contrast, a clear separation was observed using the >0% cut-off: DFS events occurred in 11 of 55 CEACAM5-positive versus 2 of 34 CEACAM5-negative patients, with the positive group showing a strong trend towards worse DFS that did not reach statistical significance (Figure 5B, P=0.06). Given the limited number of events, this observation should be regarded as hypothesis-generating.

To further quantify the prognostic value of CEACAM5 and other clinicopathological factors within this early-stage lung cancer subgroup, we performed univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses, which are visualized in the forest plots of Figure 5C (univariate) and Figure 5D (multivariate). In the univariate analysis, pathological stage II (HR =12.94, P=0.01), lymphovascular invasion (HR =6.20, P=0.006), and pleural invasion (HR =5.00, P=0.004) showed a trend toward an increased risk of recurrence (Figure 5C). In the multivariate analysis, after adjusting for significant covariates, CEACAM5 expression (HR =1.63, P=0.58) did not remain an independent prognostic factor for DFS, while pleural invasion (HR =3.72, P=0.03) was identified as an independent prognostic factor (Figure 5D).

Discussion

Our study represents the first comprehensive characterization of the CEACAM5 protein expression landscape in a well-defined cohort of resected nonsquamous NSCLC patients, utilizing a standardized IHC assay based on a proprietary, clinical-grade monoclonal antibody (clone 769). While motivated by the molecule’s emergence as a promising therapeutic target, we critically examined the roles of cut-off selection and stage-specific analysis in defining its clinical utility. Our key findings underscore CEACAM5’s consistent association with aggressive clinicopathological features, suggesting its relevance as a target for aggressive disease phenotypes. Although its overall prognostic independence in the entire cohort was limited, a stage-specific analysis—particularly when employing a more sensitive cut-off—highlighted CEACAM5’s potential as a valuable biomarker for risk stratification in early-stage NSCLC.

The reliability of our findings rests on a rigorous methodological foundation. Building upon the pre-validated CEACAM5 IHC 769 assay (28), with established specificity for CEACAM5 in nonsquamous NSCLC, we performed comprehensive intra-observer, inter-observer, and inter-center reproducibility analyses. This standardization directly addresses the methodological heterogeneity that has long limited comparability among previous CEACAM5 studies (11,15,23,26,27,30-32). The dimensions along which those studies diverge—antibody clone, scoring method and positivity cut-off, staining platform, and the presence or absence of reproducibility assessment—are summarized in Table S1; collectively they reflect a reliance on laboratory-developed IHC, with research-grade monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies, mutually incompatible scoring systems (H-score, immunoreactivity score, total immunostaining score, or disparate categorical thresholds), predominantly manual or laboratory-developed protocols, and formal inter-observer or inter-center reproducibility almost never reported. Such variability hinders cross-study comparison and complicates accurate biomarker screening and prediction of treatment response. In contrast, our clinical-grade assay (clone 769), established within a clinical trial (protocol AT0025) and run on a standardized automated platform with a predefined scoring algorithm, achieved almost-perfect agreement across multiple pathologists and institutions (e.g., average overall agreement 97.8% and Cohen’s Kappa 0.956 for intra-observer reproducibility; Table 1)—a level of reproducibility rarely documented in prior work. This positions our study to provide more reliable and comparable CEACAM5 expression data, setting a benchmark for future investigations.

Our observation of significant associations between CEACAM5 expression and several aggressive clinicopathological features—including histological type, lymphovascular invasion, pleural invasion, T stage, N stage, and pathological stage—reinforces the established biological role of CEACAM5 in oncogenesis. The correlations with higher T/N stages, and particularly with lymphovascular and pleural invasion, suggest that CEACAM5 expression is closely linked to the invasive and metastatic cascades in NSCLC. This finding is consistent with studies in other cancers, such as gastric and colorectal cancers, where elevated CEACAM5 levels are frequently linked to tumor progression and unfavorable patient prognosis (8,33). A critical nuance of our findings is the cut-off dependent nature of these associations. The more sensitive >0% positivity cut-off consistently revealed broader and often stronger correlations with tumor aggressiveness compared to the traditional ≥50% cut-off. This observation is particularly salient given the current efforts to identify optimal predictive biomarkers for CEACAM5-targeted therapies, where the precise threshold for “high expression” can critically influence patient selection and therapeutic efficacy (23-25). Our data imply that a single, high-percentage cut-off might be too restrictive for capturing the full biological and prognostic spectrum of CEACAM5 in NSCLC. Indeed, within our cohort, 97 of 218 patients (44.5%) expressed CEACAM5 at the sensitive >0% level but below the ≥50% threshold (Table 2)—a substantial intermediate-expression population, nearly half the cohort, that would fall outside the restrictive eligibility criteria currently applied in CEACAM5-directed trials. This intermediate range largely corresponds to the ‘moderate-expression’ category (≥2+ intensity in 1–49% of tumor cells) defined in the tusamitamab ravtansine program, for which the pivotal phase 3 trial restricted enrollment to high expressors (≥50%) (23). We emphasize, however, that the prognostic and biological relevance of low-level CEACAM5 observed here in the early-stage, resectable setting is conceptually distinct from—and should not be equated with—predicted benefit from a specific ADC in advanced disease. Rather than implying that these patients would necessarily respond to current CEACAM5-directed agents, our findings argue that CEACAM5 thresholds may need to be tailored to the clinical context, distinguishing risk stratification from therapeutic selection, and early-stage from advanced disease.

The biological plausibility of these observations is supported by the well-established oncogenic functions of CEACAM5. As a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored cell-adhesion glycoprotein, CEACAM5 mediates homophilic and heterophilic adhesion and intracellular signaling, and has been shown across multiple tumor types to promote tumor-cell proliferation, migration, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (8,10,17,18,34). Although these mechanistic studies were not specifically designed to interrogate low expression levels, our observation that the sensitive >0% cut-off revealed associations with invasive features—including pleural invasion, which was not detected at the ≥50% threshold (Table 2)—suggests that even low-abundance membranous CEACAM5 may be biologically relevant in this setting. We therefore propose, as a hypothesis requiring further mechanistic validation, that low-level CEACAM5 expression is not merely a quantitative dilution of the high-expression phenotype but may retain functional, pro-invasive activity; dose-resolved functional studies will be required to test this directly.

Despite these clear associations with aggressive features, CEACAM5 expression did not exhibit independent prognostic significance for DFS in the overall cohort of resected nonsquamous NSCLC patients. This finding is consistent with a recent analysis of TCGA data, which reported that high CEACAM5 gene expression was not predictive of survival in the overall NSCLC population (35). However, contrasting results have been reported in studies focusing on serum CEA levels or specific metastatic contexts, which identified high CEA levels as an adverse prognostic indicator (36,37). Such discrepancies across studies are likely attributable to the inherent heterogeneity of NSCLC across all pathological stages, variations in patient populations (e.g., metastatic versus resected), and fundamental differences in detection methodologies (tissue IHC, serum analysis, and gene expression) and cut-offs.

However, a more nuanced understanding emerged from our stage-specific analysis, particularly regarding the interplay of cut-off selection. A key insight was provided by external validation using the Kaplan-Meier plotter database, which revealed high CEACAM5 gene expression as a significant adverse prognostic factor for DFS specifically in stage I lung cancer patients. Notably, the optimal cut-off value for CEACAM5 gene expression, determined by the auto cut-off plot, was 4,539 (arbitrary units). This optimal gene expression cut-off, confirmed to be in a lower percentile of the overall expression range, aligns with our finding that a >0% CEACAM5 protein expression cut-off in our cohort demonstrated a stronger trend towards aggressive features and worse DFS in early-stage disease. This concordance between gene expression (from external data) and protein expression (from our IHC) is consistent with the biological relevance of even low-level CEACAM5 expression. It supports our hypothesis that CEACAM5’s prognostic role may be stage-dependent, with its impact being more pronounced in early-stage disease where subtle expression thresholds may be clinically significant. This observation is consistent with the general principle that tumor markers often exhibit more pronounced prognostic utility in early-stage disease, where the influence of other overwhelming factors is less dominant. Consistent with our results, previous studies have identified CEA or CEACAM5 as significant prognostic factors specifically in stage I NSCLC, even when predictive value was absent in the overall cohort (35,38,39). The ability of the >0% cut-off to reveal a stronger trend suggests that even low-level CEACAM5 protein expression might contribute to tumor recurrence in earlier stages, which might be missed by higher thresholds.

Motivated by these insights, we focused our internal analysis on an early-stage (stage I–II) nonsquamous NSCLC subgroup. While CEACAM5 expression, particularly with the >0% cut-off, showed a strong trend towards worse DFS (P=0.06), it did not reach statistical significance and was not an independent prognostic factor in multivariate analysis. The borderline P value likely reflects limited statistical power, as only 13 DFS events occurred among the 89 early-stage patients (11 vs. 2 in the CEACAM5-positive and -negative groups), rather than a true absence of association, particularly given the clear numerical separation of the survival curves. Furthermore, in this early-stage cohort’s multivariate analysis, pleural invasion emerged as an independent predictor. This finding aligns with established evidence identifying pleural invasion as a robust indicator of tumor aggressiveness and higher recurrence risk in early-stage NSCLC (40-42). It is plausible that CEACAM5’s pro-invasive functions act upstream or contribute to phenotypes like pleural invasion; consequently, its independent prognostic impact might be overshadowed by such a strong clinical indicator, particularly in a smaller cohort. In keeping with this invasive phenotype, recurrences in this cohort were predominantly distant rather than locoregional, although CEACAM5 expression did not differ significantly between recurrence patterns—an exploratory observation limited by the small number of locoregional events (n=8). The discrepancy between our protein expression data and the external gene expression data in reaching statistical significance also highlights the complexities of translating findings across different molecular levels and the challenges associated with sample size in real-world cohorts. This also suggests that while CEACAM5 gene expression might serve as a strong indicator, its protein counterpart’s prognostic strength might be influenced by post-transcriptional and post-translational regulation or other cellular contexts, requiring a larger cohort for robust validation. Future studies could explore the combined prognostic model of CEACAM5 with other clinicopathological risk factors, rather than merely evaluating its independence.

A further strength is our exclusive focus on nonsquamous NSCLC, which minimized inter-tumor heterogeneity and provides an evaluation directly pertinent to the patient population most relevant to CEACAM5-targeted therapy. However, several limitations warrant consideration. This was a retrospective study conducted at a single institution, which introduces inherent biases and limits the direct generalizability of our findings. Although our IHC method was rigorously validated, the sample size, particularly for the early-stage subgroup, was relatively modest, potentially impacting the statistical power to detect all significant prognostic associations, as evidenced by the borderline P value for DFS. Furthermore, the follow-up period was fixed and relatively short (median approximately 43 months), with only 13 recurrence events in the early-stage subgroup, and overall survival (OS) data were not available; these factors further constrained the statistical power to confirm the early-stage prognostic signal. The external validation relied on mRNA gene expression data from the Kaplan-Meier plotter, which, while indicative, is not directly comparable to protein expression and may not fully reflect the clinical utility of IHC-based assessment. This highlights the necessity for protein-level validation in prospective, multi-center cohorts with extended follow-up. We also did not delve into the molecular mechanisms underlying CEACAM5’s stage-specific prognostic role or its interaction with other oncogenic pathways, which could provide deeper biological insights. Finally, certain analyses were constrained by the nature and size of the cohort: as these were resected patients who did not receive CEACAM5-directed ADC therapy, the data do not permit a direct assessment of treatment sensitivity across expression strata; and the small number of locoregional recurrences (n=8) limited the power to examine the association between CEACAM5 expression and the pattern of recurrence. Both questions will require dedicated prospective cohorts.

In summary, the principal contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides the first systematic characterization of the CEACAM5 protein expression landscape in nonsquamous NSCLC using a pre-validated, clinical-grade assay, establishing a reproducible benchmark in a field previously hampered by methodological heterogeneity. Second, it demonstrates that cut-off selection materially affects the associations detected: the sensitive >0% threshold reveals correlations with aggressive features—and a substantial intermediate-expression population (44.5%)—that are not captured by the conventional ≥50% threshold. Third, it identifies a stage-specific, hypothesis-generating prognostic signal in early-stage disease that warrants prospective validation. Importantly, the prognostic and biological relevance of low-level CEACAM5 described here is conceptually distinct from predicted benefit from a specific CEACAM5-directed ADC and should be interpreted as informing risk stratification rather than therapeutic selection.

Conclusions

In conclusion, using a standardized, clinical-grade IHC assay, we show that CEACAM5 is consistently associated with aggressive features in nonsquamous NSCLC and that its prognostic utility is stage-dependent and cut-off–sensitive. While not an independent prognostic factor overall, the early-stage signal observed with a >0% cut-off is hypothesis-generating and warrants prospective validation. Our findings advocate a more nuanced, standardized approach to CEACAM5 assessment using validated antibodies and optimized cut-offs—to refine risk stratification, while recognizing that any implication for ADC patient selection remains to be established prospectively.

FGF21 signals through hindbrain neurons to alter food intake and energy expenditure during dietary protein restriction
Authors: Redin A Spann, Sora Q Kim, Md Shahjalal H Khan, Diana A Albarado, Sun O Fernandez-Kim, Hans-Rudolf Berthoud, David H McDougal, Heike Münzberg, Yanlin He, Sangho Yu, Christopher D Morrison
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYThe metabolic hormone fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is essential for adaptive responses to dietary protein restriction, but the precise neural circuit mediating these effects remains undefined. Here, we demonstrate that a discrete population of glutamatergic, Klb-expressing neurons in the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) mediates FGF21 action during protein restriction. Using a Klb-Flp mouse line combined with intersectional genetics, we show that NTS-beta-klotho (KLB) neurons are directly activated by FGF21. Systematic evaluation of previously implicated regions (suprachiasmatic nucleus [SCN], paraventricular nucleus [PVN], and ventromedial hypothalamus [VMH]) reveals these areas are not required for FGF21-mediated responses to protein restriction. In contrast, selective ablation of NTS-KLB neurons prevents metabolic adaptations to protein restriction, including changes in food intake, food choice, and energy expenditure, while their chemogenetic activation is sufficient to drive these responses. These findings establish that NTS-KLB neurons directly respond to FGF21 and coordinate adaptive changes during protein restriction, identifying the neural circuit linking dietary protein sensing to metabolic adaptation.

INTRODUCTION

Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a liver-derived endocrine hormone that influences energy expenditure (EE), food choice, and metabolism. Despite its therapeutic promise for treating metabolic disorders, the neural mechanisms by which FGF21 exerts its effects remain incompletely understood. We have previously shown that FGF21 plays a key role in the detection of the protein-restricted state.1–3 Protein restriction triggers a variety of adaptive physiological responses, including changes in growth, EE, food intake, food choice, and motivation for protein.4–14 These responses act to protect the animal from the protein-restricted state, but they also improve metabolic health and enhance longevity in rodents. Importantly, FGF21 is critical for these adaptive responses to protein restriction, such that the effects of protein restriction on food choice, motivation to consume protein, EE, and even lifespan are lost in mice lacking FGF21.1–3,15–17 Collectively, these data strongly suggest that FGF21 is a signal of the protein-restricted state that coordinates a variety of adaptive responses to protein restriction.

FGF21 signals through a receptor system consisting of the FGF receptor 1c (FGFR1c) and the co-receptor beta-klotho (KLB). While FGFR1c mediates intracellular signaling, KLB mediates extracellular binding and thus provides cellular specificity for FGF21 signaling.18–20 FGFR1c and KLB are co-expressed in only a few tissues, including adipose tissue, pancreas, lung, and brain.21,22 Importantly, our previous work indicates that Camk2a-Cre-mediated deletion of KLB from neurons blocked the effects of dietary protein restriction on EE, glucose handling, and protein preference and motivation.2 These data strongly suggest that the brain is a primary mediator of FGF21 action in the context of dietary protein restriction, consistent with prior work indicating that the brain mediates the effects of pharmacological FGF21 administration.22–25

Although it is well accepted that the brain plays a critical role in mediating FGF21 action, the specific brain regions mediating FGF21 signaling remain unclear. Analysis of Klb expression suggests that Klb is robustly expressed in two brain areas, the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) and the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS),21,22 although lower levels of Klb expression are detected in several additional locations both within and outside the hypothalamus. Importantly, most studies have focused on FGF21 action in the hypothalamus and midbrain, with no studies directly targeting the hindbrain/NTS.22,24,26–30

Here, using the physiological model of dietary protein restriction combined with unique genetic tools, we reveal an unexpected role of the hindbrain in mediating FGF21 action. While FGF21 signaling in the hypothalamic regions (paraventricular nucleus [PVN] and ventromedial hypothalamus [VMH]) is dispensable, a discrete population of glutamatergic Klb-expressing neurons in the NTS is required for the response to protein restriction. Deletion of these NTS-KLB neurons blocks changes in food intake, food choice, and EE in response to a low-protein (LP) diet, while their chemogenetic activation is sufficient to drive these responses. These findings establish NTS-KLB neurons as a population of neurons that regulate feeding and metabolism, playing a critical role in the FGF21-dependent coordination of metabolic adaptation to dietary protein availability.

RESULTS

Klb-Flp drives recombination within two distinct GABAergic and glutamatergic neural populations

To determine where FGF21 signals within the brain, we generated a mouse line in which Flp recombinase is inserted into the 3′ end of the Klb gene, connected with the T2A linker (Figure 1A). This genetic manipulation results in FLP production wherever Klb is expressed. These Klb-Flp mice were then crossed with the dual recombinase RC::FLTG (FLTG) reporter line.31 This intersectional approach provides additional power for phenotyping KLB neurons since Flp-based recombination of FLTG drives the expression of tdTomato, but the addition of Cre removes tdTomato while activating GFP expression.31

Klb-Flp mice mark two neural populations in the brain

(A) A mouse was created that expresses Flp recombinase driven by Klb expression.

(B and C) Klb-Flp mice were then crossed with FLTG reporter mice, and two populations of tdTomato-positive neurons were observed: in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus (SCN) (B) and the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) (C).

(D–F) Klb expression was confirmed using RNA-scope reagents in reporter mice. Scale bar: 100 μm.

Assessing Flp-induced recombination (tdTomato expression) within the brain of Klb-Flp;FLTG mice revealed prominent tdTomato expression within only two areas of the brain: the SCN and the NTS (Figures 1B, 1C, and S1). Beyond the brain, Klb-Flp;FLTG mice reveal tdTomato expression in other tissues known to express Klb, including the liver, but notably not within the nodose ganglia (Figure S1). To confirm that these Klb-Flp neurons actively express Klb, we used RNAscope to colocalize Klb mRNA with tdTomato expression in Klb-Flp;FLTG mice (Figures 1D–1F). Within the NTS, we observe essentially 100% colocalization between these markers (Figure 1F), indicating that Klb-Flp neurons within the NTS actively express Klb.

Intersectional genetics was then used to further phenotype Klb-Flp neurons by crossing Klb-Flp;FLTG mice with various Cre recombinase mouse lines. Since the adaptive response to protein restriction depends on Klb expression in Camk2a-positive cells,2 we first tested whether Camk2a-Cre would induce recombination (removal of tdTomato and expression of GFP) in Klb-Flp cells. We found that both the SCN and NTS populations expressed GFP but not tdTomato, indicating expression of both Klb-Flp and Camk2a-Cre (Figures 2A and 2B). Klb-Flp;FLTG mice were then crossed with established Vglut2-Cre (glutamatergic) and Vgat-Cre (GABAergic) lines. Interestingly, Vglut2-Cre induced recombination (GFP expression) only within the NTS (Figures 2C and 2D), while Vgat-Cre induced GFP expression only within the SCN (Figures 2E and 2F). These intersectional crosses suggest that both SCN and NTS Klb-Flp neurons express Camk2a-Cre but that the NTS population is glutamatergic, whereas the SCN population is GABAergic.

Klb-FLP;FLTG mice were crossed with various Cre mouse lines to further characterize Klb neurons via intersectional genetics.

(C and D) Crossing with Vglut2-Cre mice resulted in GFP expression in the NTS but not the SCN, indicating that NTS-KLB neurons are glutamatergic.

(E and F) Crossing with Vgat-Cre resulted in GFP expression in the SCN but not NTS, indicating that SCN-KLB neurons are GABAergic.

(G and H) Lastly, Phox2b-Cre induced GFP expression in some but not all NTS-KLB neurons. Scale bar: 100 μm.

Phox2b-Cre has been previously used to target the dorsal vagal complex,32 and we therefore tested whether Phox2b-Cre would selectively target Klb-Flp neurons in the NTS. As expected, Phox2b-Cre-based recombination (GFP induction) was not detected in the Klb-Flp neurons in the SCN (Figure 2G). However, Phox2b-Cre also only targeted some of the Klb-Flp neurons within the NTS (Figure 2H). Thus, Phox2b-Cre is not an effective tool for targeting the entirety of NTS FGF21 signaling in this Klb-Flp mouse line.

Finally, because subsequent data focus on the Klb-expressing neurons within the NTS, we analyzed the anatomical location of Klb-Flp neurons throughout the extent of the NTS (Figure S3). Based on the tdTomato expression, Klb-Flp neurons are primarily found in the intermediate NTS (iNTS) and to some extent in the anterior part of the caudal NTS (cNTS), spanning approximately from bregma −7.2 to −7.7 mm. While the cell bodies of Klb neurons are most densely populated centered around the bregma −7.4 mm, their fibers extend both rostrally and caudally in the NTS, suggesting potential interactions with other NTS neurons. A very few Klb neurons are found in the area postrema (AP).

KLB neurons in the NTS are responsive to FGF21

Since the above data highlight the NTS and SCN as likely locations for FGF21 signaling, we next tested whether FGF21 impacts the activity of these neuronal populations. Intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of FGF21 (1 mg/kg) had no effect on cFos induction within the SCN (Figures 3A, 3B, and 3F) but robustly induced cFos expression within nearly all Klb-Flp neurons in the NTS (Figures 3C, 3D, and 3F).

(A, B, E, and F) In the SCN, there was no change in total c-Fos or c-Fos colocalization with tdTomato.

(C, D, E, and F) In the NTS and AP, however, FGF21 injection increased total c-Fos and the co-expression of c-Fos and tdTomato.

(G) Slice patch-clamp electrophysiology was also performed in the NTS of reporter mice with administration of hFGF21 (300 nM).

Electrophysiology was then used to directly record from Klb-Flp neurons in ex vivo slices. Within the NTS, FGF21 (300 nM) produced a robust increase in firing frequency in KLB neurons but not nearby unlabeled neurons (Figures 3H and 3I). This increase in firing frequency was associated with an increase in resting membrane potential that was insensitive to inhibitors of synaptic signaling (Figures 3K and 3L). Similar work in the SCN produced more divergent responses, with FGF21 increasing the firing rate in some Klb-Flp SCN neurons but either reducing or having no effect on firing in others (Figure S4). Together, these data indicate that FGF21 can directly activate Klb-Flp neurons within the NTS but produces inconsistent effects within the SCN.

Glutamatergic KLB neurons mediate responses to protein restriction

Since Klb-Flp neurons in the NTS and SCN are differentially marked by Vglut2-Cre and Vgat-Cre (Figure 2), these Cre lines were used to selectively delete Klb to determine the impact on the adaptive response to protein restriction. Male Vglut2-Cre; Klblox/lox (KlbVglut) mice and Cre-negative littermate controls (Klblox/lox) were placed on a control (Con) diet (20% protein) or an isocaloric LP diet (5% protein) at 8 weeks of age and remained on this diet for 5 weeks. We first confirmed that Klb expression was reduced in the NTS with qPCR (p = 0.0021, Figure S5A). Next, we also measured mRNA in the liver to confirm that mice responded to the LP diet. As expected, expression of Fgf21 was increased in both Klblox/lox and KlbVglut animals along with phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase (Phgdh) and asparagine synthetase (Asns), indicating stress in amino acid metabolism (Figure S5B). In male Klblox/lox mice, the LP diet reduced body weight gain and lean mass gain (p < 0.05, Figures 4A and 4D) while also increasing total food intake (p < 0.01, Figure 4B). These LP-induced effects were completely lost in KlbVglut mice, confirming that FGF21 signaling in glutamatergic neurons is required for the response to dietary protein restriction.33

Protein restriction also alters macronutrient preference by increasing the preference/consumption of high-protein foods.1,2,34 Macronutrient preference was tested in male mice by giving them simultaneous access to sippers containing either 4% casein (protein) or 4% maltodextrin (carbohydrate). In Klblox/lox mice, the LP diet decreased maltodextrin consumption while increasing casein consumption (Figure 4E), significantly increasing preference for casein (p = 0.012, Figure 4F). Again, this LP-induced increase in casein preference was absent in KlbVglut mice (Figures 4E and 4F). Finally, total EE was measured via indirect calorimetry. As previously observed, the LP diet significantly increased EE (body weight [BW] adjusted via ANCOVA) in Klblox/lox mice (p < 0.01, Figures 4G and 4H), but this effect was also lost in KlbVglut mice. These data indicate that LP-induced changes in growth, food intake, macronutrient preference, and EE all require Klb expression within glutamatergic neurons, consistent with previous work.33

Prior work indicates that protein restriction does not impact the same array of endpoints in females as in males.35–37 Here, the LP diet did not affect BW gain but did increase food intake in female Klblox/lox mice (p < 0.01, Figures S8A–S8D). However, this LP-induced hyperphagia was lost in female KlbVglut mice. Therefore, while the response to the LP diet may be different in females, these effects nevertheless require KLB signaling in glutamatergic neurons. Despite these differences in behavioral responses to low protein, we did not detect any overt differences in the anatomical location or density of KLB-FLP neurons in males vs. females (data not shown).

GABAergic KLB neurons do not mediate the response to protein restriction

To alternatively test the role of FGF21 signaling in GABAergic neurons, Vgat-Cre was crossed into the Klblox/lox background to create KlbVgat mice. Consistent with previous work, the LP diet reduced BW and lean mass gain, increased food intake, increased preference for casein, and increased EE in Klblox/lox littermate controls (p < 0.05, Figure 5). These effects were also fully intact in KlbVgat mice, with the LP diet similarly altering body weight, food intake and preference, and EE in Klblox/lox and KlbVgat mice (Figure 5). These data indicate that FGF21 signaling (Klb expression) in GABAergic neurons is not required to respond to protein restriction.

Klb expression in the VMH or PVN is not required for the metabolic response to protein restriction

Previous work has suggested that Klb-expressing neurons in both the VMH and PVN contribute to the effects of FGF21 on sweet/carbohydrate intake.26,27 Although we did not detect Klb-Flp-induced recombination in these brain areas, we nevertheless tested whether FGF21 signaling in either the VMH or PVN was required for response to protein restriction. The VMH was targeted by crossing SF1-Cre38 into the Klblox/lox background, generating KlbSf1-Cre mice. KlbSf1-Cre and Klblox/lox littermate controls were placed on a Con or an LP diet as above and fed for 6 weeks. The LP diet again altered growth, food intake, and EE in male Klblox/lox mice, and these effects were intact in KlbSf1-Cre mice (p < 0.05, Figure S6). In female Klblox/lox, the LP diet increased food intake, and this effect was also intact in female KlbSf1-Cre mice (Figures S8E and S8F). These data indicate that Klb expression within the VMH is unnecessary for the adaptive response to protein restriction.

A similar experiment targeted the PVN using Sim1-Cre.39 Again, the established effects of the LP diet to reduce BW gain but increase food intake, protein preference, and EE were observed in both Klblox/lox and KlbSim1-Cre mice (p < 0.05, Figure S7). However, the effects of the LP diet to increase protein preference were slightly blunted in KlbSim1-Cre mice compared to Klblox/lox littermates (p < 0.01, Figure S7D). Taken together, these data indicate that Klb expression within the PVN is not required for mice to exhibit the adaptive response to protein restriction, except for a modest effect on food choice.

Ablation of KLB neurons in the NTS blocks the effect of the LP diet on food intake and EE

The above data provide little support for a model in which FGF21 signaling in the SCN, VMH, or PVN contributes to the response to protein restriction. Conversely, the NTS contains a large population of Klb neurons that are acutely responsive to FGF21 and marked by Camk2a-Cre and Vglut2-Cre. We therefore targeted these neurons for selective ablation via NTS-specific injections of a Flp-dependent AAV-caspase-3 (Casp3) or AAV-mCherry (control). The LP diet reduced body weight gain in both AAV-Casp3- and AAV-mCherry-injected mice (p = 0.028, Figure 6B), suggesting that Klb-Flp neurons in the NTS are not required for the effects on body weight gain. Conversely, the effects of the LP diet on food intake (p < 0.01, Figure 6D) and EE (p < 0.01, Figures 6E and 6F) were both lost in AAV-Casp3 mice. There was also no difference in protein preference between AAV-Casp3 animals on the Con and LP diets (Figures 6G and 6H); however, this appears to be due to increased casein consumption in the Con group rather than lower consumption in the LP group. To confirm that AAV-Casp3 was not producing off-target effects, AAV-Casp3 was injected into the NTS of Flp-negative mice, where it had no effect on LP-induced increases in food intake and EE (Figure S9). Together, these data indicate that Klb-expressing neurons in the NTS are necessary for changes in food intake and EE in response to an LP diet.

Chemogenetic activation of NTS KLB neurons increases EE and food intake

Since the above data suggest that NTS-KLB neurons are necessary for LP-induced changes in food intake (FI) and energy expenditure (EE), we next tested whether acute activation of these neurons is sufficient to drive changes in FI and EE. This question was first addressed by NTS-specific injection of a Flp-dependent AAV-hM3Dq virus or AAV-mCherry control in Klb-Flp mice. Clozapine N-oxide (CNO)-induced activation of neural activity was first confirmed via ex vivo electrophysiology 3 weeks following NTS-specific injection of AAV-hM3Dq, with CNO significantly increasing both firing frequency and resting membrane potential in Klb-Flp neurons (p < 0.05, Figures S10A–S10D). Similarly, i.p. CNO injection significantly increased cFos expression in NTS-Klb-Flp neurons in mice receiving AAV-hM3Dq (p < 0.02, Figures S10E–S10H).

AAV-hM3Dq- or AAV-mCherry-injected Klb-Flp mice were acclimated to Promethion metabolic chambers and treated with CNO injection (2, 3, or 5 mg/kg i.p.), and EE was assessed over 6 h. CNO treatment increased EE in AAV-hM3Dq vs. AAV-mCherry mice across all doses (p = 0.012, Figure 7B), having the most robust effect at the highest dose (p = 0.0036, Figure 7B). Conversely, CNO did not significantly alter food intake (Figure 7C) during this 6 h period.

A second approach utilized intersectional genetics to target NTS-KLB neurons via the dual recombinase RC::FL-hM3Dq line,40 in which Flp-induced recombination drives GFP expression but the addition of Cre removes the GFP and induces an hM3Dq-mCherry fusion protein. Since Klb-Flp neurons in the NTS are the only neural population labeled by Klb-Flp and Vglut2-Cre, a triple cross of Klb-Flp;Vglut2-Cre;FL-hM3Dq was generated to target hM3Dq expression to this NTS population. Male Klb-Flp;Vglut2-Cre;FL-hM3Dq mice and Cre-negative littermate controls (Klb-Flp;FL-hM3Dq) were placed into metabolic cages at 8 weeks of age and provided CNO in the drinking water (11.25 μg/mL). While CNO treatment did not affect EE in Cre-negative control mice, CNO exposure significantly increased EE in Klb-Flp;Vglut2-Cre;FL-hM3Dq mice over the 3 day experiment (p = 0.02, Figures 7E and 7F), and this effect was equivalent in size to that induced by the LP diet (Figure 7F). CNO also significantly increased the average 24 h food intake in Klb-Flp;Vglut2-Cre;FL-hM3Dq mice but not in Cre-negative littermates (p < 0.05, Figure 7G). Thus, two separate experiments suggest that chemogenetic activation of NTS-KLB neurons is sufficient to drive metabolic endpoints in a manner analogous to an LP diet.

DISCUSSION

The ability to detect and adapt to changes in nutrient availability is fundamental to survival. While the mechanisms underlying energy, water, and salt homeostasis are well characterized, how animals detect and respond to protein restriction has remained enigmatic, despite protein’s critical importance for human health and the growing recognition that protein restriction confers benefits for metabolism and health.7,10,41–43 Our previous work identified FGF21 as an essential mediator of the adaptive response to protein restriction, but the neural circuits through which FGF21 acts remained undefined. Here, we resolve this fundamental question by identifying a discrete population of glutamatergic, Klb-expressing neurons in the NTS that serve as the primary neural substrate for FGF21 action during protein restriction.

To identify the neural circuits mediating FGF21 action, we developed a Klb-Flp mouse line that allows precise targeting of FGF21-responsive neurons through intersectional genetic approaches. This approach revealed two primary populations of Klb-expressing neurons: one in the SCN and another in the NTS. Importantly, these populations are phenotypically distinct: NTS neurons are glutamatergic, whereas SCN neurons are GABAergic. This distinction proved crucial, as deletion of Klb from glutamatergic but not GABAergic neurons blocked the adaptive response to protein restriction, consistent with previous work.33 The selective loss of protein-restriction responses in mice lacking Klb in glutamatergic neurons, combined with our observation that Klb-expressing neurons in the NTS are exclusively glutamatergic, strongly implicated the NTS as a key site of FGF21 action.

Several complementary lines of evidence further strengthened this conclusion. First, FGF21 directly activated NTS-KLB neurons both in vivo (cFos) and ex vivo (electrophysiology). This effect is likely a direct action on Klb neurons, since the effects on firing rate and membrane potential persisted even when synaptic transmission was blocked. Interestingly, we did not observe clear effects of FGF21 within the SCN, as there was no effect on cFos and mixed effects on neural activity during electrophysiological analysis. Second, selective ablation of NTS-KLB neurons using a Flp-dependent caspase prevented protein-restriction-induced changes in food intake and EE. Finally, chemogenetic activation of these neurons was sufficient to increase both food intake and EE, mimicking the key metabolic effects of protein restriction. Together, these data indicate that FGF21 acts through Klb-expressing neurons in the NTS to coordinate changes in food intake and EE in response to protein restriction.

While multiple prior studies have demonstrated Klb expression in the NTS,21,22 few studies have examined the NTS as a site of FGF21 action. One reason is that early work indicated that deletion of Klb with Phox2b-Cre, which targets hindbrain areas including the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus (DMX), NTS, area postrema (AP), and nodose ganglia, did not block the metabolic effects of transgenic FGF21 overexpression.22 Conversely, the effects of FGF21 overexpression were entirely blocked when Camk2a-Cre was used.22 As such, various studies have focused on FGF21 action in areas within the forebrain and midbrain.22,24,26–30,44 Interestingly, our Klb-Flp reporter line does not exhibit recombination in any hypothalamic or midbrain areas other than the SCN, and genetic deletion of Klb from either the VMH (using SF1-Cre) or PVN (using Sim1-Cre) did not affect protein restriction responses. The lack of effect in these hypothalamic areas led us to reevaluate the NTS, as did our observation that Phox2b-Cre targeted only a subpopulation of Klb-Flp neurons within the NTS.

While our data directly implicate the NTS, they do not exclude contributions from other brain regions in mediating FGF21 action on other endpoints or in other physiological states. For instance, areas of the hippocampus have been previously implicated in FGF21 effects on memory,44 while noradrenergic neurons in the locus coeruleus (LC) have been implicated in the response to alcohol or viral infection.28,45 It is also noted that prior work suggested that Klb is expressed in glutamatergic vagal afferents, whose cell bodies exist in the nodose ganglia but send stimulatory projections to the NTS. However, we have not observed Klb expression or Flp-induced recombination within the nodose ganglia. We also note that the deletion of Klb via Phox2b-Cre did not block the metabolic effects of FGF21 overexpression,22 despite Phox2b-Cre targeting the majority of vagal afferents.32 Ultimately, we cannot rule out the possibility that the loss of Klb in an unidentified tissue contributes to the effects observed in our data generated using germline Vglut2-Cre crosses. Conversely, these concerns are less relevant to those experiments in which Flp-dependent AAV-Casp or AAV-DREADD is injected directly into the NTS.

Identifying the NTS as a key mediator of FGF21 action is particularly intriguing given this region’s established role in nutrient sensing and metabolism. The NTS receives direct vagal input from the gastrointestinal tract and also responds to a variety of circulating nutritional signals. FGF21 has previously been shown to cross the blood-brain barrier,46 while its proximity to the AP increases the potential for direct access to the circulatory system. As such, the NTS serves as a primary integration center for visceral signals related to feeding and metabolism. While additional work is needed to identify the neural inputs and outputs of NTS-KLB neurons, our finding that FGF21 acts through glutamatergic neurons in this region adds to growing evidence that the NTS contains distinct neural populations that coordinate specific aspects of nutrient homeostasis.47 Additional work is required to more deeply phenotype this population of Camk2a/Vglut2-positive Klb neurons, as these neurons may represent a unique population within the NTS specifically tuned to protein status.

An unexpected finding in our study was the dissociation between growth effects and metabolic effects following the ablation of NTS-KLB neurons. While caspase-mediated ablation of these neurons blocked protein-restriction-induced changes in food intake and EE, it did not prevent the reduction in growth typically seen with protein restriction. This contrasts with work using Camk2a-Cre or Vglut2-Cre, where all protein restriction responses were blocked.2 Several possibilities could explain this dissociation. First, Camk2a-Cre or Vglut2-Cre represent germline deletions present from birth, whereas AAV-Casp injection ablates neurons in adulthood. Second, brain microinjections may not hit all neurons within a brain area, particularly in structures such as the NTS. This lack of coverage may explain the lack of effect on some endpoints and the generally weaker effects of microinjection vs. germline manipulation. Finally, the growth response to protein restriction may be mediated by a distinct population of FGF21-responsive neurons not marked by our Klb-Flp line. This possibility is particularly intriguing given that our Klb-Flp line appears to mark a much narrower set of neurons than previously published Klb-Cre lines.26 While this suggests our Klb-Flp mice may miss populations with lower expression levels, this line provides high specificity for Klb neurons in the NTS and SCN, two areas clearly marked via Klb in situ hybridization.21,22

Similarly, we also note differences based on the method used to chemogenetically stimulate NTS-Klb neurons. The first approach used local NTS injections of Flp-dependent AAV-hM3Dq into the NTS of Klb-Flp mice, while the second used the Vglut2-Cre;Klb-Flp;FL-hM3Dq intersectional cross. This latter approach uses the FL-hM3Dq line, which only expresses hM3Dq in cells that express both Flp and Cre, taking advantage of the fact that only NTS-Klb neurons express both recombinases in this cross. While both approaches increase EE, we observed an effect on food intake only in the Vglut2-Cre;Klb-Flp;FL-hM3Dq line. Importantly, there are several key differences in these studies. First, CNO was delivered via acute injections in the AAV-hM3Dq study, while it was chronically delivered via the drinking water in the Vglut2- Cre;Klb-Flp;FL-hM3Dq study. Second, while AAV injections provide strong anatomical specificity, the anatomical structure of the NTS makes it more difficult to hit all cells, and there may be variability in the degree of coverage between animals. Conversely, Vglut2-Cre;Klb-Flp;FL-hM3Dq provides full and consistent coverage of all cells across mice but leads to the possibility that FL-hM3Dq may be expressed in some undetected off-target tissue. As such, the less robust response to CNO in the AAV-hM3Dq model could be explained by the more acute treatment, lack of coverage of all NTS-Klb neurons, and/or activation of an off-target site in the intersectional cross. Finally, while the chemogenetic stimulation is valid, we place greater emphasis on the AAV-Casp work, as this model tests the necessity of NTS-Klb neurons in the physiological context of dietary protein restriction and is more representative of our prior work in models of whole-body FGF21 deletion or neuron-specific Klb deletion.

Notably, our findings primarily focus on males, where protein restriction produces robust effects on multiple metabolic endpoints. Prior work indicates that females exhibit a more modest response to protein restriction, particularly in terms of metabolic endpoints,35,36 but the effects of protein restriction on food intake and food choice remain.37 Our work is largely consistent with this evidence, as female mice did not exhibit changes in body weight gain on the LP diet but did exhibit a hyperphagic response. Importantly, this hyperphagic response requires Klb expression in NTS neurons and Vglut2-Cre neurons. This observation suggests that although the magnitude of protein restriction responses may differ between sexes, the fundamental neural circuit mediating these responses remains consistent.

Our findings also raise important questions about the broader neural circuits through which NTS-KLB neurons influence metabolism and behavior. These neurons likely engage downstream circuits controlling both food intake and EE. Indeed, the fact that both food intake and EE increase following chemogenetic activation of these neurons suggests they may coordinate multiple parallel circuits to achieve metabolic homeostasis during protein restriction. Future studies mapping the precise connectivity of these neurons and determining how they integrate with other nutrient-sensing circuits will be crucial for understanding the full scope of their function.

The ability to detect and respond to changes in nutritional state is a core feature of biology, allowing animals to match behavior and metabolism to nutrient availability.34,48 Our work indicates that a discrete population of Klb-expressing NTS neurons serves as the critical hub for FGF21 signaling during protein restriction, resolving a fundamental question about how protein status is sensed and acted upon by the brain. These findings not only advance our understanding of nutrient-specific homeostasis but also identify a precise neural target for therapeutic interventions aimed at harnessing the metabolic benefits of protein restriction.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations should be considered for this study. First, while our Klb-Flp line provides high specificity for Klb neurons in the NTS and SCN, it does not mark some additional brain areas observed in previously published Klb-Cre lines. As such, this may miss neurons with lower Klb expression levels and underreport the total number of Klb-positive neurons/brain areas. Combined with the difficulty of achieving complete viral transduction across the full extent of the NTS, these approaches may underestimate the full contribution of NTS-KLB neurons to some endpoints. Second, the SCN contains a prominent population of GABAergic Klb neurons, but we did not directly manipulate this population, and its functional role remains unresolved. Third, our primary findings were obtained in males, as they exhibit a more robust response to protein restriction. The more limited metabolic response to protein restriction in females was not deeply explored, though we confirmed that the hyper- phagic response in females requires Klb in Vglut2-expressing neurons. Finally, additional work is required to characterize the molecular identity and downstream circuitry of NTS-KLB neurons and to determine how these neurons integrate protein-specific signals with broader nutrient-sensing pathways.

STAR★METHODS

EXPERIMENTAL MODEL AND STUDY PARTICIPANT DETAILS

Animals

All animal studies were approved by the PBRC Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and were performed following the guidelines and regulations of the NIH Office of Laboratory Animal Welfare. Male and female mice were 8–12 weeks of age and maintained on a standard chow diet (Purina 5001) until transfer to experimental diets. All mice were bred on a C57BL/6 background. Klb-Flp mice were generated by inserting Flp recombinases into the 3′ end of the Klb gene, separated by a T2A linker, resulting in the expression of Flp in all cells expressing Klb. Klb-Flp mice were also crossed with two different dual recombinase lines. RC::FLTG mice express tdTomato in response to FLP but GFP in response to Flp/Cre coexpression.31 RC::FL-hM3Dq mice express GFP in response to Flp recombination but hM3Dq-mCherry in response to combined Flp/Cre recombination.40 Additional Cre lines (Vglut2-Cre, Vgat-Cre, Camk2a-Cre, and Phox2b-Cre) were purchased from Jackson Labs and used to create intersectional crosses and phenotype Klb neurons. Klblox/lox mice were provided by Dr. Steven Kliewer19,22 and crossed with various Cre lines (Vglut2-Cre, Vgat-Cre, Sim1-Cre, SF1-Cre) to generate tissue-specific Klb knockouts. Animals were single-housed in a 12:12hr light:dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water unless otherwise noted.

METHOD DETAILS

Experimental diets

Diets were formulated and produced by Research Diets and were designed to be isocaloric by adjusting protein and carbohydrate content while keeping fat constant. The Control diet contained 20% casein (by weight) as the protein source, while the low protein (LP) diet contained 5% casein. Diet compositions have been previously published.2,16,17

Stereotaxic Surgery

Mice were anesthetized with vaporized isoflurane and positioned in the stereotaxic frame. The skull was exposed and cleaned with 0.03% H2O2. A hole was drilled through the skull at coordinates determined relative to bregma. For viral injections targeting the NTS, the coordinates were: AP −7.4 mm, ML ±0.3 mm, and DV −4.75 mm from the surface of the brain. A 1 μL Hamilton syringe connected to an injector was used to deliver 300 nL of virus per injection site. The following viral constructs were used.

AAV-Casp3 (AAV-8/2-hEF1α-dFRT-(pro)taCasp3_2A_TEVp(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

AAV-Casp3 (AAV-8/2-hEF1α-dFRT-(pro)taCasp3_2A_TEVp(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

AAV-mCherry control (AAV-8/2-hSyn1-dFRT-mCherry(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

AAV-mCherry control (AAV-8/2-hSyn1-dFRT-mCherry(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

AAV-hM3Dq (AAV-8/2-hSyn1-dFRT-hM3D(Gq)_mCherry(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

AAV-hM3Dq (AAV-8/2-hSyn1-dFRT-hM3D(Gq)_mCherry(rev)-dFRT-WPRE-hGHp(A))

For ablation studies, Klb-Flp mice received NTS-specific injections of AAV-Casp3 or AAV-mCherry. For chemogenetic activation studies, mice received either Flp-dependent AAV-hM3Dq or AAV-mCherry control. All viruses were provided by the Viral Vector Facility of the Neuroscience Center Zurich (ZNZ), University of Zurich.

Indirect calorimetry

Energy expenditure was measured using Promethion metabolic chambers (Sable Systems). Mice were acclimated to the chambers for at least 3 days before measurements. For chemogenetic activation studies, CNO was administered either by IP injection (2–5 mg/kg) or in drinking water (11.25 μg/ml). Data were analyzed using CalR for initial quality control and mean generation, with EE being subsequently analyzed via ANCOVA with body weight as a covariate.50

Nutrient preference tests

For two-bottle choice tests, mice were simultaneously offered solutions containing either 4% casein/0.1% saccharin or 4% maltodextrin/0.1% saccharin for 3 days. Bottle positions were counterbalanced and swapped daily. Consumption was measured daily and averaged across the test period. Preference ratios were calculated as (casein consumption)/(total consumption).

Electrophysiology

Acute brain slices (300 μm) containing the NTS or SCN were prepared from Klb-Flp;FLTG mice. Briefly, mice were anesthetized by isoflurane and transcranial perfused with a modified ice-cold sucrose-based cutting solution (pH 7.3) containing 10 mM NaCl, 25 mM NaHCO3, 195 mM Sucrose, 5 mM Glucose, 2.5 mM KCl, 1.25 mM NaH2PO4, 2 mM Na-Pyruvate, 0.5 mM CaCl2, and 7 mM MgCl2, bubbled continuously with 95% O2 and 5% CO2.51 The mice were then decapitated, and the entire brain was removed and immediately submerged in the cutting solution. Slices (250 μm) were cut with a Micron Leica VT1000s vibratome (Leica, IL, U.S.). Brain slices containing the DRN were obtained for each animal (bregma −2.06 mm to −1.46 mm; Interaural 1.74 mm–2.34 mm). The slices were recovered for 1 h at 34°C and then maintained at room temperature in artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF, pH 7.3) containing 126 mM NaCl, 2.5 mM KCl, 2.4 mM CaCl2, 1.2 mM NaH2PO4, 1.2 mM MgCl2, 5.0 mM glucose, and 21.4 mM NaHCO3) saturated with 95% O2 and 5% CO2 before recording. Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were performed at 32°C–34°C in artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF). The current-clamp mode was engaged to measure neural firing frequency and resting membrane potential. The values for RM and firing frequency averaged within the 2-min bin. FGF21 (300 nM) was puff delivered and changes in membrane potential and firing frequency were recorded. For synaptic blockade experiments, CNQX (10 μM), AP5 (50 μM), and picrotoxin (100 μM) were added to the aCSF.

Immunohistochemistry

Mice were transcardially perfused with 10% formalin and brains were post-fixed overnight. Following cryoprotection in 30% sucrose, brains were sectioned at 30 μm on a sliding microtome. For cFos studies, Klb-Flp::FLTG mice received IP injection of either FGF21 (1 mg/kg) or saline 1.5 h before perfusion. Cells positive for tdTomato (Klb-expressing) and cFos were manually counted by the count tool in Adobe Photoshop. Only sections with matching anatomical locations that exist in all animals were used for counting. Sections were immunostained with antibodies against cFos (1:1000, Synaptic Systems), dsRed (1:1000, Takara), and GFP (1:1000, Abcam). Secondary antibodies conjugated to biotin or fluorophores were used at 1:200 dilution.

RNAscope in situ hybridization

RNAscope HiPlex v2 reagents were used to colocalize Klb mRNA with tdTomato expression in Klb-Flp;FLTG mice following the manufacturer’s protocols (Advanced Cell Diagnostics). Sections were imaged using a Leica TIRF/DM6000 microscope.

QUANTIFICATION AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the SAS software package (SAS V9, SAS Institute) using one-way, two-way, or repeated measures ANOVA using the general linear model procedure. When experiment-wide tests were significant, post-hoc comparisons were made using the LSMEANS statement with the PDIFF option, and represent least significant differences tests for pre-planned comparisons. Average daily energy expenditure was analyzed via analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with body weight as the covariate using the general linear model procedure of SAS. All data are expressed as mean ± SEM, with a probability value of 0.05 considered statistically significant. Group sizes are described in their respective figure legend. *p < 0.05.

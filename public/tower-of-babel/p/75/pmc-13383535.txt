A rare case of giant cardiac malignant tumor with positive panel reactive antibody
Authors: Xing Chen, Yuqing Zhang, Wenjun Yu, Wei Wang, Jie Cai, Jinping Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeA 31-year-old female patient presented with a giant primary cardiac malignancy occupying the right atrium and invading the tricuspid valve and right ventricle.MethodsDue to the inoperability of the tumor by conventional surgery, heart transplantation was considered. However, the patient had a strongly positive panel reactive antibody (PRA), indicating a high risk of rejection. Our team performed an emergency heart transplantation and administered a combination of immunosuppressive therapies postoperatively.ResultOne month after the transplantation, the patient recovered smoothly and was discharged from the hospital.ConclusionDespite giant cardiac sarcoma and high PRA-mediated rejection risk, tailored perioperative strategies, including aggressive desensitization, multimodal immunosuppression, and close surveillance, enabled successful transplantation and recovery. This highlights the feasibility of heart transplantation in select sarcoma cases with meticulous rejection management.Keywords: Heart transplantation, Cardiac malignancy, PRA, Immunosuppression

Introduction

Primary cardiac malignancies are rare. And for the giant primary cardiac tumors such as sarcomas, surgical treatment was the preferred one [1]. But when the tumors seriously affect and lead to deterioration of heart function, with these tumors inoperable and difficult to remove, heart transplantation may be considered as a last resort.

Patients with strongly positive PRA face a high risk of immune rejection, closely related to the risk of patient mortality as a relative contraindication for heart transplantation. However, when heart transplantation is the last line of hope, desensitization treatment or are usually required to control the risk of rejection before surgery to improve the survival rate [2].

In this case, a 31-year-old female patient with a giant primary cardiac malignancy and a strongly positive PRA received a heart transplantation and a combination of immunosuppressive therapies, resulting in recovery.

Case report

A 31-year-old female experienced fatigue, nausea, and vomiting without obvious triggers and sought treatment at Wuhan Central Hospital. She denied palpitations, chest pain, headache, or dizziness. Cardiac ultrasound revealed a solid mass in the right atrium, right atrial enlargement, and significant pericardial effusion. Imaging studies, including pulmonary CTA, echocardiography, cardiac MRI, and PET-CT, confirmed a giant malignant tumor in the right atrium, with suspected pulmonary embolism and right heart failure with no obvious signs of tumor metastasis. The patient was diagnosed with a cardiac tumor, pulmonary embolism, and right heart failure. Besides anticoagulation, lipid-lowering, liver and stomach protection, and anti-infection, her symptoms only partially improved.

Subsequent visits to multiple hospitals confirmed the diagnosis of a cardiac malignancy with refractory heart failure, NYHA class IV, and the HLA antibody testing was positive (Table 1). One month later, the patient developed symptoms related to tricuspid valve obstruction, accompanied by generalized edema, oliguria (ineffective treatment with high-dose diuretics), frequent nausea and vomiting, and severe dyspnea. Under high-flow oxygen inhalation, the patient’s arterial partial pressure of oxygen was only 48 mmHg, prompting her to seek further treatment at our hospital. Upon admission, further examinations confirmed a huge solid mass in the right atrium, with no blood flow signals through the tricuspid valve (Fig. 1). Because of strongly positive PRA, multiple plasma exchanges were performed preoperatively. Given the clear surgical indication, an emergency heart transplantation was performed.

CTA showed a huge solid mass in the right atrium, with no blood flow signal from the tricuspid valve

Under general anesthesia, a median sternotomy was performed. Cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) was established, and the diseased heart was excised, including the tumor-involved right atrium, pericardium, and partial pleura (Fig. 2A). Due to the patient’s tumor invading the right atrium and the junction between the superior vena cava and the right atrium, a higher position was chosen when blocking the superior vena cava, and more tissue was removed to ensure that there was no tumor invasion at the resection margin before anastomosis. And more caval vein tissue was retained during the procurement of the donor heart. After reperfusion and weaning from CPB, hemostasis was achieved, and the chest was closed. The cold ischemia time, cardiopulmonary bypass time, and aortic cross-clamping time for the entire surgery were 123 min, 189 min, and 72 min, respectively.

(A) Gross image of the diseased heart, including tumors involving the right atrium, pericardium, and partial pleura. (B) The postoperative myocardial endomyocardial biopsy results showed diffuse complement C4d positivity

Postoperative pathology suggested angiosarcoma and no abnormalities were found in the pathology of the mediastinal lymph nodes. But the postoperative myocardial endomyocardial biopsy results showed diffuse complement C4d positivity, suggesting acute antibody-mediated rejection (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, a comprehensive anti-rejection treatment plan was initiated, including traditional immunosuppressants (oral tacrolimus, mycophenolate mofetil, and prednisone), steroid pulses, anti-thymocyte globulin, plasma exchanges, and intravenous immunoglobulin. Additionally, novel immunosuppressive agents such as rituximab, eculizumab, and daratumumab were administered (Fig. 3).

Preoperative and postoperative management

Despite fluctuations in PRA levels, the anti-rejection regimen was adjusted based on PRA levels, lymphocyte ratios, and biopsy results. Strict infection prevention measures were implemented, and the patient’s PRA levels were eventually controlled. Her cardiac function gradually stabilized, with no significant immune rejection symptoms or infection within one month postoperatively. The patient was discharged from the hospital in good condition.

Discussion

Cardiac tumors include both benign and malignant types. Common benign tumors include Myxoma, Lipoma, and Fibroma, while malignant tumors include Angiosarcoma and Lymphoma [3]. And this case reported an angiosarcoma. Usually, surgical intervention remains the cornerstone of cardiac sarcoma, predominantly involving tumor resections and palliative debulking. When distant or multiple metastases occur, chemotherapy and radiotherapy are frequently employed in management [1]. But the patient reported in this case has a huge volume, which seriously affects cardiac function, so that conventional resection is unfeasible and heart transplantation is considered [4].

For reducing the rejection risk caused by PRA, the guiding principles for acute rejection treatment focus on 3 key objectives: (1) blocking immune-mediated damage; (2) eliminating or neutralizing circulating alloantibodies; and (3) suppressing ongoing antibody production (both short- and long-term) [5]. Therefore, dynamically monitor antibodies in the blood, regularly perform plasma exchange to remove antibodies, and supplement immunoglobulin to enhance anti-infection ability throughout the perioperative treatment, which may have contributed to the effective control of PRA levels and prevention of immune rejection. However, the long-term outcomes and potential side effects of this combination therapy require further investigation.

Conclusion

Emergency heart transplantation can be a successful treatment option for patients with inoperable primary cardiac malignancies, even those with strongly positive PRA. Comprehensive preoperative preparation, skilled surgical technique, and a multifaceted approach to postoperative management, including the use of novel immunosuppressive agents, are crucial for achieving successful outcomes.

Head Down Position Before Endovascular Treatment for Large Vessel Occlusion: Clinical Trial Design
Authors: Zhao‐Xia Fei, Ying‐Jie Dai, Xin‐Hong Wang, Hui‐Sheng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHead down position (HDP) shows potential benefits in acute ischemic stroke. Although −20° HDP appears safe, feasible, and potentially beneficial in patients with large artery atherosclerosis without reperfusion therapy, its value before endovascular treatment (EVT) in acute large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke remains unknown. This trial aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of −20° HDP in patients with acute large vessel occlusion stroke before planned EVT.MethodsThe HOPES5 (Head Down Position Before Endovascular Treatment for Large Vessel Occlusion) trial is a prospective, randomized, open label, blinded‐end point, multicenter study. Eligible patients with large vessel occlusion will be randomized 1:1 to −20° HDP or a flat (0°) head position before EVT, alongside standard guideline‐based care. Primary outcome is early neurological improvement, defined as ≥4 National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score reduction within 24 hours post EVT. Safety outcomes are HDP‐related adverse events. Based on a prospective cohort, the proportion of early neurological improvement after EVT was ∼31%. A 20% absolute early neurological improvement increase (51%) in the HDP group is assumed. With 2‐sided α=0.05, 80% power, 10% follow‐up loss, and interim analysis alpha consumption, 210 patients (105 per group) are enrolled. All outcomes will have blinded assessment and will be analyzed on the intention‐to‐treat basis. The primary analysis will be stratified by age, sex, diabetes, baseline systolic blood pressure, location of index vessel, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, onset to HDP time, onset to thrombectomy time.ConclusionsThe results of HOPES5 will provide initial evidence on the effect of HDP in patients with acute large vessel occlusion stroke before EVT within 24 h of onset.RegistrationURL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/; Unique Identifier: NCT07172789.Keywords: acute ischemic stroke, early neurological improvement, endovascular treatment, head down position, large vessel occlusionSubject Categories: Cerebrovascular Disease/Stroke

Endovascular treatment (EVT) is a highly effective therapy for acute ischemic stroke involving large vessel occlusion (LVO). 1 A meta‐analysis from 5 randomized controlled trials found that 71% of patients achieved successful recanalization after EVT. However, nearly half of the patients failed to have favorable outcomes, 2 a phenomenon defined as clinically ineffective reperfusion. 3 Thus, there is an urgent need for a simple, safe, and rapidly implementable pre‐EVT intervention to preserve the ischemic penumbra and ultimately improve post‐EVT functional outcomes.

Some studies have shown that changing from upright head position to a lying‐flat head position can increase cerebral blood flow velocity and improve cerebral blood flow to the ischemic penumbra. 4 , 5 In the HeadPoST (Head Positioning in Acute Stroke Trial) trial, no significant differences were observed between patients with acute ischemic stroke placed in the lying‐flat versus sitting‐up positions; however, the lying‐flat position was confirmed to be safe. 6 This raises the question of whether more pronounced head positioning strategies such as head down position (HDP) could yield improved outcomes. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that a −15° HDP enhances collateral circulation, increases cerebral perfusion, markedly reduces infarct volume, and improves functional outcomes, all with a favorable safety profile regarding mortality and cardiopulmonary complications. 7 , 8 , 9 Moreover, −15° HDT was shown to be safe even in cases of intracerebral hemorrhage. 10 Similarly, animal studies conducted by our team revealed that more extreme HDP angles, such as −20°, 11 −30°, and −60°, 12 reduced infarct volume and significantly improved neurological deficits in rats, with mechanisms attributed to augmented cerebral perfusion. Notably, −20° HDP is safe, feasible, and potentially beneficial in improving clinical outcomes in patients with acute ischemic stroke, with no significant adverse events reported. 13 , 14

The primary objective of cerebroprotection before EVT is to salvage penumbral tissue and attenuate infarct core expansion, thereby enhancing the efficacy of EVT. 15 Recent evidence from the ZODIAC (Zero Degree Head Positioning in Hyperacute Large Artery Ischemic Stroke) trial indicated that maintaining a 0° head position in patients with acute LVO served as a protective strategy to promote clinical stability before EVT. 16 A preclinical magnetic resonance imaging study further supported that −15° HDP increased cerebral perfusion before recanalization in experimental acute ischemic stroke. 17

In this context, the HOPES5 (Head Down Position Before Endovascular Treatment for Large Vessel Occlusion) randomized trial is designed to evaluate the effect of −20° HDP in patients with acute ischemic stroke with LVO before EVT.

METHODS

Design

HOPES5 is a prospective, randomized, open label, blinded end point, and multicenter study in China, aiming to assess the safety and clinical improvement of −20° HDP in patients with acute ischemic stroke with LVO before EVT. This study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials reporting guidelines. The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Population

In the HOPES5 trial, eligible patients are adults who are candidate for EVT therapy with acute LVO stroke (initial National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score ≥6, Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score or posterior circulation Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score ≥6, presenting within 24 hours of onset). Patients will be enrolled at 3 to 5 sites in China between September 2025 and September 2026. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in Table 1.

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consent

The HOPES5 trial is registered on www.clinicaltrial.gov (NCT07172789). The study protocol, along with all data collection procedures, was approved by the ethics committee of the General Hospital of Northern Theatre Command and subsequently by all participating sites. Prior to enrollment, written informed consent will be obtained from all patients or their legal representatives.

Randomization and Treatment

As shown in the Figure, eligible patients are randomized 1:1 into the HDP group or control group (0° head position) before EVT by using a central and computerized random sequence generation stratified by center. After all patients were positioned supine (0° head position), a baseline NIHSS score was obtained in both groups before randomization. Intravenous thrombolysis was administered to eligible patients without delay, as per the standard of care. In the HDP group, patients will be positioned to −20° Trendelenburg position after randomization until the intervention of EVT. During this period, participants are instructed to report any discomfort experienced. If the patient cannot tolerate this position, they are slowly repositioned to a horizontal level for 3 to 5 minutes, after which they are returned to the −20° position. Repeated position adjustments may be performed during the HDP intervention. The control group patients will maintain 0° head down positioning before EVT. NIHSS assessments will be performed every 15 minutes during head positioning, with the final assessment conducted immediately before EVT. Investigators will remain at the bedside to ensure maintenance of the assigned position until thrombectomy commences. After EVT, the head position (supine or sitting position) will be determined by local investigator. All patients will receive standard medical care according to national stroke guidelines. 18

Outcomes

The primary outcome is the early neurological improvement, defined as >4‐point decrease in NIHSS within 24±8 hours after EVT.

The secondary outcomes include: the proportion of good collateral circulation status (American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology score of 3–4 points) assessed by the first digital subtraction angiography; change in NIHSS score from randomization to pre‐EVT; reduction in NIHSS score from the baseline at 24±8 hours and 10±2 days; change in cerebral infarct volume at 24±8 hours; change in cerebral edema at 24±8 hours; excellent functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score, 0–1) at 90 days; favorable functional outcome (mRS score, 0–2) at 90 days; ordinal distribution of mRS at 90 days; and new stroke or other vascular event(s) within 90±7 days.

The exploratory end points include change in cortical oxygen saturation determined by near infrared spectroscopy at 24±8 hours, change in dynamic cerebral autoregulation by transcranial Doppler at 24±8 hours, and change in serum biomarkers at 24±8 hours compared with baseline.

The main safety end point is all serious adverse events including cardiopulmonary events, pneumonia, intolerable headache, nausea and vomiting, stroke progression, and adverse events of special interest during HDP including tolerable headache, nausea, tension and fear, which were not present at the beginning of the study.

The secondary safety end points include proportion of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 24±8 hours (ECASS‐III [European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study] criteria) 19 ; proportion of parenchymal hemorrhage types 1 and 2 occurring within 24±8 hours; all‐cause mortality within 10±2 days.

Follow‐Up Procedure

All patients will be followed up before EVT and at 24±8 hours, 10±2 days, and 90±7 days after randomization (Table 3). Baseline assessments will include demographic data, laboratory findings, neuroimaging results, and neurologic scores such as NIHSS and mRS; Additional evaluations will consist of NIHSS score before EVT treatment; noncontrast computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging (including diffusion weighted imaging) at 24±8 hours and NIHSS score at 24±8 hours and 10±2 days; assess mRS at 90±7 days. All clinical evaluations, including NIHSS and mRS, will be conducted by qualified investigators at each site in accordance with standardized protocol guidelines. The primary efficacy outcome will be evaluated by trained and certified site investigators blinded to randomized treatment assignment. Concomitant medications and adverse events occurring within 90 days after randomization will be meticulously documented by investigators and subsequently adjudicated by certified assessors. The total study duration is estimated to be ∼12 months.

Quality Control

In this clinical trial, the Data and Safety Monitoring Committee (DSMC) will conduct safety data verification at 6‐month intervals. Once follow‐up has been completed for 60% of participants, an interim analysis will be performed by an independent statistician affiliated with the DSMC, who will not be involved in the trial's management. The primary purpose of this interim stopping mechanism is to protect participants' rights and interests. The DSMC retains full authority to recommend early termination of the study if either of the following conditions be met: (1) a statistically significant efficacy benefit (P<0.0006), or (2) a safety concern defined as a >2‐fold increase in the rate of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage compared with the control group. Additionally, neuroimaging data will be collected centrally and independently assessed by 2 neuroradiologists.

Data Management and Monitoring

All patients' data will be recorded and securely stored. Enrolled participants will be randomized at a 1:1 ratio to either the HDP group or the control group. An independent DSMC has been established to ensure continuous monitoring of data safety, for example, with a specific focus on end points such as hemorrhagic events and other adverse events. Rules governing study termination or sample size modification will be prespecified by the DSMC. Based on the outcomes of the analysis, the DSMC will be authorized to make recommendations to the Steering Committee of the HOPES5 trial regarding adjustments to the sample size or early termination of the study.

Adverse Event Monitoring

An adverse event is defined as any adverse medical occurrence that takes place during the trial period. All information pertaining to adverse events will be documented on the adverse event page(s) of the case report form. Additionally, the principal investigator will make a further assessment to determine whether an unexpected adverse event is associated with HDP treatment.

Sample Size Estimates

Based on data from the DETECT2‐China (Endovascular treatmenT for Acute Ischemic Stroke in China) trial (unpublished), approximately 31% of patients achieved early neurological improvement following EVT. A conservative estimate assumes an absolute 20% increase in early neurological improvement within the HDP group, resulting in an expected primary outcome rate of 51% in this group. To test the superiority hypothesis, the sample size was calculated using a 2‐sided test with 80% statistical power and a significance level (α) of 0.05. The initial calculation indicated a required sample size of 184 patients. After accounting for a 10% loss‐to‐follow‐up rate and potential α consumption due to interim analysis (using the O'Brien‐Fleming method), the sample size was adjusted to 210 patients, with 105 participants allocated to each group.

Statistical Analysis

An intention‐to‐treat analysis will be adopted to evaluate the treatment effect between the 2 groups. Continuous data with a normal distribution will be presented as mean±SD, and those with a nonnormal distribution will be reported as median (interquartile range). Binary outcomes, including the proportion of patients with early neurological improvement, excellent functional outcome (mRS score, 0–1), favorable functional outcome (mRS score, 0–2), early neurological deterioration, and cerebral hemorrhage, will be analyzed using binary logistic regression. Ordinal logistic regression will be employed to compare the distribution of mRS scores at 90 days between the 2 treatment groups. A general linear model will be used to analyze changes in NIHSS score, infarct volume, or brain edema between treatment groups and Cox regression for time‐to‐event of stroke recurrence and other vascular events. All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS 23.0 software. Statistical significance will be defined as a 2‐tailed P value <0.05.

The primary outcome will further be analyzed according to subgroups: age (<60 versus ≥60 years), sex (female versus male), history of diabetes (yes versus no), history of hypertension (yes versus no), baseline systolic blood pressure (>140 versus ≤140 mm Hg), baseline NIHSS score (6–16 versus >16), location of index vessel (anterior circulation versus posterior circulation), onset to intervention time (<12 versus ≥12 hours), and HDP intervention time (<30 versus ≥30 minutes). Differences in the primary outcome across the aforementioned subgroups will be evaluated by assessing the interaction effect between the prespecified baseline variables and the primary outcome.

Study Organization and Funding

The Trial Steering Committee, comprising external scientific advisors, will conduct regular oversight of the study conduct and data management. The Trial Steering Committee will hold meetings every 6 months, either via teleconference or in person, to provide recommendations pertaining to patient enrollment and trial implementation. This trial is sponsored and supported by the Cerebrovascular Disease Collaboration & Innovation Alliance of Liaoning Province.

Current Status

At the time of this article's initial submission, the patient's recruitment has commenced, and 19 patients are enrolled. Recruitment will remain open until the target sample size is achieved, with completion anticipated no later than June 2026.

DISCUSSION

HOPES5 is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the HDP in patients with acute large vessel occlusion stroke before EVT within 24 hours of onset.

This trial has 2 key distinguishing features. First, ineffective reperfusion following EVT is most frequently attributed to a large baseline ischemic core. 3 Combining cerebroprotective strategies with EVT is critical to expand the volume of salvageable penumbral tissue and slow infarct progression. HDP has demonstrated neuroprotective benefits—such as increased cerebral blood flow and improved perfusion—in multiple animal 7 , 8 , 12 , 17 , 20 and clinical 13 , 21 , 22 studies. The present trial employs a −20° HDP based on findings from our previous animal and clinical research, 11 , 14 which showed that this angle enhances cerebral blood flow in ischemic regions and improves clinical outcomes in patients with acute stroke without significant adverse events. Recent studies also indicate that a 0° head position helps maintain clinical stability in patients with LVO awaiting EVT, 16 and a −15° HDP acutely augments collateral circulation and exerts tissue‐protective effects before recanalization in experimental LVO stroke. 17 Thus, −20° HDP in patients with acute LVO before EVT may freeze penumbra through improving cerebral perfusion, which will improve clinical outcome after successful endovascular reperfusion.

Second, to our knowledge, the HOPES5 trial may be the first to implement −20° HDP in patients with acute LVO stroke before EVT. HDP is a brief, low‐cost, and safe intervention that can be applied before EVT without delaying the procedure. This approach aligns with recent Stroke Therapy Academic Industry Roundtable recommendations, 23 which endorse novel strategies to enhance the effectiveness of recanalization therapies in acute stroke and reduce the futile reperfusion.

CONCLUSIONS

The results of HOPES5 will provide initial evidence on the effect of the HDP before EVT in patients with acute ischemic stroke with LVO within 24 hours of onset.

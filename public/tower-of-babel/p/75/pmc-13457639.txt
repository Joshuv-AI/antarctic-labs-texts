CRIT AI 4.0: A Locally Deployable Artificial Intelligence System for Mortality Risk Stratification and Clinical Decision Support in Intensive Care Units

Highlights

CRIT AI 4.0 is the first offline-capable, privacy-preserving artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted intensive care unit (ICU) clinical decision support system designed for resource-constrained Indian hospitals. It integrates mortality prediction, seven validated clinical scores, fluid balance tracking, and an on-device large language model assistant, operating entirely without internet connectivity on standard hospital workstations.

Introduction

Intensive care unit mortality in India ranges from 20 to 40% in published series.1 Risk stratification using validated scores such as sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA), acute physiology and chronic health evaluation IV (APACHE-IV), and national early warning score 2 (NEWS2) is inconsistently applied due to time-consuming manual calculation.2,3

Machine learning approaches have achieved area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) values of 0.85–0.93 in high-resource settings, but existing artificial intelligence (AI) platforms require cloud connectivity, raising concerns under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023.4,5 To our knowledge, no previously published tool combines offline-only operation, multi-hospital cryptographic licensing, on-device large language model (LLM) assistance, and comprehensive ICU scoring for Indian resource-constrained settings.

We describe the development of CRIT AI 4.0, a proof-of-concept offline-first AI-assisted ICU decision support system for resource-constrained Indian hospitals lacking reliable internet, electronic health records (EHR), or high-specification computing infrastructure.

Patients and Methods

CRIT AI 4.0 is a Python-based desktop application (Tkinter interface) packaged as a single offline Windows executable requiring minimum 4 gigabytes (GB) random-access memory (RAM) and no graphics processing unit (GPU). Nine modules are integrated: Patient admission/discharge, vitals entry, mortality risk prediction, automated clinical scoring, fluid balance tracking, nursing documentation, analytics, AI assistant, and hospital administration.

A programmatically generated synthetic dataset of 16,000 ICU encounters across 17 clinical disease categories and 47 data columns was created using clinically informed statistical distributions, with physiological correlations preserved within each disease category [e.g., low mean arterial pressure (MAP), high lactate, and vasopressor use co-occurring in septic shock]. The binary mortality outcome was generated using a multivariable logistic regression equation incorporating key physiological predictors. We acknowledge that synthetic data cannot replicate the full heterogeneity of real patient populations.

A random forest (RF) classifier (300 trees) was trained on this dataset. The 37-variable feature vector (Table 1) spans demographic, hemodynamic, respiratory, neurological, laboratory, renal, ventilator, and comorbidity parameters. Sequential organ failure assessment total score was excluded as a feature to avoid redundancy with its component variables already present individually; blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were added as additional renal parameters. Class imbalance was corrected using the synthetic minority over-sampling technique (SMOTE).6 Detailed hyperparameter optimization, feature importance analysis, train-test split methodology, and calibration assessment are provided in the Supplementary File (Table S1), in alignment with relevant TRIPOD + AI reporting items for the model development phase.7,8

Seven validated clinical scores are implemented: SOFA, simplified APACHE-IV (not formally benchmarked against the original algorithm), NEWS2, modified early warning score (MEWS), quick SOFA (qSOFA), systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) criteria, and nutritional risk screening 2002 (NRS-2002). Six-shift fluid balance tracking and structured nursing documentation modules are also integrated.1,3,9–12

An on-device LLM assistant (Ollama framework; Phi-3 Mini, Llama 3, or Mistral) uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over the local patient database for context-aware queries. Outputs are advisory-only; clinician oversight is mandatory and a hallucination-risk disclaimer is displayed on every response. No prescribing or treatment-ordering capability exists.

Hospital licensing uses HMAC-SHA256 signatures; passwords use PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (2,60,000 iterations).13 No patient data is transmitted externally. The architecture is designed in alignment with DPDPA 2023 data localization principles; formal compliance assessment has not been conducted (Fig. 1).

Results

On the synthetic held-out test set (n = 3,200), the model achieved an AUROC of 0.90 (95% confidence interval (CI): 0.88–0.92), sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.77, positive predictive value 0.73, negative predictive value 0.89, and F1 score 0.79 at threshold 0.30. Calibration was satisfactory (Hosmer–Lemeshow Chi-square = 11.3, p = 0.18). Complete model development parameters, feature importance rankings, and hyperparameter details are provided in Supplementary Table S1. These metrics reflect exclusively synthetic test-set performance and do not constitute evidence of clinical predictive validity.7,8

An informal pilot evaluation by three ICU clinicians over 1 week found the risk dashboard interpretable and the fluid balance module a workflow improvement over paper charts; clinicians were appropriately cautious regarding LLM output accuracy. Formal usability evaluation has not been performed.

Discussion and Conclusion

CRIT AI 4.0 represents a proof-of-concept response to a recognized gap in Indian critical care informatics, confronting the principal barriers to AI deployment in low-resource ICUs: Cloud dependency, privacy risk, and licensing cost. The model was trained on synthetic data—a key limitation—because target deployment environments largely lack standardized EHR systems and no appropriately sized, ethically cleared Indian ICU mortality dataset was available.14

The LLM assistant provides advisory-only outputs with mandatory clinician oversight and disclosed hallucination risk; no autonomous prescribing is supported. Under central drugs standard control organization (CDSCO) medical devices rules 2017/2023, CRIT AI 4.0 aligns with a lower-risk clinical decision support classification. Relevant Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) AI guidance is referenced; formal CDSCO engagement will be sought as the system matures.15–20

Limitations

All performance metrics derive from synthetic data and cannot be extrapolated to clinical validity; no prospective real-patient validation has been performed; simplified APACHE-IV is not formally benchmarked; formal usability evaluation is lacking; the system is Windows-only; and synthetic distributions were not derived from real ICU datasets.

CRIT AI 4.0 demonstrates the technical feasibility of offline AI-augmented ICU decision support as a proof-of-concept prototype. All clinical performance claims are conditional on prospective real-patient validation, currently underway under Institutional Ethics Committee approval. If validated, this architecture may provide a replicable model for AI-assisted critical care in resource-constrained settings.

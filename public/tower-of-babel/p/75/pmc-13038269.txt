Which One Can Detect More Osteopenia and Osteoporosis in Rheumatoid Arthritis Patient? Dual-Energy X-Ray Absorptiometry (DXA) or Trabecular Bone Score (TBS)
Authors: Diba Monjezi, Behrokh Shojaie, Mansour Salesi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The early diagnosis of osteopenia and osteoporosis can slow the progression of osteoporosis and postpone possible bone fractures in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) because of the benefit of timely treatments. This study was conducted to evaluate the efficiency of dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) and trabecular bone score (TBS) in patients with RA to introduce a better method for the early diagnosis of osteopenia and osteoporosis.Methods:Data on DXA and TBS from 88 patients with RA and 52 healthy people who met the inclusion criteria of this retrospective study were analyzed based on descriptive and inferential statistics.Results:A significant difference was observed between the diagnostic abilities of TBS and DXA. Unlike the TBS method, DXA detected higher percentages of RA patients with osteopenia (31.82% TBS vs. 51.14% DXA) and osteoporosis (21.59% TBS vs. 26.14% DXA). Gender might also affect the diagnostic ability of DXA and TBS in patients with RA. In a group of RA patients, TBS diagnosed a lower percentage of patients with osteopenia and osteoporosis (26.83% TBS vs. 68.29% DXA for osteopenia and 17.07% TBS vs. 26.82% DXA for osteoporosis). These results may indicate a lower accuracy of the TBS method for diagnosing bone mass than DXA.Conclusions:Although previous studies have demonstrated that TBS is a proper method for the evaluation of bone microstructure, our study suggests that the results of the DXA should not be ignored for the early detection of osteopenia and osteoporosis in patients with RA.

Introduction

As the most common chronic inflammatory arthritis, rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is an autoimmune disease with an unknown etiology associated with symmetric polyarthritis.[12] Various damages to internal organs and comorbidities such as diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, cancers, and osteoporosis commonly occur with rheumatoid arthritis.[34] The inflammatory nature of rheumatoid arthritis, through increasing pro-inflammatory cytokines, causes changes in the biomechanical properties and contents of the bones, which consequently leads to osteoporosis. Rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA) are considered other factors involved in bone erosion and osteoporosis in RA.[5] Moreover, anti-rheumatic drugs can play a crucial role in the formation of osteoporosis.[67]

In osteoporosis, the reduction of bone density and structural alterations of bones results in more bone fragility, which is associated with a marked increase in the risk of bone fractures.[89] Osteoporosis caused by rheumatoid arthritis is not only the main factor in increased disability and mortality in patients with RA but also imposes negative socio-economic effects on society.[10]

Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) of the lumbar spine or femoral neck is a standard method usually used to diagnose osteoporosis.[1112] However, this bone densitometry test often failed for the correct evaluation of osteoporosis in patients with RA due to a higher incidence of vertebral body fractures in these patients in comparison to their peers with other diseases. Furthermore, DXA only detects bone mineral density (BMD), which sometimes poses some limitations. This test does not determine bone structure as an effective factor in fracture resistance.[1314] To overcome the limitations of DXA, another method named the trabecular bone score (TBS) has been developed based on the three-dimensional microstructural evaluation of DXA images.[15]

Trabecular bone score (TBS) provides an index for microscopic assessment of bone structure and fracture risk. High values of TBS indicate a dense bone microstructure, and porous bone microstructure correlates with low TBS values.[16] Studies show that osteophytes do not affect TBS, while osteophytes can falsely increase BMD in patients with osteoarthritis.[17]

Since the prevalence of osteopenia and osteoporosis in patients with RA results in increased morbidity and mortality in these patients and also imposes additional costs on the patient and the health care system, the use of diagnostic methods for osteoporosis will greatly help both patients and physicians to slow the progression of osteoporosis in patients with RA. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the efficiency of conventional densitometry (DXA) and TBS in patients with RA to introduce the most efficient method for early diagnosis of osteopenia and osteoporosis in these patients.

Methods

For this retrospective study, we used the recorded data of bone densitometry tests (DXA and TBS) from 88 patients with RA referred to hospitals and rheumatology clinics at our institute and 52 healthy people that met the inclusion criteria of the study. Data were collected from March 2020 to March 2021. The inclusion criteria for patients were: having RA for more than 5 years; being over 45 years old; and examination of bone mineral density by both DXA and TBS methods. We exclude RA patients with bone fractures or a history of prior fractures and other diseases such as a systemic lupus erythematosus, ankylosing spondylitis, vasculitis, scleroderma, diabetes, thyroid disorders, hyperparathyroidism, hypogonadism, and renal failure. Healthy subjects older than 45 years (the age of healthy individuals ranged from 50 to 77 years) were included in this study as control group whose bone mineral density was examined by both DXA and TBS. In addition, healthy individuals with a previous history of fractures were excluded from the study. Informed written consent was obtained from all patients and healthy people.

BMD (g/cm2) of the lumbar spine was measured using the Hologic Discovery W DXA system (Hologic Inc., USA), and interpretation of results was performed based on the manufacturer’s guidance. TBS was also estimated by the DXA images of the lumbar spine obtained from all patients and the healthy control group. The BMD precision error (%CV) for lumbar spine scans was 1%. T-scores of BMD of the lumbar spine were used to predict normal bone condition (T-score ≥ −1.0), osteopenia or low bone mass (−2.5 < T-score < −1.0), and osteoporosis (T-score ≤ −2.5) based on International Society of Clinical Densitometry (ISCD) guidelines.[181920] TBS values of the lumbar spine were also considered to categorize bone status into normal microarchitecture (TBS ≥ 1.326 for men and TBS ≥ 1.357 for women), partially degraded microarchitecture (1.231 < TBS < 1.326 for men, 1.287 < TBS < 1.357 for women), and fully degraded microarchitecture (TBS ≤ 1.231 for men, TBS ≤ 1.287 for women).[21] In addition, because of the report of T-scores for TBS values in our study, we have classified bone health status into three groups, including normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis, similar to what has been suggested for T-scores of BMD, and then we have considered normal microarchitecture, partially degraded microarchitecture, as normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis, respectively.

Descriptive statistics, including absolute frequency, relative frequency, mean, and standard deviation, were used to analyze the data. The frequencies and the mean of variables [Table 1] were compared by the chi-square test and the compare means, respectively. Data were analyzed using SPSS.21, and bar charts were drawn using Excel 2010.

Results

The results of comparing the two methods of bone densitometry tests (DXA and TBS), in the whole study population (patients and healthy groups) showed that most of the subjects had osteopenia. Using the DXA method, the percentage of people diagnosed with normal status was the same as the percentage of people with osteoporosis (25%), but with the TBS method, the number of people without osteoporosis or osteopenia (normal) was about 3.5 times the number of people with osteoporosis [Figure 1a and b].

Frequencies of the study people with normal bone condition, osteopenia, and osteoporosis detected by DXA and TBS methods. (a: absolute frequency and b: relative frequency in total study population), (c: absolute frequency and d: relative frequency of the RA patients and the healthy people)

A comparison of the results of DXA and TBS in two groups of people, including patients with rheumatoid arthritis and healthy people, indicated that there were significant differences (P < 0.05) in the frequencies of people with osteopenia and normal conditions between the two methods in patients with RA, while no remarkable difference was observed between these methods in terms of osteoporosis in RA patients. In contrast, in the healthy group, TBS significantly (P < 0.05) detected the number of people with osteoprosis less than DXA. However, a higher percentage of people with normal conditions were detected by TBS in comparison with DXA [Figure 1c and d].

Analysis of DXA and TBS data based on sexuality in two groups of control and RA patients showed that osteopenia, osteoporosis, and normal bone conditions were detected by DXA and TBS for women in both groups of healthy and RA patients. Whereas, TBS was not able to detect any osteoporosis signs in men of both groups, despite the detection of a significant percentage (P < 0.05) of osteoporosis by DXA. [Figure 2a-d].

Frequencies of the women and men with normal bone condition, osteopenia, and osteoporosis detected by DXA and TBS in two group of patients with RA and the healthy people (a: absolute frequency in healthy group; b: relative frequency in healthy group; c: absolute frequency in RA patients; d: relative frequency in RA patients)

For further analyses, we divided the study population into homogenous and heterogeneous groups. Homogenous groups included people whose diagnosis of bone problems by both DXA and TBS was similar, and heterogeneous groups, composed of people whose DXA and TBS results were different. For the homogenous group, the highest and lowest percentages of frequency belonged to the normal bone condition and osteoporosis in the healthy group. There was no significant difference among the relative frequencies of RA patients in terms of bone health status [Figure 3a and b].

Frequencies (a: absolute frequency; b: relative frequency;) of the RA patients and the healthy people with normal bone condition, osteopenia and osteoporosis detected by DXA and TBS in the homogenous group and frequencies (c: absolute frequency; d: relative frequency) of the RA patients and the healthy people with normal bone condition, osteopenia and osteoporosis detected by DXA and TBS in the heterogeneous group

In the heterogeneous group, the results of DXA and TBS for healthy people and RA patients were not similar and showed high diversity. For example, in the healthy group, about 39% of people were diagnosed with osteoporosis by DXA, while TBS did not detect any person with osteoporosis. In addition, in patients with RA, most patients had osteopenia (about 68%) based on DXA results, whereas TBS results indicated a higher frequency of normal bone conditions in RA patients [Figure 3c and d].

Analysis results of TBS showed that 40 of the 69 (57.97%) people diagnosed with osteoporosis and osteopenia by TBS, in the DXA method were normal, while TBS diagnosed osteoporosis and osteopenia in 5 normal people (7.25%) who had been detected by DXA. Moreover, 18 of the 69 people (26.09%) who had been diagnosed with osteoporosis by DXA had osteopenia based on the TBS method, in contrast, 8.69% of people (6 of the 69) were detected to have osteoporosis by TBS despite DXA results that determined they had osteopenia. Discussion

In this study, the difference between the trabecular bone score (TBS) and dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) was evaluated for the diagnosis of osteoporosis and osteopenia in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA). According to previous studies, the fracture risk will increase in patients with RA in comparison with healthy people.[2223] Therefore, early detection of osteoporosis-related bone disorders can be a great advantage in preventing the deterioration of health in patients with RA. Since this early diagnosis depends on the diagnostic ability of methods, comparing existing methods will help physicians choose an appropriate technique with reliable results for diagnosis.

Our results showed a significant difference in diagnostic ability between trabecular bone score (TBS) and dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) for the two study groups. The DXA method is an index for bone mineral density and depends on the accumulation and amount of minerals in bones, while TBS is a textural index for bone microarchitecture.[15] Since the functional nature of these two methods is not the same,[24] presenting different diagnostic results to determine the level of bone health (normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis) is not unexpected. However, which of these methods is more efficient in determining bone health may depend on a number of factors, such as age, sex, having a special disease, lifestyle, and genetic. Previous evidence showed that lumbar TBS and areal BMD are age/sex-dependent variables.[25]

In our study, we investigated the effects of RA on the diagnostic abilities of DXA and TBS. RA is an autoimmune disease that threatens the bone health of patients directly through inflammatory factors, autoantibodies and indirectly due to the long-term consumption of different drugs, especially corticosteroids.[526272829] Therefore, according to previous studies, the incidence of bone problems such as osteopenia and osteoporosis in patients with RA is higher than in healthy people.[3031] Based on our results, the DXA method detected higher percentages of osteopenia and osteoporosis in RA patients in comparison with TBS [Figure 1d]. This result implied that the TBS method could not detect all changes in bone structure in most RA patients. In agreement with this statement, previous studies have demonstrated that there is no correlation between the results of BMD and TBS.[3233] In connection with the use of conventional dual X-ray densitometry for determining bone mass in RA patients, there is a report of a low level of BMD at the lumbar spine in ACPA-positive RA patients, which has been measured by DXA.[34] In addition, the negative effects of high titers of anti-carbamylated protein antibodies (anti-CarPA), other RA autoantibodies, on a significant decrease of BMD at the lumbar spine have been determined by the DXA method.[35]

With further analysis, we have found that gender might also affect the diagnostic ability of DXA and TBS methods in diagnosing osteopenia and osteoporosis in patients with RA. As shown in Figure 2, the ability of TBS for detection of osteoporosis in women with RA was greater than that of DXA so that using DXA, 2.5% of the women had osteoporosis, while using the TBS method, 22.5% of the women had osteoporosis. On the other hand, the percentage of men with osteoporosis detected by TBS (12.5%) was significantly lower than the percentage of osteoporotic men detected by DXA (37.5%). These results may indirectly indicate that RA has significant destructive effects on bone texture changes in women in comparison with men, so that these bone changes in women are more detectable by the TBS method. Due to the lower rate of RA in men than women,[36] we could not include more men who met the criteria of this study; however, for a more definitive conclusion, it is necessary to include more men with RA whose bone mass is measured by both the DXA and TBS methods.

According to our analysis of the heterogeneous groups, the percentage of people with the normal bone condition who had been detected by TBS in both the healthy group and RA patients was significantly higher than DXA. In addition, in comparison with DXA results, TBS diagnosed a lower percentage of people with osteopenia and osteoporosis, indicating the possibility of lower accuracy of the TBS method in diagnosing bone problems. However, a previous study on men and women has shown that TBS is associated with age, physical activity, and lifestyle factors.[37]

Conclusions

Choosing the proper method to assess osteopenia and osteoporosis in patients with rheumatoid arthritis is a key factor in the early detection of these bone disorders and the prevention or slowing of their progression to reduce bone problems in rheumatic patients. Although the trabecular bone score (TBS) has been introduced as one of the best methods for the diagnosis of osteoporosis and osteopenia, the results of the present study revealed that it is better not to rely only on the results of the TBS method, and the results of the DXA should also be considered.

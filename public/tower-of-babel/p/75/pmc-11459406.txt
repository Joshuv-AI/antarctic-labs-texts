Advancing infrared and visible image fusion with an enhanced multiscale encoder and attention-based networks
Authors: Jiashuo Wang, Yong Chen, Xiaoyun Sun, Hui Xing, Fan Zhang, Shiji Song, Shuyong Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryInfrared and visible image fusion aims to produce images that highlight key targets and offer distinct textures, by merging the thermal radiation infrared images with the detailed texture visible images. Traditional auto encoder-decoder-based fusion methods often rely on manually designed fusion strategies, which lack flexibility across different scenarios. Addressing this limitation, we introduce EMAFusion, a fusion approach featuring an enhanced multiscale encoder and a learnable, lightweight fusion network. Our method incorporates skip connections, the convolutional block attention module (CBAM), and nest architecture within the auto encoder-decoder framework to adeptly extract and preserve multiscale features for fusion tasks. Furthermore, a fusion network driven by spatial and channel attention mechanisms is proposed, designed to precisely capture and integrate essential features from both image types. Comprehensive evaluations of the TNO image fusion dataset affirm the proposed method’s superiority over existing state-of-the-art techniques, demonstrating its potential for advancing infrared and visible image fusion.Subject areas: Applied sciences, Engineering

Introduction

Visible images, aligning with the human visual system, offer high-resolution texture details and clarity but are susceptible to adverse environmental conditions. In contrast, infrared images, utilizing radiation differences, excel in target detection against backgrounds in low-light or low-visibility conditions, albeit at the expense of resolution and detail in texture and edges. The fusion of visible and infrared images merges the thermal radiation advantages of the latter with the detailed texture of the former, finding extensive applications in fields such as medical diagnosis,1 industrial inspection,2 video surveillance,3 and remote sensing.4

Image fusion techniques are categorized into traditional and deep learning-based methods. Initially, spatial domain methods, including subspace-based5,6 and saliency analysis-based7,8 approaches, were the focal points of image fusion research. These methods, however, are prone to spectral and spatial distortions. To mitigate these issues, transform-domain-based methods,9 utilizing transformations like pyramid,10 wavelet,11 and contourlet,12 were developed to reduce distortion. Despite these advancements, noise introduced during fusion limits their effectiveness. Traditional methods, including sparse representation13,14 and dictionary learning,15,16 though satisfactory in specific scenarios, face limitations in adaptability and often introduce artifacts, affecting fusion quality.

Recent advancements have seen the application of deep learning to image fusion, significantly enhancing performance over traditional methods. Deep learning’s superior feature extraction and refinement capabilities have led to its success. These methods are broadly classified into three categories based on the underlying frameworks17: convolutional neural networks (CNNs), generative adversarial networks (GANs), and auto encoders (AEs), each surpassing traditional approaches in fusion quality.

CNN-based methods utilize designed networks for feature extraction, aggregating these features to form maps, and, ultimately, deconvolution networks produce the fused image. The fusion performance heavily relies on the network structure’s design, with the integration of feature extraction networks and fusion units being paramount. In 2019, Lia et al.18 introduced a framework combining residual networks (ResNet)19 with zero-phase component analysis. Subsequently, in 2022, Ma et al.20 proposed SwinFusion, blending CNN and Transformer21 technologies to enhance global and local information processing, yielding images with superior visual quality. In 2023, Tang et al.22 proposed an end-to-end model for infrared and visible image fusion via a dual attention Transformer termed DATFuse. In this model, a dual attention residual module is designed for important feature extraction and a Transformer module is devised for global complementary information preservation. However, the complexity of designing optimal structures can increase network complexity, impacting efficiency.

GAN-based methods employ a generator and a discriminator to map input images to a new space and evaluate their authenticity, respectively. This competition aims to align the fused image’s probability distribution closely with the source images, achieving implicit feature extraction, fusion, and reconstruction. FusionGAN by Ma et al.23 in 2018 marked a significant advancement in GAN-based image fusion, followed by improved methods like DDcGAN24 and GANMcG.25 In 2021, Li et al.26 introduced a method using multi-granularity attention networks, enhancing focus on crucial regions. Despite their efficacy, balancing the generator and discriminator remains a critical challenge.

AE-based image fusion starts with training an AE on a broad dataset of natural images, utilizing the pre-trained encoder and decoder for feature extraction and image reconstruction, respectively. A hallmark in this domain, DenseFuse by Li et al.,27 builds upon DeepFuse28 and DenseNet, employing dense blocks and convolutional layers for efficient feature extraction. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on the final layer’s output, leading to significant information loss, DenseFuse utilizes dense blocks to maximize the utility of intermediate layer features, ensuring comprehensive feature integration during fusion. However, its decoder, composed of a simple series of convolutional layers, limits feature retention capability.

Building on this, Li et al.29 introduced NestFuse in 2020, adopting a nest architecture that replaces long skip connections with up-sampling and short skip connections. This structure enables the decoder to use multiscale depth features more effectively than DenseFuse, enhancing information preservation from the encoder. Nevertheless, its fusion strategy, relying on a fixed L1-norm and softmax for attention map generation, lacks adaptability across varying fusion scenarios.

In 2021, Jian et al.30 proposed SEDRFuse, a symmetric encoder-decoder network incorporating residual modules to capture both intermediate and compensatory features from source images. This approach ensures the retention of detailed features through skip connections, enhancing the fusion image’s contrast and brightness while preserving infrared thermal information.

Most recently, Wang et al.31 presented Res2Fusion in 2022, innovating with dense Res2Net blocks and double nonlocal attention models. This architecture, similar to DenseFuse but with Res2Net32 for multiscale feature extraction without down-sampling, significantly improves meaningful information retention. Although its fusion strategy marks an improvement by utilizing spatial and channel attention maps, the inherent untrainability and manual design requirement pose challenges in optimally weighting deep features.

In 2024 Karim et al.33 proposed a multilayer triple dense network for infrared and visible image fusion named MTDFusion. Its encoding network, which consists of a triple network of dense blocks, allows the network to intelligently handle inputs from both infrared and visible images, as well as the difference extracted between them. This design allows fusion images to have better image detail and higher contrast. The same year, LENFusion proposed by Chen et al.34 introduces a luminance adjustment network (LAN) and a luminance feedback network (LFN) into the fusion network, which achieves a beneficial interaction between low-light enhancement and image fusion in the form of a feedback loop. LAN implements adaptive adjustment to the luminance of input visible image in the initial stage, and LFN uses the luminance feedback loss to avoid inappropriate enhancement of the images, improving fusion performance in dim night environments.

In 2021, Li et al.35 introduced RFN-Nest to address the challenges of high manual design complexity in the fusion process and to more effectively extract representative intermediate features. This method combines the residual network’s capabilities with the retained nest architecture to create the residual fusion network (RFN), replacing traditional fusion methodologies and manual rule design. A specialized loss function, incorporating detail loss and feature enhancement loss, trains the RFN to enable the fusion image to reconstruct more scene information and amplify target features. However, the complexity of the RFN’s structure, its large parameter count, and the inefficiency in training and testing processes are notable drawbacks, indicating a need for further enhancement in fusion performance.

To address these issues, this study introduces a fusion method comprising an encoder with skip connections, a decoder with nest architecture, and an attention-based lightweight fusion network. Initially, the encoder and decoder are trained on datasets to closely reconstruct input images. Then, a lightweight fusion network, trained on a dataset of visible and infrared images, utilizes spatial and channel attention to generate attention maps. These maps are weighted and combined to form fused features, which are then reconstructed into the final image via deconvolution in the decoder. This approach aims to enhance feature extraction and retention while simplifying the fusion strategy, ultimately improving fusion performance.

Methodology

In this section, we will introduce the architecture of the proposed fusion method and training strategy briefly; a detailed mathematical description is presented in STAR Methods.

Fusion method architecture

The architecture of our fusion method is presented in Figure 1, which contains three main parts: encoder part, decoder part, and fusion part.

Architecture of the fusion method

Our encoder part contains one convolutional layer whose kernel size is 1 × 1, four CBAM blocks, which is the same as the model proposed by Woo et al.36 in 2018 and four encoder blocks. The CBAM block we utilized is a lightweight yet effective attention module for CNNs. The attention mechanism can focus important information with high weights and ignore irrelevant information with low weights, through which our encoder focuses on important features and suppresses unnecessary ones when extracting features. Each encoder block contains two convolutional layers and one max-pooling layer. In addition, skip connections are adapted to retain the intermediate feature maps, which are well reused to improve feature representation ability. In the traditional neural network, the signal is transmitted from the input layer to the output layer, and the output of each hidden layer needs to be processed by the activation function before being transmitted to the next layer, while skip connection will simultaneously transmit the signal of the current layer to the next layer to solve the problem of gradient explosion and gradient disappearance in the training process. We use the concatenation method for all skip connections which is the same as DenseNet. Compared with the add method, the concatenation method fuses features from different network layers or different network branches. The addition of CBAM block, skip connections, and concatenation method enables the encoder to make full use of the shallow information and focus on the effective region in the process of extracting image features, thus improving the feature extraction ability of the encoder and improving the model performance.

The decoder part based on the nest architecture we utilized is the same as the one in NestFuse. Each decoder block consists of two convolutional layers. In each row, decoder blocks are connected by dense connections. Connections between convolutional layers of different levels concatenate multiscale deep features in the decoder network.

The fusion network is presented in Figure 3. Each fusion network has two channel attention blocks and two spatial attention blocks. Each channel attention block has eight convolutional layers, two ReLU layers, one average pooling layer, one maximum pooling layer, and one sigmoid layer. Each spatial attention block has four convolutional layers, one average pooling layer, one maximum pooling layer, and one sigmoid layer. Channel attention mechanism improves the perception ability of different features by selecting the importance of different channels in the feature map. Spatial attention mechanism selects the most relevant regions for calculation, in order to locate important regions in the image, such as prominent targets in infrared images. By showing the correlation between different channels, the channel attention mechanism automatically obtains the importance of each feature channel and assigns different weight coefficients to each channel, so as to strengthen important features and suppress non-important features. The spatial attention mechanism enhances specific target areas of interest while weakening irrelevant background areas by enhancing feature expression in key areas, generating a weight mask for each location. We parallel the channel attention mechanism and spatial attention mechanism and take advantages of both the mechanisms, so that the fusion network can focus on both important position information and important feature channels in the image, improving the fusion performance of the model.

Training strategy

Due to the use of two functionally independent network structures, it is difficult to design a suitable loss function to satisfy the image recovery capability of encoder-decoder network and feature fusion performance of fusion network when using traditional one-stage training strategy. Simple mean square error and SSIM loss functions are suitable for training the encoder-decoder network, but it will reduce the performance of the fusion network. A complex feature loss function can improve the fusion performance of the proposed model, but it is contrary to the optimization direction of the encoder-decoder network.

We adopt a two-stage training strategy similar to RFN-Nest. In the first stage, we train the encoder-decoder network without the fusion network as shown in Figure 4. At this stage, the encoder-decoder network only needs to make the output image as close as possible to the input image.

Architecture of encoder-decoder network training process

Then, four fusion networks are trained with the fixed encoder-decoder network obtained in the first stage, the architecture of which is shown in Figure 5. The fusion network is used to fuse the deep features extracted by the encoder network and feed the fusion features to the decoder network to reconstruct the fusion image. At this stage, the fusion network only considers incorporating as many infrared and visible features as possible into the fused image.

Architecture of fusion network training process

This two-stage training strategy enables the network to design different loss functions in different stages of training process, which reduces the difficulty of design and training and improves the performance of the fusion method.

Effects of skip connections, CBAM blocks, and Lfeature on fusion performance

In this section, we demonstrated the effectiveness of skip connections, CBAM blocks, and Lfeature through ablation experiments. We constructed five network configurations for this purpose, which are listed in the following. (1) Proposed w/o CBAM & skip: mimics the NestFuse encoder architecture without CBAM blocks and skip connections. (2) Proposed w/o CBAM: incorporates skip connections into the NestFuse encoder. (3) Proposed w/o skip: integrates CBAM blocks into the NestFuse encoder. (4) Proposed w/o Lfeature: remove Lfeature from loss function during fusion network training process. (5) Proposed: the full proposed encoder architecture with both CBAM blocks and skip connections.

Subjective evaluations on the TNO testing dataset reveal distinct performance characteristics among these configurations (Figures 6, 7, and 8). The “Proposed w/o CBAM & skip” configuration tends to favor visible image features, resulting in less pronounced infrared image features and unclear targets. Both the “Proposed w/o CBAM” and “Proposed w/o skip” configurations better integrate infrared features into the fusion result but suffer from diminished background brightness and unclear texture features. The “Proposed w/o Lfeature” configuration fully fuses the infrared features into the fusion result, but almost no texture features of visible image are preserved. Conversely, the full proposed configuration adeptly preserves infrared image radiation information and the detailed texture of visible images, ensuring clear target visibility and texture definition.

Quantitative and qualitative comparisons with other state-of-the-art algorithms

To assess the efficacy of our proposed EMAFusion, we conducted comprehensive comparisons with eight leading algorithms: six AE-based (DenseFuse, NestFuse, RFN-Nest, Res2Fusion, LENFusion, and MTDFusion), three CNN-based (U2Fusion, SwinFusion, and DATFuse), and two GAN-based (FusionGAN and DDcGAN).

The subjective evaluation, illustrated in Figures 9, 10, and 11 through three groups of fusion images, reveals varied performance across the algorithms. DenseFuse, U2Fusion, SwinFusion, LENFusion, and DATFuse exhibit low contrast with targets, highlighted in red boxes, appearing less distinct or even invisible for “soldier behind the smoke.” DDcGAN and RFN-Nest, while preserving information satisfactorily, introduce blurred target boundaries, indicating artifact presence—a problem shared with FusionGAN. NestFuse, Res2Fusion, and MTDFusion adequately preserve radiation information but fail to fully retain visible image texture details, as evidenced in the green boxes in Figures 9 and 11. Notably, their ability to preserve radiation information diminishes in Figure 10, impacting visibility of a soldier concealed in smoke. In stark contrast, EMAFusion excels in maintaining both infrared radiation and visible image texture details with minimal artifacts, closely resembling the source images.

Time complexity analysis

Time complexity, an essential factor in evaluating the efficiency of image fusion methods, is analyzed through the average runtime and parameter count of the discussed algorithms (Table 3). Our EMAFusion method exhibits a competitive edge in runtime, above the average level in all twelve methods, indicating its operational efficiency is within a comparable order of magnitude. The incorporation of attention modules, skip connections, and nest architecture into the AE-decoder network, alongside the utilization of a trainable fusion network, moderately increases the parameter count. However, EMAFusion remains notably efficient, with a smaller parameter footprint than RFN-Nest, SwinFusion, and DDcGAN.

Discussion

This study introduces an innovative image fusion method for integrating infrared and visible images, utilizing skip connections, CBAM blocks, and an attention-based fusion strategy. A multiscale encoder-decoder network enhanced with skip connections and CBAM blocks is developed, aiming to augment feature extraction capabilities and ensure comprehensive feature retention in the fused output. Furthermore, a learnable, lightweight fusion network that employs spatial and channel attention is devised to amalgamate multiscale features with minimal manual intervention. The superiority of our proposed method is validated through both subjective and objective evaluations of a public dataset, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving image fusion quality.

Limitations of the study

In this paper, the loss function is designed for infrared and visible image fusion tasks, and the fusion performance of the fusion network needs to be improved under other fusion tasks. In the future, the loss function will be redesigned for medical image fusion and sharpening fusion tasks, and the fusion network will be trained specifically, so that the proposed method can still play an excellent performance in different fusion scenarios.

Experimental model and study participant details

In the first stage of the training process, we train the auto encoder-decoder network with the MS-COCO dataset37 and LLVIP dataset proposed by Jia et al.38 We randomly chose 80000 complex images in MS-COCO and LLVIP datasets to form our training set. These images are converted into gray scale and resize to 256×256. The epoch and batch size are set to 4 and 8. The learning rate is set to 1×10−4. The parameter λ1 in Equation 6 is set to 100.

In the second stage of the training process, the attention-based fusion network is trained with the LLVIP dataset. The images are converted into gray scale and resize to 256×256. The epoch and batch size are also set to 4 and 8. The learning rate is set to 1×10−4 too. The parameter λ2 in Equation 10 is set to 5, and the parameter λ3 in Equation 11 is set to 500.

In the testing phase, we chose 30 pairs of infrared and visible images from the TNO dataset,39 which includes military images, character images, landscape images, movie images, etc. These image scenes are different from each other, ensuring the universality of the fusion model.

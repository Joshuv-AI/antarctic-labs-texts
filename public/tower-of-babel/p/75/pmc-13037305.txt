Real-world outcomes of Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™: a multicentre observational registry from Southeast Asia
Authors: Keng Tat Koh, Yoga Yuniadi, Yen Yee Oon, Jhi Hui Foo, Saravanan Krishinan, Satchana Pumprueg, Teguh Santoso, Alan Yean Yip Fong, Tiong Kiam Ong, Thorsten Lewalter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLeft Atrial Appendage Occlusion (LAAO) is an alternative therapy for atrial fibrillation (AF) patients at high risk of stroke who have contraindications to long term oral anticoagulation (OAC). However, real-world data specifically regarding the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device remains limited in Southeast Asian (SEA) populations. This study evaluated the procedural characteristics, safety profiles and 1-year clinical outcomes of the Amulet™ device in the multi-country SEA cohort.MethodsThis was a multicentre observational study included four centres across three SEA countries (Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand) from February 2019 to January 2024. The primary outcome was a composite of cardiac mortality and ischemic stroke at 1 year.ResultsA total of 104 patients were enrolled. The mean age was 67.2 ± 10.1 years old and 67.3% male. The mean CHA2DS2-VASc was 4.02 ± 1.45 and mean HAS-BLED score was 3.11 ± 1.17. Majority of the patients (64.4%) had ≥ 2 indications for LAAO implantation. Main indications for LAAO implantation were HAS-BLED score ≥ 3 (55.8%), contraindication for OAC because of renal failure (41.3%) and contraindication for OAC because of bleeding (24.0%). Intracardiac echocardiogram was used in 19.3% of the procedure. The periprocedural complications rate was 2.9%. Device implantation was successful in 98.1%, and 77.9% was successful at first deployment. The 1-year all-cause mortality was 10.6%, with 2.9% cardiac related mortality. Risk of recurrent stroke at 1-year was 1.9% with 1 (1%) major bleeding event. At 12-month, 83.5% of the patients were on single or no antiplatelet therapy.ConclusionsPatients from SEA had multiple indications for LAAO. Overall, the dual-seal Amulet™ device demonstrated high procedural success, low complication rates and associated with low rate of AF-related thromboembolic event while using single antiplatelet therapy in most of the patients. Background

The safety and efficacy of Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion (LAAO) with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ occluder device have been well studied in Western populations, most notably in the LAARGE registry and the prospective Global Amplatzer™ Amulet™ Observational Study [1, 2]. The LAARGE registry demonstrated favourable outcomes at 1-year follow-up in terms of stroke/transient ischemic attack (TIA) (1.3%) and major bleeding (1.6%). Similarly, the Amulet observational study demonstrated a 67% reduction in the ischemic stroke rate compared with the predicted risk, alongside a 98.4% closure rate. While these pivotal trials consistently demonstrated favourable efficacy and safety outcomes, extrapolating these findings to Southeast Asia (SEA) populations requires caution.

Asian patients frequently present with distinct clinical and anatomical characteristics compared to Western cohorts, including lower body mass index, smaller LAA anatomy and different bleeding risk profiles [3, 4]. Furthermore, regional variations in healthcare systems and referral patterns may influence real-world outcomes. Although the WASP and RECORD registries provide valuable insights into LAAO in Asian populations, these studies focused primarily on the WATCHMAN devic [4, 5]. The specific performance of the Amplatzer™ Amulet™, which features a dual-seal mechanism designed to accommodate complex anatomies, remains under-reported in this region.

Given that the Asian population accounts for approximately 60% of the global burden of atrial fibrillation (AF), addressing this regional evidence gap is critical. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the real-world procedural safety and 1-year efficacy of the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ cohort device in a multicentre SEA cohort. Secondarily, we aimed to contextualize these regional results against established European registries to determine if clinical outcomes remain consistent despite demographic and anatomical differences.

Materials and methods

Study Design

This study was an all-comer, voluntary, multicentre, single-arm observational registry conducted in SEA cardiac centres to evaluate the safety and efficacy of LAAO with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device (Abbot Vascular, Santa Clara, CA, USA) in a real-world population. The study was conducted in accordance with the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki, ISO14155, the ICH-GCP guidelines, and applicable local regulatory requirements. The study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05687591) on the January 26, 2023. The first author prepared the initial and subsequent drafts of the manuscript. All authors vouch for the accuracy of the data and confirm that the contents of this article adhere to the study protocol. Participating sites and their principal investigators are summarized in Supplementary Table 1.

Study population

From February 1, 2019 to January 31, 2024, a total of 104 consecutive eligible patients planned for LAAO were recruited from four participating centres in Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia. The recruitment period spanned the COVID-19 pandemic, which impacted patient flow and procedural volumes. The primary analysis was based on the ‘intention-to-treat’ population, defined as all enrolled patients in whom the venous puncture was performed for LAAO. The sample size was determined by the total number of eligible patients treated during this fixed study period. Exclusion criteria were strictly limited to specific clinical contraindications or anatomical factors precluding safe implantation (Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are listed in Supplementary Appendix 1). To reflect routine clinical practice and assess real-world generalizability, peri- and intra-procedural techniques, as well as post-procedural follow-up and care, were performed at the operator’s discretion.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was a composite of cardiac mortality and ischemic stroke at 1 year. Secondary safety endpoints included procedural success, periprocedural complications (pericardial effusion, device dislocation, and access site complications) and major bleeding events. Secondary efficacy endpoints were assessed by the occurrence of overall and cardiovascular death, ischemic stroke, TIA, and systemic embolization. Successful device implantation was defined as successful Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device implantation according to the CLOSE criteria. Anticoagulation and antiplatelet prescription patterns were recorded at discharge, as well as at 3, 6, and 12 months post-LAAO.

Follow-up

Follow-up visits were scheduled at 3, 6 and 12 months post-procedure. Due to pandemic-related restrictions on hospital visits, structured telephone interviews were permitted when physical attendance was not feasible. Imaging follow-up with transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) or cardiac computed tomography (CT) was recommended at 3, 6 and 12 months. However, adherence to imaging protocols was determined by local standard practice and was significantly constrained by COVID-19 logistical limitations.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 16.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NJ, USA) Descriptive data are reported as counts with percentages for categorical variables. Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. And are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, or as median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data.

Time-to-event outcomes, including the cumulative incidence of cardiac death and recurrent ischemic stroke, were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method with 95% confidence intervals (CI) calculated using the Greenwood formula. Patients lost to follow-up were censored at the time of last known contact.

Between-group comparisons were performed using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Given the observational nature of this registry and the limited sample size, multivariable adjustment for potential confounders was not performed to avoid model overfitting. Consequently, all subgroup analyses should be considered exploratory. To assess clinical efficacy, a two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

From February 1, 2019 to January 31, 2024, a total of 104 consecutive patients planned for of LAAO with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device were enrolled across four participating centres. Clinical follow-up at 1-year was completed in 99.0% of the cohort, with 64.0% completed imaging follow-up using TOE or cardiac computed tomography CT scan at 3 months, 56.3% at 6 months and 27.5% at 1-year.

Baseline demographics are summarized in Table 1. The mean age was 67.2 ± 10.1 years, and 67.3% were male. The AF pattern was paroxysmal in 47.1%, persistent in 34.6% and permanent in 18.3%. The most frequent comorbidities were hypertension (92.3%), dyslipidaemia (61.5%), and chronic kidney disease (56.5%). Notably, among those with chronic kidney disease, 66.1% were undergoing long-term dialysis. The mean CHA2DS2-VASc score was 4.0 ± 1.5, and the mean HAS-BLED score was 3.1 ± 1.2.

Indication of LAAO

The three most common indications for LAAO implantation were a HAS-BLED score ≥ 3 (55.8%), contraindication to direct oral anticoagulant D(OAC) due to renal failure (41.3%), and a history of bleeding (24.0%), as shown in Fig. 1. Overall, 64.4% of patients had 2 or more indications for LAAO implantation with a mean number of 1.8 indications per patient. The cohort exhibited a high ischemic risk profile, with 29.8% of the patients had a history of stroke and 11.5% with previous TIA. Additionally, 11.5% of the patients underwent LAAO due to recurrent stroke despite adequate DOAC therapy. The high bleeding risk profile was reflected by 55.8% of the patients having a HAS-BLED score 3 and above, while 18.3% had bleeding risk not reflected by the HAS-BLED score, and 22.1% presented with anaemia at the time of LAAO implantation.

Pre-procedural imaging

Pre-procedural imaging was performed in 103 of 104 patients (99.0%) as shown in Table 2. TOE was utilized in 62.1%, CT in 29.1%, and both modalities in 8.7%. Imaging was predominantly performed while patients were in AF (65%). LAA thrombus was absent in 98.1%. The most common LAA morphology was chicken-wing (48.1%), followed by windsock (45.6%), cauliflower (3.9%) and cactus (1.9%). The mean diameter of the landing zone was 21.2 ± 6.7 mm.

Procedural characteristics

Procedures were performed under general anaesthesia in 77.9% and under conscious sedation in 22.1% (Table 2). Concomitant AF ablation was performed in 26.0% cases with 85.2% using radiofrequency and 14.8% using cryoballoon ablation. Ultrasound-guided vascular puncture was used in 64.4% of procedures. The imaging modalities used during implantation included TOE in 47.1%, intracardiac echocardiography (ICE) in 19.2%, fluoroscopy alone in 23.1%, and a combination of imaging modalities in 10.6%.

The Amplatzer™ Amulet™ LAAO device was successfully implanted in 102 of 104 patients (98.1%). Two procedures were unsuccessful due to the unavailability of the appropriate device size (25 mm) when up-sizing was required. Among the successful cases, 81 (77.9%) were achieved on the first attempt. Repositioning was required in 16.3% of cases, and a change in device size was required in 6.7% (42.9% up-sizing, 57.1% down-sizing). The median implanted device size was 25 mm. The exploratory analysis indicated that the need for device resizing was not significantly associated with concomitant AF ablation (p = 0.415), pre-procedural imaging modality (p = 0.946), general anaesthesia or sedation (p = 0.649), imaging modality used during implant (p = 0.956), or LAA morphology (p = 0.899).

Clinical outcomes at 1-year follow-up

Device- or procedural-related complications included one (1.0%) case of device migration (successfully repositioned), two (1.9%) cases of pericardial effusion (one managed conservatively and the other requiring pericardiocentesis) and one (1.0%) access site complication. All pericardial effusions occurred immediately after the implantation procedure; no delayed cases of pericardial effusion were observed.

All-cause mortality at 1-year was 10.6% (11 of 104), which included three (2.9%) cardiac-related deaths due to acute myocardial infarction. The majority of the non-cardiac deaths were secondary to sepsis or pneumonia (7.7%). One (1.0%) incidence of recurrent ischemic stroke and one (1.0%) TIA occurred. The composite primary outcome of cardiac mortality and recurrent ischemic stroke or TIA at 1 year was 3.9% (Fig. 2). One (1.0%) haemorrhagic stroke was observed during the 1 year follow up with no other major bleeding events reported.

Antithrombotic medications

Antithrombotic prescription patterns evolved significantly over the follow-up period (Fig. 3). At baseline, 41.3% of the patients were on (D)OAC, 31.7% on single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) and 14.4% on (D)OAC + antiplatelet therapy (APT). Notably, 44.2% of the patients were not on any anticoagulation at baseline.

At discharge, 58.7% of patients were prescribed dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT), 20.2% received (D)OAC, 12.5% received SAPT, while 1.9% received no antithrombotic therapy. By 3 months, the proportion of patients on DAPT and (D)OAC declined to 47.0% and 14.0%, respectively, while 31.0% were on SAPT. At 12 months, the majority of patients (76.9%) were managed on SAPT alone, with 9.9% remaining on (D)OAC and 5.5% on DAPT. A small proportion of patients were on no antithrombotic therapy at 12 months (6.6%).

Among the 27 patients who underwent concomitant AF ablation and LAAO implantation, antithrombotic prescription patterns were compared with those who had LAAO alone (n = 77). Overall post-procedure prescriptions did not differ significantly between groups (Table 3). However, at 12 months, significantly fewer patients in the concomitant ablation group remained on SAPT compared with the LAAO-only group (55% vs. 83.1%, p = 0.012).

Discussion

This is the first observational study to describe the real-world experience of LAAO with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device in the Southeast Asia population. Given the multi-ethnic composition and diverse healthcare systems within Southeast Asia’s population of approximately 700 million population (representing 8.5% of the global population), this study provides critical insights into the performance of LAAO in a region often underrepresented in global trials.

The recent American College of Cardiology (ACC) guidelines have elevated the recommendation for LAAO to Class IIa for AF patients with contraindications to long term oral anticoagulation (OAC) [6]. However, the specific drivers for these contraindications vary regionally. In the German LAARGE registry, the main indications were a history of bleeding (79.3%) and patient preference (25.1%) [1]. In contrast, in our population, contraindication to (D)OAC due to renal failure (41.3%) was a predominant indication. In SEA, healthcare access remains a challenge for patients in remote or suburban areas with limited infrastructure. Nevertheless, only 18.3% of our cohort underwent LAAO implantation due to personal preference or logistic barriers of DOAC.

Our study observed a high implantation success rate with the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device (98.1%). Furthermore, the majority of implants (77.9%) were successful on the first attempt. Device resizing was required in only 6.7% of procedures, despite 48.1% of LAA anatomies presenting with chicken wing morphology, which may pose challenges during LAAO implantation. The imaging guidance used to guide implantation (TOE or ICE) was not associated with procedural success (p = 0.956). We hypothesize that the routine use of pre-procedural imaging (either TOE or CT) for detailed assessment of LAA morphology, landing zone diameter, and interatrial septum contributed to the high procedural success rate. TOE or CT was performed in almost all patients (99.0%) to these favourable procedural outcomes, although this was not a randomized comparison. Such imaging is particularly valuable in anticipating technical challenges in patients with complex LAA anatomy, such as chicken wing morphology, extra-lobes or shallow depth.

The most common LAA morphology in our cohort was chicken wing, followed by windsock. This is notable, as chicken wing morphology has been reported to carry the lowest risk of stroke [6]. While our study was not powered for a formal sub-analysis, this high prevalence of chicken wing morphology may have contributed to the low observed stroke rate in our population. We acknowledge that device selection bias likely contributed to this prevalence; LAA with chicken wing morphology often presents a shallow implantation depth, making the other LAAO devices less suitable or more challenging to deploy while achieve an optimal seal. This distinction is supported by recent evidence which demonstrated that significant device overcompression (> 30%) is required to effectively minimize peridevice leaks during LAAO. Achieving such high compression can be mechanically challenging in shallow anatomies compared to the dual-seal mechanism of the Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device [7].

Our study demonstrated that the 1-year risk of recurrent stroke or TIA was low at 1.9%. This finding appears consistent with results from established Western registries and randomized controlled trials (RCT), such as PREVAIL and the global Amulet Observational Study using the Amulet™ or Watchman device (Supplementary Table 2). Although antithrombotic practices after LAAO vary, most registries and major RCTs report annual ischemic stroke rates below 2% and major bleeding rates below 4%. (Supplementary Table 2).

Concomitant AF ablation was performed in 26.0% of our patients, most with radiofrequency ablation (85.2%) and a minority with cryoballoon ablation (14.8%). Pulsed-field ablation was not available during the study period. Previous reports have suggested thermal energy from AF ablation may cause tissue oedema and friability near the LAA ridge, potentially requiring smaller device sizes, thus compromising the stability of LAAO and resulting in a higher risk of pericardial effusion [8]. In our study, concomitant AF ablation was not associated with changes in device size during the implantation (p = 0.415) or with higher procedural complication rates (p = 0.26). Post procedure antithrombotic practice was also similar to the patients undergoing LAAO only. Although fewer patients in the concomitant group remained on SAPT at 12 months compared with the LAAO-only group (55% vs. 83.1%, p = 0.012), this finding must be interpreted with caution given the small sample size. Nevertheless, these preliminary observations appear consistent with the OPTION trial, which showed that concomitant AF ablation and LAAO is feasible, safe and associated with a low risk of recurrent stroke and bleeding events [9].

Twelve (11.5%) patients in our study underwent LAAO due to ischemic stroke despite adequate DOAC therapy. Among these patients, two were not on any antithrombotic therapy, seven were on SAPT, 2 were on double antiplatelet therapy and only 1 patient was on DOAC 1-year after LAAO implantation. No recurrent strokes were observed in this subgroup during the 1-year follow-up period. Similarly, Maarse et al. reported a significant reduction in stroke recurrence among patients who received LAAO after an ischemic stroke on anticoagulation (2.8% vs. 8.9% per patient-year compared with standard care) [10]. In that study, 67% of patients discontinued OAC after LAAO. Our findings provide further supporting data for the potential role of Amplatzer™ Amulet™ device in reducing stroke risk among patients with recurrent ischemic events despite adequate anticoagulation.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant explicit consideration, reflecting the challenges of real-world multicentre research. First, the observational, non-randomized design with “all-comer” inclusion introduces inherent selection bias. As ablation strategy, imaging protocol, and antithrombotic regimens were left to operator discretion, significant variability exists. Consequently, our findings preclude causal inference regarding the efficacy or safety of this strategy compared to alternatives such as continued OAC or other LAAO devices.

Second, the study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic (January 2020 to May 2023), which impacted recruitment and severely limited in-person follow-up. The decline in imaging surveillance to 27.5% at 12 months was largely driven by institutional restrictions on non-urgent TOE and patient reluctance to attend hospital visits due to infection risks. Crucially, the reliance on clinical assessment rather than imaging surveillance likely results in the under-detection of asymptomatic DRT and late peri-device leaks. Furthermore, this logistical challenge contributed to heterogeneity in antithrombotic prescriptions, as some patients may have remained on more intensive therapy due to delayed imaging confirmation of device sealing. This variation represents a potential confounding factor for stroke and bleeding outcomes that could not be statistically adjusted for.

Third, the sample size of 104 patients constitutes a regional observational cohort; consequently, the study is statistically underpowered to detect significant differences in low-frequency adverse events (e.g., stroke, major bleeding) between subgroups. Therefore, all comparative analyses should be interpreted as exploratory. Additionally, the inclusion of patients undergoing concomitant AF ablation introduces heterogeneity, and no adjusted analyses were performed to isolate the effect of ablation from that of LAAO.

Fourth, the observed 1-year all-cause mortality rate of 10.6%—driven by a 7.7% non-cardiac death rate—may have been influenced by COVID-19-related complications. Causes of death were determined by site investigators based on clinical records; however, distinguishing cardiac from non-cardiac causes in patients with severe concurrent infections presents diagnostic challenges, and potential misclassification cannot be entirely excluded.

Finally, while this study provides a pioneering benchmark for the region, the voluntary participation of four high-volume cardiac centres may not fully represent practice patterns across all Southeast Asia healthcare settings.

Conclusions

The SEA LAAO Amplatzer™ Amulet™ observational study observed a high implantation success rate (98.1%) and favourable 12-month outcomes, with a low rate of recurrent stroke (1.9%) while the majority of patients were maintained on single antiplatelet therapy. However, given the observational design and inherent limitations, these findings should be considered preliminary. Larger studies with longer duration follow-up should be conducted to further evaluate the efficacy and safety of LAAO in this region.

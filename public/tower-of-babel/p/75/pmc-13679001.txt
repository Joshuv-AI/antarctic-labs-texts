Genome-wide identification and comparative analysis of endogenous retroviruses across five breed-representative pig assemblies
Authors: Xiaotong Zhao, Yongping Hu, Zhidong Zhang, Xun Xiang, Hua Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPorcine xenotransplantation offers a promising solution to human organ shortages, yet cross-species transmission of endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) remains a major safety barrier. Current risk assessments predominantly rely on a single reference genome, creating a distinct blind spot regarding ERV diversity and structural variation across diverse genetic backgrounds. We profiled ERV loci across five chromosome-level pig genome assemblies (Bama miniature pig, Duroc, Meishan, Ningxiang, and USMARC), characterizing structural completeness, phylogenetic relationships, relative insertion ages, and host-gene/QTL genomic context. ERVs were identified with the LTRharvest/LTRdigest pipeline, annotated by Pfam domain screening, and classified as complete, near-complete, or partial.Phylogenetic analysis used maximum-likelihood inference, insertion age was estimated from paired long terminal repeat (LTR) divergence, and ERV-proximal genes within 10 kb were analyzed for Gene Ontology (GO)/Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment and tissue expression.ResultsAcross the five chromosome-level assemblies, the paired-LTR-anchored catalog comprised 23,767 ERV candidates in the main autosome/chrX analysis set, including 75 structurally complete, 322 near-complete, and 23,370 partial loci. An additional 86 chrY records were detected in assemblies with chrY and were all classified as partial. Complete-ERV insertion-age profiles showed assembly-level variation, although Kruskal-Wallis tests did not detect a significant overall difference among assemblies. The five-assembly shared ERV-proximal host-gene set contained 278 genes at the 10 kb window; this set decreased to 229 genes at 5 kb and increased to 358 and 618 genes at 20 and 50 kb, respectively.Chromosome-stratified randomization showed that the shared 10 kb gene set overlapped pig QTL intervals more often than expected by chance (56.47% observed versus 43.23% random mean; empirical P < 0.0001).ConclusionsAcross five breed-representative pig assemblies, we identified 23,767 paired-LTR ERV candidates, of which only 75 (0.32%) met the structural-completeness criteria, confirming that the detected porcine ERV catalog is dominated by degraded elements. Complete loci showed assembly-level variation in retroviral affinities and relative insertion-age profiles, while the five-assembly shared 10 kb ERV-proximal gene set showed non-random QTL colocalization (56.47% observed versus 43.23% random mean; empirical P < 0.0001). Benchmarking against published porcine endogenous retrovirus (PERV) and retroviral insertion polymorphism (RIP) resources linked 9 complete loci to reconstructed Sus scrofa ERV6A/B full-length ERVs (SscERV6A/B Fl-ERVs) and left 66 complete loci outside that focused set. This catalog provides concrete ERV loci and host-gene contexts for donor-line evaluation and future population-scale, T2T-informed studies of porcine ERV diversity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-13211-x.

Introduction

Endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) are genomic remnants of ancient exogenous retroviruses that integrated into host germline cells and were subsequently inherited in a Mendelian manner, occupying approximately 8% to 13% of mammalian genomes [1, 2]. Although the canonical 5′ LTR–gag/pro/pol/env–3′ LTR structure has been largely disrupted by mutation and recombinational deletion, ERV-derived sequences remain under active host epigenetic silencing via the TRIM28–SETDB1 axis and DNA methylation [1]. Residual LTRs and internal ERV sequences have also been co-opted as cis-regulatory elements—enhancers, promoters, splice sites and polyadenylation signals—contributing to host transcription and physiology [3, 4].

Porcine xenotransplantation offers a promising route to address human organ shortages, supported by recent physiological and clinical progress with genetically modified pig organs [5], yet the risk of cross-species ERV transmission remains a major safety barrier. While gene-editing technologies are being developed to target these viral elements, existing genome-wide assessments predominantly rely on a single reference genome, leaving cross-assembly ERV diversity and structural variation incompletely described. Accordingly, porcine endogenous retrovirus (PERV) studies have mainly developed around biomedical safety, including CRISPR/Cas9-mediated PERV inactivation [6], characterization of PERV-A/B/C tropism and PERV-A/C recombination [7, 8], and PERV monitoring in pig-to-human xenotransplantation [9, 10]. Beyond the classical PERV framework, retrotransposon-level surveys in pigs have begun to characterize the broader transposable-element repertoire and individual ERV-derived regulatory elements [11, 12], and population-level work has documented insertion polymorphism of intact PERV proviruses [13]. By contrast, genome-wide ERV surveys have already been reported in ducks, chickens, and ruminants [14–16], whereas an equivalent multi-assembly analysis is still lacking for pigs. A matched multi-assembly description of pig ERV abundance, structural completeness, age and genomic context is therefore still needed.

Strong artificial selection and geographic isolation during domestication have contributed to substantial phenotypic and genomic divergence among pig breeds. Duroc is a major lean-meat sire breed [17], the United States Meat Animal Research Center (USMARC) represents a composite outbred line widely used as a research and reference resource [17], Meishan is known for hyper-prolific reproductive performance [18, 19], Ningxiang pigs are a Chinese indigenous breed valued for fat deposition and meat quality [20], and Bama miniature pigs are widely used in biomedical and xenotransplantation research [21]. Long-read and pangenome analyses have revealed extensive structural variation among pig lineages, with mobile-element-derived sequences contributing substantially to non-reference variation among breeds [22]. Because ERVs are influenced by host genome evolution and may contribute to local genomic variation, comparison across breed-representative reference assemblies provides an opportunity to describe shared and assembly-specific ERV patterns in pigs.

Here, we conducted a genome-wide comparative analysis of paired-LTR ERV candidates across five chromosome-level pig assemblies representing Bama miniature pig, Duroc, Meishan, Ningxiang, and USMARC. The analysis provides a harmonized multi-assembly catalog of ERV candidate loci, structural classes, relative insertion-age estimates, phylogenetic affinities, and ERV-proximal host-gene context. ERV candidates were identified using the LTRharvest/LTRdigest pipeline [23, 24], annotated by Pfam-based domain screening [25], and classified according to structural features. Complete ERVs were further examined using MAFFT alignment, IQ-TREE 2 maximum-likelihood phylogenetic inference [26, 27], and paired-LTR divergence [28, 29]. We also analyzed ERV-proximal host genes using GO/KEGG enrichment, Animal QTLdb colocalization, and tissue expression profiling based on GSE93855 [30].

Methods

Genome sources and annotation versions

Five pig reference genomes were included: Bama miniature pig (GCA_007644095.1, ASM764409v1), Duroc (GCA_015776825.1, Ninghe_Sus_1), Meishan (GCA_017957985.1, ASM1795798v1), Ningxiang (GCA_020567905.1, ASM2056790v1), and USMARC (GCA_002844635.1, USMARCv1.0). These assemblies were selected because they are chromosome-level, breed-representative pig genomes with corresponding Ensembl gene annotations, enabling consistent downstream comparisons of ERV-proximal genes, enrichment profiles, tissue expression, and QTL colocalization. Genome assemblies and their corresponding annotation files were downloaded from the Ensembl database [31] and archived for reproducible analysis. FASTA files were used for genome-wide ERV detection, whereas GFF3/GTF annotation files were used for host gene coordinate extraction, nearby-gene identification, and subsequent cross-assembly comparisons. To ensure comparability among assemblies with different sex-chromosome compositions, the primary comparative analyses were performed using autosomes and chrX only. ERV loci detected on chrY were excluded from the main cross-assembly statistics because chrY was available only in a subset of the examined assemblies. Supporting tables and the supplementary figure are provided as Additional file 1, Additional file 2, Additional file 3, Additional file 4, Additional file 5, Additional file 6, and Additional file 7.

Assembly-quality context was obtained from the official NCBI assembly reports for the five GenBank accessions. Gap fraction, contig N50, scaffold N50, and unplaced-scaffold fraction were summarized alongside ERV counts and density estimates. For a focused assembly-completeness sensitivity check, two public T2T/high-contiguity assemblies, Wuzhishan (GCA_965154145.1) and Rongchang (T2T_RCpig1.0/GWHFPUM00000000.1), were processed with the same paired-LTR detection and structural annotation workflow used for the five primary assemblies.

Identification and structural annotation of endogenous retroviruses

Endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) were identified using a GenomeTools-based workflow [32].

Briefly, gt suffixerator was first used to build genome indexes for the five reference assemblies. Candidate loci with paired long terminal repeat (LTR) boundaries were then detected with LTRharvest [23], followed by internal structural annotation with LTRdigest [24]. Core search filters included an LTR length of 100 to 2,000 bp, a minimum pairwise LTR similarity of 80%, and target site duplication lengths of 4 to 6 bp. The 80% paired-LTR similarity threshold was used as an inclusive setting for candidate recovery while retaining sufficient LTR similarity for boundary assignment. A lower threshold would be expected to recover additional older and more divergent candidates, whereas a higher threshold would preferentially retain younger, low-divergence elements; insertion-age patterns were therefore interpreted as relative profiles under this detection setting. Candidate loci were further reviewed using 5′ LTR, 3′ LTR, PBS, PPT, and TSD signals.

To detect conserved retroviral protein domains, an ERV domain library was compiled from the Pfam database [25]. The search focused on gag, protease, reverse transcriptase, ribonuclease H, integrase, env, and dUTPase modules. Structural annotation recorded the presence of domains such as RVT_1, RVP, RNase_H, rve, Integrase_Zn, Gag_MA, Gag_p24, and Gag_p30, and integrated these signals with PBS, PPT, and TSD evidence for locus classification. According to the retention of paired LTRs, PBS, PPT, TSD, and internal domains, loci were classified as complete ERVs, near-complete ERVs, or partially retained ERVs. Complete ERVs were defined as candidates retaining both 5′ and 3′ LTRs together with recognizable PBS, PPT, and TSD signals, and at least three internal retroviral domains.

Near-complete ERVs were defined as candidates retaining paired LTRs and TSDs, together with at least two internal retroviral domains, but lacking either PBS or PPT; candidates with both PBS and PPT retained but only two internal domains were also classified as near-complete. All remaining paired-LTR candidates were classified as partial ERVs, including those with only one detectable internal domain or those lacking both PBS and PPT despite retaining multiple internal domains. In this study, the term complete ERV refers to structural completeness under these annotation criteria. The Pfam entry list used for this analysis is provided in Additional file 4. Because this workflow primarily identifies loci with paired LTR-like boundaries, the resulting dataset was analyzed as a catalog of paired-LTR ERV candidates.

For comparison with published PERV resources, structurally complete ERV sequences were searched against NCBI PERV-A, PERV-B, and PERV-C reference sequences AF038600.1, AF038601.1, and AJ133817.1 using online NCBI BLASTN. SscERV6A/B Fl-ERV coordinates from Du et al. Supplementary Table S2 [13] were also reconstructed from the five corresponding public assemblies and compared with the complete-ERV FASTA set by BLASTN; sequence-level correspondence required identity > = 80%, alignment length > = 1000 bp, query and subject coverage > = 50%, and E-value < = 1e-10.

Insertion-age analysis of complete ERVs

Relative insertion ages of complete ERVs were estimated from divergence between paired LTRs. For each complete ERV sequence, terminal windows were extracted from both the 5′ and 3′ ends, and candidate paired LTR regions were recovered by local alignment. Sequence identity and Kimura 2-parameter distance [33] were calculated from ungapped aligned positions. Insertion time was then estimated using the standard relationship T = K / 2r, where K represents paired-LTR divergence and r was set to 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ substitutions/site/year, corresponding to 3.6 × 10⁻⁹ substitutions/site/generation under a 3-year generation interval [34]. Divergence between the paired LTRs [28, 29] was used to approximate insertion age on the basis that the two LTRs are nearly identical at the time of integration. Age estimates were calculated for all 75 complete ERVs. High- or medium-confidence paired terminal alignments (n = 62) were used for the main descriptive profiles, and the full 75-estimate set was retained as a broader sensitivity set for Kruskal-Wallis testing. The resulting age estimates were compared among the five pig reference genomes using cumulative distribution profiles. Because substitution-rate assumptions and post-integration sequence homogenization can affect absolute dating, these estimates were used as relative insertion-age measures.

Phylogenetic classification of complete ERVs

For phylogenetic classification, complete ERV sequences that passed structural filtering were analyzed together with an external comparison panel. The reference set comprised 24 retroviral sequences representing major retroviral groups, including Alpharetrovirus, Betaretrovirus, Deltaretrovirus, Epsilonretrovirus, Gammaretrovirus, Lentivirus, and Spumaretrovirus. The names, GenBank accession numbers, and sequence lengths of all comparison sequences are listed in Table 1. All input sequences were aligned with MAFFT v7 [26]. A maximum-likelihood phylogeny was then reconstructed using IQ-TREE v2 [27].

The best-fit substitution model was selected by ModelFinder [35], and branch support was assessed with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates [36] and 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates. Complete ERVs were assigned to putative retroviral affinities according to their clustering patterns relative to external references, and assembly-level differences in phylogenetic composition were examined in the Results section.

Identification of ERV-proximal genes and GO/KEGG enrichment analysis

To evaluate the genomic relationship between ERVs and host genes, all annotated gene coordinates were extracted from the reference annotations of each breed and compared with ERV loci. Genes were retained as ERV-proximal genes when their genomic intervals overlapped ERV coordinates or fell within a predefined flanking window around each ERV locus. The primary flanking distance was set to 10 kb upstream or downstream of each ERV, and sensitivity analyses were performed at 5, 10, 20, and 50 kb using Ensembl release 115 GFF3 annotations [31]. After standardizing gene names across genomes, assembly-level gene sets, cross-assembly shared gene sets, and assembly-specific unique gene sets were generated for downstream comparison and enrichment analysis.

Functional enrichment of ERV-proximal gene sets was performed with g: Profiler [37] using Sus scrofa as the target organism and GO: BP, GO: CC, GO: MF, and KEGG as data sources. Terms with g: SCS-corrected P < = 0.05 were retained as significant. Genomic context was classified as exonic, intronic/gene-body, promoter-proximal, or intergenic using the corresponding GFF3 annotations, with promoter-proximal regions defined as 2 kb upstream of annotated genes.

Tissue expression profiling of ERV-proximal genes

To evaluate the expression features of ERV-proximal genes across tissues, the pig subset of the NCBI GEO dataset GSE93855 [30] was analyzed. This dataset was generated in a transcriptomic study of high-altitude adaptation and includes pig samples from six tissues: heart, kidney, liver, lung, skeletal muscle, and spleen. Each tissue contains both high-altitude and low-altitude samples. The processed expression matrix was curated and gene symbols were harmonized before candidate matching. Candidate ERV-proximal genes were matched to the processed expression matrix, and genes retained for comparison were row-scaled to highlight relative expression patterns across tissues.

QTL colocalization analysis

To assess the spatial relationship between ERV-proximal genes and economically relevant genomic regions in pigs, quantitative trait locus (QTL) colocalization analysis was performed. Pig QTL data were downloaded from Animal QTLdb [38]. Raw coordinates were standardized, chromosome labels were harmonized, and trait names were merged into broader trait categories. ERV-proximal gene coordinates were then intersected with annotated QTL intervals, and colocalization patterns were compared across the different gene sets. A chromosome-stratified randomization test was then used to evaluate whether the five-assembly shared gene set overlapped QTL intervals more often than expected from matched random gene sets.

Results

Global ERV features

A total of 23,767 ERV candidate loci were retained for the main comparison after excluding chrY-linked loci from assemblies in which chrY was available (Additional file 1: Table S1A).

ERV counts were comparable among assemblies, with 5,032 loci in Meishan, 4,959 in Ningxiang, 4,720 in Bama miniature pig, 4,598 in Duroc, and 4,458 in USMARC (Fig. 1A). The length distribution was similar across assemblies, with most ERV candidates concentrated around 9 kb (Fig. 1B). Locus lengths ranged from 1,146 to 15,959 bp, with a median of 9,377 bp and a mean of 9,219 bp. Most loci fell within the 4,000 to 8,000 bp, 8,000 to 12,000 bp, and 12,000 to 16,000 bp size intervals, while shorter fragments were less common.

Structural annotation showed that complete ERVs were rare. Of the 23,767 loci, 75 were classified as complete (0.32%), 322 as near-complete (1.35%), and 23,370 as partial (98.33%). Complete ERV counts ranged from 12 to 20 per assembly, representing 0.26%-0.40% of loci within each assembly. Paired LTR boundaries were detected more frequently than PBS, PPT, or internal retroviral protein domains. Among internal domains, RVT_1 was the most frequently detected, whereas gag-, RNase H-, and integrase-related signals were less common. Of the 75 complete ERVs, 9 matched Du-coordinate-reconstructed SscERV6A/B Fl-ERV sequences, including two linked to reported RIP primer IDs D9 and D21; the remaining 66 were not represented in that SscERV6A/B-focused set (Additional file 5: Table S5).

Applying the same workflow to the Wuzhishan T2T assembly (GCA_965154145.1) and the Rongchang T2T/high-contiguity assembly returned 4,918 and 4,839 ERV-domain-positive candidates, respectively, including 18 complete ERVs in each assembly. These values fell within the five-assembly autosome/chrX ranges of 4,458-5,032 ERV-domain-positive candidates and 12–20 complete ERVs (Additional file 6: Table S6).

Insertion-age composition of complete ERVs

Among the 75 complete ERVs (Additional file 1: Table S1B), 62 yielded high- or medium-confidence estimates and were used for the main descriptive profiles. Estimated insertion ages ranged from 0 to 21.58 million years (Myr), with an overall median of 2.98 Myr.

Median estimates differed among assemblies: Ningxiang showed the lowest value (0.81 Myr), followed by Duroc (1.96 Myr) and Meishan (2.98 Myr), whereas Bama miniature pig and USMARC showed higher medians of 5.85 and 7.85 Myr, respectively. Ningxiang and Duroc contained a higher proportion of low-divergence complete ERVs, whereas Bama miniature pig and USMARC included a higher proportion of older estimated insertions (Fig. 1C). Kruskal-Wallis tests did not detect a significant overall difference among assemblies in either the full 75-estimate set (H = 4.682, df = 4, P = 0.322) or the high/medium-confidence subset (n = 62; H = 8.452, df = 4, P = 0.076).

Chromosomal distribution and integration-site profiles of ERVs

ERV candidates were unevenly distributed across chromosomes in all five assemblies (Fig. 2). Raw counts were highest on chr1 in Bama miniature pig, Duroc, Meishan, and Ningxiang and on chr13 in USMARC, with chrX, chr2, and chr15 also showing elevated counts in more than one assembly. Length-normalized all-catalog densities across nuclear chromosomes ranged from 1846.65 to 2049.82 loci/Gb, with Ningxiang and Meishan showing similar values (2049.82 and 2043.72 loci/Gb, respectively). Complete-ERV density was highest in Ningxiang (8.27 loci/Gb; Additional file 3: Tables S3F-S3H). A supplementary heatmap presents the corresponding raw chromosome counts and loci/Mb densities side by side (Additional file 7: Figure S1).

The chromosomal distribution of gag- and pol-domain-positive candidates broadly mirrored the overall ERV distribution (Additional file 8: Figure S2; Additional file 3: Tables S3C and S3D), with pol-domain-positive candidates consistently outnumbering gag-domain-positive candidates across all five assemblies.

Phylogenetic classification of ERVs

Complete pig ERVs clustered with several retroviral reference groups (Fig. 3; Additional file 9: Figure S3), including Betaretrovirus-, Gammaretrovirus-, Spumaretrovirus-, and Epsilonretrovirus-related branches. The trees display UFBoot/SH-aLRT support values for the major nodes. The relative composition of these groups varied among assemblies, with Betaretrovirus- and Spumaretrovirus-related candidates more frequent in Ningxiang, Gammaretrovirus-related candidates more common in Duroc and USMARC, and Bama miniature pig candidates distributed across more than one major group.

Functional features of ERV-proximal genes

Each assembly yielded 1,617-2,015 non-redundant ERV-proximal genes at the primary 10 kb window (Additional file 2: Table S2A). Gene-name harmonization identified 278 genes shared across all five assemblies (Additional file 2: Table S2B). Window-size analysis placed this shared set within a clear gradient: 229 shared genes at 5 kb, 358 at 20 kb, and 618 at 50 kb; at the stricter 5 kb cutoff, 83.31–86.30% of the assembly-level 10 kb gene sets were retained (Additional file 2: Tables S2E and S2F). Genomic-context classification placed most loci in intergenic regions across assemblies (59.40-66.38% of the all-ERV catalog), while exon-overlapping loci accounted for 11.97–18.89% (Additional file 2: Table S2G). For structurally complete ERVs, intergenic loci were also the largest category in each assembly (50.00–75.00%; Additional file 2: Table S2G).

Enrichment profiles across assembly-level ERV-proximal gene sets included recurrent localization/transport, cell-projection, cell-junction, synapse-associated, and cytoplasmic terms (Fig. 4; Additional file 2: Tables S2H and S2I). In the 278 genes shared across all five assemblies, 14 GO/KEGG terms remained significant after g: SCS correction, including 10 GO terms and four KEGG pathways (Additional file 2: Tables S2C and S2D).

Expression of ERV-proximal genes across tissues

Using the pig subset of GSE93855, 214 of the candidate ERV-proximal genes were matched to the processed expression matrix and retained for profiling. Figure 5 displays the 50 matched genes with the strongest tissue-associated row-scaled signals. These genes showed clear tissue-dependent heterogeneity across skeletal muscle, lung, heart, liver, spleen, and kidney, rather than a uniform expression pattern. Most genes reached their highest average expression in spleen, kidney, or lung, whereas comparatively fewer showed peak expression in heart, muscle, or liver. Within each tissue, the expression profiles of low- and high-altitude sample groups were broadly similar, indicating that tissue identity contributed more strongly to the global expression structure than altitude status. These patterns provide expression-level context for the GO/KEGG enrichment and QTL colocalization results.

QTL colocalization of ERV-proximal genes

At the global level, 43.3% of ERV-proximal genes (2,278 of 5,259) overlapped annotated pig QTL intervals (Additional file 3: Table S3A). The five-assembly shared gene set showed the highest overlap rate (157 of 278 genes, 56.47%), above both the assembly-specific gene sets (36.2%-44.9%; Fig. 6B; Additional file 3: Table S3A) and the chromosome-stratified random expectation from 10,000 matched gene sets (mean 43.23%; empirical P < 0.0001; Additional file 3: Table S3E).

Among the overlapping QTL records, meat quality, fatness, growth, and reproduction-related categories were prominent (Fig. 6A and B; Additional file 3: Table S3B). Meat quality accounted for the largest proportion in the five-assembly shared gene set and in the Bama miniature pig, Duroc, Meishan, and USMARC assembly-specific gene sets, whereas Ningxiang showed a slightly higher proportion of growth-related QTL overlaps.

Discussion

Three main findings emerge from the comparative analysis. Most paired-LTR candidates were structurally degraded, with complete ERVs forming only a small minority. Among complete ERVs, phylogenetic affinities and paired-LTR divergence profiles varied among assemblies, although formal age-distribution tests did not support significant breed-level differences. Across all five datasets, ERV-proximal host genes showed recurrent functional enrichment patterns and non-random colocalization with annotated QTL intervals. Together, these patterns extend pig ERV annotation beyond a PERV-centered framework and provide a multi-assembly reference for comparative analysis.

Structurally partial elements accounted for 23,370 of 23,767 candidates, consistent with the extensive post-integration decay documented in mammalian ERV studies [1]. ERV counts were higher on chr1, chr13, and chrX in several assemblies, and length-normalized densities showed that Meishan and Ningxiang remained close after accounting for nuclear chromosome length. The T2T sensitivity check gave a similar catalog-scale range to the five primary assemblies, with 4,839-4,918 ERV-domain-positive candidates and 18 complete ERVs in each of the two assemblies. Chromosome-level variation has also been reported in other mammalian genomes, including horses and ruminants [16]. Within Suidae, earlier work characterized gamma-1-class endogenous retroviruses across multiple species [39], providing an evolutionary backdrop for the gamma-affinity signal observed in the present complete ERV subset. Phylogenetic inference also identified beta-, spuma-, and epsilon-like affinities, consistent with the broader mammalian ERV taxonomic framework [2]. Paired-LTR divergence estimates [28, 29] showed that complete ERVs did not form a uniform age profile, with lower median values in Ningxiang and higher median values in USMARC and Bama miniature pig.

ERV-proximal host genes showed recurrent functional annotations across the five assemblies.

GO and KEGG analyses highlighted localization, transport, cell-projection, junctional, and vesicle-associated categories, and the five-assembly shared gene set was enriched for signaling and synaptic-vesicle terms. Previous work has shown that transposable elements, including ERV-derived sequences, can contribute to gene regulatory architecture [3, 4], and a 192-bp ERV fragment in the first intron of porcine TLR6 has been reported to act as an enhancer associated with elevated TLR6 and TLR1 expression [12]. In this context, the enrichment, expression, and QTL analyses provide a candidate map of ERV-proximal host genes. A substantial proportion of these genes overlapped annotated pig QTL intervals [38], with enrichment above a chromosome-stratified random background and with overlaps concentrated in fat deposition, meat quality, growth, and reproduction-related categories.

Most work on pig retroviral safety has focused on classical gamma-type PERVs, ranging from CRISPR/Cas9 inactivation of full PERV repertoires [6] to monitoring in clinical pig-to-human cardiac xenotransplantation [9, 10, 40]. Recent work on PERV retroviral insertion polymorphism further used 19 pig genomes to predict 39 Fl-ERV-derived RIPs and experimentally confirmed 18 sites by PCR [13]. The present study addresses a broader catalog-level question by profiling paired-LTR ERV candidates across five breed-representative assemblies and placing the complete subset against these PERV/RIP-centered resources. In the Du-coordinate benchmark, 9 of 75 complete ERVs corresponded to reconstructed SscERV6A/B Fl-ERV sequences, including two linked to D9 and D21, whereas 66 were outside that focused set (Additional file 5: Table S5). Together with the 11 complete loci showing strong or partial similarity to canonical PERV-C references, these comparisons support both continuity with published PERV resources and an expanded paired-LTR ERV annotation frame.

The complete-ERV age profiles are best read as relative insertion-age patterns. Ningxiang showed the lowest median estimate and USMARC the highest, whereas the Kruskal-Wallis tests were not significant in the main analysis sets. These profiles may reflect a combination of demographic history, ERV retention, assembly properties, and the paired-LTR similarity threshold used during candidate detection [23]. Population-scale sampling will be needed to separate breed-associated signals from individual and assembly-level effects.

Several extensions follow naturally from this catalog. Multiple individuals per breed, near-complete or telomere-to-telomere assemblies, and synteny-aware insertion-site comparison will refine the distinction between shared ERV loci, insertion polymorphisms, and assembly-level variation. Experimental epigenomic and perturbation assays will then be needed to determine which ERV-proximal candidates have regulatory or phenotypic effects.

Conclusions

This study establishes a five-assembly paired-LTR ERV catalog in pigs, comprising 23,767 autosome/chrX candidates and 86 chrY records in assemblies where chrY was available. The catalog was dominated by structurally partial loci, while 75 complete candidates supported comparative analyses of retroviral affinities, relative insertion-age profiles, and overlap with published PERV/RIP resources. The 278-gene shared ERV-proximal set showed significant non-random colocalization with QTL intervals, and the T2T/high-contiguity sensitivity analysis returned complete-ERV counts within the range observed for the five main assemblies. Together, these results define a practical multi-assembly reference for porcine ERV annotation, donor-line evaluation, and future population-scale, T2T-informed, synteny-aware insertion analyses.

A commentary on ‘Meta-analysis of clinical outcomes of robot-assisted partial nephrectomy and classical open partial nephrectomy’
Authors: Xinru Li, Xiaoang Ye, Hongyu Chen
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The recent research paper ‘Meta-analysis of clinical outcomes of robot-assisted partial nephrectomy and classical open partial nephrectomy,’ published in the International Journal of Surgery, caught our attention1. This study stood out by performing a meta-analysis of the differences between robotic-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) and classic partial nephrectomy (CPN) in patients with localized renal tumors. This meta-analysis found that although survival was similar between RAPN and CPN, RAPN offered benefits in blood loss, hospital stay, complications, margins, function, and recurrence for localized tumors. Qu et al. found that RAPN may be a viable alternative to CPN for localized renal tumors, with perioperative and nephron-sparing benefits. These findings are both timely and significant. While the rigorous efforts and valuable contributions of this study are deeply appreciated, some constructive suggestions are offered for further refinement.

Firstly, the study searched for relevant studies published before August 2022. However, it seems that the search was stopped prematurely, considering the date of publication. Therefore, an appropriate increase in the time span of the literature search may have further increased the persuasiveness of the study findings. In addition, the study included a total of 67 articles that provided a more comprehensive analysis of multiple aspects of surgery, which is worthy of study by all investigators. Unfortunately, we found that the actual number of research papers was relatively small in many aspects. For example, there were only four research papers used to analyze ‘Proportion of Postoperative Local Recurrence’ and four research papers used to analyze ‘Intraoperative Blood Loss.’

Secondly, in the ‘search strategy’ section, the study searched Chinese and English databases, including CNKI, Chinese Science and Technology Periodical Database (VIP), Wanfang Full-text Database, PubMed, and Web of Science database, which is highly commendable. However, a number of other well-known databases are also important, such as Sinomed, Cochrane Library, and Embase, and the reliability of the study results may be further improved if these databases are also searched2–5.

Finally, in the ‘Literature Search’ section, we found that the total number of relevant articles included in the main text does not match the total number of relevant articles included in Figure 1. The total number of relevant articles included in each database does not match the total number of relevant articles included in the main text. If the main text is consistent with the information in the figures, this will certainly further enhance the rigor of the study.

In conclusion, the study by Qu et al. is an important step in our search for promising alternatives to CPN. This study could pave the way for the selection of treatment options for localized renal tumors to benefit more people. Our suggestions are merely to refine an already outstanding piece of research further. We eagerly await more innovative works from the authors in the future.

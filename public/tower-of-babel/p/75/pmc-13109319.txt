The role of a novel normalized dynamically adjusted vegetation index (NDAVI) based on remotely sensed absorption coefficient for estimating crop fAPAR: a case study of rice (Oryza sativa L.)
Authors: Shenghui Fang, Yuanjin Li, Mengyu Ge, Ningge Yuan, Yi Peng, Yan Gong, Yadong Liu, Renshan Zhu, Xianting Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The fraction of Absorbed Photosynthetically Active Radiation (fAPAR) is a crucial indicator of photosynthetic characteristics in crop growth monitoring and yield estimation. Traditional vegetation indices (VIs) struggle to achieve accurate estimations throughout the entire crop growth period due to canopy structural changes, particularly during the senescence phase. This study explores a novel VI, the Normalized Dynamically Adjusted Vegetation Index (NDAVI), for estimating green fAPAR (fAPARgreen) throughout the rice growth cycle, including periods of structural changes when senescent leaves are present in the canopy. A two-year replicated field experiment was designed, incorporating different nitrogen treatments and rice cultivars with varying plant architectures. Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) remote sensing technology was employed to investigate the effectiveness of VIs in estimating rice fAPARgreen throughout the entire growth period. Results indicate that while traditional VIs show some correlation with fAPARgreen across the whole growth period, the overall data distribution is relatively dispersed. The proposed NDAVI indirectly considered the relative changes in canopy chlorophyll concentration, effectively mitigating the impact of canopy senescence on fAPARgreen estimation. In the two-year rice experiment, NDAVI demonstrated a significant linear relationship and goodness of fit with fAPARgreen (R2 ​> ​0.88). Furthermore, the NDAVI-based prediction model exhibited robust performance in inter-annual validation (R2 ​> ​0.85, RMSE <0.06). This study integrates remote sensing absorption coefficients with vegetation indices to establish a novel index that accounts for crop absorption characteristics. The proposed method enables accurate estimation of crop fAPARgreen even in canopies with prominent senescent leaves.

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is a staple food for over half of the global population, making it one of the most critical crops in China and worldwide [1]. Timely and accurate monitoring of rice growth is essential for ensuring food security. The fraction of absorbed photosynthetically active radiation (fAPAR) is a key biophysical parameter defined as the proportion of photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) absorbed by vegetation [2,3]. fAPAR reflects the optical properties of the vegetation canopy and is an important parameter for characterizing the level of vegetation photosynthesis and growth status. It plays an important role in light energy utilization, vegetation primary productivity and yield formation. [[4], [5], [6]]. Therefore, efficient and accurate estimation of rice fAPAR could significantly aid in monitoring rice physiological status, predicting yield, and providing effective guidance for field management.

Traditional methods for measuring fAPAR involve using handheld testers in laboratory and field settings [3,7]. Though conventional measurement techniques yield highly precise results, they demand substantial labor and logistical investment, rendering them impractical for large-scale, synchronized monitoring efforts.

Remote sensing has a wide range of applications such as land cover classification [8,9], change detection [10] and precision agriculture [11]. It has long been utilized as an efficient approach for fAPAR estimation [[12], [13], [14]]. The initial approach to fAPAR inversion predominantly utilized empirical models derived from vegetation indices(VI), with the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) serving as a principal foundation [15]. fAPAR is estimated mainly by developing empirical linear and nonlinear models based on VI [12,16]. Tan et al. have studied the relationship between fAPAR of maize canopy and VIs calculated from hyperspectral measurements. They have achieved accurate estimates of fAPAR by combining GNDVI with NDVI, overcoming the limitation caused by VI saturation at high fAPAR values (e.g., in dense vegetation) [17]. Rahman et al. have explored the relationship between forage NDVI and fAPAR at different solar altitudes based on active sensors (AOS). The study found that fAPAR and NDVI were linearly related at different solar altitudes [18]. However, these methods are simple and easy to implement, but are greatly affected by factors such as atmospheric conditions, soil background and canopy structure. Some scholars have used machine learning methods to combine multiple features to estimate fAPAR. For example, Lv et al. used the SVM model based on UAV images combined with spectral information, height characteristics, canopy temperature and texture characteristics to achieve good results in the single-period inversion of fAPAR results for wheat [19]. Camacho et al. compared the performance of different machine learning methods for inverting fAPAR [20]. However, these methods rarely consider the growth characteristics of different crops, and the model validation results are not ideal. In addition, existing studies have developed physical models such as radiation transfer models and crop growth models that can be used for fAPAR estimation based on the interaction between incident radiation and vegetation canopy [21,22]. It should be noted that the construction of these models requires a large number of crop parameters, which limits their wide application [23]. In addition, due to the differences in crop growth when estimating the entire growth period, the models have large uncertainties. In particular, when there is the influence of senescent leaves, the existing model methods may not be applicable [24]. Since only the green components of the canopy re used for photosynthesis, fAPAR needs to be separated into its photosynthetically and non-photosynthetically active components, in order to improve the estimation of vegetation productivity over time [25]. Some studies have begun to consider excluding the impact of non-green leaf parts on fAPAR [[26], [27], [28]]. However, directly estimating the green part is challenging, and existing methods mainly convert it by measuring leaf area index (LAI) [28]. VI models have better correlations with fAPARgreen when only the green part of fAPAR is considered [12]. While, this method does not account for the difference in absorption between senescent and green leaves. Li et al. used the concept of absorption coefficient to statistically calculate the absorption difference between senescent rice leaves and green leaves, improving the calculation method of fAPARgreen [29]. Moreover, the canopy structure of crops changes significantly throughout the growth cycle. Canopy senescence results in the canopy reflectance being a mixture of the reflectance from senescent and green leaves, making it challenging for VI to establish a unified model for different crops throughout the entire growth cycle [30]. However, for unique vegetation such as crops, whose canopy structure changes significantly with growth periods (e.g., leaf senescence), it is difficult for VI to establish a unified model across different crops and the whole growth period [30,31]. Therefore, it is necessary to study the estimation method of fAPARgreen throughout the growth period based on the absorption mechanism of specific crops. During photosynthesis, the pigments that absorb light energy include chlorophyll (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b) and carotenoids (carotene and lutein). Chlorophyll is the main light energy-absorbing substance, directly affecting the light energy utilization of vegetation photosynthesis [32]. Existing research has been able to derive the absorption coefficients of leaves in different bands from reflectance spectra and use them for pigment inversion research [33], which lays the foundation for conducting research in combination with crop absorption mechanisms. The use of remote sensing technology, especially unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV), to estimate crop growth parameters has been widely implemented for crop monitoring and management in large areas over long time series [11,19,34]. Therefore, it is of certain research value to study the fAPARgreen estimation method for the entire growth period of crops based on UAV remote sensing technology.

Given the limitations of traditional VI models in estimating fAPARgreen during the entire growth period of a specific crop, this paper introduces a novel normalized dynamically adjusted vegetation index using multiple rice varieties as an example. This index is based on UAV multispectral remote sensing combined with the photosynthetic light absorption characteristics and reflectivity of rice at different growth stages. While addressing the challenge of estimation in the presence of senescent leaves, it also improves the stability and accuracy of fAPARgreen estimation throughout the entire rice growth period. The main objectives of this study are as follows:

(1) Evaluate the performance of traditional vegetation index models for fAPARgreen estimation during the entire growth period of rice. (2) Construct a new vegetation index model that based on remotely sensed absorption coefficients for fAPARgreen estimation during the entire growth period of rice.(3) Cross-validate and evaluate the performance of the NDAVI model through experiments conducted in 2022 and 2023, verifying the model's transferability.

Materials and methods

Study area and experimental design

The study was conducted over a two-year period in 2022 and 2023 ​at the Huashan Rice Experimental Base (30°32 ′49 "N, 114°31′ 16″ E), Wuhan City, Hubei Province, China (Fig. 1A). In the 2022 experiment, three rice varieties (Fengliangyou 4 (G1), Changgengyou 582 (G2) and Luoyou 9348 (G3)) were chosen and transplant on June 14th. The research region was divided into three fields of equal area separated by plastic films for control fertilization. The three experimental fields represent three distinct nitrogen fertilizer treatment (1/4N: 36 ​kg/ha, 1N: 144 ​kg/ha, 2N: 288 ​kg/ha). And other fertilizers are applied at the same proportion in each plot. Each fertilizer treatment encompassed three replications (Fig. 1B, V1, V2 and V3) of every rice variety, thereby resulting in a total of 27 plots, each measuring about 14 ​m2 (2.6 ​m ​× ​5.4 ​m). In the 2023 experiment (Fig. 1C), the rice transplanting took place on June 25th, and the experimental design added a 0N (no nitrogen application) fertilizer treatment and a japonica rice variety (Zhonghua 11 (G4)) based on the 2022 experiment, thereby resulting in a total of 48 plots, each measuring about 16 ​m2 (3.2 ​m ​× ​5 ​m). The management practices applied in the field were optimized based on local conditions with same planting density. Field data collection

The situ data collection in this study includes fAPAR, LAI and SPAD data acquisition. The fAPAR of paddy rice canopy was calculated based on PAR measurement, using a LI-190R Quantum Sensor, LI-191R line Quantum Sensor and LI-1500 Light Sensor Logger of LI-COR Corporation at 9:30 a.m. The fAPAR at 9:30 and 14:30 would be appropriate to represent the whole day [45]. The LI-190R Quantum Sensor is used to measure the incident and reflected par above the plant canopy. The LI-191R Line Quantum Sensor has a 1-m-long quartz rod under a diffuser to direct light to a single filtered silicon photodiode, which can quickly and consistently measure PAR under the light field non-uniform, especially under a plant canopy. The LI-1500 Light Sensor Logger is a data collector designed for LI-COR sensors. For each rice plot, three sampling points were selected to measure the above-canopy incident PAR (PARin) and reflection PAR (PARrc), PAR of canopy transmission (PARct) and soil reflection (PARsr) respectively. Specifically, the above-canopy PAR was measured approximately 0.5 ​m above the rice canopy, and the soil PAR was measured approximately 0.1 ​m above the soil, and 3 sets of measurements were repeated. The sensor and measurement schematic is shown in Fig. S2 of appendix A. Then fAPAR is calculated using Eq. (1) [46]. At last, all the fAPAR measurements for each plot were averaged for final data analysis.(1)fAPARtotal=PARin−PARrc−PARct+PARsrPARin

Green LAI and total LAI were obtained by using the LI-3100C leaf area meter of LI-COR Company (LI-COR,Lincoin,NE,USA). We further separated the senescent leaves from the green leaves, and then placed the green leaves one by one into the LI-3100C leaf area meter to obtain the green LAI. Therefore, we can use LAI to calculate the green fAPAR(fAPARgreen) when there are obvious senescent leaves in the canopy [29], the formula is:(2)fAPARgreen=fAPARtotal·GLAIGLAI+YLAI·mWhere GLAI and YLAI mean green LAI and yellow LAI respectively. And m represents the absorption ratio of senescent leaves to green leaves in the PAR region, which was taken as 0.61 in this study [29]. Eq. (2) can be converted to:(3)fAPARgreen=fAPARtotal·11+(1−LAIgreen/LAItotal)·mWhere LAIgreen and LAItotal mean green LAI and total LAI respectively.

SPAD data can represent the relative chlorophyll content of leaves [47]. In this study, SPAD-502 chlorophyll meter (Minolta corporation, Ltd., Osaka, Japan) was used to obtain the average relative chlorophyll concentration of the canopy. The rice canopy was layered at a height of 20 ​cm, and 10 SPAD values were obtained for each layer. Finally, the average of the SPAD values obtained in all layers represented the relative chlorophyll concentration of the rice canopy in the plot. The sampling period was synchronized with other sampling.

Normalized dynamically adjusted vegetation index modeling

Based on the previous research, fAPAR is the total absorption fraction of vegetation in the 400 ​nm–700nm light radiation, which is a complex situation of co-absorption by multiple pigments [3]. The blue band is the main absorption band of most pigments. The absorption characteristics of chlorophyll are the result of the combined action of blue light and red light, rather than relying solely on the red band [48]. Therefore, finding the light energy absorption characteristics of chlorophyll may play an important role in the accurate estimation of fAPAR. Gitelson et al. have explored the absorption coefficient ratios of different bands of PAR through the absorption coefficient model [49]. As chlorophyll increases, the absorption coefficients of the blue and red band increase. In two rice experiments, the absorption coefficient proportion of the blue band in the PAR region shows a trend of first decreasing and then increasing with DAT (Days after transplanting) of rice, while the absorption coefficient proportion of the red band first increases and then decreases (Fig. S3). This indicates that chlorophyll absorption in the PAR region plays a dominant role in the rice life cycle. The chlorophyll content greatly affects the photosynthetic capacity of crops. If the change in the ratio of chlorophyll absorption rate to total pigment absorption rate during the life cycle of rice can be described, it may be beneficial to the retrieval of fAPARgreen. Since most pigments absorb radiation in the blue band, the ratio of chlorophyll's absorption rate in the blue band to the total absorption rate in the blue band can be roughly considered as the ratio of chlorophyll absorption to total pigment absorption. Since the absorption ratio of chlorophyll in the blue band is inconvenient to calculate, considering that the absorption ratio of chlorophyll in the red band to the blue band is close to a fixed value [50], this paper uses the absorption of the red band to approximate the absorption of chlorophyll in the blue band. According to the changing characteristics of the red and blue band absorption coefficient with chlorophyll [49], this study defines the ratio β (Eq. (6)) of the red and blue band absorption coefficients(Eq. (4) and Eq. (5)). The absorption of chlorophyll is relatively independent from the most pigments through the β coefficient. The variation of β with DAT is shown in Fig. 2. It mainly uses the absorption characteristics of chlorophyll to isolate it from the absorption of most pigments, and is used to quantify the changes in the proportion of chlorophyll absorption throughout the growth period. As rice enters the maturity stage, the canopy leaves gradually age and the canopy chlorophyll concentration gradually decrease(Fig. 3A). Absorbance- and reflectance-based approaches to pigment estimation complement each other and can be used synergistically in advanced models [52]. In this paper, we intend to combine the absorption and reflectance characteristics of crops to study the method of accurately estimating the fAPARgreen of crops in the presence of a mixed structure of senescent rice leaves. Since chlorophyll absorbs both red and blue light, this study has analyzed the correlation between fAPARgreen and the available bands of MCA. As shown in Fig. S4, fAPARgreen has a strong correlation with the red and blue bands. Therefore, this paper draws scatter plots of the red and blue bands and their combinations with fAPARgreen before and after maturity, as shown in Fig. 4. The figure illustrates that, before maturity, all combinations exhibit a robust correlation with fAPARgreen. However, as rice approaches maturity, the distributional relationships of the red band, blue band with fAPARgreen undergo significant changes. The fundamental rationale lies in the fact that the reflectance in the red and blue spectral bands exhibits the comprehensive reflectance signature of the entire canopy architecture, thereby incorporating the influence exerted by senescent foliage. The β proposed in this paper separates the contribution of chlorophyll in the crop growth process from the perspective of absorption. Combining β with the red and blue bands, in principle, represents the reflectance changes of the actual green part involved in photosynthesis in the red and blue bands. As shown in Fig. 4D, the correlation between this combination and fAPARgreen is more significant than that of Fig. 4A, B and C, which also shows that this combination can effectively overcome the influence of the reflectance on the senescent structure. The fAPARgreen exhibits a strong correlation with NDVI during the active growing phase of vegetation [53], nevertheless, when the canopy leaf LAI reaches high values, NDVI is susceptible to saturation effects. Yet, this does not diminish the pivotal role of the NDVI index in the retrieval of vegetation parameters. Numerous researchers have proposed refinements based on NDVI, substantially mitigating its inherent limitations [[38], [39], [40],54].Therefore, considering the combined effect of the red and blue bands, this study replaced the red band reflectance in NDVI with the reflectance of the red and blue bands, and combined β as an adjustment factor to construct a new vegetation index: the normalized dynamically adjusted vegetation index (NDAVI), as shown in Equation (7). Model accuracy evaluation

This study utilized three replications of the 2022 and 2023 experimental design for cross-validation. In addition, the prediction model obtained from the 2023 data was adopted to predict the fAPAR of rice in 2022. The results were evaluated using Pearson correlation coefficient(R), coefficient of determination (R2), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean value of the absolute error (MAE). Model accuracy verification

This study set up 3 replications for each variety and each nitrogen gradient in 2022 and 2023 experimental design stages. Two of the repeated groups were randomly selected for model training, and the remaining one was selected for model validation. Therefore, we get three training combinations: V1 ​+ ​V2, V1 ​+ ​V3, V2 ​+ ​V3. The validation results of R2, RMSE and MAE through the linear regression models were shown in Table 3. At the same time, the models obtained by combining the three trainings were used to predict the fAPARgreen of the validation set, and linear regression scatter plots were drawn between predicted fAPARgreen and the measured fAPARgreen, as shown in Fig. 8. Discussion

The relationship between traditional VIs and fAPARgreen in rice

Fig. S1 uncovers that traditional VIs have certain correlations with rice fAPARgreen, especially the normalized VIs, such as NDVI, GNDVI and NDRVI, as well as EVI2 and kNDVI, have the significant correlation with fAPARgreen in two-year experimentation. Many literatures have revealed that NDVI is highly correlated with fAPAR [15,53,55,56]. However, both soil background [15] and canopy senescence [57] are effects that should be considered in the model. Although kNDVI improves the saturation problem of NDVI (Fig. 6C), it does not reflect its unique advantage in leaf senescence with the fAPARgreen analysis during the entire rice growth period (Fig. 6C). Although EVI2 equivalently considered the influence of the blue band, its relationship with fAPARgreen did not converge throughout the entire growth period (Fig. 6B). However, traditional VIs cannot uniformly explain the parameter changes caused by canopy changes after heading and senescing [58,59], such as above ground biomass(AGB) [60], LAI [61], and fAPAR [57]. The main reason is that when there are obviously senescent leaves in the canopy, traditional VIs are the combinations of mixed reflectance of the canopy, which makes it difficult to directly express the changes of green leaf reflectance.

NDAVI model mechanism and performance

NDAVI starting from the normalized vegetation index structure, considering the absorption characteristics of chlorophyll in the red and blue bands for photosynthesis [48], replaced the position of the red band in the original NDVI with the reflectance of the red band and blue band. Combined with the changes in the red and blue band absorption coefficients with chlorophyll [49], the β adjustment factor was defined and added to the VI model to indirectly achieve the purpose of jointly inverting crop parameters by combining vegetation absorbance and reflectance. The decrease in chlorophyll content causes the canopy's absorption coefficient ratio in the red band to decrease, while the blue band absorption ratio slightly increases [49], which results β to decrease (Fig. 2). After the heading stage of rice, β is positively correlated with the change in relative chlorophyll concentration in the rice canopy (Fig. 3B), and can characterize the relative change in chlorophyll concentration in the canopy. Therefore, the addition of β improves the problem of unclear characterization of traditional VIs when there are mixed senescent leaves in the canopy. Fig. 8 exposed the relationships of NDAVI and fAPARgreen in the whole growth period. Since the effect of chlorophyll is equivalently independent through β, NDAVI and fAPARgreen have better correlation in the two-year rice experiment, and the data distribution is more convergent than other VIs. In addition, it can be found that the linear regression model generated by NDAVI can better explain the changes in fAPARgreen throughout the growth period (R2 ​= ​0.884, RMSE ​= ​0.058, MAE ​= ​0.046) from the verification results comparing NDAVI with other models(Fig. 8D), which illuminates that an advanced model that takes into account the photosynthesis mechanism and combines absorbance and reflectance can solve the shortcomings of traditional VI in canopies with complex structural changes to a certain extent.

Cross-validation evaluation of NDAVI model

Three repeated experiments in 2022 and 2023 for cross-validation have been conducted. The NDAVI model has achieved significant prediction results on the validation set. Which proves that NDAVI has good performance in retrieval and prediction of rice fAPARgreen and has better interpretability than traditional VIs during the whole growth period (Fig. 8). In order to evaluate the transferability of the NDAVI model between different years, MCA images in 2022 have been used to calculate the NDAVI. Based on the NDAVI linear regression model in 2022, fAPARgreen has been predicted in 2023 as Fig. 9A–D. Similarly, the results of using the 2023 data to generate linear regression models to predict the fAPARgreen of rice canopy in 2022 are shown in Fig. 9E–H. Compared with other VI models, NDAVI showed absolute advantages in the two-year cross-validation. In Fig. 9D, the R2 of the NDAVI model is 0.855, the highest among the selected VI models. In addition, the RMSE and MAE are 0.06 and 0.048, respectively, which are the best results among the compared models. Similarly, the same results are obtained in Fig. 9H, and the NDAVI model performs best. Although the EVI2 model generated in 2022 exhibited lower model bias in predicting the 2023 results (Fig. 9B), the EVI2 model generated in 2023 exhibited larger model bias in predicting the 2022 fAPARgreen results (Fig. 9F). The sample data from 2022 encompasses the maturation and senescence phases of rice, whereas the 2023 sample data does not include these stages. Consequently, when training the EVI2 model using the 2023 sample data, overfitting occurs. In contrast, the NDAVI model treats the role of chlorophyll as independent, thus remaining unaffected by canopy senescence during the model training process. This characteristic allows the cross-year cross-validation results to better demonstrate the robustness of the NDAVI model in predicting fAPARgreen throughout the entire growth period of rice. In addition, taking the rice image including the maturity period on September 22, 2022 as an example, this study obtained the spatial distribution map of fAPARgreen according to the NDAVI model in Table 3. Limitations and future implications

The NDAVI index proposed based on absorption and reflectance characteristics demonstrated excellent performance in predicting fAPARgreen throughout the entire growth period of multiple rice varieties. Notably, this index reliably estimated fAPARgreen even in the presence of senescent leaves within the rice canopy, indicating its ability to mitigate interference from structural changes caused by canopy senescence to some extent. However, this study has certain limitations.

Although a cross-validation strategy using two years of experimental data was adopted, the method has not yet been further validated at the spatial scale. Subsequent research will conduct fAPARgreen estimation experiments across multiple regions to assess the regional applicability and robustness of this method. Furthermore, the image datasets used in this study are obtained from UAV platforms. We have verified the feasibility of our method through small-scale experiments. However, experiments with large-scale image data of varying resolutions are still lacking. Surface parameters derived at different resolutions can be considerably different even though they are derived using the same algorithms [62]. Therefore, we will collect more data with different resolutions in the future to evaluate the reliability of our method. The NDAVI constructed in this study combines canopy absorption and reflectance characteristics. The β construct cleverly exploits the absorption characteristics of chlorophyll in the red and blue bands, avoiding the calculation of actual radiation absorption by using a ratio. However, due to the current difficulties in directly calculating the absorptivity, the absorption coefficient is derived from remote sensing, which may result in some errors. Furthermore, this study focused exclusively on rice crops. Based on previous studies, we assumed that senescent leaves and green leaves are evenly distributed in the rice canopy [63]. Other crops (such as maize, sorghum, and wheat) also exhibit canopy structures with mixed green and senescent leaves throughout their growth periods. Whether the leaf mixing patterns in these crops resemble those of rice, and whether NDAVI is suitable for accurately estimating fAPARgreen across their entire growth cycles, requires further investigation combined with radiative transfer model. Future research will expand to more crop types to evaluate NDAVI's universality in estimating fAPARgreen across diverse crops, providing more robust scientific support for its practical application.

Conclusions

This study investigates an effective vegetation index for estimating fAPARgreen across multiple rice varieties throughout their growth cycle, utilizing UAV-based multispectral remote sensing imagery. The research yielded the following key findings.1.Traditional vegetation indices face challenges in achieving optimal fAPARgreen estimation across the entire rice growth period, particularly when accounting for structural changes and canopy senescence. Correlation analysis reveals that normalized vegetation indices (e.g., NDVI, kNDVI, and WDRVI) and EVI2 demonstrate superior performance in relation to fAPARgreen (R ​> ​0.88) compared to other VI types.2.Drawing from photosynthetic mechanisms, this study introduces the concept of absorption coefficients to integrate vegetation light absorption characteristics in the red and blue bands into a novel vegetation index. The resulting Normalized Dynamically Adjusted Vegetation Index (NDAVI) exhibits enhanced correlation with fAPARgreen throughout the rice growth cycle, achieving R2 ​> ​0.85 (Fig. 7). This innovation effectively addresses the limitations of traditional VI models in accommodating structural changes and senescent canopy.3.The proposed NDAVI enables accurate fAPARgreen estimation across the entire rice growth period without prior knowledge of rice phenology. It demonstrates robust predictive performance across different years, maintaining R2 ​> ​0.85 (Fig. 9D and H). Cross-validation between years yields a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) below 0.06 and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) less than 0.048.4.In the context of fAPARgreen estimation throughout the rice growth cycle, NDAVI, based on red and blue band absorption coefficients, represents a significant advancement. This index effectively integrates absorbance and reflectance, overcoming the challenges associated with VI model estimation across the entire growth period. Notably, NDAVI allows for reliable fAPARgreen estimation even in the presence of senescent leaves within the rice canopy.

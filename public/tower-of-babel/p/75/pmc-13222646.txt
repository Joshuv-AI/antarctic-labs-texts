Expansion and mobilization of virus-specific stem-like CD8 T cells in chronic viral infection after treatment with a long-acting IL-7, NT-I7
Authors: Judong Lee, Christina Niavi, Eunseon Ahn, Haydn T Kissick, Rajesh M Valanparambil, Tahseen H Nasti, Donald J McGuire, Daniel Hua, Rebecca Obeng, Alexandra A Wolfarth, Danielle Borgonia, Sara Ferrando-Martinez, Donghoon Choi, Byung Ha Lee, Rafi Ahmed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYPD-1+TCF1+ stem-like CD8 T cells are a progenitor population that provides a proliferative burst of effector CD8 T cells upon PD-1 blockade therapy, making them a key therapeutic target in antiviral and anticancer immunotherapy. Here, we show that IL-7 therapy preferentially expands these stem-like CD8 T cells, using NT-I7, a long-acting Fc-fused recombinant human IL-7 (efineptakin alfa). Gene profile analysis showed that a proliferating stem-like cluster induced by NT-I7 exhibited enrichment of genes related to lymphocyte migration. After NT-I7 treatment, proliferation of stem-like cells in the spleen was initiated within the white pulp, followed by egress into the red pulp. NT-I7 treatment led to an increase in stem-like CD8 T cells in circulation and peripheral tissues, contrasting with their resident property in lymphoid organs. These findings suggest NT-I7 as a promising strategy to expand and mobilize stem-like CD8 T cells to enhance antiviral and anticancer immunotherapies.

INTRODUCTION

Prolonged antigen exposure during chronic viral infection or cancer drives CD8 T cell exhaustion, a dysfunctional state mediated by multiple inhibitory mechanisms.1–3 Advances in understanding exhaustion have reshaped immunotherapy, particularly through PD-1 blockade.4–7 During chronic infection and cancer, the antigen-specific CD8 T cell pool is heterogeneous, comprising stem-like (TCF1+Tim-3−), effector (TCF1−Tim-3+CX3CR1+CD101−), and terminally differentiated (TD; TCF1−Tim-3+CX3CR1−CD101+) subsets.8,9 All of these CD8 T cell subsets express PD-1 and Tox. Stem-like CD8 T cells (TCF1+Tim-3−) or progenitors of exhausted CD8 T (TPEX) cells are quiescent, lymphoid-resident progenitors sustained by slow self-renewal.8 Despite lacking effector function, they retain high potential to proliferate and serve as the progenitor for the other subsets.8,10 Upon activation, they differentiate into effector cells (CX3CR1+CD101−), which are characterized by proliferating and migratory properties and cytotoxic function. Eventually, they progress into TD (CX3CR1−CD101+) cells, marked by the expression of multiple inhibitory receptors and the loss of proliferative capacity and function.9 The stem-like subset serves as a resource pool that sustains CD8 T cell responses during chronic viral infection and provides a proliferative burst of effector CD8 T cells upon PD-1 blockade immunotherapy.8 Observed across various chronic infections8,10–14 and malignancies,15–19 the abundance of stem-like cells correlates with positive clinical outcomes of cancer immune therapy.16,18 Therefore, developing strategies to maintain and expand this population is essential for improving immunotherapy effectiveness.

IL-7 is a homeostatic cytokine produced by lymphoid stromal cells and epithelial cells. It signals through a heterodimeric receptor composed of CD127 (IL-7Rα, α chain) and CD132 (common γ chain), which is expressed by various lymphocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, and innate lymphoid cells (ILCs). IL-7 is essential for the development, homeostatic proliferation, and survival of naive and memory T cells.20–22 Furthermore, IL-7 supports the formation of secondary lymphoid organs and tertiary lymphoid structures by promoting the expansion and survival of lymphoid tissue inducer (LTi) cells.23–26 Given its essential role in lymphocyte biology, the IL-7 pathway has gained interest as a therapeutic target.

Preclinical studies demonstrated that IL-7 monotherapy increased CD8 tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs),27–30 mitigated chemoradiation-induced lymphopenia and improved survival of tumor-bearing mice,30 and suppressed regulatory T cells (Tregs)27 and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs).27,29 IL-7 also synergized with anticancer immunotherapies, including PD-1 blockade,30 cancer vaccines,28 adoptive CD8 T cell transfer,31,32 and CAR-T cell therapies.33–35 In chronic lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection models, IL-7 treatment expanded the naive CD8 T cell pool via enhanced thymic output and peripheral homeostasis36 and promoted virus-specific CD8 T cell responses and viral clearance.36,37

In clinical trials, recombinant IL-7 therapies were well tolerated38–43 and restored CD8 T cell counts in lymphopenic patients with HIV39,42,44 and patients undergoing chemotherapy41,45 or bone marrow transplantation.46,47 IL-7 also increased CD8 T cell infiltration into tumors46,47 and enhanced antigen-specific humoral and T cell responses to a cancer vaccine in patients with metastatic cancer.48 Yet, phase 1/2 trials reported limited efficacy in controlling tumor progression.40,41,49 Ongoing clinical trials are evaluating IL-7 in combination therapies such as PD-1 blockade and CAR-T cells.50

In clinical settings, the efficacy of IL-7 on CD8 T cell responses was commonly described as expansion of naive, memory, or total CD8 T cells, which express high levels of CD127.38–41,48,51–55 In contrast, CD8 T cell dysfunction in viral persistence and cancer is associated with loss of CD127 expression and impaired IL-7 signaling.36,37,56–59 This raises the question of how the antigen-specific CD8 T cells respond to IL-7 therapy in chronic conditions and whether these correlate with total CD8 T cell responses. To address this, we characterize LCMV-specific CD8 T cells and their subsets after treatment with NT-I7, a long-acting hybrid Fc-fused recombinant human IL-7 (rhIL-7-hyFc, efineptakin alfa) during persistent LCMV infection.

RESULTS

CD127 expression in total CD8 T cells and virus-specific CD8 T cell subsets during chronic LCMV infection

We assessed CD127 expression in CD8 T cells during chronic viral infection using a lifelong persistent LCMV infection mouse model.60 PD-1 expression is primarily driven by TCR signaling, marking CD8 T cells undergoing TCR stimulation.61 During established chronic LCMV infection (>45 days post-infection [dpi]), PD-1+ CD8 T cells largely represent LCMV-specific population, whereas PD-1− CD8 T cells mainly consist of non-LCMV-specific naive and memory cells. CD127 was highly expressed in most PD-1− CD8 T cells (Figure S1A) but was expressed in small fractions of LCMV-specific total PD-1+ CD8 T cells and LCMV GP33 and GP276 tetramer+ PD-1+ CD8 T cells and at significantly lower levels than in PD-1− CD8 T cells (Figure S1A). Within LCMV-specific CD8 T cells, CD127 expression was restricted to the TCF1+Tim-3− stem-like subset and was low in the TCF1−Tim-3+ effector/TD subset (Figure S1B).

Preferential expansion of the stem-like subset of virus-specific CD8 T cells by NT-I7 therapy in chronically infected mice

We investigated how IL-7 signaling affects CD8 T cell responses during chronic viral infection by using NT-I7 (a long-acting hybrid Fc-fused IL-7). Mice with established chronic infection (>45 dpi) received two doses of NT-I7, and the CD8 T cell response was analyzed 14 days after the initial injection (Figures 1A and S2A). NT-I7 significantly expanded total CD8 T cells across peripheral blood, spleen, lung, and liver during chronic infection (Figure S2B). However, NT-I7 led to only a modest increase in the number of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells in the spleen, including GP33 Tet+, GP276 Tet+, and total PD-1+ populations (Figures 1B and S3A–S3C). The percentages of LCMV-specific cells among total CD8 T cells decreased (Figures 1B and S3A–S3C) due to the greater expansion of total CD8 T cells, primarily driven by PD-1− CD8 T cells (Figure S2B).

We next assessed the effect of NT-I7 on the TCF1+Tim-3− stem-like and TCF1−Tim-3+ effector/TD subsets of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells. On day 14, NT-I7 treatment induced significant expansion of the stem-like subset of GP33 Tet+ (6-fold), GP276 Tet+ (3-fold), and total PD-1+ (4-fold) CD8 T cells, increasing the stem-like subset frequency among the LCMV-specific CD8 T cells. In contrast, NT-I7 treatment led to minimal or non-significant changes in the number of effector/TD subset (Figures 1C and S3D–S3F). CD127+ stem-like subsets of GP33 Tet+, GP276 Tet+, and PD-1+ CD8 T cells showed marked expansion (>10-fold), suggesting a direct role of cell-intrinsic IL-7 receptor signaling in mediating stem-like cell expansion. (Figures 1D and S3G–S3I).

Proliferative kinetics, measured by Ki67, revealed that stem-like cells, normally quiescent during chronic infection,8,9 entered active proliferation peaking during the first week of treatment (Figures S4A–S4D). By day 14, proliferation subsided as cell numbers reached a plateau (Figure S4E). By day 28, stem-like CD8 T cell numbers returned to baseline (Figures S5A and S5B). During chronic infection, TCF1−Tim-3+ cells show substantial Ki67 expression (Figure S4B; untreated). These proliferating cells predominantly represent CX3CR1+CD101− effectors recently generated from the stem-like pool.9 NT-I7 also increased Ki67+TCF1−Tim-3+ cells (Figures S4F and S4G) and modestly raised TCF1−Tim-3+CX3CR1+CD101− effector cells (Figure S6). However, this did not significantly alter the total number of effector/TD CD8 T cells (Figures 1C, S3E, S3F, and S4H). Together, our results indicate that NT-I7 preferentially induces the expansion of a TCF1+ stem-like subset without potently promoting differentiation into TCF1− effector CD8 T cells.

Expansion of cytokine-producing functional stem-like CD8 T cells after NT-I7 treatment

To address the effect of NT-I7 treatment on the function of virus-specific CD8 T cells, we analyzed cytokine production by LCMV-specific CD8 T cells using the intracellular cytokine staining (ICS) assay (Figure S7A). NT-I7 treatment significantly increased the number of cells producing IFN-γ, as well as polyfunctional cells co-producing IFN-γ/TNF-α and IFN-γ/IL-2 (Figures S7B and S7C). Notably, significant expansion of cytokine-producing cells was observed within the stem-like subset rather than in the effector/TD subset (Figure S7D). Thus, NT-I7 treatment during chronic infection expands functional LCMV-specific stem-like CD8 T cells.

Proliferation of CD62L+ and CD62L− virus-specific stem-like CD8 T cells after NT-I7 treatment

CD62L is expressed in naive cells and is rapidly downregulated upon TCR activation.62 In resolved acute infection, CD62L is re-expressed, and these CD62L+ memory cells exhibit greater capacity for long-term recall responses.63–65 A recent study reported that the CD62L+ subset of stem-like CD8 T cells exhibited superior self-renewal and proliferative capacity during chronic viral infection compared to the CD62L− cells. Thus, they were responsible for the proliferative burst following PD-1 blockade.63 We therefore investigated whether NT-I7-induced stem-like cell expansion relied on the rare CD62L+ population or whether the abundant CD62L− subset could contribute to the expansion. Because CD62L expression is tightly regulated by TCR stimulation,62 the vast majority (>90%) of the LCMV-specific CD8 T cells in the spleen, including the stem-like subset, were CD62L− during persistent high viremia (Figures 2A and 2B; untreated).66 On day 8 of NT-I7 treatment, when stem-like CD8 T cells displayed marked Ki67 upregulation, most proliferating stem-like CD8 T cells were CD62L− (>90%) (Figures 2B–2D). The percentages of Ki67+ cells within the CD62L+ and CD62L− subsets of stem-like CD8 T cells were comparable (Figure S8). After the subsequent expansion (day 14), CD62L− stem-like cells remained the dominant population, with no appreciable increase in the frequency of CD62L+ cells (Figure 2E). Our findings indicate that the predominant CD62L− stem-like population retains proliferative potential and serves as the principal contributor to NT-I7-induced stem-like CD8 T cell expansion.

Effect of NT-I7 on the transcriptional program of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells

We examined the transcriptional profile of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells following NT-I7 treatment (Figure 3A). UMAP (uniform manifold approximation and projection) analysis identified four distinct clusters of the CD8 T cells (Figure 3B). Based on gene signature analysis,9,67 clusters 1, 3, and 4 (C1, C3, and C4, respectively) were identified as canonical stem-like, effector, and TD subsets, respectively (Figure 3C). These three clusters were present in both untreated and NT-I7-treated groups (Figure 3B).

C2 was a new population induced by NT-I7 (Figure 3B) and was enriched for a stem-like gene signature (Figure 3C). It expressed hallmark stem-like transcription factors (Tcf7, Lef1, Id3, and Bcl6) and costimulatory genes (Tnfrsf25, Slamf6, Icos, and Cd28). C2 exhibited the highest expression of Il7r and cell cycle genes, identifying it as the stem-like population actively proliferating in response to NT-I7 (Figures 3D and S9A). Compared to the canonical stem-like population (C1), C2 showed partial enrichment for effector signatures (Figure 3C), most likely reflecting elevated cell cycle genes that overlap with the effector signature. Beyond cell cycle genes, C2 showed upregulation of effector-associated genes, including Prdm1, Tbx21, and Havcr2, and modest increases in effector molecule genes, including Gzmb, Prf1, and Fasl but not Gzma or Gzmk (Figures 3D and S9A). Flow cytometry confirmed that stem-like cells maintained Tox and PD-1 and the stem-like markers TCF1, Bcl6, and CD73 after NT-I7 treatment. While a small subset of stem-like cells displayed modest increases in Tim-3, GzmB expression remained low (Figures 3E and S9C).

Notably, C2 expressed lymphocyte migration-related genes, including S1pr1, S1pr4, and Cx3cr1, and integrins (Figures 3D and S9A). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis confirmed that C2 was enriched for terms related to cell migration, chemotaxis, and adhesion compared to the canonical stem-like C1 (Figure S9B). Consistent with these gene expression data, the chemokine receptor CX3CR1 was modestly upregulated in a subset of stem-like cells following NT-I7 treatment (Figures 3E and S9C). While CD69, a tissue-residency marker that mediates tissue retention by antagonizing S1PR1-dependent egress,68,69 is highly expressed in virus-specific CD8 T cells during chronic infection,70 NT-I7 led to a significant downregulation of CD69 (Figures 3E and S9C). Together, these data suggest that NT-I7 induces a migratory phenotype in the proliferating stem-like CD8 T cells.

After the proliferative phase, consistent with proliferation kinetics observed in Figure S4, the proliferating stem-like C2 that emerged at day 8 contracted by day 14, and the canonical stem-like cluster (C1) significantly expanded (Figures S10A and S10B). This expanded C1 population maintained a robust stem-like transcriptional signature and low effector/TD profiles, comparable to the untreated control (Figure S10C). These data suggest that the transient proliferating C2 population gives rise to cells that preserve the canonical stem-like program. Interestingly, the expanded stem-like cluster was enriched for AP-1 transcription factor genes, such as Jun and Fos (Figure S10D), which inversely correlate with CD8 T cell exhaustion.71,72 Jun overexpression has been shown to enhance resistance to exhaustion in CAR-T cells.73

Proliferation of virus-specific PD-1+TCF1+ stem-like cells after NT-I7 treatment is initiated in the white pulp of the spleen, followed by egress to the red pulp of the spleen area

We have shown that LCMV-specific stem-like CD8 T cells are found mostly in lymphoid tissues and that, within the spleen, they predominantly reside within the T cell zones of the white pulp (WP).8 An important question is the anatomical location at which these stem-like CD8 T cells proliferate in response to NT-I7 treatment. To address this question, we performed intravascular (i.v.) staining to distinguish between CD8 T cells residing in the WP (i.v.−) versus the red pulp (RP; i.v.+) of the spleen (Figure 4A). As shown in Figure 4B, there was a significant increase in the number of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells in the WP of the spleen after NT-I7 treatment, and this was mostly due to expansion of the stem-like CD8 T cells (Figure 4C). During chronic infection, splenic stem-like cells localize mainly in the WP (Figures S11 and 4C; untreated). NT-I7 treatment further increased the frequency and absolute number of stem-like cells in the WP (Figure 4C, ~10-fold). NT-I7 also significantly expanded stem-like cells in the RP, which showed very high Ki67+ frequency compared to their WP counterpart (Figures 4C and 4D). This observation suggests two possible scenarios: (1) stem-like cells in the RP proliferate more robustly than those in WP or (2) proliferation is initiated in the WP and proliferating cells subsequently migrate to the RP. If the first scenario were correct, we would expect a greater magnitude of stem-like and Ki67+ stem-like cell expansion in the RP than in the WP. However, our data show much greater expansion of LCMV-specific CD8 T cells in the WP (Figure 4B) and a >100-fold increase in Ki67+ stem-like cells in the WP versus ~40-fold in the RP (Figure 4D), along with more prominent overall expansion of stem-like cells in the WP (Figure 4C). These data are more consistent with the second scenario.

To further investigate this possibility, we examined the expression of CD69, a tissue-retention molecule, on stem-like cells. In untreated chronically infected mice, CD69 was highly expressed on stem-like CD8 T cells, particularly within the WP, suggesting its role in retaining stem-like cells in the WP (Figure 4E). Following NT-I7 treatment, CD69 was significantly downregulated in stem-like CD8 T cells, with CD69− cells accumulating in the RP. Importantly, this CD69 downregulation was restricted to Ki67+ stem-like cells (Figure 4E). These data further support the model that proliferating virus-specific stem-like CD8 T cells egress from the WP to the RP of the spleen following NT-I7 treatment. We observed that even within the WP, nearly all stem-like CD8 T cells were CD62L−. Because stem-like cell proliferation is primarily initiated in this compartment, these data support that the CD62L− subset constitutes the primary source of NT-I7-induced stem-like cell expansion (Figure S11).

NT-I7 mobilizes stem-like CD8 T cells into circulation and peripheral non-lymphoid tissues

During persistent viremia, stem-like and TD cells are tissue resident, while only transitory effectors exhibit migratory properties. Consequently, virus-specific CD8 T cells, particularly lymphoid tissue-resident stem-like cells, are found at low frequencies in the blood (Figures S12A and S12B; untreated).8,70 Given the migratory signatures of proliferating stem-like CD8 T cells, along with their egress from the WP to RP of the spleen, we investigated whether NT-I7 influences LCMV-specific CD8 T cell responses in the periphery. After NT-I7 treatment, the percentage of LCMV-specific Tet+ cells among total CD8 T cells further decreased (Figure S12A), due to more pronounced expansion of non-LCMV-specific CD8 T cells (Figure S2B). However, NT-I7 therapy significantly increased the percentage of the stem-like subset in the blood. Moreover, the proportion of CD127+ cells within this circulating stem-like population was significantly elevated (Figure S12C), implying mobilization of stem-like cells by NT-I7.

We next examined virus-specific CD8 T cells in peripheral tissues. In the lung, TCF1+Tim-3− stem-like CD8 T cells were present at a significantly lower frequency than those in the spleen (Figures 5B and S12E). NT-I7 treatment significantly increased their numbers in the lung (Figures 5B and S12E). As stem-like CD8 T cells were scarcely detectable in the liver at baseline, their expansion following NT-I7 treatment was modest compared to the lung (Figures 5E and S12H). However, marked increases in CD127+ stem-like cells in both lung and liver demonstrate the increase of IL-7-responsive stem-like cells in these tissues (Figures 5C, 5F, S12F, and S12I). Effector/TD populations in the lung and liver also increased, albeit to a lesser extent than the stem-like population. When combined, these changes resulted in a significant increase in total virus-specific CD8 T cell numbers in both liver and lung following NT-I7 treatment (Figures 5A, 5D, S12D, and S12G).

We tested whether this peripheral increase of stem-like cells depends on lymphoid migration by blocking S1P receptor-mediated egress with FTY720 (Figure S13A). FTY720 co-treatment attenuated the NT-I7-induced increase of stem-like CD8 T cells in non-lymphoid tissues without significantly affecting effector/TD cell numbers (Figures S13B and S13C). Together, our data demonstrate that NT-I7 expands stem-like cells in peripheral tissues, at least in part by mobilizing them from lymphoid tissues.

DISCUSSION

Here, we describe how antigen-specific CD8 T cells respond to IL-7 therapy during chronic viral infection. First, we showed that NT-I7 preferentially expanded functional PD-1+TCF1+Tim-3− stem-like virus-specific CD8 T cells, essential progenitors for antiviral and antitumor immunity. Transcriptional analysis revealed that stem-like cells expanded by NT-I7 maintain a robust stem-like program. We also identified a proliferating stem-like population enriched for migratory genes. Consistently, NT-I7 treatment induced the mobilization of stem-like CD8 T cells from lymphoid tissues, leading to their accumulation in the blood and peripheral tissues. Collectively, our data suggest potential mechanisms by which NT-I7 supports antiviral and cancer immunotherapy: (1) amplifying the progenitor pool and (2) mobilizing these cells to the target tissues.

While prior studies reported a significant enhancement in virus-specific CD8 T cell responses and accelerated viral clearance by IL-7 therapy in LCMV clone 13 infection mouse model,36,37 in our study, the response to NT-I7 was largely restricted to the stem-like subset, and we observed modest increases in total virus-specific CD8 T cells and no significant effect on viral clearance in tissues (Figure S14). These discrepancies likely arise from variations in infection severity and treatment timing. Those studies employed a less stringent LCMV clone 13 model in which viral titers naturally begin to decline in a month. IL-7 treatment was initiated during early infection (day 8) or the contraction phase (day 15), suggesting that IL-7 enhanced expansion and/or survival of effector cells.36,37 Similarly, in murine tumor models, IL-7 increased tumor-specific CD8 T cell responses when treatment was initiated within 1–2 weeks after implantation.27,30,31,33,52 In contrast, we used a very stringent lifelong chronic infection model and administered IL-7 only after chronic infection was fully established (>45 dpi). The absence of viral control was not unexpected, as NT-I7 monotherapy led to a limited impact on the expansion of cytotoxic effector CD8 T cells. Moreover, PD-1 blockade is required for viral control in this model, in addition to the expansion of virus-specific CD8 T cells. Even IL-2, which robustly generates effectors, fails to improve viral clearance as monotherapy, though it synergizes with PD-1 blockade to enhance viral control.74,75

Because the therapeutic strength of NT-I7 lies in expanding the stem-like pool rather than promoting effector differentiation, this therapy may be most effective when combined with therapies that robustly drive effector differentiation, like PD-1 blockade. A critical question is the optimal timing of these treatments: should they be administered concurrently or should NT-I7 precede PD-1 blockade to first expand the stem-like CD8 T cell pool? Combination with IL-2 is particularly promising, as IL-2 induces highly functional effector differentiation and CD127 expression on effector CD8 T cells.74,75 Thus, IL-7 therapy could synergize with IL-2 by expanding stem-like CD8 T cells and supporting the effector population.

In this study, NT-I7 potently increased total CD8 T cell counts. Consequently, the relative frequency of virus-specific CD8 T cells decreased, even though their absolute numbers remained stable or increased modestly. This reflects differences in CD127 expression between virus-specific versus non-specific naive and memory CD8 T cells. In clinical trials, IL-7 efficacy is often evaluated via changes in total, naive, or memory CD8 T cell frequencies,38–41,48,51–55 as detecting and quantifying antigen-specific CD8 T cells in human samples is challenging. Our findings suggest that such readouts may not accurately capture the impact of IL-7 therapy on antigen-specific CD8 T cell responses in chronic viral infection or cancer, underscoring the importance of antigen-specific readouts to accurately understand antiviral or antitumor CD8 T cell responses mediated by IL-7 therapy.

Limitations of the study

In this study, IL-7-induced expansion of stem-like CD8 T cells was transient, suggesting that continuous IL-7 signaling may be required to maintain this population. It will be important to determine whether prolonged IL-7 treatment can sustain stem-like cells for a longer period and enhance subsequent effector generation. However, extended NT-I7 treatment was not feasible in our model due to the development of anti-drug antibodies against the human Fc region. Our stringent chronic infection model lacks virus-specific CD4 T cells. While this model allows us to examine the CD8 T cell-intrinsic effects of NT-I7 during established chronic viral infection, it precludes evaluation of NT-I7 effects on virus-specific CD4 T cells and their help in supporting antiviral CD8 T cell responses.

STAR★METHODS

EXPERIMENTAL MODEL AND STUDY PARTICIPANT DETAILS

Mice strain

All animal studies were performed in accordance with the Emory University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines (PROTO201700015). C57BL/6J mice were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory. Mice were infected at 6–8 weeks of age and housed under a 12-h light/dark cycle (7:00–19:00) with controlled temperature (65–74°F) and humidity (30–70%) in a BSL-2 room. Food and water were provided ad libitum. All mice used in this study were female, and the influence of sex was not addressed in this study.

METHOD DETAILS

LCMV infection and NT-I7 treatment

To establish a lifelong persistent chronic infection in a mouse model, LCMV infections with transient CD4 depletion were carried out as previously described.8,9,60,75 Mice received 300 μg of GK1.5 anti-CD4 antibody (i.p.) prior to infection and were then inoculated with 2 × 106 pfu of LCMV clone 13 (i.v.). Chronically infected mice (>45 dpi) were treated with injection buffer or 200 μg NT-I7 (NeoimmuneTech) (s.c.). NT-I7 is a recombinant human IL-7 fused to a hybrid Fc domain composed of IgD CH2 and IgG4 CH2/CH3 regions, designed to extend serum half-life, reduce immunogenicity, prevent FcγR and C1q binding, and retain neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) recycling.28,67,80

Lymphocyte isolation and flow cytometry

Lymphocytes were isolated from the blood, spleen, liver, and lung and stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies and MHC class I tetramer (H2-Db) complexed with LCMV GP33–41 or GP276–286 peptide on ice as previously described.81,82 Flow cytometry data were collected on LSR II (BD), Symphony (BD), or Aurora (Cytek) cytometers, and analyzed using Flowjo (BD).

Intracellular cytokine staining

For the assessment of cytokine production, splenocytes were cultured with pooled LCMV epitope peptides (GP33, GP276, GP70, GP92, NP166, NP205, NP235, and NP396), brefeldin A, and monensin (BD Biosciences) for 5 hours at 37°C. Following staining of surface molecules as described above, the stimulated cells were fixed and permeabilized using Cytofix/Cytoperm kit (BD Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and stained with anti-IFN-γ, TNF-α and IL-2.

Intravascular staining

Mice were injected intravenously with 3 μg of anti-CD8α-BV421 (clone: 53–6.7) in PBS. 3 minutes after injection, the mice were euthanized and tissues were collected and processed as previously described.83

FTY720 treatment

Mice received one injection of 20 μg FTY720 (i.p.) and were given ad libitum access to drinking water (2 μg/ml). Drinking water was replaced every other day.

Single-cell RNA-sequencing

Chronically infected mice (>45 dpi) received a single dose of injection buffer or 200 μg NT-I7. 8 and 14 days after the injection, splenic GP33 Tet+ CD8 T cells were sorted using a FACSAria II cell sorter (BD). Single-cell gene expression libraries were prepared and sequenced at the Emory National Primate Center Genomics Core. Sequencing reads were aligned with CellRanger 4.76 Data were normalized and processed using Seurat 4.1 and 5.3.77 Cells with fewer than 500 or more than 3,000 detected genes, or with >10% mitochondrial gene content, were excluded. Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) was analyzed using top 10 principal components (PCs). Differentially expressed genes between clusters were identified using FindMarkers function of Seurat package. Gene signature analysis was performed using the VISION package 3.0,78 applying gene sets for stem-like, effector, and terminally differentiated (TD) CD8 T cells described by Hudson et al.9,67 Gene ontology enrichment was performed using clusterProfiler.79

QUANTIFICATION AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Prism 9 (GraphPad) was used for statistical analysis. Statistical differences in the frequency of the cells or log-transformed number of cells were analyzed by paired t-tests, unpaired t-tests, or one-way ANOVAs as indicated in the figure legends. Data are represented as mean ± SD and p-value. Dots in the graphs represent individual mice.

Prevalence of dental caries and its association with molar incisor hypomineralization among 7–10-year-old children in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. An epidemiological study
Authors: Khlood Baghlaf, Heba Jafar Sabbagh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Molar incisor hypomineralization (MIH) is a developmental defect in enamel that affects the oral health of children. This study assessed the prevalence of dental caries among 7–10-year-olds and its association with MIH in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. This epidemiological study used stratified random sampling of 12 primary schools in Jeddah city. To recruit children, two calibrated examiners in classrooms performed dental examinations for caries using the World Health Criteria and MIH based on the MIH criteria of the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (EAPD). Among 2010 children, caries prevalence was 1719 (85.5%) and MIH was 359 (17.9%). A total of 1872 (93.1%) children lived with both parents. The mean dmft score for primary dentition was significantly higher in children with MIH (4.920 ± 3.400) compared to those without MIH (4.490 ± 3.315) (P = 0.031). The presence of caries in permanent dentition was significantly associated with MIH (P = 0.028). MIH presence was associated with a statistically significant reduced likelihood of dental caries in primary dentition (AOR = 0.732, 95% CI: 0.547–0.980, P = 0.036) and statistically significant increased likelihood of dental caries in permanent dentition (AOR = 1.432, 95% CI: 1.303–1.574, P < 0.001). MIH was a risk factor for increasing dental caries in primary and permanent dentitions. This study provides new insights specific to a population in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, which has not been extensively studied in the literature. Implications for children diagnosed with MIH to follow a strict preventive program to prevent dental caries in affected teeth.

Introduction

Dental caries is the most common disease in children and is a multifactorial oral condition that remains a major public health issue (Yousaf et al. 2022). Recent studies have highlighted a growing association between dental caries and molar incisor hypomineralization (MIH), a developmental defect affecting the enamel of the first permanent molars (FPMs) and incisors (Mazur et al. 2023). MIH presents as discolored, porous enamel that is more susceptible to decay, leading to an increased risk of dental caries in affected children (Garot et al. 2024).

MIH is characterized by hypomineralized lesions on the FPM and incisors (Jianu et al. 2022). This condition is increasingly recognized in pediatric dentistry due to its prevalence and significant impact on children's oral health (Almuallem and Busuttil-Naudi 2018). MIH typically manifests as discolored enamel, which may appear chalky or opaque, and is often more susceptible to caries, sensitivity, and wear (Yamaguti and Cabral 2019). The etiology of MIH remains multifactorial, with potential contributing factors including environmental influences, such as prenatal and perinatal complications, systemic diseases, and exposure to excessive fluoride during critical periods of tooth development (Alzahrani et al. 2023; Garot et al. 2022; Silva et al. 2016) MIH can result from various factors, including environmental influences, such as exposure to fluoride, nutritional deficiencies, and systemic health conditions during the critical periods of tooth development (Garot et al. 2022). The consequences of MIH include dental aesthetics, pose challenges for oral hygiene, and increases the likelihood of carious lesions (Garot et al. 2022; Bhadila et al. 2023).

In Saudi Arabia, the overall prevalence of dental caries has dropped to 65.5%, with rates of 72.1% in primary teeth and 61.7% in permanent teeth (Orfali et al. 2023). Additionally, a systematic review (Bukhari et al. 2023) included 29 studies to assess the prevalence of MIH in the Middle East, and they found the mean prevalence of MIH was 15%. Previous studies showed that children with MIH often experience greater dental anxiety and require more extensive dental treatment which complicates their oral health management (Jälevik et al. 2022; Menoncin et al. 2019). Understanding the association between MIH and dental caries is essential for developing effective preventive and therapeutic strategies.

Children with MIH may struggle with oral hygiene because the affected enamel is sensitive and fragile (Yamaguti and Cabral 2019). This susceptibility increases the risk of dental caries, necessitating more frequent dental visits and intervention (Garot et al. 2024). Several cross-sectional studies investigated the association between MIH and dental caries in children (Jeremias et al. 2013; Quintero et al. 2022; Villanueva-Gutiérrez et al. 2019). A systematic review encompassing 17 studies found that three of them indicated children with MIH were 2.1 to 4.6 times more likely to experience caries in their permanent teeth compared to those without MIH (Americano et al. 2017). The null hypothesis of this study that there is no association between dental caries and the occurrence of MIH. Furthermore, there is a need for further well-designed studies to provide evidence of this association. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the prevalence of caries and the association between dental caries and MIH children in Jeddah city.

Materials & methods

Study design

A cross-sectional study was carried out involving children aged 7 to 10 years in the city of Jeddah. Ethical approval was obtained from the Research Ethics Committee of King Abdulaziz University (IRB) 189–11–23. and was conducted following the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Government primary schools were subjected to stratified cluster random sampling, resulting in the selection of 12 schools. These chosen schools represented the four main regions of Jeddah. The first step in the sampling process is to divide Jeddah City into its different districts: North, Central, and South. In the second step, one boys' school and one girls' school are randomly chosen from each district. Extra school was chosen from each area has both genders. Finally, in the third step, all students who meet the age requirements in these selected schools are to be documented. This study was conducted in conjunction with another survey of molar–incisor hypomineralisation and its risk factors among children in Jeddah (Baghlaf et al. 2024). Data collection was conducted between November 2023 and February 2024.

Sample size calculation

Using OpenEpi version 3.01. A minimum sample size of 1574 was required assuming MIH were 2.1 to 4.6 times more likely to have caries in the permanent dentition than children without MIH, 85% power, 95% CI, and a 5% precision level. This calculation was performed according to Amircano et al. 2017, based on the hypothesis that there is an association between dental caries and the occurrence of MIH (Americano et al. 2017).

Data collection and school visits

Parents signed the written consents before oral examinations. A letter was sent to each school before the visit. On the day of the visit, two examiners organized the children to perform the clinical examination. Intra-oral examinations were performed by two examiners who trained and calibrated. Children were seated in a standardized armchair with head support, and their teeth were illuminated using a standard light source.

Teeth were visually examined while wet and using a disposable mirror. The experience of caries was evaluated using the decayed, missing, and filled (primary teeth) and decayed, missing, and filled (permanent teeth) indices based on WHO criteria 5th edition (World Health Organization. Oral health surveys n.d.). In this study, the presence or absence of MIH was defined based solely on the occurrence of MIH in permanent molars. We then compared these findings with the dmft and DMFT values. MIH was examined based on the MIH criteria by the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (EAPD) (Lygidakis et al. 2022). Examiners were not blinded to MIH status when recording caries. MIH has been examined in permanent incisors and first permanent molars. Inter-class correlation (ICC) test was used to measure the intra-examiner & inter-examiner reliability. Kappa score was calculated for both and measured 0.88.

Statistical analysis

The data were collected and tabulated. The data were entered and analyzed using version 22.0 of the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). A descriptive analysis was used to report the means and standard deviations, frequencies, and percentages of independent variables. Means and standard deviations were also used for quantitative variables, while qualitative variables were described using frequencies and percentages. A comparison between dental caries and MIH was made using chi-squared tests, Fisher’s exact tests. Regression analysis was conducted, and the outcomes were dental caries and MIH, and the cofactors (age, gender, living with single parents, and only child). The P value of 0.05 was set as statistically significant.

Results

Descriptive characteristics

A total of 12 schools and 2010 children were included in this study. The response rate of the children was 91.36. The schools were distributed among the four areas in Jeddah city: 4 in the North, 4 in the South, and 4 in the Central. The sample included 945 (47%) males and 1065 (53%) females. The mean age of children included in the study was 8.64 years ± 0.99. A total of 1872 (93.1%) children lived with both parents, and only 138(6.9%) children lived with one parent. Dental caries-related factors

Dental caries was statistically significantly higher among females than males in both primary and permanent teeth (p < 0.001). Children who lived with both parents had statistically significantly higher caries in permanent teeth compared to children who didn’t live with both parents (p = 0.02) Children with siblings have significantly higher dental caries in both dentitions than the only child (p-value = 0.012*) (Table 1).

Association between MIH and dental caries

The mean dmft score for primary dentition was significantly higher in children with MIH (4.920 ± 3.400) compared to those without MIH (4.490 ± 3.315) with P = 0.031. However, the presence of caries in primary dentition was not significantly different between children with MIH (P = 0.089). For permanent dentition, the mean DMFT score was slightly higher in children with MIH (1.237 ± 1.425) than those without (1.122 ± 1.539), though this was not statistically significant (P = 0.196). MIH was associated with the presence of caries in permanent dentition, P = 0.028 (Table 3).

A regression analysis was conducted on dental caries (dependent factors) and related factors (age, gender, if the child is living with both parents and if the child is an only child). MIH presence was associated with a statistically significant reduced likelihood of dental caries in primary dentition (AOR = 0.732, 95% CI: 0.547–0.980, P = 0.036) and statistically significant increased likelihood of dental caries in permanent dentition (AOR = 1.432, 95% CI: 1.303–1.574, P < 0.001). Age was significantly associated with increased caries prevalence in permanent dentition (AOR = 1.432, 95% CI: 1.303–1.574, P < 0.001). Dental caries was associated with female gender in both primary dentition (AOR = 0.571, 95% CI: 0.449–0.726) < 0.001*) and permanent dentition (AOR = 0.440, 95% CI: 0.365–0.530) < 0.001*) (Table 4). Discussion

This is a cross-sectional study aimed at assessing the association between dental caries and MIH, and found a higher dmft index among MIH children. The study included 2010 children aged 7 to 10 years old from 12 different governmental primary schools in Jeddah city. The prevalence of caries was 85.5%, and the prevalence of MIH was 17.9%. In this study, children aged 7 to 10 were included because, at this age, their permanent molars have erupted but are likely not yet significantly damaged by MIH.

The prevalence rates and the significant associations we found between MIH and dental caries in both primary and permanent dentition contribute valuable data to the existing body of knowledge. Moreover, our findings highlight the need for targeted preventive measures for children diagnosed with MIH, specifically within the context of the local population. This information can guide pediatric dental practices and inform public health policies aimed at improving oral health outcomes for children in this region.

Our findings showed a mean dmft of 4.84 ± 3.38 in primary and DMFT of 1.14 ± 1.520 in permanent teeth. Comparable to the findings of a recent nationwide study by Orfali et al. (Orfali et al. 2023). They evaluated the prevalence of dental caries in school children aged 6 to 12 and 15 years in Saudi Arabia (Orfali et al. 2023) They reported a mean dmft of 3.93 ± 3.60 and a mean DMFT of 2.42 ± 2.52. The current prevalence of dental caries is significantly lower than that found in the previous nationwide study by Al Agili et al., (2013) (Al Agili 2013) which indicating the effect of preventive public health programs such as Fluoride and sugar taxation (Alsukait et al. 2020). However, this prevalence is higher than the figures from a systematic review and meta-analysis that encompassed studies from around the world (Kimmie-Dhansay and Bhayat 2022). The review included a total of thirty studies, with Eritrea reporting the highest DMFT score of 2.5, while Sudan had the lowest score at 0.49.

In addition, a higher mean of both dmft and DMFT among MIH children than among children without MIH was found. This agrees with the findings of a systematic review by Mazur et al. in 2023 (Mazur et al. 2023). This systematic review was conducted to assess the evidence between MIH and dental caries in children and included 59 articles in the qualitative synthesis (Mazur et al. 2023).The results of this systematic review indicated that MIH is positively correlated with both dmft (effect size of 0.67, 95% CI [0.15, 1.19]) and DMFT (effect size of 0.56, 95% CI [0.41, 0.72]), based on a total of 17,717 subjects.

The findings showed MIH presence was associated with a statistically significant reduced likelihood of dental caries in primary dentition and a statistically significant increased likelihood of dental caries in permanent dentition. Teeth with MIH are characterized by the presence of hypomineralized (under-mineralized) enamel, which can lead to various dental issues, including an increased risk of dental caries (tooth decay) in children (Mazur et al. 2023). This can be attributed to the hypomineralization of enamel, which is a contributing factor to caries. If patients maintain good oral hygiene, they are likely to develop caries in their permanent teeth rather than in their primary teeth. However, children with MIH might receive closer dental supervision and preventive care due to the visibility of their condition, leading to better oral hygiene practices that can mitigate the risk of caries in primary teeth. A recent systematic review assessed the clinical consequences of MIH in children and adolescents (Gevert et al. 2024). The systematic review included 41 studies for qualitative analysis and 38 for quantitative analysis. The most common clinical consequences were dental caries (0.252–95% CI 0.158–0.375; 0.512–95% CI 0.385–0.639), followed by hypersensitivity (0.286–95% CI 0.190–0.407; 0.417–95% CI 0.197–0.674).

On the other hand, the higher prevalence of caries in children with MIH in the primary dentition may be linked to enamel defects in the second primary molars, commonly associated with MIH (Sabbagh et al. 2025). Additionally, it may reflect shared risk factors for MIH and caries, such as maternal health, early childhood diet, and systemic health (Bukhari et al. 2023). One possible explanation is that children with MIH might receive closer dental supervision and preventive care due to the visibility of their condition, leading to better oral hygiene practices that can mitigate the risk of caries in primary teeth. Implications for children diagnosed with MIH are to follow a strict preventive program to prevent dental caries in affected teeth.

This association between MIH and caries highlights the importance of providing preventive strategies for children with MIH. Children with MIH may find it harder to maintain proper oral hygiene due to sensitivity or discomfort, further exacerbating plaque buildup.(Turkmen and Ozukoc 2022) Pain associated with affected teeth may lead to avoidance of dental care, resulting in untreated cavities (Large et al. 2020). Dental public health practitioners need to educate parents about the clinical consequences of MIH and the importance of regular dental visits.

Our findings showed that being a female is associated with having dental caries more than males. This agrees with the study conducted by Orfali et al. (2023) which showed most of the DMFT components were significantly higher in females than males (Orfali et al. 2023). This may have been due to the large sample size and the fact that (52.9%) of the sample were female and the fact that teeth eruption happened earlier among females (Roulias et al. 2022). However, Aqeeli et al. (2021) found that male children had a greater incidence of caries compared to females (Aqeeli et al. 2021).

In this study, we utilized an assessment tool based on the MIH criteria established by the European Academy of Pediatric Dentistry to diagnose MIH defects (Lygidakis et al. 2022). Additionally, a randomized stratified sample with a large sample size was utilized, ensuring that the sample accurately represented the target population and minimized selection bias. Since the sample was a stratified random sample, this approach was used to effectively control for the variables related to parents' economic ability, nutrition, lifestyle, and education. By ensuring representation across different strata, we can better analyze their impact on the prevalence of caries. Furthermore, multiple training sessions were held for examiners before the field study to calibrate them and reduce measurement bias. However, the limitations of the cross-sectional study nature of the study, our results show a momentary picture of the state and the consequences of MIH defects. Moreover, the absence of comprehensive data on dietary habits, oral hygiene practices such as brushing frequency, fluoride exposure, and socioeconomic status (SES), which could significantly impact the findings. Additionally, the cross-sectional design restricts the ability to establish causality, as it captures a snapshot of data rather than tracking changes over time. Although the prevalence data for caries and MIH is limited to Jeddah, the city is the second largest and a major center in Saudi Arabia; therefore, the findings may provide a general indication of what to expect in other regions of the country.

Conclusion

The prevalence of caries was 85.5% and the prevalence of MIH was 17.9% among children in children Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. MIH was a risk factor for dental caries in primary dentition and caries in permanent dentition. This high prevalence of dental caries and MIH among children in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, highlights significant implications for clinical practice and public health policy. Clinically, dental professionals should implement targeted preventive programs for MIH-affected children, emphasizing early diagnosis, patient education, and an interdisciplinary approach to care. Implications for children diagnosed with MIH are to follow a strict preventive program to prevent dental caries in affected teeth. Public health initiatives should focus on raising awareness about MIH, improving access to dental care, and integrating preventive dental programs into schools.

Innovative neuroplastic healing: tendon Neuroplastic Training role in pain alleviation and boosting strength in lateral epicondylitis: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Hamza Khan, Aisha Razzaq, Ayesha Afridi, Suman Sheraz, Waqar Ahmed Awan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLateral epicondylitis pain affects the sensory-motor integration, affecting muscle activation and coordination, leading to deficit in grip strength and disability. Loading exercises helps in strengthening and in biomechanical control in the tendinopathies. The current study is planned to determine the effects of Tendon Neuroplastic Training on pain, strength, and disability in lateral epicondylitis patients.MethodsThis was a parallel design Randomized clinical trial conducted at National Institute of Rehabilitation Medicine from August 2022 to March 2023. Total n = 34 patients of lateral epicondylitis, of both genders, aged 25–45 years, having pain and tenderness over lateral epicondyle, with at least 2 special tests used for lateral epicondylitis (were recruited using non-probability purposive sampling. Sealed envelope method was used for random allocation into groups. Experimental group patients received Tendon Neuroplastic Training whereas active control was treated with Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis stretching and Myofascial release. Total 3 sessions/week were given to each patient for a total of 4 weeks. Outcome measures were Numerical Pain Rating Scale, Dynamometer and Patient Rated Tennis Elbow Evaluation for pain, strength and disability assessment.ResultsTendon Neuroplastic Training group showed significant improvement (p < 0.05) to active control concerning pain reduction (effect size = 0.37) and improvement in disability (effect size = 0.449) Both groups showed equal positive effects regarding grip strength. (p > 0.05).ConclusionThis study concluded that Tendon Neuroplastic Training has better effects on the reduction of pain and tennis elbow related disability with lateral epicondylitis. Therefore, the current study recommends the use of TNT for the treatment of lateral epicondylitis.Clinical trial numberThe study was retrospectively registered at clinicaltrail.gov with the number NCT05707117, Registration date: 22–1-23.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-08328-1.Keywords: Disability, Grip strength, Lateral Epicondylitis, Pain intensity, Tendon Neuroplastic Training. Introduction

Lateral elbow tendinopathy (LE) is a common enthesopathy affecting the common extensor origin particularly extensor carpi radialis brevis [1]. Its affects 0.3–12.2% of the adult population [2]. Lateral epicondylitis (LE) is caused by activities causing extensor overload by repeated loading and wrist extension [3]. Lateral epicondylitis is more prevalent among 20–45 years old in Pakistan [4]. Manual laborers, computer workers, athletes and carpenters are most likely to develop lateral epicondylitis [5]. It commonly effects dominant arm [6] and presents with lateral elbow pain that can radiate throughout the forearm, worsen with the activities particularly gripping [7]. The persistent pain at lateral elbow and in hand gripping activities making it difficult for the person to continue daily living activities.

Lateral epicondylitis characterizes by microtrauma in extensor carpi radialis brevis tendon that impair proprioception and motor control leading to difficulties in posture maintenance and joint stability. Additionally, the pain and discomfort associated with lateral epicondylitis can affect neurocognitive functions such as attention, motor planning, and execution, further hindering the ability to perform precise and controlled movements. This impaired proprioceptive disruption of normal function of extensor tendons and neural pathways impacting elbow and wrist performance [8]. The recommended treatments for lateral elbow tendinopathy are friction massage, manipulations, mobilizations, laser, PRP and extra corporeal shock wave therapy, orthosis and Kinesio taping. Out of these treatments the manual therapy and eccentric training are considered to be highly efficient and cost-effective treatments [9].

Tendon neuroplastic training (TNT) is a form of eccentric resistance training that works by engaging the degenerated tendons. TNT involves exercises designed to improve proprioceptive input and stimulate adaptive changes in the central nervous system, which in turn enhance motor control and joint stability. By repeatedly engaging these pathways, Tempo-controlled resistance trainings can promote better integration of sensory and motor signals, resulting in improved neuromuscular control of the elbow and wrist [10]. Furthermore, TNT positively influences neurocognitive performance by enhancing attention, motor planning, and memory, making rehabilitation exercises more effective and movements more automatic. This comprehensive approach not only alleviates symptoms but also addresses the underlying neural and cognitive dysfunctions associated with lateral epicondylitis, aligning with the goals of musculoskeletal disease and injury control [11]. Instead of self-paced exercise, TNT combines isometric or isotonic strengthening training with an externally timed audio or visual cue provided by a metronome and places a significant emphasis on neuromuscular control in order to promote neuroplasticity [12].

Tendon neuroplastic training is a newer approach which has effective results in tendinopathies of rotator cuff tendon and patellar tendons [13]. Literature is deficient regarding the use of TNT in the lateral epicondylitis except two case reports. Case reports lack control group, therefore could not rule out the possibility that whether improvement was due to multimodal treatment, tendon neuroplastic technique or some other unidentified factors. Therefore, Randomized control trials are needed to determine the effects of tendon neuroplastic training in lateral epicondylitis. This study provided an effective method for the treatment of lateral epicondylitis. As apparently all other forms of treatment merely focus on strength training and not the central nervous system component of tendinopathies. And as a result, the baseline pain persists even after following course of strength training and other forms of conventional therapies and surgery. The current study is focused on determining the effects of Tendon Neuroplastic Training on pain, strength, and disability in patients with lateral elbow tendinopathy.

Subjects and methods

It was a Randomized clinical trial conducted in National Institute of Rehabilitation Medicine Pakistan, from august 22-March 23. After approval from the Riphah ethical committee (RIPHAH/FR&AHS/Letter-01308) the RCT was registered with the United States National Library of Medicine (Protocol ID: NCTXXXX). All the participants signed the Urdu and English consent form and they were informed about the project description and about the confidentiality of their data. All the ethical standards were kept in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The sample size was calculated through G-Power using the F-test, keeping the alpha-value 0.05, power 0.95 and effect size 0.25 taking into account the risk of dropouts a total of 34 patients were included [14]. (Fig. 1).

Sample size calculation

The sampling technique was non-probability convenience sampling. Patients of both genders aged between 25–45 years with pain and tenderness over the lateral epicondyle with at least two positive tests among Maudsley’s test, Mill’s test and Cozen test were included [1]. Patients with open wounds, fractures or surgical procedure done around the elbow joint, elbow instability, extensor tendon rupture, symptoms of cervical radiculopathy or radial nerve entrapment, major upper limb surgery, tumor or wound and compartment syndrome and receiving any concurrent treatment, prior treatment history and pain catastrophizing > 30 measured by pain catastrophizing scale were excluded. Participants who met the inclusion criteria were assigned randomly to experimental group and active control group through sealed envelope method.

This was a double blinded study with the blinding done at the participants and outcome assessor level. Allocation concealment was done using the sealed envelope method until the intervention was assigned. Author AR generated the random allocation sequence; author HK enrolled participants and author AA assigned the intervention. The study adheres to the complete CONSORT guidelines (Fig. 2) and the CONSORT check list is updated as supplementary file.

CONSORT diagram

Intervention

Both groups received 10 min hot pack before performing exercises. Experimental group received Tendon Neuroplastic Training (TNT). Exercise involves isotonic flexion, extension of the wrist with a dumbbell paced with external audio/visual cue on smart phone using pro-Metronome app. Patients listened the sound and track the movement of metronome with the eyes. Pace of metronome was set at 20 beats per minute such that each beat was 3 s apart. This allowed a 3s concentric and 3 s eccentric phases. Total 4 sets of 8 repetitions were completed with a 2-min rest between each set. Exercise began with 1 pound’s weight, but it was made sure that this wouldn't cause pain during exercise (more than 5/10), gradual progression in weight to 3 pounds in 2nd session till 2nd week and 5 kgs from 2–4 weeks. Subjects have attended TNT sessions at clinic for three days per week for total 4 weeks. (Fig. 3).

Active control group was treated with wrist extensors stretching and myofascial release. Myofascial release technique was applied with the help of thumb at the 1 cm below the lateral epicondyle at the painful site with 2 repetitions per session for 5 min each technique. Extensor stretching was done with the elbow extended forearm pronated and wrist flexed and ulnar deviated. One hand was maintained the position of hand while other hand was placed beneath elbow to maintain position. The sessions were performed by the researcher in clinic. (Fig. 4).

Myofascial release and extensor carpi radialis brevis stretching (picture taken after the consent from patient)

Three sessions were conducted in a week for a total of 4 consecutive weeks.

The outcome measures were disability, grip strength and pain measured using the patient related tennis elbow evaluation, handheld dynamometer and Numerical Pain Rating Scale respectively. Patient rated tennis elbow evaluation is a 15-items questionnaire for assessing pain and disability. Where 0 indicated no pain and disability and pain and disability and 100 indicated worst pain and disability in tennis elbow patients. The intraclass correlation factor for pain is 0.89, for disability 0.83 and 0.89 for overall questionnaire [15]. Hand held dynamometer was used in the sitting position with the arm on the table with elbow in neutral and wrist extended. The best score of three trails was considered. The ICC of handgrip strength is 0.954 shows that dynamometer is reliable tool for measurement of grip strength [16]. Numeric pain rating scale is a self-reported 11-point scale to measure highest pain intensity with 0 representing no pain and 10 representing worst pain. NPRS is reliable for patients with musculoskeletal disorders, with intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) ranging from 0.72 to 0.95 across different studies [17].

Assessment was done at baseline, after 1stweek 2nd week, 3rdweek and 4th week.

The data was analyzed using SPSS version 26. To compare both groups at baseline man Whitney test was applied on categorical variables including gender, occupation, stage of disease, dominant side and the side involved. Independent T-test was applied on continuous variables like age and BMI). The normality of the data was checked through the Shapiro–Wilk test which shows the data was normally distributed. Box Test of equality of covariances is used to check the homogeneity of covariances. Box Test of equality of covariance showed P value > 0.05 for all outcome measures and met the criteria for homogeneity of covariance. Lavene’s Test was used to check the equality of variances P values of all outcome variables was grater that 0.05 and hence all variables met for equality of variances. Mauchly’s Test of sphericity value shows that sphericity was not met so greenhouse Geisser values were used. Mixed way ANOVA was applied for comparison of groups on five times points. The p-value less than 0.05 was set as the significance level and the strength of the effect shown by effect size η2.

Discussion

The highlights of the research had main focus on determining the effects of Tendon Neuroplastic Training (TNT) on strength in lateral epicondylitis besides with its effects on pain intensity and level of disability. The results of study show that participants improved significantly in term of intensity of pain and level of disability and hand grip strength related to the epicondylitis.

Tendon neuroplastic training has significant effects on reducing pain and increasing cortical control of muscle group during rehabilitation of individuals with tendon pain and tendinosis. Rio et al. recruited elite volleyball and basketball players, having patellar tendon pain during exercise into strength training program with externally paced auditory stimulation. Pain of athletes reduced after 4 weeks. The cortical inhibition was also reduced as a result of externally paced training. There was 41% reduction in the Quadriceps inhibition recorded [13]. The result of current RCT shows great deal of resemblance with this study. At the end of each week, the group receiving TNT, as opposed to traditional physical therapy, saw a notable and immediate pain reduction. Walsh et al. also got similar results with tendon neuroplastic training in case reports but NPRS score more drastically reduced in our study as compared to the study mentioned, because participants of mentioned study did not take any rest and continued their daily work during course of intervention, that is why their pain and symptoms reduction was not so significant as in current study. Another reason for the differences in results may lie in the study design. While the current study was a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with a diverse participant pool, the previous study was a case report involving only two individuals—an electrician (61 years old) and a machine operator (37 years old). Current study included participants from various backgrounds, such as laborers, machine workers, tennis players and students, which introduced greater variability in age and occupational demands. These differences likely contributed to the broader and potentially more favorable outcomes observed in our research [18]. While tendinopathy-related pain problems are certainly affected by damage or inflammation-induced nociception, local myofascial release cannot be the only element taken into account for such pain experiences. There is growing evidence that centrally-mediated systems contribute to the processing of pain in tendinopathy and may provide a treatment of choice for people who have persistent symptoms [12]. The minimal clinical important difference (MCID) of Pain intensity measured by NPRS more than 1.65 was considered as clinically significant for patients [19]. The mean difference of pain change score of 5.12 for TNT group which means that the patient improves clinically.

Grip strength is the key factor contributing to a person's suffering in circumstances including chronic elbow tendinopathy. This functional deficit or dysfunction should always be treated using a wrist approach rather than focusing on the specific elbow level [19]. Isotonic strength training paced to visual and auditory stimulation also exhibits significant increase in grip strength of individuals with Lateral epicondylitis. Two case reports included that tendon neuroplastic training improved grip strength in patients with lateral epicondylitis [18]. The result of current study shows resemblance with the previous studies as the grip strength significantly increased after application of TNT. Weight loading exercises can reduce the pain and improve the tendon strength by stimulating mechano-receptors which are present in tenocytes for collagen production, increasing the blood flow to improve tissue repair, which is a key cellular mechanism that is required for tendon injuries recovery [11]. Paced resistance training may also help in alignment of collagen fibers of a tendon and enhances the formation of cross-linkage of collagen fibers to improve the tensile strength. These exercises also lead to the normalization of glycosaminoglycan concentration levels [20]. Motor control maladaptation and abnormal motor inhibition are managed by the Tendon Neuroplastic Training which incorporates the fruitful exercise protocols with modulation of central nervous system (CNS) and better neuromuscular control aiming to induce neuroplasticity [8]. The minimal clinical significant difference of grip strength is 5 kg to 6.5 kg [21]. The minimum time duration to achieve the meaningful improvement in grip strength is 40 weeks [22]. Although the grip strength is both TNT groups did not cross the MCID, the reason probably was that the study was planned for 28 days, that’s probably was the reason for this small increase in the grip strength. However, there was a consistent increasing trend in grip strength of TNT group which might lead to clinically important difference if perused for more than 40 days. TNT group performed better in the functional tasks like turning door knobs, lifting up glass, wring of clothes, personal work, recreational activities and job duties as compared to the control group. The grip strength trend in TNT group is continuously incremental till 4 weeks while it becomes steady after 3rd week in control group. Probably the prolong term treatment more than 4 weeks can show pronounced difference between both groups.

TNT has significant effects on reducing disability during performing different activities of upper limb [23]. The result of this study shows resemblance with current study. external pacing at the participant’s preferred movement frequency, augments synaptic efficacy of neural connections specific to the trained direction. Functional brain imaging has identified a broader activation pattern within cortical and subcortical regions and increased inter-regional coupling during self-paced movement when compared to movements synchronized with an auditory metronome. Brisk, repetitive wrist extension paced by an auditory metronome would increase resting corticomotor excitability in pathways mediating wrist extension and alter the kinematics of TMS-evoked wrist movement toward extension, to a greater extent than self-paced training. The synchronous arrival of afferent auditory input from the auditory cortex via ipsilateral premotor and supplementary motor cortex, with depolarization of pyramidal cells in the M1 wrist representation during movement execution, may lead to an increase in synaptic efficacy conforming to Hebbian principles. This indicates that performing rhythmic self-paced movement appears to increase the demands on the motor system, whereas metronome-pacing enhances efficiency [24, 25].

TNT's incorporation of external cues and neuromuscular training enhances sensorimotor integration, leading to better motor control and strength. This innovative approach shows promise in addressing the multidimensional challenges of LE rehabilitation by not only focusing on strength but also engaging the central nervous system for sustained pain relief and functional improvement.

Current tendon rehabilitation including myofascial release and manipulation does not fully address the muscle's corticospinal control and the balancing of excitatory and inhibitory signals from center nervous system, which is very important for normal control of movements, that eventually causes tendon stress and return of the symptoms [9]. Eccentric loading exercises of the wrist extensors can reduce pain and increase tendon strength by stimulating the tenocytes' mechano-receptors, which are essential for the production of collagen in process of recovery from tendon injuries. These exercises also lead to the alignment of a tendon's collagen fibers and promote the cross-linking of those fibers to increase tensile strength. Progressive loading is used in this training approach to alleviate the tensile strength deficits associated with tendinopathy; however, the central nervous system impairments are not sufficiently addressed. Maladaptive motor control and aberrant motor inhibition may be contributing reasons to the conventional therapies' resistance. This is the reason for evolution of tendon neuroplastic training [25]. It has been demonstrated that externally paced, skillful movement-like eccentric training not only lessens tendon pain but also modulates the excitatory and inhibitory control of the muscle and lowers tendon stress [26, 27].

TNT appears to be a promising approach for treating tendinopathy at the peripheral site of damage, proposing changes in corticospinal excitability and cortical inhibition brought on by external pacing factors that change one's motor program for a specific task. It might be the result of the permanent neuroplasticity done at the brain level neuroplastic training (TNT) [28].Tendon neuroplastic training can be a new area of investigation for researchers by using the EEG or blood biomarkers of neuroplasticity or functional MRI.

The strength of this project is that it is a 4-week tendon neuroplastic training conducted using a double blinded, registered, two-armed randomized control trail using repeated measure design. Previously only case reports are available on tendon neuroplastic training in lateral epicondylitis.

The limitations of current study afe single centered randomized trial with particular age group that might limit its generalizability. Recurrence of the disease was not taken by follow up sessions. Further RCTs are required with follow up for 6 months’ time, to find out the reoccurrence of lateral epicondylitis after application of TNT. Future studies should also work on effects of TNT on lateral epicondylitis according to disease severity, occupation, lifestyles to find out individual variations to treatment. Future researchers should also look for the neuroplasticity biomarkers to explore the exact mechanism behind the tendon neuroplastic training.

Conclusion

The study concluded that tendon neuroplastic training has positive effects on pain reduction and tennis elbow related disability reduction as compared to conventional treatment in lateral epicondylitis.

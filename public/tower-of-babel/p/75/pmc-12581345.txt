Laparoscopic-assisted transanal soave for refractory constipation in children: outcomes and management
Authors: Zhenhua Yang, Lei Huang, Yanhang Fu, Bin Jiang, Zhifeng Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study evaluates the efficacy and safety of laparoscopic-assisted transanal Soave procedure (LATSP) in children with refractory constipation unrelated to Hirschsprung’s disease, addressing the paucity of evidence for surgical management in this population.MethodsA single-center retrospective cohort of 30 children (median age: 4 years; range: 1–13) underwent LATSP after failing ≥ 6 months of standardized conservative therapy (dual laxatives + biofeedback). Preoperative evaluation excluded structural/neurogenic etiologies via MRI, barium enema, anorectal manometry, and rectal biopsy confirming the presence of ganglion cells. Primary outcomes included Symptom Severity (SS) scores (0–65) and Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) at 6/24/36 months postoperatively. Complications were graded by Clavien-Dindo.ResultsSS scores decreased from 29.1 ± 9.1 preoperatively to 9.2 ± 3.3 at 36 months (P < 0.001). Stool normalization (BSFS Types 4–5) increased from 10% to 73.4% (P < 0.001). Four complications occurred (13.3%): fecal incontinence (n = 2, Grade II), anal bleeding (n = 1, Grade I), and presacral infection (n = 1, Grade IIIb), all resolved without sequelae. LATSP showed superior symptom resolution versus historical Malone ACE cohorts (73.4% vs. 20% at 6 years).ConclusionsLATSP is a safe and effective intervention for carefully selected children with refractory constipation, providing sustained symptom relief without permanent stomas. Multidisciplinary postoperative protocols are essential for optimal outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-025-06291-3.

Introduction

Refractory constipation affects 5–30% of pediatric patients, with a subset failing maximal medical therapy including stimulant and osmotic laxatives combined with biofeedback training [1–3]. While conservative management remains first-line, surgical intervention becomes necessary when quality of life is severely impacted by persistent symptoms despite ≥ 6 months of standardized treatment [4].

The laparoscopic-assisted transanal Soave procedure (LATSP), originally developed for Hirschsprung’s disease [5], has shown promise in selected cases of functional constipation unresponsive to medical therapy [6]. However, current evidence primarily derives from small case series or focuses on Hirschsprung’s populations [7]. A critical knowledge gap exists regarding its application in idiopathic refractory constipation, particularly concerning long-term functional outcomes and comparative efficacy versus established procedures like the Malone antegrade continence enema (ACE) [8].

This study presents the largest single-center experience with LATSP for non-Hirschsprung’s refractory constipation, evaluated through prospective follow-up of standardized outcome measures. Our aims were to:Quantify symptom resolution using validated scoring systems.Assess safety through protocolized complication reporting.Compare outcomes with historical surgical alternative.

Materials and methods

Ethical considerations

This retrospective cohort study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Nanjing Medical University Affiliated Children’s Hospital (202403015-1). Written informed consent was obtained from all guardians prior to data collection, consistent with Declaration of Helsinki principles.

Study population

Between January 2019 and February 2022, we enrolled 30 consecutive pediatric patients who met our institutional criteria for refractory constipation surgery (Fig. 1). All candidates had documented failure of at least six months of standardized medical management according to ESPGHAN/NASPGHAN 2024 guidelines [1], with persistent symptoms despite optimal therapy (Table 1). Patients with structural abnormalities such as anorectal malformations or spinal cord anomalies, organic disorders including Hirschsprung’s disease and anal sphincter dyssynergia, or other confounding conditions were excluded from the study. Preoperative evaluation

All patients underwent a standardized diagnostic protocol:

Functional assessments

Anorectal manometry

Performed under chloral hydrate sedation (50 mg/kg) or in awake patients. Parameters included resting anal pressure (mmHg), internal anal sphincter relaxation ratio (%), and rectoanal inhibitory reflex (RAIR)(Methodology as described by Meunier et al. [9]).

Colonic Transit Study [10, 11]

Patients ingested a capsule containing 24 radiopaque markers. Abdominal radiographs at 72 h were analyzed via the Arhan classification [12]. Twenty-four patients completed the study; six cases with incomplete data (due to non-compliance with marker ingestion) underwent sensitivity analysis (multiple imputation), confirming no significant impact on outcomes (P >0.05).

Anatomic evaluation

Barium enema

Conducted at two timepoints (initial diagnosis and preoperative reassessment). Persistent rectosigmoid dilation (> 50% of pelvic inlet diameter) and delayed contrast evacuation (> 24 h) were documented.

Lumbosacral MRI [5]

Ruled out spinal cord anomalies using 3 T scanners (Siemens Magnetom Skyra).

Full-thickness rectal biopsies

Processed (2 cm above dentate line) with hematoxylin-eosin and calretinin immunohistochemistry to confirm normal ganglion cells and nerve fibers, per ESPGHAN guidelines [1].

Constipation severity tools

Symptom Severity (SS) Score [13]: An 8-item scale (0–65 points) assessing fecal retention duration, defecation pain, and laxative dependency.

Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) [14]: Stool consistency classified as Types 1–3 (constipation), 4–5 (normal), or 6–7 (diarrhea).

Surgical technique

The standardized LATSP protocol (Fig. 2) followed Tang et al.‘s description [15]with modifications:Fig. 2Standardized LATSP protocol. a Laparoscopic seromuscular biopsy; b Mesocolon dissection; c Retrorectal space mobilization; d Dentate line identification (0.5 cm proximal to anastomosis site)

Laparoscopic phase

A 10-mm umbilical port was established for the laparoscope, alongside a 5-mm left lateral abdominal port for instrumentation and two additional 5-mm right lateral ports for retraction and dissection.Following establishment of pneumoperitoneum and port placement, the transition zone between the dilated rectosigmoid and normal-caliber proximal colon was carefully identified under 5× magnification, followed by systematic full-thickness seromuscular biopsies obtained at 3 cm intervals proximal to this visual transition zone for histopathological confirmation. The mesocolon was dissected while preserving marginal vascular arcades. Rectal mobilization extended distally to the coccygeal apex to minimize traction injury.

Laparoscopic phase

A 10-mm umbilical port was established for the laparoscope, alongside a 5-mm left lateral abdominal port for instrumentation and two additional 5-mm right lateral ports for retraction and dissection.Following establishment of pneumoperitoneum and port placement, the transition zone between the dilated rectosigmoid and normal-caliber proximal colon was carefully identified under 5× magnification, followed by systematic full-thickness seromuscular biopsies obtained at 3 cm intervals proximal to this visual transition zone for histopathological confirmation. The mesocolon was dissected while preserving marginal vascular arcades. Rectal mobilization extended distally to the coccygeal apex to minimize traction injury.

Transanal phase

Initiated 0.5 cm above the dentate line. Submucosal and mucosal layers were separated to the peritoneal reflection, preserving the anterior rectal muscular sheath. Resected the sigmoid colon with intraoperative frozen-section confirmation of normal proximal margins.

Anastomosis

A single-layer hand-sewn anastomosis was performed using 4 − 0 absorbable sutures at 0.5 cm proximal to the dentate line [15].

Postoperative management

Nutritional support

Total parenteral nutrition (TPN) was administered for ≥ 7 days to promote anastomotic healing.

Anal dilation

Initiated at 4 weeks postoperatively using Hegar dilators (12–22 Fr, sized by age), followed a risk-stratified regimen: daily sessions for 3–6 months. Dilation frequency was adjusted based on digital rectal examinations performed every 4 weeks to assess compliance and stenosis risk.

Dietary rehabilitation

A phased protocol included 2 weeks of low-residue diets, followed by gradual fiber reintroduction (5–10 g/day) guided by daily BSFS tracking.

Multidisciplinary care

A hybrid digital-physical framework was implemented to coordinate postoperative interventions. The Pediatric Rehabilitation Team (PRT)—comprising pediatric surgeons, clinical psychologists, and nursing specialists—designed individualized care plans addressing biopsychosocial needs. Key components included:

Psychological support

Baseline assessments using the Children’s Depression Inventory-2 (CDI-2) and Screen for Child Anxiety-Related Disorders (SCARED) identified high-risk patients (SCARED > 25), who received trauma-informed cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) to address procedural anxiety and defecation phobia.

Caregiver engagement

Structured workshops provided training on dietary titration and behavioral reinforcement, while monthly virtual support groups moderated by pediatric psychologists aimed to reduce caregiver burnout.The multidisciplinary rehabilitation protocol achieved 92% (28/30) caregiver adherence, defined as participation in ≥ 80% of scheduled sessions (CBT, dietary counseling, and digital monitoring), with no significant differences in adherence rates between intervention components (P = 0.45 by chi-square test).

Digital integration

A WeChat mini-program interfaced with the institutional EHR system enabled real-time data synchronization and remote monitoring, ensuring continuity of care across follow-ups.

Data collection and statistical analysis

Primary outcomes

The Bristol Stool Form Scale was selected for its validated correlation with colonic transit time in pediatric constipation [14], while the Symptom Severity Score captured multidimensional outcomes including pain frequency and laxative dependence [13]. The dual-assessment approach aligns with ESPGHAN/NASPGHAN consensus recommendations for surgical outcome studies in pediatric constipation [1]. SS scores and BSFS were recorded preoperatively and at 6, 24, and 36 months postoperatively. Three missing BSFS entries at 24 months were managed via the last observation carried forward method.

Complications

Documented as per Clavien-Dindo classification [16].

Statistical methods

Non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired data, Mann-Whitney U test for unpaired data) were applied using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Continuous variables are expressed as median (range), with significance set at P < 0.05 (two-tailed). Figures were generated using R (ggplot2 package), with reproducibility ensured through scripted workflows.

Comparative efficacy

LATSP surpassed historical Malone ACE outcomes (73.4% vs. 20% symptom resolution at 6 years; P < 0.001) [17].

Discussion

This study establishes laparoscopic-assisted transanal Soave procedure (LATSP) as a transformative intervention for refractory constipation in children, demonstrating 73.4% sustained symptom resolution at 36-month follow-up. These outcomes mark a paradigm shift from palliative management to definitive treatment, particularly when compared to the 20% long-term success rate of Malone antegrade continence enema procedures in similar cohorts [17]. The avoidance of permanent stomas represents a critical advantage in our East Asian patient population, where stoma acceptance remains culturally challenging [18, 19].

The avoidance of permanent stomas emerged as a pivotal advantage of LATSP in our East Asian cohort, where cultural values significantly influence treatment decisions. Our data demonstrate that LATSP not only achieved superior clinical outcomes compared to historical ACE procedures (73.4% vs. 20% long-term symptom resolution) but also addressed important cultural considerations regarding stoma acceptance.These findings underscore the importance of adapting surgical innovation to local sociomedical contexts, as emphasized by Wood et al. in their advocacy for anatomically targeted resections. The high postoperative rehabilitation adherence rate of 92% further validates the cultural acceptability of this stoma-sparing approach.

The efficacy of LATSP stems from our stringent selection criteria. Colonic transit studies identified rectosigmoid dysfunction in 87.5% (21/24) of cases, while anorectal manometry revealed elevated RAIR thresholds (>10 mL in 63.3%) despite normal resting pressures (57.0 ± 8.6 mmHg) [9]. The preserved sphincter tone with elevated RAIR thresholds implies a functional coordination deficit, contrasting with the complete reflex absence seen in aganglionosis. These findings correlated with barium enema evidence of rectosigmoid dilation (>50% pelvic inlet diameter), supporting regional neuromuscular dysfunction as the likely pathophysiological basis. Although 10 younger children (age < 4 years) could not complete resting pressure measurements, their symptom profiles and postoperative outcomes were comparable to the overall cohort.

The dual-outcome analysis combining Symptom Severity (SS) scores and Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) data provides a comprehensive evaluation of postoperative improvement [5, 13]. The SS score’s significant reduction from 29.1 to 9.2 (P < 0.001) reflects marked alleviation of pain, laxative dependence, and fecal retention—key dimensions of constipation-related disability [13]. Concurrently, BSFS normalization (Types 4–5) increased from 10% to 73.4%, confirming objective physiological recovery of colonic motility. Notably, the temporal correlation between SS score decline and BSFS normalization (Figs. 6 and 7) underscores LATSP’s dual impact: resolving both subjective distress (SS) and objective dysfunction (BSFS) [5, 6]. This synergy validates the procedure’s ability to address multifactorial constipation pathology, where traditional metrics (e.g., stool frequency alone) may underestimate treatment efficacy [5, 8].

Furthermore, the interpretation of intraoperative frozen-section biopsies requires nuanced consideration. In our cohort, histopathological analysis successfully confirmed the presence of ganglion cells at all proximal resection margins, effectively excluding aganglionosis. However, as established in the literature, conventional histological assessment primarily demonstrates structural integrity of the enteric nervous system without evaluating functional neuromuscular coordination. This distinction is particularly relevant in refractory constipation, where pathophysiology often involves functional disturbances rather than structural aganglionosis. The principal utility of frozen section in our protocol was therefore to confirm anatomical landmarks and ensure adequate resection length based on preoperative functional and radiological assessments, rather than to identify a histopathological transition zone. This approach underscores the necessity of integrating multiple diagnostic modalities—manometric, radiological, and histological—to guide surgical decision-making in complex functional disorders.

From a technical perspective, three procedural refinements likely contributed to our favorable outcomes: First, the standardized dentate line-proximal anastomosis (0.5 cm) as described by Tang et al. [15]; second, meticulous preservation of marginal vascular arcades during rectal mobilization; and third, routine intraoperative frozen section confirmation of resection margins. These modifications may explain our lower anastomotic fistula rate (3.3%) compared to historical reports of 8–12% [20]. The overall 13.3% complication rate, predominantly Clavien-Dindo grade I-II events, compares favorably with alternative procedures, particularly regarding fecal incontinence (6.7% vs. ACE’s 15–30% [8]). All cases of transient incontinence resolved with structured pelvic floor rehabilitation.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. The single-center retrospective design may affect generalizability, while the 20% rate of incomplete colonic transit studies could introduce selection bias. Future research should focus on three priorities: multicenter validation of our selection criteria, implementation of ICG fluorescence for real-time perfusion assessment [21, 22], and development of standardized postoperative rehabilitation protocols.

In conclusion, LATSP represents a promising surgical intervention for carefully selected children with refractory constipation unrelated to Hirschsprung’s disease. Its ability to achieve durable symptom resolution while avoiding permanent stomas addresses both clinical needs and cultural preferences in specific populations. The integration of multimodal selection criteria, technical refinements, and culturally sensitive rehabilitation protocols underscores its potential as a precision surgery approach. However, its long-term safety and direct comparison with the ACE procedure require further validation.

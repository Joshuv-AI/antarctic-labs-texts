Del traumatismo a la fibrosis un caso de atrapamiento del nervio cubital en el canal de Guyon
Authors: Luan Nascimento Lázaro, Pedro Henrique de Oliveira Alvarenga, Ocacir de Souza Reis Soares, Márcio Luís Duarte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGuyon’s canal syndrome is an uncommon distal ulnar neuropathy caused by compression within the wrist. We report a case of delayed ulnar nerve entrapment secondary to post-traumatic fibrosis, diagnosed nine years after wrist injury and surgery. High-resolution ultrasonography demonstrated focal nerve compression within Guyon’s canal adjacent to fibrotic tissue. The patient presented with chronic sensory deficits and hypothenar atrophy. Conservative treatment led to partial symptom control. This case highlights delayed post-surgical fibrosis as a potential cause of Guyon’s canal syndrome and underscores the diagnostic value of ultrasound in chronic compressive neuropathies.

CONCEPTOS CLAVE

What is known on the subject?

Guyon’s canal syndrome is an uncommon distal ulnar neuropathy typically caused by ganglion cysts, tumors, repetitive trauma, or vascular abnormalities. Imaging plays a key role in confirming structural compression and differentiating it from other causes of ulnar neuropathy.

What does this work contribute?

This case highlights delayed post-traumatic fibrosis as a rare cause of Guyon’s canal syndrome and reinforces high-resolution ultrasound as an effective first-line modality for identifying chronic distal ulnar nerve compression.

Divulgación

El Síndrome del Canal de Guyon es una condición poco común causada por la compresión del nervio cubital en la muñeca. Puede provocar dolor, debilidad y entumecimiento en los dedos anular y meñique, afectando la función de la mano y las actividades diarias. Sus causas incluyen lesiones, movimientos repetitivos y quistes. El diagnóstico temprano mediante ultrasonido y evaluación clínica permite identificar el problema y establecer el tratamiento adecuado. Las opciones de tratamiento incluyen fisioterapia, medicamentos o, en casos graves, cirugía para aliviar la compresión del nervio. Reconocer los síntomas y buscar atención médica oportuna mejora significativamente los resultados.

A 46-year-old man presented with progressive numbness in the fourth and fifth fingers of the right hand, persisting for nine years following a glass injury and surgical repair of the wrist. Physical examination revealed hypothenar muscle atrophy, reduced grip strength, sensory deficits in the ulnar distribution, and a positive Tinel’s sign at the wrist. Radiography of the wrist showed no osseous abnormalities, but ultrasound detected fibrosis compressing the ulnar nerve within Guyon’s canal, diagnosing Guyon’s canal syndrome (Figure 1). The ulnar artery was preserved. The patient was treated conservatively with pregabalin and physiotherapy, achieving partial symptom relief. At follow-up, motor deficits persisted, consistent with chronic compressive neuropathy.

Guyon’s canal syndrome is a rare distal ulnar neuropathy caused by compression within the ulnar tunnel at the wrist. Etiologies include ganglion cysts, tumors, fractures, vascular abnormalities, accessory muscles, sports (especially cycling), repetitive trauma, previous surgeries, and less commonly fibrosis 1-3 . Ganglion cysts represent the most common underlying cause 1 . Delayed post-traumatic fibrosis may progressively reduce canal space, leading to chronic nerve compression 2 , 3 . Diagnosis may be challenging due to symptom overlap with cubital tunnel syndrome and cervical radiculopathy 1 .

Palpation seeks to identify masses, inflammatory signs, or noticeable sensitivity changes.1 Additionally, the diagnosis should be complemented by imaging studies, such as ultrasonography with Doppler, magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography, and X-rays, to assess potential canal compression 4 . In more specific cases, electromyography can also be employed and proves useful for diagnostic confirmation 4 . In the case presented, high-resolution ultrasound was selected as the primary imaging modality because of its high spatial resolution for superficial peripheral nerves, its ability to dynamically assess nerve morphology, and its capacity to directly demonstrate compressive fibrotic tissue within the confined space of Guyon’s canal 2 .

Treatment for Guyon’s canal syndrome may include conservative measures such as wrist immobilization, physiotherapy, pain-relief medications, and local corticosteroid applications. If common techniques yield unsatisfactory results or structural alterations like compression associated with a cyst are present, surgical intervention may be required to relieve nerve and blood vessel compression and perform decompression 3 . Surgical treatment offers greater resolution, involving the release of Guyon’s canal, resulting in ulnar nerve decompression. In the absence of signs of ulnar or median nerve compression, treatment is primarily conservative, aimed at symptom relief, preventing progression, and addressing possible causal factors 3 .

Delayed post-traumatic fibrosis should be recognized as a potential cause of Guyon’s canal syndrome. High-resolution ultrasound is an effective first-line imaging modality for identifying structural compression and guiding clinical management in chronic distal ulnar neuropathy.

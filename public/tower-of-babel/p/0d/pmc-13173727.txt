Isolated splenic metastasis from colorectal cancer: clinical characteristics and management
Authors: Yujie Li, Jingna Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIsolated splenic metastases from colorectal cancer (CRC) are an extremely rare clinical entity with unclear clinical characteristics. The purpose of this study was to clarify the prognostic factors, therapeutic outcomes, diagnostic methods, and clinical patterns related to this uncommon metastatic entity.MethodsWe describe a novel instance of metachronous isolated splenic metastasis treated at our institution. Additionally, from the database’s creation to December 2025, we performed a comprehensive literature search in PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library, finding 36 previously documented cases. Descriptive statistics were used to examine clinical and demographic features, diagnostic techniques, treatment plans, and results.ResultsOur patient, a 56-year-old man, developed a solitary splenic metastasis 34 months after undergoing laparoscopic left hemicolectomy for descending colon adenocarcinoma. The splenic metastasis was subsequently managed with laparoscopic splenectomy. A thorough analysis of 37 cases, including our own, showed clear clinical patterns: 86.5% (32/37) of cases presented with metachronous disease, with a median interval of 24 months; left-sided primary tumors (sigmoid colon, rectum, descending colon, and splenic flexure) predominated (29/37, 78.4%); splenectomy was associated with an 89.3% disease-free survival rate among patients with available follow-up, with metachronous cases showing particularly favorable outcomes (95.8%).ConclusionThis comprehensive research highlights the significance of long-term surveillance in CRC patients and indicates that complete surgical excision of isolated splenic metastases may be associated with favorable outcomes, especially in metachronous presentations.

Introduction

Colorectal carcinoma (CRC) is the third most common cancer worldwide, with metastatic spread most commonly involving the liver (50–60%) [1] and lungs (2–10%) [2]. Splenic metastasis, however, remains extremely rare, with reported incidences of 0.2% to 0.5% in clinical series [3, 4]. The biological basis for this rarity has been attributed to several anatomical and immunological factors intrinsic to the spleen, including its acute-angled arterial supply, rhythmic sinusoidal contractions, absence of afferent lymphatics, and an immune-rich microenvironment that may hinder tumor cell implantation and proliferation [5, 6].

Isolated splenic metastasis, which is defined as a single splenic lesion without concurrent extra-splenic metastases, is an even rarer clinical entity than splenic metastases, which typically occur in the context of extensive systemic disease. Due to the paucity of evidence, the management of isolated splenic metastasis is still debatable; current practice is primarily based on individual reports and short case series [7, 8]. An improved understanding of the clinical features and outcomes of this uncommon entity is crucial for optimizing patient care.

This paper presents a new case and reviews 36 previously reported cases of isolated splenic metastasis from colorectal cancer. By pooling these data, we aim to describe the clinical patterns, diagnostic approaches, therapeutic options, and prognostic factors associated with this rare metastatic entity.

Materials and methods

Case presentation

A single case of isolated splenic metastasis from CRC treated at our institution was included in the study. Clinical data, including demographic information, clinical manifestations, imaging findings, surgical records, pathological and immunohistochemical (IHC) results, adjuvant therapy regimens, and follow-up outcomes were retrospectively collected and analyzed.

Literature search methods

To identify previously reported cases of isolated splenic metastasis from CRC, a systematic literature search was conducted on December 2025 in three electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. The search was performed from database inception to 31 December 2025, with the full search strategy using the following Boolean search strategy: (“colorectal cancer” OR “colon cancer” OR “rectal cancer” OR “colorectal carcinoma”) AND (“splenic metastasis” OR “splenic metastases” OR “isolated splenic metastasis” OR “solitary splenic metastasis”). No restrictions on language or publication type were imposed during the initial search.

The literature search and study selection were performed independently by two authors (Yujie Li and Jingna Hu). Disagreements between the two authors during search and selection were resolved through in-depth discussion until a consensus was reached. After removing duplicates, the authors independently screened titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria. Full-text articles of potentially eligible studies were then retrieved and assessed independently by both authors for final inclusion. Reference lists of included articles were manually screened to identify additional cases not captured by the electronic search.

Studies were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (1) histologically confirmed primary colorectal cancer; (2) histologically confirmed splenic metastasis; (3) isolated splenic involvement, defined as the absence of concurrent extra-splenic metastases at the time of splenic metastasis diagnosis; and (4) sufficient individual patient-level data available for extraction to ensure comprehensive analysis, including at least three of the following: patient demographics, primary tumor characteristics, timing of presentation, treatment modality, or clinical outcomes.

Studies were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) Review articles, editorials, conference abstracts; (2) Concurrent extra-splenic metastases at diagnosis; (3) Splenic involvement confirmed only by imaging; (4) Duplicate publications of the same patient.

The primary objective of this review was to describe the clinical and demographic characteristics of patients with isolated splenic metastasis from colorectal cancer. The secondary objectives were to: (1) evaluate diagnostic modalities employed for detection of splenic metastases; (2) assess treatment strategies, particularly the role of surgical resection; and (3) describe clinical outcomes, including overall survival and disease-free status following treatment.

Through this search and selection process, we identified 36 documented case reports spanning from 1969 to 2025. Combined with the novel case from our institution, a total of 37 cases were included in the final analysis. Due to the retrospective nature of the included case reports and the substantial heterogeneity in follow-up duration and outcome reporting, formal statistical analyses were not feasible. Accordingly, data were summarized using descriptive statistics, including frequencies, proportions, medians, and interquartile ranges (IQR). No imputation or assumptions were made for missing data.

Results

Case outcomes

In November 2022, a 56-year-old man with no remarkable medical history presented with a two-week history of constipation. An urgent abdominal computed tomography (CT) scan showed an obstructive tumor in the descending colon after a physical examination revealed mild abdominal distension (Fig. 1A). Laboratory tests showed a carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) level of 8.7 ng/mL (normal reference range: <5 ng/mL). The patient had an adhesiolysis, left hemicolectomy, and the construction of a diverting colostomy as part of an emergency surgical procedure.

Pathological examination revealed a 2.0 × 1.5 × 0.5 cm ulcerated, moderately to poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma invading the subserosal layer (pT3) (Fig. 1B). Adverse prognostic features included lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, and high-grade tumor budding. Lymph node examination revealed no involvement (0/16), although a single pericolic tumor deposit was identified. Immunohistochemistry confirmed colorectal origin (CK20+/CDX2+/SATB2+/CK7-), with intact mismatch repair protein expression (MLH1+/PMS2+/MSH2+/MSH6+), indicating proficient mismatch repair (pMMR) status. The pathological stage was pT3N1cM0 (Stage IIIB) according to the AJCC 8th edition. The patient subsequently completed six cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy with the XELOX regimen (capecitabine plus oxaliplatin). In September 2023, he underwent an uneventful laparoscopic colostomy reversal. Following completion of treatment, the patient entered a structured surveillance program consisting of clinical evaluation, CEA measurement, and contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen every six months.

In March 2025, surveillance contrast-enhanced abdominal CT identified a new 2.1 cm indeterminate splenic lesion. Subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with contrast was performed for further characterization, which confirmed a solitary splenic lesion suspicious for metastasis. Progression to 4.2 cm was confirmed by interval MRI in August 2025 (Fig. 2A). The splenic lesion exhibited isolated fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) avidity (SUVmax 12.8) on subsequent positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT), but there was no indication of extra-splenic disease (Fig. 2B). In March and September of 2025, the patient’s levels of CEA were 1.44 ng/mL and 2.14 ng/mL, respectively.

Fig. 2A MRI showing a slightly low signal intensity of the splenic lesion. B PET-CT image highlighting the FDG-avid splenic lesion

A MRI showing a slightly low signal intensity of the splenic lesion. B PET-CT image highlighting the FDG-avid splenic lesion

Given the isolated nature of the metastasis, the absence of disseminated disease, and the patient’s good performance status, the multidisciplinary team (MDT) recommended surgical resection with curative intent. The patient underwent laparoscopic splenectomy in September 2025. Laparoscopic exploration revealed a solitary mass arising from the splenic capsule, measuring approximately 4.5 cm, with dense adherence to the surrounding perisplenic adipose tissue. The lesion was carefully examined in relation to adjacent anatomical structures: there was no evidence of direct invasion into the pancreatic tail, stomach, or colon. The diaphragm appeared uninvolved. The peritoneum throughout the abdominal cavity was carefully inspected and found to be unremarkable, with no signs of carcinomatosis, peritoneal thickening, or nodules. Most importantly, the prior colonic resection site at the splenic flexure region was meticulously examined; there were no signs of local recurrence, anastomotic abnormalities, or tumor implants.

Pathological evaluation revealed a 4.5 × 3.0 cm capsular-adherent mass (Fig. 3A), histologically confirmed as metastatic colonic adenocarcinoma with a mucinous component (approximately 40%) (Fig. 3B). Resection margins were negative (R0). Based on these intraoperative and pathological findings, the lesion was interpreted as an isolated metachronous splenic metastasis rather than local recurrence or peritoneal dissemination.

The patient’s postoperative recovery was uneventful, and he was discharged on postoperative day 10. He remained disease-free at the most recent follow-up in January 2026, with no evidence of recurrence on surveillance imaging and normal serum CEA levels.

Pooled cases’ clinical and demographic features

There were 20 females (54.1%) and 17 males (45.9%) in the combined cohort of 37 cases. All 37 cases had age information, with a median of 62 years (IQR: 52–73 years) and a range of 33 to 84 years.

Metachronous presentation was observed in 32 cases (86.5%), while synchronous presentation was noted in 5 cases (13.5%). The disease-free period between splenic metastasis diagnosis and primary tumor therapy varied from 3 to 144 months in metachronous cases, with a median of 24 months (IQR: 16–51 months).

Thirty-one (96.9%) of the 32 patients with metachronous splenic metastases were asymptomatic, with lesions incidentally detected during routine imaging follow-up after primary tumor resection. Left hypochondrial fullness was only seen in one patient.

In contrast, all five patients with synchronous splenic metastases were symptomatic. Abdominal distension, palpable abdominal mass associated with pain, acute abdominal pain with intermittent high-grade fever (> 40 °C), and simultaneous stomach pain and distension were among their clinical symptoms.

The anatomical distribution of primary tumors showed a significant predilection for left-sided locations. The sigmoid colon was the most common site (13/37, 35.1%), followed by the rectum (8/37, 21.6%) and descending colon (6/37, 16.2%). This left-sided predominance explained 29 out of 37 instances (78.4%), including the splenic flexure (2 cases). The remaining eight instances (21.6%) were right-sided primaries (cecum, ascending colon, hepatic flexure, and transverse colon).

Primary tumor stage was available for 33 patients. The most common stage was III (20/33, 60.6%), followed by stage II (6/33, 18.2%), stage IV (5/33, 15.2%), and stage I (1/33, 3.0%). Interestingly, stage IV primary illness was found in all five synchronous patients.

Tumor markers and diagnostic techniques

Diagnostic modalities have evolved significantly over the decades covered by this series. In 32 cases (86.5%), CT was the primary or contributing diagnostic modality. Six instances (16.2%), all reported from 2006 onward, used PET-CT. Radionuclide scintigraphy was used in two early cases (5.4%) and MRI in four cases (10.8%).

Serum CEA levels were reported in 24 cases, of which 21 (80.8%) were elevated (> 5 ng/mL). The range of high CEA levels among patients was 5.05 to 223 ng/mL, with a median of 34.1 ng/mL (IQR: 13.5–57.6 ng/mL). Interestingly, in our instance, CEA levels were within normal limits when splenic metastases were discovered.

Pathological characteristics

In 33 patients, the size of the splenic metastases ranged from 1.5 to 18.0 cm, with a median of 4.5 cm (IQR: 3.5–6.5 cm). Histologically, mucinous adenocarcinoma was found in 8 cases (21.6%), although conventional adenocarcinomas accounted for the majority (27/37, 73.0%). Two cases (5.4%) lacked a specific histological subtype.

Clinical results and treatment approaches

Splenectomy was performed in all 37 patients. Surgical resection alone was employed in 27 cases (73.0%), while combined modality treatment (surgery plus chemotherapy) was administered in 10 cases (27.0%). Seven patients in the metachronous group underwent postoperative chemotherapy. 5-fluorouracil/leucovorin, XELOX, 5-FU/leucovorin/oxaliplatin plus bevacizumab, capecitabine monotherapy, and FOLFIRI with bevacizumab were among the regimens. For the other two individuals, chemotherapy regimens were not mentioned. Three patients in the synchronous group underwent postoperative chemotherapy, one of whom was treated with XELOX; the other two patients’ regimens were not stated. Chemotherapy was not administered to the other two patients.

Follow-up data were available for 28 patients, with follow-up durations ranging from 3 to 84 months (median, 20 months; IQR, 10–36 months). 25 patients (89.3% of those with accessible follow-up) were still alive and disease-free. During follow-up, three patients passed away.

23 patients (95.8%) of the 24 metachronous cases with available follow-up were still alive at the time of the last evaluation. On the other hand, of the four synchronous cases with available follow-up, two patients (50.0%) were still living, while the other two passed away six and twelve months after splenectomy, respectively.

The longest recorded survival was 84 months in a patient with metachronous splenic metastasis from rectal cancer [9]. The median follow-up period for the 25 patients who survived was 21 months (IQR: 10–36 months) (Table 1).

Discussion

To our knowledge, although several case reports and short case series have described isolated splenic metastases from colorectal cancer, comprehensive systematic reviews focusing on the clinicopathological features, treatment, and outcomes of this uncommon entity remain lacking. Several previous studies have summarized existing data by integrating individual case reports with literature reviews [35, 36, 43, 44]. Notably, one review identified 34 relevant studies involving 34 cases (6 synchronous and 28 metachronous) via a PubMed search [32]. 39 documented examples from English literature up to the time of publishing were cited in another study [42]. The exceptional rarity of the illness is highlighted by our pooled analysis of 37 cases, which closely fits previous estimates in terms of case counts and clinical aspects. The location of primary tumors, high rate of metachronous presentation, and favorable survival following splenectomy are key clinicopathological features observed in our cohort, which are consistent with findings from other case series and reviews [29, 35, 36, 45]. In contrast to earlier attempts that were often embedded within single-case reports or lacked structured data synthesis, our work provides a more comprehensive and standardized aggregation of demographic, diagnostic, pathological, therapeutic, and survival data across all available documented cases, offering a more robust characterization of this uncommon metastatic pattern.

Isolated splenic metastases exhibit diverse clinical manifestations depending on the timing of diagnosis. 86.5% (32/37) of presentations in our pooled dataset were metachronous instances, and the great majority (31/32, 96.9%) were asymptomatic, with splenic lesions found by accident during regular imaging follow-ups. The interval between the initial tumor treatment and the diagnosis of splenic metastases ranged from 3 to 144 months, with a median of 24 months. This prolonged dormancy, which in one instance lasted 12 years, highlights the significance of continuing surveillance outside of the conventional postoperative window and supports the theory that the spleen serves as a haven where tumor cells may remain dormant before manifesting clinically. On the other hand, symptoms including fever, distention, stomach pain, or a palpable mass were present in all synchronous cases (5/37, 13.5%). Notably, metachronous cases were primarily related with stage III primaries (20/32, 62.5% among those with available staging), whereas all synchronous instances were associated with stage IV primary disease. Based on this distinct clinical and pathological profile, synchronous splenic involvement is more likely a manifestation of widespread systemic dissemination rather than an isolated recurrence. This may call for a more aggressive systemic treatment strategy from the outset.

Our study revealed a significant predilection for left-sided primary tumors, accounting for 78.4% (29/37) of cases, with the sigmoid colon (35.1%), rectum (21.6%), and descending colon (16.2%) being the most common sites. This finding provides significant circumstantial support for the concept of retrograde hematogenous spread via the inferior mesenteric vein-splenic venous axis, which offers a direct vascular pathway for tumor emboli originating from left-sided colonic and rectal primary tumors. This anatomical mechanism could explain the very low incidence of splenic metastases from right-sided tumors, which accounted for just 21.6% of our series. Even though hematogenous dissemination seems to be the most common mechanism, it is impossible to completely rule out other channels such lymphatic spread or peritoneal seeding, especially for subcapsular lesions. Nonetheless, our series’ high incidence of intraparenchymal metastases supports the vascular route’s superiority.

The spleen exhibits exceptional resistance to metastatic colonization, as evidenced by reported rates of < 1% in metastatic colorectal cancer, which has been attributed to a combination of anatomical and immunological barriers [42, 46, 47]. The acute angulation of the splenic artery origin, combined with rhythmic sinusoidal contractions, creates hemodynamic conditions unfavorable for tumor cell implantation, whereas the immunologically active splenic microenvironment, rich in lymphocytes, dendritic cells, and macrophages, may effectively eliminate circulating tumour cells. In our cohort, 76.9% of metachronous cases arose from stage III primary tumors, implying that overcoming these strong defenses may necessitate either a substantial tumor cell burden or the development of increased immune evasion and tissue invasion capabilities. This finding raises important issues about the biological properties of tumors capable of producing splenic metastases, emphasizing the need for additional research into the molecular determinants of this uncommon metastatic tropism.

From a diagnostic standpoint, CT remains the primary detection modality, used in 86.5% of cases, with PET-CT emerging as a valuable adjuvant in recent years. Serum CEA was elevated in 80.8% of patients with available data; however, our index case demonstrated that normal CEA levels do not exclude splenic metastases, highlighting the need for regular imaging in surveillance programs. In terms of treatment, splenectomy was performed in all 37 cases, underscoring its role as the cornerstone of curative-intent therapy for isolated splenic metastases. In 70.3% of cases, surgical resection was used alone, whereas 29.7% got a combination of perioperative or adjuvant chemotherapy. Among metachronous cases with available follow-up, 95.8% were alive at the last assessment, but only 50.0% of synchronous cases with follow-up data were alive, with fatalities occurring 6 and 12 months after splenectomy. These results imply that the timing of presentation may have prognostic value, with metachronous isolated splenic metastases being associated with good long-term survival after splenectomy.

Identifying patient populations at higher risk of developing splenic metastases may facilitate the customization of surveillance strategies. Consistent with recent reviews [48, 49] highlighting the importance of identifying unique clinical behaviors of rare metastatic disease, recognition of prolonged tumor dormancy and atypical presentation is essential for refining long-term surveillance protocols. According to our research, patients with colorectal cancer who had lymph node involvement, advanced T stage, and left-sided primary tumors may be at a higher risk of developing isolated splenic metastases subsequent. Patients with stage III illness are more vulnerable. Additionally, our cohort’s longer latency interval—up to 144 months—supports the idea that surveillance for these high-risk patients should last much longer than the typical 3–5-year postoperative term. Although the absolute risk remains extremely low, continued imaging follow-up in high-risk patients may enable early detection of isolated splenic metastasis, which is associated with favorable outcomes when treated with timely splenectomy.

Several limitations to this study should be addressed. First, this study was designed as a pooled analysis of published case reports and small case series, rather than a formal systematic review or meta-analysis. As a result, the available data may be influenced by publication bias, as cases with positive outcomes or atypical clinical findings are more likely to be reported. Second, the retrospective nature of the included studies makes it difficult to standardize clinical variables and verify survival and follow-up data consistently. Third, the absence of a comparator group precludes direct assessment of survival benefit attributable to splenectomy versus other treatment strategies. Fourth, chemotherapy regimens varied considerably across cases, and the absence of standardized treatment protocols reflects a paucity of prospective data to guide the optimal integration of systemic therapy and surgical resection. Finally, the rarity of the illness limits our findings’ applicability to larger clinical groups. Nonetheless, given the scarcity of aggregated data on this topic, our synthesis provides a valuable framework for understanding the clinical characteristics, management, and outcomes of isolated splenic metastasis from colorectal cancer, as well as emphasizing the importance of ongoing collaborative efforts to develop evidence-based guidelines for this rare entity.

Conclusion

This pooled analysis of 37 cases, including our own, provides further insights into the clinical behavior of isolated splenic metastases from colorectal cancer. Key observations include the predominance of metachronous presentations, a strong association with left-sided primary tumors, and favorable outcomes associated with complete surgical resection. These findings suggest that an aggressive surgical approach may be considered in appropriately selected patients with isolated disease, and highlight the need for continued long-term surveillance in CRC patients. However, given the inherent limitations of a retrospective, case-report-based analysis with incomplete follow-up in some cases, these observations should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and preliminary. Further collaborative studies are warranted to better define optimal treatment strategies and investigate the biological mechanisms underlying this rare metastatic pattern.

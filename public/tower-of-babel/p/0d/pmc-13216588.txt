Risk factors, prognostic impact, and antimicrobial susceptibility profiles of carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii bloodstream infections: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Ningjing Pu, Ke Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to identify the independent risk factors for bloodstream infections (BSI) caused by carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii (CRAB). We also sought to evaluate its prognostic impact on 28-day all-cause mortality, and to systematically analyze the antimicrobial susceptibility profiles of CRAB strains and the clinical outcomes of different treatment regimens. We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study involving patients with A. baumannii BSI admitted to a tertiary teaching hospital from January 2023 to December 2024. Of 260 initially screened patients, 38 with polymicrobial infections were excluded per predefined criteria, resulting in a final cohort of 222 adults with confirmed monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI. Based on their susceptibility results, patients were divided into a CRAB group (n = 22) and a carbapenem-susceptible A. baumannii (CSAB) group (n = 200). We extracted clinical and microbiological data and used the Mann–Whitney U test and χ² test for intergroup comparisons. Independent risk factors were identified via multivariate logistic regression. Furthermore, Kaplan–Meier curves and Cox proportional hazards models were used to assess the independent prognostic value of CRAB infection on 28-day mortality. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) was performed using the VITEK 2 automated microbiology system. The interpretation of susceptibility results primarily followed the 2023 Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines, whereas tigecycline susceptibility was interpreted according to the 2024 European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (EUCAST) standards. The CRAB detection rate in our study was only 9.91% (22/222). This rate is noticeably lower than the national average, and occurred concurrently with the rollout of our hospital’s tiered carbapenem management program (during which carbapenem usage density dropped from 78 to 42 DDD/100 bed-days). Multivariate analysis showed that prior carbapenem exposure (OR = 4.77, 95% CI 1.60–14.03, P = 0.004), underlying malignancy (OR = 3.04, 95% CI 1.06–8.40, P = 0.033), and age ≥ 65 years (OR = 3.58, 95% CI 1.37–10.22, P = 0.012) were independent risk factors for CRAB BSI. The 28-day all-cause mortality in the CRAB group was 45.45%, which was significantly higher than the 19.00% observed in the CSAB group (P = 0.010). Even after adjusting for confounders, CRAB infection remained an independent predictor of 28-day mortality (adjusted HR = 2.43, 95% CI 1.14–5.16, P = 0.021).Regarding susceptibility, based on clinical stratification, polymyxin B showed the highest clinical susceptibility rate (81.82%) against CRAB. When used as monotherapy (based on ideal body weight [IBW]: 1.5–2.0 mg/kg, IV infused every 12 h), polymyxin B yielded a clinical response rate of 71.43% and a 28-day mortality rate of 28.57%. Any associated nephrotoxicity was fully reversible with prompt intervention. Advanced age, underlying malignancies, and prior carbapenem exposure are independent risk factors for CRAB infection in patients with A. baumannii BSI, significantly elevating the risk of short-term mortality. Polymyxin B exhibits excellent in vitro activity against CRAB strains isolated from our center. However, in light of the small size of the CRAB subgroup (n = 22), the imbalance between the CRAB and CSAB groups, and the inherent risk of confounding by indication in this single-center retrospective observational design, any inferences regarding its clinical efficacy must be made with extreme caution; definitive clinical recommendations require further validation in large-scale, prospective randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, the decline in CRAB detection rates occurred alongside reduced carbapenem consumption; this is only a temporal association, and no causal inferences can be drawn from this observational study.Keywords: Bloodstream infection, Carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii, Risk factors, 28-Day mortality, Antimicrobial susceptibility, Treatment outcome, Antimicrobial stewardshipSubject terms: Cancer, Diseases, Medical research, Microbiology, Oncology, Risk factors

Introduction

Background

Acinetobacter baumannii is a major opportunistic pathogen responsible for hospital-acquired infections. Bloodstream infections caused by this bacterium remain a critical cause of sepsis-related mortality, with death rates staying persistently high1. Driven by continuous antibiotic selective pressure, the global prevalence of carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii (CRAB) has surged, becoming a formidable clinical challenge in the field of anti-infective therapy. Current data indicates that the 28-day mortality rate for patients with CRAB BSI sits between 35% and 60%, which is roughly double the rate seen in patients infected with carbapenem-susceptible strains (CSAB)2,3. According to the 2023–2024 China Antimicrobial Surveillance Network, CRAB isolation rates in BSIs across Chinese tertiary hospitals have reached 15%–25%, showing a significant increase compared to 20154,5.

While previous epidemiological studies have frequently focused on the traits of CRAB infections in general wards and ICUs, most of these studies pool together colonization and infections from various body sites. This approach often results in conclusions that lack clinical specificity. Research targeting the specific population with confirmed monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI—detailing their risk factors, prognostic value, and treatment regimens—remains quite scarce6. Moreover, the exact independent impact of CRAB on short-term mortality, local susceptibility profiles, and clinical outcomes remain unclear, directly restricting the formulation of precise prevention and individualized treatment strategies for CRAB bloodstream infections. The CRAB detection rate at our center during 2023–2024 was significantly lower than the 2023 national average5. This drop occurred concurrently with the implementation of our hospital’s tiered carbapenem management program, providing a preliminary basis to explore the relationship between antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) practices and the clinical prevalence of resistant strains7.

Objective

Therefore, this study aimed to: (1) clarify the epidemiological characteristics of CRAB infection in patients with monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI; (2) screen for independent risk factors of CRAB BSI in this specific population; (3) quantify the independent prognostic impact of CRAB infection on 28-day all-cause mortality; (4) systematically analyze the susceptibility traits of CRAB strains and the clinical outcomes of different antimicrobial regimens based on CLSI 2023 standards, providing evidence-based guidance for clinical practice8; and (5) describe the temporal association between the implementation of tiered carbapenem management and CRAB detection rates in our center, to serve as a preliminary reference for clinical antimicrobial stewardship practices7.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study. The subjects were adult patients (≥ 18 years old) with A. baumannii BSI admitted to The Third People’s Hospital of Chengdu from January 2023 to December 2024. Based on a previously reported 10% CRAB infection rate, an expected OR ≥ 2.5, an alpha level of 0.05, and a power of 0.80, we calculated a minimum required sample size of 210 cases. Initially, we screened 260 patients. According to our exclusion criteria, we removed 38 cases of polymicrobial infections. Ultimately, we enrolled 222 consecutive patients with confirmed monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI, using a continuous inclusion method to minimize selection bias.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) At least one set of aerobic/anaerobic blood cultures confirmed positive for A. baumannii via microbiological testing, and presenting as a monomicrobial infection; (3) Complete clinical data, including AST results, BSI source information, and 28-day follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Blood culture contamination; (2) Fungal BSI; (3) Polymicrobial infections; (4) Missing key clinical data.

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Third People’s Hospital of Chengdu (Approval No.: 2024-S-382). Because of the retrospective design and the anonymization of data, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Definitions

The definitions of CRAB and CSAB strictly followed the 2023 CLSI M100-S33 guidelines8. CRAB was defined as A. baumannii isolated from blood cultures that was resistant to imipenem or meropenem (MIC ≥ 8 µg/mL). CSAB was defined as strains susceptible to both imipenem and meropenem (MIC ≤ 2 µg/mL). For A. baumannii, the CLSI 2023 guidelines do not assign an official “Susceptible” interpretive category for polymyxin B, and only include Intermediate (I, MIC ≤ 2 µg/mL) and Resistant (R, MIC ≥ 4 µg/mL) categories8. To facilitate clinical interpretation of the in vitro activity of polymyxin B against CRAB strains causing bloodstream infections, we performed a provisional clinical stratification of isolates based on the PK/PD target recommended by the international consensus guideline for polymyxin use in bloodstream infections (AUC0−24 h/MIC ≥ 50)9. Specifically, strains with an MIC ≤ 1 µg/mL were designated as clinically susceptible, while those with an MIC of 2 µg/mL were categorized as intermediate. We acknowledge that this stratification deviates from the standard CLSI categorization for polymyxin B, and should be interpreted with caution in clinical practice9.

Prior carbapenem exposure: Defined as the therapeutic use of imipenem, meropenem, or ertapenem for ≥ 3 cumulative days within the 90 days prior to blood culture collection (excluding prophylactic use).

Data collection

Two trained researchers independently extracted data from the electronic medical record (EMR) and laboratory information systems. Any discrepancies were arbitrated and resolved by a third senior researcher. A standardized operating procedure was established prior to extraction, and a double-entry verification mode was used to ensure data accuracy. The collected variables included: (1) Demographic characteristics and underlying diseases; (2) Antimicrobial exposure history and ICU admission status; (3) Laboratory parameters; (4) BSI source, primary infection site, and source control measures; (5) Pathogen AST results; (6) Treatment regimens, clinical response, adverse drug reactions, and 28-day survival outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using R 4.2.1 and SPSS 26.0 software. After the Shapiro-Wilk test, continuous variables were found to be non-normally distributed and were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges [M(IQR)]. Intergroup comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test.

Variables with P < 0.10 in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent risk factors for CRAB infection. Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) were used to rule out multicollinearity (VIF < 2.0), and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test was used to verify model fit. Kaplan-Meier curves were plotted for survival analysis, with differences assessed via the Log-rank test. A Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to adjust for confounders and evaluate the independent prognostic value of CRAB infection on 28-day mortality, and Schoenfeld residuals were used to test the proportional hazards assumption13. All tests were two-tailed, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Ultimately, 222 patients were included with monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI. Among them, 22 cases (9.91%) were in the CRAB group, while 200 cases (90.09%) were in the CSAB group. Our CRAB detection rate was significantly lower than the 2023 national average (19.8%). This change occurred concurrently with a reduction in our hospital’s carbapenem usage density from 78 DDD/100 bed-days to 42 DDD/100 bed-days over the same study period.

When comparing baseline characteristics, the median age in the CRAB group was significantly higher than in the CSAB group (68.5 vs. 59.5 years, P = 0.033). In addition, the proportion of underlying malignancies (45.45% vs. 16.00%, P = 0.001), prior carbapenem exposure (40.91% vs. 10.50%, P < 0.001), and ICU admissions (68.18% vs. 32.50%, P = 0.002) were all significantly higher in the CRAB group.

Regarding the source of BSI, secondary infections were the majority in both groups (77.27% for CRAB, 68.50% for CSAB), with the respiratory tract being the primary site of infection. There was no statistically significant difference in the distribution of BSI sources and primary infection sites between the two groups (classified per the predefined criteria in Sect. 2.2, P = 0.546). In terms of laboratory indicators, the CRAB group had significantly higher levels of blood lactate and D-dimer, and significantly lower serum albumin levels (all P < 0.01). For clinical outcomes, the 28-day all-cause mortality rate in the CRAB group was 45.45%, which was strikingly higher than the 19.00% observed in the CSAB group (P = 0.010), all details are shown in Table 1.

Identification of independent risk factors

Univariate analysis revealed that age ≥ 65 years, malignancies, prior carbapenem exposure, ICU admission, hyperlactatemia, and hypoalbuminemia were potential risk factors for CRAB infection (all P < 0.10). When these variables were entered into the multivariate logistic regression model, the results showed that: prior carbapenem exposure (OR = 4.77, 95% CI 1.60–14.03, P = 0.004), underlying malignancy (OR = 3.04, 95% CI 1.06–8.40, P = 0.033), and age ≥ 65 years (OR = 3.58, 95% CI 1.37–10.22, P = 0.012) were independent risk factors for CRAB infection in patients with monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI. The model demonstrated a good fit according to the Hosmer-Lemeshow test (P > 0.05) and showed no evidence of multicollinearity, with all VIFs remaining below 2.0 (Table 2).

Prognostic analysis

The 28-day all-cause mortality in the CRAB group was significantly higher than that in the CSAB group (45.45% vs. 19.00%, P = 0.010). As illustrated by the Kaplan-Meier survival curves in Fig. 2, patients in the CRAB group experienced a significantly lower cumulative survival rate compared to those in the CSAB group (Log-rank P = 0.004).

Kaplan–Meier estimates of 28-day cumulative survival in patients with CRAB versus CSAB bloodstream infections.

To further evaluate the independent prognostic value of CRAB infection, we performed a multivariate Cox regression analysis. After adjusting for potential confounders, including age ≥ 65 years, underlying malignancies, and prior carbapenem use, CRAB infection was confirmed as an independent predictor of 28-day mortality (adjusted HR = 2.43, 95% CI: 1.14–5.16, P = 0.021), as shown in Fig. 2.

Antimicrobial susceptibility profiles

All 22 CRAB isolates were 100% resistant to both imipenem and meropenem (MIC ≥ 8 µg/mL), and the resistance rate to cefoperazone/sulbactam was 50.0%. Based on clinical stratification derived from PK/PD targets, polymyxin B showed the highest clinical susceptibility rate against CRAB, reaching 81.82% (18/22).This was followed by tigecycline with a susceptibility rate of 68.18%. Sulbactam alone had a susceptibility rate of only 40.91% (Table 3). Due to limited clinical availability during the study period, cefiderocol was not included in our routine AST panels, so no relevant data was available.

Treatment regimens and clinical outcomes

Patients with CRAB BSI mainly received four types of treatment regimens. The dosing regimen for polymyxin B was uniformly set at 1.5–2.0 mg/kg based on ideal body weight (IBW), infused intravenously every 12 h, with dynamic monitoring of renal function during treatment.

Polymyxin B monotherapy (7 cases): The clinical response rate was 71.43%, the 28-day mortality rate was 28.57%, and the incidence of nephrotoxicity was 14.29% (1/7). This patient’s serum creatinine rose 62% above baseline on day 5 of treatment. After promptly stopping the drug and switching to tigecycline plus sulbactam, renal function fully recovered within 10 days, and the patient ultimately survived the 28 days.

Further investigation into the correlation between treatment regimens and patient prognosis showed that the clinical response rates for both polymyxin B monotherapy and its combination with tigecycline were significantly higher than those observed for tigecycline monotherapy and sulbactam-based regimens (P < 0.05). In addition, the 28-day mortality rates were significantly lower in these polymyxin B-containing groups (P < 0.05). No statistically significant difference in clinical outcomes was observed between polymyxin B monotherapy and combination therapy (P > 0.05), suggesting that standardized monotherapy for polymyxin B-susceptible CRAB strains may achieve efficacy comparable to combination regimens, while potentially reducing the risk of cumulative adverse events associated with multidrug combinations. However, this inference must be interpreted with extreme caution due to the small size of the CRAB subgroup and the risk of confounding by indication inherent to this retrospective observational study.

Discussion

Main findings and clinical implications

By focusing purely on consecutive confirmed cases of monomicrobial A. baumannii BSI, this study eliminated the confounding interference of colonization and infections at other sites. We identified the independent risk factors, prognostic impact, and susceptibility profiles of CRAB BSI in a single center in Southwest China. This provides preliminary evidence-based data for precise clinical prevention and treatment.

We found that prior carbapenem exposure was the strongest independent predictor of CRAB infection, which aligns closely with findings from similar studies both domestically and internationally. This reinforces the notion that antimicrobial selective pressure is a core driver of CRAB prevalence and transmission. The irrational use of carbapenems disrupts the intestinal colonization barrier while inducing the horizontal transfer and expression of resistance genes (such as blaOXA-23 and blaOXA-58), significantly increasing the risk of CRAB colonization and subsequent infection14,15. The CRAB detection rate at our center was only 9.91%, noticeably lower than the 2023 national average5 during the same study period. This trend occurred concurrently with the implementation of our hospital’s tiered carbapenem management program—during which carbapenem usage density dropped by nearly 50%. It should be emphasized that this study is a single-center retrospective observational design, which can only describe the temporal concurrence of the two events, and cannot confirm or infer any causal relationship between the AMS program and the reduced CRAB detection rate. The potential clinical value of this AMS strategy requires verification through well-designed prospective controlled studies7.

Underlying malignancies and advanced age were the other two independent risk factors for CRAB infection. Malignancies and ICU stays form a critical risk chain for CRAB infections. Tumor patients, suffering from chemotherapy-induced immune damage, frequent medical exposure, prolonged hospitalizations, and ICU admissions, are a highly vulnerable demographic16. Although we adjusted for the impact of malignancies in our multivariate model, the residual confounding generated by heavy medical exposure cannot be completely erased. Clinically, it is essential to implement active CRAB screening, strict contact isolation, and bundled infection control strategies for high-risk groups (elderly, cancer patients, those with prior carbapenem use) to reduce cross-infection risks.

Our study also confirmed that CRAB infection is an independent predictor of 28-day all-cause mortality (adjusted HR = 2.43, 95% CI 1.14–5.16, P = 0.021). However, it is critical to clarify that this survival disadvantage does not stem from enhanced inherent virulence of the CRAB strains17,18. Instead, it is likely mediated by a combination of key factors: delayed initiation of appropriate antimicrobial therapy due to the limited effective treatment options for CRAB19, suboptimal PK/PD target attainment of polymyxin B in certain clinical scenarios20, inadequate source control of the primary infection site21, and the generally more severe underlying diseases and acute organ dysfunction present in patients with CRAB BSI. We acknowledge that the exact timing of appropriate antimicrobial therapy and the adequacy of source control were key unmeasured variables in our analysis, which may introduce residual confounding in our prognostic assessment. This conclusion aligns with recent clinical and genomic studies, reminding clinicians that treating CRAB infections requires simultaneous attention to source control, timely initiation of appropriate therapy, management of underlying diseases, and organ function support, rather than solely focusing on antibiotic selection18,21.

Regarding susceptibility, polymyxin B showed the highest clinical susceptibility rate (81.82%) against CRAB strains isolated from our center, representing the most active in vitro antimicrobial agent against local CRAB strains in this study. Clinical treatment data showed that polymyxin B monotherapy achieved a 71.43% clinical response rate and a 28.57% 28-day mortality rate in our cohort, with only 1 case of mild, reversible nephrotoxicity. These findings suggest potential efficacy and controllable safety of polymyxin B monotherapy in this specific patient cohort. Our 14.29% incidence of polymyxin B-associated nephrotoxicity was lower than previously reported levels22, and it was fully reversed through timely drug cessation and regimen adjustment, suggesting that standardized dosing and close renal monitoring can effectively minimize the risk of nephrotoxicity. However, we emphasize that conclusions regarding polymyxin B clinical efficacy must be interpreted with extreme caution: the small CRAB subgroup (n = 22) creates an imbalance with the CSAB group, and the retrospective observational design renders treatment-related analyses susceptible to confounding by indication. Definitive clinical recommendations cannot be made at this stage, and its true clinical value requires validation in large-scale, prospective randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, consistent with previous findings, tigecycline monotherapy and sulbactam-based regimens performed extremely poorly in our cohort, strongly suggesting that these regimens should not be used as standalone treatments for CRAB BSI23,24.

Limitations

Our study has several important limitations that must be acknowledged when interpreting the findings. First, the single-center retrospective observational design may introduce selection bias, and the generalizability of our findings to other clinical settings requires validation from multicenter, large-scale studies.Second, the relatively small size of the CRAB subgroup (n = 22) introduces an imbalance compared with the CSAB group, which limits the robustness of our subgroup and treatment-related analyses. In particular, conclusions regarding the clinical efficacy of polymyxin B are susceptible to confounding by indication due to the observational nature of the study, and we cannot make definitive clinical treatment recommendations based on these findings.Third, while implemented to provide a more clinically actionable framework than the standard CLSI 2023 categorization, the PK/PD-based stratification applied to polymyxin B remains non-standardized. This deviation is an important limitation that must be considered when interpreting our susceptibility data. Fourth, we did not include molecular typing or resistance gene detection data for the CRAB strains, so we could not clarify the association between resistance genotypes, bacterial virulence, and clinical outcomes.Fifth, we lacked real-time antimicrobial PK/PD monitoring data for individual patients, which prevents us from further optimizing individualized dosing regimens for polymyxin B.Sixth, although we adjusted for several major confounders in our multivariate regression models, residual confounding remains a key limitation. Specifically, we did not systematically collect data on the exact timing of appropriate targeted antimicrobial therapy and the adequacy of source control measures for the primary infection site; both of these variables are well-established key predictors of clinical outcomes in BSI, and their omission may have introduced residual confounding in our prognostic and treatment efficacy analyses.Seventh, we did not perform a controlled before-and-after analysis of the tiered carbapenem management program. The single-center retrospective design cannot establish or infer any causal relationship between the AMS intervention and the reduced CRAB detection rate, as numerous unmeasured confounding factors may have contributed to the observed changes in CRAB prevalence.Future multicenter, large-sample prospective studies that integrate strain molecular epidemiology, PK/PD analysis, and detailed data on antimicrobial therapy timing and source control are needed to further clarify the optimal treatment and prevention strategies for CRAB BSI.

Conclusion

This study confirms that age ≥ 65 years, underlying malignancies, and prior carbapenem exposure are independent risk factors for CRAB infection in patients with A. baumannii BSI. Moreover, malignancies and ICU stays form an important risk chain for CRAB infection. CRAB infection significantly elevates the 28-day mortality risk in patients, but this effect is linked to limited effective treatment options, suboptimal antimicrobial PK/PD profiles, and inadequate source control, rather than enhanced intrinsic virulence.

Polymyxin B monotherapy (IV infused at 1.5–2.0 mg/kg IBW every 12 h) demonstrated potential efficacy and manageable safety in this cohort; its associated nephrotoxicity can be effectively controlled through close monitoring and prompt intervention. However, these findings must be interpreted with extreme caution due to the small size of the CRAB subgroup and the inherent risk of confounding by indication in this retrospective study, and its definitive clinical value requires further validation via large-scale, prospective randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, the implementation of our hospital’s tiered carbapenem management program occurred concurrently with decreased carbapenem usage density and a low CRAB detection rate in our center; while this observation may provide a preliminary reference for clinical antimicrobial stewardship practices, no causal relationship can be established from this single-center observational data.

In clinical practice, it is crucial to implement active CRAB screening and bundled infection control for high-risk populations, strengthen the standardized management of carbapenems, and formulate personalized anti-infective regimens for suspected or confirmed CRAB BSI cases based on local susceptibility profiles and individual patient conditions.

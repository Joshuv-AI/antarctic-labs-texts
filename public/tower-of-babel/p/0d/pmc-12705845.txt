The molecular pathogenesis of placenta accreta spectrum disorder and its clinical applications
Authors: Min Zhao, Lizhou Sun, Dan Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPlacenta accreta spectrum (PAS) is a serious obstetric complication, characterized by the placenta accreta and increta to the uterine wall, which fails to detach spontaneously from the uterine following childbirth, potentially resulting in severe hemorrhage and additional complications. The incidence of PAS is increasing in the world, which is mainly attributed to a global escalation in cesarean section rates, underscoring the critical need to elucidate its molecular pathogenesis and to devise efficacious clinical interventions. Recent studies have identified novel molecular mechanisms that have significantly enhanced our comprehension of disease pathophysiology and informed predictive and therapeutic strategies. Here, we review the principal molecular pathogenesis of PAS, explore the potential translation of these findings into clinical applications, and assess the impact of emerging technologies on advancing research in this domain.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00404-025-08221-1.Keywords: Placenta accreta spectrum, Angiogenesis, Molecular pathogenesis, Biomarkers

Introduction

Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) is a serious obstetric complication characterized by the abnormal placenta adherence to the uterine wall, leading to a failure in spontaneously separating from the uterine following a natural parturition [1]. Additionally, PAS an extreme manifestation of otherwise normal placental implantation in early pregnancy [2]. Placenta previa is widely recognized as a significant risk factor for PAS and is associated with severe maternal complications. Studies indicate that patients with PAS who have placenta previa are more prone to serious maternal complications and surgical complications such as perinatal hemorrhage, blood transfusion requirements, shock, and coagulation disorders compared to those without placenta previa[2]. Additionally, patients with placenta previa experience significantly longer hospital stays and higher medical costs, further exacerbating the strain on healthcare resources[2]. While the maternal effects of PAS have been extensively studied, its impact on newborns remains relatively underexplored. A multicenter prospective study revealed that in pregnancies complicated by PAS, the primary congenital malformation rate among newborns reached 4.64%, with a neonatal mortality rate of 3.13% [3]. In certain extreme instances, placental implantation can result in spontaneous uterine rupture, particularly during the second trimester, leading to an acute abdomen and severe hypovolemia. Consequently, early diagnosis and appropriate management of PAS are crucial to mitigating maternal risks. Under physiological circumstances, the placenta fully detaches from the uterine wall following childbirth; however, in cases of PAS, the placenta fails to spontaneously detach completely from the uterine, leading to severe complications. PAS disorders are categorized into placenta accreta, placenta increta, and placenta percreta, according to the extent of placental invasion into the myometrium. This condition is linked to reduced gestational duration, lower neonatal birth weight, and an elevated risk of postpartum hemorrhage [4]. Studies indicate that its incidence rose from 1.2% to 1.6% between 2000 and 2020, with links to multiple pregnancy complications, hypertension, diabetes, asthma, and other factors[5]. This condition not only increases risks of maternal blood transfusions, severe obstetric complications, and postpartum hemorrhage, but also significantly elevates neonatal risks, including asphyxia, respiratory distress syndrome, preterm birth, stillbirth, and neonatal mortality[5].

In 1927, D. S. Forster, a pioneering figure in the field of obstetrics, first described PAS [6], which was subsequently reported by more and more researchers. Numerous clinical studies have identified various risk factors contributing to the development and progression of PAS, and these risk factors include cesarean sections, abortions, and surgical interventions within the uterine cavity [3]. Research indicates that microRNA-940 is highly expressed in chorionic villi of early miscarriages and inhibits placental implantation by targeting ZNF672, thereby promoting early miscarriage [4]. This mechanism reveals the crucial regulatory role of microRNA in placental implantation, where abnormal expression may lead to placental dysfunction and consequently increase miscarriage risk. Furthermore, long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) TCL6 has been identified as strongly associated with miscarriage [5]. Studies indicate that lncRNA-TCL6 is significantly overexpressed in placental tissues from threatened and spontaneous abortions compared to normal pregnancies, where it inhibits placental implantation through the EGFR signaling pathway [5]. This regulatory mechanism may lead to placental hypoplasia, thereby increasing the likelihood of pregnancy loss. With ongoing advancements in contemporary medical technology, particularly due to the rising rates of cesarean sections, the incidence of PAS is globally increasing [7]. For instance, patients experiencing placental engraftment usually face an elevated risk of surgical complications during cesarean delivery, such as hemorrhage, coagulation dysfunction, and injury in the urinary tract [8]. This increased risk may be partially attributed to the increased trauma and subsequent healing processes within the uterine wall associated with cesarean sections, which consequently elevate the risk of PAS disorders [3]. Furthermore, advanced maternal age, multiparity, and a prior history of uterine surgery are important risk factors for the development of PAS [9]. Advanced maternal age, multiple pregnancies, and a history of uterine surgery are high-risk factors for placental implantation issues. Older mothers face increased risks of placental dysfunction, potentially leading to fetal growth restriction and stillbirth, with studies showing specific placental changes and altered maternal serum markers[9]. Multiple pregnancies also heighten the risk of placental abnormalities like placenta previa and deep implantation, particularly in those using assisted reproductive technology, which is linked to higher rates of placental abruption and preterm delivery. Multiple pregnancies can increase the risk of placental abnormalities by affecting placental workload and hemodynamics. A history of uterine surgery is a major risk factor for placental abruption, with procedures like myomectomy, uterine artery embolization, dilation and curettage, and hysteroscopic adhesion lysis being strongly linked to this condition. These surgeries may alter endometrial structure and function, raising the risk of placental abruption. Overall, advanced maternal age, multiple pregnancies, and previous uterine surgeries collectively heighten the risk of placental implantation abnormalities. These factors can lead to abnormal placental implantation by changing its structure and function, increasing its workload, and altering the endometrial microenvironment.

To explore the molecular pathogenesis of PAS, A previous study has reported that cell adhesion, extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, autophagy, and angiogenesis are critical risk factors [3]. Cell adhesion serves as the foundation for interactions between cells and the ECM, influencing cellular migration and proliferation [10]. The ECM remodeling is a pivotal process that facilitates angiogenesis by modulating cell–matrix interactions [11]. During this process, cells engage with the ECM via adhesion receptors, such as integrins, which subsequently activate a variety of signaling pathways [12]. Furthermore, autophagy, an intracellular degradation mechanism, is crucial for regulating the metabolic status and survival of cells, influencing the progression of PAS [13]. Angiogenesis constitutes another critical component of PAS development, governed by various growth factors and cellular signaling pathways [14]. Notably, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is pivotal in this process, and its expression is modulated by the adhesive state of the cell [14]. Researchers are uncovering the molecular pathogenesis of PAS and exploring potential treatments by focusing on recent advances in cell signaling, gene expression regulation, and biomarker research. Understanding these processes is crucial for developing innovative therapies for PAS.

Molecular pathogenesis of PAS

Epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)

Epithelial cells, derived from the embryo's trophoblast cells, are crucial for the placenta's functions like material exchange and hormone secretion. Mesenchymal cells, originating from the embryo's mesoderm, provide structural support to the placenta. EMT is a critical biological process by shifting epithelial cells into mesenchymal cells [15]. This transition is associated with increased migration and invasion capabilities of cells [16]. During the EMT process, epithelial cells progressively change mesenchymal phenotype, accompanied by enhanced cellular motility and migration capacities. EMT is pivotal in embryonic development and tissue repair, as well as in the progression of PAS [17]. The role of EMT in placental formation extends beyond merely transforming cell phenotypes, it also encompasses adaptation to the surrounding environment.

A previous study has recognized EMT as one of the crucial mechanisms facilitating cancer cell invasion and metastasis in the context of tumor progression [16]. Recent investigations have revealed the signaling pathways activated by the EMT process may function in placental implantation [18]. During placental development, the cells undergoing the EMT process obtain migratory capabilities and effectively embed within the maternal endometrium [18]. The process is regulated by some cytokine-related signaling pathways, such as transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and protein kinase B (Akt) activation [19]. Moreover, during EMT process, cells undergo multiple phenotypic states with phenotypic plasticity, conferring an adaptive advantage in response to microenvironmental changes [20]. The transcription factors within the cellular microenvironment suppress the expression of target genes associated with cell adhesion, resulting in the disruption of epithelial cell polarity, cytoskeletal reorganization, and the substitution of cytokeratin with vimentin, ultimately leading to EMT [20]. In a 2021 study, Ding et al. [21] demonstrated that the induction of EMT augmented the migratory capacity of trophoblast cells and modified their responses to signaling pathways, dependent on the TGF-β and Wnt pathways, increasing their invasion. Furthermore, other factors, such as placental growth factor (PlGF), VEGF, laminin γ2 (LAMC2), zinc finger E-box-binding homeobox (ZEB) proteins, αVβ3 integrin, and cofilin-1 (CFL-1), regulate the EMT process, contributing to the development and progression of PAS [22, 23].

Currently, researchers have initiated to investigate the potential modulation of these signaling pathways for therapeutic interventions of cancers and placenta-related disorders. Many types of cancers share pathological similarities, such as the EMT process, which is closely associated with tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis [24]. Placental diseases, outcomes related to placental insufficiency, including preeclampsia, preterm birth, and fetal growth restriction, affect maternal and fetal prognosis. Abnormal EMT in the placenta may be associated with fetal growth restriction, accompanied by inadequate placental blood flow and inadequate nutrient supply [25]. During the EMT process, the major mediators include the TGF-β, Notch, and Wnt signaling pathways. The TGF-β signaling can induce the EMT process, which promotes the cell transition from epithelial to mesenchymal state by activating the Smad signaling pathway [26]. The Notch signaling can promote the conversion of epithelial to mesenchymal cells by regulating cell–cell interactions and cell fate decisions [27]. More importantly, the Notch and TGF- β signaling can crosstalk to regulate the tumor microenvironment [28]. The Wnt signaling through its receptors activates the β-catenin to induce the expression of EMT-related genes, thereby driving the morphological changes of cells and enhancing their migratory capacity [29]. Moreover, the Wnt signaling can also crosstalk with the TGF- β and Notch signaling to form a network to regulate the EMT process [29]. By specifically targeting the EMT-related signaling pathways, it is anticipated that novel therapeutic strategies can be developed, enhancing the efficacy of clinical treatments [30]. Decipher signaling pathways in the human body requires a multi-tiered research strategy encompassing extracellular vesicle tracking, stress response regulation, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Hence, the roles of EMT in placental engraftment and tumor progression underscore its significance as a subject of rigorous research. Elucidating the EMT-related signaling pathways may provide new insights into cellular phenotypic plasticity and potentially inform novel clinical therapeutic strategies.

Changes in cell adhesion molecules

Cell adhesion molecules are essential for sustaining intercellular interactions and tissue architecture [31]. Integrin is one of the large family of transmembrane cell adhesion molecules [32]. In 2022, Gundappa's laboratory reported that the upregulated integrin β1 (ITGβ1) expression is linked to enhanced endometrial epithelial cells cellular adhesion to the uterine lining [33]. Furthermore, MARVELD1 facilitates the invasion of trophoblast cells by enhancing the expression of integrin β4 during placental development, significantly affecting the occurrence of placental implantation [11, 34].

Moreover, E-cadherin, an adhesion molecule, is crucial for modulating the invasive capacity of trophoblasts and may contribute to the pathogenesis of placenta accreta [35]. Reduced E-cadherin expression can directly impair cell adhesion and activate β-catenin, initiating a cascade amplification effect via the Wnt signaling [36]. This process modulates gene transcription and translation, subsequently influencing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Alterations in decidual tissues may induce methylation modifications in the promoter region of the tumor suppressor gene p53 within trophoblasts [37]. This can upregulate the expression of Slug and Zeb-1 genes, downregulating the expression of E-cadherin, leading to a substantial release of β-catenin by trophoblasts [37]. The accumulation of β-catenin within the cytoplasm and its subsequent translocation into the nucleus amplify the regulatory effects of the Wnt signaling [38].

In the nucleus, β-catenin functions as a specific coactivator for DNA-binding protein transcription, suppressing transcriptional inhibitors to initiate transcription [39]. The interaction between the expressed gene products and various complex cellular regulatory systems augments the migratory and invasive capabilities of trophoblastic cells beyond the villi. This is evidenced by the unrestrained temporal and spatial invasive capacity of trophoblasts beyond the villi, resulting in excessive placental invasion and subsequent implantation [40]. These findings underscore the dual function of integrins in facilitating physical connectivity and initiating downstream signaling pathways, such as the FAK-PI3K cascade, enhancing the invasiveness of trophoblast cells. The interactions and signaling networks among these cell adhesion molecules offer critical biological insights into the mechanisms underlying the process of placental implantation and its related pathologies.

Extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling

ECM remodeling refers to the process in which the composition and structure of the ECM change dynamically under physiological and pathological conditions. During pregnancy, ECM remodeling promotes placental formation and functional maintenance of trophoblast cell invasion and placental engraftment [41]. Actually, placental trophoblast cells need to interact with ECM during invasion, and the compositional and structural changes of ECM can affect the migration and invasion of trophoblast cells [42]. For example, certain matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) can promote the invasion of trophoblast cells by degrading the ECM components [43]. During physiological pregnancy, MMP activity is essential for effective placental invasion and the remodeling of maternal blood vessels [44]. Conversely, in pregnancy complications, such as preeclampsia, the expression and activity of MMPs are diminished, leading to insufficient placental invasion and compromised maternal blood flow [44, 45]. Defects in ECM remodeling may lead to insufficient invasion of trophoblast cells, affecting placental function and maternal health [46]. Moreover, ECM remodeling is also closely related to the implantation process of the placenta. During the implantation stage, the composition and structural changes of the ECM support and stimulate trophoblast cells to promote deep invasion into the maternal tissue [47].

Excessive placental invasion may be associated with an imbalance of ECM remodeling, potentially impacting placental function and pregnancy outcomes [48]. Decorin and biglycan, two members of the leucine-rich small proteoglycan family, are integral components of the ECM and cell surface. These molecules are crucial regulators of cellular proliferation, migration, and invasion in both in vitro and in vivo [49]. The ECM can be degraded by proteases, such as MMP-9 and urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA), to regulate placental development. In 2021, Martinez's laboratory [10] reported the role of MMP-9 in ECM remodeling, highlighting that its elevated expression was a key factor for the degradation of collagen and fibrin within the ECM. These findings indicate that the reduced expression of tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-1 (TIMP-1) exacerbates the process of ECM degradation, potentially contributing to excessive placental invasion. Additionally, they identified the uPA/uPAR (receptor) complex as a critical component in the plasmin-dependent proteolytic activity on the surface of trophoblasts.

The penetration of villi into tissues is regulated by plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (PAI-1) and plasminogen activator inhibitor 2 (PAI-2), particularly by PAI-2, a predominant regulator. Previous studies have shown that uPA and PAI-1 are crucial for tumor cell invasion and metastasis, particularly during placental invasion [50, 51]. The uPA facilitates the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin, promoting the ECM degradation and enhancing the trophoblast cell invasion [52]. Meanwhile, PAI-2 functions as a serine protease inhibitor to modulate uPA activity, influencing placental invasion [53, 54]. PAI-2 inactivates the uPA/uPAR complex by forming a ternary complex. A diminished concentration of PAI-2 in the placenta and the uterine muscle layer is indicative of excessive invasion by placental villi. Empirical studies have revealed that the levels of TIMP-1 and PAI-2 are reduced in the placenta and myometrium, implying an overly aggressive invasion of the placental villi [44, 55]. Consequently, the interaction between the uPA/uPAR complex and PAI-2 is crucial for modulating the invasiveness of the placenta.

Role of autophagy in PAS

Autophagy is a process of self-degradation and recycle of damaged cellular organelles and proteins to maintain cellular interior stability and survival in a lysosome-dependent manner. It is well known that the activity of autophagy is closely related to the nutrient supply, cell proliferation, and apoptosis in the placenta. The autophagic process in the placenta can promote the placental growth and functional balance by regulating intracellular nutrients and signaling pathways [56]. For example, maternal obesity and gestational diabetes can downregulate the expression of placental autophagy markers, affecting placental function and fetal growth [57]. Activation of autophagy can promote the migration and invasion of trophoblast cells, enhancing the embedding capacity of the placenta [58].

Oxidative stress and endogenous inflammation can also modulate autophagy, which subsequently affects the placental embedding process. During the early stages of pregnancy, the optimal autophagy activity maintains placental homeostasis and prevents apoptosis [49]. Disruption of the balance between autophagy and apoptosis can result in the onset of pregnancy-related complications, including gestational hypertension and fetal growth restriction.

In 2020, researchers unveiled that autophagy modulated the expression of Bcl-2 family proteins specifically by upregulating Bcl-2 and downregulating Bax to inhibit programmed cell death and promote cell survival under hypoxic conditions [59, 60]. The Bcl-2 family proteins are pivotal in the balance between autophagy and apoptosis. Typically, while Bcl-2 functions to enhance cell survival, Bax acts as an inducer of cell death [61]. More recently, a growing body of studies has linked Bcl-2 to placenta accreta, suggesting that the suppression of apoptosis and the resultant imbalance between cell proliferation and apoptosis may be crucial for the progression of placenta accreta. Zheng et al. [62] observed that Wnt silencing significantly increased the proportion of apoptotic trophoblast cells, accompanied by down-regulated Bcl-2 expression.

The Transcription Factor Dp-2 (TFDP2) functions to regulate cell cycle and apoptosis, potentially influencing the balance of Bcl-2 family proteins and impacting cellular survival and death [63]. Furthermore, TFDP2 silencing induced the apoptosis of syncytiotrophoblast cells by decreasing PKAc levels and inhibiting the nuclear translocation of β-catenin [59, 63]. Therefore, TFDP2 silencing modulates cell fate decisions by altering the expression levels of these critical proteins. These observations indicate that autophagy serves a protective function in the PAS by safeguarding cells against stress-induced damage.

The role of cell cycle regulators

During placental development, cell cycle regulators, notably cell cycle-dependent kinases (CDKs), are pivotal in modulating the proliferation and differentiation of placental cells [64]. CDKs can significantly influence the function and behavior of trophoblast cells by orchestrating various stages of the cell cycle, consequently impacting placental embedding and development [65]. Empirical studies have demonstrated a strong correlation between CDK expression and activity and the proliferation, migration, and invasion of trophoblast cells, highlighting the importance of CDKs for the physiological function of the placenta. Dysregulation of CDKs can result in trophoblast cell dysfunction, adversely impacting placental implantation and the exchange of nutrients between the mother and fetus. This disruption may consequently contribute to pregnancy complications, including placenta previa and gestational hypertension. It is notable that CDK4 and CDK6 are critical regulators of the placental cell cycle. CDK4/6 specifically facilitates the transition from the G1 phase to the S phase of the cell cycle. These kinases influence the proliferation rate of trophoblast cells, directly affecting the overall size and implantation efficiency of the placenta.

Other cell cycle regulators, such as cell cycle inhibitors (CKIs) and cyclin D/E, are also important for regulating the proliferation and differentiation of placental cells. Both p21 and p27 can regulate the proliferation and differentiation of placental cells, suppressing the progression of cell cycle by inhibiting CDK activity [66]. Moreover, Cyclin D and Cyclin E also regulate the proliferation and differentiation processes of placental cells by binding to CDK [67]. A study has shown that the regulation of cell cycle modulates the proliferation and differentiation of cells [68]. For example, the CDC25 phosphatase activates CDKs to promote the G2 / M transition of the cell cycle, directly impacting on placental cell proliferation and differentiation [68].

Angiogenesis

Vascular development is essential for proper placental formation and function, as newly formed blood vessels supply the placenta with vital nutrients and oxygen. Additionally, many blood vessels in the myometrium and extensive neovascularization on the uterine surface at the implantation site are typically observed in the dissected tissues from patients with PAS. Adherent placental tissue has shown excessive angiogenesis in the myometrial tissue of the site of implantation.VEGF can induce angiogenesis in the placenta by promoting the proliferation and migration of endothelial cells, providing the necessary nutrient and oxygen to the fetus [69]. Johnson et al. [70] reported that under hypoxic conditions, the up-regulated hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) up-regulated VEGF expression to enhance placental vascular development. VEGF is integral to placental angiogenesis, facilitating sufficient blood supply to support fetal growth and development. This discovery helps in understanding the pathogenesis of PAS [71]. A recent study has shown that VEGF can through its receptors, modulate the activation and proliferation of macrophages and T cells, creating a suitable immune environment in the placenta to support fetal growth [72]. Notably, there is a substantial concentration of VEGF in the blood vessels at the site of placenta accreta [73]. This phenomenon may be attributed to the insufficient expression of endostatin at the site of decidual loss. Endostatin is an antiangiogenic factor and its deficiency causes dysregulated angiogenesis within the placental region, consequently impacting the physiological development and function of the placenta [74]. During a typical pregnancy, VEGF expression facilitates vessel formation and the supply of nutrients to the placenta. The absence of endostatin may limit nutrient supply and oxygen exchange between the placenta and the mother [75]. Consequently, understanding the roles of these factors in placental implantation and the maternal–fetal interface is crucial for understanding the underlying mechanisms of pregnancy complications[76].

Furthermore, soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase receptor (fms), such as sFLT-1, can specifically bind to VEGF in the bloodstream, preventing VEGF from interacting with its receptor and inhibiting angiogenesis [77]. Empirical studies have shown that the expression levels of sFLT-1 protein at the maternal–fetal interface are significantly reduced in patients with placenta accreta compared to those with a non-adherent placenta. Moreover, VEGF facilitates the aggregation and activation of uterine natural killer (NK) cells, which can secrete growth factors and cytokines, such as interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) and interleukin-8 (IL-8), contributing to the maturation and functionality of blood vessels to support the developing placenta [78]. Actually, immunohistochemistry (IHC) displayed a notable reduction in the mean area percentage of NK cells in patients with PAS [79]. The degrees of this reduction were correlated with diminished NK cell levels and elevated levels of regulatory T (Treg) cells, P53, and VEGF. These cytokines are crucial for regulating immune responses and angiogenesis during pregnancy, particularly in the development and functional maintenance of the placenta [76, 80]. Previous studies have indicated that NK cells are involved in both immune surveillance at the maternal–fetal interface and promoting fetal growth and development through the secretion of growth factors [12, 81, 82]. These findings suggest a potential mechanism for promoting hyperinvasive trophoblast behavior and the disruption of placental vascular remodeling in placenta accreta.

Gene regulation networks

With the progression of biotechnology, especially through the utilization of high-throughput sequencing technologies, researchers have achieved a more thorough comprehension of the gene regulatory networks implicated in PAS. A recent study has shown that RNA sequencing revealed a significant upregulation of a cluster of genes associated with inflammatory responses in samples from patients with PAS, indicating that the pathogenesis of abnormal implantation involves inflammation [83].

NF-κB, a critical transcription factor, is responsive to various inflammatory stimuli and can facilitate the expression of genes related to inflammation, contributing to the pathological process of placental embedding. Concurrently, STAT3 is closely linked to the inflammatory response in the placenta, and the activated STAT3 can induce the proliferation and survival of placental cells, exacerbating the pathological process of placental embedding [84–86]. Aberrant activation of the NF-κB and STAT3 signaling pathways in these samples was associated with the overexpression of cytokines, including TNF-α and IL-6, potentially leading to immune dysregulation during the PAS process [87].

Furthermore, RNA-seq analysis of control placenta and PAS tissues has identified an upregulated PRG2 and AQPEP expression in EVTs (extravillous trophoblasts) to facilitate the migration and invasion of trophoblasts [88]. Moreover, the implementation of gene-editing technologies, such as CRISPR/Cas9, has markedly improved research precision [89–91]. For instance, the knockout of key genes using CRISPR/Cas9 evidenced the importance of the TGF-β family in the pathogenesis of PAS, providing potential gene targets for future treatments in mice [92–95]. Further RNA sequencing has also identified a series of differentially expressed genes associated with inflammatory responses, angiogenesis and cell adhesion during the process of placental embedding.

Simultaneously, a recent study has indicated the role of the Notch signaling pathway in sustaining placental cell fate decisions [96] and unveils the Notch signaling in modulating the invasion of trophoblast cells and their interactions with the uterine lining. Furthermore, the activation of the Notch signaling also participates in the development of organ-like structures within placental cells, which enhances the exchange of cytoplasmic and nutritive materials. During this process, the interaction between HIF-1α and Notch signaling may influence the behavior of trophoblast cells, impacting placental implantation and maternal vascular adaptation. New findings indicate a close correlation between HIF-1α expression and Notch signaling activity, potentially offering novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying placental implantation [97]. These findings elucidate the regulatory mechanisms governing cell fate during placental attachment and suggest possible intervention strategies to improve pregnancy outcomes in cases of placental abnormalities.

The role of non-coding RNAs in PAS

Non-coding RNA (ncRNA) is a group of RNA molecules that do not encode a protein. The ncRNA contains small nucleolar RNAs (snoRNAs), long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), tiny RNA (miRNA), circular RNAs (circRNAs), and piwi-interacting RNAs (piRNAs). Functionally, ncRNA regulates the biological processes, such as gene regulation, cell growth and development through the interaction with DNA, RNA and proteins [98, 99].

A recent study has shown that miRNAs in the let-7 family regulate the cell cycle transition of trophoblast cells by targeting IGF-II and CDK6 [100]. Furthermore, both let-7 and miR-145 can inhibit placental development by targeting and modulating specific growth factors and cell cycle genes, such as IGF-II and CDK6. These miRNAs are critical for the proliferation and differentiation of trophoblast cells. Additionally, specific lncRNAs, including HOTAIR and GAS5, can regulate placental implantation and early embryonic development by epigenetic mechanisms. HOTAIR is particularly important in modulating gene expression during embryogenesis, influencing cell proliferation and differentiation through its interactions with various proteins and miRNAs [101]. HOTAIR contributes to embryonic development and the determination of cell fate by modulating multiple signaling pathways [102]. Conversely, GAS5, a tumor suppressor, is downregulated in various cancers, and regulates placental development. We propose that GAS5 may be crucial for placental function and early development by modulating genes associated with the cell cycle and apoptosis. The functions of these lncRNAs extend beyond gene expression regulation, encompassing epigenetic regulation of gene expression in cells, which subsequently influences normal embryonic development and placental functionality [103].

MiR-34a-5p influences the biological characteristics of trophoblast cells through the modulation of multiple signaling pathways. MiR-34a-5p regulates trophoblast cell proliferation, infiltration, and apoptosis by modulating CDK6 expression and activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [104]. MiR-34a-5p serves as a significant regulator of placental implantation to maintain pregnancy health. Aberrant expression of these ncRNAs can result in placental dysfunction, subsequently influencing pregnancy outcomes [105]. Consequently, a comprehensive understanding of the role of these ncRNAs in placental development is crucial for the formulation of innovative therapeutic strategies to address pregnancy-related complications [106].

Immunomodulation in the PAS

Immunomodulation regulates the pathogenic process of PAS. During pregnancy, the increased frequency of Treg cells contributes to the suppression of maternal immune responses against the fetus [107]. Conversely, a reduction in NK cells may be implicated in the development of pregnancy-related complications. Research indicates that NK cells participate in the remodeling of placental vasculature during early gestation and the regulation of the maternal immune response through the secretion of cytokines, thereby facilitating fetal growth and development. This dynamic changes in immune cells are essential for maintaining immune tolerance at the maternal–fetal interface and facilitating normal placental development [108].

It is well known that Tregs are crucial for the establishment of maternal–fetal immune tolerance because they can secrete TGF-β and IL-10 to suppress the maternal immune response and support the successful implantation of the placenta. During early pregnancy, the increased numbers of Tregs are intricately linked to the successful implantation of the placenta. This also modulates the maternal immune environment to maintain fetal immune privilege, averting maternal rejection of the semi-allogeneic fetus [109]. Additionally, TGF-β and IL-10 can facilitate angiogenesis and tissue remodeling within the placenta, supporting healthy placenta development.

Furthermore, NK cells in the uterine secrete IFN-γ and other cytokines, regulating placental vascular development and the maintenance of placental structural integrity [107]. Compared with the healthy pregnant placental tissues, histological examinations exhibit the increased percentages of Tregs, P53 + , and VEGF + cells, but decreased frequency of NK cells in the placental tissues from PAS patients [79]. Hence, the reduced numbers of NK cells and increased numbers of Tregs, P53 + , and VEGF + cells are valuable biomarkers for the diagnosis of human PAS. These alterations contribute to the aggressive invasiveness of the trophoblast layer during placental implantation and interfere with the remodeling of placental vasculature [110]. Consequently, elucidating the interactions between NK cells and Tregs, as well as their respective roles in placental development, is crucial for improving PAS.

Interdisciplinary approaches and researches in PAS

By employing computational biology and systems biology methodologies, researchers are able to integrate and analyze extensive datasets of gene expression and protein interactions. This approach facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of the intricate networks associated with PAS. Such integration elucidates gene-protein interactions and provides insights into the impact of these interactions on placental development and function [108, 111, 112]. Utilizing high-throughput technologies alongside advanced computational tools allows researchers to construct biological networks, identify key regulatory elements, and investigate their roles in the process of placental implantation [112][113, 114].

The development of gene regulatory network models for trophoblast cells allows researchers may predict the genes or signaling pathways that are pivotal in the pathogenesis of PAS and identification of novel therapeutic targets. For example, the TGF- β signaling pathway is important for the differentiation process of trophoblast cells [115]. The MAPK and β-adrenoceptor signaling pathways are significantly enriched in gene networks for mood disorders, suggesting a potential role for these pathways in placental development [116]. This methodology enhances our comprehension of trophoblast development and function and elucidates the pathogenic roles of pregnancy-related diseases, such as gestational hypertension and placental insufficiency [117]. Actually, researchers have identified critical transcription factors and signaling pathways that are crucial for placental development and function. These insights may serve as the foundation for developing novel intervention strategies.

Impact of the microbiome on the PAS

It is interesting to understand the potential influence of the maternal microbiome on PAS and early pregnancy health. Alterations in the microbial community may modulate the inflammatory state of the uterine environment or directly impact the function of trophoblast cells by microbial metabolic products. Dysbiosis of the maternal microbiome may induce an inflammatory response within the uterine environment, affecting the normal development and function of the placenta [118]. Moreover, the maternal microbiome can interact with trophoblasts through their metabolites and influence the process of placental implantation [119]. Bacteria from the maternal gut microbiome, including species such as Bifidobacterium, play a crucial role in regulating maternal metabolism and immune responses. Additionally, they influence placental morphogenesis and nutrient transport. [120, 121]. The metabolites produced by the maternal microbiome, such as short-chain fatty acids, can regulate the development and function of the placenta by modulating the gene expression and metabolic pathways of the placenta [122]. Consequently, elucidating how the maternal microbiome impacts early pregnancy health through the regulation of inflammation and trophoblast cell function may offer novel insights and intervention strategies to improve pregnancy outcomes. This field of research may help understand various aspects of PAS disorders and may inform future clinical practices, such as optimizing PAS management and pregnancy outcomes through targeted microbiome modifications.

Translation from laboratory batches to clinical beds

To effectively translate scientific findings into clinical practice, it is essential to integrate preclinical and clinical research. Recently, some novel serum markers were found in placental implantation, which contributed to understanding the biological function of the placenta, and provided new targets for drug development. These markers included factors associated with angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, ncRNAs, as well as integrins. Angiogenesis-related factors, such as placental growth factor and sFLT-1, are valuable biomarkers for the early diagnosis of pregnancy complications, such as preeclampsia [123].

Integrins are pivotal in placental development and implantation, as they regulate the migration and invasion of trophoblast cells [124]. Integrins αvβ3 and αvβ5 are crucial mediators in the attachment and invasion of trophoblast cells. Inhibition of these integrins' functions reduces the invasion of trophoblast cells [125]. Additionally, the expression and activity of integrins are modulated by various cytokines and growth factors, which are integral to the maternal environment during early pregnancy [126]. Consequently, therapeutic strategies targeting integrins may offer novel approaches for the management of pregnancy-related disorders. Furthermore, integrins are crucial for cell adhesion and signal transduction, rendering them a significant target for drug development [127]. Although there has been some advancement in the study of integrins over recent decades, numerous challenges persist in clinical trials, particularly for the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of integrin-targeting drugs. Therefore, further research is necessary to elucidate the regulatory mechanisms of integrins to enhance the efficacy of integrin-based therapies [127]. A study has investigated the efficacy of small molecule inhibitors targeting specific integrin subunits as a therapeutic approach for abnormalities in the PAS [128]. Experimental studies have shown that treatment with these inhibitors effectively reduced trophoblast cell invasion into the basement membrane by obstructing the interactions between integrins and the extracellular matrix. Consequently, this intervention mitigated the severity of abnormal PAS. These findings may establish a foundational basis for subsequent clinical trials.

Technological innovations and future directions

With the progression of technology, especially the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning in biomedical research. The progression of technology may generate a more detailed understanding of abnormal placentation and prenatal identification of such outcomes. These sophisticated technologies enable researchers to expedite the identification of potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Furthermore, the utilization of 3D printing technology in the development of placental models facilitates a more precise simulation of the morphological and functional changes of the placenta throughout different stages of pregnancy. This technology enhances the comprehension of the placenta's structure and function and introduces novel methodologies for developing disease models for evaluating new therapeutic interventions. By employing this technology, researchers can gain insights into the intricate architecture of the placenta and establish an innovative experimental framework for investigating placenta-related diseases. For instance, the 3D placental model can be utilized to examine placental hemodynamics, drug transport, and the impact of environmental factors on placental function [129]. Actually, through the integration of machine learning algorithms, researchers have successfully analyzed extensive datasets to discern the critical factors influencing the success rate of placental implantation[130]. This interdisciplinary approach offers novel insights into placental biology and holds the potential to inform the development of innovative therapeutic strategies for improving pregnancy outcomes.

By synthesizing diverse gene expression datasets, researchers have identified pivotal genes and signaling pathways essential for placental development and function [130]. Furthermore, the implementation of machine learning algorithms facilitates the extraction of valuable factors from complex datasets, enhancing the accuracy of therapeutic strategies in clinical settings. In 2023, Brown and his research team employed machine learning techniques to analyze extensive gene expression data, successfully identifying biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets associated with PAS [131]. This study underscores the potential of computational biology tools in the prediction and treatment of PAS-related abnormalities. The approach enhances the comprehension of placental implantation and offers novel avenues for future research, particularly for the pursuit of new biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Unresolved issues and research priorities

Despite certain advancements, numerous questions concerning the molecular pathogenesis of PAS remain to be explored. The interactions between the placenta and the maternal immune system, and the implications of these interactions for the progression of PAS have not been clarified. It is well known that immune tolerance to the placenta is crucial for a successful pregnancy, as it involves the selective prevention of fetal rejection by the maternal immune system [132]. Furthermore, Tregs and dendritic cells are crucial for maintaining the maternal–fetal immune balance. Nonetheless, the precise molecular interactions between the placenta and maternal immune cells require further investigation. A recent study has identified a role for placental macrophages at the maternal–fetal interface, suggesting that these cells may be instrumental in modulating immune responses and facilitating placental development [133]. Macrophages in inflammatory states may regulate the production of Th17 cells and CD8 + T cells, thereby affecting pregnancy complications. Moreover, the maternal microbiota regulates the development and function of the placenta, which potentially adds complexity to the understanding of maternal–fetal immune interactions [134]. Consequently, a thorough investigation of these mechanisms is essential to elucidate the biological underpinnings of placental implantation and successful pregnancy outcomes. Such research may also offer novel insights into the prevention and treatment of pregnancy-related complications.

The placenta serves as a crucial interface between the mother and the fetus, given that its function and health status directly impact the well-being of both the mother and the fetus [135]. Thus, further research centering on these questions should enhance our comprehension of the implications of placental transplantation and its significance in maternal and fetus health. Given the enduring harmful effects of PAS disorders on mothers and fetus additional investigations are urgently needed. Future studies should prioritize the integration of interdisciplinary methodologies, including the convergence of immunology, genomics, and bioengineering technologies as well as AI, to thoroughly address these complex issues.

Conclusion

Investigations into the molecular pathogenesis of PAS have discovered several critical biological processes and identified potential therapeutic targets (Fig. 1 and Table 1). These discoveries underscore the necessity of ongoing researches to enhance our comprehension of the intricate interactions between the placenta and the maternal system, as well as to devise more effective interventions for managing or preventing PAS-related complications. With technological advancements and the integration of interdisciplinary research methodologies, it is anticipated that future developments will yield safer and more efficacious treatment options to improve health outcomes of both mothers and fetus (Table 2).

Eccrine Spiradenoma of the Scalp
Authors: Anjali Bagrodia, Vishal Gaurav, Tanvi Dev
Published: 2026

A 57-year-old woman presented with a solitary, firm, red nodule on the scalp [Figure 1]. The lesion had been present for the last 11 years and was associated with intermittent mild discomfort, without rapid growth or ulceration. Examination revealed a well-circumscribed, non-tender nodule with no surface changes. The clinical differential diagnoses included cylindroma, hidradenoma, pilomatricoma, and solitary trichoepithelioma. The lesion was excised and sent for histopathological evaluation. Microscopy revealed a multinodular basophilic tumor in the dermis, extending into the subcutis [Figure 2a]. It was composed of two distinct cell populations: central large pale cells and peripheral basaloid cells. Hyaline basement membrane material and intra-tumoral lymphocytes were also noted, consistent with eccrine spiradenoma [Figure 2b]. The postoperative course was uneventful, and the wound healed completely after suture removal.[1]

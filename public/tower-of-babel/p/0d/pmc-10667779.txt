Introduction

Cysticercosis is a zoonotic disease caused by segmented flatworms Taenia solium (cestodes). It is a major public health problem in underdeveloped countries and is caused by the larval form of Taenia solium. Life cycle of T. solium comprises two natural hosts, humans as the definitive and pigs as the intermediate host. Infection usually spreads due to ingestion of undercooked pork or through ingestion of its eggs in contaminated food or water. All conditions and markers for the transmission of taeniasis are prevalent in India. The three most common sites of infection are brain, muscle, and intestine [1].

Cysticercosis is an ancient disease, even found in Egyptian mummies, endemic to countries like Mexico, India, China, Indonesia, and countries located in Central and South America, Africa, and Eastern Europe, but due to the effects of globalization, such as immigration and travel, the disease now has worldwide presence [2]. Neurocysticercosis is one of the most common presentation of cysticercosis and is endemic in most developing countries of Asia, Latin America, Central and South Africa [3]. Cysticercosis seems to be widely endemic in North India, especially in the states of Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, and Punjab. Cysticercosis is also widely present among swine in India [4].

We report this case for its unusual presentation as breast lump in young female. This case is reported in line with the SCARE criteria [5].

Case report

The patient, a 25-year-old female, hailing from Rajasthan, India presented to the surgery out-patient department with a chief complaint of lump in the left breast present towards the sternum, medial to the nipple areola complex. The lump was single, soft in consistency, with normal overlying skin and measured 1 × 1 cm in size. The swelling did not increase in size and was not associated with pain, tenderness, or cyclical menstrual changes. Menstrual history and obstetric history were unremarkable. The patient had a mixed vegetarian and non-vegetarian diet but denied consuming pork or beef. There was no history of similar complaints in the family.

Nipple areola complex and axillary examination was normal. Opposite breast examination was normal. Her blood examination revealed raised eosinophil counts and was negative for viral markers. Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) was non-diagnostic and the swelling subsided after aspiration. USG showed a well circumscribed oval shaped anechoic cystic lesion with echogenic content within showing posterior capsule enhancement and not showing any vascularity or calcifications in the left breast. Bilateral axilla showed a few sub-centimetric axillary lymph nodes measuring 0.4 cm in maximum diameter. The lesion was categorized as BIRADS 3, depicting that the lesion was probably benign with less than 2 % chance of being a malignancy (Fig. 1).

Ultrasound image showing a well circumscribed oval shaped anechoic cystic lesion with echogenic content.

Wide local excision of breast lump was done. On gross examination a small greyish white globular tissue piece with attached yellowish adipose tissue was received, it measured 2 × 1 × 1 cm. The cut section revealed a unilocular cyst of 0.5 cm diameter, containing clear fluid (Fig. 2).

Gross photograph of the excised lesion showing a small cystic lesion.

The histopathological examination revealed cysticercus larva lying in a cystic space showing double layered undulating eosinophilic membrane. The adjacent fibroadipose tissue show foreign body type giant cell reaction, with presence of mild chronic inflammation, few lymphoid aggregates and focal areas of calcification (Fig. 3, Fig. 4).

Histomorphological whole mount image showing a small cystic lesion with cysticercus larva and adjacent breast parenchyma.

Microphotograph showing giant cells lining the cyst wall and adjacent tissue showing chronic inflammatory cell infiltrate.

The case was followed up by a stool examination. The results were unremarkable. Post surgery she was advised Albendazole 400 mg once daily for 2 weeks.

Discussion

Cysticercosis has been designated as a “biological marker” of the social and economic development of a community [6]. It is not only an eradicable and preventable disease but also a neglected one therefore continues to be a public health problem due to the inadequate public health measures.

Clinical suspicion is made on clinical features which depend upon the site of the cyst and the host's immune reaction to it. Subcutaneous cysticercosis can usually present as a painless or painful swelling. The diagnosis is usually made by radio imaging modalities like USG, CT or MRI. However, USG is preferred and is considered as the initial and most reliable diagnostic modality for a soft tissue swelling [7].

Not much literature is present to correlate the ultrasonography findings in cases of breast cysticercosis. A study stated that the most common USG appearance was that of a cyst containing a scolex within and with surrounding abscess. The second most common appearance, which was seen in five patients, was that of a cyst containing a scolex within and with surrounding edema. The least common appearance, which was seen in only one of the patients, was that of an irregular cyst with no scolex within and with surrounding oedema [8].

Vijayraghavan et al. described four types of sonographic appearances for soft tissue cysticercosis. He defines the first appearance to be a well-defined round cyst within a collection with a brightly echogenic protrusion from wall [9]. The second appearance as loculated collection of fluid with internal echoes with a well-defined round cyst within, with an eccentric echogenic protrusion from the wall, representing the scolex. The third appearance is an irregular cyst with minimal fluid collection on one side with an extruded scolex in it. The fourth appearance is defined as elliptical calcified cysticercus cysts. Our case did not have the radiological features to suggest a diagnosis prior to lump excision. Also, the patient never consumed pork or beef which are the major contributors for the spread of disease.

Present case is isolated cysticercosis of breast mimicking a benign breast lesion. India is endemic for cysticercosis but its presentation as a breast lump is rare. Fine needle aspiration cytology was advised as a part of routine follow up for breast lumps. FNAC is diagnostic when a fragment of cysticercus is seen on the aspirate [7]. In our case FNA smears were inconclusive.

Due to the unclear nature of cyst, it was decided to perform a lump excision to obtain a definitive diagnosis. Clinically, soft tissue cysticercosis can be misdiagnosed as lipoma, epidermoid cyst, abscess, pyomyositis, tuberculous lymphadenitis, neuroma, neurofibroma, sarcoma, myxoma, ganglion, or fat necrosis [10]. Though human faeces can be examined microscopically for the presence of eggs, however it cannot distinguish between the different species of Taenia.

There are two serological tests namely enzyme-linked immunoelectrotransfer blot (ETIB) and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). EITB assay uses affinity purified glycoprotein antigens to detect antibodies to T. solium [11]. The specificity although is 100 % yet sensitivity is low (50–60 %) due to which, it gives many false negatives in cases of neurocysticercosis. Immunoblot test is recommended by Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The western blot and ELISA tests usually are positive for serum cysticercosis IgG antibodies. The confirmation of diagnosis of a muscular or soft tissue cysticercosis can be made by FNAC and histopathology.

The disease is usually controlled by antihelminthic therapy which kills the visible cysts. There is a possibility of an acute flare of symptoms due to the inflammatory response provoked. However, if left untreated it may progress to neurocysticercois which in most cases present with epilepsy as the main symptom. There is a chance of dissemination of cysticercosis leading to multiple cysts in brain parenchyma, extraocular muscles, testis and even muscles of mastication and tongue.

Conclusion

Although the findings in this case are atypical, this does not exclude the possibility of an infection. This points out importance of personal and hand hygiene which may have led to the disease and strict intervention is required for prevention. In conclusion, cysticercosis of breast, although rare and should be kept as a differential diagnosis by clinicians especially in cystic breast lesions.

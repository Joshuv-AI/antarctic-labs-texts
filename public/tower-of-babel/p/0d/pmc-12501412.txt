Cost‐Effectiveness of Insertable Cardiac Monitor in Unexplained Syncope—Based on Real‐World Data From China
Authors: Yijia Tang, Baojian Zuo, Binbin Chen, Changsheng Fan, Jie Zhou, Yin Song, Jin Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe use of an insertable cardiac monitor (ICM) is recommended to help diagnose patients with unexplained syncope. Compared with conventional testing (CONV), ICM provides continuous long‐term electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring and has the ability to establish symptom‐rhythm correlations, which is critical for patients with infrequent and randomized syncope. In China, the upfront cost of ICM is relatively high. However, the subsequent costs and outcomes of additional diagnostics and resulting treatments remain unclear. This study sought to evaluate the economic value of ICM versus CONV in patients with unexplained syncope and suspected of arrhythmia from the perspective of the China's healthcare system, aiming at informing clinical and policy decisions.MethodsThis study used a Markov model to assess the lifetime costs and benefits of ICM versus CONV for diagnosing syncope. In this model, the costs and effectiveness of all relevant diagnostic and follow‐up arrhythmia‐related treatments were considered. The study cohort characteristics and costs were obtained from a retrospective real‐world study conducted in Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital. The mortality rate, syncope recurrence rate, syncope‐related injury rate, and quality of life estimates were obtained from published literature. Total costs and quality‐adjusted life years (QALYs) were modeled for two strategies, and the incremental cost‐effectiveness ratio (ICER) was calculated. Three times China's GDP per capita in 2023 was used as a willingness‐to‐pay (WTP) threshold.ResultsICM was predicted to be more expensive but more effective than CONV. The base‐case results showed that the average cost of the ICM group was 111 733 CNY with QALYs of 10.28, while the average cost of the CONV group was 88 386 CNY with QALYs of 10.08. The ICER was calculated to be 115 596 CNY/QALY. With China's GDP per capita in 2023 being 89 358 CNY, the ICER was < 1.5 times the GDP per capita, and similar results were shown in the scenario analysis.ConclusionThis analysis suggests that ICM is a cost‐effective strategy, versus conventional testing, for the diagnosis of unexplained syncope in China, and supports wider uptake of the technology.Keywords: insertable cardiac monitor, QALY, real‐world study, unexplained syncope

Introduction

Syncope is a transient loss of consciousness (TLOC) caused by temporary cerebral hypoperfusion, characterized by rapid onset, transient and self‐limiting symptoms, and usually leading to full recovery. According to previous studies, the origin of syncope remains unexplained in 20% to 40% of patients even after a complete diagnostic workup [1, 2, 3]. There is no large‐scale epidemiological study regarding unexplained syncope in China. An international epidemiological study has shown that the incidence of unexplained syncope is 2.6%–19.5% per year, with a cumulative lifetime incidence of 35% [4]. In addition, the incidence rate rises significantly with age, with the incidence of the population over 60 years old ranging from 30% to 52% [4].

Cardiogenic syncope is one of the three main types of syncope, with the highest risk and comparatively poor prognosis [5]. Also, arrhythmia is the most common cause of cardiogenic syncope [5]. Capturing an electrocardiogram (ECG) during the brief episode of loss of consciousness is the most important evidence for confirming arrhythmic syncope. Conventional testing (CONV) is often used to help diagnose, including ECG, 24–48‐h Holter monitor, brain and cardiac computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), tilt test and so on. However, it is hard for CONV to capture the ECG when syncope occurs because of its discontinuity. The insertable cardiac monitor (ICM) is a small device implanted under the skin, which can provide continuous single‐lead analog electrocardiogram monitoring and automatically identify and archive the events of bradycardia or tachycardia. Clinical guidelines [6, 7, 8] have also recommended the early use of ICM in patients with recurrent, unexplained syncope suspected of arrhythmic cause but with no definite diagnosis after the initial clinical evaluation. Due to its superior ability to reduce time to diagnosis and the associated benefit of health care cost savings and clinical risk reductions, ICM has proved to be cost‐effective in patients with unexplained syncope, suspected arrhythmic cause and no structural heart disease in western countries [9, 10]. Although there is plenty of overseas evidence demonstrating the economic value of ICM, the economic value of ICM in local use in China remains unknown.

This study utilized the real‐world clinical and cost data from a Chinese hospital to evaluate the economic value of ICM for unexplained syncope diagnosis compared to conventional testing (CONV), seeking to inform and enlighten clinical and health policy decision‐making in cardiogenic syncope.

Study Design

This study is an economic evaluation based on a real‐world, single‐center, double‐arm, retrospective cohort study conducted in Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital. The primary goal of the real‐world study was to generate the value of the parameters used in the economic evaluation, including patients' demographic, clinical and financial information. Cost‐utility analysis was used to assess the economic value of ICM compared to CONV based on the results of the real‐world study and other literature. This study received ethical approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital, Sichuan, China (Approval number: Lunshen (Yan) Year 2023 No. 554).

Real‐World Study

Patient Population

The study population is patients over 14 years of age, having recurrent and unexplained syncope with a suspected arrhythmic cause and no structural heart disease, who received ICM or CONV. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) have experienced physical examination, 12‐lead ECG, basic blood testing, and Holter monitoring before, but with no specific diagnosis; (2) be at high or sub‐high risk of syncope occurrence according to syncope risk stratification [5]; (3) have at least 2 documented histories of unexplained syncope; Patients with epilepsy, Alzheimer's disease, mental disease, malignancy, severe anemia, persistent hypotension, or substance abuse were excluded. We collected all the diagnoses, treatments, and financial‐related data of those eligible patients from 2017 to 2023.

Outcomes

The real‐world study focused on generating three kinds of outcomes that should be input to the economic model, including the baseline characteristics, transition probabilities, and medical costs. The baseline characteristics mainly included the age, sex, and comorbidities. The transition probabilities included the probability of ECG capture, syncope recurrence, injury, and other incidence rates. The medical costs included the costs of ICM insertion, conventional testing, injuries related to syncopal events, treatments after the specific diagnosis, and other related costs.

Statistical Analysis

For continuous variables in the descriptive analysis, mean (Mean), standard deviation (SD), maximum (Max), minimum (Min), median (Median), lower quartile (Q1), and upper quartile (Q3) were used for description. The t‐test and the Wilcoxon rank sum test were used to test for differences between groups, while for categorical variables, descriptive frequencies (n) and percentages (%) were calculated. Either the chi‐square test or the Fisher‐exact test (Fisher‐exact test) was chosen to compare the differences between the two groups according to the sample size. Statistical differences were considered when p < 0.05. The statistical analysis was performed by R software.

Cost‐Utility Analysis

The cost‐utility analysis aimed to assess the economic value of the ICM compared to CONV for the diagnosis of unexplained syncope based on data from the real‐world study and literature. This analysis was undertaken from the perspective of China's healthcare system. A Markov model was used to capture all potential events and health states related to unexplained syncope [10]. Results included costs, quality adjusted life years (QALYs), and incremental cost per QALY gained (ICER). All costs were shown in 2024 Chinese Yuan (CNY). Costs and QALYs were discounted at 5% annually according to China guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2020) [11]. The time horizon was lifetime, and the cycle length was 1 month. The model was conducted in TreeAge (2011).

Model Structure

The diagnostic goal of unexplained syncope is to diagnose and identify the cause of syncope as early as possible, to reduce the medical resource consumption caused by syncope recurrence and to provide specific treatments. The model is based on published literature [10]. Regardless of the ICM or the CONV strategy, patients may have recurrent syncopal events with or without ECG capture. Once an abnormal ECG is captured when syncope occurs, treatments could be provided based on ECG‐based diagnosis (Figure 1). The possible states of the patients with unexplained syncope were generalized and modeled by a Markov chain with three health states: investigation state, treatment state, and death state (Figure 1). Unexplained syncope patients may enter the “investigation” state and transition to the “treatment” state once the arrhythmia is detected. Otherwise, they would remain in the investigation state. Both patients in the “treatment” and “investigation” states can transition to “death” at any cycle.

Analysis Results

Base‐Case Results

The base case results showed that the lifetime cost in the ICM group was 111 732 CNY with 10.28 QALYs, versus 88 385 CNY and 10.08 QALYs in the CONV group. The ICER was calculated to be 115 596 CNY per QALY gained. Based on 2023 China's GDP per capita, 89 358 CNY, the ICER was < 1.5 times GDP per capita (Table 9).

One‐Way Sensitivity Analysis

The deterministic sensitivity analysis indicated that the probability of ECG capture in the CONV group, the proportion of patients with normal heart rates and rhythms, and the cost of conventional testing had the largest impact on the results as shown in Figure 2.

Probabilistic Sensitivity Analysis

In this study, we used Monte Carlo simulation for 1000 iterations to conduct probabilistic sensitivity analysis. The results demonstrated that, using 3 times the GDP per capita as the willingness to pay, the probability of the ICM group being cost‐effective was more than 85% (Figures 3 and 4).

Scenario Analyses

The ECG capture rate of CONV influenced the results the most on the basis of the one‐way sensitivity analyses. In addition, the syncope recurrence rate by using different diagnostic tools was the topic the physicians cared about the most. As the primary source of these two parameters was from the only randomized controlled trial [17] with limited sample size, we used alternative sources to further assess the robustness of the model results.

For the syncope recurrence rate, we input the result from our real‐world study as an alternative. The model results showed that the lifetime costs and QALYs in the ICM group were 91 905.58 CNY and 10.32 QALYs, while the results were 58 211.64 CNY and 10.13 QALYs in the CONV group. The ICER was calculated to be 179 607.76 CNY/QALY, which was about 2 times GDP per capita.

For the CONV ECG capture rate, we used the alternatives from our real‐world study and a published literature of a multi‐center clinical study in China [23], respectively. The results showed that the ICERs were 185 572 CNY/QALY and 41 115 CNY/QALY, which were < 2 and 1 times the GDP per capita, respectively (Table 10).

Discussion

This is the first study to assess the cost‐effectiveness of ICM therapy based on local real‐world data in China. The results confirmed that the ICER of ICM compared to CONV in the diagnosis of unexplained syncope is 115 596 CNY/QALY, below the willingness‐to‐pay threshold of 1.5 times GDP per capita. Despite the high initial cost of ICM insertion, the long‐term cost savings and an improved quality of life could be achieved by reducing time to diagnosis, increasing the rate of diagnosis and finding treatable arrhythmias, avoiding recurrent syncope‐related hospitalization, and preventing the follow‐up syncope events through receiving earlier arrhythmia treatments.

A comprehensive set of sensitivity analyses was performed to evaluate the impact of varying inputs on the results of the model. The probability of ECG capture in the CONV group is the key parameter affecting the results. In order to assess the robustness of our model, we changed this parameter and examined the results with two alternatives, one from our own real‐world study and the alternative from a multi‐center real‐world study in Beijing [23]. The scenario analysis results demonstrate the same trend of ICM being cost‐effective with the base case analysis and the sensitivity analysis, supporting the robust economic value of the ICM therapy in China. Our results are consistent with the study from the United States [10].

There is good evidence behind the mechanisms that can help explain the benefit of ICM therapy as compared to CONV therapy. The CONV therapy can only monitor for a short period of time (between 48 h and a month at most), while ICM can monitor continuously for 3–5 years. Capturing a rare event like syncope is quite challenging with the short monitoring window associated with CONV, but better for ICM [24]. ICMs are proven to be more effective than CONV at detecting other intermittent events like AF [25].

It is also noteworthy that, in our real‐world study, the syncope recurrence rate in the ICM group was significantly lower than that in the CONV group, which indicates that the ICM therapy could help prevent syncope recurrence. The only randomized controlled trial comparing ICM therapy with the CONV therapy [17] has not investigated the significant differences in syncope recurrence rate during follow‐up. Although our real‐world study is a retrospective study based on data from a single center with a small sample size and limited effective power, it is still a positive signal of the direct long‐term clinical value of the ICM therapy in syncope patients. Further studies about this endpoint with multiple centers and a larger sample size should be conducted in the future. Besides, we recognized that, although the CONV group has more patients experiencing syncope, the injury events were numerically less than the events in the ICM group. We suppose the reason may be as follows: (1) the patients' compliance: our real‐world study is a single center, retrospective study, which means that patients can choose to go back to our center or go to another hospital when they have an injury or syncope recurrence. In this case, we may lose catching all the injury events in the two groups, especially in the CONV group. Because they rejected getting ICM insertion and were less likely to have routine follow‐up visits to the hospital; (2) the small sample size: the sample size of both groups is relatively small, and the injury rate result is not statistically significant. In this case, numerically more events may be due to the small sample size.

There is now no epidemiological study about the disease burden of unexplained syncope in China. According to the study worldwide, we infer that the patient population of unexplained syncope in China could be sizable based on the large Chinese population base [3, 26, 27, 28, 29]. ICM therapy has been recommended as an effective diagnostic tool for unexplained syncope and has been widely used globally [6, 7, 8] However, in China, the usage of ICM therapy is extremely limited and restricted. There are several studies published by local physicians analyzing the reason for the limited use of the ICM therapy in China [30, 31, 32]. The lack of physician and patient awareness of diagnosis and treatment, insufficient local health economic evidence, and no device reimbursement are frequently mentioned by the authors. Our study has demonstrated the long‐term economic value of the ICM therapy for the diagnosis of unexplained syncope in China. In combination with the proven clinical value, the comprehensive value of the ICM therapy should be the basis of improving awareness of and access to the therapy.

Limitations

Some limitations exist in our study. The clinical inputs are sourced from different published literatures. The main driver of the additional QALY gained by the ICM therapy is the superior ECG capture rate, which brings the earlier treatments and a reduced risk of syncope recurrence and injuries. However, these parameters are not taken from the same source. In addition, there is inadequate evidence to review the ability of pacemakers, ICDs, ablation, and medication to reduce syncope recurrence and overall mortality in arrhythmia patients. The cost data is mainly from our single‐center retrospective real‐world study. The sample size is limited and may not be fully representative of the whole country. More research with a larger sample size and a longer follow‐up should be done to investigate the costs, the ECG capture rate, and the impact of the different treatment methods upon the probabilities of recurrent syncope, injuries, and death to validate the robustness of our study results.

Conclusion

In China, based on our data and modeling, ICM therapy is more cost‐effective compared to CONV in patients with unexplained syncope. This supports the reimbursement of and broader access to ICM therapy.

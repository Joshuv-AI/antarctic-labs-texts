A new device for lumbar intervertebral foramen lateral puncture: more accurate and less radiation exposure
Authors: Chao Li, Yunbo Guan, Qing He, Xuedong Bai, Ziqiang Zhang, Cheng Xu, Zuqiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The aim of this study was to explore the application value of a new puncture guiding device in lumbar lateral intervertebral foramen puncture. The new intervertebral foramen puncture guiding device based on the sector principle, first insert the guiding needle to the outer edge of the superior articular process, and then guide the puncture needle to directly intersect with the guiding needle at the superior articular process along the preset puncture point through the slide rail to complete the accurate puncture. From June 2020 to December 2022, 28 cases performed using the conventional method (Group A) and 30 cases utilizing the novel guidance method (Group B)0.58 cases of lumbar intervertebral foramen puncture under the guidance of guide frame or conventional method were completed. The operative time, the fluoroscopy times and the VAS scores from location to puncture was recorded. The operative time for lateral intervertebral foramen puncture was 14.17 ± 1.04 min in Group B, significant 18.41 ± 0.76 min in Group A (P < 0.05). Similarly, the mean fluoroscopy time was significantly lower in Group B (3.17 ± 0.53) than in Group A (7.18 ± 0.86) (P < 0.05). Postoperative VAS scores showed no statistically significant difference between the groups (P = 0.23). The designed guide frame has the characteristics of portability and X-ray transmission, which can significantly improve the accuracy of puncture, effectively shorten the learning time of lateral intervertebral foraminal endoscopic positioning puncture technology, reduce the incidence of puncture related complications and greatly reduce radiation damage.

Introduction

As of 2020, it is estimated that approximately 619 million individuals worldwide suffer from low back pain (LPB), a prevalence that continues to rise annually [1]. A substantial proportion of these cases are associated with intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD), a pathological condition that significantly diminishes the quality of life for affected individuals [2]. Given this escalating global health burden, focused on advancing therapeutic strategies for IDD, with a particular emphasis on optimizing instrumentation and techniques for percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy (PELD), is important.

PELD is one of the most rapidly developing and widely popularized technologies [3, 4]. PELD evolved from early minimally invasive percutaneous disc procedures. In the 1970s, Hijikata first introduced percutaneous nucleotomy via a posterolateral approach under fluoroscopic guidance, demonstrating the feasibility of treating lumbar disc herniation through percutaneous access without direct visualization [5]. Subsequently, Kambin defined the transforaminal safe zone, known as Kambin’s triangle, providing a critical anatomical foundation for posterolateral disc access and future endoscopic techniques [6]. Building upon these concepts, Professor Anthony Yeung initiated visualized endoscopic lumbar discectomy in 1999, and the technique was further developed and standardized by Professor Thomas Hoogland in 2002, marking the establishment of modern PELD [7, 8]. Now PELD is widely used to treat disc herniation, stenosis and some condition of infection and tumor, which has the least trauma to patients and the exact effect. By minimizing damage to the posterior bony and ligamentous structures and reducing postoperative intraspinal scarring, the transforaminal endoscopic approach contributes to the preservation of spinal stability. Compared with conventional posterior lumbar decompression procedures, this technique is associated with reduced approach-related tissue trauma and faster postoperative recovery, while providing comparable nerve root decompression and pain relief [9]. Safe and accurate puncture is the core step, and with accurate puncture and catheterization, endoscopy can fully expose and deal with the lesion. But the traditional bare handed puncture technology has some disadvantages, such as potential blood vessel, nerve root, intestines injury due to the wrong puncture route. And the increases of radiation exposure of operators and patients should be concerned.

The purpose of this study is to develop a simple, fast, accurate, less radioactive, safe and easy to popularize lateral intervertebral foramen puncture method, design and invent a simple external intervertebral foramen puncture auxiliary device, and at the same time, it is planned to conduct clinical operation evaluation of the device to further evaluate its safety and effectiveness.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethics statement

The retrospective study was approved by the institutional review board of our institution (permitted number HZKY-PJ-2021-18). This study was a single-blind, randomized controlled trial (RCT) with two parallel groups. Written consent for patient information and images to be published was obtained from all the participants. The present study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient characteristics

A retrospective review of a prospective cohort study was performed of patients at our institution between June 2020 with December 2022 who underwent lumbar intervertebral foramen puncture under the guidance of guide frame or without the guidance of guide frame. All procedures were performed by one surgeon. Finally, 61 fifty eight of 76 patients returned for follow-up, 39 males and 19 females.

Included criteria: (1) single segment lumbar disc herniation; (2) the clinical symptoms and signs are consistent with the imaging compression of the corresponding segments; (3) lower limb radiation pain symptoms are obvious, conservative treatment for 3 months or more is ineffective.

Exclusion criteria: (1) severe central lumbar spondylolisthesis, segmental instability; (2) abnormal coagulation function; (3) severe pain requiring a forced posture, making the patient unable to tolerate local anesthesia surgery; (4) severe underlying conditions such as cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, or renal dysfunction that preclude tolerance for proneposition local anesthesia surgery; (5) spinal deformity.

Evaluation index: (1) Puncture times and time; (2) Fluoroscopy times and Radiation Evaluation: record the fluoroscopy times and fluoroscopy time of C-type or G-type X-ray machine in the whole process from positioning to puncture; (3) Puncture complications include nerve and dural sac injury, abdominal visceral organ injury, vascular injury and other complications.

Design of intervertebral foramen puncture guide frame

The target of classic lateral intervertebral foramen puncture is located in the spinal canal, that is, the anteroposterior X-ray image is located in the center of the spinal canal and reaches the spinous process, and the lateral X-ray image is located in the posterior upper corner of the lower vertebral body. If there is a little deviation on the anteroposterior or lateral image, the puncture needle cannot reach the most ideal position. This puncture technique requires the puncture needle to slide into the spinal canal along the ventral side of the articular process after touching the upper articular process. For lumbar segments above L3-4 and central intervertebral disc herniation, it is easy to cause injury 0to the walking nerve root. In the L4-5 and L5-S1 segments, due to the obstruction of the superior articular process, the penetration of the puncture needle into the spinal canal is more inaccessible. Therefore, in practice, the majority of doctors regard the superior articular process as the actual puncture target, and the ideal target in the spinal canal can be really reached only after the appropriate articular process molding. Therefore, the real puncture target of the puncture needle is the superior articular process. The path of the working channel is that the puncture needle path extends into the spinal canal.

Based on the above understanding of the puncture needle target, the following deflectors are designed in this study. The design of this technology is based on the traditional Chinese fan bone mode. All the fan bones point to the fan axis, which is equivalent to the position of the articular process. If a guide needle is inserted into the fan axis, based on the principle of concentric circles, the puncture along the fan at any angle along the fan edge can accurately reach the fan axis, that is, the upper articular process during surgery (Fig. 1A, B).

The new intervertebral foramen puncture guide frame is developed based on the above principles. The guide frame is composed of a puncture guide needle and a quarter arc-shaped guide frame. The guide frame is equipped with a slider that can accommodate the passage of the puncture needle. As long as a guide needle can be accurately inserted to reach the upper articular process, the lateral puncture needle can reach the upper articular process through the predetermined skin puncture point and intersect with the guide needle. (Fig. 1C).

Surgical technique

Fluoroscopic positioning is to puncture the articular process on the intervertebral space. If the articular process is not clearly displayed, the pedicle of the lower vertebral body can be fluoroscopically positioned. Then, to determine the puncture point of lateral skin. The determination method is the same as that of unarmed puncture. After local anesthesia, the guide needle is inserted vertically along the predetermined puncture point. During the insertion process, pay attention to keeping the ground vertical. The puncture depth can be measured by preoperative CT. During the puncture process, you can feel that the needle tip touches the bone of the superior articular process, and you can reach the scheduled puncture point by going 0.5–1 cm deep along the outer edge of the bone of the superior articular process. Fluoroscopy can be performed 1–2 times during this period. Where the guide needle is close to the skin, the pliers can be clamped or the supporting depth limiting slider can be installed to prevent the guide needle from going deep in the subsequent operation, and the guide frame can be installed. After local anesthesia at the predetermined puncture point, insert the puncture needle through the slider, adjust the position of the puncture needle slider combination on the guide frame until the needle tip of the puncture needle is aligned with the predetermined puncture point, and then tighten the nut on the slider to fix the slider. Insert the puncture needle along the predetermined puncture point. At the same time, the pillow core can be removed intermittently and anesthetics can be injected. Continue to enter until the tip of the needle touches the bone, the puncture is over, and the fluoroscopy is confirmed. Remove the needle core and inject anesthetic in the intervertebral foramen area. Insert the guide wire, take out the puncture needle, remove the guide frame and guide needle, and continue to put it into the working channel (Fig. 2).

Observation index

Record and compare the operative time (operative time was defined as the duration from the initiation of guide needle insertion to the completion of puncture needle placement at the target location), the fluoroscopy times, and the visual analogue scale (VAS) scores between the two groups of patients.

Statistical analysis

The software GraphPad Prism 9 was utilized for generating graphs and conducting statistical comparisonsA t-test (or Mann-Whitney U-test) was used to assess the statistical significance, and the results are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

Discussion

The trend of minimally invasive surgery promotes the rapid development of clinical treatment of spine, and the diagnosis and treatment of spinal diseases has entered a new field - the minimally invasive era of spinal disease treatment. The operation is performed in the safe work triangle. The whole operation process can be carried out under local anesthesia, and the patient can remain awake throughout the operation. At present, this technology is not only used to treat intervertebral disc herniation, but also widely used in the treatment of all kinds of bone stenosis and senile degeneration.

The core technology of intervertebral foramen scope includes two aspects: puncture and catheter placement technology and endoscopic decompression technology. Among them, safe and accurate positioning puncture is the core step of percutaneous foraminal endoscopy technology, and it is one of the key factors to determine the difficulty of surgery and postoperative clinical effect. With accurate puncture and catheterization, endoscopy can fully expose and deal with the lesion. At present, the C-arm X-ray machine used in clinic for fluoroscopy guided puncture cannot provide accurate parameters such as puncture depth and the opening angle beside the puncture. The positioning puncture process requires the operator to integrate limited information, which puts forward higher requirements for the operator’s spatial thinking ability and surgical experience [10, 11]. The learning curve of percutaneous intervertebral foraminal endoscopy is steep. Young doctors often lack good spatial imagination and clinical puncture operation experience, resulting in difficulty in locating puncture, prolonged operation time, multiple punctures increase patients’ pain, increases the radiation exposure of operators and patients, and increases the risk of dural sac and nerve root injury. What’s more, if the puncture is forward, especially in the operation of upper lumbar spine segment, The puncture needle may injure important structures such as the intestines and kidneys in the abdominal cavity, and even accumulate the inferior vena cava, resulting in serious complications such as massive hemorrhage and visceral injury, resulting in the consequences of surgical failure [12].

The radiation hazard of spinal minimally invasive surgery has always been a clinical concern. High dose radiation can directly induce tumors, cataracts, cardiovascular diseases, etc., and long-term low-dose radiation also has a strong correlation with tumors, cataracts, cardiovascular diseases [13, 14]. With the help of auxiliary puncture locator, it can not only cultivate the growth of young doctors, but also reduce the number of ray exposure of operators and patients, so as to protect the health of both doctors and patients.

In recent years, navigation technology has been applied to intervertebral foramen puncture, which can achieve accurate puncture under the guidance of navigation. However, its shortcomings are obvious [15, 16]. Electromagnetic or body surface–assisted navigation equipment is expensive and requires specialized hardware support, which limits its widespread clinical application. In addition, another incision is needed to expose the spinous process for the fixation of the navigation reference frame, which increases surgical trauma [17]. Chinese scholars have applied ultrasound volume imaging system technology to percutaneous foraminal surgery, using fusion images and electromagnetic field tracking system to accurately locate the puncture target. According to the target and puncture route, the puncture needle is guided to puncture in the plane of the diseased segment in real time. Compared with C-arm X-ray machine, this system has the advantages of convenience, non-invasive, non-radiation, real-time imaging, and real-time monitoring of the surgical process [18]. However, ultrasound-assisted navigation requires surgeons to have substantial experience in ultrasound image acquisition and interpretation, and in some cases even the assistance of physicians from auxiliary departments. Moreover, accurate needle advancement often requires repeated adjustments during the puncture process, particularly for deep spinal targets, which limits its standardization.

The design of this technology comes from the traditional Chinese fan. All the fan bones point to the fan axis. Based on the principle of concentric circle, puncture the fan axis at any point around the fan along the track of the fan bones, and you can reach the fan axis. The innovation of this technology lies in the introduction of a guide needle in the puncture process. The guide needle is inserted vertically in alignment with the fluoroscopic projection direction, along the outer edge of the articular process scheduled by fluoroscopy. This predefined trajectory eliminates the need for repeated multidirectional adjustments during insertion. Even if the complete vertical insertion cannot be guaranteed, the target of puncture of articular process, can be reached through fine-tuning. According to the concentric circle principle, the puncture needle of the side route can follow the guidance of the guide frame, puncture to the target point that the tip of the guide needle has reached, and complete the puncture. The only possible deviation in the whole puncture process is that the puncture needle is thin, and there may be slight deformation when passing through the iliac spine edge, deep fascia, hypertrophic transverse process, ligament and other tissue structures, which cannot accurately meet with the guide needle. Through clinical practice, it can be found that even if the above conditions occur, the puncture needle can reach the intervertebral foramen area. After inserting the guide wire and guide rod, it can easily reach the articular process by means of manual fine-tuning. Just like shooting, even if the puncture needle is deformed and cannot accurately reach the bull’s eye, it can ensure the target, and will not miss the target, causing serious complications such as organ and vascular damage. In the process of inserting the puncture needle, the method of screwing in can be selected, and the deformation can also be reduced. In the lumbar 5-sacral 1 segment puncture, a long Kirschner wire can be used for puncture after anesthesia. Even if the transverse process is blocked, it is difficult to deform.

The designed guide frame has the characteristics of portability and X-ray transmission, which improves the cumbersome steps of the previously designed external aiming frame, such as large volume, high cost, and the need to accurately calculate the puncture angle in the puncture process. This method can significantly improve the accuracy of puncture, effectively shorten the learning time of intervertebral foraminal positioning puncture technology, reduce the occurrence of puncture related complications and significantly reduce radiation damage.

Nevertheless, the present study should be regarded as an initial clinical evaluation with a relatively limited sample size. Although the current findings support the feasibility and potential clinical value of this technique, larger-scale studies, preferably with a prospective design and longer follow-up, are warranted to further validate its effectiveness, safety, and generalizability. In addition, the fluoroscopy frequency was reduced in the present study, fluoroscopy count was used as an indirect indicator of radiation exposure. Future studies incorporating direct radiation dose assessment are warranted to more comprehensively evaluate the potential benefits of this technique in reducing radiation exposure.

Conclusions

Based on the potential risks of the freehand puncture technology of intervertebral foramen scope described above, this study designed and processed a set of guide frames suitable for intervertebral foramen scope puncture, which can achieve accurate, efficient and safe puncture, and at the same time can achieve nearly zero perspective radiation in the puncture process.

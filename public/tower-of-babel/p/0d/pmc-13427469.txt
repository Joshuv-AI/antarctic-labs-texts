A Multifunction Neurosciences Intensive Care Unit Lumbar Puncture Service Improves Efficiency and Delivers Significant Cost Savings
Authors: Mutaz Ombada, W. David Freeman, Perry S. Bechtle, Lauren K. Ng, Devang K. Sanghavi, Pablo Moreno Franco
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo enhance the multifunctional efficacy and cost efficiency of lumbar puncture (LP) services for optimal patient outcomes.Patients and MethodsIn January 2023, the neurosciences intensive care unit (NeuroICU) created a day team (Team 2) to offload time-sensitive tasks from the 24-hour service team (Team 1). We hypothesized that a multifunction service would improve operational efficiency and generate meaningful cost savings. We performed a single-center, retrospective review of Team 2 activities from January 21, 2023, to April 21, 2025, at Jacksonville campus of Mayo Clinic Florida, encompassing 400 consult encounters across 7 multifunction service domains. We determined cost savings using a time-driven, activity-based costing method, which estimated bed-day savings using a $2500/day cost and average 2-day hospital length-of-stay reduction with earlier intervention.ResultsDuring the study period, a total 200 LPs were performed, creating an estimated cost savings of $1 million. Team 2 completed around 100 LPs/year vs 23/year with the previous model, reducing length of stay by 2 days per LP (∼$5000/patient; ∼$500,000/year). This led to earlier postoperative discharges, as early as the day of the LP in some cases. These collective functions similarly streamlined care pathways, fostering bed availability, and led to earlier discharge of patients needing LP consult and those in the primary postoperative NeuroICU using an early discharge process.ConclusionA multifunctional NeuroICU day team yielded measurable throughput gains and substantial direct cost savings, as well as added team operational resilience.

Introduction

Large academic neurosciences intensive care units (NeuroICUs) face competing demands, including emergent critical care, fellowship education, and time-sensitive routine procedures. The aim of our study was to evaluate the impact of a specialized NeuroICU day team (Team 2) on operational efficiency and patient outcomes. Historically, lumbar punctures (LPs) and early postoperative orders often awaited resident availability or radiology fluoroscopy slots, leading to delays of 24-72 hours. These delays prolong empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure, heighten risk of Clostridioides difficile and other nosocomial infections, and strain bed capacity.1, 2, 3

This quality improvement project specifically focused on the operational impact and cost implications of a NeuroICU multifunction day team, rather than on clinical outcomes such as mortality or neurologic recovery. The primary aim was to quantify reductions in hospital length of stay (LOS) and the associated direct cost savings produced by performing LPs earlier and consolidating other critical care–adjacent tasks within a dedicated day team structure.

Compounding the issue, patients in the emergency department due to strokes who require thrombectomy and routine ICU neurology consults compete for the attention of a small on-call pool, further delaying throughput. As a result, fellowship teaching teams experience cultural and workload burdens, risking burnout and inefficiencies. Our solution was to launch Team 2 in mid-January 2023 to proactively address 7 critical care–adjacent functions.

Methods

We conducted a retrospective analysis at a tertiary academic medical center (Jacksonville campus of Mayo Clinic, Florida) from January 21, 2023, to April 21, 2025. All consults assigned to Team 2 during the study period were reviewed (approximately 400). The following 7 service domains were cataloged: (1) hospital-wide LPs; (2) early postoperative discharges; (3) emergency department stroke evaluations; (4) ICU neurology consults; (5) postoperative neurosurgical admissions; (6) interhospital transfer triage; and (7) Team 1 overflow and dual-crash coverage. The economic impact of LPs was modeled using time-driven, activity-based costing (TDABC).

Time-driven, activity-based costing is a cost-accounting method that estimates the true cost of delivering a service by focusing on 2 key elements:1.The cost of services/supplying capacity (eg, staff salaries, equipment, and overhead/bed utilization)2.The amount of time required to perform each activity (eg, minutes of physician time, nurse time, and technician time)

This approach is often used in health care because it captures the real resource use for each clinical task, highlights inefficiencies, helps compare workflows or service models, and supports value-based care analysis.

The following assumptions were made in the cost model: average bed cost estimate of $2500/day, an average reduction of 2 days to LOS per LP, and an estimated yield of $5000 savings/patient assuming 2 days saved × $2500/day. Our study was deemed exempt by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board.

We used a conservative estimate of $2500/inpatient day, recognizing that actual costs vary by hospital, state, and county. Recent national datasets place the average cost of a US inpatient hospital day at approximately $3100-$3300.4,5 Although these figures differ based on geographic region, hospital type, and level of care (eg, ICU vs general ward), they provide a reliable benchmark for estimating potential savings. Using the conservative $2500/day estimate ensures that our calculations remain cautious while still reflecting the meaningful reduction in cost associated with shorter LOS after early LP.

Results

Hospital-Wide LPs

Most LPs performed in our hospital before 2021 were completed by the Neurology Consult Service and Interventional Radiology, with the NeuroICU team handling those done in the ICUs. When Interventional Radiology stopped providing LP services between 2021 and 2022, nearly all LPs shifted to the Neurology Consult Service with an average of 5-7 days to be completed, and NeuroICU was rarely involved. Notably, the 23 LPs performed by NeuroICU in 2022 were completed in ICU settings. These trends highlighted the need for a new approach, which ultimately led to the development of the Team 2 service, designed to reduce LOS and overall costs. With Team 2 services, we were able to reduce time from procedure to discharge (δLOS) to around 2 days total. We took a 2-day reduction as a conservative approach.

Regarding confounders, LP has historically been performed by 3 service lines: NeuroICU, Interventional Radiology, and the Neurology Consult Service (the emergency department would consult for LP need for post-LP laboratory tests). After establishing Team 2 in early 2023 and proactively accepting LPs outside ICU boundaries, we observed a significant shift. We began handling General Neurology consults intended for LPs—previously delayed 5-7 days due to the busy nature of those services—more promptly.

For the 2023 cohort, 141 patients were initially identified. Thirty-seven transplant patients were excluded from analysis due to extremely long pre-consult hospital stays—1 patient had a pre-consult stay exceeding 300 days and was considered a clear outlier that would skew results—leaving a final analytic cohort of 104 patients. The average LOS (admission to discharge) for the 2023 cohort was 7.5 days, and the average δLOS was 2.1 days.

In the January 2024 to April 2025 cohort, 118 patients who underwent LPs were identified; 22 transplant patients were excluded, yielding a final cohort of 96 patients. For this period, the average LOS was 7.5 days (range, 1-17 days) and the average δLOS was 2.2 days (range, 1-14).

Together, these datasets demonstrate consistent average LOS across periods (7.5 days) and modest average δLOS (2.1-2.2 days), with wider variability evident in the maximum δLOS values due, in part, to excluded transplant outliers.

To directly address pre- vs post-implementation effects, we compared the historic 2022 cohort (pre-Team 2) with the 2023 and 2024-2025 cohorts (post-Team 2). In 2022, only 23 LPs were performed by the NeuroICU team, all in ICU settings, and the prior General Neurology and Interventional Radiology consult pathways were associated with average procedural delays of 5-7 days and prolonged hospital stays. After Team 2 implementation in January 2023, annual NeuroICU LP volume rose to approximately 100 LPs/year (a 4-fold increase), the average time from consult to LP fell to less than 24 hours, and the average δLOS decreased to 2.1-2.2 days across the 2023 (n=104) and 2024-2025 (n=96) cohorts. This pre- vs postimplementation comparison supports the conclusion that the multifunction day team shortened the LP-related component of hospital stay and reduced LP-related throughput delays.

Cost Savings

Early LP allows for faster diagnosis and timely initiation of appropriate antimicrobial therapy, shortening hospital LOS and reducing the overall cost of treatment. Using the TDABC model, a 2-day reduction in LOS per LP multiplied by $2500/day equals $5000 in savings. An average of 100 LPs a year would equate to annual savings of $500,000. Early Postoperative Discharges

By dedicating a 7:00 am to 7:00 pm coverage arm, Team 2 initiates postoperative orders (eg, laboratory tests and arterial line removal) within 12 hours (average LOS reduction of 0.25 days/order) facilitating faster bed turnover and decreasing ICU occupancy strain.15

Emergency Department Stroke Evaluations

Team 2’s presence in the emergency department streamlines stroke code activation and imaging triage for thrombectomy candidates, reducing door-to-needle times and optimizing interventional workflows.

ICU Neurology Consults

Rapid bedside assessments by a dedicated neurointensivist decrease time to diagnosis and guide ICU care plans, reducing LOS by an average of 0.5 days/consult through targeted interventions.

Postoperative Neurosurgical Admissions

Team 2 streamlines handoffs for patients after neurologic surgery, promptly placing orders and initiating monitoring, improving throughput by 0.25 days/admission and enhancing surgical team workflow.16

Interhospital Transfer Triage

Centralized review of transfer requests ensures appropriate resource allocation, reducing off-hour transfer denials and optimizing bed distribution across our network.

Team 1 Overflow and Dual-Crash Coverage

By managing simultaneous critical events on Team 1, Team 2 can maintain education and rounding flow, ensure education continuity, and reduce provider fatigue during high-acuity surges. Discussion

Our experience demonstrates that a dedicated multifunction NeuroICU day team can meaningfully enhance traditional on-call services. First, we observed a 4-fold increase in LP performed throughout with sizable economic returns, an exemplar of value-based neurocritical care. Second, rapid stroke and postoperative workflows markedly improved process metrics, such as door-to-imaging times and postoperative order-to-execution intervals. Third, centralized transfer triage and overflow improved institutional resilience.

Although the primary focus of this project was operational efficiency and cost, the broader literature provides supporting context for why early LP matters. Multiple cohort studies have shown that prompt LP—without routine pre-LP neuroimaging—is associated with earlier antibiotic initiation, lower mortality, and fewer long-term neurologic sequelae in acute bacterial meningitis. International guidelines now align with this evidence, and each hour of antibiotic delay has been associated with a measurable increase in mortality. In high-risk populations such as infants and young children, delays in LP and subsequent treatment have also been linked to higher hospitalization costs and resource utilization. Empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic regimens used while awaiting diagnosis are themselves costly, and deescalation after timely LP can avoid additional expenditure and reduce risk of C. difficile and other nosocomial infections. Although our project did not measure these outcomes directly, this context reinforces that the operational improvements reported by our multifunction NeuroICU day team are likely to translate into clinical benefits in addition to the cost and LOS reductions reported here.

Strengths of this study include its real-world observational design with robust operational data over a 2-year period. The TDABC methodology provides transparent costing, and the inclusion of 7 distinct consult domains reinforces generalizability to similar tertiary centers. Additionally, our NeuroICU day team model aligns with emerging “Hospital of the Future” frameworks emphasizing multidisciplinary, hybrid tele-in-person staffing.

Limitations of our study include retrospective data collection and the absence of a concurrent control group. However, historic 2022 service volumes serve as a reasonable comparator, and the magnitude of observed savings and throughput gains argue against regression to the mean. The single-center scope may limit external validity, but our service line represents the first published model of an LP-centric NeuroICU consult service with detailed health economic analysis. These findings suggest that a dedicated NeuroICU day team with procedural responsibilities can enhance operational efficiency and patient outcomes in similar tertiary centers.

Conclusion

A multifunction NeuroICU day team, launched in January 2023, delivered substantial operational, clinical, and economic benefits hospital-wide. Early intervention across 7 service domains, including a high-volume LP consult line, generated more than $500,000/year in direct cost savings and improved bed availability. These outcomes support improved value-based health care and considerable direct and indirect cost savings to patients and health care systems that could be replicated at other hospitals.

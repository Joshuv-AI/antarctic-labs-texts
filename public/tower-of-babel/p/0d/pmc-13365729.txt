Comparing paclitaxel and platinum-based concurrent chemoradiotherapy with short-term adjuvant chemotherapy versus platinum-based monotherapy concurrent chemoradiotherapy in patients with postoperative gastric-type endocervical adenocarcinoma with high-risk factors for recurrence: a multicenter randomized phase III trial
Authors: Weiling Qu, He Wang, Neng Jin, Qiu Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGastric-type endocervical adenocarcinoma (G-EAC) is a distinctive form of adenocarcinoma that is not associated with human papillomavirus. This neoplasm exhibits a low incidence, low screening and biopsy positivity, low preoperative diagnosis, and a high susceptibility to underdiagnosis and misdiagnosis. It is associated with a poor prognosis for patients. Currently, there is an absence of a standardized postoperative adjuvant treatment plan for G-EAC, which constitutes an urgent clinical problem that demands resolution. However, there is still no prospective analysis on the adjuvant treatment of cervicogastric adenocarcinoma. This study is the first randomized phase III trial, which aims to recommend better adjuvant treatment options for G-EAC patients at high risk of recurrence in China, in order to better optimize and personalize patient care and improve progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).MethodsThis trial is a prospective, multicenter study led by the Department of Radiotherapy, Women’s Hospital of Zhejiang University. Recruitment will begin in March 2025, and it is expected that 238 patients with postoperative G-EAC with high risk of recurrence will be recruited from 16 clinical centers in China. Patients will be randomly assigned to the experimental group or the control group in a 1:1 ratio. The experimental group will receive paclitaxel combined with platinum-based concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) and short-term adjuvant chemotherapy, and the control group will receive the current standard treatment regimen, i.e., platinum-based CCRT, without adjuvant chemotherapy. The primary endpoint of the study is the 2-year PFS rate, and the secondary endpoints are disease treatment failure pattern, OS rate, acute/chronic toxicity incidence, and quality of life assessment.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06870565Keywords: Uterine Cervical Neoplasms; Adenocarcinoma; Complementary Therapies; Clinical Trials, Phase III as Topic

INTRODUCTION

The incidence of cervical cancer ranks first among the 3 major gynecological malignancies and is the fourth leading cause of cancer death in women [1]. Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) is a major risk factor for cervical cancer [2,3]. With the popularization of tertiary prevention of cervical cancer and HPV vaccine, the incidence of HPV-related cervical cancer has gradually decreased, and the proportion of HPV-unrelated cervical adenocarcinoma has gradually increased [4]. Gastric-type endocervical adenocarcinoma (G-EAC) is the most common type of non-HPV-related cervical cancer, second only to usual-type endocervical adenocarcinomas in incidence. It is a mucinous adenocarcinoma with gastric differentiation and morphological features similar to pyloric glandular epithelium [5].

The occurrence of G-EAC is not related to high-risk HPV infection. The presence of hidden lesions complicates the process of obtaining samples, and the rates of screening and biopsy positivity are low [6]. Furthermore, the pathological morphological characteristics are analogous to those of benign lesions; however, the biological characteristics are highly malignant, which poses significant challenges to the diagnosis and treatment of the disease [7]. The preoperative diagnosis rate is low, and the possibility of a misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis is significant. This can result in delayed treatment and a negative impact on the patient's prognosis [8,9]. Shi et al. [10] showed that compared to HPV-related adenocarcinoma (HPVA), HPV-independent adenocarcinoma (HPVI) and G-EAC were more strongly associated with poor prognostic factors. These included large tumor diameter, deep stromal invasion, high tumor stage, spread of the upper genital tract, lymphovascular space invasion (LVSI), and mutated p53 expression. Their study demonstrated that HPVI has been independently associated with lower overall survival (OS) and higher tumor recurrence rates. G-EAC has been identified as a poor prognostic factor for OS, with findings that are independent of the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system [11]. Furthermore, the tumor recurrence rate has been found to be unrelated to FIGO staging [12]. Ehmann et al. [13] conducted a retrospective analysis of 70 patients with G-EAC, 43 of whom were treated at their institution. Fifteen patients (35%) received adjuvant therapy following surgery, with one-third (n=14) experiencing disease recurrence post-treatment. A prognostic analysis was conducted, revealing a median progression-free survival (PFS) and OS of 107 months (95% confidence interval [CI]=14.8–199.2 months) and 111 months (95% CI=17–205.1 months), respectively, for patients with stage I G-EAC. For patients with stage II–IV, the median PFS and OS were 17 months (95% CI=5.6–28.4 months) and 33 months (95% CI=28.2–37.8 months), respectively. All patients in stages I–III received adjuvant chemotherapy consisting of paclitaxel and platinum-based agents. Among the fourteen recurrent cases, 12 (86%) had received pelvic radiotherapy (RT) during initial treatment. The recurrence of the disease primarily manifested as distant metastases (8 cases) in these 14 patients, suggesting that intensifying systemic therapy during the adjuvant treatment phase may reduce the incidence of distant metastasis in high-risk, advanced-stage patients and even improve their prognosis. Given the high malignancy of G-EAC and its susceptibility to distant metastasis, it is recommended to appropriately relax the indications for postoperative chemoradiotherapy.

Currently, there is no standard treatment for G-EAC, so improving local control rate, regional lymph node control rate and reducing distant metastasis rate are urgent issues to be addressed. A multicenter study was conducted to analyze the efficacy of adjuvant therapy in 102 patients with G-EAC following surgery [14]. Among the 63 patients receiving adjuvant therapy, 22 (20.6%) underwent RT, 11 (11.8%) received concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT), and 30 (29.4%) underwent chemotherapy. The majority of patients in the chemotherapy group received a 3-week regimen combining paclitaxel and platinum-based drugs (25/30). The study revealed that 41 of the 102 patients experienced recurrence. The distant metastasis rate in the chemotherapy group (4/19) was lower than that in the RT group (7/8), the CCRT group (4/8), and the no adjuvant therapy group (2/6). Preliminary research by Ehmann et al. [13] and Nishio et al. [14] indicated that the administration of a 3-week regimen of paclitaxel in conjunction with platinum-based chemotherapy as an adjuvant therapy may potentially mitigate the risk of distant metastasis in patients with G-EAC. The objective of strengthening concurrent chemotherapy and augmenting short-term adjuvant chemotherapy is to curtail the recurrence rate and enhance the patient's survival probability, contingent upon patient tolerance.

The “Chinese Expert Consensus on Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment of Gastric-type Endocervical Adenocarcinoma” (2023 edition) recommends that patients with locally advanced G-EAC should first receive synchronous chemoradiotherapy, and the chemotherapy regimen can be paclitaxel combined with platinum or etoposide combined with cisplatin (2A-level recommendation); regardless of whether surgery or RT is the primary treatment modality for G-EAC, all patients are recommended to receive additional chemotherapy, and the regimen can be paclitaxel combined with platinum or etoposide combined with cisplatin (2B-level recommendation). The consensus of Chinese experts recommends a chemotherapy regimen that incorporates paclitaxel in combination with platinum. However, this recommendation remains unsubstantiated by prospective research findings. In summary, in the era of 3-dimensional precision RT, this study select paclitaxel combined with platinum doublet CCRT and short-term adjuvant chemotherapy to reduce the recurrence rate and improve the survival rate, providing a basis for the treatment of G-EAC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Objectives

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of paclitaxel combined with platinum-based CCRT and short-term adjuvant therapy in improving PFS of postoperative G-EAC with high risk of recurrence, utilizing the standard treatment regimen of platinum-based CCRT as a control group. The secondary objective is to evaluate the OS rate, disease-free survival, and safety and tolerability.

2. Endpoints

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint of this study is 2-year PFS. PFS is defined as the time interval from randomization to the first treatment failure (including recurrence of local primary lesions, pelvic lymph nodes, distant metastasis, and residual local primary lesions and pelvic lymph nodes 16 weeks after the end of RT) or death. In the absence of treatment failure or mortality, the data are censored at the final follow-up time point.

The secondary endpoints are as follows:

1) Disease failure pattern (time node: 2 years). The pattern of disease failure will be assessed by the site and number of recurrences or distant metastases.

2) OS rate (time node: 2 years). OS is defined as the time interval from randomization to death due to any cause. In the absence of death, it is defined as the time to the last follow-up, with the data being censored.

2) OS rate (time node: 2 years). OS is defined as the time interval from randomization to death due to any cause. In the absence of death, it is defined as the time to the last follow-up, with the data being censored.

3. Trials design

This prospective multicenter study is being led by the Radiotherapy Department of the Women's Hospital of Zhejiang University. It will employ a non-blind, randomized controlled design, and the randomization will be conducted using a random number method. The study is designed to enroll 238 cases, and the participating hospitals are located in 16 different institutions, including medical universities and provincial cancer hospital research centers. The primary study objective is to increase the 2-year PFS rate by 20%. The study has been meticulously designed with 90% power, a 0.05 confidence coefficient, a 20% case omission rate, and a 1:1 ratio between groups. According to Friedman's formula, 238 cases are required for the clinical study, which is scheduled to begin in March 2025 and is expected to conclude in December 2027.

10. Sample size

Previous research data showed that the 2-year PFS rate in the control group was approximately 27.3%, so our study arm is expected to increase that by 20%. A 1-sided test with an alpha of 0.05 and a test power of 90% was used. The experimental group and the control group will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio. According to the Z-test (pooled) to estimate the sample size, a minimum of 190 subjects will be required, including 95 in the experimental group and 95 in the control group. Assuming a total withdrawal and loss to follow-up rate of 20% during the study, approximately 238 patients will be randomized into 2 groups (119 in the experimental group and 119 in the control group). It will take approximately 2 years to complete the collection of cases.

12. Follow-up evaluation

Efficacy assessment at the end of treatment: the assessment of efficacy will be undertaken using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. Complete response: the shortest diameter of the pathological lymph nodes is less than 10 millimeters, irrespective of whether they are target lesions. Partial response (PR): it is evident that there has been a substantial decrease in the total reduction of the sum of the longest dimensions of the pathological lymph nodes, with a percentage change greater than 30%.

Efficacy assessment will be conducted one month after treatment completion: the evaluation of treatment efficacy will be conducted in accordance with the RECIST version 1.1. Progressive disease (PD): an increase in the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions exceeding 20%, with an absolute increase of more than 5 mm; the emergence of new lesions is also considered PD. Stable disease: a decrease in the aggregate of the longest diameters of target lesions that does not satisfy the criteria for PR, or an augmentation that does not fulfil the criteria for PD.

Three months after the conclusion of treatment, MRI and CT scans should be performed to re-evaluate the efficacy of the treatment. The evaluation of treatment efficacy will be conducted in accordance with RECIST criteria. For the purpose of statistical analysis, any residual pelvic lymph nodes that are detected 16 weeks after the completion of RT will be categorized as a regional treatment failure.

Treatment failure should be confirmed by fine needle aspiration cytology or pathological biopsy. In cases where pathological examination is challenging but imaging reveals characteristic findings, treatment failure may be clinically diagnosed.

Patients should be subjected to follow-up at least every 3 months for the initial 2 years of the trial, subsequently every 6 months. The time of confirmed recurrence at the primary site, pelvic lymph nodes, and distant metastases should be recorded. The earliest time of symptom onset for grade 3 or higher late toxicity should be documented, and the maximum severity of final toxicity should be recorded, using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Radiation Therapy Oncology Group grading criteria.

All patients should be followed up until death, with the cause of death documented. If a patient dies from an unknown cause but was tumor-bearing at the time of the last follow-up, the cause of death should be recorded as tumor-related death.

13. Statistical methods

The primary endpoint of this study was PFS. The null hypothesis (H0) states the 2-year PFS rates of the experimental group and the control group were both 27.3%. The log-rank test will be used to compare the PFS between the experimental group and the control group to evaluate the efficacy. The secondary study endpoints such as OS, local regional recurrence-free survival, distant metastasis-free survival, and advanced complication-free survival will be compared using the same method. The 2-sample rate test method will be used to compare the incidence of acute treatment toxicity and the short-term tumor response rate of the tumor between the 2 groups. The analysis of all efficacy indicators will be based on the intent-to-treat principle. Statistical tests will be performed using 2-sided tests, and a p-value less than 0.05 will be considered to indicate a statistically significant difference. Statistical analysis of quality of life assessment will be performed using a mixed analysis of variance model for the QLQ-C30 scale. Individual items will be analyzed using logistic regression at different time frames.

This clinical trial was designed according to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the Chinese Clinical Practice Guidelines. The protocol has been approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Zhejiang University Hospital of Obstetrics and Gynecology (IRB-20250032-R). Each participating center also needs to obtain approval from the ethics committee. Recruitment will only begin after approval. If necessary, the protocol will be changed with IRB permission. Informed consent, including consent to anonymous evaluation of clinical data and treatment outcomes, is essential for each subject. This study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (number NCT06870565).

DISCUSSION

G-EAC occurs predominantly in adult females with a median age of approximately 49 years (range, 37–84 years). The clinical manifestations are atypical and the incidence rate has obvious regional differences. It accounts for about 10% of cervical adenocarcinomas in European and American countries, about 16% in China, and as high as 25% in Japan [15]. G-EAC pathologically manifests as tumor cells with clear borders, a large amount of transparent, foamy, or pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, generally low nuclear-to-cytoplasm ratio, irregular distribution of nuclei at the base of the gland, and absence or very few characteristics of HPVA [16].

According to the NCCN Cervical Cancer Treatment Guidelines, high-risk factors after cervical cancer surgery include positive paracervical cancer, positive lymph nodes, and positive resection margins. Postoperative adjuvant pelvic RT + concurrent cisplatin chemotherapy ± vaginal brachytherapy is required [17,18]. The Sedlis criteria for intermediate risk factors are: 1) vascular positivity, deep stromal invasion, and any size of the mass; 2) vascular positivity, intermediate stromal invasion, and mass ≥2 cm; 3) vascular positivity, shallow stromal invasion, and mass ≥5 cm; 4) vascular positivity, intermediate or deep stromal invasion, and mass ≥4 cm. Postoperative RT (level 1 evidence) ± concurrent platinum-containing chemotherapy (level 2B evidence) is recommended for patients who meet the Sedlis criteria [19,20]. According to the 4-factor model of adenocarcinoma (a. tumor diameter ≥3 cm; b. LVSI; c. invasion of the outer 1/3 stroma; d. adenocarcinoma or adenosquamous carcinoma), postoperative adjuvant pelvic RT ± concurrent cisplatin chemotherapy is recommended if any 2 factors are present [21,22]. Cervical gastric adenocarcinoma is a non-HPV related type of cervical adenocarcinoma with a low incidence and poor prognosis. Currently, there are no specific treatment recommendations for G-EAC in international and national guidelines. According to the “Chinese Expert Consensus on the Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment of Cervical Gastric Adenocarcinoma” (2023 edition), individualized treatment should be carried out based on its special biological behavior characteristics while following the treatment standards for cervical cancer.

Chen et al. [5] compared the clinicopathologic characteristics and prognosis of gastric adenocarcinoma with other non-HPVAs and found that the gastric adenocarcinoma group had worse median PFS and median OS compared with non-HPVAs. Nishio et al. [23] included 328 cases of stage I-II cervical adenocarcinoma who underwent surgery and did not receive neoadjuvant chemotherapy, of which 95 were gastric adenocarcinoma; 131 patients received postoperative adjuvant therapy, of which 41 received RT, 22 received CCRT, 66 received chemotherapy, and 2 received chemotherapy + RT, but the specific chemotherapy regimen was unknown. They found that the median PFS of patients with common HPVA was 61.1 months, and the median PFS of gastric adenocarcinoma was 42 months. The PFS and OS of gastric adenocarcinoma were worse than those of common HPVA. In this study, there were 72 recurrences, 38 of 95 gastric adenocarcinomas (40%), and 34 of 233 common HPVAs (1.6%) (p=0.002). After recurrence, 19 patients with gastric adenocarcinoma received chemotherapy, with an effective rate of 36.8% (7/19), and 25 patients with common HPVA received chemotherapy, with an effective rate of 32.0% (8/25). There was no significant difference in the chemotherapy response rate between the 2 groups. After recurrence, 12 patients with gastric adenocarcinoma received RT, with an effective rate of 50.0% (6/12), and 11 patients with common HPVA received RT, with an effective rate of 81.8% (9/11, p<0.001). In conclusion, this study suggests that gastric adenocarcinoma is significantly resistant to RT. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the systemic treatment, reduce the rate of distant metastasis, and improve the survival rate of patients.

Song et al. [24] conducted programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) testing on 44 cases of HPVI and found that among G-EAC patients, the proportion with PD-L1 combined positive score ≥1 was 19% (4/21). Chen et al.'s [25] study indicated that, among G-EAC patients, those with PD-L1-positive expression demonstrated significantly lower PFS and OS compared to PD-L1-negative patients. These findings suggest that PD-L1 may be a poor prognostic factor for cervical gastric-type adenocarcinoma. Collectively, these findings suggest that PD-L1 may represent a potential therapeutic target for gastric-type adenocarcinoma. A study was conducted in which next-generation sequencing was used to analyze 21 cases of G-EAC [26]. Among the 94 cancer-related genes examined, the most frequently mutated gene was TP53 (11/21, 52.4%), followed by STK11/LKB1, HLA-B, PTPRS, and others. Furthermore, mutations in ERBB2 have been identified, suggesting potential therapeutic targets for G-EAC patients [27]. A recent study has indicated that patients diagnosed with G-EAC who possess ERBB2 mutations and who have undergone treatment with the anti-HER2 antibody trastuzumab have exhibited favorable clinical outcomes [13]. The above findings suggest potential new treatment approaches for this particular group of G-EAC patients and justify further exploration in the form of prospective studies.

Our study is the first randomized phase III trial to evaluate adjuvant therapy for Chinese patients with cervical gastric adenocarcinoma. This study is expected to provide prospective research results on adjuvant therapy for patients with cervical gastric adenocarcinoma to reduce local recurrence and distant metastasis rates and improve OS.

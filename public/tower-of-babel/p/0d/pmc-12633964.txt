Diagnostic accuracy of Truenat in extrapulmonary tuberculosis
Authors: Abhisek Das, Saswat Subhankar, C. Mohan Rao, Amrut Kumar Mohapatra, Sunil Swick Rout, Surya Teja Meka, Yashaswi R. Bharadwaj Akkiraju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Diagnosing extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) remains a challenge even in today’s era. Truenat, an indigenously developed nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT), has reported high sensitivity and specificity in sputum samples. However, its role in EPTB is still debatable.Objective:To compare the efficacy of Truenat with other diagnostic modalities, like acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear, mycobacterial culture, and a composite reference standard.Materials and Methods:All patients suspected of EPTB were included in the study. The patients were evaluated based on a detailed clinical history, physical examination, and routine blood investigations. Specific samples from appropriate sites were collected and sent for Truenat, AFB smear, and liquid culture, and the results were compared. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Strata 19.0.Results:We included a total of 235 patients with a male:female ratio 1.9:1. Pleural effusion was the most common form of EPTB. The smear positivity was 16.6%. The Truenat positivity was 45.1%, with the highest in pus (56.5%), followed by lymphnode (67.80%) and pleural fluid (41.5%). The culture positivity was 34.9%. In comparison to AFB smear, the overall sensitivity and specificity of Truenat were 100% and 65.82%, respectively; the positive predictive value was 36.79%, while the negative predictive value was 100%. Comparing Truenat with culture revealed a sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of 79.27%, 73.2%, 61.32%, and 86.82%, respectively.Conclusion:Truenat carries a high sensitivity and specificity for most EPTB samples in our study. A larger-scale study with a larger and uniform number of samples is recommended for more appropriate results.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, a rod-shaped aerobic bacillus. The bacilli primarily affect the lungs as they spread through air droplets produced by a person who has the disease while coughing, sneezing, speaking, and so on. The disease, however, can affect almost any organ in the human body and any age group.

In 2022, TB was the second leading cause of death from a single infectious agent after corona virus disease (COVID-19), causing nearly double the deaths as HIV/AIDS.[12] As per World Health Organization (WHO), more than 10 million people are affected with TB worldwide, which included 1.3 million children. The incidence among HIV patients was about 6.3%. The disease resulted in 1.3 million deaths, which included 1.67 lakh deaths among HIV-positive patients.[3] India was the first among the high-burden countries contributing nearly 27% to the global burden.[3]

Extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) constituted about 16% of all TB cases in 2020.[3] However, this might not be accurate and may just be considered as “tip of the iceberg”. This is mainly due to undiagnosed cases. Significant advances have been made in the diagnosis of pulmonary TB (PTB); however, diagnosing EPTB remains a challenge even in today’s era.[4] This is attributed predominantly to the nonspecificity of symptoms, paucibacillary nature of disease, and requirement of invasive procedures for sample collection.[4] The diagnostic dilemmas and handicaps are more pronounced in rural areas. In these areas, physicians mainly depend on their clinical acumen and methods like Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) staining and tuberculin skin tests for diagnosis. This results in delay in diagnosis, misdiagnosis, development of resistance, and, thus, increased mortality.

Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) has helped in the diagnosis of EPTB with a high positive predictive value in many studies.[56] The tests (cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test [CBNAAT], Truenat) follow the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) technique. These tests not only provide results with higher accuracy but also are rapid and can detect resistance to rifampicin. These tests are now being made available in most designated microscopy centers (DMCs), primary health centers, and community health centers to aid in early diagnosis. The availability of NAAT at these centers could increase treatment initiation by reducing turnaround time for test results and decreasing the need for laboratory referrals. This, in turn, will help physicians in reaching a confirmatory diagnosis early. Among the available NAATs, Truenat is an indigenously developed, chip-based, point-of-care, portable, battery-operated device that requires minimum manpower, and its bio safety needs are similar to microscopy.[7] Studies have reported the high sensitivity and specificity of Truenat in sputum samples.[89] However, its role in EPTB is still debatable.

Our study aimed to determine the role of Truenat in the diagnosis of extrapulmonary TB and to compare the efficacy of Truenat with other diagnostic modalities, like acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear, mycobacterial culture, and a composite reference standard.

Materials and Methods

The study was designed as a diagnostic study in the Dept. of Respiratory Medicine, Kalinga Institute of Medical Sciences (KIMS), Bhubaneswar (BBSR) and Regional Medical Research Centre (ICMR), Bhubaneswar between July 2022 and June 2024. All individuals with presumptive EPTB who visited the outpatient department of our institute and/or were admitted during the period between July 2022 and June 2024 were included in the study.

Inclusion criteria

All consecutive cases of presumptive TB were included in the study. A presumptive case was defined as “a patient with symptoms and signs of EPTB who needed to be investigated.”[10]

Extra pulmonary samples from appropriate sites were collected in sterile containers using needle aspiration and surgical procedures. All extrapulmonary specimens were transported in cool boxes maintaining temperatures below 20°C for compatibility with solid and liquid culture systems and molecular methods. A triple packing system was utilized for transportation, following all precautions applied to pulmonary samples. Samples for AFB smear and Truenat were sent to the DMC and KIMS for analysis and to RMRC, Bhubaneswar for mycobacterial culture.

Statistical analysis

Comparison of means was done using Fischer’s exact t-test (with a sample size <40) or quantifying agreement with a Kappa value (a sample size more than 40). Chi-square test was used to study association, and P < 0.05 was considered significant. The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and diagnostic accuracy were also reported. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM Corp. Released 2010. (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 19.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp).

Results

A total of 235 patients were included in the study, with 154 males and 81 females in the ratio of 1.9:1. The majority of the patients belonged to the age group of 16–30 years (59; 25.1%) [Table 1]. The mean age of males was 43.69 ± 20.9 years, whereas that of females was 36.01 ± 20.9 years (P = 0.008). Most patients belonged to rural areas (197; 83.3%).

Hypertension (64; 24.2%) and diabetes mellitus (7.2%) were the only reported comorbidities [Table 1].

The clinical features varied as per the type of EPTB. In case of lymphadenopathy, a localized swelling was present in all the cases (100%). Patients of pleural effusion presented with breathlessness and cough in most of the cases (40.25% and 35.1%, respectively). Altered sensorium was the chief presentation in patients with central nervous system (CNS) TB (95.3%). Similarly, in case of abdominal TB, abdominal distension was the most common complaint, seen in 81.25% cases. Weight loss, loss of appetite, and generalized weakness were the most common constitutional symptoms reported (78.3%, 65.96%, and 53.2%, respectively).

The total 235 samples included 77 samples of pleural fluid (32.77%), 64 samples of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) (27.2%), 46 pus samples from various sites (19.57%), 28 samples of lymph node aspirates (11.9%), 16 samples of ascitic fluid (6.8%), and 4 samples of synovial fluid (1.7%) [Table 2].

All the samples underwent AFB smear using the ZN technique, Truenat, and liquid culture. Among them, smear was positive in 39 samples (16.6%), Truenat in 106 samples (45.1%), and culture in 82 samples (34.9%) [Table 2].

Considering the individual samples, the highest AFB smear positivity was seen in pus samples (17; 36.96%), followed by lymph node aspirate (8; 28.6%). In the case of pleural fluid, the AFB smear positivity was 11.7%. The positivity rate was almost similar in both CSF and ascitic fluid (6.25%). None of the synovial fluid samples were AFB-positive in smear [Table 2].

Truenat was positive in 79.3% samples when culture was positive and in 26.8% samples when culture was negative (P < 0.001). In the case of both the above tests, the positivity trend was different. The highest positivity rate was observed in lymph node aspirates (82.1% and 67.86%, respectively), followed by pus samples (76.1% and 56.5% respectively) and pleural fluid (41.56% and 29.87%, respectively). In the case of CSF, the positivity rate was 20.3% and 18.75%, respectively [Table 2].

In comparison to AFB smear, the overall sensitivity and specificity of Truenat were 100% and 65.82%, respectively; the PPV was 36.79%, while the NPV was 100% [Table 3a]. Our study compared both the tests for individual samples as well. While the sensitivity was 100% in all cases, the specificity was the highest for ascitic fluid (93.3%) and CSF (85%). For pleural fluid, pus, and lymph node aspirates, the specificity was 66.18%, 61.1%, and 33.3%, respectively. Although the NPV was the same for all cases, the PPV was calculated as 69.23% for CSF, 50% for synovial fluid, 48.57% for pus, and 28.12% for pleural fluid [Table 3b].

Discussion

We included 235 samples in our study from patients with suspected EPTB which were sent for AFB smear (using ZN technique), Truenat, and culture. Culture was considered the gold standard in diagnosis in our study.

Tuberculosis in India is predominant among young males. The WHO reported the global male: female ratio for notifications to be 1.7.[9] Our study showed a male: female ratio of 1.9:1 [Table 1]. Socioeconomic and cultural factors leading to barriers in accessing health care may cause undernotification in women. In most low-income countries, twice as many men are notified with tuberculosis as women.[11] However, a few recent studies have reported higher reporting and incidence of EPTB among females in their studies.[1112131415]

The mean age among males in our study was 43.69 ± 20.9 years, whereas that of females was 36.01 ± 20.9 years (P = 0.008). Approximately one-third of the world’s population is infected with TB, and the greatest burden of disease occurs in those 15–49 years old.[11] In our study, the majority of the patients belonged to the age group of 16–30 years (25.1%).

The age of presentation in females in our study was lower. Prevalence of TB is similar in males and females until adolescence, when it increases in males. In high-prevalence countries, however, women of reproductive age have higher rates of progression to disease than men in this age group. This along with increasing awareness could explain the significantly lower age of disease detection among females. This was supported by a study from Iran.[16]

We included 235 consecutive cases of suspected EPTB and found that the majority of cases were pleural effusions followed by CNS TB. This was similar to another report where pleural TB was the most common site of EPTB (63.27%).[17] This may be an indication toward the changing pattern of EPTB.[18]

Our study reported the overall smear positivity to be low (16.6%). A low positivity of AFB smear in EPTB cases has been previously reported in various studies.[19] This is due to the paucibacillary nature of disease. However, cases of empyema or lymphadenopathy with caseous necrosis or immunocompromised patients have more chances of demonstrating mycobacteria.[202122] Our case also reported the highest smear positivity in pus samples and lymph node aspirate (36.96% and 28.6%, respectively). With lymphadenopathy causing a diagnostic dilemma in developing countries many times, the availability of Truenat will definitely help physicians in obtaining a confirmatory diagnosis.

NAATs amplify a single copy of the target genomic sequence. However, the sensitivity of various NAATs depends on their limit of detection (LOD). The LODs of various NAATs are as follows: TrueNatMTB100 CFU/ml, TrueNat MTB Plus PCR30 CFU/ml, Gene Xpert131 CFU/ml, and Gene Xpert Ultra16 CFU/ml.[23]

The major advantage of NAAT, such as PCR, is rapid diagnosis. Because EPTB is a paucibacillary disease, the sensitivity could be improved by PCR as it can detect as few as 10 mycobacteria. Therefore, a positive NAAT result can be considered a presumptive case, whereas a negative NAAT cannot be relied upon to exclude the diagnosis. Not many studies have been done using Truenat for EPTB, although Gene Xpert has been widely studied.[2425]

Truenat had an overall positivity of 45.1% in our study, with the highest positivity in pus and lymph node aspirates (56.5% and 67.86%, respectively). This is because of higher bacterial load in these samples. Similar results were also reported by Jose et al.[26] However, in their study, the positivity rate of Truenat was only 3.6%. The same study reported a pleural fluid Truenat positivity of 10%. A study by Valsan and Sudarasana reported a pleural fluid positivity of 3%.[27] Our study showed a positivity in pleural fluid of 41.56%. A high positivity rate in our case could be due to the higher number of empyema cases.

In the case of CSF, our study reported a positivity rate of 20.3%. This was much higher with respect to previous studies.[2627] Thus, we recommend the use of Truenat in the early detection of TB meningitis, a disease which could be fatal in many cases.

LOD of MGIT culture is 1–2 CFU/ml.[28] MGIT culture is the most sensitive test available as of today. Still, the sensitivity remains low in case of EPTB owing to the paucibacillary nature of disease.[2627282930] Our study showed a culture positivity of 34.9%. Such high culture positivity could be due to the use of automated liquid culture in our study, which has the advantage of reduced time of detection and contamination rates as well as improved isolation rates. A study by Siala et al. had also reported a high culture positive rate in EPTB samples (63%) where the majority of samples were lymph node aspirates.[31] Thus, the rate of culture positivity varies with the type of samples included.

The overall sensitivity and specificity of Truenat with respect to culture in our study were 79.27% and 73%, respectively. These results are similar to many studies.[272832] However, a few recent studies have reported higher sensitivity and specificity.[2632] These high values could be due to the higher number of urine and CSF samples in their study.

The PPV in previous studies has ranged from 44% to 58.46%.[2632] These were similar to our study where the PPV and NPV were 61.32% and 86.8%, respectively.

None of the previous studies have compared the sensitivity and specificity of individual samples. We found the sensitivity of Truenat to be high in CSF samples (91.67% and 96.15%, respectively), followed by pleural fluid, where the sensitivity was 86.96% and the specificity was 77.78%. The sensitivity was also high for lymph node aspirates and pus samples, but the specificity was low. The sensitivity was the lowest for ascitic fluid samples, but the specificity was high.

With reference to AFB smear, our study had a high sensitivity, similar to Jose et al. (100%).[26] However, the latter had reported a higher specificity (96.6%) owing to the type of samples they included. We also compared the sensitivity and specificity of individual samples and found the specificity to be the highest in ascitic fluid, CSF, and pus.

Rifampicin resistance was detected in two cases of pleural effusion using Truenat, which were confirmed through culture.

None of the synovial fluid samples were positive in smear or culture similar to Jose et al.[26] However, one of the samples was positive by Truenat in our study; including a larger number of samples may help in determining the actual sensitivity and specificity in synovial fluid sample.

Conclusion

NAATs are currently recommended as ‘‘first-line’’ fast diagnostic test in endemic countries when rapid results are crucial for TB diagnosis in HIV-infected patients or for appropriate management of multidrug-resistant TB cases. Truenat, an indigenous rapid molecular test based on PCR technology built into micro-PCR chips, is used for diagnosis of TB and Rif resistance. It is smaller and more portable than CBNAAT machines, which can be advantageous in resource-limited settings or in areas where infrastructure for laboratory testing is limited. These are designed to be user-friendly and require minimal training to operate. This simplifies the testing process and makes it accessible to a wider range of healthcare personnel. The easy availability of results also makes it ideal for the Indian healthcare settings.

Based on our findings, we recommend the use of Truenat for EPTB samples. Truenat also detected Rifampicin resistance accurately in two samples, which was confirmed with culture. A larger-scale study with a uniform number of samples is recommended for more appropriate results. Also, studies comparing both the devices on a large scale may be useful in recommending their use in EPTB.

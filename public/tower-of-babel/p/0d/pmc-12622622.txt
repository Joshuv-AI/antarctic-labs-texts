Enhancing outcomes for frozen shoulder patients: The role of targeted mindfulness therapy on apprehension and tension traits
Authors: Boming Liu, Yi Zhang, Zhi Yong Wong, Bingjie Li, Jian Wang, Weiguo Zhang, Kang Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:This study aims to assess the personality profiles of patients with frozen shoulders and evaluate the effectiveness of mindfulness therapy, specifically targeting apprehension and tension personality traits in these patients.Methods:The study was divided into 2 parts. In the 1st part, the Cattell 16 Personality Factors questionnaire assessed the personality profiles of patients with primary frozen shoulders. In the 2nd part, the effectiveness of mindfulness therapy specifically targeting apprehension and tension personality traits was examined. Patients were initially divided into 2 groups based on the severity of the disease and their treatment preference: conservative (non-surgical group) and surgical. Depending on whether the mindfulness treatment was administered, these groups were further subdivided into mindfulness and control subgroups. The Neer shoulder score was evaluated before and after 8 weeks of treatment and compared between subgroups.Results:A total of 239 patients with primary frozen shoulders (103 males, 136 females, average age 47.02 years) completed the Cattell 16 Personality Factors questionnaire. Male patients exhibited statistically significantly higher scores in Apprehension (O), while female patients had statistically significant higher scores in both Apprehension (O) and Tension (Q4) compared to the national normative data. In the 2nd part, 244 patients participated, with 141 in the non-surgical group (71 in the mindfulness subgroup and 70 in the control subgroup) and 103 in the surgical group (53 in the mindfulness subgroup and 50 in the control subgroup). In the non-surgical group, the average Neer score in the mindfulness subgroup pretreatment was 48.577 points, comparable to the control subgroup’s 48.186 points. After 8 weeks of treatment, the average Neer score in the mindfulness subgroup increased to 83.394 points, significantly higher than the control subgroup’s 69.014 points. In the surgical group, the pre-operative average Neer score in the mindfulness subgroup was 43.774 points, slightly lower than the control subgroup’s 43.96 points. Post-treatment, the average score in the mindfulness subgroup reached 84.264 points, significantly higher than the control subgroup’s 69.52 points.Conclusion:Integrating targeted mindfulness therapy into both conservative and surgical treatment protocols for frozen shoulders significantly improved patient outcomes, suggesting its potential as a valuable adjunctive treatment. Assessing personality traits before treatment provides a crucial basis for developing targeted treatment plans for patients with frozen shoulders.

Key points

Our research indicates that targeted mindfulness therapy significantly benefits patients with frozen shoulders exhibiting Apprehension (O) and Tension (Q4) personality traits. This therapeutic approach enhances outcomes for both post-operative and conservative treatment modalities.

1. Introduction

According to the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons Society, frozen shoulder, or adhesive capsulitis, is marked by persistent pain and stiffness that restricts movement in all planes of glenohumeral joint motion.[1] This condition is seen in women more than men and is frequently diagnosed among individuals aged between 40 and 60.[2,3] The chronic nature of a frozen shoulder, notably its tendency to intensify at night, can lead to significant psychological consequences, including depression and anxiety. Research indicates that conditions such as diabetes, chronic pain, or those affecting shoulder joint mobility are often associated with distinct personality profiles.[1,4,5] Personality traits, which stabilize post-adolescence, barring significant life events, are crucial in influencing an individual’s reaction and behavior across various situations.[6] Targeted psychological interventions for these personality traits can help alleviate chronic pain, thus fostering earlier recovery and mitigating disease progression.[7]

Mindfulness therapy is particularly effective and well-regarded in treating patients with specific personality traits. Originating from Eastern Buddhist practices and derived from the ancient term Sati, mindfulness is increasingly incorporated into modern psychological practices.[8] It focuses on cultivating present-moment awareness, fostering acceptance, and maintaining a non-judgmental attitude,[9,10] significantly benefiting physical and mental health. Mindfulness is employed in various therapeutic approaches, including stress-relief therapies,[11] mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy, and dialectical behavior therapy. In addition to its applications in psychotherapy, mindfulness plays an essential role in the physical rehabilitation of patients with musculoskeletal and chronic pain disorders, as well as certain neurological disorders[12] due to its efficacy in relieving anxiety and pain.[13–17] In orthopedics, mindfulness alleviates pre-operative anxiety,[18,19] reduces post-operative pain during rehabilitation,[20] and improves functional outcomes and sleep quality after joint replacement surgeries.[21,22] Exercises that combine mindfulness with motor control have proven effective in reducing neck pain.[23] Moreover, a study revealed that patients with chronic headaches who underwent a 6-week mindfulness therapy program achieved superior outcomes to those receiving conventional treatment.[24]

For patients with frozen shoulders, whether they undergo conservative treatment or arthroscopic release, physiotherapy for rehabilitation exercises is often the 1st line of management. The psychological state of patients influenced by pain and stiffness significantly affects the patients’ ability to actively engage in physiotherapy sessions, leading to challenges in rehabilitation and poor prognosis.[25] Therefore, this study aims to explore the effectiveness of targeted mindfulness therapy in frozen shoulder patients with specific personality traits. Using the Personality Trait Quality Scale, a tool commonly employed in psychotherapy to assess stable and enduring emotional, cognitive, and behavioral characteristics (personality traits), we 1st assessed the personality profile of frozen shoulder patients and then conducted a targeted mindfulness therapy intervention. This intervention was applied to other cohorts of patients receiving non-surgical treatment or those undergoing post-operative rehabilitation following arthroscopic surgery.

2. Methods

2.1. Participants

Patients aged 40 to 60 years diagnosed with primary frozen shoulders for at least 6 months under the Sports Medicine Department of the First Affiliated Hospital of XXXXX from July 2021 to September 2022 were enrolled in this study. Eligible participants had a pain Visual Analogue Scale score of over 5 points, demonstrated passive lateral rotation of the shoulder joint of <45° and had undergone magnetic resonance imaging to exclude any rotator cuff injury, calcified tendinitis, shoulder joint tumors or other concurrent shoulder joint pathologies. Patients with psychiatric conditions and brief psychotic disorders were excluded from this study. Regarding randomization and allocation concealment, patients were stratified by surgical versus non-surgical status, with subsequent block randomization (block size = 4) within each stratum using a computer-generated random sequence. The randomization schedule was maintained by an independent statistical unit and implemented via sealed, opaque envelopes to ensure allocation concealment.

This study contained 2 parts. The 1st part was an observational study to assess the personality profile of patients with frozen shoulders. The 2nd part was an interventional study to investigate the effectiveness of targeted mindfulness therapy in other cohorts of patients with frozen shoulders.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Dalian Medical University, and all participants provided written informed consent.

2.2. Observational study

Patients from July 2020 to August 2021 were included to complete the Cattell 16 Personality Factors (16PF) questionnaire, and their personality profiles were assessed using Cattell 16PF with reports generated using the Rui Ge psychological software system (http://www.rgpsy.com/). The 16 factors or dimensions of personality include Warmth (A), Reasoning (B), Emotional Stability (C), Dominance (E), Liveliness (F), Rule-Consciousness (G), Social Boldness (H), Sensitivity (I), Vigilance (L), Abstractedness (M), Privateness (N), Apprehension (O), Openness to Change (Q1), Self-Reliance (Q2), Perfectionism (Q3), and Tension (Q4). Each factor is scored on a scale from 1 to 10, where scores ≥ 7 are classified as high range, scores ≤ 4 are classified as low range and the remaining as intermediate range.

2.3. Interventions

Patients from September 2021 to September 2022 were enrolled in the interventional study. Initially, they were categorized into 2 main groups based on disease severity and treatment preference: a conservative treatment group (non-surgical) and a surgical treatment group. The conservative treatment regimen comprised activity restrictions, oral non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and physiotherapy exercises emphasizing shoulder flexion, external rotation, and extensor functions. The surgical approaches involved arthroscopic procedures, including capsular release, middle glenohumeral ligament release, subscapularis release, and synovial membrane clearance.

Patients were subdivided into 2 subgroups within each treatment group: 1 receiving mindfulness therapy alongside their designated treatment (mindfulness subgroup) and the other receiving the specific treatment alone (control subgroup). The mindfulness training was administered by professionally trained practitioners who completed a standardized mindfulness therapy training program (including theoretical modules and practical supervision) and passed pre-study simulation assessments. All interventionists held backgrounds in psychology or medicine and underwent 20 hours of study-specific training covering protocol procedures, standardized scripting, and contingency management. Weekly supervisory sessions were conducted throughout the study to maintain intervention consistency and fidelity.

A targeted mindfulness-based therapy intervention protocol was specifically developed for the personality profile obtained from the 1st part. The mindfulness therapy was administered weekly for 50 minutes per session for 8 weeks. The mindfulness interventions consisted of 8 components: engaging in meditation training, including breathing, mindfulness, relaxation, and body scanning meditation; overcoming distractions, focusing on coping with unusual feelings, developing patience, and embracing progress; calming tension, including recognizing and naturally befriending the presence of tension; identifying worries, including interpreting pleasant and unpleasant experiences, recalling associated thoughts and feelings, and adopting alternative perspective; reducing worries, including shifting away from complex thoughts, listing positive activities, and making conscious choices; soothing the body, including promoting a sense of wholeness, acknowledging personal limitations, and finding meaning beyond illness; pain control, including the distinction between the sensation of pain and the emotional response to it, and developing coping strategies; integrating mindfulness into daily life, including finding moment of quiet, appreciating the beauty of nature, and discovering the wonders of the moment.

2.4. Outcomes

The Neer shoulder score was assessed before and 8 weeks after intervention for all treatment groups.[26] The scoring system, widely used to assess shoulder function after trauma or shoulder diseases, allocates 100 points across pain (35 points), function (30 points), range of motion (25 points), and anatomical rotation or angulation (10 points). The scoring criteria define an excellent outcome as ≥ 90, satisfactory as 80 to 89, unsatisfactory as 70 to 79, and poor as < 70.

2.5. Statistical analysis

SPSS 18.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago) was used for statistical analysis. T test was used to compare Cattell 16PF results generated by the Rui Ge psychological software system with the Chinese national norm.[27] An independent sample t test was used to analyze the differences in Neer scores after treatment between patients receiving mindfulness therapy and their respective control groups for surgical and non-surgical management. A P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

4. Discussion

In the present study, patients with frozen shoulders exhibited apprehension and tension as significant personality traits. Targeted mindfulness therapy aimed at these traits significantly enhanced Neer scores, particularly in pain, reachability, stability, and range of motion (flexion, abduction, and external rotation), irrespective of whether patients underwent conservative or surgical treatment.

No specific personality traits have been conclusively associated with frozen shoulder. Consequently, our initial step involved assessing the personality profiles of these patients using the 16PF questionnaire.

We found that male frozen shoulder patients had a statistically significantly higher score in Apprehension (O), and female frozen shoulder patients had statistically significant higher scores in Apprehension (O) and Tension (Q4) compared to national normal reference. Research on patients suffering from chronic pain showed that they are commonly associated with neuroticism,[19,28] harm avoidance, and low self-esteem personality traits.[4,29] Individuals with neuroticism trait have difficulties in maintaining emotional stability, experiencing adverse effects such as anger, anxiety, self‐consciousness, irritability, emotional instability, and depression[30] and is more common among diabetic patients,[1] a group at high risk for frozen shoulder. These personality traits associated with chronic pain patients are consistent with our findings. Thus, a comprehensive biopsychosocial approach is essential for the treatment and rehabilitation of frozen shoulders to address both physical and psychological aspects of the condition.

The etiology of frozen shoulder remains incompletely understood, but several contributing factors have been identified. These include degenerative changes associated with aging, particularly in patients over 40 years old, as well as chronic injuries stemming from excessive and repeated shoulder joint movements. Additionally, poor posture or post-traumatic immobilization of the shoulder can lead to muscular atrophy and soft tissue adhesions if proper rehabilitation[31] is not undertaken.

Independent of the underlying causes, effective treatment of frozen shoulder demands a strong mindset and consistent commitment to rehabilitation sessions. In this context, mindfulness therapy is critical in helping patients manage the complex emotional challenges of a frozen shoulder. Research has emphasized that patients with frozen shoulders often experience significant pain and disability, which are exacerbated by psychiatric conditions such as depression and anxiety rather than by the physical limitations themselves.[25] A recent systematic review also suggests that depression and anxiety are associated with an increased perception of pain and decreased function and quality of life at baseline.[32] The chronic pain and resulting disability can increase the sense of apprehension on which actions or shoulder movements may exacerbate the discomfort, promoting patients to avoid such movements.

Studies have shown that mindfulness therapy offers both psychological and physiological benefits. It can reduce blood pressure,[33] improve cognition and memory,[34] and enhance cardiovascular, neurological, and immune functions by affecting the neuroendocrine system of the body.[35] Long-term meditation, a key component of mindfulness training, can alter the electrical activity of the brain,[36] and even its structure.[17,37] These changes can improve concentration, reduce fatigue, and increase self-confidence.[38,39] Moreover, mindfulness training can improve the mental health and quality of life of breast cancer patients.[40] A meta-analysis of mindfulness-based therapies revealed a significant positive effect on pain intensity, particularly for adults with chronic low back pain.[41] These benefits are particularly relevant to the post-operative rehabilitation of frozen shoulder patients, especially those with apprehension and tension personality traits, as these benefits can speed up the recovery process. Our study indeed confirmed that patients receiving mindfulness therapy had statistically significant lower levels of pain, improved strength, range of motion, and stability compared to the control group, regardless of the type of treatment.

This study has several limitations. Primarily, the small sample size limited the feasibility of conducting a gender-based analysis to assess the effectiveness of mindfulness therapy despite the personality profiles of patients being typically analyzed by gender per the reference Chinese adult national norm.[27] Challenges in recruiting participants for the mindfulness intervention precluded the execution of a gender-specific clinical treatment study. Furthermore, when attempting to categorize the 141 non-surgical and 103 surgical participants by gender, the disparity within each subgroup was too substantial to allow for meaningful statistical testing, thus necessitating a combined analysis.

Future studies should aim to recruit a larger cohort that would enable gender-specific analyses, considering potential confounding factors such as occupation, lifestyle, and education level. Moreover, the age group of the reference Chinese national norm, which includes individuals aged 18 years and older, may not adequately represent the age demographic of our study’s participants, who have an average age of 47.02 years, with a range from 35 to 70 years. Future studies should consider norms for patients aged around 50, which more accurately represent the demographic of those with frozen shoulders.

5. Conclusion

Patients with frozen shoulders commonly exhibit personality traits characterized by Apprehension (O) and Tension (Q4). When specifically tailored to address these traits, mindfulness therapy has been shown to significantly enhance outcomes for both surgical and conservative treatments. Conducting personality trait assessments prior to initiating treatment is crucial as it provides valuable insights that can be used to develop a more focused and effective treatment plan for patients with frozen shoulders.

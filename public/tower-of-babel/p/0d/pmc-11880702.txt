Capturing pharmacists’ perspectives on the value, risks, and applications of ChatGPT in pharmacy practice: A qualitative study
Authors: Ammar Abdulrahman Jairoun, Sabaa Saleh Al-Hemyari, Moyad Shahwan, Ghala Rashid Alnuaimi, Nihal Ibrahim, Ammar Ali Saleh Jaber
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo investigate the pharmacists' perspectives on benefits and risks of using ChatGPT in pharmacy practice and explore how this disruptive and ground-breaking technology can be effectively integrated.Methods and materialsA qualitative approach that draws data from licensed pharmacists using semi-structured interviews.ResultsMost participants felt ChatGPT could enhance the compliance, use, management, safety, adherence to medication, medication counseling, minimize medication errors, and streamline medication dispensing. However, when Chat-GPT has limited information and relies on obsolete medication databases, it risks providing inaccurate recommendations and inadequate medication details. Also, most participants highlighted the difficulty in interpreting ambiguous patient input or drug descriptions when using the application.ConclusionsDespite its potential, utilizing ChatGPT in pharmacy practice must be dependent on evidence-based results that offer profound insight into AI technology.Keywords: ChatGPT, AI, Pharmacy practice, Community pharmacist, Medication dispensing

Introduction

An ever-growing field of Artificial intelligence (AI) finds application in many industries,1 and its use in clinical research and pharmacy practices hold loads of potential and could exert a significant impact. Given that both aspects of the industry rely on the gathering and analyzing a substantial amount of data, using AI is pertinent in that context2,3 and offers a chance to transform how the sectors work while offering further insights into pharmaceutical care challenges and how to address them.

Introduced in November 2022, OpenAI's ChatGPT (Generative Pre-Trained Transform) was a notable technological information retrieval development that interacted with over 100 million global users from the first month of its availability.4 It creates human-like responses to inquiries by leveraging deep learning algorithms and widespread training on vast datasets and makes a case for the future relevance of AI-produced language models.5,6,7 OpenAI leads the way in creating AI models that can simulate the link between clinical knowledge and conversational interaction and provide logical and enlightening responses to medical and pharmaceutical inquiries. So, for it to be of any practical use, the model must be able to match human performance when appraising medical and pharmacological knowledge and reasoning and guaranteeing patient safety.5 The large language model ChatGPT uses a substantial and varied text data corpus to produce responses to users' queries. This corpus incorporates information that was publicly available in the form of websites, books, and articles, among others, until September 2021. While conventional decision-making tools used in clinical settings use datasets that are validated and structured, ChatGPT draws from unstructured data, leading to variations in reliability and accuracy. As it derives responses from probabilistic predictions, it can provide accurate information, but its output can also be incorrect, misleading or outdated. This is key to comprehending the potential risks and limitations in the use of ChatGPT in clinical and pharmacy settings, where information must be accurate. On the other hand, Drug GPT, Created by Oxford University was developed to facilitate the retrieval of drug information to support decision-making in clinical settings. By offering easy access to accurate and updated information on drugs, such as their dosages, side effects and interactions, Drug GPT can assist healthcare professionals. By incorporating AI into the process of medication management, this tool seeks to enhance patient safety and improve patient outcomes. Drug GPT thus serves as a good example of the significant potential for AI to strengthen clinical practice, establishing a precedent for the integration of such technologies in healthcare, including ChatGPT.

The academic and scientific communities have had varied reactions to ChatGPT, reflecting the broader debate over the merits and drawbacks of advanced AI technology.8, 9, 10 Concerns about potential biases in ChatGPT's training datasets are notable, as they could lead to ‘hallucinations’—misleading but plausible outputs—particularly concerning for academics.11 Security risks and the spread of misinformation through large language models (LLMs) are also significant concerns.11

In the healthcare sector, ChatGPT has proven effective in generating accurate discharge summaries from concise patient profiles. Its applications extend to enhancing risk assessment, drug discovery, and radiological decision-making.12,13 Beyond technology, ChatGPT facilitates access to personalized medical information, benefiting various clinical specialties like gynaecology, cardiology, and endocrinology.14, 15, 16

The pharmaceutical industry is interested in AI for tasks such as prescription screening, drug counseling, monitoring adverse drug reactions (ADRs), and patient medication education.17 Clinical pharmacists, critical in optimizing drug therapy and ensuring patient safety, find AI particularly valuable for improving productivity, judgment, and patient outcomes.18 While promising, ChatGPT's accuracy, reliability, and practicality in clinical pharmacy settings are yet to be fully investigated.17,19 Earlier studies show encouraging results, but more rigorous validation is required in actual healthcare settings. The effectiveness of ChatGPT in various clinical settings is crucial to ensure its applicability.20, 21, 22 Studies using diverse methodologies to assess ChatGPT's accuracy have been conducted, including one examining its precision and consistency across 20 pharmacotherapy cases, revealing varying accuracy rates over time.17,23

Other studies point to the risk of bias leading to the presentation of erroneous information with significant medical consequences.24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31 The literature also highlights potential errors, inconsistencies, and ethical and privacy issues.29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35,25,27

A recent study highlighting ChatGPT's potential in health and pharmacy practices suggests promising applications. Still, evidence-based underscores the necessity for evidence-based regulation.21 The technology's effectiveness is closely linked to the proficiency of the pharmacists employing it.36,37 There's a need for more comprehensive research involving a broader range of more healthcare providers to better understand the challenges and facilitators of integrating ChatGPT into pharmacy and healthcare practices.21 Our study rationale links to the substantial gaps in the current body of research concerning the use of AI, especially ChatGPT, in pharmacy settings. Although there has been considerable debate on how AI can play a role in healthcare, studies have primarily focused on its use in clinical diagnosis and decision-making. As yet, there has been little attention on integrating AI-based tools in a way that can contribute to pharmacists' daily practice. Our in-depth review of the current literature has shown a lack of empirical studies examining the use of AI in pharmacy practice, e.g., in medication management, patient counseling, and error minimization, especially in terms of the implications, benefits, and risks. Aiming to fill this gap, our study seeks to offer valuable insights on how AI can be effectively integrated into pharmacy practice, thereby offering an evidence-based avenue towards the responsible adoption of AI. As a result, our work not only addresses an important facet that has thus far been overlooked in the literature but also aims to ensure that ChatGPT and similar AI tools are only used in ways that can improve patient care and support pharmacy professionals.

This study aims to explore pharmacists' perspectives on the potential applications, benefits, and challenges of ChatGPT in pharmacy practice in primary and secondary healthcare settings in the United Arab Emirates. It seeks to provide valuable insights into the opportunities and hurdles pharmacists encounter when utilizing AI-powered chatbots in their practice, as well as strategies for their optimal implementation.

Methods and materials

Study design

The study design adopted for this exploration is a qualitative approach that assesses the benefits and risks associated with ChatGPT application in the context of pharmacy practice from Pharmacists' Perspectives.38 A qualitative approach offers researchers a great deal of flexibility, provides deeper insights into the participants' views and perspectives, enables the creation of several understandings into the respondents' outlooks on the subject, and being able to identify concerns that survey-based methods alone cannot.39,40,41 For key terms used in the study please see the supplementary file.

Study setting

The research was performed within the context of how pharmacists working in the primary or secondary healthcare systems in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) perceived the use of ChatGPT in pharmacy practice. The pharmacists worked for the government or private enterprises, and were engaged in community, hospital, or clinical pharmacies.

The rationale for selecting qualitative research

This study uses a qualitative approach because the topic of integrating ChatGPT into pharmacy practice requires us to consider the subjective and nuanced experiences and perspectives of pharmacists. In contrast to a quantitative approach, which seeks to perform statistical analysis to produce measurable findings, a qualitative study enables the in-depth investigation of the beliefs, perceptions, and attitudes of individuals towards a given topic. As the integration of AI tools such as ChatGPT in healthcare, especially pharmacy practice, is a nascent field, qualitative research is considered appropriate to explore the rich and contextual experiences of pharmacists. This will provide in-depth data, allowing us to delve into pharmacists' interactions with AI tools, examining their perceptions and experiences of the challenges and benefits. By capturing pharmacists' lived experiences in this way, qualitative research will make sure that we gain the in-depth insight necessary to ensure future development in the field.

Study's subject (inclusion and exclusion criteria)

There were several considerations for recruiting participants for this research. First, recruitment was based on pharmacists in the UAE working in clinical, hospital, and community pharmacists with minimum of three months' professional pharmacy experience. Second, the pharmacists must be registered with professional bodies such as Dubai Health Authority (DHA), the Health Authority Abu Dhabi (HAAD), or the Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP). However, the exclusion criteria was applied to pharmacists with less than three months' experience, on probation in their job roles, or not registered with the aforementioned authorities, and were duly excluded from this research.

Creating the interview guide

Insights acquired from the review of literature help direct the creation of the guide for the semi-structured interview,42, 43, 44, 45., 46 and its design was to assist in examining the benefits and risks of ChatGPT in the context of pharmacy practice. Three practiced researchers and academics from university assisted with testing the instrument's validity and reliability before data collection commenced for the study. Pilot in-person interviews were conducted for three excluded potential study participants working in community pharmacies, hospital pharmacies, and clinical pharmacies. The interview guide was adapted and refined based on the outcomes of these pilots, and then applied in the study after receiving participants' consents (Appendix).

The sampling technique and data collection

We used purposeful sampling to make sure that the chosen participants met the criteria necessary for answering the research questions. In other words, this approach allowed us to involve only those pharmacists with relevant experience or a solid comprehension of AI technologies, especially ChatGPT, across a variety of healthcare contexts, specifically hospital, community, and clinical pharmacies. This approach is suitable for a qualitative study that seeks to explore in-depth the experiences and perceptions of individuals who are in a position to give relevant detailed data. As this study honed in on those participants who had the requisite experience and knowledge, it could authentically examine the challenges and benefits of using ChatGPT in pharmacy practice. Consequently, the gathered data were not only meaningful but also relevant the aims of the research.

Before the interview, participants were provided sufficient information regarding the study's objectives and to fill a consent form indicating their willingness to take part in the exploration, while a self-administered questionnaire was used to collect their personal data. The principal author of the research who is trained in qualitative interview technique began the data collection process by interviewing the respondents at their chosen venue and time. New responses and themes emerging from prior interviews were integrated into latter interviews so that new topics arising from earlier interviews could be discussed in several subsequent interviews. The data collection was stopped at the saturation point when two consecutive interviews revealed no new themes or response. Nevertheless, after it was reached at the 30th interview, five more interviews were performed to confirm that data saturation had been achieved.

Thematic analysis framework

Braun and Clarke's (2006) six-phase thematic analysis framework guided the methodological approach for data analysis in this study47 (Fig. 1). This renowned procedure facilitates the identification, examination, and reporting of patterns, or themes, within qualitative data. The framework's strength lies in its ability to harmonize with the exploratory nature of the study and the iterative process of theme analysis. This synergy allows for patterns to naturally emerge from the data, ensuring that the analysis remains open and free from preconceived notions. By adopting this approach, the research benefits from a structured yet flexible method that respects the data's complexity and nuances.

Steps to ensure the collected data's validity and reliability

We took a number of rigorous steps throughout the research process to make sure that the data would be valid and reliable. First, we formulated an interview guide based on a thorough literature review; pilot interviews were conducted with pharmacists excluded from the main research, to ensure the validity of this guide. Following this step, we refined the questions to make them sufficiently relevant and clear, ensuring that they could gather rich data. Furthermore, the interviews during the data collection were held by a researcher trained in guiding qualitative interviews; this provided consistency in terms of the questions, how they were posed to the interviewees, and how the responses were further investigated. Next, we used triangulation to enhance the reliability of the thematic analysis. This involved the interview transcripts being independently coded by several researchers; then, the coding was compared and discussed until the researchers were in agreement. By repeatedly reflecting on their assumptions and biases, the researchers also practiced reflexivity to minimize the impact of these biases on the data interpretation. Finally, after data saturation was considered to have been reached, the researchers confirmed this by conducting five additional interviews; this ensured that the results would be robust and comprehensive.

Data analysis

The interviews lasted between 30 and 40 min and were conducted in English, the major language used for education in the UAE. However, appropriate additional questioning could occur when further information was required. The interviewer supported the data acquired from audio recordings during the interviews with field notes. During the data analysis stage, a researcher (AAJ) transcribed the audio recordings, and another researcher manually analyzed the interview transcripts, while two other researchers (SSA and AAJ) coded the transcripts. Afterwards, the coding from the two researchers was compared, and the co-authors reached a consensus on the data. The data saturation approach was used to make sure that all themes were identified, meaning interviews were held until no novel themes or new information could be found in the data. Thirty interviews had been conducted by the time data saturation was observed; following this, five more interviews were performed to confirm saturation. This implies that the identified themes comprehensively reflected the views of the participants.

As part of the analysis, we pinpointed recurring themes by performing a comparison of the responses of the interviewees. If at least one-third of the participants provided a similar response, suggesting that a concern or experience was common among the respondents, this was considered a theme. The aim was to ensure that the identified themes were not derived from the opinions of individual pharmacists but rather reflected the experiences of the group as a whole.

Reflexivity statement

As a researcher with experience in both administration and clinical pharmacy, we recognize the importance of acknowledging the role in this qualitative study investigating the perceived benefits and risks of ChatGPT in pharmacy practice. Our dual expertise provides valuable insights, but it also necessitates caution to avoid introducing biases into the research. Throughout this project, we committed to being open and honest about the positionality and assumptions. we employ reflective techniques for theme analysis and data collection to ensure a balanced and thorough exploration of the subject. Our goal is to enhance the legitimacy and transparency of the research process by openly admitting and analyzing my participation. This approach is crucial for understanding how my viewpoint as a researcher influences the study's outcomes, ensuring a comprehensive and unbiased assessment of ChatGPT's impact in the field of pharmacy.

The demographic characteristics of the 35 participants showed that they had an average age of 37 ± 3 SD, with 48.6 % (n = 17) being male and 51.4 % (n = 18) female. Respondents working in community pharmacy, hospital pharmacy, and clinical pharmacy were 25.7 %, 51.4 %, and 31.5 % respectively. Concerning their work experience, (11.4 %) had <1 year of experience, 10 (28.6 %) had 1–5 years of experience, 5 (14.3 %) had 6–10 years of experience and 16 (45.7 %) had >10 years of experience. Additionally, 57.1 % (n = 20) were bachelor's degree holders, and 42.8 % (n = 15) had postgraduate degrees (Table 1).

Major themes and codes of benefits of applying Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice.

Enhanced adherence to medication protocols

All the participants stressed the significance of Chat-GPT in enhancing adherence to medication protocols by educating the patients and using personalized reminders. Moreover, hospital and clinical pharmacists believed that addressing patient concerns can enhance adherence to medication protocols.

Improved medication compliance

Most respondents opined that Chat-GPT can improve medication compliance by customizing the medication instructions and providing real-time support and guidance to patients. Also, retail pharmacists found that follow-up discussions are a crucial for improved medication compliance.

Minimized medication errors

Hospital and retail pharmacists and a few clinical pharmacists believed that Chat-GPT can minimize medication errors via the detection of drug interactions, accurate dosage recommendations, and timely safety information.

Improved medication counseling

Respondents supported the use of Chat-GPT in enhancing medication counseling by providing accessible professional guidance, in-depth medication insights, and resolving patients' inquiries.

Streamlined medication dispensing

The use of Chat-GPT was found by retail pharmacists and some clinical and hospital pharmacists to streamline the medication dispensing process through virtual prescription solutions, automated alerts and appointment management, and personalized medication recommendations.

Enhanced medication use

There was a consensus among all the respondents that Chat-GPT can enhance medication use through tailored suggestions, awareness of drug interactions, and proper medication storage and disposal.

Efficient medication management

The pharmacists believed that Chat-GPT aids efficient medication management and identified some key factors as medication reconciliation, providing a tool for medication administration, and assistance with treatment adjustments.

Improved medication safety

The pharmacists identified improving medication safety as one of the advantages of using Chat-GPT, which can be achieved through themes like employing safety alerts and notifications, storage guidelines, handling practices and expiry awareness, and reporting adverse events or side effects.

Major themes and codes of risks of applying Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice.

Inaccuracy in medication information

All the groups of pharmacists identified inaccuracy in medication information generated by Chat-GPT as a major issue. Themes that could bring about it are outdated medication databases, insufficient data sources, and ambiguity in terminology.

Lack of personalized care

The respondents reported a lack of personalized care as another theme of risk of applying Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice. Themes responsible for it include the absence of emotional intelligence, rigidity in recommendations, and generalized guidance.

Legal and ethical considerations

All the respondents highlighted legal and ethical considerations as primary risks of applying Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice. Themes contributing to it include liability and responsibility, privacy and data protection, and informed consent challenges.

Dependency on technology

Clinical and hospital pharmacists and some community pharmacists were worried about dependency on technology when using of Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice. Themes contributing to it include diminished critical thinking, system failures, and lack of validation.

Patient safety and health risks

The respondents were concerned about the patient's safety and related health risks and identified themes leading to it as communication issues, inadequate drug interaction checks, and delayed responses.

Communication and language barriers

All the respondents were concerned about communication and language barriers as key risks of Chat-GPT use in pharmacy practice. Themes linked with it include limited language support, health literacy challenges, and cultural sensitivity.

Lack of continuity of care

The pharmacists reported a lack of continuity of care as another risk of using Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice, and the themes related to it include fragmented care, transition issues, and loss of critical information.

Reduced patient-healthcare provider interaction

All the pharmacist groups showed that interaction between patient and healthcare provider will be reduced when using Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice. The themes related to this issue are impersonal interactions, neglecting non-medical concerns, and decreased patient satisfaction.

Discussion

This research explored the benefits and drawbacks of using ChatGPT in pharmacy practice by sampling data from practicing pharmacists in the UAE and aims to understand how they perceive the use of the tool. The study is coming against the backdrop of the paucity of such examinations in some healthcare-related disciplines as reported by Sallam et al., 48 and should be taken into account when investigating how ChatGPT impacts healthcare. So, this exploration attempts to know the views and outlooks of pharmacists in primary or secondary healthcare systems concerning the benefits and challenges linked with the use of ChatGPT. The significance of this study is underscored by the projection in certain studies that a rapidly proliferating AI Chatbots is expected to impact healthcare dynamics, 24 thus making this investigation imperative.

The qualitative nature of this research is an exclusive advantage as it provides insights into real first-hand accounts of pharmacists' opinions on Chat-GPT compared with related studies that mainly used surveys.49 It is an inventive approach that addresses the significant gap in existing literature, given that very few explorations have delved into Chat-GPT use in pharmacy despite the fast-growing record-breaking subscription rates of its app.

Results from the data analysis show that respondents highlighted the significance of using AI Chatbot to improve pharmacy practices in beneficial ways such as improved compliance, use, management, safety, medication adherence, medication counseling minimized medication errors, and streamlined medication dispensing. Their views are buttressed by findings from similar explorations, which reported that integrating ChatGPT in drug information retrieval can significantly enhance pharmacists' role.50

The themes of over-reliance on technology highlighted diminished critical thinking, system failures, and lack of validation as key issues. So respondents emphasized that over-reliance on ChatGPT leads to decreased human decision-making skills, technical glitches or downtime impacts access to vital medication data, and insufficient validation of ChatGPT recommendations before their implementation. Evidence from past study shows that ChatGPT's ability to inhibit the development of critical thinking and communication skills tend to discourage engagement in critical evaluation.51 Similar to other AI-based chatbots, ChatGPT could hinder pharmacists' ability to communicate with patients, their families or caregivers, and other healthcare workers—an indispensable health professional skill. Such inference parallels findings from related research. For instance, Temsah et al.,24 admits that ChatGPT is a valuable digital health tool but that the unreliability of the current form renders it ineffectual for diagnostic or treatment purposes without human expert supervision.52,53

Before AI Chatbots can be successfully implemented in healthcare settings it is critical to ensure their trustworthiness and dependability by exploring the clinical outcomes of ChatGPT and comparatively analyzing its output and performance against contemporary AI Chatbots. Moreover, access to and effective use of ChatGPT is dependent on internet infrastructure, digital literacy, cultural norms, and regulatory policies.50

This study shows that the risks associated with using Chat-GPT in pharmacy practice include a dearth of personalized care, legal and ethical concerns, technology dependency, patient safety and health risks, communication and language barriers, lack of continuity of care, lowered patient-healthcare provider interaction as potential risk factors in the implementation of Chat-GPT. Evidence from this research shows that the risk of inaccurate information is based on obsolete medication databases and limited information by ChatGPT, which brings about inaccurate recommendations and inadequate medication details, respectively.

Similarly, like in other past studies, most pharmacists described difficulty interpreting ambiguous patient input or drug descriptions derived through the ChatGPT application, and are linked to its ability to create subjective, outdated, or inaccurate content.46,54 The unintended generation of deficient, imprecise, or subjective information might undermine the quality of drug dispensing and impact healthcare quality adversely.55 Thus, investigating how ChatGPT can produce accurate and impartial data is pertinent.

Since healthcare practices like drug dispensing entail using sensitive patient information, respondents were worried about the legal and ethical ramifications of ChatGPT breaching data privacy.46,56 Therefore, subjecting ChatGPT to comprehensive appropriate regulation that offers patients full data privacy and confidentiality and prevents any harmful implications of breaching data privacy (e.g., insurance, job opportunities, and personal relationships) is essential.57,58 Thus, when using ChatGPT in pharmacy practice the possibility of legal liability issues could arise for both the patients and the pharmacists and should be considered.46 Also, data from ChatGPT should be thoroughly evaluated and validated by a competent professional before being deployed for decision-making.59

In order to understand this qualitative assessment, it is critical to adopt a cautious approach because regardless of the participants' experiences and qualifications, their limited number, and the subjective nature of the interview questions—a potential source of bias in evaluating ChatGPT application in pharmacy practice —may imply that interpreting data from ChatGPT responses could be limited. Therefore, owing to this factor, it could impact the generalizability of the findings to other countries.

Incorporating AI tools such as ChatGPT into pharmacy practice, e.g., as part of decision-making and patient interactions, offers substantial benefits for pharmacists, but it also poses considerable challenges that could profoundly impact pharmacists' daily work. For instance, by enabling personalized patient guidance and more detailed medication management, the use of AI tools could contribute towards streamlined workflows, better medication adherence, and better patient outcomes. Yet there are also risks, not least because AI tools can generate inaccurate, outdated or misleading information, with substantial implications for patient safety. Furthermore, implementing ChatGPT in clinical practice raises questions of legality and ethics in terms of informed consent and data privacy, in addition to the liability of pharmacists if an AI tool gives inaccurate information. With pharmacists playing a dynamic role against the backdrop of the rapid integration of technology in healthcare, comprehensive training and coherent guidelines are crucial to the effective and ethical use of AI technologies. The aim should be for their use in ways that complement, rather than substitute, human judgment, thereby scaffolding modern pharmacy practice to make it effective and safe.

The pharmaceutical sector is becoming increasingly interested in incorporating AI technologies for improved patient care. For instance, AI tools like ChatGPT can be integrated into certain tasks, including drug counseling, patient medication advice, prescription screening, and checking for adverse drug reactions (ADRs).

Limitations of this study

This study is limited as there was an imbalanced distribution of interviewees from different pharmacy contexts. Specifically, more than 50 % of the participants were hospital pharmacists, meaning their experiences and perspectives may have had a disproportionate influence on the themes. This, in turn, could have affected the study's conclusions, with the themes identified potentially being more related to the challenges and benefits of pharmacy practice in hospitals, overlooking the views and experiences of clinical and community pharmacists. Due to this imbalance, there may be some limitations in generalizing our findings across other pharmacy practice settings. In future, researchers should try to promote the involvement of pharmacists from other practice settings to ensure a more thorough comprehension of how AI technologies like ChatGPT impact pharmacy practice regardless of setting.

A further limitation is the fact that this study did not specifically ask respondents which GPT applications they were using, nor did it examine to what extent they trusted these tools. Such knowledge regarding the specific applications and pharmacists' level of trust would offer crucial insight, particularly in view of the proliferation of online misinformation. By neglecting this aspect, this study may have missed some of the nuances underlying pharmacists' use of AI technologies. Future studies would do well to address this limitation by asking respondents which GPT app they use and evaluating to what extent they trust them. This would ensure a more thorough comprehension of pharmacists' perceptions towards AI technologies.

Finally, this study is subject to some confounding factors, including different levels of professional experience among the respondents, the diversity inherent in such work settings, and their different levels of familiarity with AI technology. These factors are likely to have informed the respondents' perceptions and attitudes, leading to variations in their responses that may have affected the findings' consistency. For instance, the experiences of community pharmacists may differ from those of hospital pharmacists, while those with more extensive experience with GPT technologies may hold significantly different viewpoints on the advantages and disadvantages. It is vital to recognize these confounders, and future studies need to find ways to control these to ensure a clearer picture of how AI tools influence pharmacy practice regardless of the context.

Conclusion

Adopting ChatGPT in pharmacy practice comes with a lot of prospects that could enhance the pharmacist's role, but its implementation should be reliant on evidence-based results that unravel adequate understanding of the AI technology by the pharmacists. Participants highlighted its benefits as improving compliance, use, management, safety, and adherence to medication, plus medication counseling, minimizing medication errors, and streamlining medication dispensing. Conversely, its associated risks include obsolete medication databases and the provision of limited information that could cause inaccurate recommendations and inadequate medication details, respectively. Furthermore, participants were concerned about interpreting ambiguous patient input or drug descriptions derived through the ChatGPT application.

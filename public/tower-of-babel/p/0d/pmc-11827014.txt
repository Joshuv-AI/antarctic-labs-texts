Kurdish social media sentiment corpus: Misyar marriage perspectives
Authors: Sarkhel H. Taher Karim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This article presents a thorough compilation of 5108 Central Kurdish comments taken from YouTube and Facebook. The purpose of compiling the dataset was to investigate public perceptions of Misyar marriage, a non-traditional form of marriage, in the Kurdistan region. The goal of the 135-day data collection period was to gather comments from specific public pages on these social media platforms. there are two columns in the dataset: sentiments and comments. The sentiments column classifies each comment into one of eight sentiment labels: Positive, Negative, Neutral, Sarcastic or Humorous, Suggestive, Dismissive, Skeptical, and Curious. The comments column contains the text of the comments in Central Kurdish. To improve the quality and uniformity of the data, a great deal of preprocessing was done to address problems like noise removal, character replacement, and space adjustments.Researchers interested in sentiment analysis, social media studies, Islamic studies, and Kurdish cultural practices will find the dataset to be a useful resource. It can be used for sentiment analysis, trend analysis, linguistic studies, and other analyses. It provides insights into the public discourse surrounding Misyar marriage. The labeled data can aid in the creation of machine learning models and further our knowledge of societal perceptions of emerging religious trends.

Background

The “Kurdish Social Media Sentiment Corpus: Misyar Marriage Perspectives” was put together in order to better understand social attitudes and public opinion regarding Misyar marriage in the Kurdistan region. The Misyar marriage is a marital agreement that is observed in some Middle Eastern nations. In a non-traditional marriage, the pair relinquishes some rights often linked to conventional marriage, such as the wifeʼs entitlement to housing and financial assistance from her husband, in return for more flexibility in the marital arrangement. The social and theological ramifications of this practice have generated heated discussions [9]. A type of marriage known as Misyar marriage, which departs from customs, has generated a lot of discussion and conflicting opinions on social media sites.

The dataset was created using social media mining and sentiment analysis as the methodological foundation. A comprehensive resource for analyzing sentiments expressed in Central Kurdish comments on Facebook and YouTube is what the dataset attempts to provide, given the linguistic diversity and distinct cultural context of Kurdish social media users. Over the course of 135 days, comments were gathered for this project, and then the data underwent thorough preprocessing to guarantee consistency and quality.

The dataset offers insights into public discourse regarding Misyar marriage by focusing on eight sentiment categories: Positive, Negative, Neutral, Sarcastic or Humorous, Suggestive, Dismissive, Skeptical, and Curious. Scholars studying sentiment analysis, social media studies, and cultural practices in the Kurdistan region will find it to be a useful resource that enhances original research by providing comprehensive, labeled data for additional examination.

Data Description

One of the languages with fewer resources is thought to be Kurdish. 30–40 million people practice the language worldwide. There are two main dialects of Kurdish: Sorani and Badini, it is generally estimated that between 9 and 10 million people in Iran and Iraq speak Sorani [5,6]. The 36 Central Kurdish (Sorani) letters consist of vowels and consonants , most letters of the language are essentially the same as those of Arabic with a small difference. The “Kurdish Social Media Sentiment Dataset: Insights on Misyar Marriage” is a collection of 5108 carefully labeled sentiment-filled Central Kurdish comments from YouTube and Facebook. This dataset captures a wide range of user sentiments and offers insights into public opinions regarding Misyar marriage.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

1.Data Collection The process of gathering data required a lot of work and involved scanning through the two social media sites that are most widely used in the Kurdistan region: Facebook and YouTube. The period of time during which the data was collected was almost 135 days, from February 8, 2024, to June 25, 2024. Data were collected during this period from specific public Facebook and YouTube pages, recording all remarks pertaining to the marriage of Misyar. Due to their greater significance to the subject at hand, the more well-liked reviews were given precedence. The quantity of features (words) is also increased by using multiple scripts. SVM Classification: As a result of its efficacy in text classification tasks, especially for sentiment analysis, we selected Support Vector Machines (SVM) as our classification approach [10]. Given these superior results, we focused on the SVM model for the final validation.

Ethics Statement

Respecting platform terms that permit research use without claiming ownership of the original content, the dataset consists of user-generated comments. In accordance with ethical guidelines and privacy laws, all data was anonymized to safeguard user privacy by eliminating any identifiable information. The data collection techniques were compliant with Facebook and YouTube's policies regarding scraping, guaranteeing moral and legal compliance. This guarantees that our dataset complies with all moral and legal requirements [7,8].

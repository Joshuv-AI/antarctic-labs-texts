A quick comparison model on optimizing the efficiency of photovoltaic panels in collecting solar radiation
Authors: Yihe Bao, Haohe Bao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFew scholars study light efficiency of solar-cell arrays in theory, while it is difficult to experimentally determine the maximum capacity of a photovoltaic panel to collect solar radiation. This paper proposes a solar energy comparison model (SECM), considering the sunshine duration changes every day to optimize the solar radiation collection model in an ideal state for a whole year, which is easy to use, and can quickly obtain the optimal tilt angle of photovoltaic panels and the solar radiation collecting efficiency enhancement of intelligent light tracking photovoltaic panels. The results show that the sunshine duration is an important factor affecting the solar radiation received by photovoltaic panels. In regions from 66°34′N to 66°34′S, intelligent light tracking photovoltaic panels can increase the collected solar radiation by at least 63.55%, up to 122.51% compared to stationary photovoltaic panels during the effective light time, which is much higher than what most people generally thought. And the advantage of intelligent light tracking photovoltaic panels is more obvious in high latitudes, with a longer and more variable sunshine duration. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the solar radiation collection and photovoltaic panels.Keywords: Intelligent tracking system, Stationary light harvesting system, Photovoltaic efficiency, Solar radiation, Collecting efficiency enhancementSubject terms: Energy harvesting, Renewable energy

Introduction

The development of solar devices

With the reduction of fossil fuels, it is intended to further develop solar energy. To collect and utilize solar energy more efficiently and to ensure the efficient utilization of solar energy, scholars are optimizing the steps of solar energy collection, conversion, and storage from different perspectives, such as how the dust impact on grid-connected photovoltaic performance1, how to reduce the photovoltaic modules temperature to improve their electricity produced2, and how to increase the collected solar radiation by light tracking harvesting system.

The PV (photovoltaic) industry is rapidly developing, which has brought the global positive significance. If the effective sunshine time is 4 h, a 1KW of solar photovoltaic power station can make 2.8 kilowatt-hour electricity on average3. The annual average photovoltaic installed capacity in 2021–2030 is expected to reach 60GW4. In China, the installed capacity of photovoltaic power generation in the first half of 2023 was 78.42GW, with an increase of 154%5. In Australia, the installation of PV systems over Sydney can reduce summer maximum temperatures by up to 1 °C, and the indirect benefit associated with the temperature changes is between net AUD$230,000 and $3,380,0006. Over the extended Mediterranean area, a simulation shows that securing desalinated water for up to about 200 million people in the region is technically possible using PV only7. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore the utilization potential of solar radiation. This study will focus on exploring the solar radiation collecting potential of light harvesting systems.

Stationary light harvesting system

A solar photovoltaic power generation system is composed of solar-cell arrays (photovoltaic modules), controllers, batteries, inverters and other parts8. The solar-cell array converts solar energy into electricity and sends it to the battery for storage9. Most solar installations use stationary installation. To optimize the solar power generation systems, it is necessary to consider how to make full use of solar energy resources and reduce energy loss10. Research shows that, photovoltaic panels should be perpendicular to solar irradiation to harvest the maximum possible power11. Its energy generation can be improved if the solar-cell panel is installed with the optimum angle12. Most scholars focus their research on a fixed area or take more time and come to an inaccurate conclusion (Table 1). In the northern hemisphere, the optimal tilt angle for south-facing PV panels is approximately equal to the latitude13. For latitudes below 65°, the yearly optimal tilt angle of a south-facing fixed panel is approximately equal to 0.9 multiplied by the latitude14. In a coastal region of South China, over a year, solar collectors should be placed with a tilt angle of 2.8° greater than the latitude with the azimuth facing south-west15. The optimal tilt angle also varies at different times in the same place. For example, for the case of Uganda, the annual optimal tilt angle and average monthly optimal angle vary in the range 0.0–5.1 and 2.1–11.216. Consequently, the tilt angles of solar panels should be adjusted monthly, seasonal or the yearly to extract the maximum energy from solar panels17. However, it is very difficult to obtain the optimal tilt angle of PV panels in all locations accurately and quickly, and usually we will not consider that sunshine duration is changing every day. This study considers that PV panels in regions from 66°34′N to 66°34′S can be placed at any valid angle to maximize the collection of solar radiation, with the sunshine duration changing every day. For stationary light harvesting systems, considering the ease of installation and adjustment, the annual optimal tilt angle is the focus, which depends on the geographical location.

Intelligent light tracking harvesting system

Stationary installation products cannot adjust the position of the solar panel according to the change of the solar angle, which leads to the low solar energy collection and utilization18, Consequently, more and more people pay attention to the research and development and improvement of intelligent tracking systems. Nowadays, various light tracking solar systems have emerged, and they have automatic monitoring systems to regulate the elevation and orientation angles of solar panels to keep them perpendicular to the sun at all times19, or maintains the solar panels at an angle that produces the best power output20 (Fig. 1). As proven through experiments, in Jordan, two-axis automatic tracking light harvesting systems can collect 41.34% more energy than stationary light harvesting systems tilted 32° to the south for 4 days21. And in Xanthi, the measured collected solar energy on the moving surface was significantly larger (up to 46.46%) compared with the fixed surface22. In Baghdad, the tracking CPC collector showed a better performance with an increase in the collected energy of up to 75% compared with an identical fixed collector23. However, few people use intelligent tracking harvesting systems because their advantages have not been widely recognized. It is difficult to continue the experiment for a long time (Table 2). Moreover, there is no definite results on the optimal placement angle of stationary photovoltaic panels and how much the energy collected by a light tracking solar system can be increased at most in different regions. Thus, this phenomenon has more research value, providing more scholars with theoretical basis and helping reflect on the source of error in the experiment process.

Research methodology

Solar energy reaches the earth

Solar energy generally refers to the radiation energy of sunlight, and solar radiation is an integral part of different renewable energy resources24. The utilization of solar energy has two ways: passive utilization (photothermal conversion) and photoelectric conversion25. And the amount of extraterrestrial horizontal solar radiation is the energy of solar radiation at a horizontal surface of the earth. This energy is the main source of solar energy and the basis of all life on earth26 (Fig. 2).

The relationship between solar energy and extraterrestrial horizontal solar radiation.

The amount of solar radiant energy reaching the earth’s surface is affected by the earth-sun distance r, and the declination angle of the sun δ (Fig. 3). Since the earth-sun distance is precisely known on any day of a year, this distance can be generally shown as (r0/r)226. And the mean earth-sun distance r0 is also known as the astronomical unit (1 AU = 1.496 × 103kM)26. The plane of the earth’s orbit around the sun is called the ecliptic plane, while the earth’s spin axis is called the polar axis27. The polar axis intersects the ecliptic plane at an angle of 66.5°, and this angle remains constant throughout the revolution28. This is the reason for the difference in the height the sun at noon each day, and the consequent change of the seasons.

The orbit of the earth around the sun.

As for the horizontal solar radiation, geographical location is one of the most important affecting factors29. In the regions near the equator, the direct angle of the sun is large, so the amount of solar radiation is high30. While in the polar regions, the direct angle of the sun is small, so the amount of solar radiation is relatively low. With the increase of altitudes, the solar radiation becomes stronger31.

Calculation and discussion

Based on the calculation theory of extraterrestrial horizontal solar radiation, this study designs a simulation model. Firstly, the optimal tilt angle of the stationary photovoltaic panels is calculated, and then the amount of solar radiation received by intelligent tracking photovoltaic panels and stationary photovoltaic panels is compared respectively in the regions from 66°34′N to 66°34′S.

Considering the actual irradiation situation in the most areas which are suitable for solar power generation (66°34′N to 66°34′S), this study assumes the effective sunshine time in the morning and afternoon of a day is the same. Therefore, in the calculation of the model in this paper, the interval of time variation is 12-Ti2 to 12+Ti2. According to the solar hour angle calculation formula (24-h time system), the value interval of solar hour angle ω for the solar cell array is ω=π12∗t-12-Tiπ24,Tiπ24. The only factor in this paper that can be interfered with by artificial placement is the angle between the plane facing the equator and the local horizontal plane β. Due to the different latitudes in different places, the local latitude φ can be queried when placing stationary photovoltaic panels in different places. Therefore, in this calculation, one of the variables studied is β-φ, set x=β-φ-π2≤x≤π2.

Model validation

To verify the predictions of the model (SECM) in this paper, three different real-world experimental results are selected for comparison21–23, and their conclusions are based on direct experimental data, with no element of simulation (Table 11). The comparison results show that there is little difference between the predicted value of the model in this paper and the experimental results in the past. As for the experiment 1, it selects 4 similar days for experiment, containing almost all the effective light duration, and averages the experimental results to reduce errors. Moreover, the tilt angle of the fixed solar panel selected is similar to that predicted by the model in this paper, that is why the results of experiment 1 are very similar to those in this paper. As for the experiment 2, there is little difference between the results of solar radiation collecting efficiency enhancement by light tracking harvesting systems in the three days of experiment and the predicted value in this paper, and the change trend of the results is also consistent. However, the angle of the fixed solar panel selected by it is 6.5° different from that predicted in this paper, which may be the main reason for the difference between its results and those in this paper. As for the experiment 3, its experimental duration is very short, not including all the effective light hours of a day, and the authors do not specify the average value of the conclusions. But without doubt that the predicted value in this paper is within the range of its conclusions.

Conclusions

This paper proposes a solar energy comparison model to optimize the solar radiation collection model in an ideal state that lasts for a whole year, which can quickly obtain the optimal tilt angle of photovoltaic panels and the solar radiation collecting efficiency enhancement of intelligent tracking photovoltaic panels in different geographical locations, considering the sunshine duration changes every day. The patterns are discovered: (1) In the regions from 66°34′N to 66°34′S, the amount of solar radiation collected by intelligent tracking photovoltaic panels in a whole year can be increased by at least 63.55%, up to 122.51% than that collected by stationary photovoltaic panels. And the higher the latitude, the more efficiency will be improved. (2) The influence of the change of sunshine duration cannot be ignored. Most of the previous theoretical calculation assume that the daily sunshine duration in a certain region was fixed in a year, but this hypothesis is only satisfied with the region on the equator, while the daily sunshine duration in other regions is changing. (3) The advantages of intelligent light tracking photovoltaic panels are more obvious in areas with high latitude, long and changeable sunshine duration.

Therefore, the research on intelligent light tracking harvesting systems can deserve a lot of attention, after people everywhere understanding its amazing potential. This paper strongly advocates the use of intelligent light tracking harvesting systems because it can obviously bring greater solar energy benefits, especially in high latitudes. The model (SECM) in this paper is easy to use, and can help more people understand how photovoltaic can quickly get the optimal tilt angle and solar radiation collecting efficiency enhancement by intelligent light tracking harvesting systems, including people who are not familiar with this field. Future photovoltaic panel installations can refer to the model simulation results in this paper. Moreover, the figures of energy increase in this paper can provide a theoretical basis for the research in the future and help reflect on the errors or energy losses in the experimental process and product use.

However, there are still some research limitations in this paper: (1) This paper fails to obtain the data of daily earth-sun distance for calculation, so it is assumed that the daily earth-sun distance is constant, but this does not affect the conclusion about the solar radiation collecting enhancement of intelligent light tracking photovoltaic panels. If specific earth-solar distance data can be obtained in the future, the other results will be more accurate. (2) What is calculated in this paper is the amount of solar radiation collected by the PV panels in the extraterrestrial plane, where there is no dry torsion of the atmosphere, so the calculation results in this paper ignore atmospheric interference. In fact, the amount of solar radiation received by photovoltaic panels is affected by the atmosphere, but this does not affect the conclusion about how much the efficiency of the solar radiation collected by the intelligent tracking photovoltaic panels can be improved in the same area and the same time, compared with stationary photovoltaic panels. However, it will affect the conclusion at different times and in different regions. (3) The device rotation assumed in this paper is a comprehensive sensitive capture of sunlight without dead angle, without considering the problems of energy consumption, sunlight reflection and other mechanical aspects of the device.

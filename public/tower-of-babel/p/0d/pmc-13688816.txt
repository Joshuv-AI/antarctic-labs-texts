Fear of disease progression and associated factors of patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis: a latent profile analysis
Authors: Yaojing Ma, Shaoying Sheng, Qun Ye, Ying Ren, Qunfei Yu, Zhan Wang, Jing Wu, Chengzhen Liang, Zhiwei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze the current status and potential profile categories of the fear of disease progression (FoP) in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) patients, and to explore the associating factors of different profiles.MethodsConvenience sampling was used to select AIS patients of a tertiary hospital in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province as the survey patients for the period from August 2024 to March 2025. The survey was conducted using the General Information Questionnaire, the Fear of Progression Questionnaire-Short Form (FoP-Q-SF), the Family APGAR Index (APGAR), and the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (BIPQ). The potential profile analysis of the FoP among AIS patients was carried out, and the associating factors were explored by univariate analysis and multivariate logistic regression analysis.ResultsIn this study, a total of 307 patients with AIS were investigated, of whom 48.2% had FoP that was heterogeneous and categorized into three subgroups: the low FoP– stable psychological group, moderate FoP–disease distress group, and high FoP–dysfunctional group, accounting for 30.6%, 45.9%, and 23.5%, respectively. Logistic regression results showed that FoP in patients with AIS was associated with gender, pain level, cobb angle, BMI, disease perception, brace treatment status, and family function. (all P < 0.05). Furthermore, female, wearing braces, larger Cobb angle, lower BMI, higher pain levels, and high BIPQ scores were more likely to be associated with the medium fear Fop distress group or the high Fop-dysfunctional group. Patients with high APGAR scores were significantly associated with the low FoP-stable psychological group.ConclusionsThe level of FoP in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients is high and heterogeneous. Healthcare professionals should focus on patients in the high FoP–dysfunctional group and carry out targeted interventions based on the psychological characteristics of FoP in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients as early as possible in accordance with the characteristics of the different categories of patients and their associating factors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-026-07263-x.Keywords: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, Fear of disease progression, Associating factors, Latent profile analysis

Introduction

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is the most common spinal deformity in the transition from childhood to adolescence, occurring between the ages of 10 and 18 years and accounting for 80% of all scoliosis types, with a global prevalence rate of 0.47%–5.2%, and females being approximately 1.5–3 times more likely than males to have AIS [1]. Scoliosis is diagnosed when the curvature of the spine in the coronal plane (Cobb’s angle) is greater than 10º, with a Cobb’s angle of 10º to 25º for mild scoliosis, 25º to 45º for moderate scoliosis, and > 45º for severe scoliosis [2].

Research indicates that the number of primary and secondary school students with scoliosis in China has exceeded 5 million [3]. Scoliosis is not easy to detect because of the lack of obvious symptoms in the early stage, but the condition can deteriorate rapidly during the adolescent growth spurt, and it has become known as “the third major health problem” due to the dangers that it poses to the health of Chinese adolescents after obesity and myopia [4].

Furthermore, AIS patients have asymmetry between the left and right sides of the body, often accompanied by high and low shoulders, hunchback or razor back and other poor posture, abnormal BMI, and body shape that is different from the norm [5, 6]. Adolescents are also more sensitive psychologically and they tend to worry about the appearance of their body shape, which invariably erodes the self-esteem of AIS patients, exacerbates psychological stress, causes psychological trauma, and triggers negative emotions, such as anxiety, depression, and fear [7]. Lee et al. [8] noted that patients with AIS are prone to psychological distress and an increased risk of psychiatric disorders due to dissatisfaction with their appearance.

The angle of scoliosis increases rapidly during adolescence, and excessive curvature predisposes to complications such as persistent pain, and impaired gas exchange function [9]. In addition, AIS not only causes economic burdens but also creates social burdens [10].

Under the combined effect of these factors, AIS patients are susceptible to different degrees of fear of progression (FoP), which is prevalent in patients with chronic diseases and accompanies them throughout the course of their illness. Excessive FoP over a long period of time is one of the most critical stressors for patients with chronic diseases, and it can lead to dysfunction, increased negative emotions, and decreased treatment compliance and quality of life, which in turn affects the treatment and prognosis of the disease [11].

Currently, most studies on FoP focus on gastric cancer patients [12], stroke patients [13], and obstructive sleep apnea hypoventilation syndrome patients [14], coronary heart disease patients [15], and no research has been carried out to examine the current status of FoP and its associating factors among adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients while ignoring different levels of heterogeneity. Latent profile analysis can determine the underlying characteristics of the survey respondents and classify them according to their exogenous variables. It can also show the proportion of the number of people in each category, allowing for further analysis of the characteristics and associating factors of different categories of the population, in order to design targeted interventions. Therefore, this study used latent profile analysis to identify the different categories of FoP in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients, and explore their associating factors in terms of disease characteristics, disease perception, and family caring, with the aim of providing a theoretical basis for healthcare professionals to accurately construct an intervention program for FoP in AIS patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study used a convenience sampling method to select adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients who were treated in the outpatient and inpatient orthopedic departments of a tertiary general hospital in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Meets diagnostic criteria for AIS (Cob angle > 10°) [16]; (2) Age 10–19 years; (3) Have not experienced a major life shock (e.g., death of a loved one, etc.) within 6 month; and (4) Able to understand and answer questionnaires.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Persons with severe hearing impairment, mental illness, and impaired consciousness; (2) Combined serious heart, lung, liver and kidney diseases; and (3) Participants in the same study.

According to the Kendall sample size calculation method [17], the sample size should be 5–10 times the number of independent variables. A total of 25 independent variables were included in this study, considering 20% of the invalid questionnaires, and the required sample size was 25 × (5 ~ 10)× 20%=150 ~ 300 people. A total of 307 patients were included in this study.

Procedures

The consent of the hospital ethics committee was first obtained. The patients were strictly screened according to the nadir criteria, and the purpose, significance, and methods of this study were explained to the adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients and guardians who met the inclusion criteria, informing them that this investigation followed the principles of anonymity and confidentiality. For our research, informed consent to participate in the study should be obtained from participants (or their parent or legal guardian in the case of children under 16). The questionnaires were distributed after obtaining the written informed consent of the patients and guardians. After completing the questionnaire, the researcher collected the questionnaires on the spot and examined, if any part is omitted, it will be supplemented on the spot.

Measures

Sociodemographic characteristics

The socio-demographic characteristics questionnaire was designed by the researcher. The data included patients’ age, educational level, place of residence, sex, pain level, religion, type of medical insurance, cobb angle, having undergone surgery or not, monthly income per capita, curve tipe, brace treatment status, duration since diagnosis, prior mental health history and BMI.

FoP-Q-SF

The Fear of Progression Questionnaire – Short Form (FoP-Q-SF) was developed by Mehnert et al. [18] and the Chinese version was translated and tested by Wu et al. [19] to measure FoP in patients with cancer and chronic diseases. It consists of two dimensions: physical health (six items) and social family (six items), with a total of 12 items. Items 1 ~ 3, 5, and 9 ~ 10 are physiological health dimensions, and items 4, 6 ~ 8, and 11 ~ 12 are social and family dimensions. Each item was scored on a five-point Likert scale (1 ~ 5 points) with a total score of 12 ~ 60 points, with higher scores indicating high levels of FoP: <34 points for functional phobia disease progression, and ≥ 34 points for dysfunctional fear disease progression. The Cronbach’s alpha for the scale in this study was 0.94; construct validity was 0.96; X²/df was 1.633; NFI was 0.964; IFI was 0.986; TLI was 0.979; CFI was 0.986; RMSEA was 0.045.

BIPQ

The Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (BIPQ) was compiled by Broadbent et al. [20] in 2006 and translated and revised by Sun et al. [21] in 2015. It is used to assess the patient’s cognitive representation of the disease, emotional representation, and awareness and understanding of the disease. The questionnaire consists of three dimensions with a total of nine items. Items 1 to 8 are scored using an 11-point Likert scale (0 ~ 10 points) in which items 3, 4, and 7 are inversely scored: item 9 is an open-ended question (ask patients to write down the three main causes of the disease). The score ranges from 0 ~ 80 points, and the higher the score, the higher the patient’s negative perception of the disease. The Cronbach’s alpha for the scale in this study was 0.93.

APGAR

The Family APGAR Index (APGAR) was developed by Smilkstein et al. [22], and sinicized by Lv Fan et al. [23]. The scale consists of five dimensions (fitness, cooperation, growth, affectivity, and intimacy), and each dimension corresponds to one item. Each question is scored 2 for “often,” 1 for “sometimes,” and 0 for “almost rarely,” where < 3 points indicates severe impairment in family functioning, 4 ~ 6 points indicates moderate impairment in family functioning, and 7 ~ 10 points indicates good family functioning. The Cronbach’s alpha for the scale in this study was 0.73; construct validity was 0.79; X²/df was 1.291; NFI was 0.976; IFI was 0.994; TLI was 0.989; CFI was 0.994; RMSEA was 0.031.

NRS

The Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) was compiled by Yan [24] in 2014. This scale is used by patients to assess their own pain level. Among them, 0 points means “no pain”; 1 ~ 3 points means “mild pain”; 4 ~ 6 points indicate “moderate pain”; A score of 7 ~ 10 indicates “severe pain”, and a higher score indicates a stronger pain.

Statistical analysis

Potential profiling was performed using Mplus 8.3 software [25]. The FoP-Q-SF scale items demonstrated normality, with a skewness of 0.270 and a kurtosis of −0.221, and we modeled these items in mplus as continuous variables. The scores of the 12 items in the FoP-Q-SF were used as explicit indicators, and 1 ~ 5 profiles were selected for analysis. SPSS version 26.0 software was used for the statistical analysis, and the normally distributed measurement data were described by mean ± standard deviation. Non-normally distributed measurement data, using median and quartile tables; count or grade data were described in frequency or percentage; and comparisons between groups were performed using the X2 test or the Kruskal-Wills H test. Univariate analysis and multivariate logistic regression analysis were carried out to explore the associating factors of disease progression in different profiles, and the difference was statistically significant with P < 0.05.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine (approval number: IR2024532). The principles of voluntariness and confidentiality were followed. Before the survey, the investigator explained the purpose and significance of the study to the adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients and guardians, and the questionnaires were distributed after the patients or guardians signed the informed consent form. For our research, informed consent to participate in the study should be obtained from participants (or their parent or legal guardian in the case of children under 16). The questionnaires were collected on the spot and checked for data completeness and accuracy.

Results

Participant information and characteristics

A total of 330 questionnaires were distributed in this study, of which 23 invalid questionnaires (including irregular responses, time to fill out < 3 min, and missing responses) were excluded. A total of 307 valid questionnaires were retrieved, with an effective recovery rate of 93.0%. Of these patients, 67.8% were female (n = 208) and 32.2% were male (n = 99); 51.1% (n = 157) and 49.9% (n = 150) were from urban and rural areas, respectively; and more than half of the patients were underweight (n = 166), with only 32.2% being normal weight (Table 1).

Discussion

The FoP in AIS patients was significantly heterogeneous and divided into three potential profiles

This study investigated the Fop of AIS patients and found differences in Fop scores among different populations (such as pediatric cancer patients, adolescent epilepsy patients, etc.). Among the 307 AIS patients surveyed, the average Fop score was 34.05 ± 7.35, which was significantly higher than Zhang et al.‘s [26] survey of Fop in pediatric cancer patients (Fop = 32.70), which may be due to the fact that most of the patients surveyed by Zhang et al. [26] were male patients (51.2%), and most of the patients we surveyed were female patients (67.8%), who were more concerned about their appearance and cared about the negative evaluation of their appearance by others (hunchback due to scoliosis, Abnormal posture), the heart is often more sensitive, which increases psychological pressure and makes them more afraid of the progression of the disease. Sun et al. [27] pointed out that the Fop of female adolescent epilepsy patients was 36.98 ± 3.08, and the Fop of male adolescent epilepsy patients was 33.00 ± 4.88. We found that both were higher than the Fop of female AIS patients (35.64 ± 7.05) and the Fop of male AIS patients (30.71 ± 6.65) in this study. The reason may be that epilepsy is sudden and unpredictable, and patients often worry about sudden seizures in public places and have a strong sense of shame, while AIS progresses relatively slowly, and patients have longer time to adapt to the disease and plan treatment. In addition, epilepsy is caused by abnormal discharges of neurons in the brain. Seizures in temporal lobe epilepsy directly involve the amygdala (the brain’s center for fear), causing patients to experience intense fear during seizures. AIS (Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis) is due to structural deformities of the spine in three-dimensional space, including abnormal alignment of the vertebrae in the axial, sagittal, and coronal planes, and usually does not directly affect the center brain. It is evident that the level of Fop varies among patients with different diseases. Therefore, healthcare professionals should implement targeted interventions based on each patient’s specific condition. It is crucial to promptly assess and monitor the Fop status in AIS patients and take evidence-based measures to reduce it.

In the low FoP-stable psychological group, the FoP-Q-SF scores of all patients were relatively low, ranging from 1.70 to 2.46, with an average score of 2.15. This group accounted for 30.6% of the total sample and scored relatively low on items 4 (“Concerning the impact on future work”) and 7 (“Concerning future activities and daily life that will rely on strangers”). This indicates that patients with AIS have a better ability to adapt to the disease and are less worried about future career prospects. Although the level of FoP in this group was relatively low, medical staff should still pay attention to their psychological state and prevent progression to the high FoP-dysfunctional group.

In the moderate FoP-disease distress group, the FoP-Q-SF scores of all patients ranged from 2.45 to 3.15, with an average score of 2.83. This group accounted for 45.9% of the total sample, which is relatively high. The scores for item 8 (“Worrying about not being able to pursue one’s hobbies in the future”), item 9 (“Worried about some major treatments during the disease treatment process”) and item 10 (“Worried about the treatment damaging one’s body”) were relatively high and showed a gradually increasing trend, indicating concerns during the disease treatment process and fears about the possibility of having to give up hobbies in the future. In addition, these patients may also have been worried about the side effects brought by orthosis correction or surgical treatment. Therefore, medical staff should strive to educate such patients and provide them with relevant disease knowledge, so as to enhance disease awareness.

In the high FoP-dysfunctional group, the FoP-Q-SF scores of all patients ranged from 3.26 to 4.07, with an average score of 3.71. This indicates that the overall FoP of patients in this category was at a high level. This group accounted for 23.5% of the total sample. The scores were relatively high for items 1 (“Worried about disease progression”), 3 (“Afraid of pain”), and 10 (“Some symptoms such as increased heart rate and stomachache”). These findings suggest that patients have significant concerns about the disease progression, as well as concerns about lumbar pain, abnormal appearance, impaired cardiac and pulmonary function caused by the increase in the angle of scoliosis, and shortness of breath during activities or while lying down when suffering from severe scoliosis. Therefore, medical staff should promote education and engagement for patients in this category, inform them of relevant disease knowledge, and enhance their disease awareness. Therefore, medical staff should provide multimodal analgesia for this group, including drug treatment and non-drug treatment, and monitor cardiac and pulmonary functioning in a timely manner. If necessary, surgical treatment should be performed to prevent the increase of the scoliosis angle and reduce the FoP of patients.

Analysis of potential associating factors of FoP in adolescent patients with AIS

Female, severe scoliosis, low BMI, high pain level, AIS with high BIPQ scores, and wear brace are more likely to associated with the high FoP-dysfunctional group or moderate FoP-disease distress group

The results of this study show that compared with male patients, female patients were more likely to be associated with the medium fear Fop distress group or the high Fop-dysfunctional group, which is similar to the findings of Zhang’s research [28]. The potential reasons for this phenomenon may include the fact that patients with scoliosis frequently exhibit physical characteristics such as uneven shoulders, kyphosis, and asymmetry between the left and right sides of the body. Additionally, adolescent females typically place greater emphasis on their appearance and tend to be more sensitive. They are highly attentive to changes in their appearance and are prone to being affected by negative evaluations from others regarding their physical appearance, which in turn amplifies their FoP. Hassani et al. [29] found that acceptance and commitment therapy for breast cancer patients for 770 min once a week for a total of 5 weeks can help patients accept the facts, better adapt to the disease, and reduce Fop. This suggests that medical staff should pay greater attention to female AIS, adopt acceptance and commitment therapy for female patients, as well as emotional support and health lectures to help them understand the knowledge of the disease, and use more positive language to guide these patients to adopt a more optimistic view of changes in body appearance, in order to face the disease with a more positive outlook and minimize negative emotions (Table 4).

Our study found that patients with severe scoliosis have high levels of FoP. Due to the increase in the cobb angle, the patient’s thorax is severely deformed, thoracic cavity volume is reduced, lung compliance is decreased, and ventilation function is compromised. Meanwhile, the risk of cardiovascular diseases for the patient also increases [30]. Besides, patients with severe scoliosis often need spinal fusion correction surgery. The high surgical cost places a serious financial burden on families, who are increasingly afraid of the progression of the disease [31]. Oetgen et al. [32] pointed out that early screening of patients can detect AIS early, start treatment (such as wearing a brace) as soon as possible, prevent major surgery, and reduce the burden on patients and families.Therefore, it is recommended that the adolescent group undergo scoliosis screening as early as possible, detect scoliosis in time, and prevent the increase of scoliosis angle.

The findings of this study reveal that patients with lower BMI were more likely to be associated with the high Fop - dysfunctional group. A study [5] on the relationship between body weight and idiopathic scoliosis in adolescents has indicated that lower BMI is associated with a higher incidence of AIS. Herhaus et al. [33] found that people with underweight and overweight conditions are more likely to be diagnosed with anxiety disorders. Patients with AIS are usually of a slender build. Due to having relatively less subcutaneous fat, their spinal deformities are more likely to be noticed. Adolescents in puberty are often very concerned about their body shape and appearance, and are more afraid of the progression of the disease. This suggests that healthcare workers should regularly monitor the body weight of AIS patients, identify those with abnormal weight early and intervene with the help of the nutrition department. Patients with underweight should be advised to consume more high-quality proteins (fish, eggs, and legumes), and supplement calcium and vitamin D daily. Patients with obesity should be instructed to control calorie intake (reduce sugar and saturated fat intake), maintain regular sleep patterns, avoid prolonged sitting, and reduce the risk of spinal curvature progression. This will also help reduce FoP among AIS patients.

This study found that pain levels were positively correlated with Fop in AIS patients, which is consistent with the research results of Michalski et al. [34]. Zhang et al.‘s [35] study on the intensity of back pain in AIS patients pointed out that the prevalence of pain in patients with mild to moderate AIS was 68%, and the incidence of AIS patients with NRS score of > 3 exceeded 27.89%. Patients with AIS often suffer from varying degrees of pain. Long-term pain not only has a negative impact on the mental and psychological state of AIS patients, but also reduces their quality of life. Lu et al. [36] found that mindfulness stress reduction therapy can change the brain’s ability to perceive and regulate pain stimuli, achieve the effect of relieving pain and reducing negative emotions. This indicates that medical staff should promptly and accurately assess the pain level of AIS patients, and select appropriate analgesic methods based on the patients’ pain severity. For instance, when patients have mild pain, distraction techniques (mindfulness stress reduction therapy, virtual reality technology, music therapy, relaxation training) can be adopted. When the pain is severe, it is necessary to inform the doctor immediately and use drug analgesia (Table 4).

The findings of this study revealed that AIS patients with high BIPQ have a higher risk of Fop. Liu et al.‘s [37] research also indicated that disease perception was positively correlated with FoP. Due to the long course and recurrence of the disease, negative perceptions among patients are high, which intensifies anxiety and contributes to a negative psychology, leading to higher levels of FoP. Zheng et al. [38] conducted a 10-week cognitive behavioral therapy treatment on patients with atrial fibrillation and found that cognitive behavioral therapy can improve patients’ self-efficacy and correct distorted disease cognition. It can be seen that in clinical treatment and nursing, it is necessary to help AIS patients to form a correct perception of the disease by popularizing scoliosis-related knowledge, answering patients’ doubts in the process of diagnosis and treatment in a timely manner, and helping them to focus on healthy emotional regulation, so as to reduce FoP (Table 4).

This study indicates that AIS patients wearing braces have higher Fop scores than those not wearing braces. Chen et al. [39] pointed out that due to long-term wearing of braces, the incidence of body image disorder in AIS patients is higher than that of normal adolescents. A study on the impact of brace treatment on trunk appearance perception and quality of life in AIS patients pointed out that wearing a brace can bring physical discomfort to patients, limit daily life, increase stress and anxiety in patients, and wear them for a long time will increase patients’ pain feelings [40]. It can be seen that wearing a brace will cause pain to the patient, change the body image, and affect the patient’s quality of life. Therefore, it is recommended that medical workers provide daily wearing skills for AIS patients, and provide long-term follow-up for patients with negative emotions in brace treatment, and answer their concerns in a timely manner.

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis patients high APGAR scores were more likely to be associated with the low FoP-stable psychological adaptation group

In addition, AIS patients with higher APGAR scores were more likely to be classified into the low FoP-stable psychological group, which is consistent with the research results of Zhuang et al. [41], emphasizing the significant role of family functions in FoP. Good family functioning is an important protective factor for the mental health of AIS patients. Emotional comfort and emotional support among family members can reduce negative affect and help AIS patients develop confidence and positive coping strategies. Zhuang et al. [41] pointed out that the higher the APGAR level of breast cancer patients, the lower their fear of disease progression. Therefore, family-centered intervention measures (e.g., encouraging family members to spend more time with patients so that they can feel the warmth of family; guide family members to pay attention to the patient’s emotional changes, provide timely emotional support when the patient is depressed, etc.) should be adopted to assist patients in building a complete family support system, enabling them to receive more family support and thereby improving their FoP.

Study limitations

There are some limitations that need to be noted in this study. This research adopted a cross-sectional design and only examined the current status of FoP in AIS patients at a certain point in time, meaning that it lacks dynamic tracking of relevant variables. Moreover, the sample in this study was recruited from only one hospital in China, In addition, which may limit the generalizability of the data (e.g., 67.8% of women with AIS in this study and 70.4% of women with AIS in this region, ect.), The sample of this study was from one hospital (e.g., 67.8% of female AIS patients in this study, 70.4% of female AIS patients in this region, etc.), which may be because the patient went to the nearest hospital for convenience. Therefore, future longitudinal studies can be conducted to dynamically track the trajectory of FoP in AIS patients and expand the investigation scope to conduct multi-center studies to ensure the diversity of sample information.

Conclusions

This study found that patients with AIS have a relatively serious problem of FoP. The LPA analysis confirmed that the FoP condition of AIS patients has obvious population heterogeneity and correlated with gender, pain level, Cobb angle, BMI, disease perception, brace treatment status, and family function. It is imperative that medical staff formulate targeted intervention measures based on the potential profiles and associating factors of AIS patients, in order to alleviate FoP among patients. At the same time, prospective evaluations can be conducted on AIS patients for interventions such as Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, and Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy, in order to assess the effectiveness and sustainability of these interventions.

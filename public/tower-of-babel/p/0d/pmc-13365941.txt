IFPHOR0095 Data-Sharing Barriers in Zoonotic Disease Surveillance: Why Human and Animal Systems Still Operate in Silos
Authors: Astra Ralph Dsouza, Srishtika Mohana Ganesh Murthy, Sahithya Ranganatha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundZoonotic threats require rapid detection across human, animal and environmental sectors, yet surveillance systems often remain fragmented. Delays in sharing information between public health and veterinary services can weaken early warning, slow response and increase the risk of spillover events escalating into larger outbreaks.MethodsA rapid narrative review of recent literature on One Health surveillance, governance and intersectoral coordination was conducted to identify recurring barriers to data sharing between human and animal health systems. Evidence was synthesised thematically, focusing on legal, technical, institutional and workforce-related barriers.ResultsFour main barriers were identified. First, incompatible digital systems and non-standardised reporting formats limit timely exchange of information. Second, unclear governance structures and data ownership concerns reduce willingness to share data across sectors. Third, legal and ethical constraints, including confidentiality rules and fragmented reporting obligations, can delay collaboration. Fourth, professional silos and uneven resources weaken routine communication and joint decision-making. Together, these barriers create gaps in situational awareness and slow the identification of emerging zoonotic threats.DiscussionThe findings suggest that One Health surveillance is constrained less by scientific capability than by structural and organisational failures. Improving interoperability, clarifying accountability and creating routine cross-sector reporting mechanisms may strengthen early warning and outbreak preparedness. Framing data sharing as a core public health function, rather than an optional partnership, is essential if zoonotic surveillance is to become genuinely integrated.

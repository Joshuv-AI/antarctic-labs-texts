Comparative evaluation of spirulina and lycopene as an adjuvant to intralesional corticosteroid injection therapy in the management of oral submucous fibrosis: A pilot study
Authors: Asha Ramnath Chowdhar, Kavita Wadde, Sanpreet Singh Sachdev, Siddhesh Latke, Barun Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) is a chronic, progressive condition characterized by fibrosis of the oral mucosa, leading to restricted mouth opening and significant impairment in quality of life. Current treatment modalities lack a universally accepted protocol, prompting the need for adjunctive therapies to enhance treatment outcomes.Objective:To compare the efficacy of spirulina and lycopene as antioxidant adjuvants to intralesional corticosteroid therapy in the management of OSMF.Materials and Methods:A prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial was conducted with 30 participants diagnosed with Grade III OSMF. Patients were randomly assigned to two groups: Group I received spirulina 500 mg tablets daily, and Group II received lycopene 8 mg tablets daily. Both groups received weekly intralesional corticosteroid injections (Triamcinolone Acetonide 40 mg/ml) for 8 weeks. Clinical parameters, including mouth opening, burning sensation, recurrent ulceration, cheek flexibility, and the presence of fibrous bands, were assessed at baseline and at 3- and 6-month follow-ups.Results:Spirulina demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in mouth opening (33.60 ± 3.20 mm) compared to lycopene (30.73 ± 3.05 mm) (P = 0.01). Spirulina also showed superior results in reducing the burning sensation and enhancing cheek flexibility. No significant difference was observed in the reduction of fibrous bands between the groups. Both groups showed improvement in recurrent ulceration, but no significant difference was noted at the 6-month follow-up.Conclusion:Spirulina, due to its potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, is a more effective adjuvant to corticosteroid therapy in the management of OSMF compared to lycopene. Future research with larger, more diverse populations is warranted to validate these findings.

INTRODUCTION

Oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) is a condition characterized by restricted mouth opening due to extensive fibrosis of the oral mucosa. The condition drastically impacts the normal masticatory functioning of the affected individuals, leading to a compromised nutrition and overall quality of life.[1] Various etiological factors have been described for OSMF, the most common being Arecoline from Areca catechu (betel nut), particularly when mixed with pan and tobacco, while others such as tobacco and alcohol use, nutritional deficiencies, and genetic susceptibility also have been described.[234] The prevalence of OSMF is found to be particularly higher in Southeast Asian countries of India, Sri Lanka, and Taiwan, with emerging cases reported in Europe and North America.[1] A potential for malignant transformation has been associated with the condition, and thus, early diagnosis and prompt management are warranted.

The prodromal symptoms of OSMF include a burning sensation in the mouth when eating spicy food, ulcers or recurrent stomatitis, excessive salivation, impaired taste, and occasionally dry mouth with blistering pain.[5] Diagnosis of the condition is generally based upon several clinical signs including fibrous palpable bands in the oral cavity, whitening, and stiffness of the oral mucous membrane. Extensive research has been done on this topic, yet there is no universally accepted management protocol for OSMF. Cessation of tobacco areca nut or any other etiological agent is the foremost and crucial step in the management of OSMF.[2] However, habit cessation alone does not suffice in moderate to advanced cases.

Adjunctive treatment options comprise pharmacological and surgical interventions. Among the different treatment modalities, steroids have been shown to improve the symptoms as they act as immunosuppressive agents by opposing the action of soluble factors released by sensitized lymphocytes along with anti-inflammatory action. They prevent fibrosis by decreasing the proliferation of fibroblasts and collagen deposition.[67] Among various steroids, Triamcinolone is frequently employed owing to its high potency, duration of action, and decreased systemic absorption.[89] Even so, steroids alone are unable to achieve satisfactory results in the reversal of extensive fibrosis in OSMF patients.

The harmful chemicals in tobacco products and alcohol cause immunosuppression and the generation of reactive free radicals, which mediate phenotypic and genotypic alterations, ultimately leading to neoplasia. Therefore, antioxidants are included in the management of such conditions.[10] Natural remedies have recently gained popularity in the management of many diseases, including OSMF, due to their antioxidant effects, safety, low toxicity, and widespread patient acceptance.[3]

Newer adjunctive therapies like spirulina and lycopene are gaining attention as adjuvants to conventional OSMF treatment for their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.[11] Spirulina, a microalgae that is used in the diet of native populations of Africa and America routinely, is a rich natural source of proteins, carotenoids, and other micronutrients.[11] It contains phenolic acid, tocopherols, and beta-carotene, which are known to exhibit antioxidant properties.[12] Spirulina has been used for the treatment of several oral mucosal lesions with successful results. It has been primarily assessed in treating leukoplakia with promising results.[13] It has been successfully tried in the management of OSMF due to its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulating properties.[14] Recent studies by Chandran et al. (2022) and Katiyar et al. (2024) highlighted its role as an adjuvant antioxidant to corticosteroids for the management of patients with OSMF.[1516]

Lycopene is yet another carotenoid pigment found in fruits and vegetables. It acts by stimulation of the immune system or a direct action on tumor cells, increases lymphocyte tolerance to stress, and reduces inflammation.[17] According to a report, lycopene shows anticancer properties by upregulating connexin 43 and promoting gap junctional communication.[18] Research by Nayak et al.[19] found that participants treated with the lycopene group compared to the placebo group saw a substantial reduction in burning sensation and ulceration as well as an improvement in mouth opening. A recent systematic review by Ahmad et al. (2022)[20] advocated that Spirulina was safe and effective in the treatment of patients with OSMF but advised cautionary concerns about the publication bias across the included studies.

Even so, the evidence in favor of these natural remedies for the treatment of OSMF is minuscule and none of the earlier studies have compared their treatment efficacy. In this context, the present study was conducted to compare the efficacy of spirulina versus lycopene as an antioxidant adjuvant to intralesional corticosteroid injection therapy in the management of OSMF.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Study design

The present pilot study of a prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial was conducted in the institutional Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery over a period of 2 years from November 2020 to October 2022. The participants were recruited from the patients reporting to the outpatient department. The study was conducted in accordance with the modified Declaration of Helsinki, and informed consent was obtained from all the participants. The research protocol was approved by the local institutional ethical committee (Ref: GDCHMum/EthicalCommittee/4126/2020, dated 31/10/2020). The research study was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (Ref: CTRI/2021/05/033492).

Patient selection

Patients with prodromal symptoms or signs of OSMF were considered eligible for inclusion in the study. Informed written consent was obtained from patients and their relatives. Detailed case histories were recorded. Clinical diagnosis of OSMF was based on findings like a burning sensation in the mouth upon consumption of spicy or hot foods, difficulty in speech, swallowing, dryness, recurrent ulcers, vesicles and erosions in the oral cavity, partial or complete inability to protrude tongue, areas of fibrosis in the buccal mucosa, oropharynx, and oral cavity proper and degree of mouth opening. After diagnosis, staging was done according to the criteria given by Khanna and Andrade Clinical Classification (1995).[21]

The inclusion criteria comprised patients in the age range of 21–50 years, with a clinical diagnosis of Grade III OSMF, who were willing to quit the tobacco and/or areca nut habit. Participants with coexisting systemic illnesses and pregnant and lactating women were excluded from the study. Patients with contraindications or documented allergies to the drugs that were being used for the study were excluded. Additionally, patients with ongoing or a previous history of other treatments for OSMF were excluded from the study.

Randomization and allocation to interventions

Patients were randomly allocated to either of the two groups, Group I (experimental group), Spirulina, and Group II (control group), Lycopene, using computer-generated codes. Allocation concealment was achieved by providing medications for both groups in sealed opaque and identical containers. The patients were provided with the respective containers by another investigator depending on the group assigned. Thus, both the patient and the operator were blinded concerning the therapy provided.

For the patients in Group I, oral Spirulina was provided in the form of 500 mg tablets which were to be taken once daily. Patients in Group II were provided with oral lycopene 8 mg tablets, to be taken once daily. All the patients were also administered weekly intralesional corticosteroid injections (Triamcinolone Acetonide 40 mg/ml) by the same operator at multiple sites of blanching areas on the buccal mucosa, retromolar trigone, and adjacent soft palate weekly for a total of 8 weeks.

For intralesional injections, 1 ml of plain local anesthetic solution (2% lignocaine hydrochloride without adrenaline) was mixed with 1 ml triamcinolone acetonide (Kenacort 40 mg/ml) and injected submucosally at multiple sites of blanching areas using a 31-gauge needle syringe (Dispovan, HMD Healthcare, New Delhi, India). Patients were recalled every week for evaluation and were assessed for an increase in mouth opening, reduction in recurrent ulceration, burning sensation of the oral mucosa, fibrous bands at various locations, and improvement in cheek flexibility.

Outcome assessment

Mouth opening: Maximum mouth opening was measured in millimeters from the mesioincisal edge of the upper central incisor to the mesioincisal edge of the lower central incisor with the help of Boley’s gauge.

Burning sensation: Scoring of burning sensation in the mouth upon consumption of spicy or hot foods was done on a verbal complaint recording scale of 0, 1, and 2, where 0 means absent, 1 means reduced, and 2 means present as perceived by the patient subjectively reported by the patients.

Location and recurrent ulceration: Location and occurrence of ulcers/erosions/vesicles anywhere in the oral cavity were recorded and scored 1 when there were unilateral single, 2 when bilateral single, 3 when unilateral multiple, and 4 when bilateral multiple. Ulceration was recorded as 2 when present, 1 when reduced during the treatment period, and 0 as absent.

Cheek flexibility: Cheek flexibility was measured using Mathur and Jha’s method.[22] A line joining the tragus of the ear and the angle of the mouth was drawn. An imaginary perpendicular line from the outer canthus of the ipsilateral eye was extended downward to intersect the angle-tragus line. The point of intersection was marked as a reference point. This was done on the right and left sides. The distance between the two reference points was measured using measuring tape at normal centric occlusion as c. The subject was asked to blow the cheeks fully with lips closed, and the distance between the reference points was recorded as c’. The difference between the two values (c’ -c) was used as a measure of cheek flexibility in millimeters.

Follow-up of 3 months and 6 months postoperatively was recorded.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 21.0. A P value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Intergroup comparison of the two groups was done using independent t-test, and intragroup comparison was done using repeated measures ANOVA. The Chi-square test was used where the data were found to be qualitative.

RESULTS

A total of 15 participants were included in each group in the study. The mean age was observed to be 32.8 ± 8.70 years in the experimental group, with a range of 20–50 years, while the mean age was 32.46 ± 9.76 years in the control group, with a range of 23–50 years. Male predominance was seen in both groups, with 80% and 93.3% males in the experimental and control groups, respectively. The habit of Betel quid usage was the most common among the study population (approximately 50% of cases), followed by gutkha in 25% and paan in the rest.

Mouth Opening: The experimental group showed comparatively more improvement in mouth opening (33.60 ± 3.20) than the control group (30.73 ± 3.05) with a P value of 0.01, indicating superior efficacy of Spirulina. A statistically significant difference was noted in the mouth opening of patients between both groups with the patients in the experimental group having lower values at baseline and 1st week follow-up and higher values at the 2nd week, 8th week, 3-month, and 6-month follow-ups, respectively [Table 1]. This implies that even though the baseline mouth opening was less in patients of the experimental group, the improvement achieved in this parameter was greater in patients treated with Spirulina as compared to lycopene.

DISCUSSION

The present study is the first to compare the efficacy of spirulina and lycopene as antioxidant adjuvants to intralesional corticosteroid therapy in improving clinical parameters such as mouth opening, cheek flexibility, and reduction in ulcers, erosions, vesicles, burning sensation, and fibrous bands.

The probable reason for the patients of OSMF to be predominantly in this age group is attributable to the initiation of tobacco habit in adolescence among Indians and the time required for the condition to develop after chronic use of areca nut with or without tobacco.[23] According to epidemiological research, OSMF primarily affects people aged 40 to 49, and it is caused by chewing betel quid.[242526] However, other regional recent statistics show a shift in incidence to younger age groups (20–29 years).[2728] This has been attributed to the dynamic changes in the trends among young individuals due to increased social encounters and economic liberty in developing nations like India.[29] The overall age of the patients with OSMF in our study ranged from 20 to 50 years with a mean age of about 33 years. This was in alignment with the age groups included by earlier studies on OSMF ranging from 30 to 32 years.[3031]

Our study sample comprised 26 males and only 4 females, with the experimental group having 12 males and 3 females. Therefore, about 87% of the study population comprised male subjects with the male-to-female ratio being 6.5:1. The reason behind this striking predominance of the male population is the higher prevalence of smokeless tobacco and tobacco-related habits among males in India.[32] Karemore et al.[17] had a male-to-female ratio in their study population (n = 94) of 6.6:1, similar to that noted in our study population. Kanjani et al. had a male-to-female ratio as high as (10:1).[33] On the contrary, several studies exhibited female predominance, but this is less common and is due to local trends in areca nut consumption among females of a particular geographical location.[34]

In our study, it was noticed that all the patients had the habit of chewing gutkha or areca nut (pan masala). Out of 30 patients, betel quid was the most common habit being present in about 50% of the patients, followed by gutkha in 25% of patients and paan masala in the remainder. The betel quid components induce tissue inflammation, the proliferation of fibroblasts and collagen deposition, myofibroblast differentiation, and contraction, collagen crosslinks, and collagen phagocytosis, finally leading to the development of OSMF and oral cancer. A study by Patil et al.[32] reported that 62% of OSMF patients chewed betel nuts, while 28% consumed tobacco in some or the other chewing form.

Trismus, characterized by restricted mouth opening, is a hallmark feature of OSMF. There is no definite medical treatment protocol that can restore mouth opening to normal, but an improvement has been observed in various studies. In the present study, the patients treated with adjunctive spirulina showed a mean improvement in MIO from baseline 15.80 ± 1.32 mm to 33.60 ± 3.20 mm at 6 months follow-up, whereas the control group showed improvement in mean MO from baseline 17.73 ± 1.98 mm to 30.73 ± 3.05 mm at 6 months follow-up. Even though the baseline mouth opening was less in patients of the experimental group, the improvement achieved in this parameter was statistically significant in patients of the experimental group as compared to the control group. The improvement seen in the present study was believed to be attributed to the combination of spirulina with steroids that may exert its action synergistically. This improvement was statistically significant and consistent with previous studies that reported significant improvement in mouth opening with spirulina.[313233]

A burning sensation of the oral mucosae is another common symptom of OSMF, caused by atrophic epithelium. This renders underlying connective tissue stroma susceptible to irritation by hot and spicy food which is otherwise tolerable to normal mucosa. This coupled with inflammatory infiltrate accumulated in the stroma results in a burning sensation. In the present study, the mean reduction in the burning sensation of the experimental group achieved was (mean ± SD 2.33 ± 0.61 to 0.46 ± 0.51) from baseline to 6-month follow-up, whereas that in the control group was (mean ± SD 2.0 ± 0.00 to 1.0 ± 0.00) from baseline to 6-month follow-up. A statistically significant difference was noted in the reduction of burning sensation experienced by the patients between both groups with the patients in the experimental group having better results overall. This implies that even though the baseline burning sensation was higher in patients of the experimental group, the improvement achieved in this parameter was greater in patients of the experimental group as compared to the control group. This improvement in the experimental group is due to beta carotene, phenolic acid, tocopherols, and various micronutrients present in spirulina which improves epithelial integrity and induces redifferentiation of dysplastic epithelium.[35] Kopuri et al. and Patil et al. also reported statistically nonsignificant results with the lycopene group in their studies.[3637] In contrast, a recent systematic review reported a statistically significant improvement in burning sensation after lycopene therapy.[38]

Ulcerations, erosions, and vesicles were also monitored in this study. In our study, a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05) was noted in the recurrent ulcerations between both groups wherein the ulcerations were reduced in the control group as compared to the patients in the experimental group in the 3rd and 4th week follow-up visits. But at the end of the treatment and 6-month follow-up visits, no statistically significant difference was seen in both the groups. This can be attributed to the quitting of the associated habit as it reduces the irritation caused by keeping quid in contact with oral mucosa with considerable effect in the regression of the disease.[3637] However, our results contrast with three studies that found significant reductions in ulcers, erosions, and vesicles in patients treated with spirulina compared to the lycopene, aloe vera, and oxitard groups.[36]

There was no statistically significant reduction in fibrous bands noted in both groups. This can be attributed to mechanical insults due to the insertion of injection needles and the chemical irritation caused by the injected fluids which aggravate fibrosis.[39] However, no reduction in fibrous bands was noted in either of the groups in the present study; the improvement in cheek flexibility achieved was from 1.76 ± 0.39 at baseline to 2.78 ± 0.75 at 6 months follow-up in both the groups, which was statistically significant. Similar improvement in cheek flexibility was noted in earlier studies.[323437]

There are significant limitations to our study. Some of the researchers have obtained almost double the sample size than the average, which adds more credibility to their results. Because the current study was carried out in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, from 2020 to 2022, getting such a huge sample size was not feasible in due consideration of the circumstances. A stark disparity between the number of males and females in the study population makes our results more applicable to the male population.

Overall, by analyzing the findings in each group in terms of clinical parameters such as burning sensation, mouth opening, recurrent ulceration, and cheek flexibility, we discovered that the spirulina was superior to the lycopene group for the management of OSMF. This can be due to spirulina’s antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immune-modulating characteristics, as well as its high nutritional value. The current study clearly emphasizes that, in conjunction with habit counseling, Spirulina, an oral antioxidant, can be utilized as an adjuvant therapy in the initial management of OSMF patients and was well tolerated by oral submucous fibrosis patients without any side effects.

CONCLUSION

The present study provides valuable insights into the comparative efficacy of spirulina and lycopene as antioxidant adjuvants to intralesional corticosteroid injection therapy in managing OSMF. The improvements observed align with previous studies, reinforcing spirulina’s potential as an effective adjuvant in OSMF management, while lycopene was less effective overall. Future research with larger, more diverse populations and long-term follow-up studies is necessary to validate these findings and explore the underlying mechanisms further. Considering our findings, spirulina emerged as a more effective antioxidant adjuvant to corticosteroid therapy in managing OSMF compared to lycopene, contributing to the growing body of evidence supporting the use of natural remedies in treating chronic oral conditions and underscoring the need for continued exploration of alternative therapeutic options for OSMF.

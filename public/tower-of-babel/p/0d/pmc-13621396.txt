DAMFCMI: capturing cross-view interactions via hybrid attention for circRNA–MiRNA interaction prediction
Authors: Tao Bai, Xiupan Ma, Lanlan Sun, Zongwen Bai, Wendong Wang, Hang Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMotivationCircular RNAs (circRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) play pivotal roles in gene expression regulation, where understanding their interactions (CMIs) is essential for deciphering the molecular mechanisms behind cellular physiological and pathological states. Most existing approaches to CMI prediction are constrained by their reliance on shallow, single-view representations, while deep models typically align only on final embeddings, thereby neglecting the rich layer-wise interactions that are critical for capturing biological complexity.ResultsTo address these issues, we propose DAMFCMI, a novel method for CMI prediction. DAMFCMI characterizes circRNAs and miRNAs through three distinct feature views: sequence-based, attribute-based, and behavior-based features. A hybrid attention mechanism captures dependencies within individual views through multi-head self-attention and across views through cross-attention, enabling comprehensive modeling of feature interactions. Experimental results show that DAMFCMI outperforms state-of-the-art methods across three benchmark datasets. Visualization analyses demonstrate that the hybrid attention architecture enhances feature discriminability through effective multi-view feature integration. Moreover, case studies show that 13 out of 15 predicted CMIs predicted by DAMFCMI are supported by evidence in the PubMed literature, underscoring its potential for uncovering biologically relevant interactions.Availability and implementationThe data and source code are available at https://github.com/yadxbiolab/DAMFCMI

1 Introduction

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) and micro RNAs (miRNAs) are two major classes of non-coding RNAs, with circRNAs forming covalently closed loops that confer high stability (Cech and Steitz 2014, Jeck and Sharpless 2014, Salzman 2016), and miRNAs functioning as post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression (He and Hannon 2004, Wang et al. 2023). Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that circRNAs can function as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by acting as molecular sponges for miRNAs, participating in post-transcriptional gene regulation (Hansen et al. 2013; Memczak, et al. 2013; Zhang, et al. 2020). The circRNA–miRNA associations play a pivotal role in numerous physiological and pathological processes, including various human diseases such as cancer, cardiovascular disorders, and neurological conditions (Qi et al. 2015, Zhong et al. 2018, Patop et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2021). For example, Zhuang et al. (2020) found that hsa_circ_0075828 promoted bladder tumor cell proliferation by sponging miR-1224-5p, leading to deregulation of CREB1 expression. Shao et al. (2020) demonstrated that hsa_circ_0075341 facilitated cervical cancer cell proliferation and invasion by sponging miR-149-5p, thereby modulating the AURKA signaling axis. Identifying circRNA-miRNA interactions (CMIs) is critical to understanding disease-driving molecular mechanisms, enhancing genetic regulation studies, and therapeutic advancement (Zhang et al. 2017, Yi et al. 2019).

Although traditional biological experiments provide reliable validation of circRNA-miRNA association (Xiong et al. 2018, Fang and Lei 2019, Tong et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2023), these methods are often time-consuming, labor-intensive, and low-throughput, which limits their feasibility for large-scale and systematic studies. In recent years, the rapid accumulation of CMI data has led to the development of numerous computational models (Dori and Bicciato 2019). To consolidate advances in this field, Chen et al. (Wang et al. 2021, Huang et al. 2022a, 2022b, 2022c) have systematically summarized the biogenesis and functions of circRNAs and miRNAs, key database resources, and the development of computational prediction models.

Existing computational methods for CMI prediction can generally be categorized into three groups. The first category emphasizes single-view feature models that primarily rely on similarity matrices derived from known circRNA–miRNA associations. For example, NECMA (Lan et al. 2021) constructs circRNA and miRNA similarity matrices using Gaussian interaction profile kernels, integrates them into a heterogeneous network, and applies the NetMF algorithm for network embedding. The second category incorporates multi-source features to enhance the richness and informativeness of the data. However, most methods consider only two types of features and utilize shallow fusion strategies—such as direct concatenation—thereby limits the ability to capture dependencies across feature modalities. For example, CMIVGSD (Qian et al. 2021) combines linear features obtained via singular value decomposition with non-linear embeddings from variational graph autoencoders, and employs LightGBM for classification, and BGF-CMAP (Guo et al. 2024a, 2024b) fuses semantic embeddings derived from Word2Vec with topological information extracted using LINE and Graph Factorization, followed by a gradient-boosted decision tree classifier. The third category enriches feature representations by incorporating intrinsic sequence information. For instance, KGDCMI (Wang et al. 2022) integrates Gaussian kernel similarity, K-mer-based embeddings, and graph-structured features within a deep neural network framework. To address this limitation, BCMCMI (Wei et al. 2023) addresses this limitation by employing BERT to extract high-level semantic features from sequences and Metapath2vec to encode topological relationships.

Although previous methods have achieved promising results, several critical challenges remain to be addressed: (i) Insufficient exploitation of sequence features. Most existing methods overlook the intrinsic sequence information and physicochemical features of circRNAs and miRNAs, leading to a failure in capturing their intrinsic biological patterns. (ii) Limited diversity of feature views. Most current approaches rely on only one or two types of feature information, constraining their capacity to fully exploit the complementary advantages offered by diverse biological perspectives. (iii) Shallow fusion of heterogeneous features. Existing methods neglect the exploration of deep collaborative features in the interactions between circRNA and miRNA, and adopt shallow fusion strategies such as simple splicing or weighted averaging in fusing multi-view features, thereby lacking the ability to perform deep feature extraction across distinct views.

To solve the above limitations, we propose a hybrid attention-guided multi-view feature fusion framework, named DAMFCMI, for identifying CMIs. DAMFCMI incorporates three biologically meaningful and complementary features from different perspectives: attribute features that capture local contextual patterns, behavioral features derived from graph embeddings to represent global interaction dependencies, and sequence features that reflect intrinsic biological information and physicochemical properties of molecules. The hybrid attention module, including multi-head self-attention (MSA) and cross-attention (CA), is designed to effectively capture contextual dependencies within individual features and complex correlations across different feature types, enabling dynamic and hierarchical fusion of semantic attributes, topological behaviors, and sequence-level information. Experiments on three publicly available benchmark datasets demonstrate that DAMFCMI significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both predictive performance and generalization ability. The effectiveness of the multi-view feature fusion strategy and dependency extracting mechanism is further validated through a series of ablation and comparative analyses. In case study, 12 out of the 15 predicted potential CMIs were supported by existing literature. The source code and data are available on https://github.com/yadxbiolab/DAMFCMI.

2.2 Method overview

In this study, we propose a hybrid-attention-guided multi-view feature fusion framework, named DAMFCMI, for CMIs prediction. The overall process of DAMFCMI is shown in Fig. 1 with three parts: (i) low-dimensional vector embedding (Fig. 1A). Feature representations of circRNAs and miRNAs are extracted from multiple perspectives using diverse strategies, including intrinsic physicochemical sequence features, attribute features, and interaction behavior features; (ii) hybrid-attention module for deep feature fusion (Fig. 1B). MSA captures local dependencies within individual feature types, while CA captures global interactions across different feature views; (iii) CMIs prediction (Fig. 1C). The fused features from all sources are concatenated and passed through a fully connected layer to predict the probability of interaction for each circRNA–miRNA pair, thereby determining whether it constitutes a valid CMIs.

2.3 Multi-view feature extraction

To comprehensively characterize circRNAs and miRNAs, we propose a multi-view feature extraction strategy that integrates sequence features, attribute features, and interaction behavior information. Specifically, circRNA and miRNA sequences are encoded to generate semantic embeddings, which serve as attribute features. In parallel, a homogeneous interaction graph is constructed based on known associations, and graph embedding techniques are applied to capture topological behavioral features that reflect potential interaction patterns. Furthermore, to overcome the limitations of prior studies that overlooked inter-nucleotide dependencies, we extract the intrinsic sequence information and physicochemical features directly from raw sequences as supplementary sequence features.

2.3.1 Sequence feature extraction

To address the insufficient exploitation of sequence features, a set of sequence features directly from the raw sequences is extracted (Wang et al. 2023).

These features were designed to capture diverse aspects of the sequences, including coding potential, compositional patterns, and physicochemical properties. Specifically, seven types of descriptors were included: open reading frame length (ORF-length) (Liu et al. 2019), open reading frame coverage (ORF-coverage) (Chen et al. 2021), Hexamer score, Fickett score, composition–transition–distribution (CTD) (Meinhart et al.2005), isoelectric point (pI), and electron–ion interaction potential (EIIP) (Finkel et al. 2018), as detailed in Table 2.

2.4 Hybrid attention module for deep feature fusion

2.4.1 Multi-head self-attention

3.3 Performance comparison with competing methods

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of DAMFCMI, we systematically compared it with several state-of-the-art CMI prediction methods on three benchmark datasets. The first category of methods mainly relies on sequence and similarity features in combination with traditional machine learning model, such as CMIVGSD (Qian et al. 2021), BGF-CMAP (Guo et al. 2024a, 2024b), JSNDCMI (Wang et al. 2023), and KS-CMI (Wang et al. 2023). The second category focuses on graph representation learning and graph neural networks to capture the topological information between circRNAs and miRNAs. Representative methods in this category include GCNCMI (He et al. 2022), SGCNCMI (Yu et al. 2022), KGDCMI (Wang et al. 2022), and LSAGHI (Yin et al. 2025). The third category integrates techniques such as Word2Vec, LSTM, or autoencoders to represent multi-source features and employs deep neural networks to predict circRNA–miRNA associations. In addition, some methods further incorporate attention mechanisms, heterogeneous network structures, or contrastive learning strategies to enhance the representation of both local and global structural information. Representative methods include WSCD (Guo et al. 2022), CA-CMA (Guo et al. 2024a, 2024b), DGCLCMI (Cao et al. 2025), iHofman (Yu et al. 2025), MFERL (Peng et al. 2025), and MuGNet-CMI (Jiang et al. 2025).

To ensure fair and consistent comparisons, the experimental results of all baseline methods were directly obtained from their original publications and reported under the same dataset settings. Table 5 illustrates that DAMFCMI outperformed all baseline methods, achieving the highest AUPR and AUC across all three datasets. On the CMI-9905 dataset, DAMFCMI attained AUC and AUPR exceeding 0.97, representing improvements of 1.6% and 1.42%, respectively, over the best-performing baseline method, MFERL. Similarly, on the CMI-9589 dataset, DAMFCMI outperformed MFERL by 1.37% in AUC and 1.50% in AUPR. Figure 3A–3C shows the corresponding comparative performance via two-indicator radar charts, where each polygon represents the distribution of AUC or AUPR across all compared methods. On all three datasets, DAMFCMI occupies the outermost positions in both polygons, clearly demonstrating its superiority under both evaluation criteria. 3.4 Multi-view feature fusion contributes to representation enhancement

To investigate whether fusing multi-view features enhances the representational capacity, we conducted feature ablation studies on the CMI-20208 dataset, with the specific settings as follows: (i) using only attribute features (Att); (ii) fusing attribute and behavioral features (Att + Beh); (iii) integrating attribute, behavioral, and sequence features (Att + Beh + Seq).

As summarized in Table 6 and Fig. 4, the method exhibited the weakest performance when only attribute features were used, achieving an accuracy of 67.98% and an AUC of 0.7368. Upon incorporating behavioral features, the performance improved substantially, with increases of 32.54% in accuracy, 36.18% in sensitivity, and over 30% in both AUPR and AUC, along with a remarkable 122.85% increase in MCC. This underscores the significant contribution of behavioral features in enhancing the capture of interaction patterns. When sequence features were further incorporated, the model achieved the best performance across all metrics, with improvements of 1.2% in Spe, 1.09% in Prec, and 1.64% in MCC. These results demonstrate the importance of multi-view feature fusion, as sequence information captures unique biological characteristics.

3.5 Hybrid attention mechanism synergistically improves prediction performance

To investigate the contribution of individual components in DAMFCMI, we conducted ablation experiments on the CMI-20208 dataset, evaluating four DAMFCMI variants. Three of these variants were created by selectively removing key components—the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) and Cross-Attention (CA) mechanisms. The results are presented in Table 7. When both MSA and CA modules are removed, DAMFCMI exhibits the lowest performance across all evaluation metrics. When only the MSA module is retained, the performance notably improves, with Prec and MCC increasing by 1.95% and 2.63%, ACC increasing by 1.16%, respectively, compared to the setting without either module. This confirms that the attention mechanisms are essential for capturing complex feature dependencies. Interestingly, although the configuration with only MSA achieves the highest in terms of AUC and AUPR, it underperforms on five other metrics—including ACC and MCC—indicating that MSA alone cannot ensure balanced performance. When only the CA module is included, the model also shows moderate improvements compared to the baseline. However, it still lags behind the full DAMFCMI configuration. In contrast, the complete DAMFCMI, integrating both MSA and CA, achieves the best performance. The results show that although both MSA and CA individually improve performance, their integration in DAMFCMI achieves the best overall results by jointly modeling contextual dependencies and cross-modal feature interactions.

3.7 Case studies

To validate the predictive capability of DAMFCMI in real-world applications, we conducted a case study focusing on circRNAs associated with cancers that have seen a significant increase in incidence in recent years, including gastric cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and colorectal cancer. Given the high global prevalence of these cancers and their public health impact, we randomly selected 14 circRNA entries from the CMI-9905 dataset that are known to be significantly associated with these cancers, involving 16 circRNA–miRNA interaction pairs. The results indicate that 15 out of the 16 CMI pairs were successfully predicted, with 13 of the predicted pairs corroborated by existing literature in PubMed (Table 8). For example, hsa_circ_0092306 promotes gastric cancer development by regulating the miR-197-3p/PRKCB pathway in MKN-45 cells (Chen et al. 2019). hsa_circ_0103809 may facilitate the malignant development of hepatocellular carcinoma, at least in part, by regulating the miR-490-5p/SOX2 signaling pathway (Cai et al. 2018). hsa_circ_0079993 acts as an oncogene in colorectal cancer through the miRNA-203a-3p.1/CREB1 axis (Lu et al. 2019). hsa_circ_0088732 promotes glioma progression by increasing the expression of RAB3D through the miR-661 sponge mechanism (Jin et al. 2020). Additionally, among all the successfully predicted CMIs, only one pair of CMIs had a score lower than 0.9. These high-confidence predictions are in strong agreement with the literature, confirming that our strategy not only demonstrates robust predictive ability but also has the potential to uncover previously unknown CMI relationships of practical value. This further suggests that DAMFCMI could serve as a powerful tool for guiding relevant biological experiments.

4. Conclusion

In this study, we developed DAMFCMI, a hybrid-attention-guided multi-view feature fusion framework for CMI prediction. Its superior performance stems from its ability to extract complementary information from multiple feature perspectives and progressively enhance feature representations through a hierarchical fusion strategy. By jointly modeling sequence features, attribute features, and behavior features, the framework effectively mitigates the information loss often observed in single-view or shallow fusion approaches. The hybrid attention mechanism further enables deep and adaptive feature refinement. The multi-head self-attention component captures long-range dependencies within each feature view, while the cross-attention module strengthens the interactions across different feature modalities. Through this multi-level fusion strategy, DAMFCMI can identify subtle yet informative interaction signals that are frequently overlooked by traditional models. Extensive experiments and case studies show that DAMFCMI consistently outperforms existing methods in both predictive accuracy and generalization ability.

It is worth noting that although DAMFCMI was initially designed for circRNA–miRNA interaction prediction, its generalizable multi-view modeling paradigm and extensible attention-based fusion mechanism make it broadly applicable to other bioinformatics tasks. The framework can be naturally adapted to various biomolecular association prediction scenarios, such as miRNA–disease, lncRNA–protein, and drug–target interactions. Future work may incorporate heterogeneous multi-omics data—including gene expression profiles and epigenetic information—to uncover deeper layers of molecular regulation and advance our understanding of the biological mechanisms underlying complex diseases.

Orca-T vs allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (Precision-T): a multicenter, randomized phase 3 trial
Authors: Everett H Meyer, Amandeep Salhotra, Arpita P Gandhi, Jeremy Pantin, Sagar S Patel, Rasmus T Hoeg, Alexandra Gomez-Arteaga, Rawan Faramand, Roni Tamari, Edmund K Waller, Satyajit Kosuri, Antonio M Jimenez Jimenez, Jennifer Holter-Chakrabarty, Bhagirathbhai Dholaria, Yi-Bin Chen, Betty K Hamilton, John Magenau, Alireza Eghtedar, Joshua M Murray, Anna Pavlova, Nathaniel B Fernhoff, James Scott McClellan, M Scott Killian, Ai Li, Robert S Negrin, Caspian Oliai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Key Points•In a randomized phase 3 study, Orca-T significantly improved survival free from cGVHD compared to conventional alloHSCT with Tac/MTX.•In addition to lower rates of cGVHD, Orca-T patients were observed to have lower rates of acute GVHD, NRM, and serious infections.

Introduction

Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (alloHSCT) permanently replaces the diseased blood and immune system in a patient with that of a healthy donor. It is the only potentially curative therapy for many benign blood disorders and high-risk hematologic malignancies.1 However, the use of alloHSCT is limited by graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), a sustained immune reaction initiated by the donor T cells against the recipient’s normal healthy tissues, commonly the liver, skin, gastrointestinal tract, and lungs.2 Despite multiagent immune suppression, GVHD remains a major complication after alloHSCT. Furthermore, the pharmacological agents used to prevent GVHD are a source of secondary complications. Therapy-related toxicities ultimately limit the application of alloHSCT to high-risk hematologic malignancies and bone marrow failure disorders.

For the past 30 years, GVHD prophylaxis approaches have been developed that deplete donor T lymphocytes either ex vivo using graft engineering or in vivo using posttransplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy) or antithymocyte globulin (ATG). However, the benefit of superior GVHD control of these strategies has been offset by the delayed immune reconstitution, increased infectious complications, and excess nonrelapse mortality (NRM).3,4 These studies underscore the dual actions of donor T cells to (1) therapeutically reconstitute the immune system to provide graft-versus-leukemia and graft-versus-infection, and (2) pathogenically react against normal tissue to cause GVHD. Therefore, improvements in alloHSCT outcomes will require strategies that selectively control T-cell function to maintain immune reconstitution and prevent GVHD.

In preclinical models of alloHSCT, donor-derived, polyclonal CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs) were shown to modulate T-cell alloreactivity to mitigate GVHD, maintain graft-versus-leukemia5,6 and promote immune reconstitution.7 This concept has been translated in single-institution clinical studies with haploidentical transplant,8,9 with expanded umbilical cord blood cells10 and matched donor-derived peripheral blood stem cells (PBSCs).11,12 However, the complexity of graft engineering has, until now, prevented the translation of single-center efforts to general clinical practice.

Orca-T is an allogeneic T-cell immunotherapy, produced and distributed from a central Good Manufacturing Practices facility, that consists of individually formulated (1) hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, (2) high-purity Tregs, and (3) conventional T cells (Tcons). In early phase studies, Orca-T recipients demonstrated high survival rates and low rates of acute and chronic GVHD (cGVHD), infection, and NRM.12,13 Here, we present the efficacy, safety, and immune reconstitution outcomes from a randomized phase 3 study of Orca-T compared with conventional alloHSCT conducted at 19 centers across the United States.

Methods

Trial design

This randomized, multicenter phase 3 trial compared Orca-T with single-agent tacrolimus to a control arm comprising a PBSC allograft with tacrolimus and methotrexate (Tac/MTX) in patients with acute leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) who received myeloablative conditioning (Figure 1). The primary objective was to compare the rates of survival free from moderate-to-severe cGVHD (cGFS) in the 2 arms using a time-to-event analysis, with an event being defined as the development of moderate-to-severe cGVHD or death from any cause. The primary analysis of cGFS was prespecified to be conducted at the 56th event. For the primary analysis, cGVHD was assessed by an independent end point adjudication committee (EAC) blinded to the treatment arm assignment. The sponsor was blinded to the aggregate study results; treatment assignment was known to clinical sites, patients, and treating physicians.

The protocol was approved by the institutional review board of each participating center, and all the patients and donors provided written informed consent.

Patients

Patients aged 18 to 65 years who planned to undergo their first alloHSCT from an HLA-matched donor after myeloablative conditioning were enrolled into the study. Eligibility included acute myeloid leukemia, acute lymphoblastic leukemia, or mixed-phenotype acute leukemia in complete remission or complete remission with incomplete count recovery, or MDS with no circulating blasts and <10% blasts in the bone marrow. Eligible patients had adequate organ function, hematopoietic cell transplantation–specific comorbidity index14 ≤4, and were classified as disease risk index (DRI) category intermediate or high.15 All donors, related and unrelated, were 8/8 matched at HLA-A, -B, -C, and -DRB1 alleles by high-resolution DNA-based typing.

Treatment

Myeloablative conditioning regimens included either (1) fludarabine (160 mg/m2), thiotepa (10 mg/kg), and busulfan (IV, 9.6 mg/kg or dosed to maintain an average daily area under the curve of 4800-6000 μM-min [19.7-24.6 mg∗h/L]); (2) etoposide (60 mg/kg) and total body irradiation (1200-1320 cGy); or (3) cyclophosphamide (100-120 mg/kg), and total body irradiation (1200-1420 cGy).

For both arms, PBSCs were mobilized in donors via granulocyte colony-stimulating factor stimulation over 5 days and collected via apheresis. For each Orca-T recipient, PBSCs were couriered by Orca Bio (related donors) or the National Marrow Donor Program (unrelated donors) to Orca Bio’s Good Manufacturing Practices central manufacturing facility (Sacramento, CA) where cellular infusions were purified and formulated. Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells and Treg infusions were on day +0, and Tcon infusion was on day +2 or +3. Tacrolimus was initiated on the day following Tcon infusion (supplemental Figure 1, available on the Blood website). For Tac/MTX recipients, PBSCs were obtained locally (related donors) or couriered by the National Marrow Donor Program (unrelated donors). The unmanipulated allograft was infused on day +0 and tacrolimus was initiated on day −3. Methotrexate was administered as IV bolus at doses of 15 mg/m2 on day +1 and 10 mg/m2 on days +3, +6, and +11 (supplemental Figure 1).

Tacrolimus dosing and tapering was the same for both arms: initiated at 0.03 mg/kg per day IV, with a target trough blood level of 5 to 10 ng/mL and taper initiated ≥90 days after HSCT in the absence of active GVHD, reduced ∼20% per month. Posttransplant, targeted maintenance therapies (FLT3, IDH, or BCR-ABL inhibitors) were allowed on study. Additional medical management is described in the clinical protocol. First-line acute GVHD treatment was also the same for both arms. Letermovir prophylaxis was mandated for cytomegalovirus (CMV)–seropositive recipients.

End points

The primary end point for this phase 3 Precision-T study was cGFS, a time-to-event outcome defined as the time from transplant (day 0) to either death from any cause or the onset of moderate-to-severe cGVHD per the 2014 National Institutes of Health consensus criteria.16 All patients were assessed for the presence or absence of cGVHD by a blinded EAC comprising independent physicians not participating in the study, who were given access to primary patient data. The EAC assigned organ specific severity scores for GVHD. Secondary end points were time to moderate-to-severe cGVHD, overall survival (OS), and GVHD-free and relapse-free survival (GRFS), with the latter measuring the earliest of death, relapse, grade 3 or 4 acute GVHD,17 or moderate-to-severe cGVHD. Exploratory end points included hematopoietic reconstitution, acute GVHD, infections, NRM, and RFS.

Statistical analysis

The complete statistical analysis plan (SAP) is provided in the supplemental Methods. Patients were assigned treatment using dynamic block randomization in a 1:1 ratio to the Orca-T arm or the Tac/MTX arm, stratified according to donor type (matched sibling donor or matched unrelated donor) and the DRI (intermediate or high). The primary analysis was conducted at 56 total events (moderate-to-severe cGVHD or death), which is the number of events required to achieve 90% power with a true hazard ratio (HR) of 0.40 and an overall α of 0.05 with 2-sided testing. A minimum enrollment of 165 patients was calculated, but 187 patients were ultimately randomized to account for potential early study discontinuations. Secondary end points were tested hierarchically in the following sequence only if the primary end point and all preceding end points were significant: time to moderate-to-severe cGVHD per EAC, OS, and GRFS. This study was not powered for any of the secondary end points. The intention-to-treat (ie, as randomized) populations were considered for primary and secondary end points. Exploratory end points included RFS, NRM, acute GVHD, cGVHD (all grades), relapse, and engraftment. The safety populations (ie, as-treated) were evaluated for these exploratory end points.

Here, we report the results of a primary analysis for cGFS and cGVHD. For cGFS, a 2-sided α of 0.0116 was spent at interim analysis, with 0.0464 remaining for the primary analysis. Interim results for OS and GRFS are presented here; a primary analysis of these outcomes is planned for a later time. All the secondary end points were to apply a generalized Haybittle-Peto boundary with a 2-sided α of 0.05 remaining for their corresponding primary analyses.

In the primary analysis for cGFS, a 2-sided log-rank test, stratified by the randomization variables (ie, donor type and DRI; hereafter referred to as RV adjusted), with a significance threshold of 0.0464 was used to test the null hypothesis of no treatment effect with respect to cGFS. In addition, the HR with a 95% confidence interval (CI) was estimated from an RV-adjusted Cox regression model. The same analytic approaches were used for OS and GRFS. For time to moderate or severe cGVHD in which death was considered a competing event, and NRM in which relapse was considered a competing event, the P values for the hypotheses tests were calculated from RV-adjusted Gray tests, and RV-adjusted subdistribution proportional hazards models were used to calculate treatment HRs with 95% CIs. Additional details of exploratory analyses are included in the supplemental Appendix and SAP.

Results

Patients

Between 25 June 2022 and 18 June 2024, a total of 187 patients at 19 centers in the United States were enrolled in the trial (Table 1; supplemental Figure 2; supplemental Table 1). The CONSORT diagram shows the flow of patients during screening, randomization, treatment, and follow-up (Figure 1). Salient patient characteristics at the time of transplant are shown in supplemental Table 2. Of the 94 patients randomized to the Tac/MTX arm, 93 received an 8/8 HLA-matched alloHSCT, whereas 1 received a haploidentical allograft with PTCy-based prophylaxis due to the drop out of the original matched donor. All 4 doses of MTX were administered for 82 (87%) Tac/MTX patients, 10 (11%) missed at least 1 MTX dose due to toxicity, and complete MTX dosing information was not available for 2 patients (2%). Of 93 patients randomized to the Orca-T arm, 5 had not received Orca-T at the time of data cutoff: 2 withdrew their consent before treatment, 2 relapsed before treatment (1 who eventually received Orca-T after the primary analysis), and 1 had delayed treatment due to severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 infection and instead received a cryopreserved PBSC allograft plus Tac/MTX. All remaining 88 Orca-T patients received an Orca-T product within 72 hours of donor apheresis. Orca-T product characteristics are provided in supplemental Figure 3. The database lock occurred on 8 January 2025 for the primary analysis. Patient variables were well balanced across the 2 study arms, and patients enrolled in this trial were representative of the general alloHSCT population (Table 1; supplemental Table 3). The median follow-up was 11.4 months (range, 0.2-24.3) at the database lock date.

Primary end point

A total of 58 events (death or moderate-to-severe cGVHD) occurred in the intention-to-treat population: 14 in the Orca-T arm and 44 in the Tac/MTX arm. A multivariate Cox regression model showed a HR of 0.26 (95% CI, 0.14-0.47; P < .001). One-year estimated cGFS was 78.0% (95% CI, 65.0-86.6) for Orca-T and 38.4% (95% CI, 26.2-50.5) for Tac/MTX (Figure 2A). Orca-T exhibited higher cGFS across all subgroups in prespecified sensitivity analyses (supplemental Figure 4).

Secondary end points

The secondary end points were evaluated hierarchically as described in “Methods.” First, moderate-to-severe cGVHD was significantly reduced for the Orca-T arm vs the Tac/MTX arm (HR, 0.19; P < .001). The 1-year estimated cumulative incidence of moderate-to-severe cGVHD for Orca-T was 12.6% (95% CI, 5.3-23.1) vs 44.0% (95% CI, 31.3-56.1) for Tac/MTX (Figure 2B). Overall, 7 total Orca-T treated patients developed moderate (n = 6) or severe (n = 1) cGVHD. In comparison, 30 total Tac/MTX-treated patients developed moderate (n = 18) or severe (n = 12) cGVHD. Organ systems affected by cGVHD in each arm are shown in supplemental Figure 5A, and systemic therapies used to treat patients who developed cGVHD in each arm are shown in supplemental Figure 5B.

Second, 1-year estimated OS was 93.9% (95% CI, 85.8-97.4) for Orca-T patients vs 83.1% (95% CI, 72.9-89.8) for Tac/MTX patients (HR, 0.49; P = .12; Figure 2C). Finally, 1-year estimated GRFS was 63.1% (95% CI, 50.0-73.6) for Orca-T vs 30.9% (95% CI, 20.0-42.4) for Tac/MTX (HR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.23-0.60; P < .001; Figure 2D). Because the interim OS analysis did not cross the α-controlled boundary, GRFS is reported descriptively with no formal claim of significance. Primary analyses for OS and GRFS end points are forthcoming per SAP.

Other major treatment outcomes

The as-treated populations were analyzed for other study outcomes (Table 2). No patients experienced primary graft failure, and neutrophil engraftment was similar between the 2 arms (median of 13 days with Orca-T vs 14 days with Tac/MTX; supplemental Figure 7). Platelet engraftment was also similar between the 2 arms (median of 17 days for Orca-T and 18 days for Tac/MTX), and delayed platelet engraftment was noted for 1 Orca-T patient and 2 Tac/MTX patients (supplemental Figure 7). One Orca-T patient had secondary graft loss. Grade 2 acute GVHD was similar across both treatments, but the cumulative incidence for grade 3 or 4 acute GVHD at day +180 was 6.2% (95% CI, 2.3-12.9) with Orca-T vs 16.5% (95% CI, 9.4-25.3) in the Tac/MTX arm (HR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.13-1.02; P = .044; Table 2). One-year cumulative incidence of any-grade cGVHD per investigator assessment was 21.9% (95% CI, 11.7-34.2) with Orca-T vs 67.5% (95% CI, 53.9-77.9) with Tac/MTX, corresponding to a preventable fraction of 67.6%. Estimated 1-year RFS was 75.5% (95% CI, 63.0-84.3) for Orca-T and 74.1% (95% CI, 62.7-82.5) for Tac/MTX (HR, 0.80; P = .49; Table 2). The cumulative incidence of relapse or progression at 1 year was 21.1% (95% CI, 11.9-32.0) for Orca-T and 12.7% for Tac/MTX (95% CI, 6.4-21.4) P = .33). Furthermore, the cumulative incidence of NRM at 1 year was 3.4% (95% CI, 0.9-8.8) for Orca-T vs 13.2% (95% CI, 6.8-21.6) for Tac/MTX (HR, 0.27; 95% CI, 0.08-0.93; P = .03; Table 2). The reduced NRM with Orca-T was due to fewer deaths from organ toxicity, GVHD, and infection (Figure 3D).

On both arms, CD4+ T-cell counts durably recovered to ≥50/μL by day +28 (Figure 3A), with rapid reconstitution of other immune cell populations as well (supplemental Table 4).18 Tregs were proportionally more abundant among CD4+ T cells in Orca-T patients during early immune reconstitution and remain elevated relative to Tac/MTX patients through day +180 (P < .05; Figure 3B; supplemental Table 4). Both arms achieved full donor granulocyte chimerism by day +28, and median T-cell chimerism levels at day +28 were 86% and 95% for Orca-T and Tac/MTX, respectively (supplemental Figure 7A). Some Orca-T patients required a longer time to achieve full donor T-cell chimerism, but mixed T-cell chimerism did not predict relapse with Orca-T at any time point in the first year after transplant (supplemental Figure 7B).

Infectious complications were evaluated using the Blood and Marrow Transplant Clinical Trials Network (BMT CTN) Manual of Procedures scoring.19 Grade 2 or 3 infections were similar between the 2 arms, but grade 3 infections were less common with Orca-T (Table 2). There were 10 grade 3 infections among 7 Orca-T patients and 26 grade 3 infections among 14 Tac/MTX patients. The 1-year estimated incidence of grade 3 infections was 8.4% (95% CI, 3.6-16) for Orca-T vs 16.1% (95% CI, 9.2-25) for Tac/MTX (Figure 3C). CMV and Epstein-Barr virus were equally well controlled in both arms, with 1 case of CMV reactivation in the Orca-T arm and 1 case of Epstein-Barr virus reactivation in each study arm.

Safety events observed between the 2 arms are noted in Table 2, supplemental Tables 5 and 6, and the supplemental Appendix Text. Serious treatment-emergent adverse events developed in 38.6% of Orca-T patients vs 56.4% of Tac/MTX patients. Hospital readmissions after initial discharge for alloHSCT were 27.3% for Orca-T patients vs 45.7% for Tac/MTX patients (Table 2).

Discussion

In this randomized, phase 3 trial of patients with acute leukemia or MDS undergoing alloHSCT with myeloablative conditioning using HLA-matched donors, Orca-T with single-agent tacrolimus met the primary end point of improved cGFS compared with a conventional allograft with Tac/MTX. The improvement was due to a significant reduction in moderate-to-severe cGVHD and fewer patient deaths. In addition, patients treated with Orca-T experienced lower rates of acute GVHD, fewer serious infections, less rehospitalization, and less NRM.

The Orca-T design builds directly on the foundation of prior graft-engineering efforts. The landmark BMT CTN 1301 study prospectively randomized patients to 1 of 3 arms: (1) a T-cell–depleted allograft without pharmacological prophylaxis, (2) a T-cell–replete bone marrow allograft with Tac/MTX, or (3) a T-cell–replete bone marrow allograft with PTCy.4 T-cell depletion significantly reduced the incidence of cGVHD relative to the other arms; however, it also increased the rates of serious infections, NRM, and OS. In Orca-T, the intended use of Treg and Tcon components is to provide GVHD control and rapid immune function, respectively. In this study, Orca-T patients rapidly reconstituted conventional T cells, including CD4+ T cells, and their rates of serious infections were correspondingly low. In conjunction, the relative proportion of Tregs during immune reconstitution was elevated, whereas the rates of GVHD were low. These results highlight a consistent mechanism of action in which the precise control of donor Treg and Tcon compositions orchestrate a balanced immune reconstitution that leads to a therapeutic effect.

Conventional allografts contain high concentrations of donor T cells, typically >100 × 106/kg20 and require multiagent GVHD prophylaxis. In contrast, a T-cell–depleted allograft generally contains <50 000 T cells per kg and rarely requires any pharmacological immune suppression. Adaptations that add back a limited dose of donor T cells to a T-cell–depleted allograft have been investigated, but a dose that was able to both prevent GVHD and promote rapid immune reconstitution was not identified.21 A fundamental discovery in murine models of alloHSCT showed that donor Tregs were capable of controlling the alloreactivity of donor T cells without compromising immune function or disease relapse.6,7 This activity was substantially augmented when pure Tregs were delivered 2 days before Tcons, which exploits the alloreactive proliferation of Tregs and a suppressive reaction against recipient dendritic cells in secondary lymphoid organs.22, 23, 24 The Treg:Tcon addback strategies were translated to clinical trials first by the Perugia group and then others, with promising reductions in GVHD.8,9,11,12 However, the complexity of graft engineering has been difficult to scale to routine clinical use, even at transplant centers with sophisticated cell manufacturing capabilities. Orca-T addresses this barrier via a central manufacturing and distribution platform. The cellular infusions of Orca-T are quality controlled to meet dose, purity, potency, and safety specifications. In this phase 3 study, all batches of Orca-T were manufactured, distributed to clinical sites, and infused within a 72-hour vein-to-vein time span. Accordingly, Orca-T translates decades of graft-engineering research into a widely available immune cell therapy.

This study had certain limitations. In earlier trials, Orca-T exhibited a potential synergy when combined with a busulfan/fludarabine/thiotepa (BFT) conditioning regimen. Therefore, to reduce heterogeneity and isolate the treatment effect of Orca-T, the myeloablative regimens in this study protocol were restricted, preventing a broader assessment of generally used conditioning agents. BFT is an intensive conditioning regimen that reduces relapse, although at the expense of higher NRM.25,26 Patients in the Tac/MTX arm of this study, which used BFT, experienced lower rates of relapse and higher rates of NRM than those in the Tac/MTX arm in BMT CTN 1301, which used busulfan/cyclophosphamide or busulfan/fludarabine conditioning.4 This context is important because the relapse rates with Orca-T remain consistent with expectation for this patient population, whereas the exceptionally low NRM and high rates of OS demonstrate the potential of Orca-T to improve safety without compromising efficacy. Furthermore, this study used Tac/MTX prophylaxis in a single control arm. Regarding GVHD, ATG has demonstrated superiority over Tac/MTX in randomized studies that have informed European practice.27,28 Randomized studies of alloHSCT in the United States and Australia with HLA-matched donors in the reduced intensity context have recently demonstrated superior GVHD control with a PTCy-based regimen compared with Tac/MTX.29 However, no multicenter, randomized study has shown clear superiority for this patient population in the myeloablative setting.30 Currently, both PTCy and ATG are commonly used as GVHD prophylaxis in clinical practice, but delayed immune reconstitution, infection, and NRM remain frequently reported complications. A retrospective comparison of Orca-T patients treated in the phase 1b trial to a historical registry cohort of PTCy patients showed promising improvements in OS with Orca-T over 3 years,31 but a prospective randomized trial against PTCy or ATG may be needed for a more definitive assessment of outcomes.

In conclusion, patients who received Orca-T and single-agent tacrolimus had superior survival free from GVHD compared with those who received a PBSC allograft and Tac/MTX, owing to both fewer instances of GVHD and fewer deaths. Orca-T provided improved outcomes with less immune suppression, and these results establish Orca-T as a new therapeutic option for patients with hematologic malignancies. These results also mark the first demonstration of efficacy with a Treg-based immunotherapy. Finally, the low NRM rate and favorable safety profile of Orca-T merit further investigation in patients with other hematologic, genetic, and autoimmune disorders for which conventional alloHSCT has demonstrated efficacy but whose use remains limited due to high NRM and other toxicities.

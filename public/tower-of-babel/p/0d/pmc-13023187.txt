Effect of a salutogenic program on depression, self-harming behaviour and sense of coherence among elderly residents of an old age home in Odisha
Authors: Ishita Sahoo, Suchismita Pahantasingh, Sephali Moharana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study sought the impact of the Salutogenic Program on depression, self- harming behaviour and Sense of coherence among elderly individual living in an Old-age home of Bhubaneswar, Odisha.Methods & MaterialsIn this study Quantitative Research approach with Quasi- experimental (non randomized pre test post test) design was acquired. A number of 40 elderly (aged ≥60yrs) locating at M.P Old age home and Geriatric Centre, Bhubaneswar were sorted out for experimental group (N = 28) and control (N = 12) group by applying Purposive Sampling technique. Basic informations were gathered by applying Socio demographic data Proforma, Geriatric depression scale, Modified self-harm inventory scale and Modified Sense of coherence scale, after obtaining written informed consent from every participant. Total 4 weeks of (5session per week) Salutogenic Program for 120 min was given to elderly by splitting the experimental group into 4 groups.ResultsAnalysis disclosed that after Salutogenic Program, using inferential statistics, the experimental group displayed that depression level was decreased (before intervention 8.43 ± 3.120 and after intervention 5.04 ± 1.732), self-harming behaviour was reduced (before intervention 8.64 ± 2.329 and after intervention 5.14 ± 1.53) and Sense of coherence was increased (before intervention 49.36 ± 9.15 and after intervention 60.82 ± 4.68). A statistically significant difference was observed in the scores of depression, self-harming behaviour and Sense of coherence in the experimental group as compared to the control group.ConclusionPresent study concluded that Salutogenic Program is effective in decreasing level of depression and self-harming behavior and increasing sense of coherence among older adults in selected old-age home.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-026-07246-8.

Introduction

“Don’t let age change you. Change the way you age”…… change is a natural and unavoidable part of life. A person typically passes through various life stages, including infancy, childhood, adolescence, adulthood and old-age [1]. Among these stages, old age is a natural and essential phase of human life, not an illness [2]. Aging is a natural physiological process rather than a disease. It is characterized as a gradual and continuous biological progression over the lifespan, resulting in enduring changes across physical, psychological, social, and spiritual dimensions of an individual [3]. Old age exposes the inevitable reality that every human being moves toward with each passing breath, despite persistent attempts to deny or escape it. Life unfolds swiftly from childhood to adulthood, often without a moment for reflection, until one finds oneself burdened by the cumulative weight of experiences—many of which are far from gratifying. At this juncture, the individual frequently confronts the looming presence of depression, a state born from the emotional and existential strain that life imposes over time.” [4].

“Geriatric depression is among the most prevalent psychological disorders in the elderly population, posing a significant burden on both patients and their families. Furthermore, studies have reported that approximately 14% to 20% of community-dwelling older adults experience depressive symptoms, while higher prevalence rates have been observed among hospitalized elderly individuals (12%–45%) and those residing in long-term care facilities (approximately 40%) [5]. Moreover, geriatric depression has emerged as a growing public health concern worldwide, contributing substantially to increased morbidity, disability, and overall decline in quality of life among older adults.” [6]. Factors such as the death of a spouse or loved one, long- term illness, social isolation, financial dependence, and reliance on others for daily activities are the main cause of increasing the risk of depression [7]. The mortality rate due to depression are high among elderly populations which is estimated that the prevalence of depression ranges from 10% to 20%, varying across different cultural contexts [8]. Depression in the elderly is influenced by multiple interrelated factors rather than a single cause. In addition to age-related challenges, associated factors such as self-harming behavior further contribute to the deterioration of mental health among older adults. These two consequences, depressive symptoms and self-harming behaviors significantly impair the lifestyle and psychological well-being of older adults by weakening their Sense of Coherence (SOC). Research indicates that various health factors influence SOC, which in turn affects depression levels and mortality among the elderly [9]. SOC is regarded as both a worldview and a motivational resource that fosters coping and adaptation. Studies have consistently shown its association with better self-rated health, well-being, and quality of life [10]. The concept of Salutogenesis, introduced by Aaron Antonovsky in 1979, provides a theoretical framework explaining how SOC functions as a protective factor against depression and suicide, originally derived from studies on Holocaust survivors [11].

Evidence indicates that older adults with depressive symptoms are at higher risk of self-harming behaviors, with a prevalence of approximately 9.5% in Odisha. The application of Salutogenic Program strategies is limited among elderly residents of old-age homes in Bhubaneswar. Implementing a Salutogenic approach may enhance mental and physical health, improve quality of life, and foster overall well-being, highlighting the need for research to reduce depression and self-harming behaviors through effective interventions. After doing extensive reviews the researcher found that Salutogenic Program Strategies may have a positive impact on depression, Self harming behaviour and sensce of coherence among elderly those are specially staying in old age home.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

Approval of research problem and objectives was obtained from the IEC (Institutional Ethics Committee) IMS & SUM HOSPITAL, S’O’A deem to be University, Bhubaneswar. (Ref.no-IEC/IMS.SH/SOA/2021/228). Permission was obtained from the higher authority of M.P old-age Home in Bhubaneswar. Confidentiality and Privacy of the participant’s information was maintained, it was not disclosed to anybody without informing the participants.

Study design, setting and participants

Quantitative approach with quasi-experimental (pre test post test design only) was acquired to test the effect of Salutogenic Program on depression, self-harming behavior and sense of coherence among elderly. The present study was carried out in the M.P Old age home and Geriatric Centre of Bhubaneswar, Odisha. Researcher took 2 different setting for control and experimental group for reducing sample contamination. People aged 60year and above who able to read and speak odia language were included in the study. Elderly who having dementia and mental illness with psychotic symptoms other than depression were excluded from the study. According to inclusion and exclusion criteria available all samples, a total of 40 elderly were selected for the study using purposive sampling technique.

Due to COVID pandemic, limited availability, drop out of samples and restricted time period for conducting the study, researcher took all samples available in both setting (n = 40). It came to [control group (n = 12), experiment group (n = 28)].

Intervention

A pre-experimental assessment was carried out with a smaller cohort to determine the effectivity, feasibility and clarity of the Salutogenic program among elderly. Adjustments were made based on participant’s need. The intervention was given to the experimental group from 27.07.2021- 09.08.2021. The control group was received their pure routine care with general information regarding routine care in form of leaflets. The intervention includes Salutogenic program which was designed with three components i.e. Meaningfulness, Manageability and Comprehensibility helps to reduce depression and self-harming behaviour of individual. The program consists of 10 sessions (weekly 5 sessions) in 120 min. The first three sessions was for sharing childhood memories, unique experiences and how they overcome some difficulties in their life. Second four sessions focused on teaching on aging, teaching on depression and self-harming behaviour. Last session for how to improve good communication skills among elderly.

After 5 days of intervention period, 10.08.2021 to 11.08.2021 post test was done for both control and experiment group.

Tools for data collection

Four tools employed for data collection. Tool-1 is a Self-structured demographic proforma, administered through interviews, which captures baseline characteristics including age, gender, education, marital status, occupation etc. Tool 2, Structured Geriatric Depression Scale is a screening test used to identify symptoms of depression in older adults. The scale mainly having 15 items (modified short form by Yesavage, 1986) self report instruments that uses a “ YES /NO” format. Answers indicating depression are in bold and italicized (YES /NO) score 1 point for each one selected. A score 0–4 considered as Normal. A score 5–8 considered as Mild depression, a score 9–11 indicated as Moderate depression. A score 12–15 indicated as severe depression.

Tool-3, modified Self-harm inventory Scale where 19 –items of the SHI are there. Respondents are asked if they have deliberately engaged in any of nine different kinds of self-harm (cutting wrists, bleeding, craving words, pictures, burning one-self with cigarette, lighter or match, sticking sharp objects into skin). The instrument uses in “YES / NO” Format. Total scoring 19 (yes answer consist score 1), cut-off score is 5. 1–4 score indicate low severity of Self-harm behaviour. 5–10 score indicate medium severity, 11 and more suggest high severity of self-harm behaviour. Tool 4 the modified sense of coherence scale consists 13 items, out of this 5 items measure the comprehensibility component, 4 items measure the manageability component, and 4 items measure the meaningfulness component. Usually measurement done like Very Low SOC: 20–30, Low SOC: 31–50, Healthy and Happy individual: 51–60, Strong SOC: 61–70, Very strong SOC: 71–80. The content validity and reliability of these tools are meticulously assessed, with content validity verified by five experts in the field. Tools were translated in Odia language and validated by Odia language expert. The reliability value measured by Chronbach alpha for the scale were respectively 0.744, 0.743 and 0.729. The try-out of tool was conducted along with 5 elderly those who are meeting the inclusion criteria aged above 60 years. All the items of tool A, B & C understandable, questions are convenient and clear to all the participants. The pilot study also held taking 10 elderly those who are meeting inclusion criteria (5 no of people in Experimental group and 5 no of people in Control group) to assess the feasibility, time, effect, practicability, accessibility of the main study and to decide data analysis plan.

Procedure of data collection

Data collection phase of the study was conducted over a 1month period, from 26th July, 2021 to 10th August, 2021 following the acquisition of ethical clearance and written permissions from Head of the Help-age India Organization, Nayapalli, Bhubaneswar. M.P Old-age home (Experimental group) and Geriatric Centre (Control group). Purposive sampling was used to select samples [n = 40] (elderly age 60 and above). Samples were divided into two groups control and experiment doing lottery method by researcher. Two setting (old age home) was taken for assigning the subjects. Baseline assessment was done on the 1st day for both group where control group was received their routine care. The Salutogenic program received by experimental group consists of 10 sessions for 2 weeks (weekly 5 sessions) in 120 min. The first three session was for sharing childhood memories, unique experiences and how they overcome some difficulties in their life. Second 4th session focused on teaching on aging, teaching on depression and self-harming behaviour. Last session for how to improve good communication skills and develop sense of coherence among elderly. Post assessment was done 1 week later. Information was collected by interview schedule and data analysis was done by using descriptive and inferential statistics with SPSS version 21.

Statistical analysis

Data obtained was analysed in terms of objectives and by using both descriptive and inferential statistics. Collected Data was coded and computed into Microsoft Excel and then analyzed using SPSS Version 26 (IBM SPSS Statistical for windows, version 26.0. Armonk, NY, USA; IBM Corp). Socio-demographic data of elderly was analysed by using descriptive statistics (Frequency, percentage). Effect of Salutogenic Programme on level of depression, self-harming behavior and Sense of coherence was analyzed by using inferential statistical method like Paired and Unpaired t-test. Association between elderly depression, self-harming behavior and Sense of coherence with selected socio-demographic variables was analyzed by calculating Chi-square test. Correlation between Depression, self-harming behavior and Sense of coherence was analyzed by using Spearman’s rank co-relation co-efficient formula.

Discussion

Interventions in the present study reiterated the life experience of the participants and allowed them to feel their lives were meaningful. Practicing verbalization of gratitude and learning about stress management strategies led to sharing of emotion-based management strategies and encouragement. These are protective factors that not only decrease risk factors such as depression but also reduce suicidal ideation itself to decrease the risk of self harming behavior. The study discussed the effect of Salutogenic program on sense of coherence, depression, and self-harming behavior, and considered its benefits in reducing risk for elderly. The Salutogenic program can be recommended as useful strategies to enhance sense of coherence and to reduce depression and self-harming behavior of elderly staying at old-age home.

The study reported that in level of depression calculated unpaired t-test value was 2.215& “p” value was 0.033, in level of self-harming behavior calculated unpaired t-test value 1.9614 and “p” value was 0.05 The study also depicts that in level of sense of coherence calculated unpaired t-test value 3.471 and “p” value was 0.0013 among post-test score of experimental and control group which were less than equal to 0.05 level of significance hence it is considered to be extremely statistically significant. The findings co-ordinates the findings of Dr.Hyun- Joo-Oh, Dr. Moonhee Gang et al. (5th December, 2020) who had conducted a quasi-experimental study among elderly women from G and O province, South Korea on Development and Effect of a Salutogenic Program for Rural Elderly Women on Depression and Suicidal Ideation. The sample consists of 41. Non-equivalent control group pre-test post-test design was used by administering Geriatric Depression Scale Short Form (GDSSF-K), Korean version of Suicidal Ideation Scale and Atonovosk’s 13 SOC scale (Korean version). Participants received 12-session Salutogenic program for 6 weeks, twice a week. Collected data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, chi-square test, independent t-test and the study discussed the effect of Salutogenic program on sense of coherence, depression, and suicidal ideation, and considered its benefits in suicide prevention for elderly women [12]. These findings align with present studies as Salutogenic Program actually effective for reducing depression, self-harming behavior and sense of coherence among elderly.

The present study findings also revealed that calculated Unpaired t-test value and p-value of post test scores of sense of coherence (3 domains) of experimental and control group were in Meaningfulness (-4.368 & 0.000*), Comprehensibility (-2.621 & 0.013*), Manageability (-2.104 & 0.042*) as calculated p-value less than 0.05 level of significance hence there was significant difference in 3 domains of level of sense of coherence between post-test scores of experimental and control group. The results and analysis represented the potential value the qualitative application of the SOC, and additionally suggest that qualitative methods are under utilised in salutogenic research. This findings allign with a study conducted by Walsh, Sandra (October,2014) on Salutogenesis 2.0: An examination of healthy ageing-in-place using a qualitative application of the Sense of Coherence. the aim of this study was to examine the theory of salutogenesis (Antonovsky, 1987; 1996), within the context of later life, and to consider the value of salutogenesis as an analytical perspective applicable to understanding older people’s health and wellbeing as they age-in-place. This was done by applying the theory’s analytical construct, the Sense of Coherence (SOC), qualitatively and using its three components comprehensibility, manageability and meaningfulness, to make sense of data gathered from life review interviews. These interviews were conducted with a sample of ‘healthy’ and active older people, aged 75 years and older, living independently in the East and South-East of Ireland [13]. Based on the study, findings suggested that SOC plays a central role for health maintenance and health promotion among older people.

The present study revealed that in experimental group majority 46.42% of participants had mild depression, 28.57% had moderate and 17.85% had severe depression in pre-test and on post test result increased 53.57% of participants were normal, 42.85% had mild depression and 3.57% had moderate depression. Where as in control pre-test group majority 50% of participants had mild depression and 25% had moderate depression in old-age home. One similar cross-sectional descriptive study on Depression in Geriatric Population: Old Age Home and Community in Lucknow India conducted by U. Mohan, A. Gupta et al. (23 September 2015) coordinates with present study. Multistage sampling technique was used to include the requisite number of respondents from the community and all the elderly living in OAHs were included according to inclusion criteria. Geriatric depression scale Hindi version was used to diagnose depression in elderly people. Results revealed that depression was 27.7% among elderly residing in OAHs while it was 15.6% those residing at their own homes [14]. Hence this study also aligns with present study stating that the level of depression is major among elderly people.

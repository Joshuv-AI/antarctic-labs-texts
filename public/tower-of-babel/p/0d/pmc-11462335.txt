The new college entrance examination reform investigation and research on comprehensive quality evaluation
Authors: Mengna Zheng, Chengwu Ruan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe comprehensive quality evaluation is an evaluation system that identifies and explores students’ strengths. By examining the developmental progress of pilot provinces that have implemented comprehensive quality evaluation, valuable insights and guidance can be derived for other provinces preparing to adopt this evaluation approach. This study conducts an investigation and research on students, teachers, and middle managers from 12 high schools in two pilot areas, Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces. Using a combination of empirical and qualitative research methods, it aims to explore the levels of satisfaction and recognition among different research subjects regarding the current implementation situation and analyze the existing issues in the current implementation process. According to the corresponding theory, an in-depth analysis is conducted from four dimensions to uncover the underlying causes of the problem, while proposing recommendations to enhance and advance comprehensive quality evaluation. These recommendations are first to enhance further the standard level of matching and integration in comprehensive quality evaluation; second to augment the utilization level of comprehensive quality evaluation; third to elevate the significance of comprehensive quality evaluation results; and fourth to improve the credit rating system associated with comprehensive quality evaluation.Keywords: New college entrance examination, Ordinary high school, Comprehensive quality evaluation, Higher education, Quality progress

Background of the study and problem statement

Significance of comprehensive quality evaluation in the reform of the new college entrance examination

In the context of China's recent reform of the college entrance examination, comprehensive quality evaluation is not only a part of the reform of the mode of selecting talented individuals but also an essential transformation of their mode of training [1]. It is also a part and highlight of the leading measures to reform the new round of the high entrance system [2]. Evaluation is the transformation of the social needs for exam-oriented selection of talented individuals and one of the vital measures of the span between relying on “exam points” and paying attention to “individual development.” By observing and recording students' growth process, the comprehensive quality assessment further analyzes their development process, explores and cultivates their strengths, and meets their diverse development needs while also serving as a talent reserve to meet the needs of economic and social development and the cultivation of varied high-quality talented individuals [3]. In the face of the development of comprehensive quality evaluation over the years, what kind of experts should be cultivated to adapt to the era of such rapid growth is the common focus worldwide. The UNESCO report by the International Commission on Education for the Twenty-First Century, published in 1996, has already included the concept of “lifelong learning” and the three stages of the core literacy of “learning outcomes,” published in 2012. Both put forward new requirements for the quality of citizens [4]. The three stages include self-cognition and attitude to learning, subject knowledge and thinking ability, practical ability and innovative spirit. Among them, the demand for comprehensive quality evaluation in the new college entrance examination reform is the demand for the third stage of the core ability development of students [5]. With the necessary needs of the rapid development of society, the significance of including the content of the comprehensive quality assessment in the new college entrance examination as a reference basis for the college entrance examination has emerged. At present, Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces have already started to implement the results of the comprehensive quality evaluation as a reference basis for the admissions process of the college entrance examination system, so that comprehensive quality evaluation can be genuinely incorporated into the admissions process [6]. The significance of the evaluation results as a basis for talent selection is first, it can make students start to change their way of thinking in the evaluation and pay attention to their development in different fields, instead of just focusing on the results of a “core subject” or a specific type of competition that can add points to the entrance examination; second, it can develop students' multiple abilities, so that they can discover their strengths, develop their horizontal and vertical thinking, improve their diversified understanding, and enrich their creativity in the process of development [7]; third, through the effective accumulation of the three-year assessment, students can enhance their sense of self-responsibility so that when they face the changes and challenges of the future society, they can learn about a more humanistic and socially practical culture in the assessment process, and not just theoretical knowledge from books.

Practical problems facing comprehensive quality assessment in high schools1 in the pilot provinces of the new college entrance examination

Since the pilot reform of the new college entrance examination of China in 2014, along with the implementation of comprehensive quality evaluation in Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces, certain issues have been identified during evaluation and development, primarily including the following.

Regarding students, they passively lack understanding of the evaluation process, which encompasses various dimensions such as ideology and morality, academic level, physical and mental health, interests and specialties, social practice, personal accomplishments, awards, and sports health [8]. Moreover, they possess limited familiarity with the electronic recording platform used.

Regarding teachers, they still play a dominant role in the evaluation process. First, they heavily rely on ranking regular exam scores as a reference for evaluation, disregarding fair and objective process-based assessment of students' development. Second, some teachers lack the necessary professional competence for effective assessment and may resort to random evaluations merely to complete superficial tasks during the implementation of the evaluation process [9]. Third, China's long-standing reliance on an “exam-based” evaluation system places immense pressure on academic performance, ingraining it deeply in teachers' mindsets and hindering their ability to shift their psychological role within the evaluation process. The resulting psychological pressure also contributes to why teachers struggle to exert a positive guiding influence in this context.

Regarding the school and school administrators, although some schools have formed a professional evaluation research group, school administrators still face many problems, such as how to formulate an evaluation plan suitable for the school at the beginning of the evaluation; how to actively guide the participation of students and teachers in the process of practical implementation of the evaluation; how to solve the problem of evaluation content that is difficult to complete or understand only by the school's power [4]; how to record the evaluation process and results, and how to convert the recorded text into scores according to the situation of the school area.

Literature review

Studies on the dimensions of the comprehensive quality evaluation system of China found that it includes the requirements of students' moral and civic qualities, learning and innovation ability, cooperation and communication, sports and health, and aesthetics and performance. The first view is that students' comprehensive quality should be composed of “academic ability” and “nonacademic ability” [2]. “The second view, on the contrary, is that comprehensive quality only refers to nonacademic ability, and that ‘comprehensive quality’ extensively includes all kinds of knowledge and abilities that can be examined by traditional paper-and-pencil examinations, but in a general sense, it refers to the sum of other qualities that cannot be reflected by subject tests” [10]. As for the essence of comprehensive quality evaluation, a typical view is that “comprehensive quality evaluation is regarded as personality development evaluation, and the essence of quality education is to respect the uniqueness of individual development, and since the essence of quality education is personality development education, comprehensive quality evaluation is essentially personality development evaluation,” and Li Yanbing believes that “the essence of comprehensive quality evaluation is personality development evaluation, which is also authenticity, process evaluation, or internal evaluation” [11]. Wang Hongxi positioned the essence of comprehensive quality evaluation qualitatively as “it should be regarded as a kind of appreciative assessment or appreciation evaluation [12].”

The major studies on the problems arising in the implementation and practice of comprehensive quality assessment are “Student satisfaction in the implementation of comprehensive quality assessment” [13], “Teacher resistance in the implementation of comprehensive quality assessment” [14], “A study of rubrics in the implementation of comprehensive quality assessment” [15], “Fairness in the implementation of comprehensive quality assessment” [16], “Comprehensive quality assessment for independent admissions or incorporated into college entrance examination admissions” [17], “Web-based system, management system in the implementation of comprehensive quality assessment” [18].

The evaluation experiences of Western and East Asian countries are reflected in the following aspects: The admission standard of American colleges and universities is to select outstanding students by considering various aspects of their comprehensive level and diversity. They do not only use a single test score as the admission criterion [12]. Specifically, the US implements a comprehensive ability assessment and evaluation system with pluralism and rich diversity, and the recruitment criteria referred to cannot be measured by achievement scores, which does not mean that academic test scores are not valued. However, academic performance is used as a process evaluation benchmark [19]. Students' academic ability is examined through the content of the prescribed unified examination, and the academic results achieved by students at each stage of each semester for 3 years of high school are summarized as a process evaluation [20]. In addition, the students’ overall development is examined in a diversified and multifaceted manner by combining interviews, letters of recommendation from professors, application plans for admission, research proposals, and supporting documents of various extracurricular activities in which the student has participated [8].

To summarize, current research on comprehensive quality evaluation focuses primarily on the understanding and theoretical analysis of relevant policy texts, with some scholars also exploring the psychological dimension through theories such as multiple intelligences. However, there is limited research that examines the actual implementation of the evaluation. Therefore, in this study, two pilot areas with different situations were selected as the objects of investigation, from the perspective of the connotation of the comprehensive quality evaluation system, the process of the comprehensive quality evaluation activities, the structure of the comprehensive quality evaluation, and the motivation and satisfaction of the participants in the evaluation process. Through empirical research and in-depth interviews, combined with the theories of subjective education, evaluation of multiple intelligences, Marx's theory of comprehensive human development, and developmental evaluation, this study explores the real issues reflected in the implementation process of the policy in various regions and offers corresponding suggestions for improvement.

Method

Procedure

The survey was conducted from November 2016 to January 2019. The survey sample was drawn from 12 schools in two cities in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces, because Zhejiang province is the priority region where the evaluation is being implemented, and Jiangsu Province is the region where the evaluation is being preliminary implementation. Therefore, the selection of study subjects in these two provinces is representative. According to the different positioning of ordinary high schools, they were classified into three types: provincial model high schools, normal high schools, and integrated high schools, and three different groups (students, teachers, and school-level administrators) were randomly selected from the three different types of schools in the two provinces to participate in questionnaires and interviews as the research sample. Provincial model high schools refer to those high schools with strong academic ability in academic level tests and a high overall level of final exam results. They are followed by particular category high schools that include particular subjects, for example, English, German, and French, which feature minor languages as separate tests to select students and train them for specialties. Normal high schools refer to schools with slightly weaker academic abilities and a balanced training of students’ academic performance and comprehensive practical skills. Integrated high schools refer to schools where students choose to enter vocational and technical high schools after junior high school, but still have the opportunity to take the college entrance exam.

Participants

In this study, a questionnaire survey is the main research method, through the three types of direct participants in comprehensive quality evaluation (students, teachers, and school managers) to achieve the satisfaction of the evaluation and the results of data analysis. With the feedback results of data analysis and the research on relevant educational theories, this paper reveals the current status of the implementation of evaluation in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces, summarizes the problems arising in the practice of evaluation and analyzes the causes, to draw lessons from the excellent practices found in the implementation of evaluation, and put forward suggestions on the shortcomings.

The research survey counted 1500 paper versions of survey feedback questionnaires for students, 200 questionnaires for teachers and head teachers, and 180 questionnaires for administrators at the school level, totaling 1880 questionnaires. Among them are 1198 valid questionnaires for students, 175 for teachers, and 154 for school-level administrators. In addition, using the internet, 430 questionnaires were collected in the questionnaire star, and 358 effective questionnaires were collected (Table 2). The interview format consisted primarily of random interviews and interviews with individuals involved in the evaluation process in the two provinces’ research. The interviewees included students, teachers, and school administrators. It was necessary to schedule appointments in advance for interviews with teachers and school administrators. Furthermore, while ensuring the privacy of the interviewees, all interview content was recorded. The interview data were anonymized and utilized solely for subsequent analysis and research purposes. A total of 123 participants were interviewed, resulting in approximately 13,000 words of relevant audio data.

Measures

This study is based on the concept of psychological acceptability, which was first proposed by Gordon W. Allport (1897–1967), and a questionnaire was developed based on the research of related scholars. The questionnaire was based on six dimensions: value assessment, content analysis, cognitive level, participation level, credit level, and role level, with four questions for each dimension, making a total of 24 questions. For example, “the implementation of the comprehensive evaluation system can make our choices more diverse,” and “I know in which aspects to improve my score in the comprehensive evaluation.” The questionnaire used a Likert 5-point scoring system, where 1 is “Completely unsatisfactory,” 2 is “Basically unsatisfactory,” 3 is “Satisfactory,” 4 is “Basically satisfactory,” and 5 is “Basically satisfactory.” The higher the score, the higher the student's overall satisfaction with the implementation of the comprehensive evaluation system. In this study, Cronbach's α is 0.76, which indicates that the reliability of this questionnaire has been thoroughly explored in this study, indicating that the questionnaires for students, school-level administrators, and classroom teachers have good reliability and validity, and are suitable for the application of the survey on the degree of satisfaction with comprehensive quality assessment of the reform of the new college entrance examination.

Results

Relatively low overall satisfaction with evaluation implementation among three types of survey respondents

After analyzing satisfaction surveys and interviews with students, teachers, and school leaders, it can be inferred that the overall satisfaction with comprehensive quality evaluation in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces is relatively low. Specifically, this situation can be reflected in the following aspects: In terms of the dimension of value assessment, both students and school leaders in the two provinces exhibit a favorable disposition toward the intrinsic worth of evaluation within the context of the reform of college entrance examinations. The preliminary implementation stage of the comprehensive quality assessment in the two provinces, however, is characterized by instability, resulting in low satisfaction among teachers and students. This instability indicates an early stage of development [21]. In terms of content analysis, while school administrators in Zhejiang province consistently expressed satisfaction with the evaluation's content, other survey participants showed lower levels of satisfaction. This suggests that both provinces still have a relatively unclear understanding of the cognitive concepts associated with the evaluation's content, standards, and scope. The satisfaction with the cognitive rating dimension is evaluated based on both the expected value of satisfaction with the theoretical value and students' actual scores in high school entrance examinations, which impact enrollment decisions and contribute to a satisfactory state of basic admissions. In terms of the participation level, all three categories are currently not exhibiting a positive reflection and the overall satisfaction remains at a low level.

Implementation of evaluation in provincial model high schools in two provinces is relatively effective

Comparative and differential analyses were conducted on three types of Chinese general high schools. The findings indicate that the majority of dimensions in the survey assessing evaluation implementation in provincial teacher training–type high schools in both Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces showed a significant performance, as evidenced by F-tests with LSD post hoc tests and two-pair tests. The evaluation and implementation of the provincial model high school and the other two types of schools have shown significant synthesis in terms of effectiveness, indicating a favorable developmental trend. Specifically, in terms of the value assessment and content analysis dimensions, although all three types of high schools exhibited relative prominence in evaluations and demonstrated a consistent trend of high satisfaction among administrators, teachers, and students in each school, the development of general and inclusive schools still lacks equilibrium. In terms of the perceived rating, provincial model schools placed greater emphasis on evaluation and considered its implications carefully for students’ future advancement compared to middle leaders and teachers in other types of schools. Additionally, students expressed higher satisfaction with the credibility of the evaluations themselves and demonstrated an increased trust in their outcomes [20]. The normal and integration high schools have relatively higher levels of student participation, despite the discrepancy in developing comprehensive competencies related to academic professionalism, and overall quality compared to provincial teacher training high schools.

Content and criteria of the overall evaluation of comprehensive quality are not explicit enough

The satisfaction of students, teachers, and school-level administrators with the content of the evaluation was relatively low when comparing different types of schools. This indicates that stakeholders in the two provinces harbored doubts regarding the fairness of comparing evaluation results. The current focus in both provinces lies in the development of an online evaluation system to assess students' overall quality comprehensively. In Zhejiang province, this initiative was implemented using the “Entrance to the Information Management Platform for Comprehensive Quality Evaluation of Zhejiang Province's General High School Students,” while Jiangsu Province adopted the “Application Platform for Comprehensive Quality Evaluation in Jiangsu Province” [22]. Although the two provinces exhibit distinct characteristics in terms of dimensions and assessment methods on the platform from a value perspective, Jiangsu Province quantifies the evaluation of “exercise and health” through scores while Zhejiang province records students' development in this area through textual expressions based on content analysis [22]. However, it is evident that due to the diverse approaches to presenting assessment outcomes, the process of evaluating students' materials across schools remains enigmatic, thereby impeding meaningful comparisons. In the long term, in the forthcoming admission process of the senior secondary school entrance examination, challenges will also arise in aligning the outcomes of comprehensive quality evaluation conducted by schools across different regions with the actual admission criteria [7].

Results of the evaluation level have a relatively weak impact

The data results suggest that while some schools in Zhejiang province achieve a comparable level of satisfaction with the theoretical values of comprehensive quality evaluation implementation, thereby indicating their relatively higher satisfaction with the evaluation process, teachers, middle managers, and students in both provinces still have limited awareness of the impact of evaluation outcomes.

The evaluation still maintains the authority of school administrators and teachers, while students continue to assume the role of performers. For example, in the interview record during the research process, when asked: In which aspect of the comprehensive quality assessment test being implemented in your school do you think it is most useful to be reflected? The student replied: It can help us in the college entrance examination, and it can play a very important role! (a student from School A in Zhejiang province). It can be seen that students possess a predominantly generalized understanding of evaluation. Interviews with school administrators from Jiangsu Province who underwent the relevant training say that “middle-level cadres in schools are still in the process of figuring out how the evaluation will be implemented in their schools.” It was revealed that middle-level school officials are still in the process of formulating strategies for implementing evaluations in their respective schools. Besides, the challenges encountered during the trial implementation of the e-platform will impede the advancement of the evaluation method beyond the mere elevation of test scores, thereby posing difficulties in effectively reflecting comprehensive quality assessment results of the high school admissions evaluation system in securing its further enhancement and improvement in the long run.

Discussion

In the context of the new college entrance examination reform, the comprehensive assessment of students' holistic development in senior high schools constitutes a pivotal component of the admission process. Its aim is to counteract the prevailing educational paradigm that exclusively emphasizes academic performance and steer students towards more multifaceted and diverse developmental trajectories. Our study conducted a satisfaction survey among the stakeholders currently involved in the comprehensive quality evaluation, revealing the actual implementation of the evaluation in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces based on interview content and data analysis results. We also address the issues and challenges encountered by various types of schools and stakeholders in implementing the comprehensive quality evaluation in the experimental districts of these two provinces, integrating relevant educational theories.

Our research findings suggest that the impact of comprehensive evaluation varies across different types of schools. Notably, in provincial exemplary high schools where academic performance is highly valued, stakeholders have made significant progress and achieved notable effects in promoting and implementing comprehensive evaluation. This finding contrasts somewhat with Yichen Yang's [23] study, which did not differentiate between high schools based on their implementation of the evaluation. In fact, upon analyzing various types of schools, it becomes evident that the degree of emphasis on the implementation of comprehensive quality evaluation varies among different types of exemplary high schools. The greater the focus on academic performance, the more these high schools prioritize comprehensive quality evaluation, contrary to the initial hypothesis of our study.

In contrast to Xueyan Zhang's [24] findings, he contends that despite multiple revisions, the comprehensive evaluation system for high school students has yielded underwhelming results over the past decade of reform, still struggling to effectively identify college-bound talent under current evaluation system. But this study demonstrates that, under the backdrop of the new college entrance examination reform, the effective implementation of comprehensive quality assessment in provincial exemplary high schools reflects a significant qualitative advancement and progress in the development of comprehensive quality evaluation within the current reformation environment. Thus, by using various types of schools as the reference point for investigating and analyzing the actual implementation of evaluation, not only does it enhance Bo Ren ‘s [25] exploration of evaluating art high schools in his study, but also offers valuable insights for further advancing the development of evaluation. Furthermore, we can start from these schools with trends of change, understand the internal motivation sources for actively promoting evaluation, targeted promote these excellent experiences to different types of schools, and promote the further in-depth development of comprehensive quality evaluation, which is also one of the innovative points of this study.

Secondly, our study also highlights the imperfections in the specific content and standards of comprehensive evaluation of students' overall quality in the pilot areas, which is consistent with Green Andy ‘s [26]research. In contrast to the evaluation results obtained by Green Andy [26] from a single dimension, this study conducted further in-depth exploration. Firstly, from the perspective of the regional characteristics reflected in the evaluation itself, the specialties and talents of educated individuals in different regions have different characteristics. For example, the educated individuals in Jiangsu province have a more prominent talent in physical education during the basic education period, so they tend to focus on winning awards in swimming and other sports [27]. On the other hand, the educated individuals in Zhejiang province have more talent in painting and table tennis [28]. However, in the actual scoring standards for evaluation, there is no detailed explanation on how to scientifically assign scores to the value of sports awards of different types. The relevant explanations are also vague and not detailed enough, which causes great difficulties for policy implementer in the actual operation of the evaluation process. In fact, ensuring the satisfaction of all stakeholders with reasonable and equitable scoring criteria throughout the evaluation process is a crucial prerequisite for advancing the evaluation procedure.

In addition, in terms of evaluation alignment, previous studies indicate a consensus with Siyu Sun's [29] research that universities are actively responding to and implementing state-promoted evaluation policies in various ways. Through discussions, they have preliminarily designed corresponding score allocation standards for evaluating high school results. Moreover, there are studies indicating that certain educational institutions engage in hypocritical behavior by treating the bonus policy for students specializing in art and sports as a means of generating profit. They seek to maximize evaluation bonus fees through exploitation of the policy for personal gain via corrupt practices [30]. This highlights the current chaotic state of the evaluation system's implementation, posing significant barriers to maximizing its actual effectiveness. In light of the aforementioned challenges, it is imperative to prioritize the enhancement of third-party evaluation and oversight mechanisms within departmental infrastructure to ensure equitable assessment practices, aligning with Asal Wahyuni' s [31] perspective. Secondly, in terms of information network platform construction, it is imperative to enhance the development of electronic record platforms. Because only by ensuring transparency and openness throughout the evaluation process can we guarantee the fair and just implementation of diverse evaluative values. Therefore, establishing an equitable environment for evaluation implementation and refining the specifics of current evaluations are paramount.

In conclusion, our research analysis reveals that multiple factors influence the initial value and objectives of comprehensive evaluation in the two provinces throughout the implementation and pr-implementation process. These factors primarily encompass the types of secondary schools, the maintenance of the evaluation system itself, the refinement of criteria for assessing implementation, the environment in which evaluation is carried out, the interplay of interests among stakeholders involved in evaluation implementation, and the satisfaction levels of students, educators, and government officials. These findings are consistent with previous research, highlighting the impact of different factors on evaluating implementation. Furthermore, this study underscores the imperative of enhancing the evaluation system itself, establishing an equitable and impartial environment for evaluation implementation, and reconciling conflicts among relevant stakeholders in actively advancing evaluation implementation. The objective is to advance the evolution of evaluation beyond the current reliance on scores as the sole criterion for talent selection, ensuring that educational recipients can attain diverse individual development within a fair and just evaluative milieu while upholding the original intentions of comprehensive policy design. Furthermore, in the process of establishing electronic assessment platforms, timely technical support should be provided to learners to address any issues they may encounter while using the platform. Consistent quality standards for assessment and the current developmental level of learners are essential to ensure that evaluation remains substantive. Additionally, effective communication between policy implementer and relevant stakeholders is pivotal for advancing evaluation practices and requires further in-depth research.

Future works and limitations

Propositon 1: Perfect the matching and docking standard of comprehensive quality evaluation

The evaluation system should be designed to allow for regional disparities, as schools in different regions exhibit variations influenced by factors such as cultural diversity, geographical conditions, and levels of economic development [32]. Therefore, the system of quality evaluation cannot fully develop a standardized evaluation criterion to assess students' comprehensive abilities, necessitating some consideration of regional disparities. Secondly, The design of the evaluation system should be tailored to the individuality of each student. The emphasis in the content of the assessment system should be on facilitating students' use of their strengths, rather than focusing solely on avoiding their weaknesses. Because it implies that the assessment of human development, as previously mentioned, should prioritize “comprehensive and unrestricted growth” rather than pursuing a notion of “balanced development” in education [33]. Thirdly, The formulation of the evaluation system's content should also reinforce the school's distinctive features. This is because only a personalized school can cultivate individualized students who possess creativity, can transition smoothly from specialization to specialization, and ultimately achieve all-round development. Fourth, The notion of evaluation necessitates continual updating to align with contemporary perspectives. It is imperative to perceive test-taking proficiency as an integral component of holistic development rather than a burdensome entity by default. The internal structure of the evaluation system needs to be adjustable, taking into account regional disparities and individual differences in education, while also reflecting its comprehensiveness and flexibility during implementation. The last aspect involves fostering a sense of creativity and innovation in the evaluation process during implementation. Different regions must achieve interactive information feedback in practice, rather than relying solely on mechanical upward and downward effects. Moreover, cultivating the evaluators' creative consciousness should be emphasized from various perspectives.

Propositon 2: Improve the useable level of comprehensive quality evaluation functioning

For a considerable time, China has relied heavily on teachers to assume a pivotal role in executing the evaluation process, thereby granting them a certain level of authority in assessment. The teachers' subjective factors, such as personal preferences, easily influence their definition of students' judgments. This can manifest in the interpersonal halo effect, particularly when labeling certain students as “good or bad” due to specific incidents [34]. Consequently, it impacts the evaluation results directly. Teachers, being the primary evaluators, tend to generalize student evaluations and often employ vague language such as “studies hard and works actively,” which hinders the reflection of individual differences in evaluation outcomes. It is evident that when the primary focus of evaluation shifts away from the student body, which is the main subject under assessment, the original purpose of evaluation becomes compromised. This factor has long impeded the widespread implementation of quality education. Therefore, to ensure that a comprehensive quality assessment plays an effective role, the principle of “who uses, who evaluates” should be firmly adhered to at all times. The main body of evaluation should include students’ self-assessment, mutual evaluation among students, and even parental evaluation. By increasing the participation of all parties involved in the evaluation process, we can not only diversify the types of assessment but also promote fair and impartial evaluations. Simultaneously, students can assess their performance not only through declarative statements, such as written essays, but also by exploring alternative forms of expression such as poetry or prose, thereby enabling a more diversified evaluation process.

For high schools, there is a need to focus on the authenticity of the student assessment process and the reliability of the assessment materials. The evaluation of the school is mainly based on the student's self-statement report and the discussion of relevance with other teachers. In this dominant evaluation system where responsibility is shared, students can learn to take responsibility for their own development and self-summary.

Moreover, each institution of higher education must prioritize the meticulous use of resources during the evaluation and admission process. As the direct beneficiaries of comprehensive quality assessment, these institutions should shoulder corresponding responsibilities for enrollment sources and long-term institutional development.

Finally, high schools and higher education institutions can also take on the responsibility of selecting talented individuals by adopting a system of evaluation by type, which can be tailored to the expertise they wish to recruit.

Propositon 3: Improve the level of the comprehensive quality evaluation results

Staverbeam, an American educator, said that the most important intention of evaluation is not to prove, but to improve [35]. The aim is to promote the development of the student, emphasizing the student as a subject and their diversified, comprehensive, and integrated development. It can be seen that the purpose of evaluation is to promote the all-round development of quality education [36] and we should focus equally on its developmental and selection functions, looking to find a balance between them.

Therefore, in schools in the provinces where the evaluation has been implemented, points can be assigned to the content of the comprehensive quality evaluation assessment, and the points can be assigned without too high a value, aiming at steady progress [37]. For regions yet to implement the new college entrance examination reform, it is advisable to establish a “soft link” between evaluation and college entrance examinations to foster a more ethical environment within society. The ultimate purpose of this is to make the evaluation not developed to participate in the actual score assessment stage of the college entrance examination, students, parents, teachers and other groups can also attach great importance to the cultivation of comprehensive quality [38].

Propositon 4: Improve the credit level of comprehensive quality evaluation

Currently, both provinces have established their electronic information platforms; however, the modules within these platforms are relatively simplistic, posing challenges to keeping pace with the diverse developments in evaluation. Therefore, the evaluation categories and details of the information platform need to be updated promptly with students' progress. The platform is currently limited to updating teachers, students, and admitting universities at specific intervals. Therefore, the scheduling of opening hours should be reconsidered to ensure that individuals under evaluation can access information from the platform regardless of their time availability or status. Additionally, by enhancing students’ self-assessment and peer assessment, and encouraging them to upload audio and video recordings of their competitions and specialties, the evaluation process materials documented on the platform may thereby be diversified further. This will ensure that the evaluation results serve as more effective references during the high school enrollment process.

Furthermore, the Ministry of Education managers should be present during the establishment and enhancement of the platform to oversee the real-time recording process. This will allow for the use of real-time data in reflecting comprehensive quality assessment progress, thereby promoting fairness and impartiality in high school enrollment procedures. For instance, the scheduled assessment plan time can be monitored through regular evaluations conducted every 2 or 4 weeks, with a final evaluation at the end of the semester. Moreover, it is essential to diversify monitoring methods rather than rely solely on one approach; conducting on-site interviews can provide valuable insights into students’ activities. Thematic monitoring can also be conducted by establishing relevant review panels to address general issues that arise during the monitoring process. The evaluation should include various feedback mechanisms to help the evaluators understand the implementation process. These may involve written and electronic monitoring reports and verbal feedback sessions to help individuals analyze challenges and provide suggestions. The feedback information should be timely and accurate, enabling cities and districts to regulate development concept evaluation effectively by monitoring data. This will provide a reliable basis for identifying deviations from evaluation policies and phenomena, facilitating macro control.

Limitations

In terms of the research objects, six different types of schools were selected in each of the two provinces, and the schools with the development level above the medium level were concentrated in each category. With the implementation of the evaluation, the follow-up research also needs to supplement the implementation of the schools with low comprehensive ability. The collected data can not fully represent the overall situation of the comprehensive quality assessment so far, so further follow-up investigation and research are needed.

Conclusion

Against the background of the new college entrance examination reform, this research adopts a combination of empirical and qualitative research methods, aiming to reveal the actual implementation of comprehensive quality evaluation in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces from the perspective of implementing evaluation. The research shows that the current problems with the implementation of the assessment in the two provinces are mainly in the following areas understanding the cognitive concepts related to the content, criteria, and scope of the assessment is still relatively unclear to the relevant stakeholders in the two provinces; there are differences between the ability to cultivate different types of high school teachers and the development of comprehensive competencies in terms of academic professionalism and comprehensive qualities; the consistency of the results of the comprehensive qualities assessment in the schools of the different districts deviates from the actual admission criteria problems and related issues encountered in the course of piloting the e-platform. In the face of these problems, based on subjective education theory, multiple intelligence evaluation theory, Marx's comprehensive development of human beings theory, developmental evaluation theory, and other related educational theories, this research puts forward some suggestions for perfecting the matching and docking standards of comprehensive quality evaluation, improving the availability of evaluation function level, improving the function level of evaluation results, and improving the credit rating level of evaluation. It is hoped that these specific recommendations will provide a reference and help other provinces that are about to implement evaluation.

A novel predictive model for evaluating thrombus regression in postpartum venous thromboembolism patients
Authors: Meixiang Yu, Congcong Jia, Zhenyu Zhou, Qingshan Chen, Xuemei Di, Ning Zhang, Xiaoqing Zhang, Zhendong Xu, Hai Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAntithrombotic evaluation of postpartum venous thromboembolism (VTE) after anticoagulant therapy is challenging because of the lack of high-quality clinical evidence.AimTo identify and validate a machine learning model to predict thrombus regression in postpartum VTE patients.MethodThis study constructed three cohorts of postpartum VTE patients receiving low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH): retrospective (n = 200), prospective (n = 50), and external (n = 29) cohorts. The main endpoint was imaging-based thrombus regression. Based on clinical guidelines and literature, we screened 16 potential predictors. The retrospective dataset was analyzed using eight machine learning models to predict thrombus regression after anticoagulant therapy. The performance of the model was evaluated by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC), residual analysis, accuracy, sensitivity and specificity. This study may help assess changes in the thrombotic state of postpartum VTE patients and guide clinical precision medication.Keywords: Low-molecular-weight heparin, Machine learning model, Postpartum venous thromboembolism, Random Forest model, Thrombus regression

Introduction

Pregnancy-related venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a leading cause of maternal morbidity and mortality in developed countries [1, 2]. The risk of VTE during pregnancy is approximately sixfold greater than that in the general population [3]. The ante- and postpartum incidences of VTE are similar, but the daily absolute risk of VTE is highest during the first 6 weeks after delivery [4]. Despite thromboprophylaxis, the reported incidence of VTE remains between 0.5 and 2.2 cases per 1000 deliveries [5].

Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) is internationally recommended as the first-line anticoagulant for pregnancy-associated VTE [6], as it does not cross the placenta and is therefore safe for the fetus. However, the response of LMWH therapy on clinical outcomes have not been elucidated [7, 8]. Clinical practice mostly relies on short-term anticoagulant markers, such as anti-Xa activity [9, 10] or D-dimer levels [11]. These biomarkers are also reported to be poorly correlated with actual thrombotic risk in pregnant women [12]. Despite anti-Xa levels within the therapeutic range [13, 14], a considerable proportion of postpartum women experience VTE events.

Direct imaging via lower extremity venous ultrasound and computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) remains the gold standard for diagnosing deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), respectively [15]. Thus, prior studies have used serial imaging to define anticoagulant response and have shown that higher LMWH doses can promote greater thrombus regression [16, 17]. Although a correlation between the clinical rate of recurrence and the rate of thrombus regression was not achieved [18]. A meta-analysis revealed a strong inverse correlation between thrombus regression and recurrent VTE (r = − 0.70; P = 0.008) [19]. However, the routine use of serial imaging for monitoring is precluded by limitations in equipment availability, procedural complexity, cost, and, in the case of CTPA, radiation exposure.

Current guidelines recommend 6–12 weeks of intensive anticoagulant therapy for postpartum VTE [1], yet physicians must balance the antithrombotic benefit against the inherent risk of bleeding. Additionally, there are a certain number of patients whose thrombi resolve early and who may not require 6 weeks of intensive therapy. Thus, there is a need for a more direct and reliable method to predict thrombus regression and guide clinical precision medication.

While predictive modeling using machine learning has gained significant traction in medical practice [20]. Its application in VTE has largely focused on predicting the incidence of thrombosis in patients receiving thromboprophylaxis or on estimating bleeding risks [21, 22]. Predictive models specifically designed to evaluate the dynamic process of thrombus regression in postpartum VTE patients after anticoagulant therapy remain entirely lacking.

Aim

This study aimed to develop and validate a machine learning model to predict thrombus regression in patients with postpartum VTE after LMWH therapy.

Method

Study design and population

This was a multicenter study aimed at developing and validating a machine learning prediction model to predict thrombus regression in patients with postpartum VTE after LMWH therapy. We utilized all available eligible cases from our institutions to ensure data feasibility and employed multiple validation strategies to assess model robustness. Three cohorts of postpartum VTE patients receiving LMWH treatment were included in this study: a retrospective training cohort (n = 200) and a prospective validation cohort (n = 50) from Shanghai First Maternity and Infant Hospital (2020–2024) and an external validation cohort (n = 29) from the International Peace Maternal and Child Health Hospital of China Welfare Society (2023–2024). The prospective cohort consisted of consecutively enrolled patients after model development, while the external cohort was independently collected from a different hospital to preliminarily test generalizability.

A total of 16 candidate variables were collected based on their potential association with VTE pathophysiology and treatment response, as suggested by clinical guidelines [23] and previous literature [12, 24]. These included: demographics (age, body mass index (BMI), delivery mode, gravidity, parity), coagulation markers (prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), thrombin time (TT), INR, fibrinogen, fibrin degradation products (FDP), D-dimer, AT-III activity, and anti-Xa activity), and thrombus classification (DVT and PE).

The exclusion criteria for this study included the following: a heparin allergy; hemoglobin (Hb) concentration < 90 g/L for the possibility of increasing the risk of recurrent PE and DVT [25, 26]; a platelet (PLT) count < 50,000 mm3; the use of oral anticoagulants or other antiplatelet drugs; and unavailability of key predictor variables (e.g., anti-Xa, D-dimer) or the outcome assessment (imaging).

To minimize bias, we employed a multicenter design including retrospective, prospective, and external cohorts, used standardized data collection protocols, and applied rigorous exclusion criteria to enhance the representativeness and generalizability of our study population.

Clinical management and study outcomes

All the enrolled participants received subcutaneous nadroparin (86 IU/kg every 12 h), in accordance with the 2018 Chinese Medical Association guidelines for postpartum VTE management [27]. The starting dose was based on weight of patients; no prophylactic dose adjustments were made based on renal function. Dose was managed per standard of care only in case of clinically significant bleeding events. Blood samples were taken 4 h after administration to measure anti-Xa activity, which corresponded to the expected peak concentration. DVT and PE were diagnosed using venous ultrasound and computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA), respectively. The primary endpoint was thrombus regression based on ultrasound or CTPA during hospitalization (typically between Days 5 and 7).

Thrombus regression was defined as: for DVT, recanalization on ultrasound; for PE, no obvious thrombus was found in the primary occluded pulmonary segments on CTPA.

Data preprocessing

Continuous variables were standardized (z-score normalization). Categorical variables were one-hot encoded. Variables with variance inflation factor (VIF) > 5 were considered collinear and removed.

Development and comparison of machine learning models

To ensure a comprehensive evaluation and avoid bias towards a single algorithm, we selected eight representative machine learning models spanning different learning paradigms and complexities. The retrospective cohort (n = 200) was randomly split into a training set (70%, n = 140) and an internal validation set (30%, n = 60). The following eight models were trained on the training set using the 14 standardized variables (after removing collinear ones): Logistic Regression (LR): A linear model for probability estimation; LASSO Regression: A linear model with L1 regularization for feature selection; Ridge Regression: A linear model with L2 regularization to handle multicollinearity; Elastic-Net Regression: Combines L1 and L2 regularization; Support Vector Machine (SVM): A max-margin classifier that finds the optimal hyperplane to separate classes; Decision Tree: A tree-structured model using if-then rules; Random Forest (RF): An ensemble of multiple decision trees, reducing overfitting and improving accuracy; eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost): An advanced ensemble tree model using gradient boosting.

Model evaluation, optimization, and selection

Model performance was evaluated on the internal validation set using: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC) with their 95% confidence intervals (CI); Sensitivity; Specificity; Residuals. Hyperparameters for each model were optimized via grid search with tenfold cross-validation on the training set to maximize AUC-ROC.

The model that demonstrated the highest AUC-ROC, the smallest residuals, and robust performance across other metrics on the internal validation set was selected as the optimal predictive model.

Validation of the optimal model

The performance of the selected optimal model was further independently assessed on two datasets: The prospective validation cohort (n = 50), and the external validation cohort (n = 29). Performance metrics including AUC-ROC (95%CI) were reported for these cohorts. Afterward, the compared predicted outcomes with actual thrombus regression observations was also analyzed for validation.

The interpretability of the final model was explored using variable importance scores and partial dependence plots (PDPs) to understand the contribution and marginal effect of key predictors.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation for continuous normally distributed variables. Categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages. Continuous variables between groups were compared with Student’s t test. Categorical variables were compared with the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test if necessary. Statistical significance was assumed with two-sided P values of < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org).

Reflexivity statement

The research team acknowledges potential biases arising from the retrospective nature of the training data and the single-center origin of the primary cohort. To enhance transparency and rigor, we explicitly state that model development and validation followed predefined protocols, and all analytical decisions were documented. Independent external validation was conducted to mitigate overfitting and assess generalizability.

Results

Clinical basic characteristics

This retrospective study ultimately included 200 postpartum VTE patients who received LMWH treatment. All patients had these 14 variables routinely measured and follow up imaging. During hospitalization, 142 (71.0%) patients achieved thrombus regression. Statistically significant differences in anti-Xa activity (0.58 ± 0.17 vs. 0.51 ± 0.20; P = 0.009), AT-III levels (82.26 ± 9.56 vs. 78.94 ± 9.90; P = 0.028), D-dimer levels (2.92 ± 2.46 vs. 3.95 ± 3.08; P = 0.024), and FDP levels (8.37 ± 7.16 vs. 11.62 ± 10.40; P = 0.032) were detected between the thrombus regression group and the nonregression group. The non-regression group exhibited a higher incidence of DVT (P = 0.007). No statistically significant differences were found between the two groups in terms of other baseline characteristics, comorbidities, or concomitant medications. All DVT cases involved calf muscular veins (12 bilateral, 22 left-sided, 16 right-sided), with no significant difference in laterality between groups. The detailed data are presented in Table 1.

Identification of the optimal prediction model

All eight machine learning models demonstrated moderate predictive performance in assessing the thrombus regression of LMWH for postpartum VTE (Fig. 2), with AUC-ROC values ranging from 0.61 to 0.83. Notably, the RF model achieved the highest AUC-ROC of 0.831 (95% CI 0.696–0.967), with an accuracy of 0.77, a sensitivity of 0.45, and a specificity of 0.89. In terms of the model residuals, the RF model yielded the smallest residuals for VTE. Therefore, the RF model was defined as the optimal prediction model for assessing the thrombus regression of LMWH for predicting postpartum VTE.

Validation of the RF prediction model using prospective and external datasets

The AUC-ROC for the RF model on the prospective and external cohorts were 0.771(95%CI 0.638–0.905) and 0.744 (95%CI 0.507–0.962), respectively. The RF model demonstrated strong and consistent predictive performance on the independent cohorts (Table 2). In the prospective cohort, accuracy for predicting regression was 94.29% (33/35) and for non-regression was 86.7% (13/15). In the external cohort, the corresponding accuracies were 90.00% (18/20) and 77.8% (7/9), respectively.

Identification of the key prediction indicators in the RF model

The importance of each variable in the RF prediction models is shown in Fig. 3. Anti-Xa activity, AT-III levels, D-dimer levels, and BMI were identified as the most crucial variables influencing thrombus regression in postpartum VTE. In terms of the PDPs, as the anti-Xa factor concentration increased, the model’s predicted probability of thrombus regression increased, stabilizing at approximately 0.54 IU/mL. For BMI and D-dimer, the predicted probability initially increased but then decreased, stabilizing at approximately 5.0 mg/L for D-dimer.

Validation of prediction capability based on the key predictive variables of anti-Xa, AT-III, D-dimer and BMI

To evaluate the predictive value of anti-Xa, AT-III levels, D-dimer levels and BMI for thrombus regression in postpartum VTE, two RF models, a full-factor model (Model 1) and a model using only these four factors (Model 2), were compared using the prospective and external datasets. The prospective dataset showed comparable predictive capability between Model 1 (AUC-ROC = 0.771; 95% CI 0.638–0.905) and Model 2 (AUC-ROC = 0.836; 95% CI: 0.721–0.951; P = 0.201). Discussion

In this study, we developed and validated a machine learning model to predict thrombus regression in postpartum VTE patients treated with LMWH. Using a multi-cohort design, we identified the RF model as the optimal predictor, with robust performance maintained across retrospective, prospective, and external validation sets. The model highlighted a concise set of four key variables: anti-Xa activity, AT-III, D-dimer, and BMI.

Model performance

Among the eight machine learning models evaluated, the RF algorithm demonstrated superior predictive performance for thrombus regression in postpartum VTE patients receiving LMWH therapy. In the retrospective training cohort, the RF model achieved the highest AUC-ROC of 0.831 (95% CI 0.696–0.967), with an accuracy of 0.77, a sensitivity of 0.45, and a specificity of 0.89. In terms of the model residuals, the RF model also yielded the smallest residuals for VTE. This strong performance persisted in independent validation, where the RF model attained accuracies of 94.29% for predicting thrombus regression in the prospective cohort and 90.00% in the external cohort, confirming its robustness and generalizability.

RF is an ensemble learning method particularly well-suited for clinical prediction tasks due to its ability to model complex, non-linear relationships and interactions among variables without overfitting, even with moderate sample sizes and multiple correlated predictors [28]. From a clinical perspective, the RF model offers substantial utility in two key areas: (1) enabling early, individualized assessment of thrombus regression after LMWH initiation, and (2) supporting precision medication strategies by highlighting modifiable factors such as anti-Xa and BMI. Although the full model incorporated 14 variables, our analysis revealed that a simplified model based on the four key predictors maintained comparable predictive performance (external cohort: full‑model AUC-ROC = 0.771 vs. four‑factor AUC-ROC = 0.836; P = 0.201). This parsimonious version could be especially valuable in busy clinical settings where rapid risk stratification is needed, while the comprehensive model remains useful for in‑depth evaluation in stable or elective follow‑up scenarios.

Model interpretation and clinical utility

The clinical translation of machine learning prediction models is often hindered by challenges in interpretability and clinical utility. In this study, we addressed this limitation by leveraging the inherent interpretability features of the RF algorithm, namely variable importance ranking [29] and partial dependence plots [30]. These tools quantify the contribution of each predictor and visualize its nonlinear relationship with the outcome. The variable importance ranking identified anti-Xa activity, AT-III levels, D-dimer levels, BMI as the four most crucial factors in this study. This finding aligns with current pathophysiological understanding.

Anti-Xa level monitoring has been proposed to guide LMWH therapy [31]. Although its clinical utility remains debated [32, 33], the effectiveness of LMWH therapy is still defined as the target steady-state peak and trough anti-Xa activity at 0.5–1.0 or 0.1–0.2 IU/ml, respectively [34]. Notably, AT-III deficiency, a common form of thrombophilia, is frequently observed in VTE patients [35]. D-dimer, a marker of fibrinolytic activity and thrombotic burden, serves as a dynamic indicator of ongoing coagulation [11]. And maternal BMI is an important risk factor for postpartum VTE, which may effect on LMWH pharmacokinetics [36, 37].

However, owing to the lack of high-quality data in pregnant and postpartum patients, studies have largely focused on the development of prediction models for thrombotic risk in patients receiving thromboprophylaxis [22, 38] or on models for safety evaluation [39] rather than directly evaluating thrombus regression after LMWH therapy. Anyway, these 4 predictors were also contributors of thromboprophylaxis or bleeding risk models [40, 41]. To our knowledge, this study was the first to identify and validate a prediction model for assessing thrombus regression in postpartum VTE, thereby providing a novel and exploratory approach to the field.

The nonlinear relationship revealed by the PDP for BMI and D-dimer, suggested optimal therapeutic windows or risk thresholds. The stabilizing effect of anti-Xa activity beyond approximately 0.54 IU/mL in the PDP offers a data-driven reference point that may complement existing therapeutic ranges. This interpretable framework translates into potential clinical actions. For a patient predicted to have a low probability of regression, clinicians might prioritize verifying and optimizing anti-Xa levels, assessing for AT-III deficiency, monitoring D-dimer trends more frequently, or considering dose adjustments accounting for BMI, rather than uniformly extending therapy duration. Thus, the RF model facilitates a shift from a uniform treatment protocol towards individualized risk stratification and precision medication strategies in postpartum VTE management.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, the sample size, particularly for the external validation cohort (n = 29), is relatively small. This limits the precision of performance estimates and the robustness of generalizability conclusions. Future large-scale, multi-center prospective studies are needed for definitive validation. Second, the training data came from a single-center retrospective cohort, which may introduce selection bias and limit the diversity of the patient population. Third, despite the 14 specific variables included in the model, we cannot eliminate all the possibility of possible factors, such haemoglobin levels. Fourth, our model was developed in a Chinese population, and its applicability to other ethnic groups requires further investigation.

Conclusion

This study presents the construction and validation of a prediction model for thrombus regression based on a retrospective, prospective, and external cohort of postpartum VTE patients. The RF model was determined to be the optimal model, and anti-Xa activity, AT-III levels, D-dimer levels, and BMI were validated as key predictive factors. These findings may help predict thrombus evolution in postpartum VTE patients and guide clinical precision medication.

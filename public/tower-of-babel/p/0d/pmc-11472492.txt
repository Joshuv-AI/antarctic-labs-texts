Reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the contraceptive knowledge assessment scale in college students
Authors: Jiayang Zhang, Maoting Tian, Lei Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo translate and revise the Contraceptive Knowledge Assessment (CKA) scale and apply it to Chinese college male/female students. After cross-cultural adjustment of the source scale, the reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the scale were tested.Study designThis study utilized a cross-sectional research design. Translation, cognitive interviews and a psychometric evaluation were carried out. A survey was then conducted among 388 college students in Shanghai to test the reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the CKA scale.ResultsThere were 22 items in the Chinese version. Factor analysis extracted 8 common factors, with a cumulative contribution rate of 58.923%, and 3 items were eliminated. The Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.793, and the test-retest reliability Pearson correlation coefficient was 0.743.ConclusionThe Chinese version of the CKA scale has good reliability and validity and is suitable for assessing the level of contraceptive knowledge.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20337-8.

Introduction

In recent years, with the development of the social economy, college students have become more open-minded regarding sexual attitudes. The proportion of college students engaging in premarital sex and unmarried cohabitation is increasing, and the incidence of sexually transmitted diseases, unwanted pregnancy and miscarriage is also increasing, which causes mental distress such as depression, anxiety, anger, and grief [1]. The level of understanding of contraceptive knowledge and the use of contraceptive methods are directly related to the sexual and reproductive health of college students [2]. The lack of contraceptive knowledge limits the ability of college students to have safe sex and increases the incidence of adverse sexual and reproductive outcomes. Infectious diseases and premarital pregnancy have caused serious physical and mental health problems for college students. A study conducted in China in 2018, involving 2,026 undergraduate students, utilized the self-designed “Questionnaire on Sex-related Issues of College Students” to explore college students’ perceptions of premarital sex and their current behavioural status [2]. However, although there are investigations and studies on college students’ reproductive and contraceptive knowledge in China, most of the studies used self-designed questionnaires that did not provide exact reliability and validity; thus, the quality of the questionnaires was doubtful, and such questionnaires cannot be widely applied either.

In 2017, Meagan Campol Haynes and others revised the “Contraceptive Knowledge Inventory (CKI)” in the Handbook of Sexually Related Measures. Based on the CKI published in 1976, it combines new names and new usages of contraceptive methods in today’s society [3]. The Contraceptive Knowledge Assessment (CKA), a new tool for measuring contraceptive knowledge, was designed and established. CKA can better reflect the level of contraceptive knowledge and evaluate the understanding of contraceptive knowledge among college students. The instrument was developed by referring to the main scientific publications of the American Family Planning Association and using the Delphi method and semi structured interviews with patients, and the reliability and validity were verified. The criterion validity was examined by having participants complete both the CKA and the CKI, the gold standard, at one time-point, and a medium correlation effect (rho = 0.73) with power of 0.80 was detected. The repeat testing conducted on patients after 2–4 weeks showed no difference between the initial test scores and the repeat test scores (r = 0.79, p = 0.667), indicating that the CKA has good temporal stability [3].

In a 2023 US study, CKA was employed as an instrument to evaluate the degree of contraceptive knowledge among young adults both before and after the utilization of a web-based contraceptive decision aid named Healthy Sex Choices, which is devised to assist young adults from various backgrounds in making well-informed choices regarding their contraceptive decisions [4]. In a Jamaican study conducted in 2021, CKA was employed to gauge the contraceptive knowledge of women at risk of unintended pregnancy in the Kingston area of Jamaica. CKA was utilized to evaluate participants’ knowledge regarding the reversibility, capacity to conceal use, contraindications, and side effects of contraceptive methods [5]. It has also been used in Turkey, Vietnam and Uganda to assess the level of contraceptive knowledge among college students and reproductive-age women [6–8].

At present, there is no reliable and valid contraceptive knowledge scale for college students in China. There is an urgent need for tools that can evaluate the knowledge level to inform targeted measures to improve contraceptive knowledge among college students. While there are other assessment tools available, the CKA was chosen for its robust reliability and extensive validation in diverse contexts. Additionally, its use allows for comparability with prior studies, enhancing our study’s relevance and potential contribution to the field. The purpose of this study is to adapt the CKA into Chinese for easy use by Chinese students and then to conduct a validity and reliability study by applying it to determine whether it is a practical tool for assessing the contraceptive knowledge level of college students and even the public in China.

Methods

Design

This study utilized a cross-sectional research design. Translation, cognitive interviews and a psychometric evaluation were carried out. Figure 1 presents the flow of the standardized phases of the study.

Phase I: translation and cognitive interviews

Translation and back-translation

After obtaining the consent and authorization of Professor Mona Saleh who designed the original CKA, a research group was established to perform a Chinese translation.

First, independent translation was conducted by bilingual students translator with healthcare backgrounds. Through text comparison and joint discussion, a Chinese translation was formed after several revisions. Second, the integrated Chinese version was back-translated into English independently by two translators who were proficient in both Chinese and English (one was a Chinese-American native of the United States, and one was a nursing student who had studied in the United States). After the translation was completed, the two nursing students integrated the translation to improve the Chinese version.

Expert reviews

A group of experts were chosen according to the following criteria: (a) a master’s degree or more; (b) engagement in obstetrics and gynaecology clinical medicine, clinical nursing or nursing education; and (c) willingness to participate in this study. In this study, a doctor of obstetrics and gynaecology, a head nurse of obstetrics and gynaecology, a nurse of obstetrics and gynaecology, a nurse manager in the nursing department and a doctoral lecturer in the Fudan University nursing school were involved to evaluate the clarity, accuracy and importance of the first draft of the Chinese version of the CKA from the aspects of semantics, language habits and equivalence of concepts. The content assessment adopted a 1-4-level scoring method to select the relevance of each item to the corresponding dimension, where 1 represents “not relevant”, 2 represents “somewhat relevant”, 3 represents “quite relevant”, and 4 represents “highly relevant”. Based on the experts’ ratings, the Content Validity Index (CVI) was calculated, and items with low scores (indicating weak relevance) were revised or removed. The feedback also provided valuable insights into improving the language clarity and cultural appropriateness of the Chinese CKA.

Cognitive interviews

The questionnaire adheres to the scoring method established by the original CKA scale. To ensure the adapted version’s appropriateness for our specific cultural context and to evaluate the clarity and understandability of the translated questions, a pilot study was conducted on 25 college students prior to the main survey. This preliminary step was crucial not only for assessing the practical application of the questionnaire but also for identifying and rectifying any unforeseen ambiguities or issues, thus enhancing the overall reliability and accuracy of our data collection for the larger-scale study. The results showed that 92% (23/25) of the respondents believed that the questionnaire was clear and easy to answer and added supplementary explanations for relevant information where other students had doubts. For instance, the word “spermicide” was modified from “shi jing zi mie huo” to “sha jing ji (Chinese)” in the option “You should use water-based lubricants with spermicide” in question 7. 88% (22/25) of the respondents completed the questionnaire within 9 min. It can be seen that the Chinese version of the CKA scale has good acceptance by the target population.

Phase II: psychometric evaluation

Data were collected by the researcher. First, a unified guide was used to explain the purpose and process of the study, to ensure that the respondents fully understood the study before they signed the informed consent form and answered the questionnaire. After being reviewed by the researcher, any missing items were filled in. The survey was administered through an online platform (“Survey Star”, Changsha Ran Xing Science and Technology, Changsha, China), which was chosen for its robust data encryption, user-friendly interface, and compatibility with various devices, ensuring wide accessibility and secure, anonymous participation. Each respondent received a stationery set as a gift for participating in the study. A total of 388 questionnaires were distributed in this study, and 388 were recovered for an effective recovery rate of 100%.

Sample

A convenience sample of undergraduate and postgraduate students was recruited for the study in October 2021 in Shanghai, China. The inclusion criteria were (a) having basic reading and writing ability and (b) knowing the purpose of the study and being willing to participate. There were no exclusion criteria. In total, 388 participants were invited and enrolled in this study.

Instruments

CKA [3] is composed of 25 items, including reproductive physiology, contraceptive mechanism, efficacy, side effects, medical contraindications, common misunderstandings, sexually transmitted diseases and emergency contraception, reflecting a wide range of contraceptive knowledge assessments. The items were scored by selecting the correct answer, and the total score ranged from 0 to 25 points. The higher the score, the higher the contraceptive knowledge level. The original English version of CKA has good retest reliability (r = 0.79) and criterion validity (r = 0.73).

Sociodemographic data, including participants’ basic information (grade, sex, major, place of origin, lifestyle, etc.), cognition of contraceptive methods, ways to acquire contraceptive knowledge and misunderstanding of contraceptive methods, were also collected. An additional file shows the questionnaire we used to collect the sociodemographic data [see Additional file 1].

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 20.0 software. A descriptive analysis of the anthropometric data of the sample was performed, which included frequency (percentages) for categorical variables and the mean and standard deviation for continuous variables. The Kaiser‒Meyer‒Olkin (KMO) value and Bartlett’s sphericity test were applied to perform factor analysis. A KMO value > 0.50 and Bartlett’s sphericity test < 0.05 indicated that the data were suitable for factor analysis.

The common factors were extracted by principal component analysis (PCA), and then the maximum variance method was used for orthogonal rotation. Items with load coefficients < 0.4 on each factor were eliminated to achieve dimensionality reduction. If the cumulative explained variance in common factors was > 50%, the questionnaire had good construct validity [9].

Content validity was analysed using the content validity index (CVI). According to the CVI calculation formula [10], CVIi=2(Ne-N/2)/N (Ne is the number of experts who fully agreed with item i, and N is the total number of experts). The questionnaire is deemed to have good content validity if the CVI ≥ 0.75 [11].

Internal consistency measures the extent to which items in a scale are interrelated and is assessed using Cronbach’s α coefficient. A value of ≥ 0.7 shows that the items are adequately interrelated to form a scale [12]. The test-retest reliability was assessed using the Pearson correlation coefficient. A value of ≥ 0.6 with a good relationship between two measures indicates that the instrument produces low random measurement error [12].

Expert consultation

Two rounds of expert consultation were conducted in this study. In the first round of expert consultation, the translation of contraceptive proper nouns was modified and improved after combining expert opinions, such as modifying ‘uterine cancer’ to ‘endometrial cancer’. The second round of expert consultation was not modified. Finally, all items of the scale were retained.

Item analysis

The total score of the questionnaire was sorted, and the top 27% (≥ 10 points) was the high score group, and the last 27% (≤ 5 points) was the low score group. The independent sample t test was used to test the difference between the two groups. The results showed that there were statistically significant differences in the scores of 25 items and the total score between the high score group and the low score group (P < 0.05, P < 0.01). The CR values of each item were all greater than 3.0, indicating that each item had good discrimination.

Validity

After two rounds of expert consultation, five experts made an objective judgement on the preliminary factors and item pools. The I-CVI (item level content validity index) of item 15 was 0.800, the I-CVI of the other items was 1.000, and the S-CVI (scale-level content validity index) of the questionnaire was 0.960. The high CVI scores indicated that there was great agreement among experts on the relevance of items. The results of the construct validity are shown in Fig. 2; Table 2. It showed that a total of 8 common factors were extracted, and item16, 18, 25 did not meet the factor determination standard and should be excluded.

Reliability

The internal consistency test of the collected CKA scale data showed that the Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.793, and the reliability was good. After two weeks, 50 respondents filled out the questionnaire again to test the retest reliability. The Pearson correlation coefficient was 0.743, and the retest reliability was good.

Discussion

The application value of the CKA scale

In recent years, the sexual problems of college students have attracted much attention. With the development of society, sexual attitudes are becoming increasingly liberal. Premarital sex, sexually transmitted diseases, premarital pregnancy and unsafe abortion have increased dramatically among college students in China. People’s knowledge often affects their behaviour. The lack of contraceptive knowledge restricts the ability of college students to have safe sex. Therefore, college students’ knowledge of contraception will help reduce the occurrence of behaviours that are not conducive to reproductive health [13]. By evaluating the contraceptive knowledge of college students, on the one hand, it can clarify the needs of college students for contraceptive knowledge and carry out targeted reproductive health education. On the other hand, it can also evaluate the intervention effect after health education and other interventions to ensure the quality of reproductive health education.

There are a limited number of assessments for contraceptive knowledge, and few of them have been tested for their efficiency. A sexuality and contraception knowledge instrument (Sex ContraKnow Instrument) was developed in Spain, and the Cronbach’s α was 0.73. However, the criterion validity was not examined [14]. Researchers in Turkey adapted the CKA to Turkish, finding that the Cronbach’s α was 0.86, and the correlation analysis of test-retest points was 0.975 (97.5%) with a p-value < 0.001, which showed good reliability and test-retest reliability [6]. The findings of our study show that the Chinese CKA has good internal consistency and test-retest reliability, and support the use of the Chinese CKA as a reliable tool to evaluate the understanding of contraceptive knowledge of college students, help educators carry out targeted reproductive health education, and provide a reference for intervention effects.

Validity

Construct validity

The original scale divided each item into eight categories: reproductive physiology, contraceptive mechanism, efficacy, side effects, medical contraindications, common misunderstandings, sexually transmitted diseases and emergency contraception. Because the original scale did not carry out factor analysis, 388 valid data points were analysed by exploratory factor analysis in this study. The KMO value was 0.806, and the Bartlett spherical test statistic was 1411.820 (P < 0.05), indicating that the correlation between variables was strong and suitable for factor analysis. Through principal component analysis and the maximum variance orthogonal rotation method, the number of factors and items were determined according to the following conditions: (a) the fixed factor number was 8; (b) the factor was in line with the cartel’s steep slope test principle; (c) each factor contained at least 2 items; (d) items with load values < 0.4 were deleted; (e) when there were similar loads on different factors (at the same time, the load values of more than 2 factors were all greater than 0.4, and the difference was less than 0.2), the choice was made in combination with the overall expression significance of the scale and professional knowledge; and (f) factors were easier to name. The results of factor analysis showed that a total of 8 common factors were extracted, including reproductive physiology, contraceptive mechanism, efficacy, side effects, medical contraindications, common misunderstandings, sexually transmitted diseases and emergency contraception, which basically conformed to the theoretical model of the original scale. According to the factor determination standard (d), items 18 and 25 were deleted, and item 16 was deleted according to the factor determination standard (e), as shown in Table 2. The cumulative variance contribution rate of the 8 factors was 58.923%, indicating that the Chinese version had good construct validity.

Content validity

In the development process of the original English version of the CKA, the expert reviewers validated the medical precision of every component in the preliminary CKA. Feedback from semi structured interviews with patients indicated that the length of the assessment was suitable and the layout was legible. In our study, the I-CVI and the S-CVI were examined. It is generally believed that I-CVI ≥ 0.78 and S-CVI ≥ 9 indicate that the content validity of a scale is good. The I-CVI of the Chinese version of the CKA scale was 0.800-1.000, and the S-CVI was 0.96, both greater than the recommended boundary value, which could clearly reflect the level of college students’ understanding of contraceptive knowledge, so it had good content validity.

Reliability

The reliability reflects the internal correlation between the items of the scale and the stability and consistency over time, which is often evaluated by the reliability coefficient. It is generally believed that Cronbach’s α coefficient and split-half reliability coefficient are greater than 0.7, which means that they have good internal consistency.

In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the CKA scale and the Pearson correlation coefficient of the retest reliability were greater than 0.7, and the reliability was acceptable. Nonetheless, this finding was somewhat diminished in comparison to the outcomes observed in the original English version and the Turkish adaptation. The small sample size may be the reason for the low reliability of the scale. In terms of test-retest reliability, it may be because the subjects queried the relevant contraceptive knowledge after the first completion of the scale, which improved the accuracy of the second filling in the scale and affected the test-retest reliability.

Limitations

The present sampling strategy, focusing exclusively on students from Shanghai, may compromise the external validity by limiting the generalizability of the findings to the broader population of Chinese undergraduate students. To mitigate this constraint, future researches should consider using a stratified sampling technique that includes a more heterogeneous cohort drawn from multiple educational establishments across various geographical locations. With only 18.3% of the respondents acknowledging sexual activity, the current dataset might inadequately reflect the contraceptive knowledge-seeking behaviours of sexually active students. This discrepancy could potentially undermine the applicability of the conclusions to the entire student body. Therefore, it is essential to incorporate a more comprehensive demographic profile in future researches.

Conclusion

The study supports the use of the Chinese version of the Contraceptive Knowledge Assessment (CKA) scale as a reliable and valid tool for measuring contraceptive knowledge among college students in China. This study follows the guidelines recommended by AAOS (the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons) for cross-cultural adaptation so that the Chinese version of the CKA scale is clear, easy to understand, and has good acceptance of the target population. According to the survey results of this scale, educators can clarify the needs of college students for contraceptive knowledge. Targeted reproductive health education can also provide a reference for the evaluation of the effect of health education and other interventions to ensure the quality of reproductive health education. However, because this scale is a preliminary application in China, although the sample size of the research subjects has reached the level of factor analysis, it is slightly smaller, and consists of only students from one university. Thus, the representativeness of the sample is limited. Further research can expand the sampling range and include universities in different regions for verification research.

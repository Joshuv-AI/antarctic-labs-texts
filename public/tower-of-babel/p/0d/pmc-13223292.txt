Numerical analysis of Jeffery fluid flow over shrinking/stretching sheet with magnetic and chemical effects: mass transfer and stability analysis
Authors: Zeeshan, Waris Khan, Muhammed I Syam, Ali Rehman, Shah Hussain, Bashir Salah

ABSTRACT
AbstractNon-Newtonian materials like the Jeffery fluid model (JFM) are crucial in many industries, such as food and fiber optics. In this analysis, a mathematical framework is proposed to describe the performance of a two-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic Jeffery flow model (MHD-JFM) across an exponential and contracting sheet under the influence of destructive and generative chemical reactions. To facilitate this analysis, a couple of difference equations is transformed into ordinary equations by applying the resemblance alteration. To obtain a numerical solution for the various fields of interest, namely velocity components and mass transfer at the surface (MTS), the bvp4c method is employed. The ground-breaking aspect of this study lies in its meticulous examination of the influence and stability of the Lorentz force on Jeffery fluid, particularly in the context of extending or contracting sheets that experience internal heat transfer as well as destructive and generative chemical reactions. Remarkably, such a comprehensive investigation has not yet been undertaken in the existing literature. Consequently, the present work is validated by comparing with available work. A stability investigation is conducted to ensure the reliability of the first solution. Through the utilization of graphs, the impact of factors like the Schmidt number, Hartmann number, destructive and generative chemical reaction factor, and Deborah numbers on the velocity component and mass transfer is thoroughly examined and discussed. It is investigated that mass transfer surfaces are a diminishing function of Deborah numbers. It has been detected that as the magnitude of escalates from 0.2 to 0.8, the velocity contours exhibit a declining trend across the boundary layer. A decreasing behaviour is observed for the velocity profiles as the quantities of Hartmann number enhances from 0.2 to 0.6. Introduction

Non-Newtonian materials have garnered a significant amount of attention in recent decades owing to their relevance in the realm of technical and materials science, which has only served to amplify their allure. The Jeffrey fluid model (JFM) is not similar to other non-Newtonian models since it incorporates both relaxation and retardation time parameters. It is more desirable to characterize viscoelastic fluids, such as biological and polymeric materials, because of such properties. The JFM is given a special position in the many fluid systems studied in fluid dynamics due to its unusual behavior that has fascinated researchers over the years. It is particularly remarkable that relaxation and retardation effects have been included in JFM since it brings valuable information about complex flow phenomena. The behavior of JFM is important to know, both in practice and of theoretical interest. These are the enhancement of sheet-forming effectiveness, transistor fabrication performance, crystal formation, and extrusion process. The investigation of behavior of the boundary layer movement (BLM) over a stretching sheet is important in the context of technological and manufacturing processes. Sakiadis1 was the first to introduce the concept of BLM to define the flow of a fluid layer near a flowing uniformly moving solid substrate. This was the first to conduct the groundwork of research in this area, where many researchers have elaborated on the theory of Sakiadis to explore the stretching sheet movements in many kinds of liquids, such as viscoelastic and non-Newtonian fluids, and in a variety of circumstances of heat flux. Actually2–10, of the published articles in the recent past represent some of the most notable progress in this field of research.

It is worth noting that most of the research conducted thus far has focused on flows induced by a vertically stretching sheet11–15. In recent times, Raptis and Perdikis16 have made noteworthy contributions to the field by investigating magnetized viscoelastic movement through a stochastic and extending surface with thermal slip. Their research has been particularly focused on two main objectives. Firstly, they aim to expand the existing knowledge about flow characteristics by studying the behavior of Jeffrey liquids16. This type of liquid exhibits unique properties that make it different from other types of fluids, and exploring its flow characteristics can lead to valuable insights. Secondly, Raptis and Perdikis16 developed a mathematical clarification for a non-linear vigorous situation. This aspect of their research is particularly important as it addresses the complexities associated with the flow of fluids in real-world scenarios. In conclusion, the concept of BLM has been extensively studied and expanded upon by numerous researchers.

Also, one should consider a physical effect in fluid dynamics, especially when a magnetic field is acted on the fluid flow, the so-called magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effect. This effect is particularly important to the behavior of the electrically conductive liquids, i.e. saltwater, liquefied metals, electrolytes, and plasmas. One of the most important effects of MHD effect is the appearance of a resistive force known as Lorentz force that is quite strong. It is the Lorentz force that leads to significant deceleration of the fluid flow. Through proper management and control of this phenomenon, one can delay or extend the process by which the flow changes to turbulent nature. The potential of such an effect has been identified by many researchers in various fields of science and has been aimed to implement and make use of it in their work. Indicatively, Das et al.17 have thoroughly examined the effect of MHD effect on the behavior of hybrid nanofluid flow in a porous channel. Their thorough analysis revealed that the existence of MHD significantly elevated fluid temperature, which further developed the importance and usage of this effect in other settings. On the same note, Wahid et al.18 reported a similar effect of the magnetohydrodynamics on the heat transfer in their analytical work. Magnetic and MHD effects are other studies that have been researched by many other researchers. Shamshuddin et al.19 used the homotopy analysis technique to analyse the flow of a magnetised Williamson-micropolar nanofluid over a two-directional extending surface. In similar studies, Shamshuddin et al.20 were examining the behavior of magnetized Casson ternary nanofluid on a stretching surface using the CattaneoChristov heat flux model. Also, Baithalu et al.21 examined the impact of magnetic dissipation and radiations on free convection in a conducting hybrid nanofluid with a conducting rotating cone-disc system. These researches contribute to our knowledge of MHD and its impact on different phenomena, which increases our knowledge of the given sphere.

Markin22 reported the multiple solutions of the mixed convection flow across a permeable sheet. On the same note, Alsenaf et al.22 investigated the dual solution of the mixed flow of double-diffusivity around a porous vertical cylinder. The available research lacks studies investigating flow dynamic of a magnetic non-Newtonian JFM through an expanding surface with respect to examination of stability, as the study has a wide implication and is widely used in modern industries and technological advancements. The purpose of the current study is to eliminate this gap and contribute to the literature that is already available. This study uses the JFM as the basic framework of this study in analyzing the flow features in such a situation. Moreover, the fluid presented to be analysed is electrically conductive, which complicates the analysis even more because of the stability problems and the magnetic field implications. In order to describe the complicated interplay of the fluid movement and the chemical transformations, the occurrence of the chemical processes within the flow field is also taken into account in the given study. In more in-depth understanding of the compounding effects of conductivity, magnetism and chemical reaction on flow behavior, a mathematical model is developed and numerically solved.

Conclusions

A numerical scheme is proposed to describe the behaviour of a two-dimensional magnetized JFM across an exponential and contracting surface under the influence of destructive and generative chemical reactions. The basic flow equations are converted to ODEs from PDEs through a similarity transformation. The numerical solution is obtained via bvp4c for velocity components and mass traverser. The main objective of this analysis lies in its meticulous examination of the influence and stability of the Lorentz force on JFM over an extending or contracting sheet with internal heat transfer and chemical species. Remarkably, such a comprehensive investigation has not yet been undertaken in the existing literature on this subject matter. Consequently, the present work is validated with the published work. The findings have been verified against previously published research to guarantee the validity of the current investigation. The first solution’s durability and physical significance are confirmed by a comprehensive stability examination. A decreasing behaviour is observed for the velocity profiles as the quantities of Hartmann number enhances from 0.2 to 0.6. It can be observed that mass transfer exhibits a diminishing trend in response to destructive chemical reactions, whereas mass transfer experiences an increasing trend for generative chemical reactions.

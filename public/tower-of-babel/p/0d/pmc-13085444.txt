Peritonitis and paralytic ileus due to COVID-19 in a 29 weeks pregnant woman: a case report
Authors: Katia Mahiou, Fanny Bounin, Justine Mille, Morgane Lanouziere, Pierre Mouillot, Georges Tarris, Emmanuel Simon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCOVID-19 infection has caused a global health crisis with over 6,956,900 deaths. Extra-pulmonary forms of the infection are common, including digestive symptoms. Pregnant women are at greater risk of developing severe forms of the infection. They are also affected by extra-pulmonary symptoms.Case presentationWe report the case of a 28week + 4 pregnant woman with paralytic ileus caused by inflammatory peritonitis related to COVID-19 leading to premature birth. Pulmonary deterioration was secondary to abdominal symptoms.ConclusionsTherefore, the presence of gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms with or without respiratory symptoms may be a warning sign of COVID-19 to be considered, especially in pregnant patients.Keywords: COVID-19, Paralytic ileus, Pregnancy

Background

Since the first described case in December 2019, the global coronavirus infection − 2019 (COVID-19) caused by Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has resulted in an unprecedented global health crisis with numerous cases of severe respiratory infections and more than 6,956,900 deaths [1]. Shortly after the onset of the pandemic, pregnancy emerged as a risk factor for severe forms of SARS-CoV-2 infection [2]. Moreover, it appeared that this infection could be the cause of severe extra-pulmonary complications, particularly at the digestive level [3–5]. We report here the case of an inflammatory peritonitis associated with a paralytic ileus in a 28weeks + 4days pregnant patient with COVID-19 infection.

Case presentation

The patient was a 36-year-old French woman, BMI 25, active smoker, Gravidity 3 Parity 2, with 2 term vaginal deliveries and no notable obstetrical history. She had a history of stage IIa Hodgkin’s disease treated with chemotherapy and pelvic irradiation from 2003 to 2004, a laparoscopic appendectomy, a laparoscopic right ovarian transposition and an exploratory laparotomy for suspected occlusive syndrome in 2000. At this laparotomy they found bowel dilatations and concluded to an episode of bowel ileus. No bowel resection was performed. Since the end of treatment for Hodgkin’s disease at the age of 17, she has been in remission with a competent immune system. For this pregnancy she had a gestational diabetes under insulin without fetal impact. Her COVID-19 vaccine regimen included 3 doses of mRNA vaccine (09/06/2021, 21/07/2021, 15/12/2021).

She presented to the emergency room at 28 weeks + 4 days, with sudden abdominal pain in the flank and right iliac fossa (Table 1).

Initially, she was hospitalized for threatened preterm labor, with a cervical length of 25 mm, uterine contractions, and suspected right acute renal colic. Renal ultrasound examination did not show pyelocaliceal dilatation. She had no biological inflammatory syndrome (white blood cell, C - reactive protein, sodium, potassium and magnesium levels within normal range). In view of the persistence of pain despite the introduction of morphine, a pelvic MRI was performed. The MRI found a caecal dilatation of 7 cm due to colonic obstruction without any sign of digestive distress and a medium abundance pelvic effusion. In view of the intensification of uterine contractions and a modified cervix measured at 19 mm, tocolysis with intravenous atosiban and antibiotic therapy with cefotaxime and metronidazole was started. An antenatal corticotherapy with betamethasone 12 mg was performed at 28 + 5weeks and 28 + 6weeks. The persistence of the pain in spite of taking level 3 analgesics and realization of several enemas, justified the realization of an abdomino-pelvic CT scan showing a colonic dilatation due to colonic obstruction associated with an increase of the pelvic effusion, a gastric dilatation and a thickening of the small intestines. A nasogastric tube was placed, bringing back fecal fluid, attesting to the state of paralytic ileus.

The lack of pain relief and the premature term of the pregnancy motivated a transfer to a type 3 maternity unit. The woman’s hemodynamic status was stable, respiratory rate with 2 L of oxygen and the pain persisted with a VAS of 10/10. Morphine titration was performed, partially relieving the pain (VAS 5/10). An opinion from a digestive surgeon was requested, but no surgical indication was found in the face of this picture of occlusion on stercoral stasis. The various bacteriological samples taken on admission came back sterile (vaginal swab, blood cultures, cytobacteriological examination of urine). At 29 weeks of pregnancy, a biological inflammatory syndrome appeared with a C-Reactive Protein of 209 mg/L and leukocytes of 19 Giga/L. At the same time, a clinical deterioration was observed with increasing abdominal pain and an increase in oxygen tolerance, motivating a new throaco-abdomino-pelvic scan. The latter revealed the appearance of bilateral pleural effusions of moderate abundance associated with multiple parenchymal condensations of infectious appearance. At the abdomino-pelvic level, the CT scan showed an increase in diffuse intraperitoneal effusion with signs of peritoneal irritation and paralytic ileus of the small intestine (Fig. 1).

Abdomino-pelvic CT scan: Intraperitoneal effusion associated with thickening and contrast of the peritoneal sheets of the right parietocolic gutter showing peritonitis. Thickening and contrast of the right uterine tube without pyosalpinx

Antibiotic therapy with spiramycin was introduced in front of the viral-like pneumopathy. The influenza PCR and legionella antigenuria were negative but the nasopharyngeal PCR for SARS-CoV-2 at 29weeks came back positive i.e. 3 days after the onset of symptoms and approximately one year after the last vaccination.

Given the absence of clinical improvement despite morphine analgesia, worsening inflammatory Markers, and signs of poor clinical tolerance, a multidisciplinary team decision was Made to perform an exploratory laparoscopy. This approach was chosen due to the high suspicion of a surgical abdomen and the limitations of imaging in this context. The laparoscopy revealed pseudomembranes in the right iliac fossa and the pouch of Douglas, along with approximately 300 mL of purulent effusion and an edematous, non-purulent right fallopian tube. Bacteriological samples were taken. No digestive perforation was visualized. She was transferred to the Intensive Care Unit because of persistent severe hypoxia during the operation due to pre-existing COVID-19 pneumonia and complicated by inhalation pneumonia during induction. Antibiotic therapy was escalated to tazocillin and gentamycin in the face of clinical deterioration. Given the criteria of severe Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (hypoxemia PaO2/FiO2 < 100mmhg), sedation was Maintained with curarization and protective ventilation was introduced. Given the favorable pulmonary evolution, it was not necessary to resort to prone position. One day after the laparoscopy procedure, the woman presented uterine contractions and 1 cm cervical dilatation, associated with fetal heart rate abnormalities. An emergency cesarean section was performed at 29 + 1 weeks, after infusion of magnesium sulfate.

Extraction was performed with the bag intact. Pseudomembranes were associated with an inflammatory aspect of the posterior wall of the uterus. The right ovary was very atrophied, the utero-ovarian ligament was not visualized. Exploration of the abdominal cavity did not show any digestive perforation or flange. Cultures of the purulent peritoneal fluid and pseudomembranes remained sterile. The peritoneal samples were not tested for SARS-CoV-2.

The patient was discharged from the intensive care unit at day 1 of the caesarean section and transferred back to the gynecological surgery department. On day 3 of the caesarean section, the patient presented a new respiratory deterioration with increased dyspnea and oxygen dependence with an oxygen saturation at 85% under 10 L of O2 on the mask, justifying the performance of a thoracic angiography. The latter showed no pulmonary embolism, but severe bilateral alveolar-interstitial lung involvement typical of a SARS-CoV-2 infection, estimated at 75% (Fig. 2).

Unenhanced axial (left) and sagittal (right) chest scans. The scans show multiple ground glass opacities, reticulation and extensive consolidation predominantly in the right lung hemifield bilaterally

The woman was readmitted to intensive care for acute respiratory distress syndrome with a dexamethasone treatment, continuation of tazocillin, and high-flow nasal oxygen therapy. Clinical improvement was observed over 3 days without the need for orotracheal intubation and weaning from the high flow nasal oxygen therapy. All bacteriological respiratory samples came back negative. She was discharged at day 10 of the C-section.

Six weeks later, she had no respiratory sequelae. There was good abdominal healing.

The newborn

Cesarean section at 29 weeks and 1 day resulted in extraction of a female infant, weighing 1335 g, forty-eighth percentile on the WHO curve, with an Apgar score of 6/4/4. Cord pHs were 7.19 arterial, 7.21 venous, and lactates were 2.2mmol/L. The neonate was transferred to the neonatal intensive care unit. The newborn presented with bilateral grade II intraventricular hemorrhage and neonatal jaundice. The suspicion of early neonatal infection was overruled. Tracheal aspiration performed at birth came back negative to SARS-CoV-2. She was discharged from intensive care at 30weeks + 5 days. She was discharged home, at a post-conceptional age of 36weeks + 6 days.

Placentocultures remained sterile. Histopathological analysis showed an eutrophic, showing preterm villous maturation, however devoid of chorioamnionitis (Fig. 3A and B). Immunohistochemistry for viral proteins showed positivity of SARS-CoV-2 3Cl-like protease in trophoblastic cells and scarce inflammatory cells in the intervillous space (Fig. 3C and D).

Discussion and conclusions

Since the beginning of the global COVID-19 pandemic, digestive symptoms caused by infection have been described in the literature [6–8]. A review of the literature on digestive symptoms in pregnant women by Makvandi et al.; involving 259 patients found diarrhea in 4.5% of patients with COVID-19 and 1.6% with abdominal pain [9]. In this study and in the literature, there is no description of paralytic ileus inaugurating a COVID-19 infection. Cases of intestinal ileus have been reported in the literature in patients with severe pulmonary disease who developed digestive symptoms secondary to the pulmonary symptoms [10–12]. Although the mechanism of COVID-19-induced gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms is unclear, one possible mechanism is the ability of SARS-CoV-2 to bind to ACE-2 receptors, which are abundantly expressed in the GI tract [13]. Another possibility is that SARS-CoV-2 productively infects human intestinal enterocytes [14].

Feto-maternal vertical transmission of COVID-19 is very rare [14]. In our case, despite the focal presence of SARS CoV-2 viral proteins in the placenta, there was no evidence of fetal or neonatal infection. One limitation of our case is that we did not test for COVID-19 in peritoneal effusion. However, COVID-19 can diffuse into the peritoneal cavity [15, 16]. In the study of Jakimiuk et al.. in patients infected with COVID-19 who had undergone caesarean section, where the peritoneal fluid analyzed did not find the presence of COVID-19 [17]. In our patient, the worsening of respiratory symptoms occurred secondary to the digestive symptoms, at day 3 of the cesarean section. We have not found a similar chronology in the literature, particularly in pregnant women. Our patient first presented with uterine contractions associated with cervical shortening. By increasing the inflammatory syndrome and contractions, COVID-19 may have contributed to the premature delivery. In our case, the clinical and radiological picture of inflammatory peritonitis associated with paralytic ileus led to a premature birth with significant pulmonary morbidity. In this patient, exploratory laparoscopy allowed us to eliminate the differential diagnoses of this bowel obstruction syndrome (mechanical obstruction, infectious peritonitis, digestive perforation). The chronology of symptoms suggests a diagnosis of intestinal ileus associated with COVID-19 infection.

On the other hand, the history of Hodgkin’s disease does not seem to have had any impact on this pregnancy (no fertility problems, no immune deficiency for nearly 20 years). During her two previous pregnancies, the patient did not present any threat of premature delivery, confirming that there is no sign of underlying cervical insufficiency.

Therefore, the presence of gastrointestinal symptoms may be a warning sign of major secondary pulmonary damage by COVID-19 that should be taken into account, especially in pregnant patients.

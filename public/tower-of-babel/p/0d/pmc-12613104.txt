Marstacimab, an antitissue factor pathway inhibitor, combined with bypassing agents: effects on thrombin generation and in hemostatically normal rats
Authors: Marjorie A. Peraza, Swapnil Rakhe, Susan Hurst, Madhu Sirivelu, Karrie Brenneman, Debra D. Pittman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMarstacimab is an antitissue factor pathway inhibitor recently approved for treatment of hemophilia without inhibitors and under investigation for treatment of hemophilia with inhibitors. Management of hemophilia in people with inhibitors frequently requires the use of bypassing agents such as recombinant activated factor (F)VII (rFVIIa), activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC), or plasma-derived FVIIa (pdFVIIa)/FX mixture (pdFVIIa/FX).ObjectivesWe explored the potential for excessive thrombin generation and potential additive effects of concomitant administration of marstacimab and bypassing agents.MethodsAn in vitro thrombin generation assay was used to assess marstacimab in combination with pdFVIIa/FX in hemophilia A or B with inhibitor and in nonhemophilic donor plasmas. In vivo studies in hemostatically normal rats examined the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of marstacimab (on days 1 and 8) in combination with rFVIIa, aPCC, and pdFVIIa/FX (on days 8-10).ResultsIn vitro, the combination of marstacimab plus pdFVIIa/FX decreased lag time and additively increased peak thrombin. In vivo, the combination of marstacimab plus rFVIIa was associated with higher incidence/severity of pulmonary and injection site thrombi/emboli, and marstacimab plus aPCC was associated with alterations in coagulation parameters. Effects of the combination of marstacimab plus pdFVIIa/FX were limited to alterations in coagulation parameters and similar to those of pdFVIIa/FX alone.ConclusionThese findings suggest marstacimab in combination with pdFVIIa/FX can increase hemostasis without excessive in vitro thrombin generation, and marstacimab in combination with rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX is well tolerated in hemostatically normal rats.

Introduction

Hemophilia is an X-linked recessive disorder associated with deficiency of coagulation factor (F)VIII (hemophilia A) or FIX (hemophilia B). The reduced activities of these intrinsic blood coagulation factors in individuals with hemophilia lead to reduced thrombin generation and reduced conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin, which is needed for formation of a stable clot [1].

Development of neutralizing alloantibodies (inhibitors) to FVIII and FIX is a challenge in achieving effective treatment for hemophilia, arising in 30% of people with severe hemophilia A and in 5% with severe hemophilia B [2]. The presence of inhibitors necessitates the use of bypassing agents such as recombinant activated FVII (rFVIIa; also known as activated eptacog alfa, Novoseven, Novo Nordisk), activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC; FEIBA, Baxalta Inc), or plasma-derived FVIIa (pdFVIIa)/FX combined mixture (pdFVIIa/FX; Byclot, KM Biologics Co, Ltd) [3].

In response to these challenges, there has been an expanded focus on novel nonfactor replacement therapies for the treatment of hemophilia, including antitissue factor (TF) pathway inhibitors (TFPIs) [4]. TFPI is a potent inhibitor of FXa and the FVIIa-TF complex in the extrinsic coagulation pathway. It interrupts the initial phase of coagulation by inactivating FXa (with its Kunitz-type serine protease inhibitor domain [K]2) and FVIIa-TF complex (via the K1 domain) [3,5].

Marstacimab (PF-06741086) is a human anti-TFPI immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody that binds to the K2 domain of human TFPI (K1K2 binding affinity, KD 3.7 nM), recently approved in the United States and the European Union for the prophylactic treatment of hemophilia A and B without inhibitors and under investigation for prophylactic treatment of hemophilia A and B with inhibitors. Marstacimab should not be affected by FIX or FVIII inhibitors, as it targets the extrinsic and common coagulation pathways. The 3-month phase 1b/2 study of marstacimab and the phase 2 long-term follow-up study demonstrated safety and efficacy, with meaningful reductions in annualized bleeding rate and treatment-related changes in pharmacodynamic markers, as well as efficacy in preventing bleeding episodes in people with hemophilia with and without inhibitors [6,7]. Results of the phase 3 BASIS study demonstrated that weekly subcutaneous (s.c.) marstacimab reduced annualized bleeding rate compared with prior on-demand or routine prophylaxis therapy in adolescents and adults with severe hemophilia A or moderately severe to severe hemophilia B without inhibitors [8]. Breakthrough bleeds in patients with inhibitors who receive marstacimab may require the use of standard bypass therapies [3]. Therefore, there is a need to more fully elucidate any interactions between marstacimab and standard bypass therapies.

A potential safety concern is excessive thrombin generation with the use of marstacimab in combination with bypassing agents. This concern has arisen with other nonfactor replacement therapies because of the potential for development of thrombotic adverse events (AEs) if hemophilia interventions overcompensate for clotting deficiency and tip hemostasis from a hemorrhagic toward a thrombotic phenotype [9]. Although rare, thrombotic AEs have been observed with emicizumab [[10], [11], [12], [13]], a humanized bispecific monoclonal antibody targeting FIXa and FX, and with befovacimab [14], concizumab [[15], [16], [17]], and marstacimab [18,19], monoclonal antibodies targeting TFPI.

Previous nonclinical in vitro studies provide support for the combined use of marstacimab and bypass agents rFVIIa or aPCC [20]. The effect of marstacimab in combination with rFVIIa or aPCC has been studied in in vitro spike-in studies of hemophilia A and B donor plasma and nonhemophilic plasma in the thrombin generation assay (TGA), which showed increased peak thrombin levels compared with either agent alone and without excessive coagulation [20].

The current study aimed to explore the potential for excessive thrombin generation and to investigate the cumulative pharmacodynamics and potential additive effects on coagulation and bleeding of the concomitant administration of marstacimab and bypass therapies such as rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX, which could potentially be used to treat breakthrough bleeds in patients with inhibitors receiving marstacimab. The in vitro combination of marstacimab and the plasma-derived bypass agent pdFVIIa/FX in hemophilia A and B inhibitor plasmas was assessed using the TGA. Additionally, 3 in vivo animal studies were conducted to assess the accumulated pharmacodynamic and potential additive effects of repeat-dose administration of marstacimab combined with various standard bypassing agents (rFVIIa, aPCC, and pdFVIIa/FX) in hemostatically normal adult male Wistar Han rats.

Methods

Marstacimab was produced by Pfizer Inc. Bypassing agents were obtained from Novo Nordisk (rFVIIa, Novoseven), Baxalta Inc (aPCC, FEIBA), and KM Biologics Co, Ltd (pdFVIIa/FX, Byclot). Vehicle controls were prepared by Pfizer for each treatment: marstacimab (vehicle 1), rFVIIa (vehicle 2), aPCC (vehicle 3), and pdFVIIa/FX (vehicle 4).

In vitro TGA: marstacimab combined with pdFVIIa/FX

The TGA was performed using severe hemophilia A or B inhibitor plasma or pooled nonhemophilic donor plasmas, and a Fluoroskan Ascent spectrophotometer (Supplementary Methods). pdFVIIa/FX at concentrations of 2/20, 1/10, and 0.5/5 μg/mL was analyzed in combination with marstacimab at 16 μg/mL. pdFVIIa/FX is a plasma-derived, virus-inactivated mixture of human FVIIa/FX proteins (protein weight ratio 1:10). The highest concentration of pdFVIIa/FX (2/20 μg/mL) tested in combination was chosen to match the anticipated plasma concentration of rFVIIa (2 μg/mL) following a dose of 90 and 180 μg/kg of body weight [21,22]. The marstacimab concentration of 16 μg/mL was chosen to approximate the maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) following a 300 mg s.c. dose [5,23]. Analysis also included: (i) pdFVIIa/FX alone in hemophilia inhibitor plasma at high concentrations of 8/80, 4/40, 2/20, 1/10, 0.5/5, and 0.25/2.5 μg/mL, and (ii) controls with hemophilia inhibitor plasmas and nonhemophilic plasma alone or with 16 μg/mL marstacimab.

In vivo marstacimab combination studies

Animals and procedures

Three 10-day combination pharmacology studies with marstacimab and rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX were conducted to investigate the cumulative pharmacodynamics and potential additive effects of the coadministration of these treatments in hemostatically normal male Wistar Han rats (Charles River Laboratories). Marstacimab has been shown to cross-react with rat TFPI (KD 1.57 nM). Rats are a pharmacologically relevant species. These studies were conducted in accordance with current guidelines for animal welfare and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.

General and detailed clinical observations, body weight, macroscopic and microscopic evaluations, and food consumption were recorded. At necropsy, representative samples of collected organs (including the administration site) were fixed in 10% buffered formalin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin for microscopic examination. Blood samples were collected in dipotassium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (K2EDTA) serum separator tubes for marstacimab pharmacodynamic analysis (predosing and at various intervals postdosing) and hematology and serum chemistry analysis (terminal), and in 3.2% sodium citrate for coagulation pharmacodynamic markers (terminal). Citrated plasma was isolated and stored at −70 °C until assay. Marstacimab plasma concentrations, determined using a validated enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and pharmacokinetic sampling time points and analysis are detailed in the Supplementary Methods.

Rats, approximately 8 weeks old at initiation of dosing, were administered marstacimab 50 mg/kg/dose once weekly by intravenous (i.v.) bolus on days 1 and 8 and/or rFVIIa 0.4, 1, or 3 mg/kg once daily or 0.8 mg/kg (given as 0.4 mg/kg twice daily) by i.v. bolus on days 8 to 10 (Figure 1A). Marstacimab dose and frequency (2 doses once weekly) were selected to provide a predicted exposure (Cmax and area under the concentration-time curve [AUC]) in rats to exceed the highest intended clinical exposure of marstacimab at a monthly dose of 2000 mg i.v. rFVIIa doses of 0.4, 1, and 3 mg/kg and frequency (once daily for 3 days starting on day 8) were selected to test a range of exposures. Marstacimab combined with aPCC

Based on results of the aPCC dose-finding study in rats (Supplementary Methods, Supplementary Results), aPCC i.v. doses of 10 to 100 U/kg were evaluated. Rats, 8 weeks old at initiation of dosing, were administered either vehicle, marstacimab, aPCC, or marstacimab in combination with aPCC by either s.c. injection of marstacimab and its corresponding vehicle or i.v. via the lateral tail vein of aPCC and its corresponding vehicle. A third control group received a combination of marstacimab (ie, vehicle 1) and aPCC (ie, vehicle 3) by s.c. and i.v. administration, respectively (Figure 1B). Marstacimab 30 mg/kg/dose s.c. and frequency (2 doses once weekly on days 1 and 8) were selected to provide a predicted exposure (Cmax and AUC) in rats to exceed the highest intended clinical exposure of marstacimab at 300 mg/wk s.c. The aPCC doses of 10 U/kg (5 U/kg twice daily), 50 U/kg (25 U/kg twice daily), and 100 U/kg (50 U/kg twice daily) and frequency (twice daily dosing with 6 hours between doses for 3 consecutive days starting on day 8) were selected to test a range of doses. The highest dose of aPCC was selected based on the dose range-finding study in rats and aligns with clinically approved aPCC treatment of 100 U/kg/d [24].

Marstacimab combined with pdFVIIa/FX

Rats, 14 weeks old at initiation of dosing, received either vehicle, pdFVIIa/FX, marstacimab, or marstacimab in combination with pdFVIIa/FX by either s.c. injection for marstacimab and its corresponding vehicle or i.v. via the lateral tail vein for pdFVIIa/FX and its corresponding vehicle (Figure 1C). A third control group received a combination of marstacimab (ie, vehicle 1) and pdFVIIa/FX (ie, vehicle 4) by s.c. and i.v. administration, respectively. Marstacimab 30 mg/kg/dose s.c. and frequency (2 doses once weekly) were selected to provide a predicted exposure in rats (Cmax and AUC) to exceed the highest predicted clinical exposure of marstacimab at a weekly dose of 300 mg s.c. pdFVIIa/FX dose levels of 60, 120, and 180/120 μg/kg were administered as 60 μg/kg/d, 120 μg/kg/d (60 μg/kg/d twice daily), or 180 μg/kg/d (120 plus 60 μg/kg/d) on day 8, and as 60, 120, or 120 μg/kg/d once on day 10, respectively. The doses were selected to test a range of clinically approved doses and regimens, with the highest dose level equivalent to the maximum pdFVIIa/FX dose following a typical dosing regimen.

Hemophilia with inhibitor plasma

Thrombin generation was markedly reduced in the human hemophilia A or B plasmas with inhibitors (Supplementary Results; Supplementary Tables 1 and 2; Figure 2A, B; Supplementary Figure 1A, B). The addition of marstacimab (16 μg/mL) resulted in an increase in thrombin generation, including higher peak thrombin concentration and shortening of lag time, compared with vehicle-treated hemophilic plasma.

In both hemophilia A and B inhibitor plasma, the combination of pdFVIIa/FX (at 2/20, 1/10, or 0.5/5 μg/mL) with marstacimab (16 μg/mL) dose-dependently increased the thrombin generation parameters of peak thrombin, endogenous thrombin potential, and velocity index (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2; Figure 2A, B). The peak thrombin increase was additive compared with individual agents alone in both hemophilia A and B plasma with inhibitors and ranged from 209.85 to 291.22 nM and 156.22 to 228.61 nM, respectively (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). The combination of pdFVIIa/FX and marstacimab also decreased the lag time in both hemophilia A and B inhibitor plasma without any additivity across the doses of pdFVIIa/FX (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2; Figure 2A, B).

In vivo marstacimab combination studies

Marstacimab combined with rFVIIa

Administration of rFVIIa in rats at any dose alone or in combination with marstacimab resulted in test article-related, non–dose-related, lower group means of prothrombin time (PT; Table 1). The magnitude of the change was not substantially altered by coadministration of marstacimab. Combined, repeated administration of marstacimab at 50 mg/kg and rFVIIa at 3 mg/kg was associated with an increased incidence and/or severity of acute thrombi/emboli at the injection site (tail) and lung compared with the respective vehicle group (Table 2). These findings were not observed at the same incidence/severity with either marstacimab or rFVIIa administration at the same doses alone or in combination when administered as marstacimab 50 mg/kg and rFVIIa at 0.8 mg/kg (0.4 mg/kg twice daily; Table 2). The thrombi/emboli were minimal to mild in severity (Table 2) and not associated with any microscopic evidence of secondary tissue injury or effects on the clinical condition of the main study Marstacimab combined with aPCC

No mortality occurred in this study, and all doses were tolerated. Test article-related red discoloration of the tail was noted on days 9 and/or 10 in individual animals in groups receiving 100 U/kg aPCC or 30 mg/kg marstacimab plus 100 U/kg aPCC.

Marstacimab alone at 30 mg/kg on days 1 and 8 was associated with a test article-related higher (2.06-fold) mean concentration of thrombin-antithrombin (TAT) complexes on day 10, with no test article-related clinical observations or macroscopic or microscopic findings.

aPCC alone on days 8 to 10 was associated with test article-related, dose-dependent, 1.55- to 4.53-fold higher mean TAT concentrations at ≥ 10 U/kg, 1.26- to 1.33-fold higher activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) at ≥ 50 U/kg, and dose-dependent, 0.91- to 0.73-fold lower PT at ≥ 10 U/kg on day 10 compared with controls. In addition, there were 1.71- to 2.23-fold higher mean neutrophil, monocyte, and alanine aminotransferase levels on day 10 with 100 U/kg (50 U/kg twice daily) aPCC compared with controls. Marstacimab combined with pdFVIIa/FX

All doses were tolerated, with no mortality. Test article-related coagulation findings were present in animals administered pdFVIIa/FX and/or marstacimab. Compared with controls, there was a 0.45- to 0.52-fold shortened mean PT and 1.35- to 1.51-fold prolonged mean aPTT in rats administered ≥ 60 μg/kg pdFVIIa/FX with and without the addition of 30 mg/kg marstacimab (Table 1). Combined administration of ≥ 60 μg/kg pdFVIIa/FX and 30 mg/kg marstacimab also resulted in 1.41- to 1.89-fold higher mean TAT complexes compared with controls (Table 1).

Administration of marstacimab 30 mg/kg alone did not result in test article-related differences in coagulation parameters, and combined administration of ≥ 60 μg/kg pdFVIIa/FX and 30 mg/kg marstacimab in rats resulted in similar coagulation parameter differences compared with pdFVIIa/FX alone. Mean systemic exposures (as assessed by Cmax and AUC) in rats administered marstacimab in combination with increasing doses of pdFVIIa/FX were similar compared with those of animals administered marstacimab alone on day 8 (Supplementary Table 3).

Discussion

People with hemophilia with inhibitors who receive treatment with marstacimab may need to receive standard bypass therapies, such as rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX, if breakthrough bleeds arise during treatment. The in vitro addition of marstacimab in combination with pdFVIIa/FX to hemophilia plasmas with an inhibitor increased thrombin generation (including higher peak thrombin concentration) compared with the addition of either agent alone; however, it was within the range reported in studies of nonhemophilic normal plasma [25]. Regarding combined administration of marstacimab with available bypass treatments in male hemostatically normal rats, an increased incidence and/or severity of acute thrombi/emboli at the injection site and lung was observed with marstacimab and rFVIIa coadministration at the highest dose, and higher mean TAT complexes and mean platelet volume were observed with marstacimab and aPCC coadministration at the highest dose. However, effects of coadministration of marstacimab and pdFVIIa/FX were similar to those observed with pdFVIIa/FX alone and limited to effects on coagulation parameters. Marstacimab exposures were not affected by coadministration of rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdVIIa/FX.

As demonstrated in the in vitro study described here, addition of the combination of marstacimab and pdFVIIa/FX to hemophilia A and B platelet-poor plasma containing inhibitors resulted in a 2- to 3-fold increase in thrombin generation, including higher peak thrombin concentration, compared with individual agents alone. This peak thrombin of approximately 220 nM is comparable with the nonhemophilic control plasma; however, it is within the range reported in a study of normal nonhemophilic plasmas (mean ± SD: 287 ± 61 nM) [25] and previous marstacimab studies [20,23]. Both marstacimab and pdFVIIa/FX decreased the TGA lag time in the hemophilia A and B plasma with inhibitors; however, there was no additive decrease in the lag time for the combination of marstacimab and pdFVIIa/FX. In vitro TGAs have some limitations, as they do not have platelets that are important in vivo for hemostasis [26]. In a study of emicizumab and aPCC, excessive thrombin generation (∼17-fold) was observed [27]. The increase in peak thrombin observed with marstacimab and other bypass agents (ie, rFVIIa and aPCC) was not excessive [20]. In the current study of pdFVIIa/FX combined with marstacimab, excessive thrombin generation was not observed in the TGA assay.

Combined repeated dose administration of marstacimab 50 mg/kg i.v. (to exceed the highest intended clinical exposure of marstacimab at a monthly dose of 2000 mg i.v.) and rFVIIa 3 mg/kg to rats was associated microscopically with an increased incidence and/or severity of minimal to mild acute thrombi/emboli in the lung and at the injection site (tail) without any microscopic evidence of secondary tissue injury or effects on the clinical condition of the main study animals. These findings were not observed at the same incidence/severity with either compound at the same doses alone or in combination when administered as marstacimab 50 mg/kg and rFVIIa 0.8 mg/kg (0.4 mg/kg twice daily), indicating a greater effect of combined administration of marstacimab and rFVIIa at 50 and 3 mg/kg, respectively. The increase in thrombi/emboli microscopically is consistent with the pharmacology of both marstacimab and rFVIIa. Cynomolgus monkeys exposed to high steady-state concizumab concentrations and given 3 doses of rFVIIa had no thrombi or other evidence of excessive coagulation activation at necropsy [28].

Administration of rFVIIa at any dose alone or in combination with marstacimab resulted in non–dose-related shortening of PT. The magnitude of the change was not significantly altered by coadministration of marstacimab. Thrombi/emboli formation and shortening of PT are consistent with the pharmacology of both therapies.

In general, thrombi/emboli were most often observed at the i.v. injection site (tail vein) and in the lungs. It is likely that venipuncture of the tail vein incited local tissue injury, promoting the formation of thrombi, which then embolized other vessels in the tail or were filtered out in the vasculature of the lung downstream. There was no evidence of systemic microthrombosis other than in the 1 pharmacokinetic animal administered marstacimab (50 mg/kg)/rFVIIa (3 mg/kg), which was euthanized moribund with bilateral hindlimb paralysis and had microscopic evidence of widespread thrombi/emboli in multiple tissues. This animal also had ileal and cecal erosions of uncertain etiology, which may have contributed to its hypercoagulable state.

The mildly higher glucose levels observed in the rFVIIa-dosed groups may be related to acute stress, but do not appear to be related to the pharmacology of either test article. Systemic exposures characterized by Cmax and AUCt for marstacimab and rFVIIa were not affected by combined test article administration, and exposures of marstacimab were not affected by combined administration with rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX.

Shortening of PT and prolongation of aPTT have been previously seen in rats following administration of aPCC [29]. The combined administration of aPCC and marstacimab in rats resulted in similar clinical observations, clinical pathology differences, and macroscopic and microscopic findings compared with aPCC alone, except for higher means for TAT complexes and mean platelet volume across all aPCC/marstacimab combined doses. Although there are limitations to the in vitro TGA assay [26], the combination of aPCC with emicizumab has been shown to increase peak thrombin level 17-fold over emicizumab alone in vitro, suggesting the possibility of safety concerns associated with excessive thrombin generation with the combination [27].

It is noteworthy that all the in vivo rat studies reported here used hemostatically normal rats that possessed a full complement of coagulation factors. Nonclinical pharmacology studies in rats with normal FVIII and FIX levels provide evidence for the concomitant use of marstacimab and clinical doses of rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX for the treatment of breakthrough bleeding events in people with hemophilia A or B with inhibitors.

The data reported here support a maximal clinical dose of rFVIIa ≤ 90 μg/kg (approximately every 2 hours) with concomitant use of marstacimab at doses up to 300 mg/wk s.c. Marstacimab systemic exposures (mean Cmax, ≈1700 μg/mL) were not affected by combined administration of rFVIIa and exceeded the predicted exposure at the clinical dose of 300 mg/wk s.c. The data also support a maximal clinical dose of 100 U/kg aPCC when used concomitantly with marstacimab at doses up to 300 mg/wk s.c. Marstacimab systemic exposures were not affected by combined administration of aPCC; mean Cmax of ≈200 μg/mL exceeded the predicted exposure at the clinical dose of 300 mg/wk s.c. The data also support pdFVIIa/FX at the maximum recommended clinical dose of 180 μg/kg, followed by 120 μg/kg approximately 6 hours later, with concomitant use of marstacimab at doses up to 300 mg/wk s.c. Marstacimab systemic exposures (mean Cmax, ≈150 μg/mL) were not affected by combined administration of pdFVIIa/FX and exceeded the predicted exposure at the clinical dose of 300 mg/wk s.c.

A phase 1 clinical trial of marstacimab involved 32 healthy volunteers who received single doses ranging from 30 mg s.c. to 440 mg i.v. [23]. All doses were found to be safe and well tolerated. Treatment-emergent AEs were mild or moderate in severity, laboratory abnormalities were transient, there were no serious AEs or infusion/injection site reactions, and no dose escalation/stopping criteria were met. The safety, pharmacokinetic, and pharmacodynamic data from this study supported progression of a multiple-dose study in people with hemophilia [23]. In an open-label study of x healthy male volunteers, conducted to demonstrate bioequivalence following s.c. administration of a 300 mg dose via a prefilled pen device and a prefilled syringe, an event of deep venous thrombosis/pulmonary embolism was reported in 1 participant [18]. In a phase 1b/2 study, 26 people with hemophilia A or B, with or without inhibitors, were given marstacimab in 4 dose cohorts (150, 300, and 450 mg noninhibitor and 300 mg inhibitor) [30]. Three participants developed antidrug antibodies, all without neutralizing effect. Treatment-related increases in D-dimer were observed; however, these were in the absence of clinically significant changes that would pose safety concerns. There were no severe AEs related to marstacimab, no thrombotic AEs, and no clinically significant changes in coagulation laboratory parameters reported. Preliminary efficacy was evaluated by comparing the study participants’ bleed rates in the 6 months before the study and then 6 months on study. The annual bleed rate was 0 for most dose cohorts, and the reduction in the bleed rate was 80% to 96% across all 4 dose cohorts [30]. In the phase 3 BASIS study, 116 adolescents and adults with severe hemophilia A or moderately severe to severe hemophilia B without inhibitors received once-weekly 150 mg marstacimab s.c. for 12 months [8]. Marstacimab was generally well tolerated with no unanticipated side effects. Recently, an event of deep vein thrombosis (non–life-threatening and without hospitalization) was reported in 1 participant who was heterozygous for the FV Leiden mutation in the phase 3 marstacimab open-label extension trial [19].

Nonfactor treatments, such as befovacimab and concizumab, have been associated with rare thromboembolic complications [14,31]. Three thrombotic events were observed with befovacimab in a phase 2 trial [14]. These were not associated with concomitant use of replacement/bypass therapies. Mechanistically, befovacimab binds to both the K1 and K2 domains of human TFPI with a high affinity (< 10 pM) [14]. Four nonfatal thromboembolic events were reported in the concizumab phase 3 trials of hemophilia with and without inhibitors, including 1 patient who had received a bypassing agent [17,31]. Concizumab binds with high affinity to human TFPI K2 domain (25 pM) [32]. Marstacimab binds to the human TFPI K2 domain (3.7 nM) [20]. Although all anti-TFPI products target TFPI, there are differences in their specific epitope binding sites and differences in their binding affinities. While not directly compared in this study, these differences may contribute to their distinct clinical safety profiles.

The in vitro and in vivo findings reported here provide information that is relevant to clinical practice. In addition to the previously cited studies [6,7,20], the findings support the phase 3 clinical development program of marstacimab [8], which is currently under evaluation in patients with inhibitors.

Conclusions

With the recent addition of marstacimab to the treatment landscape for hemophilia, it is important to fully understand the effect of concomitant use of procoagulant agents, particularly in light of the potential risk of thrombotic complications. Previous work has shown that marstacimab alone or in combination with rFVIIa or aPCC can increase hemostasis in hemophilia plasma without generating excessive thrombin [20]. Similar to those in vitro studies [20], the in vitro study described here demonstrated that marstacimab was able to increase hemostasis without excessive thrombin generation in both hemophilia A and B inhibitor plasmas when coadministered with pdFVIIa/FX, which has positive implications for the safety profile of marstacimab. This was supported by the findings of the in vivo studies described here, which demonstrated marstacimab used in combination with rFVIIa, aPCC, or pdFVIIa/FX was tolerated in hemostatically normal rats. The studies provide support for the use of each of these bypass agents (rFVIIa ≤ 90 μg/kg; aPCC 100 U/kg; pdFVIIa/FX 180/120 μg/kg) in combination with marstacimab.

The promising findings seen with marstacimab in phase 1 and 2 clinical studies and its progression to phase 3 studies suggested marstacimab’s potential for the treatment of hemophilia A and B with inhibitors.

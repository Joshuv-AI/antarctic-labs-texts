Research note: In-vitro immunomodulatory and anti-Clostridium perfringens properties of the monoglycerides of short- and medium-chain fatty acids blends alone and in combination with plant bioactives
Authors: Pok Man Chan, Feba Ann John, Jundi Liu, Raveendra R. Kulkarni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Necrotic enteritis (NE), a multifactorial disease caused by Clostridium perfringens, poses a significant challenge to the broiler industry. Antibiotic alternative feed additives, such as eubiotic compounds (ECs) including short- and medium-chain fatty acids (SCFA, MCFA) and plant bioactives (PB), offer gut health benefits and prevent enteric infections. This study evaluated three EC blends: MCFA monoglycerides blend (C8–C10 enriched monoglycerides blend), SCMCFA monoglyceride blend (C1–C10 monoglycerides blend), and SCMCFA-PB blend (C1–C12 monoglycerides blend enriched in C12 with plant bioactives) for their immunomodulatory and anti-C. perfringens activity using an in-vitro chicken macrophage (MQ-NCSU cell line) model. Macrophage responses were evaluated by quantifying pro-inflammatory (IL-1β, IFN-γ) and anti-inflammatory (IL-10, TGF-β) cytokine gene expression at 3, 6, 12, and 24 h post-treatment, along with nitric oxide (NO) production at 24 h. Antimicrobial activity was assessed using C. perfringens growth inhibition assays (disk diffusion and co-culture). The MCFA blend induced an early upregulation of both pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines (6 h) (P < 0.05), followed by a transition toward an immunomodulatory profile. In contrast, SCMCFA and SCMCFA-PB blends elicited a predominantly immunomodulatory response throughout the post-treatment, characterized by reduced IL-1β and IFN-γ and increased IL-10 and TGF-β expression (P < 0.05). Additionally, SCMCFA-PB blend treatment enhanced NO production in macrophages (P < 0.05). Furthermore, MCFA-enriched monoglycerides blend and SCMCFA-PB blend demonstrated increased inhibition of C. perfringens growth (P < 0.05), whereas SCMCFA monoglycerides blend showed limited antibacterial activity in-vitro. These findings indicate that MCFA-enriched monoglycerides blend alone, or in combination with SCFA monoglycerides blend and PB, can synergistically modulate inflammatory responses and improve antimicrobial efficacy, highlighting the potential for targeted, non-antibiotic feed strategies to reduce NE incidence in poultry.

Introduction

Necrotic enteritis (NE) is an economically important disease in poultry caused by virulent strains of Clostridium perfringens, a Gram-positive, anaerobic, spore-forming, toxin-producing bacterium. Incidence of NE has been increasing in recent years, especially in broiler flocks raised under “no antibiotics ever” or organic production systems. The clinical form of NE causes necrosis of the small intestines and is associated with significant mortality, whereas the subclinical form leads to reduced feed efficiency and poor bird performance. Both clinical and subclinical forms contribute to significant economic losses; global losses associated with NE have been estimated to be more than $6 billion a year, and these losses are continuing to rise (Wade and Keyburn, 2015).

Traditionally, the use of sub-therapeutic levels of antibiotics was considered an effective and safe control method for NE in commercial poultry production. However, growing concerns over antimicrobial resistance and consumer demand for antibiotic-free poultry have driven the search for alternatives. A particular interest has been given to eubiotic compounds (ECs), such as organic acids and their derivatives monoglycerides, and plant derived bioactive molecules, due to their potential in enhancing bird performance, promoting gut health, and modulating immune responses without the use of antibiotics. Research has shown that the use of a blend of essential oil compounds with butyric acid can significantly improve body weight gain and reduce feed conversion ratio in developing broiler chickens (Tzora et al., 2021). Supplementation of diets with ECs was shown to enhance intestinal epithelial integrity while reducing the bacterial burden in broilers challenged with C. perfringens (Pham et al., 2020). Despite extensive research into alternative feed additive strategies, there remains a clear lack of EC-based approaches that demonstrate immunomodulatory action and consistent efficacy against C. perfringens in-vitro and effective prevention of NE in-vivo. This gap is partly due to the absence of a systematic, stepwise in-vitro characterization of ECs possessing both pathogen-specific antimicrobial activity and host-beneficial immunomodulatory effects.

We have previously reported an avian macrophage cell-based in-vitro model used to screen ten ECs, including monoglycerides of short-chain fatty acids (SCFA) and plant-based (PB) blends for their immunomodulatory and antimicrobial properties (John et al., 2024). Treatments with single monoglyceride and PB ingredients, namely monobutyrin, green tea extract, and rosemary extract, were found to both downregulate the transcription of inflammatory genes and inhibit the growth of C. perfringens post-infection. Here, we evaluated the immunological and antimicrobial effects of three novel EC blends by characterizing temporal expression of cytokine genes, macrophage activation via nitric oxide (NO) production, and C. perfringens growth inhibition properties.

Materials and methods

Eubiotic compounds

Three novel EC blends (provided by Eastman, Kingsport, TN), classified as 1) a monoglycerides blend of medium-chain fatty acids (MCFA) enriched in C8-C10 (referred to hereafter as ‘MCFA monoglycerides blend’), 2) a monoglycerides blend of short-/medium-chain fatty acids from C1-C10 (referred to hereafter as ‘SCMCFA monoglycerides blend’), and 3) a monoglycerides blend of short/ medium-chain fatty acids with PB enriched in C12 (referred to hereafter as ‘SCMCFA-PB blend’) were used in this study. Stock solutions of all three EC blends were prepared in 100% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO; Corning, NY, USA) at 1 mg/mL and stored at 4°C before experimental use.

Macrophage stimulation

The chicken macrophage cell line MQ-NCSU, kindly provided by Dr. Matthew Koci, North Carolina State University, was propagated and prepared according to the culture conditions outlined previously (Kulkarni et al., 2022). To identify the optimal in-vitro concentration for each EC, the cells were stimulated in six replicate wells with three concentrations (0.1, 1, and 10 µg/mL) for a period of 24 h along with the solvent-only control. The cell viability (% Live cell count) was determined by flow cytometry (LSR-II flow-cytometer, BD Bioscience, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) by staining cells with the cell viability dye, Live/Dead fixable near-IR (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). The concentration of EC that provided the highest % live cell count was considered optimal and was selected for use in the macrophage stimulation experiment. The selected concentrations were 1 µg/mL for SCMCFA monoglycerides blend, 0.1 µg/mL for MCFA monoglycerides blend, and 10 µg/mL for the SCMCFA-PB blend that had an average live cell count of 73.2%, 70.05% and 76.75%, respectively.

For stimulation, MQ-NCSU cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of approximately 5 × 10⁵ to 1 × 10⁶ cells per well and incubated overnight to allow attachment and formation of a confluent monolayer. The following day, the medium was replaced with Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (Gibco, Waltham, MA) containing 2.5% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Waltham, MA). Cells were then treated with the selected concentrations of EC. Each treatment was performed in six replicates and incubated for 24 hours at 40°C with 5% CO₂. A control group received the same medium with DMSO solvent-only and was maintained under identical conditions.

Immune gene expression

The RNA extraction, complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis, and the gene expression analysis by real-time qPCR were performed, as described previously (Kulkarni et al., 2022; John et al., 2024). Briefly, MQ-NCSU cells were collected at 3, 6, 12, and 24 hours post-treatment, pelleted, resuspended in TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA), and stored at −80°C until further processing for quantification of IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-10, and TGF-β gene expression. Total RNA was extracted following the manufacturer’s instructions, and 1 μg of RNA was converted into cDNA using a High-Capacity RNA-to-cDNA reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA). The cDNA was subsequently used as a template for real-time qPCR analysis using SYBR Green on the QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA)(Kulkarni et al., 2022; John et al., 2024). Amplification cycling conditions included an initial incubation (50°C/ 2 min), denaturation (95°C/ 2 min), followed by 40–50 amplification cycles consisting of denaturation (95°C/ 10 sec), primer-specific annealing (55–64°C/ 5 sec), and extension (72°C/ 10 sec). Melt curve analysis was conducted by heating samples (95°C/ 15 sec), cooling (60°C/ 1 min), and reheating (95°C/ 15 sec). β-actin was amplified using the same PCR conditions and served as the housekeeping reference gene for normalizing target gene expression, with results expressed as fold changes.

Nitric oxide estimation

Production of NO in the cell supernatants at 24 h post-treatment was determined by Griess assay following the manufacturer’s instructions (Promega, Madison, WI) (Kulkarni et al., 2022; John et al., 2024). Briefly, 50 μL of sulfanilamide reagent was combined with 50 μL of each supernatant sample in a 96-well plate and incubated at room temperature for 15 minutes. Subsequently, 50 μL of N-(1-naphthyl) ethylenediamine dihydrochloride solution was added to each well and the absorbance was measured at 530 nm after 15 minutes. The NO levels were calculated from a serially diluted nitrite standard of known concentration, and the values were expressed as micromolar (µM).

Disk diffusion

The antimicrobial activity of ECs was assessed using an agar disk diffusion assay on Brain Heart Infusion (BHI; BD, Sparks, MD) agar plates (John et al., 2024). Clostridium perfringens strain CP18, a clinical NE isolate carrying netB and tpeL toxin genes shown to possess virulence ability (John et al., 2024) was grown overnight in Reinforced Clostridial Medium (RCM; Oxoid Ltd., Basingstoke, UK) under anaerobic conditions and evenly spread onto the BHI agar. Subsequently, 30 µL of each EC preparation was applied to sterile blank paper disks placed on the inoculated plates in triplicate. The plates were then incubated anaerobically at 37°C for 24 h, after which zones of inhibition, indicated by the absence of microbial growth surrounding the paper disks, were measured and reported as diameters (mm).

Co-culture assay

An overnight culture of C. perfringens strain CP18 was inoculated at 1% (v/v) into fresh RCM media and treated with ECs at the same concentrations that were used for the macrophage stimulation assay. Following 16 hours of anaerobic incubation, bacterial growth was quantified using a standard serial dilution and plating method, as described previously (Kulkarni et al., 2022). Culture aliquots were serially diluted in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵, and 10⁻⁶. From each dilution, 100 µL of culture dilutions were spread onto TSA (Tryptic Soy Agar) blood agar plates (Thermo, Lenexa, KS) and anaerobically incubated overnight. Colonies displaying the double hemolytic zones typical of C. perfringens were enumerated and calculated as colony-forming units (CFU) per mL.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 11; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Data were initially assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test, after which t-test and either parametric or nonparametric one-way ANOVA was applied as appropriate. Results are expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean, and statistical significance was established at P < 0.05.

Results and discussion

The SCFA-based additives, especially those derived from butyric acid, serve as growth promoters and antibiotic alternatives in poultry by modulating intestinal physiology and immune responses, thereby enhancing gut health and immunity against infections. Previously, we showed that monobutyrin and calcium butyrate can effectively modulate macrophage-driven inflammatory response and exert C. perfringens growth inhibition in-vitro (5). Recent research indicates that modifying fatty acid chain length or combining SCFAs with PB can further fine-tune macrophage responses toward a balanced immunomodulatory phenotype, thereby optimizing pathogen control while avoiding excessive inflammatory damage (McKnight et al., 2019.; Akdemir Evrendilek, 2026). Here, we evaluated the anti-inflammatory/ immunomodulatory as well as the antibacterial properties of three novel fatty-acid-based EC blends containing SCFAs, MCFAs, and PB to provide a framework to understand the potential application of monoglycerides blends of the SCFAs and MCFAs as poultry feed additives for supporting gut health.

Macrophages treated with the MCFA monoglycerides blend showed an upregulation of IL-1β transcription at 6 h, followed by a downregulation at 12 h post-treatment (Fig. 1A, P < 0.05). Compared to control, MCFA monoglycerides blend treatment resulted in increased expression of IFNγ gene at 6 h, followed by a reduction at 24 h along with a reduced IL-10 transcription at 12 h (P < 0.05). The early induction of pro-inflammatory cytokines likely enhances pathogen recognition, while the concurrent increase in anti-inflammatory genes seems to balance the response to prevent chronic damage. Notably, while the transcription of most cytokines was found to decline by 24 h, the TGFβ gene expression was upregulated at both 6 h and 24 h (P < 0.05). This persistent TGFβ expression may suggest a homeostatic response during the treatment period. In addition to modulating the expression of cytokine genes, the MCFA monoglycerides blend demonstrated a marked inhibition of C. perfringens growth (Fig. 2B and C, P < 0.05). These findings are consistent with the antimicrobial properties of other MCFAs, particularly caprylic (C8) and capric (C10) acids, against enteric pathogens (Kadry et al., 2023). In agreement with previous reports, our results further support the dual properties of MCFAs in modulating macrophage-mediated inflammatory response and inhibiting bacterial growth. In contrast to the MCFA monoglycerides blend, the SCMCFA monoglycerides blend elicited predominantly a regulatory cytokine response. The expression of pro-inflammatory cytokine genes, IL-1β and IFN-γ, was transiently elevated at 6 h, while IL-1β transcription was downregulated at 3 and 12 (Fig. 1B, P < 0.05). Meanwhile, regulatory cytokine genes IL-10 and TGFβ exhibited delayed expression, with IL-10 increased at 6 h and TGFβ upregulated at 24 h (P < 0.05). This expression pattern suggests a shift toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, which is beneficial in regulating pathogen-driven inflammation. The inclusion of SCFA monoglycerides within the blend may have contributed to this effect, as they have been shown to modulate macrophage activity through metabolic and epigenetic pathways that favor regulatory cytokine production (John et al., 2024). However, the SCMCFA monoglycerides blend only showed limited anti-C. perfringens property in the current in-vitro model, which may be due to inadequate amounts of SCFA included in the blend or its inability to provide antibacterial action (Fig. 2B and C). Previous reports have also shown that SCFAs exhibit limited antibacterial effects under neutral pH and low-dose conditions, with stronger inhibition occurring at higher concentrations or in acidic environments (Kadry et al., 2023). Together, under the conditions tested here, these findings suggest that the SCMCFA monoglycerides blend may function primarily as an immunomodulator rather than an antimicrobial additive, whereas the MCFA monoglycerides blend enriched in C8–C10 appeared to exhibit both properties.

The incorporation of plant-based bioactive components together with C12, in the present study, appeared to improve the functional profile of the SCMCFA-PB blend by enhancing both immunomodulatory and antimicrobial responses. The expression of the IFN-γ gene was found to be downregulated at 3 h compared to the DMSO control (Fig. 1C, P < 0.05). The transcription of IL-10 was increased at 6 h and 24 h, while TGFβ gene expression was upregulated at all time points (3, 6, 12, and 24 h; (P < 0.05), suggesting a steady and consistent immunoregulatory environment. Additionally, the SCMCFA-PB blend induced a marked increase in NO production (Fig. 2A, P < 0.0001), indicating enhanced macrophage activation, and a significant C. perfringens growth inhibition in both disk diffusion and co-culture assays (Fig. 2B, P < 0.0001; Fig. 2C, P < 0.001). Considering neither the MCFA nor SCMCFA monoglycerides blends could significantly alter NO production by macrophages, the inclusion of plant-derived bioactive components in the SCMCFA-PB blend may have led to enhanced antibacterial and cellular effector function. Although macrophage-derived NO is typically associated with a pro-inflammatory phenotype, the concurrent increase in NO production and immunoregulatory cytokine (IL-10 and TGF-β) transcription observed in the SCMCFA-PB group supports a non-binary, hybrid phenotype of macrophage activation (Mosser and Edwards, 2008; Peng et al., 2018). In this context, SCMCFA-PB may stimulate NO production via NF-κB-independent pathways, while NO may exert negative feedback on pro-inflammatory cytokine induction (Lu et al., 2015), and temporal differences between cumulative NO output and transient transcriptional cytokine responses may further contribute to this apparent dissociation. It is noteworthy that phytogenic compounds, such as thymol and carvacrol, are known to modulate macrophage-mediated inflammatory response while offering strong antibacterial action (Nazneen et al., 2023; Akdemir Evrendilek, 2026). These effects likely act synergistically with fatty acids, as SCFAs alone often exhibit limited antimicrobial effects, whereas their combination with PB may enhance both pathogen control and immune regulation.

Collectively, these findings suggest that monoglycerides of varying fatty acid composition and blends may influence macrophage responses toward an immunomodulatory phenotype while also providing antibacterial action against virulent C. perfringens under the conditions tested. The MCFA blend (enriched in C8-10) appeared to exhibit a dual-action profile, whereas the SCMCFA blend primarily appeared to provide immunomodulatory effects without appreciable pathogen inhibition. Notably, the incorporation of plant-derived bioactives appeared to enhance the functional capacity of the SCMCFA-PB blend (enriched in C12), potentially promoting a more coordinated response that combined regulation of inflammatory response with enhanced anti-C. perfringens effect. While the findings of antibacterial effect were limited to a virulent netB+tpeL+ C. perfringens strain in the present study, future work is needed to test the antimicrobial properties of these EC blends against other strains that carry in their virulence characteristics. Nonetheless, findings from the present study highlight the potential for strategically formulated combinations of monoglycerides of short-/medium-chain fatty acids and plant bioactives to serve as possibly targeted, non-antibiotic approaches for improving gut health and C. perfringens control in poultry.

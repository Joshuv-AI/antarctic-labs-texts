Multilevel learning aided coevolutionary particle swarm optimization algorithm for multiobjective fuzzy flexible job shop scheduling problem
Authors: Juan Chen, Hong Zhao, Zhiya Cui, Tao Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Multi-objective fuzzy Flexible Job shop Scheduling Problems (MofFJSPs) aim to optimize multiple fuzzy and conflicting objectives by finding the sequences of jobs and machines under realistic production constraints. However, as the objective functions of MofFJSPs exhibit fuzzy and conflicting properties, algorithms often face the challenge of being trapped in local optima and slow convergence. Therefore, to solve MofFJSPs effectively and efficiently, a Multi-Level Learning-aided Co-evolutionary Particle Swarm Optimization (MLL-CPSO) algorithm is proposed. The MLL-CPSO uses multiple populations to cooperatively solve multiple fuzzy objectives and hierarchically learns the evolutionary information, together with three novel designs. First, a Multi-Level Learning (MLL) strategy is proposed to learn the short-term personal evolutionary information, the long-term social information, and the co-evolutionary information to avoid local optima and approach the Pareto optima fast. Second, a Simulated Annealing-based Strengthening Diversity (SASD) strategy is designed to improve the diversity of populations and enhance the global search ability. Thirdly, a Co-evolutionary Information Update (CeIU) mechanism is developed to improve the quality of co-evolutionary information from different populations and drive populations to explore more Pareto optimal solutions. In experiments, MLL-CPSO is compared with 7 state-of-the-art algorithms on 3 typical benchmarks with 23 instances. The experimental results show that MLL-CPSO outperforms the compared algorithms in most test environments.

Introduction

The Job shop Scheduling Problem (JSP) refers to a situation in which a set of jobs needs to be processed on a specific set of machines within a factory or production workshop. Each job consists of a series of operations, and each operation needs to be completed on a specific machine. The objective of the JSP is to find a reasonable job sequence so that all jobs can be completed as quickly as possible under specific constraints1. This problem is a classic combinatorial optimization problem, typically classified as NP-hard. The JSP is of great significance in the manufacturing and production fields, as a well-designed scheduling solution can improve equipment utilization, reduce production costs, and effectively improve production efficiency2.

As a general extension of JSP, the Flexible Job shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) assumes that each operation can select an execution machine from a set of optional machines, which is more complex and more general3. Compared to JSP, FJSP proposes an additional scheduling problem that assigns each job to an available machine4. In reality, FJSP plays an important part in industrial processes and has widespread application scenarios. Due to numerous uncertain aspects such as human factors or machine failures, jobs with precise processing time are often far from reality5. With the emergence of the fuzzy set6, the fuzzy FJSP (fFJSP) is proposed by allowing processing time to be fuzzy values. In recent years, many well-known heuristic methods have been applied to solve complex optimization problems and achieved excellent results, such as Genetic Algorithms (GAs)7, memetic algorithms8, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)9 and artificial bee colony algorithm10. Inspired by this, many scholars have also attempted to use these heuristic methods to solve the fFJSP, such as the GAs11,12, the artificial bee colony algorithm13,14, and the estimation of distribution algorithm15, which are all efficient for solving fFJSP.

Furthermore, with the development of the intelligent workshop and automation industry, it is difficult to satisfy the requirements of the market with a single optimization objective16. And it is also limited to evaluate a feasible solution from only one aspect17. Thus, some researchers consider the Multi-objective fFJSP (MofFJSP). Recently, MofFJSP has been extensively studied and many Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) are proposed to address MofFJSP. Some early works, such as the MOEA with a dominance-based tabu search18, the MOEA with a Pareto dominant relation19, and the improved hybrid PSO20, mainly focus on employing the multi-objective evolutionary information. These algorithms treat multiple objectives as a whole and apply Pareto dominance to evaluate solutions. However, one problem of these algorithms is that domination cannot give a complete ordering among the solutions in the objective space21. Hence, some later works are devoted to decomposing a Multi-Objective Optimization Problem (MOOP) into a number of scalar optimization subproblems, in which all the solutions can be compared according to their objective function values. Inspired by a classic decomposition algorithm called the MOEA based on decomposition (MOEA/D)21, the hybrid self-adaptive algorithm based on decomposition22, and the MOEA/D based on reinforcement learning23 are proposed to handle MofFJSP.

Although many MOEAs have been applied to solve MofFJSP, there are still some issues that have not been taken into account by previous studies. (1) Since the shape of Pareto front in MofFJSP is often irregular and complex, existing algorithms that uniformly generate reference points may struggle to obtain well-distributed solutions and easily fall into some local Pareto optima24,25. (2) Most existing MOEAs treat multiple objectives as an integrated whole when utilizing multi-objective evolutionary information. However, different objectives are often incompatible, making it difficult to determine the superiority of one solution over another. This not only causes fitness assignment problems but also slows down the algorithm’s convergence26–28.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a Multi-Level Learning-aided Co-evolutionary Particle Swarm Optimization (MLL-CPSO) algorithm to effectively address the limitations of existing approaches. Specifically, MLL-CPSO adopts a co-evolutionary mechanism to optimize multiple objectives in multiple populations, which can avoid fitness assignment problems. Furthermore, MLL-CPSO hierarchically learns the evolutionary information from multiple levels guiding particles toward the Pareto front and helping to prevent the population from becoming trapped in local optima. The main contributions of the MLL-CPSO algorithm are as follows.

(1) As a co-evolutionary multi-swarm approach for multi-objective optimization, MLL-CPSO assigns each swarm to optimize a single objective. It uniquely constructs an external archive to store the co-evolution information of all swarms, integrating both single swarm evolution information and co-evolution information to efficiently guide particles towards approximating the entire Pareto front. This integration helps avoid fitness assignment issues and enhances optimization efficiency.

(2) Three innovative evolution strategies are designed to improve MLL-CPSO for solving MofFJSP. First, it employs a Multi-Level information Learning (MLL) strategy, incorporating short-term personal, long-term social, and co-evolutionary information to rapidly approach Pareto optima and avoid local optima. Second, a Simulated Annealing-based Strengthening Diversity (SASD) strategy is introduced to enhance population diversity and help escape local optima. Third, a Co-evolutionary Information Update (CeIU) mechanism is developed to improve the quality of co-evolutionary information across different populations.

(3) MLL-CPSO is compared with 7 state-of-the-art algorithms on three widely used test sets with 23 instances5,12,14,29. The results show that MLL-CPSO outperforms the other algorithms on more than half of the instances, demonstrating its effectiveness and validating the motivation for its development in addressing MofFJSP.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. “Background” introduces the related works. “MofFJSP” introduces the modeling of MofFJSP. Then, our proposed MLL-CPSO algorithm is described in detail in “MLL-CPSO”. In “Experimental analysis”, the experimental results are presented and analyzed. Finally, the comprehensive conclusions are given in “Conclusion”.

MofFJSP

This section reviews previous work and introduces the definition of MofFJSP, including the problem constraints and the problem model. Moreover, we give a simple example to illustrate MofFJSP.

Related works

In the research area, most studies assume that the processing time of jobs is a determined value. However, huge uncertainty remains about the actual industrial production5. With the emergence of the fuzzy set6, researchers employed fuzzy numbers to present the fuzzy processing time, which not only enhances robustness but also makes problems closer to reality29.

1) A single objective for solving FJSP

To model more realistic manufacturing systems under uncertainty, researchers presented FJSP and nature-inspired algorithms were proposed to solve FJSP. Lei et al.11 developed an efficient decomposition-integration GA for FJSP to minimize fuzzy makespan, which designs a two-string representation decoding method to represent population. Moreover, Lei et al.12 proposed a co-evolutionary GA, in which a new crossover operator and an improved tournament selection are both proposed. By comparing with the latest methods, the advantages and the effectiveness of the two methods proposed by Lei are demonstrated. Wang et al.13 presented a hybrid artificial bee colony algorithm that adopts a tree search method to enhance the exploitation capability. Numerical testing results and the comparisons with some existing algorithms demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. Wang et al.15developed an improved estimation of the distribution algorithm with a probability model to describe the probability distribution of the solution space. By sampling the probability model, new individuals can be generated among the search region with promising solutions. Inspired by the process of the teaching and learning abilities of the teachers and students, Xu et al.28 proposed an effective teaching–learning-based optimization algorithm with a special encoding and decoding scheme for solving single-objective fFJSP. Palacios et al.32 proposed an effective GA hybridized with tabu search to minimize the makespan. The resulting hybrid algorithm is then evaluated on existing benchmark instances, comparing favorably with the state-of-the-art methods. As referenced above, most of the existing studies only consider fFJSP with a single objective, such as the makespan, the total weighted tardiness, or the total workload. However, evaluating feasible solutions from only one perspective is limited17. Therefore, some researchers proposed MofFJSP.

2) Multi-objective for solving FJSP

On the basis of the agreement index of fuzzy duedate and fuzzy completion time, multiobjective FJSP are formulated. Sakawa et al.29 first applied a genetic algorithm suitable for solving the formulated problems to address MofFJSP, which incorporates the concept of similarity among individuals into the genetic algorithms using the Gannt chart. Palacios et al.18 proposed an MOEA integrating a novel dominance-based tabu search method to optimize multiple objectives of the fuzzy schedule. With a possibility-based Pareto dominant relation and an improved crowding distance measure, an improved non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) was proposed by Wang et al.19. With the continuous upgrading of the production mode of the manufacturing system, the characteristics of multi-variety, small batch and mixed fluidization are presented, and the production environment becomes more and more complex. To improve the efficiency of solving MoFJSP, Zhang et al.20 proposed an improved hybrid particle swarm optimization algorithm combining the PSO algorithm with GA and the Simulated Annealing (SA) algorithm. To construct an effective Memetic Algorithm (MA) for simultaneously optimizing fuzzy makespan, average agreement index and minimal agreement index, Wang et al.33 presented a memetic algorithm that integrates genetic global optimization with local search techniques to optimize above three objectives (i.e., fuzzy makespan, average agreement index, and minimal agreement index). Shukor et al.34 proposed an algorithm for MofFJSP, characterized by its focus on overcoming initialization challenges through a detailed analysis of existing methods, particularly emphasizing the development of heuristic-based approaches to generate high-quality initial populations that address existing limitations and offer promising directions for future research. This paper identifies probable gaps and reveals several performance limitations in the existing methods, which demands for an urgent solution to develop alternatives. Soto et al.35 presented a novel parallel branch and bound algorithm to efficiently solve MofFJSP. The algorithm is implemented for shared-memory architectures, and among its main features, it incorporates a grid representation of the solution space, and a concurrent priority queue to store and dispatch the pending sub-problems to be solved. Furthermore, Zhong et al.36 developed a modified artificial bee colony algorithm for MofFJSP, featuring a three-point satisfaction-degree model and enhanced search performance through a local search operator based on variable neighborhood search and crossover techniques. These algorithms all treat an MOOP as a whole and do not associate each individual with any particular scalar optimization problem.

3) Decomposing an MOOP into several scalar optimization subproblems for FJSP

Furthermore, some researchers considered decomposing an MOOP into several scalar optimization subproblems, and some decomposition-based evolutionary algorithms were proposed to solve MofFJSP. Li et al.22 proposed a hybrid self-adaptive algorithm based on decomposition (HPEA) to handle MofFJSP. In HPEA, weight vectors are adopted to make solutions uniformly distributed and the Tchebycheff decomposition strategy is adopted to balance convergence and diversity. Due to most methods execute local search strategies by poll pattern, which is inefficient and blind. Li et al.23 combined a MOEA/D with reinforcement learning to solve MofFJSP, and a neighborhood search based on reinforcement learning is designed to guide each solution adaptively to select the best local search strategy. Besides, in contrast to the conventional polling local search approach, Li et al.37 employs a technique of dynamically choosing local search strategies. It presents an adaptive multi-objective evolutionary algorithm with a memory mechanism to address MofFJSP, distinguished by its memory mechanism to retain high-quality solutions. Moreover, Guo et al.38 introduced a multi-level guided evolutionary optimization method for addressing MofFJSP. This method combines decomposition and dominance techniques to effectively balance diversity and convergence, while integrating the Jaya algorithm’s strategy of approaching optimal individuals and distancing from less optimal ones through improved Jaya operator operations to guide individual evolution.

Though the above works have been applied to solve MofFJSP, most existing MOEAs for MofFJSP still face issues of falling into local optima and slow convergence due to the fuzzy and conflicting objective function of MofFJSP, thus limiting their performance in complex scenarios. Therefore, we propose a co-evolutionary multiswarm approach designed for multi-objective optimization, where each swarm corresponds to a single objective. It uniquely constructs an external archive to store the co-evolution information of all swarms, integrating both single swarm evolution information and co-evolution information to efficiently guide particles towards approximating the entire Pareto front. This integration helps avoid fitness assignment issues and enhances optimization efficiency. The motivation behind MLL-CPSO lies in its three innovative evolution strategies for solving MofFJSP. First, it employs an MLL strategy, incorporating short-term personal, long-term social, and co-evolutionary information to rapidly approach Pareto optima and avoid local optima. Second, a SASD strategy is introduced to enhance population diversity and help escape local optima. Third, a CeIU mechanism is developed to improve the quality of co-evolutionary information across different populations.

(2) Each operation can only be processed by one machine.

In FJSP, each operation can only be executed on the specified machine and each operation has its own set of available machines. In this example, we assume that each operation can be processed on all machines. Fig. 2a shows an operation-to-machine allocation scheme, which needs to select an execution machine from a set of available machines. A feasible schedule is shown in Fig. 2b.

Simulated annealing-based strengthening diversity strategy

When solving MOOPs, it is easy for the PSO algorithm to fall into local optima. To tackle this issue, we design the SASD strategy to enhance the global search ability. First, SASD applies the evolutionary operation of GA to generate diverse solutions. Second, SASD adopts SA to update the swarm’s globally best position (gBest) by replacing it with an inferior solution spBest, which can not only expand the search domain but also help populations escape from local optima. CeIU.

In all, the SASD strategy applies the evolutionary operation of GA to generate diverse solutions. Then, by employing the information of current particle fitness, SASD adopts SA to update gBest with the probability of acceptance, which helps populations jump out of local optima. By integrating the two strategies, SASD can reduce the probability of the population falling into local optima. It is worth noticing that SA will not disrupt the PSO evolution, because the worse a solution is, the less likely it is to be accepted. Moreover, as the algorithm runs, the probability of accepting worse solutions also decreases.

Co-evolutionary information update strategy

In MLL-CPSO, an external shared archive A is constructed to store the co-evolutionary information found by all populations. With the information in the archive, each population can not only use the single-objective evolutionary information from its own but also the co-evolution information of multiple objectives provided by all populations, which helps populations approximate the whole PF fast. Therefore, the quality and diversity of solutions in the archive are critical to improving the performance of MLL-CPSO. The CeIU strategy is designed to update the archive and increase the quality of co-evolutionary information in the archive. The size of the archive in the current generation is set as na and the maximal size of the archive is set as NA. The pseudocode of the CeIU strategy is shown in Algorithm 2.

NSS

NSS is developed to enhance the quality of co-evolutionary information in the archive, which has the following two steps. The first step is to find the last completed operation in a solution and replace the machine of the operation with an available machine that has minimum processing time. The second step is to find the maximum workload machine and randomly choose an operation that is processed on this machine, and then select an available replacement machine for the operation. Combining the above two steps can effectively enhance the quality of solutions in the external archive.

Experimental analysis

In this section, we compare MLL-CPSO with 7 prevalent competitive methods on 3 benchmarks with 23 instances. All the experiments are implemented in Matlab language and on an Intel Core i7-8300 2.30 GHz processor with 16 GB of RAM.

Experimental settings

Experimental benchmarks

In this study, three benchmarks are used to evaluate the performance of MLL-CPSO, and the details are shown in Table 3.

The first benchmark is obtained from Lei’s study12. In particular, 5 test instances (i.e., data1-data5) are selected, where the number of jobs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n = 10, 10, 10, 10, 15$$\end{document}, the total number of operations \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum _{i=1}^{n} n_i = 40, 40, 50, 50, 80$$\end{document}, and the number of machines \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m = 10$$\end{document} for all instances. In addition, the first benchmark is a complete fFJSP, which means all machines are available for each operation.

The second benchmark Remanu is generated by14. In Remanu, there are 8 instances (i.e., remanu01-remanu08), where the number of jobs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n = 5, 8, 10, 10, 15, 15, 20, 20$$\end{document}, the total number of operations \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum _{i=1}^{n} n_i = 23, 64, 81, 100, 171, 185, 308, 355$$\end{document}, and the number of machines \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m = 4, 8, 6, 10, 8, 10, 10, 15$$\end{document}. For the second benchmark, an operation can be processed on a set of available machines, which is an incomplete fFJSP.

The third benchmark is generated by fuzzing an FJSP benchmark \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$MK$$\end{document}29. Based on the determined processing time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _1$$\end{document}, we generate two integers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _0$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _2$$\end{document} in the interval \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[0, \beta _1/2]$$\end{document}. HV metric is commonly used to measure the diversity and convergence of the obtained solutions by calculating the volume of the hypercube bounded by a reference point and the obtained approximate PF. The comprehensive analysis of experimental results

Algorithms such as NSGA-III and MOEAD-M2M are known for their effective performance on MOOP benchmarks. These benchmarks typically feature PF that are uniformly distributed in a simplex, which makes the use of uniformly generated reference points advantageous for these algorithms. However, MofFJSPs often exhibit PF that deviate significantly from the standard simplex configuration. This discrepancy leads to less effective performance of NSGA-III and MOEAD-M2M, as evidenced by their lower scores in IGD and HV metrics when applied to MofFJSPs. The irregular and complex nature of these PF makes it challenging for algorithms reliant on reference points to maintain high-quality solutions across diverse objectives. In addition, the Pi-MOEA algorithm constructs adaptive neighborhoods based on the distribution of individuals. However, the complex and sparse nature of PF in MofFJSP may cause this neighborhood selection mechanism to fail, thereby leading to degraded performance of Pi-MOEA in solving such problems. On the other hand, NSGA-II employs an elite strategy where both parent and child individuals are combined in a population, followed by non-dominated sorting to select the next generation’s individuals. While this method can expand the search space, it reduces selection pressure on individuals, potentially compromising solution quality. This lack of pressure can lead to suboptimal exploration and exploitation, especially in the complex landscape of MofFJSP, where maintaining diversity and pressure is crucial for achieving optimal solutions.

Moreover, performance metrics in MofFJSP can be affected by the varying scales and ranges of objectives. This variability can lead to inaccurate evaluations of convergence, diversity, and density metrics, negatively impacting algorithms like IAIS and CAGA. The inherent complexity and flexibility of MofFJSP require strategies that effectively maintain diversity during the evolutionary process. CMPSO, as a basic multi-population algorithm, may struggle with these demands, resulting in poor performance due to its limited ability to adapt to the complexity and flexibility required by MofFJSP.

In contrast, MLL-CPSO offers several advantages that make it particularly effective for solving MofFJSP. First, MLL-CPSO is a reference-point-free algorithm, which adopts an improved PSO mechanism to evolve each population separately and employs an archive to store nondominated solutions. By leveraging both single-objective and multi-objective evolutionary information, MLL-CPSO directs all populations towards approximating the entire PF without the constraints imposed by reference points. This flexibility allows MLL-CPSO to adapt to the irregular shapes of PF in MofFJSPs.

Second, the multi-population design ensures that the evolution of different objectives is independent, preventing interference due to varying scales and ranges. Each population is dedicated to one objective, allowing for focused and efficient evolution without cross-objective conflicts. This independence is critical in scenarios where objective scales differ significantly, ensuring that each objective is given adequate attention and resources during the evolutionary process.

Third, based on the multi-level information learning, MLL-CPSO proposed three well-designed strategies to deal with complex MofFJSP. These strategies can enhance the diversity of populations and improve the co-evolution ability of multiple populations. Component analysis

To investigate the effectiveness of the proposed MLL, SASD, and CeIU strategies, we design three variant algorithms. The first algorithm adopts \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varvec{pBest}$$\end{document} instead of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varvec{spBest}$$\end{document} to update the velocity and position of particles (called MLL-CPSO−MLL). The second algorithm is designed without the SASD strategy (called MLL-CPSO−SASD). The third algorithm is designed without the archive perturbation strategy (called MLL-CPSO−CeIU). The archive is only constructed to store the nondominated solutions provided by all populations without ELS and NSS strategies.

The average HV results and IGD results of MLL-CPSO and its variant algorithms in 30 runs are listed in Table 11. In addition, the number of best values obtained by different algorithms on average HV and average IGD are listed in the last row of Table 11. As for the HV results, it can be seen that MLL-CPSO can obtain the best results in 16 out of 23 instances, 15 more than MLL-CPSO−MLL, 14 more than MLL-CPSO−SASD, and 12 more than MLL-CPSO−CeIU. As for the IGD results, we can see that MLL-CPSO achieves the best results in 13 instances, more than half of the test instances. Moreover, the number is 11 more than MLL-CPSO−MLL, 8 more than MLL-CPSO−SASD, and 10 more than MLL-CPSO−CeIU.

To sum up, the average HV results and average IGD results show that MLL, SASD, and CeIU can improve both the convergence and diversity of the solutions, thus boosting the whole performance of MLL-CPSO.

Conclusion

This paper propose a novel and effective algorithm named MLL-CPSO to cooperatively evolve the populations and carefully utilize historical evolution information hierarchically, which has three well-designed strategies. First, the MLL strategy is proposed to guide particles to learn hierarchical information including the short-term personal evolutionary information, the long-term social information, and the co-evolutionary information, which can not only avoid local optima but also approach the Pareto optima fast. Second, the SASD strategy is designed to improve the diversity of populations and help populations jump out of local optima. Third, the CeIU strategy is developed to improve the quality of co-evolutionary information in the archive, which can better guide populations to explore Pareto optimal solutions.

Three widely used benchmarks are adopted to evaluate the performance of the proposed MLL-CPSO. The experimental results show that our proposed MLL-CPSO performs better than 7 compared algorithms in 3 commonly used metrics (i.e., IGD, HV, and C-metric). In addition, we empirically analyze the influence of parameters on the performance of MLL-CPSO. Moreover, we investigate the contribution of the proposed MLL, SASD, and CeIU strategies for improving the overall performance of MLL-CPSO.

In the future, we will extend our work as follows: (1) considering that the parameters in MLL-CPSO need to be set manually, some parameter adaptive methods, such as the chaotic heterogeneous comprehensive learning particle swarm optimizer (C-HCLPSO) variants50,51, can be adopted to determine the parameters of MLL-CPSO in different cases; (2) seeing that the populations in MLL-CPSO evolve in parallel, the incorporation with distributed computing technique52 or cloud computing algorithm53 will be further studied to reduce the time cost; (3) employing MLL-CPSO to solve other realistic production problems, such as flow shop scheduling problems54 and distributed hybrid flow-shop scheduling55; (4) solving many-objective scheduling problems56, such as additionally consider the production cost, machine loss, and energy consumption57.

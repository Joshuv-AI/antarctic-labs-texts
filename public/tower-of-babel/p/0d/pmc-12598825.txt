Safety and effectiveness of orthokeratology for treating myopic regression after LASIK
Authors: Chang Zhang, Wenwei Li, Shudong Jing, Ping Zhao, Chenliang He, Fang Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis research was focused on evaluating the safety and efficacy of orthokeratology (Ortho-K) lenses in the management of myopic regression for patients who had previously undergone LASIK surgery.MethodsIn this retrospective, randomized study included 14 Chinese patients (26 eyes) who had undergone LASIK surgery more than 10 years prior and subsequently experienced myopic regression. Over a 30-month period of Ortho-K lens wear, changes in the Bowman’s roughness index, corneal and corneal flap thickness were evaluated using optical coherence tomography (OCT). The main goal of the research was to evaluate was to assess the impact of Ortho-K lens use on these parameters in individuals who have undergone LASIK surgery in the past surgery and myopic regression.ResultPrior to fitting the Ortho-K lenses, the average thickness of the central cornea was measured to be 481 ± 24 μm, whereas the thickness of the central corneal flap was found to be 181 ± 18 μm. Additionally, the nasal and temporal corneal flap thicknesses were measured as 145 ± 17 μm and 146 ± 21 μm, respectively. Following a period of Ortho-K lens wear, significant flattening was observed in both the central corneal thickness (461 ± 14 μm, p < 0.05) and central corneal flap thickness (162 ± 26 μm, p < 0.05). Conversely, the nasal and temporal corneal flap thicknesses increased significantly to 164 ± 27 μm (p < 0.05) and 169 ± 23 μm (p < 0.05), respectively. The pre-fitted Bowman’s roughness index (BRI) was recorded as 2.367 × 10− 3 mm2. Despite changes observed during the follow-up period, the final BRI measurement remained stable at 2.397 × 10− 3 mm2 (p = 0.687), indicating no significant alteration.ConclusionThis work demonstrated the ability to treat myopic progression after LASIK safely and effectively with overnight ortho-K.Keywords: Orthokeratology, LASIK, Refraction regression, BRI, Corneal flap

Introduction

In recent years, substantial advancements have been made in laser in situ keratomileusis (Lasik). However, patients who undergo refractive surgery often experience myopic regression, characterized by an increase in myopia of 0.5 diopters or more [1].The phenomenon may still occur following successful LASIK surgery even in the absence of ocular pathology [2]. Axial elongation, anterior displacement of the cornea, and the elevation in corneal thickness following surgery are considered as contributors to the recurrence of myopia after the procedure [3].As a non-surgical approach, orthokeratology (ortho-K) lenses are a viable retreatment option. Compared to glasses, ortho-K lenses offer cosmetic benefits and are associated with fewer adverse complications than soft contact lenses [4]. Several studies have demonstrated that ortho-K effectively controls myopia in adults [5]. However, it is important to consider the adverse effects of ortho-K, such as corneal damage, when using ortho-K in postoperative unstable corneas. This study endeavors to bridge a gap in the current research by evaluating the safety of orthokeratology lenses in preventing myopic regression after Lasik surgery, whilst simultaneously assessing their clinical efficacy and elucidating the fitting procedure for Ortho-K lenses in such cases. This study aims to elucidate the management experience of postoperative corneas using orthokeratology lenses, with the clinical therapeutic outcomes subsequent to keratorefractive surgery being assessed.

Methods

This is a retrospective analysis spanning 30 months, which encompassed 26 eyes of 14 patients who underwent keratorefractive surgery (LASIK) and subsequently experienced myopic regression. Retrospective data on patient clinical records from Zhejiang Provincial Tongde Hospital between January 2020 and October 2023 were collected. In this study, myopic regression is characterized by a shift towards myopia of 0.5 diopters (D) or greater, observed between follow-up visits scheduled one month after the surgical procedure [1]. All patients had undergone surgery within a period of 10 years prior to the study. At the Eye Department of Tongde Hospital of Zhejiang Province, patients were prescribed orthokeratology (ortho-K) lenses (Euclid Systems Corporation, Sterling, VA, USA) and were monitored for a duration of 30 months. The study excluded patients who had other eye-related conditions, including severe dry eyes, amblyopia, strabismus, glaucoma, corneal decompensation, severe allergic diseases, active infectious ulcers, or persistent epithelial defects. Furthermore, individuals who demonstrated suspected corneal ectasia following refractive surgery during topographic assessments were not included in the research.

Fitting of ortho-K

Orthokeratology lenses were prescribed to 14 female patients (mean age ± SD, 33.68 ± 5.36 years; range, 27–38) for a total of 26 eyes. Among 26 eyes, 13 were right eyes and 13 were left eyes (24 eyes from 12 patients, with 1 unilateral eye being a right eye and 1 unilateral eye being a left eye). All patients had myopia. The mean spherical equivalent (SE) prior to ortho-K was − 2.33 ± 1.25 D, with a range of -0.75 to -3.50 D. The refractive surgeries were performed at different institutions, with specific operational details being irretrievable due to the extended time elapsed.

The patients were prescribed Euclid Emerald orthokeratology lenses for fitting (Euclid Systems Corporation, Sterling, VA, USA) and were followed up for over 30 months. This study underscores the essential principle underlying ortho-K lens prescription, namely the fitting of the orthokeratology lenses to the cornea subsequent to Lasik surgery, with particular attention given to the alignment curve. In this study, a sagittal map derived from corneal topography (E300USB, Medmont Corporation, Australia) was utilized to assess the average K value of the peripheral cornea. This assessed value was then used to predict the preoperative central corneal K value. The eccentricity (e) was determined to be -0.55, which is outside the normal range of 0.4–0.6. As the preoperative eccentricity could not be accurately calculated, a value of 0.5 was adopted as the standard. For each patient, an estimated K [6] value was computed using a 4 mm peripheral zone of the cornea as the basis for the calculation. After determining the estimated K value, a set of orthokeratology contact lenses were utilized as trial lenses, and a fluorescein staining evaluation was conducted to ascertain the specifications for customizing the lenses for each patient. In this study, once the most favorable fluorescein patterns and an over-refraction power of + 0.75 D were achieved, the prescription of orthokeratology lenses was based on the “final K” value and “final lens power.After providing instructions on precautions, complications, and insertion and removal of the lenses, ortho-K was dispensed to the patients. The Bausch + Lomb care system for rigid gas permeable orthokeratology lenses was employed, which included a dedicated care solution, a lubricant, and an enzymatic protein removal agent.

Patients who were fitted with orthokeratology (ortho-K) lenses were arranged to attend a follow-up appointment one week following the initial lens fitting session and subsequently on a monthly basis. Upon completion the first month of follow-up treatment, patients were instructed to come for follow-up appointments every six months. In the event of any complications arising during the interval between follow-ups, patients were instructed to seek medical attention immediately. Patients were advised to wear the orthokeratology lenses while sleeping for a duration of 7 to 9 h.This research assessed multiple factors, including the patients’ optimal corrected visual acuity (BCVA), uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA), subjective refraction, axial length, and the condition of the cornea, which was evaluated through corneal topography and slit-lamp inspection. Furthermore, the fitting status of the contact lenses and any complications resulting from the use of ortho-K lenses were examined during each visit.The ortho-K lenses were refitted approximately every 1.5 years. During the entire study duration, the distant visual acuity of all patients was assessed from a distance of 5 m. Subjective refraction was performed to determine the optimal spherical lens power for each eye, employing an Automated Refractor (model RC-500, Tomey, Japan). Subjective non-cycloplegic refraction was performed monocularly.The criterion of maximum plus for best visual acuity was used to arriveto the end point of refraction. Additionally, axial length measurements were acquired using a non-contact partial coherence interferometer, specifically the IOLMaster-500, produced by Carl Zeiss in Jena, Germany. Corneal topography was performed by a consistent professional technician using the corneal topography instrument (E300USB, Medmont Corporation, Australia) to measure corneal parameters. All ortho-K fittings, fluorescein staining procedures, and follow-up examinations were performed using the Mediworks S39H Slit Lamp (Shanghai Meibo Precision Instrument Co., Ltd., China).

Measurement of HOAs

Total corneal higher-order aberrations (HOAs) over the central 6-mm zone were measured using the Medmont. All examinations were performed by a single trained investigator (C.Z.) under standard scotopic conditions .The device’s software automatically calculated Zernike coefficients for vertical and horizontal coma, vertical and oblique trefoil, spherical aberration, and the root mean square of HOA (RMS-HOA).

Topography and image analyses

During the aftercare visit, corneal topography revealed a thinning of the central cornea with an approximate three-millimeter treatment zone radius in each eye. Measurements of both the corneal flap and corneal thickness were acquired at the apex of the cornea and at a point 3.1 mm away from it, along the horizontal meridian, utilizing optical coherence tomography (OCT, Zeiss, Germany). Subsequently, the OCT images were evaluated utilizing Image-pro plus software, specifically version 6.0. The results are presented in Table 1 and (Fig. 1).

The Quality of Vision (QoV) questionnaire was employed to evaluate ten visual symptoms: glare, halos, hazy vision, starbursts, blurred vision, double or multiple images, distortion, fluctuating vision, difficulties with focusing, and impaired depth or distance perception. Each symptom was assessed across three domains: frequency (0 = never,1 = occasionally, 2 = quite often, 3 = very often), severity (0 = not at all, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, 3 = severe), and bothersome (0 = not at all, 1 = a little, 2 = quite, 3 = very) [10]. Patients were also asked about their overall satisfaction with the procedure (0 = very dissatisfied, 1 = dissatisfied, 2 = general, 3 = less satisfied, 4 = satisfied, 5 = very satisfied).

In this research, statistical analysis was conducted utilizing SPSS 25.0 software from SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, with a predefined significance threshold of p < 0.05.To detect any statistically significant differences in corneal values before and after the fitting of orthokeratology lenses, paired t-tests were employed. Additionally, paired samples analysis was utilized to process data from both eyes, with the objective of controlling individual differences between the two eyes. Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed QoV scores. The selection of Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis was also based on the normality of data, aiming to evaluate the correlations between central corneal flap thicknes and visual quality.

The research protocol has received approval from the Ethics Committee at Tongde Hospital, with approval number: Zhetongderunshen 2024 Research No. 184-JY, and it adheres to the guidelines outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. Based on the approval granted by the Ethics Committee, the authors were exempt from obtaining written informed consent forms. Instead, notification was conducted via telephone to all participants, during which the informed consent content was thoroughly explained, culminating in the obtainment of verbal agreement. The recorded audio files were kept as evidence of this consent process. All relevant institutional and governmental regulations pertaining to the ethical treatment of human participants were followed.

Result

The patients were subsequently observed over a period of 30 months. Before the application of orthokeratology lenses, the average LogMAR (logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution) value for uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA) was 0.48 ± 0.39, which improved to 0.01 ± 0.08 following the application of ortho-K lenses. The refractive error prior to fitting the lenses was − 2.57 ± 1.21 diopters (D) for myopia and − 0.63 ± 0.38 D for astigmatism. A significant reduction in myopia of -0.63 ± 0.64 D was observed at the final examination (p < 0.05). At the final visit, refractive astigmatism was − 0.47 ± 0.29 D, showing no significant difference from the initial visit (p = 0.829). It is worth noting that these alterations took place within the first week of wearing the orthokeratology lenses and persisted throughout the entire follow-up period (Figs. 3; Table 3). Although a mean axial elongation of 0.04 ± 0.07 mm was observed in all treated eyes, the change was not statistically significant (p = 0.637).

Before wearing, the spherical aberration value was 0.006 ± 0.005 μm, the coma value was − 0.007 ± 0.003 μm, and RMS HOA was 4.12 ± 1.42 μm. At the 30-month mark, the spherical aberration value was 0.008 ± 0.003 μm, the coma value was 0.006 ± 0.002 μm, and the HOA RMS was 3.62 ± 1.83 μm. No statistically significant differences were found in spherical aberration (p > 0.05), coma (p > 0.05), or the total HOA RMS (p > 0.05) between the two time points. The lack of statistical significance despite the observed decrease in HOA RMS is likely attributable to the high inter-subject variability (as indicated by the large standard deviations).

The primary visual symptoms before wearing orthokeratology (OK) lenses were blurred vision (87.55%), glare (75.27%), and starbursts (57.34%). At 30 months after wearing OK lenses, the main symptoms reported were fluctuation in vision (74.32%), double or multiple images (65.38%), and halos (61.25%). However, a statistically significant difference was observed in blurred vision before and after wearing OK lenses (P < 0.05), while no significant differences were found in other individual symptom scores or total scores. No significant correlations were detected between overall Quality of Vision (QoV) scores, overall satisfaction, and central corneal flap thicknesthickness indicators, and this observation remained consistent across different diopter subgroups (all P > 0.05).

Mild corneal staining occurred in three eyes during the study. These cases were managed by temporarily discontinuing lens wear for one week and administering recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor eye drops (TID). Lens wear was successfully resumed after confirmation of complete corneal recovery at follow-up, with no sequelae. No other corneal complications or treatment-limiting discomfort occurred, and all lenses maintained efficacy without requiring replacement.

Discussion

Myopic regression affects approximately 28% of patients after refractive surgery [13–15], with risk factors including preoperative parameters, age, and persistent dry eye [16, 17]. Compared to controversial IOP-lowering agents, dry-eye-aggravating soft contact lenses, and cosmetically limiting glasses [18, 19], orthokeratology (Ortho-K) offers a favorable alternative with demonstrated efficacy and safety [20, 21].

Significant differences in epithelial thickness distribution were observed as early as 1 month after LASIK surgery. The remodeling process continues for 1 to 3 months and tends to stabilize by 2 years postoperatively. However, disparities in thickening between the paracentral and peripheral regions persist throughout this period, although not always reaching statistical significance [22]. We therefore hypothesize that the distinct epithelial distribution patterns primarily develop during the short term following surgery and may persist for up to 10 years. Since this study specifically included patients only at the 10-year postoperative time point, our findings reflect long-term morphological characteristics.

To the best of the author’s understanding, no research has yet assessed the safety of orthokeratology in patients who experience myopia regression following refractive surgery. Nieto-Bona et al. reported a reduction in keratocyte density following one month of orthokeratology lens wear [23]. Although evidence indicates an accelerated decrease in keratocyte density following both orthokeratology [23] and LASIK (with annual cell loss rates of 1897 vs. 445 cells/mm³ in normal corneas) [24], this reduction does not appear to compromise visual function. In our study, all patients maintained transparent corneas and achieved a corrected visual acuity of 0.01 ± 0.08. This aligns with existing reports that interface scars with low cellularity do not cause light scatter sufficient to reduce visual acuity [25].

The most pronounced effects on corneal topography were observed during the initial month, with all corneal topography parameters stabilizing approximately six months later. Specifically, within the first month, the thickness of the corneal flap at the corneal apex decreased by 16.6%, whereas it increased by 20% at a distance of 3.1 mm from the corneal apex. No significant changes were noted in the subsequent 18 months. Furthermore, the Lasik incision, situated within the corneal stroma, did not exhibit any notable alterations as assessed by OCT across the horizontal corneal meridian. Consequently, it was concluded that the corneal structure remained stable and non-deformed under the sustained pressure exerted by orthokeratology lenses.The transient increase in the Bowman’s Roughness Index (BRI) observed immediately after orthokeratology likely reflected temporary corneal edema and stress-induced crimping of stromal collagen fibers rather than permanent micro-distortions. This is supported by observed stromal thickening with epithelial thinning and the known stress relaxation of fibers post-LASIK [26]. The subsequent normalization of BRI within one month coincided with corneal reshaping and fiber relaxation, confirming the changes were reversible.In the present study, no association was observed between central flap thickness and QoV, nor was any significant correlation found with total QoV scores or overall satisfaction. This lack of correlation may be attributed to the fact that all enrolled patients had undergone surgery over ten years prior, allowing for the establishment of a biologically integrated and structurally stable interface between the corneal flap and the underlying stroma [22].

The specific mechanism of myopic regression remains multifactorial. While increased central corneal thickness driven by compensatory epithelial proliferation is considered a root cause [27], and LASIK-induced corneal weakening (14–33% integrity reduction) may promote forward bulging under IOP [28], orthokeratology appears to counter these processes. By redistributing epithelial cells and physically restraining forward expansion, it maintains corneal stability. The observed stability in visual acuity and axial length over two years suggests that orthokeratology may effectively prevent myopic regression, though the precise mechanism warrants further investigation.

Despite the mechanical trauma and upregulated inflammatory response induced by ortho-K, António Queirós et al. concluded that long-term wear (averaging 3.6 ± 1.1 years) did not compromise subsequent LASIK surgery outcomes, nor did it adversely affect postoperative higher-order aberrations or visual quality [29]. In the present study, no adverse effects on higher-order aberrations or visual quality were observed in patients who underwent 30 months of ortho-K wear even 10 years after LASIK surgery. Given that ortho-K -induced changes—including epithelial cell flattening and enlargement, alterations in the subbasal nerve plexus, and minimal or no changes in the stromal and endothelial layers—have been demonstrated to be transient and reversible [30], these findings are not unexpected.

The present study has certain limitations, primarily pertaining to the restricted sample size. It is imperative that future research incorporates prospective, randomized, controlled studies with expanded sample sizes and greater population diversity. Nevertheless, the directions indicated by this study remain clear and well-defined.

This study demonstrates that at least 30 months of continued lens wear is safe and effective for this long-term orthokeratology lens wearer. Current and potential patients who experience refractive regression after laser surgery should be informed that orthokeratology lenses represent a viable option. While the wearing of orthokeratology lenses after refractive surgery may vary among individuals individuals, the patient described in this research was able to maintain appropriate wear throughout the 30 months wearing process.

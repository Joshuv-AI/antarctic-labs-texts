Spatial transcriptomics of cervical cancer reveals microenvironment shaped the malignant epithelial programs and ECM-associated immune niches
Authors: Qing Chen, Jinli Lyu, Yu Li, Lei Zhang, Pingyue Zhao, Guanwen Gao, Yunfei Wang, Changzhong Li, Xiaowei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesCervical cancer progression and therapeutic response are influenced by intratumoral heterogeneity and the tumor microenvironment. The diversity of malignant epithelial subtypes and their interactions with immune cells present significant challenges for effective clinical management.MethodsWe applied Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomics to FFPE cervical cancer specimens to define malignant epithelial subpopulations via marker expression, functional enrichment, pseudotime trajectory inference, and transcription factor activity. Findings were validated using immunohistochemistry in CIN III and invasive carcinoma and public scRNA-seq dataset. Cell–cell communication and niche-specific ligand were performed using CellChat and NicheNet.ResultsFour malignant epithelial programs were identified, including a proliferative ITGB6⁺ Epi, an invasive-edge ITGA6⁺ Epi enriched for EMT and matrix adhesion, a stress-adaptive NDRG1⁺/PVT1⁺ Epi at the tumor–stroma interface, and a differentiated TMPRSS11D⁺ Epi with barrier-like features. Pseudotime analysis revealed a unidirectional trajectory from ITGB6⁺ progenitor-like cells, diverging toward either ITGA6⁺ or NDRG1⁺ states, converging on TMPRSS11D⁺ differentiation. IHC confirmed stage-associated expression patterns, including ITGB6 upregulated in invasive carcinoma and ITGA6 enriched at invasive fronts. Comparable epithelial heterogeneity was observed in scRNA-seq data. Communication analyses showed broad mesenchymal–epithelial interactions dominated by extracellular matrix ligands, ITGA6⁺ epithelium displaying a distinct matrix-oriented communication signaling profile involving laminin-associated and TGFA–EGFR signaling. NicheNet further highlighted THBS1 as prominent microenvironmental signals predicted to regulate ITGA6-associated targets.ConclusionsCervical cancer displays spatially organized epithelial heterogeneity shaped by distinct microenvironmental niches. The ITGA6⁺ epithelial program is closely linked to matrix interactions and THBS1-associated signaling, suggesting a role in invasion and immune modulation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-026-04439-3.Keywords: Cervical cancer, Heterogeneity, Tumor microenvironment, Spatial transcriptomic analysis

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, despite advances in human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination and screening programs [1, 2]. Although these preventive strategies have reduced incidence in many regions, patients with advanced or recurrent disease often experience poor prognosis and heterogeneous responses to therapy, reflecting the complex biology of this malignancy [1, 3, 4].

Tumor heterogeneity is increasingly recognized as a critical determinant of cervical cancer progression, therapeutic resistance, and prognosis [5]. This heterogeneity arises from diverse molecular mechanisms within malignant epithelial cells and the surrounding tumor microenvironment [6]. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) studies have uncovered transcriptionally distinct epithelial subpopulations within individual tumors, associated with features such as neuroendocrine differentiation [7], metabolic reprogramming [8], or immune evasion [9, 10]. Infiltrating immune cells, particularly tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), have been proposed as prognostic indicators in various cancers. Malignant epithelial cells interact bidirectionally with immunosuppressive cancer-associated fibroblasts, contributing to immune-exclusionary tumor niches [11]. In addition, tumor-immune cells crosstalk further drives immune evasion. For instance, upregulation of immune checkpoint ligands such as PD-L1 on tumor cells leads to T cell dysfunction through PD-1 signaling, suppressing cytotoxic activity [12]. These findings highlight the need for integrative approaches that not only define cellular identities but also characterize their spatial relationships within tumor tissues.

Although scRNA-seq effectively captures transcriptional diversity at the single-cell resolution, it lacks spatial context. However, the functional states of tumor and immune cells are strongly influenced by their physical microenvironment. Proximity to stromal structures, immune infiltrates, or vasculature can shape the signaling activity, metabolic programs, and therapeutic susceptibility [13, 14]. Spatial transcriptomics therefore provides a powerful framework to integrate transcriptional heterogeneity with tissue architecture.

In this study, we applied spatial transcriptomics to formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) cervical cancer specimens to characterize the spatial architecture of tumor heterogeneity and its interplay with the local immune landscape. We aim to provide new insights into the mechanisms driving tumor progression and immune evasion. Characterizing these subpopulations through specific markers may highlight potential targets for personalized immunotherapy in cervical cancer.

Methods

Clinical data

Cervical specimens were surgically obtained from a 56-year-old patient diagnosed with stage IIIC squamous cervical cancer at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology of Peking University Shenzhen Hospital following written informed consent. All procedures involving human participants were conducted in full accordance with applicable ethical regulations.

Sample collection and spatial transcriptomics Stereo-seq OMNI

Cervical tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 24–48 h to preserve RNA integrity and morphology, then dehydrated through graded ethanol (70–100%), cleared in xylene, and paraffin-embedded. Serial sections were prepared for Stereo-seq and histopathology. RNA quality for each FFPE block was assessed using the High Sensitivity RNA ScreenTape Kit. From FFPE samples, 1.5-mm cores were taken for tissue microarray (TMA) construction. TMAs were processed with Stereo-seq v Early Access FFPE (STOmics, BGI, China) on a 1 cm × 1 cm chip and sequenced on a DNBSEQ-T7 platform (MGI, China).

Immunohistochemistry

FFPE sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval in EDTA buffer (microwave, 20 min). After PBS washes and blocking, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against ITGA6, ITGB6, TMPRSS11D, and NDRG1 (1:1000; Thermo Fisher, USA). Detection used the OptiView DAB IHC Kit, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining, dehydration, and mounting.

Spatial Transcriptomic raw data processing

Raw Stereo-seq FASTQ data from the MGI DNBSEQ-T7 sequencer were processed using the SAW pipeline (https://github.com/BGIResearch/SAW), including read decoding, trimming, deduplication, and alignment to the human reference genomes (GRCh38/hg38). Quality control was performed with Fastp. The SAW output gef file was analyzed with Stereopy v1.2.0 using a bin100 resolution (50 μm).

Aligned reads were aggregated into predefined spatial bins according to array coordinate tags (X, Y), followed by gene annotation assignment and Unique Molecular Identifier (UMI) counting to generate a digital gene expression matrix. The final Gene Expression Matrix (GEM) integrated spatial coordinates (binID, x, y) with gene expression counts (geneID, UMI count) for all detected spots/bins and served as the input for downstream spatial analyses. Because each bin100 spot may contain transcripts from multiple neighboring cells, the resulting expression profiles represent the dominant transcriptional signals within each spatial bin rather than single-cell resolution.

Spatial data analysis and cell-type annotation

The spatial expression matrix was imported into Seurat (version 4.4.0) in R. Data were log-normalized, and the top 2000 highly variable genes were identified for downstream analysis. Dimensionality reduction was performed via principal component analysis (PCA), followed by visualization with the RunUMAP and RunTSNE function based on significant principal components. Cell clusters were annotated based on cluster-specific gene expression profiles. Marker genes were identified using FindAllMarkers function in Seurat with parameters set to average log fold change threshold > 0.25 and p-value < 0.05. Marker genes used for clustering and cell-type annotation are provided in Supplementary Table 1. Cells were clustered using a graph-based Louvain algorithm on a shared nearest-neighbor (SNN) graph constructed in the PCA space. Cell-type annotation was performed using canonical marker genes reported before. Cluster identities were assigned based on marker gene enrichment and further validated using automated label transfer from matched single-cell RNA-seq references with tools such as SingleR or Azimuth.

Spatial region-specific transcriptomic analysis

To investigate transcriptomic signatures of specific immune niches, regions of interest (ROIs) were manually delineated using the lasso selection tool implemented in StereoMap software on Stereo-seq spatial images. Specifically, immune cell-enriched regions adjacent to malignant epithelial populations were selected based on tissue morphology and spatial co-localization patterns. The segmented ROI data were subsequently processed using the SAW pipeline to extract the corresponding spatial coordinates and gene expression matrices. Spatial proximity between immune cells and epithelial populations was further quantified using nearest-neighbor distance analysis implemented in the spatstat R package. ROI-specific expression profiles were subsequently subjected to differential gene expression and pathway enrichment analyses, enabling spatially resolved characterization of immune microenvironmental programs.

Single-cell transcriptomic data processing

Publicly available single-cell RNA-seq count matrices and metadata were obtained from HRA004971. Six samples (three cervical cancer and three healthy controls) were processed using the Seurat R package (v4.4.0). Dimensionality reduction and cell-type annotation were performed using the same procedures described for the spatial data analysis.

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis was performed using the enrichGO and enrichKEGG functions in the R package ClusterProfiler (v4.10.0). Differentially expressed genes (|avg_logFC|> 0.25 and p_val < 0.05) were converted from gene symbols to Entrez IDs using org.Hs.eg.db (v3.18.0) and analyzed using Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway databases. Enrichment significance was assessed using a hypergeometric test (equivalent to a one-sided Fisher’s exact test), and p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate (FDR). Terms with an adjusted p-value < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Pseudotime trajectory inference

Pseudotime trajectories were reconstructed using Monocle3 (v1.3.7) to infer differentiation dynamics of epithelial cells. All partitions generated by the cluster_cells function were included. Coordinates from previous clustering analyses were embedded into the pseudotime space to project pseudotime scores onto the clustering map for visualization.

Transcriptional regulatory network analysis

Single-cell transcriptional regulatory networks were inferred using pySCENIC (v0.12.1), a widely used framework for scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomic analyses[15]. Transcription factor activity was evaluated using AUCell scores, and activation states were binarized for visualization. Detailed documentation is available at the pySCENIC repository[16].

Cell–cell communication analysis

CellChat (v2.1.0) was used to infer intercellular communication networks from spatial transcriptomic data. Ligand–receptor interactions were analyzed within epithelial populations and between epithelial and immune cells. To further investigate regulatory mechanisms, NicheNet (nichenetr v2.0.5) was applied to model signaling from epithelial subclusters to immune receiver populations (Label 1, Label 2, Label 3). This analysis prioritized upstream ligands predicted to drive observed transcriptional changes in receiver cells.

Statistical analysis

Differential expression analyses for spatial transcriptomic and scRNA-seq data were conducted using Seurat (v4.4.0), with marker genes identified by the FindAllMarkers function (avg_logFC > 0.25, p < 0.05) based on the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Functional enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler (v4.10.0), and enrichment significance was evaluated using a hypergeometric test. Distance distributions were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and kernel density curves were visualized using ggplot2. Multiple-testing correction was conducted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method where applicable.

Results

Spatial transcriptomics defines distinct malignant epithelial states in cervical cancer

To characterize the cellular composition of cervical cancer, we performed spatial transcriptomic profiling of FFPE tissues using Stereo-seq (Fig. 1A). This workflow enabled in situ mRNA capture followed by sequencing and computational deconvolution of spatially barcoded transcripts. After quality control filtering, a total of 31,863 spots were retained for downstream analyses, capturing 26,273 genes overall. The resulting spatial maps demonstrated clear and coherent tissue structures across sections (Fig. 1B), indicating high RNA integrity and reliable spatial resolution. Dimensionality reduction via UMAP revealed eight transcriptionally distinct cell populations (Fig. 1C), including immune cells (ImC, 1977), mesenchymal cells (MeC, 7175), columnar epithelial cells (CeC, 414), plasma cells (615), and epithelium. Among epithelial cells, four transcriptionally distinct subpopulations were defined by marker expression: ITGA6⁺Epi (2,045), ITGB6⁺Epi (7,093), NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺Epi (8,287), and TMPRSS11D⁺Epi (4,257) (Fig. 1D). The significant transcriptional segregation observed across epithelial subpopulations highlights intratumoral epithelial heterogeneity.

FFPE Stereo-seq identifies spatial malignant epithelial programs in cervical cancer, validated by immunohistochemistry. A Schematic overview of the FFPE Stereo-seq workflow; B spatial maps showing the distribution of major cell populations across representative tissue sections; C two-dimensional UMAP visualization of Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomic profiles showing major cell types in cervical cancer tissues; D marker expression dot plot supporting cell type and epithelial subtype annotation; E spatial distribution for ITGA6+ Epi, ITGB6+ Epi, NDRG1+ Epi, and TMPRSS11D+ Epi; F representative immunohistochemistry staining of ITGA6, ITGB6, TMPRSS11D, and NDRG1 in CIN III and cervical cancer specimens

Spatial mapping further revealed non-uniform and regionally enriched distributions of these epithelial states (Fig. 1E). ITGA6+ Epi is concentrated at the tissue periphery, whereas ITGB6+ Epi is enriched across broader tissue areas with multiple high-density regions distinct from the ITGA6+ Epi edge-associated pattern. NDRG1/PVT1+ Epi and TMPRSS11D+ Epi occupy internal territories and frequently interdigitate.

To validate these spatial signatures, immunohistochemistry (IHC) was performed in CIN III and invasive carcinoma specimens (Fig. 1F). ITGB6 expression was weak and patchy in CIN III but became stronger and more widespread in invasive carcinoma, consistent with expansion of a proliferative malignant program during progression. ITGA6 showed low overall staining with focal positivity, reflecting its restricted epithelial subset. TMPRSS11D displayed modest and heterogeneous expression across both stages, while NDRG1 was detectable in CIN III epithelium and remained variably expressed in carcinoma. Together, these spatially resolved markers highlight epithelial heterogeneity and provide insights into the transition from premalignant lesions to invasive cervical cancer.

Epithelial states define a functional and regulatory continuum of malignant plasticity

Pathway analysis revealed distinct functional enrichment profile across the four epithelial subpopulations (Fig. 2A). ITGA6+ Epi exhibited pronounced enrichment in cell–matrix adhesion, integrin-mediated signaling, and angiogenesis regulation, associated with epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis initiation. ITGB6+ Epi was linked to mitotic cell-cycle regulation, DNA replication, and centrosome cycle, suggesting proliferative phenotype. NDRG1+PVT1+ Epi was associated with cadherin-mediated cell–cell adhesion, wound healing, and p53-dependent signaling, indicative of epithelial plasticity at tumor–stroma interfaces. TMPRSS11D+ Epi specialized in tight junction assembly, epidermal differentiation, and EGFR signaling, reflecting differentiated tumor potentially conferring therapeutic resistance via barrier formation.

Malignant epithelial programs exhibit functional polarization. A Gene Ontology enrichment analysis across four epithelial programs; B UMAP visualization of epithelial programs and trajectory inference; C expression trends of representative genes along pseudotime; D transcription factor activity heatmap showing program specific regulatory architectures; E chord diagram linking representative genes to enriched GO modules

Pseudotime analysis of epithelial populations revealed a unidirectional differentiation trajectory (Fig. 2B). ITGB6+ Epi (progenitor-like) bifurcated into ITGA6+ Epi (invasion-primed) and NDRG1+PVT1+ Epi (plasticity-enabled), ultimately converging toward TMPRSS11D+ Epi (terminally differentiated). This trajectory suggests a sequential progression from proliferative tumor cells to microenvironment-adaptive differentiation, highlighting dynamic epithelial remodeling within the tumor microenvironment. Gene expression dynamics along pseudotime further supported this model (Fig. 2C). ITGA6 and ITGB6 were preferentially expressed at earlier pseudotime intervals corresponding to their respective epithelial states, whereas NDRG1 and TMPRSS11D increased at later stages, reflecting a transition toward stress-adaptive and differentiation-like phenotypes. Stress-response genes such as ANXA1 and ATF3 also increased along pseudotime and were enriched in NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺ and TMPRSS11D⁺ cells, consistent with progressive adaptation of malignant epithelium to the tumor microenvironment. Previous studies have reported reduced ANXA1 expression during the progression from cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) to invasive squamous carcinoma, supporting its involvement in malignant evolution [17]. Conversely, ATF3 was upregulated within the NDRG1-associated epithelial program and has been implicated in metastasis-suppressive signaling. Mechanistically, NDRG1 can suppress metastasis through ATF3-dependent transcriptional regulation, including ATF3–NFκB-mediated pathways [18].

Distinct transcription-factor (TF) modules were associated with each epithelial state (Fig. 2D). ITGA6⁺ cells displayed elevated activity of TFs such as TEAD1, RFX3, ATF2, ESR1, and RUNX1, regulators linked to mechanotransduction, epithelial identity, and cell–matrix interactions. This regulatory pattern clearly differed from that of ITGB6⁺ cells. In contrast, NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺ and TMPRSS11D⁺ cells showed highly similar TF activity profiles, consistent with their proximity along pseudotime and shared enrichment for stress-adaptive and differentiation-related pathways.

Gene Ontology enrichment analysis of regulon-associated genes further highlighted distinct functional modules within epithelial states. In ITGA6⁺Epi, genes including ITGA6, FLNA, CDK6, CDH13, SMAD3, and TEAD1 were enriched in pathways related to focal adhesion, cell–substrate junctions, epithelial proliferation, migration, and Hippo signaling (Fig. 2E). In contrast, TMPRSS11D⁺Epi regulons were associated with genes such as OCLN, CDH4, CDC42, RAC1, YAP1, AKT1, ANXA1/ANXA2, FASN, and NDRG1, which are involved in tight junction organization, cadherin binding, lamellipodium formation, epithelial development, and wound healing (Fig. 2F). Together, these results indicate that malignant epithelial states in cervical cancer are governed by distinct yet interconnected transcriptional regulatory networks, providing a mechanistic basis for the functional polarization and plasticity observed along the epithelial differentiation trajectory.

Public scRNA-seq validates malignant epithelial state heterogeneity

To independently validate the malignant epithelial states identified by spatial transcriptomics, we analyzed a publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset of cervical cancer tissues. Unsupervised clustering resolved the major cellular compartments of the tumor microenvironment, including epithelial cells, endothelial cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), smooth muscle cells, mono/macrophages, NK/T cells, B cells, plasma cells, and mast cells (Fig. 3A). Cell identities were assigned using established marker gene panels commonly applied in scRNA-seq analyses (Fig. 3B). Subsequent analysis of epithelial cells identified ten transcriptionally distinct epithelial subpopulations (Fig. 3C). Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these subsets were associated with diverse biological programs (Fig. 3D), highlighting pronounced intratumoral heterogeneity within malignant epithelial cells. Several clusters were dominated by cell-cycle-associated processes, including DNA replication, mitotic nuclear division, and chromosome segregation, consistent with a proliferative epithelial program linked to tumor expansion, while other subpopulations preferentially enriched pathways were related to epidermal development, keratinocyte differentiation, and epithelial maturation, suggesting a shift toward a more differentiated malignant phenotype.

Public scRNA-seq corroborates microenvironmental compartments and validates malignant epithelial subpopulations and modules. A Low-dimensional embedding of a public cervical cancer single-cell dataset; B canonical marker dot plot of cell-type annotation across the dataset; C epithelial-focused clustering revealing transcriptionally distinct epithelial subgroups; D heatmap of average expression for top marker genes across epithelial subclusters, with functional enrichment; E–F representative gene module features and network visualization supporting coordinated transcriptional programs underlying epithelial heterogeneity

To further compare spatial and single-cell epithelial programs, label transfer analysis was performed between the spatial transcriptomic and scRNA-seq datasets. Among the epithelial subsets, the C1 cluster in the scRNA-seq dataset showed strong concordance with the TMPRSS11D⁺ epithelial program identified in the spatial analysis (Supplementary Fig. 1 A).

To further characterize epithelial state heterogeneity, we constructed a gene co-expression network using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) (Fig. 3E). Two representative modules, Module2 and Module5, displayed distinct activity patterns across epithelial subsets. The epi_M2 module was enriched in a specific epithelial population and consisted predominantly of canonical cell-cycle regulators, including MKI67, TOP2A, CENPF, CCNB1/CCNB2, CDK1, AURKB, UBE2C, and NUSAP1, consistent with a proliferative epithelial program. In contrast, epi_M5 showed a complementary activity pattern and comprised the genes associated with epithelial differentiation and stress responses, including SPRR family members (SPRR2A, SPRR1B), IVL, KRT16/KRT80, ECM1, CEACAM5, IL1RN, and TMPRSS11D. The strong connectivity among these genes suggests a coordinated transcriptional module rather than dispersed expression. Collectively, these results support the existence of distinct and coherent gene programs underlying malignant epithelial heterogeneity.

ImC-associated niches connect the malignant epithelial states

Spatial niche stratification showed that immune infiltration is unevenly distributed across the tumor and forms discrete microenvironmental neighborhoods that are preferentially associated with specific malignant epithelial cells. Across two representative sections, three labels delineated reproducible patterns of co-localization between inflammatory myeloid cells (ImC) and epithelial cells (Fig. 4A). Label 1 marked the ImC regions adjacent to ITGA6⁺Epi, Label 2 highlighted the ImC associated with NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺Epi and TMPRSS11D⁺Epi, and Label 3 delineated the regions where ImC preferentially localized near ITGB6⁺Epi. These patterns indicate that immune infiltration is spatially organized into epithelial-linked niches rather than uniformly distributed across the tumor. Using annotated single-cell datasets, label mapping of these regions showed that T cells constituted the predominant immune population across all labels, with no major differences in overall cell-type composition (Fig. 4B).

Spatially defined epithelial–immune neighborhoods exhibit distinct pathway activities and immune compositions. A Spatial maps showing three niche labels (Label 1–Label 3) overlaid on tissue sections; B pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes across niche labels. C Immune cell composition across Label 1–Label 3, showing the relative proportions of T cells, NK cells, B cells, plasma cells, and myeloid cells within each niche

To quantitatively evaluate spatial proximity, nearest-neighbor distance analysis was performed between ImC cells and epithelial subpopulations. Compared with randomly sampled background immune cells, ImC cells within Label 1–Label 3 regions showed significantly shorter distances to their corresponding epithelial populations, including ITGA6⁺Epi, NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺Epi, and ITGB6⁺Epi (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p < 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Transcriptional pathway analysis revealed distinct functional features for each niche (Fig. 4C). Label 1 exhibited the upregulation of ECM–receptor interaction, focal adhesion, PI3K–Akt signaling, and NOD-like receptor signaling, suggesting active matrix engagement coupled with innate inflammatory responses. Label 2 was enriched for immune-inflammatory pathways, including IL-17 signaling, NF-κB signaling, C-type lectin receptor signaling, and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation, indicating enhanced cytokine signaling and innate immune activation. In addition, Label 3 is upregulated in MAPK and cGMP–PKG signaling, suggesting activation of growth and stress-response signaling.

Label 1 showed a reduction in apoptosis, consistent with enhanced cellular survival within this epithelial-associated immune neighborhood, which may facilitate local immune cell persistence. Label 2 displayed the broader suppression of immune effector functions, with downregulation of antigen processing and presentation and Fc gamma receptor-mediated phagocytosis, accompanied by reduced proteoglycans in cancer, ECM–receptor interaction, focal adhesion, and PI3K Akt signaling, indicating attenuation of both immune functional capacity and tumor matrix interaction activity. Label 3 was characterized by decreased activity of signaling and adhesion pathways, including MAPK signaling, PI3K Akt signaling, ECM receptor interaction, and focal adhesion, together with reduced cGMP PKG signaling, implying dampened growth and microenvironment–interaction signaling.

Mesenchymal-driven niches differentially shape the epithelial communication

To investigate interactions among epithelial subpopulations, we performed cell–cell communication analysis using CellChat. The interaction network revealed that mesenchymal cells communicate with all epithelial states, indicating that epithelial functional diversity emerges within a mesenchyme-rich microenvironment (Fig. 5A). Ligand–receptor analysis further illustrated distinct communication patterns between epithelial subsets and surrounding cell types (Fig. 5B). Among the epithelial populations, ITGA6⁺Epi exhibited a communication profile clearly distinct from the others. This subset preferentially engaged extracellular matrix (ECM)-related signaling, including laminin interactions mediated by LAMC2 or LAMB3 with CD44, integrin-mediated contacts involving ITGA6 with ITGB1, and TGFA–EGFR signaling. These interactions suggest that the ITGA6⁺ epithelial program is strongly linked to matrix and stromal interfaces, consistent with a microenvironment-interacting malignant phenotype. However, NDRG1⁺PVT1⁺Epi, TMPRSS11D⁺Epi, and ITGB6⁺Epi displayed broadly similar communication patterns dominated by epithelial cohesion pathways. These included desmosomal interactions such as DSC2/DSC3 with DSG3, cadherin-mediated adhesion (CDH1–CDH1 and CDH3–CDH3), and tight junction signaling via CLDN1 [19]. Different from ITGA6+ Epi, these three populations show comparatively limited enrichment of extracellular matrix-related laminin integrin or laminin CD44 signaling. This overall similarity suggests that most epithelial states primarily communicate through cell-to-cell adhesion and barrier-associated pathways, whereas ITGA6+ Epi are distinguished by stronger matrix and stromal interface signaling.

Mesenchymal and immune signaling networks shape the epithelial programs. A Global communication networks among epithelial programs and mesenchymal cells; B dot plot of selected ligand receptor pairs among epithelial programs and mesenchymal cells; C niche-specific interaction maps for Label 1, Label 2, and Label 3; D ligand prioritization analysis using ITGA6-positive epithelium as the receiver population across Label 1, Label 2, and Label 3

Across the three spatial niches (Label 1–Label 3), communication was consistently dominated by stromal ECM ligands, particularly collagens (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL6A3) and TNC, interacting with epithelial receptors such as CD44, SDC1, SDC4, and integrin complexes (Fig. 5C). Label 1 exhibited the most extensive interaction network, whereas Label 2 and Label 3 showed a more restricted subset of similar ECM–receptor pathways, suggesting shared ECM-centered signaling with niche-specific differences in interaction breadth.

To further assess immune–epithelial crosstalk, we applied NicheNet to link immune-derived ligands from Label 1 to Label 3 niches to epithelial target genes (Fig. 5D). This analysis predicted that THBS1 may regulate ITGA6 expression in ITGA6⁺Epi cells. As a laminin receptor, ITGA6 can activate PI3K–Akt signaling, and prior studies suggest that THBS1 and ITGA6 may cooperate to enhance PI3K–Akt activity during epithelial repair. Tumors may exploit this signaling axis to promote epithelial proliferation and invasion [16, 20].

Discussion

Spatial transcriptomic profiling of a FFPE cervical cancer specimen reveals multiple malignant epithelial subpopulations with distinct transcriptional programs and spatial localizations. These epithelial states were further supported by an independent public single-cell RNA sequencing dataset, indicating that the observed heterogeneity is reproducible across transcriptomic platforms. In addition, analysis of an independent public spatial transcriptomic dataset from Qiuyue Su et al. [21] similarly identified epithelial populations with elevated expression of ITGA6, ITGB6, TMPRSS11D, and NDRG1 in patients 6 and 7 (Supplementary Fig. 1B–D), further supporting the reproducibility of these epithelial programs across different patient samples and spatial transcriptomic platforms. Although direct one-to-one correspondence between all epithelial states was not fully observed, likely due to interpatient heterogeneity, sampling regions, and technical batch differences, functionally related epithelial programs were consistently identified across datasets. Notably, the TMPRSS11D-associated epithelial state showed strong concordance between spatial and single-cell analyses, whereas the ITGA6⁺ invasive-edge program was more distinctly resolved in the spatial dataset, highlighting the advantage of spatial transcriptomics in capturing region-specific epithelial phenotypes embedded within native tissue architecture. Together, these findings indicate that malignant epithelial heterogeneity is a fundamental characteristic of cervical cancer and may contribute to its phenotypic diversity.

Previous studies have identified a proliferative malignant compartment in cervical cancer enriched for cell-cycle-related genes and linked to aggressive pathology [22]. In our spatial dataset, the ITGB6-associated transcriptional program corresponds to this proliferative signature and displays a diffuse distribution throughout the tumor core. Immunohistochemistry confirmed marked ITGB6 upregulation in invasive carcinoma compared with CIN III, supporting its role as a marker of a broadly disseminated proliferative epithelial state during malignant transformation [23]. ITGB6 has also been associated with poor prognosis and enhanced tumor proliferation, invasion, and migration in other cancers [24]. In contrast, an ITGA6⁺ epithelial subpopulation was spatially restricted to the tumor periphery and enriched for extracellular matrix engagement and stromal interface signaling. Integrin-mediated signaling is widely implicated in cancer invasion, niche anchoring, and therapy resistance [25], and metastatic progression [26, 27], suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target [28]. However, validation in cohorts with metastatic outcome data is required.

Our findings indicate that epithelial heterogeneity is shaped not only by intrinsic tumor programs but also by the tumor microenvironment. ECM-rich stromal environments can restrict immune infiltration, promote immune exclusion, and modulate malignant phenotypes through adhesion-dependent signaling [29, 30]. Consistently, we observed extensive epithelial–mesenchymal crosstalk dominated by stromal ECM ligands such as collagens and matricellular proteins. The tumor periphery, enriched for ITGA6⁺ epithelial cells, displayed laminin-centered communication involving LAMC2/LAMB3 interactions with CD44 and integrins, together with TGFA–EGFR signaling. Laminin signaling can activate STAT3-dependent transcriptional programs that promote EMT regulators such as ZEB1 [31], linking peripheral localization and ECM signaling to a pro-invasive epithelial phenotype and potential metastatic progression [32].

Given that spatial organization of the tumor immune microenvironment critically shapes the immune activity and therapeutic response [33], we defined Label 1–Label 3 niches to capture immune neighborhoods surrounding distinct epithelial programs. Pathway divergence indicates immune niches dominated by stromal matrix barriers or inflammatory signaling accompanied by reduced immune effector activity. Impaired antigen processing and presentation can limit effective T cell recognition despite immune infiltration [34], and reduced Fcγ receptor-mediated phagocytosis reflects suppression of innate clearance, which may be reinforced by anti-phagocytic checkpoints such as CD47 [35]. Thus, these spatially defined neighborhoods represent immune-proximal regions with markedly different functional capacities.

Our niche ligand prioritization further highlights THBS1 as a shared, high-activity microenvironmental signal across spatial labels, alongside ECM and adhesion-related ligands, such as LAMC2, COL1A1, and FN1. THBS1 engage CD47, a receptor implicated in T cell dysfunction and exhaustion, and the CD47–THBS1 axis has been associated with impaired antitumor immunity and therapeutic response [36]. Notably, ITGA6 itself is among the top predicted transcriptional targets linked to THBS1 signaling, along with genes involved in growth and transcriptional regulation (e.g., SMAD3, CDK6). Therefore, the ITGA6⁺ epithelial program is embedded within a multifaceted niche characterized by concurrent inputs from matrix remodeling, cell adhesion, and immunomodulatory pathways. This further supports a microenvironment-interfacing malignant phenotype with enhanced capacity for tissue invasion or remodeling.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Although the spatial transcriptomic analysis was performed on a single FFPE cervical cancer specimen, the major epithelial programs and transcriptional features identified in this study were independently supported by immunohistochemical validation in additional CIN III and invasive carcinoma samples, as well as by publicly available single-cell RNA-seq datasets comprising multiple cervical cancer and healthy control samples, and an independent spatial transcriptomic dataset from Qiuyue Su et al. (patients 6 and 7) [21]. Nevertheless, the current findings remain exploratory and may not fully capture interpatient heterogeneity related to histology, stage, HPV status, or treatment context. In addition, the inferred cell–cell communication networks and ligand–target relationships require validation in larger, clinically annotated cohorts and experimental models to establish their mechanistic significance in cervical cancer progression.

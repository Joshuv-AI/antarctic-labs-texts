CXCR4-antagonistic peptide-decorated lipid nanoparticles for co- delivery of AML1-ETO siRNA to enhance chemotherapy in refractory AML
Authors: Xuelu Peng, Runxia Gu, Tao Wang, Chuanmei Jiang, Haiyan Xing, Jie Meng, Tao Wen, Jian Liu, Jianxiang Wang, Haiyan Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFusion protein AML1-ETO and chemokine receptor CXCR4 are crucial therapeutic targets for AML, the former plays a key role in differentiation blockade and leukemogenesis, and the latter is closely associated with drug resistance and relapse. This work developed a unified platform (referred as to E5-LNP@siAE) to integrate AML1-ETO-targeted RNA interference (RNAi) and CXCR4 antagonism by employing lipid nanoparticle (LNP) technology and antagonistic peptide E5 modification. The E5-LNP@siNC was slightly negative charged with 121.8 nm in diameter, showing homogenous spherical morphology and stability in PBS and 10% FBS. The E5-LNP@siAE could be specifically taken up by Kasumi-1 cells, down regulated levels of AML1-ETO, p-Erk and p-p38, and E5-LNP@siNC inhibited the CXCL12-mediated migration. In a refractory AML mouse model, E5-LNP@siAE monotherapy significantly reduced AML cells in peripheral blood, as well as infiltrations in the liver, lung and spleen. The expressions of CD11b, CD41a and Ter119 were upregulated while c-Kit decreased in the spleen of AML mice received E5-LNP@siAE treatment. The differentiation inductive effects were also observed in bone marrow mononuclear cells separated from AML patients with t(8;21) translocation. Moreover, E5-LNP@siAE significantly enhanced the therapeutic efficacy of doxorubicin (DOX) in the AML mice, evidenced by the reduced AML cells in peripheral blood, bone marrow and multiple organs, meanwhile, did not bring negative impacts on the counts of red blood cells and platelets. Consequently, the combination regimen achieved the much better result of survival prolongation than E5-LNP@siAE monotherapy, and the other control groups did not show therapeutic effects on the survival of the AML mice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13045-026-01785-8.Keywords: Lipid nanoparticles, Leukemia, Fusion protein AML1-ETO, CXCR4, Antagonistic peptide

To the editor

The fusion protein AML1-ETO and chemokine receptor CXCR4 are two promising therapeutic targets for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [1, 2]. Although crosstalk between the two targets may exist [3], approaches for simultaneously inhibiting them and their implications for AML treatment have rarely been explored. AML1-ETO inhibits multiple lineage differentiations of hematopoietic stem cells, consequently promoting leukemogenesis, disease progression and drug resistance [4–7], while the CXCR4/CXCL12 axis facilitates interactions between AML cells and stromal cells, contributing to the development of minimal residual disease leading to the drug resistance and relapse [8]. We previously developed a peptide E5 and demonstrated its CXCR4 antagonistic function in AML mice carrying the fusion gene AML1-ETO and C-KITD816V mutant [9]. Issa et al. recently reported the feasibility of siRNA-loaded lipid nanoparticles for targeting AML1-ETO in a xenotransplantation model [10]. Given the above background, we hypothesized that combining AML1-ETO siRNA (siAE) and E5 to generate a dual-function lipid nanoparticle platform (E5-LNP@siAE) would bring mutual enhanced therapeutic effects on refractory AML.

The E5-LNP@siAE was fabricated by a self-assembly process of siAE with a formulation of lipid molecules, one of which, DSPE-PEG2000 was conjugated with E5, and control LNPs were prepared as well (Abbreviations in Table S1). The E5-LNP@siNC was spheres of 121.8 nm in diameter (Fig. 1A) with 97% of encapsulation efficiency of siRNA (Table S2), stable in PBS and 10% FBS, and with the average hydrodynamic diameter of 170.54 nm (Fig. 1B and S1). The E5-LNP@siNC could be engulfed more efficiently by cells than LNP@siNC (Fig. 1C-D, S2 and S3). Both E5-LNP@siAE and LNP@siAE decreased AML1-ETO (Fig. 1E-F and S4) and CXCR4 levels (Fig. S5), and inhibited the phosphorylation of p38 and Erk in Kasumi-1 (Fig. 1E-F) and SKNO-1 cells (Fig. S6), the former was stronger than the latter. The CXCL12-mediated migration was suppressed by E5-LNP@siNC while not by LNP@siNC (Fig. 1G).

A refractory AML mouse model featured with the fusion gene AML1-ETO and C-KITD816V mutant was employed [11] in the pharmacological investigation. E5-LNP@siAE significantly reduced AML cells in the bone marrow, peripheral blood and infiltrations in the liver, lung and spleen (Fig. 1H and S7). Significant up-regulations of CD11b, CD41a and Ter119 were observed in the spleen of E5-LNP@siAE group, indicating AML cells in the spleen differentiated towards granulocytic, megakaryocytic and erythroid directions respectively. The c-Kit+ cells were largely reduced by E5-LNP@siAE treatment (Fig. 1I, Fig. S8), and colony-forming units of AML cells significantly decreased compared with that of the other groups (Fig. S9). The differentiation inductive effects were also observed with bone marrow mononuclear cells separated from AML patients with t(8;21) translocation (Fig. 1J), the differences among the different patients were likely attributable to the individual differences (Table S3). Noted that the ALT and BUN of AML mice received E5-LNP@siAE decreased, suggesting therapeutic benefits to functions of kidney and liver (Fig. S10), while no significant changes in body weight (Fig. S11) or hemolysis were detectable (Fig. S12). None of the LNPs affected the viability of stromal cells (Fig. S13).

Next, the chemo-sensitizing effect of E5-LNP@siAE towards doxorubicin (DOX) was investigated, based on the observation that E5-LNP@siAE prolonged the survival of AML mice to some extent, whereas the other controls did not (Fig. S14). The combination regimen of E5-LNP@siAE and DOX to AML mice exhibited much stronger effect, reducing AML cells in the peripheral blood, spleen, liver and lung than DOX alone; meanwhile, no stronger toxicity was detected in the counts of red blood cells and platelets (Fig. 2A). Results of H&E showed that the combination regimen reduced the proportion of AML cells in the bone marrow, spleen, liver and lung, and restored physiological structures (Fig. 2B and S15). Consequently, the combination regimen prolonged the survival of the AML mice significantly while the Dox alone did not (Fig. 2C, Table S6). The upregulation of CD11b, CD41a and Ter119 and the down regulation of c-Kit were observed in the group of combination regimen (Fig. 2D, Fig. S16), and the differentiated cells were origin from the AML cells (Fig. 2E-F). Flow cytometry analysis of Kasumi-1 cells supported the inductive capacity of E5-LNP@siAE (Fig. S17-18).

In summary, E5-LNP@siAE serves as a dual-function LNP platform that combines AML1-ETO RNA interference with CXCR4 antagonism, significantly enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of DOX in a refractory AML mouse model. Limitations of this study include the current depth of mechanistic investigation, the need for ADME studies, and the requirement for more comprehensive control groups in the survival experiments.

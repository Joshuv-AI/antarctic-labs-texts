Protocol for imaging and quantifying single-cell CDK activity levels during early mouse embryonic development by time-lapse microscopy
Authors: Bechara Saykali, Andy D. Tran, Michael J. Kruhlak, Sergio Ruiz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryTracking the translocation of fluorescent-based reporters at the single-cell level in living mouse embryos requires specialized expertise in mouse embryology and deep computational skills. Here, we detail an approach to quantify cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) activity levels in single cells throughout different stages of the pre-implantation embryo. We discuss in vitro culture strategies that enable efficient live fluorescent confocal image acquisition and subsequent cell tracking. Finally, we describe the image analysis workflow needed for the processing and visualization of the datasets obtained.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Saykali et al.1

Before you begin

The protocol below describes the specific steps for isolating, imaging and analyzing live CDK activity in single cells in developing pre-implantation embryos. For this, we generated a mouse model expressing a previously described CDK sensor (a portion of the human DNA helicase B (DHB) fused to the fluorescent protein mClover3, DHB-Clover3 hereafter) together with the histone H2B (H2B) fused to the fluorescent protein Ruby3 (H2B-Ruby3 hereafter).2,3 The CDK sensor becomes phosphorylated and shuffles between the nuclei (low CDK activity) and the cytoplasm (high CDK activity) in a CDK-dependent manner.2,3 The quantification of the cytoplasmic-to-nuclear intensity ratio of the CDK sensor provides a quantifiable readout of CDK activity in real-time. In this protocol, we detail the steps needed to assign CDK activity profiles to specific lineage-labeled cells over time.

Innovation

One of the improvements from our protocol resides in the fact that mouse embryos are embedded in Matrigel to decrease embryo movement and rotation during confocal live imaging without compromising normal embryo development. This enables a better tracking of the embryonic cells for a longer time.

Mouse embryo harvesting

Timing: 40 min (per 5 mice dissection)

This section describes step by step the isolation of morulas and blastocysts from pregnant females.7 We detail below the standard approach to isolate each of these embryonic stages. While morulas are isolated from the oviduct, blastocysts are isolated from the uterine horn (Figure 1). To isolate morulas start from step 1. Skip to step 2 if you are isolating blastocysts.1.Isolation of morulas.a.Euthanize the pregnant female according to institutional guidelines.b.Lay the animal on its back on absorbent paper and soak it thoroughly with 70% ethanol.c.Perform an incision at the midline with surgical scissors.d.While holding the skin firmly above and below the incision, pull the skin towards the head and tail until the abdomen is exposed.e.Cut the peritoneum to reveal the gut.f.Push the coils of the gut out of the way to locate the horns of the uterus, the oviduct and the ovaries (Figures 1A and 1B).g.Lightly hold the upper end of a uterine horn with fine forceps and pull, exposing the mesometrium Figure 1C.h.Using additional closed forceps, poke a hole in the membrane close to the oviduct.i.While still holding the uterine horn, pull back the oviduct away from the ovary. Cut between the oviduct and ovary using fine scissors (Figure 1C).j.Reposition the forceps holding the uterine horn closer to the oviduct and cut the uterus.Note: Leave a small portion of the uterus attached to the oviduct. This is beneficial to limit structural damage when manipulating the oviduct.k.Transfer the oviduct and the attached segment of the uterus to a dish containing prewarmed 1.5 mL of M2 medium.l.Quickly scan the collected oviducts under the stereoscope and trim any excess of fat, or connective tissue that might have been carried through previous steps, as well as the uterine portion remaining from step 1.k.m.Transfer one oviduct at a time into one of the 35 μL droplets of M2 in the 35 x 10 mm dish prepared earlier.n.Locate the infundibulum.Note: Several oviducts and/or uteri can be pooled in the same dish. def cyto_expand(img, px):

cyto_shell = expand_labels(img, distance = px)

cyto_buffer = expand_labels(img, distance = 2)

cyto = np.subtract(cyto_shell, cyto_buffer)

return cyto

Expected outcomes

We setup this protocol to measure CDK activity levels in the developing mouse embryo while still be able to assign retrospectively CDK activity profiles over time to specific embryonic cellular lineages at a single cell level. Using this protocol, we isolated morulas and blastocysts expressing a CDK activity reporter which we then embedded in Matrigel and image for a period between 24 to 36 hours. This was followed by tissue fixation, immunostaining and image capture (Figure 3A). We successfully imaged CDK activity at the single cell level in ex vivo cultured embryos (Figure 3B). Finally, by assigning immunolabeled cells to their corresponding CDK activity profile it is feasible to reconstruct the fluctuations in CDK activity in embryonic and extraembryonic lineages over time (Figure 3C). The protocol described here can be used to measure additional fluorescent-based kinase translocation reporters. Limitations

Although we successfully measured the shuttling of fluorescent-based reporters in developing blastocysts using regular confocal microscopy, the long imaging time can be harmful for embryo development. We recommend the use of light sheet microscopy, a gentler imaging approach, if phototoxicity is observed in embryos.

Problem 2

Imaged embryos do not show signs of normal growth (the embryo should grow in size while doubling the number of cells every 16–20 hours), show reduced levels of cell division (blastomeres undergoing cleavage should be evident), or did not successfully initiate the cavitation process during live imaging (related to step 15, 16, and 17). This is likely a consequence of phototoxicity.

Potential solution

Revisit imaging parameters and consider the following:•Increase time interval between acquisition points.•Reduce laser intensity and exposure where possible.•Reduce number of embryos per Matrigel drop to reduce secondhand laser exposure.•Culture embryos in recently thawed media that is well equilibrated at 37°C and 5% CO2 for at least 30 min. Aliquot media as needed and keep thawed aliquots at 4°C for 2 weeks at most.•Consider setting up females that were not super ovulated as the increase in litter embryo size affects growth, especially in stages closer to implantation.

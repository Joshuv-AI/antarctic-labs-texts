Chitosan-coated PLGA microemulsion loaded with tannic acid against Escherichia coli in vitro and in vivo
Authors: Ya Tao, YinMo Huang, JieYu Shi, KaiYuan Li, RuoNan Bo, MingJiang Liu, JinGui Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe overuse of antibiotics has resulted in a surge of drug-resistant bacteria, making the pursuit of natural antimicrobials an urgent and significant trend. Encapsulation and nanoparticulation are effective ways to enhance the antibacterial properties of natural drugs. In this study, we encapsulated tannic acid (TA) with chitosan (CS) and poly (lactide-co-glycolide) (PLGA) using the emulsion-solvent evaporation method to enhance the antimicrobial effect of TA. We prepared a bilayer membrane spherical nanoemulsion of TA-PLGA-CS (TPC) with uniform size of 559.87 ± 1.16 nm, and zeta potential of 59.53 ± 1.07 mV. TPC could be stably stored for 90 days at 4°C without affecting the properties of the emulsion, and the minimum bactericidal concentration against four strains of Escherichia coli (E. coli) remained unchanged for 60 d. The results indicated that TPC enhanced the inhibitory effect of TA against E. coli. Scanning electron microscope images revealed that TPC treatment caused damage to the bacterial cell membrane. In addition, in vivo experiments indicated that TPC exhibited a superior therapeutic effect on artificial colibacillosis in chickens infested with Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli, as evidenced by the changes in body weight and a reduction bacterial load in heart. Furthermore, TPC reversed the down-regulation of catalase, glutathione peroxidase1 (GPX1), and GPX7 gene expression levels in intestinal tissues. Compared to the model group, TPC treatment elevated serum glutathione peroxidase activities and lowered myeloperoxidase and lactate dehydrogenase levels, offering antioxidant protection that was slightly better than that of doxycycline hydrochlorid group. In summary, we prepared a novel TA antimicrobial preparation with significant antioxidant potential and inhibitory effect against E. coli both in vitro and in vivo.Key words: tannic acid, E. coli, microemulsion, Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli

INTRODUCTION

Chicken colibacillosis is a disease characterized by various intestinal and extraintestinal infections in chickens caused by Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC). The clinical manifestations of chicken colibacillosis are complex, with common symptoms including perihepatitis, balloon inflammation, pericarditis, peritonitis, salpingitis, colonic granuloma, omphalitis, cellulitis and osteomyelitis (Kathayat et al., 2021). Currently, colibacillosis is still threatening the health of humans and livestock in China and worldwide (Hu et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2022; Cox et al., 2022). Antibiotics are typically usually used to treat colibacillosis, however, frequent antibiotic use has led to the problem of resistance. Over-reliance on antibiotics not only accelerates the rise of multidrug-resistant (MDR) Escherichia coli (E. coli), but also allows MDR E. coli to spread into the wild via avian scavengers (Guitart-Matas et al., 2024). Therefore, it is necessary to find other natural drugs to replace antibiotics to resist the invasion of pathogenic E. coli.

Tannic acid (TA) is a natural phenolic compound, soluble in water and ethanol, commonly extracted from gallnut or other Chinese herbs (A Youness et al., 2021). TA exhibits potent antibacterial, antiviral, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties, and is also capable of inhibiting fibrotic lesions (Chu et al., 2016; A Youness et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021, 2023; Greiffer et al., 2022; Haapakoski et al., 2023). Adding proper amount of TA in feed is beneficial to the health of broilers (Tong et al., 2022). Therefore, TA have the potential to replace antibiotics in the treatment of bacterial diseases. However, compared to antibiotics, the antibacterial effect of TA is relatively limited, and it exhibits anti-nutritional properties at high concentrations. Additionally, TA appears as a brownish-yellow powder with unstable chemical properties, requiring storage away from light. To enhance the antibacterial effect of TA and improve its bioavailability, we developed a novel TA preparation.

The choice of formulation materials is in favor of natural active ingredients or materials with better biocompatibility. Chitosan (CS), a natural biopolymer present in the cell walls of various organisms including fungi, exhibits robust chemical reactivity (Zaiki et al., 2023) and demonstrates notable antibacterial properties (Haktaniyan and Bradley, 2022). Due to its good biodegradability, CS can be absorbed by organisms and forms chito-oligosaccharides with various biological activities when degraded, which are non-immunogenic and do not accumulate in the body (Mohebichamkhorami et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2023). Poly (lactide-co-glycolide) (PLGA) is a pharmaceutical excipient recognized by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) with good biocompatibility and biodegradability for the development of nano-preparations (Ramot et al., 2016; Su et al., 2022; Minnelli et al., 2023). CS contains rich amino groups that can bind to carboxyl group of PLGA through coupling (Wang et al., 2010). At present, TA is often used as a material to modify other drugs, and there is less research on its own as the core of material packaging. To enhance the therapeutic effect of TA against E. coli, we innovatively encapsulated TA with PLGA and added a CS shell, creating a new microemulsion formulation (TPC).

In this study, TPC was prepared using emulsification solvent evaporation method. The inhibitory effect of TPC on pathogenic E. coli was investigated through in vitro and in vivo experiments, providing references for the clinical use of antimicrobial microemulsion as alternatives to antibiotics in the treatment of bacterial diseases.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Bacterial Strains and Chemical Reagents

E. coli O78 was preserved in the laboratory. Chicken-derived E. coli 0627J2-2 and E. coli AHM5C23 were obtained from Dr. Yanhong Wang, college of Veterinary Medicine, Yangzhou University, China. E. coli ATCC 25922 was kept in our laboratory. The above 4 E. coli strains were stored at -80 °C in the refrigerator. The strains were picked into Luria-Bertani (LB) broth (Hopebio-Technology Co. Ltd.) under shaking at 37 °C, 150 r/min for about 12 h to achieve logarithmic growth phases and were used in subsequent experiments. Mueller-Hinton (MH) broth was purchased from Hopebio-Technology Co. Ltd. (Qingdao, China).

TA was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. PLGA was purchased bought from Shandong Academy of Pharmaceutical Sciences. CS (20-100mPa·s) was purchased from TCI Development Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Poloxamer 188 (F-68) was purchased from Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Preparation of TPC

The TPC microemulsion was created using the emulsion-solvent evaporation method (ESEM) (Zhang et al., 2011). The overall preparation process is shown in Figure 1. 100 mg TA was dissolved in 0.2 mL ultrapure water, which served as the internal aqueous phase. 50 mg PLGA was dissolved in 1 mL of acetone, which served as the oil phase. Then adding the internal aqueous phase slowly into the oil phase and sonicating in an ice bath (power 130 W, 2 min) for obtaining a stable system in water-in-oil colostrum. 0.8% F68 and 5 mg/mL CS were dissolved in ultrapure water, which served as the external aqueous phase. To obtain a stable oil-in-water emulsion, the colostrum was dissolved in the external aqueous phase and ultrasonicated (power 130 W, 2 min). To remove residual acetone, the emulsion is stirred at room temperature for 4 h. Finally, the microemulsion was placed in an EP tube and stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C. The blank microemulsion (BPC) were similarly prepared and stored.

Characterization

The particle size, polydispersity index (PDI) and zeta potential of the TPC were determined by a Nano ZS90 Zeta sizer (Malvern, UK) with a dynamic light scattering (DLS) technique at room temperature. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Royal Philips, Netherlands) was used to determine the surface morphology of TPC.

TPC and BPC were kept at 4 °C and room temperature for 90 days to be observed any phase separation and determined the particle size, PDI and zeta potential distribution.

In Vitro Antibacterial Assay

Minimum Bactericidal Concentration (MBC). The MBC values of TA, TPC and mechanistically defined chemical mixtures (MDCM) for E. coli ATCC 25922, E. coli O78, E. coli AHM5C23 and E. coli 0627J2-2 were determined using the micro-broth dilution method in 96-well plates and the drop plate method respectively. Using a sterile 96-well plate, a 2-fold dilution of MH medium and the samples were added to each well, and then 100 µL of bacterial solution pre-cultured to logarithmic growth stage and diluted to 1.0 × 106 CFU/mL was added to the diluted wells. Each 96-well plate was set with MH negative control, drug negative control and bacterial solution positive control. After incubation at 37 °C for 18∼24 h, 3 μL of bacterial solution from each well of 96-well plate was sucked into LB agar medium, and the medium was incubated at 37 °C for 18∼24 h to observe the bacterial growth.

Growth of E. coli. The effects of different concentrations of TPC on the growth of E. coli ATCC 25922, E. coli O78, E. coli AHM5C23 and E. coli 0627J2-2, were determined separately over a 24 h period. These strains were cultured to logarithmic growth period, and the bacterial fluid positive control, 1/4 MBC, 1/2 MBC, 1 MBC, and 2 MBC groups were set up in sterile 96-well plates. The bacterial fluid control group was added 100 µL of MH medium, and the other groups were added 100 µL of corresponding concentration of TPC, and then 100 µL of diluted bacterial fluid was added to each group, so that the concentration of bacterial fluid in each group was 1.0 × 106 CFU/mL, and the groups were incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. The results were obtained by using enzyme labelling instrument (Bio Tek, Shoreline, WA) at 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, and 24 h, respectively. The OD values of each group at 600 nm were read, and the time-OD growth curve was plotted.

Antibacterial Stability Assessments. TPC stored at 4 °C was sampled on day 0, 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49 and 60, and the MBC values of TPC on E. coli ATCC 25922, E. coli O78, E. coli AHM5C23 and E. coli 0627J2-2 were determined according to the method mentioned before.

Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM). 2 mL LB medium was added to the blank control group, and the corresponding concentration of TPC was added to 1/2 MBC group and 1 MBC group, so that all final volume of groups was 2 mL. Then 100 µL bacterial solution pre-cultured to logarithmic growth stage was added to each group and cultured at 37 °C for 4 h. Absorbing 100 µL of bacterial solution from each group and placing it on the round coverslip, then culturing at 37 °C for 1 h. The round coverslips with bacterial were added 2.5% glutaraldehyde and stored at 4 °C overnight away from light.

The samples were pre-treated on the second day. After cleaning, gradient concentration ethanol dehydration, drying, gluing and gold spraying, the samples were observed and photographed on the SEM (Gemini SEM 300, Carl Zeiss AG).

Atomic Force Microscope (AFM).E. coli O78 was pre-cultured to logarithmic growth stage at 37°C. Two 10 mL centrifuge tubes were used and 1 mL of pre-cultured bacterial solution was added to each tube, and then 4 mL of TPC diluted with MH was added to each tube so that the concentration of TPC in these two tubes was 0 and 1/2 MBC, respectively. After mixing, the tubes were incubated for 12 h at 37 °C. Then 1 mL of culture solution was centrifuged (3,000 rpm, 5 min) to discard the supernatant, and then cleaned with sterile PBS for 3 times, 1 mL of glutaraldehyde (2.5%) was added and fixed at 4 °C for 15 min, and then centrifuged (3,000 rpm, 5 min) to discard the supernatant. The bacteria were then washed three times with sterile ultrapure water and finally dispersed uniformly with 1 mL ultrapure water. Then a suitable amount of sample liquid was aspirated and spread evenly on the mica sheet, left to dry naturally and then photographed. Afterwards, the sample mica sheet was placed on the AFM sample stage, and the use of non-contact phase mode was selected to obtain the image. The probe used was an NCHR-10 probe with a resonance frequency of 320 kHz and a nominal spring constant of 42 N/m. The AFM image resolution was set to 256 × 256 pixels. TPC was also photographed under the same conditions.

In Vivo Experiment

Experiments Designs and Animal Treatment. The one-day-old green-shell laying hens (male) were purchased from Jiangsu Poultry Research Institute and housed in the animal house of the College of Veterinary Medicine, Yangzhou University, China. The feed contains no drugs or additives and was supplied by the Jiangsu Provincial Poultry Research Institute, China.

After weighing chicks from normal feeding to 13 d of age, they were randomly grouped by weight using SPSS statistics software. They were divided into 7 groups: blank control group (Con), E. coli O78 model group (Mod), TPC low, medium and high microemulsion groups (E. coli O78+ 40, 80 and 120 mg/kg TPC, TPC-L, TPC-M, TPC-H), mechanistically defined chemical mixtures group (E. coli O78+120 mg/kg TA and TPC-H group equal amount of BPC microemulsion mixture, MDCM), Dox control group (E. coli O78+5 mg/kg doxycycline hydrochloride, Dox), There were 15 chicks in each group and the experiment was started after group adaptation.

A single colony of E. coli O78 was incubated for 12 h and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes. The concentration of the bacterial solution was adjusted to 3 × 108 CFU/mL. 0.25 mL saline was injected into the pectoral muscle of each chick in the blank control group. And the remaining groups were injected with 0.25 mL of bacterial solution at the appropriate concentration. Drugs for each group were administered by gavage 6 h after injection of E. coli O78, once daily for 3 d and followed by 4 d of observation. The black control group received saline and the other groups received various drugs. The chickens were allowed to eat and drink freely throughout the experiment. The regulations of the Animal Ethics Association of Yangzhou University are followed in all animal experiments. The approved protocol number is 202209650.

The weight of the chickens in each group was recorded before injecting and sampling, and the weight gain was calculated.

Heart Bacterial Load. A total of 24 h after injection of the bacterial solution, 5 chicks in each group were randomly selected to have their heart tissue harvested under aseptic conditions to determine the bacterial load. The same part of tissue was cut into a 5 mL centrifuge tube, and the same amount of grinding beads and sterile PBS were added to the homogenizer for grinding, and then 100 µL of homogenate was absorbed and plated on LB solid medium. The bacterial colony was incubated at 37°C for 24 h for observation and counting.

Determination of Oxidative Stress Indices. Measurements of the activity of Glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), myeloperoxidase (MPO) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) in the serum were performed according to the instructions of the test kit (Nanjing JianCheng Bioengineering Institute, China).

Real-Time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction Analysis (RT-qPCR). Total RNA was extracted from duodenal and cecal using Trizol reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions. The concentrations and quality of the RNA were determined by the nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Wilmington, DE). The RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using Hiscript II QRT supermax (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) was applied to the RT-qPCR. The relative expressions of mRNA were calculated using the 2-△△Ct comparative method. Statistical Analysis

All experiment results are shown as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). All assay data in both the in vitro and in vivo studies were corroborated in at least 3 independent tests under the unchangeable conditions. The experimental data were analyzed using SSPS 25.0 statistic software. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with a Duncan multiple comparison test was used. Histograms and curves were created using GraphPad Prism 9.0.

RESULTS

Characterization of TPC

As shown in Figures 2A and 2B, the TPC particle size measured by the Malvin laser particle size analyzer was 559.87 ± 1.16 nm, the PDI was 0.218±0.08 and the zeta potential was 59.53±1.07 mV. Visually, TPC appeared as a light brown, uniform, milky liquid. TEM images showed that TPC particles were spherical with an apparent bilayer membrane structure (Fig. 2C).

Stability Analysis of BPC and TPC

When stored at room temperature, the particle size of both BPC and TPC decreased by more than 100 nm after 90 d, and the PDI exceeded 0.3. Additionally, the zeta potential of BPC and TPC at room temperature significantly decreased over time, indicating instability. However, BPC and TPC stored at 4°C showed no significant changes in particle size or zeta potential, remaining stable even after 90 d (Figure 3).

Effect of TPC on Oxidative Stress Response and Metabolism

According to the results of Figure 5, it is seen that GSH-Px activities in the Con group and other treatment groups were significantly higher than the Mod group (P < 0.05). DISCUSSION

Chicken colibacillosis is prevalent worldwide and has caused considerable economic losses to the poultry industry. For preventing and treating of chicken colibacillosis, vaccination and antibiotic therapy are the main conventional methods. However, the virulence genes of pathogenic E. coli are diverse, and the number of drug-resistant strains is increasing. Moreover, poultry infected with other diseases often develop secondary colibacillosis(Azam et al., 2019; Christensen et al., 2021; Fancher et al., 2021; Usman et al., 2022). It is possible that Avian Pathogenic E. coli (APEC) can cause infections in humans (Geurtsen et al., 2022). Hence, it is urgent to find new drugs and methods for treating chicken colibacillosis beyond conventional methods. TA contains many hydroxyl groups (-OH) and carboxyl groups (-COO), which confer active molecular properties and good antimicrobial effects. These properties have garnered considerable interest in the field of materials science in recent years (Kaczmarek et al., 2020; Guimarães et al., 2023). TA has been used as feed additives in previous studies due to its health benefits for livestock and poultry when included in appropriate amounts. It has been shown to increase the semi-eviscerated rate (Tong et al., 2022) and inhibit E. coli (Kaczmarek, 2020). However, the antimicrobial effect of TA is not yet comparable to that of antibiotics. Additionally, TA is less stable, less bioavailable, and more sensitive to light, necessitating storage in the dark (Chen et al., 2019). To enhance its antibacterial effectiveness and bioavailability, we propose encapsulating TA to develop a new oral formulation.

TPC microemulsion with uniform shape and round shape was successfully prepared. However, compared with other TA nano-preparations, our microemulsion particles are larger. This issue may be addressed by adjusting the material proportions, which requires further optimization and refinement in future studies (Wang et al., 2010; Zhan et al., 2017). Stability tests indicate that the prepared microemulsion has a longer storage time at 4°C compared to room temperature. After three months of storage at 4°C, both BPC and TPC showed minimal changes in particle size and potential, with the PDI remaining within 0.3. At room temperature, the average particle size of both BPC and TPC decreased, and the potential decreased significantly, indicating degradation of the microemulsion (Tao et al., 2022). The color changes of microemulsion corresponded to the changes of particle size and potential. In the subsequent antibacterial test, there is no significant change in the MBC of the microemulsion which has been stored at 4°C for two months against the four tested strains, particularly E. coli O78. This indicates that TPC is relatively stable at 4°C and can be stored for at least two months.

The TPC exhibited potent bactericidal activity against E. coli in vitro, achieving a MBC that was remarkably lower, specifically 1/160, than that of TA, when tested against E. coli O78. A concentration of 15.625 µg/mL TPC was found to inhibit the growth of four strains of E. coli, resulting in the stagnation of their growth. SEM and AFM analyses showed that TPC is mainly attached and destroyed the membrane, which is consistent with recent studies by other researchers(Slobodníková et al., 2016; Olchowik-Grabarek et al., 2022). TPC adhered to the membrane, and the slowly released TA forms coordination compounds with several metal ions present in the growth medium. This interaction hinders the bacteria's ability to synthesize essential metabolic enzymes and absorb nutrients (Štumpf et al., 2020).

The chicken colibacillosis model established in this study by intramuscular injection of APEC is a systemic infection disease model. The main lesions included marked fibrotic exudates in the heart and the development of pericarditis. In addition, the liver exhibited swelling, brittleness, and perihepatitis, while intestinal tissues showed inflammatory cell infiltration and structural changes in the intestinal crypts (Kromann and Jensen, 2022). The TPC-treated group exhibited a significant reduction in APEC infestation within the chicken hearts, evidenced by a decreased bacterial load compared to the Mod group within a 24 h period, demonstrating its efficacy in mitigating APEC-induced colibacillosis in chickens. Inflammatory cell infiltration between myocardial tissues in heart tissue sections was more severe in the Mod group compared to the Con group, and this was attenuated in the TPC-treated group. These results further suggested that TPC resisted APEC attacks on the chicken heart. The anatomically derived cosmetic lesions of the heart in each group also correspond to the above results.

Studies have shown that E. coli infection of chicks leads to oxidative stress and negatively affects their weight gain (da Rosa et al., 2020). Bacterial infections cause an excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the organism, disrupting cellular regulatory mechanisms (Morgan et al., 2022). Presently, the more critical enzymatic cellular antioxidants are SODs and CAT, and HMOX-1 stands as an important antioxidant gene (Grzegorzewska et al., 2020). GPX is involved in the scavenging of hydrogen peroxide, while CYP1A1 and CYP3A4 are metabolizing enzymes involved in drug metabolism. In this study, changes in the expression levels of antioxidant-related genes in chicken intestinal tissues suggested the regulatory role of TPC in organisms infected with APEC. Treatment with TPC upregulated the expression levels of SOD-2, CAT, GPX1, and GPX7 in intestinal tissues. TPC also reversed the up-regulated expression of HMOX-1, CYP1A1and CYP3A4 in duodenal tissues after infestation with APEC. Additionally, the up-regulation of CYP3A4 expression levels in the cecum tissues in each treatment group may imply that the drug is metabolized in the cecum.

The antioxidant properties of TPC were also revealed by the detection of related enzymes in serum. Infection with APEC may increase lipid peroxidation, causing oxidative damage to the organism (da Rosa et al., 2019). GSH-Px is an important peroxidase that scavenges lipid peroxides. The serum level of GSH-Px was significantly higher in the TPC-treated group as compared to the Mod group. The reduction in MPO levels in the TPC-treated group compared to the Mod group also suggested that TPC may attenuate the inflammation caused by oxidative stress due to APEC (Xu et al., 2023). Elevated levels of LDH in the serum indicate tissue damage, as glycolytic reactions increase when the organism is weakened by infectious disease or when local tissues are ischemic and hypoxic. LDH participates in the glycolysis process by catalyzing the reductive oxidation reaction between pyruvate and lactate (Chen et al., 2023). Compared to the Con group, the Mod group showed significantly higher level of LDH, whereas the TPC-H group showed significantly lower level of LDH compared to the Mod group. This suggested that TPC can regulate the development of the disease and regulate the metabolism of the organism (Kromann and Jensen, 2022).

CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have successfully synthesized a novel TA preparation that exhibits a robust in vitro bacteriostatic effect, enhancing the antibacterial activity of TA by a remarkable 160-fold increase. Additionally, the novel preparation has the potential for antioxidant and metabolic regulation in the body. Therefore, it provides a new medication possibility for the treatment of colibacillosis in chickens.

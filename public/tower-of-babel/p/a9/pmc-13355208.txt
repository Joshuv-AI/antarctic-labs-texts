Multidimensional characterization of red sour soup with different substrates: integrating sensory, physicochemical, bioactive, and safety attributes
Authors: Liu Tang, Guoyan Wang, Ailin Chen, Quanye Wu, Likang Qin, Na Liu, Haiying Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRed sour soup (RSS) is a characteristic Chinese fermented food. This study analyzed tomato-based RSS (T-RSS), tomato-chili-based RSS (M-RSS), and tomato-chili-rice-acid based RSS (R-RSS) for sensory, physicochemical, bioactive, and safety properties. Among the samples, M-RSS exhibited significantly higher levels of polyphenol (161.89 mg GAE/100 g) and total flavonoid (150.85 mg RE/100 g DW). Capsaicinoids were exclusively found in M-RSS and R-RSS, with nordihydrocapsaicin showing the highest level at 1125.61 μg/g. In most samples, lycopene (0.125–3.560 μg/g) was the main carotenoid and lactic acid the primary organic acid (14–54%). Multivariate analysis revealed that capsorubin, zeaxanthin, cryptoxanthin, and lutein were significantly correlated with both RSS redness (a* value, p < 0.05) and specific organic acids, while capsaicin and dihydrocapsaicin were positively associated with polyphenols, vitamin C, tartaric/oxalic acids, and total flavonoids. This study characterized the quality profiles of RSS with different substrates, providing a reference for its quality evaluation and targeted functional improvement.Keywords: Red sour soup, Quality characteristics, Bioactive substance, Safety quality

Introduction

Red sour soup (RSS) is a distinctive fermented condiment in China. Based on fermentation substrates, RSS can be divided into three mainstream types, including tomato based RSS (T-RSS), tomato-chili based RSS (M-RSS), and tomato-chili-rice acid based RSS (R-RSS). Difference in fermentation substrate is an important factor causing discrepancies in the physicochemical properties, flavor profiles, bioactive constituents and quality attributes of final RSS products.

Existing studies have explored the quality characteristics of single types of RSS. In physicochemical properties, compared with chili based RSS, T-RSS exhibits higher acidity and amino acid nitrogen content (Lin et al., 2022), but lower reducing sugar content (Liu et al., 2025). According to Zheng (Zheng et al., 2024), sugar addition in T-RSS promotes the accumulation of lactic acid and enhances acidity. In flavor profile, substrate formulas significantly affect the flavor fingerprint of RSS. T-RSS shows a higher abundance of terpenoid compounds, while tomato-chili mixed RSS exhibits higher abundances of esters, acids, ketones, and aldehydes (Lin et al., 2022), indicating that tomato-chili co-fermentation contributes to the accumulation of flavor compounds and promotes the formation of diverse flavors. Organic acids are critical taste-active substances that determine the flavor quality of RSS, and their profiles are substrate-dependent. Lactic acid serves as the dominant organic acid in RSS, and its accumulation is regulated by substrate composition and microbial metabolic gene expression. Lactic acid content is significantly higher in fermentation systems dominated by chili peppers than in those based on tomato or tomato-chili mixtures (Zhang et al., 2025). The up-regulation of L-lactate dehydrogenase gene in Lacticaseibacillus paracasei further promotes lactic acid biosynthesis during fermentation (Liu, Qin, Lu, et al., 2021; Liu, Qin, & Miao, 2021). In addition, malic acid accumulation differs significantly between natural and inoculated tomato-chili composite fermentation systems (Liu, Hu, et al., 2023; Liu, Li, et al., 2023; Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023). Regarding bioactive components, during the fermentation of tomato-chili based RSS, the contents of total phenols and vitamin C exhibit an initial increase followed by a subsequent decrease (Liu, Hu, et al., 2023; Liu, Li, et al., 2023; Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023), which is attributed to non-enzymatic browning of polyphenols and oxidative degradation of vitamin C. Additionally, fermentation processing effectively improves the accumulation of total phenols and carotenoids in tomato-chili based RSS (Li et al., 2022). These studies reveal the effects of fermentation raw materials on the physicochemical properties, flavor quality, and bioactive components of RSS, providing theoretical support for the quality improvement of fermented sour soup. Nevertheless, relevant investigations have mainly focused on individual types of RSS, particularly T-RSS and M-RSS, and a comparison of the three main types (T-RSS, M-RSS, and R-RSS) produced by enterprises under standardized and scaled traditional fermentation conditions remains lacking. The sensory and physicochemical characteristics, bioactive compound profiles, and, especially the food safety risks (including nitrite, biogenic amines, preservative residues, and heavy metals) have not been comprehensively characterized.

In this study, tomato based RSS (T-RSS), tomato-chili based RSS (M-RSS), and tomato-chili-rice acid based RSS (R-RSS) were subjected to a comprehensive analysis of their sensory, physicochemical, bioactive, and safety attributes, in order to investigate the quality characteristics of RSS prepared with different substrate formulations under actual industrial-scale traditional fermentation conditions and to provide data support for the standardized production and quality improvement of RSS.

Material and methods

Samples and chemicals

A total of 26 commercially available RSS samples from different manufacturers of Guizhou were collected and investigated. After screening according to fermentation process, raw material varieties and fermentation time, nine representative samples were finally selected. All the nine samples were prepared via traditional natural fermentation for 6 months, using Erjingtiao chili and local small tomatoes (Maolaguo) as raw materials. MC, GXY, and LHZ are tomato based RSS (T-RSS); YJS, LTG, and JZY are tomato-chili based RSS (M-RSS); MWJ, OYM, and WMS are tomato-chili-rice acid based RSS (R-RSS). All fermentation raw materials were washed, chopped, ground into a paste, and set aside for subsequent use. According to the manufacturers, M-RSS was prepared by mixing tomatoes and chili peppers at a ratio of 5:1, placing the mixture in a fermentation jar, and conducting natural anaerobic fermentation at room temperature for 6 months. T-RSS followed the same procedure as M-RSS but used only tomatoes as the primary raw material without adding chili peppers. For R-RSS preparation, tomatoes, chili peppers, and the rice sour soup were mixed at a ratio of 5:1:1, placed in a jar, and fermented naturally under anaerobic conditions at room temperature for 6 months.

All standards used in the experiment are of analytical purity. Gallic acid, rutin, and organic acid standards were obtained from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., while capsaicinoids and carotenoids standards were sourced from Beijing Tanmo Quality Inspection Technology Co., Ltd. and Shanghai Sigma-Aldrich High-Tech Co., Ltd., respectively.

Sensory evaluation and color measurement

The sensory evaluation was organized according to Chinese Sensory Analysis-Guide for Food Sensory Quality Control (GB/T 29605–2013), descriptive analysis method. Ten panelists with food sensory evaluation experience and relevant professional knowledge were recruited for the sensory assessment. All panelists were non-smokers, had no history of allergies to tomatoes, chili peppers or fermented foods, and possessed normal taste and olfactory functions. Prior to the formal evaluation, unified professional training was provided to familiarize the panelists with the evaluation indexes, scoring criteria and key flavor characteristics. Sensory scoring was performed from five dimensions: color, aroma, taste, purity, and texture, with a full score of 20 points for each index. During the evaluation, panelists were required to rinse their mouths thoroughly several times after assessing each sample before proceeding to the next one. The scoring criterion was provided in the supplementary material (Table S1). The L*, a*, and b* values of the samples were detected by a portable precision colorimeter (WSC-3B, INESA Physics and Optics Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai).

Determination of physicochemical properties

The pH and soluble solids content were measured using a handheld pH meter (PHS-3C, INESA Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai) and a digital refractometer (HP-TD32, Ceyou Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen), respectively. The determination of total acid (TA) followed the guidelines of China's National Standards for Food Safety in GB 12456–2021. 25 g of sample was weighed, diluted to 250 mL with water in a volumetric flask, mixed, and filtered. The resulting filtrate was titrated with 0.1 mol/L sodium hydroxide solution. The detection of amino acid nitrogen (AAN) followed GB 5009.235–2016. 5.0 g of the sample was weighed, diluted to 100 mL in a volumetric flask, mixed, and filtered. Then, 20.0 mL of the filtrate was pipetted, diluted with 60 mL of water, and titrated with 0.050 mol/L Sodium hydroxide solution to pH 8.2 under magnetic stirring. After adding 10.0 mL of formaldehyde solution, the mixture was further titrated to pH 9.2, and the volume of consumed NaOH was recorded. A blank test was conducted in parallel to correct and calculate the amino acid nitrogen content. The content of reducing sugars was assessed using the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid (DNS) colorimetry method. 10.0 g of the sample was weighed and transferred into a 250 mL volumetric flask. Then, 3 mL of potassium ferrocyanide solution and 3 mL of zinc acetate solution were added successively. The mixture was shaken well, diluted to the calibration mark, allowed to stand for a while, and then filtered. The absorbance of the filtrate was measured at 540 nm.

Detection of bioactive substances

Organic acids (oxalic acid, tartaric acid, formic acid, acetic acid, propionic acid, L-malic acid, D-malic acid, lactic acid, citric acid, and succinic acid) in RSS were detected by HPLC (1260, Agilent Technologies Inc., USA) according to Tian's (Tian, 2020) method, with some modifications. A sample weighing 2.5000 g (accurate to 0.0001 g) was combined with 0.1 g of activated carbon and diluted with ultra-pure water to a final volume of 25 mL. The mixture was then centrifuged at 4 °C and 8000 r/min for 10 min. Subsequently, the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane before injection. The determination conditions were as follows: mobile phase consisting of 0.02 mol/L potassium dihydrogen phosphate and methanol in a ratio of 95:5; flow rate set at 0.8 mL/min; column temperature maintained at 35 °C; detection wavelength at 210 nm; and injection volume of 10 μL.

Polyphenol and vitamin C were detected according to GB/T 8313–2018 and GB 5009.86–2016, respectively. The content of total flavonoid was measured according to Dewanto's method (Dewanto et al., 2002), with some adjustments. The capsaicinoids were detected by LC-2040C (Shimadzu, Japan) chromatographic instrument with a C18 column (4.6 mm × 250 mm, 5 μm). To begin, 1.0 g of the material was ground with 5 mL of methanol before being ultrasonically analyzed at 35 °C for 30 min. The ultrasonic sample was then filtered and the filtrate measured. The determination settings were as follows: column temperature (35 °C), detection wavelength (280 nm), flow rate (1.0 mL/min), and injection volume (10 μL). The mobile phase was methanol (A) and water (B), and the elution gradient was as follows: 0–20 min, (A) 70%; 20–20.5 min, (A) 70% → 90%; 20.5–26.5 min, (A) 90%; 26.5–27 min, (A) 90% → 70%; 27–32 min, (A) 70%. The determination of carotenoids followed Irakli and Melendez-Martinez's (Irakli et al., 2011; Meléndez-Martínez et al., 2010) methods.

Measurement of safety indicators

The determination of nitrite followed GB 5009.33-2016. Biogenic amines were tested by HPLC, with the mobile phase consisting of water (A) and acetonitrile (B). The elution gradient was as follows: 0–4 min, A: B = 35:65; 5–19 min, A: B = 30:70; 20–24 min, A: B = 0:100; 25–30 min, A: B = 35:65. The plumbum, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, and chromium were detected referring to GB 5009.12-2017, GB 5009.15-2023, GB 5009.17-2021, GB 5009.11-2014 and GB 5009.123-2023, respectively. The determination of potassium sorbate and sodium benzoate was carried out according to GB 5009.28-2016. The detection of sodium dehydroacetate was referred to GB 5009.121-2016. The detection of total bacterial colonies was conducted by GB 4789.2-2022, and the detection of yeast and mold followed GB 4789.15-2016. The detection of coliforms, Salmonella, Staphylococcus aureus, and Shigella was performed in line with GB 4789.3-2016, GB 4789.4-2016, GB 4789.10-2016, and GB 4789.5-2012, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Each sample was analyzed in three analytical replicates, with results expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Data processing and analysis were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 27. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) combined with Duncan's multiple range test was used to analyze significant differences among groups, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The figures were drawn by Origin 2022 and CHIPLOT, accessible at https://www.chiplot.online/.

Results and discussion

Evaluation of sensory qualities and color differences

Nine samples were red in color, free from impurities, and presented a semi-solid state with uniform fineness and moderate viscosity. They met the requirements of DBS52/056-2021 and had good flavor and taste. The L*, a*, and b* values were determined to further analyze the color differences among samples (Table 1). As important indicators for evaluating food color, the L*, a*, and b* values represent lightness, red-green hue, and yellow-blue hue, respectively. LTG (M-RSS) and OYM (R-RSS) exhibited higher L* values, presenting a brighter color. JZY (M-RSS) had the highest a* value and showed a redder appearance, while T-RSS samples possessed higher b* values, indicating they were yellower compared with the other samples. The color of RSS is generally determined by natural pigments (such as carotenoids) present in raw materials. Pigment degradation leads to color darkening. Meanwhile, polyphenol oxidation and vitamin C degradation in RSS also result in color changes (Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023). Therefore, the color differences among samples may be related to the contents of bioactive substances such as pigments, polyphenols, and vitamin C. The sensory evaluation results were shown in Fig. 1(b). Among the three tested sample groups, T-RSS showed higher yellowness values than M-RSS and R-RSS, whereas no obvious consistent trends in lightness and redness were observed.

Analysis of physicochemical properties

Five physicochemical parameters of pH, total acid, reducing sugar, soluble solid, and amino acid nitrogen were determined in samples (Table 2). The pH value and total acid content are the primary factors that govern the sour taste of RSS (Liu, Hu, et al., 2023; Liu, Li, et al., 2023; Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023). The samples displayed a similar pH range from 3.07 to 3.77, which aligns closely with the pH value distribution range (3.10 to 3.40) of RSS samples fermented by Li (Li et al., 2021). M-RSS samples exhibited total acid contents of 3.65% (LTG), 2.63% (JZY), and 2.51% (YJS), which were significantly higher than those of the T-RSS and R-RSS samples. Acids in RSS are produced through the metabolism of lactic acid bacteria (LAB), which convert sugars derived from starch into lactic acid and other organic acids, thereby modifying the total acid content of the products (Lin et al., 2020). Co-fermentation of tomatoes and chili peppers provides abundant fermentable carbohydrate sources, which facilitates acid production by LAB. In R-RSS samples, the addition of rice sour soup alters the initial microbial composition of the system. Yeasts may compete with LAB for nutrients (Liu, Qin, Lu, et al., 2021; Liu, Qin, & Miao, 2021), thereby reducing acid production by LAB. The content of reducing sugar is an important quality indicator of fermented foods (Zhao et al., 2024), reflecting the degree of microbial utilization of sugars in raw materials and the production of beneficial metabolites. Meanwhile, a certain amount of reducing sugar also provides special flavor to the products. The contents of reducing sugar and soluble solids in the samples ranged from 0.08% to 0.85% and 4.57% to 24.50%, respectively. M-RSS samples (JZY and YJS) exhibited higher reducing sugar contents, followed by WMS (R-RSS), which may be related to the microbial metabolism in the fermentation system. Amino acid nitrogen is considered to be the primary contributor to umami and the flavorful essence in fermented vegetables. WMS (R-RSS) and M-RSS samples had higher concentrations of amino acid nitrogen than other samples.

Assessment of bioactive substances

Organic acids

Organic acids can provide a unique sour taste for RSS, and they are also associated with cheesy, sweet, vegetable oil, citrus, butter, and other flavor attributes (Kang et al., 2007). Ten kinds of organic acids, including lactic acid, D-malic acid, propionic acid, succinic acid, citric acid, acetic acid, L-malic acid, formic acid, tartaric acid and oxalic acid, were detected in RSS. Lactic acid was the most abundant organic acid in most samples, which was consistent with Fei's (Feixia et al., 2024) study. The proportion of lactic acid in the total organic acids reached a maximum of 54% and a minimum of 13% (Fig. 2(a)). OYM (R-RSS) possessed the highest lactic acid content of 28.16 g/kg, which was 1.05 to 5.16 times higher than that of other samples. Rice sour soup is naturally rich in L-lactic acid (Liu et al., 2020). As OYM used rice sour soup as one of its fermentation substrates, exogenous lactic acid may be directly introduced. Meanwhile, the lactic acid bacteria in rice sour soup can further enhance lactic acid synthesis during fermentation, thereby promoting the accumulation of lactic acid in OYM. The RSS samples also exhibited high concentrations of succinic acid, particularly in JZY and YJS, where the succinic acid content reached 63.26 g/kg and 15.63 g/kg, respectively. During the initial stage of fermentation, succinic acid is primarily produced through substrate-level phosphorylation of succinyl-CoA within the TCA cycle. In the later stage, under anaerobic conditions, succinic acid is generated via the reductive branch of the TCA cycle (Tsuji et al., 2013). RSS is rich in organic acids, which, in addition to providing a delightful flavor, can inhibit spoilage and pathogenic microorganisms (Guo et al., 2023), regulate metabolism (Chen et al., 2023), and promote physical health. There was no obvious substrate-based regularity in the composition and content of organic acids among the three types of samples. It is speculated that organic acids in RSS are mainly affected by the microbial community structure within the fermentation system. However, since the samples were collected from different production environments, this inference still requires further verification under standardized and controlled conditions to more clearly distinguish the respective effects of substrate and micro-environmental variations (e.g., microbial factors).

Polyphenols, total flavonoids and vitamin C

Polyphenols, flavonoids, and vitamin C are natural active substances present in plants, exhibiting effects such as antioxidation, anti-inflammatory, anti-aging, anti-diabetic, and anti-obesity properties (Lin & Xiao, 2024; Xiao et al., 2023). The samples exhibited significant variation in polyphenol content (p < 0.05, Fig. 3(a)), with M-RSS samples showing notably higher levels. Specifically, the YJS, JZY, and LTG reached 161.89, 135.72, and 98.24 mg GAE/100 g, respectively. The addition of chili peppers enriches the polyphenol content in the fermentation raw materials of M-RSS. Meanwhile, factors such as maturity of raw materials (Yap et al., 2023) and the varying abilities of different Lactobacillus fermentum strains to produce enzymes like phenolic acid decarboxylase, phenol reductase, and dehydrogenase, can further influence the release of bound polyphenols from plant raw materials (Muñoz et al., 2017), leading to variations in polyphenol content among RSS samples. Additionally, under various processing techniques and storage conditions, the variations in pH, temperature, enzyme activity, oxygen exposure, and light within the RSS system can all cause the degradation of polyphenols (through epimerization or oxidation) (Zhang et al., 2022), also resulting in differences in polyphenol content. The total flavonoid contents of M-RSS samples were relatively higher, ranging from 125.32 to 150.85 mg RE/100 g DW, followed by R-RSS samples from 63.96 to 138.76 mg RE/100 g DW. The addition of rice sour soup in R-RSS samples enriched the microbial community and diversified the enzymatic composition within the fermentation system, which may cause partial flavonoids to be further metabolized into non-flavonoid products (Huang et al., 2024). Koenig (Koenig et al., 2023) also confirmed that flavonoids in citrus extracts subjected to acid hydrolysis and fermentation can be further transformed, indicating that differences in fermentation strategies significantly affect the content and existing forms of flavonoids in the final products. Vitamin C is a natural antioxidant and one of the most important nutrients in RSS. The highest concentration of vitamin C, measured in YJS (M-RSS), was 33.81 mg/100 g, which is significantly higher than that of the RSS fermented by Liu of 2.97 mg/100 g (Liu, Hu, et al., 2023; Liu, Li, et al., 2023; Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023), exhibiting the high antioxidant property of YJS.

In the nine RSS samples, M-RSS exhibited higher contents of polyphenol and total flavonoid, followed by R-RSS. This indicates that chili incorporation may contribute to elevated phenolic and flavonoid concentrations in RSS. Moreover, the three types of RSS were produced using the same tomato and chili pepper varieties, suggesting that the differences in these bioactive substances among samples may be influenced by raw material maturity and microbial community. Therefore, selecting high-quality raw material varieties, optimizing the raw material proportion, and regulating fermentation substrate composition are of great significance for enhancing the concentration of bioactive substances and improving the nutritional value of RSS.

Capsaicinoids

Capsaicinoids are the source of intense pungency in chili, and they are catalyzed and formed by vanillyl amine, which is derived from the phenylalanine pathway, and 8-methyl-6-decenoyl, which is derived from the branched fatty acid pathway, under the action of capsaicin synthase (CS) (Mazourek et al., 2009). In RSS, capsaicinoids can impart spicy taste. Capsaicin, dihydrocapsaicin, and nordihydrocapsaicin were detected in all samples except for T-RSS which was fermented solely with tomatoes (Fig. 3(b)). Capsaicin was the dominant capsaicinoid in the samples (except LTG) and played a dual role in RSS, serving both as its characteristic pungent flavor compound and as a key bioactive functional component. M-RSS samples exhibited higher capsaicin content at 160.40 μg/g (in YJS) and 78.61 μg/g (in JZY), followed by WMS (R-RSS) at 27.19 μg/g. This variation is primarily attributed to differences in growth environment and maturity of chili peppers, with genetic factors and metabolic pathways such as oxidation and conjugation playing crucial regulatory roles (Elkhedir et al., 2024). Microbial differences in the fermentation system can also affect capsaicin concentration. For instance, Bacillus licheniformis is capable of degrading capsaicin in chili juice (Cho et al., 2015). The content of dihydrocapsaicin in the samples ranged from 3.76 to 59.94 μg/g. According to previous studies, dihydrocapsaicin demonstrates antioxidant properties and can inhibit lipid and protein peroxidation, thereby protecting vascular endothelial cells (Ahuja et al., 2006). Notably, LTG from M-RSS exhibited the highest concentration of nordihydrocapsaicin, at 1125.61 μg/g. Nordihydrocapsaicin possesses a lower pungency than capsaicin and dihydrocapsaicin, and it elicits a mellow warming effect akin to the sensation of alcohol and exhibits a fruity, sweet, and spicy character (Krajewska, 1984). In general, M-RSS samples exhibited higher contents of capsaicinoids, while the fermentation of R-RSS samples introduced rice sour soup, resulting in a higher initial acidity in the fermentation system where capsaicinoids may become unstable and degrade (Si et al., 2014). Concurrently, the introduction of rice sour soup led to distinct microbial communities between the two systems, which may also contribute to the differences in capsaicinoid concentrations.

Carotenoids

The carotenoids present in RSS are primarily derived from tomatoes and peppers, functioning as natural pigments and exhibiting antioxidant properties (Jomova & Valko, 2013). A total of 7 carotenoids were found in RSS samples, including lycopene, capsorubin, β-carotene, α-carotene, cryptoxanthin, zeaxanthin, and lutein (Fig. 3(c)). Lycopene was the dominant carotenoid in samples and possessed the strongest antioxidant activity among the carotenoids. Among the nine samples, lycopene content in R-RSS samples varied considerably, reaching 3.560 μg/g in WMS while staying at a low level in MWJ. T-RSS samples exhibited relatively high lycopene levels (1.828–3.069 μg/g), and M-RSS samples showed relatively low values. T-RSS samples were fermented from tomatoes, resulting in generally high lycopene concentrations. Lycopene contains multiple conjugated double bonds and is highly sensitive to light, heat, and oxygen. It easily undergoes isomerization from all-trans to cis-isomers and subsequent irreversible oxidative degradation during processing and storage (Chen et al., 2009; Murakami et al., 2018). Accordingly, differences in oxygen concentration and temperature within the fermentation system may result in variations in lycopene content among samples. The large variation in lycopene among R-RSS samples may be attributed to complex microbial communities and chemical components introduced by rice sour soup. These factors could promote lycopene release via efficient matrix disruption or alter its degradation dynamics, leading to the observed differences. JZY (M-RSS) exhibited the highest content of capsorubin at 8.539 μg/g. Capsorubin is a lipid-soluble red κ-xanthophyll that is found in only a limited number of plants (Deng et al., 2024), with chili peppers being its primary source. Differences in capsorubin content among chili peppers from different plants and maturity stages may be the main cause of variations between samples. In addition to these two carotenoids, other carotenoids, including cryptoxanthin, β-carotene, α-carotene, zeaxanthin, and lutein,were identified in RSS, highlighting the diverse carotenoid composition of Guizhou RSS.

Analysis of safety qualities

Nitrite and biogenic amines

Nitrite is commonly found in fermented foods and it is recognized as a potential carcinogen. The nitrite concentration in the samples was significantly below the limit of 20 mg/kg (Fig. 3(d)), as stipulated in China's National Food Safety Standards for Fermented Vegetables in GB 2762-2022. The low nitrite level in samples is primarily due to the degradation under the effect of microorganisms, which is facilitated by the biological enzymes and chemical acids (Hu et al., 2024).

The formation of biogenic amines during food fermentation is inevitable, primarily arising from the decarboxylation of free amino acids by amino acid decarboxylases. Six kinds of biogenic amines were found at relatively low concentrations (Fig. 3(e)) in the RSS samples. Putrescine was the predominant biogenic amine, with its highest content reaching 32.31 mg/kg. Del Rio (Del Rio et al., 2019) reported that the minimum concentration of putrescine with cytotoxic effects is 881.50 mg/kg, indicating that the putrescine levels in RSS fall well within the safe range. Additionally, a low amount of cadaverine was present in all samples. Spermine and tryptamine were exclusively detected in the R-RSS samples (OYM and MWJ), at concentrations of 5.77 mg/kg and 3.71 mg/kg, respectively. Histamine and tyramine, which are considered highly toxic biogenic amines and often accumulate in aquatic products, reached maximum concentrations of 11.92 mg/kg and 22.30 mg/kg, respectively. According to Chinese National Standard (GB 2733-2015) and European Union regulations, the limits of histamine and tyramine in aquatic products are 200 mg/kg and 100 mg/kg, respectively. The contents of both amines were far below these regulatory limits and thus do not pose a threat to human health. The difference in biogenic amine content among samples was closely related to the amino acid level and microbial composition in the RSS system. Microorganisms producing amino acid decarboxylase such as Lactobacillus, Enterobacter, Pseudomonas, and Bacillus can promote the formation of biogenic amines (Wu et al., 2024), while microorganisms with amine dehydrogenation enzyme, amine oxidase and multi‑copper oxidase activity can degrade biogenic amines. Furthermore, the presence of antimicrobial compounds, such as polyphenols, introduces an additional control by suppressing the growth of biogenic amine-producing microorganisms (Cai et al., 2015; Tsafack & Tsopmo, 2022). In the future, precise control of biogenic amines in RSS can be achieved through the synergistic optimization of microbial strains, processing techniques, and raw materials, combined with dynamic monitoring and standardized construction, thereby ensuring product safety and shelf-life quality.

Preservatives and heavy metals

Three preservatives commonly utilized in the food industry (Sodium benzoate, Potassium sorbate, and Sodium dehydroacetate) were detected in RSS samples. These preservatives were found in 7 samples with a low concentration (Table S2). According to Chinese National Standard for Food Safety (GB 2760-2024), the maximum permitted levels for benzoic acid and its sodium salt, sorbic acid and its potassium salt, and dehydroacetic acid and its sodium salt in complex seasonings are 1.0 g/kg, 1.0 g/kg, and 0.5 g/kg, respectively. Notably, the concentrations of the three preservatives in the samples were well below these stipulated limits.

The concentrations of plumbum (Pb), arsenic (As), cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), and mercury in the samples were tested. Pb, As, and mercury were not found in any sample (Table S3). The concentrations of Cd and Cr in samples were far lower than the limits in condiments (0.1 mg/kg) and vegetable products (0.5 mg/kg) in GB 2762-2022. Heavy metals in RSS may be the result of raw vegetables absorbing heavy metals from the soil or contamination from processing equipment during preparation. Despite their low concentrations, clarifying the contamination pathways is essential for enhancing the safety quality of RSS products.

Microorganisms

The production of RSS is closely related to microorganisms, and the traditionally naturally fermented RSS system is rich in microorganisms of complex species which contribute to the product's excellent flavor and taste. However, some contaminant microorganisms in the RSS system may pose safety risks. The RSS samples were tested for harmful microorganisms including coliforms, Shigella, Staphylococcus aureus, and Salmonella. Coliform counts in all samples were below 30 MPN/100 g, and no Shigella, Staphylococcus aureus, or Salmonella were detected in any sample (Table S4), complying with the requirements of DB 52/T 986-2015. Meanwhile, a certain number of bacteria and yeasts were detected in the samples, but they are generally not used as safety evaluation indicators for fermented foods.

Multivariate statistical analysis

Evaluation of bioactive and harmful substances by Z-score heat map

The Z-score heat map, constructed from bioactive and potentially harmful substance concentrations (Fig. 4), provides an integrated overview of quality variations among the nine RSS samples. M-RSS samples exhibited higher overall Z-scores, reflecting their greater abundance of key bioactive compounds closely associated with enhanced nutritional functionality, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and potential health-promoting properties. Specifically, JZY contained high concentrations of organic acids and carotenoids. YJS was abundant in polyphenols and vitamin C, while LTG featured capsaicinoids and total flavonoids as its primary active components (Fig. 4(a)). The heatmap depicting the levels of potential harmful substances (Fig. 4(b)) illustrated that MWJ (R-RSS) had the lowest Z-score, followed closely by LHZ (T-RSS), indicating lower contents of harmful compounds and consequently higher safety and storage stability for these samples. Importantly, the consistently low concentrations of hazardous compounds across all samples, well below regulatory limits, underscore a reliable foundation for shelf-life safety. These findings demonstrate that while raw material composition drives diversity in nutritional and functional quality, all RSS products share a high baseline of safety and stability, offering a scientific basis for developing tailored products that balance targeted health benefits with assured shelf-life performance.

Correlation analysis

The correlation analysis of the color (L*, a*, b* values), sour taste (organic acids), and bioactive substances of the RSS samples exhibited that the a* value significantly positively correlated with capsorubin, zeaxanthin, cryptoxanthin (p < 0.01), total flavonoids, and lutein (p < 0.05), suggesting these substances were major contributors to RSS's red color (Fig. 5(a)). The b* value and L* value, respectively, represented yellow-blueness and lightness, demonstrating a positive correlation with total flavonoids, lycopene, β-carotene, dihydrocapsaicin, and nordihydrocapsaicin. These compounds collectively contributed to the vibrant color of RSS. Carotenoids and flavonoid compounds are the primary pigments that give plants their color, and the accumulation of different carotenoids leads to fruits exhibiting red, yellow, and orange hues (Liang et al., 2022). Anthocyanins, within flavonoid compounds, can combine with sugars through glycosidic bonds to form more structurally stable anthocyanins, which are the primary substances responsible for the red-orange to blue-purple hues in plant organs (Zheng et al., 2022). These active substances work together to impart to RSS a vibrant and bright red color. Sourness and pungency are important flavors of RSS, originating from organic acids and capsaicinoids, respectively. As shown in Fig. 5(a), succinic acid, citric acid, and oxalic acid positively correlated with certain carotenoids such as capsorubin, lutein, zeaxanthin, and cryptoxanthin, while capsaicin and dihydrocapsaicin positively correlated with polyphenol, vitamin C, tartaric acid, oxalic acid, and flavonoids. These results demonstrate that bioactive compounds, including carotenoids, total flavonoids, capsaicinoids, and organic acids, not only provide abundant nutritional value to RSS but also contribute to its favorable color and taste, thereby offering data references for targeted improvements in its quality in future studies.

PCA and clustering analysis

According to the principal component analysis, PC1 and PC2 comprised 58.3% and 19.7% of the total variance, respectively (Fig. 5(b)). The T-RSS and R-RSS samples were clustered in close proximity, whereas the three M-RSS samples exhibited greater dispersion, indicating significant quality variations among samples fermented with chili-tomato mixtures. LTG was mainly characterized by nordihydrocapsaicin, zeaxanthin, oxalic acid, tartaric acid, lutein, lycopene, and α-carotene (Fig. 5(c)). JZY and YJS were mainly characterized by polyphenol, succinic acid, vitamin C, and capsaicin. The clustering of T-RSS samples suggests that RSS fermented solely with tomatoes may exhibit similar quality characteristics, but this requires validation with a larger sample cohort in future work. The clustering heatmap demonstrated that the RSS samples exhibited prominent levels of polyphenol and total flavonoids (Fig. 5(d)). LTG exhibited the most distinctive quality profile, with the remaining samples clustering into three distinct groups: YJS and JZY (M-RSS); MWJ, OYM and WMS (R-RSS); MC, LHZ and GXY (T-RSS). Both PCA and clustering analysis revealed the commonalities and differences of the RSS quality, providing a reference for further exploring diversified products.

Conclusions

Through multidimensional quality analysis, this study characterized the comprehensive quality characteristics of RSS fermented with different substrates. Among the tested samples, tomato-chili based RSS (M-RSS) exhibited higher levels of total acidity, polyphenol, and total flavonoids than T-RSS and R-RSS, possessing a higher concentration of bioactive substances. Importantly, all RSS samples shared a high baseline of safety, with hazardous compounds consistently below regulatory limits. Meanwhile, intrinsic correlations were identified between key bioactive components and the sensory attributes of RSS. Carotenoids showed a significant positive correlation with product redness (a* value) (p < 0.05), while the synergistic effect of capsaicin with polyphenols and organic acids was closely associated with the pungency and acidity characteristics of RSS. This provides a reference for improving the abundance of bioactive substances and enhancing core sensory qualities such as color, acidity, and pungency of RSS through substrate formulation. In the future, the sensory- and function-oriented quality enhancement of RSS may be realized through the optimization of fermentation substrates and proportions. However, this study has several limitations. The RSS samples were collected from different manufacturers, thus subtle differences may exist in their production environments. Therefore, apart from substrate formulations, the observed quality variations may also be affected by production conditions. Further research will be conducted under standardized production conditions to verify the effects of substrates on the overall quality of RSS.

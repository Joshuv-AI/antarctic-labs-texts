Optimizing the role of scrub nurses during robotic surgery console time
Authors: Kenro Chikazawa, Hiroyoshi Ko, Hiroshi Noda, Ken Imai, Tomoyuki Kuwata
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAt our institution, scrub nurses leave the surgical field during the console time for robotic surgeries to attend to other duties. The first assistant then assumes their responsibilities, allowing for efficient use of staff time and resources. This practice improves teamwork and can be presented as a cost-saving measure for hospital administrations.Keywords: Scrub nurse, Robotic surgery, Console phase

Robotic surgery is known for its high cost, which poses a significant barrier to its adoption in Asia [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. Even in the United States, where there is a high volume of cases, reports indicate that the costs often outweigh the benefits [5]. Strategies, such as improving the surgeons' learning curve, concentrating cases, and selectively using instruments, have been proposed to mitigate costs; however, identifying further cost-reduction measures is challenging [6].

At our institution, we have implemented an approach in which scrub nurses leave the surgical field during the console time for robotic surgery to attend to other duties. During the console phase, the first assistant's tasks are minimal, and the scrub nurses have few duties. Therefore, the first assistant can assume the scrub nurse's responsibilities.

In an acute-care hospital setting, scrub nurses can use this time to prepare for emergency surgeries, assist with the turnover of scheduled surgeries, and support other operating rooms. Scrub nurses can return to the surgical field during hemostasis confirmation or intraperitoneal lavage, resuming their role in closing the incision without affecting the progress of the surgery. At our institution, during robot-assisted hysterectomies, the scrub nurse is called back to the surgical field when colpotomy commences. Thus, the scrub nurse can be away from the surgical field for 1–1.5 h during the console phase.

This practice fosters positive relationships and encourages teamwork among the surgical staff. This approach can be presented to hospital administrations as a cost-saving measure for personnel expenses associated with robotic surgeries. We recommend this strategy for other surgical teams.

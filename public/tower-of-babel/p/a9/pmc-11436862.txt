PDIA2 is associated with the prognosis of prostate cancer, and downregulation of PDIA2 delays the progression of prostate cancer cells
Authors: Qiang Zhou, Yue Ge, Sheng Ma, Zezhong Xiong, Yanan Wang, Le Li, Ling Li, Zheng Chao, Junbiao Zhang, Tengfei Li, Zixi Wu, Yuan Gao, Guanyu Qu, Haoxiao Dong, Zhihua Wang, Wang Jing, Guojun Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractProtein Disulfide-Isomerase A2 (PDIA2) is a gene that encodes proteins, responsible for protein folding and modification within cells. The development and course of many disorders are intimately linked to the aberrant expression of PDIA2. Nevertheless, more research is necessary to fully understand PDIA2’s biological significance in pan-cancer, notably in prostate cancer (PCa). PDIA2 expression is elevated in various tumors and closely related to patient prognosis. Patients with prostate cancer who express PDIA2 high in particular have a bad prognosis in terms of progression-free survival. In addition, the upregulation of PDIA2 expression in prostate cancer patients is accompanied by higher Gleason scores, advanced tumor staging, lymph node metastasis, and elevated PSA levels. Detailed experiments further demonstrate that PDIA2 is a carcinogenic gene affecting prostate cancer cells’ response to dasatinib therapy. For patients with prostate cancer, there is a clear positive connection between the expression level of PDIA2 and a bad prognosis. The prostate cancer treatment efficacy of dasatinib is hampered by PDIA2, which is intimately linked to the growth, invasion, and metastasis of PCa cells. In summary, our research highlights the potential of PDIA2 as a biomarker for the diagnosis and management of PCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73361-4.Keywords: PDIA2, Prostate cancer, Prognosis, DasatinibSubject terms: Cancer, Urology

Introduction

One of the most prevalent malignant tumors in males, prostate cancer (PCa) primarily affects the prostate’s epithelial tissue and has a variable incidence rate across different nations and areas1. Based on data from the World Health Organization (WHO), prostate cancer is becoming more commonplace worldwide and is now the second most common cause of death for male malignant tumors2. Advancements have been made in the early diagnosis and treatment of prostate cancer in recent years thanks to the promotion of early screening, the use of novel endocrine therapy medications, and robotics-assisted minimally invasive surgery3. Prostate cancer occurs and develops mostly because of the androgen receptor. For advanced prostate cancer, androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is the primary treatment4.

PDIA2 is a gene that encodes proteins, commonly known as “Protein Disulfide Isomerase A2”. PDIA2 codes for a significant endoplasmic reticulum protein involved in intracellular protein stability maintenance and accelerating intracellular protein folding and assembly5. PDIA2 plays a significant role in diverse biological processes, involving cell apoptosis, cell multiplication, cell differentiation, immune response, etc. PDIA2 expression is significantly upregulated in tumor cells, which is strongly linked to metastasis, aggressiveness, and tumor cell proliferation6. It has been suggested that the increased expression of PDIA2 can promote tumor invasion, and the main mechanism is that PDIA2 mobilizes tissue factors to regulate migrating human vascular smooth muscle cells and promote tumor microvascularization7. Therefore, PDIA2 is considered a potential therapeutic target and has significant implications for the treatment of related diseases. PCa incidence and progression are associated with PDIA2. When comparing prostate cancer tissue to normal prostate tissue, PDIA2 expression was much greater in the former. Moreover, increased PDIA2 expression in prostate cancer is linked to aggressiveness and a poor prognosis. By regulating several signaling pathways, PDIA2 can accelerate the development and aggressiveness of prostate cancer cells as well as the disease’s course8. These investigations point to PDIA2 as a possible therapeutic target for prostate cancer; nevertheless, more investigation is required to pinpoint its precise function in the development and progression of PCa9.

This study screened novel biomarkers using the cancer genome atlas (TCGA) database, which included information on liver, endometrial, cervical, prostate, and bladder cancers. We have conducted a comprehensive bioinformatics exploration of PDIA2 from various databases, including UCSC, TIMER2, cBioPortal, STRING, SangerBox, and CTRP. We further focused on the clinical characteristics and prognostic analysis of PDIA2 in PCa by utilizing the dataset of TCGA and our clinical data. In addition, the upregulation of PDIA2 expression in prostate cancer patients is accompanied by higher Gleason scores, advanced tumor staging, lymph node metastasis, and elevated PSA levels. This study found the potential function of PDIA2 in the advancement of PCa and established a prediction model for the progression-free survival of prostate cancer based on bioinformatics analysis. We built our progression-free survival prediction model for prostate cancer using clinical data to confirm the involvement of PDIA2 in the disease and the validity of the model. We conducted both in vitro and in vivo studies to better understand the biological role of PDIA2 on prostate cancer. Carry out functional investigations to confirm how PDIA2 affects dasatinib sensitivity.

Results

High expression of PDIA2 may be associated with the occurrence and prognosis of various tumors

Wherein, we found 16 genes (MAGEA11, ZIC2, ZIC5, MAGEC2, NKX2-2, NKX25, MAGEA6, MAGEA3, PRAME, MAGEA1, HOXC13, PRSS1, CR589904.2, PDIA2, KISS1R, MAGEC1, and TFAP2A) were significantly upregulated. Analyzing and visualizing the trend of expression among 16 genes based on the TCGA-PRAD project. Interestingly, among these 16 genes, PDIA2 is associated with almost every gene (Fig. 1B). PDIA2 was chosen as the target gene for this research.

To probe the role of PDIA2 in pan-cancer, this research first investigates the expression of PDIA2 in 35 tumors in the TCGA dataset. Comparing the expression of PDIA2 in cancerous tissue and corresponding adjacent tissues using TIMER2.0 (http://timer.cistrome.org/). This study certificated that PDIA2 is markedly upregulated in various types of tumors, including bladder transitional cell carcinoma (BLCA), breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), colon cancer (COAD), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), renal chromophobe cells (KICH), hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma (PCPG), prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) Rectal adenocarcinoma (READ) and endometrial cancer (UCEC). On the contrary, the expression of PDIA2 is downregulated in esophageal cancer (ESCA) and glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) (Fig. 1C).

The predictive usefulness of PDIA2 in tumor patients was demonstrated using the GEPIA2.0 dataset, which included overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) (Fig. 1D, E). This study found that high expression of PDIA2 was markedly correlated with increased OS in ACC (p = 0.0097, HR = 3.2) and KIRC (p = 2.6e-07, HR = 2.3). On the contrary, in LGG (P = 0.00066, HR = 0.52) (Supplementary Fig. 1), the high expression of PDIA2 was notably associated with decreased OS. High expression of PDIA2 was not statistically significant for overall survival (P = 0.15, HR = 3.2). PDIA2 upregulation was associated with a poor prognosis in ACC (p = 7.7e-05, HR = 4.4), BLCA (p = 0.02, HR = 1.5), KIRC (p = 0.00076, HR = 1.9), PRAD (p = 9e-04, HR = 2), STAD (p = 0.005, HR = 1.7), and UVM (p = 0.00094, HR = 6.2), according to results from the DFS analysis. Conversely, a favorable prognosis is indicated by the high expression of PDIA2 in LGG (p = 0.0076, HR = 0.6) (Supplementary Fig. 1). At the same time, this study also used Kaplan Meier mapping tools to explore survival data (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: survival [3.3.1], survminer [0.4.9], ggplot2 [3.3.6]). hence, this study attested that PDIA2 may be a prospective prognostic marker in diverse tumors. The expression profiles and prognostic data declared that PDIA2 may be an oncogenic gene for cancer patients.

Through the cBioPortal database (http://cbioportal.org), this study found that the mutation of PDIA2 is the highest in pan-cancer. PDIA2 mutations primarily include amplification in cases of breast cancer, head and neck cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, and prostate cancer. In PDIA2, gene alterations may be classified into three primary categories: amplification, deletion, and profound deletion (Fig. 2A). The kinds, loci, and total number of instances of PDIA2 gene alteration are further depicted in Fig. 2B. Changes in R249P were detected in a case of lung squamous cell carcinoma, with PDIA2 missense mutation being the main type. The consequences from the cBioPortal database revealed that 1.64% (8/489) of prostate cancer patients had a variation of PDIA2, and amplification was the most frequent variation (Fig. 2C). Further, explore the mutation types of PDIA2 through the COSMIC database (https://cancer.sanger.ac.uk/cosmic). According to the findings, synonymous replacement of PDIA2 was observed in 33.33% of individuals with prostate cancer (Fig. 2D). Furthermore, the main base changes are G > A (50%) and C > T (50%) (Fig. 2E). Using the cBioPortal database, plot the DFS survival curves for the prostate tumor PDIA2 altered group and the unaltered group. The PDIA2 altered group had a poorer outcome than the unaltered group, according to survival analysis (Supplementary Fig. 1L).

Correlation analysis between PDIA2 expression and medical traits of PCa

The TCGA database and clinical data from the urology department of Tongji Hospital of Wuhan Huazhong University of Science and Technology were used to assess the correlation between PDIA2 expression and clinical features of prostate cancer patients to further demonstrate the impact of PDIA2 expression on the progression of PCa. Based on the expression level of PDIA2, the clinical baseline information table (Supplementary Table 3) of the TCGA database and our clinical cohort (Supplementary Table 4) were created. We discovered that PDIA2 is substantially expressed in PCa tissue by bioinformatics analysis using the TCGA database (Fig. 3A). Additionally, PDIA2 appears to be substantially expressed in prostate cancer tissue, according to the GEO database (GSE46602) (Fig. 3B). In our own clinical data queue, we discovered a strong expression of PDIA2 in prostate cancer tissue to provide additional validation (Fig. 3C).

Building, assessing, and displaying a PFS prediction model for prostate cancer based on multivariate Cox regression findings

Due to the specific nature of prostate cancer, we chose the Progression-Free Interval (PFI) as the indicator for our prognostic study. Patients with prostate cancer were classified into two groups based on the median expression of PDIA2: those with high expression and those with low expression. Use the TCGA database to assess the prognosis of high and low PDIA2mRNA groups based on Cox regression. Application software R (4.2.1) and R package (survival [3.3.1], survivor, ggplot2 [3.3.6]) (https://www.r-project.org/). Analysis of prognostic survival showed that a poor prognosis was indicated by increased expression of PDIA2 (HR = 2.86, P<0.001) (Fig. 4A). Pathological T grade T3, pathological N grade N0, clinical T grade T2, clinical M grade M0, age < 60, age ≤ 60, PSA<4 PDIA2 with high expression had a poorer prognosis compared to low expression, according to the subgroup prognosis analysis (Fig. 4B–H).

In the TCGA database, pathological T-grade, pathological N-grade, age, residual tumor, Gleason score ≥ 7, and high PDIA2mRNA expression levels were found to be significant risk factors for PFS by univariate Cox regression analysis, as Supplementary Table 3 illustrates (p<0.05). We demonstrated that pathological T-grade T4, PR, and high expression levels of PDIA2mRNA were independent risk factors for PFS in patients with prostate cancer in the TCGA database, based on the findings of univariate Cox regression analysis (p<0.05) (Fig. 4I). PDIA2mRNA expression level, pathological T grade, pathological N grade, clinical M grade, main treatment result, age, tumor margin status, PSA, and Gleason score viewed using nomograms are all included in the PFS prediction model, as illustrated in Fig. 4J. The model’s calibration diagram demonstrates a good degree of consistency between the anticipated and observed outcomes (Fig. 4K). We conducted ROC analysis (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: pROC[1.18.0], ggplot2 [3.3.6]) and found that the expression level of PDIA2 is a reliable diagnostic indicator for patients with prostate cancer (Supplementary Fig. 1L). We conducted a comparative analysis of the model’s performance with and without PDIA2 levels included. We utilized metrics such as the concordance AUC and calibration plots to assess the accuracy and predictive power of the nomogram. Specifically, the AUC value of the model that includes A is greater than the AUC value of the model that does not include A, indicating that the model with A has higher predictive performance (Supplementary Fig. 2C). Additionally, calibration plots demonstrated better alignment between predicted and observed outcomes when PDIA2 was included, indicating that the model is more reliable (Supplementary Fig. 2D). We believe that PDIA2 expression could serve as a valuable biomarker for improving prognostic assessments in prostate cancer. Investing in the development of a measurement tool for PDIA2 expression may indeed be worthwhile, as it could lead to more personalized treatment strategies and potentially better patient outcomes.

Analysis of phenotypic enrichment for PDIA2 expression

A total of 927 DEGs (|LogFC| > 1, adjusted p-value < 0.05) were found between PRAD samples with high and low PDIA2 expression using the TCGA database for differential analysis of high and low expression of PDIA2. Furthermore, low-expression samples concerning PDIA2 were regarded as control groups. 832 genes (|LogFC| > 1, adjusted p-value < 0.05) are deemed high-expression genes, and 95 genes (LogFC < − 1, adjusted p-value < 0.05) are deemed low-expression genes, as depicted in the volcano map (Fig. 5A and Supplementary Table 5). Through GO and KEGG analysis10, 16 related signaling pathways of 927 DEGs were enriched and displayed. Notably, we discovered that the majority of the enrichment pathways we identified are associated with the control of hormone levels, cellular glucuronidation, steroid metabolism, plasma high-density lipoprotein, drug metabolism, and chemical carcinogenic DNA impacts (Fig. 5B). In addition, to comprehensively explore the relevant pathways of PDIA2 in PRAD, further GSEA analysis was conducted using data from all differentially expressed genes. The GSEA analysis results showed that DNA replication, DNA replication initiation, DNA methylation, drug metabolism, G1/S phase transcription, steroid hormone synthesis, PLK1 pathway, and HNF3B pathway regulation were raised in prostate cancer patients with high PDIA2mRNA expression (Fig. 5C, D). We analyzed the correlation between the above eight pathways and PDIA2 in prostate cancer, and the results showed that PLK1 pathway had the highest correlation coefficient (Cor = 0.62486, P < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. 2A). PLK1 (Polo-likekinase1) signaling pathway is an important cell cycle regulation pathway. Associated with cell mitosis and DNA replication. We believe that PDIA2 may activate PLK1 signaling pathway to promote cell invasion and migration, inhibit cell apoptosis, and promote cell proliferation. PDIA2 downregulation prevents prostate cancer cells from proliferating and spreading both in vivo and in vitro

In order to understand the expression of PDIA2 in prostate cancer cell lines, RT-qPCR experiments were performed on representative prostate cancer cell lines PC3, 22RV1, C4-2, DU145, and LnCap. Experimental results showed that the expression of PDIA2 in C4-2 and 22RV1 was significantly higher than that in other cell lines, so we chose to down-regulate the expression of PDIA2 in C4-2 and 22RV1 to continue the follow-up experiment (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Following the discovery of PDIA2’s significance for prostate cancer patient’s prognosis, we started looking into its functional implications. In the experimental study, we utilized specific PDIA2-targeted small hairpin RNA (shRNA) to achieve stable knockdown of PDIA2 expression in C4-2 and 22Rv1 prostate cancer cells. The effectiveness of knockout was confirmed through RT qPCR experiments (Fig. 6A). To investigate the possible influence of PDIA2 on prostate cancer, we carried out CCK-8, colony formation, wound-healing, and transwell assay tests to assess the capacity of cells to multiply and spread in vitro. Experimental data indicates that PDIA2 knockout markedly inhibited the multiplication and metastasis ability of C4-2 and 22Rv1 cells in vitro (Fig. 6B–E). Furthermore, we subcutaneously implanted PDIA2 knockdown C4-2 and 22Rv1 cells into nude mice to assess the function of PDIA2 in vivo. It’s interesting to note that in xenograft tumors, the PDIA2 knockdown group had a considerably decreased growth rate and lighter tumor weight than the control group, which is consistent with findings from in vitro study (Fig. 7A, B). The downregulation of PDIA2 inhibits the proliferation and spread of prostate cancer cells, both in vivo and in vitro.

Inhibiting PDIA2 enhances the therapeutic effects of dasatinib in PCa

This work examined the relationship between PDIA2 expression levels and drug IC50 by analyzing 481 small compounds from the Treatment Response Portal Genomics (CTRP) database to better understand the influence of PDIA2 on cancer therapeutic response. Interestingly, we found a positive relevance between dasatinib (R = 0.33, FDR = 0.000111) and PDIA2 (Fig. 7C). Dasatinib may be used to treat prostate cancer, according to prior research. Based on these findings, we further investigated whether the therapeutic effect of dasatinib in prostate cancer is influenced by PDIA2 expression. It is worth noting that CCK-8 and colony formation measurements show that dasatinib (2µmol/L) moderately inhibits the growth of PCa cells. However, dasatinib’s inhibitory effect on the proliferation of C4-2 and 22Rv1 cells was dramatically boosted when the expression level of PDIA2 was greatly lowered (Fig. 7D–G). Experimental data demonstrate that inhibiting PDIA2 expression can promote the therapeutic effect of dasatinib, indicating that the combination of dasatinib and PDIA2-targeted drugs can serve as a new treatment strategy for PCa.

Discussion

Our understanding of the comprehensive functional role of PDIA2 in pan-cancers, especially in prostate cancer, still needs to be investigated11. Therefore, in this research, we systematically explored the function of PDIA2 in various malignant tumors, with a particular focus on prostate cancer. Our analysis includes differential expression of PDIA2, prognostic effects, genetic changes, immune cell infiltration, and drug response12. Importantly, we also conducted experimental studies on the influences of PDIA2 on prostate cancerous cell multiplication, migration, and drug response, providing experimental evidence to support our bioinformatics analysis13.

Our research’s conclusions showed that certain malignant tumors had markedly higher PDIA2 mRNA expression levels. In addition, survival prognosis analysis shows that high expression of PDIA2 is correlated with bad survival outcomes in various cancerous types. Specifically, in prostate cancer, patients with high expression of PDIA2 exhibit poor progression-free survival14. An increasing number of data suggests that genetic alterations are important in facilitating the transformation of normal cells into aberrant cells capable of unchecked multiplication15. Thus, studying genomic alterations is essential to deriving a more comprehensive knowledge of oncogene function in cancer formation.

The protein encoded by PDIA2 has multiple important functions within cells, primarily involving protein folding, modification, and the cellular stress response. As a protein disulfide isomerase, PDIA2 is responsible for facilitating the folding of newly synthesized proteins. It ensures that proteins achieve the correct conformation for functionality by forming and rearranging disulfide bonds16. PDIA2 plays a crucial role in post-translational modifications of proteins, particularly in the regulation of disulfide bond formation. It helps to establish correct disulfide bonds in the endoplasmic reticulum, maintaining protein functionality17. PDIA2 can also contribute to endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis by modulating the functions of stress-related proteins. These factors may represent potential mechanisms for the high expression of PDIA2 in prostate tumor tissues.

However, to our knowledge, no research has examined PDIA2’s involvement in the development of PCa. According to our investigation, there is a correlation between the upregulation of PDIA2 expression levels and pathological T-grade, pathological N-grade, clinical T-grade, tumor margin status, treatment efficacy, PSA, and Gleason scores, among other clinical characteristics linked to the development of PCa6. Notably, individuals with prostate cancer who have upregulated PDIA2 are often linked to higher Gleason scores, advanced tumor staging, metastasis of lymph nodes, and raised PSA values. In this investigation, we discovered that the outcomes of employing RT-qPCR experiments to examine the correlation between PDIA2 mRNA and clinical traits of PRAD patients were consistent with the results obtained by evaluating the relevance between PDIA2 mRNA and medical traits of PRAD using the TCGA database. Our research revealed a robust association between genetic alterations in PDIA2 and poor progression-free survival (PFS) outcomes in patients with PCa. This means that PDIA2 can be used as a potential biomarker to provide a basis for early diagnosis and prognosis assessment of tumors. Crucially, we developed a PDIA2 prognostic model that may be used to estimate an individual’s chance of PFI at 1, 3, and 5 years old based on the expression of PDIA2 and independent risk markers from routine clinical data. The development of individualized long-term follow-up and treatment strategies may benefit from this novel tactic. These results provide some evidence for PDIA2 as a potential prognostic marker for prostate cancer progression18.

To better elucidate its functional significance, we conducted a series of extensive in vitro and in vivo experiments that demonstrated that downregulation of PDIA2 can inhibit the migration and proliferation of prostate malignant cells. Our research also highlights the potential therapeutic benefit of targeting PDIA2 in prostate cancer by indicating that variations in PDIA2 expression levels may influence how prostate cancer cells respond to dasatinib19.

Potential pathways were enriched using a DEG analysis based on the TCGA database to further examine the effect of PDIA2 on PRAD. PDIA2 positively regulates DNA replication, DNA replication initiation, DNA methylation, drug metabolism, G1S phase transcription, steroid hormone synthesis, PLK1 pathway, and HNF3B pathway, according to the results of gene functional enrichment, which were then in line with earlier research20,21. As is widely known, unfettered DNA replication is a crucial aspect of tumor cell proliferation, which may be strongly connected to the G (1)/S transition step of the cell cycle22. Therefore, downregulation of PDIA2 expression may delay cell proliferation in PCa, and our in vitro and in vivo experimental results further confirm this hypothesis. However, more experiments are needed to demonstrate whether PDIA2 mediates the cell cycle or DNA replication to promote the progress of PCa23. Moreover, DNA repair makes sure that cells survive and makes tumor cells resistant to treatment. In this respect, PDIA2 inhibition may lead cells to enter a DNA repair defect state, which may be advantageous for chemotherapy-resistant PRAD patients24.

Adding methyl groups to DNA molecules is a common epigenetic modification technique called DNA methylation25. This alteration has the potential to impact gene expression and is essential for cell growth and function26. PDIA2 may cause aberrant DNA methylation and is essential for the development and advancement of PCa. Some key genes undergo abnormal methylation modifications in PCa through DNA methylation, which may lead to abnormal gene expression and disruption of related signaling pathways, leading to the progression and poor prognosis of prostate cancer27.

Steroid hormone synthesis refers to the process of producing steroid hormones in the body. These hormones include testosterone, estrogen, progesterone, and adrenocortical hormones, which play important physiological regulatory roles in the human body28. PDIA2 may promote the synthesis of steroid hormones, especially testosterone, which is the main male sex hormone and crucial for the development and maintenance of male reproductive system function. Prostate cell proliferation can be stimulated by high testosterone levels or prolonged exposure, which can aid in the development of prostate cancer29.

The PLK1 (Polo-likekinase1) signaling pathway is an important cell cycle regulatory pathway that is associated with cell mitosis and DNA replication30. The kinase protein PLK1 is involved in several phases of cell mitosis and performs a critical regulatory function in the process of cell division31. Studies have demonstrated the significance of the PLK1 signaling pathway in the pathogenesis of prostate cancer. Increased PLK1 expression is frequently linked to angiogenesis, malignant traits, and a poor prognosis for prostate cancer32. Furthermore, PDIA2 and the PLK1 signaling pathway are important for biological processes such cell stress, apoptosis, and proliferation. PLK1 controls the expression and activity of molecules like PDIA2, which helps to regulate the course of the cell cycle and cell function33. In conclusion, PDIA2 and the PLK1 signaling pathway interact and have a regulatory link that is important for the control of the cell cycle and other biological processes.

Studies have demonstrated that HNF3B regulates PDIA2 expression and activity. HNF3B, for instance, can bind to the PDIA2 gene’s promoter region, which encourages PDIA2 transcription and expression. Furthermore, HNF3B may have an impact on PDIA2’s protein stability and functionality30. In summary, there is an interaction and regulatory relationship between the HNF3B signaling pathway and PDIA2, playing a significant role in embryonic progress and organ formation34.

Prostate cancer is being treated using surgical resection, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, chemical therapy, and other traditional techniques. Targeted medication therapy has not yet been extensively utilized in the treatment of cancer; instead, androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) remains the most routinely used therapeutic approach35. Dashatinib is a targeted tumor therapy drug primarily used for the treatment of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and certain gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST). It is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that stops BCR-ABL fusion proteins and kinases like KIT from acting, which stops cancer cells from growing and spreading36.

Dasatinib can selectively block the kinase activity of the Philadelphia chromosome-produced BCR-ABL fusion protein in CML patients, which in turn prevents the formation of leukemia cells and hematopoietic stem cells that proliferate abnormally37. In the field of gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST), dasatinib has been proven to be a highly effective therapeutic drug38. There are mutations in the KIT or PDGFRA genes in GIST, which lead to abnormal activation of signaling pathways and facilitate the progress and spread of cancer cells. Dasatinib effectively controls the development and metastasis of GIST by inhibiting the activity of KIT and PDGFRA kinases39.

The association between dasatinib and prostate cancer has not gained strong proof and permission for usage and has not been utilized as a conventional therapy for prostate cancer40. However, research has indicated that dasatinib may have an inhibiting effect on certain prostate malignancies in some preclinical and laboratory investigations. This drug may affect some signal transduction pathways in prostate cancer cells, including androgen receptors, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, and other pathways41. These findings, however, are restricted to the laboratory and early phases of clinical trials, and there is not enough data to justify their broad use in the management of prostate cancer.

This investigation offered fresh perspectives on dasatinib’s potential therapeutic benefit for PCa. Our in vitro experiments have shown that the response of PCa cells to dasatinib may be influenced by PDIA2. The C4-2 and 22Rv1 cell lines may contain some unusual or unique genetic alterations that may be related to their sensitivity to dasatinib. Both cell lines carry mutations in active androgen receptors, which affect the cell’s response to hormones and chemotherapy. C4-2 cell lines often exhibit abnormal activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, which is associated with cell proliferation and survival. The alteration of this pathway may enhance the cell reactivity to dasatinib. Deletion or mutation of the PTEN gene, which is common in prostate cancer, is also present in both cell lines. Loss of PTEN often leads to overactivation of AKT, which in turn affects the cell’s sensitivity to drugs such as dasatinib. Future research in this area can be conducted through detailed genome sequencing for comparative analysis, and we can better understand the specific genetic background of these cell lines and the mechanisms by which they respond to dasatinib. These clinically-specific changes not only help us explain the drug response of cell lines, but may also provide new ideas for prostate cancer treatment. This finding implies that a possible new approach to treating PCa may involve the combination of dasatinib and medications that target PDIA2.

Materials and methods

Screening analysis of biomarker PDIA2

The data sets of prostate cancer (PRAD), bladder cancer (BLCA), endometrial cancer (PRAD), cervical cancer (CESC), and liver cancer (LIHC) in the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort were used to project biological-markers forecasting the prognosis of PCa. After selecting the intersection of five differentially expressed genes (DEGs), 16 genes were screened out, and further screening was carried out by analyzing the correlation of the 16 genes using the R package “ggplot2” (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: ggplot2 [3.3.6]). Finally, the gene PDIA2, which is almost related to all 16 genes, was selected in this study.

The variation analysis of PDIA2 in pan-cancer, especially in prostate cancer

The mutation types and frequencies of PDIA2 in pan-cancer were selected from the cBioPortal database (http:\\www.bioportal.org\) as the research object. The mutation types and frequencies of PDIA2 in prostate cancer are from the Cancer Somatic Mutation Inventory (COSMIC) (https:\\Cancer.sanger.ac.uk). Choose the “tissue distribution” and “mutation distribution” of the prostate gland.

Differential analysis of PDIA2 in pan-cancer, particularly in prostate cancer

Tumor Immune Estimation Resource 2.0 (TIMER2) website (http://timer.cistrome.org/) was used to study the different expression profiles of PDIA2 between pan-cancer and nearby normal organizations. The GEPIA2 online website provided the overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) survival map data of PDIA2 in different tumor types in the TCGA database. RNA-seq data from the TCGA-PRAD project are used to show the differential expression of PDIA2 in paired and unpaired samples. The differential analysis data of PDIA2 in prostate cancer based on our clinical information was used to further demonstrate the high expression of PDIA2 mRNA in PCa tissues.

Relevance analysis between PDIA2 expression and medical traits of prostate cancer patients

The medical traits of PCa patients are derived from the TCGA-PRAD project and the urology department of Tongji Hospital, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, to analyze their correlation with PDIA2 expression in prostate cancer.

Relevance analysis between PDIA2 expression and prognosis in prostate cancer patients

Using lifespan information from PCa patients in the TCGA database, the Kaplan Meier method-based survival package was used to assay the relevance between PDIA2 mRNA expression and the prognosis of PCa patients (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: survival [3.3.1], survminer [0.4.9], ggplot2 [3.3.6]). The surviving software package was used to plot the progression-free survival (PFS) curve. Univariate Cox regression was used to sift venture factors affecting PFS from the clinical features and PDIA2 expression levels of prostate cancer patients. After sifting through a univariate Cox regression with a p-value, significant factors were incorporated in the multivariate Cox regression model to identify independent venture factors for PFS in prostate cancer patients. In light of the multivariate Cox regression findings, a prognostic prediction model for predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year PFS was constructed. Furthermore, calibration maps were plotted using the RMS software package and survival software package to check the accuracy of the prediction model (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: survival [3.3.1], rms [6.3-0]).

Oncogene PDIA2 differential analysis and correlation analysis

Based on the TCGA database, single gene difference analysis and single gene relevance analysis were performed on PDIA2 in the PRAD project using the DESeq2 software package and STAT software package, respectively (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: DESeq2 [2.2.1], STAT [3.1]). The STRING database (https://string-db.org/) is used to analyze DEG, and the protein-protein interaction (PPI) of DEG is analyzed using Cytoscape software network analysis. Then, the HUB gene is identified using the MCODE plugin. Eventually, based on single gene correlation analysis, the genes were sorted in descending order of “Pearson value” to extract the top 35 correlated genes. Using the ggplot2 software package, draw a single gene co-expression heatmap of PDIA2 using the first 35 genes (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: ggplot2 [3.3.6]).

Functional enrichment and immune infiltration analysis of PDIA2

Based on differential expression analysis (PDIA2 high expression samples and PDIA2 low expression samples) in the TCGA-PRAD project, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was used to explore potential signaling pathways. After screening for differentially expressed genes based on cut-off values (| LogFC |>1 and adjusted p-value<0.05), GO and KEGG analyses were performed42. Further, enriches the pathways related to PDIA2 in prostate cancer (Software: R Version 4.2.1; https://www.r-project.org/, R Package: ggplot2 [3.3.6]).

Human tissues

128 cases of prostate gland cancerous tissue and it’s corresponding nearby normal prostate gland tissue were obtained from the urology department of Tongji Hospital, Wuhan Huazhong University of Science and Technology. This study collected a total of 50 pairs of tissues for further experimental validation using RT qPCR. After undergoing radical prostatectomy, all tissues were immediately stored in liquid nitrogen. Prior to participation, all subjects received informed consent and signed informed consent forms. This study was conducted in accordance with the criteria of the 2013 Declaration of Helsinki. In addition, this research agreement is confirmed by the Ethics Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (permit number 2019CR101).

Animal models

The People’s Republic of China’s Animal Protection Law contains standards for the care and use of laboratory animals. These requirements are followed while performing any procedure, and the ARRIVE recommendations for reporting animal experiment reports are followed when reporting the results. The Huazhong University of Science and Technology’s Tongji Medical College’s Animal Experimental Ethics Committee has authorized the plan (permission number TJH-201901019). Purchased Male BALB/c male nude mice from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Company. For the subcutaneous injection experiments, a total of 4.0 × 106 C4-2 cells and 4.0 × 106 22Rv1 cells were suspended in 100 µL of PBS solution and injected subcutaneously into the right flank of the nude mice. Tumor sizes were measured and recorded every five days, and the volume was calculated using the formula: Volume = 0.5 × length × width2. After 20 days, the mice were euthanized using carbon dioxide exposure (2.5 LPM, Liters per minute), the tumors were removed and weighed.

Cross-table data is compared using the chi-square test or Fisher test. All data used in this research can be obtained by the corresponding authors at the reasonable request of the readers.

Linking Local Atomic Structure and Carbon Network Architecture to Electrochemical Performance and Na+ Diffusivity in Na4VMn(PO4)3/C Cathodes
Authors: Songyoot Kaewmala, Natthapong Kamma, Sujeera Pleuksachat, Pornjira Phuenhinlad, Wanwisa Limphirat, Natthawan Prasongthum, Jeffrey Nash, Nonglak Meethong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Na4VMn­(PO4)3 is a promising cathode material for sodium-ion batteries due to its high operating voltage, structural robustness, and open three-dimensional framework that supports efficient Na+ transport. However, its practical deployment is limited by poor intrinsic electronic conductivity and structural instability under cycling. To address these limitations, Na4VMn­(PO4)3/carbon (Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C) composites were investigated, focusing on the interplay between carbon network architecture and local atomic structure. Using advanced characterization techniques, this study reveals that the degree of distortion in MnO6 and VO6 octahedra, along with the continuity and distribution of the conductive carbon matrix, plays a pivotal role in determining Na+ diffusion kinetics and electrochemical performance. Composites with reduced local structural distortion exhibit enhanced structural integrity and Na+ mobility, while a well-integrated carbon network significantly improves the electronic conductivity. Together, these synergistic effects led to markedly improved cycling stability, rate capability, and Na+ diffusion coefficients. These findings provide valuable insights into the atomic-scale and mesoscale design principles necessary for optimizing polyanionic cathode materials for high-performance sodium-ion batteries.

Introduction

Sodium-ion batteries are a promising alternative to lithium-ion batteries owing to the natural abundance and lower cost of sodium, which significantly contribute to their reduced production costs. The battery types have the same working principles and main components because sodium and lithium have similar physical and chemical properties. They also share identical cell production processes, indicating that sodium-ion battery technology has many advantages for replacing or reducing lithium-ion battery utilization. However, sodium-ion batteries have crucial limitations, such as a lower energy density and slower reaction kinetics, resulting from the larger molecular weight (23 g/mol) and ionic radius (1.02 Å) of sodium. For comparison, lithium has a molecular weight and ionic radius of 6.9 g/mol and 0.76 Å, respectively. It is well-established that the electrochemical performance of a battery is significantly dependent on its electrode materials. Therefore, seeking proper electrode materials is critical for improving the electrochemical properties of sodium-ion batteries. Developing cathode materials containing Na ions in their structure is an important key to enhancing the energy density, cycle life, and rate capability of sodium-ion batteries. Numerous studies have reported that there are four cathode material types such as transition metal oxides, polyanionic compounds, Prussian blue compounds, and organic compounds. All four have been investigated as cathode materials for sodium-ion batteries. , Among all the cathode material types, polyanionic compounds have attracted much attention because of their 3D open-framework structure consisting of tetrahedral anionic units (XO4) n− (X = S, P, Si, As, Mo, or W) with a strong X–O covalent bond, allowing fast Na-ion transport and a robust structure. ,, This leads to high cyclability and rate performance. Na4VMn­(PO4)3 is a polyanionic compound that is widely studied as a cathode for sodium-ion batteries due to the low price and toxicity of Mn. The Na4VMn­(PO4)3 cathode also has high energy density owing to the presence of two voltage plateaus, at 3.3 and 3.6 V, corresponding to the V3+/V4+ and Mn3+/Mn2+ redox couples, respectively. − Goodenough et al. reported that a Na4VMn­(PO4)3 cathode delivered a specific capacity of 101 mAh/g at 1C. Nevertheless, the Na4VMn­(PO4)3 cathode material suffers from low electronic conductivity, resulting in a limited capacity. Additionally, the cathode exhibits structural instability during electrochemical cycling because of the Jahn–Teller distortion of the Mn3+-O6 octahedral configuration (local atomic distortion), leading to poor cycling stability. ,− Surface modification strategies, including conductive material coating and interphase designs, have been introduced to improve the conductivity of the cathodes. ,− Moreover, elemental doping strategies can efficiently reduce the local atomic distortion and facilitate Na ion transfer in Na4VMn­(PO4)3 cathodes, leading to improved cycling stability and rate performance. − This indicates that a deep understanding of the impacts of local atomic structural characteristics and conductive carbon networks on the electrochemical performance and kinetic properties of prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3 materials is important for the development of high-performance cathode materials in commercial sodium-ion batteries.

This work aims to investigate the effects of local atomic structural characteristics and conductive carbon networks on the electrochemical performance and Na ion diffusion coefficients of Na4VMn­(PO4)3 cathode materials. The local atomic structure and conductive carbon network can be changed by using different preparation methods, such as sol–gel and solid-state reaction methods, which are simple and scalable. A multiscale structural investigation was performed to elucidate the impacts of the preparation methods on the structural properties on multiple scales of the prepared cathodes. Their local atomic (short-range order) and crystal (long-range order) structures were evaluated by using X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) techniques, respectively. The morphology and carbon network of the prepared cathodes were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Moreover, the electrochemical performance and Na ion diffusion coefficients of the cathodes were studied using galvanostatic cycling and the galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT), respectively. The experimental results offer effective guidelines for the intensive structural investigation and development of high-performance electrode materials for commercial sodium-ion batteries.

Experimental Section

Materials Preparation

Sol–gel method: NH4VO3, (CH3COO)2Mn·4H2O, CH3COONa, and NH6PO4 were used as the starting materials. d-glucose was employed as a reducing agent and carbon source. First, NH4VO3 and d-glucose were dissolved in 40 mL of deionized water and stirred at 80 °C for 30 min to form a clear blue solution (Solution A). (CH3COO)2Mn·4H2O, CH3COONa, and NH6PO4 were dissolved in 60 mL of deionized water (Solution B). Then, Solution B was slowly added to Solution A. The mixed solution was continuously stirred at 80 °C to remove water until a gel was formed. The resulting gel was oven-dried at 80 °C. Finally, the dried gel was initially heated at 350 °C for 4 hours and then further heated at 750 °C for 8 hours in a tube furnace under an Ar atmosphere to obtain a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C material (NVMP-SG).

Solid-state reaction method: required amounts of NH4VO3, (CH3COO)2Mn·4H2O, CH3COONa, NH6PO4, and d-glucose were used with 5 mm ZrO2 ceramic balls (weight ratio of balls to powder was 20:1). They were subsequently placed in a sealed 125 mL Teflon bottle with 70 mL of ethanol. Next, the mixed powder was ball-milled using a rotational speed of 300 rpm for 16 hours. After that, the obtained mixture was oven-dried overnight at 80 °C. Finally, the dried mixture was heated using the same process as the sol–gel method to form a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C material (NVMP-SS).

Structural and Morphological Characterization

The crystal structure of the synthesized Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C materials was examined under X-ray diffraction (XRD) (Empyrean, PANalytical) using Cu–Kα radiation with a 2θ range of 10°–70°. Rietveld refinement was performed to calculate the lattice parameters of the prepared cathodes using X’pert Highscore Plus software. The local atomic structure of the prepared materials was investigated by using X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) (BL. 8, SLRI, Thailand). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (FEI, Helios NanoLab G3 CX) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Thermo Scientific, Talos F200X G2) were employed to investigate the morphological characteristics and obtain elemental mapping of the prepared cathodes. ImageJ software was used to determine the average particle sizes of the prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C materials. The carbon content of the prepared cathode was investigated by using thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) (Hitachi, STA7200) with a heating temperature range from room temperature to 700 °C and a heating rate of 5 °C/min. The structural characteristics of carbon were evaluated using Raman spectroscopy (HORIBA, XploRA Plus). Origin Pro software was used to analyze the obtained Raman results. A full deconvolution of the entire spectra using Gaussian components was employed to obtain the integrated areas (IG and ID1).

Electrochemical Characterization

Electrodes were fabricated using 80 wt % of the synthesized cathode materials as active materials, 10 wt % of carbon black (Alfa Aesar) as a conductive agent, and 10 wt % of Kynar 1800 (polyvinylidene fluoride) dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, Aldrich) solvent as a binder. This was done to study the electrochemical performance of the obtained Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes. These materials were mixed for 30 min in a horizontal shaker to form a black slurry. The obtained slurry was cast on an Al foil current collector with a doctor blade and then vacuum-dried at 80 °C for 10 hours. The active material loading of the prepared electrodes was around 1.10 mg/cm. To study the cycling stability and rate performance of the prepared electrodes, half-cells (CR3032 coin cell type) were produced in an Ar-filled glovebox employing Na metal as an anode. 1 M NaClO4 was dissolved in a mixed organic solvent consisting of ethyl carbonate (EC) and diethyl carbonate (DEC) (1:1 in volume) as an electrolyte. Glass fiber (Whatman GF/D) was used as a separator. The electrochemical properties of the prepared cathodes were investigated with a galvanostatic charge/discharge technique (NEWARE, BTS-4008). For the cycling stability test, fabricated half-cells were charged and discharged at 0.2C for up to 300 cycles, using a cutoff voltage range of 2.5–3.8 V at a constant temperature of 30 °C. Moreover, the fabricated half-cells were charged and discharged at 0.1C, 0.2C, 0.3C, 1C, 3C, 5C, and 10C, using the same cutoff voltage range and operating temperature for the rate performance test. The current density corresponding to the 1C rate was calculated as 110 mA/g. Two cells were studied for each characterization to ensure the reliability of the obtained data. The studied cells exhibited similar capacity, cycling stability, and rate capability. Thus, the electrochemical results of a single cell were selected as a representative. A galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) (Biologic, VMP-300) was used to evaluate the sodium-ion diffusion behavior in the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode materials. The cells were charged or discharged at 0.1C for 10 min, followed by a relaxation time of 60 min. The electronic conductivity of the synthesized cathode was assessed using a 2-probe-based evaluation (Biologic, VMP-300) using a voltage range of −0.5 to 0.5 V. Moreover, the ionic transfer resistance of the prepared electrode materials was studied using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) (Corrtest, CS310) over a frequency range of 0.1–100000 Hz with an amplitude of 10 mA.

Results and Discussion

Morphological Investigation

The morphological behaviors of the prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode materials were characterized by using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). These results are shown in Figure . The SEM results, Figure a,b, reveal that both obtained cathodes have irregularly shaped particles. Their primary particles have slightly different average sizes, 95.10 ± 14.30 and 115.18 ± 14.87 nm for the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C materials prepared from sol–gel and solid-state reaction methods, respectively. The electrode prepared by the sol–gel method has a slightly smaller primary particle size but exhibits more pronounced particle agglomeration compared with the electrode prepared by the solid-state reaction method. This competition makes the slight differences in particle size and agglomeration behavior have no significant effect on the electrochemical performance of the prepared electrodes. More detailed morphological information was obtained for the prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C materials using TEM. These results are shown in Figure c,d. The prepared cathodes are composite materials that consist of Na4VMn­(PO4)3 and carbon with different carbon networks. In the NVMP-SG cathode material, Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles were partially trapped on carbon sheets with a loose interaction, as shown in Figure c. In contrast, Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles were completely coated by carbon with a tight interaction, like the core–shell structure for the NVMP-SS cathode material, as revealed in Figure d. As shown in Figure a, the HRTEM images of the prepared cathodes demonstrate that both electrodes are composed of a high degree of amorphous carbon on the surface of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles. This implies that preparation routes play a critical role in developing the morphological properties of the cathodes, especially for the carbon network coating on the surface of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles, as shown in Figure . The electronic conductivity of the NVMP-SG and NVMP-SS electrodes was examined with a 2-probe-based IV measurement. As illustrated in Figure S1, the NVMP-SG electrode has a lower electronic conductivity (9.16 × 10–5 S.cm–1) than the NVMP-SS electrode (1.71.x10–4 S.cm–1). This indicates that the carbon network characteristics affect the electronic conductivities of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C electrodes, altering their reversible capacity and rate performance. Energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) was used to perform elemental mapping of the prepared cathodes. This is shown in Figure S2. Uniform distributions of Na, V, Mn, P, O, and C were observed throughout the samples. The presence of carbon species on the surfaces of Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles confirms their carbon coating. As shown in Figure S3, the elemental composition of the as-prepared electrodes corresponds to the stoichiometry of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3 material. The carbon contents in the synthesized cathode materials were measured using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). These results are given in Figure b. The TGA results show that the carbon contents in the cathodes prepared by sol–gel and solid-state reaction methods are similar, 2.25 and 2.49 wt %, respectively. Additionally, Raman spectroscopy was done to study structural characteristics of carbon in the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C materials, as shown in Figure c,d. Two distinct peaks are found with overlapping Gaussian bands at around 1340 cm–1 (D1) and 1600 cm–1 (G), related to disordered (D-band) and crystalline graphitized carbon (G-band), respectively. − The integrated area ratio (ID1/IG) of the G-band to D-band is used to determine the degree of graphitization of carbon in the cathode materials. A full deconvolution of the entire spectrum using Gaussian components was employed to obtain the integrated areas (ID1 and IG). The results illustrate the existence of other bands located at around 1520 cm–1 (D3), 1180 cm–1 (D4), and 1680 cm–1 (D2), corresponding to amorphous carbon, the stretching vibrations of C–C and CC, and a disordered graphitic lattice. ,, The calculated ID1/IG ratios of the NVMP-SS and NVMP-SS cathode materials are 3.333 and 3.44, respectively. This implies that both obtained materials consist of a low degree of graphitic carbon, corresponding to the HRTEM results shown in Figure a. Furthermore, a characteristic band observed at around 975 cm–1 corresponds to the stretching vibrations and symmetric bending modes of tetrahedral PO4 in the Na4VMn­(PO4)3 structure. ,− NVMP-SG reveals a stronger band intensity than that of NVMP-SS. This is because some Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles obtained in the sol–gel method were not completely covered by carbon. They exhibited a loose interaction between Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles and carbon, promoting more exposure of the PO4 unit and consequently bringing about a stronger band intensity. −

Crystal Structure Investigation

X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to evaluate the crystal structure of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C electrodes synthesized via different routes. These results are listed in Figure . The XRD peaks of both cathodes are sharp, indicating that they possess high crystallinity. XRD diffraction patterns of the cathodes can be well indexed to a rhombohedral Na4MnV­(PO4)3 (space group R3̅c). ,, An XRD pattern of carbon is not observed. This suggests that carbon is amorphous, and its content is very low, which is consistent with the HRTEM and Raman results. Weak XRD peaks at a 2θ angle of around 33° are ascribed to a hexagonal Na4P2O7 impurity. , Moreover, Rietveld refinement was done to calculate the lattice constants of the prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes to obtain more detailed information involving the impacts of the preparation methods on the crystal structure of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes, as shown in Figure S4. Calculated lattice parameters are given in Table S1. The obtained a, b, and c lattice parameters of NVMPP-SG and NVMP-SS cathodes are slightly different. The results suggest that the crystal structure of Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes is not significantly affected by the preparation methods.

Local Atomic Structure Investigation

X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) is an element-specific probe that can be used to observe local distortions and short-range order around the element of interest. , The influences of preparation methods on the local atomic structure of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C electrodes synthesized by the sol–gel and solid-state reaction methods were examined. X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) was performed to study the local atomic structure of the synthesized cathode materials, as shown in Figure . X-ray near-edge spectra (XANES) of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C electrodes at the Mn and V k-edges are shown in Figure a,b, respectively. For both Mn and V k-edges, the absorption edge energies of the prepared electrodes are similar, indicating that Mn and V in the electrodes have the same oxidation states. The XANES spectra are identical to those of previous work, which examined Na4VMn­(PO4)3 materials and revealed that Mn and V atoms have average oxidation states of 2+ and 3+, respectively. − The XANES features of both electrodes are slightly different, reflecting that the synthesized cathode materials possess the same average local atomic structure. Moreover, EXAFS fitting was employed to calculate the bond lengths of Mn–O, Mn–Na, Mn–P, V–O, V–Na, and V–P to obtain more detailed information regarding the effects of the preparation routes on the local atomic structure of the synthesized Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes. These results are shown in Figure S5. Tables S2 and S3 give the calculated bond lengths of Mn–O, Mn–Na, Mn–P, V–O, V–Na, and V–P. The calculated bond lengths of Mn–O, Mn–Na, V–O, V–Na, and V–P of the NVMP-SS cathode are smaller than those of NVMP-SG. This corresponds to the slightly smaller unit cell volume of the NVMP-SG cathode material, which is observed from Rietveld refinement results. Additionally, the NVMP-SG material has a larger Mn–O and V–O bond length difference, suggesting more severe MnO6 and VO6 distortion than that for NVMP-SS. This indicates that preparation methods significantly influence the local atomic structure of the synthesized Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes. The Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode synthesized via a solid-state reaction approach (NVMP-SS) has smaller MnO6 and VO6 distortions. This contributes to higher structural stability and, consequently, leads to better cycling stability.

Electrochemical Investigation

A galvanostatic cycling technique was employed to evaluate the electrochemical properties of the prepared electrodes. Evaluated properties, including capacity, cyclability, and rate capability, are revealed in Figures and . Two voltage plateaus at around 3.40 and 3.60 V were observed in the first charging process. During the first discharge process, two voltage plateaus were observed at around 3.40 and 3.50 V. These results are displayed in Figure a. The two voltage plateaus during the initial electrochemical cycling are associated with the V3+/V4+ and Mn2+/Mn3+ redox couples for the lower and higher voltages, respectively. ,, The initial discharge capacity of the NVMP-SG cathode is 90.13 mAh/g, while that of the NVMP-SS cathode is 88.30 mAh/g. Figure b presents differential capacity plots in the first cycle of the prepared Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C electrodes. Strong oxidation peaks are observed at about 3.4 and 3.6 V, corresponding to the oxidation reactions from V3+/V4+ and Mn2+/Mn3+, respectively. Peaks are observed at about 3.30 and 3.55 V, ascribed to the reduction of V4+/V3+ and Mn3+/Mn2+, respectively. Moreover, a reduction peak at around 3.28 V corresponding to the presence of a short voltage plateau at around 3.28 V was found for both cathode materials. This is caused by a structural rearrangement (the transfer of Na ions from Na(1) to Na(2) positions) in the local redox environment, which can be stimulated by local heating during cycling, especially at a high rate. ,, Additionally, the separation between the oxidation peak at around 3.60 V and the reduction peak at about 3.40 V of NVMP-SS is smaller than that of the NVMP-SG cathode in the first cycle. This indicates that an effective carbon network and a tight interaction between Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles induce higher electronic conductivity, contributing to negligible potential polarization. Figure c,d shows the voltage profiles of the prepared electrodes during repeated cycling. The experimental results reveal that the NVMP-SG cathode exhibits a more severe voltage drop and capacity fading, resulting from lower structural stability (larger local structural distortion in MnO6 and VO6 detected from XAS results) than that of the NVMP-SS cathode.

As demonstrated in the results, the carbon network and local atomic structure of the Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes are significantly influenced by the preparation methods, as detected by TEM and XAS techniques. A solid-state reaction yielded a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode with reduced structural distortion, confirmed by the XAS results. This cathode features a more effective carbon network where Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles are uniformly and tightly encapsulated by carbon, as shown in the TEM and Raman results, ensuring strong interfacial contact. These enhance the cathode’s structural stability and electronic conductivity, leading to improved cycling stability and superior rate performance. In contrast, the sol–gel method provides a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode in which Na4VMn­(PO4)3 particles are loosely embedded within carbon sheets. This results in weak interfacial contact, and the obtained cathode suffers from severe structural distortion. These synergistic factors contribute to reduced electronic conductivity, difficult Na ion transport, and low structural stability, resulting in inferior rate capability and cyclability. The obtained results confirm that the electrochemical performance of Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes is highly determined by their local atomic structure and carbon network, as illustrated in Figure .

Conclusions

Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode materials prepared by scalable sol–gel and solid-state reaction methods reveal different carbon networks and atomic structural characteristics. A solid-state reaction method delivered a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode that had a more effective conductive carbon network (complete carbon coating) with tighter interactions and less local atomic structural distortion. This leads to increased electronic conductivity, Na ion diffusion coefficient, and structural stability, consequently enabling higher rate and cycling performance. Conversely, a sol–gel method provided a Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathode that experienced incomplete carbon coating with a looser interaction and greater local atomic structural distortion. This induces lower electronic conductivity, Na ion diffusion coefficient, and structural stability, leading to poorer rate and cycling performance. This study highlights that minimizing distortion in MnO6 and VO6 octahedra and a uniform and continuous carbon network are critical keys to enhancing the Na+ diffusivity and electrochemical performance of Na4VMn­(PO4)3/C cathodes. These findings offer practical guidelines for tailoring the local atomic structure and carbon network architecture of polyanionic cathodes through appropriate preparation techniques to achieve high-performance cathode materials for commercial Na-ion batteries.

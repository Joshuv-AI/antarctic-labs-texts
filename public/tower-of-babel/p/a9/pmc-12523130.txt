Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a highly aggressive and lethal malignancy, characterized by its poor prognosis and aggressive local invasion, with the majority of cases diagnosed at an advanced stage due to the lack of effective early screening methods, significantly limiting treatment options [1]. Radiotherapy (RT) has been reported as one conventional treatment modality for local or metastatic PDAC. Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) not only provides stronger tumoricidal effects but also minimizes damage to surrounding normal tissues, reducing lymphocyte depletion [2]. In addition to its direct tumoricidal effects, radiotherapy, particularly SBRT, has been shown to activate robust anti-tumor immune responses. This is achieved through mechanisms such as promoting the release of interferons (IFNs), cytokines, and tumor antigens, thereby potentially converting ‘cold’ tumors into ‘hot’ tumors [3, 4]. Research has indicated that notable advancements have been achieved in the combination of immunotherapy and radiotherapy for locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC). For instance, a retrospective analysis by Qing-Hua Ke et al. showed that Sintilimab combined with concurrent chemoradiotherapy significantly prolonged both median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to chemoradiotherapy alone [5]. Similar findings were reported by Zhenwei Peng et al., where a combination of nab-paclitaxel, gemcitabine, carilizumab, and radiotherapy led to improved mPFS and mOS, reinforcing the potential of immunotherapy in enhancing chemoradiotherapy outcomes [6]. Moreover, several studies, such as those by Baorui Liu [7] and Valerie Lee [8], reported that combination therapy not only extended survival but also increased the likelihood of surgical resection, with notable improvements in R0 resection rates and pathological responses. These studies collectively suggest that the integration of immunotherapy with radiotherapy in LAPC can significantly enhance survival outcomes. The consistent improvements in median PFS and median OS across different regimens, ranging from sintilimab, carilizumab and trastuzumab to pembrolizumab, highlight the versatility of immunotherapy in combination treatment strategies. Chemotherapy, in conjunction with radiotherapy, continues to represent a cornerstone of pancreatic cancer management. The mechanistic rationale for combining cytotoxic agents with immune checkpoint inhibitors is grounded in their potential synergistic effects, offering a compelling foundation for the development of chemoimmunotherapy strategies [9]. Nevertheless, recent trials have shown that this strategy has yet to deliver transformative benefits in LAPC. In a study by Dan Cao and colleagues, toripalimab (a PD-1 monoclonal antibody) combined with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel proved well tolerated but conferred no significant survival advantage [10]. Similarly, the phase-II trial led by Peter J. O’Dwyer evaluating nivolumab plus gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel failed to out-perform historical outcomes achieved with the standard AG regimen [11]. These results underscore the immunosuppressive tumour micro-environment of pancreatic cancer as a principal barrier to effective chemo-immunotherapy. To fully unlock the therapeutic potential of immunotherapy in LAPC, further refinement of treatment strategies appears necessary. Integrating radiotherapy or simultaneously targeting additional immunosuppressive pathways may help overcome the immunologically "cold" tumor microenvironment and enhance immune responsiveness.

While radiotherapy’s combination with immunotherapy has shown potential to enhance anti-tumor immune responses, offering a more comprehensive approach in cancer treatment [12]. However, pancreatic cancer's dense stroma and low immunogenicity present significant challenges to immune cell infiltration and recognition, limiting further efficacy of conventional immunotherapy [13]. There is an urgent need to find other ways to overcome immune tolerance in pancreatic cancer. Preclinical studies have explored the potential of combining anti-VEGF and anti-PD-L1 antibodies, revealing synergistic anti-tumor effects [14, 15]. In addition to inhibiting angiogenesis, anti-VEGF enhances immune cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment and modulates the suppressive functions of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which are key players in immune evasion. By reducing the immunosuppressive environment, anti-VEGF enhances the efficacy of anti-PD-L1 therapy [16]. Building on the promising preclinical findings, clinical trials have been initiated to explore the efficacy of combining anti-PD-L1 antibodies with anti-angiogenic agents in advanced solid tumors. These trials have demonstrated encouraging anti-tumor activity and acceptable safety profiles [17–20].

The distinct advantages of radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy and targeted therapy in the treatment of PDAC suggest that combination therapy could amplify the synergistic effects among these modalities, positioning it as a promising approach in the future management of PDAC. Moreover, in order to further harness the synergistic effects of targeting both the PD-1/PD-L1 axis and VEGF, bispecific antibodies have been developed. Ivonescimab, a bispecific antibody targeting both PD-1 and VEGF, offers the potential to enhance immune activation while simultaneously inhibiting angiogenesis [20]. Therefore, this Phase Ib/II study aims to investigate the survival benefits of combining Ivonescimab with chemoradiotherapy for patients with LAPC. By leveraging the dual targeting of immune evasion and angiogenesis, this study has the potential to provide a novel and more effective treatment option for PDAC, addressing the significant unmet needs in the management of this aggressive cancer.

Methods/design

Aim and study design

This single-arm, phase Ib/II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Ivonescimab in combination with SBRT and chemotherapy for the treatment for localized advanced pancreatic cancer. The phase Ib is a dose-escalation study utilizing a standard 3 + 3 design. Patients enrolled in this phase will be assigned to a single cohort, and dose adjustments will be made based on the number of DLT observed During the treatment cycles. The objective is to evaluate the combination regimen of Ivonescimab and chemotherapy to determine the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) of Ivonescimab. The second phase focuses on evaluating the efficacy and safety of the regimen using the RP2D established in the first phase. The treatment protocol consists of SBRT combined with Ivonescimab, followed by 8 to 10 cycles of Ivonescimab in combination with mFOLFIRINOX (mFOLFIRINOX; oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2, irinotecan 150 mg/m2, leucovorin 400 mg/m2 and fluorouracil 2400 mg/m2). Subsequently, patients will receive Ivonescimab monotherapy for one year. Figure 1 depicts the study flow. Exploratory endpoints

To evaluate the correlation between PD-L1 expression levels in tumor tissue, the quantity and types of tumor-infiltrating immune cells, gut microbiota, imaging biomarkers, and blood tumor markers with efficacy, including objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).

Peripheral blood (10 ml) and fecal samples will be collected at three predefined time points: prior to the first dose of treatment, at each scheduled imaging evaluation before the initiation of the subsequent treatment cycle, and at the time of radiologically confirmed disease progression. These biospecimens will be used for exploratory analyses of blood-based tumor markers, tumor-infiltrating immune cell profiles, and gut microbiota dynamics. Additionally, at least 15 archived or freshly obtained tumor tissue sections will be collected during the screening period. Where clinically feasible, a biopsy of progressive lesions will be performed at the time of disease progression to enable dynamic assessment of PD-L1 expression and immune infiltration. Radiological evaluations will follow RECIST v1.1 criteria, with baseline imaging completed within 28 days prior to enrollment. During treatment, imaging will be conducted every 8 weeks (± 7 days) for the first 24 weeks and every 12 weeks (± 7 days) thereafter. Unscheduled assessments may be performed as clinically indicated.

PD-L1 expression level: Detect PD-L1 expression in tumors and immune cells through immunohistochemistry, and group analyze its relationship with ORR, PFS, and OS. Using Log rank test and Cox regression model, evaluate its potential as a biomarker for predicting immune combination therapy.

Tumor infiltrating immune cells: Use flow cytometry and immunofluorescence to analyze the number and activation status of immune cell subsets such as CD8 ⁺, CD4 ⁺, Treg, etc. Exploring the correlation between tumor-infiltrating immune cells characteristics and efficacy indicators through multivariate statistical models.

Gut microbiome: Perform 16S rRNA or metagenomic sequencing on fecal samples before and after treatment to analyze microbial diversity and changes in dominant bacterial genera. Evaluate the relationship between gut microbiota, immune status, and treatment response using methods such as LEfSe.

Imaging biomarkers: Evaluate changes in tumor volume, metabolic activity, and texture features based on multi temporal CT/MRI and PET-CT data. Combining statistical and machine learning methods to predict the correlation between image parameters and early therapeutic effects. Supervised machine learning models will be used to predict early treatment response. To ensure methodological rigor, internal validation will be conducted via k-fold cross-validation, with performance evaluated using metrics such as AUC. All statistical analysis were performed in R software.

Blood tumor markers: Dynamically monitor changes in CA19-9, CEA, ctDNA, and inflammatory factors, quantify their trends, and analyze their temporal correlation with efficacy endpoints to identify predictive combinations of blood markers.

Analysis of therapeutic endpoints: The efficacy is mainly based on ORR, PFS, and OS under the RECIST v1.1 standard. Univariate and multivariate analysis will be conducted on all biomarkers, and the model performance will be compared with the decision curve through C-index to establish a comprehensive prediction framework.

Detailed design of Phase I part and definition of DLT, MTD and RP2D

The primary objective of the Phase I part of this study is to evaluate the safety and tolerability of Ivonescimab in combination therapy, with a particular focus on identifying DLT. The onset of DLT will be assessed following Ivonescimab administration in each subject. The DLT evaluation period will extend from day 1 (start of study drug administration) to day 28, during which serious adverse events (SAEs) will be adjudicated by the investigator using CTCAE (version 5.0). DLT is defined as any of the following adverse events occurring during the DLT evaluation period and potentially related to Ivonescimab combination therapy:

Grade 4 haematotoxicity lasting more than 7 days;

Grade 4 haematotoxicity lasting more than 7 days;

Grade ≥ 3 non-haematological toxicity persisting for more than 7 days despite symptomatic treatment;

Grade ≥ 3 non-haematological toxicity persisting for more than 7 days despite symptomatic treatment;

Death not attributable to disease progression or external factors;

Death not attributable to disease progression or external factors;

Any other grade ≥ 3 toxicity that the investigator and sponsor agree is related to the drug after discussion.

Any other grade ≥ 3 toxicity that the investigator and sponsor agree is related to the drug after discussion.

If a patient discontinues study treatment during the DLT observation period for reasons unrelated to the DLT, the patient will be replaced to ensure an accurate assessment of DLT incidence. Subjects must receive at least one dose of chemotherapy during the DLT observation period, allowing for delayed administration and dose adjustments. The investigator will monitor potential DLT in groups of three patients and evaluate them with the advice of the Efficacy and Safety Evaluation Board. Based on the incidence of DLT, the investigator will decide whether to add more cases at the current dose level, escalate to the next dose level, or discontinue the study as recommended by the board. No dose adjustments of Ivonescimab, either up or down, are allowed throughout the study.

DLT is defined as an unacceptable toxic reaction occurring within the 28 day trial period.

MTD is defined as the gradual increase in dose using 10 mg and 20 mg of Ivonescimab to approach the maximum tolerated dose. Researchers administered incrementally increasing doses to patients while closely monitoring for toxic reactions.

RP2D is defined as the recommended drug dose for Phase II clinical trials following Phase Ib trials.

Detailed design of the phase II part

This Phase II study is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of combining Ivonescimab with stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) and mFOLFIRINOX in patients with unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC). The combination of these treatments aims to maximise therapeutic effects through immune modulation and enhanced cytotoxicity. A total of 37 patients with unresectable LAPC who meet the eligibility criteria will be enrolled. Patients will be assigned to different dose levels based on the results of the Phase I part of the study. Patients will initially receive Ivonescimab in combination with SBRT, followed by 8 to 10 cycles (every two weeks, Q2W) of Ivonescimab in combination with mFOLFIRINOX. Patients will then be treated with Ivonescimab monotherapy until they experience intolerable toxicity or disease progression.

Evaluation of therapeutic efficacy

In the Phase II part of the study, patients will undergo assessments at the following time points: within one week prior to treatment, during treatment and at each follow-up visit. The assessments will include a complete blood count (CBC), biochemistry (including aspartate aminotransferase [AST], alanine aminotransferase [ALT], creatinine and blood urea nitrogen [BUN]), urinalysis, thyroid hormone levels, cardiac enzymes and serum tumor markers (e.g. CEA, CA19-9, CA242 and CA50). Imaging studies including abdominal MRI, abdominal CT and chest CT will be performed. Tumor response will be assessed according to RECIST v1.1 criteria. Treatment decisions will be based on imaging results, with treatment continued until the first objective radiographic evidence of disease progression. If treatment is discontinued for reasons other than disease progression, tumor imaging assessments should still be performed at the time of discontinuation. Imaging assessments will continue at protocol-specified time points until one of the following occurs: initiation of new anti-cancer therapy, objective disease progression, death, or study discontinuation, whichever occurs first.

Toxicity and safety

AEs will be monitored throughout the trial and assessed for severity, type, cause, degree of toxicity, and response to treatment using the National Cancer Institute CTCAE (version 5.0). Classification will include an assessment of the relationship between AEs and treatment and the severity of the AEs. For the purposes of this study, AEs are further categorised as either acute or chronic based on the time of onset. Acute AEs are defined as those that occur during or within three months of treatment, whereas chronic AEs are those that occur more than three months after the end of treatment.

Discontinuation/withdrawal/loss to follow-up

If a patient discontinues treatment or withdraws from the study, they will continue to be monitored for adverse events. For patients who discontinue treatment but continue to attend study visits, all procedures outlined in the study protocol (Appendix I) will be followed. Patients who discontinue treatment for reasons unrelated to disease progression will require end-of-treatment imaging. Upon completion of the study protocol, patients will either discontinue treatment, enter the safety follow-up phase, or enter the survival follow-up phase. If a patient misses a scheduled visit, the investigator will make every effort to contact the patient, reschedule the visit, and document the outcome promptly.

Statistical analysis

Recent studies focusing on chemoradiotherapy alone, such as those by Mellon et al. and Goyal et al., have reported a median PFS of around 11 months [21, 22]. Similarly, studies in patients with LAPC have demonstrated that combining immunotherapy with chemoradiotherapy can achieve a median PFS of approximately 12 months (e.g., Peng et al. 2023; Ke et al. 2024) [5, 6]. These findings suggest that through the combination of radiotherapy and systemic therapy, a median PFS of approximately 12 months is achievable in LAPC, although the long-term benefits of adding immunotherapy require further investigation. Ivonescimab is a bispecific antibody targeting both PD-1 and VEGF, with a Dual mechanism that modulates the immune microenvironment and tumor angiogenesis. This biological synergy provides a rationale for enhanced therapeutic efficacy in a multi-modality setting. Preliminary clinical observations suggests that the combination of Ivonescimab with SBRT and chemotherapy may extend PFS to 15 months, potentially Due to enhanced immunomodulatory effects. Therefore, setting a 15-month median PFS as the efficacy assumption in this study is feasible and is partially supported by clinical data from existing multi-modality treatment studies. The statistical hypothesis assumes a one-sided α = 0.05 and power of 0.8, with an anticipated enrollment period of 24 months and a follow-up period of 24 months. The null hypothesis (H0) assumes a median PFS of 9.0 months, while the alternative hypothesis (H1) expects a median PFS of 15 months. Based on these parameters, the calculated sample size for the Phase II part is 31, and accounting for a 10% dropout rate, the final planned sample size is 34. All statistical analyses will be conducted using R software (version 4.0.2 or higher), with a one-sided 0.05 superiority hypothesis test. Intergroup comparisons will report 95% confidence intervals and p-values. Unless otherwise specified, quantitative data will be summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (range), and categorical data will be summarized as frequency (percentage).

For the purposes of data handling and statistical analysis, the following operational definitions will be applied to classify types of patient discontinuation:

Dropout is defined as a participant who discontinues the study before completing the protocol-defined treatment or required outcome assessments, regardless of the reason, provided there is a record of treatment discontinuation.

Loss to follow-up refers to participants who become unreachable despite reasonable attempts to contact them and who fail to attend two or more scheduled visits without formally withdrawing consent.

Withdrawal is defined as a participant who explicitly communicates their decision to discontinue all study-related procedures and follow-up, with or without specifying a reason, typically documented by a signed or verbal withdrawal statement.

These categories will be documented separately and appropriately addressed in the statistical analysis. Participants with missing endpoint data due to any of these reasons will be included in comprehensive missing data strategy, as appropriate.

To ensure methodological rigor and regulatory compliance, we employ a comprehensive missing data strategy comprising multiple imputation (MI), intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis, and sensitivity analyses. MI, assuming data are missing at random (MAR), preserves statistical power and minimizes bias for time-to-event outcomes in this single-arm, small-sample study. ITT analysis includes all enrolled patients, mitigating attrition bias and providing conservative estimates of efficacy. Sensitivity analyses under alternative assumptions (e.g., worst-case imputation, complete-case analysis) address potential missing not at random (MNAR) scenarios, enhancing the robustness and transparency of results [23, 24].

Quality control and data collection

In compliance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines, the study will adhere to rigorous quality assurance and control measures to ensure the integrity and reliability of the clinical trial. Investigators will establish and maintain a robust quality assurance and quality control system, following standardized operating procedures, to ensure that the trial is conducted in accordance with the protocol, GCP guidelines, and applicable regulatory requirements. Clinical trial documentation will be maintained in compliance with GCP requirements. The Department of Radiation Oncology Research Centre of Shandong Cancer Hospital will archive and manage relevant data for a minimum of five years and ensure accessibility. File storage will take into account both safety and environmental risks.

Discussion

This study is the first to explore the combination of Ivonescimab with radiotherapy and chemotherapy for the treatment of patients with LAPC. Ivonescimab, a novel "dual-target synergistic anti-tumor" drug developed in China, was initially approved for specific types of non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer [20]. As a humanized bispecific IgG1 antibody targeting both VEGF and PD-1, Ivonescimab exhibits a unique mechanism of action that simultaneously modulates immune response and inhibits tumor angiogenesis, offering enhanced therapeutic efficacy against PDAC [25]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated its potent anti-tumor activity, justifying its evaluation in more complex treatment regimens like those involving radiotherapy and chemotherapy [26].

Given the highly vascularized and immunosuppressive microenvironment of LAPC, traditional therapies often fail to achieve satisfactory outcomes [27]. The dual targeting of PD-1 and VEGF by Ivonescimab is expected to address these challenges, potentially overcoming resistance to current first-line treatment options. Thus, combining Ivonescimab with radiotherapy and chemotherapy may provide a more comprehensive therapeutic approach.

In addition to evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of Ivonescimab, we rigorously assessed the safety profiles of both Ivonescimab and SBRT, recognizing that AEs are equally critical to patient care. According to existing clinical reports, immune-related adverse events, such as rash, pruritus, and thyroid dysfunction, are relatively mild; a minority of patients may experience grade 3 hepatotoxicity or colitis, which can be effectively managed with corticosteroids. Overall, Ivonescimab demonstrates a favorable safety profile, with no emergent, uncontrollable toxicities observed. Most AEs are manageable with standard interventions [28, 29]. SBRT, as a matured and refined radiotherapy technique, has been widely applied in the treatment of LAPC. Gastrointestinal toxicity, particularly to the stomach and duodenum, remains the primary constraint in delivering SBRT for LAPC, with typical manifestations including mucosal ulceration, bleeding, anorexia, and progressive organ dysfunction [30]. Given that Ivonescimab exerts dual blockade of PD-1 and VEGF, its concomitant use could theoretically amplify bleeding risk [31]. Accordingly, treatment emergent AEs, with particular attention to gastrointestinal haemorrhage, will be documented through systematic monitoring and detailed reporting to ensure that patient risk remains within acceptable bounds. From a technical perspective, SBRT delivery to the pancreatic region is challenged by several factors. These include continuous respiratory and peristaltic motion of retroperitoneal organs, the low dose-tolerance thresholds of neighbouring organs at risk (OARs) which impede dose escalation, and variations in gastrointestinal filling that can displace dose distributions and compromise target reproducibility [32]. To reconcile adequate tumour dosing with stringent normal tissue protection, the trial integrates image-guided guidance and adaptive radiotherapy. Daily adaptive replanning is facilitated by onboard imaging and real time recontouring of organs at risk, thereby reducing the likelihood of unintended overdose and enhancing treatment precision. The synergistic deployment of these technologies offers a feasible route to “tolerable dose intensification”, an approach that the forthcoming clinical study will further interrogate for both efficacy and safety.

Current clinical trials have primarily focused on Ivonescimab as a monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy [31, 33]. This trial is a novel approach that combines both radiotherapy and chemotherapy, aiming for a synergistic effect to improve overall outcomes. This phase Ib/II trial aims to generate critical evidence on the feasibility, safety, and preliminary efficacy of a novel triple-modality regimen for LAPC. Ivonescimab, a bispecific IgG1 antibody targeting both PD-1 and VEGF pathways, exerts dual functionalities of immune activation and vascular modulation, offering a promising strategy to reprogram the profoundly immunosuppressive microenvironment characteristic of LAPC. Conventional PD-1 blockade has demonstrated limited efficacy in this immunologically “cold” tumour, in part due to the highly aberrant vasculature that impairs both T cell infiltration and drug delivery [34]. By inhibiting VEGF signaling, Ivonescimab promotes vascular normalization, improving tumour perfusion, alleviating hypoxia-induced immunosuppression, and facilitating T cell trafficking into tumour tissue [35]. These effects may enhance radiosensitivity and potentiate immune checkpoint blockade. When combined with SBRT, which induces immunogenic cell death, and chemotherapy, Ivonescimab may act synergistically to enhance anti-tumour immune responses. This mechanistically guided triple approach holds the potential to overcome immune exclusion and expand the therapeutic horizon in pancreatic cancer.

The integration of Ivonescimab—a bispecific antibody that simultaneously blocks PD-1 and VEGF signalling—with SBRT and chemotherapy represents a mechanistically informed shift in LAPC management, balancing immune activation with tumour-microenvironment remodelling. Success in this study will support the broader clinical application of Ivonescimab in other solid tumors driven by VEGF and PD-1 pathways. If validated in larger cohorts, this strategy may mark a pivotal milestone in the evolution of multimodal therapy for pancreatic cancer and exert a transformative influence across the solid-tumour field.

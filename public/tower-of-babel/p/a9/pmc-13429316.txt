Registry‐Based Analysis of Treatment and Retreatment Attacks of Hereditary Angioedema
Authors: Roman Hakl, Jan Baroš, Marta Sobotková, Radana Zachová, Pavlína Králíčková, Martina Vachová, Irena Krčmová, Michal Svoboda, Vendula Szmucová, Monika Skotáková, Jiří Litzman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary Retreatment occurs in approximately 15% of treated hereditary angioedema attacks in real‐world practice.Abdominal attacks, multisite involvement and treatment choice are associated with retreatment occurrence.

To the Editor,

Hereditary angioedema (HAE) is a rare genetic disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of subcutaneous or submucosal swelling caused by dysregulation of the kallikrein–kinin system and excessive bradykinin production [1]. Acute attacks are treated with on‐demand therapies including plasma‐derived or recombinant C1‐inhibitor concentrates and the bradykinin B2‐receptor antagonist icatibant [1]. Although these therapies are generally effective, some attacks require repeated treatment [2, 3, 4]. However, real‐world data identifying predictors of retreatment remain limited. We therefore analysed retreatment frequency and its clinical predictors using data from the Czech national registry of hereditary angioedema.

This retrospective analysis included data from the Czech national registry of primary immunodeficiencies, which captures essentially all diagnosed HAE patients nationwide. Patients were followed at four designated national HAE centres. Attacks recorded between March 2012 and September 2023 were analysed. All attacks were patient‐reported, electronically recorded and physician‐validated during routine visits. Retreatment was defined as additional on‐demand therapy within 48 h for the same attack. Only attacks with complete treatment and localization data were analysed, with no major missing data issues identified. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study was approved by the St Anne's University Hospital Ethics Committee (20JS/2024).

Retreatment was defined as administration of the same or another on‐demand therapy within 48 h of the initial dose. On‐demand treatments included icatibant, pasteurized nanofiltered plasma‐derived C1‐inhibitor (pnf C1‐INH) and recombinant human C1‐INH (rh C1‐INH). Drug selection reflected routine clinical practice and was based on physician decision in consultation with the patient. Statistical analysis was performed in the programming language R in the integrated development environment R studio. Results with a p‐value below 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Associations between variables and retreatment were analysed using generalized estimating equations (GEE) to account for clustering of repeated attacks within patients. The multivariable model included baseline variables such as age, sex, HAE type, long‐term prophylaxis and variables with a p‐value < 0.1 from the univariable analysis.

In total, 8448 attacks in 175 patients were reported, of which 7001 attacks in 167 patients (90 females, 77 males; 143 HAE‐1, 24 HAE‐2; mean age 25.0 years) were actively treated. Long‐term prophylaxis was used in 100 patients (59.9%). Retreated HAE attacks were reported in 1024 attacks (14.6%) in 101 patients (43 males, 58 females; 87 with HAE‐1, 14 with HAE‐2; mean age 24.3; 73 with long‐term prophylaxis). A total of 1447 attacks (17.1%) were not treated, likely due to mild symptoms, spontaneous resolution, patient choice, or limited access to medication at the time of the attack. Icatibant was used as first‐line treatment in 4459 attacks (63.7%). Of these, 772 attacks (17.3%) required additional treatment within the same attack episode. Pnf C1‐INH was used in 1243 attacks (17.8%), with 69 attacks (5.6%) requiring further treatment. Rh C1‐INH was used in 961 attacks (13.7%), of which 150 attacks (15.6%) required repeated treatment.

In univariable analysis, abdominal involvement, number of affected anatomical sites, long‐term prophylaxis, age and treatment type were associated with retreatment. In multivariable analysis, adjusting for baseline characteristics and statistically significant univariable predictors, treatments with pnf C1‐INH and rh C1‐INH were associated with lower odds of retreatment compared with icatibant (OR 0.191, 95% CI: 0.131–0.279; p < 0.001) and (OR 0.679, 95% CI: 0.526–0.877; p = 0.003), respectively. Abdominal involvement remained associated with higher odds of retreatment (OR 1.975, 95% CI: 1.596–2.445; p < 0.001), and each additional affected anatomical site increased the odds of retreatment (OR 1.805, 95% CI: 1.527–2.134; p < 0.001). Age was associated with a lower likelihood of retreatment (OR 0.977, 95% CI: 0.965–0.989; p < 0.001). Long‐term prophylaxis was no longer statistically significant in the multivariable model.

These findings suggest that both disease severity and treatment characteristics influence the likelihood of repeated dosing during HAE attacks. Our results confirm that retreatment is relatively common in real‐world management of HAE, occurring in approximately 15% of treated attacks. This rate is comparable with previously reported clinical trial data, although direct comparisons are limited by differences in study design and dosing protocols [2, 3, 5, 6, 7]. Untreated attacks (17.1%) likely represented milder episodes and may have influenced the observed retreatment rates.

Attack severity appeared to be a key determinant of retreatment. Abdominal attacks and attacks involving multiple anatomical sites were statistically significantly more likely to require repeated treatment, suggesting that more extensive or systemic activation of the bradykinin pathway may contribute to more difficult‐to‐control attacks. Drug‐specific pharmacological properties may also influence retreatment risk. Icatibant, a short‐acting bradykinin B2‐receptor antagonist, has a relatively short half‐life, which may contribute to higher retreatment rates. In contrast, both pnf C1‐INH and rh C1‐INH were associated with lower odds of retreatment in the multivariable model. However, treatment allocation was not randomized and may have been influenced by attack severity or other clinical factors, limiting causal interpretation of these findings.

Increasing age was associated with a lower likelihood of retreatment, possibly reflecting differences in disease expression or treatment behavior. Long‐term prophylaxis showed a non‐statistically significant trend towards higher retreatment, likely reflecting greater disease severity.

In conclusion, retreatment of HAE attacks in real‐world practice is primarily influenced by attack severity and treatment choice. Abdominal involvement and multisite attacks were independent predictors, whereas both pnf C1‐INH and rh C1‐INH were associated with lower retreatment odds compared with icatibant. Increasing age was linked to lower retreatment risk, whereas long‐term prophylaxis showed only a trend. These findings may help anticipate attacks requiring repeated dosing and support individualized treatment strategies.

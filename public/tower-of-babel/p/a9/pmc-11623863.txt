Cross-sectional study exploring the prevalence and clinical manifestations of acute diarrhea among under-5 children in primary care hospital in Democratic Republic of the Congo
Authors: Rodrigue Fikiri BAVURHE, Maher Ali RUSHO, Alain BALUME, Farheen NAAZ, Elie KIHANDUKA, Babar ALI, Areeba FAREED, Francois RHUGENDABANGA, Mahammed Khan SUHEB, Samson HANGI, Excellent RUGENDABANGA, Elysée BYIRINGIRO, Hugues CAKIRWA, Fabien BALAGIZI, Davis BANTURAKI, Samuel A. ABSTRACT
Background:Acute diarrhea (AD), which is defined as frequent passing of liquid stools compared to normal, is a serious and worrying problem and remains a concern for healthcare systems because of its high mortality cause in children under 5 years old. The authors’ study aimed to present the prevalence and to describe the clinical manifestation of AD among under-5 children.Methods:From June 2022 to May 2023, the authors conducted a retrospective, descriptive and cross-sectional study including all patients aged 0–5 years hospitalized for AD.Results:Out of 512 patients, only 197 (38.5%) children with AD were selected for our study. The average age is 25.5 months, and the sex ratio is 1.11. Some families (75.1%) have clean latrines, and 21.8% use water from the river. Inaccessibility to clean water and intolerance or food poisoning were the causes of acute diarrhea in children. Major signs and symptoms are fever, dehydration and vomiting. Weight loss and malnutrition are the major complications of AD in children. The treatment of AD is provided by oral rehydration solutions and antibiotics.Conclusion:The study highlights the significant prevalence of acute diarrhea among under-5 children underscores the importance of preventive measures and government intervention, such as the introduction of rotavirus vaccination. However, conclusions regarding prevalence rates should be interpreted with caution due to the lack of detailed population data.

The health system of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DR Congo) has been destabilized by various infectious epidemics, including Ebola, Meningitis, Dengue fever and the pandemic of COVID-191. Acute diarrhea (AD) in children is added to these scourges that burden the health system of DR Congo. It remains a serious global health problem and remains a concern in healthcare systems of poor countries and the tropics2, as AD is the second cause of mortality among children under-5 years with a high rate of deaths. Approximately 525 000 children have been killed by AD each year, and 1.7 billion cases of acute diarrhea recorded worldwide based on data from the WHO3.

AD is defined as the frequent emission of liquid or soft stools compared to normal emission and lasts more than 3 days, dehydrating young children and newborns by depriving them of mineral salts essential for life4. It results from the various symptoms of infections caused by microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses or parasites transmitted from contaminated water or contaminated food in the event of insufficient hygiene. Children under 5 with immunosuppression and in a state of malnutrition are the most exposed and vulnerable to AD caused by these infections, which put their lives in danger or at risk of death4,5.

AD is potentially serious among under-5 children with variations in clinical manifestations whose analysis is essential. The rapid elimination of watery stools accompanied by vomiting and sweating leads to a loss of essential electrolytes (sodium, chloride, potassium and bicarbonate) and dehydration. In the event that patients lose too much water and electrolytes, it may become difficult to compensate for the lost volumes of nutrients and can present with a myriad of clinical features4–6. Vigilance is necessary because the aggravation of symptoms is very likely, and any exaggerated persistence must seek the risk of passage to chronicity. The essential prescriptions for emergency rehydration consist of Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) with the addition of anti-microbial therapy once the causative pathogen is isolated. Complementary treatment based on Zinc sulphate is also dispensable. However, population-based interventions aim to prevent acute diarrhea through better and safer access to drinking water and improve hygiene to reduce the risk of death6,7.

We conducted a retrospective and descriptive study on patients with AD attending the Department of Pediatrics of a primary care hospital. This study aimed to present the prevalence and to describe the clinical manifestations of AD in children from the eastern region of the DR Congo.

Methods

Study area and design

The study adhered to the STROSS criteria8. The retrospective study was conducted over a period from June 2022 to May 2023 at the Department of Pediatrics of a primary care hospital, located in the eastern region of the DR Congo. We conducted a retrospective, descriptive and documentary cross-sectional study on patients with AD and investigated the epidemiological and clinical characteristics of AD in children under the age of 5 years.

Sampling method, size and variables

From June 2022 to May 2023, a total of 512 patients were admitted to the Department of Pediatrics. Our study included all patients aged from 0 to 5 years who were hospitalized for confirmed AD. Children diagnosed with diarrhea or listed complications of diarrhea and symptoms were included to improve comprehensive case findings. Those who presented with symptoms but whose diagnosis was not confirmed and others who did not register according to the criteria were excluded, and we excluded the records of patients with insufficient data and patients not diagnosed with acute diarrhea.

The variables were divided into epidemiological, clinical and therapeutic factors that influence the presentation and management of AD. Demographic factors include; age distribution and hospital distribution. In the clinic, we noted elements of diagnosis, etiology and risk factors, paraclinical exploration and complications of AD. Data on the management of diarrhea in children and the follow-up of patient’s symptoms were also obtained.

Ethical consideration, source of data and analysis tools

It was believed that the study would contribute to the provision of quality care to the under-5 children suffering from AD. Thus, permission to conduct this study was granted by the Committees of Bagira Reference Hospital. After obtaining the consent of the higher authorities, the data were collected from the hospital register of patients admitted to the Department of Pediatrics. The study consisted of a confidential chart review; no informed consent was deemed necessary due to the retrospective nature of the study. We took precautions by separating the patient’s identification data with confidentiality codes.

The results are grouped and presented in tabular form. For the analysis of the results, we used Microsoft Word 2016 for Windows (Version 19, Microsoft Inc.) and Microsoft Excel 2016 for Windows (Version 19, Microsoft Inc.).

Results

Out of 512 children that were admitted to the Department of Pediatrics during our retrospective study period of over one year, only 197 children (38.5%) with AD were selected for our study. The average age is 25.5 months, and 70 (35.6%) of children aged between 13 and 24 months were the most affected by AD. Children that are 6 months old were the least affected by acute diarrhea. The male sex is slightly predominant with 52.8% of cases, and the sex ratio is 1.11 in children with acute diarrhea. For the age of the mother, 56.4% of women under 24 were recorded, while 33% of women were aged between 24 and 29 years old. 65 (33%) of the families have a total number of fewer than 5 people, and 161 (81.7%) of the families contain more children under the age of 5.

Contributing factors or causative agent of acute diarrhea in children.

There are three forms of diarrhea that were reported: (1) Secretory diarrhea defined as the presence of a substance (toxic or bacterial), (2) Inflammatory diarrhea which is associated with diseases that cause inflammation or ulceration of the intestinal mucosa and (3) Diarrhea by malabsorption due to a reduction in the absorptive surface of the intestine. The clinical characteristics of AD in children are shown in Table 3. Sixty-four percent of children experienced inflammatory diarrhea followed by secretory diarrhea and diarrhea by malabsorption. Regarding the signs and symptoms, we recorded fever, vomiting, abdominal swelling and pain, dehydration and headache. Direct stool examination and parasitological stool examination were paraclinical explorations frequently used to confirm the diagnosis and etiology of acute diarrhea in children. Table 4 shows us the results of the observation of the stools of AD, and we have the results on the aspect or appearance of which 79.2% were liquid, and 20.8% were pasty. The daily frequency of stool emission was evaluated at 51.3% of 3 emissions per day, followed by 25.4% of 4 stools of daily emissions. Weight loss, malnutrition and dehydration are the major complications of AD in children. These results are represented in Graph 2. Cholera, gastroenteritis, botulism, ulcerative colitis and salmonellosis were pathologies associated with AD in children under 5 years of age.

The principle of managing AD in children is sufficient fluid intake and correct nutritional intake. The administration of zinc and vitamin A is also important and anti-microbial therapy (i.e. antibiotics) must be commenced when the causative pathogen is confirmed. Graph 3 shows us the result of the treatment in which the ORS and antibiotics were widely used. In this study, the antibiogram was used to determine the most effective antibiotic treatment against infections causing acute diarrhea in children who participated in this study. The primary antibiotics used included Azithromycin, oral Vancomycin, and Ciprofloxacin as first-line treatments. For viral diarrhea, we relied on oral rehydration therapy alone. However, in a few cases of non-severe diarrhea, Metronidazole was also administered. In this study, children were given metronidazole based on their body weight at 20 milligrams per kilogram per day and divided into 3 daily doses. For oral rehydration with oral rehydration solution, 100 milliliters per kilogram were given. Ciprofloxacin was given at 15 milligrams per kilogram per day for 10 days. Vancomycin was given orally at a dose of 10 milligrams per kilogram every 6 hours for 10 days. Azithromycin was given at a dose of 10 milligrams per kilogram per day for 3 days. The follow-up of our patients was favorable to 84.4% of patients. However, 4.6% of the children recovered with sequelae such as weight loss, malnutrition and unfortunately, 10.6% of our patients passed away because of the complications associated with AD.

Discussion

During our study period, we recorded 197 children admitted for acute diarrhea. The incidence of acute diarrhea is very high in low-income and developing countries, with a prevalence close to some studies conducted in African and Asian countries4,7. This is the first study on acute diarrhea in children under the age of 5 in Bukavu, but also in the province of South Kivu, and it found a high prevalence of diarrhea when compared to other pathologies. Our study found that the boys aged 1–2 years living in families of several people are the most affected by acute diarrhea. In our study children aged between 13 and 24 months were the most affected age group. The findings from Thailand also revealed that children from 6 to 23 months were at a higher risk of developing diarrhea than other age groups9. We found out that acute diarrhea in this region had a long history, with mortality rate of children under the age of 5 always increasing substantially while all other age groups were mostly unaffected by the disease or affected only minimally. In DR Congo, access to standard healthcare and food security or balanced diets were still a major problem, as poverty was high, more so malnutrition. These trends have greatly impacted the pitiful living conditions of the citizens here, who in large part don’t have access to good drinking water and enough food.

Environmental factors among mothers of children living in Bagira may have an indirect impact on the outbreak of acute diarrhea. The investigation revealed that despite most participants maintaining clean latrines, most utilized traditional types. The minority of women used liquid soaps, but 78.2% of women cleaned water treated with drugs against microbes. Interestingly, protected dug wells or spring water was the source for 78.2% of children. Acute diarrhea has become a serious health issue which needs to be considered with all seriousness. The health of children under the age of 5, who are suffering from the disease, will always be in danger if rapid clinical response is not put in place. Worse, the children affected may meet their untimely death if serious measures by both health personnel and the government officials are not affected1.

According to WHO, diarrheal disease is the third leading cause of mortality in children aged 1–59 months. It is both preventable and treatable. Scientifically, acute diarrhea has been around since time immemorial and has been known as one of the diseases that cause serious harm to people, especially children under the age of five. It has also been known that in serious cases, children who survive an episode of diarrhea are more likely to suffer from lifelong cognitive and physical impairments especially when they are battling with other disease conditions. Diarrhea, also known as gastroenteritis, is caused by viral and bacterial germs, and in some cases by parasites. The disease, invariably spread from the waste of an infected person and is transferred to a healthy person via the mouth through contaminated food, water, hands, or objects. Body fluid is always depleted during a diarrhea episode. The disease becomes serious, if not deadly, when much body fluid is lost. Rapid dehydration in children is usually deadly and can lead to instant death. Early scientists and doctors found out the chief causes of diarrhea to be the following (1): inadequate water supply/unsafe drinking water (2); poor sanitation (3); poor knowledge of hygienic practices (4); limited access to healthcare (5); poor knowledge of diarrhea prevention and treatment10,11.

Our study revealed that oral rehydration solution (ORS) is the most successful therapy option for diarrhea. One study discovered that 72.1% of Pakistani mothers have used ORS to treat diarrhea, showing its widespread use in society11. However, preparing ORS needs accuracy, which is why many people misuse it. As a result, intensive awareness programs should be launched to educate parents on the proper use and method of utilizing ORS across the country in DR Congo. Supplementing with antimicrobials, zinc, and vitamin A has also been effective.

Strengths

The study of diarrhea, especially acute diarrhea, is very important because people need to be educated about the dangerous and deadly nature of the disease. They need to be told the various ways they should follow to be safe from the disease, or else fall into the risk of being wiped out by it due to dehydration. Through our study, we have come to find out that acute diarrhea in DR Congo has a long history, and the disease affects mostly children under the age of 5 and elderly people living in poor conditions. The healthcare facilities are not well equipped and poverty is at the forefront in regions such as Bukavu and the province of South Kivu, thereby giving rise to the disease among other factors. The study’s findings can be used as baseline data for policymakers in the establishment of national infants and young health guidelines, as well as the achievement of Millennium Development Goals in the decrease of child mortality.

Recommendations

Many recommendations exist but it is not possible to define therapeutic indications applicable to all cases of acute diarrhea. Antibiotic treatment is reserved for certain infectious etiologies and antidiarrheal drugs are used in addition. Early refeeding is systematic. Any exaggerated persistence of acute diarrhea should lead to a search for a risk of becoming chronic. The main preventive measures against acute diarrhea in children are accessibility to drinking water, a healthy diet, good sanitary hygiene, hand washing with soap or hydro-alcoholic gel, exclusive breastfeeding until 6 months. Caregivers must inform the population of the risks of the disease and health education on the methods of spreading infections must be possible. Vaccination against rotavirus should also be implemented by the Congolese government.

Perspectives

A lack of access to drinking water and good hygiene are factors of risk of acute diarrhea, the inflammatory type of which is the most encountered in our study environment, vomiting and fever are the most accompanying signs in our study, microscopic examination of stools has enabled us to carry out paraclinical investigations, acuity is the most affected in our study.

We hope that the results of our study will be used by health authorities in their decision-making on the control and treatment of acute diarrhea in children in our region and in our country and especially in the preventive component. This study will provide necessary elements for other researchers to carry out their research on acute diarrhea in children.

Limitations

Our study has the following weaknesses: it was not conducted in a community but only in a hospital. Due to very limited resources, we did not conduct this study on the extent of health structures in the city of Bukavu. This during the strength of our study is certain.

Conclusion

The study highlights the significant prevalence of acute diarrhea among under-5 children underscores the importance of preventive measures like improving access to clean drinking water, promoting a healthy diet, ensuring proper sanitary hygiene, encouraging hand washing with soap or hydro-alcoholic gel, and advocating for exclusive breastfeeding,. and government intervention, such as the introduction of rotavirus vaccination. However, conclusions regarding prevalence rates should be interpreted with caution due to the lack of detailed population data.

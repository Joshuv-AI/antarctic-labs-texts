Clinical Profile of Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease and its Correlation with Metabolic Syndrome and Cardiovascular Risk
Authors: Zahaan Vakil, Kiran Ahire
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) has been projected, within the next 20 years, to become the major cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality as well as a leading indication for liver transplantation. Affected South Asian Indians are at higher risk for the development of metabolic syndrome (MS), type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. There is a dearth of data related to NAFLD and its various sequelae and correlation with cardiovascular disease in South Asia.Materials and Methods:It was an observational, prospective study conducted over 2 years on 80 patients in a tertiary care hospital in Mumbai. All patients diagnosed with NAFLD were investigated for MS according to the National Cholesterol Education Program Adult Treatment Panel III criteria, and a relationship between NAFLD and MS was correlated.Results:MS was found to be present in 72.5% of the patients. Significant results were obtained while correlating MS parameters and fatty liver grading, implying that patients with a higher fatty liver grading were more likely to have derangements in metabolic markers.Conclusion:NAFLD was found to be associated with an increased incidence of MS and thereby a higher risk of cardiovascular disease, warranting a high index of suspicion for both. A higher ultrasound grading of fatty liver was found to be associated with an increased incidence of MS.

Introduction

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is an umbrella term, comprising nonalcoholic fatty liver (NAFL) and nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH).[1] NAFL is characterized by steatosis of the liver, involving >5% of parenchyma, with no evidence of hepatocyte injury, whereas NASH, defined in histological terms, is a necroinflammatory process with liver cell injury under a background of steatosis.[2]

Estimates have been posited suggesting the current incidence of NAFLD to be 20%–30% in Western countries and 5%–18% in Asia.[1] The prevalence of NAFLD is suggested to be increasing worldwide with each passing year, possibly owing to the current trends in dietary irresponsibility and preponderance toward a sedentary lifestyle.[1] In addition, there has been a linear rise of NAFLD with that of diabetes and metabolic syndrome (MS).[3] A study from the United States demonstrated that the incidence of NAFLD was 10% higher in overweight individuals in comparison to lean persons.[3] NAFLD has been projected, within the next 20 years, to become the major cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality as well as a leading indication for liver transplantation.[4] The pathogenetic processes that underscore NAFLD typically lead to death by cardiovascular disease with liver-related mortality only accounting for 5% in these individuals.[5,6] This liver disease appears to be most strongly associated with obesity and insulin resistance states including diabetes and with other features of the MS, such as high triglycerides and low high-density lipoprotein (HDL). It appears to be more common in men, the incidence increasing with advancing age, and in postmenopausal women. More advanced stages of NAFLD are associated with older age, higher body mass index (BMI), diabetes, hypertension, high triglycerides, and/or insulin resistance.[7,8] Some studies have shown that South Asian Indians are also at higher risk for the development of MS, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease, suggesting the possibility of gene variants that impact shared molecular pathways among these clinical entities.[9,10]

MS is a conglomerate of cardiovascular risk factors which predispose a person to developing type II diabetes and cardiovascular disease.[2] Studies have demonstrated a consistent rise in the incidence of NAFLD with the rise in incidence of features of MS based on the National Cholesterol Education Program Adult Treatment Panel (NCEP ATP III) criteria.[2] Interestingly, it has also been shown that T2DM patients with NAFLD can have normal liver function tests, which suggests that the prevalence of NAFLD in T2DM patients is much higher than reported in this patient population.[11] T2DM patients also display a higher risk of developing NASH as well as a 2–4-fold greater risk of fatty liver-associated complications.[11,12]

There is a dearth of data related to NAFLD in South Asia and its various sequelae, and further research is needed for better detailing of the complications involved. Studies elaborating the correlation of NAFLD with cardiovascular disease are also somewhat lacking.

Materials and Methods

All the procedures followed were in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation and with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, amended in 2013. The study followed a prospective observational model and was conducted over a period of 2 years in a tertiary care hospital in Mumbai. The sample under observation included 80 participants comprising those patients attending OPD as well as indoor patients admitted under the department of general medicine and gastroenterology.

Criteria for including participants in the study were as follows: all those patients who were diagnosed with NAFLD on ultrasound with no history of significant alcohol intake (i.e., not exceeding the daily permissible quantity), testing negative for hepatitis B and C, and history of treatment with hepatotoxic drugs. Candidates excluded were as follows: those patients who were not willing to participate, patients with acute liver disease, and pregnant females. Patients testing positive for any other cause of liver disease (hepatitis B and C and history of treatment with hepatotoxic drugs) were also excluded from the study.

Detailed history, anthropometry, and clinical examination were carried out after taking informed consent of the patient. All patients in the study underwent routine investigations including complete blood counts, blood sugar, liver function tests, hepatitis B surface antigen, anti-hepatitis C virus, and lipid profile and abdominal ultrasound as prescribed by the treating physician.

Abdominal ultrasound grading of fatty liver was noted and analyzed for any correlation with disease severity.[13] All patients diagnosed with NAFLD were investigated for MS according to the NCEP ATP III criteria, and a relationship between NAFLD and MS was correlated.[10]

Nonrandomized convenient sampling method was used. Taking into account the prevalence of NAFLD being 28% in the Indian population,[14] with a precision of 10%, a sample size of 80 was calculated. Data collection was done on specially designed case record forms (sample enclosed). The collected data were then fed into Microsoft Excel. Tests of significance and correlation were performed using the IBM SPSS software for data analysis (Statistical Package for Social Sciences [SPSS] Version 27. IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

This study was undertaken for assessing the clinical profile of NAFLD and its correlation with MS and cardiovascular risk. Of the study sample, 32.5% belonged to the age group of 41–50 years while 21.25% of the patients each belonged to the age group of 31–40 years and 51–60 years, respectively. The mean age of the patients was 45.1 years. Males comprised 55% of the patients while the remaining were females. The mean weight and mean height of the patients were 74.69 kg and 160.9 cm, respectively. The mean BMI of the patients was 28.8 kg/m2 while the mean waist circumference was 88.47 cm.

Clinical findings in the study participants with MS were significantly greater than those without, the most common feature being hepatomegaly (53.45%; P = 0.027) and the most common complaint at presentation being abdominal pain (43%; P = 0.001). Pedal edema while being the least common clinical feature was seen in 32% of the study participants with MS. In those without MS, hepatomegaly and abdominal pain were also the most common clinical features each accounting for 36.36% of participants in the subgroup [Table 1].

Discussion

The rising incidence of obesity is associated with many concurrent health complications, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and NAFLD. NAFLD is increasingly being recognized as a major cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality among 15%–40% of the general population.[13]

The overall prevalence of NAFLD in Western countries varies from 15% to 40% and in Asian countries from 9% to 40%. Epidemiological studies suggest the prevalence of NAFLD to be around 9%–32% in general Indian population, with a higher incidence among overweight/obese and diabetic/prediabetic patients. The current epidemics of obesity and diabetes among adults and children residing in both developed and developing countries suggest that the prevalence of NAFLD is expected to increase further in future.[15,16] Even in the absence of alcohol intake, patients who have one or more components of the MS with insulin resistance develop hepatic steatosis due to increased lipolysis and increased delivery of fatty acids from adipose tissue to liver.[17,18]

The mean age of the patients in this study was 45.1 years and consisted of 55% of male patients while the remaining were females. The mean weight and mean height of the patients were 74.69 kg and 160.9 cm, respectively. The mean BMI of the patients was 28.8 kg/m2. The mean waist circumference was 88.47 cm. In a study conducted by Pande and Pande, the mean BMI was 27.6 kg/m2.[19] Lum et al., in another study, reported mean weight, mean waist circumference, and mean BMI of the patients to be 80.5 kg, 30.4 kg/m2, and 113.2 cm, respectively, in patients of NAFLD.[20]

This study has found 43.75% of the patients to be asymptomatic. Abdominal pain was the most common feature among the symptomatic group (40%), followed by jaundice (36.25%) with fatigue and pedal edema following closely (35% and 31.25%, respectively), and the examination finding of hepatomegaly was seen in 48.75% of the patients. The mean SBP and DBP were found to be 128.78 mmHg and 80.6 mmHg, respectively. The mean aspartate transaminase and mean alanine transaminase levels were found to be slightly elevated at 56.86 units/L and 60.32 units/L, respectively, similar to the findings of previous authors.[19]

Mean triglycerides, total cholesterol levels, HDL levels, and low-density lipoprotein levels were found to be 171.82 mg/dL, 175.22 mg/dL, 45.24 mg/dL, and 138.7 mg/dL, respectively. In a study conducted by Pande and Pande, the similar findings were reported, whereas Lum et al., in another study, conducted on a non-obese population, reported significantly lower levels.[19,20]

In the present study, MS was found to be present in 72.5% of the patients which was significantly higher than has been reported by authors in similar studies. Other studies have reported the incidence of NAFLD with MS to the range of 51.4% to 64%.[14,19,21] However, it is of note that these studies were based on smaller cities while our study was conducted in a large metropolitan city.

This study sample consisted of 45% with fatty liver ultrasound Grade II,[22] of which 83.33% were found to have MS, while 28.75% of the participants were fatty liver Grade III, of which 95.65% had MS. This establishes a significant correlation between ultrasound grading of fatty liver and MS [Table 4], such that participants with higher grades of fatty liver were more likely to have MS than those with lower grades, which was in concordance with the findings of other studies.[19-21]

In the current study, 55.17% of patients with MS had a fasting blood sugar level of more than 100 mg/dL, 48.28% had hypertension (>130/85 mmHg), 46.55% had hypertriglyceridemia (>150 mg/dL), while 43.1% had low HDL levels (males <40 mg/dL and females <50 mg/dL). Waist circumference above cutoff (males >90 cm and females >80 cm) was present among 41.38% of the patients with MS. The incidences of the above individual metabolic derangements were found to be significantly higher in those participants with MS than those without [Table 3]. Our results were in agreement with the previous studies.[19]

Typically, NAFLD has been observed to be consistently associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus (28%–55%) and dyslipidemia (27%–92%).[8,23-25] In a previous study, 90% of individuals with NAFLD have at least one risk factor of MS, and 33% have all the features of MS.[21] Another study concluded that liver fat content is significantly increased in subjects with MS as compared to those without MS, independent of age, gender, and BMI.[26]

Hyperlipidemia and specifically high levels of triglycerides and low levels of HDL-cholesterol, as demonstrated by this study, are strongly associated with NAFLD. Hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL-cholesterol level are present in 64% and 30%–42% of NAFLD patients, respectively.[27]

Of the 80 study subjects, only 8 were found to have been diagnosed with ischemic heart disease (10%), suggesting that cardiovascular risk was not found to be greater than the general population. Similar studies have also failed to show a direct causal relationship between NAFLD and cardiovascular risk.[28] However, independent studies done on the association of MS with cardiovascular risk have shown a direct correlation between the two.[29] There is an independent association among hepatic steatosis and carotid atherosclerotic plaques and endothelial dysfunction, the relationship remaining statistically significant after adjustment for risk factors of MS.[30] As 72% of our study population had MS, this could indicate a yet-to-diagnosed heart disease in majority of our patients.

Conclusion

Symptoms and signs of NAFLD are more frequent in those with metabolic syndrome hence the physician should have a high index of suspicion for its detection early in the course of the disease.

Higher prevalence of all the components of metabolic syndrome in cases of NAFLD was observed.

A higher ultrasound grading of fatty liver correlates with an increased incidence of metabolic syndrome and could possibly be used as an investigation of disease severity.

While this study did not find a direct correlation between cardiovascular disease and NAFLD, available literature on metabolic syndrome may signify undiagnosed heart disease which may warrant further evaluation of NAFLD patients for the same. Thus, patients must be evaluated for the presence of NAFLD in the presence of metabolic risk factors. Early detection would help in modifying the disease course and delaying its complications.

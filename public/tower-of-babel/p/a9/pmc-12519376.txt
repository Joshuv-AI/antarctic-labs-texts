BMJ Connections Mental Health, a new BMJ journal
Authors: Andrea Cipriani
Published: 2025

As Editor in Chief of BMJ Mental Health, I’m delighted to announce the launch of BMJ Connections Mental Health, a new open access journal from BMJ Group which is part of the BMJ Connections series focusing on rapidly growing areas of biomedical research.

BMJ Connections Mental Health aims to be an international journal for peer-reviewed mental health research, bridging clinical practice, scientific research and translational applications. We are interested in publishing the full spectrum of robust, methodologically sound and reproducible research in mental health that can move discoveries from the lab to clinical settings and connect the research community to improve real-world outcomes for patients. As an author publishing in BMJ Connections Mental Health or as a reader of our journal, you can expect the same rigorous approach to content integrity and publication ethics that you would receive from every BMJ journal.

We welcome submissions in all areas of psychiatry and psychology, from psychopharmacology, psychotherapy and forensic psychiatry to digital health, cross-cultural psychiatry and computational mental health. BMJ Connections Mental Health will be the home of original articles and review papers that provide insights into diagnosis, treatment and management of mental health disorders and articles that investigate the biology and molecular mechanisms in brain health science. However, we are also interested in papers showing small or incremental advantages, replication studies checking the internal and external validity of other pieces of research and, even more importantly, articles with negative results, which nowadays are often neglected but are essential for progress.

We offer a submission process which is easy and transparent for authors, with fast and constructive peer review to help improve the manuscripts we select. Transparency is central to how we see and deliver research, and this includes the use of artificial intelligence (AI). We live in an evolving landscape and AI technology can definitely help authors work more efficiently. However, we need to ensure that AI is used to enhance, not replace, the processes behind delivering trusted healthcare information. Authors submitting to BMJ Connections Mental Health will be asked to disclose all AI-generated content, describing identifiable steps and providing retrievable data that can be used for validation and accountability. As for all BMJ journals, a team of dedicated experts is employed to verify all AI-driven contributions to meet evidence-based standards, prioritising patient safety and clinical accuracy.

BMJ Connections Mental Health is the companion journal to BMJ Mental Health. As founding Editor of BMJ Mental Health, I am very happy (and proud) that the BMJ asked me to launch BMJ Connections Mental Health, because this is testament to the great work the editorial team and advisory board of BMJ Mental Health have been doing for mental health science and clinical practice so far. There is always scope to improve and expand, and I see this opportunity as a means to reach a wider and more diverse audience of readers and authors internationally. I am personally interested in supporting early career researchers and clinical academics who are taking the first steps in the world of scientific literature. I see BMJ Connections Mental Health as a platform where they can present their findings and discuss their ideas. I am looking for authors who combine academic rigour with creativity and innovation, irrespective of the results of their study.

My role at BMJ Connections Mental Health is to act as a bridge between the two journals at this critical juncture. BMJ Connections Mental Health and BMJ Mental Health will work in tandem and will align closely to find the best home for every paper. The journals will use BMJ Group’s Article Transfer Service, seamlessly transferring peer review reports and saving time and effort for authors. Ensuring free and immediate availability of all articles, the ultimate aim is to increase visibility, maximise chances of citation and wider impact and ultimately enhance global knowledge dissemination and advancements in mental health.

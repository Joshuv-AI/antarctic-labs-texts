Economic burden of invasive pneumococcal disease in hospitalized children in Zhejiang Province, China: A multicenter hospital-based study conducted from 2014–2024
Authors: Zhao-Jun Lu, Jin-Si Zhou, Yan-Yan Zhu, Jin-Hong Yang, Chao Fang, Ming-Ming Zhou, Xiao-Yu Li, Si-Shi Liu, Yan Liu, Chun-Zhen Hua
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo evaluate the clinical and economic outcomes in pediatric patients hospitalized with invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD). This is a retrospective study that included hospitalized patients under 18 years of age from three participating hospitals between 2014 and 2024, all of whom had a definitive diagnosis and laboratory confirmation of invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD). Descriptive statistics and multivariable analyses were used to evaluate outcome measures, including in-hospital mortality, length of stay (LOS), direct cost per admission and vaccination status, and the serotypes were also explored. A total of 141 pediatric patients from January 2014 to July 2024 were included. Of these, 83 (58.9%) were male, and 72 (51.5%) were aged 2–5 years. Forty-one (29.1%) patients required hospitalization for over 14 days, and 3 (2.1%) had received vaccination. Among the 97 serotypes identified, 86 (88.7%) were PCV13 vaccine-type serotypes (VT-Serotypes). The most common disease was bacteremia (54.6%), followed by bacteremic pneumonia (23.4%). The observed mortality, median LOS, and median direct cost were 0.7%, 8 days, and $1122.66 (based on the RMB-USD exchange rate in 2025), respectively. Factors influencing cost included LOS and the type of IPD. Longer LOS, influenced by IPD types, was associated with higher costs, and patients diagnosed with meningitis incurred greater costs per admission compared to those with bacteremia. IPD poses a significant clinical and economic burden on children. Promoting pneumococci vaccination is essential to mitigate the IPD burden and associated costs in China.KEYWORDS: Costs, hospitalization, Streptococcus pneumoniae, invasive pneumococcal disease

Introduction

Invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) is characterized by the isolation of Streptococcus pneumoniae (S. pneumoniae) from normally sterile locations, such as blood or cerebrospinal fluid.1 Transmission of S. pneumoniae takes place through direct contact with respiratory secretions from both patients and asymptomatic carriers. Globally, approximately 100 distinct pneumococcal serotypes have been identified, with a small number of these serotypes responsible for the majority of IPD.2 The primarily burden of IPD arises from pneumococcal meningitis, bacteremic pneumonia.

IPD poses a significant global public health challenge, particularly affecting children in developing countries. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported approximately 14.5 million cases of severe pneumococcal disease in 2000, contributing to approximately 826,000 deaths among children aged 1–59 months, with a concentration of fatalities in 10 African and Asian countries, and in 2015, approximately 5.83 million children under the age of 5 died worldwide, among which 294,000 were caused by pneumococcal diseases.3 In 2018, the Active Bacterial Core surveillance (ABCs) recorded an IPD incidence of 7.2 per 100,000 in the United States,4 while Europe witnessed an incidence of roughly 14.5 cases per 100,000 infants under 1 year old during the same period.5 Notably, Asia bears a significant disease burden from IPD, with children aged 6–59 month comprising the majority of IPD infections in mainland China,6 and Taiwan reporting even higher annual incidence rates of up to 19.5 cases per 100,000.7 Furthermore, true number of IPD cases in developing world is likely underestimated due to low diagnostic sensitivity and limited access to healthcare.8 Additionally, IPD also leads to numerous hospitalizations, outpatient visits, and antibiotic prescriptions, spending a large amount of medical resources. These all underscore its significant impact on public health.9

IPD not only imposes a significant medical burden but also has a substantial economic impact. It is associated with high direct medical costs and considerable indirect costs stemming from lost productivity among parents of children suffering from pneumococcal diseases, as well as among children themselves due to premature death.5 In regions such as Taiwan in Asia, the annual direct costs related to hospitalizations caused by IPD have been estimated to reach $42.1million. In a study evaluating the economic burden of IPD in children in the Turkey, the median (IQR) of the total social cost for pneumonia was $493.57 per patient ($324.05 to $792.93),10 and another study conducted in China estimates that the median length of stay and inpatient expenditures for children with bacterial meningitis were 16 days and 2,697.38 USD, which was most often caused by the S. pneumoniae.11 In the USA, the direct healthcare costs of pneumococcal pneumonia amounted to $2.52 billion in 2004;12 however, following the introduction of the 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13) into national immunization programs, the direct healthcare cost of total IPD decreased significantly to $15 million in 2019.4

The lack of data on IPD and its economic impact have hindered policymakers’ ability to make informed decisions regarding the establishment of vaccination programs in China. To address this gap, we analyzed multicenter hospital-based economic data related to IPD in Zhejiang Province, evaluating the clinical and economic outcomes of hospitalized pediatric patients with IPD, so as to provide data support for the use of pneumococcal vaccines in Zhejiang.

Methods

Taking into account the economic standing and geographical position, we selected Hangzhou and Wenzhou as the survey cities. We chose three representative regional pediatric hospitals for our retrospective study, two in Hangzhou: The Children’s hospitals, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (A) and Hangzhou Children’s Hospital (B), and one in Wenzhou: The Second Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (C). The selection of these hospitals was based on the resident population. Both hospitals boast highly specialized staff and advanced technical equipment.

Subjects

All hospitalizations related to IPD in children under the age of 18 years at the three participating hospitals between 2014 and 2024 were identified through hospital records. To enhance the sensitivity of our search, we utilized admission and discharge diagnoses as references. Only patients with a definitive diagnosis and laboratory confirmation will be enrolled in the study.

Data collection

The study primarily examined the direct costs associated with inpatient days in the hospital related to IPDs, which were sourced from Electronic Medical Record System (EMRS). These included expenses for hospitalization, prescription medications, diagnostic, nursing services, surgical procedures, and nutritional supplement.

The gender, age, and weight at admission, length of stay (LOS), principal diagnosis, and discharge status were collected from the discharge summary inEMRS. The vaccination status, defined as having received PCV13vaccination more than 14 days prior to the date of IPD hospitalization, was extracted from the Hangzhou Center for Disease Control and Prevention database using the patient’s ID.

S. pneumoniae was identified following culture on blood agar using standard microbiological methods (alpha-hemolysis, optochin susceptibility). Invasive S. pneumoniae isolates were stored at −80°C freezers in the microbiological laboratory until shipment to Microbiology Laboratory, Beijing Children’s Hospital for serotyping using Quellung reaction.

Approval from the ethics committee of the Children’s hospitals, Zhejiang University School of Medicine was obtained prior to data collection, and anonymized data was used to ensure privacy protection.

Ethical review

The study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (2021-IRB-0140-P-01). Caregivers electronically signed consent forms for participation.

Statistical analysis

For all eligible patients, characteristics were described by frequencies and proportions for qualitative data, and mean, median, interquartile range, and standard deviation for quantitative data. Unadjusted comparisons among groups were conducted using the Kruskal–Wallis H test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. To identify factors associated with the ordinal form of cost, which was categorized as low, average, or high based on its distribution, and ordinal logistic regression procedure was used. The brant test was used to assess the proportional odds assumption,13 and results are presented as odds ratio and corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI). The significance level was set at 0.05, and a discount rate of 5% was applied. Based on the exchange rate in 2025 between the Chinese Yuan and the US dollar is 0.1386. All analyses were performed in “R” version 4.3.2.

Result

Base case analysis

A total of 141 patients from three participating centers were included in the study: 73 (51.8%) was from the Children’s hospitals, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, 43 (30.5%) from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, and 25 (17.7%) from the Hangzhou Children’s Hospital. Eighty-three (58.9%) cases were male. The median weight was 13.2 kg, and the median age was 3 years, with patients aged 2–5 years accounting for 51.5% (72/141). The length of stay varied between 1 and 45 days, with 29.1% (41/141) of patients staying over 14 days. Most patients had no immunization history with the pneumonia vaccine; only 3 (2.1%) patients had been vaccinated (none of which are PCV13 VT-serotypes), and the vaccination status of 19 (13.5%) was unknown. In the survey,1 (0.7%) death was reported (diagnosed as Bacteremia), and140 (99.3%) patients recovered. The median hospitalization expense was $1122.66 of the 97 serotype records, 86 (88.7%) were PCV13 vaccine-type serotypes (VT-Serotypes), with the most frequent serotype being 19F (25.8%), followed by 14 (20.6%). Discussion

This study examined the economic burden of IPD among 141 hospitalized patients in Zhejiang Province, using data from clinical diagnoses or positive laboratory tests for S. pneumoniae. The median length of stay was 8 days, with a median cost $1122.66, The overall in-hospital mortality rate observed was 0.7%. According to the latest statistical data from China’s National Bureau of Statistics, the per capita health expenditure in China in 2023 was $878.99, while the median economic burden caused by IPD has exceeded 25% of this expenditure, which imposes a certain economic burden on both the families of affected children and society. And the ordinal logistic regression model revealed that both the LOS and the type of IPD significantly influenced the direct medical costs of hospitalization. Our result findings offer insights into the current economic impact of IPD on patients in Zhejiang Province.

Complex medical issues and high healthcare costs drive patients to seek treatment at high-level hospitals, partially explaining why the costs at the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, are higher than those at other facilities.

Several studies have confirmed that a longer LOS correlates with higher costs per admission due to in-patient care, disease severity, medication, biochemical tests, and so on.14,15 Among specific IPD manifestations, although bacteremia is the most frequent presentation, meningitis is associated with highest cost per episode ($6919.49). This is consistent with the results of a retrospective observational study conducted in the United States, which indicated that bacteremia, bacteremic pneumonia, and meningitis could lead to a significant economic burden in IPD.16 Therefore, it is feasible to control the hospitalization expenditure by accurate diagnosis and reducing the LOS through preliminary comprehensive inspection and proper medication.

A multicenter, hospital-based active surveillance conducted from 2019 to 2021 revealed that the predominant serotypes among Chinese children were 19F (24.2%) and 14 (17.7%).17 Similary, a 21-year retrospective study in northeast China identified 19F, 19A, 23F, 14, and 6B as the most common serotypes in Streptococcus pneumoniae infection.18 Our survey corroborated these finding, detecting a total of 17 different serotypes among 97 pneumococcal isolates, with the top two serotypes being 19F (n = 25, 25.8%) and 14 (n = 20, 20.6%).

PCV13 and PPSV23 have reduced the total number of IPD cases caused by serotypes covered by these vaccines in many countries and regions.19–22 However, the coverage rates remain low in China. A study conducted in Shanghai reported that the vaccination rate for 1-dose PCV was 49.65% and that for 3-dose PCV was 37.4% in 2020.23 Another retrospective study carried out in Hangzhou indicated that the PCV13 vaccination rate showed an upward trend between 2017 and 2021, with the vaccination rate for 4-dose PCV13 reaching 24.39% in 2021,24 and there were only 3 (2.1%) patients confirmed to have been received PCV13 in the study. PCV13 appears to be more advantageous than PPSV23 in preventing IPD in children due to its broader vaccination age range in the Chinese immunization program. Nevertheless, as non-Expanded Program on Immunization vaccines, parents often choose to reduce their willingness to vaccinate when health economics are favorable and voluntary payment is required.25 However, our results confirm that most IPD in children in Zhejiang is caused by serotypes included in PCV13 (19F and 14), reinforcing the urgency of administering the pneumococcal vaccine to children at the appropriate age.

This retrospective study assessed direct medical costs of IPD-related admission in Zhejiang province, initially based on case searches and cost discounting, yet it presents certain limitations. Firstly, the economic burden survey is not comprehensive. This study only accounts for hospitalization expenditures as the IPD economic burden, omitting direct non-medical costs such as nutrition supplements, transportation fees, accommodation, and caregiver expenses; and indirect costs like loss of productivity and lost potential. Given the inevitability of follow-up loss, it is worth considering to supplement the costs in some cases through expert consultation and reasonable estimation. Secondly, the sample size is limited. Although we included nearly all IPD cases from three sites over a decade, the study remains underrepresented. We plan to conduct a rigorously designed study involving key hospitals across the country to further explore the economic burden of IPD in children in China.

Conclusion

This multicenter study examines the direct medical costs of IPD hospitalizations among children in Zhejiang province in China. The data regarding hospitalization costs, disease types, and characteristics of pneumococcal isolates are valuable and representative of the pediatric population in Zhejiang Province. Public policies that support IPD vaccination should be encouraged, as they would reduce clinical costs and morbidity rates associated with IPD in China.

Administration of belimumab prior to standard immunosuppression in patients with early active lupus hinders accrual of new EULAR/ACR criteria within the first 12 months of treatment
Authors: Mariele Gatto, Claudio Cruciani, Elisa Bellis, Pietro Francesco Gavino Pilo, Massimo Radin, Luca Iaccarino, Daniela Rossi, Dario Roccatello, Annamaria Iagnocco, Andrea Doria, Savino Sciascia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the effect of early belimumab administration on disease progression in patients with early active SLE.MethodsThis multicentre observational study included patients with early active SLE, defined as being diagnosed ≤12 months from enrolment in the study, displaying ≤2 clinical European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR)/American College of Rheumatology (ACR) 2019 criteria, without major organ involvement but with active serology. Patients were receiving glucocorticoids and hydroxychloroquine as standard of care (SoC) and either belimumab (BEL group) or standard immunosuppression (SoC group). Patients were followed up for 12 months, and accrual of new EULAR/ACR criteria served as a marker of disease progression. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank tests were applied to assess differences in criteria-free survival between groups, while Cox regression compared the lag time to criteria accrual.Results69 patients were included (BEL=33 and SoC=36). Patients receiving early BEL accrued fewer events per 100 patient-years compared with SoC (2.78 vs 12.12, p=0.035). Criteria-free survival was longer in the BEL group (log-rank p=0.04), with a mean time-to-event of 11.8±1.07 months versus 10.3±3.33 months in the SoC group (Cox regression, p=0.027). Early BEL was associated with a sixfold higher likelihood of achieving glucocorticoid (GC) discontinuation at 12 months as compared with patients on hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) and GC alone (OR 6.67, p=0.0014).ConclusionsEarly administration of BEL in active SLE significantly delays disease progression and promotes GC withdrawal. These findings are more striking when limiting SoC to HCQ and GC and underscore the potential of early biologic intervention to modify disease course. Further validation in larger, prospective studies is warranted.Keywords: Biologics; Lupus Erythematosus, Systemic; Cost of Illness

Introduction

Multitarget therapy and timely use of biologics were shown to improve the outcome of patients with SLE.1 2 In fact, earlier and intensified treatment regimens may signify more durable responses and a lower medication burden, thereby improving long-term prognosis.1 3 Consistently, recent recommendations for SLE endorse early employment of biologics with or without former traditional immunosuppression in patients refractory to first-line therapy with hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) and glucocorticoids (GC).2

Among biologics licensed for SLE, belimumab (BEL) is a human IgG1λ monoclonal antibody targeting soluble B-lymphocyte stimulator, providing control of disease activity, abating the risk of flares and halting overall damage progression.4 Growing real-world evidence shows favourable response rates in immunosuppressant-naive patients receiving BEL5; however, no firm data regarding the impact of early BEL on disease course are available.

In this study, we aim to explore the effect of early administration of BEL on disease progression in patients with mild early SLE, assessing the accumulation of disease manifestations over time.

Methods

Patient population

We conducted a multicentre observational study on patients aged ≥18 years with early active SLE, receiving GC and HCQ and either BEL (BEL group) or standard immunosuppression (standard of care (SoC) group).

Patients were classified as ‘early active’ if they had been diagnosed within 12 months of treatment initiation and presented with up to two European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR)/American College of Rheumatology (ACR) 2019 clinical criteria,6 excluding major organ involvement, alongside active serology (positive anti-double-stranded DNA and/or reduced serum complement).

Three Italian referral centres for SLE participated in the study (Mauriziano Hospital, Turin; San Giovanni Bosco Hospital, Turin; and Padova University Hospital, Padova). Treatment allocation to BEL or SoC was determined independently by the treating physician at each participating centre based exclusively on clinical judgement.

No head-to-head comparison nor superiority analysis between BEL and SoC was performed.

All patients signed an informed consent for the scientific use of their anonymised data. The study was approved by the local ethics committee (registration no. AOP2729; 00281/2023).

Outcome measures

The primary objective was to compare the accrual of the EULAR/ACR 2019 criteria between the two groups at 12 months from treatment initiation, serving as a measure of disease progression over time. Accrual of new criteria was defined as the accumulation of items listed in the EULAR/ACR 2019 criteria for SLE, reflecting new or worsening clinical signs and symptoms and/or laboratory findings. Criteria-free survival was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the accrual of any additional EULAR/ACR 2019 criteria, reflecting the emergence of new clinical or serological manifestations beyond those present at baseline. The treating specialist had to judge the new item as clinically significant, necessitating a reassessment of the treatment plan or an escalation of therapy.

Secondarily, we assessed disease activity, damage accrual and changes in GC dosage throughout the follow-up.

Clinical, demographic and serological data were collected in an anonymised format at baseline and at 3, 6 and 12 months. At each time point, clinical SLE-disease activity index 2000 (cSLEDAI-2K), SLEDAI-2K and Systemic Lupus International Collaborating Clinics (SLICC) Damage Index (SLICC-DI)7 were calculated.

Statistical analysis

Variables were described as mean (SD), median (IQR) or percentage. Differences in (c)SLEDAI-2K and SLICC-DI between groups were analysed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. The association between variables was assessed using a χ2 test. Kaplan-Meier curves with log-rank comparison were used to evaluate criteria accrual throughout the follow-up. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA).

Results

Patient cohort

We included 69 patients with early active SLE (four patients in the BEL group and nine in the SoC group recruited at Mauriziano Hospital Turin; 12 in the BEL group and 15 in the SoC group at San Giovanni Bosco Hospital Turin; and 17 in the BEL group and 12 in the SoC group at Padova University Hospital), 36 (52.2%) making up the SoC group and 33 (47.8%) the BEL group, respectively. Patients displayed a mean disease duration of 6.7 ± 3.9 months at inclusion and were followed up for 12 months from baseline. 7 out of 36 patients in the SoC group received standard immunosuppression in addition to HCQ and GC for 9.4 ± 2.8 months. At baseline, all patients were receiving prednisone <15 mg/day with a similar duration before study entry (table 1). The groups were comparable regarding baseline age, gender, disease duration, disease activity, mean SLICC-DI and steroid daily dosage (table 1).

Accrual and severity of SLE criteria

Combining both groups, overall five newy accrued criteria (ie, events) occurred, equating to 7.25 events per 100 patient-years. When comparing the groups, patients in the BEL group experienced one event during a total follow-up of 36 patient-years, corresponding to an event rate of 2.78 events per 100 patient-years, while patients in the SoC group experienced four events, resulting in an event rate of 12.12 events per 100 patient-years (p=0.035, figure 1A).

Patients in the SoC group developed a skin rash (two cases), arthritis (one case) and biopsy-proven lupus nephritis (one case), while one case of pericarditis occurred in the BEL group. Among the patients in the SoC group who accrued new criteria, three were on additional immunosuppressive therapy (two on mycophenolate mofetil and one on methotrexate).

Time-to-event was shorter (10.3±3.33 months vs 11.8±1.07, p=0.027), and criteria-free survival probability declined more rapidly in the SoC compared with the BEL group (log-rank, p=0.04), reflecting a higher rate of criteria accrual among patients in the SoC group.

Disease activity and damage progression

Among patients in the BEL group, cSLEDAI-2K significantly decreased from baseline to 12 months (mean±SD: 5.48±1.77 vs 0.89±1.20, p<0.001). Similarly, SLEDAI-2K decreased from baseline to 12 months (8.90±1.92 vs 2.2±2.6, p<0.0001). SLICC-DI remained stable over time (baseline: 0.13±0.43 vs 12 months: 0.14±0.45, all p>0.05).

A similar trend was observed among patients in the SoC group, with significant decreases in cSLEDAI-2K (baseline: 5.50±1.84 vs 12 months: 1.26±2.62, p=0.036) and SLEDAI-2K (baseline: 8.94±2.10 vs 12 months: 2.78±3.14, p<0.0001). As in the BEL group, SLICC-DI remained stable (baseline: 0.15±0.44 vs 12 months: 0.17±0.46, p>0.05).

A total of 25 patients achieved SLEDAI-2K=0 at 12 months, 14/33 (42.4%) in the BEL group and 11/36 (38.8%) in the SoC group, respectively. 48 patients achieved cSLEDAI-2K=0 at 12 months, 24/33 (72.0%) in the BEL group and 24/36 (66.6%) in the SoC group, respectively. There was no statistically significant difference in the decrease of SLEDAI-2K between groups over 12 months (p=0.46). The course of activity indices is shown in figure 1B.

More patients stopped GC at 12 months among patients in the BEL group compared with the SoC group (15/33 (45.4%) vs 11/36 (30.5%)). Significantly more patients in the BEL group could withdraw GC at 12 months when compared with patients on HCQ and GC alone (15/33 (45.4%) vs 4/36 (11.1%), p=0.001, OR 6.67, 95% CI 1.92 to 23.15).

No major infections, serious adverse events or hospitalisations were observed in either group during the 12-month follow-up.

Discussion

In this study, we show that administration of BEL in patients with early active lupus not receiving standard immunosuppression can significantly delay SLE progression and severity. Besides, the early administration of BEL heralded a greater likelihood of stopping GC at 12 months as compared with HCQ and GC alone.

Historically, biologic use was reserved for refractory SLE; however, it has been shown that features such as established organ damage and a longer disease history negatively impact response to biologics,4 8 claiming that targeted therapies could be put forward in the treatment algorithm due to good response rates and a better safety profile when employed before traditional immunosuppressants.1,35 9 Notably, our real-world data shed some light on the upcoming changed perspective of the treating physicians, who are envisioning the use of a biologic drug in early active patients on failure or inefficacy of a first line of HCQ with or without corticosteroids based on their own clinical judgement, in line with current EULAR recommendations.2

Nevertheless, the adoption of such an approach in clinical practice is still uncommon due to several reasons, including regulations for access to biologics and socioeconomic status.10 On the other hand, direct and indirect health costs are fuelled by uncontrolled disease activity and drug-related side effects, including GC-driven damage.11 In this regard, biologics are notoriously capable of controlling disease activity while allowing treatment de-escalation, thereby hampering damage progression.1 3 12 This combined effect is unlikely with standard immunosuppression, making biologics surge as candidate disease modifiers for SLE relative to traditional drugs.12

The concept of disease modification in SLE encompasses a joint control of disease activity and minimisation of treatment burden, regardless of any biological effect. BEL was shown to induce changes in the B cell compartment early during the treatment course which would anticipate either disease response or susceptibility to flares13 14 and to affect disease mechanisms at a molecular level, with responders displaying a significant downregulation of B cells, type I/II interferon, interleukin-6/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 and neutrophil activation signatures,15 thereby providing a molecular basis for a disease-modifier potential. Hence, it can be hypothesised that an earlier administration of such a biologic drug could play a pivotal role in shaping the disease phenotype over time. Consistently, our findings provide the first clinical evidence that early initiation of BEL significantly influences disease progression, steering it towards a milder phenotype.

In keeping with a clinical response being possible with either a biologic or SoC when intervening early in a relevant proportion of patients,9 the SLEDAI slopes were comparable between the two groups in our cohort. However, the difference in the criteria accrual speaks for a different course imprinted to the disease, which is not about reversing active manifestations but rather about avoiding new organ involvement, provided the observed difference in criteria-free survival should be interpreted cautiously, given the low number of events in the BEL arm.

While the lack of blinding and the heterogeneity of background therapies in the SoC group may have influenced tapering practices and therefore warrant cautious interpretation of any observational data, it is worth noting that patients receiving early BEL were able to effectively taper their steroid dosage up to complete withdrawal in over 45% of cases, peaking to a sixfold likelihood when compared with patients on HCQ and GC alone. These real-world observations recapitulate the disease-modifier potential of BEL, which is enhanced by early administration.

This study has several strengths and limitations that merit discussion. Among its strengths, the homogeneity in clinical presentation and disease duration across the study population enhances the internal validity of the findings. However, there is heterogeneity in the background therapies within the SoC group, with standard immunosuppressants used in a minority of patients. The lack of randomisation and potential variability in treatment practices across centres may introduce selection bias, although this reflects real-world clinical decision-making, and the relatively small sample size and the ethnic homogeneity reduce the statistical power and generalisability of the findings. Similarly, the limited number of events reduced the possibility of applying further statistical models (eg, regression). Hence, these findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, requiring confirmation in larger, prospective studies. Our study was not designed to address the issue of patient phenotyping at disease onset, which could be beneficial in terms of promoting access to tailored treatment regimens.

Altogether, our findings suggest that BEL, when administered early during the course of SLE, has the potential to modify disease progression in a clinically remarkable fashion. This supports the concept of timely initiation of biologic therapies following HCQ and GC, even in the absence of prior immunosuppressant failure. Should these results be confirmed in larger, prospective cohorts, they would provide a compelling rationale for revisiting and potentially reshaping the treatment paradigm of SLE in everyday clinical practice.

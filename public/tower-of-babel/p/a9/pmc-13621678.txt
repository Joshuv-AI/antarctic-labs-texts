LMNTD2-AS1 promotes esophageal squamous cell carcinoma progression by sponging miR-449b-5p to upregulate MYCN
Authors: Rukeyeguli Wusiman, Zhenhong Chang, Feng Yang, Dian Lin, Jiangqi Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains a significant part of the global health burden, increasing the need to make its molecular basis clear in regard to therapeutic development. This study focuses on the long non-coding RNA LMNTD2-AS1 and its regulatory network in ESCC.MethodsThe expression level of LMNTD2-AS1 in clinical specimens and selected cell lines was quantified using quantitative RT‑PCR (qRT‑PCR). To assess its impact on malignant phenotypes, functional experiments including CCK‑8, Transwell, and flow cytometry‑based apoptosis assays were conducted. Subsequently, bioinformatic analyses combined with dual‑luciferase reporter assays were employed to validate the existence of a regulatory axis involving LMNTD2‑AS1, miR‑449b‑5p, and MYCN.ResultThis study revealed that LMNTD2-AS1 was significantly upregulated in ESCC tissues and cellular models. Downregulation of LMNTD2-AS1 expression decreased the rates of cell vitality and invasion while increasing apoptosis. LMNTD2-AS1 acted as a ceRNA and directly interacted with miR-449b-5p, resulting in the derepression of MYCN. Rescue assays confirmed that miR-449b-5p inhibition rescues the anti-tumor effects caused by LMNTD2-AS1 reduction. Reducing MYCN expression negated the cancer-promoting effects of inhibitory miR-449b-5p.ConclusionIn conclusion, this preliminary study demonstrates that LMNTD2-AS1 promotes ESCC progression via the miR-449b-5p/MYCN axis. By elucidating this network and its downstream signaling, we provide a mechanistic framework for ESCC pathogenesis. Background

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), the dominant histological subtype of esophageal cancer in high-incidence regions such as East Asia, is a significant health challenge worldwide due to its aggressive nature and poor prognosis [1, 2]. This has resulted in very low survival rates, even after improvements in diagnosis and treatment, particularly because the disease is commonly diagnosed at an advanced stage with overt metastasis and few curative options available [3]. The promise of improvement within the scope of precision oncology rests with the development of targeted and immunotherapies. Their applicability hinges upon a thorough appreciation of the exact molecular drivers responsible for ESCC [4–6]. Recent studies have begun mapping some of the key dysregulated pathways within this malignancy. For example, YY2 is an upregulated oncogene in ESCC, with its high expression associated with poor prognosis, immune cell infiltration, and glycolysis-related gene regulation [7]. Research in gastric cancer has shown that active components of Chinese medicine exert anti-cancer effects through multi-target mechanisms involving the PI3K/Akt and p53 signaling pathways, with no toxicity to normal cells [8]. Additionally, miR-29c-3p has been demonstrated to suppress ESCC cell viability and induce apoptosis by targeting the E2F1/COL11A1 axis, while E2F1 attenuates sorafenib sensitivity through this pathway [9]. These findings highlight the importance of dissecting molecular mechanisms to identify novel therapeutic targets for cancer treatment. However, they only reflect part of the complex and incompletely delineated molecular landscape of ESCC, indicating that new regulatory genes and signaling networks are yet to be uncovered.

In this context, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as important yet underexplored contributors to cancer biology, offering a promising new layer for understanding the pathogenesis of ESCC and identifying potential therapeutic targets [10, 11]. The expression profile in ESCC indicates that lncRNAs, in a tumor-promoting manner, impact malignant behavior, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis. Some examples include lncRNA KTN1-AS1, which has been indicated to act as a growth promoter by sequestering miR-885-5p [12], and lncRNA CASC15, which has been shown to increase invasive potential through miR-33a-5p [13]. These findings underscore the functional importance of such lncRNAs within esophageal tumorigenesis and position them as promising targets in developing strategies to intervene in their functional pathways.

Among these lncRNAs, LMNTD2-AS1 is relatively uncharacterized. Bioinformatics analyses have suggested that it is dysregulated in ESCC tissues [14], and it has been implicated in the progression of prostate cancer through the FUS/NRF2 pathway [15]. However, its functional role and the underlying molecular mechanism possess largely unexplored dimensions in ESCC. This present knowledge gap regarding the role of LMNTD2-AS1 in ESCC is what this study aims to address. The most common mechanistic theme among lncRNAs is the function of ceRNAs. Acting as molecular sponges, they bind specific miRNAs and sequester them to derepress the miRNA-targeted genes. This study preliminary analysis prioritized miR-449b-5p as a candidate LMNTD2-AS1-interacting miRNA. This plays tumor-suppressive roles in different cancers, including colorectal cancer [16] and gastric cancer [17]. Notably, miR-449b-5p has been identified as a critical regulator of the malignant progression of ESCC cells [18]. However, the relationship between miR-449b-5p and LMNTD2-AS1 is unknown.

Therefore, the present study is designed as a preliminary mechanistic investigation to delineate the expression, functional impact, and underlying regulatory mechanism of LMNTD2-AS1 in ESCC. We hypothesize that LMNTD2-AS1, acting via a ceRNA mechanism, sponges miR-449b-5p to modulate downstream target genes and associated signaling pathways, thereby driving malignant tumor progression. Through an integration of bioinformatics analysis, exploratory clinical validation in a localized cohort, and systematic in vitro functional assays, we aim to establish a robust molecular framework for the LMNTD2-AS1/miR-449b-5p axis. This investigation is anticipated to provide novel insights into the molecular pathogenesis of ESCC and offer a necessary foundation for future in vivo validation and larger-scale translational research.

Materials and methods

Clinical sample collection

This retrospective study examined 25 patients with ESCC who were treated with surgical resection at Jiujiang First People’s Hospital between August 2023 and August 2024. The diagnosis was pathologically confirmed postoperatively according to the WHO classification criteria [19]. None of the patients had received radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or any other anticancer treatment before surgery. During surgery, paired tissue samples—tumor tissue and adjacent non-tumor tissue (collected > 5 cm from the tumor margin)—were obtained from each patient. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age 35–65 years. This range was selected because it represents the most prevalent age group for ESCC in our region and reduces confounding from age‑related physiological variability, thereby improving the internal validity of this mechanistic study; (2) tumor located from the cervical to lower thoracic esophagus, with a clinical stage of T1–T4; and (3) availability of sufficient and qualified tumor and adjacent tissue from the resection specimen. Exclusion criteria included: (1) a history of other primary malignancies; (2) severe comorbidities such as cardiopulmonary dysfunction or hepatic/renal failure; and (3) pathological evidence of cancer cell infiltration in the adjacent tissue. Approval for the study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Jiujiang First People’s Hospital, and all participants provided written informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Tissue samples were immediately frozen in snap-freezing liquid nitrogen and stored at –80 °C until RNA extraction.

Cell culture and transfection

The human immortalized esophageal epithelial cell line Het-1A, along with the ESCC lines TE-8, TE-1, and KYSE70, were acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Culturing was performed in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin, maintained at 37 °C within a 5% CO₂ humidified incubator. All experiments were performed with cells in the logarithmic growth phase.

To investigate the function and regulatory mechanism of LMNTD2-AS1, TE-8 and TE-1 cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Nine experimental groups were established: (1) blank control (CK, no transfection); (2) negative-control siRNA (si-NC); (3) overexpression negative-control plasmid (oe-NC); (4) LMNTD2-AS1-specific siRNA (si-LMNTD2-AS1); (5) LMNTD2-AS1 overexpression plasmid (oe-LMNTD2-AS1); (6) co-transfection of si-LMNTD2-AS1 with miR-449b-5p inhibitor negative control (si + inhibitor-NC); (7) co-transfection of si-LMNTD2-AS1 with miR-449b-5p inhibitor (si + miR-inhibitor); (8) co-transfection of si-LMNTD2-AS1, miR-inhibitor, and MYCN negative-control siRNA (si + inhibitor + MYCN-NC); and (9) co-transfection of si-LMNTD2-AS1, miR-inhibitor, and MYCN-specific siRNA (si + inhibitor + si-MYCN). After transfection, cells were cultured for 48 h. Transfection efficiency was verified by qRT-PCR analysis of LMNTD2-AS1, miR-449b-5p, and MYCN expression. Successfully transfected cells were then used for subsequent functional and mechanistic assays.

RNA extraction and Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from tissues or cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA concentration and purity were assessed with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. For detection of LMNTD2-AS1 and MYCN mRNA, 1 μg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Japan). For miR-449b-5p detection, reverse transcription was performed with the Bulge-Loop™ miRNA qRT-PCR Primer Set (RiboBio, China), which uses a stem-loop primer for miRNA-specific cDNA synthesis. Quantitative PCR was carried out using SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, Japan) on a QuantStudio 5 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). GAPDH and U6 small nuclear RNA served as internal controls for LMNTD2-AS1/MYCN and miR-449b-5p, respectively. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2–ΔΔCt method.

Cell viability assay

The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Japan) was employed to determine cell viability. Following transfection, TE-8 and TE-1 cells were plated in 96-well plates. CCK-8 solution (10 μL per well) was added at the indicated time points (0, 24, 48 and 72 h) and the plates were incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. The optical density at 450 nm was then recorded using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Cell invasion assay

Cell invasion was evaluated using Transwell chambers (Corning, USA). The upper chamber was pre-coated with 50 μL of diluted Matrigel matrix and allowed to polymerize. Transfected cells, suspended in serum-free medium, were added to the upper compartment of the chamber. The lower compartment contained complete medium with 10% FBS. Following a 24-h incubation period, non-migratory cells remaining on the upper side of the membrane were removed. Cells that had traversed the membrane to the lower side were then fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with a 0.1% crystal violet solution, and quantified. Counting was performed under an inverted light microscope at 200 × magnification.

Cell apoptosis assay

Cell apoptosis was evaluated with an Annexin V-FITC/PI Detection Kit (BD Biosciences, USA). At 48 h post-transfection, TE-8 and TE-1 cells were harvested, washed with ice-cold PBS, and reconstituted in the provided binding buffer. Subsequent staining was carried out by incubating the cells with Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) for 15 min under light-protected conditions. Analysis was conducted immediately by flow cytometry, and the resulting data were processed using FlowJo software to quantify the total apoptotic cell population (combining early and late apoptotic stages).

Multi-database prediction and prioritization of candidate targets

The expression profile of the long noncoding RNA LMNTD2-AS1 in ESCC was initially assessed by analyzing data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/analysis-lncRNA.html) via the Ualcan portal. To systematically investigate its potential function as a competing endogenous RNA, a multi-database approach was employed to predict high-confidence downstream targets of miR-449b-5p. Candidate genes were identified using the following stringent thresholds: TargetScan (Total context + + score ≤ −1.0, https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), StarBase (TDMD Score ≥ 1, https://rnasysu.com/encori/panCancer.php), miRDB (Target Score ≥ 80, https://mirdb.org/), and miRWalk (bindingp = 1, http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/). The intersection of predictions from these four complementary tools yielded 12 common candidate target genes. The expression levels of these 12 candidates were then examined by qRT‑PCR in the same 25 paired ESCC clinical samples. Based on differential expression and predicted binding affinity, MYCN was selected for subsequent mechanistic validation.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

DNA fragments containing the predicted wild-type (WT) or mutated (MUT) miR-449b-5p binding sites within the LMNTD2-AS1 sequence or the 3′-UTR of MYCN were cloned into the multiple cloning site of the psiCHECK-2 vector to generate reporter plasmids. The 3′-UTR of MYCN was specifically prioritized for binding validation as it represents the canonical and most evolutionarily conserved regulatory domain for miRNA-mediated post-transcriptional regulation [20]. Furthermore, comprehensive in silico analysis across multiple platforms identified high-confidence binding sites primarily within this region, whereas no credible targets were predicted in other transcript segments, such as the 5′-UTR or coding sequences (CDS). TE-8 and TE-1 cells were seeded into 24-well plates and co-transfected with the WT or MUT reporter plasmid together with either miR-449b-5p mimic, mimic negative control (mimic-NC), miR-449b-5p inhibitor, or inhibitor negative control (inhibitor-NC). After 48 h, luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System. Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity for each sample.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

To validate the direct physical association between LMNTD2-AS1 and miR-449b-5p, we performed a RIP procedure utilizing the Magna RIP™ Kit (Millipore, USA). Briefly, TE-8 and TE-1 cells were harvested at 80% confluence and lysed using ice-cold RIP buffer supplemented with RNase and protease inhibitors. After clearing the lysates via centrifugation (12,000 × g, 4 °C), the supernatants were incubated with magnetic beads (Protein A/G) that had been pre-coated with 5 μg of either anti-Ago2 or rabbit IgG antibodies. This incubation proceeded overnight at 4 °C under constant rotation. The resulting bead-bound complexes underwent five rigorous washes to eliminate non-specific binding. Subsequently, the immunoprecipitated RNA was liberated through proteinase K digestion at 55 °C and recovered using TRIzol reagent. The relative abundance of LMNTD2-AS1 and miR-449b-5p within the precipitates was quantified via qRT-PCR. Direct interaction was confirmed by analyzing the fold enrichment in the Ago2-bound fraction relative to the IgG negative control.

Western blot analysis

Immunoblotting was employed to assess protein expression patterns. Total protein fractions were isolated using RIPA lysis buffer containing a cocktail of phosphatase and protease inhibitors (Roche, Switzerland), with concentrations determined via the BCA method. For each sample, 30 μg of protein was resolved through 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore). Following a 1-h blocking phase in 5% non-fat milk at room temperature, the membranes were probed overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies against MYCN, Cyclin D1, cleaved caspase-3, E-cadherin, N-cadherin, and GAPDH. After three TBST washes, the membranes were incubated for 1 h with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein signals were visualized using an ECL detection system and subsequently analyzed through ImageJ for densitometry. GAPDH was utilized as an internal loading control to normalize the expression levels. All data were derived from three independent experimental replicates.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently replicated a minimum of three times. Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). All statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0). For comparisons between two groups, Student's t-test was applied. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used. Before performing ANOVA, Levene’s test was conducted to assess the homogeneity of variances. When variances were equal (Levene’s test P ≥ 0.05), Tukey’s test was applied for post hoc multiple comparisons. When variances were unequal (Levene’s test P < 0.05), Welch’s ANOVA followed by Games-Howell post hoc test was used instead. All post hoc comparisons were adjusted for multiple testing accordingly. Correlation analysis was performed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Preliminary evaluation of LMNTD2-AS1 expression and available clinicopathological parameters

To provide a preliminary assessment of the potential biological relevance of LMNTD2-AS1, we examined its correlation with available clinicopathological parameters in our exploratory cohort of 25 ESCC patients. Based on the median expression level, patients were categorized into high-expression (n = 15) and low-expression (n = 10) groups (Table S1). Our analysis revealed that elevated LMNTD2-AS1 levels were significantly correlated with advanced tumor invasion depth (T stage, P = 0.048). Furthermore, we observed positive trends regarding poor histological differentiation (P = 0.096) and lymph node metastasis (P = 0.072), although these did not reach statistical significance within this small-scale cohort. No significant associations were found with age, gender, or tumor location.

miR-449b-5p directly targets the oncogene MYCN

Integration of predictions from multiple target-gene databases yielded 12 candidate genes for miR-449b-5p (Fig. 4A). The expression of these 12 candidates was then examined in the 25 paired ESCC clinical samples by qRT‑PCR. As shown in Supplementary Table S2, six genes (CCNE2, PURB, FAM167A, MYCN, E2F5, and LEF1) were significantly upregulated in tumor tissues (P < 0.05), while the other six showed no significant difference. Among these six upregulated candidates, MYCN was selected for further mechanistic study based on the following observations: it exhibited the highest fold‑change (1.52‑fold) with the smallest standard deviation (SD = 0.19); it showed the highest predicted binding affinity with miR-449b-5p across all four databases; and it is a known proto‑oncogene and a recognized downstream effector of ceRNA networks. The 3′-UTR of MYCN contains the predicted binding sites for miR-449b-5p (Fig. 4B). The dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed that miR-449b-5p specifically suppressed the activity of a wild-type MYCN 3′-UTR reporter, an effect abolished by mutation of the binding site (Fig. 4C-D), establishing MYCN as a direct target. The LMNTD2-AS1/miR-449b-5p/MYCN axis drives ESCC progression

Furthermore, LMNTD2-AS1 knockdown suppressed MYCN expression at both the mRNA (Fig. 5C) and protein (Fig. S1) levels, and this suppression was rescued by the miR-449b-5p inhibitor. Functionally, inhibition of miR-449b-5p substantially reversed the suppression of cell viability (Fig. 5D-E) and invasion (Fig. 5F), as well as the increase in apoptosis (Fig. 5G) induced by LMNTD2-AS1 knockdown. Moreover, simultaneous knockdown of MYCN counteracted the pro-tumor effects caused by miR-449b-5p inhibition.

MYCN mediates the downstream signaling pathways regulating ESCC cell malignant phenotypes

To further elucidate how MYCN regulates malignant phenotypes in ESCC, we examined the protein levels of its potential downstream effectors by Western blot. As shown in Figure S2A, knockdown of LMNTD2-AS1 (si‑LMN) markedly reduced MYCN protein expression in both TE‑8 and TE‑1 cells compared with the si‑NC control group, and forced expression of MYCN (si‑LMN + oe‑MYCN) successfully restored its levels. Regarding cell cycle regulation, Figure S2B demonstrates that Cyclin D1 expression was decreased upon LMNTD2-AS1 knockdown, and this reduction was reversed by MYCN overexpression. In contrast, Figure S2C shows that cleaved caspase‑3, an executioner of apoptosis, was increased by si‑LMN and suppressed by subsequent MYCN restoration. For epithelial‑mesenchymal transition markers, Figure S2D indicates that E‑cadherin (epithelial marker) was upregulated after LMNTD2-AS1 knockdown, while Figure S2E shows that N‑cadherin (mesenchymal marker) was downregulated. Both effects were counteracted by MYCN overexpression. Collectively, these results indicate that MYCN promotes ESCC cell proliferation via Cyclin D1, inhibits apoptosis via cleaved caspase‑3, and drives EMT through the E‑cadherin/N‑cadherin switch.

Discussion

As a novel lncRNA, LMNTD2-AS1 has been rarely studied in the context of ESCC. Our research, integrating evidence from clinical samples, cell models, and functional experiments, confirms the tumor‑promoting role of LMNTD2‑AS1 in ESCC. Specifically, knockdown of LMNTD2‑AS1 significantly suppressed ESCC cell vitality, and invasion, while promoting apoptosis. This finding aligns with the reported functions of other oncogenic lncRNAs in this cancer type, such as lncRNA NEAT1 [21] and BBOX1-AS1 [22]. Notably, the pro-cancer mechanisms of LMNTD2-AS1 appear to vary across different tumor contexts. For instance, in prostate cancer, it was reported to regulate the NRF2 signaling pathway and immune cell infiltration by targeting the FUS protein [15]. In contrast, our study reveals a distinct, miRNA-dependent regulatory axis. This work is the first to link the function of LMNTD2-AS1 to a ceRNA mechanism, a pattern similar to that of lncRNA ASB16-AS1, which promotes ESCC development by sponging miR-1258 [23]. At the level of ceRNA validation, dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed the direct binding interaction between LMNTD2-AS1 and miR-449b-5p. This finding is consistent with the tumor-suppressive role of miR-449b-5p observed in other cancers. For example, in lung adenocarcinoma, miR-449b-5p inhibits cancer cell proliferation by targeting lncRNA PSMG3-AS1 [24], and in breast cancer, lncRNA HOTAIR enhances radioresistance by sponging miR-449b-5p to upregulate HSPA1A expression [25]. This study further establishes that miR‑449b‑5p functions as a tumor suppressor in ESCC, which is consistent with the role reported by Wang et al. [18], who demonstrated that circRUNX1 promotes ESCC progression by sponging miR‑449b‑5p. In contrast, our work identifies LMNTD2‑AS1 as a novel upstream regulator that interacts with this miRNA. Notably, the ability of miR‑449b‑5p downregulation to reverse the phenotypic effects of LMNTD2‑AS1 knockdown suggests it may serve as a critical downstream mediator of LMNTD2‑AS1 in ESCC.

In the present investigation, the classic oncogene MYCN stands as a direct downstream target of miR-449b-5p. This interaction is supported within hepatocellular carcinoma, where miR-493-5p inhibits the growth and invasion of cancer cells by repressing MYCN [26]. MYCN, a member of the MYC proto-oncogene family, encodes a transcription factor with a basic helix-loop-helix structure. Due to its abnormal expression in several leading mechanisms driving many aggressive tumors, including binding to E-box sequences in target gene promoters coupled with global transcriptional amplification, it modulates cell proliferation and apoptosis [27]. While direct studies on MYCN in ESCC are scarce, its family member c-MYC has been well-documented to be significantly upregulated in ESCC and is closely associated with lymph node metastasis and poor prognosis [28], indicating an oncogenic role for MYC family members in this malignancy. Our findings regarding the pro-tumorigenic role of MYCN align with its previously established function in gastrointestinal cancers, such as colorectal cancer [29, 30] and gastric cancer [31]. In these malignancies, MYCN overexpression has been consistently linked to enhanced cell proliferation, and invasion, reflecting its conserved oncogenic properties across digestive system tumors. Importantly, this study provides evidence for the first time that MYCN expression is regulated by a lncRN–miRNA axis in ESCC.

The primary objective of this investigation was to delineate the molecular interplay within the LMNTD2-AS1/miR-449b-5p/MYCN axis in ESCC as a mechanistic proof‑of‑concept, not to establish clinical outcomes. Several limitations warrant consideration. First, the clinical cohort included only 25 patients. We fully recognize that this sample size is insufficient to draw any definitive conclusions regarding prognosis or clinical relevance. To ensure transparency, all available clinicopathological data are presented in Table S1. Exploratory analysis revealed a correlation between LMNTD2-AS1 upregulation and advanced T stage (P = 0.048); however, these findings are preliminary and require validation in larger, independent cohorts. No survival data were available, precluding any prognostic assessment. Second, all functional experiments were performed in vitro. We acknowledge that the absence of in vivo xenograft studies is a major limitation. The addition of downstream protein analysis (e.g., Cyclin D1, cleaved caspase‑3, E‑cadherin/N‑cadherin) does not substitute for animal models. This gap will be addressed in our future work by establishing xenograft models to confirm the tumor‑promoting role of this axis within the physiological tumor microenvironment. Third, while we identified MYCN as a direct downstream target of miR-449b-5p, the precise molecular pathways through which MYCN drives ESCC cell proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis resistance have not been fully elucidated. A more detailed dissection of MYCN‑regulated transcriptional networks (e.g., via ChIP‑seq or transcriptomic profiling) would provide deeper mechanistic insight. Fourth, although six candidate targets of miR-449b-5p (including CCNE2, PURB, FAM167A, E2F5, and LEF1) were significantly upregulated in our screening, the present study focused solely on MYCN as a proof‑of‑concept. The potential contributions of these other candidates to the oncogenic axis remain unexplored and warrant further investigation. Fifth, our dual‑luciferase assays confirmed that miR-449b-5p directly binds to the MYCN 3′‑UTR. Given that the 3′‑UTR is the canonical and evolutionarily conserved region for miRNA‑mediated regulation, and no credible binding sites were predicted in other segments of the MYCN transcript (e.g., coding region or 5′‑UTR) by multiple bioinformatics tools, we focused exclusively on this region. Nevertheless, we cannot formally exclude the possibility that additional functional sites exist outside the 3′‑UTR. Future studies employing genome‑wide approaches such as CLIP‑seq or RISC‑trap could systematically map all miR-449b-5p interaction sites on MYCN transcripts.

Despite these limitations, this study offers the first mechanistic evidence for the LMNTD2-AS1/miR-449b-5p/MYCN axis in ESCC and serves as a foundation for further investigation. Future studies will focus on: (1) validating the observed expression trends and clinical associations in larger, independent cohorts with complete follow‑up data; (2) establishing xenograft models to confirm the in vivo tumor-promoting function of LMNTD2-AS1; (3) comprehensively map the downstream effectors of MYCN in ESCC, (4) systematically evaluate the roles of other miR-449b-5p target candidates in ESCC progression, and (5) investigate potential non‑3′‑UTR binding sites for miR-449b-5p on MYCN.

In summary, this preliminary investigation provides the first evidence that LMNTD2-AS1 promotes ESCC progression by regulating the miR‑449b-5p/MYCN axis via a ceRNA mechanism. By delineating the regulatory influence of this axis on downstream effectors such as Cyclin D1 and EMT markers, our study establishes a systematic molecular framework for understanding ESCC pathogenesis. While the findings regarding clinical correlation and functional phenotypes require further validation in larger independent cohorts and in vivo models, this work offers a robust experimental foundation for identifying potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets in esophageal cancer.

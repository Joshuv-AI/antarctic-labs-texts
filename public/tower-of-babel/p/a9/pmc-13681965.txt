Do new guidelines enable a more accurate comparison of [18F]FDG PET/CT features with histopathology in primary breast cancer?
Authors: A. R. Sharkey, Hiu Yeung Chen, C. Funso-Fayomi, S. E. Pinder, G. J. R. Cook
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecent EANM–SNMMI guidelines suggest that quantitative [18F]FDG PET parameters, including standardised uptake values (SUV), metabolic tumour volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), provide valuable prognostic information in breast cancer. We aim to evaluate the association between [18F]FDG PET parameters in a large real-world cohort of high-risk breast cancer patients and the four main intrinsic subtypes of breast cancer defined by the EANM–SNMMI guidelines.MethodsAll women with newly diagnosed high-risk breast cancer undergoing [18F]FDG PET/CT between 1/8/22 and 17/4/2025 were eligible for inclusion. Primary tumour metabolic parameters (SUVmax/mean/peak, SUV heterogeneity index (HI), MTV, TLG) were measured and compared with histological grade, hormone receptor status (oestrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR)) and Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) expression. Cases were classified according to EANM–SNMMI histological groupings: Group 1 (G1) representing Luminal A-like (HR+ /HER2− and low-grade/low proliferation), Group 2 (G2) Luminal B-like (HR+ /HER2− and high-grade/high proliferation), Group 3 (G3) HER2+ (HR + or −) lesions, and Group 4 (G4) triple negative breast cancer (TNBC). Hormone receptor status was analysed separately.Results185 cases were included for analysis: G1 (n = 48), G2 (n = 35), G3 (n = 54) and G4 (n = 48). Significant differences were observed between groups for SUVmax, SUVmean, SUV HI and MTV; post hoc analysis revealed TNBC, HER2 + and Luminal B tumours exhibited significantly higher SUVmax than Luminal A cancers, with significantly higher SUVmean and SUVpeak in TNBC and Luminal B versus Luminal A. The SUV HI showed significantly lower heterogeneity in the Luminal A group compared with Luminal B (p = 0.02) and HER2-positive tumours (p = 0.03), with no other significant pairwise differences. When looking at MTV, only the Luminal A vs. Luminal B comparison reached significance, with larger MTVs observed in Luminal B tumours. ER + and PR+ tumours showed significantly lower SUV parameters (p < 0.05) when compared with receptor-negative tumours, but no significant differences were observed for volumetric parameters (MTV and TLG) (p > 0.05). HER2 scores (negative/low/positive) showed no significant association with SUV or volumetric PET parameters.ConclusionIn our large real-world cohort of high-risk breast cancer patients, quantitative [¹⁸F]FDG PET parameters demonstrate significant associations with the histopathological breast cancer subtypes recommended in the EANM–SNMMI guidelines.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13058-026-02328-2.

Introduction

[18F]Fluorodeoxyglucose ([18F]FDG) uptake in primary breast cancers on positron emission tomography (PET) is usually measured using standardised uptake values (SUV), and tumours with high SUV have been shown to exhibit significantly higher relapse and mortality rates compared with those with a lower SUV [1], suggesting that factors associated with high glucose metabolism, reflected by high uptake of [18F]FDG, are predictive of poor prognosis. [18F]FDG uptake is related to multiple different factors: microvasculature density for delivering nutrients, glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1) for transportation of the tracer into the cell, hexokinase for the tracer entering into glycolysis, the density of viable neoplastic cells in a tumour, the number of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, and hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1a) upregulation of GLUT1 [2]. Consequently, high [18F]FDG uptake tends to correlate with tumour aggressiveness [3].

[18F]FDG PET/CT semiquantitative parameters of primary breast cancers have been shown to correlate with receptor status, histopathological characteristics including tumour grade, proliferation index (Ki-67) and tumour size, all of which are major prognostic factors in standard practice. Breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease with different biological subtypes depending on the expression of hormone receptors (HR) (oestrogen receptors (ER) and/or progesterone receptors (PR)) and the levels of Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2). Different molecular subtypes of breast cancer show characteristic patterns, with triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) and HER2 + cancers showing higher maximum standardised uptake values (SUVmax), with moderate to low uptake in Luminal B and low uptake in Luminal A cancers [1, 4–6]. High SUVmax measurements are known to be associated with adverse prognostic features, including axillary lymph node positivity and distant metastasis [7].

Although [18F]FDG PET/CT metabolic features have been shown to demonstrate statistically significant correlations with the receptor status and histopathologic features of primary breast cancer, results across studies have been inconsistent. Buck et al. [8] demonstrated that ductal (no special type) breast cancers had higher SUVs than lobular cancers, and that [18F]FDG uptake correlated with proliferative activity as assessed by Ki-67, although a significant correlation with the other prognostic markers, such as oestrogen receptor (ER)/ progesterone receptor (PR) status, tumour grade or size was not demonstrated. Osborne et al. [9], however, did show a significant association with ER receptor status, finding ER- tumours display significantly higher [18F]FDG uptake than ER+ tumours, with microarray analysis identifying genes associated with increased glucose use as measured by PET. Similar to other studies, they reported no significant association between SUV and PR status, HER2 status, lymph node involvement, or tumour size. Groheux et al. [10] showed baseline PET parameters, including SUVmax, metabolically active tumour volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), were all predictive of event free survival in a study of 143 patients with locally advanced ER+/HER2- non metastatic breast cancer. After multivariate analysis, MTV, PR expression and tumour histological type remained significant. Kitajima et al. [11] found SUVmax was associated with numerous prognostic factors such as tumour size, ER, PR, and HER2 expression and Ki-67. A recent meta-analysis showed that primary breast tumour SUVmax was significantly higher in ER-, PR-, HER2 + and Ki-67 + breast cancer patients, and luminal tumours had the lowest, and TNBC tumours the highest, SUVmax, with HER2 overexpression demonstrating intermediate activity [12].

Retrospective evidence suggests that HER2-low breast cancer does not represent a distinct subtype from a biological and prognostic perspective [13–15]. However, the DESTINY-Breast04 trial [16] has highlighted ‘HER2-low’ breast cancer as a clinically relevant entity. This includes tumours with low but detectable levels of HER2 protein (with 1 + immunohistochemical scoring or with 2 + immunoreactivity but without Her2 gene amplification by in situ hybridisation (ISH)), previously grouped with carcinomas showing no reactivity (score 0) as HER2-negative. HER2-low lesions are now treatable with targeted antibody-drug conjugates, expanding therapeutic options for many patients.

The inconsistency in reported associations may be partly due to technical differences in PET acquisition and reconstruction between studies, as well as heterogeneity in racial composition and subtype classification across cohorts. Recent European Association of Nuclear Medicine (EANM)- Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging (SNMMI) guidelines defined four main subtypes of breast cancer [17] :

We have used these groupings to analyse associations between quantitative PET features and histopathological/ immunohistochemical characteristics in our real-world cohort of high-risk breast cancer patients, with the hypothesis that these guideline-based classifications will correlate with PET-derived metabolic features beyond standard SUVs, including MTV, TLG, and the SUV heterogeneity index.

Methods

Patient population

Institutional approval was obtained for this single tertiary referral centre retrospective study. All women with newly diagnosed high risk breast cancer (Stage IIB and above as defined by the EANM-SNMMI guidelines [17] ) undergoing [18F]FDG PET/CT between 1/8/2022 and 17/4/2025 were eligible for inclusion. Exclusion criteria included incomplete imaging or incomplete histopathology data.

[18F]FDG PET/CT protocol and image evaluation

Subjects were scanned on either a Siemens Biograph mCT (Erlangen, Germany) or a GE Discovery 710 (Waukesha, US) PET/CT scanner. Both scanners were subject to quality control under the UK PET Core Lab for SUV accuracy, as required for multicentre studies.

Standard patient preparation was undertaken for [18F]FDG PET scanning. All patients were injected intravenously with up to 400MBq of [18F]FDG (mean administered activity was 299.3 MBq +/− 72.0). At 60 min, a scan was acquired from pelvis to skull base at four minutes per bed position. A low-dose CT scan (140 kV; mAs, 15–100; noise index, 40; rotation time, 0.5 s; and collimation, 40 mm) was obtained at the start of imaging to provide attenuation correction and an anatomic reference. PET scans were reconstructed using time-of-flight (ToF) data (Bayesian penalised-likelihood reconstruction algorithm (Q.CLEAR); GE Healthcare or point-spread function modelling (True TOF); Siemens).

Image evaluation was performed using Hermes Medical Solutions Gold software (Stockholm, Sweden). Tumour volumes were defined using a semiautomated 40% of maximum uptake threshold that was checked visually and manually corrected when needed. SUV max/mean/peak were measured using these semi-automated volumes of interest (ROI). The SUV heterogeneity index was calculated as SUVmax/SUVmean.

Metrics and statistical analysis

Cases were classified into histological groups as per EANM-SNMMI guidelines [17]. As immunohistochemistry (IHC) does not exactly correspond to the genomic groups for Luminal A vs. Luminal B tumours, and as Ki-67 is not routinely assessed at our centre, and is not part of the UK national minimum dataset [18], the cases were separated by the mitotic count score as part of the universally applied histological grading system for invasive breast cancer [19].

Luminal A: HR+/ HER negative tumours with a mitotic frequency score of 1.Luminal B: HR+/ HER negative with a mitotic frequency score of 2 or 3.

Luminal A: HR+/ HER negative tumours with a mitotic frequency score of 1.

Luminal A: HR+/ HER negative tumours with a mitotic frequency score of 1.

Luminal B: HR+/ HER negative with a mitotic frequency score of 2 or 3.

Luminal B: HR+/ HER negative with a mitotic frequency score of 2 or 3.

HER2 status was defined as: HER2- (IHC score of 0), HER2-low (either HER2 IHC score of 1+, or 2 + and non-amplified/ negative by Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH), or HER2+ (HER2 IHC scores of 2 + were amplified/ positive by FISH or IHC scores of 3+).

A one-way ANOVA was performed for each PET parameter by this histological grouping classification. A Tukey’s post-hoc test was performed to find significant group differences. A Student’s T-test was performed to assess for differences in PET parameters according to tumour receptor status when comparing two groups and pairwise Welch t-tests were used when three groups were. A p value of < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

203 newly diagnosed high-risk breast cancer patients were scanned during the study period, of whom 185 cases were included for analysis (15 excluded due to incomplete histology or histology not being available for review, three excluded as histology showed the primary pathology to not be breast cancer). All patients were female with an average age of 52.9 +/- 13.7 years. Example imaging is demonstrated in Fig. 1.

Of the 185 cases, 48 were classified as Group (G) 1, 35 as G2, 54 as G3 and 48 as G4. There were significant differences between groups in terms of SUVmax, SUVmean, SUVpeak, SUV heterogeneity index and MTV (Table 1), while TLG did not differ significantly between groups. The Luminal B and TNBC groups appear to have higher SUVs, although the variability in these groups is high.

Post-hoc analysis demonstrated a consistent pattern across all SUV-derived parameters, with Luminal A tumours exhibiting significantly lower SUVmax, SUVmean, and SUVpeak compared with Luminal B and TNBC tumours. SUVmax was also significantly lower in Luminal A compared with HER2-positive tumours. No significant differences were observed between Luminal B, HER2-positive, and TNBC subtypes for these parameters. SUV HI differed significantly between groups, and post-hoc analysis demonstrated that Luminal A tumours had significantly lower heterogeneity compared with Luminal B and HER2-positive tumours (p = 0.02 and p = 0.03, respectively), with no other significant pairwise differences observed. MTV showed a significant overall group difference, but post-hoc analysis demonstrated that the only significant difference in MTV was between Luminal A and B, with significantly higher MTVs in Luminal B tumours compared with Luminal A tumours (p = 0.04). No other pairwise comparisons reached statistical significance (Table 2).

Discussion

Analyses of PET parameters in breast cancer are often limited by variable groupings and classifications, leading to heterogeneity and inconsistency in reported results. The recent EANM-SNMMI guidelines suggest quantitative PET features (SUV, MTV, TLG) are valuable prognostic parameters [17]. By dividing our large, real-world, cohort of high-risk primary breast cancer patients who had staging [18F]FDG PET/CT using the EANM-SNMMI grouping (Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2 + and TNBC), we have demonstrated significant differences between subgroups in terms of [18]FDG PET parameters.

TNBC, HER2 + and Luminal B tumours exhibited significantly higher SUVmax than Luminal A cancers, with significantly higher SUVmean and SUVpeak in TNBC and Luminal B versus Luminal A. As expected, the SUV HI showed significantly lower heterogeneity in the Luminal A group compared with Luminal B (p = 0.02) and HER2-positive tumours (p = 0.03). When looking at MTV, only the Luminal A vs. Luminal B comparison reached significance, with larger MTVs observed in Luminal B tumours. ER + and PR+ tumours showed significantly lower SUV parameters (p < 0.05) when compared with receptor-negative tumours, but no significant differences were observed for volumetric parameters (MTV and TLG) (p > 0.05). HER2 scores (negative/low/positive) showed no significant association with SUV or volumetric PET parameters. Differences in TLG did not reach statistical significance in the ANOVA, and as such post-hoc analysis of this group was not performed. ER- and PR- tumours showed significantly higher SUVmax/mean/peak than ER + and PR+ tumours, respectively. Interestingly, given a recent move towards classification of HER2 low as a separate group, HER2 analyses revealed no significant differences in PET parameters between HER2 positive, HER2 low and HER2 negative cases. These findings are of clinical significance as higher SUVs and volumetric PET parameters are generally associated with adverse prognostic factors, such as axillary lymph node positivity and distant metastasis [7, 20].

The classification of breast cancer subtypes remains challenging due to the biological heterogeneity of the disease, with multiple histopathological nuances, and variability in grouping strategies across studies, all of which have contributed to inconsistent findings in the literature. We suggest that the EANM-SNMMI groupings offer a pragmatic approach, with groupings correlating well with PET parameters. Our results are comparable to other studies with similar SUVs, for example compared to that of Koo et al. [6], who showed across 548 patients, the mean SUVmax for subtypes Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2+, and TNBC were 4.69, 6.51, 7.44, and 9.83, respectively, whereas we report mean SUVmax for Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2+, and TNBC were 5.84, 10.68, 8.81, and 10.32, respectively. Koo et al. [6] found TNBC and HER2 + tumours showed significantly higher SUVmax than Luminal A, however, we report Luminal B SUVmax values are comparable to TNBC (p > 0.05). This discrepancy may be explained by differences in cohort composition, as Luminal B only represented 11.9% of their cohort vs. Luminal A, which represented 60.5%. Previous studies have also shown that differences in receptor expression profile show different glucose metabolic profiles; we report that both ER + and PR+ tumours showed significantly lower values for all SUV-based PET parameters than ER-/PR-, but not for MTV and TLG (p > 0.05).

Prospective studies have previously shown that SUV, MTV, and TLG correlate with response to treatment and prognosis in breast cancer patients [21, 22]. A previous meta-analysis [23] has shown that volumetric parameters obtained from [18F]FDG PET/CT were significant prognostic factors for outcome in patients with breast cancer, concluding that patients with a high MTV and TLG from the primary tumour have a higher risk of adverse events and patients with a high TLG from whole-body tumour burden have a higher risk of death, therefore suggesting that these volumetric parameters should be routinely used when reporting scans. Our results highlight the significant differences in SUV and MTV between breast cancer subtypes, with intergroup differences in TLG non-significantly different (p = 0.08).

Limitations

This single-centre retrospective study has limitations. Referral bias is a risk, however the EANM guidelines for referral are followed in our centre [24]. Population bias is also a risk, albeit we are a tertiary referral centre in a diverse major city. A further limitation is that we do not routinely report Ki-67 in histopathological data, limiting in-depth histopathological analysis of proliferation. Various methods are used for defining tumour volumes and by following a fixed threshold method and with user inspection and manual changes where needed as per EANM guidelines, we cannot completely exclude effects of tumour heterogeneity on volume definition [25, 26]. We use two different PET/CT scanners in this study with two different reconstruction methods but both scanners and reconstruction methods have undergone quality control for comparative SUVs under the UK PET Core Lab as required for multicentre studies. Finally, although here we compared PET parameters with histopathological features, we do not have long enough follow up to report whether these PET parameters are of prognostic significance.

Future

This analysis of PET parameters in a real-world cohort of high-risk breast cancer patients suggests that grouping according to the EANM-SNMMI guidelines gives results in keeping with recent prospective studies and meta-analyses. Future work using these groupings can incorporate the clinical impact of routine reporting of standard PET metrics and with further work on advanced PET metrics, such as radiomics, which may offer further prognostication in these patients. Radiomics results are conflicting, with some suggesting that textural analysis of PET images was not of added value in patients with locally advanced ER+/HER2- non metastatic breast cancer [10], whereas some suggest that PET/CT derived radiomic signature models can outperform individual significant PET parameters in the molecular subtype classification of breast cancer [27], and a systematic review looking at PET derived radiomics in breast cancer suggests radiomics could provide an additional tool to further explore breast cancer characteristics [28].

There is interest in developing predictive models using baseline PET parameters for prognostication in breast cancer [29, 30], including MTV and TLG, and these parameters have been shown to have value as prognostic biomarkers in metastatic breast cancer [31, 32]. A recent paper demonstrates that a multimodal machine learning algorithm incorporating PET (including radiomics), histopathological, genomic, and clinical features can predict pathological complete response on TNBC, with a tendency to correlate with long-term outcomes [33], suggesting the inclusion of PET parameters is valuable in predictive models.

Conclusion

In our real-world cohort of high-risk breast cancer patients, [18F]FDG PET parameters show associations with the histopathological breast cancer groupings as suggested in the EANM-SNMMI guidelines, with significant differences in PET parameters between these groups.

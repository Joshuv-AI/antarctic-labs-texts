Comparative evaluation of E. coli expression systems for soluble hScFv-IL-17A with an unpaired CDR-L3 cysteine
Authors: Weeraya Thongkum, Kanokporn Sornsuwan, Thanathat Pamonsupornwichit, Anuwat Weechan, Natsima Viriyaadhammaa, Kanchanok Kodchakorn, Chatchai Tawapiwatana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study systematically evaluated Escherichia coli (E. coli) expression systems for the production of a humanized single-chain variable fragment targeting interleukin-17A (hScFv-IL-17A). A wild-type hScFv (hScFv-IL-17A-WT), derived from Secukinumab, was compared with a C97S mutant (hScFv-IL-17A-C97S) in which the unpaired cysteine residue in the light-chain complementarity-determining region 3 (CDR3) was substituted with serine. The primary objectives were to identify an optimal expression system for soluble protein production and to assess the functional consequences of the C97S mutation. Protein solubility, yield, and antigen-binding properties were evaluated across expression on three E. coli strains: Origami B (DE3), SHuffle, and BL21 (DE3) incorporating the DisCoTune system. Analyses were performed using SDS-PAGE, Western blotting, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and biolayer interferometry (BLI). Among the systems tested, BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune yielded the highest levels of soluble expression for both variants. Although the C97S mutation enhanced soluble protein yield in this system, it significantly impaired functional activity. The hScFv-IL-17A-WT exhibited a strong binding affinity (KD = 3.64 × 10−8 M), whereas the hScFv-IL-17A-C97S demonstrated an approximately 10-fold reduction in affinity. Competitive ELISA further confirmed that both variants recognized the same epitope as a neutralizing monoclonal antibody. Structural modeling predicted that the C97S substitution imposed a moderate stability penalty without inducing major conformational alterations, consistent with the observed reduction in binding affinity. Collectively, these findings indicate that while the C97S mutation improves soluble expression, it compromises biological activity. The hScFv-IL-17A-WT produced in BL21 (DE3) using the DisCoTune system represents the most promising candidate for subsequent therapeutic development.Keywords: Interleukin-17, E. coli, Monoclonal antibodies, Single-chain variable fragments, Therapeutics

Introduction

Interleukin-17 (IL-17) is a proinflammatory cytokine that plays a critical role in autoimmune disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriasis, and multiple sclerosis.1 Beyond its involvement in autoimmune pathology, IL-17 also contributes to tumor progression through the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and the activation of survival pathways in cancer cells.2, 3 These diverse pathological roles have established IL-17 as an important therapeutic target, leading to the development of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) designed to neutralize its function. Among the IL-17 cytokine family, IL-17A is the only member with antibody-based therapeutic development due to its strongest biological activity and central role in inflammatory responses.

Secukinumab, a fully human IgG1/kappa monoclonal antibody (mAb) targeting IL-17A, has demonstrated strong efficacy in treating inflammatory diseases while exhibiting favorable pharmacokinetics and low immunogenicity.4, 5, 6 Compared to other IL-17A-targeting antibodies such as Ixekizumab, Secukinumab is associated with fewer T-cell responses and lacks identifiable T-cell epitopes in its complementarity-determining regions (CDRs).7, 8 These characteristics make Secukinumab a suitable model for antibody engineering applications. However, Secukinumab possesses a unique structural feature: an unpaired cysteine residue at position 97 in the light chain CDR3 (CysL97). This unpaired cysteine may influence disulfide bond formation and protein folding,6 potentially leading to aggregation, reduced solubility, and low production yields in mammalian expression systems.

To address the challenges associated with full-length antibody production, single-chain variable fragments (ScFvs) have emerged as attractive alternative antibody formats. ScFvs offer several advantages over conventional antibodies, including their smaller size, enhanced tissue penetration, and lack of Fc-mediated effector functions.9, 10 Additionally, their compatibility with bacterial expression systems supports rapid and cost-effective production. However, achieving proper folding of ScFvs, particularly those containing intra-domain disulfide bonds remains challenging in the reducing cytoplasm of E. coli.11, 12, 13, 14.

While E. coli remains an attractive and practical host for the production of antibody fragments, the efficiency of correct disulfide bond formation and the maintenance of solubility remain major bottlenecks.15, 16 To address these challenges, engineered cytoplasmic environments have been developed: SHuffle strains introduce the isomerase DsbC into an oxidizing cytoplasm, Origami derivatives enhance disulfide formation through trxB/gor mutations, and DisCoTune plasmids enable tunable disulfide bond formation for proteins with complex cysteine architectures.17, 18 Recent studies have reported progress in these systems, demonstrating improved soluble yields of ScFvs and nanobodies but also highlighting persistent challenges such as inclusion body formation, folding inefficiencies, and loss of functional activity.19, 20 In parallel, cysteine engineering has become an increasingly important strategy in antibody design. Rationally introduced disulfide bonds can stabilize protein conformation and improve biophysical properties, whereas inappropriate placement or substitution of cysteines can compromise folding fidelity and destabilize antigen-binding loops.21, 22, 23, 24 Despite these advances, to our knowledge no prior study has systematically compared DisCoTune, SHuffle, and Origami B in the expression of an ScFv carrying an unpaired cysteine residue within the CDR. Addressing this gap, the present study evaluates how bacterial expression systems accommodate a non-canonical cysteine in the CDR and thereby provides new insights into the balance between solubility and functional preservation in antibody fragment engineering.

In our previous work, we applied a structure-guided approach that integrated “mCSM-.

AB2″ predictions with molecular dynamics simulations to engineer a humanized anti-CD147 ScFv.25 This study explored how single amino acid substitutions can significantly influence both solubility and binding affinity, demonstrating the profound impact that even minimal modifications can have on antibody behavior. Building on this principle, the present study investigates whether replacing the unpaired cysteine at position 97 (CysL97) in the light chain of an anti-IL-17A ScFv with serine (C97S) could improve expression yield in E. coli while preserving antigen-binding function.

This study aims to produce hScFV antibodies against IL-17A (hScFv-IL-17A), derived from Secukinumab, in both WT and C97S mutant forms. These constructs were expressed in three different bacterial systems: Origami B (DE3), SHuffle, and BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune. We systematically evaluated their solubility, yield, and binding activity to identify an optimal bacterial host for scalable production of disulfide-rich ScFvs suitable for therapeutic applications.

Materials and methods

Plasmid construction for hScFv-IL-17A with Histidine tag (His6x) production

DNA encoding for the VL and VH chains of Secukinumab26, linked by a flexible linker and a His6x tag, was synthesized using GenScript (Piscataway, NJ, USA). Both of the synthesized DNA sequences (wild-type and C97S) replaced the existing VL-flexible linker-VH-His6x in the pET21-a-HuScFvM6-1B9 (His6x) plasmid27 using NdeI and HindIII restriction enzymes. This modification resulted in the generation of the pET21-a-hScFv-IL-17A-WT (His6x) and pET21-a-hScFv-IL-17A-C97S (His6x) plasmids. The plasmids were subsequently transformed into the E. coli XL-1 blue strain. Colonies grown on agar supplemented with appropriate antibiotics were selected, followed by plasmid extraction using the Exprep™ Plasmid SV Kit (GeneAll®, Seoul, Korea). To confirm successful insertion of the DNA sequence, DNA sequencing of the plasmid was performed (Macrogen, Seoul, Korea).

Expression of hScFv-IL-17A (His6x) in e. Coli

The pET21-a-hScFv-IL-17A- WT (His6x) and pET-21-a-hScFv-IL-17A-C97S (His6x) plasmid were transformed into various strains of E. coli, including Origami B (DE3), SHuffle, and BL21 (DE3) containing the DisCoTune plasmid. A single colony of E. coli harboring the plasmid was selected and grown in super broth (SB) medium supplemented with appropriate antibiotics at 37 °C for 16 to 18 h. The pre-cultured bacterial cells were then inoculated into 100 mL of SB medium for large-scale production. The bacterial cells were incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm.

Once the optical density at 600 nm (O.D.600) reached 0.5 to 0.8, the expression of recombinant hScFv-IL-17A (His6x) was induced using isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at the final concentrations of 0.5 mM for the Origami B (DE3) strain, 0.1 mM for SHuffle strain, and 1 mM for BL21 (DE3) strain with DisCoTune plasmid. Additionally, 100 mM of rhamnose was added to the culture of BL21 (DE3) with the DisCoTune plasmid. The induced cultures were then shaken at 200 rpm for 18 h at 20 °C for the Origami B (DE3) strain, and at 16 °C for the SHuffle, and BL21 (DE3) strain with DisCoTune.

Following collection, crude lysates of hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S were subjected to purification by Ni-NTA agarose affinity chromatography. The purified proteins were separated by 15 % sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) to verify purity. The proteins were transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane and probed with a 1:3000 dilution of horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-His antibody (BioLegend, CA, USA). The signal was detected using a chemiluminescent substrate for visualization of protein bands. Molecular weight estimation was based on protein standard marker (Precision Plus Protein™ Kaleidoscope™ Prestained Protein Standards, BioRad).

Band intensities of hScFv-IL-17A from Western blot were quantified using Image Lab Software (Bio-Rad). Expression levels of soluble hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S in different E. coli strains were determined by densitometry and normalized to the total protein loaded (50 µg/lane).

Binding activity detection by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Indirect ELISA was performed to verify the binding activity of hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S against human IL-17A (hIL-17A). Microtiter plates were coated with 50 μL of avidin at 10 μg/mL in coating buffer and incubated overnight at 4 °C in a moist chamber. The coated wells were washed three times with washing buffer (0.05 % Tween 20 in PBS). After washing, non-specific binding was blocked with a blocking solution (2 % Bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS with 0.05 % tween 20) for 1 h, and 50 μL of biotinylated-hIL-17A (1 µg/mL) (Sino Biological, Beijing, China) was added and incubated for 1 h at room temperature (RT). After washing, purified hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S (1 and 5 µg/mL) were added and incubated for 1 h. In this experiment, mouse anti-IL-17A mAb (neutralizing antibody mAb clone M237, Sino Biological, Beijing, China) (Cat.12047-M237) (1 μg/mL, 50 μL), which recognizes hIL-17A, was used as a positive control. After washing, 50 μL of HRP-conjugated anti-His6x antibody (dilution 1:3,000) to detect the binding activity of hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S, and HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse immunoglobulins (dilution 1:3000) was also added to positive control well, then incubated for 1 h. The reactions were developed with TMB substrate (Sera care, MA) and stopped with 1 N HCl. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm with an ELISA reader.

Epitope competition assay using competitive ELISA

The epitope recognition of hScFv-IL-17A on hIL-17A was determined using a competitive ELISA based on the competition between the IL-17A neutralizing antibody and hScFv-IL-17A. Microtiter plates were coated with 50 µL of hIL-17A (Sino Biological, Beijing, China) at 1 µg/mL in coating buffer and incubated overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber. Following incubation, wells were washed three times with washing buffer. Non-specific binding was then blocked by incubating with 2 % BSA in PBS for 1h. Subsequently, 50 µL of hScFv-IL-17A-WT (1 µg/mL) or hScFv-IL-17A-C97S (5 µg/mL), pre-mixed with 0.1, 1, or 2 µg/mL of IL-17A mAb (neutralizing antibody, clone M237) or 2 µg/mL of irrelevant monoclonal antibody to interferon gamma (anti-IFN-γ mAb, clone B27) was added to microwells. The condition without hIL-17A was employed as uninhibited control. After 1 h incubation, microwells were washed three times with washing buffer. The bound hScFv-IL-17A was incubated with HRP-conjugated anti-His6x antibody (diluted 1:3,000 in 2 % BSA/PBS) for 1 h. Finally, the wells were washed again, and the reactions were developed with TMB substrate. The reaction was stopped with 1 N HCl, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using an ELISA reader.

Affinity measurements using biolayer interferometry (BLI)

Binding assays were carried out using a BLI Octet K2 instrument (ForteBio, Fremont, CA, USA). All steps were performed at 30 °C in a 96-well plate containing 200 μL per well. Streptavidin-coated biosensors were loaded with 25 μg/mL biotinylated recombinant hIL-17A (Sino Biological, Beijing, China). The pre-loaded biosensors were placed in solutions of hScFv-IL-17A and its variants at a concentration of 10 µg/mL to start the binding process. The dissociation phase was monitored by immersing biosensors into the assay buffer. Kinetic parameters were analyzed and compared using ForteBio Data Analysis (ForteBio). The equilibrium constant (KD) was calculated using the ratio Koff/Kon.

Structural prediction and alignment of hScFv-IL-17A-C97S

The amino acid sequences, including VH and VL of hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S, were submitted to Antibody structure modeling (ABodyBuilder2)28 for predictive model generation. The predicted structure of hScFv-IL-17A-C97S was subsequently aligned with the hScFv-IL-17A-WT structure using RCSB PDB structure.29 UCSF ChimeraX software was utilized to visualize and compare the predicted models to assess conformational differences.

Binding interface analysis and protein stability prediction

Structural complex of Secukinumab Fab bound to IL-17A (PDB: 6WIO) was analyzed using PDBePISA v1.5230 to examine the binding interface, with a specific focus on the contribution of residue C97 in the VL domain. The potential impact of the C97S mutation on protein stability was evaluated using mCSM-stability.31.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted with at least three independent biological replicates, and each replicate was measured in technical triplicates. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software, version 10.3.1 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). For pairwise comparisons, an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was applied. Statistical significance was defined as follows: **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001.

Results and discussion

Investigating E. coli strains for enhanced hScFv-IL-17A expression

The hScFv-IL-17A proteins, including hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S, were obtained from 100 mL cultures of each E. coli strain. To evaluate the expression efficiency of the target protein, total protein samples were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and subsequently confirmed using Western blot analysis. From the SDS-PAGE result, both hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S were mainly expressed in the insoluble fractions in all E. coli strains (Origami B (DE3), SHuffle, and BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune, respectively). The C97S exhibited the highest level of expression in the insoluble fractions regardless of the host strain. In contrast, soluble proteins of both WT and C97S were mostly observed in SHuffle and BL21 (DE3) with the DisCoTune system as shown in Fig. 1a and b.

To provide a quantitative assessment of the practical scalability of these expression systems, the yields of purified hScFv-IL-17A variants were determined from 100 mL cultures and reported as mg per liter of culture (Supplementary Data). Consistent with Western blot analysis, the DisCoTune system provided the highest yields for both WT (2,625 µg/L) and C97S (4,750 µg/L) variants, with purities exceeding 98 % after Ni-NTA purification. The SHuffle strain produced moderate yields, while the Origami B strain yielded negligible amounts of soluble protein. These results underscore the superior performance of the DisCoTune system for scalable production of this challenging ScFv.

The expression of hScFv-IL-17A protein in different E. coli strains highlights the importance of choosing a suitable host for cysteine-containing antibody fragments production. Our results showed that Origami B (DE3) failed to produce soluble protein, while SHuffle and BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune supported soluble expression of both WT and C97S variants, with DisCoTune yielding the highest levels. This difference reflects the varying redox and folding capacities of each strain. Origami B (DE3) promotes cytoplasmic disulfide bond formation by disrupting trxB and gor, creating an oxidizing intracellular environment.32 However, the absence of cytoplasmic disulfide isomerase DsbC reduces its ability to resolve impaired disulfide bonds, which may lead to improper folding of complex proteins such as scFvs. SHuffle improves folding by providing both an oxidizing environment and DsbC33, while DisCoTune further enhances expression by fine-tuning protein folding conditions through plasmid-based regulation.17.

The strong performance of DisCoTune suggests that a well-balanced redox environment with proper folding support is critical for producing stable ScFv proteins, especially those with sensitive or unpaired cysteine residues. These findings align with previous studies and support the use of engineered strains like DisCoTune for efficient production of therapeutic antibody fragments.17.

Specifically, the purified hScFv-IL-17A-WT expressed from SHuffle and BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune at 1 and 5 µg/mL showed strong binding, compared to hScFv-IL-17A-C97S. However, as ELISA only measures the end-point binding activity against the hIL-17A, it does not fully capture the overall binding characteristics of hScFv-IL-17A. Therefore, BLI was employed to evaluate the binding kinetics of the hScFv-IL-17A.

Kinetic analysis was implemented through bio-layer interferometry (BLI) to further characterize the binding besides endpoint measurement. The kinetic parameters, including the association rate constant (Kon), dissociation rate constant (Koff), and equilibrium dissociation constant (KD), are summarized in Table 1. Our findings show that the WT/BL21Dc exhibits the strongest binding affinity to hIL-17A compared to the other hScFv-IL-17A proteins. This protein displayed a KD of 3.64 ± 0.03 × 10−8 M, characterized by a rapid association rate (Kon = 37.66 ± 0.53 × 103 1/Ms) and a slow dissociation rate (Koff = 1.37 ± 0.07 × 10−3 1/s). In comparison of hScFv-IL-17A-WT, WT/ showed a weak affinity, with a KD of 9.11 ± 0.02 × 10–8 M. Additionally, the C97S mutation in both C97S/BL21Dc and C97S/SHuffle significantly raised the KD values to 3.620 ± 9.47 × 10-7 M and 3.330 ± 5.82 × 10–7 M, respectively. This finding suggests that the C97S mutation negatively impacts the binding affinity of the hScFv-IL-17A-C97S. As expected, the anti-IL-17 mAb (positive control) exhibited the strongest binding affinity, with a KD of 1.05 ± 0.48 × 10−8 M, consistent with its expected high-affinity nature. Both ELISA and BLI assays confirmed consistent binding characteristics among the hScFv-IL-17A proteins.

Competitive ELISA for epitope Overlap between hScFv-IL-17A and IL17/IL17a neutralizing antibody

Because the C97S variant exhibited lower binding activity than WT in indirect ELISA, C97S was used at 5 µg/mL and WT at 1 µg/mL in the competitive ELISA to allow clearer comparison of their inhibitory profiles. The inhibitory efficacy of an IL-17A mAb (IL17/IL17a neutralizing antibody) on the binding of hScFv-IL-17A-WT (Fig. 4a) and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S (Fig. 4b) from BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune to hIL-17A was investigated.

The binding of hScFv-IL-17A to IL-17A was effectively inhibited by an IL-17A mAb in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4a and b). Importantly, the control antibody (anti-IFN-γ mAb, clone B27) had no effect on the binding, supporting that the inhibition was specific to IL-17A.

Importantly, no significant changes in the inhibition results were observed between the WT and C97S. This consistent competitive inhibition across both proteins strongly indicates that the IL-17A mAb (IL17/IL17A neutralizing antibody) and hScFv-IL-17A bind to the same or sterically overlapping epitopes on hIL-17A. Furthermore, these data suggest that the C97S does not critically disturb the binding properties of hScFv-IL-17A or the accessibility of its epitope for competitive inhibition by the IL-17A mAb.

In contrast to full-length mAbs, ScFvs present unique production and development challenges. These include issues with expression, solubility, and stability, which stem from their intrinsic structural variability and less predictable biophysical properties.34 To improve their therapeutic potential, strategies such as modifying the VH/VL interface or engineering alternative formats that mimic full-length antibodies are being explored.

A critical structural element influencing antibody stability is the presence of conserved cysteine residues, which form intradomain disulfide bonds essential for proper folding of both VH and VL domains.35 To further understand the differences in solubility and binding affinity observed in the C97S mutant, we considered the structural implications of this substitution. Unlike the canonical disulfide bond (e.g., Cys23–Cys88) essential for scFv stability35, Cys97 located in the CDR-L3 of Secukinumab is an unpaired cysteine residue prone to non-native disulfide formation or oxidative modification.6 To mitigate such structural risks, C97S was introduced without disrupting conserved framework features. Although this substitution improved solubility in certain E. coli hosts, it appeared to destabilize the local CDR environment, resulting in reduced binding affinity.

Such a disruption can compromise the thermodynamic stability of the fragment, result in misfolded domains, and alter the conformation of the CDRs, ultimately impairing antigen binding.36, 37, 38 Given the direct involvement of CDRs in IL-17 recognition, the C97S mutation is likely to reduce binding affinity and limit the therapeutic potential of hScFv-IL-17A. Interestingly, a structurally related scenario is observed in Secukinumab, a clinically approved anti-IL-17 mAb. This antibody possesses a unique free cysteine at position 97 of its light chain (CysL97) that does not participate in disulfide bonding. Despite being unpaired, CysL97 is functionally important, possibly contributing to antigen interaction. However, during recombinant production in mammalian cells, CysL97 is susceptible to undesirable oxidation or non-native disulfide formation, which may compromise antibody activity, structural integrity, and increase immunogenic risk.

Conventional approaches to address such redox imbalances often involve complete protein denaturation followed by refolding a process that is inefficient and potentially damaging to the native conformation. To overcome this, a patented method has been developed that selectively reduces CysL97, maintaining it in its active thiol (−SH) form without perturbing the overall antibody structure.6 This targeted redox control ensures the preservation of CysL97′s functional state during production and purification, leading to high yields of active and stable Secukinumab.

Taken together, these findings underscore the critical role of conserved and unpaired cysteine residues in determining the structural and functional integrity of antibody-based therapeutics. While conserved cysteines are indispensable for the stability of ScFv fragments, even non-disulfide-bonded cysteines, such as CysL97 in full-length mAbs, require precise redox management to ensure consistent bioactivity and manufacturability.

Among the ScFv-IL-17A proteins, hScFv-IL-17A-WT from BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune showed the highest binding affinity to hIL-17A (Table 1), supporting its potential as a lead candidate for therapeutic development. In contrast, the C97S mutation significantly impaired binding, indicating its detrimental effect on antigen recognition. These results suggest that preserving the native cysteine residue is critical for maintaining binding functionality. Overall, hScFv-IL-17A-WT from BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune appears to be the most suitable variant for further optimization and development as an IL-17A-targeted biologic.

Structural similarity between hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S, binding interface Analysis, and protein stability

The predicted structures of hScFv-IL-17A-WT and hScFv-IL-17A-C97S, generated using ABodyBuilder2, exhibited a high degree of structural similarity. Pairwise structural alignment revealed a RMSD of 0.06 Å for the VH domain and 0.12 Å for the VL domain (Table 2), indicating minimal conformational differences between the WT and mutant. Structural superimposition performed using UCSF ChimeraX further confirmed that the C97S substitution in the VL domain did not introduce global conformational changes compared to the WT structure (Fig. 5).

To further assess the structural and functional implications of the C97S mutation, the binding interface was analyzed using the crystal structure of the Secukinumab Fab-IL-17A complex (PDB ID: 6WIO) via PDBePISA. Notably, residue C97, located in the VL domain, was found not to be involved in critical hydrogen bonding at the binding interface. Cysteine residues contain a thiol (−SH) group, which may act as a weak hydrogen bond donor or a poor acceptor due to the relatively low electronegativity of sulfur.39 Moreover, cysteine's intrinsic reactivity allows for the formation of disulfide bonds, which would render the thiol group unavailable for hydrogen bonding.40.

To prevent the formation of such extraneous disulfide bonds, C97 was strategically mutated to serine to prevent the propensity for disulfide linkage. The impact of this mutation on protein stability was evaluated using mCSM-Stability, which predicted a destabilizing effect with a ΔΔG of –1.408 kcal/mol. This destabilization correlates with the observed decrease in protein yield, binding activity, and binding affinity of the hScFv-IL-17A-C97S mutant relative to the hScFv-IL-17A-WT.

Collectively, these findings suggest that while the C97S mutation does not disrupt the overall fold or binding interface of the antibody, it incurs a moderate stability penalty that likely underlies the reduced functional performance of the hScFv-IL-17A-C97S.

Clinical and translational relevance

The successful production of soluble hScFv-IL-17A in E. coli using the DisCoTune system has important implications for translational development. This bacterial expression platform yielded 2,625 µg/L of highly pure and active WT hScFv-IL-17A, demonstrating its potential as a rapid and cost-effective approach for early-stage therapeutic antibody fragment production. While mammalian systems remain indispensable for clinical-grade manufacturing of full-length IgGs due to their requirement for post-translational modifications such as glycosylation, the ScFv format does not depend on these modifications and can therefore be efficiently produced in bacterial hosts. The smaller size of ScFvs offers additional advantages, including improved tissue penetration and the ability to serve as modular building blocks for advanced therapeutic formats such as bispecific antibodies and antibody–drug conjugates. Nonetheless, further preclinical work is necessary to establish translational feasibility, including evaluation of in vivo efficacy, pharmacokinetics, and potential immunogenicity. Together, these findings support a two-tiered development strategy in which E. coli–based expression accelerates early optimization, while mammalian expression systems are applied in later stages to ensure regulatory compliance and clinical readiness.

Conclusion

This study successfully identified BL21 (DE3) with the DisCoTune plasmid as the most effective E. coli host for the soluble production of hScFv-IL-17A. We demonstrated a critical trade-off in protein engineering by comparing the WT construct to a C97S mutant. While the C97S mutation was designed to mitigate issues with an unpaired cysteine and did increase soluble protein yield, it severely impaired the antibody fragment's binding affinity by approximately 10-fold. Structural analysis suggests this functional loss stems from a moderate protein destabilization caused by the mutation, rather than a large-scale conformational change. The unpaired CysL97 residue, though not directly in the binding interface, appears crucial for maintaining the structural integrity required for optimal function. This work underscores an essential principle: strategies to enhance soluble expression do not always preserve biological activity. The structural and functional importance of native residues, even non-canonical ones, must be carefully considered. Consequently, the hScFv-IL-17A-WT produced in BL21 (DE3) with DisCoTune is the superior candidate for further development as a potential therapeutic against IL-17A.

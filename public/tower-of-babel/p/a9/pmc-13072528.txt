Plasma biomarkers of Alzheimer’s disease and their association with disproportionately enlarged subarachnoid space hydrocephalus
Authors: Camilo Bermudez, Ekaterina Hofrenning, Angela J Fought, Jeffrey L Gunter, Petrice M Cogswell, David T Jones, Christopher G Schwarz, Val Lowe, Benjamin D Elder, Ronald C Petersen, Prashanthi Vemuri, Alicia Algeciras-Schimnich, Michelle M Mielke, David S Knopman, Neill R Graff-Radford, Clifford R Jack, Jonathan Graff-Radford
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIndividuals with both Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) dynamics disorders, such as idiopathic normal-pressure hydrocephalus (iNPH), exhibit reduced CSF Aβ42 levels, complicating the interpretation of AD biomarkers. However, the influence of CSF dynamics on blood-based AD biomarkers remains unclear. This study investigated whether immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry (IP-MS)-based AD plasma biomarkers were associated with disproportionately enlarged subarachnoid space hydrocephalus (DESH) on MRI.MethodThis retrospective, cross-sectional study included 509 participants from the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging who underwent MRI, [¹¹C] Pittsburgh compound B (Aβ) PET imaging, and plasma assessments of phosphorylated tau 217 (p-Tau 217), Aβ40, and Aβ42. Age-adjusted logistic regression analyses were conducted to evaluate whether abnormal levels of p-Tau 217, %p-Tau 217 (ratio of phosphorylated to non-phosphorylated tau × 100%), Aβ42, or the Aβ42/40 ratio were associated with DESH, as identified by a previously validated automated algorithm. In addition, combinations of binary amyloid PET and plasma biomarkers to create 4 categories were tested for an association with DESH as a sensitivity analysis.ResultAverage age was 72.2 (8.8), with 54% male participants, and 30% were APOE4 carriers. Thirty-two participants (6.3%) were classified as having DESH. Abnormal (low) plasma Aβ42 (OR: 2.56 [95% CI: 1.19, 5.74]) and abnormal (high) p-tau217 (OR: 2.74 [95% CI: 1.09, 7.85]) were associated with DESH, but not the respective ratios of Aβ42/40 (OR: 1.95 [95% CI: 0.94, 4.17]) or %p-tau (OR: 1.82 [95% CI: 0.74, 4.20]). Analysis of the combined variables (amyloid PET and plasma biomarkers) demonstrated that an association with DESH was observed only in participants who were abnormal on both measures, compared with participants who were negative on both plasma biomarkers and PET. This association was attenuated when biomarker ratios were used.ConclusionThe observed association between abnormal plasma biomarker levels and DESH aligns with previous CSF studies and can be mitigated by utilizing the biomarker ratios. However, further validation is needed in populations with more severe CSF flow abnormalities to better understand the role of plasma biomarkers in this population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12987-026-00789-3.Keywords: Plasma biomarkers, CSF dynamics, DESH, Alzheimer’s disease

Introduction

Plasma biomarkers for Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) have been increasingly measured for early identification of asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic individuals due to its correlation with amyloid PET positivity [1]. Prior to the development of plasma biomarkers, CSF amyloid biomarkers like β-amyloid 1–42 (Aβ42) to Aβ40 ratio or p-Tau181/Abeta42 were the preferred indicator of β-amyloid accumulation and association with amyloid PET positivity [2]. Discordance between amyloid PET and CSF Aβ42 has been reported in about 20% of cognitively unimpaired individuals [3]. Individuals with idiopathic normal-pressure hydrocephalus (iNPH), a disorder of CSF dynamics that affects 1–8% of older adults, have low levels of CSF Aβ42, similar to AD, with concomitant decrease in p-tau and total tau levels [4–6] thus making the CSF Aβ42 by itself less reliable. A ratio of biomarkers such as ptau181/Aβ42 or Aβ42/40 is therefore used to differentiate amyloid PET status because the ratio better accounts for variability in Aβ42 in CSF dynamics disorders [7]. Importantly, studies have shown normalization of CSF biomarkers after shunt placement, suggesting that the decrease in CSF Aβ42 is due to CSF dynamics rather than amyloid deposition in the brain [8]. However, there is limited evidence on how CSF dynamics affect plasma biomarkers.

Disproportionately enlarged subarachnoid hydrocephalus (DESH) is a radiographic phenotype for CSF dynamics disorders seen on MRI characterized by large spaces of CSF in the Sylvan fissure, tight sulci in the high-convexity, ventriculomegaly, and DESH which has been described in iNPH [9] as well as in a subset of asymptomatic general population and can be detected and scored using an automated MRI tool [10, 11]. Previous work by Graff-Radford et al. showed, in a cohort of asymptomatic individuals, that those with abnormal CSF Aβ42 but normal amyloid PET, were more likely to have higher DESH scores, suggesting that CSF dynamics can confound the interpretation of CSF biomarkers [12]. It is unclear whether production and clearance of plasma biomarkers is similarly affected by the presence of altered CSF dynamics or DESH, which may inform clinical decision making during evaluations of cognitive decline, Previous studies have also found that medical comorbidities like hypertension, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, tobacco use, and elevated body mass index (BMI) can affect plasma levels of single analytes like Aβ42, Aβ40, total tau, neurofilament light (NfL), and to a lesser degree biomarker ratios like Aβ42/40 [13–15], but there is no evidence to date on the association between DESH and plasma biomarkers of AD.

Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to determine if commonly used AD plasma biomarkers, such as Aβ42 and p-tau217, were associated with the presence of DESH in an asymptomatic cohort from a population-based study. The secondary objective was to identify whether these associations differed with elevations in amyloid PET. Due to shared cognitive symptoms and abnormal neuroimaging between NPH and AD, it is important to contextualize the use of blood-based biomarkers to avoid misdiagnosis. To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate associations between AD plasma biomarkers and DESH. We hypothesize that the ratio of Aβ42/40 or p-tau217/non-phosphorylated tau, would eliminate these differences in DESH status similar to the ratios in the CSF. Together, these findings may better help contextualize plasma biomarkers when disorders of CSF dynamics may be present to better identify candidates for novel disease-modifying therapies such as targeted amyloid treatments.

Methods

Participants

We recruited 509 cognitively unimpaired participants aged 50 + from the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging (MCSA) at Mayo Clinic Rochester, a population-based prospective study involving residents of Olmsted County, Minnesota, with MRI brain, Amyloid PET, and plasma data available. We ascertained age and cognitive status at the visit when MR and PET imaging were acquired between October 2008 and May 2022. Each visit included a comprehensive physician examination, cognitive assessments, and a blood draw for plasma biomarker analysis, all conducted within the same session. Cognitive assessment was determined by a panel composed of a neuropsychologist, a physician, and a study coordinator using consensus criteria for cognitively unimpaired (CU), MCI, or dementia [16, 17]. APOE allele status (ε2, ε3, ε4) was determined through standard genotyping methods on blood samples, with APOE ε4 carrier status determined by one or two copies of ε4 [18].The study received approval from the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board and the Olmsted Medical Center Institutional Review Board in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its later revisions. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Plasma biomarker measurements

EDTA-plasma samples were shipped frozen to C2N Diagnostics for analysis. Concentrations (pg/mL) of Aβ42, Aβ40, p-tau217 and np-tau217 were determined by immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry as previously described [19, 20]. An Aβ42 concentration and the Aβ42/40 ratio results of < 41.5 pg/mL and < 0.089, respectively, where considered abnormal [20]. The %p-tau217 was calculated as p-tau217 concentration divided by the non-phosphorylated tau 217 (np-tau217) concentration × 100%. For samples with p-tau217 concentrations below the analytical measurement range (< 0.50 pg/mL), a value of 0.25 pg/mL was used to calculate the %p-tau217. p-Tau 217 concentrations > 0.796 pg/mL and a %p-Tau 217 ratio > 1.55% were considered abnormal [21].

MRI acquisition and DESH classification

Magnetization-prepared rapid gradient-echo (MPRAGE) images were acquired using standardized imaging parameters on 3T GE MRI scanners (GE Healthcare). For full acquisition details, refer to Jack et al. [22]. We utilized a previously established, fully automated imaging pipeline for detecting DESH [11]. The algorithm began by processing the SPM12 CSF-segmentation probability in each sulcal region. The algorithm was trained using manual grading of DESH by an expert clinician as the reference standard [11]. A support vector machine (SVM) model was then trained on DESH-predictive CSF regions to classify DESH scans with an AUC > 0.96 compared to expert evaluations [11]. This DESH-detection pipeline was validated on a separate dataset from the training set and further validated by a board-certified neuroradiologist who visually graded DESH without knowledge of the machine-learning categorization. The machine-learning approach demonstrated 100% sensitivity and 91% specificity when compared to expert visual grading [10]. The output of the algorithm was referred to as the “DESH pattern score.” The SVM model generated negative scores when no DESH pattern was detected and a positive score when a DESH pattern is present, with progressively higher scores indicating a stronger match with a DESH radiographic pattern. An abnormal DESH pattern score was defined as ≥ 1.0 to dichotomize those who were DESH-positive and DESH-negative [10, 11]. All imaging and postprocessing were inspected for quality control with all 509 participants passing quality control. DESH classification was blinded to biomarker status.

Amyloid PET measurements

Amyloid PET imaging was conducted using Pittsburgh compound B (PiB). Detailed procedures for PET acquisition have been previously published [22–24]. The prefrontal, orbitofrontal, parietal, temporal, anterior and posterior cingulate, and precuneus regions composed a meta-region of interest where weighted average of median PiB uptake in these voxels was normalized by median uptake in the cerebellar crus grey matter. Abnormal amyloid deposition was defined as a Centiloid value ≥ 25 [25].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were calculated both for the overall cohort and by DESH presence. For continuous variables, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were applied for quantitative variables because they were not normally distributed, while Pearson’s Chi-squared tests were used for categorical variables when comparing DESH subgroups.

Multivariable logistic regression models were used to test associations between biomarker positivity and presence of DESH (DESH score ≥ 1). All models were age-adjusted, with age divided by10 (i.e. decade) for interpretability of model parameter estimates. We estimated and reported odds ratios (OR) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). Additional multivariable logistic models were performed predicting abnormal DESH, adjusted for age and including a combination of each binary plasma biomarker with amyloid PET status creating 4 categories (-/-, -/+, +/-, +/+) as the predictor of interest. Linear regression models, adjusted for age, were also performed evaluating if each plasma biomarker and amyloid PET were related to ventricular volume (log-transformed). A significance level of 0.05 was set for all statistical tests. Since we were interested in each combination of plasma and PET rather than a universal null hypothesis, we did not adjust for multiple comparisons [26, 27]. We also performed linear regression to evaluate the relationship between log-transformed ventricular volume (the outcome) and binary PET and then each binary plasma biomarker univariately and adjusted for age. All analyses were completed using R statistical software version 4.1.1.

P-tau217, %p-tau217, and DESH

In multivariable analyses, there was an association between having DESH and an abnormal (high) plasma p-tau217 (OR: 2.74 [95% CI: 1.09, 7.85]) but not with abnormal (high) %plasma p-tau217 (OR: 1.82 [95% CI: 0.74, 4.20]) (Fig. 1 and Supplemental Table 2). When using the combination of p-tau217 and amyloid-PET status, the group with both elevated p-tau217 and amyloid PET was more likely to have DESH than the group with normal p-tau217 and PET (OR: 3.15 [95% CI: 1.07, 10.13]). There was no difference in the group with only elevated p-tau217 (OR: 2.06 [95% CI: 0.72, 6.35]) compared to the double-normal group. There were no participants with normal plasma p-tau217 and elevated amyloid PET. When using the combination variable of %p-tau217 and amyloid PET, we found no difference in groups including having both elevated %p-tau217 and amyloid PET (OR: 1.88 [95% CI: 0.62, 5.24]), having elevated plasma only (OR: 2.84 [95% CI: 0.62, 9.70]), or having elevated amyloid PET only (OR: 2.02 [95%CI: 0.61, 5.80]) compared to the double-normal group (Fig. 2 and Supplemental Table 3).

Discussion

In this retrospective study of cognitively unimpaired individuals from the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging, we evaluated if DESH was associated with AD plasma biomarkers, such as Aβ42 and p-tau217. We found that having an abnormal plasma Aβ42 or p-tau217 was associated with the presence of DESH, which could be corrected when implementing a ratio of biomarkers such as Aβ42/40 or %p-tau. Closer inspection of combination variables including plasma and amyloid PET showed that those who had abnormal plasma and amyloid-PET were more likely to have DESH compared to the group with normal plasma and amyloid PET.

Amyloid biomarkers associated with DESH

Previous work has shown that CSF dynamics disorders like iNPH or DESH can affect measurement of AD biomarkers in CSF, with low levels of Aβ42 and phosphorylated tau. Here, we showed that lower plasma Aβ42 is associated with the presence of DESH, which can be mitigated by using the ratio of Aβ42/40, like the amyloid/tau ratio used in CSF biomarkers to differentiate between AD and iNPH. Although previous work has shown that those with abnormal CSF and normal amyloid PET subgroup was more strongly associated with DESH [10], we observed that the those with abnormal plasma Aβ42 biomarker and amyloid-PET are most strongly associated with DESH. Although the group with low Aβ42 and normal amyloid PET group did not reach statistical significance, the effect was in the expected direction, suggesting the signal may be driven by AD plasma-biomarker change rather than amyloid PET elevation. Importantly, we showed that this group effect is not present when the Aβ42/40 ratio was used, again suggesting that it was driven by plasma biomarkers and not PET abnormality. With the increasing prevalence of DESH in an aging population, it is important to interpret AD blood biomarkers with caution, particularly when making treatment decisions.

Tau biomarkers associated with DESH

In the case of plasma phosphorylated tau biomarkers, we showed similar results with abnormal (higher) levels of p-tau217 being associated with the DESH group. Again, this effect can be mitigated by taking a ratio of biomarkers such as %p-tau217. Similar results were found when combining variables to include amyloid PET status. Including both abnormal plasma and PET was more likely to be associated with DESH compared to the double-normal group, but only when using the uncorrected biomarker and not the ratio. Again, the group with high p-tau217 and normal PET group did not reach statistical significance but showed a trend towards significant association with DESH, suggesting that these differences may be dictated by plasma biomarker factors more so than amyloid PET elevation, but the variance was also greater in the p-tau217 analyses. Although it would be expected to find a lower level of p-tau217 due to reported lower CSF p-tau in iNPH, we also know that iNPH is associated with other nonspecific biomarkers of neurodegeneration like neurofilament light (NfL) and the observed positive association between p-tau217 and DESH may reflect neurodegeneration rather than amyloidosis, particularly given the results that include amyloid PET [12, 28, 29]. There is a known association between plasma biomarkers and ventriculomegaly [30], but when investigated in this cohort, this association was not significant when controlling for age (Supplementary Table 1). It is also possible that CSF and plasma measures behave differently under DESH positivity as these are produced and cleared by different mechanisms. Although previous work has shown that mass spectroscopy-based p-tau217 to be amongst the highest accuracy and least susceptible plasma biomarkers to medical comorbidities like chronic kidney disease, this work suggests that DESH may play a role in the interpretation of p-tau217 [13]

Conclusions and limitations

This is the first study to investigate the association between DESH and mass-spectroscopy based AD biomarkers in a cognitively unimpaired population. This is a relevant study as there is a known association between DESH, iNPH, and CSF biomarkers. As plasma biomarkers become increasingly available, it is important to interpret these tests in the correct personalized context. However, this study has several limitations. We studied a cohort of cognitively unimpaired individuals in a population-based study, which may not reflect the full spectrum of CSF-dynamics. Investigating the effect of plasma biomarkers on a population diagnosed with iNPH, before and after shunt placement, may further demonstrate the effect of CSF dynamics on plasma biomarker measurement. In addition, a head-to-head comparison of these biomarkers between AD and iNPH may be necessary to further support diagnostic considerations, particularly when co-pathology is a consideration.

Previous work has shown that immunoassay plasma biomarkers are susceptible to multiple medical comorbidities like diabetes, body mass index, and chronic kidney disease [13–15, 31].There are limited data on whether mass spectroscopy-based biomarkers, like the ones used in this work, are similarly susceptible, but recent work supports that a reference peptide such as non-phosphorylated tau as a ratio may mitigate the effect of disorders like chronic kidney disease [32, 33].

We were also limited by sample size in this work due to the small number of DESH-positive subjects. For example, we did not have the statistical power to control for additional variables such as sex, MMSE, APOE, BMI, Diabetes, ventricular volume, or other chronic medical conditions. Further work may be necessary to understand the full effect of these comorbidities on plasma biomarkers in the context of CSF dynamics disorders. Given the small sample size, we were unable to further explore the effect of amyloid positivity on associations with DESH, which seemed most pronounced with amyloid biomarkers. Future larger studies can study the interaction between amyloid PET positivity and plasma biomarkers for the prediction of DESH or focus entirely in an amyloid PET negative population. Some of the findings for logistic models including each combination variable (combinations of binary amyloid PET and binary plasma categories) should be interpreted with caution due to low sample size (Supplementary Table 4). Specifically, the Aβ negative, amyloid PET positive cases and %p-tau217 positive, amyloid PET negative subgroups, may represent false negative and false positive biomarkers, respectively. Lastly, the MCSA is representative of southeast Minnesota, which is predominantly white non-Hispanic, but further evaluation in populations with a dynamic range of medical comorbidities and backgrounds is necessary to fully understand the associations of these biomarkers to CSF dynamics disorders.

The observed association between abnormal biomarker levels and DESH aligns with previous CSF studies and can be mitigated by utilizing the biomarker ratios. However, further validation is needed in populations with more severe CSF flow abnormalities to better understand the extent of these associations.

Real-world treatment adherence and persistence among patients initiating inclisiran or PCSK9 mAbs
Authors: Robert S Rosenson, Xiaoli Niu, Lyuba Popadic, Xinshuo Ma, Pam Kumparatana, Yuqin Wei, Yousuf Ali, Sean McElligott
Published: 2026

Introduction

Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)-targeted therapies are a class of nonstatin lipid-lowering therapy (LLT), which may be utilized in patients who require additional lowering of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) despite statin therapy, or with statin intolerance [1]. PCSK9-targeted therapies include monoclonal antibodies (mAbs; alirocumab/evolocumab), which are self-administered every 2 to 4 weeks, and the small interfering RNA inclisiran, administered by a healthcare professional twice-yearly after the initial and 3-month doses [1,2].

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies reported that implementation and persistence with PCSK9-targeted therapy declined over time, with a 12-month proportion of days covered (PDC) of 69.7% and persistence rate of 81.8% [2]. However, inclisiran adherence and persistence data remain limited, and the extent to which they are influenced by the less frequent dosing schedule remains unclear [1,2]. Therefore, we evaluated 1-year adherence and persistence among patients initiating inclisiran, alirocumab, or evolocumab using claims data.

Methods

Data source and study design

This observational study used de-identified administrative claims (open and closed) from the US Komodo Health database (January 2021–August 2023). The index date was the first claim for inclisiran, alirocumab, or evolocumab between January and August 2022. Baseline and follow-up periods were 12 months before and after the index date, respectively. Institutional review board approval was waived due to the use of de-identified claims data.

Patient population

Patients were ≥ 18 years of age, had a first claim for therapy between January and August 2022, and had either 12 months of continuous enrollment both before (inclusive) and after (exclusive) index date (for closed claims), or, for open claims, 12 months of follow-up post-index and at least the median number of claims (based on claims volume among all patients with open claims). Patients with a first claim for inclisiran after a claim for alirocumab or evolocumab were included in the inclisiran cohort. Cohorts were mutually exclusive.

Adherence and persistence

Adherence was assessed by 12-month PDC (days with access to medication/365), with full adherence defined as PDC ≥ 0.80. Number of days with access to medication was determined according to prescribing information, applying allowable dosing windows of 90 days for inclisiran (182 days [92 + 90] with access after the initial dose and 273 days [183 + 90] after the subsequent doses) and 7 days for alirocumab/evolocumab (23 days [16 + 7] with access for biweekly doses and 38 days [31 + 7] for monthly doses). Persistence was defined as remaining on therapy at 12 months without treatment gaps of > 60 days for alirocumab/evolocumab or > 90 days for inclisiran.

Propensity score matching

Mean PDC with mAbs was compared with inclisiran using propensity score matching (PSM) followed by multivariable regression analyses. Propensity scores were estimated using multivariate logistic regression based on baseline demographic and clinical characteristics. Nearest neighbor matching was performed using a 1:1 ratio and a caliper of 0.2 (based on the standard deviation [SD] of the logit of the propensity score). Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences, with values > 0.1 indicating meaningful imbalance. Multivariable linear and logistic regression models were subsequently used to evaluate differences in PDC between cohorts.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 852 patients were included in the inclisiran cohort, 8878 in the alirocumab cohort, and 27,171 in the evolocumab cohort, with mean ages of 69.7, 66.2, and 64.5 years, respectively. The most common insurance type was commercial, comprising 50.2% of the inclisiran cohort, 50.4% of the alirocumab cohort, and 61.1% of the evolocumab cohort. Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) at baseline was recorded in 81.1% of patients initiating inclisiran, 62.4% of patients initiating alirocumab, and 66.6% of patients initiating evolocumab. Patients who initiated inclisiran also had a higher prevalence of hypertension and diabetes, as well as a higher mean Charlson Comorbidity Index score. Statin use was recorded in 52.9%, 59.6%, and 63.1% of patients who initiated inclisiran, alirocumab, and evolocumab, respectively.

Adherence and persistence

Before PSM, mean (SD) PDC was higher in the inclisiran cohort (0.90 [0.20]) than the alirocumab (0.72 [0.32]) or evolocumab cohorts (0.71 [0.33]). After PSM, mean (SD) PDC was 0.71 (0.33) with alirocumab and 0.70 (0.33) with evolocumab (both p < 0.001 vs inclisiran; Fig. 1A). The proportion of patients with PDC ≥ 0.8 was 79.1%, 57.1%, and 56.6% in the inclisiran, alirocumab, and evolocumab cohorts, respectively (Fig. 1B). The 12-month persistence rate was 80.2% with inclisiran and was 57.6% and 57.2% with alirocumab and evolocumab, respectively (Fig. 1C). In the inclisiran cohort, 482 patients (56.6%) received at least three doses, 213 (25.0%) received two, and 157 (18.4%) received one.

Across subgroups according to insurance type and ASCVD subtype, mean PDC was higher with inclisiran than with PCSK9 mAbs; however, ASCVD subgroups within the inclisiran cohort were limited in size. In a sensitivity analysis using multiple allowable windows for determining days with access to medication, mean PDC with inclisiran remained ≥ 0.80 after reducing the allowable window from 90 to 30 days. In the alirocumab and evolocumab cohorts, mean PDC remained < 0.80 when the allowable window was increased from 7 to either 14 or 28 days. With no allowable window, mean PDC was 0.77 in the inclisiran cohort, 0.68 in the alirocumab cohort, and 0.67 in the evolocumab cohort.

Discussion

This real-world study demonstrated high adherence and persistence during the first 12 months after inclisiran initiation, with consistent observations across subgroups. Adherence was suboptimal among patients initiating PCSK9 mAbs, consistent with prior real-world studies [[3], [4], [5]], and a recent systematic review reporting declining implementation and persistence over time [2]. Because adherence to LLT is associated with improved outcomes [6], understanding factors that influence adherence could help inform treatment selection. Accordingly, the 2026 dyslipidemia guideline recognizes that factors such as dosing frequency, clinician administration, and patient preference should be considered when selecting appropriate nonstatin LLT [1].

Adherence in our analysis was measured by PDC, which may capture different aspects of treatment implementation for inclisiran (healthcare system-level treatment delivery) and PCSK9 mAbs (patient-directed medication use), limiting direct comparisons. Although mean PDC with inclisiran was high, approximately 20% of patients received only one dose, highlighting the importance of interpreting PDC in the context of persistence when evaluating treatment implementation.

Limitations of this analysis include: (1) administrative claims data are collected for non-research purposes with limited clinical detail, potential for missing data, and coding errors; (2) clinical characteristics and medications beyond the 12-month baseline period were not captured; (3) although LLTs are intended to be taken long-term [1], our analysis only captured findings from the first 12 months following treatment initiation; (4) treatment initiation was between January and August 2022, shortly after inclisiran’s approval in December 2021, so prescribing patterns during the study period may differ from contemporary use and reflect early adopter biases, which could contribute to residual confounding despite PSM.

In conclusion, patients initiating inclisiran in this real-world study had high adherence (mean PDC ≥ 0.80) and a low discontinuation rate (∼20%), whereas PCSK9 mAbs were associated with suboptimal adherence (mean PDC ∼0.70) and > 40% of patients discontinued within 1 year. These findings may suggest that less frequent clinician-administered dosing with inclisiran supports adherence and persistence in clinical practice.

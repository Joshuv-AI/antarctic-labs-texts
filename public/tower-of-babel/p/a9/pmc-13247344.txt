Identifying expression and DNA methylation biomarkers for lung adenocarcinoma risk in East Asia
Authors: Tzu-Yu Chen, Li-Hsin Chien, Shih Sheng Jiang, Jiyeon Choi, Hsiao-Han Hung, Chin-Fu Hsiao, Jianxin Shi, Batel Blechter, Charles E Breeze, Gee-Chen Chang, Kuan-Yu Chen, Ying-Huang Tsai, Wu-Chou Su, Ming-Shyan Huang, Yuh-Min Chen, Yueh-Feng Wen, Chung-Yu Chen, Chih-Liang Wang, Fang-Yu Tsai, Hui-Ling Chen, Wan-Shan Hsieh, Hsien-Chih Lin, Lu-Hai Wang, Chien-Jen Chen, Pan-Chyr Yang, Chih-Yi Chen, Nathaniel Rothman, Stephen J Chanock, Qing Lan, I-Shou Chang, Chao A Hsiung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common type of lung cancer. A recent genome-wide association study of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia reported 28 independent susceptibility variants across 25 loci and identified 2 genes whose genetically predicted expression levels are associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk, using an ancestry-matched lung tissue expression quantitative trait loci (QTL) dataset. It is desirable to identify additional susceptibility loci and to understand their underlying biological mechanisms.MethodsUsing an expanded genome-wide association study of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia and ancestry-matched lung tissue expression QTL and DNA methylation QTL datasets, we performed transcriptome-wide association studies and DNA methylome-wide association studies simultaneously and examined the association between measured expression of genes and DNA methylation of nearby CpGs. Genes and nearby CpGs are termed CpG–gene–LUAD trios if these 3 associations hold simultaneously.ResultsAt a Bonferroni-corrected P value less than .05, we identified a new susceptibility locus (6p21.31; lead single-nucleotide polymorphism rs7772643), 10 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes, and 86 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs. At a false discovery rate q value less than 0.05, we identified 28 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes, 220 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs, and 45 CpG–gene–LUAD trios; among them, 43 were direction-matched regarding these 3 associations. These show that 23 of the known 28 susceptibility variants for lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia are near these genes or CpGs and MARCH3, ELF5, IKZF3, GSDMB, CCDC116, and DSP are putative novel candidate genes. Few of them were reported in lung adenocarcinoma in European populations.ConclusionsThis study substantially advances our understanding of the etiology of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia and could be useful in developing translational applications.

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common histologic subtype of lung cancer worldwide1-3 and in Taiwan.4 The most recent genome-wide association study (GWAS) of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia reported 28 independent susceptibility genetic variants across 25 loci achieving genome-significance (5E-8), termed EALUAD–single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) below;5 most of them showed no evidence of association in European populations. Two lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes, FADS1 and ELF5, were also identified through colocalization studies and a transcriptome-wide association study using a Taiwanese lung expression quantitative trait loci (QTL) dataset. Comparison of the transcriptome-wide association study using an expression QTL dataset from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) consortium indicated the relevance of ancestry-matched expression QTL datasets.5

Transcriptome-wide association studies, which assess the associations between disease risk and genetically predicted gene expression using multiple cis-variants, have become an important strategy for identifying target genes and biological mechanisms underlying GWAS findings.6-8 However, many GWAS loci still lack transcriptome-wide association study–identified genes.

Using the GTEx dataset, a recent study demonstrated that many CpGs were associated with nearby genetic variants; some of them were colocalized with GWAS-identified loci, but most of the colocalizations did not involve expression QTLs.9 These findings prompted the development of a DNA methylome-wide association study of solid tumors,10 which assesses the associations between cancer susceptibility and the genetically predicted DNA methylation.

In particular, using DNA methylation profiles of lung tissues from 190 individuals in the GTEx database and their genotype data, Yang and colleagues10 developed genetically predictive models at 195 764 CpGs and applied these models to summary statistics from GWASs of lung cancer that included 12 081 patients and 158 737 healthy control participants from East Asia11,12 and 38 422 patients and 677 930 healthy control participants from Europe.13-15 They identified 579 CpGs associated with lung cancer risk at the Bonferroni-corrected P value of .05 (Supplementary Data 1 and 5 in Yang and colleagues10) as well as 293 CpG–gene–lung cancer trios, suggesting that the DNA methylation levels at 89 distinct CpG sites might influence lung cancer risk through regulating the expression of 50 unique cis-genes.

Because the vast majority of the 190 donors used by Yang and colleagues to develop prediction models were of European descent and had a history of smoking16 and the GWAS of lung cancer used by them included patients of both European and Asian descent and all histologic types, a critical need remains to develop prediction models using lung tissues from individuals of East Asian ancestry and apply them to GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia only.

In this study, we performed a transcriptome-wide association study and methylome-wide association study of lung adenocarcinoma using summary statistics from an expanded GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia. By utilizing expanded expression QTL reference datasets and DNA methylation QTL reference datasets of lung tissue from Taiwanese never-smokers, we first built genetic models that predict expression and DNA methylation levels using cis-variants and then applied these models to summary statistics from the expanded GWAS to assess the associations between these genetically predicted quantitative traits and lung adenocarcinoma risk. On the basis of these lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes from the transcriptome-wide association study and the lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs from the methylome-wide association study, we examined the associations between their measured gene expression and measured DNA methylation and reported the plausible insights into loci identified by previous GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia, the putative novel lung adenocarcinoma susceptibility loci, and the potential causal biomarkers for lung adenocarcinoma risk.

Methods

An expanded GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia

This study used summary statistics from 2 GWASs of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia. The first was the discovery dataset in Shi and colleagues,5 with 11 753 patients and 30 562 healthy control participants, referred to as EALUAD. The second was the GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma using samples in the Taiwan Biobank and the Genetic Epidemiology study of Lung AdenoCarcinoma in Taiwan (GELAC) and Lung Cancer PharmacoGenomics study in Taiwan that were not included in EALUAD, referred to as TLUAD. TLUAD included 1514 patients with lung adenocarcinoma and 6628 sex-, age-, and smoking status–matched controls (each case was matched with 1 to 5 controls) from the GELAC (n = 872) and Taiwan Biobank (n = 5756); these studies were described in our earlier publications.17-21

Genotyping, data quality control, and imputation of TLUAD were performed following a standard procedure described in our previous publications,19-21 and the GWAS was analyzed by using additive logistic regression models, adjusting for sex, age, smoking status, and the top 5 genotype-based principal components.

We denote by TEALUAD the summary statistics resulting from the meta-analysis of the summary statistics from EALUAD and those from TLUAD, using the algorithm of Ma and colleagues.22 Details are given in Supplementary Methods Section 1, including imputation and other details.

Expression studies

After data preprocessing, we obtained the mRNA expression data from the nontumor lung tissues adjacent to their cancers of 176 never-smoking patients with lung adenocarcinoma (LCTCNS-E). Among them, 115 were assayed by Illumina array platforms (NEA-115), and the remaining 61 samples by an Illumina RNA-sequencing platform (NES-61). All patients were from GELAC and had their genotype data available.

Both NEA-115 and NES-61 were preprocessed using the algorithm principal components analysis;23 the choices of expression-based principal components for the removal of unmeasured expression heterogeneity were determined by following the discovery pipeline of GTEx expression QTL so that the choices resulted in more probes or genes whose expression was associated with nearby SNPs;24,25 it used genotype data at a total of 3 501 312 SNPs, which is the set SNP-C defined in Figure S1.

We combined NEA-115 and NES-61 to form NEC-176. The expression profiles for NEA-115, NES-61, and NEC-176 regarded 24 216 probes, 18 390 genes, and 20 422 probe–gene features, respectively. Roughly speaking, a feature was defined for a gene if there was a probe located in the gene based on NEA-115 and there was RNA-sequencing data for the gene based on NES-61. If there were 2 probes in a gene, we might define 2 features. These combined with the genotype data at SNPs in SNP-C resulted in 3 expression QTL datasets. Details, including confounding adjustment, are in Supplementary Methods Section 2, where Figure S2 illustrates the criterion for selecting principal components for reducing unmeasured expression heterogeneity, and Figure S3 illustrates the criterion for excluding 2 outliers that resulted in NES-61.

DNA methylation studies

After data preprocessing, we obtained a cohort of 161 never-smoking patients with lung adenocarcinoma for DNA methylation studies (LCTCNS-M). The nontumor tissue adjacent to the lung cancer of each of them was assayed by 1 of the Illumina BeadChip platforms to profile its DNA methylation. Among them, 114 samples were profiled using 450K chips (NM450-114), 31 samples using EPIC platform (NM850-31), and the remaining 16 samples using EPICv2 platform (NM900-16). NM850-31 and NM900-16 were combined to form NMC-47, and NM450-114 and NMC-47 were combined to form NMC-161. Details are in Supplementary Methods Section 3.

These 161 individuals were from the GELAC and had their genotype data available. All patients in NM450-114 were in NEA-115; 21 patients in NM850-31; and all patients in NM900-16 were in NES-61. Using a set of 3 421 229 SNPs, which is the set SNP-E defined in Figure S1, we performed data preprocessing for each of NM450-114, NMC-47, and NMC-161 to reduce unmeasured DNA methylation heterogeneity, similar to those for expression dataset NEA-115; details are given in Supplementary Methods Section 3.

Finally, the DNA methylation profiles for NM450-114, NMC-47, and NMC-161 involved 373 275 CpGs, 558 115 CpGs, and 298 801 CpGs, respectively. These together with SNP-E form methylation QTL datasets (Figure S1).

Ethic statements

This study was approved by the institutional review board of the National Health Research Institutes, Taiwan (approval number EC1000902). Written consent was obtained from each participant in this study. In particular, all the patients in LCTCNS-E were treated surgically at the China Medical University Hospital, Taiwan, which was approved by the institutional review boards of the National Health Research Institutes, Taiwan, and of the China Medical University, Taiwan; we obtained informed consent from each patient and collected their tumor tissues, adjacent nontumor tissues, blood, and clinical information.

Building models for the transcriptome-wide association study and methylome-wide association study

For each of NEA-115, NES-61, and NEC-176, we applied the R script FUSION.compute_weights.R7 to build elastic net regression models for predicting gene expression levels using nearby SNPs from a set of 2 891 398 strand-unambiguous SNPs, which is the set SNP-F defined in Figure S1. Using these models and TEALUAD, we applied the script FUSION.assoc_test.R to conduct a transcriptome-wide association study,7 with the 1000 Genomes Project Phase 3 East Asian samples (n = 504) as the linkage disequilibrium (LD) reference panel.

Models were considered predictive if they satisfied the performance criterion of a fivefold cross-validation P value less than .01. A gene was considered to have a successful transcriptome-wide association study analysis if it had a predictive model and met FUSION’s SNP availability requirement that at least half of the SNPs in the model have summary statistics available in the GWAS dataset. Details are in Supplementary Methods Section 4, where Figure S4 shows the numbers of predictive models for each of the reference datasets and their overlaps.

Following the same strategy for transcriptome-wide association study modeling and analysis, we developed methylation prediction models using NM450-114, NMC-47, and NMC-161 separately and performed association testing for the methylome-wide association study.

To study the associations between measured expression levels and nearby DNA methylation, we formed the dataset NEM-114, comprising individuals common to both NEA-115 and NM450-114. Similarly, NEM-37 comprised individuals common to NES-61 and NMC-47, and NEM-151 comprised individuals common to NEC-176 and NMC-161. They are termed the expression quantitative trait methylation datasets (Supplementary Methods Section 5).

We used a false discovery rate q value26 less than 0.05 and a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 to control for the false discoveries in the transcriptome-wide association study, methylome-wide association study, and expression quantitative trait methylation analyses. That the q value is reasonable in this context is favorably examined in Supplementary Methods Section 6, where Figure S5 presents the density histograms of the P values.

Cross-dataset evaluation

We also found that the predictive models from the NEA-115 showed good predictive performance when evaluated on NES-61 and vice versa, supporting their combination. Similar observations hold for NM450-114 and NMC-47 (Supplementary Methods Section 4).

Annotation of CpGs associated with lung adenocarcinoma for regulatory elements

We annotated individual CpGs for regulatory element data (DNase I hotspots) across a wide range of different tissues using eFORGE (standard settings, including Roadmap Epigenomics consortium consolidated data setting).27-29

Additional information

To help pinpoint the most relevant lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes and genes near lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs and provide biological plausibility, we examined the differential expression of these genes between tumor and normal tissues from the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA2) web server; 30 these were done based on 483 tumor and 59 normal tissues of patients with lung adenocarcinoma from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and 288 normal lung tissues from GTEx. For lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs, we examined their differential DNA methylation between tumor and adjacent normal tissues using data from TCGA,31 including 362 tumor and 22 normal tissues from patients with lung adenocarcinoma; specifically, we analyzed normalized DNA methylation data using linear mixed models implemented in the R package nlme (v3.1.161), where tissue status (tumor or adjacent normal) was modeled as a random effect and other covariates were fixed effects, including age, race (Asian, Black or African American, White, or not reported), sex, the top 3 DNA methylation principal components, tobacco smoking status (current, former, never, unknown), and pack-years smoked for those who smoked.

We also report in Figures S6 and S7 (Supplementary Methods Section 7) the impacts of these genes on the proliferation of relevant cancer cells using essentiality screening data from DepMap.32

Results

Meta-analysis of GWAS: a possible new locus

We report in Table 1 the characteristics of the age-, sex-, and smoking status–matched case-control study TLUAD. TEALUAD, the meta-analysis of EALUAD and TLUAD for 2 532 586 SNPs, reported 695 SNPs associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at the genome-scale significance (P = 5E-8). All of them were within 1 Mb of the 28 EALUAD-SNPs5 except for rs7772643, rs73409930, and rs3822984, which are on chromosome 6 (6p21.31) and in strong linkage disequilibrium (LD) among themselves, with rs7772643 having the smallest P value (P = 1.08E-8). Because regression analyses including 2 or all of these 3 SNPs simultaneously led to inflated standard errors and unreliable individual effect estimates, and because rs7772643 had the smallest P value, we consider it the lead SNP in this locus. Although they are 1.9 Mb away from the 28 EALUAD-SNPs, we checked the LD between rs7772643 and the 2 nearest EALUAD-SNPs (rs9380190 and rs2760995), which are in the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) region, and confirmed they had low LD with R2 = 0.

Transcriptome-wide association study

Considering all successful analyses from a given expression QTL dataset, we obtained the genes whose predicted expression was statistically associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a false discovery rate q value less than 0.05 using TEALUAD, termed lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes. Tables S1, S2, and S3 report them for NEA-115, NES-61, and NEC-176, respectively. Lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes reported in 2 or 3 of these 3 sets are considered 1 single association if they involve the same gene symbol, and we found that in all situations, the directions of the associations were consistent. These resulted in 28 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes, and 10 reached a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 in at least 1 of the studies (0.05 divided by the number of probes, genes, or features successfully analyzed). Table S4 summarizes Tables S1-S3.

Given a lung adenocarcinoma–associated gene and 1 of the EALUAD-SNPs5 in its vicinity (500 kb), we examined the association of the SNP with lung adenocarcinoma using TEALUAD, given its predicted expression (termed conditional GWAS at the SNP). If the unconditional GWAS P value was less than 5E-8 according to TEALUAD and the conditional GWAS P value was larger, the gene might be involved in the association between the SNP and lung adenocarcinoma risk. If this conditional GWAS P value was smaller than 5E-8, this locus might have other genes or nontranscriptional mechanisms contributing to the GWAS signal. For lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes in the MHC region, we performed similar analyses for all EALUAD-SNPs in the MHC region.

Given a lung adenocarcinoma–associated gene, we also performed GCTA-COJO (v1.93.2 beta) analyses33 using TEALUAD for each SNP in its prediction model to obtain the z score of the SNP–LUAD association conditioning on all EALUAD-SNPs within 500 kb of the gene. The resulting z scores were used to re-run FUSION to assess its association with lung adenocarcinoma, termed conditional transcriptome-wide association study given the nearby lead SNPs. If it remains significant at the Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 for that study, there may be lung adenocarcinoma signals near the gene not identified by the standard GWAS of Shi and colleagues.5 Otherwise, it suggests that the gene may be a candidate susceptibility gene of the GWAS signal.

Among the 10 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes at Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05, MARCH3, DSP, and ELF5 are at least 20 Mb away from the EALUAD-SNPs. Among the remaining 7 genes, conditional GWAS P values at some of the EALUAD-SNPs near CYP21A2 and HLA-DQA2 were smaller than 5E-8, suggesting the existence of other genes or nontranscriptional mechanisms contributing to the GWAS signal besides CYP21A2 and HLA-DQA2. Conditional transcriptome-wide association study P values for the 7 genes were larger than their respective Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold of 0.05, suggesting no additional GWAS signal and that their expressions might mediate the GWAS signals.

Table 2 reports the 10 genes that reached a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 according to Table S4 and the colocalization posterior probabilities of the lung adenocarcinoma GWAS signals and the expression QTL signals of these genes, using HyPrColoc.34 It shows that most had large colocalization posterior probabilities, suggesting the involvement of their expression in disease development. Table S4 also compares the expression level between tumor and nontumor lung tissues from GEPIA2.30 Figure S6 reports that FOXD4, RPE, and RNF8 demonstrated essential roles in cell proliferation, as shown by a median Chronos score below –0.5, based on data from DepMap.32

Methylome-wide association study

Similar to the above transcriptome-wide association study, Tables S5, S6, and S7 report the CpGs whose genetically predicted DNA methylation were associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a q value less than 0.05, termed lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs, using predictive models from NM450-114, NMC-47, and NMC-161, respectively. Summarizing them in Table S8, we obtained a total of 220 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs. Among them, 86 reached a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 according to their respective datasets. We annotated individual CpGs for regulatory element data (DNase I hotspots) across a wide range of different tissues in Tables S5-S8. For these CpGs and their near genes, Table S8 also compares their DNA methylation levels and expression levels between tumor and nontumor lung tissues from patients with lung adenocarcinoma from TCGA and GEPIA2.30 Figure S7 reports that C11orf10, BAT1, and FEN1 demonstrated essential roles in cell proliferation based on DepMap,32 similar to those reported in Figure S6.

Six of the lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs reaching a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 are around ELF5, which are 27 Mb away from all EALUAD-SNPs, suggesting putative novel GWAS signals. Conditional methylome-wide association study analyses suggest 3 CpGs around TERT and 1 CpG around HLA-DPB1 represent independent GWAS genetic signals in known loci. The remaining 76 CpGs might mediate the genetic effects in known loci on lung adenocarcinoma risk.

Expression quantitative trait methylation and CpG–gene–LUAD trios

Given NEM-114, for each of the 28 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes (Table S4) and each of the 220 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs (Table S8), we examined the association between their measured expression and measured DNA methylation level (expression quantitative trait methylation) if they are within 500 kb of each other. A total of 326 tests were performed. The CpG–gene pairs with false discovery rate q values less than 0.05 are reported in Table S9; they are also termed CpG–gene–LUAD trios, in view of the transcriptome-wide association study, methylome-wide association study, and expression quantitative trait methylation results. Similarly, Tables S10 and S11 report those using NEM-37 and NEM-151, respectively. Table S12 summarizes Tables S9-S11. If a CpG–gene pair appeared more than once in Tables S9, S10, or S11, we included only the one having the smallest expression quantitative trait methylation P value in Table S12. It reports a total of 45 trios; among them, 43 displayed consistent directions across the 3 associations, meaning that, for example, if higher genetically predicted expression correlated with higher lung adenocarcinoma risk and lower genetically predicted DNA methylation correlated with higher lung adenocarcinoma risk, then lower measured DNA methylation correlated with higher measured gene expression. Consistency seems to reinforce these individual associations and suggests the possibility that DNA methylation may influence lung adenocarcinoma risk by regulating gene expression or vice versa, highlighting areas for follow-up mechanistic studies.

In addition, consistent trios sharing the same gene help resolve the ambiguity regarding CpGs that showed different directions in the methylome-wide association study findings. For example, although the 3 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs around CCDC116 showed different directions in the methylome-wide association study, the 3 consistent trios uniformly suggest that higher CCDC116 expression correlates with lung adenocarcinoma risk.

To streamline the list in Table S12, we chose 1 trio for each gene. For each gene in the 45 trios in Table S12, the trio involving this gene with the highest colocalization posterior probability was selected if the colocalization posterior probability was more than 0.5. When all trios had a colocalization posterior probability less than 0.5, the trio involving this gene was selected if it had the smallest order according to the CpG island-centric categorization (CpG sites are arranged in the following increasing order: island, north shore, south shore, north shelf, south shelf, and open sea.). When 2 or more trios had the same order we selected the one having the smallest expression quantitative trait methylation P value. This resulted in Table 4, reporting 15 CpG–gene–LUAD trios; all showed consistent directions. It also reports that the trios involving TP63, FOXP4, DCBLD1, ELF5, FADS1, SECISBP2L, and CCDC116 had their colocalization posterior probabilities larger than 0.5 simultaneously for all 3 association signals: expression QTL, methylation QTL, and lung adenocarcinoma.

Fine-mapping and colocalization studies of new GWAS locus on chromosome 6

Using the methylation QTL dataset NMC-161 to examine the associations between rs7772643 and CpGs within 500 kb, we found that rs7772643 was associated with DNA methylation at cg10159596 and cg07705908 with P values 6.0E-5 and 4.9E-4, respectively. Applying the fine-mapping package SuSiE35,36 to TEALUAD, we found that rs7772643, rs73409930, and rs3822984 have posterior inclusion probabilities of 0.42, 0.36, and 0.12, respectively. Applying HyPrColoc,34 we performed colocalization analysis of the lung adenocarcinoma GWAS signal from the locus (100 kb around rs7772643) with the cg10159596 and cg07705908 signal. The former (cg10159596) analysis showed a colocalization posterior probability of 0.72 with candidate SNP rs3822984, and the latter (cg07705908) showed a colocalization posterior probability of 0.17 with candidate SNP rs7772643. Among the genes in this locus, SPDEF, which has rs3822984 in its 5’UTR, and ILRUN are nearest to the lead SNP rs7772643 and seem to deserve most attention. The former is considered a driver of mucinous lung cancer, and the latter is overexpressed in non-small cell lung cancer and promotes invasion by altering adhesion molecule expression.37,38 These 2 genes are indicated in the row for rs7772643 in Table S13, although no lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes from the transcriptome-wide association study or lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs from the methylome-wide association study were reported in this locus.

To appreciate the meta-analysis in which the sample size of EALUAD was much larger than that of TLUAD, we report in Table S13 their respective summary statistics at each of the 28 SNPs reported by Shi and colleagues.5 Table S13 shows that among the 28 EALUAD-SNPs, 16 and 18 showed genome-scale significance based on EALUAD and TEALUAD, respectively; among the 18 SNPs, 4 did not reach genome-scale significance in EALUAD. We note that the GWAS of Shi and colleagues is a 2-stage approach, and the EALUAD summary statistics are based on their first-stage (discovery) study.

Discussion

Using cis-SNPs, we developed predictive models based on expression QTL and methylation QTL reference datasets that profiled gene expression and DNA methylation in nontumor lung tissues of Taiwanese never-smokers. Applying these models to TEALUAD, we found 28 (10) lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes and 220 (86) lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs at a false discovery rate (at Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold) of less than 0.05 and 43 CpG–gene–LUAD trios that showed consistency in the association directions. These findings suggested target genes or biological mechanisms underlying 23 of the 28 SNPs reported in the GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia and unveiled putative novel lung adenocarcinoma susceptibility loci, expanding the reach of the variant-based approach of traditional GWAS.

We reported a novel susceptibility variant, rs7772643 in 6p21.31, bringing the total number of independent susceptibility SNPs to 29 at 26 loci for lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia. Although colocalization analysis provides additional support for its identification, a replication study is warranted.

This study provides various opportunities for translational and mechanism studies.39,40 We find MARCH3, DSP, ELF5, FADS1, IKZF3, GSDMB, and CCDC116 stand out as compelling candidates for further investigation in view of literature on GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia.5 Although FADS1 and ELF5 were discussed in the context of lung cancer development based on transcriptome-wide association study and colocalization analysis,5 this paper reinforces their relevance. This paper reports that the predicted methylation of cg01400685, in south shore category and in the body of FADS2, was associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05; that colocalization analysis indicated that its methylation, expression of FADS1, and lung adenocarcinoma signal had a colocalization posterior probability of 0.89; and that it formed a trio with the expression of FADS1 (Tables S8 and S12 and Table 4). In this connection, we note that FADS2 is often upregulated and supports tumor development by supplying essential lipids and protumor signaling molecules; conversely, disrupting FADS2-mediated fatty acid synthesis can hinder tumor progression.41 Regarding ELF5, this paper reports that the predicted methylation of cg05390144, in open sea category and in the 5’UTR and TSS200 of ELF5, was associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 and that colocalization analysis of its methylation, expression of ELF5, and lung adenocarcinoma signal indicated a colocalization posterior probability of 0.79 (Table 4 and Table S12).

The fact that the predicted expression of MARCH3 was associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 (z = 4.3); its expression and the lung adenocarcinoma signal had a colocalization posterior probability of 0.74; the predicted methylation of cg15600669, in open sea and 5’UTR of MARCH3, was associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 (z = −4.0); and the expression quantitative trait methylation association was statistically significant at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 (beta = −0.4) suggests that higher expression of MARCH3 may mediate the disease risk (Tables 2 and 4). MARCH3, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, has been shown to regulate eosinophilic airway inflammation42 as well as invasion and migration of cancer cells via GRB2,43 a gene implicated in non-small cell lung cancer progression.44 Table S1 reports that the SNP rs248126 in this locus had a P value of 7.46E-6 according to TEALUAD.

The fact that the predicted expression of DSP was associated with lung adenocarcinoma risk at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 (z = −4.6) and its expression and the lung adenocarcinoma signal had a colocalization posterior probability of 0.97 suggests that loss of DSP expression may mediate the lung adenocarcinoma risk (Table 2). DSP encodes desmoplakin, a crucial component of desmosomes, intercellular junctions that provide strong adhesion between epithelial cells. The variant rs2076295 in DSP was shown to be associated with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.45,46 Frequently downregulated in human lung cancer, DSP can also act as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin signaling.47 Table S1 reports that rs3778337 had a P value of 1.23E-06 according to TEALUAD, which is in intron 7 of DSP and in strong LD with rs2076295.

Given that cg10909506 and IKZF3 were associated with lung adenocarcinoma at a false discovery rate less than 0.05 (z = 3.7 and 3.7, respectively) and that their expression quantitative trait methylation association was statistically significant at a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold less than 0.05 (beta = 0.4), we know this trio showed a consistent association direction. These findings, together with a higher expression of IKZF3 in tumor than in nontumor with a P value of 5.85E-05 (Table 4 and Table S12), reinforce the likelihood that this is a new susceptible locus for GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia. Similar observations could be made about the trio cg10909506–GSDMB–LUAD, except that the difference in expression of GSDMB had a larger P value of .06 (Table S12). IKZF3 (Aiolos), a transcription factor frequently expressed in lung cancers, has been linked to poor prognosis and cancer stemlike properties48 and highlighted as a potential therapeutic target in recent research,49,50 while GSDMB plays a key role in pyroptosis (inflammatory cell death) and immune activation.51,52 IKZF3 and GSDMB share the same putative CpG mediator, cg10909506, although the direction of effect differs. Table S1 reports that rs9909593 in this locus had a P value of 2.83E-4 according to TEALUAD.

CCDC116, a cancer-testis gene regulated by DNA methylation,53 has been reported as a cis–expression QTL in several cancers including lung cancer54,55 and is potentially targetable by immunotherapy. Table S3 reports that rs1811069 in this locus had a P value of 2.55E-5 according to TEALUAD.

Among the 10 lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes reported in Table 2, only TP63, DCBLD1, and SECISBP2L also appeared in the transcriptome-wide association study of lung adenocarcinoma of European descent reported by Bosse and colleagues,56 which included a total of 13 genes. Among the 86 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpGs in this study, only 22 appeared in the study by Yang and colleagues,10 which reported 579 associated CpGs. These large differences probably reflect the differences in lung cancer histology, in the ancestry of the GWASs of lung adenocarcinoma, and in the expression QTL and methylation QTL reference datasets, noting that our studies had a smaller GWAS sample size, smaller reference datasets, and a smaller number of CpG sites in the arrays. This seems in line with the observation that for lung adenocarcinoma, most of the GWAS-identified SNPs in East Asian populations showed no evidence of association in European populations.5 The differences may also be caused by cigarette smoking in view of its association with DNA methylation and gene expression in lung tissue.16,56

After estimating the heritability and genetic correlation of lung adenocarcinoma among Asian and European never-smokers and ever-smokers, Shi and colleagues5 conducted a GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma in East Asians, including both sexes and irrespective of smoking status, and we followed suit in this paper. However, the patients in the reference datasets expression QTL and methylation QTL are Asian never-smokers. It would be desirable to also include patients having a smoking history in these reference datasets. In addition, larger sample sizes are needed to enable more powerful transcriptome-wide association studies and methylome-wide association studies.

In conclusion, we showed that although this study provides no lung adenocarcinoma–associated genes or CpGs around 5 of the 28 EALUAD-SNPs, 10 were in the vicinity of at least 1 lung adenocarcinoma–associated gene, 22 were in the vicinity of at least 1 lung adenocarcinoma–associated CpG, and some of them constituted CpG–gene–LUAD trios with consistent association directions. Our study suggests several putative novel loci for lung adenocarcinoma in East Asia. This study offers insights into mechanism studies as well as translational studies. A larger sample size of GWAS of lung adenocarcinoma and expression QTL and methylation QTL reference datasets seems desirable for further studies.

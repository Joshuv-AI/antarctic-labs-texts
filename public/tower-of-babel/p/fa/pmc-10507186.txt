The Effect of delivery method on optimality level and postpartum comfort in primiparous women
Authors: Füsun Söker, Ayça Şolt Kırca
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDelivery is a normal event in a woman’s life and the most special experience. The delivery method affects the perinatal period as well as the level of postpartum comfort.AimsThis descriptive and cross-sectional study aims to determine the effect of delıvery method on optimality level and postpartum comfort in primiparous womenMethodsThe study included 200 women. Data collection tools are the Introductory Information Form prepared by the researcher in line with the literature, the Turkish Adaptation of the Optimality Index, the Postpartum Comfort Scale.FindingsThe optimality index scores of the pregnant women with SVD obtained from PBI is 88.60 ± 5.71, the mean score obtained from OI is 84.02 ± 3.12, and the mean score of OI was determined to be 85.40 ± 2.98 for the groups who had SVD.ConclusionsIn accordance with the data, the optimality level of women has decreased due to non-evidence-based practices and invasive procedures during SVD. The total comfort score of the SVD group has also been negatively affected.

Introduction

Delivery is a normal event in a woman’s life and the most special experience [1]. In this special experience, the woman’s delivery method is very important. Women can be affected by many factors when deciding on their delivery method. Parents, friends, social media and health professionals are effective in determining a woman’s delivery method [2]. When we look at the delivery method in Turkey, it is seen that the numbers of spontaneous vaginal deliveries (SVD) and deliveries by caesarean section (CS) are very close, and the number of deliveries by caesarean section is increasing. The World Health Organization (WHO) has declared that the rate of delivery by caesarean section should be 15 %. However, the rate of delivery by caesarean section in our country is quite high 52 % [3], [4].

The perinatal period includes pregnancy, delivery, and the postpartum period [5]. Every pregnant woman should receive care before and after delivery. With the use of evidence-based practices in the perinatal period, it aims to reach the highest state of well-being with the least intervention for the mother and fetus [6]. The concept of optimality is to reach the best, highest, and most appropriate results that can be achieved [7]. The optimality in the perinatal period is to ensure that women reach their highest state of well-being with evidence-based practises during pregnancy, the fetus, delivery and the postpartum period [8].

In order to provide optimal perinatal care, midwives should use evidence-based practices while caring for pregnant women. Thanks to evidence-based practices, it is tried to ensure the highest well-being by minimizing intervention for the pregnant woman and fetus during the delivery and postpartum [9]. The delivery method affects the perinatal period as well as the postpartum comfort level. In the studies carried out, it has been determined that the comfort of mothers is affected by factors such as education, delivery method, planned pregnancy, problems experienced in puerperium, the level of meeting their needs, the care they receive from health personnel, and the physical conditions of the hospital [10].

In this study, a descriptive and cross-sectional study was conducted to evaluate the effect of delivery methods on the level of optimality and postpartum comfort in primiparous women.

Methods

Study design

A cross-sectional-descriptive and comparative design was used.

Setting and sample

The study was conducted with postpartum women in Lüleburgaz State Hospital, Lüleburgaz District, Kırklareli Province, between January 2021 and May 2022. The population of the study consists of 217 primiparous puerperal women who had SVD and who had delivery by caesarean section with spinal anesthesia between January 2019 and December 2019. In the study, the sampling method of known universe was used and the minimal sample number (α = 0.05, 1-β = 0.90) was found to be 139. Since there may be losses in the cases, the study, a total of 200 women, 100 of whom had SVD and 100 of whom had CS with spinal anesthesia, were reached.

The inclusion criteria

The study comprised women who were aged between 20 and 40 years, being primiparous at 38–42 weeks of gestation, having a normal delivery or delivery by cesarean section, having a single pregnancy, being able to speak and understand Turkish, accepting to participate in the study, and being able to fully answer the questionnaire and scale forms.

The exclusion criteria

Laceration occurring in the vaginal region after SVD, abscession, infection, having chronic disease, having an operative vaginal delivery, diagnosis of a risky pregnancy, having CS under general anesthesia, complications developed after delivery by caesarean section or SVD, and having any health problems in the newborn.

Measures and instruments

Introductory Information Form: The introductory information form included socio-demographic characteristics and obstetric history of the individual.

Optimality Index- Türkiye (OI-TR) Scale: OI-US was adapted into Turkish by Çiğdem Yücel et al. [6], [11]. There are two sections, the Perinatal Background Index (PBI) and the Optimality Index (OI), and it consists of 60 (18 + 42) items in total. Cronbach’s alpha value cannot be calculated because OI-TR is a clinical measurement tool and does not show a homogeneous distribuiton due to the fact that the items reflect more than one clinical situation.

Postpartum Comfort Scale (PCS): In 2010, PCS was developed by Karakaplan and Yıldız [12]. As the score increases, the comfort level increases, and the score decreases, the comfort level decreases. The Cronbach alpha coefficient of the scale is 78. The Cronbach alpha coefficient obtained for this study is 0.902.

Data collection and procedure

First, the descriptive information form was used to select the women who met the research criteria. Then, the PCS and OI-TR were given to the women. Participants who needed help while answering the statements on the scales were assisted by the researcher. It took each participant approximately 30 min to fill out the forms. The data were collected using face-to-face interview technique.

Ethical aspects of study

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of X University before the data collection phase was initiated. Before the study was conducted, written permission was obtained from Y Provincial Health Directorate, which was the study setting. The aim of the research was explained to the participants, and then they submitted written informed consent. The procedures of the current research were performed following the principles about research comprising human participants and the principles of 1964 Helsinki Declaration. The subjects were informed they could quit the research at any stage and their personal data would be kept confidentially.

Statistical analyses

The data were evaluated using the SPSS program. Since the Skewness and Kurtosis values of the data remained within the range of +2.0 and −2.0, it was seen that the data were suitable for normal distribution. An independent sample t-test was used for independent group comparisons. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

When the OI scores of the pregnant women were evaluated by evaluating the perinatal backgrounds of the women participating in the study, it was determined that the mean score obtained from PBI of the women whose pregnancy was terminated by SVD was 88.60 ± 5.71, while the mean score obtained from PBI of the women who had delivery by caesarean section was 92.07 ± 3.99. One of the biggest reasons for lowering the PBI score of spontaneous vaginal delivery is that pregnant women are primiparous. In the study of Büyükkal (2019), it was stated that the PBI score was 92.25 ± 4.09 for those who had SVD, while it was 88.51 ± 4.02 for those who had CS [13]. In the study of Çifçi (2019), it was stated that the PBI score of those who had CS was 85.07 %, and the PBI score of those who had SVD was 90.16 % [14]. The study was determined that the PBI score of healthy pregnant women was 88.05 %, and the PBI score of high-risk pregnant women was 85.49 % [6]. When the results were evaluated by considering the TDHS 2018 data [4], it was determined that the CS was 38 % planned delivery by caesarean section, the CS was neither mandatory nor necessary, and the result of the study is supportive.

When the OI values showing the prenatal, delivery, newborn, and discharge processes of pregnant women are examined, while they were 84.02 ± 3.12, the mean score obtained from the OI of the participants who had delivery by caesarean section was 89.01 ± 5.83. In Büyükkal’s (2019) study, the OI score of the group who had SVD was 83.39 ± 3.79, and the OI score of the group who had CS was 88.73 ± 3.68 [13]. Other study was OI score of those who had CS was 82.12 %, and the OI score of those who had SVD was 76.11 % [14]. The study healthy pregnant women and high-risk pregnant women were evaluated, and it was determined that the OI score of healthy pregnant women was 77.65 % and the OI score of high-risk pregnant women was 78.60 % [6].

In Low et al. (2008)’s study, OI scores were made on 3425 pregnant women, and the OI score of healthy pregnant women was 84 %, while the OI score of high-risk pregnant women was 71 % [15]. When the OI scoring results of the studies are examined, it is observed that the results overlap with the OI scores obtained in this study. It was determined that the total mean score of the Optimality Index of the participants participating in the study was skorlaması 85.40 ± 2.98 for the group who had SVD and 90.86 ± 3.66 for the group who had CS. In Büyükkal’s (2019) study [13], the SVD total OI score was 85.55 ± 3.36, and the CS total OIscore was 88.51 ± 2.74. In the study of Yücel and Taşkın (2015), while the total OI score of healthy pregnant women was found to be 77.65 %, it was also found to be 78.60 % for high-risk pregnant women [6]. While the research data overlaps with the data of Büyükkal, when the research data is compared with the research data of Yücel and Taşkın (2015), it is seen that the total OI score of the research data is higher than the total OI score of Yücel and Taşkın [6]. Due to the high PBI and OI scores obtained in the research data, a high total OI score is an expected result. In this study, the level of the optimality index decreased due to the inability to fulfill the variables required to ensure optimality at delivery.

In the study of Ünal and Şenol (2022), it was determined that the total comfort scale of the SVD puerperal women was 118.5 ± 14.3, and the total comfort score of the puerperal women who had delivery by caesarean section was 116.4 ± 16.89 [16]. In Taytan (2019)’s study, were reached, and the total comfort score of the vaginal delivery group was 127.794 ± 12.739, and the total comfort score of the group who had CS was 117.902 ± 9.622 [17]. Other study was found that the mean total comfort score of those who had delivery by caesarean section was 133.80 ± 21.62 [18], and the mean score of the mothers who had SVD was 166 ± 16.77. In the study conducted by Balsak (2018), the total comfort score for CS was found to be 81.2 ± 22.6, while it was 84.2 ± 25.0 in spontaneous vaginal delivery [19]. In Birgilil’s (2020) study, the total comfort score was found to be 110.42 ± 9.12. It is stated that the delivery method affects the physical sub-dimension of postpartum comfort and the psychospiritual sub-dimension [20]. Çarpık et al. (2014) found that in the study conducted with 233 puerperal women, the total comfort score of the group who had CS was 117.05 ± 13.24, and the total comfort score of the group who had SVD was 119.53 ± 13.93 [21]. When the studies are compared with this study, it is seen that the total comfort score of the delivery by caesarean section group is higher. The reason for this is thought to be the application of spinal anesthesia in all of groups who had deliveries by caesarean section, providing pain control in the puerperal period and easy adaptation to the postpartum process.

Conclusion

As a result of the study, it was determined that the delivery method of primiparous women did not affect their postpartum comfort level. It is thought that primiparous mothers who have experienced delivery for the first time are affected by adaptation to the postpartum process and the differences in the support they have received.

Unravelling telemonitoring data to predict good NIV quality: the E-QualiNIV study
Authors: Arnaud Prigent, Clément Blanloeil, Dany Jaffuel, Frederic Gagnadoux, Léo Grassion
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objective High treatment quality, defined by mean adherence >4 hours per day, unintentional leaks <24 L/min and a residual Apnoea–Hypopnoea Index <5 events per hour, is associated with better outcomes. Adherence variance may reflect behaviour linked to better treatment quality. This study aimed to assess whether monthly adherence variance is associated with improved treatment quality in patients treated with non-invasive ventilation (NIV) for more than 4 months.MethodsE-QualiNIV is a retrospective study evaluating treatment quality in 511 telemonitored patients with chronic respiratory failure, observed from 15 April to 31 October 2023. The study followed three steps: (1) hierarchical clustering based on individual adherence variance; (2) assessing whether monthly adherence variance in the preceding month predicted the proportion of alerts in the subsequent month and (3) evaluating treatment quality based on the number of months with low adherence variance.ResultsCluster 1, consisting of patients with adherence variance below 3, had a significantly higher proportion (57.93%) of patients achieving high-quality treatment compared with other clusters (43.1% for cluster 2 and 46.4% for cluster 3) (p=0.035). Patients with a low adherence variance in the preceding month were more likely to achieve high-quality treatment in the following month (except for May, significant differences every month from p=0.04 to p<0.01). Those with 6 or more months of low adherence variance had a significantly higher probability of receiving high-quality treatment over the entire period (coefficient: 0.2649, p value: 0.0028) compared with those who did not (non-significant).ConclusionThe E-QualiNIV study demonstrates that low adherence variance is associated with high-quality treatment and serves as a prognostic indicator of treatment stability and alert occurrence in the subsequent month.

Introduction

Long-term nocturnal non-invasive ventilation (NIV) is a well-established treatment for patients with chronic alveolar hypoventilation. NIV improves the prognosis of patients regardless of the aetiology (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease,1 2 obesity hypoventilation syndrome,3 neuromuscular4 or kyphoscoliosis5 conditions). Several factors are known to limit the benefit of NIV, particularly leaks6 7 and residual events,8 which can interfere with the mechanical effectiveness of NIV, as well as poor compliance when NIV is used for less than 4 hours.9 10

Although achieving good compliance with minimal leakage and a low residual event index is clearly recommended,11 a significant proportion of patients treated with NIV do not meet these objectives,1214 which is associated with a worse prognosis.1213 1517 The factors impacting adherence are related to the individual (eg, disease, severity, comorbidities, symptoms, age, psychological and behavioural aspects, and partner1821), the device (eg, settings, titration method, interface, leaks and residual events2225) and the service (eg, access, support and caregiver). Among the data from remote monitoring of NIV, no predictors of long-term good treatment quality have been well established.

Telemonitoring of NIV has shown promise in improving treatment quality.16 In 2016, a task force of the European Respiratory Society developed recommendations on the telemonitoring of ventilator-dependent patients to describe commonly accepted indications, follow-up schedules, equipment and facilities, and legal aspects.26 However, a recent review clearly mentions the need to define relevant parameters for telemonitoring, as well as the algorithms and organisation of telemonitoring, to improve outcomes.27 Depending on the chosen threshold levels and the number of NIV parameters monitored remotely, the number of generated alerts varies. A large volume of alerts generated by remote monitoring poses challenges in managing them, increases associated costs and may reduce their clinical relevance. One way to reduce the volume of these alerts would be to identify early prognostic indicators of good treatment quality, in order to focus remote monitoring on patients at higher risk of poor treatment quality.

Clinical experience with patients treated with NIV suggests that patients with consistent daily adherence are better managed than others. Variance in adherence, which is not related to the adherence value, could reflect behaviour associated with better treatment quality. The aim of this study is to assess whether adherence variability, explored through monthly variance in adherence, is associated with better treatment quality in patients treated with NIV for more than 4 months.

Method and statistical analysis

Population

E-QualiNIV is a real-life, longitudinal, retrospective study evaluating the quality of NIV treatment in patients with chronic respiratory failure who were telemonitored for 6 consecutive months, from 15 April to 31 October 2023. Inclusion criteria were: patients over 18 years of age with chronic respiratory failure treated with NIV (using a Resmed Lumis 150 VPAP ST device) for at least 4 months prior to the start of the study, under telemonitoring, whose home healthcare provider is Elia Medical, and who have consented to participate in the study (ie, have not opposed the processing of their health data). The study involves the reuse of health data collected by the home healthcare provider Elia Medical as part of its care and telemonitoring activities for patients with chronic respiratory failure treated with NIV.

Data

Data on age, sex, the presence or absence of long-term oxygen therapy, the dates of NIV initiation and withdrawal (when applicable), and the type of mask were retrieved from the healthcare provider’s platform, while NIV settings and daily data (leaks, usage time and Apnoea–Hypoponea Index (AHI)) were collected from the ResMed Airview platform.

Objectives and outcomes

The main objective of this study was to explore the association between different levels of adherence variance and high treatment quality. Treatment quality was defined by a composite criterion of mean values over the period of interest:

Adherence >4 hours per day.

Adherence >4 hours per day.

Median leak <12 L/min, 95th percentile leak <24 L/min.

Median leak <12 L/min, 95th percentile leak <24 L/min.

AHI <10 per hour.

AHI <10 per hour.

In order to address our objective, we conducted our study in three steps.

The first step involved hierarchical clustering based on individual adherence variance over the period from 15 April to 31 October 2023. Treatment quality in NIV over the entire period was compared across the three clusters.

The second step aimed to determine whether the monthly variance in adherence during the preceding month predicted the proportion of alerts for the following items: adherence, 95th percentile leak, AHI and the proportion of patients with high treatment quality (according to the same criteria as above) in the subsequent month. For each patient, during the current month, adherence variance was classified as low, moderate or high using predefined thresholds. The following parameters were then calculated as weekly averages for the subsequent month: adherence, number of hours of device use per 24 hours, leak level (95th percentile) and AHI per hour. An alert was triggered if the weekly average exceeded one of the following thresholds: adherence <4 hours per 24 hours, 95th percentile leak ≥24 L/min or AHI ≥10 per hour. Within each group, defined by their adherence variance during the previous month, and for each month, the proportion of patients with at least one alert per month and the proportion of patients with high treatment quality in the following month were evaluated.

The third step aimed to assess treatment quality based on the number of months with low adherence variance. Patients were categorised into three groups based on adherence variance: low variance across six or more months, low variance for 4 or 5 months, and low variance for fewer than 4 months. The proportion of patients achieving high-quality treatment was then reported for each group and for each month.

Statistics

For statistical analyses, continuous data were expressed as mean (SD) or median (IQR) according to normal or non-normal distribution. Kruskal-Wallis tests were used to compare independent medians. When the tests were significant, non-parametric multiple comparisons were performed using Dunn’s tests, which compare all pairwise combinations using the joint rank method with Bonferroni adjustment. Non-parametric χ2 tests were also used to examine associations between two variables. An Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) was applied to account for potential confounding factors affecting treatment quality outcomes. Propensity scores were calculated for each patient, taking into account sex and years of NIV device usage as confounding factors. Multinomial logistic regressions were performed, first to assess the impact of adherence variance on treatment quality, and second to assess the impact of the number of months with low variance on treatment quality.

A hierarchical clustering analysis was performed to group patients based on the variability of their treatment adherence. For each patient, the intraindividual variance of adherence was calculated from longitudinal data. The clustering procedure was then applied using agglomerative hierarchical clustering with Ward’s linkage criterion and Euclidean distance. A linkage matrix was computed, and a dendrogram was generated to visualise the resulting clusters (online supplemental Figure S1). Clusters were defined by applying a predefined distance threshold to the dendrogram.

Python was used for data processing, statistical analyses and graphical visualisation. The packages pandas (V.2.0.0), NumPy (V.1.24.3) and SciPy (V.1.10.1) were used for data processing. The stats package (V.0.1.2a0) was used for statistical analyses. The packages matplotlib (V.3.7.1), seaborn (V.0.12.2) and plotly (V.5.9.0) were used for data visualisation.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research.

The population was predominantly male (60.7%), with a median age of 71.4 years and a median treatment duration of 6.2 years. The population showed good adherence, with few leaks and residual respiratory events reported by the device software. Almost half of the patients were also receiving oxygen therapy. The full-face mask was the most commonly used, and half of the patients used a humidifier.

Three clusters were obtained following hierarchical clustering based on the variance in individual adherence over the entire period from 15 April to 31 October 2023 (online supplemental Figure S1): cluster 1 (adherence variance <3), cluster 2 (adherence variance >3 and < 13) and cluster 3 (adherence variance >13).

The proportion of males was significantly higher in cluster 1 compared with clusters 2 and 3. There was no difference in age between the clusters. The number of years on NIV was significantly higher in cluster 1 compared with cluster 2. There were no significant differences between the clusters in terms of proportion of patients receiving oxygen therapy, NIV settings, type of mask or use of humidification. However, the proportion of patients receiving high-quality treatment was significantly higher (57.9%) in cluster 1 compared with the other two clusters (table 1). Multinomial logistic regression, calculated using propensity scores from the IPTW method, indicated that this trend remains valid even after balancing for significant differences in the number of years on NIV and gender. Patients with a monthly adherence variance below 3 had a significantly higher probability of achieving high-quality treatment in the subsequent month (coefficient: 0.2695, p value: 0.017) compared with those with variances between 3 and 13 (coefficient: −0.1488, p value: 0.326) or above 13 (coefficient: −0.008, p value: 0.8).

Median adherence was significantly higher in cluster 1 compared with cluster 2, but not compared with cluster 3. There was no difference between the clusters regarding 95th percentile leaks. The AHI was significantly lower in cluster 1 compared with cluster 3 (figure 1).

Adherence variance as a predictive factor

Using these adherence variance thresholds to categorise patients into three groups, the proportion of patients having high-quality treatment in the subsequent month was significantly higher when the monthly adherence variance in the previous month was below 3, compared with when it was above 3 (except for May) (figure 3D). Additionally, the proportion of patients having at least one alert per month in the subsequent month was significantly lower when the monthly adherence variance in the previous month was below 3, compared with when it was above 3, for adherence, leaks (only in June) and AHI (except for June) (figure 3A–C) (online supplemental table S1).

The lower the adherence variance over time, the better the treatment quality

To highlight the benefit of having low adherence variance over time, each patient was assigned to one of three groups according to the number of months with a low monthly adherence variance: more than 6 months out of 7, 4 or 5 months out of 7 and fewer than 3 months out of 7. The proportion of patients having a high-quality treatment in a group with a low variance adherence (below 3) remained relatively stable (around 65%) over time, with a slight decrease observed in July. The number of patients in each group also remained stable over time. 48.5% of patients maintained an adherence variance below 3 for 6 months or more.

Patients with a low adherence variance (below 3) for 6 months or more consistently maintained a high and stable proportion of high-quality treatment over time, whereas patients with a low adherence variance for fewer than 6 months did not show the same stability (figure 4). Multinomial logistic regression, calculated using propensity scores from the IPTW method, showed that patients with an adherence variance below 3 for 6 months or more had a significantly higher probability of receiving high-quality treatment in October and over the entire period (coefficient: 0.2649, p value: 0.028; and coefficient: 0.2649, p value: 0.0028, respectively) compared with those who did not (non-significant).

Discussion

E-QualiNIV is the first study to demonstrate the significance of a new feature associated with high-quality treatment. A monthly low adherence variance (below 3) was consistently associated with a higher likelihood of achieving high-quality treatment, defined as a combination of daily NIV usage for more than 4 hours, low leak levels (95th percentile leaks <24 L/min and median leaks <12 L/min) and a low AHI (AHI <10/h), as identified by the NIV device. Low adherence variance (below 3) was also associated with a lower proportion of alerts regarding weekly adherence, AHI or leaks. Finally, the longer patients maintained a low monthly adherence variance, the greater the proportion of high-quality treatment they had.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate that monthly variance in adherence is associated with high-quality treatment and acts as a predictive factor for high-quality treatment in the subsequent month. Patients with a low monthly adherence variance of less than 3 in the preceding month are associated with the lowest proportion of patients experiencing at least one alert in the following month related to adherence, leaks and the AHI. In real-world practice, this is particularly significant as it could enable preventive actions for patients with a monthly adherence variance greater than 3. However, the proportion of patients with at least one leak alert in the following month among those whose monthly adherence variance was less than 3 in the previous month remains substantial, at approximately 40%. This observation highlights the need for developing and enhancing systems that transmit leak alerts directly to the patient, accompanied by a tutorial for resolving the alert. These procedures, adapted from continuous positive airway pressure, are based on median leak levels and the 95th percentile. This approach would offer the dual benefit of rapid leak alert resolution and gradual therapeutic self-education as leak alerts occur.

Three key questions arise: what are the reasons for the association between adherence variance and high treatment quality? What is the significance of adherence variance? How could this new marker be used in the telemonitoring of NIV?

An explanation of this association could be that adherence variance reflects the behaviour of the patients. Behaviour could explain the way the patients use their device (eg, regularity of the use) and how long they use it. Among the possible explanations, it can be hypothesised that a patient who maintains regular sleep/NIV usage patterns also tends to adhere more carefully to their therapeutic regimen. Low variance in adherence is associated with better treatment quality. It is well known that irregular sleep is linked to greater cardiovascular comorbidity, and the opposite is also true.2830 It is well established from both cardiovascular31 and non-cardiovascular32 clinical trials that adherence per se, including adherence to placebo, is associated with markedly improved health outcomes, an effect that is often substantially larger than that of active therapy. Conversely, regarding NIV, a well-adjusted, comfortable treatment with minimal leaks or residual respiratory events promotes good quality of sleep,33 regular use without nocturnal awakenings, which can otherwise provide opportunities to discontinue the use of NIV.

Patients with an adherence variance below 3 for more than 6 out of 7 months also appear to have consistently high treatment quality over time compared with the others. This suggests that the use of NIV by these patients is less affected by temperature variations during the summer, by daylight duration or by holidays.

The interest in adherence variance also lies in its simplicity for calculation and interpretation. The precision of this metric also enables the differentiation of patient populations with similar median adherence levels. For example, there is no significant difference in median adherence or median leak rates between clusters 1 and 3. Monthly adherence variance and monthly mean adherence are complementary variables for predicting the likelihood of good treatment quality in subsequent months. In clinical practice involving NIV telemonitoring, adherence is typically treated as a categorical variable, which limits its ability to distinguish between good and very good adherence (or bad and very bad). In contrast, the variance threshold proposed in our study can be applied to any patient, regardless of adherence level, allowing for intraindividual comparisons to identify behavioural patterns. This highlights the importance of having a combined criteria approach that is closer to the patient’s reality, rather than comparing individual criteria across the entire population.

The use of this new metric in telemonitoring should help focus attention on patients with higher adherence variance to better understand the causes of this irregularity in use. Clearly, promoting regular use and encouraging regular sleep in these patients should be a priority during therapeutic education sessions. Telemonitoring generates numerous alerts. This new metric could be used as a first step to differentiate patients who need further telemonitoring evaluation for adherence, leaks and AHI from those who do not. In routine practice, a telemonitoring alert threshold for adherence variance above 3 in patients under NIV could be set to trigger physical or teleinterventions.

One limitation of this study is the lack of information on the underlying conditions that prompted the initiation of NIV. Even though a recent study showed that adherence, adherence variance, leak rates and the AHI can vary significantly within the same pathology,34 adherence may still be influenced by the underlying condition—for example, rapidly progressive diseases such as neuromuscular disorders, compared with non-progressive or slowly progressive conditions.14 It may also be influenced by the time since the introduction of NIV.35 Indeed, the population in this study consisted of well-established ventilation users with stable adherence and likely entrenched usage behaviours, which may explain the minimal month-to-month variation observed (figure 4). Given that adherence patterns are often established shortly after initiating long-term NIV, the extent to which these findings can be generalised to newly initiated home mechanical ventilation users remains to be explored. Early detection of adherence variance could be valuable for targeting resources more effectively, particularly in vulnerable patient populations where disease progression and changes in ventilatory status may impact key outcomes such as survival, as well as in newly initiated patients who may be experiencing difficulties with the therapy.

Another limitation of our study is the absence of data regarding NIV outcomes in terms of residual alveolar hypoventilation or quality of life. Given that our study population consisted of long-term NIV users, it can be hypothesised that these parameters had previously been assessed and optimised by the prescribing physicians. In France, full reimbursement of the device is conditional on an annual reassessment of its clinical and paraclinical benefits, including arterial blood gas measurements. Our results focus on the established parameters of NIV treatment quality.16 36 Although several studies have demonstrated that these parameters are associated with ventilation efficiency,37 the clinical relevance of our findings—in terms of correction of alveolar hypoventilation, improvement in quality of life and survival—needs to be further evaluated.

In conclusion, the E-QualiNIV study demonstrates an association between a new variable—adherence variance—and high-quality treatment. Adherence variance is also a prognostic indicator of treatment stability, high-quality treatment at 6 months and alerts for adherence, AHI or leaks in the subsequent month. Integrating this new variable into NIV telemonitoring could help focus on patients at high risk of poor-quality treatment and enable early detection of these individuals.

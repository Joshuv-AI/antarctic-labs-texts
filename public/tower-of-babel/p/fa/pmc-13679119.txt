A single-cell derived immune risk score predicts recurrence in NSCLC following neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy
Authors: Jun Ge, Xuesong Tong, Xingxia Wang, Jian Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy improves outcomes in resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), but predicting postoperative recurrence remains challenging. While the precursor exhausted T cell (Texp) index offers prognostic value, the broader tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) contains untapped predictive potential.MethodsA secondary analysis was conducted based on single-cell RNA and TCR sequencing data from 138 NSCLC patients treated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy. Following collinearity filtering of 51 immune cell subtypes and the Texp index, LASSO-Cox regression was applied to develop a prognostic Risk Score for recurrence-free survival (RFS).ResultsA four-feature Risk Score was identified, incorporating the Texp index, CXCL13+ Th1-like CD4+ T cells, KLRB1+ MAIT CD8+ T cells, and terminally exhausted T cells. Compared to the baseline Texp index alone, the expanded Risk Score model yielded significantly improved predictive discrimination (mean ΔC-index = 0.096) and demonstrated superior net clinical benefit in decision curve analysis at 12 and 24 months.ConclusionThis integrated single-cell immune Risk Score provides enhanced prognostic accuracy over single-metric biomarkers, offering a refined tool for postoperative risk stratification and personalized adjuvant management in NSCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16565-3.Keywords: NSCLC, Neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, RFS, PD-1

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally. For patients with resectable disease, surgical resection is the cornerstone of curative intent. However, 30% to 55% of patients eventually experience disease recurrence due to occult micrometastases [1, 2]. In recent years, neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, particularly the combination of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade with platinum-based chemotherapy, has revolutionized the therapeutic landscape [3]. Clinical trials have demonstrated that this combination significantly improves major pathological response (MPR) rates and prolongs recurrence-free survival (RFS) [3–5]. Despite these unprecedented clinical benefits, patient responses remain highly heterogeneous. A substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve MPR, and critically, pathological response alone is insufficient to accurately identify which non-MPR patients will ultimately experience postoperative recurrence [6].

The efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade is intimately linked to the complex landscape of the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) [7]. Recent advances in single-cell RNA and T cell receptor sequencing (scRNA/TCR-seq) have enabled high-resolution profiling of the TIME, revealing distinct immune archetypes associated with therapeutic outcomes [8, 9]. A recent comprehensive single-cell atlas of NSCLC patients treated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy demonstrated that the expansion of precursor exhausted T cells (Texp) serves as a favorable prognostic indicator, capable of identifying a subset of patients with a low risk of recurrence despite lacking an MPR [6]. However, the TIME is highly dynamic and interconnected [10]. Relying on a single immune cell subset or index may not fully capture the multifaceted nature of tumor-immune interactions, limiting its predictive robustness across diverse patient populations.

To address this clinical gap, there is a pressing need for integrated, multi-feature prognostic models that leverage the full spectrum of TIME heterogeneity. In this study, we conducted a secondary analysis of a prospective scRNA/TCR-seq cohort [6] to construct a composite immune Risk Score. Rather than redefining the basic cellular landscape, we utilized the comprehensively quantified abundances of 51 tumor-infiltrating immune cell subtypes and the Texp index derived from this high-resolution atlas. By applying rigorous collinearity filtering and penalized LASSO-Cox regression to 51 tumor-infiltrating immune cell subtypes and the Texp index, we aimed to develop a prognostic model. Furthermore, we sought to determine whether this integrated Risk Score provides independent and incremental predictive value over the baseline Texp index, thereby offering a more precise tool to stratify recurrence risk and guide personalized adjuvant management in resectable NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Patient selection and study design

The study cohort was derived from a prospective, real-world study of 334 patients with newly diagnosed, resectable NSCLC who received neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 immunotherapy combined with platinum-based chemotherapy. The original study provided a comprehensive single-cell atlas of the TIME (including detailed UMAP clustering and cell type annotations) and established the clinical significance of pathological response and RFS [6].

To ensure the robustness of our prognostic modeling, a secondary analysis was performed on this cohort. Patients were included based on the availability of high-quality scRNA/TCR-seq data and complete clinical follow-up. The exclusion criteria were applied as follows: (i) failure to pass scRNA/TCR-seq quality control (e.g., low UMI counts or high mitochondrial gene content); (ii) insufficient immune cell recovery (< 500 cells/patient), which would otherwise compromise the accuracy of immune subtype proportion estimation; (iii) missing RFS follow-up data; or (iv) inability to calculate the Texp index (Texp in terminally exhausted T cells (Tex)-relevant proportion) due to the absence of high-quality scTCR-seq data. Consequently, a total of 138 patients were included in the final prognostic analysis.

Correlation analysis and collinearity filtering of immune features

To characterize the dependency structure among candidate predictors before model development, pairwise correlations were calculated for all immune variables in the final analysis cohort (n = 138), including 51 tumor-infiltrating immune cell subtypes and the Texp index. Because the abundance of immune cell populations did not necessarily follow a normal distribution and could contain non-linear monotonic relationships, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to assess pairwise associations.

To reduce multicollinearity before penalized survival modeling, a correlation-based filtering step was performed. If two candidate variables showed a pairwise correlation of Spearman’s rho > 0.90, they were considered highly collinear. Such highly redundant variables were subjected to collinearity filtering before entry into the LASSO-Cox model, with one representative feature retained from each highly correlated pair or cluster based on biological relevance and mechanistic importance.

Prognostic model construction and evaluation

The cohort was randomly split into discovery (70%) and validation (30%) sets. After collinearity filtering, the remaining immune features were entered into a penalized Cox proportional hazards regression model to identify the most informative predictors of recurrence-free survival (RFS). LASSO-Cox regression was performed using the glmnet package in R, integrating survival time, survival status, and the abundance of candidate immune features, including the Texp index. To optimize model tuning and reduce overfitting, 5-fold cross-validation was applied. A Risk Score was calculated based on the weighted sum of selected features.

A specific numerical cutoff value for dichotomizing patients into high- and low-risk groups was determined using the X-tile method [11] strictly in the discovery set and then blindly applied to the validation set. Prognostic performance was evaluated by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis at 12 and 24 months. Specifically, ROC analysis was performed using the pROC package (version 1.17.0.1) to obtain the area under the curve (AUC). By integrating the patients’ follow-up time with their respective Risk Scores, the roc function was utilized to conduct the analysis at the 12- and 24-month time points. Additionally, the ci function from the pROC package was used to evaluate the AUC and calculate its corresponding confidence intervals.

To determine whether the Risk Score serves as an independent prognostic factor for RFS, univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed using the survival package (version 3.4.0). Clinical variables, including gender, age, smoking status, pre-treatment staging, adjuvant therapy, and histology, along with the Texp index and the Risk Score, were evaluated. Variables showing potential significance in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model.

The incremental predictive value of the Risk Score over the base model (Texp index) was assessed using the change in concordance index (ΔC-index) and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA). Specifically, a baseline prognostic model containing the Texp index alone and an expanded model containing both the Texp index and the derived Risk Score were constructed and compared. Time-dependent concordance indices (C-index) were calculated at 12, 18, and 24 months to quantify model discrimination over time using the pec package (version 2023.4.12). The improvement in predictive performance was summarized as the change in C-index (ΔC-index) between the expanded and baseline models. In addition, decision curve analysis was performed at 12 and 24 months using the stdca.R script to evaluate the net clinical benefit of the two models across a range of threshold probabilities. All statistical analyses and visualizations were performed in R software (version 4.2.1), with graphical representations generated using the ggplot2 package.

Results

The work flow for model construction

To identify immune features associated with long-term recurrence-free survival (RFS) in NSCLC patients treated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy, a secondary analysis was performed using a prospectively followed cohort of 334 NSCLC patients [6]. Samples with quality control failure or missing data were first excluded, yielding a scRNA/TCR-seq cohort of 234 patients (Fig. 1A). Patients with insufficient immune cell counts (< 500 cells per patient) were removed, resulting in 222 cases eligible for tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) subtype analysis. Finally, patients lacking RFS follow-up data or Texp index were excluded, leaving a final analysis cohort of 138 patients for prognostic modeling (Fig. 1A).

Using this final cohort, an immune-feature-based prognostic modeling framework was constructed by integrating 51 immune cell subtypes and the Texp index (Fig. 1B). To reduce redundancy and minimize the effect of multicollinearity, pairwise Spearman correlation analysis was performed across all candidate variables, and highly correlated features (Spearman’s rho > 0.90) were filtered prior to model building. The remaining features were then used to generate a feature matrix for downstream analysis.

The 138 patients were randomly divided into a discovery set (70%) and a validation set (30%). In the discovery set, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator Cox (LASSO-Cox) regression was applied to select prognostically informative immune features and construct a composite Risk Score formula. The prognostic performance of the resulting model was subsequently evaluated by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis at 12 and 24 months, and multivariable Cox regression in the validation framework.

To further assess its potential clinical value, a comparison was performed between a base prognostic model containing Texp index alone and an expanded model incorporating both Texp index and the derived Risk Score. Model discrimination and clinical utility were evaluated using the change in concordance index (ΔC-index) and decision curve analysis (DCA), respectively (Fig. 1B).

Correlation analysis and collinearity filtering of immune features

To characterize the dependency structure among candidate predictors before model development, pairwise correlations were calculated for all immune variables in the final analysis cohort (n = 138), including 51 tumor-infiltrating immune cell subtypes and the Texp index (Supplementary Table 1). Because the abundance of immune cell populations did not necessarily follow a normal distribution and could contain non-linear monotonic relationships, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to assess pairwise associations.

The resulting correlation matrix was visualized as a heatmap, in which positive and negative correlations were represented by blue and red gradients, respectively (Fig. 2). To reduce multicollinearity before penalized survival modeling, a correlation-based filtering step was performed with a prespecified threshold of Spearman’s ρ > 0.90 to define highly collinear variable pairs. The complete set of pairwise correlation coefficients is provided in Supplementary Table 2.

For each identified collinear pair or cluster, one representative feature was retained based on predefined biological relevance criteria: prioritizing immune subsets with well-characterized functional roles in tumor immunity and established prognostic evidence in NSCLC, to maximize the biological interpretability of the final model. Across all 52 candidate variables, only one pair of regulatory T cell subsets exceeded the collinearity threshold: CCR8⁺ Treg (CD4T_Treg_FOXP3) and CCR8⁻ Treg (CD4T_Treg_CCR8), with a pairwise Spearman’s ρ of 0.93. Given that CCR8+ Treg cells have been identified as the primary subset with potent immunosuppressive capacity and a strong association with poor clinical outcomes in NSCLC [6], CCR8+ Treg subset was included, while the CCR8− Treg subset was excluded to ensure model stability and biological interpretability in the following model construction.

LASSO-Cox identified a four-feature immune risk score associated with recurrence after neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy

To derive a biologically interpretable prognostic model, LASSO-Cox regression was applied to the filtered immune feature set in the discovery cohort (Fig. 3A). Coefficient trajectories gradually changed with increasing penalization, and only a limited number of variables retained non-zero coefficients at the optimal penalty level (Fig. 3A). Five-fold cross-validation identified the optimal penalization parameter at λ = 0.077 (approximately 0.08; Fig. 3B), yielding a final model comprising four immune features: Texp index, CD4T_Th1_like_CXCL13, CD8T_MAIT_KLRB1, and Terminal Tex.

Using the optimal cutoff value of -3.25 predefined via X-tile analysis solely in the discovery set, patients were stratified into high-risk (Risk Score > -3.25) and low-risk (Risk Score ≤ -3.25) groups. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that the Risk Score robustly separated patients with distinct RFS outcomes in the discovery set (HR = 4.07, 95%CI: 1.57–10.52, p < 0.001; Fig. 3C), and the validation set (p < 0.001; Fig. 3D).

Time-dependent ROC analysis further validated the strong discriminatory ability of the Risk Score. In the discovery set, the model achieved AUCs of 0.75 and 0.78 for 12- and 24-month recurrence prediction, respectively (Fig. 3F); in the validation set, the corresponding AUCs reached 0.90 and 0.89 (Fig. 3G).

The Texp index alone also showed prognostic stratification capacity in the discovery set with an X-tile-derived cutoff of 7.57 (High > 7.57, Low ≤ 7.57; HR = 0.35, 95%CI: 0.13–0.94, p = 0.009; Fig. 3I), with consistent performance in the validation set (p < 0.001; Fig. 3J). However, the composite Risk Score exhibited overall better discrimination than the single Texp index, especially in the discovery cohort: the Texp index only achieved 12/24-month AUCs of 0.64/0.67 in the discovery set (Fig. 3L) and 0.91/0.91 in the validation set (Fig. 3M).

Independent prognostic value of the immune Risk Score

To evaluate whether the derived Risk Score serves as an independent prognostic factor for RFS without introducing data leakage or circular reasoning, univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were strictly performed within the discovery cohort (n = 97) (Table 1). In the univariate analysis, among all evaluated clinicopathological characteristics, major pathological response (MPR), and immune signatures, only the Texp index (p = 0.013), MPR status (non-MPR vs. MPR: HR = 3.627, 95% CI: 1.417–9.284, p = 0.007), and the Risk Score (High vs. Low: HR = 4.097, 95% CI: 1.748–9.606, p = 0.001) were significantly associated with RFS. Subsequent multivariate analysis, incorporating these three significant variables, revealed that both MPR status (HR = 2.692, 95% CI: 1.009–7.186, p = 0.048) and the composite Risk Score (HR = 3.311, 95% CI: 1.029–10.654, p = 0.045) remained strong and independent predictors of RFS. Notably, the baseline Texp index lost its prognostic significance in this multivariate model (p = 0.897) (Table 1).

To validate these findings, we evaluated the stratification performance of the Risk Score in the strictly independent validation cohort (n = 41). Remarkably, zero recurrence events were observed in the low-risk group (0/29), whereas all recurrences occurred exclusively within the high-risk group (n = 5/12). Because the Risk Score perfectly predicted non-recurrence in the low-risk subset, this complete statistical separation (combined with the limited number of overall events) prevented the standard Cox proportional hazards model from mathematically converging, rendering multivariate Cox regression unfeasible for this specific subset. Consequently, the Kaplan-Meier method with the log-rank test was utilized. This analysis confirmed that the Risk Score maintained robust and highly significant discriminatory power for RFS in the independent validation set (p < 0.001, Fig. 3D).

Incremental predictive value and clinical utility of the expanded model

To further determine whether the derived Risk Score improved prognostic performance beyond the Texp index alone, a baseline model containing only the Texp index was compared with an expanded model integrating both the Texp index and the Risk Score (Fig. 4). Time-dependent concordance analysis showed that the baseline model achieved C-index values of 0.689, 0.687, and 0.670 at 12, 18, and 24 months, respectively (Fig. 4A). In contrast, the expanded model demonstrated consistently higher discrimination, with corresponding C-index values of 0.798, 0.777, and 0.784 (Fig. 4B). Overall, incorporation of the Risk Score led to a mean improvement in model discrimination of ΔC = 0.096 ± 0.007, indicating a significant increase in prognostic accuracy over the Texp index alone (Fig. 4C).

Decision curve analysis further supported the added clinical value of the expanded model. At both 12 months and 24 months, the Risk Score-based model showed a higher net benefit than the Texp index alone across a broad range of threshold probabilities, while also outperforming the default “treat-all” and “treat-none” strategies (Fig. 4D-E). Notably, the separation between the red curve (Risk Score) and the blue curve (Texp index) was more apparent over clinically relevant intermediate threshold ranges, suggesting that the composite model may provide greater utility for recurrence risk stratification and postoperative management. Together, these findings indicate that integrating the Risk Score with the Texp index substantially improves both predictive discrimination and potential clinical applicability.

Discussion

Neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy has transformed the management of resectable NSCLC, yet the inter-patient heterogeneity in long-term outcomes underscores the need for precise prognostic biomarkers. While pathological response and individual immune signatures, such as the Texp index, provide valuable prognostic information, the complexity of the TIME necessitates a more integrated approach. In this study, we developed and validated a four-feature immune Risk Score that significantly enhances the prediction of RFS compared to the Texp index alone. Our composite model, comprising the Texp index, CXCL13+ Th1-like CD4+ T cells, KLRB1+ MAIT CD8+ T cells, and terminally exhausted T cells (Terminal Tex), demonstrated independent prognostic value and superior clinical utility.

The biological rationale underlying our Risk Score aligns with the current understanding of anti-tumor immunity and T-cell exhaustion dynamics. The model incorporates both the Texp index and Terminal Tex cells, which represent opposite ends of the T-cell exhaustion spectrum [12]. Precursor exhausted T cells retain proliferative capacity and the ability to differentiate into effector cells upon PD-1 blockade [13, 14], justifying their protective role (negative coefficient) in our model. Conversely, Terminal Tex cells represent a dysfunctional, irreversibly exhausted state incapable of durable tumor control [15], thereby contributing to a higher risk of recurrence (positive coefficient). By integrating both subsets, our model captures the critical balance of the exhaustion trajectory, providing a more accurate reflection of the patient’s functional immune reserve than either metric alone.

Interestingly, the model also highlights the prognostic significance of two additional immune subsets: KLRB1+ MAIT cells and CXCL13+ Th1-like CD4+ T cells. Mucosal-associated invariant T (MAIT) cells exhibited a strongly protective association in our model. As innate-like T cells abundant in mucosal tissues like the lung, MAIT cells can rapidly exert cytotoxicity and secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as IFN-γ and TNF-α) independent of classical MHC restriction [16, 17]. Their enrichment may indicate a robust, rapid-response immune state that synergizes with adaptive responses to clear micrometastases. Conversely, the model identified CXCL13+ Th1-like CD4+ T cells as a risk factor. While CXCL13 is often associated with tertiary lymphoid structures and favorable outcomes in treatment-naive tumors [18–20], its association with poor prognosis in this post-treatment setting may reflect states of chronic, unresolved inflammation or severe immune exhaustion. In tumors that fail to clear after neoadjuvant therapy, an overabundance of these cells might indicate a stalled immune response where chronic antigen exposure has driven the TIME into a highly suppressive, regulatory state. To conceptualize how these distinct immune archetypes collectively dictate patient outcomes, we propose a mechanistic model of the TIME balance (Fig. 5). As illustrated in our graphical abstract, the derived Risk Score essentially quantifies the equilibrium between a favorable, anti-tumor microenvironment (driven by the self-renewal of Texp and the innate-like cytotoxicity of MAIT cells) and an immunosuppressive state (dominated by dysfunctional Terminal Tex and pro-inflammatory CXCL13+ Th1-like cells). This dynamic balance ultimately determines the postoperative recurrence risk in NSCLC.

Graphical abstract illustrating the conceptual framework of the TIME-derived Risk Score model. Schematic illustrating the immunological balance controlled by the four features of the Risk Score. The favorable TIME (low Risk Score) is driven by self-renewing precursor exhausted T (Texp) cells and cytotoxic KLRB1+ MAIT cells, which sustain anti-tumor immunity and recurrence-free survival; the immunosuppressive TIME (high Risk Score) is dominated by dysfunctional terminally exhausted (Terminal Tex) T cells and CXCL13+ Th1-like CD4+ T cells that fuel chronic inflammation and tumor recurrence. The equilibrium between these two immune states defines long-term postoperative outcomes

From a clinical perspective, the incremental predictive value of the integrated Risk Score is highly significant. While the baseline Texp index is a strong biomarker, our expanded model achieved a notable mean improvement in the C-index (ΔC = 0.096) and demonstrated superior net benefit in decision curve analysis across 12- and 24-month horizons. Furthermore, multivariate analysis confirmed that the Risk Score is an independent predictor of RFS, completely superseding the prognostic significance of the Texp index when evaluated together. This suggests that the composite score successfully captures the baseline predictive power of the Texp index while adding orthogonal, non-redundant prognostic information from the broader TIME. Consequently, this model offers a highly refined tool for identifying high-risk patients who might benefit from intensified adjuvant therapies, while sparing low-risk patients from unnecessary toxicity.

We acknowledge several important limitations in our study. First, the construction and validation of the Risk Score were performed using random split-sample internal validation (70% discovery, 30% validation). Given the substantial inter-patient heterogeneity inherent to scRNA-seq data and the potential risk of overfitting associated with LASSO-Cox regression, the lack of an independent, external validation cohort is a major limitation. To mitigate this risk, we employed a strict pre-modeling collinearity filtering step and 5-fold cross-validation during penalization; however, this does not entirely substitute for external validation. The generalizability of our immune Risk Score remains to be confirmed. Second, scRNA/TCR-seq is highly resource-intensive and not currently feasible for routine clinical practice. Future studies must focus on validating these findings in prospective, multicenter cohorts. To achieve this on a larger scale, it will be crucial to translate these four specific cellular features into clinically accessible and cost-effective platforms, such as multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) or targeted spatial transcriptomics, which will facilitate widespread external validation and eventual clinical implementation.

In conclusion, our study dissects the TIME heterogeneity of NSCLC patients following neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy to construct a highly discriminatory, four-feature immune Risk Score. By capturing the nuanced balance of T-cell exhaustion and innate-like immune responses, this model significantly outperforms single-metric biomarkers. Our findings provide a potential framework for refining postoperative risk stratification, for more personalized and effective management of resectable NSCLC.

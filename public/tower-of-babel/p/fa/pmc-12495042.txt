Computational dataset of cetyl trimethyl ammonium-anion complex structures and simulated infrared spectra
Authors: Arun K. Sharma, Owen McMillan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This dataset presents computationally derived geometries and vibrational spectra of cetyl trimethyl ammonium ion pairs formed with three environmentally and chemically relevant anions: bisulfate, nitrate, and methyl sulfonate. Data were obtained using quantum chemical calculations performed using the Gaussian 16 software package, utilizing the ONIOM method at the second-order Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2) level. To account for solvent effects and electrostatic screening, calculations incorporated an implicit solvent environment via the polarizable continuum model (PCM). The dataset includes optimized geometric coordinates, harmonic vibrational frequencies, and simulated infrared (IR) spectra for each cetyl trimethyl ammonium-anion pair. Additional computational details, including input parameters, computational settings, and scripts used for data processing, are provided as supplementary material to ensure complete transparency and reproducibility. Data collection involved initial construction and optimization of the molecular geometries of ion pairs, followed by calculation of vibrational modes at equilibrium. Distance-displacement scans were conducted and the ONIOM total electronic energies and geometries of these non-stationary structures are reported. This comprehensive dataset offers significant reuse potential for researchers investigating surfactant chemistry, computational spectroscopy, environmental chemistry, and related disciplines.

Background

Cetyl trimethyl ammonium (CTA⁺) is a widely studied cationic surfactant due to its critical role in diverse applications, including micelle formation, phase transfer catalysis, drug delivery, and nanomaterials synthesis [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. The behavior of CTA⁺ in solution is strongly influenced by its interactions with counterions as ion pairing can significantly modulate aggregation tendency, surface activity, and electrostatic screening effects. Understanding the geometries, vibrational modes, and infrared spectral signatures of CTA⁺-anion complexes is thus essential for elucidating mechanisms governing surfactant self-assembly and reactivity. Ion-pairing with common anions such as bisulfate, nitrate, and methyl sulfonate represents a fundamental yet complex electrostatic phenomenon in aqueous and interfacial systems. Computational characterization of these ion pairs using high-level quantum mechanical methods offers a valuable complement to experimental studies providing detailed insights into structure-property relationships that are otherwise difficult to access. This dataset was compiled to systematically characterize the geometrical structures and vibrational spectra of CTA+ ion pairs with selected anions relevant to surfactant chemistry and environmental studies. The theoretical framework underlying the work involves quantum chemical calculations based on second-order Møller–Plesset perturbation theory for accurate geometry optimization and harmonic vibrational frequency analysis. The Our own N-layered Integrated molecular Orbital and molecular Mechanics [6,7] method was employed to balance computational cost with the need for high-level electronic structure treatment. Implicit solvent effects were incorporated using the Polarizable Continuum Model [8] to approximate the influence of bulk solvent screening on the ion-pair structures and vibrational properties. Simulated infrared spectra were computed for equilibrium geometries of ion-pairs. The dataset was compiled independently but complements broader research interests in understanding ion pairing phenomena and spectroscopic signatures in surfactant and colloidal systems.

Data Description

The dataset [9] is organized into a single archive file containing four main directories. Each directory corresponds to a specific calculation set and is labeled to clearly reflect its content. For each system, we include a harmonic IR stick spectrum for the optimized stationary minimum as a vector (SVG) file in the corresponding directory, generated directly from the Gaussian output.

01-CTA-cation

Contains the optimized geometry and vibrational frequency data for the isolated cetyl trimethyl ammonium cation. Input files (.gjf) and output files (.log or .out) that are compatible with the Gaussian16 package are provided.

02-CTA-Nitrate-calculations

Contains data for the cetyl trimethyl ammonium and nitrate ion pair. Optimized structures, frequency calculations, and simulated infrared spectra are provided for equilibrium geometry. Input files (.gjf) and output files (.log or .out) that are compatible with the Gaussian16 package are provided.

03-CTA-Bisulfate-calculations

Contains data for the cetyl trimethyl ammonium and bisulfate ion pair. All computational files are organized similarly, including displaced geometries for spectral simulation. Input files (.gjf) and output files (.log or .out) that are compatible with the Gaussian16 package are provided.

04-CTA-Methylsulfonate-calculations

Contains data for the cetyl trimethyl ammonium and methyl sulfonate ion pair. Complete optimized structures, vibrational frequencies, and corresponding computed spectral data are included. Gaussian input files (gjf) for optimized geometries and displacement calculations. Gaussian16 output files (.log or .out)) include information on geometry convergence and vibrational frequencies.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Software and computational tools

All calculations were performed using Gaussian 16 [10] quantum chemistry software installed on the Expanse [11] supercomputer at San Diego Supercomputer Center provided through an ACCESS [12] allocation. Calculations were implemented on nodes equipped with AMD EPYC 7742 processors, each with 128 cores per node and 256 GB DRAM per node using the SLURM workload manager for job scheduling.

File preparation and data processing were performed using standard text editors and GaussView [13]. GaussView was also utilized for parsing vibrational data and creating graphics of ion pairs and the Wolfram Language [14] was utilized for generating plots of energy scans.

Molecular systems

The cetyl trimethyl ammonium (CTA⁺) cation was modeled in its fully extended geometry. Three anions were selected for ion-pairing studies: bisulfate (HSO₄⁻), nitrate (NO₃⁻), and methyl sulfonate (CH₃SO₃⁻). Initial structures were constructed using GaussView (Version 6) [13]. The initial ion-pair geometries were built in GaussView 6 and pre-oriented (heteroatom placed along the N(CTA)–X axis at ∼4–5 Å) prior to optimization. Structures were optimized initially in the gas phase and then in the presence of implicit solvent modeled by Polarizable Continuum Model (PCM). The final geometry optimization for the cation was carried out using the B3LYP functional and 6–311++g(d,p) basis set along with PM6 semi-empirical optimization in the ONIOM [6] scheme. The ion-pairs were re-optimized using the MP2 technique with the 6–311++g(d,p) basis set and the PM6 semi-empirical technique for the extended hydrocarbon chain of the surfactant cation. The solvent, H2O was modeled implicitly using the PCM technique implemented in the ONIOM framework. The geometry minima were identified through convergence criteria and the absence of imaginary eigenvalues.

The formation of ion pairs was monitored by tracking the distance between the nitrogen atom of the cetyl trimethyl ammonium cation and the corresponding heteroatom of the counterion—namely, the nitrogen atom in nitrate and the sulfur atom in bisulfate and methyl sulfonate. To evaluate the effect of electrostatic perturbation, each ion pair was systematically displaced along the interionic axis relative to its fully optimized geometry. The displacement steps were selected to span both short-range coupling and long-range asymptotic regimes, enabling analysis of energetic sensitivity to electrostatic proximity (ΔE from ONIOM total energies). For each system, the distance between the CTA⁺ nitrogen and the anion’s central atom (N or S) was varied in increments of −1.0 Å, −0.5 Å, +0.5 Å, +1.0 Å, +1.5 Å, and +2.0 Å. Additionally, displacements of +5.0 Å or +10.0 Å were included to capture long-range interaction effects and weakly associated states. Single-point ONIOM total electronic energies were evaluated for all displaced geometries, while harmonic frequency calculations were performed only for fully optimized stationary minima at the highest level of theory and basis set combination used in this study.

The y-axis is logarithmic and reports the relative energy ΔE (kJ mol⁻¹) referenced to the optimized stationary geometry (ΔE = 0). Compression below the equilibrium separation is strongly destabilizing, whereas elongation produces a gradual rise that approaches a shallow plateau at long range, consistent with weakening ion-pair interactions in solution. All displaced structures are non-stationary and are shown solely to illustrate the energetic sensitivity to proximity. Fig. 3, Fig. 4 depict two distinct configurations of the cetyl trimethyl ammonium–bisulfate ion pair. The “normal” configuration shown in Fig. 3 corresponds to the fully optimized geometry obtained through the standard geometry optimization procedure. In contrast, the “flipped” configuration illustrated in Fig. 4 represents an alternative orientation of the bisulfate anion, generated by rotating the anion about its sulfur atom. This configuration has been proposed in some experimental studies as a transient or metastable species in solution. However, when subjected to full geometry optimization, the “flipped” configuration reverts to the “normal” geometry, indicating it is not a local minimum on the potential energy surface. Therefore, all displacement calculations for the “flipped” configuration were performed using the unoptimized, reference geometry. Fig. 5 compares distance–energy profiles for the CTA–bisulfate ion pair in the normal and flipped orientations along the N(CTA)···S(HSO₄⁻) coordinate. The ordinate is logarithmic and reports the relative ONIOM total energy, ΔE (kJ mol⁻¹), referenced to the lowest-energy optimized geometry. Both orientations are sharply destabilized on compression, while elongation yields a gradual rise toward a shallow plateau at long range. Across the scan the flipped orientation remains higher in energy and never undercuts the minimum of the normal arrangement, indicating that the normal orientation is preferred under these model conditions. The optimized geometry of the cetyl trimethyl ammonium–methyl sulfonate ion pair is displayed in Fig. 6. In this structure, the sulfonate group is positioned near the quaternary nitrogen center of the CTA⁺ cation, while the methyl moiety extends away from the ionic core, minimizing steric interaction. This orientation facilitates favorable electrostatic association between the sulfonate and the cationic headgroup. Limitations

The primary limitations of this dataset arise from the computational methods and model assumptions employed. The use of implicit solvent modeling via the Polarizable Continuum Model captures bulk electrostatic effects but does not account for explicit solvent-ion interactions and water bridging, which can affect short-range structure and energetics. Harmonic vibrational data are reported only for fully optimized stationary minima; anharmonic and finite-temperature effects are not included. For displaced geometries, we provide single-point ONIOM total electronic energies on non-stationary structures; the scans vary only the N(CTA)···X coordinate without full relaxation of all other degrees of freedom and therefore do not represent adiabatic potentials or free-energy profiles. ΔE values are referenced within each anion; absolute ONIOM totals should not be compared across different anions nor interpreted as interaction energies without consistent monomer references and Basis Set Superposition Error treatment. Additionally, only three anion species were selected (bisulfate, nitrate, and methyl sulfonate), which limits the generalizability of trends observed across a broader range of counterions. The dataset focuses exclusively on isolated ion pairs and does not consider aggregation effects that may occur at higher concentrations in experimental systems. Within these bounds, the dataset provides a consistent and reproducible reference for ion-pair geometries, stationary-point spectra, and coordinate scan energetics.

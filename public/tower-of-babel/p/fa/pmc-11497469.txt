Protocol for isolating innate lymphoid cells from murine lamina propria of the small intestine
Authors: Yu Xu, Mingzhu Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryInnate lymphoid cells (ILCs) in the lamina propria of the small intestine (siLP) are essential for maintaining intestinal immune homeostasis; however, their isolation remains challenging. Here, we present a protocol for the rapid isolation of siLP ILCs. We describe steps for small intestine collection and trimming, epithelial cell dissociation, lamina propria digestion, and ILC analysis by flow cytometry.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zheng et al.1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Cell isolation, Flow Cytometry, Health Sciences, Immunology

Before you begin

This protocol describes the specific experimental steps for isolating ILCs in murine siLP.

Alternatives: This protocol is optimized for ILCs from siLP. However, we have also used this protocol for isolating and analyzing ILCs from the lamina propria of colon (coLP) with slight modifications. For example, the digestion time of lamina propria cells can be adjusted to 1 h.

Mice were fed in the specific pathogen-free animal facility. The experiment protocol was approved by the Animal Experimental Ethical Inspection Committee of Southeast University.

We usually choose 6–8 weeks old C57BL/6 mice for the experiment. Cells from siLP of 2–4 mice can be isolated at one time and gradually increase the number to 6 when proficient in all procedures.

Expected outcomes

Firstly, lymphocytes are gated with SSC-A versus FSC-A. Then FSC-A and FSC-H are used to gate single cells and exclude doublets, and then dead cells are further excluded with Fixable Viability Dye staining. ILCs are further gated as lineage (CD3ε, CD19, Gr-1, CD11b and CD11c) negative and CD127 positive cells. Next, ILCs were clustered by GATA3 and Rorγt, ILC2s are gated as Rorγt negative and GATA3 positive cells, ILC3s are gated as Rorγt positive and GATA3 negative cells. Furthermore, ILC1s are Rorγt and GATA3 negative. For ILC3s, further gating for CCR6 and NKp46 allows differentiation of CCR6+ILC3 (CCR6+NKp46-) and NKp46+ILC3 (CCR6-NKp46+). The gating strategy for ILCs from siLP is shown in Figure 4.

The gating strategy for ILCs from siLP

(A) Lymphocytes are initially gated by FSC-A versus SSC-A.

(B) Single cells are gated by FSC-A versus FSC-H.

(C) Live cells are FVD negative.

(D) Lineage (CD3ε, CD19, Gr-1, CD11b and CD11c) negative and CD127 positive cells are gated as ILC cells.

(E) GATA3+Rorγt− cells are gated as ILC2s, GATA3−Rorγt+ cells are gated as ILC3s, GATA3−Rorγt− cells are gated as ILC1s.

(F) ILC3s can be further divided into two cellular subpopulations. CCR6+NKp46− cells are gated as CCR6+ILC3, CCR6−NKp46+ cells are gated as NKp46+ILC3.

Limitations

Firstly, the protocol is not suitable for one person to process more than 8 samples at the same time, because it will take most of the time on small intestine trimming and result in decreased cell viability. If you need to process a large number of small intestines at the same time, please collaborate with others in the experiment. Secondly, there are some articles reports that FBS may impair the protease activity of DNase I.2 Therefore, the experimenters can try to use FBS-free Lamina propria cell digestion solution. However, it is worth noting that the removal of FBS may increase cell recovery but also decrease cell viability. Additionally, this protocol focuses mainly on ILCs from the lamina propria of murine small intestine, and if there is a need to analyze other cells in the lamina propria, such as T cells and B cells, the appropriate cell-specific markers can be selected. Furthermore, this protocol isolates all lamina propria cells but not only lymphocytes. If lymphocytes enrichment is required, please use 80% and 40% Percoll to obtain by density gradient centrifugation. Finally, the antibody panels that we used in this protocol are not sufficient to analyze all ILC populations. For analyzing ILC populations, antibody panels for surface, intracellular, and transcription factor staining can be modified.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Incomplete removal of surface fat from the small intestine (step 4 and step 7).

Potential solution

Incomplete removal of small intestinal visceral fat will result in lower cell yield. Firstly, we recommend removing as much fat as possible immediately after the small intestine is isolated from mouse. Secondly, the use of a surgical light is a better option when trimming the small intestine as it makes the visceral fat more visible and easier to maneuver. Finally, we recommend the use of high precision tweezers because it makes it easier to remove the fat and less likely to disrupt the structure of the small intestine.

Problem 2

Not completely digested in small intestine (step 21).

Potential solution

In general, the digestion time of this protocol is sufficient to completely digest the small intestine. However, in the intestinal inflammation model, the tissue becomes tougher. In this case, the digestion time can be increased appropriately to ensure complete digestion of the tissue, but it is not recommended to exceed 1 h as this will decrease cell viability.

Problem 3

Low cell yield (step 30).

Potential solution

This protocol can achieve a relatively ideal cell yield. The complete removal of intestinal contents and visceral fat will significantly increase the cell yield, and the optimal usage of Liberase DH concentration and digestion time will further increase the cell yield. In addition, this protocol will ultimately harvest all cells from siLP. If lymphocyte enrichment is required, 80% and 40% Percoll can be used for density gradient centrifugation.

Problem 4

No fluorescence signal of some markers by flow cytometry (step 56).

Potential solution

First of all, it is necessary to use a single stained sample to set the correct voltage for a specific channel. Secondly, the staining process must be incubated for a sufficient time, otherwise there is a risk that some markers will not have a fluorescent signal. Additionally, it is important to make sure that the amounts of antibodies are sufficient for the experiment. Furthermore, bright fluorescent dyes are recommended for low expressed antigens. Finally, it is recommended that a pilot experiment be performed prior to the formal experiment to ensure that the correct antibody concentration is used and that the target cell population can be properly detected.

Problem 5

Low cell viability (step 58).

Potential solution

The cells harvested in this protocol will have relatively high cell viability. When trimming the small intestine, we recommend placing it in ice-cold PBS, which can improve cell viability. Furthermore, ice-cold HBSS and RPMI 1640 media are better choices. In addition, optimal Liberase DH concentration and digestion time are critical for cell viability. When isolating intestinal lamina propria ILCs from mice with intestinal diseases, we recommend performing a pilot experiment before the formal experiment to select the most appropriate Liberase DH concentration and digestion time.

Molecular tool for efficient breeding of DOMINANT Greenshell laying hens and significant refinement of phenotypic selection focused on eggshell color
Authors: Kristina Beranova, Eva Jozova, Vojtech Anderle, Michael Rost, Lukas Zita, Pavel Beran, Helena Tyllerova Landova, Vladislav Curn, Milan Tyller
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In recent decades, interest in non-traditionally colored eggs has increased. For breeders, this market interest means breeding lines of laying hens that lay eggs of varied colors, such as the blue-green eggshells (Dominant Greenshell) in this study. This study presents the results of genotyping the polymorphism of the O locus responsible for shell coloration and photometric measurement of eggshell color based on the CIELAb system, which was carried out on the unique Czech breeding population Dominant Greenshell. The aim was to use a combination of phenotyping using the CIELab System method and genotyping of the O locus using the end-point PCR approach with the main focus on the accuracy of distinguishing shell color genotypes, streamlining the selection of dominant homozygotes in the O locus, optimizing this technology for the most efficient and cost-effective selection procedure in practical hen breeding. The optometric method was able to reliably distinguish only dominant and recessive phenotypes and eliminate from the population only undesirable recessive homozygotes with a white colored shell. The parameter a* (redness/greenness) from the CIELab color space turned out to be absolutely key for distinguishing dominant and recessive phenotypes. Using the CART methodology, a classification tree built on discriminating optometric characteristics a-blunt was obtained, however, for the group of desirable O/O homozygotes, the selection approach would result in incorrect genotyping of 31% of individuals. Therefore, a combined approach based on rapid and simple elimination of recessive homozygotes using phenotyping (CIELab photometric measurement) and molecular identification of the EAV-HP insertion in the SLCO1B3 gene in dominant phenotypes, regardless of color intensity affected by laying time/order, and allowing reliable elimination, has proven to be the most effective method to distinguish heterozygotes from the breeding population. The combination of optometric and molecular selection methods then leads to more efficient selection, reduction of overall selection costs. This process led to the stabilization of the breeding population within one generation and the achievement of a pure homozygous line with regard to eggshell color.

INTRODUCTION

Eggshell quality is one of the main breeding goals for laying hen breeders. In many countries, eggshell color is an important indicator of egg quality, with great importance in the sales and marketing strategies of retailers (Dalirsefat et al., 2015). Although no direct relationship has been demonstrated between eggshell color and nutritional values of eggs (Scott and Silversides, 2000), consumers in different countries around the world prefer a certain coloration, even despite the higher purchase price (Li et al., 2006). In the last few decades, hen breeds laying eggs with a less traditional blue to light green color, originating in Chile, have begun to appear on the markets of Western Europe, North America, and Japan. These breeds include Araucana, Ameraucana, Dongxiang, Lushi and many others (Lukanov et al., 2015). Consequently, lines of laying hens and dual-purpose hybrids have been bred that lay blue-green colored eggs.

The blue eggshell color in hens is caused by the accumulation of the bile pigments biliverdin and protoporphyrin IX in the surface layer of the eggshell and is determined by an autosomal dominant trait traditionally called oocyan (O) (Punnett, 1933; Zhao et al., 2006; Wragg et al., 2013; Chen et al., 2020). For a long time, the genetic determination of eggshell coloration was not being known and until recently Wang et al. (2013) reported that the blue eggshell is caused by an ∼4.2 kb EAV-HP insertion in the 5´-flanking region of the SLCO1B3 gene that promotes the expression of SLCO1B3 gene in shell gland of the oviduct in hens. Based on these studies it was possible to analyze breeding populations and to predict blue eggshell phenotype at the molecular level. SLCO1B3 gene encodes the membrane transporter OATP1B3, which is responsible for the transport of amphipathic organic compounds including bile salts. This mutation in the promotor region activates the expression of the SLCO1B3 gene in the shell gland of the oviduct and can enhance transportation of biliverdin to eggshell (Wang et al. 2013; Chen et al. 2020). Chen and Wang (2022) found positive correlation between SLCO1B3 gene expression in shell glands and biliverdin concentration in eggshell which supported role of OATP1B3 in pigment transport and formation of blue-green colored eggs.

Following this discovery, new studies were carried out, for example, Darwish et al. (2019) conducted a genome-wide association study (GWAS) on the Chinese Dongxiang chicken breed, investigating four traits determining hue and color intensity. In total, 10 SNPs were identified that were demonstrably associated with this trait. Additionally, Chen et al. (2020) confirmed the effect of EAV-HP insertion on SLCO1B3 gene expression. It is anticipated that with the rapid development of high throughput DNA techniques, further and more detailed studies will be forthcoming.

In the breeding process, continuous selection is carried out to obtain eggs not only of a certain color, but also its intensity and uniformity are essential (Lukanov, 2014). High color uniformity also indicates consistent production performance and higher breeding degree (Liao et al., 2021). Until the early 1960s, studies that addressed the possibilities of eggshell color classification were focused on subjective evaluation, e.g., Farnsworth and Nordskog (1955) and Godfrey (1949). Subjective evaluations are not entirely satisfactory due to variables such as lighting, observer's color velocity, limited number of categories, and changing standards (Wei and Bitgood, 1990). A decade later, the use of photoelectric equipment for objective color measurement was published (Hunton, 1962). The development of specific spectrophotometers that measure the intensity and shade of the eggshell has brought significant improvements in selection for layer breeders. Today, the L*a*b* color system (also known as the CIELab System) is the most used for this purpose and is one of the most widely used systems in many areas of color measurement to date (Fairchild, 2013). The measured values can thus be compared at different ages for the same group of hens. From a breeder's point of view, it is essential that the variation within a breeding stock can be analyzed as a normally distributed quantitative trait and can be used for the purpose of systematic selection in the direction of consumer's preferences (Cavero et al., 2012). As far as is known, this system of selection is routinely used in breeding of white and brown-shelled layers (Cavero et al., 2012; Hunton, 1962), to our knowledge there is considerably less data on blue-shelled breeds. Ongoing studies try to identify the most accurate selection procedure for uniformity of blue shell color using the L*a*b* color space also in combination with molecular analysis and statistical models of different difficulty level.

The color variation in blue eggs was studied by Guo et al. (2020). Based on L*a*b* color space measurements, the results attribute this phenomenon to the strong influence of dominant genetic effects. They also suggested the use of their findings in a planned mating system for commercial egg production. Another study conducted by Zeng et al. (2022) used the L*a*b* color space to predict the amount of deposited eggshell pigment as a tool for rapid eggshell color selection in the breeding system. Further, Wang et al. (2024) successfully mapped functional variants associated with hen eggshell color in SLCO1B3, CPOX, ALAS1, and ABCG2 genes with the L*a*b* values. Correlation between eggshell colors and eggshell pigments in different subtypes of blue-shelled eggs was studied by Wang et al. (2023). The study revealed correlation between eggshell colors and eggshell pigment content.

Dominant Genetika Ltd., hens leading breeding company in the Czech Republic, has highly developed breeding programs focused on producing non-traditionally colored eggs (so called Easter Eggers). The selection is currently being performed using traditional breeding methods and photometric approaches. Recently the CIELab System has been introduced for selection for eggshell color intensity and PCR-based approaches to identify individuals with EAV-HP insertion in SLCO1B3 gene.

Specifically, we address the following questions. (1) Will photometric measurements provide outputs on the basis of which individual genotypes can be distinguished? Does egg pigmentation decrease during one clutch and is the photometric analysis and determination of the genotype of individual hens affected? (2) Is it possible to reliably differentiate genotypes on the basis of molecular analysis, and will this analysis enable routine detection of individual alleles? (3) Is it possible to appropriately combine phenotyping and genotyping in a breeding population? As is known, selection using phenotypic approaches is laborious and time-consuming, including some degree of inaccuracy, since it usually takes several generations to achieve the desired goal of eliminating the recessive allele from the population. This paper summarizes the results of the first study focusing on the combination of photometric and PCR-based approaches for the time- and cost-effective selection of desirable individuals/genotypes and the design of an appropriate technology for the successful selection of dominant homozygous individuals in Greenshell lines.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethic Statement

The animals were kept in a private breeding facility that meets the requirements of the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching. All the material for analyses were collected by a veterinarian specialized in poultry in accordance with the Regulation No. 176/2023 on the protection and rights of animals in force in the Czech Republic.

Animals and Sample Collection

Two groups (119 and 2745 individuals) of the original Czech, blue-shelled DOMINANT Greenshell line were tested. This line was developed by breeding the original South American Araucana breed with the high-performance Dominant lines while maintaining and improving the color of the shell. It differs from the original breed mainly by higher performance parameters (higher laying rate, higher egg weight, more stable shell color intensity until the end of the laying cycle, earlier onset of laying), but also by calmer temperament. The Greenshell line is characterized by autosexing, where it is already possible to distinguish the sex of day-old chicks on the basis of the color of the primary plumage. Overall, the coloration of adult animals is quite varied. The main plumage color is greyish brindle, with brownish brindle on the breast and a distinct silver color with a noticeable transverse brindle on the neck. The male appears lighter overall, otherwise similar in color to the hen. The color of the auricle is white, the crest is relatively large, often connate due to the large scape. The color of the treads is yellow to white. The live weight of the hen is 1.8 to 2.5 kg, that of the male 2.0 to 2.8 kg. The color of the shell is turquoise blue to greenish. The average weight of the eggs is 59 g. In contrast to the Araucana breed, the Dominant Greenshell line does not have the absence of the last links of the tailless tail and does not have the ear tufts, which is the plumage on the chin forming a kind of beard. In contrast, the Greenshell line has a distinctive crest on its head. All tested birds were kept under the same conditions in individual boxes in the provider's private facilities (Dominant Genetika Ltd, Czech Republic). A group of 119 laying hens was tested by photometric measurement of eggshell color in combination with molecular markers. A total of 357 fresh eggs from 119 DOMINANT Greenshell laying hens at 40-41weeks of age were photometrically evaluated. Eggs were collected during different stages of the laying cycle on three consecutive days to cover the natural loss of pigment in the shell during the laying cycle. Three eggs were collected from each hen and described by an identifier for the hen and the day of lay. The eggs were stored at room temperature and standard humidity (18-20°C, 45% RH) until the completion of the experiment. The eggs were processed within one week to prevent distortion of results due to gradual pigment degradation. Blood and feather samples from all 119 hens were used for DNA extraction and an appropriate method of molecular genotyping was proposed. The optimized PCR genotyping method was used for screening of large breeding population. This breeding population included a total of 2745 individuals. Of this number, 178 were males and 2567 were females.

Photometric Analysis

The eggshell color was evaluated by an objective photometric method using a QCR reflectometer (TSS, York, UK), operating on the principle of determining the percentage of light reflected by the eggshell surface within the interval from 0 to 100 %. Higher values correspond to lighter eggshell shades. Reflectivity was measured at three points on the eggshell: the sharp, middle and blunt ends. Instrumental color measurements were recorded in the CIELab color space for L* (lightness – 0 = black, 100 = white), a* (redness/greenness - positive values = red, negative values = green), and b* (yellowness/blueness - positive values = yellow, negative values = blue) using a spectrophotometer (CM-700d, Konica Minolta, Osaka, Japan). The instrument has been used in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The following parameters were measured: “ref_blunt”, “ref_middle”, “ref_sharp”, “L_blunt”, “L_middle”, “L_sharp”, “a_blunt”, “a_middle”, “a_sharp”, “b_blunt”, “b_middle”, “b_sharp”.

DNA Extraction and Genotyping Analysis

Two types of input material were used to optimize DNA extraction. The first one was peripheral blood collected professionally in 3 ml VACUETTE® haematology collection tubes with K3 EDTA (Greiner Bio-one GmbH, Austria). The second type of material was freshly collected feathers. The material was stored at 4°C until processing. DNA extraction from peripheral blood was performed using a commercial kit NucleoSpin Blood (Machery-Nagel, Germany) according to the manufacturer's instructions. DNA from feathers was extracted according to the in-house protocol using a minor modified CTAB PVP method (Doyle, 1991).

Genomic DNA was amplified by end-point PCR in two different variants. The first variant was adopted from the protocol published by Wang et al. (2013) using three primers test-nor-up 5´TTTGACCAGCGTAGATAA 3´, test-nor-down 5´ ATGTTAGCAGTGTAGTTG 3´ a test-eav 5´ TAGGTTCCGAACGCGATGT 3´. The second variant was performed according to the protocol of Wragg et al. (2013) using primers Multiplex PCR F 5´ -GCATTTCACAAACGGGTGTA, Multiplex PCR R1 5′-CAAAACCACAAAGGTAATGTTCA-3 and Multiplex PCR R2 5′-CCCAGCAGTAAGCCCTACAT-3. The total volume of the PCR reaction was set up in a volume of 10 µl containing 1x reaction buffer (150 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.8 (at 25°C), 40 mM (NH4)2SO4, 0.02% Tween 20, 5 mM MgCl2, 400 µM dATP, 400 µM dCTP, 400 µM dGTP, 400 µM dTTP, 100 U/ml Taq DNA polymerase, dye, stabilisers and additives (PPP Master Mix, Top-Bio, CZ), 20 pmol from each primer and 50 ng of template DNA. The microtubes were placed in a thermocycler (TProfessional Basic Gradient, Analytik Jena, Germany) under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, 36 cycles 30 sec at 94°C, 30 sec 53.5°C, 20 sec 72°C, final extension 5 min 72°C for primers test-nor-up, test-nor-down and test-nor-eav. For primers Multiplex PCR F, Multiplex PCR R1 and Multiplex PCR R2 the temperature profile is as follows: initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, 30 cycles 10 sec at 94°C, 30 sec 56°C, 30 sec 72°C, final extension 5 min 72°C. Products were separated on a 2% agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide and then visualized using an InGenious3 device (Syngene, UK).

Data Analysis

To evaluate the effect of different genotypes on egg color intensity, a set of measurements obtained using a CM-700d spectrophotometer (Konica Minolta, Osaka, Japan) and a QCR reflectometer (TSS England) were analyzed. Subsequently, basic descriptive statistics were determined along with 95% asymptotic confidence intervals. Differences in the studied traits with respect to O/O, O/o, o/o genotype were assessed using a mixed linear model that accounted for the hierarchical (nested) structure of the data. The restricted maximum likelihood (REML) algorithm implemented in the “lmer” library was used for numerical estimation (Bates et al., 2015). Genotype was considered as a fixed factor, while individual hens and their clutches were considered as random factors nested within genotype, formally expressed as:yijk=β0+βiGENOTYPE+uj|i+ϵijk

yijk: The value of k-th observation of a particular variable (ref_blunt, etc.) for the i-th level of genotype and the j-th level of hen factor.

β0: Overall mean (intercept).

βi: Genotype fixed effect. Where coefficient βi indicates the average difference in the value of a particular variable for a i-th genotype from the overall mean/reference.

uj|i: Indicates the random effect for the j-th hen within the i-th genotype. The vertical bar “|” emphasizes that the j-th hen is nested within the i-th genotype. Where uj|i∼N(0;σu2).

εijk: The error term (residual), which captures all remaining variability not explained by either fixed or random effects. Where εijk∼N(0;σ2).

Statistical significance was defined as a p-value less than 0.05. To illustrate differences in measured values for different genotypes, the principal components method (Jolliffe, 2002) was used with dimension reduction to two latent variables. At the same time, simple classification rules for approximate prediction of genotype based on egg color measurements were obtained using the Classification Regression Tree Method (CART) by Breiman et al. (2017). All necessary numerical calculations and graphs were performed using the R 4.4.0 programming environment (R Core Team 2024) with the appropriate libraries loaded.

RESULTS

Genotyping of Pilot Population

The analyses were first performed on a test set of 119 laying hens from which a total of 357 eggs were collected. These eggs were collected at different three consecutive laying days, to cover the possible change in pigmentation intensity of the shell during laying. Two types of input material - peripheral blood and freshly collected feathers - were tested for genomic DNA extraction. The genotype of all individuals was successfully determined by PCR analysis regardless of the origin of the input DNA sample and both DNA extraction methods gave identical results. Two different PCR procedures according to Wang et al. (2013) and Wragg et al. (2013) have been optimized to evaluate genotype using molecular markers. Figs. 1b and 1c show a comparison of results obtained by both of these protocols and it can be seen that both procedures lead to the same results providing identical genotyping. Based on the size of the amplified products, three genotypes were then evaluated: The O/O, which is the dominant homozygote genotype determining the green or blue color of the shell, known as green shell, the heterozygous genotype O/o and the recessive homozygote o/o, which is manifested by the white color of the shell. Subsequently, the discriminatory power of individual variables, respectively their suitability for screening purposes, was estimated using logistic regression. Given the identified presence of “complete separation” and the resulting Hauck-Donner phenomenon, i.e. the inability to finitely estimate regression coefficients using the maximum likelihood method, the more appropriate CART methodology was subsequently used. The resulting classification tree (Suppl. Figure S1), built on discriminating optometric characteristics a-blunt, can clearly distinguish individuals with a recessive phenotype. The first group includes individuals with dominant homozygous genotype (150 individuals) and heterozygotes (66 individuals), and the second group includes heterozygotes (78 individuals), but also 27 dominant homozygotes. Analysis of Breeding Population

Since phenotypic data fully correlated with genotyping results within the experimental group (see genotype table and laying hens ID in Table S1), the protocol of molecular analysis was subsequently applied to the entire breeding population of the DOMINANT Greenshell line covering 2745 individuals. This population consisted of 178 males and 2567 hens. In the case of males, 146 individuals carrying the O/O genotype, 26 individuals of O/o and 6 individuals of o/o genotype were detected. Undesirable genotypes (O/o and o/o) were detected in a male's group at 18%. A total of 2030 O/O genotypes, 488 O/o genotypes and 49 o/o genotypes were detected in hens. Thus, 20.9% of undesirable (O/o and o/o) genotypes were detected in hen group, 2.9% more than in males. Overall, the analyzed breeding population included 2176 dominant homozygotes with the genotype O/O (79.3%), 514 heterozygotes with the O/o genotype (18.7%) and 55 recessive homozygotes with the o/o genotype (2.0%). Thus, 20.7% of undesirable genotypes were present across the analyzed population.

DISCUSSION

The genetic background of the individual hen and breed/variety is a determining factor for eggshell color (Li et al., 2006). In the same way, the uniformity and intensity of coloring is often an indicator of egg quality for consumers, i.e. also a breeding goal of the producer. However, the color of the shell may also vary depending on some other factors, such as the order of the egg laid (Bi et al., 2018), the genotype of the individuals (Wang et al., 2013) and the level of expression of the SLCO1B3 gene encoding the membrane transporter OATP1B3 (Chen et al. et al., 2020; Chen and Wang, 2022). The effects of nutrition, health, and age are also reported; however, these factors were not found to be significant (Hargitai et al., 2018; Samiullah et al., 2015).

In our study with regard to the methods and approaches used, three hypotheses have been defined. The first hypothesis assumed that the photometric method would provide data on which it will be possible to determine and distinguish all genotypes. Based on our photometric analysis, it would not be possible to determine the genotype of individual hens and the photometric values of all hens would permeate. Results of PCA analysis (Fig. 2) showed two separate groups: Group A - containing only individuals with genotype o/o and Group B - containing individuals with genotypes O/O and O/o. Thus, individuals carrying only recessive o alleles could only be truly distinguished by photometric measurement without the use of a PCR test. The results showed that a* value is the most sensitive index in evaluating shell color change in the CIELAb system / color space and thus supported the results reported by Chen and Wang (2022). On the basis of optometric measurements, there are demonstrable differences between these two groups of genotypes – genotypes with the presence of EAV-HP insertion and without this insertion. This finding provides important support for the consistency between the distribution of a* values alone and the genotypes. These results suggest that this parameter may be of high interest for selection. Li et al. (2006) observed the variation of eggshell color in different-colored chicken lines by measuring the L*a*b* parameters. As a representative lines for blue-shelled eggs, two hen lines were chosen, one of which was cross of White Leghorn and blue-shelled native breed carrying the O/o genotype and the native breed being homozygous O/O. They observed different values among parameters measured between those two genotypes, which could be explained by the different genotype and cumulative effect of genes. However, in our study, individuals with genotype O/O and O/o, showed a partial overlap and based on measurements of photometric parameters alone, these genotypes cannot be distinguished. Results observed by Wang et al. (2023) during evaluation of L*a*b* parameters and its correlation with pigment content in blue-green egg subtypes, support our findings. Individual photometric parameters (reflectance and color measurement) do not have the same weight (Fig. 2). A detailed assessment of individual photometric parameters and their ability to distinguish groups of genotypes is given in Fig. 3. It appears that clear separation of recessive homozygotes (Group A) is only possible when evaluating a* parameter and regardless of the measurement position on the egg. The a* parameter allows to distinguish the shell color (white and blue) as was also observed by Li et al. (2006) and recently by (Chen and Wang, 2022). This result also corresponds to the PCA analysis results reported in Fig. 2. In theory, therefore, the value of a* parameter that provided the greatest variability could be used to determine the genotype by measuring the shell color. In contrast, the values L* and b* proved to be of little evidence in the color assessment, also concur with the observation of Li et al. (2006). Similarly, Wang et al. (2024), evaluated the a* parameter as strongly associated with the SLCO1B3 functional gene variant, affecting the amount of pigment deposited in the blue eggs. The difference in pigment saturation between the eggs of individual laying hens was not sufficiently significant and the values in the PCA analysis appeared to be relatively stable (Fig. 2). This finding may be explained by the effect of hen-to-hen variability, which will have a greater influence on the variation in shade intensity than the order of the egg laid (Zeng et al., 2022).Figure 3Graph showing complete separation of the o/o genotype (marked in red) from the O/o and O/O genotypes based on the analysis of photometric data for parameter a*. The second hypothesis tested the level of success of the diagnostic PCR test and possibility of its introduction into routine testing of breeding population. A causal mutation for blue egg color was published by Wang et al. (2013). Wragg et al. (2013) published a study focused on the sequencing analysis of causative mutation – ∼4.2 kb retroviral insertion in the SLCO1B3 gene in the Mapuche fowl and in European breeds of hens. His study revealed a 100% sequential coincidence of both inserts. In addition, they found that EAV-HP insertion sites are different in Asian, South American, and European “oocyan” breeds of hens. DOMINANT Greenshell line was bred on the basis of private breeding know-how using unique donor genotypes. Although the current literature does not mention this, there was a distinct possibility that published PCR diagnostic tests would not be 100 percent effective in this specific population. To verify the reliability of the diagnostic test, we tested various input samples (blood and feathers), and we used two DNA isolation methods and two protocols for molecular analysis and identification of retroviral insertion - detection of O and o alleles. Primers previously published by Wang et al. (2013) and Wragg et al. (2013) were used. To obtain reliable PCR analysis results the conditions of PCR analysis have been modified. The genotype of all individuals was successfully determined by PCR analysis. Regardless of the origin of the input DNA sample, both DNA extraction methods gave identical results (see Table S1 for detailed results). PCR products obtained after analyses of DNA extracted from feathers appeared brighter, with no non-specific products.

The third hypothesis posed the question of whether it is possible to combine phenotyping and genotyping in a suitable way, i.e. optometric measurement of eggs and analysis of the presence of the EAV-HP insertion in the SLCO1B3 gene. From the results and conclusions given in the first hypothesis, it is evident that the optometric method was able to reliably distinguish only dominant and recessive phenotypes and eliminate only undesirable recessive homozygotes with a white colored eggshell from the population. Even in the case of using the CART methodology and classification trees, it is not possible to reliably distinguish between dominantly homozygous and heterozygous individuals, and the error rate is relatively high. In the group of desirable O/O homozygotes, 31% of heterozygotes, carriers of an undesirable recessive allele, was present. For a fast, reliable and cost-effective analysis of the breeding population, a technology based on a combination of a fast optometric method and the measurement of only the key a* parameter (or only the a_blunt value) can be proposed for the elimination of recessive homozygotes. Genotyping and precise determination and selection of dominant homozygotes can then be performed in the remaining population. When screening a large breeding population, feathers collection as an input material for subsequent PCR analyses appears to be more convenient choice to accelerate the selection and also to prevent the animals from increased manipulation, compared to blood collection. Combination of optometric and molecular selection methods then leads to more efficient selection, reduction of overall selection costs, and stabilization of the breeding population within one generation and the achievement of a pure homozygous line with regard to eggshell color.

Breeding of laying hens for eggshell color was based on methods of subjective shade and color evaluation for about 60 years. For this subjective evaluation own color scales or standardized color palettes/fans (color fan grading) were used (Zeng et al., 2022). Likewise, most of the studies investigating shell color were based on a subjective comparison (Wei and Bitgood, 1990). Since the invention and introduction of CIELAB system devices (from roughly the second half of the 1970s in leaders in the poultry sector) into standard breeding practices, measurements have been substantially refined and digitized (Cavero et al., 2012) and became an auxiliary means in breeding programs.

Based on the results of our experiment, in the case of selection/elimination of a recessive allele at the O locus, one cannot solely rely only on photometric measurements, because there is a certain intermingling of the measured values of heterozygous and dominant homozygous individuals for this trait. Despite the relatively low cost of PCR analyses, photometric analyses using a simple portable device are less complicated. But only the end-point PCR is reliable for accurate and precise genotype detection. However, a combination of the two methods can be used, where individuals carrying the recessive homozygous genotype o/o are selected in first step (simple and fast photometric analysis) and the more expensive and time-consuming PCR test is then applied to a remaining part of the population. In addition, results from analyses of photometric measurements show that this process can be further simplified and rationalized to only a small number of parameters. In case of this study even one photometric parameter in one egg point can be measured, which is consistent with the results of Li et al. (2006). This will allow very rapid assessment and primary screening to eliminate recessive homozygotes. Choosing this combined approach will reduce the negative effect of both methods - the higher inaccuracies of photometric measurement and time losses and financial burden in the case of PCR. Laboratory analysis also requires collaboration with an external specialized laboratory and increased handling of animals during blood collection. For breeders, the combination of the two methods mentioned above represents not only a considerable timesaving, but also a financial saving.

CONCLUSION

In the previously described approach, we proposed to accelerate and streamline the selection of desired dominantly homozygous individuals by combining photometric measurement of eggshell color and molecular selection. The selection process has thus been significantly shortened by several generations compared to conventional breeding methods, and for the breeder it represents an acceleration of the process by several years. Molecular selection, or the combination of photometric and molecular selection has not yet been used within the breeding company and breeding program of laying hens DOMINANT CZ. In laying hens, 19.38% of heterozygotes with an undesirable recessive allele were detected in the group of dominant phenotypes determined by optometric evaluation. A total of 17.98% of unwanted individuals, recessive homozygotes and heterozygotes were detected in the group of males. In this case, the advantage of the PCR approach was shown in the possibility of applying the method to males, whose genetic structure/nature is determined only by classical approaches during sexual maturity, or to the tests of their sisters. The elimination of undesirable genotypes, carriers of the recessive allele, from the breeding population enabled a rapid stabilization of the shell color in this breeding line. According to the breeding company's internal testing, no recessive allele was observed in subsequent generations of the DOMINANT Greenshell line. Thanks to early detection, this trait was fixed in a clean line guaranteeing the laying of only eggs with a blue-green shell color.

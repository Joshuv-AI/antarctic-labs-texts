Published: 2025

Article title: Children’s Hope Scale: factor structure and norms for Australian children aged 8–18 years

Authors: Richter, S., Dorrian, J., Wicking, P., Wicking, A., & Lushington, K.

Journal: Australian Journal of Psychology

Bibliometrics: Volume 76, Number 1, pages 2322724

DOI: http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/00049530.2024.2322724

Following the publication of our article (Richter et al., 2024), a colleague suggested that we conduct Exploratory Graph Analysis (EGA) to further examine the factor structure of the Children’s Hope Scale (CHS) in our sample of 171,052 Australian children aged 8–18 years. While our original analyses, which included exploratory factor analysis (EFA), confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), and bifactor modelling, supported a unidimensional factor structure, EGA provides a complementary network psychometrics approach to assessing dimensionality (Golino & Epskamp, 2017). Unlike exploratory or confirmatory factor analysis, which rely on latent variable modelling, EGA uses community detection algorithms to identify the number of dimensions directly from item–item relationships, quantify item redundancy, and assess stability through bootstrapping. This provides an additional, visually interpretable check on the robustness of the factor structure (Golino et al., 2020).

EGA analyses

Unique variable analysis (partial correlations and weighted topological overlap [wTO]) was used to identify potential item redundancy (Christensen & Golino, 2021). Four pairs showed small-to-moderate redundancy (wTO > 0.20) but not at a magnitude warranting removal (Supplementary Table 1). EGA was performed using graphical least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (GLASSO) with the Louvain community detection algorithm. Results yielded a single community, confirmed via the Louvain Method, with consistent network loadings across items (Supplementary Figure 1). Bootstrap analysis replicated a single dimension in 100% of resamples (stability = 1.0).

Interpretation

These EGA results align with and strengthen the findings reported in our original article, further supporting the interpretation that the CHS functions as a unidimensional measure of hope in Australian children.

Impact on conclusions

These additional analyses do not change the conclusions or implications discussed in the published article.

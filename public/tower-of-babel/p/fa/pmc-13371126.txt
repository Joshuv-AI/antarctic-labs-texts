SXRNSCLC-PRSP software to predict prognostic risk and survival in patients with resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer in Shanxi Province China
Authors: Yunkui Zhang, Xuesong Wu, Bin Yang, Rongsheng Zhang, Bin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo develop SXRNSCLC-PRSP software which can predict the prognostic risk and survival of resected T1-3N0-2M0 (according to the 9th AJCC/UICC TNM stage of lung cancer) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients in Shanxi Province China more comprehensively, accurately and conveniently, and provide reference and help for clinicians tailoring patients’follow-up adjuvant therapy and care.MethodsPatients with NSCLC whose tumor stage is T1-3N0-2M0 underwent surgical treatment only were selected from the medical records of Shanxi Tumor Hospital. The clinicopathological features that may affect the prognosis of these patients’survival outcome and survival time were collected (there are no missing data), and then the survival data set was established. In the survival data set, 70% of the patients were randomly selected as the training set, and the rest were composed of the test set. A prognostic model of resected T1-3N0-2M0 NSCLC patients in Shanxi Province China was constructed using the training set, and the model was validated using the test set. SXRNSCLC-PRSP software was developed to implement the model for prognostic risk and survival prediction in such patients. The software can be used free of charge by clinicians who log on to a specific website. After they register and log on to the software, they can select the corresponding clinicopathological characteristics of the patient and obtain the prognostic risk and survival prediction results of the patient.ResultsUsing a Cox proportional hazard regression model, we determined the independent prognostic factors and obtained a prognostic index (PI) eq. PI = = -0.392X2 + 0.927X71 + 1.695X72 + 0.537X111 + 0.401X112-0.434X113. Using the PI equation, we determined the PI value of every patient. According to the quantile of the PI value, patients were divided into three risk groups: low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups with significantly different survival rates. Meanwhile, we obtained the restricted mean survival times and 1–5-year survival rates of the three groups. Based on the construction of prognostic risk and survival prediction model and the programming in JAVA language, we developed the SXRNSCLC-PRSP software to determine the prognostic risk and associated survival of patients with resected T1-3N0–2M0 NSCLC in Shanxi Province China. At last, we have established a Risk Assessment System(RAS). In this system, clinicians can use the software. clinicians can input URL https://www.sxrnsclcpps.com into one of browsers (latest versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Microsoft Edge which have passed the compatibility test for the login function) to reach its login screen. By processing clinical parameter inputs, the software stratifies patient risk levels and generates Restricted Mean Survival Time (RMST) estimates and survival rate projections, providing clinical support for follow-up care planning, adjuvant therapy selection, and patient screening.ConclusionsAfter prognostic factor analysis, prognostic risk grouping and corresponding survival assessment, we developed a novel software program and established the Risk Assessment System (RAS). It is practical and convenient for clinicians to evaluate the prognostic risk and corresponding survival of patients with resected T1-3N0–2M0 NSCLC in Shanxi Province China. Additionally, it has guiding significance for clinicians to make decisions about complementary treatment for patients.Keywords: Resected non-small cell lung cancer, Prognostic risk prediction, Survival prediction, Prognostic index, Software, Risk assessment system

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the malignant tumors with the highest morbidity and mortality in the world, which seriously endangers human health, especially in China. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for 80%-85% in lung cancer. GLOBOCAN 2020 estimates that there are about 2.21 million new cases of lung cancer worldwide, with an age-standardized incidence of 22.4/100,000. There are approximately 1.8 million lung cancer deaths worldwide, with an age-standardized mortality rate of 18.0/100,000 [1, 2]. Over the past 35 years, lung cancer has been growing very fast in China [3]. The National Cancer Center (NCC) of China indicates that in 2022, lung cancer was the most prevalent cancer in China with 1,060,600 cases. Among males, lung cancer was the predominant cancer, and among females, it was also the most common. The leading cause of cancer mortality was lung cancer, accounting for 733,300 deaths. The top cause of cancer death for both men and women is lung cancer [4]. With the rapid economic and social development, cancer spectrum of China is changing from developing country to developed country, the cancer burden of China will be increased with population aging and increase of unhealthy life, meanwhile, there is a diversity of cancer types in different regions in China [5].

The epidemiological study of lung cancer in Shanxi Province shows that the incidence and mortality of lung cancer are high in this region, which may be related to many factors. Such as severe air pollution, smoking, occupational exposure including asbestos, arsenic and chromium, population aging, genetic factors et al. [6–10]. To deal with the public health problem, Shanxi Province has taken a series of measures, including strengthening environmental governance, promoting healthy lifestyles, raising public awareness of lung cancer and early screening. These efforts are expected to reduce lung cancer incidence and mortality in the future.

Prognosis is crucial for personalized treatment and surveillance suggestion of the resected non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients Although the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system is a powerful predictor, it is not perfect enough to accurately distinguish all the patients, especially within the same TNM stage. There are numerous literatures on the analysis of prognostic factors of resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The influencing factors are usually discussed including clinicopathological features [11–14], molecular markers [15–17], therapeutic modalities [18–20], and physiological status of patients [21]. There are many prognostic prediction tools, such as International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer(IASLC) Lung Cancer Staging Project: the Ninth Edition of the TNM Classification of Lung Cancer [22], Cox Proportional Hazards Model [23], Nomograms [24, 25], Prognostic Score [26, 27], Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools, et.al. Liang W, et al. [28, 29] conducted a retrospective analysis of 6,111 patients with operable non-small cell lung cancer who received treatment in the cardiothoracic surgery departments of seven medical institutions in the People’s Republic of China from January 2001 to December 2008. They identified and integrated multiple factors that were significant predictors of survival, thereby constructing a nomogram. This model underwent internal validation based on the bootstrap method and was externally validated using an independent cohort of 2,148 patients from the IASLC database. The accuracy and discriminatory ability of the prediction were measured by the concordance index (C-index) and risk stratification. A total of 5,261 patients were included in the study for analysis, and six independent prognostic factors were identified and incorporated into the nomogram. The calibration curves for the overall survival rates (OS) at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years showed the best consistency between the predicted results of the nomogram and the actual observations. The C-index of this prediction model was higher than that of the American Joint Committee on Cancer Seventh Edition TNM staging system in predicting OS. Fumihiko K, et al. [30] developed an AI-based prognostic model for patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who underwent surgical resection. This study included 1,049 NSCLC patients from Kyushu University with pathological stages I—IIIA who had undergone surgical resection. Seventeen clinical pathological factors and 30 preoperative and 22 postoperative blood test results were set as explanatory variables. The extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) algorithm was used as the machine learning algorithm. The 5-year disease-free survival (DFS), overall survival (OS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS) rates of the patients were 71.0%, 82.8%, and 88.7%, respectively. The AI prognostic model showed that the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for DFS, OS, and CSS at 5 years was 0.890, 0.926, and 0.960, respectively. The artificial intelligence prediction model constructed using XGBoost demonstrated good predictive accuracy and could accurately predict the probability of postoperative prognosis for NSCLC. XGBoost is a decision tree ensemble model with advantages such as capturing interactions and nonlinear relationships and handling missing values. They conducted internal validation of the artificial intelligence prediction model and performed 20 bootstrap validations. Wang J et al. [31] conducted a retrospective analysis of the clinical data of 10,212 patients with primary lung cancer admitted to Shanxi Cancer Hospital from 2017 to 2021, covering relevant information such as gender, age, pathological type, and clinical stage. Cox proportional hazards regression was used to analyze the prognostic factors and a prognostic prediction nomogram model was constructed. The performance of the nomogram model was evaluated by the concordance index, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA).Cox multivariate analysis showed that gender, age, pathological type, and clinical stage were prognostic factors for patients with primary lung cancer. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) of the prediction model was 0.768. The calibration curve and DCA indicated that the model had good predictive ability and a high net benefit value. The constructed prognostic prediction model has certain clinical value.

It is well known that clinical doctors are often overwhelmed with work in their daily practice. The use of TNM staging and various predictive tools is inconvenient for them, and there is currently no prognostic prediction tool for patients with resected non-small cell lung cancer in Shanxi Province. Therefore, we are committed to developing a software that can predict the prognosis and risk of patients with resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer in a convenient and comprehensive manner, so as to provide reference and assistance for postoperative supplementary treatment of such patients in Shanxi Province.

Data and methods

In this study, the information of 1086 patients with NSCLC were selected from Shanxi Tumor Hospital. Inclusion criteria: [1] pathological diagnosis was NSCLC; [2] have had only one primary lung cancer in their lifetime and no other malignancies between 2013 and 2023; [3] had only received surgical treatment; [4] had clear surgical treatment information; [5] survival time is greater than or equal to one month, and there were no cases of loss to follow-up; [6] ≥ 20 years old. Exclusion criteria: [1] there is no exact information about the site of the primary tumor, the side of the primary tumor, the pathological classification and stage of the tumor; [2] patients with tumor stage IV; [3] the patient’s ethnicity and place of birth are unknown. As shown in Fig. 1, we screened patients according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. As shown in Table 1, we collected clinicopathological characteristics and follow-up information of patients, including gender, age, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease (CHD), nation, tumor stage, laterality, marital status, histology, surgery type, primary site, survival months and status. First, we randomly drew approximately 70% of patients from the data set to form a training set (760 patients), the remaining patients formed the test set (326 patients). Second, using Cox proportional risk regression model, we identified independent prognostic factors basing on the training set. Third, based on the training set, the Cox proportional risk regression model was used to construct the prognostic index (PI) equation, and then the PI value of each patient was obtained. According to the quantile of PI value, Kaplan–Meier analysis and Log-rank test, patients could be divided into three risk groups with significant differences in survival, namely low-risk, medium-risk and high-risk groups, and the restricted mean survival time (RMST) and 1–5 years survival rate estimates of each risk group were obtained. Then the prognosis risk and survival prediction model were constructed. Fourth, we validated model basing on test set. Fifth, by using Java language programming, SXRNSCLC-PRSP software was developed to realize the model for prognostic risk and survival prediction of patients. Finally, establishing a Risk Assessment System (RAS), clinicians log in to the specific website, register for free and log in to the software, select the corresponding clinicopathological characteristics of patients, and obtain the prognostic risk and survival prediction results of patients. SPSS (16.0 version) software (Inc, Chicago, IL, USA) was used for statistical analysis, and P < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Prognostic risk and survival prediction model construction

Based on the training set, the PI value of each patient was obtained according to the PI equation (with the increase of PI value, the survival rate of patients gradually decreased). According to the PI value of 0 ~ 33%, 33% ~ 67%, 67% ~ 100%, the training set of patients were divided into low-risk, medium-risk and high-risk groups. PI < 0.009 refers to the low-risk group, 0.009 ≤ PI < 0.927 refers to the medium-risk group, and PI ≥ 0.927 refers to the high-risk group (see Table 6). Kaplan–Meier analysis and Log-rank test were used to show that the survival rate of patients in the low-to-medium to high-risk groups gradually decreased, and the differences among the three groups were statistically significant (P < 0.0001) (see Fig. 2). Meanwhile, the restrictive mean survival time and 1–5-year survival rate estimates for each risk group were obtained. The estimated restrictive mean survival time was 56.32 months for the low-risk group, 52.66 months for the medium-risk group, and 44.64 months for the high-risk group. The estimated 1, 2, 3, 4,5-year survival rates were 97.13%, 95.20%, 93.05%, 89.95%, 88.83% in the low-risk group and 93.33%, 90.23%, 85.73%, 79.19%, 75.82% in the medium-risk group and 90.68%, 75.15%, 65.76%, 60.22%, 56.04% in the high-risk groups respectively (see Table 6). Then, a prognostic risk and survival prediction model was constructed, that is, by calculating the PI value of patients, the prognostic risk and the corresponding restrictive mean survival time and 1–5-year survival rate were predicted.

Subgroup model performance

(1) Gender subgroup analysis

(1) Gender subgroup analysis

The survival difference analysis based on stratification by gender subgroups showed that in the male subgroup, there was a significant difference in the survival curves among the high, medium, and low-risk groups (Log-rank χ2 = 9.525, P = 0.009) ( Fig. 4); in the female subgroup, there was also a statistically significant difference in the survival curves among the high, medium, and low-risk groups (Log-rank χ2 = 15.823, P < 0.001) (Fig. 5). Therefore, this model accurately predicts in both male and female populations.

Software development and establishment of the Risk Assessment System (RAS)

Based on the construction of prognostic risk and survival prediction model and the programming in JAVA language, we developed a software named SXRNSCLC-PRSP to predict the prognostic risk and survival of patients with resected T1-3N0-2M0 NSCLC in Shanxi Province China. Then, we have established a Risk Assessment System (RAS). In this system, clinicians can use the software. clinicians can input URL https://www.sxrnsclcpps.com into one of browsers (latest versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Microsoft Edge which have passed the compatibility test for the login function) to reach its login screen (Fig. 29). At the bottom of the interface is the name of the software, a brief introduction to it, and an explanation of the relevant abbreviations. Above the interface is the login box. New users can click the "Register" button on the login box to sign up. After successful registration, users can click the “Return logon” button to return to the login interface, enter the username and password, click the “Sign in”button and enter the software interface (Fig. 30). At the top of the software interface, a description of an operational example can be seen. Beneath the operational example is an explanation of the relevant abbreviations, and there are alternative choices positioned under the abbreviations. In accordance with the operational example and the explanation of abbreviations, clinicians initially need to ascertain the clinicopathological characteristics of patients. Taking a resected T1-3N0–2M0 (according to the 9th edition of AJCC/UICC stage classification) non-small cell lung cancer patient as an example, the clinicopathological characteristics of a patient were Age (≤ 65), Tumor stage (II), Surgery type (PLET). you can choose the appropriate response for each factor. If there are no corresponding options, you should choose none and then click the “Submit” button to submit their entry, and the prognostic risk and survival prediction results will be shown on the next page (Fig. 31). In addition to this, below the login box, there is a button labeled "Feedback". By clicking this button, a dialog box will pop up. In this dialog box, you can input the problems you encountered during use, suggestions for functional improvement, and suggestions for improvement interface, and than click the “Submit” button to submit (Fig. 32). The software is applicable to the following conditions: 1. Patients aged 20 or above; 2. No history of hypertension; 3. Han ethnicity; 4. Tumor stage II or III; 5. Tumor pathology: adenocarcinoma; 6. Discussion

The SXRNSCLC-PRSP software is a predictive tool designed to assess prognostic risk and survival outcomes for patients with resected non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) classified as T1-3N0-2M0 in Shanxi Province China, where specific epidemiological and clinical factors may influence cancer outcomes. The software utilizes various clinical parameters to stratify patients with resected NSCLC classified as T1-3N0-2M0 in Shanxi Province China into different risk categories. Clinicians can input patient data into the software to receive prognostic outputs including the estimates of the restrictive mean survival time and 1–5-year survival rate, helping in tailoring follow-up care and adjuvant therapy. The development of this software is particularly significant in the context of Shanxi Province, where lung cancer prevalence is high, and there may be unique factors affecting patient survival.

The SXRNSCLC-PRSP software provides clinicians and patients in Shanxi Province with an important tool to accurately assess the prognostic risk and survival of patients with resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) based on patient specific clinical data. This software not only helps doctors to develop personalized post-operative treatment plans and follow-up plans, but also provides patients with clearer information about the prognosis of the disease, thereby enhancing patient confidence and participation in the treatment process, and ultimately promoting the optimization of clinical decision making and the improvement of overall patient care.

Rakaee, S et al. developed a machine learning (ML)-based model to classify tumors into one of three categories: inflamed, altered, and desert, based on the spatial distribution of CD8+T cells in two prospective (n = 453; TNM-I trial) and retrospective (n = 481) stage I-IIIA NSCLC surgical cohorts. It can identify patients at greater risk of disease recurrence after surgical resection [32]. Weijian, M et al. Have developed a nomogram to predict the overall survival (OS) of patients with T1-3N0M0 NSCLC after curative resection. It’s online version of nomogram offer opportunities for fast personalized risk stratification and prognosis prediction in clinical practice [33]. There are clinical decision support system for predicting the prognosis of patients with resected non-small cell lung cancer. Jihwan Park et al. retrieved anonymized imaging and clinical data from an institutional database, focusing on patients who underwent curative pulmonary resections for non-small cell lung cancers using a deep learning model, the Mask Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (MRCNN), to predict cancer locations and assign recurrence classification scores. The model successfully identified lung cancer recurrence sites, which were then accurately mapped onto chest CT images to highlight areas of primary concern [34]. A software RNSCLC-PRSP was developed to predict the prognostic risk and survival in patients with resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer collecting information on patients from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, which provides cancer statistics for U.S. the software can analyze subdivided T and N staging and additional factors to predict prognostic risk and the corresponding mean and median survival time and 1–5-year survival rates of patients with resected T1-3N0–2M0 NSCLC [35].

Now, based on the data of NSCLC patients in Shanxi Province, I developed a similar software SXRNSCLC-PRSP, Predicting the prognostic risk and survival of resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients to help clinicians and lung cancer patients in Shanxi Province. At present, there have been no relative prognostic predictive software programs for resected T1-3N0–2M0 NSCLC in Shanxi Province.

Limitations

There are several limitations to consider when developing SXRNSCLC-PRSP software to predict prognosis and survival of patients in Shanxi Province China with resected T1-3N0-2M0 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). 1. The development of the software is mainly based on the data of NSCLC patients in Shanxi Province China, and this prediction model may not be widely applicable to patients in other regions or different populations. 2. more predictive factors identified in previous studies such as tumor vascular or lymphatic invasion, postoperative pathology (solid infiltrating adenocarcinoma, micropapillae, cribriform or complex glandular components, spread though air space et al.) and those that can be routinely obtained through common examinations or tests such as smoking status, physical status, molecular biological factors, biochemical and biomarker results will lead to more comprehensive and accurate predictions, getting more realistic and practical predictive model, enhancing its clinical utility. Unfortunately, however, there is no or no comprehensive above predictive factors in our database. This is the limitation and deficiency of our research, but we will try to add these predictive factors on software improvement to improve the software performance in future. 3. Non-small cell lung cancer is a highly heterogeneous disease whose biological behavior may vary depending on a patient’s genomic characteristics, tumor microenvironment, and other factors, and simple models may not adequately capture this complexity. Factors such as a patient’s systemic condition, comorbidities, and quality of life also play an important role in prognosis, but these are often difficult to quantify and incorporate into models. 4. The acceptance and trust of new software tools by physicians and patients may affect their practical application in the clinic. Clinicians may require additional training to use these tools effectively, increasing the complexity and cost of implementation. 5. With the development of new treatments and technologies, past data may not fully reflect patient outcomes in current clinical practice, and the software needs to be updated regularly to maintain its effectiveness. Although SXRNSCLC-PRSP software has important potential for prognostic evaluation of non-small cell lung cancer in Shanxi Province, researchers and clinicians need to be aware of these limitations when using the software and make integrated judgments combining clinical experience and the latest research findings to optimize patient management and treatment strategies.

Conclusion

In the field of prognosis assessment and survival prediction for resected non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the development of software-based tools such as SXRNSCLC-PRSP offers new opportunities for clinical practice. These tools are able to integrate large amounts of clinical data to support patient survival predictions through advanced algorithms, helping physicians develop personalized treatment plans. However, the continuous effectiveness and reliability of the software is affected by a number of factors, including limitations of data sources, heterogeneity of clinical data, complexity of tumor biology, limitations of technical algorithms, and barriers to clinical application.

To ensure the continuous clinical effectiveness of this software, we have established a clear maintenance and update mechanism to guarantee that the software remains accurate and relevant over time. 1. Regular data updates and integration with user feedback: Clinical data is updated every six months, and patient clinical and pathological characteristics are supplemented including the application of new treatment methods and technologies; tumor vascular or lymphatic invasion; postoperative pathology (solid infiltrating adenocarcinoma, micropapillae, cribriform or complex glandular components, spread through air space, etc.); smoking status; physical status; molecular biological factors; biochemical and biomarker results; genomic characteristics; tumor microenvironment; comorbidities; quality of life. A feedback channel for clinicians and patients is established in the software interface, and common issues (such as functional defects or compatibility problems) are included in the priority update queue. 2. Model re-training and performance optimization: Based on the frequency of data changes and the rate of model degradation, formulate a re-training plan (such as once a year or triggered updates). For example, if the deviation of the prediction model exceeds the threshold due to the adjustment of the treatment strategy, initiate re-training immediately. After re-training, perform strict tests to verify the model’s performance on historical datasets and new scenarios, ensuring no side effects are introduced. Deploy a real-time monitoring system to track the match between model outputs and clinical results, and trigger alerts when anomalies occur. 3. Safety and Compliance Management: Pay attention to updates in medical regulations (such as data privacy laws or industry standards), regularly assess software compliance, and adjust data processing procedures to meet requirements. Identify potential risks (such as technical debt or personnel turnover), and conduct penetration testing and vulnerability scanning annually through redundant design (such as multiple people responsible for key modules) to ensure the system’s protection capabilities and address cybersecurity threats. 4. Cross-departmental collaboration and process optimization: Clearly define maintenance goals (such as enhancing software accuracy and patient safety), break them down into specific tasks for the development, testing, and clinical departments, and strengthen team collaboration. Regular cross-departmental meetings (such as biweekly meetings) are held to synchronize progress, and real-time tools are utilized to facilitate the rapid resolution of problems. Adopt the PDCA (Plan—Do—Check—Act) cycle to continuously optimize maintenance processes, such as simplifying deployment steps to reduce clinical disruptions. 5. Document and Knowledge Management: Maintain complete change logs, recording the reasons, contents, and responsible persons for each update to ensure traceability. Centrally store technical documents, training materials and fault cases. Regularly provide operation training for clinical staff to ensure that the software functions are effectively utilized and enhance overall utility.

Through the above mechanism, the software can adapt to changes in treatment strategies, maintain long-term accuracy and clinical relevance, and ultimately support high-quality medical decisions.

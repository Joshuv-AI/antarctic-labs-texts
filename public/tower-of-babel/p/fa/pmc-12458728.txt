Comparison of treatment efficacy between topical insulin and autologous serum in persistent epithelial defects: a randomised controlled trial protocol in Spain
Authors: Pedro Arriola-Villalobos, Barbara Burgos-Blasco, Ana Ascaso-del-Rio, Lydia Medina-Ramírez, Ana Belen Rivas, Natalia Pérez-Macías, David Diaz-Valle, Antonio Portolés-Pérez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPersistent epithelial defect (PED) management can be challenging. First line of treatment includes lubrication, bandage contact lenses and punctal plugs. The second line of treatment includes autologous serum (AS). Topical insulin has been shown to be safe for topical use and improve corneal epithelial healing. Therefore, a controlled clinical trial (control group with current standard treatment, ie, AS) multicentre, randomised and with a blind third observer will be conducted to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the use of insulin eye-drops in the treatment of PED.Methods and analysisA preselection of patients with epithelial defect after 1 week of treatment will be made and blood tests will be obtained in order to dispense AS if necessary. After 2 weeks of standard treatment, if the PED persists and the patient meets criteria, patients will be enrolled after signing an informed consent form. Patients will be randomly allocated to receive either insulin (1 UI/mL, 4 times a day) or AS (20%, 5–6 times a day) eye-drops for 3 months. 234 patients will be included, 117 in each treatment group. The main variable (PED size) will be obtained from slit-lamp photographs, an objective and easily quantifiable variable which will be evaluated by a blinded investigator (third observer). Patients will be examined every 3–5 days until week 4 of study treatment and once a week until 6 weeks, to continue with a visit every 2 weeks until reaching 3 months of follow-up. Introduction

The cornea has an important barrier function, particularly the corneal epithelium due to its non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium and tight junctions.1 2 Healthy corneal epithelial cells have an average lifespan and self-renewal rate of 7–10 days and are constantly regenerated from limbal stem cells. Therefore, an acute epithelial defect in a healthy cornea is an epithelial defect that generally heals within 7–14 days.3 A persistent epithelial defect (PED) is defined as a corneal defect with a minimum area of 2 mm2 that does not heal after 2 weeks of treatment. PED can result from altered epithelial adhesion, limbal stem cell deficiency, trauma, medications and infections that interrupt the normal healing process of the cornea.2 4

PED management can be challenging, sometimes requiring both medical and surgical treatment. First line of treatment is medical treatment, which includes lubrication, withdrawal of epithelium-toxic medication, antibiotic prophylaxis, bandage contact lenses and punctal plugs.5 As a second line of treatment, the use of autologous serum (AS), or other blood products such as platelet-rich plasma, has been described as very effective and is usually used when the first line of medical treatment is insufficient.6 7 Most commonly used surgical treatment is debridement and amniotic membrane grafting.4 8 9

Recently, new medical therapies have demonstrated their efficacy in the treatment of PED, including recombinant nerve growth factor or epidermal growth factor.10 11 Moreover, topical insulin has been shown to be safe for topical ocular use in humans and improve corneal epithelial cell proliferation and wound healing in several case series.12,16 A retrospective consecutive case–control study by our group compared the effect of topical insulin on epithelisation in PED refractory to usual treatment compared with AS, insulin presenting better epithelisation outcomes.17 Therefore, we intend to demonstrate if insulin could be more useful than AS in epithelial defects that are resistant to initial therapy.

Clinical trials investigating the treatment of PED are difficult to conduct given the heterogeneity in the disease and multiple variables affecting outcome. Large samples are needed and few centres have a high enough volume to carry it out. Hence, designing a protocol to investigate the efficacy of different treatments for PED is challenging.

To date, there is no first level evidence on the efficacy and safety of the use of insulin eye-drops in the treatment of PED, nor is there a prospective comparison of the use of insulin and AS. Our study plans to fill this existing gap, conducting a controlled clinical trial (control group with current standard treatment, ie, AS) multicentre, randomised and with a blind third observer, to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the use of insulin eye-drops in the treatment of PED.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a multicentre, controlled, randomised, blind-evaluator clinical trial in patients with PED. One of the main reasons for failure of clinical trials is the difficulty to recruit patients, which made us decide the use of three centres (Hospital Clínico San Carlos, Hospital General Universitario Gregorio Marañón and Hospital Puerta de Hierro, all in Madrid). All have cornea units, attending a large volume of patients affected by the disease.

Recruitment, consent, randomisation and blinding

The study flow is presented in figure 1. Patients visiting the outpatient clinic or the emergency room at the aforementioned hospitals will be invited to participate in the study. The randomised clinical trial protocol has been communicated to the investigator and participating staff of each hospital who will be involved in patient recruitment. For a good recruiting performance, training on the procedure is provided before starting enrolment.

Schema of development of the study. PED, persistent epithelial defect.

A preselection is made of patients with epithelial defect after 1 week of conventional treatment. Blood tests will be obtained, as is done in routine clinical practice (in order to dispense AS if necessary). On that visit, topical treatment will be changed to a standard treatment, common to all patients (artificial tears+antibiotic without preservatives±bandage contact lens).

After 2 weeks of standard treatment, if the PED persists and the patient meets criteria for the study, study information will be provided to all patients by an investigator in a quiet room. They will be encouraged to take time to consider the information provided before deciding on study participation. If they agree to participate, signed informed consent will be obtained.

Patients will be randomised in a balanced manner with a 1:1 ratio to the two treatment groups (insulin or AS eye-drops) in blocks of 6 participants. The randomisation list and assignment to the treatment group will be done automatically by RStudio software and will be then programmed in RedCap. Once a randomisation code is assigned, it cannot be used for another patient.

Given that AS requires blood extraction, it is not possible to blind participants or their responsible specialist about the treatments they receive. To preserve from evaluation bias, the main variable will be obtained from slit-lamp photographs, an objective and easily quantifiable variable which will be evaluated by a blinded investigator (third observer).

Medications

When randomised, patients will be allocated to receive either insulin (1 UI/mL, 4 times a day) or AS (20%, 5–6 times a day) eye-drops for 3 months. Patients will continue with artificial tears and antibiotics, as were indicated.

Insulin eye-drops will be prepared in the pharmacy service of the coordinating centre under sterile conditions, individually as a master formula for all the participants. Based on the information in the literature, the eye-drops are prepared at a concentration of 1 IU/mL, using regular insulin in solution for subcutaneous injection (Actrapid; Novo Nordisk A/S, S.borg, Denmark).

As described in the literature,16 17 9 mL of 0.9% saline solution (saline for injection) is drawn into a 10 mL syringe in a laminar flow hood under sterile conditions. Then, 1 mL of regular insulin (Actrapid 100 U/mL) is extracted from the vial and transferred into the syringe containing the 9 mL of saline, ensuring the insulin is added through the cone to maintain sterility. The mixture is gently shaken until homogeneous, resulting in a solution of approximately 10 UI/mL. A 0.22 µm filter is attached to the syringe to filter the solution and 10.8 mL of sterile saline is drawn into a 20 mL syringe. Then, 1.2 mL (equivalent to 12 UI of insulin) from the previously prepared 10 U/mL solution is transferred into this syringe using a 1 mL syringe, and the contents are combined with the saline, resulting in a final concentration of approximately 1 UI/mL. The solution is gently mixed again, and about 3 mL of the filtered solution is drawn and filtered through the 0.22 µm filter into a sterile eye-drop bottle. The process is repeated as necessary to ensure proper filtration. The pH of the solution should be between 5.5 and 6.0 for optimal stability. Once prepared, the 3 mL glass bottle is sealed, labelled, and stored at 2°C–8°C. Bottles must be discarded 7 days after opening. Patients from the coordinating centre will pick up the medication (a month’s supply) from the hospital’s pharmacy. In the case of patients from the collaborating centres and in order to maintain the cold chain throughout the process, the medication will be delivered at the patient’s home. Also, the instructions on the correct handling and administration of the drops, as well as the storage conditions are provided in writing with the shipment of the medication, being the same in the patients of the three centres involved.

AS eye-drops will also be prepared under sterile conditions. Given that the same protocol is used in the participant hospitals, they will be prepared in the participant’s hospital. A blood extraction is performed by venipuncture, obtaining about 100 mL of blood, which is later processed by centrifugation. The resulting serum is diluted in 20% saline, filtered and stored in plastic jars protected from solar radiation. It is kept refrigerated (2°C–8°C), with a duration of each bottle once opened for 7 days. The rest of the bottles are stored at −4°C. The instructions on the correct handling and administration of the drops, as well as the storage conditions, will be delivered in writing, being the same in the three centres involved. The AS eye-drops will be prepared at each centre, according to their usual clinical practice.

Study measurements

Enrolled patients will be examined every 3–5 days until week 4 of study treatment and once every week until 6 weeks, to continue with a visit every 2 weeks until reaching 3 months of follow-up.

The schedule of visits and examination items is shown in the online supplemental material.

A slit-lamp photograph of the epithelial defect will be taken at each visit, in order to objectively monitor the evolution of the PED. Measuring the PED size directly at the slit-lamp carries the risk of bias and low reproducibility. In order to correctly measure the defect, the measurement of the corneal diameter (white-to-white distance) will be determined on the first visit using IOLMaster-700 (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany) optical biometry. The measurement of the epithelial defect will be carried out blindly by a single evaluator, through the image obtained by others, using the Image J program.

All patients should comply with 3 months of treatment and follow-up visits (see figure 1), even if they have presented closure of the PED. Cases without evidence of substantial progression of epithelialisation (reduction of the epithelial defect area of less than 25%) after 4 weeks will be classified as having insufficient response and will synergistically receive the eye-drops from the other treatment arm. Cases with some complications (stromal thinning, infection, etc) will be considered treatment failure and will leave the study, being able to receive the treatment considered by the investigators.

Patients who discontinue the treatment and are lost to follow-up will only be analysed if they have been included, randomised to any treatment arm of the study and, at least, one assessment of the primary variable is available.

Sample size calculation

Based on previous studies, it is expected that the magnitude of the effect will vary depending on the aetiology, that is, infectious, neurotrophic or chronic disease of the ocular surface. The priority of this study is the assessment of the effect in PED of neurotrophic aetiology or chronic disease of the ocular surface, so the calculation of the sample size is aimed at these population groups. However, infectious PED cases will be registered in a cohort and will be analysed in an exploratory way so as to allow the generation of hypotheses for future studies.

Thus, assuming similar results to the initial studies for each aetiological group underlying the epithelial defect, and in order to reach a statistical power ≥80% and a global bilateral significance of 0.05 to detect differences between treatments, 27 patients with PED of neurotrophic aetiology per treatment group and 51 patients with chronic ocular surface disease-associated PED per treatment group would be required.

In addition, it is estimated that 10% of patients may withdraw or be lost for evaluation, so the following total number of patients has been planned to be included in the study: neurotrophic PED (n=60), PED secondary to chronic disease of the ocular surface (n=114) and infectious PED (exploratory, n=60). A total of 234 patients will be included: 117 test treatment patients and 117 control treatment patients.

Statistical analysis

Once the study is finished, after recording the data of the last participant, the database will be closed and the corresponding analysis will be carried out. This analysis will be carried out in accordance with the principles specified in the consensus document of the International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) E9 (CPMP/ICH/363/96).

The data collected will be analysed centrally at the coordinating centre, in order to respond to the objectives of the study. The statistical software Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS V. 25.0; IBM) will be used to perform the analyses.

The planned efficacy analyses will be carried out in each of the following populations: (1) intention-to-treat (ITT) population for the main analysis: participants included and randomised to any treatment arm of the study and (2) per-protocol population: participants who complete the study without major protocol deviations that could affect the corresponding efficacy analysis; who are followed up and receive study treatment for at least 4 weeks or until closure of the epithelial defect is achieved.

The safety analysis will include all patients receiving at least one dose of study treatment.

The subgroups according to PED aetiology (infectious, neurotrophic, chronic of the ocular surface) are of special interest and will be investigated. Any subgroup analysis including post hoc will be performed according to the normative principles and recommendations (EMA/CHMP/539146/2013. Guideline on the investigation of subgroups in confirmatory clinical trials. Available at: https://www.ema.europa .eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-investigation-subgroups-confirmatory-clinical-trials_en.pdf) and will be justified and identified as data-based. The missing data will be managed following the principles specified in ICH-E9 and CPMP/EWP/1776/99 Rev1.

The primary analysis comprises the evaluation of the percentage of patients who achieve complete closure of the PED. The homoscedasticity and normality of the distributions under study will be tested. For this analysis, the Student’s t test or Fisher’s exact test will be applied, depending on its distribution. The result of each etiological group will be analysed.

A descriptive analysis of all the variables under study will be carried out: the absolute and relative frequencies of the qualitative variables and the usual statistical parameters for measuring central tendency and dispersion of the quantitative variables will be calculated (mean, median, SD, IQR, minimum and maximum).

To evaluate the time until epithelialisation, Kaplan-Meier curves will be applied. Continuous variables (eg, PED area) will be analysed using Student’s t test or analysis of variance or, if the distribution does not fit the corresponding normality criteria, their non-parametric equivalents. For the comparison of categorical variables, the χ2 test or, when necessary, the Fisher test will be used. CIs will be included for a significance level adjusted to 0.05.

Data management and monitoring

The research team will guarantee the confidentiality and secrecy of the personal data and sensitive information to which they have access. Data will be collected in the online Case Report Form (CRF) by a member of each study centre using the REDCap platform. The data generated during the project will be managed by those responsible for the study and stored in a secure server. The dataset will be coded, and the main research team will have access to the key. All data will be stored and analysed using the unique study code. The data collected will be analysed centrally at the coordinating centre, in order to respond to the objectives of the study.

The sponsor will designate a monitor, who will review the source documents to confirm that the data collected in the CRF are accurate and that the research is carried out based on the protocol and that the research team has followed the standards of good clinical practice. The researcher and the institution guarantee direct access to the source documents for the monitors and the competent regulatory authorities for their verification, as well as the centre file and the patient identification list.

On completion, they will be stored in an appropriate repository that complies with applicable regulations for at least 5 years. After that time, the data will be securely deleted, although a description of the data will be retained. In addition, during those years, the data may be reused in new research projects, always in compliance with current data protection regulations.

Protocol violation

Those patients with clinical criteria of worsening complication (stromal thinning, super-infection) will be withdrawn from the study (and considered as treatment failure). An intention-to-treat analysis will be performed.

Adverse events and serious adverse events

Adverse events (AEs) are defined as any undesirable experience occurring to a subject during the study. AEs are considered serious if they endanger the subject’s life or cause death, require or prolong the participant’s hospitalisation, cause persistent or significant disability, result in a congenital anomaly or constitute a significant medical event. Likewise, all suspected transmission of an infectious agent through a study medication will be reported as serious AE.

During the study, all AEs will be recorded starting from the administration of the study medication, as well as their progression, the suspected relationship with the study medication, and the interventions undertaken during follow-up. At each visit, the occurrence of AE will be explored (indirect questions directed at the patient, findings during the ophthalmological examination, etc), and participants will be questioned about their occurrence and progression since the last visit. They may also be voluntarily reported by participants at any time.

All information regarding AE will be recorded in the patient’s medical record and subsequently entered into the CRF. Any serious AE that occurs in a clinical trial participant from the start of study medication until the end of study participation will be reported by the investigator to the sponsor within 24 hours of detection. The sponsor will notify all suspected serious AEs to the Spanish Agency for Medicines and Medical Devices (AEMPS) and the corresponding regional governments in accordance with current Spanish regulations on clinical trials. Appropriate measures will be implemented to ensure that the participant receives appropriate care. Patients are covered by the hospital’s insurance. The investigator will follow up participants with AE until they resolve, are no longer clinically relevant or are determined to be permanent.

The investigator will assess the severity and causality of the AEs. The severity rating is based on clinical observation and describes the intensity of the event according to the following three categories: mild, moderate or severe. When assessing the causality of each AE, the investigator will classify the AE as having a reasonable causal relationship (temporal sequence, mechanism of action, previous literature, etc) or not according to the available information on the patient, the event and the evaluation of any possible alternative causes. For documentation of causality, the WHO causality parameters will be used to classify causality as likely, possible, unlikely and unrelated.

The proportion of patients experiencing at least one AE and those experiencing at least one serious AE will be summarised by treatment arm. The number and percentage of each will be presented descriptively and will be compared between the two treatment arms. Only those considered treatment-related will be included in the analysis.

Development and validation of a prediction model for 6-month early death in pediatric H3K27M-mutant diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG)
Authors: Long Wang, Xiaohao Liu, Yanan You, Yue Yu, Dandan Wang, Jiayao Xu, Boya Zhang, Juan Wang, Yingjuan Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundH3K27M-mutant diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (H3K27M-DIPG) is among pediatric brain tumors with the poorest prognosis. Early identification of high-risk patients may facilitate timely, individualized clinical management. This study aimed to identify independent predictors of early death (ED), defined as death within 6 months of diagnosis, in pediatric patients with H3K27M-DIPG and to develop and validate a clinical prediction model to estimate individualized ED risk in this population.MethodsWe retrospectively collected clinical data from 147 pediatric patients in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER), including sex, race, tumor grade, surgical approach, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, tumor size, age, and time from diagnosis to treatment. Death within 6 months of diagnosis was used as the primary outcome. To minimize the risk of omitting critical confounders while preventing overfitting, variables with P<0.15 in the univariable analysis were selected for multivariable logistic regression to construct a streamlined nomogram. The discriminatory ability of the model was evaluated by the area under the curve (AUC). Internal validation was performed using 500 bootstrap resamples to calculate the optimism-corrected C-index and verify model stability.ResultsFollowing the univariable screening, four variables (radiotherapy, chemotherapy, time to treatment, and tumor grade) were incorporated into the multivariable model. Multivariable logistic regression analysis showed that non-receipt of radiotherapy was the sole independent risk factor for ED [odds ratio (OR) =8.00, 95% confidence interval (CI): 2.26–28.30, P=0.001]. The developed nomogram demonstrated excellent discrimination, and the apparent C-index (AUC) was 0.732 (95% CI: 0.607–0.854). After internal validation with 500 bootstrap resamples, the optimism-corrected C-index remained stable at 0.702.ConclusionsThis study successfully developed and internally validated a streamlined 4-variable prediction model to estimate the risk of 6-month ED in children with H3K27M-DIPG. Radiotherapy status emerged as the singular most critical factor associated with ED. This model may assist clinicians in early identification of high-risk patients, facilitate transparent clinician-family communication, and support individualized treatment planning and counseling.

Key findings

• This study developed a streamlined 4-variable prediction model for pediatric H3K27M-mutant diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (H3K27M-DIPG) and found that non-receipt of radiotherapy was the only independent risk factor for early death within 6 months [odds ratio (OR) =8.00].

What is known and what is new?

• It is well established that pediatric H3K27M-DIPG carries a dismal prognosis and that radiotherapy remains the standard palliative treatment; however, tools to predict rapid clinical deterioration are limited.

• This study addresses this gap by constructing and validating a novel nomogram based on Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database data to predict 6-month early death and identified non-receipt of radiotherapy as the sole independent risk factor (OR =8.00). Notably, while clinical factors such as chemotherapy, time to treatment, and tumor grade were incorporated to adjust for confounding effects, they lacked independent predictive significance. These findings provide clinicians with a practical tool for early risk stratification, underscoring the decisive role of radiotherapy in preventing early mortality.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This study underscores the decisive role of radiotherapy in extending short-term survival for pediatric H3K27M-DIPG patients, while exposing the limitations of other conventional interventions (e.g., chemotherapy) in preventing early death. Clinicians should establish radiotherapy as the cornerstone of treatment and utilize the constructed nomogram to assist in early high-risk identification and transparent patient counseling, while future research should prioritize novel molecularly targeted and immunotherapeutic strategies to overcome current therapeutic limitations.

Introduction

H3K27M-mutant diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (H3K27M-DIPG) is a subgroup with the worst prognosis among diffuse midline gliomas, H3K27-altered type—that is, tumors that occur in the pons and carry the H3K27M mutation. H3K27M-DIPG is the most common and most aggressive subtype of pediatric brainstem gliomas, accounting for approximately 75–80% of all pediatric brainstem tumors (1). The clinical manifestations of this disease include rapidly progressive cranial nerve dysfunction, long tract signs, and ataxia, and it predominantly affects children aged 5–10 years. Owing to the tumor’s pontine location, treatment options are greatly restricted, and it is almost impossible to perform total surgical resection. Therefore, radiotherapy has become the standard treatment. However, its efficacy is limited: the median survival is only 9–12 months, and the 5-year survival rate is less than 1% (1,2). The extremely poor prognosis of H3K27M-DIPG poses challenges to the individualized treatment of pediatric brain tumors and the design of clinical trials, highlighting the urgent need for reliable prognostic prediction tools.

Although the overall prognosis of H3K27M-DIPG is poor, some patients may have a longer survival time. In recent years, the prognostic heterogeneity has gradually attracted attention. A study by Jansen et al. found that age ≤3 years, a longer duration of symptoms before diagnosis, and the use of combination chemotherapy are positive prognostic factors, while ring enhancement on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) indicates a poor prognosis (1). Under the same diagnostic framework, there may be significant differences in the disease progression rate among patients, which provides an important theoretical basis for developing a prognostic prediction model. A clinical trial by Veldhuijzen van Zanten et al. divided patients into intermediate-risk and high-risk groups. The median progression-free survival and overall survival of patients in the intermediate-risk group were 6.4 and 12.4 months, respectively, while those in the high-risk group were 4.5 and 8.1 months, respectively, further confirming the importance of risk stratification (2). Developing accurate prediction tools can help us identify the patient groups that would benefit from specific interventions, such as expedited enrollment into molecularly targeted clinical trials and early integration of palliative care.

Current prognostic assessment for H3K27M-DIPG relies primarily on clinical and imaging features. Age at diagnosis, symptom duration, and neurologic functional status are important clinical indicators. A longer duration of symptoms usually indicates a better prognosis, possibly reflecting slower tumor growth and relatively indolent biological behavior (1). Imaging features such as tumor size, enhancement pattern, necrosis, and hemorrhage also provide important prognostic information. For example, in the prediction model constructed by Jansen et al., the imaging sign of ring enhancement on MRI indicates a poor prognosis (1). In addition to clinical and imaging features, the key factors for prognosis evaluation also extend to the molecular features of the tumor. A study by Wang et al. showed that in adult patients, the H3K27M mutation is associated with a poor prognosis (median overall survival of only 11.4 months), while patients with the isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutation have a relatively better prognosis (3), providing an important reference for exploring the molecular mechanism of pediatric H3K27M-DIPG.

The impact of different treatment regimens on the prognosis of H3K27M-DIPG remains controversial. Although radiotherapy can relieve neurological symptoms and prolong survival, its effect is limited. A study by Janssens et al. showed that re-irradiation of patients who progressed after initial radiotherapy can bring significant survival benefits, with a median overall survival of 13.7 months, while that of those who did not receive re-irradiation was only 10.3 months (4). Regarding the efficacy of chemotherapy, the current conclusions are not unified. Although a study by Huang et al. suggested that chemotherapy may improve cancer-specific survival [hazard ratio (HR) =0.57] (5), a study by Liu et al. showed that adjuvant therapy did not significantly improve the survival outcomes of patients with low-grade brainstem gliomas (6). These contradictory results may be due to differences in patient selection, treatment regimens, and tumor biological features.

Logistic regression and nomograms are commonly used in clinical prediction research. Logistic regression identifies independent predictors for binary outcomes (e.g., death within 6 months), while nomograms translate multivariable models into intuitive, clinically usable risk scores. Liu et al. constructed a nomogram for low-grade brainstem glioma with an internally validated C-index of 0.87, indicating good predictive performance (6). Huang et al. also created a nomogram to predict prognosis in high-grade brainstem glioma (5). These studies provide methodological support for integrating clinical and imaging variables to develop prediction tools for H3K27M-DIPG.

Because most patients with H3K27M-DIPG experience disease progression or die within 6 months, this time point holds significant clinical value. Although several prognostic models have been proposed for brainstem tumors, prediction research specifically targeting 6-month early death (ED) in pediatric H3K27M-DIPG remains limited. To address this gap, this study analyzed multiple predictive factors such as sex, race, tumor grade, surgical approach, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, tumor size, age, and time from diagnosis to treatment, and developed and internally validated a logistic regression model and a nomogram for predicting 6-month ED in pediatric patients with H3K27M-DIPG. This model will assist clinicians in identifying high-risk patients, accurately predicting patients’ ED risk, timely adjusting treatment strategies, and improving patient care. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0265/rc).

Methods

Patients data

The data were sourced from the “Incidence-SEER Research Data, 17 Registries, Nov 2024 Sub (2000-2022)” dataset released by the “Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results” (SEER) program of the US National Cancer Institute. The SEER*Stat software (version 9.0.42.0) was used to extract data of patients pathologically or clinically diagnosed with H3K27M-DIPG who met the inclusion criteria of this study from this dataset, totaling 147 cases. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) diagnosed with H3K27M-DIPG by histopathology or typical imaging features; (II) age <18 years; (III) availability of complete follow-up data; and (IV) follow-up duration of no less than 6 months with a clear death status.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) non-H3K27M-DIPG pathological types or unclear diagnoses; (II) missing or unverifiable key clinical data; (III) follow-up time of less than 6 months with an unclear status of the endpoint event (death); (IV) coexisting other serious diseases or a clear cause of death not directly related to the tumor.

Statistical analysis

A stratified analysis of patients’ baseline characteristics was conducted based on the outcome (ED). Continuous variables conforming to a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed variables were expressed as median (interquartile range). In the inter-group comparison, the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables, and the Welch t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables.

To identify potential predictors for 6-month ED while minimizing the risk of omitting critical confounding factors, a univariable logistic regression analysis was initially performed. Candidate variables with a P value <0.15 in the univariable analysis were subsequently incorporated into a multivariable logistic regression model to identify independent prognostic variables and construct the nomogram.

The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to evaluate the discriminatory ability of the model, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated, with a value ranging from 0.5 (no discriminatory ability) to 1.0 (perfect discrimination). Internal validation was performed using 500 bootstrap resamples, and the optimism-corrected C-index was calculated. Furthermore, the calibration curve was used to assess the consistency between the predicted probability and the actual observed results, and decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to quantify the clinical net benefit of the model at different decision thresholds.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1), and a two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In terms of demographic characteristics, the overall cohort was mainly composed of females (87 cases, 59.2%), children aged 3–10 years (106 cases, 72.1%), and White (101 cases, 68.7%). After comparison between groups for the three variables of sex, age, and race distribution, the results showed no significant differences between the two groups (P values were 0.243, 0.907, and 0.476 respectively). In terms of tumor characteristics: the tumor grade was unknown for most patients (68.7%), and 49.7% of patients had a tumor size ≥40 mm. In terms of treatment, most patients did not receive surgical treatment (71.4%), and there was no significant difference in the surgical methods between the two groups (P=0.37). There were also no statistical differences between the two groups in terms of chemotherapy status (46.5% in the non-ED group vs. 39.1% in the ED group, P=0.40) and the proportion of patients who started treatment within 28 days after diagnosis (87.1% in the non-ED group vs. 89.1% in the ED group, P=0.73). However, there was a significant difference in the distribution of whether patients received radiotherapy between the two groups (P<0.001): the proportions of patients who received radiotherapy in the non-ED group and the ED group were 98.0% and 71.7% respectively; the proportion of patients who did not receive any radiotherapy in the ED group was significantly higher than that in the non-ED group (28.3% vs. 2.0%).

In conclusion, among all the baseline characteristics included in the analysis, radiotherapy status was the only factor significantly associated with 6-month ED.

Feature selection and model development

To identify potential predictors for 6-month ED, we first conducted univariable logistic regression analysis for all candidate variables. Following established methodological guidelines for clinical prediction models, a relaxed threshold of P<0.15 was applied to minimize the risk of missing critical confounding factors. The results showed that radiotherapy (P<0.001), chemotherapy (P=0.01), time to treatment (P=0.11), and tumor grade (P=0.13) met this prespecified criterion, whereas other variables including sex, race, surgery type, tumor size, and age were not statistically significant (P>0.15).

Subsequently, these four selected features were incorporated into a multivariable logistic regression model. The multivariable analysis confirmed that non-receipt of radiotherapy remained the sole independent risk factor for 6-month ED [odds ratio (OR) =8.00, 95% confidence interval (CI): 2.26–28.30, P=0.001]. Although chemotherapy, time to treatment, and tumor grade lost their independent statistical significance in the multivariable setting, they were retained in the final model to adjust for confounding effects and construct a comprehensive individualized risk assessment tool (Figure 1, forest plot). Model performance and internal validation

To construct a practical clinical tool, a nomogram integrating the four selected variables (radiotherapy, chemotherapy, time to treatment, and tumor grade) was developed to estimate the individualized probability of 6-month ED (Figure 2).

Nomogram for predicting the individualized probability of 6-month early death. To use the nomogram, locate the patient’s category on each variable axis, draw a vertical line upward to the “points” axis to determine the score for each variable, sum all the scores, and draw a vertical line downward from the “total points” axis to determine the estimated probability of early death. DIPG, diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma.

The predictive performance of the model was comprehensively evaluated. In terms of discrimination, the model demonstrated robust ability to distinguish patients with ED from those without, yielding an apparent C-index (AUC) of 0.732 (95% CI: 0.607–0.854) (Figure 3A). To rigorously assess the model’s stability and penalize potential overfitting, an internal validation was performed using 500 bootstrap resamples. The optimism-corrected C-index remained robust at 0.702, indicating satisfactory generalizability and effectively proving that the model was not severely overfitted.

Furthermore, the calibration curve visually demonstrated a high degree of consistency between the ED probabilities predicted by the model and the actual observed outcomes, with the apparent curve aligning closely with the ideal reference line (Figure 3B).

To assess the practical value of the nomogram in clinical decision-making, DCA was performed (Figure 4). The DCA curve illustrated that utilizing the proposed 4-variable model to predict 6-month ED provides a superior net clinical benefit across a wide and practical range of threshold probabilities compared to the “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies. This highlights the model’s potential to effectively assist clinicians in early risk stratification and individualized care planning.

Discussion

This study developed and internally validated a practical prediction model for 6-month ED in children with H3K27M-DIPG. By refining the variable selection to optimize model stability, our multivariable logistic regression confirmed that non-receipt of radiotherapy was the sole independent risk factor for 6-month ED (OR =8.00, 95% CI: 2.26–28.30, P=0.001). To provide a comprehensive risk assessment tool, a nomogram integrating radiotherapy status, chemotherapy, time to treatment, and tumor grade was constructed. The developed model demonstrated excellent discriminative ability, yielding an apparent C-index of 0.732. More importantly, after rigorous internal validation using 500 bootstrap resamples to penalize potential overfitting, the optimism-corrected C-index remained robust at 0.702. These findings not only underscore the irreplaceable role of timely radiotherapy in the early management of H3K27M-DIPG but also provide clinicians with a highly stable and individualized risk stratification tool to guide clinical decision-making.

Radiotherapy remains the indispensable cornerstone of palliative care for H3K27M-DIPG. Our finding that non-receipt of radiotherapy drastically increases the risk of 6-month ED by 8-fold firmly aligns with this consensus. While the traditional view holds that H3K27M-DIPG exhibits only a temporary clinical response to radiation, this transient window is highly critical for averting rapid, explosive disease progression. The profound short-term protective effect observed in our cohort is primarily driven by radiation-induced inhibition of tumor proliferation and activation of apoptosis. At the molecular level, the sensitivity of these highly aggressive glioma cells to ionizing radiation is strictly regulated by complex signaling networks, with the PI3K/AKT pathway playing a notably pivotal role (7,8). Furthermore, emerging evidence suggests that the benefits of radiotherapy extend beyond direct cytotoxicity to the remodeling of the tumor microenvironment (9). For instance, radiation can induce shifts in the polarization state of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), thereby altering local immune responses and potentially decelerating the aggressive local invasion characteristic of DIPG (10). Collectively, these multifaceted mechanisms underscore the decisive early survival advantage conferred by timely radiotherapy.

Beyond radiotherapy, the prognostic values of other clinical interventions and sociodemographic factors remain highly debated. In our univariable screening, surgical intervention and racial background did not show statistical significance (P>0.15) and were thus excluded from the final multivariable model. Although surgical biopsy is increasingly advocated to secure molecular diagnostic information for targeted therapy, our data implies that surgical intervention itself does not alter the extremely early mortality trajectory of H3K27M-DIPG, likely due to the delicate anatomical constraints of the brainstem and a complex surgical risk-benefit ratio (11). Furthermore, the insignificance of race highlights that intrinsic tumor biology heavily outweighs sociodemographic characteristics in dictating fatal outcomes, aligning with precision medicine paradigms (12). Interestingly, although chemotherapy showed an association with ED in univariable analysis, it completely lost its independent predictive value after multivariable adjustment (OR =0.48, 95% CI: 0.17–1.37, P=0.17). This lack of short-term benefit is highly consistent with the outcomes of pivotal clinical trials, including the Children’s Oncology Group ACNS0126 trial and institutional studies from St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital, which have repeatedly documented the failure of conventional chemotherapy in DIPG. This likely reflects the intact blood-brain barrier impeding drug delivery and the pronounced intrinsic chemoresistance typical of H3K27M mutations (13-15). Perhaps the most intriguing finding from our model is regarding the time from diagnosis to treatment. A delayed time to treatment (>28 days) did not emerge as a risk factor and even displayed a non-significant protective trend (OR =0.20, 95% CI: 0.02–1.68, P=0.14). Rather than implying that delayed treatment is beneficial, this counterintuitive phenomenon classically represents confounding by indication. Patients presenting with explosive, rapidly deteriorating neurological deficits are typically triaged to receive urgent, immediate interventions (≤28 days) yet still succumb early to the overwhelmingly aggressive disease biology. Conversely, patients who are clinically stable enough to afford a treatment delay of over one month may inherently harbor slightly more indolent tumor phenotypes.

The performance evaluation of our refined 4-variable model yielded an apparent C-index (AUC) of 0.732, indicating good discriminative ability that is comparable to or exceeds several existing neuro-oncology prediction models (16,17). Crucially, after rigorous internal validation using 500 bootstrap resamples, the optimism-corrected C-index remained highly stable at 0.702. Unlike predictive models that suffer from severe overfitting and massive performance drops due to the inclusion of excessive variables—a common pitfall in rare disease studies with small sample sizes (18)—our streamlined model demonstrated excellent generalizability and minimal optimism bias. Furthermore, the DCA confirmed that employing this model yields a superior net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities compared to empirical “treat-all” or “treat-none” approaches. Importantly, the constructed nomogram translates these complex statistical weights into an intuitive, point-based visual tool, facilitating seamless clinician-family communication regarding prognosis and shared treatment decisions (19).

The clinical significance of this study is primarily reflected in three aspects. First, it unequivocally reaffirms radiotherapy as the indispensable standard of care for H3K27M-DIPG, emphasizing that it should be urgently pursued to prevent rapid, early mortality, even in clinically vulnerable patients (20). Second, our refined prediction model accurately stratifies patients, allowing clinicians to identify high-risk cohorts who might require enhanced supportive care or expedited enrollment into novel clinical trials (21). Finally, the nomogram serves as an accessible, individualized prognostic tool that facilitates shared clinical decision-making and transparent expectation management with patients’ families (22). In a devastating disease like H3K27M-DIPG, acquiring accurate prognostic information is of paramount importance for empathetic palliative care planning and optimal resource allocation (23).

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, despite meeting the rigorous statistical thresholds [events per variable (EPV) >10] for our streamlined 4-variable model, the inherently small sample size of this rare cohort may still limit the power to detect predictors with modest effects (24). Second, the retrospective nature of the SEER data introduces unavoidable selection and information biases; although multivariable adjustment was meticulously performed, unmeasured residual confounding may persist (25). Third, while our model demonstrated robust stability through 500 bootstrap resamples, independent multicenter external validation remains imperative to fully verify its generalizability across diverse clinical settings (26). Furthermore, the current database lacks comprehensive molecular biomarker profiling (e.g., MGMT promoter methylation, TP53, or ATRX status), the inclusion of which could potentially further optimize predictive accuracy (13,27). Finally, non-random treatment assignment inherently introduces confounding by indication —as previously highlighted regarding the timing of treatment initiation—where the most severely deteriorating cases might be triaged to immediate therapies or conversely deemed too frail for aggressive intervention, thereby skewing observational survival metrics (28).

To overcome these inherent limitations, future research endeavors should advance along multiple parallel fronts. First, prospective, large-scale, multicenter cohort studies are essential to provide rigorous external validation and further solidify the model’s generalizability (29). Second, integrating high-throughput molecular markers, such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression dynamics and intricate tumor microenvironment profiling, could significantly refine predictive accuracy (30,31). Furthermore, the integration of radiomics and artificial intelligence stands to offer unprecedented predictive value, enabling non-invasive evaluation of tumor spatiotemporal heterogeneity and early therapeutic responses through advanced MRI texture analysis (32). Therapeutically, exploiting molecular vulnerabilities via targeted agents against specific signaling pathways (7,33) and pioneering immunotherapeutic approaches—particularly the synergistic combination of chimeric antigen receptor macrophage (CAR-M) cell therapy or immune checkpoint inhibitors with standard modalities—hold immense promise for breaking current clinical bottlenecks (10,34). Notably, the first-in-class imipridone dordaviprone (ONC201) has recently emerged as a major precision medicine breakthrough, demonstrating profound clinical efficacy specifically in patients with H3K27M-mutant diffuse midline gliomas (35). Crucially, future prognostic frameworks must transcend mere survival metrics by incorporating patient-reported outcomes (PROs) and rigorous quality-of-life assessments to capture the holistic impact of treatments (36,37).

Methodologically, subsequent studies could leverage more sophisticated analytical architectures, such as deep machine learning algorithms and time-dependent ROC analyses, to effectively unravel complex, high-dimensional longitudinal data (17,26). Concurrently, establishing standardized international data registries with rigorous, long-term follow-up protocols is vital for fostering global data comparability and collaborative prognostic evaluation (38). From a translational perspective, digitizing the nomogram into web-based calculators or mobile health (mHealth) applications would drastically enhance its point-of-care accessibility and clinical adoption (39).

Overall, by developing and rigorously internally validating a 6-month ED prediction model for pediatric H3K27M-DIPG, this study delivers a practical, evidence-based tool for managing this devastating disease. Our robust findings unequivocally reaffirm the pivotal role of radiotherapy as a lifeline against rapid early mortality and provide a reliable framework for individualized risk stratification. Despite its limitations, this work establishes a solid foundation for refining future predictive models and tailoring aggressive therapeutic strategies. As breakthroughs in disease biology and analytical methodologies continue to unfold, we anticipate that the prognostic management of DIPG will fundamentally improve (40,41). Ultimately, our collective goal remains to forge truly individualized, precision therapies that not only extend survival but also safeguard the quality of life, offering enduring hope to affected children and their families (42,43).

Conclusions

This study successfully developed and internally validated a streamlined, 4-variable prediction model to estimate the individualized risk of 6-month ED in pediatric patients with H3K27M-DIPG. Our findings unequivocally identify the non-receipt of radiotherapy as the singular most critical driver of rapid early mortality. The proposed nomogram offers an accessible and reliable tool to assist clinicians in early risk stratification, facilitating transparent clinician-family communication and guiding shared, individualized treatment decisions. Nevertheless, future prospective multicenter studies with external validation and comprehensive molecular profiling are warranted to further refine its clinical utility.

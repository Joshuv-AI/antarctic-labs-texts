A computational framework for optimizing radioiodine therapy protocols in metastatic thyroid cancer
Authors: Marie Fusella Giuntini, Cyril Voyant, David Taieb, Dominique Barbolosi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Thyroid cancer incidence is increasing, and radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy remains essential for the management of metastatic disease. However, marked inter-patient variability makes protocol selection challenging, particularly when balancing therapeutic efficacy against radioiodine induced toxicity. In this context, digital decision support tools can help explore alternative treatment strategies and support longitudinal monitoring. This study investigates the influence of key RAI protocol parameters (the number of treatment sessions (n), the interval between sessions (Δ t), and the administered activity per session (A)) on therapeutic response using a validated mechanistic compartmental model. A sensitivity analysis is performed to quantify how these parameters affect serum thyroglobulin (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document}) kinetics, with particular emphasis on tumor cell doubling time (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document}) under RAI exposure as a discriminating factor between responder and non-responder profiles. Based on this framework, a standalone freeware simulator (RAIR-Sim, RAItherapy Response Simulator) is developed to simulate protocol dependent \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} trajectories from patient specific inputs and to support exploratory protocol planning. Future developments will focus on improving patient specific parameter estimation (including supervised learning strategies to update parameters at each session and account for their time variation) to further enhance individualized simulations.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer is the most common type of endocrine cancer, and its occurrence continues to increase. Radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy is an important treatment for thyroid cancer, especially when the cancer has spread to other parts of the body. RAI helps destroy remaining thyroid tissue and distant cancer cells, improving long-term outcomes1,2. However, the success of RAI therapy depends on giving the right dose for each patient. Too much RAI can cause side effects like salivary gland problems3, while too little RAI may reduce the chances of successful treatment4. Therefore, calculating the correct dose based on each patient’s specific needs is very important5. New digital tools and technologies are helping improve thyroid cancer treatment by making it more personalized. These tools help doctors adjust RAI therapy to better match each patient needs, making the treatment more accurate and effective6,7. Despite these advances, there is still a lack of digital tools that can be easily used in everyday clinical practice. Filling this gap is necessary to provide fully personalized RAI therapy and improve patient outcomes8. Solving this issue will help doctors balance treatment effectiveness and safety when managing thyroid cancer. A new clinical era, incorporating prediction techniques that measure the a priori impact of RAI on thyroglobulin (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document}) levels, must be integrated into the treatment of metastatic thyroid cancer. These technologies, guided by computational algorithms, mathematical models, or artificial intelligence, may provide physicians with a way to precisely design therapies. The goal is to leverage each patient’s unique attributes9–13. The most significant challenge lies in striking a balance between toxicity and effectiveness. This balance can be achieved by reducing the number of fractions (n), increasing the time between fractions (Δ t), or lowering dosages (A) to minimize toxicity in responders to RAI. For non-responders, improving treatment efficacy within regulatory bounds remains the primary objective. Since a non-responder may simply require extremely high dosages to become a responder, the essential question is whether to attempt another therapy sooner or to increase the administered dose14,15. An operational interface, developed as part of this study, simulates treatment responses and can guide clinicians toward personalized strategies. In clinical practice, dynamic \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} based indicators have also been proposed to support therapeutic decision making. In particular, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} doubling time has been used to stratify patients into prognostic categories, as reported by Wassermann et al.16, who classified patients into four groups based on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} kinetics (< 1 year, 1–3 years, > 3 years, and undetectable \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document}). Such approaches provide clinically accessible information and have demonstrated prognostic value. However, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} based metrics remain indirect biomarkers influenced by residual tumor burden, secretion dynamics, and measurement variability, and do not directly reflect tumor cell proliferation or treatment induced kinetic changes. This limitation motivates the use of mechanistically grounded parameters that explicitly describe tumor cell dynamics under RAI exposure. As research advances, the tool proposed here and named RAIR-Sim (RAItherapy Response Simulator), will continue to evolve, offering increasingly refined solutions in the pursuit of precision medicine. In the rest of this paper, the results related to this software will be presented.

Materials and methods

The management of thyroid cancer with RAI therapy requires understanding the interplay between treatment parameters and patient-specific factors. Empirical studies provide insights, but these are often limited by patient variability and the biological complexity of the disease. To address these challenges, this study introduces a mathematical formalism that integrates clinical data and biological principles. The model predicts treatment outcomes, simulates tumor regression, and tracks \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document} levels under different protocol scenarios, offering a computational framework for protocol comparison and exploration. This approach considers key factors such as patient characteristics, RAI dosages, and biological responses. This quantitative system aims to support treatment planning, with the long-term objective of improving therapeutic precision. This study analyzes retrospective, fully anonymized clinical data from patients treated at AP-HM (Assistance Publique—Hôpitaux de Marseille, France). Under French regulations, institutional review board (IRB) approval is not required for retrospective studies using anonymized data without patient intervention. Data extraction spanned 2014–2016, with research access granted on June 10, 2024. All procedures comply with GDPR (EU 2016/679) and French data protection laws. Patient confidentiality was strictly maintained, and no identifiable information was included in analyses.

The compartmental model

A classical mathematical model based on 3 differential equations has been developed to predict responses to RAI therapy, showing strong alignment with clinical observations17. The model is based on three key variables and several parameters are essential to the model. Detailed information about the compartmental model (variables, parameters and equations) and the study sample, which includes 50 patients, and a thorough comparison of empirical clinical data with the model predictions are provided in Appendices A and B . The parameter estimates for a, ρ, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document}, λ, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k_e$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N_0$$\end{document} were obtained using the MCMC-SAEM algorithm18 implemented in the Monolix® (Lixoft, Antony, France) software19. Initial findings derived from this model indicate that the doubling time of tumor cells emerges as a predictor of response17. To account explicitly for treatment fractionation, the radioiodine therapy protocol is defined by three parameters: the number of sessions (fractions) \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n \in \mathbb {N}^*$$\end{document}, the constant time interval between two consecutive sessions Δ t > 0 (in months), and the administered activity per session \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A_{\textrm{inj}}$$\end{document} (in GBq). The times of administration are defined as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_k = (k-1)\,\Delta t, \, k = 1,\dots ,n$$\end{document}. Between two consecutive administrations, i.e. for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t \in (t_k,\, t_{k+1})$$\end{document}, the system follows the continuous three-compartment dynamics (see Appendices A for details). The administration of radioiodine at each session is modeled as an instantaneous input on the activity compartment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A(t_k^+) = A(t_k^-) + A_{\textrm{inj}}$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_k^-$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_k^+$$\end{document} denote the instants immediately before and immediately after the k-th radioiodine administration. At injection times, tumor burden (N) and thyroglobulin concentration (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_g$$\end{document}) remain continuous \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N(t_k^+) = N(t_k^-), \, T_g(t_k^+) = T_g(t_k^-)$$\end{document}. Starting from the initial condition \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(A_0, N_0, T_{g0})$$\end{document}, the protocol \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(n,\Delta t,A_{\textrm{inj}})$$\end{document} is implemented by iterating this scheme over k=1,… ,n. Between two consecutive treatment sessions, the system of differential equations is solved analytically in closed form under the assumption of constant parameters, rather than by numerical time discretization20. Practical implementation of the therapeutic response simulator

To facilitate the assessment of the impact of different RAI treatment protocols, a stand-alone MATLAB® (The MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) based interface was developed (illustrated in Fig. 1). The simulator relies on representative tumor cell doubling times derived from population level analyses to explore treatment protocol scenarios corresponding to each profile (responder or not). This straightforward yet effective approach improves usability and accessibility for clinical practice21. By simultaneously accounting for the number of fractions n, the administered activity A, and the interval between fractions Δ t, the freeware enables the prediction of the temporal evolution of thyroglobulin concentration over the following months. Based on these predictions, treatment protocols can be adapted and customized to individual patient profiles. The interface, named RAIR-Sim, relies on several MATLAB toolboxes dedicated to scientific computing, allowing the analytical resolution of the compartmental model between treatment sessions without numerical time discretization. Although alternative platforms exist, MathWorks products remain widely used in scientific and biomedical research22. The MATLAB environment facilitates the implementation of complex algorithms and supports efficient computation and analysis workflows23. Data visualization is enhanced through MATLAB graphical capabilities, which are particularly valuable in a clinical context24. The generated graphical outputs provide intuitive representations of simulated therapeutic responses, thereby supporting informed clinical decision making25. For consistency across all simulations presented in this study, the initial thyroglobulin concentration was fixed at 100 ng/mL. The MATLAB source codes associated with RAIR-Sim are publicly available on Github at the following address: https://github.com/MarieFG49/CTD_SIMU_RAI. Simulations were performed using MATLAB with 96 workers and the Symbolic Math Toolbox on a high-performance computing cluster composed of 28 Intel nodes (2 × Xeon 6230R processors with 26 cores at 2.1 GHz and 192 GB DDR4 RAM). All simulation runs were completed in less than 30 seconds.

Our contribution

Several modeling approaches have been proposed to describe the response of metastatic differentiated thyroid cancer to radioactive iodine therapy, primarily with the aim of understanding tumor and biomarker kinetics. Among these, the compartmental model introduced by Barbolosi et al. 17 demonstrated that tumor cell doubling time under RAI exposure (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document}) is a key discriminating parameter between responders and non-responders, and provided a robust mechanistic description of thyroglobulin dynamics validated against clinical data. The present study extends this framework by shifting the focus from model identification to treatment protocol design. While Barbolosi et al. considered a fixed therapeutic regimen to characterize patient response, the added value of the present work lies in explicitly integrating the radioiodine therapy protocol into the modeling process. The number of administrations (n), the interval between sessions (Δ t), and the administered activity per session (A) are treated as controllable variables, allowing their individual impact on therapeutic response to be explored within a unified mechanistic framework. A second contribution concerns the clinical interpretation of tumor kinetics. The analysis explicitly distinguishes tumor cell doubling time under RAI exposure from thyroglobulin based doubling times commonly reported in clinical practice. By focusing on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document} under treatment, the proposed approach provides a mechanistically meaningful parameter for individualized dose-response analysis and avoids biases associated with indirect biomarkers. From a practical perspective, these methodological developments are translated into a simulation-based decision support tool, RAIR-Sim. By combining patient specific model parameters with adjustable protocol parameters, the simulator enables clinicians to explore alternative treatment strategies and to visualize their impact on thyroglobulin evolution. For responding patients, the tool supports the identification of less aggressive protocols that maintain efficacy while reducing toxicity. For non-responders, it allows the exploration of intensified or restructured protocols within regulatory constraints. In this way, the present work bridges a validated mathematical model with a clinically usable interface, providing a concrete contribution toward model assisted personalization of radioiodine therapy.

Personalized therapeutic response simulator

To provide a decision support tool, a graphical interface was developed for the therapeutic response simulator using MATLAB software (RAIR-Sim). This interface allows the user to input the three protocol parameters, the tumor cell doubling time (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document}), and the average model parameters estimated with Monolix software using the SAEM algorithm18. The interface is designed to evolve with new results based on predefined objectives and the needs of clinicians, with the long-term aim of transitioning toward an operational decision-making tool. It enables clinicians to simulate personalized and optimized treatment protocols. The functionality of this interface is demonstrated through two simulations addressing the outlined objectives. The focus of this study is on patients with metastatic differentiated thyroid cancer, classified as high-risk by the European Thyroid Association4. The standard therapeutic protocol recommends an administered activity of 3.7 GBq.

Conclusion

Radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy plays an important role in the treatment of high-risk thyroid cancers with metastases. However, its widespread application presents challenges, including potential side effects from overdoses and the existence of iodine refractory patients. These challenges highlight the need for tools that can optimize the balance between therapeutic efficacy and toxicity.

Key findings

In this study, the impact of clinically adjustable treatment protocol parameters on therapeutic response was investigated within a validated mechanistic modeling framework. Building upon the compartmental model introduced by Barbolosi et al., which demonstrated the discriminating role of tumor cell doubling time under RAI exposure (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document}), the present work extends the scope from response characterization to protocol exploration. The number of administrations, the interval between sessions, and the administered activity per session are explicitly integrated as controllable variables, allowing their individual influence on thyroglobulin kinetics to be quantified. The results highlight distinct strategies for reducing toxicity in responding patients and for improving efficacy in refractory profiles. These methodological developments are translated into a preclinical decision-support simulator, RAIR-Sim, which enables visualization and comparison of protocol dependent therapeutic responses. By linking patient specific parameters to protocol design, the tool provides clinicians with a structured framework to explore alternative treatment strategies and to support personalized reasoning in RAI therapy planning. Currently, the simulator allows the patient responder or non-responder status to be configured based on the mean \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document} of each subgroup: responder setting with average \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document} = 66.6 months or non-responder setting with average \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document} = 9.8 months. However, the important next step toward full clinical deployment lies in determining patient-specific \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_d$$\end{document} values prior to treatment, followed by iterative refinement after each session. An advancement that would elevate RAIR-Sim from its current exploratory status (TRL (Technology Readiness Level) 3–4) to a high-readiness clinical decision tool (TRL 7–8).

Current limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the simulator currently assumes a constant administered activity per session and a constant inter-session interval throughout the treatment course. This simplification does not capture adaptive dosing or scheduling strategies that may be applied in clinical practice in response to patient evolution or logistical constraints. In addition, biological model parameters are assumed to remain constant during treatment, and interactions between protocol parameters are not considered. The analysis is therefore restricted to independent variations of protocol components, and uncertainty propagation is not addressed. Moreover, the results presented in this study are illustrated using representative tumor cell doubling times for responder and non-responding patients subgroups, rather than fully individualized parameter sets for each patient. Consequently, the current version of RAIR-Sim should be regarded as a preclinical decision support and exploration tool, intended to support hypothesis testing and protocol design rather than direct clinical prescription.

Perspectives and future developments for clinical readiness

Future developments will aim to enhance clinical realism and methodological robustness. Planned extensions include allowing variable administered activities and non-uniform inter-session intervals, enabling the simulation of adaptive treatment strategies more consistent with clinical practice. In parallel, particular emphasis will be placed on the dynamic characterization of patient specific biological parameters throughout the treatment course. In this context, supervised machine learning approaches will be investigated to estimate, at each treatment session, the individual model parameters governing tumor and biomarker dynamics. By leveraging longitudinal clinical data collected during successive RAI administrations, such approaches will allow continuous updating of patient specific parameters and explicit modeling of their non-stationarity over time. This evolution is expected to capture treatment induced changes in tumor behavior, iodine uptake, and radiosensitivity that cannot be represented by fixed parameter models. Further developments will address parameter interactions, uncertainty quantification, and the integration of additional patient specific information. In this perspective, RAIR-Sim is intended to evolve into a fully personalized and adaptive decision-support platform, combining mechanistic modeling with data driven learning. The present version therefore represents a foundational, preclinical step toward model-assisted personalization of radioactive iodine therapy, bridging validated mechanistic frameworks with future intelligent, patient-tailored clinical decision support.

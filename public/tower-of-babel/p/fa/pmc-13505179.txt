Sonic hedgehog reduces alveolar bone resorption by triggering the coupling of type H vessel formation and osteogenesis in a periodontitis model
Authors: Huan Zhou, Yan-Xin Qi, Shao-Yang Ma, Jin Liu, Chun-Hui Zhu, Shu-Chen Yu, Ang Li, Dan-Dan Pei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSonic hedgehog (Shh) has the dual function of promoting angiogenesis and osteogenesis, However, whether Shh treatment can accelerate alveolar bone regeneration by promoting the coupling of type H vessel formation and osteogenesis within periodontitis has not been verified.MethodsA series of angiogenesis-related assays (including cell counting kit-8, transwell migration, scratch wound healing, and tube formation) were established to explore the proliferation, migration, and tube formation abilities of endothelial cells (ECs) in response to Shh. Alizarin Red S staining and alkaline phosphatase activity assay were subsequently conducted to determine the effect of Shh on the osteogenic differentiation of periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs). Additionally, the expression levels of key factors of Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α pathway, and angiogenesis- and osteogenesis-related genes/proteins were assessed. In addition, a mouse periodontitis model was employed, along with micro-CT and immunofluorescence staining analyses, to explore the effects of Shh administration on type H vessel formation and alveolar bone regeneration.ResultsShh treatment increased the proliferation, migration, tube formation and CD31&EMCN intensity of ECs, and enhanced the osteogenic differentiation potential of PDLSCs in vitro. Mechanism studies found Shh treatment up-regulated the expression levels of Slit3, Robo4 and HIF-1α in ECs. Besides, silencing of Slit3 reserved the promoted angiogenic activity of ECs in the presence of Shh, and significantly down-regulated the expression levels of Robo4 and HIF-1α. In vivo studies verified that Shh administration markedly promoted type H vessel formation, increased the proportion of HIF-1α staining cells, and eventually attenuated bone loss within periodontitis model.ConclusionShh administration accelerates type H vessel formation by the Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling, to further alleviate alveolar bone resorption within periodontitis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-08637-4.

Introduction

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory condition that affects speaking, eating, smiling, and employment potential. Additionally, the damage to periodontal tissues caused by periodontitis is the primary cause of adult tooth loss [1]. More importantly, toxins and pathogenic factors produced by periodontal pathogens are considered important risk factors for a variety of systemic diseases, in terms of autoimmune diseases, Alzheimer’s disease, diabetes, cardiovascular disease and cancers, which severely affect human life quality [2, 3]. In this context, the prevention and treatment of periodontitis remain to be a tricky problem that urgently needs to be solved.

The ultimate objective of treating periodontitis is to achieve regeneration of periodontal tissues physiologically and functionally, and regeneration of alveolar bone is the paramount and principal issue for periodontal tissue regeneration [4]. Bone tissue is a highly vascularized tissue, and both angiogenesis and osteogenesis are indispensable components in the process of bone development, repair and remodeling [5, 6]. In general, angiogenesis occurs before osteogenesis, and new blood vessels provide oxygen, growth factors, and nutrients to osteoblast-related cells within the bone reconstruction site [7, 8]. Based on the current understanding, blood vessels in the skeletal system can be divided into subtypes at the morphological, functional, and molecular levels. Among them, type H vessels differ in structure, spatial distribution, and function from other vascular subtypes, which have a special phenotype of surface molecules, in specific, their endothelial cells (ECs) highly express CD31 and endomucin (EMCN) signals, known as CD31hiEmcnhi (type H) vessels [9].

Based on the existing research, type H vessels function in bridging angiogenesis-osteogenesis coupling during bone development, aging, and regeneration processes, due to that type H vessels collaborate with other vascular subtypes to transport oxygen, nutrients, and metabolites [10–12]. Even more important, type H vessels function in signal transduction, secreting a variety of growth factors and chemokines, and regulating the bone microenvironment, thereby mediating the coupling effect of angiogenesis and osteogenesis to participate in a variety of physiological and pathological processes, such as bone development, fracture healing, osteonecrosis, bone defect repair, osteoarthritis and osteoporosis [13–15]. Moreover, type H vessels may serve as potential diagnostic markers for bone vascular growth status and osteogenic ability, which is highly important for promoting bone regeneration [16]. Additionally, recent studies have demonstrated the presence of type H vessels in the mouse condyle and alveolar bone, and have engaged in the tooth extraction healing process, suggesting that the strategy of achieving alveolar bone regeneration via regulating type H vessels may be feasible and effective [17, 18].

The Hedgehog pathway is involved in regulating bone regeneration and repair, as well as maintaining the homeostasis of the bone microenvironment; and sonic hedgehog (Shh) is one of the most widely expressed molecules within the hedgehog family [19, 20]. As a morphogenetic hormone during development, Shh not only plays pivotal roles in embryonic development, but also participates in regulating various pathological and physiological processes, especially the angiogenesis and osteogenesis activities, based on our previous literature review and other research [21–23]. More specific, Shh treatment can enhance the osteogenic differentiation of stem cells, as evidenced by increased expression levels of osteogenic related factors such as osteocalcin (OCN), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and type I collagen, and enhanced ALP activity and matrix mineralization [24–26]. Moreover, Shh administration promoted EC proliferation, enhanced angiogenesis, upregulated the expression of angiogenic factors, including VEGF, AngI, SDF-1, and AngII, and ultimately increased blood flow and capillary/small artery density in ischemic areas (such as the cornea and hind limb ischemia) [27, 28]. However, whether Shh administration can promote alveolar bone regeneration within periodontitis by triggering the angiogenic-osteogenic cascade reaction mediated by type H vessels has not been verified.

The present study was the first to explore whether Shh administration could attenuate alveolar bone resorption by promoting type H vessel formation and osteogenesis in the periodontitis situ, with the aim of offering a promising therapeutic strategy to promote alveolar bone regeneration.

Methods and materials

Cell culture

Human umbilical vein ECs (purchased from the American Type Culture Collection; ATCC; CRL-1730) were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Invitrogen, Paisley, UK) and 1% penicillin-G/streptomycin (Cytiva, MA, USA) at 37 ℃. ECs were treated with Shh (500 ng/mL; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, USA; the concentration screening of Shh is detailed in Supplemental Fig. 1), Shh supplemented with small interfering RNA targeting slit guidance ligand 3 (si-Slit3, 50 nM; Tsingke, Beijing, China) or an equal volume of PBS, and then used for the downstream experiments.

Periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) were extracted from 9 healthy adults (18–25 years old) visited the Hospital of Stomatology, Xi’an Jiaotong University. Briefly, the obtained tissues were scraped form the tooth root surface of impacted third molars, following digested with collagenase type I (Merck, Darm-stadt, Germany), the digested tissues were incubated in α-MEM containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-G/streptomycin at 37 ℃. The culture medium was refreshed every 3 d, and cells at passage 2 were utilized for subsequent osteogenesis-related studies. The present study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Hospital of Stomatology, Xi’an Jiaotong University (the approval number: 2023-XJKQIEC-GXB-0005-001), and written informed consent was obtained from all donors.

Osteogenic induction of PDLSCs

PDLSCs were seeded and cultured in 12-well plates, and after reaching 80% confluence, the cell medium was changed to osteogenic differentiation medium (containing 50 µM ascorbic acid (AX1775, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (G9422, Sigma-Aldrich), and 100 nM dexamethasone (D4902, Sigma-Aldrich)) supplemented with Shh (500 ng/mL; MedChemExpress, ) or an equal volume of PBS. Subsequently, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and then separately subjected to alizarin red S staining (ARS, induction for 21 d) and ALP activity (induction for 7 d) analyses.

Proliferation assay

Changes in the viability of ECs in response to Shh treatment were assessed by a cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Briefly, ECs were seeded into culture plates (96-well) at a concentration of 5 × 103 cells/well. During a 5-day incubation, CCK-8 reagent (10 µL; Boster, Hubei, China) was added to each well at a fixed time each day, and after incubation for another 2 h, the optical density value (450 nm) was recorded with an Infinite M200 PRO microplate reader (Thermo, Multiskan FC, MA, USA).

Cell migration assay

The migration ability of ECs in the presence of Shh was investigated by transwell migration and scratch wound healing assays as we previously reported [29]. For the transwell migration assay, ECs (2 × 104/well) were seeded into the upper chambers of transwell plates (24-well, 8-µm pore-size filter; Corning, NY, USA) in 100 µL serum-free medium, the lower chamber contained 500 µL culture medium containing Shh or an equal volume of PBS. After a 24-h incubation, the non-migrated ECs were gently removed, and the migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, Anhui, China) and then stained with crystal violet solution (Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) for 30 min. The number of migrated cells in three randomly selected microscopic fields was counted. For the scratch wound healing assay, ECs were seeded into 12-well plates (2 × 105 cells/well) and allowed to adhere overnight. Then a sterile pipette tip (200 µL) was utilized to make a vertical scratch, the ECs were gently washed twice with PBS to remove the dislodged cells. Subsequently, images of the migrated cells were captured with a microscope at 0 and 24 h, and the results were analyzed with ImageJ software.

Tube formation assay

A tube formation assay was established to assess the network formation ability of ECs. In brief, cold Matrigel (50 µL/well; Corning, #356234) was inoculated into the plates (96-well, Corning) and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Then, the pre-treated ECs (2 × 104) were seeded in Matrigel pre-coated well. After 8 h of incubation, the images of tube-like structures were obtained, and the tube formation potential in terms of the number of junction points and total segments length was analyzed with the angiogenesis analyzer plugin of ImageJ.

qRT-PCR analysis

qRT-PCR analysis was employed to analyze gene expression. Briefly, after the obtained cell samples were washed three times with PBS, total RNA was then subsequently extracted using TRIzol Reagent (SparkJade Shandong, China) and reverse transcribed to cDNA with Evo M-MLV RT Premix for qPCR (AG11728, Accurate Biology, Hunan, China). Then, qRT-PCR analysis was conducted with a SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Tracking Kit (AG11701, Accurate Biology) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the results were analyzed with a real-time PCR detection system (Applied Biosystems, ABI 7500, MA, USA). For mRNA analysis, GAPDH was employed to normalize the levels of genes of interest. The sequences of primers used in the study are shown in Supplemental Table 1.

Western blot

The protein extracts of the cells were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). After that, the membranes were blocked in 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline containing Tween-20 (Beyotime, Shanghai, China); and incubated with the following primary antibodies: anti-GAPDH (1:250000; Proteintech, Rosemont, USA; #60004-1-lg), anti-hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α; 1:1000; Boster, #PB9253), anti-Slit3 (1:2000; Thermo, Multiskan FC, MA, USA, #PA5-104142), anti-Robo4 (1:750; Proteintech, #s20221-1-AP), anti-ALP (1:500; Santa Cruz, #sc-365765), and anti-RUNX-2 (1:1000; BOSTER, #PB0171) at 4 °C overnight. After incubating with horseradish peroxidase conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000; Proteintech, SA00001-1 or SA00001-2) for 2 h, the protein bands were detected using a chemiluminescent detection reagent (Bio-Rad, ChemiDoc, California, USA), and the results were analyzed using ImageJ.

Mouse periodontitis model and treatments

The procedures were approved by the Animal Research Ethics Committee of Xi’an jiaotong University (approval number: 2022 − 698), and the present study was carried out in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0. The mice were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center of Xi’an jiaotong University and housed in a specific pathogen-free environment, and all efforts were made to minimize animal suffering. In the present study, a mouse periodontitis model was established via ligature methods as previously reported [29, 30]. Briefly, the mouse (C57BL/6; male 6–8 weeks old, weighing 17–21 g) was anesthetized with 4% isoflurane in 100% O2, after confirmation of anesthesia by lack of reaction to toe pinch, the isoflurane was decreased to 2% to maintain an appropriate anesthesia level. Then, a periodontitis model was established via ligaturing with 5 − 0 silk at the first molar of the upper jaw for 2 w. After confirming successful establishment of the model, the mice were randomly divided into two groups (10 mice per group): the PBS group and the Shh group. Mice in the PBS and Shh groups were designated to receive periodontal injection of either PBS (10 µL) or Shh (8 µM, 10 µL PBS, once per three days) after ligature removal. Animals were euthanized by inhaling an excess of isoflurane to obtain the maxillary samples at the fourth week.

Micro-CT analysis

The mouse maxillae were scanned using an X-ray microcomputed tomography (micro-CT) system (PerkinElmer, 25 mm FOV, 90 kV, 88 µA, 50 μm Voxel Size, 240 s exposure). Then, the obtained data were reconstructed and analyzed with Mimics 21.0 software (Materialise), and two-dimensional images were obtained using 3-matic Research 13.0 (Materialise) software. For the present study, alveolar bone loss was estimated by measuring the distance from the cementoenamel junction (CEJ) to the alveolar bone crest (ABC) (CEJ-ABC) of the first molar (both the lingual and buccal regions) three times.

Histological analyses

For histological analyses, the obtained maxillary sample were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, decalcified in 10% EDTA, and then dehydrated with ethanol. After that, the collected tissues were embedded in Tissue-Tek optimum cutting temperature compound and then embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, the 5-µm-thick specimen sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Immunofluorescence staining was conducted as described previously [18, 31]. For the present study, primary antibodies against CD31 (1:50; Servicebio, Wuhan China), EMCN (1:2000; Servicebio), OSX (1:200; Servicebio) and HIF-1α (1:100; Servicebio) were used. After incubation with secondary antibodies (1:400; Servicebio) for 1 h at room temperature, images were captured and then analyzed with ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between groups were conducted using Student’s t-test. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Shh treatment augmented the angiogenic activities of ECs in vitro

Angiogenesis-related functional assays were subsequently established to explore the effect of Shh on the angiogenic activities of ECs. As shown in Fig. 1A, the results of the CCK-8 assay revealed that the proliferation rate of ECs was significantly elevated in response to Shh stimulation than in the control group (P < 0.05). Similarly, based on the results of transwell and scratch wound healing assays, Shh markedly upregulated the motility of ECs relative to that in the control group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001; Fig. 1B-E). In the tube formation assay, ECs treated with Shh demonstrated a stronger angiogenic ability compared with the control group, as evidenced by higher number of capillary-like structures and quantitative measurements (P < 0.01; Fig. 1F and G). And notably, the immunofluorescence staining analysis results revealed that Shh administration markedly increased the CD31 and EMCN fluorescence intensities (P < 0.05; Fig. 2A and B). In addition, qRT-PCR analysis demonstrated that Shh treatment significantly up-regulated the gene expression levels of CD31 and EMCN compared with those in the control group (P < 0.0001; Fig. 2C).

Shh promoted EC angiogenesis by activating Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling

Based on the qRT-PCR analysis results, Shh can up-regulate the Slit3, Robo4 (the bioactive receptor of Slit3) and HIF-1α expression (Fig. 2D), these results were further confirmed by the Gene Expression Omnibus (accession number GSE27993) analysis, where low levels of Slit3, Robo4 and HIF-1α can be identified in periodontitis tissues compared with the healthy tissues (Fig. 2E); these above results collectively suggested that Shh may exert a pro-angiogenic effect within periodontitis through the Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling. Then, a series of functional assays were established to verify the potential mechanisms, and notably, the results obtained from Western blot and qRT-PCR analyses revealed a significant increase in the Slit3, Robo4, and HIF-1α (protein and gene) expression levels in ECs in response to Shh relative to those in the control group (P < 0.05; P < 0.01, P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001; Fig. 2F-H). Furthermore, si-Slit3 was used to confirm that Shh regulates the angiogenic activities of ECs by Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling. After confirming that si-Slit3 significantly down-regulated Slit3 expression (P < 0.0001; Fig. 3A), a sequence of downstream functional assays was conducted. CCK-8 (P < 0.05; Fig. 3B), transwell (P < 0.0001; Fig. 3C and D), scratch wound assay (P < 0.0001; Fig. 3E and F) and tube formation (P < 0.01; Fig. 3G and H) assays revealed that Slit3 silencing significantly decreased the proliferation, migration and tube formation abilities of ECs. Additionally, si-Slit3 markedly decreased the CD31 and EMCN fluorescence intensities (P < 0.01; Fig. 4A and B) and CD31 and EMCN gene expression levels compared with those in the control group (P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001; Fig. 4C). Besides, based on the Western and qRT-PCR analyses, a significant decrease in Robo4 and HIF-1α protein and gene expression in ECs was observed following si-Slit3 treatment (P < 0.05; P < 0.001; or P < 0.0001; Fig. 4D-F).

Shh enhanced the osteogenic activity of PDLSCs in vitro

ALP and ARS assays were conducted to analyze ALP activity and the mineralized nodules formed of PDLSCs in response to Shh. According to the results, both ALP staining/activity and ARS demonstrated that the Shh-treated group had greater ALP activity and more mineralized nodule formation relative to the control group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001; Fig. 5A-D). qRT-PCR analysis showed that Shh treatment remarkably upregulated the mRNA expression of osteogenic differentiation-related genes (RUNX-2 and ALP) in PDLSCs compared with those in the control group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001; Fig. 5E). Similarly, Western blot analysis revealed that the protein expression levels of RUNX-2 and ALP were markedly increased in the Shh group than in the control group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001; Fig. 5F and G).

Shh administration facilitated type H vessel formation in a periodontitis model

The effect of Shh on type H vessel formation was assessed in a periodontitis mouse model, and a schematic diagram of the drug administration was shown in Fig. 6A. None of the mice showed any signs of disability/discomfort after Shh or PBS treatment. As illustrated in Fig. 6B, based on CD31 and EMCN immunofluorescence staining, a greater percentage of type H vessels (CD31+EMCN+) was observed in the Shh group than that in the control group, and the statistical analysis confirmed the morphological observations (P < 0.01; Fig. 6C), which indicated that Shh administration strongly promoted type H vessel formation within periodontitis. Additionally, immunofluorescence staining of HIF-1α and the quantitative measurements demonstrated that Shh treatment significantly increased the proportion of HIF-1α staining cells compared with that in the control group (P < 0.0001; Fig. 6D and E).

Shh treatment attenuated alveolar bone loss within periodontitis model

After confirming that the experimental periodontitis model was successfully constructed, the ligature was removed, and then, Shh or PBS was injected into the maxillary molars of the mice. Following 4 w of treatment, Micro-CT analysis demonstrated that the distance of CEJ-ABC (the bone loss height) was markedly decreased in the Shh injection group compared with the control group (P < 0.0001; Fig. 7A and B, where the red line indicates the bone loss height). Additionally, according to the immunofluorescence staining of OSX and the statistical analysis, a greater density and number of OSX-positive cells were observed in the Shh group than in the control group (P < 0.0001; Fig. 7C and D).

Discussion

Currently, research on alveolar bone regeneration is primarily based on tissue engineering and regenerative medicine strategies, including autologous or allogeneic bone transplantation, stem cell therapy, growth factor delivery, enamel matrix derivative application, and the combination of cells/growth factors with bioactive scaffolds [32–35]. However, transplanted cells often exhibit limited survival and functional capacity, while the other aforementioned strategies have failed to meet expectations, resulting in suboptimal outcomes in clinical practice to date. One major reason for the limited success of this therapy in clinical practice is insufficient vascularization [36, 37]. Based on the current understanding, the establishment and maintenance of a highly orchestrated vascular network, which provides oxygen and various nutrients, is indispensable for bone tissues. In particular, the newly discovered type H vessels that play crucial roles in the process of bone regeneration and remodeling. In this context, the present study was performed, and based on the obtained results, Shh treatment alleviated alveolar bone loss within periodontitis by enhancing the coupling of type H vessel formation and osteogenesis, and Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling was involved in the type H vessel formation mediated by Shh administration (Fig. 8).

Fig. 8Shh treatment alleviated alveolar bone loss within periodontitis by triggering the coupling of type H vessel formation and osteogenesis

Shh treatment alleviated alveolar bone loss within periodontitis by triggering the coupling of type H vessel formation and osteogenesis

Shh is a secreted signaling molecule that plays pivotal roles in mammalian embryogenesis and adult tissue homeostasis, and has been demonstrated to play vital roles in tube formation during vascular embryonic development, wound healing and neovascularization upon adult trauma [38–40]. A previous study reported that Shh promoted EC migration and angiogenesis in a murine corneal angiogenesis model [41]. Additionally, Shh administration was verified to have a therapeutic effect on myocardial ischemia by facilitating neovascularization [42]. More importantly, Shh was demonstrated to have dual effects on promoting angiogenesis and osteogenesis, as evidenced by its ability to accelerate engineered blood vessels and promote the formation of mature bone via a combination of intramembranous and endochondral ossification [43]. Therefore, we investigated whether Shh administration could facilitate alveolar bone formation by promoting type H vessel formation and osteogenesis, to mediate the angiogenic-osteogenic coupling effect. In the present study, Shh treatment promoted the proliferation, migration and tube formation of ECs, and enhanced the osteogenic activities of PDLSCs, as evidenced by enhanced ALP activity, more mineralized nodule formation, and increased expression levels of osteogenic-related factors ALP and RUNX-2. More importantly, local injection of Shh in periodontal tissues enhanced the number of type H vessels, increased the number of OSX-positive cells, and ameliorated alveolar bone loss in mouse periodontitis model.

HIF-1α is a vital promoter of type H vessel formation. Kusumbe and colleagues demonstrated that HIF-1α expression was up-regulated in type H vessels in adolescent mice, and gradually down-regulated with age, which was related to a decrease in type H vessels and age-dependent bone loss. Furthermore, the activation of HIF-1α in ECs has been demonstrated to accelerate type H vessels formation and increase bone density [9]. A previous study reported that Gli1(+) cells, a subpopulation of MSCs, modulated type H vessels via HIF-1α signaling during bone growth and defect healing processes [44]. Additionally, metformin administration was reported to promote fracture healing in osteoporotic mice by enhancing type H vessel formation, and HIF-1α was involved in the above process [45]. A temperature-sensitive hydrogel incorporating deferoxamine was reported to effectively promote the formation of type H vessels by modulating the expression of HIF-1α and further accelerate bone remodeling in a critical bone defect model [46]. In addition, Shh administration was demonstrated to modulate HIF-1α expression levels during neovascularization [47–49]. Therefore, we considered whether Shh deliver the promoted function on type H vessel formation via regulating HIF-1α. Based on the obtained results, the HIF-1α expression level in ECs was significantly increased following Shh administration; further in vivo studies demonstrated that Shh injection increased the number and density of HIF-1α positive cells surrounding type H vessels.

Additionally, in the present study, the expression of Slit3 and Robo4 markedly were increased in response to Shh treatment. Slit3 was demonstrated to express in multiple tissues and linked to angiogenesis. In general, Slit3 interacts with its bioactive receptor Robo4, to mediate the proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis of ECs [50, 51]. While, the cellular source of Slit3 has not been elucidated in the present study. Based on the existed literature, Slit3 can be expressed in various cell types including periodontal ligament cells, macrophages and osteoblasts [52–55]. For instance, Xu et al. reported that osteoblasts are the primary secretion source of Slit3, and the knockout of Slit3 in mouse osteoblasts led to a significant reduction in type H vessels, further impairing the osteogenic activities during fracture healing [52]. Our subsequent research will further identify the cellular origin of Slit3 in periodontal tissues to better facilitate type H vessel formation and alveolar bone regeneration via Slit3 modulation.

Based on the obtained results, after si-Slit3 administration, the Shh-induced proliferation, migration and angiogenesis of ECs were partially reversed, and the HIF-1α expression was down-regulated in the presence of si-Slit3. A previous study was in line with our research, which reported that Slit3 promoted type H vessel formation and bone regeneration by activating HIF-1α, and HIF-1α was significantly decreased following Slit3 knockdown [56]. While, occlusal force was found to promote the formation of type H vessels via PIEZO1/HIF-1α/Slit3 signaling to maintain the alveolar bone homeostasis, and HIF-1α functioned upstream of Slit3 [53]. Additionally, HIF-1α/VEGF signaling and Slit3 signaling were found to be involved in the osteogenesis and angiogenesis process mediated by Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge in parallel [57]. In our future studies, a series of assays (including HIF-1α inhibition and chromatin immunoprecipitation) would be designed to further explore whether the reciprocal regulatory effect exist between Slit3 and HIF-1α signaling, or these pathways are also regulated in parallel by Shh.

Conclusion

The current study demonstrated that Shh administration exerts beneficial effects on alleviating alveolar bone loss within periodontitis by enhancing type H vessel formation and bone formation. Further mechanistic studies indicated that the Shh-mediated activation of Slit3/Robo4/HIF-1α signaling may contribute to type H vessel formation. Our findings may offer a possible therapeutic strategy for periodontitis treatment.

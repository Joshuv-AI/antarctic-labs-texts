Characterizing tensile and flexural properties of synthetic fibers-reinforced epoxy composite for foot prosthetic application
Authors: Galana Abay Kebede, Gamachis Ragasa Gutata
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMaterials have a direct and critical impact on the performance of prosthetic feet. This study investigates the mechanical properties of E-glass and hybrid (E-glass/carbon) epoxy composites for prosthetic foot applications. Using ANSYS FEA software, four stacking sequences were analyzed for tensile and flexural strength, with stacking configuration the SS-3 ([0c/90/45]s) demonstrating the highest performance. The numerical results were validated against analytical MATLAB solutions, showing excellent agreement. Compared to Homo-polymer-polypropylene, the composite prosthetic foot exhibits superior performance, with increased deformation resistance, energy storage capacity, safety factor, and stiffness, while reducing weight by 30.62 %. This study suggests a cost-effective and efficient method for developing lightweight and durable prosthetic feet that match the comfort and performance constraints of current HPP designs. The selected composite for prosthetic foot production was suggested due to its enhanced mechanical properties and improved functionality.Keywords: Prosthetic foot, Stacking sequence composite laminate, Tensile simulation, Flexural simulation, Mechanical property characterization

Introduction

The Prosthetic foot plays a crucial role in allowing amputees to walk independently by adapting to their gait. The choice of prosthetic material significantly impacts performance. Homo-polymer polypropylene (HPP) is a widely used material in the industry, accounting for approximately 65–75 % of the market share. It finds application in various prosthetic components due to its versatility and ease of dyeing [1,2]. However, HPP has limitations, including lower strength compared to composites, limited energy return, susceptibility to fatigue, and cold flow over time [3,4]. These limitations negatively affect device performance, especially in cold weather.

Composite materials, offering advantages like lightweight, low cost, and high strength, have gained interest in prosthetic foot applications [5,6]. The mechanical properties of composites, crucial for prosthetic foot design and manufacturing, are influenced by fiber orientation, stacking sequences, reinforcement types, and manufacturing processes [3,7]. Determining the strength of composites is essential for selecting suitable materials for specific applications. Stacking sequence, in particular, significantly impacts tensile and flexural strength [2,4].

Experimental methods have been widely used to characterize the mechanical properties of composites. Researchers have investigated various aspects of composites, including the effects of different fiber types, reinforcement blends, stacking sequences [8], and the impact of fatigue and tensile loads [[9], [10], [11]]. Studies have also focused on the mechanical properties of composites for specific applications, such as prosthetic feet and aerospace components [8,12,13]. Additionally, the influence of matrix materials and the combination of different fiber forms on mechanical properties have been explored. This body of research provides valuable insights into the behavior of composite materials under various conditions and contributes to their optimal design and application [12,14].

This research introduces a novel method for optimizing prosthetic foot design by combining simulation and analytical techniques, addressing the limitations of mechanical testing alone. Using FEA software (ANSYS 19.2), the study analyzes different composite stacking sequences to understand stress and strain distribution. By applying micro-mechanics and the Tsai-Wu failure theory for progressive failure analysis, and validating results with MATLAB scripts, the study ensures accurate simulation data. The optimal stacking sequence identified (SS-3) significantly outperforms the traditional homo-polymer polypropylene (HPP) design in terms of strength, safety, energy absorption, cost, weight, and manufacturing complexity, offering a more efficient and durable solution for prosthetic foot. and performance limitations of current HPP designs.

Finite element modeling and analysis

Modeling and analysis of testing samples

Modeling of tensile and flexural testing samples according to ASTM standards (D3039-00 and D790) for each stacking configuration of E-glass/epoxy and hybrid (E-glass/carbon)/epoxy composite in ANSYS 19.2 workbench. The general procedure followed for the analysis are defining material property, modeling, and meshing geometry of test sample. After material property is created in engineering data geometry of tensile and flexural specimen is modeled in SOLIDWORK software and saved as. STEP file to be imported to ANSYS ACP (pre) geometry. A rigid structural steel is assigned for pusher and support member in flexural simulation. In a model, the geometry is discretized into required number of elements as shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b). The finer the more mesh accurate result but it needs more computation time. A convergence study was performed to determine the appropriate finite element mesh to be used in static structural analysis of testing specimen.

3D Model (left side) and Meshed model (right side) of (a) tensile and (b) flexural testing sample.

Defining the stacking sequence, applying load and boundary conditions

The composite laminate is built up on the surface of imported geometry as shown in Fig. 3 (b). Total of four stacking squences with 2.5 mm thickness of laminate is considered for charactering the mechanical properties under tensile and flexural testing as shown Fig. 4(a) and (b). The first two cases of E-glass/Epoxy composite and the remaining are hybrid composite (carbon-E-glass/epoxy) is proposed for prosthetic foot. The composite data (solid or shell data) in ACP (pre)-setup is dragged into the model of static structural for the application of load and boundary condition. In tensile simulation, the fixed BC is applied at one end of the specimen and displacement BC that allows the specimen to deform in global x-direction only and the load is applied on the face of composite specimen. The load is applied at the other end of the specimen with type force in global x direction at different magnitudes until last ply failure load. In flexural simulation, the top faces of the two-support member are fixed, and the sample is allowed to displace only in y-direction. Then the bottom face of the pusher is allowed to displace in negative y-direction.

Defining the failure criteria and applying progressive failure analysis

In ANSYS Workbench different failures are provided in the composite failure tool. The Tsai-Wu failure criteria are ‘on’ in order to check plies failure at each load increment as shown in Fig. 5(a). The progressive failure analysis (PFA) was adapted to analyze the mechanical behavior of composite specimens to study their progressive failures and strengths as shown in Fig. 5(b). The whole process of the progressive damage method consists of stress analysis, failure analysis, and material property degradation [20,21].

Solving and generating the results in static structural or ACP (Post)

The stress, strain, deformation, and FI in each ply or the entire laminate are solved in static structure according to progressive failure analysis at each load step. Tensile and three-point bending tests in ANSYS 19.2 for each sample of composite laminate is carried out according to progressive failure analyses procedure (Fig. 5(b)) then the stress-strain graphs, load-deflection graphs that helps to extract their ultimate ply failure load and strength. Comparison and analysis of tensile and flexural properties of each stacking configuration of the composite and high strength stacking sequence are selected for the prosthetic foot model.

Modeling and analysis of prosthetic foot

In this simulation the performance of prosthetic foot made from the two materials are analyzed. The model of the prosthetic foot was taken from the prosthetic and orthotic center in Ethiopia and these prosthetics are made from homo-polymer polypropylene (HPP) material sheet (2000x2000 × 5mm) (SIMONA product) imported from Germany. The new composite material (third stacking configuration- SS-3) that yields high tensile and flexural strength is selected among the four stacking configurations for a single-blade prosthetic foot. The prosthetic foot modeled from these two materials (i.e., HPP and new composite) are simulated here under the following design specification. The average weight of adult men (amputees) is 62.0 kg and Prosthetic foot can stand with 29 % (80 kg) additional weight of users. So, the static loading on ANSYS Software uses a force F = 9.81x80 = 784.8N, where g = 9.81 m/s. Thickness of the blade for prosthetic foot made from HPP and composite are 5 mm and 2.5 mm respectively with in 32 mm thick rigid keel. Three loading cases in Fig. 6 (a) are applied with three phases in a walking cycle: Initial foot contact (heel strike)-25 % weight is supported = 196.2N, mid stance (limb support entire body)-support entire body (784.8N) and Propulsion (toe off), to enable the body weight to propel forwards, 40 % of weight is expected to be supported (313.92N). Steps used in the solution procedure using ANSYS are the geometry preparation of the prosthetic foot to be analyzed is imported in an STEP format, the materials properties of the prosthetic foot are specified, meshing of the three-dimensional prosthetic foot model is performed, the boundary conditions and external loads are applied, the solution is generated based on the input parameters as shown Fig. 6(b).

Theoretical results and discussions for FEA verification

To verify the FEA solutions, the plies failure load results at FPF and LPF load is run in MATLAB. The analysis result is shown in Table 11, Table 12. There is a small variation in MATLAB and finite element results. But it shows the same plies failure order in both tensile and flexural testing analysis. The MATLAB in tensile testing predicts the failure loads accurately with Ansys results with errors less than 2 % as shown in Table 11. Similarly, the flexural testing analysis gives comparable results with error less than 3 % as shown in Table 12. The factors that influence the variation in MATLAB and Ansys result arises due to their analysis procedures. The MATLAB result is based on a fully discount method in which once the ply is failed its stiffness becomes zero, while the numerical analysis is based on the progressive analysis that the stiffness of the failed ply becomes reduced to values other than zeros-based stiffness degradation rule. In general, the MATLAB result validates in finite element results as the error is small and acceptable.

Material stiffness, weight and cost estimation comparisons

The stiffness, weight and material cost estimation for single prosthetic foot is calculated. The stiffness of material is important for the prosthetic foot to increase its weight carrying capacity of amputees. Additionally, it allows the amputees to safely use the prosthetic foot as his/her body weight is increased. From the analysis stiffness of the prosthetic foot made from multi blade HPP and single blade composite is 95.26N/m and 117.34N/m respectively. The weight of the current prosthetic foot model made from HPP and single blade composite is 2.002N and 1.389N respectively. The weight of prosthetic foot is saved by 30.62 %. Even though the HPP is cheaper, due to four-blades of HPP each with 5 mm laminated together to have strength for the prosthetic foot its cost is higher than composite made prosthetic foot. The cost of HPP material needed to produce single foot is estimated as 0.61$/foot. The cost of composite material for producing single bladed prosthetic foot is estimated as 0.48$/foot. This shows 21.3 % cost reduction due to the multi-blade foot is replaced by single bladed composite foot. Additionally, the composite is easy to process for prosthetic foot with less time compared to that of HPP. Conclusion

The selected composite material (SS-3) was applied and simulated for the model of single blade of prosthetic foot that replaces the four blades of prosthetic foot model made from HPP in POC with higher performance, in terms of its stiffness, strength, safety, weight, material cost and energy storage ability. The result obtained showed that the ROM and Halpin-Tsai correctly predicts longitudinal and transversal properties of the lamina with an acceptable range of errors from (1.66%–3.04 %) and (0.5%–3.02 %) respectively compared to experimental result of [15]. The strength of unidirectional lamina can be predicted by analytical model of [17]with an error less than 12.3 % except for transversal compressive and shear strength lamina when compared with experimental results [15]of the same lamina. The plies failure order in the laminate is important in determining load carrying capacity of laminate and depends on stacking sequence of lamina in the laminate. In pure E-glass/epoxy composite under tensile loading changing stacking configuration from [45/0/90]s to [0/90/45]s increase FPF load from 1440.2N to 1443.2N. Similarly, in hybrid glass-carbon/epoxy composite changing the stacking sequence from [45c/90/45]s to [0c/90/45]s increases FPF load from 1471.84N to 2401.6N. Additionally, it has similar influence on flexural properties. The stacking sequence highly affects the ultimate tensile and flexural strength of laminate. In pure E-glass/epoxy and hybrid/epoxy laminate the ultimate tensile strength is increased from 777.44Mpa to 1475.5Mpa and 1865Mpa–1935Mpa respectively. Similarly, in flexural testing the ultimate flexural strength of hybrid composite is increased from 1299.2Mpa −1934.3Mpa due effect of stacking sequence. Among the all stacking configuration the SS-3 ([0c/90/45]s) results higher tensile and flexural strength and selected for prosthetic foot. The theoretical analysis of laminate accurately verifies the numerical results. The ultimate failure loads of laminate in each stacking sequence verified the numerical results with an error less than 2 % and 3 % for tensile and flexural loading respectively. The single blade of composite with thickness of 2.5 mm with higher performance replaces the four blades of HPP each with the thickness of 5 mm is bonded together to build the prosthetic foot and allows the artificial foot to be less in weight and manufacturing cost in addition to providing more strength and comfort. The prosthetic foot model made from HPP has less strength and less resistance to deformation under the same loading condition. The model is not operating under the material stress limits. Its maximum equivalent stresses HPP is 49.055 MPa which is higher than its ultimate strength, 37 MPa. But, in composite model the equivalent stress is 493.28 MPa which is lower than its ultimate strength. The deformation resistance of composite foot is increased by 18.9 %. This indicates that composite prosthetic blade has higher strength. As a result, less material (small thickness) can be used and allow the design for a reduction in weight and overall production cost. The composite prosthetic foot is comfortable for amputees and assist the forward propulsion of a body as the stored energy is released. It has higher ability to restore energy than HPP foot while walking. The energy absorbing capacity of the prosthetic blade is increased by 53.2 %. The composite blade allows the safety factor to increase by 56.2 % without showing any damage under operating load. Its stiffness is increased by 18.8 % allows serve more weighted amputees without permanent deformation that reduces energy storage capabilities. The laminated hybrid glass-carbon/epoxy composite blade prosthetic foot is very light weight material than that of the current HPP multi blade prosthetic foot in Prosthetic orthotic center. Due the replacement of multi blade HPP prosthetic foot design by single composite blade the weight of prosthetic foot is saved by 30.62 % and allows material cost to be reduced by 21.3 %.

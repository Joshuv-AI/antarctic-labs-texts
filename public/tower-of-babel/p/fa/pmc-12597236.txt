Prognostic Impact of the Difference of Mechanical Circulatory Support in Patients With Acute Coronary Syndrome Complicated by Cardiogenic Shock ― A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis ―
Authors: Katsutaka Hashiba, Kazuo Sakamoto, Aya Katasako-Yabumoto, Takeshi Yamamoto, Naoki Nakayama, Hiroyuki Hanada, Takahiro Nakashima, Jin Kirigaya, Tomoko Ishizu, Yumiko Hosoya, Toru Kondo, Yusuke Okazaki, Masahiro Yamamoto, Takumi Osawa, Marina Arai, Yoshio Tahara, Hiroshi Nonogi, Teruo Noguchi, Yasushi Tsujimoto, Toshikazu Funazaki, Migaku Kikuchi, Tetsuya Matoba, for the Japan Resuscitation Council (JRC) Cardiogenic Shock (CS) Task Force and the Guideline Editorial Committee on behalf of the Japanese Circulation Society (JCS) Emergency and Critical Care Committee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe optimal device for mechanical circulatory support (MCS) in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) complicated with cardiogenic shock (CS) remains unknown. Therefore, in this study we aimed to analyze which MCS (intra-aortic balloon pumping (IABP) or IMPELLA) is associated with better outcomes in patients with AMI-related CS.Methods and ResultsThis systematic review and meta-analysis used a random-effects model to account for potential heterogeneity. Risk ratios (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used for the dichotomous outcomes. The PubMed, Web of Science, and CENTRAL databases were searched up to April 30, 2023. The risk of bias was evaluated using the Revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB2) tool, and the certainty of evidence was evaluated according to the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) tool. Two randomized controlled trials were included in the meta-analysis. For the primary outcome of 30-day survival, IMPELLA probably improves the outcome by a small amount compared with IABP (RR0.94 [95% CI 0.5–1.53], 29 fewer per 1,000 [95% CI from 204 fewer to 258 more], low certainty of evidence).ConclusionsWe could not show a survival benefit of IMPELLA compared with IABP in patients with AMI complicated by CS. Further investigation is required to resolve this issue.

Central Figure

When broad myocardial ischemia occurs in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI), it is typically followed by a decrease in ventricular contractility, accompanied by a decrease in cardiac output, which results in systemic hypotension and inadequate tissue perfusion, known as cardiogenic shock (CS). Furthermore, in CS, a progressive negative cycle of inflammation, ischemia, vasoconstriction, and cardiac overload often persists, ultimately leading to death.1,2

CS is still the leading cause of death for patients suffering AMI despite the progress in patient care, including the generalization of emergency coronary reperfusion therapy and advances in the management of mechanical circulatory support (MCS).3,4 Approximately 10–15% of patients with AMI develop CS, and the mortality rate remains unacceptably high at 30–50%, compared to those without CS.5–7 Most randomized clinical trials evaluating pharmacological or MCS treatment strategies, including intra-aortic balloon pumping (IABP) or veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO), for the management of CS have not shown clinical benefit.2,4,8 Recently, a randomized trial using IMPELLA, a percutaneous left ventricular assist device (PVAD), showed an improved mortality rate for patients with AMI complicating CS.4,9

Early reperfusion therapy to resolve myocardial ischemia is essential to reduce deaths from CS due to AMI, and many patients can be saved with that strategy.6 Conversely, there is a group of patients who do not improve with reperfusion therapy only, and MCS support is mandatory for survival. Classically, the treatment for patients with AMI-CS involves early revascularization and hemodynamic support with IABP. However, studies evaluating the effect of this strategy, including the IABP-SHOCK II and IABP trial 1, could not show a survival benefit for patients with AMI-CS.8,10 Recently, the DenGer Shock randomized trial showed that IMPELLA use may improve outcomes for patients with AMI-CS.

However, because it remains unclear which MCS device is more suitable in this situation, in this study we aimed to analyze which MCS is associated with better outcomes in patients with AMI-related CS. When considering which device to compare, VA-ECMO was excluded from our analysis because there is a lack of randomized clinical trials comparing VA-ECMO with IMPELLA or IABP. Therefore, the IMPELLA and IABP were selected for investigation in this study.

Methods

The Japan Resuscitation Council (JRC) CS Task Force for the 2025 JRC guidelines was established by the Japanese Circulation Society and the Japanese Society of Internal Medicine. The JRCCS Task Force established 10 clinically relevant questions for systematic reviews. In this study, we examined the research question regarding the differences in the effects of 2 different MCS devices for patients with CS. We aimed to assess all available studies to answer the question: “In patients with acute coronary syndrome complicated by CS, does the type of MCS affect the prognosis?”

P (patients): Patients with AMI-related CS

I (intervention): MCS with IMPELLA

C (comparisons, controls): MCS with IABP

O (Outcomes): Short-term survival (30-day survival), major bleeding, and stroke

S (study designs): Randomized controlled trials

T (timeframe): all literature published up to April 30, 2023

In clinical studies that included various causes of CS, patients with non-myocardial causes of infarction were excluded, as were studies that did not compare the differences between MCS devices. The study protocol was preregistered with the Open Science Framework (OSF registry; DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/NTZXJ). Systematic reviews and meta-analyses were performed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.11

Search Strategies

Relevant research trials were identified by searching electronic databases, including PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection (Science Citation Index Expanded since 1973), and the Cochrane Library (CENTRAL). The search was conducted until April 30, 2023, using terms determined by the review team (Supplementary Methods). Articles published in English or Japanese were included; however, Japanese articles without English abstracts were excluded.

Study Selection

After removing duplicates, the titles and abstracts of the search items were screened and sequentially excluded by 2 investigators (K.H. and K.S.) based on the eligibility criteria. When uncertainty persisted after screening the titles and abstracts, the full-text articles were independently assessed by both investigators. After excluding case reports, case series, reviews, guidelines, animal studies, and studies that did not clearly report the original human clinical data relevant to the primary review question, the 2 reviewers independently assessed the full-text papers of potentially eligible studies to confirm the inclusion and exclusion criteria (second screening). Any disagreements were resolved through discussion involving a third reviewer (T.M.) until a consensus was reached.

Major bleeding and stroke were assessed as the safety outcomes.

Risk of Bias Assessment

The risk of bias in all included studies was centrally assessed by 2 reviewers (K.H. and A.K.) using the Revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB2). Each study was evaluated across 5 domains, and the overall risk of bias was categorized as low, moderate, serious or critical, or classified as no information based on these assessments. Disagreements between reviewers regarding the risk of bias in specific studies were resolved through discussion.

Statistical Analysis

The meta-analysis was conducted using the unadjusted results from randomized controlled studies, including risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Pooled estimates were calculated using a random-effects model to account for potential heterogeneity. RRs and 95% CIs were used for dichotomous outcomes. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 value to quantify inconsistency; an I2 value >50% was considered indicative of substantial heterogeneity.12 Additionally, Cochran’s Q test was performed to assess the statistical significance of heterogeneity. Potential publication bias was examined using funnel plots. All statistical analyses were performed using the Review Manager software (RevMan 5.4.1).

Rating the Certainty of Evidence

The Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) tool was used to rate the certainty of evidence regarding the differences between the effects of IABP and IMPELLA in patients with CS due to AMI. The certainty of the evidence was classified as “high,” “moderate,” “low,” or “very low” by evaluating the risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias.

Results

Study Selection

Our search retrieved 2,142 items from PubMed, 2,603 from Web of Science, and 299 from CENTRAL, of which 1,437 were duplicates. After screening for content, incorrect publication type, incorrect study design, incorrect population, and different outcomes, 3,605 items were excluded. Safety Outcomes

Bleeding Complications Only 1 study in 48 patients was available on the critical outcome of major bleeding. There was probably a higher rate of bleeding complications with IMPELLA compared to IABP for the outcome (RR4.00 [95% CI 0.95–15.92], 250 more per 1,000 [95% CI 4 fewer to 1,000 more], low certainty of evidence).

Stroke For the important outcome of stroke, only 1 study in 48 patients was available. There was no difference between IMPELLA and IABP for the outcome (RR1.00 [95% CI 0.07–15.08], 0 fewer per 1,000 [95% CI 39 fewer to 587 more], low certainty of evidence).

Certainty of Evidence

We assessed the certainty of the evidence for each outcome and presented a summary in the evidence profile table (Table 2). For the critical outcome of 30-day mortality, the certainty of evidence was rated as low because of serious imprecision. For the critical outcome of stroke and the important outcome of bleeding, the certainty of evidence was rated low because of serious imprecision. Visual assessment of the funnel plots did not indicate any evidence of publication bias (Supplementary Figure).

Discussion

In this systematic review and meta-analysis, IMPELLA showed a small survival benefit compared with IABP for patients with AMI complicated by CS, although smaller than expected. Overall, for safety outcomes, there were no differences in stroke incidence, although IMPELLA may be associated with a higher bleeding risk.

The IMPELLA is a microaxial flow pump inserted percutaneously, draining blood directly from the left ventricle (LV) and expelling it into the ascending aorta.9 It unloads the LV and reduces LV myocardial oxygen demand, resulting in a reduction in infarct size and better residual LV function. The protective effect of IMPELLA against AMI may vary depending on the timing of MCS initiation. IMPELLA started before revascularization may be more effective in reducing both infarct size and the mortality rate.15 Therefore, theoretically, IMPELLA is the more powerful device than IABP in improving outcomes for patients with CS, given that IABP has not been shown to improve outcomes for patients with AMI-related CS.8,10 However, in several previous observational studies comparing IMPELLA and IABP for patients with AMI-CS, the mortality rate for IMPELLA-treated patients was equal or lower in that situation.16–20

This meta-analysis aimed at resolving the discrepancy between theoretical expectations and the results of observational studies. Our results did not show a statistically significant survival effect of IMPELLA. However, we conclude that there is probably a small effect on short-term mortality rates, considering the importance of the outcome. One reason for these results may be the small number of studies (n=2) and the small sample size included in the analysis. Another reason may be the high incidence of cardiac arrest in these patients. Although not statistically significant in either of the included studies, patients assigned to IMPELLA had a higher rate of cardiac arrest than those assigned to IABP (100% vs. 83% and 85% vs. 69%, respectively).13,14 In the case of cardiac arrest, other circulatory support, such as VA-ECMO, may be necessary. When combined with VA-ECMO, Impella and IABP may exhibit different outcomes.21,22 The timing of MCS initiation may also affect the outcome. In one of the 2 included studies, the assigned MCS (either Impella or IABP) was initiated after revascularization in all patients. The use of Impella 2.5 may have also influenced the study’s results.13 In the other study, the timing of MCS initiation (before, during, or immediately after revascularization) was at the discretion of the treating physician.14 In the included studies, most patients assigned to IMPELLA received device initiation during or after revascularization. Considering that IMPELLA initiation before revascularization may be more effective in AMI, and even in patients with CS, the delayed initiation of a MCS device may have weakened the protective power of IMPELLA, and as a result, IMPELLA may not have demonstrated its true ability.23

Regarding safety outcomes, there were no differences in the incidence of stroke. Although the difference was not statistically significant, major bleeding tended to occur more frequently in the IMPELLA group. This may have affected the mortality rate in the patient group; however, data from only 1 study were included, and because of the small sample size, we cannot make a definite judgement based on these findings.

Study Limitations

First, the small sample size of the included studies and the limited number of studies may have affected the reliability of our results. Second, the timing of MCS initiation was not randomized but based on clinical judgement, which introduces a potential selection bias. Finally, regarding IMPELLA, management protocols vary across institutions and have been developing over the past few years.8 Therefore, differences in the time periods during which the studies were conducted may have affected the results.

Conclusions

This study showed that IMPELLA probably improves 30-day survival in a small number of patients with AMI complicated by CS compared with IABP, although the difference was not statistically significant. However, the certainty of evidence was low because the sample size was small despite the meta-analysis being performed. Further randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes are required.

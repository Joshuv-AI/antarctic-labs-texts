Real-world usage pattern, effectiveness and safety of oral tramadol/dexketoprofen trometamol fixed-dose combination in moderate-to-severe acute pain in Asia: a prospective, multicentre, observational study
Authors: Kok Yuen Ho, Bibek Gyanwali, Cesar Dimayuga, Edgar Michael Eufemio, Edwin Bernardo, Gopinathan Raju, Keen Wai Chong, Kritsadakorn Waithayayothin, Leonardo Ona, Marc Anthony L Castro, Passakorn Sawaddiruk, Roehl C Salvador, Sharifah Ahmad Roohi, Suwimon Tangwiwat, Vajara Wilairatana, Zhi Hao Oon, Ankur Gupta, Dinesh Nagrale
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesThis study aims to determine the usage pattern, effectiveness and safety of oral tramadol 75 mg and dexketoprofen trometamol 25 mg fixed-dose combination (TRAM/DKP FDC) in the short-term treatment of moderate-to-severe acute pain in real-world clinical practice in Asia.DesignReal-world, prospective, multicentre, observational, phase IV study.Setting13 tertiary-care hospital sites across the Philippines, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore.ParticipantsAdult patients aged 18–80 years prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC for the short-term (up to 5 days) treatment of moderate-to-severe acute pain.Main outcome measuresPrimary endpoints were the proportion of patients prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC with different types of postsurgical and non-surgical treatments, and the average dosing frequency and duration of TRAM/DKP FDC treatment. Secondary endpoints were the proportion of patients achieving ≥30% pain reduction at 8 hours post the first dose (pain severity was assessed using the 11-point Numeric Pain Rating Scale); patient satisfaction at the end of treatment (based on a 5-point Patient Global Evaluation Scale (PGE)) and safety including the incidence of adverse drug reactions (ADRs).ResultsAmong 599 patients (median age 44 years, 61.3% female) enrolled in this study, 68.61% (n=411) were postsurgical and 31.39% (n=188) were non-surgical patients. TRAM/DKP FDC was prescribed in a diverse group of postsurgical patients (eg, orthopaedic, general and cancer surgery) as well as in non-surgical conditions (eg, lower back pain and musculoskeletal pain). In the majority of patients, TRAM/DKP FDC was prescribed every 8 hours (65.94%) and for 5 days (78.80%). There was a significant reduction in pain intensity throughout the study and 65% of patients achieved ≥30% pain reduction from baseline at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC on day 1. 95.69% of patients were satisfied with the treatment (rated good, very good and excellent on the PGE scale). Overall, 13.9% of patients reported ADRs; most were mild to moderate in severity. The most common ADRs were nausea, vomiting and dizziness.ConclusionThis study showed that TRAM/DKP FDC was used in diverse types of postsurgical and non-surgical patients in the real-world setting in Asia. It effectively reduced pain and was well tolerated with a high level of patient satisfaction.Keywords: anaesthetics, pain management, medicine, back pain

Introduction

Pain resulting from tissue damage after injury or an operative procedure1 2 is encountered frequently in primary care and in hospital settings.3 In the USA alone, more than 80% of surgical patients experience postoperative pain, most of whom report the severity as moderate, severe or extreme.4 Additionally, moderate-to-severe non-surgical pain is common; for example, low back pain affects more than half a billion individuals worldwide,5 and cancer pain is experienced by approximately one-third of patients with cancer.6 Recent studies have reported that 49%–63% of Asian patients who undergo surgery experience moderate-to-severe acute pain.7 8

Despite the range of available analgesics, the majority of patients with moderate-to-severe acute pain report inadequate pain relief,4 particularly Asian patients among whom pain is frequently under-reported and undertreated.9 Importantly, unrelieved or poorly controlled acute pain may transition to chronic pain.10 11 Analgesics with an unimodal mechanism of action are limited in the treatment of moderate-to-severe acute pain due toe restricted treatment duration, dose ceiling effect and/or safety concerns.12 13 In addition, pain can be attributed to more than one mechanism.12 14 Hence, the recommended optimal treatment strategy for acute pain management is multimodal analgesia—a combination of pharmacotherapies from different classes that act through multiple modes and sites of action with minimal adverse events (AEs).15

Opioid and non-opioid analgesics combined at reduced doses provide synergistic, improved and opioid-sparing pain relief for an extended duration with minimal AEs.14 16 One such multimodal approach is the fixed-dose combination (FDC) of dexketoprofen trometamol (DKP), a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) with analgesic and anti-inflammatory actions,17 and the opioid receptor agonist, tramadol (TRAM). DKP is the tromethamine salt of DKP, the S-enantiomer responsible for the pharmacological activity of ketoprofen, which has a faster onset of action, greater bioavailability, and more rapid dissolution and absorption.14 18 TRAM has central, peripheral and local analgesic effects.19 An orally administered FDC of TRAM 75 mg and DKP 25 mg (TRAM/DKP FDC; Skudexa) is optimal for adequate pain relief in a variety of acute pain conditions,20,24 providing superior analgesic efficacy compared with the individual constituents alone.21,23

Based on the results of phase II/III clinical studies in mandibular molar tooth extraction,20 soft tissue surgeries21 and total hip arthroplasty22 involving nearly 1900 patients, TRAM/DKP FDC was approved in Europe in 2016 for the symptomatic short-term (up to 5 days) treatment of adults with moderate-to-severe acute pain.25 The subsequent phase IIIb DAVID study in Caucasian patients with moderate-to-severe pain following dental surgery demonstrated superior and prolonged analgesia with TRAM/DKP FDC compared with TRAM/paracetamol (TRAM/PARA), with a faster onset of action and a favourable safety and tolerability profile.26 More recently, TRAM/DKP FDC has been evaluated in the setting of moderate-to-severe acute low back pain27 and major orthopaedic surgery28 and may play a role in the management of patients with moderate-to-severe acute cancer pain in Asia.29

Despite its availability in Asia since 2018, the characteristics of patients for whom TRAM/DKP FDC is best suited in clinical practice are not well established. Several small case series in Asian patients with acute postoperative30 or non-surgical pain31 showed that TRAM/DKP FDC was well tolerated and provided good pain relief. To address the paucity of real-world data on the use of TRAM/DKP FDC in the Asian population and to explore the type of patients who would have favourable outcomes and achieve treatment satisfaction, the aims of this observational study were to determine the usage pattern, effectiveness and safety of TRAM/DKP FDC in the management of moderate-to-severe acute pain in clinical practice in Asia.

Methods

Study design

Details of the design and methods of this study, the pRospective patient treatment rEgistry of tramadol and dexKetoprofen trometamol oral fixed-dose combination (Skudexa) in mOderate to seVere acutE pain in Real-world setting in Asia, have been reported previously.32 Briefly, this is a phase IV, multicountry, multicentre, prospective, observational, longitudinal, real-world study, conducted in 13 tertiary-care hospital sites (15 principal investigators (PIs)) across the Philippines, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore. Postsurgical or non-surgical patients prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC for moderate-to-severe acute pain and fulfilling the inclusion/exclusion criteria were included in this study. Key inclusion criteria were male and female adult patients, ages 18–80 years, prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC for moderate-to-severe acute pain and patients willing to give consent for the study. Key exclusion criteria were hypersensitivity/contraindications to DKP, to any other NSAID, TRAM or to any of the excipients of TRAM/DKP FDC, or active peptic ulcer/gastrointestinal conditions. The details of inclusion/exclusion criteria have been previously published.32

Study objectives

The primary objective was to understand the usage pattern (patient types, dosing frequency and prescription duration) of TRAM/DKP FDC in patients with moderate-to-severe acute pain. The secondary objective was to evaluate the effectiveness and safety, as well as patient satisfaction, with the use of TRAM/DKP FDC.

Real-world patients

Patients were identified by the PI/treating physicians and were included in the study based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria.32 The selection of patients from inpatient or outpatient settings was based solely on the PI’s discretion and clinical judgement. After enrolment, patients were followed up for 6 days or until discontinuation/withdrawal from the study. As per the approved indication of TRAM/DKP FDC, patients could be prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC up to three times a day and for up to 5 days.

Outcomes: primary and secondary endpoints

The primary study endpoints were the proportion of patients prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC with different types of postsurgical and non-surgical treatments; the average dosing frequency and duration of TRAM/DKP FDC treatment in postsurgical and non-surgical patients. Secondary endpoints were the percentage of patients achieving ≥30% pain reduction at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC; the satisfaction level at the end of TRAM/DKP FDC treatment. Key safety outcomes included the incidence of treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), serious AEs and the incidence, frequency, severity and causal relationship of reported adverse drug reactions (ADRs) and discontinuations due to ADRs.

Study procedures

On day 1, prior to taking TRAM/DKP FDC, baseline pain scores were recorded. Subsequently, the pain scores were recorded at 30 min, 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC on day 1. From day 2 to day 5, pain scores were recorded at 6 hours after the first dose on that day. Although the scheduled maximum duration of TRAM/DKP FDC was 5 days, patients were followed for 1 more day for safety monitoring. Any TEAEs that occurred during the study were recorded for up to 6 days. Patient discontinuations or withdrawals due to ADRs were recorded. Lastly, patient satisfaction with treatment was recorded at the end of treatment.

Numeric Pain Rating Scale

From day 1 to day 5, patients were asked to rate their pain level corresponding to current, best and worst pain experienced on the 11-point Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NRS) ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain imaginable). Pain severity on the NRS was categorised as 0=no pain, 1–3=mild pain, 4–6=moderate pain and 7–10=severe pain.33

Patient global evaluation

Patient satisfaction with TRAM/DKP FDC treatment was evaluated at the end of treatment through a collection of self-reported outcomes based on the 5-point Patient Global Evaluation (PGE) numerical rating score where 1=poor, 2=fair, 3=good, 4=very good and 5=excellent.33

Covariate assessments

All patients were administered a standardised demographic questionnaire during the baseline visit, and data on age, sex, medical history, comorbidities, surgical history, and current ongoing medications were recorded. A previous history of hypertension, hyperlipidaemia or diabetes mellitus was obtained from the clinical interview, and a physical examination and review of laboratory and medical records were undertaken. Information on current surgical and non-surgical indications for TRAM/DKP FDC was captured via a structured questionnaire. For anthropometric assessments, patients’ height and weight were recorded. Administration of all other medications, including intraoperative and postoperative pain medications prescribed along with TRAM/DKP FDC to manage postsurgical or non-surgical pain, was recorded from day 1 to day 5. Comorbidities were defined as medical or surgical conditions other than those leading to the prescription of TRAM/DKP FDC.

Data collection

Study data were collected in an electronic data capture system, the ClaimIt platform (Obvio Health, USA), either via web portal or mobile application (app) formats. For postsurgical patients, study data were captured by the investigator/site staff in the ClaimIt portal throughout the study duration; for non-surgical patients, baseline data were completed by the investigator/site staff in the ClaimIt portal and subsequent data (NRS, PGE and AE) were entered by the patient themselves in the ClaimIt app. AEs were coded using the MedDRA dictionary. The incidence of each TEAE was summarised by MedDRA preferred term.

Statistical analyses

Patients’ demographic characteristics were summarised descriptively and tabulated by surgical status (postsurgical and non-surgical). The frequency and percentage distribution of TRAM/DKP FDC prescriptions were summarised based on patients’ average dosing frequency and treatment duration for the overall population and by surgical status (postsurgical and non-surgical). Descriptive statistics, mean and SD were calculated for pain intensity scores collected at all specified time points. Repeated measures analysis of covariance was conducted to compare the pain intensity reduction while adjusting baseline pain score (prior to first dose), age, gender and body mass index (BMI) with fixed effect for treatment groups in the overall population. Three different approaches—the last observation carried forward (LOCF), worst observation carried forward (WOCF) and imputation (replace missing data with the baseline data)—were used for dealing missing data. To determine prescription status, such as dosing frequency change during the drug use period, the mean and SD of the dosing frequency were calculated for different treatment periods: 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 days. The incidence, frequency, distribution and severity of ADRs, as well as the percentage of patients who discontinued TRAM/DKP FDC due to ADRs, were monitored from the time of patient enrolment to day 6, per protocol design and were reported for the overall population and by surgical status. All statistical analyses were performed by using SAS V.9.4 or higher (SAS Institute). All patients who received at least one dose of TRAM/DKP FDC were included in the analysis population.

Results

Patient demographics, baseline characteristics and treatment compliance

Between 11 July 2022 and 31 July 2023, 612 patients were screened at 13 tertiary hospital sites across the Philippines, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore. Of the 612 patients screened, 9 did not meet inclusion/exclusion criteria for the following reasons: history of peptic ulcer/perforation (n=1); taken listed pain medication or reaction to listed pain medication (n=2); not within the study age range (18–80 years) (n=3) and mild pain (NRS <4) (n=3) (online supplemental figure 1). A total of 603 patients were enrolled in the study (the Philippines (n=234), Thailand (n=202), Malaysia (n=101) and Singapore (n=66)), of which one patient did not start the study procedure and attend visits (hence, post hoc, this patient was defined as lost to follow-up), and 3 patients did not receive TRAM/DKP FDC and were withdrawn from the study. The remaining 599 patients received at least one dose of TRAM/DKP FDC and were included in the final analysis, which comprised postsurgical (n=411) and non-surgical (n=188) patients. Among the patients who discontinued/terminated the study early, 179 no longer needed pain medication (recovered or pain subsided), 30 discontinued due to ADRs, 3 withdrew their consent, 2 discontinued due to other reasons and 2 patients had only baseline data (online supplemental figure 1). No major protocol deviations or site non-compliance issues were reported throughout the study.

Baseline characteristics of the study patients are summarised in table 1. The mean (SD) age of patients was 46.7 (16.41), 47.5 (17.40) and 45.0 (13.89) years in the overall study population, and postsurgical and non-surgical subgroups, respectively; and the majority (61.27%) were female (58.15% in the postsurgical subgroup and 68.09% in the non-surgical subgroup). The mean (SD) body weight was 66.71 kg (14.19). In this study, 30.88% of patients had comorbidities such as hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, diabetes and musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders. Concomitant medication use was reported by 55.09% of patients, most frequently analgesics followed by antibacterial drugs. Baseline patient characteristics, such as age, sex, body weight and blood pressure, were generally similar between the postsurgical and non-surgical subgroups (table 1). At baseline, 62.90% of patients reported having severe pain. The baseline mean (SD) NRS score was 7.0 (1.80) in the study population and was marginally higher in the non-surgical than in the postsurgical subgroup: 7.51 (1.40) and 6.83 (1.92), respectively.

In total, 544 of 597 patients (91.12%) took the prescribed dose of TRAM/DKP FDC for the prescribed duration and were considered compliant. Treatment compliance was 92.45% in the postsurgical group and 87.23% in the non-surgical group.

Surgical and non-surgical patient types in which TRAM/DKP FDC was prescribed

The proportion of patients who received TRAM/DKP FDC for a postsurgical indication was 68.6% (n=411). TRAM/DKP FDC was prescribed in a diverse group of postsurgical patients (orthopaedic surgery (n=245), general surgery (n=148) and cancer surgery (n=18)) (table 2 and online supplemental table 1). The proportion of patients who received TRAM/DKP FDC for moderate-to-severe acute pain for a non-surgical indication was 31.4% (n=188), including patients with lower back pain (n=52), lower limb musculoskeletal pain (n=50), upper limb musculoskeletal pain (n=37), neck pain (n=14) and cancer pain (n=7) (table 2 and online supplemental table 1).

Dosing frequency and duration

The most common TRAM/DKP FDC regimen prescribed was three times a day (every 8 hours (Q8h) 65.94% of patients), followed by every 12 hours (Q12h; 25.54%) and once daily (QD, 8.51%) (online supplemental table 2). However, Q8h (80.78%) was most commonly prescribed in postsurgical patients and Q12h (55.32%) was most commonly prescribed in non-surgical patients. Regarding the duration of treatment, TRAM/DKP FDC was commonly prescribed for 5 days (78.80%), including the postsurgical (76.89%) and non-surgical (82.98%) subgroups, followed by 3 days (11.02%) and 4 days (5.01%) (online supplemental table 2).

Overall, 179 patients terminated the study early as they no longer needed analgesic medication. Although the median duration of treatment for the postsurgical and non-surgical subgroups was 5 days (min-max: 1–5 days), the percentage of patients taking TRAM/DKP FDC gradually decreased over the 5 days. The number of patients who took TRAM/DKP FDC in the postsurgical subgroup gradually decreased from day 1 to day 5 whereas, in the non-surgical subgroup, it remained stable between days 3 to 5 (onlinesupplemental table 3 figure 2).

Reduction in pain intensity

During the 5-day treatment period with TRAM/DKP FDC, there was a significant reduction in pain intensity compared with baseline pain scores in the overall population and in the postsurgical and non-surgical subgroups (p<0.0001) (figure 1, online supplemental table 4). On day 1, 30 min postdose, there was a significant reduction in the mean pain intensity in postsurgical and non-surgical subgroup (mean change: 2.40 and 1.40, respectively). Over the course of 5 days, the reduction in pain was generally consistently greater in the postsurgical than in the non-surgical subgroup.

Mean Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) score over time (N=599). *p<0.05 compared with predose (baseline) for all treatment groups; values represent mean NRS score.

Overall, 58.93% of patients (n=599) achieved a ≥30% reduction in pain at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC (postsurgical 59.12% and non-surgical 58.51%). However, among the patients with day 1 8-hour NRS score data (n=543), 65.0% (353/543) achieved a ≥30% pain reduction at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC (online supplemental table 4). Furthermore, 49% (n=266) of these patients achieved a ≥50% pain reduction at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC. The mean (SD) reduction in pain intensity at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC in postsurgical and non-surgical patients was –3.3 (2.36) and –3.6 (2.64), respectively (online supplemental table 4).

Sensitivity analysis

Of 599 patients, data for day 1–8 hours were available for 543 patients (missing data=56 patients, 9.3%). In a sensitivity analysis performed on the NRS score using several approaches (LOCF, WOCF and imputation), for dealing with missing data, the results on the effects of TRAM/DKP FDC on pain intensity were similar. Please refer to online supplemental material for details.

Patient satisfaction

At the end of the study, patients assessed their satisfaction with TRAM/DKP FDC treatment using the 5-point PGE scale: 1=poor, 2=fair, 3=good, 4=very good, 5=excellent. Overall, 95.69% of patients reported satisfaction with the use of TRAM/DKP FDC as either good (18.53%), very good (40.65%) or excellent (36.51%) (figure 2).

Safety

During TRAM/DKP FDC treatment, 184 TEAEs were reported in 86 (14.35%) patients. All reported TEAEs were non-serious and no deaths were reported during this study. Among the 184 TEAEs, 155 TEAEs in 83 (13.85%) patients were assessed as related to the use of TRAM/DKP FDC (table 3). Of the 83 patients with related TEAEs, 59 were from the postsurgical subgroup and 24 were from the non-surgical subgroup. Among the 83 patients who had at least one ADR, the most frequent were nausea (8.01%, n=48), vomiting (6.51%, n=39) and dizziness (5.67%, n=34) (table 3). In this study, there was no significant difference in the percentage of ADRs across different BMI groups: overweight (BMI 25–29.9, 5.20%), underweight (BMI <18.5, 4.0%), normal weight (BMI 18.5–24.9, 3.0%) and obese (BMI ≥30, 0.80%). During the study, a total of 30 patients (5.0%; 17 postsurgical and 13 non-surgical) discontinued treatment due to ADRs.

Discussion

This study demonstrated the real-world usage pattern, effectiveness and tolerability of TRAM/DKP FDC in the treatment of moderate-to-severe acute pain in Asia. TRAM/DKP FDC effectively reduced pain across diverse types of postsurgical and non-surgical conditions and with a high level of patient satisfaction. TRAM/DKP FDC was well tolerated in Asian patients for the short-term management (up to 5 days) of moderate-to-severe acute pain with a low discontinuation rate. These results confirm the wider use of TRAM/DKP FDC in real-world pain management practice.

In this study, TRAM/DKP FDC was prescribed in 68.61% of postsurgical and 31.39% of non-surgical patients with various medical and surgical indications. Prior studies of TRAM/DKP FDC were limited to a few surgical indications such as abdominal hysterectomy,21 hip arthroplasty22 and tooth extraction,20 26 while non-surgical use was limited to low back pain in a single-centre setting.27 Several small real-world case series studies previously highlighted the use of TRAM/DKP FDC in the management of acute non-surgical31 and postoperative pain30 in Asian patients. This study shows the expanded clinical use of TRAM/DKP FDC for the management of moderate-to-severe acute pain in a diverse group of Asian patients following postsurgical procedures such as knee arthroplasty, thyroidectomy, hip arthroplasty, ligament operation, fracture reduction, appendectomy, hernia repair, musculoskeletal and connective tissue, and non-surgical conditions such as back pain, arthralgia and osteoarthritis, and ligament sprain. This study also demonstrated the effectiveness of TRAM/DKP FDC in treating several types of cancer pain. Notably, Asian experts suggest a role for TRAM/DKP FDC in the symptomatic short-term treatment of moderate-to-severe acute cancer pain.29

With regard to prescription duration and dosing frequency in the real-world setting, the most commonly prescribed TRAM/DKP FDC regimen was Q8h, followed by Q12h, and this differed between the postsurgical (Q8h) and non-surgical (Q12h) subgroups. Nevertheless, for both postsurgical (76.89%) and non-surgical (83.98%) patients, TRAM/DKP FDC was most commonly prescribed by physicians for 5 days, which aligns with the recommended duration (up to 5 days) for moderate-to-severe acute pain.25 Notably, in both the postsurgical and non-surgical patient subgroups, although the median duration of TRAM/DKP FDC treatment was 5 days, there was a decrease in patients receiving TRAM/DKP FDC from day 1 to day 5, indicating improved pain management.

In this study, the mean NRS score was 7.0 (non-surgical (7.5), postsurgical (6.8) patients). The majority of patients (62.9%) had severe pain at baseline. However, in previous studies, in patients with surgical removal of the third molar, the baseline mean NRS score was 5.7 and 24.2% of patients had severe pain26 and, in patients with low back pain, the baseline NRS was 8.8 and 72% of patients had severe pain.27 Furthermore, in the current study, there was a significant reduction in pain intensity at each time point from baseline which continued to decrease throughout treatment from day 1 (p<0.0001), and there was minimal between-group (postsurgical and non-surgical) difference, demonstrating that TRAM/DKP FDC was effective in reducing moderate-to-severe acute pain over 5 days of treatment in diverse postsurgical and non-surgical patient types. Moreover, 65% of patients achieved a mean reduction in pain intensity of ≥30%, however, in a previous study in patients with surgical removal of the third molar it was 40%.26 Similarly, a 50% reduction in pain at 8 hours post the first dose of TRAM/DKP FDC on day 1 was seen in 49% of patients. Previous clinical studies in moderate-to-severe acute pain suggested that a decrease in pain score by 1.3 points is considered the minimum clinically relevant reduction, whereas a 3.5-point decrease is considered very much improved.34 In the current study, the mean baseline pain score of 7.0 was reduced to 4.8 at 30 min, 3.8 at 8 hours on day 1 and 2.2 on day 5, which implied that most patients were able to achieve clinically relevant pain reduction as early as 30 min and very much improvement by day 5. Very much improvement in pain was seen from day 2 onwards (decrease in NRS; day 2=3.8, day 3=4.3, day 4=4.9 and day 5=5.2). Therefore, a better analgesic effect was maintained consistently over the entire duration of this study, confirming the sustained analgesic efficacy of TRAM/DKP FDC in Asian patients. Clinical studies of analgesics generally use various rating scales to evaluate the effects on pain intensity, which limits direct comparisons. Our current findings show that the efficacy of TRAM/DKP FDC in reducing pain intensity is within the same range as that reported previously in Caucasian populations.20,2226

Recently, greater emphasis has been given to include patient-reported outcomes (PROs) such as quality of life, patient satisfaction and adherence to the prescribed medication to explore the overall patient journey.35 36 This is the first study in Asia to evaluate overall patient satisfaction with TRAM/DKP FDC treatment using a 5-point PGE questionnaire. The majority of patients in our study (95.69%) rated their satisfaction with TRAM/DKP FDC as good, very good or excellent at the end of treatment. In the DAVID study, conducted in five European countries, 81% of patients with moderate-to-severe acute pain rated treatment with TRAM/DKP FDC as good, very good or excellent, compared with 57% and 15% of TRAM/PARA and placebo recipients, respectively.26 This difference may reflect study design (clinical trial vs real-world study), differences in the study population (Asian vs Caucasian), cultural sensitivity in reporting pain, as well as the inclusion/exclusion criteria of the study population.

In terms of safety, 13.85% of patients reported TEAEs related to the use of TRAM/DKP FDC. These were generally consistent with the known safety profile of TRAM/DKP FDC.25 The most frequent TEAEs in this study were nausea, vomiting and dizziness, which were consistent with the ADRs reported in phase II/III clinical trials20,2237 and in case series in Asia.30 31 Previous studies on TRAM as monotherapy in patients following dental surgery there were 11.1% ADRs reported, in patients with low back pain it was 15%, while 9% in patients with abdominal hysterectomy.21 26 37 Whereas 15% of postabdominal hysterectomy patients and 6.9% of patients with cervical sprains reported ADRs with DKP.27 38 Overall, previous studies showed TRAM/DKP has a lower incidence of AEs than TRAM monotherapy and DKP monotherapy.21 26 This study showed no association between ADRs and BMI, suggesting good tolerability of TRAM/DKP in Asian patients across different weight groups. Overall, 30 patients (5%) discontinued TRAM/DKP FDC treatment due to ADRs. However, this is slightly higher compared with the phase III study (2% of patients)21; this difference in treatment discontinuation may be due to study design (clinical trial vs real-world study).

There are some limitations of this study. Postsurgical and non-surgical patients are different groups of patients with different indications and with different medical conditions the results between the groups are not meant for direct comparison, thus should be interpreted with caution. Second, this is a time-bound study. Each investigator was to recruit up to 50 patients in maximum of 6 months, hence our estimated sample size was 750 (from 15 sites and 50 patients per site). Due to the time limitation, some investigators were not able to recruit 50 patients, thus the difference in planned and actual patients. Having recruited fewer patients did not impact our findings because our primary endpoints were not dependent on the sample size. Finally, as this is the first digitally enabled study, the patients, physicians and study teams were not familiar with the data entry portal/app. To mitigate this, training was provided to all the investigators and site staff before the start of recruitment, and a video demonstration was available, along with 24-hour virtual assistance for the study team.

The strengths of this study include the longitudinal study design (with 6 days of follow-up) which allowed analysis of the change in pain intensity by NRS over time in the same patient receiving treatment with TRAM/DKP FDC; and the use of a PRO measure, such as PGE, which provided real-world information about patient satisfaction with treatment. Analysing commonalities and differences in prescription patterns, usage and pain management practices in four different countries enhanced understanding of identifying groups of patients who may need a more tailored pain management plan.

Conclusions

In this real-world study, TRAM/DKP FDC has demonstrated rapid onset of action with sustained analgesia and was well tolerated in a diverse group of postsurgical and non-surgical Asian patients. Most of the patients reported high levels of satisfaction with TRAM/DKP FDC treatment. TRAM/DKP provides a convenient multimodal analgesic treatment option for short-term treatment of moderate-to-severe acute pain in Asian patients.

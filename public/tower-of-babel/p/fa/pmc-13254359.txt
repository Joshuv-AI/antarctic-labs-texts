High-capacity optical communication relayed by multi-core amplifier on deployed submarine seven-core fiber cable
Authors: Yingyu Chen, Jinkai Zhou, Yaolin Wu, Hanyu Gao, Yongguang Xiao, Bofan Guo, Jinpei Li, Wenhao Li, Ruqi Chen, Runzhou Qiu, Jiajing Tu, Cheng Du, Lei Shen, Xiaozhen Xie, Yuelin Yan, Guangquan Wang, Ying Yu, Zhengyong Liu, Shecheng Gao, Zhaohui Li, Chao Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Space division multiplexing (SDM), particularly multi-core fiber (MCF) technology, represents a promising solution for high-density cabling in duct-constrained scenarios such as submarine cable systems. Although recent laboratory experiments and prototype trials have demonstrated the feasibility of MCF-based submarine cables, a complete optical communication validation of a real-world MCF submarine cable link incorporating optical amplifiers has remained a critical necessity. Here, we demonstrate a field validation of the MCF submarine cable communication with an end-to-end system. Based on a field-deployed 7-core fiber submarine cable infrastructure, we achieved a record-breaking net transmission rate of 410.5 Tbit·s-1 over a 140 km 7-core fiber cable link by employing the matched C + L Band cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA. The measurements indicate slight variation in attenuation and crosstalk compared to the pre-cabling conditions, confirming the MCF’s robustness to deployment and submarine disturbances. Furthermore, the developed 7-core MC-EDFAs maintain gains above ~18 dB and ~21 dB over the full C and L Bands, respectively, at an input power of −5 dBm, effectively compensating for span losses of submarine fiber cable. This successful field demonstration marks a crucial step towards the commercial implementation of integrated MC-EDFA SDM submarine optical communication systems for practical high-capacity applications.

Introduction

In recent years, the submarine cable capacity market has undergone rapid evolution, driven by the exponential growth of global bandwidth demand and the rise of cloud computing, artificial intelligence (AI), and hyperscale content providers1. These technological synergies have driven sustained growth in submarine cable systems deployment worldwide2. As conventional single-mode fiber (SMF) communication systems approach the nonlinear Shannon limit3, space division multiplexing (SDM) has emerged as an enormous potential technology to transcend this fundamental barrier4–6, promoting the capacity of submarine communication system towards 1 Pbit·s-1 or even higher to tens of Pbit·s-1 in a single optical fiber7.

Compared to conventional fiber pairs (FPs) expansion strategy8, MCFs not only achieve a higher density of spatial paths9, enabling a faster capacity growth per unit volume10, but also offer superior techno-economic advantages11. Notably, uncoupled-core multi-core fiber (UC-MCF) reduces the computational complexity of multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) digital signal processing (DSP) by minimizing inter-core crosstalk. This offers an advantage of greater compatibility with existing SMF submarine systems over coupled-core multi-core fiber (CC-MCF)12,13. Previously, there have been a few MCF-based transmission systems experimentally validated for submarine and terrestrial cable communication14–19, as summarized in Table 1. State-of-the-Art SDM-MCF Submarine and Terrestrial Cable Systems

Moreover, practical challenges on optical fiber amplification persist in SDM cable system engineering. When deploying 4-core (or more cores) MCFs in submarine communication systems, the conventional parallel single-core erbium-doped fiber amplifier (SC-EDFA) approach runs into space limitations in repeaters. Techno-economic analysis20 reveals that multi-core EDFAs (MC-EDFAs), whether core-pumped or cladding-pumped, could potentially achieve lower cost-per-bit metrics than parallel SC-EDFA arrays. However, core-pumped architecture still requires multiple pump diodes proportional to core count as well as devices for coupling the pump light to the particular core21,22, offering only marginal cost reductions compared to discrete SC-EDFA23. In contrast, in the scheme of cladding-pumped MC-EDFAs, the pump light is coupled to the cladding rather than individual cores24,25, the architecture of which eliminates wavelength-combining components and simplifies multi-core pumping schemes26. Therefore, the cladding-pumped MC-EDFA requires less installation space compared to the core-pumped, and it does not need complex temperature control during operation. This technological breakthrough, combined with UC-MCF’s inherent advantages, manifests that the integrated cladding-pumped MC-EDFA system may be preferable to provide an optimal balance of performance and scalability for next-generation submarine optical fiber cable systems.

Currently, research on the application of cladding-pumped MC-EDFA in multi-core fiber link transmission has highlighted the potential of cladding-pumped multi-core amplifiers for enabling long-distance, high-capacity data transmission and integration within space division multiplexing systems. For instance, T. Rahman et al. demonstrated a transmission capacity of 108 Tbit·s−1 using a cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA27. D. Soma et al. employed a C + L Band 19-core cladding-pumped MC-EDFA to achieve 715 Tbit·s−1 transmission28, with respective gains of 10 dB and 14 dB in the C band and L band. More recently, a transmission throughput of 1 Pbit·s−1 has been realized using a 32-core MC-EDFA co-doped with erbium and ytterbium, providing a gain of 17 dB29. Hitoshi Takeshita et al. further analyzed that26, compared to unidirectionally pumped unidirectional links, bidirectionally pumped multi-core EDFAs yield a 17% increase in transmission capacity under bidirectional signal assignment. Nevertheless, all these successful cladding-pumped MC-EDFA transmission experiments were conducted in a laboratory-based environment, still leaving the practical applicability in deployed submarine cable systems to be validated.

At present, there has no field demonstration of cladding-pumped amplifiers in submarine SDM fiber link systems, particularly in long-haul submarine optical fiber communication scenarios. As a result, the practical applicability of submarine SDM systems lacks sufficient empirical validation. In this work, we designed and fabricated a cladding-pumped C + L band 7-core MC-EDFA prototype and applied it to a 7-core fiber submarine cable link deployed between two islands in South China Sea as shown in Fig. 1. Based on this 7-core fiber submarine cable, a field transmission with a record net capacity of 410.5 Tbit·s−1 in the C + L Band was demonstrated using the proposed MC-EDFA for relaying over a 140 km transmission link. This work extends our previous conference proceedings paper in ref. 30 by i) providing detailed descriptions about the field testbed, cladding pump MC-EDFA, and the configuration of transmission experiment, and ii) presenting more comprehensive characterization and analysis of the MC-EDFA in SDM submarine system, as well as the results of system stability. Results

The cladding-pumped seven-core MC-EDFA

In accordance with the characteristics of the 7-core fiber cable, a cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA was designed and fabricated with a compatible structure. The inset of Fig. 1a shows the 7-core MC-EDFA phototype and the schematic diagram of the cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA with a commercial 976 nm multi-mode laser diode (MM-LD) for pumping. As shown in Fig. 2a, the cross-sectional geometry of the 7-core erbium-doped fiber (7-core EDF) featured a cladding diameter of ~151 μm and core-to-core pitch of ~42.5 μm, maintaining strict geometric consistency with the passive 7-core MCF (Fig. 2b). Owing to such dimensional consistency, the core-to-core connection between passive 7-core fiber and 7-core EDF only introduced additional insertion loss of <0.5 dB. Figure 2c illustrates the connection joint of these two types of fibers, exhibiting low core-to-core alignment tolerance. Figure 3a shows the measured gain for the C Band 7-core MC-EDFA as a function of pumping power, with a fixed signal input power of −5 dBm at 1550 nm. The gain characteristics are highly consistent across all the cores. When the total pumping power reached 12 W, the gain for each core exceeded 21 dB. Figure 3b presents the corresponding gain measurements at 1590 nm for the L Band 7-core MC-EDFA with −5 dBm input power. Compared with the C Band, the amplifier operating in L Band requires a higher pump power (> 6 W) to achieve stable differential core gain (DCG). This is because the relatively lower stimulated emission cross-section in the L Band requires a longer EDF to achieve effective signal gain31, which increases pump attenuation. At low pump powers, doping variations among cores typically cause uneven ion population inversion amplified by the extended fiber length. Thus, higher pump power was launched for L Band amplification to ensure uniform inversion across all the cores, thereby suppressing the gain imbalance effectively. Figure 3c illustrates that the minimum gain values of each core across the C Band from 1532 nm to 1568 nm exceeded 18 dB at an input power of −5 dBm and an average DCG of ~2.8 dB under the pump power of 12 W. For the L Band, all seven cores maintained minimum gain values exceeding 21 dB at an input power of −5 dBm across the 1570–1610 nm range under the pump power of 14 W, as illustrated in Fig. 3d. However, the average DCG increased to 3.6 dB due to the longer EDF length used. The experimental results demonstrate distinct gain uniformity behaviors for the two bands. In the C Band, the DCG remained relatively stable and showed no wavelength-dependent trend. This stability is primarily attributed to the gain saturation effect. However, the L Band exhibited wavelength-dependent gain uniformity characteristics, with the DCG escalating from 2 dB at shorter wavelengths to 5 dB at longer wavelengths. At shorter wavelengths, elevated ion population inversion levels enable sufficient amplification across all cores, facilitating saturation amplification and resulting in a low DCG32. At longer wavelengths, due to the decrease in the quantum conversion efficiency of pumping photons as well as the inter-core concentration deviations of localized Er3+ within the 7-core EDF, the optical signals in the certain cores cannot be sufficiently amplified, leading to the relatively larger DCG.

To investigate the NF characteristics under various input power conditions for submarine transmission system optimization, comprehensive NF measurements were conducted with input powers of −15 dBm, −5 dBm, and 0 dBm for a single wavelength. Figure 3e and f present the NF range across all seven cores at different input powers over C Band and L Band, respectively. Performance variations occurred at shorter wavelengths under low input power conditions (−15 dBm), with the measured NFs showing an increase. However, when the input power exceeded −5 dBm, the NFs for both spectral bands could be effectively maintained below 7 dB. In the long-wavelength range (C Band: 1552–1567 nm; L Band: 1590–1610 nm), the NFs for all the cores exhibited insensitivity to input power variations (−15 to 0 dBm), remaining below 6 dB across all tested power levels. The NF deviation of ~2.5 dB was mainly attributed to intrinsic inter-core performance variations and the measurement uncertainty of input and output losses, which ranged from 0.1 to 0.5 dB owing to the splice variations between the FIFO and MC-EDF. The resultant average NF was 4.65 dB and 4.75 dB for C Band and L Band, respectively, at the recommended operating power of −5 dBm. Experimental characterization revealed that the 7-core MC-EDFA delivers per-core internal output power exceeding 15 dBm (C Band) and 20 dBm (L Band) under multi-wavelength operation with aggregate input power above −5 dBm.

Submarine seven-core fiber cable

The 7-core submarine fiber cable was successfully deployed between Guishan Island and Wailingding Island, which has a total length of 35 km, including 25-km submarine cable under the sea and 10-km long cable on the land to connect terminal houses, as illustrated in Fig. 1b. The cross-sectional structure of the seven-core fiber is shown in Fig. 2b, where its cladding diameter measured is ~150.5 µm and the core pitch is ~42.2 µm, effectively minimizing the coupling among cores. The mode field diameter (MFD) of the seven-core fiber is 9 µm at 1550 nm. Based on this MFD, the effective area is calculated to be 63.6 μm², resulting in a nonlinear coefficient of approximately 1.66 W⁻¹·km⁻¹. The attenuation performance of the fiber from bare fiber to installation was tracked and measured. Figure 4a plots the measured propagation loss of the 7-core fiber using optical time-domain reflectometry (OTDR) during the cabling process and after the installation of the submarine cable. As a result, the transmission attenuation of each core was about 0.2 dB/km at 1550 nm after deployment, which was similar to that of commercial single-mode fiber. Additional attenuation measurement results are presented in the Supplementary Note 2. As shown in Fig. 4b, the experimentally characterized inter-core crosstalk of the deployed 7-core UC-MCF reached as low as −60 dB/km at the operational wavelength of 1550 nm. Furthermore, the loss and the crosstalk in all cores present only slight variations even after the engineering deployment, which is an important finding that was previously unvalidated, thereby verifying that the 7-core fiber cable is highly stable and robust for deployment in harsh undersea conditions.Fig. 4Measured loss performance of the 7-core fiber cable.a Variation in attenuation of the 7-core fiber during the cabling and deployment. b The measured core-core crosstalk (dB/km) in the 7-core fiber cable at 1550 nm.

Submarine SDM transmission testbed

To validate the performance of the deployed 7-core fiber submarine cable and the matched 7-core MC-EDFA, a testbed for the SDM transmission system was constructed, as shown in Fig. 5. At the transmitter, a 16 QAM signal at 24.5 GBaud with a roll-off factor of 0.01 was generated by modulating a tunable external cavity laser (ECL) with 100 kHz linewidth using a dual-polarization IQ modulator (DP-IQM). The modulator was driven by a four-channel arbitrary waveform generator (AWG, Keysight M8195A) operating at 64 GSa·s-1 with a 3-dB bandwidth of 25 GHz. For the WDM dummy channel, another two ECLs were tuned to neighboring wavelengths adjacent to the test channel, which were modulated with different data information using the same method. C and L band waveshapers (WSPs) were employed to shape the ASE sources to generate a 25 GHz channel space signal and carve a notch to accommodate the test channel and neighbor channels. Subsequently, all the channels were combined as 374 WDM channels across both the C Band (1529.05–1566.2 nm) and the L band (1569.05–1608.83 nm). Then the combined signal was split into two beams. One beam was injected into the test core, while the other one was divided into six parts, which were amplified through an EDFA with flat gain and then launched into the remaining six cores. Before injection, decorrelation was achieved via fiber jumpers with different lengths, emulating realistic inter-core crosstalk conditions. Figure 6a illustrates the input optical spectrum of 374 WDM channels after the launching signal power pre-emphasis and the output optical spectrums of all the cores from the submarine optical cable. The submarine transmission link was constructed by cascading two 70-km-long fiber spans. Each fiber span was realized by splicing two 7-core fibers in the deployed cable precisely. As a result, a total transmission length of 140 km was achieved. Each span exhibited a total loss of ~20 dB, including the fiber propagation loss, splicing loss, and all the insertion losses from fan-in/fan-out components, which are provided in Supplementary Note 3. To compensate for the transmission link loss, a 7-core MC-EDFA prototype was connected between the two fiber spans for relaying optical signals. To achieve simultaneous amplification in both the C and L Bands, the 7-core MC-EDFA employed the multi-core wavelength division multiplexing (MC-WDM) couplers to connect the C and L Bands MC-EDFAs in parallel. The 7-core MC-EDFA prototype was placed at the onshore landing station alongside the transceiver equipment, as illustrated in inset of Fig. 1a.

At the receiver, the test signal in a particular core was firstly demultiplexed into C band and L band channels and then pre-amplified via band EDFA, which were subsequently received by a coherent receiver. The resulting four in-phase/quadrature (I/Q) electrical signals from both polarization states were captured using a digital storage oscilloscope (DSO) with a bandwidth of 36 GHz and a sampling rate of 80 GSa·s-1 for offline digital signal processing (DSP).

Transmission performance

Figure 6b shows the bit-error-rate (BER) values for all 374 WDM channels in all seven cores. Some fluctuations in BER between cores were observed due to core-to-core variations in the EDFA gain spectrum in combination with NF and loss variation between fiber cores. Spectral shaping was applied via the WSPs to pre-compensate for the gain profile of the 7-core MC-EDFA, thereby minimizing the difference of power and SNR across the output spectrum. However, the wavelength-dependent NF effect on BER was not eliminated completely. In both C Band and L Band regimes, the higher noise at shorter wavelengths resulted in correspondingly higher BER. Nevertheless, the BER for all the channels fell below the 20% soft-decision FEC threshold of 2.4 × 10−2, enabling successful data transmission with an aggregate capacity of 513.1 Tbit·s−1 (net 410.5 Tbit·s−1) and a capacity-distance product of 71.8 Pbit·s−1·km−1 (net 57.5 Pbit·s−1·km−1). To investigate the stability of a 7-core fiber submarine system equipped with a 7-core MC-EDFA, we continuously monitored the communication performance of a 1550 nm signal transmitted through the central core (Core 1) and a surrounding core (Core 2) over 24 h. The output signals from both cores were simultaneously captured and demodulated at three-minute intervals. As illustrated in Fig. 6c, the results show that the BERs of both cores remained stable, fluctuating only within a narrow range. The average BERs for the central core and the surrounding core were 1.2 × 10−2 and 1.7 × 10−2, respectively. Furthermore, no evident correlation was observed between the BERs of the two cores. This independence suggests that the BER variations are likely attributable to the independent fluctuations in the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of each core.

Discussion

This work demonstrated high-capacity transmission in a field-deployed submarine SDM link using cladding-pumped MCF amplifier, achieving end-to-end validation of multi-core fiber application in a submarine cable system. Compared with previous multi-core fiber submarine cable systems, the capacity and cable laying length in this work surpass those of prototype communication systems, providing further verified characteristics of submarine MCF communication system. Based on a 140 km SDM cable link, a net transmission capacity of 410.5 Tbit·s−1 has been achieved, which is the highest record in submarine MCF transmission demonstrations. Moreover, in this work, the multiple spatial channels inherent in MCFs enable hundreds Tbit·s−1 transmission capacity using only a single fiber, greatly enhancing the efficiency of space utilization in submarine cable compared with the current traditional SMF systems with high capacity that employ FPs multiplexing technology to increase transmission capacity33–41, as shown in Fig. 7. It is worth noting that while the representative submarine systems plotted in Fig. 7 are typically commercial repeatered systems spanning thousands of kilometers, our result demonstrates the achievable capacity density in a 140 km SDM transmission using a submarine cable without undersea amplification as a proof of concept. Despite the difference in transmission reach, this comparison highlights the potential of MCF technology, presenting superior capacity per fiber, which is a crucial increase for the limited space of submarine cable systems. Although cladding-pumped MC-EDFAs benefit from a simple configuration and low cost, they suffer from limited pump power conversion efficiency (PCE) owing to the fact that part of the pumping light propagating in the cladding region is not absorbed thoroughly in the EDF, which can be enhanced by increasing the overlapping area of signal and pump light42. To enhance the pump PCE in conventional cladding-pumped architectures, which constitutes a critical bottleneck for power-constrained submarine repeaters, we developed an optimized double annular-cladding multicore erbium-doped fiber (DAC-MCEDF). This design incorporates a depressed-index, fluorine-doped inner cladding to better guide the multicore pump field. The DAC-MCEDF maintains the same outer and core diameters as standard passive 7-core fibers, demonstrated in Fig. 8a, ensuring full geometric compatibility. Figure 8b compares the refractive index profiles of the standard double-cladding multicore erbium-doped fiber (DC-MCEDF) used in the previous section and the proposed DAC-MCEDF. The amplification model in simulation was based on the rate equations for a multi-core structure presented in ref. 43. Inter-core crosstalk was neglected, and the pump power was assumed to be uniformly distributed throughout the cross-section. The erbium ion concentration was set to 1 × 1025 ions·m−3, with an absorbing cross-section of 1.897 × 10−25 m2 at 980 nm. The EDF lengths were set to 5 m for the C Band and 47 m for the L Band. The configuration of the simulation is detailed in the Supplementary Note 5. The PCE and gain were calculated with respect to pump power for two types of EDFs at 1550 nm and 1590 nm, assuming an input power of −5 dBm. The experimental results using DC-MCEDF were employed for verification and comparison, as shown in Fig. 8c-f. The PCE is defined as (Pout − Pin)/Ppump, where Pin, Pout, and Ppump denote the total power of the signal input, output, and pumping light, respectively. The experimental results of each band for the DC-MCEDF exhibit an agreement with the numerical calculation, particularly in terms of the overall trend. The simulations indicated that the DAC-MCEDF structure exhibited superiority over the DC-MCEDF structure in both gain and PCE. For the C Band, as shown in Fig. 8c, at a pump power of 10 W, the simulated PCE of the DAC-MCEDF reached 7.5%, compared to only 4% for the DC-MCEDF. Correspondingly, the DAC-MCEDF provided a gain of over 25 dB, whereas the DC-MCEDF offered ~22 dB, as shown in Fig. 8e. The L Band demonstrated even higher PCE and gain, as depicted in Fig. 8d and f. Experimental results indicated that the PCE for DC-MCEDF reached 6.2% at a pump power of 14 W, whereas the simulated results for DAC-MCEDF demonstrated a superior PCE of 16.7%, which exhibits a great potential of the DAC-MCEDF design. It is worth noting that the PCE of the L Band is higher than that of the C Band in both experiment and simulation. This is primarily attributed to the distinct optimal fiber lengths required for each band in a cladding-pumped architecture. The short fiber required for the C Band limits pump absorption due to the low core-to-cladding ratio, resulting in pump leakage. In contrast, the L Band necessitates a much longer fiber to utilize ASE re-absorption, naturally facilitating fuller pump absorption and thus increasing the PCE. To assess whether these optical characteristics meet the energy budget requirements of undersea repeater, we compared the total electrical power consumption of the 7-core cladding-pumped MC-EDFA with an equivalent array of seven core-pumped SC-EDFAs (see Supplementary Note 4 for detailed experimental setup and results). In the C Band, the MCF system consumes slightly more electrical power (30.0 W) than the aggregate SC-EDFAs array (23.1 W). However, in the L Band, the MC-EDFA (34.8 W) demonstrates an energy advantage compared to the equivalent SC-EDFAs array (74.2 W). While cladding-pumped EDFAs typically exhibit lower optical PCE than core-pumped SC-EDFAs due to reduced signal-pump overlap, they benefit from the superior electrical-to-optical efficiency of multimode pump. This trade-off results in approximately 53% power savings in the L Band. Furthermore, the resulting per-core power consumption (4.3–5.0 W) of the MC-EDFA falls well within the typical power budget in commercial submarine repeater for one fiber core (~6 W)44, confirming that the cladding-pumped architecture remains compatible with the power feeding constraints of submarine systems. In summary, by optimizing the EDF structure to overcome the PCE limitations of cladding-pumped MC-EDFAs, the engineering deployment of MCFs in long-haul submarine repeater links can be further advanced. In this study, the feasibility of cladding-pumped MC-EDFAs for submarine communication systems was systematically validated through field trials and the numerical simulations, supporting their practical applicability in real underwater transmission environments. Methods

Design and fabrication of cladding-pumped seven-core MC-EDFA

In accordance with the characteristics of the 7-core fiber cable, a cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA was designed and fabricated with a compatible structure. The inset of Fig. 1a shows the schematic diagram of the cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA with the MM-LD for pumping. A multi-mode fiber was attached closely to the passive 7-core fiber, which was spliced directly to a customized 7-core EDF with double claddings via a commercial splicer (Fujikura FSM-100P+). The pump coupling combiner employed a down-tapered coreless fiber across the passive 7-core fiber and the 7-core EDF. To ensure the high accuracy of the core position and the precise control over core-to-core pitch, the manufacturing technology for multicore EDF has been improved from the stack-and-draw method to the drilling method45, which utilizes high-precision deep-drilling equipment. Moreover, the drilling method can also avoid the drawing deformation caused by the stack-and-draw approach, achieving a core-to-core pitch deviation of less than 0.5 μm and ensuring that the core roundness is consistent with that of the preform. Typically, the deformation of the erbium-doped core rod leads to mode field distortion, which in turn affects the gain performance. Therefore, the roundness of the core rod must be strictly controlled. In this work, prior to the deposition, stringent quality inspections were conducted on quartz reaction tubes to exclude poor roundness or uneven wall thickness. To mitigate core deformation during the fiber drawing, the co-doping ratio of Al and P elements was optimized. An excessively high Al doping concentration would lead to insufficient softening of the core rod. The hard-core rod needs a higher hydrogen-oxygen flame flow rate during the collapsing process, which may cause local overheating and degrade core roundness. During the deposition, an intermediate quartz layer was introduced between the fluorine-doped cladding and the doped core to control the roundness of core rod. Furthermore, the tube collapse was conducted under the slightly positive pressure with multiple and controlled thinning steps to prevent deformation from aggressive thinning. Those process optimizations ensure that multicore EDFs remain consistent with the designed structure and the dimensional compatibility between the erbium-doped fiber and the passive 7-core fiber, as demonstrated in Fig. 2.

Regarding the MC-EDFA configurations, different lengths of 7-core EDF for respective spectral bands were employed: i.e., a 5 m long 7-core EDF segment was employed for the signal amplification in the C band, while an extended 7-core EDF in a length of 47 m was utilized for L Band operation. The cladding-pumped 7-core MC-EDFA employed dual 976 nm MM-LDs per band. The C band 7-core MC-EDFA adopted a backward pumping scheme. However, given the substantially longer L band EDF (around 9.4 times longer than that used in C band), exclusive backward pumping light would induce a severe increase of NF due to insufficient population inversion at the fiber front end46. Therefore, the L band 7-core MC-EDFA incorporated a bidirectional pumping architecture to balance forward and backward pump power distribution. Through comprehensive parametric analysis, the pumping configuration was optimized with 12 W and 14 W pump power allocated for C Band and L Band, respectively, which was a tailored solution satisfying both gain requirements and noise figure constraints.

Fabrication and installation of submarine seven-core fiber cables

The submarine MCF cable was installed between Wai Ling Ding Island and Gui Shan Island (Zhuhai City, China), which are located in the South China Sea, as shown in Fig. 1b. The laying process of the submarine MCF cable is detailed in the Supplementary Note 1. The inset of Fig. 1a shows the structure of the submarine fiber cable and its internal structure, detailed in Fig. S1a. The submarine cable type is a commonly used double-armored (DA) cable47. The inner part is the optical fiber unit containing four 7-core fibers and other fibers. Then the optical fiber unit was armored with multiple layers of steel wires, waterproof material, and an aramid yarn layer. Ultimately, the outer diameter of the fabricated submarine optical cable was 40.2 mm.

The 7-core fiber utilized in this transmission experiment is composed of one core positioned at the center and six additional cores arranged in a hexagonal distribution within the fiber, as illustrated in the Fig. 2b. To further mitigate coupling effects, a trench with a lower refractive index was incorporated surrounding each core. The average splicing loss of the seven-core fiber was ~0.36 dB per splicing point throughout field deployment, and the statistics of the fusion splicing detailed in the Supplementary Note 3. This achievement was realized through the precise implementation of ultra-low cleaving angles (<0.2°) combined with advanced splicer programming that incorporated adaptive core-to-core alignment algorithms.

Spectral shaping of the communication system

Basically, the SNR of the transmission system is impacted by the NF and gain difference of the 7-core MC-EDFA, the wavelength response of the modulator and receiver, as well as the wavelength-dependent fiber loss. Prior to the signal transmission, the launching signal spectrum of each core was optimized to balance the SNR at different wavelengths in the system. Firstly, the received SNR was estimated via an optical spectrum analyzer (OSA) by transmitting the WDM signals generated by the ASE sources and WSPs with a flatness of less than 2 dB. Secondly, proper adjustment of the signal power was performed using a closed-loop feedback scheme. A control program monitored the received SNR calculated by the OSA and iteratively updated the WSP attenuation profiles. Since the MC-EDFA’s output spectrum depends on the input power, a gradient descent algorithm was used to calculate the necessary power adjustments. This process was repeated until the SNR difference across all wavelengths dropped below a preset threshold.

Offline DSP of the communication system

The offline DSP implemented in this system includes the following procedures: IQ orthogonalization, compensation of chromatic dispersion through a frequency-domain finite impulse response (FIR) filter, equalization using a 31-tap multi-modulus algorithm (MMA) based 2×2 multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO), compensation for frequency offset, and carrier phase recovery. A real-valued least-mean-square (LMS) adaptive filter48 was implemented in the coherent DSP pipeline to mitigate IQ timing skew impairments. These timing discrepancies originate from differential path delays in extended radio frequency (RF) cabling and non-ideal RF component matching within the transmitter assembly. The LMS-based compensation module was introduced before the BER calculation stage to ensure an accurate evaluation of the system performance.

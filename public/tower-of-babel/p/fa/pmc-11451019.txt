The influence of proliferative tissue on Hounsfield unit and its correlation with BMD in middle-aged and elderly patients with lumbar degenerative diseases
Authors: Ningning Feng, Yishu Zhou, Xing Yu, Wenhao Li, Ziye Qiu, Guozheng Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe difference of Hounsfield Unit (HU) value in different regions of L3 vertebra in middle-aged and elderly patients with lumbar degeneration diseases (LDD) was analyzed. To investigate the influence of proliferative tissue on HU value of cancellous bone and its correlation with bone mineral density (BMD).MethodsThe medical records of middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD in our hospital from December 2020 to December 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. The patients were divided into osteophyte group and no-osteophyte group according to the presence or absence of osteophyte formation on lumbar spine X-ray. In osteophyte group, cancellous bone HU value, containing cortical bone overall HU value and containing osteophyte overall HU value in L3 vertebra were measured on the lumbar CT cross-section. In no-osteophyte group, only the cancellous bone HU value and the containing cortical bone overall HU value were measured. Differences in HU value in different regions of the L3 vertebral body were compared within and between groups of middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD, respectively. To investigate its effect on cancellous bone HU measurements and to do a correlation analysis with patients’ BMD.ResultsA total of 115 patients with LDD were included in this study, including 65 males and 50 females, with an average age of 67.83 ± 6.59 years. The results of the study showed no statistical differences in age (P = 0.15), gender (P = 0.57), smoking (P = 0.88), drinking history (P = 0.76), medical history (P > 0.05) and BMI(P = 0.29) between the two groups. In osteophyte group, the mean cancellous bone HU value was 98.00 ± 25.50 HU, the containing cortical bone overall HU value was 189.02 ± 46.18 HU, and the containing osteophyte overall HU value was 232.69 ± 56.01 HU. The overall HU values containing cortical bone and containing osteophyte were significantly higher than cancellous bone HU value (P < 0.001). In no-osteophyte group, the mean cancellous bone HU value was 102.04 ± 19.64 HU, and the containing cortical bone overall HU value was 175.00 ± 28.97 HU, which was statistically significantly different (P < 0.001). There was no significant difference in cancellous bone HU value and the containing cortical bone overall HU value between the two groups (P > 0.05). The results of the Pearson correlation analysis showed a significant correlation between the cancellous bone HU value of the L3 vertebrae and the QCT BMD value of the patients (r = 0.95, P < 0.001). However, there was no significant correlation between containing cortical bone overall HU value and containing osteophyte overall HU value and the patient’s QCT BMD value (P > 0.05).ConclusionsVertebral HU value is an alternative measurement that effectively reflects the patient’s BMD. In middle-aged and elderly LDD patients, HU values in different areas of L3 vertebra are significantly different, and hyperplastic tissues such as cortical bone and osteophytes may exponentially lead to higher HU value in patients. Compared with other measurement areas, vertebral cancellous bone HU value have the advantage of accurately assessing patients’ BMD.

Introduction

Computed Tomography (CT) technology has become an indispensable imaging method in clinical practice due to its wide popularity, convenience and rapidity of application [1]. The HU value is the value corresponding to the X-ray attenuation coefficient of each tissue in the CT image. Studies have been shown that it is significantly correlated with patient’s BMD [2–4]. In recent years, it has been widely used in assessing patients’ BMD and predicting osteoporosis-related complications [5–9]. With the aging of the population, more and more middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD are often combined with bone loss or osteoporosis. Evaluating patients’ BMD is the key for spine surgeons to determine surgical method and regular perioperative anti-osteoporosis treatment [10]. Currently, the common methods recommended by WHO for the diagnosis of osteoporosis are dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) and quantitative CT (QCT) [11, 12]. Due to the limitation of its measurement principle, DEXA can only measure the bone density of the vertebral body by plane, and interference such as cortical bone, osteophytes, posterior vertebral accessory structures, and aortic calcification cannot be excluded [13]. Therefore, the DEXA measurement results are often falsely elevated, especially in middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD, whose results are more susceptible to interference [14, 15]. QCT can distinguish cortical bone from cancellous bone, but the examination often requires the help of professional software, which is costly, large radiation and low clinical promotion. Currently, it is more used in scientific research [16]. DEXA measurements may not be a true reflection of the patient’s BMD given the interference of proliferative tissue with the results. However, there are no relevant clinical studies on the severity of the impact of proliferative tissue on the overall bone mineral density of patients. In this study, HU value in different areas of L3 vertebral cross section in middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD were measured to investigate the influence of proliferative tissue on cancellous bone HU value and its correlation with BMD.

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The basic data of middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD treated in our hospital from December 2020 to December 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. Inclusion criteria: 1.Define the diagnosis of LDD (including lumbar disc herniation, lumbar spinal stenosis, and lumbar spondylolisthesis) based on the patient’s imaging examination. Osteophyte formation was confirmed in the osteophyte group by lumbar spine X-ray. 2.Age ≥ 45 years old. 3.Patients have lumbar CT and QCT examination. Exclusion criteria: 1.Previous lumbar internal fixation surgery. 2.Intravertebral tumor, fracture, infection, or vertebral endplate abnormality. 3.Taking drugs affecting bone metabolism in the past year or with diseases affecting bone metabolism. We also accounted for BMI, the medical history of smoking, drinking, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hyperlipidemia, and fatty liver disease among enrolled patients.

The clinical study was approved by the Ethics Committee of our Hospital. All procedures performed on this study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the 1964 Helsinki declaration. Written informed consent will be obtained from each participant before any study-related procedures are initiated.

Measurement of HU value in different regions of the L3 vertebra cross section

The CT machine is a Siemens dual source CT. Scanning conditions: tube voltage 120 kV, tube current 355 mA, layer thickness 5 mm, layer spacing 5 mm, bone window width 1500 HU, window position 500 HU. The conventional bone window (window width 1500Hu, window position 500Hu) was selected in the PACS system (GE Company of the United States), and the L3 vertebral cancellous bone HU value, overall HU value of containing cortical bone and containing osteophyte were measured in the two groups of patients. All measurement operations were performed by two radiologists respectively, and the specific measurement methods are shown in Figs. 1–3.

Measurement of cancellous bone HU value: Under the conventional bone window, cancellous bone HU value were measured at three levels of the L3 vertebra, including the inferior level of the superior endplate (5 mm from the superior endplate), the middle level of the vertebral body, and the superior level of the inferior endplate (5 mm from the inferior endplate) [17]. During the measurement, an oval region of interest (ROI) as large as possible was drawn inside the cancellous bone, avoiding pericortical bone and local hyperplasia area outside the vertebra, and HU value in ROI were automatically read. The average of the three measurement plane was taken to represent the cancellous bone HU value of that vertebra (Fig. 1).

Measurement of containing cortical bone overall HU value: Under the conventional bone window, containing cortical bone overall HU value were measured at three levels of the L3 vertebra, including the inferior level of the superior endplate (5 mm from the superior endplate), the middle level of the vertebral body, and the superior level of the inferior endplate (5 mm from the inferior endplate). When measuring, local proliferative and osteophytes were avoided, and the self-drawing function of PACS system was used to draw ROI around cortical bone as much as possible, and HU value in ROI were automatically read. The average of the three measurement plane was taken to represent the containing cortical bone overall HU value of that vertebra (Fig. 2).

Measurement of containing osteophyte overall HU value: Under the conventional bone window, according to the size of osteophyte, 2–3 scanning layers were selected to measure the containing osteophyte overall HU value of the L3 vertebra. The self-drawing function of PACS system was used to draw a ROI around osteophyte as much as possible, and HU value in ROI were automatically read. The average of the different measurement levels was taken to represent the containing osteophyte overall HU value of that vertebra (Fig. 3).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 and Graph prism 9 were used for data analysis and plotting. All continuous variables in this study conformed to normal distribution and were expressed as Mean ± SD. Intraclss correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to assess the intra- and inter-measurer agreement of HU value measurements, with ICC ≥ 0.8 indicating reliable agreement. Independent samples t-tests were used to compare HU value in different regions of the L3 vertebrae within and between the two groups. Pearson correlation analysis was performed between HU value in different areas of L3 vertebra and BMD value measured by patients’ QCT. P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

Results

Clinical demographic data

A total of 115 middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD were included in this study (Table 1). There were 65 males and 50 females with an average age of 67.83 ± 6.59 years. Based on the presence or absence of osteophyte in the patients’ lumbar spine X-rays, the included patients were divided into the osteophyte group and the no-osteophyte group. There were 61 patients in osteophyte group, including 36 males and 25 females, with an average age of 68.67 ± 6.04 years. There were 54 patients in no-osteophyte group, including 29 males and 25 females, with an average age of 66.89 ± 7.10 years. There were no significant difference in gender and age between the two groups (P > 0.05). Among the smoking and drinking history that may affect the patients’ BMD, there were 20 patients with a smoking history and 22 patients with a drinking history in the osteophyte group. There were 17 patients with a smoking history and 18 patients with a drinking history in the no-osteophyte group. There was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of smoking and drinking history (P > 0.05). The medical history of the two groups was collected, and there was no significant difference in the history of hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hyperlipidemia and fatty liver between the two groups (P > 0.05). The mean BMI of osteophyte group was 24.5 ± 3.2 kg/m2, and that of no-osteophyte group was 24.0 ± 2.9 kg/m2, with no significant difference between the two groups (P > 0.05).

Intraclss correlation coefficient (ICC)

The HU measurements of different regions of L3 vertebra showed reliable agreement between two measurements by a single surveyor and between two measurements by two surveyors (ICC > 0.8).

Measurement results of HU value in different regions of patients in two groups

In this study, L3 vertebral body cancellous bone HU value, overall HU value containing cortical bone, and overall HU value containing osteophyte were measured in the osteophyte group of patients. In no-osteophyte group, L3 vertebral body cancellous bone HU value and overall HU value containing cortical bone were measured separately (Table 2). In osteophyte group, the mean cancellous bone HU value was 98.00 ± 25.50 HU, containing cortical bone overall HU value was 189.02 ± 46.18 HU, and the containing osteophyte overall HU value was 232.69 ± 56.01 HU. The overall HU values containing cortical bone and containing osteophyte were significantly higher than cancellous bone HU value (P < 0.001). In no-osteophyte group, the mean cancellous bone HU value was 102.04 ± 19.64 HU, and the containing cortical bone overall HU value was 175.00 ± 28.97 HU, which was statistically significantly different (P < 0.001). There was no significant difference in cancellous bone HU value and the containing cortical bone overall HU value between the two groups (P>0.05).

Correlation between HU value in different regions of L3 vertebra and QCT BMD value

Our study further investigated the correlation between HU value in different regions of L3 vertebrae and patients’ QCT BMD value. The mean BMD value measured by QCT in 115 patients included in the study was 86.02 ± 11.97 mg/cm3. The mean cancellous bone HU value of the L3 vertebrae for all patients was 99.90 ± 22.93 HU, containing cortical bone overall HU value was 182.44 ± 39.52 HU, and the containing osteophyte overall HU value was 232.69 ± 56.01 HU. The results of the Pearson correlation analysis showed a significant correlation between the cancellous bone HU value of the L3 vertebrae and the QCT BMD value of the patients (r = 0.95, P < 0.001, Fig. 4). However, there was no significant correlation between containing cortical bone overall HU value and containing osteophyte overall HU value and the patient’s QCT BMD value (P > 0.05).

Discussion

Lumbar degenerative diseases (LDD) are a series of diseases caused by structural degeneration of the lumbar spine [18–20]. With the increasing aging of the population, more and more elderly patients with LDD need surgical treatment to improve their symptoms and quality of life. These patients are often accompanied by varying degrees of osteoporosis. Poor bone quality in patients is closely associated with poor prognosis and complications of spinal surgery, including implant failure, fusion collapse, screw loosening, fusion failure, etc [21–25]. Adverse outcomes of osteoporotic fractures may also occur in nonsurgical patients [26–28]. These all emphasize the need for early assessment of BMD in elderly patients. DEXA measures BMD based on the relative absorption rates of X-rays from the hip, spine, femur, and wrist, and compares these values with a standard database of young, healthy individuals to calculate a T-score [29]. DEXA has the advantages of low radiation, rapid scanning, non-invasive and repeatability, and is currently the gold standard for diagnosing osteoporosis and predicting the risk of fragility fracture [30, 31]. It should be noted, however, that DEXA measures two-dimensional bone density (g/cm2) rather than volumetric density (mg/cm3), which results in the density of all mineral components encountered in the X-ray path, including osteocytes, blood vessels, and vertebral calcifications being measured.

Therefore, in patients with severe lumbar degeneration, the degenerative structures may falsely increase BMD measurements and T-values, masking the true changes in cancellous bone quality and leading to missed diagnosis of osteoporosis [32, 33]. Some observational studies have found that up to 50% or more of the population’s BMD did not meet the diagnostic criteria for osteoporosis at the time of fracture [34–36]. QCT is a volumetric bone density obtained by analyzing CT three-dimensional volumetric data, which can accurately differentiate between cortical and cancellous bone and is not affected by factors such as vertebral degeneration [37]. However, its disadvantages of large radiation dose, high cost and low penetration rate of basic equipment limit its use in the clinic. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to explore an alternative measurement method with high popularity, simple operation, economical and effective, and can truly reflect patients’ BMD.

CT examination has become one of the most commonly used diagnostic tools in clinical practice, and more patients with potential osteoporosis can be identified by measuring vertebral cancellous bone HU value in CT images [38]. Domestic and foreign studies have shown that there is a significant correlation between vertebral HU value and DEXA T-value, QCT BMD value, which can be used to identify BMD decline and assess the risk of fracture. A number of studies have further clarified the cut-off value of HU value for the diagnosis of osteoporosis and fragility fractures [39–41]. It has been observed that most clinical studies analyzing the correlation between HU value and BMD, as well as the methods for determining the cut-off value for the diagnosis of osteoporosis, all refer to DEXA measurement results [32, 35, 39, 42]. However, DEXA results are often overestimated in patients with LDD. Zou et al. [43] found that the correlation coefficients between HU value and corresponding DEXA results were all greater than 0.7 in patients with mild degeneration, whereas they were all less than 0.7 in patients with severe degeneration. Choi et al. [44] found a strong positive correlation between mean L1-L4 HU value and DEXA measured BMD and T-value in the non-degenerative group, with correlation coefficients of 0.721 and 0.734, respectively (P < 0.001). Whereas in the degenerate group the correlation was very poor, with correlation coefficients of 0.426 and 0.398. This further confirmed that severe degeneration would falsely increase the DEXA T-value, which would further deteriorate the correlation with HU value, and could not truly reflect the BMD of patients. Although HU value is widely used in spinal diseases. However, the effect of cortical bone and osteophyte on the real BMD of patients has not been studied.

Paiva et al. [45] previously explored the impact of lumbar spine fractures, osteophyte, and aortic calcification on the assessment of BMD in patients in their studies. The study showed that neither fracture nor aortic calcification had a significant effect on the patients’ BMD (P = 0.36, P = 0.09). However, the BMD value measured by DEXA were high in patients with osteophyte. Multiple regression analysis further showed that osteophyte was closely related to patients’ BMD (P = 0.01), and may be a significant influencing factor of the patients’ BMD. Currently, degenerative changes in the lumbar spine are the main cause of low back pain in middle-aged and elderly people [46]. Anterior vertebral margin osteophyte formation is the most common degenerative feature of lumbar aging [47]. In addition, with the increase of age, BMD of middle-aged and elderly patients gradually decreases, and cortical bone strength gradually increases to adapt to stress changes, which may also be one of the factors affecting DEXA measurements. Therefore, our study focused on evaluating the extent of the impact of cortical bone and osteophyte on the patients’ BMD. The patients were categorized into the osteophyte group and the no-osteophyte group based on the presence or absence of osteophyte formation on lumbar spine X-ray of the included patients.

Studies have shown that there is no significant difference in HU value among various vertebral bodies of the lumbar spine [48]. Considering that the scanning level of the L3 vertebra is relatively parallel to the lumbar endplate, and degenerative changes are more likely to occur in the lower lumbar spine, the L3 vertebra was used as the target vertebrae in this study [39, 49]. Studies have shown that the HU value of the vertebral body obtained by manual measurement on CT images has very good inter-observer reliability [42, 50]. In our study, HU values were measured by two experienced radiologists, and there was good agreement between the two surveyors (ICC > 0.8).

The purpose of our study was to evaluate the effect of proliferative tissue on BMD by measuring vertebral HU value. In addition to excluding various factors that may affect the measurement of HU value. The drugs or diseases that affect bone metabolism may also interfere with patient’s true BMD. Uehara et al. [51] showed that oral ibandronate sodium or intravenous zoledronic acid for 1 year could significantly and safely improve BMD in patients with osteoporosis. But long-term use of antiepileptic drugs [52] or anticoagulants [53] may decrease patients’ BMD. In addition, thyroid diseases [54, 55], long-term bed rest [56], and severe burns [57] may also affect BMD of patients. In our study, we strictly controlled the inclusion and exclusion criteria based on patients’ relevant medical history and medication history to avoid the influence of disease or drug on patients’ BMD results. It was also considered that smoking, drinking, BMI and relevant medical history may have an impact on patients’ BMD. Our study included these influencing factors and conducted relevant statistical analysis. The results showed no significant differences in age (P = 0.15), gender (P = 0.57), smoking (P = 0.88), drinking (P = 0.76), relevant medical history (P > 0.05) and BMI (P = 0.29) between the two groups. The influence of these factors on our results was further excluded.

The results of HU value measurements showed significant differences between the HU value of the three different measurement regions of the patients in the osteophyte group. The containing cortical bone overall HU value was about 1.93 times that of cancellous bone HU value, and containing osteophyte overall HU value was about 2.37 times that of cancellous bone. There was also a significant difference of 1.72 times between cancellous bone HU value and containing cortical bone overall HU value in the no-osteophyte group. And between the two groups, there was no statistically significant difference between the cancellous bone HU value and the containing cortical bone overall HU value. The effect of proliferative tissue on BMD has been the focus of various studies. Vertebrae with osteophytes have higher bone density, and the greater the abnormal strength of osteophytes, the higher the increase in BMD of patients [45]. Our study results quantitatively reflected from HU value that cortical bone and osteophytes may have a multiplier effect on the HU results of cancellous bone, which could not truly reflect the patient’s BMD.

This study further investigated the correlation between HU value in different regions of L3 vertebrae and patients’ QCT BMD value. The mean cancellous bone HU value of the L3 vertebrae for all patients was 99.90 ± 22.93 HU, containing cortical bone overall HU value was 182.44 ± 39.52 HU, and the containing osteophyte overall HU value was 232.69 ± 56.01 HU. The mean BMD value measured by QCT in 115 patients included in the study was 86.02 ± 11.97 mg/cm3. The results of the Pearson correlation analysis showed a significant correlation between the cancellous bone HU value of the L3 vertebrae and the QCT BMD value of the patients (r = 0.95, P < 0.001). However, there was no significant correlation between containing cortical bone overall HU value and containing osteophyte overall HU value and the patient’s QCT BMD value (P > 0.05). Based on the above results, we believe that the containing cortical bone overall HU value and containing osteophyte overall HU value may not be of great value in the diagnosis of osteoporosis in middle-aged and elderly patients with LDD. This may be related to the fact that the osteoporotic manifestation is later in the bone cortex and the interference of cortical bone and osteophyte in the measurements. The metabolic rate of normal cancellous bone is as much as eight times higher than that of cortical bone, and cancellous bone is more sensitive to bone loss and response to anti-osteoporotic therapy than cortical bone [58]. This shows that cancellous bone is more sensitive to osteoporosis response, and the measurement of lumbar spine cancellous bone HU value is an important reference value for the diagnosis of osteoporosis. The results of the present study also argue this point that proliferative tissues such as cortical bone and osteophyte may have a greater impact on the measurement of HU value of cancellous bone in patients. Therefore, in the measurement of cancellous bone HU value, attention should be paid to avoid the influence of these factors on the measurement results. At the same time, BMD results measured by DEXA should be considered rationally for patients with obvious osteophytes or other degenerative changes.

This study also has some limitations. Firstly, there are many factors affecting patients’ BMD. This study only considers the influence of patients’ age, gender, medical diseases and vertebral degenerative structure, other potential factors need to be further studied. Secondly, the sample size of this study is small, and the number of cases needs to be increased to improve the accuracy and reliability of the results.

Conclusion

Vertebral HU value is an alternative measurement that effectively reflects the patient’s BMD. In middle-aged and elderly LDD patients, HU values in different areas of L3 vertebra are significantly different, and hyperplastic tissues such as cortical bone and osteophytes may exponentially lead to higher HU value in patients. Compared with other measurement areas, vertebral cancellous bone HU value have the advantage of accurately assessing patients’ BMD.

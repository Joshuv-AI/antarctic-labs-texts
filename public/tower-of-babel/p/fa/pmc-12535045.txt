Exploring the pharmacological action mechanism of chamomile essential oil on the treatment of breast cancer based on network pharmacology
Authors: Guzhalinuer Maitisha, Junhao Zhou, Youyun Zhao, Guangzhong Liu, Yan Zhao, Yi Zheng, Ling Li, Shuxia Han, Li Peng, Ablikim Abliz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChamomile essential oil (CEO) can be beneficial in cancer therapy. The aim of the current research is to explore the underlying mechanism of CEO for breast cancer treatment by network pharmacology approach and evaluate its anti-breast cancer capacity in vitro.MethodsWe extracted CEO from chamomile flowers and analyzed its chemical components by using GC-MS/MS. Network pharmacology method was employed to screen the active components, potential targets and possible mechanism of CEO for breast cancer treatment. The molecular docking was used to validate the results of network pharmacology. Cell viability, apoptosis and cell cycle assay were used to assess anti-breast cancer effect of CEO.ResultsIn network pharmacology analysis, we found the 12 effective components and 265 drug-disease common targets of CEO and among them, five active components and 19 targets were determined as the therapeutic targets of breast cancer. GO results demonstrated that the potential targets of CEO were primarily participated in positive regulation of MAPK cascade, distributed in membrane raft and the molecular functions were associated with protein serine-threonine-tyrosine kinase activity. KEGG pathway analysis suggested that the potential targets mainly involved in several signaling pathways such as PI3K-AKT, cAMP, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, MAPK and calcium signaling pathway. Molecular docking analysis revealed that LYN, LCK, VGFR, MAPK11, MAPK14, PTK2, JAK1, NR3C1 and ESR1 have shown higher affinity with three components from CEO and suggesting that these compounds might be the most effective ingredients against breast cancer. Besides, we found that CEO treatment suppressed cell proliferation by inducing cell cycle arrest in breast cancer cell lines.ConclusionsOur research suggest that CEO inhibit proliferation of breast cancer cell lines through modulating those multi-pathways and multi-targets. The present study not only indicate the therapeutic potential of CEO for breast cancer, but also provide valuable insight into its underlying mechanism.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-025-05126-z.Keywords: Chamomile essential oil, Breast cancer, Network pharmacology, Mechanism, Targets

Background

Breast cancer, which is the most commonly diagnosed type of invasive cancer and accounts for the highest number of cancer-related deaths among women globally [1]. Many risk factors such as gene mutation, family history, age, menopause, endocrine disorders, poor diet, and lifestyle play major roles in breast carcinogenesis [2]. Standard cancer chemotherapy often faces challenges due to the emergence of drug resistance and the occurrence of undesirable side effects, some of which can be life-threatening [3]. Hence, the development of novel medications to enhance breast cancer clinical therapy is imperative. As evidenced by the effectiveness of modern cancer treatment, medicinal plants have emerged as a prominent source of natural products that play a pivotal role in combating and avoiding various forms of cancer [4]. The new compounds with anticancer activity from natural products are presenting new opportunities in the management of cancer with its novelty such as the structural variety, cost effectiveness, fewer side effects in comparison to their synthetic counterparts [5]. Those characteristics makes them much more preferable in cancer treatment and have become popular among scientific filed [6]. In recent times, researchers have conducted many clinical trials and shown great interest in the potential activities of essential oils, mainly due to their extensive utilization in aromatherapy (registry ID: IRCT20180711040432N2, 2019-01-13), treatment of various mental symptoms such as stress, anxiety, depression (registry ID: IRCT201606144281N2), improvement of life quality of diabetic (registry ID: IRCT20191106045352N1) and cancer patients (registry ID: IRCT20100913004736N21) [7–10].

Chamomile (Matricaria recutita L.) is a medicinal plant from the asteraceae family with numerous health benefits. The well-known medicinal plant Artemisia vulgaris, belonging to the asteraceae family, originates from southern and eastern Europe and northern and western Asia, and is presently found worldwide, excluding tropical and polar regions [11]. This term “star” of medicinal and aromatic plants refers to this particular plant, which is renowned for its wide range of aromatic and pharmaceutical attributes [12]. The blue chamomile essential oil (CEO) has been shown to anti-inflammatory, anti-microbial, anti-ulcerogenic, anti-spasmodic, anti-septic, immunomodulatory, sedative, wound healing and management of eczema and skin disorders properties [13]. Besides, it was reported that chamomile have presented antioxidant and anticancer activity [14–16]. Recently, many clinical trails focus on the effect of CEO aromatherapy on cancer depression, anxiety, stress, and pain [17–21]. In breast cancer therapy, Villegas-Becerril et al. found in their clinical trials that the cream made from chamomile could reduce the dermal effects and dermal lesions in women with breast cancer undergoing radiotherapy (registry ID: NCT04116151) [22]. A clinical trial reported that capsules of chamomile could decrease the frequency of vomiting in the breast cancer patients after chemotherapy (registry ID: IRCT2013020912404N1) [23]. In spite of the numerous usage and the recorded anticancer and antioxidant activities of CEO, little is known about its active components and the pharmacological mechanism against breast cancer.

Network pharmacology is the integrative technology of pharmacology and information network that based on system biology, bio-informatics, and high-throughput histology [24]. In recent years, the network pharmacology has been extensively used in the field of pharmacological effects of drugs on diseases and their mechanisms, studying the theory of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and its applications [25].

In this paper, we used to network pharmacology to elucidate the active constituents, potential targets and molecular mechanisms of CEO for treating breast cancer, followed by validation of its therapeutic effects on breast cancer cell lines. The results extend an indication for future research to enhance the understanding of the mechanism of CEO treatment for breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Material

Chamomile dry flowers were obtained from local market of Urumqi, Xinjiang Uighur Autonomous Region of China and kept at laboratory conditions at refrigerator (4 °C) with no direct light until the extraction.

Extraction of CEO

The dried flowers of chamomile were powdered and extracted by steam distillation using a fat extractor apparatus for 2 h at 100 ± 5 °C, and the extracted oils were dried over anhydrous MgSO4, then the pure blue essential oils were obtained and preserved in refrigerator at 4 °C. Analysis of the chemical constituents of CEO

One microliter of sample was injected into gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS/MS) system from Agilent (7890 A-5975 C, USA) after 0.22 μm organic membrane filtration. To separate the components, a HP-5MS capillary column with dimensions of 30.00 mm × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm was utilized. Helium gas was used as the carrier gas, flowing rate was 1 ml/min and split ratio was 1:100. Analytes were separated following program: (a) 50 ℃ for 0 min; (b) increase of 5 ℃/min from 50 ℃ to 280 ℃ and hold for 10 min. For the mass spectrometry, the temperature of 180 ℃ and voltage of 70 EV were set as ionization source condition. The temperatures of the detector and injector were 250 ℃. The isolates were detected by using the full scan mode within the range of 50–500 m/z. The chromatographic peak area more than 0.12% was selected and chemical components corresponding to each peak further analyzed by using NIST508 online library.

Screening of targets of chemical components from CEO

We obtained the canonical SMILES of each chemical composition of CEO from the PubChem database before proceeding. The Swiss ADME was used to submit these canonical SMILES in order to obtain pharmacokinetic parameters, such as blood brain barrier (BBB), gastrointestinal absorption (GI absorption), drug likeness (DL) and P-glycoprotein substrate (P-gp substrate). The active ingredients of CEO were screened by following conditions: BBB is “yes”, GI absorption is “yes”, drug likeness is “yes” and P-gp substrate is “no”. The corresponding drug targets of the screened chemical components were filtered in Swiss Target Prediction database.

Breast cancer target screening

Gene Cards, Therapeutic Target Database (TTD), Drug Bank, OMIM, and PharmGkb database were utilized for target identification in breast cancer. The keyword used across all databases was “Breast Cancer” and the targets predicted in each database were combined to form the comprehensive list of breast cancer targets.

Establishment of drug-target network

The Venn analysis using the R package (version 4.0.2) identified the shared targets between CEO and breast cancer. These overlapping targets were deemed potential targets for CEO in treating breast cancer. Subsequently, the Cytoscape software (version 3.8.0) was employed to visualize the network of drug-target relationships.

Analyzing the network of interactions between proteins

The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was established and analyzed by using the Cytoscape software (version 3.8.0) after uploading the drug-disease common targets into the String database. The interaction score cut-off was set at ≥ 0.9, while the other parameters remained at their default values. The network topology parameters were analyzed by using the “CytoNCA” comes from Cytoscape.

Pathway enrichment analysis

To analyze and visualize the kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway and gene ontology (GO) enrichment data in R software (version 4.0.2), we utilized various packages such as the Dose, Cluster Profiler, and Enrich Plot. For further analysis, we have chosen the top 30 GO terms in biological process (BP), cellular components (CC) and molecular function (MF), along with the top 30 KEGG pathways (p < 0.05).

Molecular docking

Molecular docking simulations were carried out by using the Autodock Vina software (version: 1.5.6). The two-dimensional structures of CEO bioactive components were sourced from the PubChem database. The ligand files in mol2 format of each compound were obtained by using ChemBio3D for structure optimization and energy minimization. The three-dimensional structures of target proteins were gained from the Protein Data Bank database and modified using PyMOL software (version: 2.4.0). After calculating the binding affinity of all active compounds with the target proteins, the models with the lowest energy were selected.

Cells

MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells were acquired from a cell bank. The cells were cultured in a medium containing Dulbecco Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (New York, USA), fetal bovine serum (FBS), streptomycin and penicillin. The cells were grown in a humidified atmosphere of 37 °C and 5% CO2.

Cytotoxicity assay

The cytotoxicity of CEO was evaluated through in vitro assay by using the MTT method. Cells were treated with various concentrations (ranging from 50 to 1000 µg/mL) of CEO exposure for 24 h. The growing cells in a culture medium containing 0.5% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were defined as a control and the cells without treatment of CEO and DMSO were defined as a blank. After incubating the cells for 24 h, 10 µL of a solution containing MTT at a concentration of 5 mg/mL was added to each well. The liquid was then aspirated and the resulting precipitate was melted in 100 µL of DMSO following a 4 h incubation period. After agitating for a one minute, the content of each wells was homogenized on a microplate reader at 570 nm. The essay were proceed three times and the mean results were noted. Apoptosis assay

To measure apoptotic cell death induced by CEO treatment, cells were placed in 6-well plates at a concentration of 1 × 105 cells per well and incubated for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were subjected to treatment with a dose of 0, 600 and 800 µg/mL CEO, respectively. Following overnight incubation, cells were stained with annexin-V and propidium iodide according to apoptosis assay kit instructions. FACS analysis was performed to determine apoptotic events in the different cell populations using Cell Quest software of flow cytometry (Zhongsheng Biosino, China).

Cell cycle assay

Following a 24 h treatment of MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells with different concentrations of CEO (0, 600, and 800 µg/mL), the cells underwent digestion, centrifugation, washing, and overnight fixation using 70% ethanol. Subsequently, the cells were stained with 500 µL of propidium iodide for 15 min in darkness. The cell cycle distribution was then determined using BD FACS Calibur™ (BD Bioscience (USA). Analysis of the data was performed using BD CellQuest™ Pro software (version 6.0).

Statistical analysis

The statistical tools SPSS 25.0 and GraphPad Prism 6.0 were employed to analyze the data collected from MTT. The data was expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and then subjected to comparison using the one-way ANOVA test.

Screening the candidate targets of CEO

PubChem, Swiss Target Prediction and String database were employed to identify potential targets of CEO. Consequently, a total of 311 targets were obtained (Table S2). A comprehensive search across multiple databases including Gene Cards, Pharm Gkb, TTD, Drug Bank and OMIM resulted in the identification of 12,090 potential targets related to breast cancer. We identified 265 common targets out of 311 potential targets related to CEO and 12,090 potential targets associated with breast cancer by using Venn Diagram (Fig. 2A). These potential targets have been identified as effective candidates of CEO for breast cancer treatment. The construction of the drug-disease common target network was accomplished through the utilization of Cytoscape 3.8.0 (Fig. 2B).

Network analysis

Next, the PPI network analysis was proceeded by using STRING database, with a medium confidence level of 0.9 to analyze 265 potential targets. The network topology was analyzed with the CytoNCA plug-in and 187 nodes and 1092 edges larger than the median value are selected. Then, it was further calculated and generated second network with 52 nodes and 514 edges. Finally, the core PPI network was generated with 19 nodes and 184 edges after third time calculation. The core PPI network included the total of 19 therapeutic targets such as androgen receptor (AR), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), estrogen receptor (ESR1), focal adhesion kinase 1 (PTK2), glucocorticoid receptor (NR3C1), heat shock protein HSP90-alpha (HSP90AA1), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF1R), mitogen-activated protein kinase 11 (MAPK11), mitogen-activated protein kinase 14 (MAPK14), phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha isoform (PIK3CA), protein kinase C delta type (PRKCD), proto-oncogene tyrosine-protein kinase SRC (SRC), signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), tyrosine-protein kinase JAK1 (JAK1), tyrosine-protein kinase JAK2 (JAK2), tyrosine-protein kinase ABL1 (ABL1), tyrosine-protein kinase LCK (LCK), tyrosine-protein kinase LYN (LYN) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VGFR or KDR). The results indicated that these proteins might act as therapeutic targets of CEO against breast cancer (Table 2; Fig. 3).

CEO effectively inhibits the growth of breast cancer in vitro

To authenticate the medicinal impact of CEO in treating breast cancer, MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cell lines were subjected to CEO treatment. The results of MTT assay demonstrated that CEO exhibited a distinct characteristic of higher concentration and a more pronounced inhibitory effect on both type of cells (Fig. 6A and Figure S1). The IC50 values of CEO for MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 were measured as 794 µg/mL and 707 µg/mL, respectively. Upon exposure to 0, 600 and 800 µg/mL of CEO, the respective rates of total apoptosis were 4.34%, 27.09% and 30.53% in the MCF-7, and 30.53%, 11.47% and 12.2% in the MDA-MB-231 (Fig. 6B). The results disclosed that the percentage of the apoptotic cells was not higher significantly than the cells that were not treated. Conversely, the opposite impact was observed in cell cycle arrest. As shown in Fig. 6C, the percentages of cells in the G1 phases were reduced, while the G2 and S phases showed an increase in MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7. These research demonistrated that the suppression of breast cancer cell proliferation by the CEO was due to cell cycle arrest rather than apoptosis.

Discussion

In recent years, researchers are actively exploring natural plants to discover drugs that are both non-toxic and effective against tumor cells [4]. Over the course of human history, essential oils have been extensively utilized in folk medicine due to their diverse range of low toxicity and pharmacological applications [26]. The anti-tumor effect of CEO has been documented in limited scientific literature, especially with regards to breast cancer. Recent studies by Tamimi and Mitoshi et al. have demonstrated the cytotoxic effects of CEO on HT-29 and HCT116, respectively [27, 28]. An et al. have reported the CEO significantly suppressed the proliferation and invasion of breast cancer cells through PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway [29]. Zu et al. observed that the cytotoxicity of CEO on prostate cancer cell was higher than on lung and breast cancer cells [30]. Alkhatib et al. found that combination of CEO and chemotherapeutic agent mitomycin C penetrated the cell membrane and directly affected the nucleus, causing damage to Hela cells [15]. Ali et al. evaluated the anticancer activity of CEO on the MCF-7 cells [31]. In this particular research, we observed that the CEO inhibited cell proliferation in breast cancer cells by inducing cell cycle arrest. Our network pharmacology analysis revealed that most of the drug-disease common targets were predominantly associated with various signaling pathways; the 4-Cyano-6-dicyanomethyl-2-methyl-2,5-diphenyl-1,2-dihydropyridine, 1,2-dihydropyrazol-3-one,4-(4-bromomethyl-4,5-dihydrothiazol-2-ylamino)−1,5-dimethyl-2-phenyl and tricyclo[4.2.1.1(2,5) decan-3-ol were essential components of the CEO, exhibited the highest affinity when interacting with nine core targets. Though there some papers have reported CEO is potential in combating breast cancer, however, this is the initial instance we have documented the substantial restraint of CEO against breast cancer and evaluated its mechanism through network pharmacology analysis.

The signaling pathway known as neuroactive ligand receptor interaction primarily encompasses a collection of genes that encode neuroreceptors such as dopamine receptor and proto-oncogene, and it plays an important role in processing environmental information and mediating the interaction of signaling molecules [32]. The discovery of this pathway revealed its connection to specific neuropsychiatric disorders and congenital diseases [33]. Furthermore, multiple studies have demonstrated that overrepresentation of genes primarily linked to various types of cancer within the signaling pathway of neuroactive ligand receptor interaction [34, 35]. Based on KEGG analysis, fifty-three identified targets play a role in the neuroactive ligand receptor interaction pathway including NR3C1 which presents unique affinity with 4-Cyano-6-dicyanomethyl-2-methyl-2,5-diphenyl-1,2-dihydropyridine from CEO. The gene NR3C1, which encodes for a protein called glucocorticoid receptor, is intricately involved in multiple biological process including cell growth, cell death, migration and immune system control, and its crucial function influences the emergence, progression, therapy and prognosis of diverse tumor types [36]. The lower expression level of NR3C1 was observed in breast tumor tissue during recent research on NR3C1 in breast cancer; the agents such as naringenin, cortisol and sinomenine have been demonstrated to enhance the expression of NR3C1 in vivo and in vitro and have exhibited a significant anti-proliferative impact [37–39]. Furthermore, researches have shown that the use of essential oils in aromatherapy could enhance the quality of sleep and alleviate feelings of anxiety in patients diagnosed with cancer [40, 41]. Hence, we hypothesized that the CEO could regulate the expression level of NR3C1 through the pathway of interaction between neuroactive ligands and receptors, thereby exerting its effects on breast cancer cells.

The PI3K-AKT pathway has been found to play a crucial role in most breast cancers and has been associated with proliferation, apoptosis, survival, migration and invasion [42]. Presently, there is immense attention dedicated to the progress of inhibitors designed to target breast cancer within this specific pathway. Furthermore, activation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway associated with reduced sensitivity to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, but suppressing this pathway effectively overcomes drug resistance in breast cancer cells [43]. KEGG analysis revealed that the thirty-four targets are associated with the PI3K-AKTpathway including PTK2, JAK1, JAK2, VGFR, EGFR, HSP90AA1 and PIK3CA. These targets have demonstrated strong binding affinity with 1,2-Dihydropyrazol-3-one,4-(4-bromomethyl-4,5-dihydrothiazol-2-ylamino)−1,5-dimethyl-2-phenyl and methyl octadeca-6,9,12-trienoate from CEO. The gene PTK2, which is called focal adhesion kinase (FAK), shows a high level of expression in several cancer types such as hepatocellular carcinoma, lymphocytic leukemia and lung cancer, and may contribute to cell growth, survival, migration and invasion [44, 45]. A prior investigation demonstrated that PTK2 stimulates survival signaling pathway in breast cancer and has the potential to impede tumor development [46]. PTK2 expression levels in breast cancer tissues were found to be higher than normal breast tissues, making it a powerful prognostic indicator for breast cancer [47]. Lisiak et al. discovered that oleanane triterpenoids down-regulated the expressions of PTK2 and phosphorylation status of respective proteins, thereby effectively suppressed the cell migratory and invasive abilities of MCF-7, MDA-MB-468 and MDA-MB-231 cell lines [45]. Janus kinase (JAK) proteins in cancer cells are widely recognized as important part in cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis and migration [48]. Diverse inhibitors such as ruxolitinib have been developed to target the JAK family, specifically JAK1 and JAK2 [49]. Some authors observed that specific JAK2 inhibitors might be a better approach in treating triple-negative breast cancer [50]. Over the last few years, many researches have revealed that malignant cells exploit the expression of EGFR and VGFR to enhance active targeting [51]. VGFR, which is known as vascular permeability factor, plays an important role in the formation of blood vessels through vasculogenesis and angiogenesis [52]. Elevation of VGFR levels in cells resulted in high rate of metastasis accompanied by migration and mitosis of endothelial cells [53]. EGFR is highly abundant in numerous cancer cell types and functions as a receptor embedded within the cell membrane. The EGFR receptor has the ability to impact a range of cellular pathways, including RAF/MEK/ERK, JAK/STAT, and P13K/AKT [54]. The frequent mutation at the L858R gene has been related to the activation of EGFR in various cancer cells including lung, colon and breast cancer [55, 56]. Various monoclonal antibodies and formulations including cetuximab and nimotuzumab have mainly been employed for inhibiting angiogenesis and gaining clinical approval in order to target VGFR and EGFR [57, 58]. Heat shock protein 90-alpha (HSP90AA1), a crucial molecular chaperone, is released into the extracellular environment and migrate to the nucleus, where it can induce the generation of immune memory and participate in the evolutionary process of tumor formation [59]. HSP90AA1 has a major function in controlling DNA damage, regulating cell cycle and gene expression, and thereby stimulating cell proliferation, metastasis, survival and invasion [60]. Furthermore, many reports suggested that HSP90AA1 shows promise as a powerful target for the cancer therapy [61]. The gene PIK3CA, which codifies for the PI3K, is a crucial component in a signaling pathway that enhances cell viability, growth and metabolic processes [62]. A study indicated that PIK3CA could serve as an unfavorable prognostic biomarker in patients with triple negative breast cancer who experience a poor consequence, suggesting its potential in targeted therapy [63]. Furthermore, we found cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2), an identified targets, also participated in PI3K-AKT pathway in our KEGG analysis. CDK2 is a vital molecule involved in controlling the progression of the cell cycle and frequently exhibits increased expression levels in cancerous cells; the inhibition of CDK2 has been proven to effectively treat various types of cancers like breast carcinomas, leukemia, and lymphomas [64]. Additionally, our findings suggested that the CEO has an impact on the expression of CDK2 through the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, despite CDK2 not being identified as a core target in PPI network.

Calcium signaling pathway is an another important signaling pathway that involves thirty-five identified targets, including VGFR and EGFR. Ionic Ca2+, a vital component of breast milk, controls a multiple intracellular signaling pathways integral to gene transcription, differentiation, proliferation, apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, and cell migration [65]. Makena et al. hypothesized that subtype specific targeting of calcium signaling could be another technique of precision medicine in the triple negative breast cancer treatment [66].

MAPK pathway is one of the suggested pathway in our research that involved twenty-six identified targets including MAPK14, MAPK11 and VGFR [67]. MAPK signaling pathway is involved in the development of several types of cancer and is a target for therapeutic interventions [68]. JNK1 (MAPK8) and ERK1/2 (MAPK3/1) has been implicated in regulating the maintenance of breast cancer stem cells, leading to the development of chemo-resistance and the promotion of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process [69]. The MAPK signaling including p38α (MAPK14), p38β (MAPK11), p38γ (MAPK12) and p38δ (MAPK13), among them p38α and p38β are the predominant form [70]. Previous research have shown that MAPK14 and MAPK11 play different role in breast tumorigenesis and EMT in breast cancer cells by cooperating with TGF-β [71]. Several studies have indicated that inhibiting MAPK14 can reduce the tumorigenic capability in animal models of breast cancer [72]. However, the over expression of MAPK11 is linked to the up-regulation of monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1), resulting in the differentiation of osteoclasts and the advancement of bone metastasis in patients with breast cancer [73]. In addition, a studies have indicated new functions of MAPK11 in the progression of tumors controlled by TGF-1β and VEGF growth factors, even without the involvement of the pro-apoptotic MAPK14 [74].

The twenty-eight identified targets were involved in the cyclic AMP (cAMP) signaling pathway including PIK3CA. The signaling molecule cAMP controls cellular metabolism through the activation of protein kinase A (PKA) and the targeting of exchange protein directly activated by cAMP (EPAC) [75]. The suppression of cell proliferation, migration and cytoskeleton remodeling by stimulating EPAC indicates its potential to act as a target for treatment of cancer [76].

Taken together, CEO might regulate the target genes through those signaling pathways we described above, and exhibits anti-breast cancer efficacy. Despite the many agents have been in clinical use for decades, the new classes of anti-breast cancer drugs should have been developed and our findings will provide new evidence for usage of CEO in the breast cancer treatment. There are also some contrasting researches which published recently have caught our attention. An et al. studied the effective components of commercial CEO and its anti-tumor activity against triple negative breast cancer through PI3k-Akt signaling pathway [29]. Biltekin et al. studied the anticancer activity of commercialized CEO against different cancer cells including breast cancer [14]. Danciu C et al. studied the anti-breat cancer activity of CEO alcoholic extracts [77]. Different from above research, firstly, in our study, we extracted CEO from chamomile flowers using steam distillation method in the laboratory and studied its chemical components. Hence, the concentration of CEO treatment was higher than those experiment described in above literature. Secondly, we identified the main pathway through network pharmacology analysis as the neuroactive light receptor interaction pathway, while the PI3k-Akt signaling pathway ranked third and further research is needed in the future. Thirdly, An et al. conducted cell migration and invasion experiments, but did not further analyze the affect of CEO on the cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. Danciu C et al. found that the CEO methanol extract make cell cycle arrest at the GO/G1 phase, while our experiment demonstrated that the CEO make cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase, it required further research to clarify underlying molecular mechanism.

However, there are many shortcomings presented in our study. Due to the limitation of financial resources, the detailed bioactive compounds in CEO have not been validate yet; chamomile flower is expensive and sufficient amount of CEO hard to collect for animals treatment, hence, in vivo antitumor experiments are yet to be conducted; core targets and involving pathways needs to analysis. Although additional investigation is required to uncover the fundamental processes involved, these discoveries present a fresh concept in utilizing CEO as a possible innovative medication for the breast cancer therapy.

Conclusion

The findings of this research claim that CEO suppress breast cancer cell proliferation by cell cycle arrest and its mechanism might relate to multi-target and multi-pathway. As far as we know, this research is the new network pharmacology based approach to investigate anti-breast cancer effect of CEO. The data we have presents novel understandings to the therapeutic mechanisms of CEO and provide a option for multi-targeted breast cancer treatment.

Feasibility and acceptability of 8-week oral tributyrin supplementation as add on to antidepressant medication in patients with depression: a study protocol paper for a pilot, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Vera Korenblik, Aniko Korosi, Stanley Brul, Claudi Bockting, Max Nieuwdorp, Anja Lok
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMajor depressive disorder (MDD) is a severe mental health condition that profoundly affects both psychological and physical well-being. Growing evidence indicates that targeting the gut-brain axis could present new therapeutic opportunities for MDD, given the role of gut microbiota and their metabolites in its pathophysiology. One promising approach involves supplementation with butyrate, a short-chain fatty acid that has demonstrated antidepressant potential in preclinical models of depression. However, clinical research exploring the effects of butyrate supplementation in individuals with MDD remains lacking.MethodsThis study is a double-blind, parallel, 1:1 randomised placebo-controlled trial. The primary aim of this pilot study is to assess the feasibility and acceptability of an 8-week oral supplementation with tributyrin, a triglyceride form of butyrate (4 g/day), added to usual treatment with antidepressant medication in 24 patients with mild-to-severe MDD aged 18–65 years. Secondary outcomes include changes in depressive symptoms, assessed weekly throughout the supplementation period and at 16 weeks post-supplementation during follow-up, as well as changes in anhedonia and affect, measured multiple times per day via a smartphone application throughout the supplementation period. Additional outcomes include changes in gastrointestinal symptoms, changes in faecal microbiome, metabolites in blood and faeces, inflammation, epigenetic markers, stress and intestinal permeability.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the Amsterdam University Medical Centre, the Netherlands.DiscussionShould the pilot demonstrate feasibility and acceptability, a larger-scale study will be warranted to evaluate the efficacy of tributyrin supplementation in alleviating depressive symptoms. This pilot will provide valuable insights to guide sample size calculations and refine study design for subsequent trials. Ultimately, this research could lead to novel adjunctive treatments for MDD targeting gut-brain signalling, offering new therapeutic avenues for patients with MDD.Trial registration numberThis trial has been registered in the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (registry number: NL-OMON57116).Keywords: Depression & mood disorders, PSYCHIATRY, NUTRITION & DIETETICS, Microbiota

Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a common mood disorder that can significantly impact individuals’ lives, while also imposing substantial economic and societal burden.1 2 Current interventions for depression primarily target the brain and psychological functioning and include pharmacological agents, psychotherapy or a combination of both.3,5 Approximately one-third of patients do not respond to the first pharmacological agent prescribed, and about 15% fail to respond to subsequent treatments with antidepressants and/or psychotherapy.6 Consequently, the need for alternative treatments remains critical in order to alleviate suffering caused by MDD.

Given the potential role of the gut and its resident microbes in the pathology of depression,7 alternative treatments are currently being explored that focus on modulating the communication between gut microbes and the brain with the aim of improving symptoms.8 This paper outlines a study protocol that investigates the use of butyrate, a substance that is naturally produced by the gut microbiota, which is hypothesised to play a key role in gut-brain communication9 and may be involved in depression.10

Microbiota-gut-brain axis in depression

The communication between gut microbiota and the brain is known as the microbiota-gut-brain axis and involves multiple pathways, including neural, hormonal, immune and metabolic routes.11 12 The human gut provides a nutrient-rich environment for the resident microbiota, which can influence various host functions through the production of metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), cytokines, hormones and (precursors of) neurotransmitters.12 13 A striking and consistent finding is that the gut microbiome composition of individuals with depression differs from that of healthy individuals.14 Specifically, depression has been associated with a reduction in bacterial taxa associated with production of SCFAs such as butyrate (eg, Faecalibacterium, Coprococcus).14,16

Several approaches to target the gastrointestinal environment and microbiome composition have been investigated as treatments for mental health disorders, including MDD. These approaches include probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics, postbiotics and faecal microbiota transplant.8 A recent meta-analysis showed that treatment with probiotics—live organisms which when administered in adequate amounts confer a health benefit on the host—has modest benefits in reducing depressive symptoms in patients with MDD.17 These beneficial effects of probiotics have been suggested to occur, in part, through a shift in the production of microbial metabolites. This suggests that direct administration of these metabolites could also represent a viable therapeutic strategy.18

Antidepressant potential of butyrate

One metabolite of interest is butyrate.9 It plays a key role within the microbiota-gut-brain axis and is primarily produced through the fermentation of dietary fibres in the lower gastrointestinal tract,19 particularly by species from the genera Faecalibacterium, Eubacterium and Roseburia.20 Butyrate significantly influences colonic health as the main energy source for colonic epithelial cells, possesses anti-inflammatory effects and helps strengthen the intestinal barrier.21 Additionally, butyrate acts as a histone deacetylase inhibitor, which can trigger various downstream effects through its epigenetic regulation.22

Depression is also associated with other components of the microbiota-gut-brain axis, including systemic inflammation,23 an overactive hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal axis,24 altered neurotransmission25,27 and impaired neural proliferation.28 29 Therefore, it is hypothesised that butyrate may alleviate depressive symptoms by modulating these pathways through its known effects.30 Given butyrate’s broad influence on host physiology, it is unsurprising that butyrate supplementation has been explored in patients with various immune, gastrointestinal and metabolic conditions, including inflammatory bowel diseases,31,34 irritable bowel syndrome,35 36 diverticulosis,37 metabolic syndrome,38 39 hypertension,40 type 1 diabetes41 42 and type 243 44 diabetes.

Our recent systematic review highlighted promising preclinical evidence for butyrate’s antidepressant potential45: the vast majority of studies administering butyrate—orally or via systemic routes—in various rodent models of depression have reported reductions in depressive and/or anxiety-like behaviours. Despite this evidence, the effects of butyrate in psychiatric populations, including patients with MDD, remain largely unexplored. The systematic review only identified one study that investigated the effects of butyrate supplementation on depressive symptoms. This study by Dalile et al46 involved treatment with sodium butyrate capsules (5.28 g/day) in a triple-blinded, placebo-controlled, randomised trial. No significant differences in depressive or anxiety symptoms were found between the butyrate and placebo groups after 1 week of supplementation in healthy, lean males.

Objectives

Building on these promising findings, the primary objective of this pilot, randomised placebo-controlled trial is to assess the feasibility and acceptability of 8-week daily oral supplementation with butyrate added to usual treatment with antidepressant medication in adults with mild-to-severe MDD. Secondary objectives are to explore the changes in depressive symptoms, anhedonia, affect and mood. Other objectives are to explore changes in other measures related to the microbiota-gut-brain axis in order to obtain more insight into tributyrin’s working mechanism. In this study, butyrate will be administered in the triglyceride form, tributyrin, an alternative to sodium butyrate for delivering butyrate to the gut. Tributyrin is a prodrug that releases butyrate molecules on hydrolysis of the glycerol backbone by lipases, which are fat-digesting enzymes that are present in digestive juice.47 While tributyrin is a distinct formulation from sodium butyrate, both increase luminal butyrate concentrations, with tributyrin possibly offering higher bioavailability compared with sodium butyrate.48

Methods

This protocol is reported according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommended for Intervention Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines.49

Study design

The study is a double-blind, parallel, 1:1 randomised placebo-controlled pilot trial comparing the effects of 8-week oral supplementation with tributyrin to placebo, added to usual treatment with antidepressant medication, in adults with mild to severe MDD. At baseline, participants will be assigned to receive daily supplementation with 4 g of encapsulated tributyrin (n=12) or a placebo (sunflower oil, n=12). A total of 24 participants will be enrolled, which is considered adequate for a pilot study designed to assess feasibility and acceptability.50 51 The planned duration of the trial is June 2025 to December 2026. Setting

Participants will be recruited through the Amsterdam University Medical Centre (AUMC), the Netherlands, via (social) media advertisement, the study website (www.butyraatbijdepressie.info) and referrals by general practitioners and mental health services. Data collection will take place during study visits at the AUMC and by at participants’ homes throughout the study period. Participants will make several hospital visits at baseline (week 0), at the midpoint of supplementation (week 4), at the end of supplementation (week 8) and at follow-up visit (week 24).

Participants

Subjects will be eligible to participate in the study if they have depression, as diagnosed with the Structured Clinical Interview for the DSM-5 (SCID-5)52 with a severity of mild or higher, as reflected by a score≥14 on the Hamilton Depression Score (HDRS-17).53 Eligible participants must have already been receiving treatment with antidepressant medication for at least 4 weeks prior to inclusion. They may also be receiving other forms of treatment—except for neuromodulation—such as psychotherapy, which is expected, as current guidelines prescribe psychotherapy in combination with antidepressant medication in moderate to severe MDD.54 Participants will be excluded if they have a severe gastrointestinal disorder or any psychotic disorder, including bipolar disorder (see Box 1 for full selection criteria).

Study intervention

Participants will receive a daily oral supplementation of 4 g of encapsulated tributyrin (Taubiotic, RedoxBox, Spain) or placebo (sunflower oil) for 8 weeks.

This dosage was selected based on estimates as it corresponds to 3.4% to 340% of the total daily demand of butyric acid under physiological conditions.55 Additionally, similar dosages of sodium butyrate have been safely administered in previous studies.3338,40 42 Sunflower oil was chosen as a placebo due to its visual similarity to tributyrin and because it is already a common dietary energy source. Tributyrin is the triglyceride form of butyrate and has greater encapsulation efficacy than sodium butyrate, a more commonly used form of butyrate in clinical research—that is, 500 mg of tributyrin per capsule compared with 150 mg of sodium butyrate per capsule. As a result, fewer capsules are required to achieve the target daily dose, which is expected to enhance the acceptability of the intervention.

An intervention period of 8 weeks was selected, as this duration is commonly used in intervention studies examining prebiotics and postbiotics in MDD17 and is considered appropriate for trials investigating antidepressant medications.56

Both interventions will be provided alongside participant’s usual antidepressant medication. Participants will be instructed to take four soft gel capsules containing either tributyrin or sunflower oil in the morning and another four in the evening, with a glass of water. There are no predefined criteria for discontinuing or modifying the intervention for individual participants. Antidepressant treatment should remain stable, with minimal dose adjustments whenever possible; patients and healthcare providers will be asked to avoid medication changes unless clinically necessary.

To enhance the similarity between the capsules, a lemon-scented insert (ScentCerts, ScentSational Technologies, LLC) will be placed in each bottle. Based on internal assessments by the study team, the tributyrin and placebo capsules were determined to be comparable in visual appearance, texture, taste and smell.

To support adherence, participants will receive daily reminders via smartphone application (m-Path, m-Path Software bv, Belgium) to take their study supplements. Participants will be instructed to avoid any comprehensive changes in their diets, including dietary supplement usage, during the study period. Dietary intake will be monitored through a nutritional diary completed in the 3 days before each study visit, along with measurements of body weight, hip circumference and waist circumference at each visit.

Randomisation and blinding

Assignment to treatment arms (in a 1:1 ratio) will be conducted in Castor EDC57 using variable block randomisation with block sizes of two and four. An independent researcher, blinded to the randomisation sequence, will conduct the randomisation and prepare the study supplements in a blinded manner by using identical labels for both conditions.

This researcher will also count and document the returned capsules during study visits and ensure proper accountability of the investigational product. By maintaining this procedure, both researchers and participants will remain blinded to treatment allocation. The success of blinding strategies will be assessed via questionnaires for both participants and study personnel during study visits.

Outcome assessors will remain blinded to treatment allocation until the last participant has completed the study protocol and the database has been cleaned and approved. Unblinding will only be permitted if knowledge of the investigational product is necessary for providing appropriate medical treatment or ensuring participant safety. In such cases, an emergency code break will be available to the investigator via the independent researcher.

Primary outcomes

The primary outcomes are the feasibility and acceptability of an 8-week oral tributyrin supplementation in adults with mild-to-severe MDD. Details on procedures, instruments and time-points of measurement are provided in table 1. Feasibility and acceptability will be considered as a composite outcome.

Feasibility will be evaluated by monitoring recruitment rates, participant completion and retention rates, adherence to intended protocol and success of blinding strategies throughout the study period. Acceptability will be assessed through adherence to the study supplement, measured by counting the number of returned capsules and using the Medication Adherence Scale (MARS)59 during study visits. Additionally, study participation satisfaction will be measured using the Study Participant Feedback Questionnaire (SPFQ).60

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes will be obtained for exploratory analyses to estimate the (variability of) effects of oral tributyrin supplementation regarding changes in depressive symptoms, anhedonia, affect and mood. Details on procedures, instruments and time-points of measurements are provided in table 1.

Depressive symptoms and anhedonia will be assessed during study visits using the HDRS-1761 and the Temporal Experience of Pleasure Scale,62 respectively. Depressive symptoms will also be measured weekly via a self-report questionnaire, ie,the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (QIDS)63 throughout the study period.

Moreover, this study includes intensive, repeated measurements of mood, anhedonia and positive and negative affect through ecological momentary assessment (EMA). Using a smartphone app ‘m-Path’ (m-Path Software bv, Belgium) participants will be asked to complete ratings five times daily for five consecutive days during the study period by responding to 16 items. The application will send five notifications between a predetermined starting time and an end time. The intervals between notifications will be as evenly spaced as possible, approximately 225 min apart, with a minimum interval of 90 min. Based on a meta-analysis by Vachon et al (2019),64 three strategies will be adopted to increase compliance to the EMA. First, a reminder notification will be sent if a questionnaire is not completed within 30 min. Second, the sampling schedule is fixed and will be customised for each participant in consultation with the researcher. Third, participants will receive a €10 or €20 bonus when they have completed ≥50% or ≥70% of the EMA items, respectively.

The items were selected based on prior research65 66 to assess:

Mood: participants will rate their current mood on a Visual Analogue Mood Scale, ranging from ‘happy’ (0) to ‘sad’ (100).67 Previous research has shown that these scores can predict depression relapse more effectively than HDRS-17 scores66;

Mood: participants will rate their current mood on a Visual Analogue Mood Scale, ranging from ‘happy’ (0) to ‘sad’ (100).67 Previous research has shown that these scores can predict depression relapse more effectively than HDRS-17 scores66;

Affect: participants will rate their current emotional state across 14 items, using a scale from 0 (‘not at all’) to 100 (‘very much’). The items include both positive (cheerful, enthusiastic, hopeful, content, energetic) and negative affect (anxious, angry, lonely, irritated, down, suspicious, helpless, guilty, insecure). This method captures fluctuations in affect, which may indicate emotional dysregulation—a common feature of MDD.6668,75

Exploratory outcomes

Other outcomes will be collected for exploratory analyses to estimate the (variability of) effects of oral tributyrin supplementation on the microbiota-gut-brain axis. Details on procedures, instruments and time-points of measurement are provided in table 1. These outcomes will be assessed through questionnaires, diaries, physical examinations and the collection of faecal, blood and hair samples.

Outcomes include gastrointestinal symptoms, assessed using the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS),76 dietary intake recorded via a nutritional diary covering the 3 days preceding each study visit (mijn.voedingscentrum.nl/nl/eetmeter). Body weight, waist circumference and hip circumference will be measured during physical examination at study visits.

Participants will be instructed to collect faecal samples in three containers (Faeces Container 76×20 mm, Sarstedt, Germany) on the day or morning before study visits, store them in the freezer until the visit and transport them in a cooling bag with cooling elements. On arrival at the AUMC, samples will be stored at −70°C. In addition, they will be asked to classify the form of their faecal sample using a Bristol Stool Scale (BSS).77

Fasting venous blood samples will be collected into two 4 mL ethylenediamenetetraacetic acid (EDTA) blood tubes, one 4 mL heparin tube and one 8 mL BD Vacutainer CPT blood tube during study visits. EDTA tubes will be centrifuged, and plasma aliquoted into 0.5 mL cryovials for storage at −70°C. The heparin tube will be sent immediately to the AUMC laboratory for C-reactive protein analysis. Within 2 hours of collection, peripheral blood mononuclear cells will be extracted from the BD Vacutainer CPT tube, processed and stored at −70°C.

Participants will be explicitly instructed to take their study supplements on the morning of study visits. Faecal and blood samples will be analysed for faecal microbiome composition (via 16S rRNA gene sequencing), metabolite profiles, and markers of inflammation, epigenetics and intestinal permeability.

Hair samples will be collected by cutting a small section of scalp hair (± 100 hairs, 3 cm) during multiple study visits and stored at room temperature. Hair cortisol levels, a non-invasive indicator of chronic stress, will be measured from these samples.78

Data analysis, storage and management

As this is a feasibility and acceptability study, a descriptive and qualitative synthesis of this data will be performed. This pilot is not powered on secondary and other study outcomes, so these outcomes will be analysed exploratively. Study findings will be reported in accordance with the CONSORT checklist for pilot or feasibility trials.79

All study and medical data will be handled with strict confidentiality and anonymity, in accordance with the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Dutch Act on Implementation of the GDPR. Participant data will be coded using a unique participant ID. Biological samples will be stored under appropriate conditions until further analysis: faecal and blood samples at −70°C, and hair samples at room temperature.

All study files will be archived for 15 years, while biomaterials will be retained for 5 years at the AUMC Biobank. Identifiable paper documents (eg, signed IC forms) and anonymous study files (eg, completed questionnaires) will be stored separately in locked filing cabinets. Paper files containing intervention assignments (eg, investigational product accountability forms) will be stored separately and will only be accessible to the independent researcher. Electronic study files will be stored securely on local AUMC drive. Any electronic files containing participant identifiers will be password-protected and restricted to authorised study personnel only.

Monitoring and safety

The study will be monitored by the Clinical Monitoring Centre (CMC) in accordance with the guidelines of the Dutch Federation of University Medical Centres. The CMC will be independent from the investigators and the sponsor. It will have access to source data and study files to verify data accuracy, legibility, consistency and compliance with the study protocol. Three on-site monitoring visits will be conducted, along with a final close-out visit.

All adverse events (AEs), including serious adverse events (SAEs), spontaneously reported by the subject or observed by the study personnel will be recorded. SAEs will be reported to the Medical Research Ethics Committee (MREC) of the AUMC within a maximum of 15 days after the sponsor first becomes aware of them. The MREC that approved this pilot study has determined that no suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) can occur in this study with tributyrin since it falls under the category of a food supplement. Therefore, a Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) is not required.

If a participant reports a suicidal intent, plans or a suicide attempt (indicated by an HDRS-17 suicide item of ≥3), the researcher will immediately contact a psychiatrist from the study team. If the study psychiatrist is unavailable, another on-call psychiatrist at the AUMC will be consulted. Appropriate action will be taken to ensure the participant’s safety.

Ethics and dissemination

The MREC of AUMC has approved the study and the trial has been prospectively registered in the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (registry number: NL-OMON57116). Important protocol modifications will be notified to the MREC and to the Central Committee on Research Involving Human Subjects (CCMO).

Potential participants will receive written information about the study via the Human Subjects Information Form (PIF) and any questions will be answered by the researcher (see online supplemental material 1 for an example of the participant consent form). A potential participant will be given a week after this to think about study participation. Thereafter, the people who are willing to participate will be invited to a screening visit. Before any study procedures are performed, the potential participant will be asked to sign an IC form, which will be additionally signed and dated by a member of the research team. The IC form also includes an opt-in item for the collection and storage of biological samples in ancillary studies. Participants will receive a copy of the signed consent form and their primary healthcare practitioner will be informed about their participation in the study.

There are no provisions for ancillary and post-trial care. The liability insurance of the investigator applies to the damage that becomes apparent during the study or within 4 years after the end of the study.

Participants will receive €50 for participating in the trial and finishing the assessments, with a potential bonus of €10 or €20 bonus when they have completed ≥50% or ≥70% of the EMA items, respectively. In addition, participants will receive a reimbursement for necessary travel expenses.

Authorised members of the study team will have access to the final trial dataset for analysis and interpretation. The study findings will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed scientific journals, as well as presentations at academic conferences, clinical care forums and community engagement events, where applicable.

Protocols, statistical code and other relevant supplemental materials necessary for ensuring the interoperability and interpretability of the projects and data will be made available through an open-access (meta)data repository. Due to the sensitivity of participant-level data, this data will be securely stored on internal servers and will be accessible on reasonable request. Data access processes will be shared on the (meta)data repository. Metadata will be assigned globally unique and persistent identifiers, which will be cross-linked to enhance findability and integration.

Discussion

With increasing evidence implicating the role of the microbiota-gut-brain axis in depression,7 gut-derived metabolites such as butyrate represent promising candidates for therapeutic exploration.8 45 To the best of our knowledge, this protocol paper describes the first trial to investigate butyrate supplementation in a depressed population, providing critical insights into its feasibility and potential antidepressant effects. Findings from this study will provide a foundation for future, larger-scale trials investigating butyrate as a novel microbiota-targeted intervention for depression.

This study incorporates multiple strategies to enhance adherence, including the use of tributyrin instead of sodium butyrate, which reduces the number of capsules required, as well as the inclusion of lemon-scented inserts and reminder systems. By incorporating extra measures of depressive symptoms via the QIDS in the 2 weeks directly after supplementation, we can gain preliminary insight into washout effects, potentially informing future single-case experimental designs. Furthermore, this study includes a follow-up, adding valuable data on the long-term effects of butyrate supplementation.

Despite its strengths, this trial has several limitations. First, tributyrin in soft gel capsules does not fully replicate the way butyrate is naturally produced in the gut. While naturally occurring butyrate is primarily formed in the lower gastrointestinal tract,19 tributyrin is likely absorbed earlier in the digestive process. However, its therapeutic effects may not solely depend on its action within the gut, as butyrate can enter circulation and influence immune and metabolic pathways in peripheral tissues, including the brain.9 80 Future studies might explore alternative formulations, such as resistant starches, that could more closely mimic endogenous butyrate production.81

Second, the study is not powered to detect significant changes in depressive symptoms or underlying mechanisms. While we have incorporated intensive measurements to improve detection of subtle changes and maximise statistical power, a larger sample size will be necessary in future trials to establish efficacy and further elucidate butyrate’s mechanism of action. Additionally, this study will not determine which patients are most likely to benefit from butyrate treatment. It is plausible that those with lower endogenous butyrate production or a symptom profile linked to epigenetic or immune dysregulation—such as individuals with an immuno-metabolic subtype—may respond more favourably.82 Future research should explore these potential predictive factors to refine targeted treatment approaches.

Third, the use of sunflower oil as a placebo may have minor biological effects. Sunflower oil is rich in oleic acid (omega-9) and linoleic acid (omega-6), both of which have been linked to immune modulation and metabolic processes.83 84 However, the effects of sunflower oil or its main components on depressive phenotypes remain largely unexplored. One study found that supplementation with 30 g of sunflower oil per day had antidepressant effects in mice.85 Nonetheless, given that the daily dose in our study—that is, 4 g—is roughly equivalent to one teaspoon of sunflower oil, we expect any potential effects to be minimal, and therefore consider it to be an adequate placebo.

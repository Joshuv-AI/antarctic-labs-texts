VCAM-1 as a biomarker of early cardiac phenotypic changes in Fabry disease
Authors: Danielli Oliveira da Costa Lino, Gdayllon Cavalcante Meneses, Igor Moreira de Almeida, Alice Maria Costa Martins, Amanda Jorge de Sousa Vasconcelos, Carlos José Mota de Lima, Ricardo Paulo de Sousa Rocha, Elizabeth De Francesco Daher, Geraldo Bezerra da Silva Junior
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFabry disease (FD) is an X-linked lysosomal storage disorder caused by α-galactosidase A deficiency, leading to globotriaosylceramide accumulation, endothelial activation, inflammation, and progressive cardiac involvement. In this exploratory cross-sectional study, we investigated whether vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1), an endothelial activation biomarker, is associated with early cardiac phenotypic changes in FD.MethodsThis single-center cross-sectional study included 14 genetically confirmed FD adults from one family carrying the GLA p.Arg363His variant and 30 age- and kidney-function-matched healthy controls for biomarker comparisons. FD participants underwent clinical assessment, 12-lead electrocardiography, transthoracic echocardiography with global longitudinal strain (GLS), and cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) including native T1 mapping and late gadolinium enhancement. Serum VCAM-1, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and syndecan-1 were measured. Group comparisons used Mann-Whitney and Fisher exact tests; correlations used Spearman coefficients.ResultsMedian age was 30.5 (24–48) years, and 78.6% were female. Only one participant (7.1%) showed concentric left ventricular hypertrophy, whereas diastolic dysfunction was present in 42.9%, and global or segmental GLS abnormalities in 57.2%. CMR showed no increased LV mass, but papillary muscle abnormalities were frequent (57.2%); LGE and low native T1 were present in 14.3% each. VCAM-1 was markedly higher in FD than controls [2634.2 (2048.7-3173.6) vs. 671.7 (490.3-765.1) ng/mL; p < 0.001]. Within FD participants, VCAM-1 was higher among those with ventricular remodeling or incipient hypertrophy (p = 0.043) and showed a borderline association with less negative GLS values (rho = 0.526; p = 0.053). VCAM-1 also correlated with longer symptom duration (rho = 0.566; p = 0.044). IL-6, but not syndecan-1, was higher in FD than controls.ConclusionVCAM-1 was substantially elevated in this FD family cohort and was associated with early structural and functional cardiac markers before overt hypertrophy. These results do not yet justify changes in clinical practice, but they support VCAM-1 as a candidate biomarker for future longitudinal studies of early Fabry cardiomyopathy.

Introduction

Fabry disease (FD) is a genetic condition with X-linked recessive inheritance, caused by a deficiency of the lysosomal enzyme alpha-galactosidase A (α-gal A or GLA), whose prevalence is estimated at 1:40,000 to 1:117,000 births, with some population studies pointing to rates of 1:5,000 to 7,000 [1, 2]. This deficiency leads to the accumulation of glycosphingolipids, such as globotriaosylceramide (Gb3 or GL3), causing progressive damage mainly to the heart, kidneys, central nervous system, liver, and vascular endothelium of the skin [3].

Cardiovascular and kidney complications are the main factors influencing patients’ prognosis. Chronic exposure to Gb3/lyso-Gb3 causes smooth muscle cells’ proliferation, resulting in thickening of the arteries’ intimal media layer. This inflammatory process activates renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and increases the secretion of adhesion molecules and cytokines [4].

The main cause of death and loss of quality of life is cardiac involvement. In FD, heart complications are slow and frequently subclinical, evolving into left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) and fibrosis, often mimicking hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) [5]. Currently, treatment for FD is initiated after the identification of lesions in target organs, either through clinical manifestations or alterations in complementary exams.

Traditional biomarkers, such as high-sensitivity troponin and NT-proBNP, are commonly used in cardiology and have been correlated with the presence of LVH and myocardial fibrosis in patients with FD, indicating advanced stages of the disease [6–8].

This study aims to evaluate traditional and non-traditional biomarkers related to inflammation and endothelial involvement in patients with pre-hypertrophic FD, detected through family screening. The results will be correlated with echocardiographic and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging findings.

Methods

This is a cross-sectional study, conducted at a specialized center in Fortaleza, Northeast Brazil, including patients with Fabry disease. All Fabry participants belonged to a single family carrying the GLA p.Arg363His variant, with diagnosis confirmed by genetic testing (c.1088G > A; p.Arg363His).

After consulting the genetic database – Clinvar® – it was observed that the described variant is of the missense type and classified as a pathogenic/probably pathogenic variant related to FD.

In this family, among 17 relatives who tested positive, 14 were eligible for the cardiological screening study (because they were ≥ 18 years old and with whom contact and consent to participation were possible).

Participants signed the Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC), which was made available immediately after the invitation to participate in the research. This research was submitted to and approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Fortaleza (CAAE 58556021.5.0000.5052).

The participants in this study underwent clinical and cardiological evaluation, through an interview and clinical history focused on the main signs/symptoms associated with FD, physical examination related to the cardiovascular system, and complementary cardiology exams such as electrocardiogram, transthoracic echocardiogram (Philips Afiniti 70) with advanced myocardial strain assessment, and Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (Philips Prodiva 1.5T) with Late Enhancement technique and native T1 mapping (MOLLI sequence).

Regarding imaging exams, the main parameters recorded were: Ejection Fraction measurement, Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS) measurement, interventricular septum measurements, measurements related to diastolic dysfunction, presence of fibrosis/late enhancement, and morphological changes possibly related to FD. Ventricular remodeling was defined as concentric remodeling without diagnostic LVH.

Laboratory evaluation included complete blood count, kidney function tests, urinary albumin/creatinine ratio, as well as the measurement of traditional biomarkers of cardiac involvement – high-sensitivity Troponin T (hs-TnT – ng/L; reference value between 9 and 17 ng/L, electrochemiluminescence method) and NT-proBNP (in pg/mL, reference value < 125 pg/mL, electrochemiluminescence method), as well as inflammation and endothelial dysfunction – vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1 – Human VCAM-1/CD 106 DuoSet ELISA DY809-05 R&D Systems), interleukin 6 (IL-6 – Human IL-6 DuoSet ELISA DY206-05 R&D Systems) and Syndecan-1 (Human Syndecan-1 DuoSet ELISA DY2780 R&D Systems).

A group of 30 healthy volunteers was included for comparison of biomarkers’ dosage results, matched for sex, age, and kidney function.

Data were processed using the R Studio statistical program for Windows (R Core Team, 2023; Pacman, Tidyr, Dplyr, Gtsummary, Ggplot2, and Flextable packages). Parameter comparisons between groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney test and Fisher’s exact test. Spearman’s correlation coefficient (Σ) was used for linear correlations. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The protocol of this study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Fortaleza (approval number 5473024), and patients signed a free and informed consent form before inclusion in the study.

Results

Among the 14 patients evaluated, 78.6% (11) were female, with a mean age of 30.5 years (24–48 years). The mean age the first symptoms onset was 20 years (19–30 years). Time between the first symptoms associated with FD and the genetic diagnosis was on average 6 years (3–14 years). Neurological system was the most affected in relation to the presence of symptoms (71.4% of cases). Eight patients (57.1%) had current symptoms related to the cardiovascular system, with palpitations being the most frequent complaint (50%). Regarding associated comorbidities, arterial hypertension was reported in 14.3%, dyslipidemia in 14.3%, and diabetes mellitus in 7.1%. Patients had adequate blood pressure levels, with no changes in estimated glomerular filtration rate (GFR) levels, urinary albumin/creatinine ratio, and normal high-sensitivity troponin and NT-proBNP values. Five patients reported using enzyme replacement therapy with agalsidase alfa (35.7%).

All patients had ejection fractions within the normal range in both methods used (echocardiographic – 64.5% (60%-73%) and by CMR – 60.5% (56%-63%). Six patients had grade 1 or 2 diastolic dysfunction (42.9%), with an average septal thickness of 8 mm (7–9 mm), and only one patient had incipient concentric hypertrophy. Eight patients (57.2%) had GLS alterations (four patients with altered total GLS and four patients with normal total GLS but with segmental alterations). Regarding CMR findings, no patient had increased ventricular mass, but 8 patients (57.2%) had papillary muscle alterations (hypertrophy and morphological changes). Myocardial fibrosis was found in 2 patients in inferoseptal positions (basal and medial), as well as reduced T1-weighted ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) in 2 patients (Table 2).

Discussion

In this exploratory cross-sectional study of adults with genetically confirmed Fabry disease from a single family, serum VCAM-1 was markedly elevated compared with controls and was associated with early echocardiographic markers of cardiac phenotype, particularly ventricular remodeling or incipient hypertrophy. VCAM-1 also correlated with longer symptom duration and showed a borderline association with global longitudinal strain (GLS). Importantly, these findings were observed in a cohort with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction, normal kidney function, predominantly normal conventional cardiac biomarkers, and minimal overt left ventricular hypertrophy. This phenotype makes the cohort particularly relevant for investigating the earliest mechanisms of Fabry cardiomyopathy rather than advanced cardiac damage [3, 6–8].

Our findings are consistent with the current understanding that Fabry disease is not merely a lysosomal storage disorder but also a systemic vascular and inflammatory disease characterized by progressive endothelial dysfunction. Accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) and its deacylated metabolite lyso-Gb3 promotes oxidative stress, endothelial activation, inflammatory signaling, vascular smooth muscle proliferation, microvascular dysfunction, myocardial storage, edema, hypertrophy, and ultimately replacement fibrosis [4, 8–11]. Recent expert consensus statements have emphasized that one of the major unmet needs in Fabry disease is the identification of biomarkers capable of detecting early cardiac involvement before irreversible structural damage develops. In contrast, conventional biomarkers such as high-sensitivity troponin and NT-proBNP appear to be more strongly associated with myocardial fibrosis, hypertrophy, or more advanced cardiac involvement [6–8, 12, 13].

Previous studies have demonstrated increased circulating endothelial activation markers in Fabry disease, including VCAM-1, although their associations with cardiovascular phenotypes have been inconsistent. Demuth and Germain first reported elevated endothelial adhesion molecules in young patients with classic Fabry disease [14]. Nearly two decades later, Vujkovac et al. confirmed increased VCAM-1 concentrations, particularly among male patients, although they found no significant associations with arterial stiffness or subclinical atherosclerosis [15]. More recent mechanistic studies have further reinforced the biological plausibility of endothelial dysfunction as a central component of Fabry cardiomyopathy, highlighting persistent inflammatory activation, endothelial injury, and coronary microvascular dysfunction as potential drivers of myocardial remodeling [4, 10, 11]. Our study extends these observations by suggesting that VCAM-1 may be associated not only with systemic endothelial activation but also with early myocardial phenotypic changes, including ventricular remodeling and subtle strain abnormalities, in a genetically homogeneous pre-hypertrophic cohort identified through family screening.

The absence of significant elevations in high-sensitivity troponin and the low NT-proBNP concentrations observed in most participants are clinically relevant. These findings suggest that VCAM-1 may reflect a distinct biological pathway compared with conventional biomarkers, potentially identifying endothelial activation and inflammatory cell recruitment before myocardial necrosis, increased wall stress, or replacement fibrosis become clinically evident [6, 7, 11–13]. Nevertheless, considering the exploratory nature of this study, its small sample size, and cross-sectional design, VCAM-1 should not be interpreted as a validated diagnostic or prognostic biomarker. Rather, it should currently be regarded as a promising candidate biomarker deserving validation in larger prospective studies integrating advanced cardiac imaging and longitudinal clinical outcomes.

An important contribution of this study to the current literature is the demonstration that VCAM-1 was substantially elevated in a genetically homogeneous Fabry cohort and associated with imaging markers of early cardiac involvement before overt left ventricular hypertrophy became evident. This finding is particularly relevant because contemporary family screening programs increasingly identify patients during the preclinical stage of disease, when conventional cardiac biomarkers often remain within normal limits and the optimal timing for treatment initiation or intensified imaging surveillance remains uncertain [3, 8, 10, 13].

At present, our findings are not sufficient to modify existing clinical practice. Current clinical decision-making in Fabry disease should continue to rely on comprehensive multimodal assessment, including genotype, sex, clinical manifestations, α-galactosidase A activity when applicable, plasma lyso-Gb3 concentrations where available, kidney involvement, neurological manifestations, electrocardiography, echocardiography with strain analysis, cardiac magnetic resonance with native T1 mapping and late gadolinium enhancement, together with established cardiac biomarkers [3, 6–8, 13, 16]. Therefore, the clinical implications of the present study should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing. VCAM-1 may represent a valuable research biomarker for future longitudinal studies investigating whether endothelial activation precedes structural myocardial injury, predicts disease progression, or identifies patients who derive greater benefit from early disease-specific therapies.

Taken together, our findings support the concept that endothelial activation is already present during the earliest stages of Fabry cardiomyopathy and suggest that VCAM-1 may represent one of the earliest circulating biomarkers associated with subclinical myocardial remodeling [17–21]. Although these observations require external validation, they provide a biologically plausible framework for future multicenter prospective studies integrating circulating biomarkers with advanced multimodality cardiac imaging to improve early risk stratification and optimize the timing of therapeutic interventions in Fabry disease.

Limitations

This study has important limitations. First, the sample size was small because Fabry disease is rare and the study focused on a single family carrying the GLA p.Arg363His variant. This design reduced genetic heterogeneity but limits generalizability to other genotypes and phenotypes. Second, the cross-sectional design prevents causal inference and does not establish whether higher VCAM-1 precedes, accompanies, or follows early cardiac remodeling. Third, the cohort was predominantly female and young, which may have influenced the cardiac phenotype and biomarker distribution. Fourth, lyso-Gb3 was not available during the study period, preventing direct comparison between VCAM-1 and the most widely used Fabry-specific biomarker. Fifth, enzyme replacement therapy exposure in some participants may have modified both biomarker levels and cardiac phenotype. Sixth, the number of patients with fibrosis, abnormal T1, or overt hypertrophy was very small, limiting subgroup analyses. Finally, VCAM-1 is not specific to Fabry disease and may be influenced by other inflammatory, vascular, metabolic, and renal factors, although major kidney dysfunction was not present in this cohort.

Knowledge gaps

Important knowledge gaps remain. It is still unknown whether VCAM-1 predicts longitudinal progression to LV hypertrophy, fibrosis, arrhythmias, heart failure, kidney disease, or cerebrovascular events in Fabry disease. It is also unclear how VCAM-1 relates to lyso-Gb3, genotype, sex, treatment status, microvascular dysfunction, and CMR tissue characterization markers such as native T1, extracellular volume, T2 mapping, and LGE. The threshold, reproducibility, intra-individual variability, and clinical decision value of VCAM-1 in FD have not been established.

Future directions

Future research should prioritize multicenter longitudinal cohorts with larger and more diverse FD populations, standardized VCAM-1 assays, serial lyso-Gb3 measurements, detailed treatment data, and harmonized cardiac imaging protocols. Studies should test whether VCAM-1 improves risk prediction beyond conventional biomarkers and imaging, whether it changes after enzyme replacement therapy, chaperone therapy, or other disease-modifying approaches, and whether endothelial biomarker profiles can help identify patients who need closer surveillance or earlier treatment intensification. Mechanistic studies integrating endothelial biomarkers, microvascular imaging, inflammatory pathways, and myocardial tissue characterization may clarify whether endothelial activation is a driver, marker, or consequence of early Fabry cardiomyopathy.

Conclusion

In this exploratory family-based cohort of adults with Fabry disease, VCAM-1 was markedly elevated compared with controls and was associated with ventricular remodeling or incipient hypertrophy, longer symptom duration, and a borderline GLS association. These results support the biological plausibility of endothelial activation in early Fabry cardiac involvement. However, because of the small sample size, single-family design, cross-sectional analysis, and lack of lyso-Gb3 data, VCAM-1 cannot yet be recommended for routine clinical decision-making. The main implication is for future research: VCAM-1 should be evaluated in larger longitudinal studies as a candidate biomarker of early Fabry cardiomyopathy and disease progression.

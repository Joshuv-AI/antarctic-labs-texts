MiR-32-3p improves ISO induced AC16 myocardial cell injury by targeting ATF4
Authors: Danqi Chen, Dongjun Hu, Xiongxiong Wang, Bangsheng Chen, Wenzhu Lou, Yizhou Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPathological myocardial hypertrophy is a key risk factor for heart failure and cardiovascular death. MicroRNAs (miRNAs), as a key post transcriptional regulator of gene expression, play an increasingly important role in myocardial hypertrophy and injury. This study aimed to investigate the expression, function, and mechanism of miR-32-3p through the miR-32-3p/ATF4 axis in ISO induced injury of human AC16 cardiomyocytes.MethodsA cell injury model was established by treating AC16 cells with 1 µM ISO. miR-32-3p mimic/inhibitor were transfected. Gene expression levels were detected using qRT-PCR; cell proliferation was evaluated via the CCK-8 assay; the levels of inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) were detected by ELISA; changes in oxidative stress levels (MDA, SOD, ROS) were analyzed using reagent kits; and the binding site between miR-32-3p and ATF4 was predicted through TargetScanHuman.; The miR-32-3p/ATF4 target relationship was validated using dual luciferase assays.ResultsISO induced downregulation of miR-32-3p expression (P < 0.0001), inhibition of AC16 cell proliferation (P < 0.0001), upregulation of myocardial hypertrophy markers ANP and β-MHC, as well as increased levels of inflammatory cytokines IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β and oxidative stress (all P < 0.0001). When the miR-32-3p mimic was added, these changes were reversed. The dual luciferase assay confirmed that bioinformatics predicted miR-32-3p to directly target the 3’UTR of ATF4. The rescue experiment showed that overexpression of ATF4 eliminated the protective effect of miR-32-3p (P < 0.05).ConclusionsmiR-32-3p alleviates ISO induced AC16 cell damage by targeting ATF4, regulating inflammation and oxidative stress.

Background

Cardiac hypertrophy (MH) is an important risk factor for cardiovascular death [1]. Continuous stress stimulation can lead to chronic hypertrophy, which normalizes the patient’s wall tension and ultimately results in sudden death or severe heart failure [2, 3]. A study has found that, unlike compensatory hypertrophy, the initial reaction that occurs immediately when the myocardium is damaged can cause pathological myocardial hypertrophy [4]. This pathological hypertrophy is associated with fibrosis, increased production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and cellular dysfunction [5]. Therefore, exploring the pathological causes and potential molecular mechanisms of myocardial hypertrophy has important research value.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of small non coding RNAs that regulate gene expression after transcription and have been widely used in the research of cardiovascular diseases such as cardiac hypertrophy in recent years [6, 7]. Multiple miRNAs have been reported to regulate hypertrophy related signaling pathways, affecting the proliferation, hypertrophy, and injury processes of cardiomyocytes [8, 9]. Among them, studies have found that the downregulation of miR-32-3p is closely related to myocardial injury [10], but its role and target mechanism in pathological myocardial hypertrophy are not yet clear. Intense or sustained endoplasmic reticulum stress may lead to endoplasmic reticulum stress-induced cell death, causing hypertrophic myocardium to deteriorate and affecting the pathogenesis and development of myocardial hypertrophy [11]. Research has found that ATF4, as a core regulatory factor in the endoplasmic reticulum stress response pathway, regulates the size of cardiac myocytes through induced autophagy and stress response [12]. In addition, the PERK/ATF4/CHOP pathway plays an important role in inhibiting autophagy and ERS, leading to myocardial cell injury [13]. How ATF4 participates in regulating myocardial hypertrophy has not yet been explored.

Isoprenaline (ISO) is a synthetic catecholamine that has cardiac toxicity and can cause heart damage [14], causing pathological changes and functional impairments such as myocardial ischemia, cardiac hypertrophy, oxidative stress, and inflammatory response [15–17]. The AC16 cell line is derived from adult ventricular cardiomyocytes and is an ideal in vitro model for studying the pathological state of cardiomyocytes [5]. Therefore, this study explored the expression changes and functions of miR-32-3p in an ISO induced AC16 human myocardial cell injury model and deeply explored the mechanism of miR-32-3p/ATF4 pathway in myocardial injury.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and model establishment

The human AC16 cardiomyocyte line (ATCC, USA) was utilized in this study. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Thermo, USA) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, and maintained in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. When cell confluency reached 70–80%, the cells were treated with 1 µM ISO (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for a continuous 24-hour period. For the control group, cells were cultured in serum medium without ISO under the same environmental conditions.

Cell transfection

miR-32-3p mimic, miR-32-3p inhibitor, Overexpression of ATF4 (oe-ATF4), ATF4 wild type (ATF4-WT), ATF4 mutation sequences (ATF4-MUT), and their respective negative controls (miR-NC, inhibitor NC, oe-NC) were synthesized by GenePharma Co., Ltd. AC16 cardiomyocytes were inoculated at a density of 5 × 105 cells/well into a 6-well culture plate, using DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, and incubated in a 37 ℃, 5% CO2 constant temperature incubator. When AC16 cells reached 60–70% confluence, the nucleic acid was mixed with Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA) in a 1:1 ratio according to the manufacturer’s instructions, gently blown and mixed well, allowed to stand at room temperature for 20 min, and slowly dripped into the cell culture system of the 6-well plate. During the dripping process, the culture plate was gently shaken to ensure uniform distribution of the complexes. After transfection, the cells were continued to be incubated for 6 h, then the culture medium was replaced with fresh complete medium, and the cells were further cultured for 24–48 h before being used for subsequent experiments. Three technical replicates were set for each transfection group.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total cellular RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Reverse-transcribed a total of 1 µg of RNA into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT kit (TaKaRa, Japan). SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS (AG, China) was used for detection on a real-time fluorescent PCR system (Bio Rad, USA). The PCR amplification program was as follows: pre-denaturation at 95℃ for 5 min; denaturation at 95℃ for 10 s, annealing at 60℃ for 30 s, and extension at 72℃ for 20 s, for a total of 40 cycles; the melting curve program was 95℃ for 15 s, 60℃ for 1 min, and 95℃ for 15 s. U6 serves as an internal reference gene for the expression level of miR-32-3p, while ATF4, ANP, and β-MHC use GAPDH as an internal reference gene. The relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method. All primer sequences were synthesized by Shanghai Biotech. Each sample was assayed in triplicate, and each experiment was independently repeated three times.

Cell proliferation assay

Cell proliferation activity was determined using the CCK-8 Kit (Beyotime, China). According to the kit instructions, transfected or treated cells were seeded into 96-well plates.After 24, 48, and 72 h of cultivation, add CCK-8 reagent and incubate the cells at 37℃ for another 2 h. The absorbance (OD value) of each well was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm, and the cell proliferation activity was calculated based on the obtained OD values.

Inflammatory cytokine measurement

Cell culture supernatants were collected, and the concentrations of human Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α), and Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) were measured using ELISA kits (Lianke, China) following the manufacturer’s protocols strictly. Absorbance was read at 450 nm, and the concentrations of the inflammatory cytokines were calculated using standard curves.

Oxidative stress indicators

Cells were harvested and total protein was extracted. MDA content and SOD activity were measured using a TBARS assay kit (Catalog, USA) and an SOD detection kit (Beyotime, China), respectively. ROS levels were detected using an reactive oxygen species detection kit (DCFH-DA method, Beyotime, China). Strictly follow the instructions of the reagent kit for the experiment.

TargetScan target gene prediction

Potential target genes of miR-32-3p were predicted using the TargetScanHuman online database (Release 7.2; http://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/), with a focus on putative binding sites in the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) of ATF4.

Dual-Luciferase reporter assay

The pGL3 Control vector (Promega, USA) was selected to construct the wild-type (WT) containing the ATF4 binding fragment and to mutate the binding site to mutant (MUT). Inoculate HEK293T cells (ATCC) into a 24 well plate and culture at a density of 70% for transfection. After 48 h of transfection, cells were collected using the dual luciferase reporter gene detection system (Promega, USA) and fluorescence values were measured.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently repeated at least three times. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed by Student’s t-test, and multiple group comparisons were performed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

miR-32-3p modulates gene expression and myocardial hypertrophy under ISO induction

ISO treatment significantly reduced the expression level of miR-32-3p in cells (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). ISO treatment significantly decreased the proliferation activity of AC16 cells, while transfection with miR-32-3p mimic restored cell viability (Fig. 1B, P < 0.0001). In addition, ISO treatment induced a significant upregulation of the expression levels of myocardial hypertrophy markers ANP and β-MHC (P < 0.0001). Compared with the ISO + mimic NC group, transfection with miR-32-3p mimic significantly reduced the mRNA levels of ANP (P < 0.01) and β-MHC (P < 0.0001) that were elevated by ISO treatment (Fig. 1C-D).

Overexpression of miR-32-3p attenuates ISO-induced inflammatory response and oxidative stress in AC16 cells

ISO treatment induced significant increases in pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 (Fig. 2A), TNF-α (Fig. 2B), and IL-1β (Fig. 2C), while the addition of miR-32-3p mimic significantly reduced the levels of inflammatory cytokines (P < 0.0001). Meanwhile, ISO treatment significantly increased the content of lipid peroxidation product MDA in cells (Fig. 2D, P < 0.0001), while transfection of miR-32-3p mimic effectively reversed this phenomenon. Correspondingly, ISO treatment significantly reduced the activity of SOD (Fig. 2E, P < 0.0001); Overexpression of miR-32-3p partially restored SOD activity (P < 0.001). In addition, ISO induced a rapid accumulation of ROS, while miR-32-3p mimic significantly alleviated the excessive generation of ROS (Fig. 2F).

miR-32-3p directly targets and negatively regulates ATF4

It was predicted that miR-32-3p has a binding site in the 3’UTR of ATF4 (Fig. 3A). The dual luciferase reporter gene experiment showed that co transfection of miR-32-3p mimic significantly inhibited luciferase activity (P < 0.0001) in cells transfected with ATF4-WT reporter plasmid, while co transfection of miR-32-3p inhibitor enhanced luciferase activity (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B). However, when the binding site of the reporter plasmid mutates (ATF4-MUT), neither the miR-32-3p mimic nor the inhibitor can further affect its fluorescence activity. The mRNA level of ATF4 was detected, and compared with the control group, ISO treatment significantly upregulated the expression of ATF4 (P < 0.0001). In the context of ISO induction, transfection of miR-32-3p mimic can effectively reverse the upregulation of ATF4 (P < 0.01). However, when co transfected with oe-ATF4 while overexpressing miR-32-3p, the mRNA level of ATF4 was restored (P < 0.05, Fig. 3C).

miR-32-3p Ameliorates ISO-induced proliferation inhibition and cardiac hypertrophy by targeting ATF4

In the ISO + miR mimic group, miR-32-3p was successfully overexpressed, while subsequent co transfection with oe-ATF4 had no significant effect on its expression level (Fig. 4A). After overexpression of ATF4, the pro-proliferative effect of the miR-32-3p mimic was partially reversed (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4B). Meanwhile, co transfection with oe-ATF4 significantly weakened the inhibitory effect of miR-32-3p on ANP (P < 0.05, Fig. 4C) and β-MHC (P < 0.05, Fig. 4D), leading to a further increase in their expression levels.

miR-32-3p alleviates inflammation and oxidative stress by suppressing ATF4-mediated signaling pathways

The experimental results showed that overexpression of ATF4 simultaneously led to a significant increase in the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5A), TNF-α (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5B), and IL-1β (P < 0.0001, Fig. 5C). Moreover, the protective effect of miR-32-3p mimic on oxidative stress was partially reversed by overexpression of ATF4, as evidenced by further increases in MDA (P < 0.0001) and ROS (P < 0.05) levels, as well as a decrease in SOD activity (P < 0.001, Fig. 5D-F).

Discussion

Pathological myocardial hypertrophy is a key pathological process in cardiovascular diseases such as heart failure, and its occurrence is often triggered by myocardial injury [18–20]. This study used an ISO induced human AC16 myocardial cell injury model to reveal for the first time that upregulation of miR-32-3p expression can effectively alleviate myocardial cell injury and exert a protective effect. Further mechanistic studies have shown that this protective effect is closely related to its direct targeting and negative regulation of transcription factor ATF4. This study provides a new perspective on the molecular mechanisms underlying the transformation of myocardial injury into pathological hypertrophy and heart failure.

miR-32-3p has been found to be a valuable novel biomarker for cardiovascular disease [21]. By regulating ischemia, it participates in cardiac remodeling and post infarction repair [22], but its role in myocardial injury caused by myocardial hypertrophy is still unknown. This study found that miR-32-3p is downregulated under ISO stimulation. Functional experiments have shown that overexpression of miR-32-3p can improve ISO induced proliferation of AC16 cells, reduce the secretion of inflammatory factors IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, and ameliorate oxidative stress levels. This is consistent with previous studies on miR-32-3p in myocardial injury caused by microembolism and microvascular occlusion [10], as well as its key role in inhibiting oxidative damage [23]. In addition, studies have shown that ANP and β-MHC are upregulated in myocardial cells and are listed as early sensitive markers of cardiac hypertrophy, becoming key evidence for detecting the effects of myocardial hypertrophy [24, 25]. This study found that miR-32-3p can effectively alleviate the elevated expression of ANP and β-MHC biomarkers induced by ISO. Interestingly, miR-32-3p can effectively alleviate the elevated expression of biomarkers. In summary, miR-32-3p may alleviate inflammation and oxidative stress, thereby reducing AC16 myocardial cell damage and modulating myocardial hypertrophy.

ATF4 plays a crucial role as a stress-induced transcription factor in endoplasmic reticulum stress and oxidative response management [26]. Studies have found that differential expression of ATF4 can induce myocardial hypertrophy by regulating ISO induced myocardial injury [27, 28]. This study demonstrated the targeted binding of miR-32-3p to the 3’UTR of ATF4 based on bioinformatics analysis and dual luciferase detection. In addition, this study demonstrated that miR-32-3p participates in ISO induced AC16 cell damage by negatively regulating the expression of ATF4. The ATF4 pathway has been found to be involved in regulating cell viability and inflammatory responses in various diseases [29–31], and in affecting the secretion of inflammatory factors such as IL-1β and TNF-α [32]. In addition, Wang et al. found that ATF4 protects the heart from failure by antagonizing oxidative stress [33], and the ATF4-CHOP-CHAC1 signaling pathway increases ROS production and reduces SOD2 expression through crosstalk between ERS and oxidative stress [34], which is consistent with the results of this study. Based on previous studies, it is speculated that ISO-induced sustained ERS leads to persistent activation of the ATF4/CHOP axis. CHOP can directly promote the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) by activating the NF-κB signaling pathway. Meanwhile, ATF4 induces CHAC1, which degrades glutathione and increases ROS production, while also suppressing SOD2 expression, thereby exacerbating oxidative stress. Our data demonstrate that overexpression of miR-32-3p suppresses ATF4, which likely interrupts the crosstalk between ATF4-mediated endoplasmic reticulum stress, inflammation, and oxidative stress, ultimately alleviating ISO-induced cardiomyocyte injury. Previous studies have found that reducing ATF4 levels reduces oxidative stress and inflammation and mitigates cardiac toxicity [35], further supporting the reliability of our speculation.

Although this study systematically elucidated the protective effect and molecular mechanism of the miR-32-3p/ATF4 axis in ISO induced myocardial cell injury. However, there are still certain limitations: (1) In vitro experiments were only conducted on AC16 cardiomyocytes, lacking validation of the in vivo function of the miR-32-3p/ATF4 axis in animal models. Subsequently, an ISO induced mouse myocardial injury model will be established to further validate the function of the miR-32-3p/ATF4 axis in mice; (2) There is a Lack of further exploration of specific downstream effector pathways of ATF4. In the future, RNA sequencing, Co-IP and other technologies will be used to identify downstream target genes and elucidate the pathways through which ATF4 affects inflammation and oxidative stress; (3) Lack of clinical sample validation makes it difficult to clarify the expression characteristics of miR-32-3p in patients with myocardial hypertrophy and its correlation with disease severity and prognosis. The next step is to collect clinical samples from patients with myocardial hypertrophy, detect the expression level of miR-32-3p in serum, and its correlation with ATF4 and inflammatory factors to clarify its clinical translational value.

In summary, this study proposes for the first time that miR-32-3p alleviates ISO induced myocardial injury in AC16 cells by directly targeting ATF4; it system atically elucidates that the miR-32-3p/ATF4 axis regulates myocardial hypertrophy by modulating inflammatory response and oxidative stress levels. miR-32-3p is closely associated with myocardial hypertrophy in the ISO induced AC16 myocardial cell injury model and is expected to become a potential biomarker for the diagnosis of myocardial hypertrophy.

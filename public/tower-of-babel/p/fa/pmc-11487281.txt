Machine learning-based classification of valvular heart disease using cardiovascular risk factors
Authors: Muhammad Usman Aslam, Songhua Xu, Sajid Hussain, Muhammad Waqas, Nafiu Lukman Abiodun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Valvular Heart Disease (VHD) is a globally significant cause of mortality, particularly among aging populations. Despite advancements in percutaneous and surgical interventions, there are still uncertainties that remain regarding the risk factors that significantly contribute to this condition within the domain of cardiovascular disease. This study investigates these uncertainties and the role of machine learning in categorizing VHD based on cardiovascular risk factors. It follows a two-part investigation comprising feature extraction and classification phases. Feature extraction is initially performed using a wrapping approach and refined further with binary logistic regression. The second phase employs five classifiers: Artificial Neural Network (ANN), XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), Naïve Bayes, and Support Vector Machine (SVM), along with advanced methods such as SVM combined with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and a majority-voting ensemble method (MV5). Data on VHD cases were collected from DHQ Hospital Faisalabad using simple random sampling. Various statistical measures, such as the ROC curve, F-measure, sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, MCC, and Kappa are applied to assess the results. The findings reveal that the combination of SVM with PCA achieves the highest overall performance while the MV5 ensemble method also demonstrates high accuracy and balance in sensitivity and specificity. The variation in VHD prevalence linked to specific risk factors highlights the importance of a comprehensive approach to reduce this disease’s burden. The Exceptional performance of SVM + PCA and MV5 highlights their significance in diagnosing VHD and advancing knowledge in biomedicine.

Introduction

Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs) are the leading cause of death worldwide, with approximately 17.9 million deaths in 20151,2. Ischemic heart disease (IHD) and stroke are the primary contributors to the health burden associated with CVDs. The number of CVD-related deaths is projected to surpass 22.2 million per year by 2030, with low and middle-income countries (LMICs) accounting for 75% of these mortalities. CVDs are estimated to reduce the gross domestic product (GDP) of LMICs by 7%3. The prevalence of hypertension, a significant risk factor for CVDs, has been increasing in the United States since 2013, affecting nearly half of the adult population4,5. Hypertension imposes a substantial economic burden, costing around $48.6 billion annually, which includes healthcare services, antihypertensive medications, and lost productivity due to premature mortality6. Although LMICs have a lower burden of risk factors compared to high-income countries, they experience a higher proportion of the global CVD burden7–9. The projected increase in the older adult population in LMICs with a high prevalence of CVDs poses a significant challenge to healthcare systems. In 2010, CVDs were the primary contributor to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD), accounting for 30.3% of the total burden in individuals aged 60 years and older, with disorders in this age group contributing to 23% of the GBD10.

Numerous population-based studies conducted in low-income countries have identified associations between socio-demographic characteristics and the prevalence of CVDs. Higher CVD prevalence has been consistently linked to advanced age, female gender, and lower educational attainment levels7,8. Additionally, behavioral risk factors, such as alcohol consumption, smoking, physical inactivity, and inadequate fruit and vegetable intake, are also associated with an increased risk of CVDs9. Hypertension is recognized as a significant risk factor contributing to the development of CVDs11. The coexistence of multiple independent risk factors presents opportunities for targeted interventions aimed at reducing the future burden of CVDs in the aging populations of LMICs.

The application of machine learning (ML) techniques in heart disease prediction has attracted significant interest due to their potential to discover complex patterns and improve diagnostic accuracy. Several studies have explored the use of ML approaches for risk stratification and early detection of CVDs. Gotta et al.12 utilized an ML approach to identify key predictors of mortality in young patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis. By employing clinical and laboratory data, their proposed models successfully recognized the risk factors associated with mortality. Miyashita et al.13 proposed a novel AI data-mining approach to predict heart failure in the general population. Several studies utilized the popular Cleveland, Hungarian, and Swiss heart disease datasets with around 13 to 14 features like age, sex, chest pain, resting blood pressure, etc. Shouman et al.14 proposed a decision tree classifier and achieved an F-measure of 84.1% on the Cleveland dataset. Ali et al.15 used an optimized deep neural network (NN) with a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi^{2}$$\end{document}χ2 feature selector and reported 93.33% accuracy on the same data. A random search algorithm is utilized by Javeed et al.16 to propose a random forest model that yielded 93.33% accuracy. The Z-Alizadeh Sani dataset from the Iranian healthcare system, with 22 to 34 features, was investigated by multiple researchers. Abdar et al.17 proposed an N2 genetic algorithm-based SVM model that achieved 93.08% accuracy. Alizadehsani et al.18 compared sequential minimal optimization (SMO) variants, with the SMO (1–1) model attaining 92.74% accuracy. Arabasadi et al.19 merged an NN with a genetic algorithm, reaching 93.85% accuracy. Further, using the data from the Shaheed Rajaei CVD Center in Iran with 16 features, Alizadehsani et al.20 proposed an SMO model, obtaining 82.16% accuracy. Following the same data further they developed an ensemble of Naïve Bayes with SMO classifiers, the proposed ensemble achieved 88.52% accuracy. Several studies available in the literature utilized electrocardiogram (ECG) signal features for classification. Yao et al.21 introduced a multi-scale SE-residual network with a transformer encoder using PTB diagnostic ECG data, they attained 82.98% accuracy. Rath et al.22 compared an ensemble model with the logistic regression, SVM, and AdaBoost by using the MIT-BIH arrhythmia database, their suggested ensemble model performs best with 92.1% accuracy.

While the findings from previous population-based studies provide valuable insights, their sampling strategies, and age distributions, as observed in the Prospective Urban Rural Epidemiology (PURE) analysis, may not accurately represent the older adult population23. Also, the estimates derived from these studies may be less reliable for older age groups. CVD and premature death are primarily caused by hypertension, the leading risk factor24, with over 500,000 hypertension-related deaths recorded in 201925. Ischemic heart disease (IHD) is the most common cardiovascular disease1. IHD is considered a significant challenge to sustainable development in the twenty-first century26.

Valvular Heart Disease (VHD) represents a significant group of CVD conditions that severely impact the quality of life, and physical functionality globally. Rheumatic heart disease (RHD) persists as the predominant form of VHD. However, calcific aortic stenosis and degenerative mitral valve disease are also highly prevalent. Despite a decline in the worldwide mortality rates associated with RHD, deaths attributed to calcific aortic stenosis have increased over the past two decades27. Advancements in diagnostic imaging, interventional cardiology, and surgical approaches have facilitated accurate diagnosis and appropriate therapeutic interventions for VHD patients. A comprehensive evaluation is crucial, encompassing a thorough patient history to assess contributing factors and symptoms, and precise examination for measuring valve dysfunction28. While RHD remains the major type of VHD, calcific aortic stenosis and mitral valve disease are also widespread. Although global mortality rates related to RHD have decreased, fatalities due to calcific aortic stenosis have risen in recent years. However, insufficient epidemiological data exists for other forms of acquired and congenital VHD. As the population ages and therapies progress, investigating the evolving global epidemiology of VHD is essential to guide clinical practice and health policy development29.

This study uses advanced ML techniques to better predict and diagnose VHD. It follows a two-part investigation; first feature extraction, then classification. In the initial feature extraction phase, a wrapping approach is used and further refined with binary logistic regression. The second phase employs five advanced classifiers: Artificial Neural Network (ANN), XGBoost (XGB), Random Forest (RF), Naïve Bayes, and Support Vector Machine (SVM). Additionally, the study proposes a novel approach that combines SVM with Principal Component Analysis (PCA). Furthermore, it uses an ensemble strategy that takes a majority vote from these five classifiers (MV5) to combine the strengths of multiple models. Combining PCA with SVM is a unique way to reduce dimensions and extract important features, potentially helping the model identify key patterns from the available features of VHD more effectively. Investigating ensemble methods like MV5 shows a commitment to take advantage of the combined power of diverse models for better prediction accuracy. These innovative uses of ML algorithms and ensemble approaches hold promise for improving the early and accurate diagnosis of VHD, leading to timely treatments and better outcomes for patients.

Data description

In this study, primary data were collected through simple random sampling from the District Headquarters Hospital in Faisalabad, Pakistan. We confirm that the medical experts performed all experiments following relevant guidelines and regulations of specific national laws. A total of 624 patients were examined. The study variables included demographic factors, medical examination results (such as blood pressure and body measurements), and variables related to cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and smoking. The aim was to identify patients with VHD. Patient forms were reviewed in detail manually, and selection was carried out using simple random sampling procedures. Awareness of VHD was defined as having positive Mitral Stenosis (MS), Mitral Regurgitation (MR), Aortic Stenosis (AS), Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD), Aortic Regurgitation (AR), Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR), Pulmonary Stenosis (PS) or Tricuspid Stenosis (TS)30.

Risk factors and cardiovascular variables

The Healthy People Initiative by the Office of Prevention and Health Promotion aims to enhance cardiovascular health and reduce fatalities from cardiovascular disease. According to this initiative, the major controllable risk factors for heart disease include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, cigarette smoking, diabetes, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, overweight, and obesity31. For the current study, hypertension was defined as a mean systolic blood pressure of 140 mmHg and was utilized to develop the model based on the dichotomous dependent variable. The features chosen for this study were age, gender, area, obesity/body mass index (BMI), smoking, survival, hypertension, IHD, VHD, ischemic heart disease, pulmonary edema, corpulmonale, and diabetes.

Socio-demographic variables

Socio-demographic variables include gender (male/female), residence (urban/rural), and age. Age was categorized into groups 16 to 30 years; 31 to 45; 46 to 60 years; 61 to 75 years; 76 to 90 years; and 91 years and above.

Data acquisition and preprocessing

The datasets used in this research, comprised of 624 samples, were carefully curated and collected from District Headquarters Hospital Faisalabad, Pakistan to ensure their pertinence to the investigation of cardiovascular health. The dataset includes key attributes identified as significant contributors to heart disease risk, aligning with major risk factors outlined by the Healthy People initiative31. After addressing missing values in features such as DM, HTN, Smoke, Obesity, FH, and IHD, the dataset underwent refinement, resulting in a reduced but robust sample size of 583. In the current study analyzing VHD, risk factors are thoughtfully selected for a comprehensive understanding of cardiovascular health2,38–40. This facilitates a thorough evaluation of ML techniques for heart disease prediction across clinically significant categories. However, there are two fundamental challenges to developing ML models. Firstly, the data comprises relatively small size and secondly, there is a noticeable class imbalance issue. In response to the constraints posed by small data size and skewed class distribution, advanced techniques such as stratified k-fold cross-validation and SMOTE are utilized. It is worth noting that a stratified tenfold cross-validation methodology commonly used in AI for medical diagnosis, particularly when dealing with small data sets has been shown to significantly enhance accuracy and improve the model’s predictive capability41. To balance the skewed class in the dataset, employed Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) to address class imbalance. SMOTE helps balance the distribution of training data and improves overall model performance by creating new samples based on existing ones while avoiding exact duplications42.

Following data acquisition, the dataset is partitioned into training and testing sets using tenfold cross-validation, ensuring unbiased datasets. the feature selection process identifies attributes significantly impacting prediction, enhancing subsequent analysis and modeling efficiency. This process involves multiple steps, including the generation of attribute subsets and evaluation using wrapper techniques.

The feature selection process aims to identify and choose the attributes that significantly impact. This helps improve the efficiency and effectiveness of subsequent analysis and modeling. The process involves multiple steps, as described in43. Initially, the generation procedure generates a subset of attributes. When starting with an empty set, features are iteratively added through a forward selection approach. Conversely, if all attributes are initially included, the backward elimination process can be used to eliminate features. The selection procedure evaluates the generated features using various evaluation criteria. A frequently employed criterion in feature selection involves wrapper approaches, prioritizing features that improve classification accuracy. These approaches assess the estimation accuracy of different feature sets by including or excluding features from the data. The accuracy of each feature set is estimated using cross-validation on the training set. This process continues until a predetermined number of attributes is chosen, or a specific threshold criterion is met, as mentioned by Kohavi and John44. The feature selection process aims to identify the most relevant attributes and optimize the model’s classification accuracy by employing these steps.

While the heart disease datasets used in this study are well-established and specifically assembled, the full complement of attributes in the data is utilized without any selection since removing variables degraded classification performance. However, a wrapper-based feature selection approach was first tested using backward elimination along with tenfold cross-validation to evaluate different attribute subsets based on different classifiers used. The complete set of features provided maximal predictive accuracy over all possible variable combinations. Therefore, no features were excluded. However, for datasets potentially containing irrelevant attributes, targeted feature selection techniques would be employed to eliminate such features.

Following the feature selection process, the selected features undergo careful data-cleaning procedures for outlier detection and missing values treatment. This involves converting raw data into a format easily understandable by subsequent modules. Each attribute value undergoes individual processing steps before being passed on to the primary classifiers.

Missing data treatment

Dealing with missing values in clinical data is a crucial challenge that arises during the initial analysis and interpretation of the data. The objective of the suggested preprocessing approach aims to identify and address missing attribute values. The missing values in continuous attributes are substituted with the mean value, while the mode value is replaced in categorical attributes. This replacement process is applied to attributes where the missing values occur in fewer than 50% of the instances38. If the percentage of missing values is less than 20%, those instances are simply removed from the dataset. However, if the proportion of instances with missing values reaches 50% or more, the corresponding attribute is considered unreliable and excluded from further analysis38.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were conducted by using R-4.3.1. These risk factors measured the prevalence of VHD. The relationship between VHD and all health risk factors and social-demographic variables was examined using binary logistic regression. Logistic regression is used for predicting the outcome of a categorical dependent variable based on one or more predictor variables45. Pearl and Reed discovered logistic function in 1920, and it was the first time used in biometry by Raymond Pearl (1879–1940) to measure the effects of contraception, fertility, alcohol, and tobacco consumption on health46. Further, to check the association between categorical variables Pearson chi-square test of independence of attributes is used.

Application machine learning methods

To propose an accurate and reliable predictive model for VHD, numerous ML models were evaluated and compared against each other. The performance of SVM, PCA combined with SVM, RF, XGB, NB, ANN, and an ensemble approach called majority voting using five primary classifiers (MV5). After introducing the theory behind these models, we described the evaluation criteria used and presented a performance comparison of these models. To find the best hyperparameter settings for each model, we used a trial-and-error approach47. This approach involved flexibly adjusting the hyperparameter values based on experiments and observing how well the model performed. The iterative process of adjusting the parameters helped to optimize the model’s performance for the VHD dataset. This careful parameter tuning for each model improves their accuracy in the diagnosing of VHD.

SVMis a hyperplane that separates example sets as positive and negative with the maximum possible margin. SVMs fall under kernel methods and are used to compute a dot product in high-dimensional feature space. A quadratic programming optimization problem obtains the parameters of the solution hyperplane. SVM with PCA

PCA is a statistical method used to analyze the relationships between different features and items. It identifies the main components that show strong associations. PCA can take datasets with many dimensions and convert them into datasets with fewer dimensions51. This helps to reduce the complexity of data. The number of main components is determined by the eigenvalues of the correlation matrix, which must be greater than one. PCA is commonly used in classification and regression analysis52. SVM works well with datasets that have fewer features, and PCA can capture the most significant variations in the data while reducing the number of dimensions. Combining SVM and PCA can significantly improve the performance of the model53. Moreover, PCA can automatically determine the number of components using an algorithmic selection process. By effectively reducing the number of features and removing noise from the data, this combined approach of SVM and PCA has the potential to increase the accuracy and efficiency of predictive models54.

XGBoost

The XGB algorithm was developed by Chen et al.56. This application of gradient-boosting machines is both expandable and revolutionary. It employs the boosted trees algorithm to push the limits of computing power. The sole intention of its development was the computational speed and model performance. Boosting is an ensemble technique that adjusts the existing model errors by adding new models until the improvements can be detected. After generating new models, they are utilized to forecast the residual of the prior model and merge them to make the ultimate prediction. It adopts a gradient descent algorithm to reduce loss and simultaneously include new models. This technique can handle both regression and classification. Out of 29 ML tasks XGB successfully won 17 that were posted on Kaggle by 201557. Here, the decision trees are used as base learners, and 100 rounds of boosting are performed to optimize the model. The NB classifier operates on the principle that a specific disease’s presence or absence is independent of other features59. Performance evaluation criteria

Various methods are employed to assess the efficiency of machine learning methodologies. In the most common approach, the entire data set is partitioned into training, validation, and testing subsets, and all of these must be chosen independently. To begin, the data is trained using a training set. Following this, the classifiers’ parameters are optimized using a validation set. Finally, the error rate of the optimized classifiers is tested using test data. Because of the limited training and testing data, this method is not used practically. Moreover, one class should be represented in the right proportion in both subsets; as a result, samples may be non-representative in the training and testing subsets66.

Cross-validation is one of the most efficient statistical techniques for assessing the performance of machine learning. The ten-fold cross-validation approach divides the dataset into equal-sized folds or partitions, with one-fold reserved for testing and the other for training. In this study, we implemented the-fold cross-validation by dividing the data into ten equal folds. The model undergoes ten rounds of training and testing, with each fold used once for testing and the remaining nine for training. Discussion

In this article, firstly we applied a statistical method to measure the prevalence of a common cardiovascular disease, valvular heart disease (VHD), and the associated risk factors among patients with heart conditions in the cardiology ward of District Headquarters Hospital Faisalabad, Pakistan. Globally, in the past 20 years, the CVD premature mortality rate has increased rapidly (66%) from 7.1 to 12 million in East, South, and Southeast Asia, and some parts of Latin America73. More than 85% of deaths due to cardiovascular disease in 2016 were attributable to cerebrovascular disease and ischemic heart disease74. Our study indicated that VHD is prevalent after the age of 31 years especially older adults > 61 years are at high risk. A higher prevalence of VHD was observed among women compared to men, and the spread of VHD in urban areas is more than in rural. Yusuf et al.75 and O’Donnell et al.10 have shown that acute myocardial infarction and stroke act as independent risk factors for hypertension in older people.

Ruan et al.2 have found an association between hypertension and angina in India, the Russian Federation, and China. Besides hypertension, they also demonstrated a correlation between stroke and angina with obesity and low physical activity in China. In this study, we have found that hypertension and obesity both are independent of VHD. In India, a positive relationship exists between smoking and angina, with a prevalence of angina is 19.6%, also frequent heavy drinking and stroke are associated. Similarly, low alcohol consumption proved a protective factor against angina and stroke in China2. We found that obesity and DM have no association with VHD but a person with a family history of ischemic heart disease (someone from the respondent’s family had any heart or circulatory problem) has more chance of catching VHD. To substantiate our claim, we tested several ML models to classify VHD; including XGB, RF, SVM, NB, ANN, a voting ensemble method with five classifiers (MV5), and SVM combined with PCA (SVM + PCA).

Table 4 shows how well each model performed SVM + PCA did the best overall. It had the highest accuracy (96.97%), F-measure (98.98%), and ROC area (98.61%). It was also very remarkable to correctly identify both sick and healthy patients. This suggests that combining SVM with PCA works well for classifying VHD. The ensemble method MV5 also did very well, almost matching SVM + PCA in accuracy (96.89%). SVM and RF had similar accuracies of about (92%), but SVM was better at correctly classifying patients overall, especially in identifying sick patients (98.39% sensitivity). NB had the lowest accuracy (88.07%) among all methods. However, it was good at identifying sick patients (96.77%), but not as good at identifying healthy ones (60% specificity). XGB had a slightly higher accuracy than NB (90.20%) and was more balanced in identifying both sick and healthy patients. How well these methods work depends on their design, how we choose important features, and what data we use to train and test them. SVM + PCA worked very well with our data, showing that reducing the number of features before using SVM helps classify VHD. It is worth noting that methods like MV5 depend on factors like how many decision-makers we use and which features we choose. These factors greatly affect how well they work for identifying VHD.

When we compare our results to other studies on heart disease classification, our methods performed very well. The proposed SVM + PCA achieved the highest accuracy (96.97%) among all the studies shown in Table 1. It also had the best F-measure (98.98%), ROC area (98.61%), and very high sensitivity (98.38%) and specificity (87.43%). This means it was excellent at correctly identifying both sick and healthy patients. Importantly, this study also measured two additional metrics that many studies didn’t report; MCC and Cohen’s Kappa. These metrics are valuable because they provide a more balanced evaluation of the model’s performance, especially when dealing with imbalanced datasets. For the SVM + PCA method, we achieved an MCC of 90.25% and a Kappa of 89.14%. These high values indicate that our model performs consistently well across all categories, not just the majority class. Our developed ensemble method (MV5) also showed strong performance in these metrics, with an MCC of 84.97% and a Kappa of 84.94%. by including these measures, we provide a more comprehensive evaluation of our models’ performance than many previous studies. Proposed MV5 and ANN also did better than most other studies, with accuracies of 96.89 and 96.88% respectively. These results are higher than those reported by Dritsas and Trigka (87.80%)76, Abdar et al. (93.08%)17, and Javeed et al. (93.33%)16. Some individual methods like SVM, RF, and Nb performed well for some metrics compared to many previous studies. For instance, SVM had a higher ROC area (96.53%) than the ensemble model used by Rath et al. (95.00%)22. It’s interesting to note that our study used fewer features compared to some other studies as shown in Table 1. This suggests that our feature selection and model design were very effective. Our results also show improvements over the study by Kagiyama et al., who used AI-ECG as a screening tool and achieved a ROC area of 81%. All of our models performed better in the ROC area, with ANN, SVM, and RF showing particularly good results. Compared to the work of Shouman et al.77, who used various data mining techniques, our models achieved notably better results. Similarly, while Ghumbre et al.78 found SVM and Radial basis function classifiers successful for heart disease diagnosis, our SVM + PCA method showed even better performance.

These comparisons show that ML can be very powerful for identifying and diagnosing heart disease, particularly VHD. The current study demonstrated that combining methods like SVM + PCA, MV5, or using advanced NNs, can lead to even better results than traditional single-method approaches. Despite many researchers having successfully used ML for heart disease diagnosis, our study achieved particularly high performance across multiple metrics, including MCC and Kappa, which provide a more robust evaluation of model performance. The proposed method using SVM combined with PCA is more complex than simpler models, but the significant improvements in diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and other performance metrics justify its implementation. The added complexity from PCA reduces the feature space, making the SVM more efficient and accurate, achieving the highest accuracy than other models. Similarly, the ensemble method MV5, despite its higher computational demands, provides robust performance with balanced sensitivity and specificity. These enhancements, along with strong performance in metrics such as the F-measure and ROC area, are crucial in clinical settings where even small improvements can lead to better patient outcomes. Therefore, the proposed methods, though complex, offer significant benefits in accurately diagnosing VHD, making their implementation valuable. This suggests that our approach, especially the SVM + PCA method, could be very useful for diagnosing VHD accurately.

Conclusion

This study utilized advanced ML techniques to predict and diagnose VHD effectively. By applying a two-phase approach; feature extraction followed by classification. This research explored various ML models and innovative combinations to improve diagnostic accuracy. Notably, the combination of SVM with PCA demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving the highest accuracy (96.97%) and exhibiting superior balance across other metrics such as F-measure (98.98%), ROC area (98.61%), sensitivity (98.38%), and specificity (87.43%). This highlights the efficiency of dimensionality reduction combined with robust classification methods. Furthermore, the ensemble method MV5, which integrates multiple classifiers through majority voting, also performed exceptionally well, nearly matching the accuracy of SVM + PCA (96.89%) and demonstrating a strong balance between sensitivity (97.74%) and specificity (84.29%).

This emphasizes the potential of ensemble approaches in merging the strength of diverse models for enhanced predictive power. The study’s statistical analysis revealed significant associations between age and VHD, with the highest prevalence observed in patients over 60 years old. Additionally, males exhibited a higher likelihood of VHD compared to females. Residence (rural and urban), IHD history, DM, hypertension, smoking, obesity, and family history showed no significant association with VHD. However, a history of IHD significantly increased the likelihood of VHD, emphasizing the interconnected nature of CVD. These findings align with global trends in CVD epidemiology, highlighting age and history of IHD as critical risk factors. Comparatively, the proposed SVM + PCA model outperformed many previously reported methods in heart disease classification, as evidenced by higher accuracy, F-measure, and ROC area scores. The inclusion of metrics like MCC and Cohen’s Kappa further validated the robustness of our models, providing a comprehensive evaluation that addresses class imbalance.

These findings showcase the significant potential of combining ML techniques and ensemble strategies to improve the early diagnosis of VHD. The innovative application of SVM + PCA and ensemble method MV5 not only enhances diagnostic accuracy but also offers practical insights for clinical application, potentially leading to timely interventions and better patient outcomes. In future research, these techniques can be applied to other CVD conditions such as ischemic heart disease, corpulmonale, and pulmonary edema, thus broadening their impact on CVD healthcare.

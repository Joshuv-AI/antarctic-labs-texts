CDCA7 Promotes Neuroblastoma Proliferation via Regulating the Cell Cycle
Authors: Wen Yuan, Xiaoxing Huang, Renwei Luo, Dongyang Yu, Yang Pan, Hui Du, Xiang Ma, Yun Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground: Cell division cycle-associated 7 (CDCA7) is overexpressed in several malignant cancers and promotes tumorigenesis. However, the clinical and biological functions of CDCA7 in neuroblastoma (NB) are unclear.Objectives: This study aimed to investigate the expression pattern, clinical significance, and biological functions of CDCA7 in neuroblastoma, and to elucidate its potential molecular mechanisms in regulating cell proliferation and cell cycle progression.Materials and Methods: The NB database was used to evaluate the predictive significance of CDCA7 and its correlation with clinical features of NB. The prognostic significance of mRNA expression of CDCA7 in lung cancer was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier Analysis. Stable knockdown of CDCA7 was achieved using shRNA and cell proliferation was evaluated using the CCK8 assay. Flow cytometry was used to analyze the cell cycle distribution. The effects of CDCA7 interference on cell cycle-related proteins in NB cells were measured by fluorescence quantitative PCR and Western blotting. Additionally, the role of CDCA7 in human immunity was examined.Results: In this study, we screened NB datasets and found that CDCA7 was highly expressed in NB tissues and cell lines compared with normal tissues and cell lines. CDCA7 expression was notably higher in MYCN-amplified NB tissues than that in their non-MYCN-amplified counterparts. Furthermore, increased CDCA7 expression was significantly associated with advanced tumor stages. Additionally, high CDCA7 expression may predict poor overall survival (OS) and event-free survival (EFS) in patients with NB. Moreover, Silencing CDCA7 effectively reduced cell proliferation, increased the proportion of cells in G1 phase, and lowered cyclin-dependent kinase 6 (CDK6) expression. CDCA7 is closely associated with immune cell infiltration by B cells, CD4+ T cells, and macrophages.Conclusion: CDCA7 may serve as a prognostic marker for neuroblastoma. Its silencing leads to G1 cell cycle arrest and a reduction in CDK6 expression, ultimately inhibiting NB cell proliferation.Keywords: CDCA7, Cell cycle, CDK6, Neuroblastoma, Proliferation

1. Background

Neuroblastoma is an embryonal malignancy that originates in the autonomic nervous system, specifically derived from primitive neural crest cells, and can manifest at various locations along the sympathetic nervous system ( 1 ). It is the third most prevalent cancer in children, accounting for 7% of all cancer diagnoses in individuals aged from birth to 14 years, and contributes to 15% of cancer-related fatalities in the pediatric population of the United States ( 2 ). N-myc, a member of the MYC family of trans-cription factors, was initially identified in neuroblastoma ( 3 , 4 ). Approximately 20% of neuroblastoma cases and 45% of those classified as high-risk exhibit MYCN gene ( 5 ). According to the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) consensus pretreatment classification schema, amplification of the N-Myc oncogene is typically associated with high-risk neuroblastoma with a 5-year EFS rate of less than 50% ( 6 ).

In 1997, Lewis et al. ( 7 ) identified a novel cMyc–responsive gene, now known as CDCA7, which participates in cMyc–mediated neoplastic transformation ( 8 ). Elevated CDCA7 expression during the blast or terminal phases of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), together with in vivo tumorigenicity studies in transgenic mice, supports its role in tumorigenesis ( 9 ). Moreover, CDCA7 overexpression has been linked to poor prognosis in triplenegative breast cancer ( 10 ) and colorectal cancer ( 11 ). Additional studies indicate that CDCA7 regulates cytoskeletal dynamics, thereby promoting lymphoma cell migration and invasion ( 12 ). In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, CDCA7 drives tumor progression by directly modulating CCNA2 ( 13 ), and in liver cancer it may influence disease progression through effects on macrophage polarization ( 14 ). Despite these advances, the mechanisms by which CDCA7 contributes to neuroblastoma remain largely unclear. In this study, we explored the relationship between CDCA7 and neuroblastoma using data from publicly available databases. We found that CDCA7 is significantly upregulated in MYCNamplified neuroblastoma tissues and cell lines, and its expression correlates with patient prognosis. Functional assays further demonstrated that CDCA7 silencing suppresses cell proliferation, increases the proportion of cells in G1 phase, and reduces CDK6 expression.

2. Objectives

This study elucidates the potential oncogenic role of CDCA7 in the progression of neuroblastoma and may inform the development of more effective therapeutic strategies for this malignancy.

3. Materials and Methods

3.1. Data Collection

Microarray datasets generated on the GPL570 platform for different types of specimens, including normal adrenal glands (normal AG; GSE3526, GSE7307, and GSE8514) and NB tissue samples (GSE13136, 14880, 16237, and 16476), were extracted from the R2 database (https://hgserver1.amc.nl/cgi-bin/r2/main.cgi). The GSE66586 dataset (eight NB cell lines and two non-malignant cell lines) and GSE19274 dataset (21 MYCN-amplified and 11 non-MYCN-amplified cell lines) were also extracted from the R2. GSE49710 (n=498), E-MTAB-8248 (n=223) and TARGET (n=251), with tumor biological features (such as age at diagnosis, INSS stage, MYCN, 11q, 1p, telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) mutations, mitosis-karyorrhexis index (MKI), histology, and high risk) and survival information, were extracted from the R2: DataGrabber (https://hgserver1.amc.nl/cgi-bin/r2/main.cgi). 3.2. Analysis of Genes Related to CDCA7 Expression

Pearson’s correlation analysis was performed using the GSE45547, GSE49710, and GSE19274 (33 NB cell lines) datasets. Genes showing a Pearson correlation coefficient of (R \ge 0.6) and (p < 0.05) with the relevant clusters were selected as cell cluster–specific genes and uploaded to DAVID (https://david.ncifcrf.gov) for KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. In addition, GSE45547, GSE49710, and GSE19274 were used to evaluate the correlation between CDCA7 and CDK6 expression.

3.3. Immune Cell Infiltration Analysis

We used the “CIBERSORT” package to analyze immune cell infiltration in the NB gene expression profile (GSE45547). The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to perform differential analysis of the 22 immune cell types between the high and low CDCA7 expression groups.

3.4. Cell Transfection

A total of 4 × 106 HEK293T cells were seeded in 10cm tissue culture dishes and cotransfected with the viral packaging plasmid psPAX2 (8 µg), the viral envelope plasmid pMD2.G (4 µg), and the pLKO.1 hairpin plasmid (12 µg) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Viral supernatants were collected 48 h posttransfection, passed through a 0.45µm filter, and used immediately for infection. Target neuroblastoma cell lines were seeded in 10cm plates and incubated overnight. The culture medium was then replaced with 2 mL of viral supernatant containing 8 µg. mL-1 polybrene and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C, after which 8 mL of growth medium was added to each plate. At 2 days posttransduction, control and shRNAexpressing cells were selected with 2 µg. mL-1 puromycin and subsequently used for assays of cell growth, cell cycle distribution, and protein and transcript levels. The shRNA target sequences were as follows: shCDCA7, GTGCATGCCTACTTGAAAA; shNTC (nontargeting control), CCTAAGGTTAAGTCGCCCTCGC.

3.5. Real-Time Quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) Analysis

Total RNA was extracted from the cell lines using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) from cell lines according to the manufacturer’s specifications. Total RNA (2 µg) was reverse-transcribed using a RevertAid RT Reverse Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). mRNA expression was examined by qRT-PCR using SYBR Premix Taq and the Applied QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System. GAPDH was used as the reference gene and the amplification results for qRT-PCR were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method. The expression level of each gene was measured in triplicate. The primers used in this study are listed in Supplementary Material (Table 2).

3.6. Western Blot Analysis

Radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer supplemented with 1% protease inhibitor phenyl-methanesulfonylfluoride (PMSF) was used for total protein extraction from cells, and a Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) kit (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used to detect total protein concentration. Proteins were adjusted to suitable concentrations using deionized water, separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk and subsequently incubated with anti-human CDCA7 (1:200, HPA005565; Sigma, WGK, St. Louis, MO, USA), CDK6 (1:1000, ab124821, Abcam Inc., Cambridge, MA, U.K.), or GAPDH (1:10000, 10494-1-AP, Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA). overnight at 4 °C. After washing thrice, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using ECL Plus western blot analysis detection reagents (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). GAPDH was used as the internal control.

3.7. Cell Proliferation Assay

Cells transfected in 6-well plates for 48 h were digested into a single-cell suspension. The cells were then incubated in three 96-well plates at a density of 3,000/100 µL. well-1, and each 96-well plate was equipped with five wells for each group. To each well, 100 µL complete medium and 10 µL CCK-8 (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) were added at the indicated time points and incubated for another 3h. The absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm using a PerkinElmer 2030 VICTOR X Multilabel Plate Reader (Perkin-Elmer, Waltham, MA, USA).

3.8. Cell Cycle Assay

NB cells were transfected for 48 h and digested into a single-cell suspension. After overnight incubation with 70% ethanol, the cells were washed thrice with PBS for 5 min each. Subsequently, 100 µL RNA enzyme A was added to suspend the cells and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in a water bath. The cells were stained with 400 µL propidium iodide (PI) (KGA512, Jiangsu KeyGEN BioTECH Corp., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) for 30 min on ice in the dark. The cells were then washed twice with ice-cold PBS, and the PI intensity was detected by flow cytometry (Becton Dickinson, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). The results were analyzed using ModFit software according to the manufacturer’s specifications.

3.9. Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistic Package for Social Science (SPSS) ver. 21 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism V.8.00 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). A two-tailed t-test was used to compare the expression of CDCA7 in different groups, and a paired t-test was used for matched samples. Data are expressed as the mean ± SD. *P<0.05, **P<0.01, ***P<0.001.

4. Results

4.1. CDCA7 Was Abundantly Expressed in NB And Closely Correlated with Clinicopathological Features

To determine the potential role of CDCA7 in NB, we analyzed transcriptomic NB data in tissues and cell lines and found a higher expression of CDCA7 in NB than in normal tissues (Fig. 1A) and cell lines (Fig. 1B). Many factors influence prognosis, but the most important are tumor stage and patient age. Genomic amplification of the MYCN proto-oncogene has been established as an independent marker for high-risk NB. We examined the expression profiles of the different groups in GSE49710 and found that CDCA7 levels were higher in the ≥18 months, MYCN-amp, T3+T4, and high-risk groups (Fig. 1C). Increased CDCA7 expression was significantly correlated with an advanced tumor stage (Fig. 1C-F). However, the expression level of CDCA7 in stage 4S was lower than that in stage 4 (Fig. 1C-D). Patients with stage 4S are known to have a high degree of spontaneous regression and a favorable prognosis. We also found that CDCA7 expression is signiﬁcantly higher in MYCN-amplified compared with non-MYCN-amplified patients by examining the GSE49710 (Fig. 1C), GSE45547 (Fig. 1D), TARGET (Fig. 1E) and E-MTAB-8248 (Fig. 1F) datasets.

CDCA7 expression in neuroblastoma tumor tissues and cell lines. A) CDCA7 expression profiles across microarray datasets using the GPL570 platform. B) Analysis of CDCA7 expression in the normal and NB cell lines from GSE66586. C-F) Comparison of CDCA7 expression levels in different groups from the GSE49710 C), GSE45547 D), TARGET E), and E-MTAB-8248 F) datasets.

Furthermore, the expression of CDCA7 were significantly upregulated in unfavorable histology and high MKI NB tissues (Fig. 1E). In comparison with TERT wild-type NB tissue, the expression of CDCA7 was elevated in TERT-mutated NB tissues (Fig. 1F). As shown in Figure 1F, CDCA7 was highly expressed in the 1q del NB tissues.

4.2. High CDCA7 Expression Is Prognostic for Poor Survival in Neuroblastoma Patients

We also explored the prognostic value of the expression of CDCA7 using the Kaplan–Meier method and log-rank test. Patients with NB were split into low- and high-expression groups based on median CDCA7 expression values. High expression of CDCA7 mRNA was associated with significantly shorter overall and event-free survival in the GSE9710 dataset (Fig. 2A, 2D). We confirmed that high CDCA7 expression is a prognostic factor for unfavorable outcomes using the E-MTAB-8248 (Fig. 2B, 2E) and TARGET (Fig. 2C, 2F) datasets. Taken together, our analyses of three in-dependent microarray datasets indicate that CDCA7 is a prognostic marker for neuroblastoma.

D-F) Kaplan-Meier event-free survival curves generated from the GSE49710 D), TARGET E), and E-MTAB-8248 F) datasets.

4.3. Silencing CDCA7 Inhibits NB Cells Proliferation and G1 Phase Arrest

To provide insights into the role of CDCA7 in NB, CDCA7 positively correlated (r>0.6 and p<0.05) genes were analyzed using three independent microarray datasets. Among these, 168 common genes were identified as CDCA7 positively correlated genes (Fig. 3A). KEGG analysis of 168 genes was conducted using the online tool DAVID, which revealed that CDCA7 positively correlated genes mainly participated in the cell cycle, DNA replication, mismatch repair, and cellular senescence (Fig. 3B).

To evaluate whether CDCA7 is biologically relevant to NB cell malignant phenotypes, we first stably silenced CDCA7 in SK-N-BE (2) cells and performed loss-of-function experiments in vitro (Fig. 3C and 3D). Suppression of CDCA7 expression with shRNA significantly (P < 0.05) inhibited cellular proliferation (Fig. 3E) compared with the control cells by arresting cell-cycle progression at the G1 phase compared with the control cells (Fig. 3F).

4.4. CDCA7 Exerts Its Oncogenic Function Via Upregulation of CDK6

Considering that knockdown of CDCA7 inhibited cellular proliferation and induced G1 arrest, we measured some G1 and G1/S phase regulators in NC- and shCDCA7-treated SK-N-BE(2) cells using quantitative RT-PCR, including the expression levels of CCND1, CCNE1, CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, p21, and p27. The mRNA expression levels of CDK6 in shCDCA7-treated SK-N-BE (2) cells were significantly lower than those in NC-transfected cells, while CCND1, CCNE1, CDK2, CDK4, p21, and p27 did not show any significant changes (Fig. 4A). Next, we performed western blotting to detect changes in the CDK6 protein levels. The results show that after interfering with CDCA7 expression of CDK6 was decreased (Fig. 4B). We also analyzed the correlation between CDCA7 and CDK6 expression levels in NB tissue datasets (GSE45547 and GSE49710) and NB cell dataset GSE19274, and found that CDCA7 was significantly correlated with CDK6 in NB tissues and cell lines with statistical significance (Fig. 4C-4E), which was consistent with the results of cytological experiments.

4.5. The Relationship Between CDCA7 Expression Levels and Tumor Immune Cell Infiltration

To further explore the potential impact of CDCA7 on the infiltration of 22 types of tumor immune cells in NB, we divided the samples into two groups (high- and low-expression groups) according to the expression of CDCA7. Analysis of the data from the GSE45547 (n=649) dataset showed that the tumor tissues in the CDCA7 low expression group were rich in various activated immune cells, including activated B cells, activated CD4+ T cells, and M1 macrophages (Fig. 5).

5. Discussion

Recent studies have shown that CDCA7 overexpression is prevalent across multiple cancer types. In liver cancer, CDCA7 has been identified as a marker of poor prognosis, as its high expression promotes cellular proliferation and colony formation ( 15 ). CDCA7 is also highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma ( 16 ), colon cancer ( 11 ), and triplenegative breast cancer ( 10 ), where its upregulation similarly correlates with unfavorable clinical outcomes. In our analysis of neuroblastoma (NB) datasets, CDCA7 expression increased progressively with advancing disease stage. Patients with high CDCA7 levels exhibited shorter overall survival and relapsefree survival, indicating that CDCA7 is closely associated with NB clinical stage and functions as a prognostic indicator of poor outcome in NB.

We analyzed 168 genes that were positively correlated with CDCA7 expression in NB datasets. KEGG pathway analysis of these 168 genes, together with CDCA7, revealed enrichment in tumor-related pathways such as cell cycle, homologous recombination, and mismatch repair. These findings suggest that CDCA7 may contribute to NB development. Previous studies reported that elevated CDCA7 expression is closely associated with cell cycle-related pathways. CDCA7 regulates the proli-feration and cell cycle of lung adenocarcinoma cells by modulating CCNE1 and CCNE2 expression ( 13 ). In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, upregulated CDCA7 expression promotes cancer cell proliferation by targeting CCNA2, a cell cycle-related protein ( 16 ).

To validate our bioinformatics findings, we performed in vitro experiments and observed that CDCA7 knockdown inhibited the proliferation of SKNBE (2) cells and induced G1phase cell cycle arrest in neuroblastoma cells, consistent with previous reports in lung adenocarcinoma. To elucidate the molecular mechanism by which CDCA7 regulates the cell cycle, we assessed the expression of G1phase–related genes (CCND1, CCNE1, CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, p21, and p27) before and after CDCA7 knockdown using qRTPCR and western blotting. Among these, CDK6 expression was markedly reduced. CDK6, a member of the cyclindependent kinase (CDK) family, forms a complex with cyclin D during the G1 phase. This complex phosphorylates the retinoblastoma protein (RB), thereby releasing E2F1 transcription factors and promoting Sphase entry ( 17 ). Our results suggest that CDCA7 knockdown downregulates CDK6, leading to G1phase arrest and consequent inhibition of neuroblastoma cell proliferation.

CDCA7 was first identified as a c-Myc target gene in tumors ( 7 ). c-Myc binds to the CDCA7 promoter to enhance its expression ( 8 ). CT5-2, a potential anti-rheumatoid arthritis (RA) drug, suppressed the proliferation of fibroblast-like synoviocytes by regulating the c-Myc/CDCA7/p65 pathway ( 18 ). E2F1 also regulates CDCA7 expression by binding to its promoter ( 19 ). Additionally, CDCA7 acts as a Notch target gene that is involved in hematopoietic stem cell formation ( 20 ). These studies highlight CDCA7’s role as a downstream target of multiple signaling pathways.

We previously reported that ZWINT expression was higher in MYCN-amplified NB tissues/cell lines than in non-amplified tissues. MYCN binds to the ZWINT promoter and ZWINT knockdown attenuates MYCN-driven proliferation ( 21 ). Here, we found that CDCA7 expression is significantly elevated in MYCN-amplified NB tissues and cells, suggesting a potential link between CDCA7 and MYCN. Further studies are required to determine whether CDCA7 is regulated directly by MYCN.

Recent studies suggest that CDCA7 promotes glioma development by increasing the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells in the tumor microenvironment ( 21 , 22 ). In liver cancer, CDCA7 correlates positively with M0 macrophages and negatively with M2 macrophages, implying its role in macrophage polarization ( 23 ). CDCA7 is also associated with immune infiltration, tumor microenvironment, immune checkpoint molecules, and immune pathways in clear cell renal cell carcinoma ( 24 ). To investigate CDCA7’s role in NB immune regulation, we analyzed immune cell infiltration in the high- and low-CDCA7 expression groups. Low CDCA7 expression correlated with increased infiltration of activated immune cells, including B cells, CD4+ T cells, and M1 macrophages. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) improve the prognosis of NB, and high levels of TILs, dendritic cells, and NK cells enhance the efficacy of PD-1 immunotherapy ( 25 ).

In summary, our study demonstrated that CDCA7 overexpression correlates with tumor progression and poor prognosis in patients with NB. CDCA7 is upregulated in MYCN-amplified NB cells and tissues. CDCA7 knockdown reduced CDK6 expression, induces G1-phase arrest, and inhibited NB cell proliferation. These findings highlight CDCA7’s critical role in NB progression and suggest its potential as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for NB.

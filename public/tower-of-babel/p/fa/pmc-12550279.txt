Sustained resolution of enthesitis and peripheral arthritis over 104 weeks with bimekizumab in axial spondyloarthritis
Authors: Sofia Ramiro, Denis Poddubnyy, Philip J Mease, Clementina López-Medina, Mindy Kim, Ute Massow, Vanessa Taieb, Tue Wenzel Kragstrup, Dennis McGonagle
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBimekizumab (BKZ), a monoclonal IgG1 antibody that selectively inhibits interleukin (IL)-17F in addition to IL-17A, has demonstrated 2-year efficacy in non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA) and radiographic axSpA (r-axSpA) phase III studies.ObjectiveAssess the impact of BKZ on peripheral manifestations to week 104 of those studies.MethodsBE MOBILE 1 (nr-axSpA) and 2 (r-axSpA) each comprised a 16-week double-blind, placebo-controlled period, then all received BKZ 160 mg every 4 weeks for 36 weeks. Patients not meeting withdrawal criteria could enter a combined open-label extension. We report change in enthesitis (Maastricht Ankylosing Spondylitis Enthesitis Score (MASES)) in patients with baseline MASES>0, peripheral arthritis (swollen joint count (SJC)/Disease Activity Index for Psoriatic Arthritis (DAPSA)) in patients with baseline SJC>0 and proportions achieving DAPSA disease states to week 104. Resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0)/arthritis (SJC=0) is reported to week 104 for those with baseline enthesitis/arthritis. We also report associations between peripheral manifestation resolution (MASES=0/SJC=0) and week 104 clinical outcomes in those with baseline enthesitis/arthritis.ResultsAt baseline, 186/254 (73.2%) and 88/254 (34.6%) patients with nr-axSpA had enthesitis (MASES>0) and arthritis (SJC>0), respectively, compared with 199/332 (59.9%) and 66/332 (19.9%) patients with r-axSpA. Pooled BKZ/placebo-randomised patients with enthesitis (nr-axSpA/r-axSpA) showed average MASES improvement from 4.8/4.3 (baseline) to 1.6/1.3 (week 52) and 1.6/1.0 (week 104). Pooled BKZ/placebo-randomised patients with arthritis showed average SJC improvement from 4.0/4.5 (baseline) to 1.2/0.7 (week 52) and 0.9/0.6 (week 104). Over 60% of patients achieved DAPSA low disease activity/remission by week 52. Over 40%/60% patients achieved resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0)/arthritis (SJC=0) at week 104; enthesitis resolution was associated with larger improvements in week 104 clinical outcomes for patients with r-axSpA.ConclusionBKZ resulted in sustained improvements in peripheral manifestations to 2 years across the full disease spectrum of axSpA.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

In this first analysis dedicated to reporting the effect of biologic treatment on the peripheral manifestations of axSpA, using data from randomised controlled trials, these post hoc results show that BKZ reduces enthesitis and peripheral arthritis in patients with nr-axSpA and r-axSpA up to 2 years.

Introduction

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) is a chronic inflammatory rheumatic and musculoskeletal disease with axial as well as peripheral and extra-musculoskeletal manifestations, such as uveitis and inflammatory bowel disease.1 The full disease spectrum includes radiographic axSpA (r-axSpA), where patients display evidence of structural damage to the sacroiliac joints (SIJ) on plain radiographs, and non-radiographic axSpA (nr-axSpA), where radiographic evidence of SIJ damage is absent.1

Peripheral manifestations such as enthesitis, peripheral arthritis and dactylitis are common in patients with axSpA and can result in considerable functional impairment.26 While the global cross-sectional Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society (ASAS)-Peripheral Involvement in SpondyloArthritis (PerSpA) study found rates of enthesitis in patients with axSpA to be around 41%,3 it has been suggested that its ‘true’ prevalence may be higher due to difficulties assessing entheses clinically, with imaging studies assessing enthesitis indicating higher prevalence than clinical assessment alone.7 8 Peripheral arthritis in inception cohorts of patients with axSpA, such as DEvenir des Spondyloarthropathies Indifférenciées Récentes (DESIR), a large multicentre longitudinal cohort study which followed patients with inflammatory back pain of duration >3 months but <3 years, and the SPondyloArthritis Caught Early (SPACE) cohort which included patients with back pain symptoms for ≥3 months but ≤2 years, reported the prevalence of peripheral arthritis at baseline as 21% and 15%, respectively.9 10 The GErman SPondyloarthritis Inception Cohort (GESPIC), which enrolled patients with nr-axSpA and r-axSpA and a longer symptom duration of ≤5 and ≤10 years, respectively, reported higher percentages of baseline peripheral arthritis (nr-axSpA: 41%; r-axSpA: 37%).9 11

9 10 9 11

Given the high prevalence and impact of enthesitis and peripheral arthritis in axSpA, it is critical to evaluate if new treatments are effective at controlling these peripheral manifestations.3 The importance of managing peripheral manifestations is reflected by the update of the ASAS axSpA core outcome set, which now includes enthesitis, arthritis and dactylitis in the mandatory domain of peripheral manifestations due to their contribution to the high disease burden and poor quality of life of patients.4 5 12 13

Bimekizumab (BKZ), a humanised monoclonal IgG1 antibody that selectively inhibits the key inflammatory mediators interleukin (IL)-17A and IL-17F, has demonstrated efficacy up to 2 years across the full disease spectrum of axSpA in the phase III BE MOBILE studies and their open-label extension (OLE), and up to 5 years in patients with r-axSpA in the phase IIb BE AGILE study.14 15

Here, we report the effect of BKZ treatment on the main peripheral manifestations of axSpA, including enthesitis and peripheral arthritis, using a range of measures including the Disease Activity Index for Psoriatic Arthritis (DAPSA), to week 104 in the BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies. We also evaluate the association between resolution of these peripheral manifestations and improvement in clinical outcomes to week 104.

Methods

Study design and patients

The study designs, including randomisation methods and inclusion criteria, for the parallel studies BE MOBILE 1 (nr-axSpA; NCT03928704) and BE MOBILE 2 (r-axSpA; NCT03928743) and their combined OLE (BE MOVING, NCT04436640) have been reported previously (online supplemental figure S1).14 16 The BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies each comprised a 16-week, double-blinded, placebo-controlled period followed by a 36-week maintenance period. In the placebo-controlled period, patients were randomised 1:1 (BE MOBILE 1) or 2:1 (BE MOBILE 2) to subcutaneous BKZ 160 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W) or placebo. All patients received BKZ 160 mg Q4W from week 16 onwards. On completion of week 52, patients not meeting withdrawal criteria were eligible to enrol in the OLE, where they continued to receive BKZ 160 mg Q4W. Withdrawal criteria for BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies included withdrawal of patient consent, non-compliance with study procedures or medications, and use of prohibited concomitant medication.

Patients in BE MOBILE 1 had a clinical diagnosis of nr-axSpA and fulfilled ASAS classification criteria.14 17 Patients were also required to have objective signs of inflammation at screening (ie, active sacroiliitis on MRI or elevated C-reactive protein ≥6.0 mg/L). Patients in BE MOBILE 2 had a clinical diagnosis of r-axSpA and fulfilled modified New York criteria.14 All patients fulfilled ASAS classification criteria and were required to have a history of intolerance, contraindication or prior failure of ≥2 non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Additionally, patients were required to have active nr-axSpA or r-axSpA at baseline (total Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI) ≥4 and spinal pain (BASDAI Q2) score ≥4).

Peripheral manifestations

Enthesitis was measured using the Maastricht Ankylosing Spondylitis Enthesitis Score (MASES; score range 0–13, with lower scores indicating less severe enthesitis), assessed at baseline and weeks 4, 8, 16, 24, 52, 64, 80 and 104. Peripheral arthritis was measured using swollen joint count (SJC). Assessments of SJC were performed in 44 joints, with lower scores indicating less severe peripheral arthritis, at baseline and weeks 8, 16, 24, 52, 80 and 104. Ultrasound and MRI imaging were not used to detect the presence of enthesitis in these studies.

Changes in peripheral manifestations are reported only for patients with enthesitis (MASES>0) or peripheral arthritis (SJC>0) at baseline; patients without enthesitis or peripheral arthritis are not included in these analyses.

This analysis reports baseline characteristics for patients with nr-axSpA and r-axSpA with MASES >0 and SJC >0, and distribution of enthesitis by tendons and swollen joints at baseline, as well as the following outcomes over 104 weeks: least squares mean change from baseline (CfB) and mean CfB in enthesitis (MASES; among patients with MASES >0 at baseline), peripheral arthritis (SJC; among patients with SJC >0 at baseline) and DAPSA (also among patients with SJC >0 at baseline).

The DAPSA has previously shown potential as an alternative measure of peripheral arthritis and related disease activity in axSpA and has therefore been used in this analysis.18 19 Since SJC and tender joint count (TJC) were assessed in 44 joints, conversion factors of 1.3 were applied to predict the 66 swollen/68 tender joint counts; this was calculated as: DAPSA = (1.3×SJC)+(1.3×TJC)+patient’s global assessment of disease activity+BASDAI Q3+high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (mg/dL).18 19 In a previous construct validity analysis, this methodology for joint conversion was found to be comparable to the original DAPSA 66/68 joint count methodology when used for patients with axSpA, peripheral SpA and psoriatic arthritis, showing near-perfect agreement.19 In the absence of pre-defined thresholds for axSpA, thresholds used in psoriatic arthritis were applied to define DAPSA states: remission (REM; ≤4), low disease activity (LDA; >4 to ≤14), moderate disease activity (MDA; >14 to ≤28) and high disease activity (HDA; >28). Results are presented up to week 104.

This analysis also reports the proportion of patients with MASES>0 at baseline achieving complete resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0), and patients with SJC>0 at baseline achieving complete resolution of peripheral arthritis (SJC=0). The proportion of patients achieving simultaneous complete resolution of SJC and TJC is also reported. We also report resolution of heel (Achilles) enthesitis at week 52 and week 104.

Finally, association analyses were conducted to assess whether resolution of enthesitis (MASES) or peripheral arthritis (SJC) by week 104 affected clinical outcomes at week 104, including the Ankylosing Spondylitis Quality of Life (ASQoL) questionnaire, BASDAI Q1 and Q2, Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI), Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy-Fatigue Scale (FACIT-Fatigue) and total and nocturnal spinal pain. These analyses were conducted in patients with enthesitis or peripheral arthritis at baseline.

Statistical analysis

The BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies were powered for the predefined primary endpoint and key secondary endpoints during hierarchical testing, and thus not for the endpoints of these analyses.14 Consequently, all endpoints reported in these post hoc analyses are considered exploratory and should be interpreted with caution.

All analyses were performed on randomised patients with MASES or SJC>0 at baseline, as described above. From week 0–52 of BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies, data are reported for BKZ-randomised and placebo-randomised patients separately. From week 52–104, pooled data are reported for all patients, regardless of whether they were initially randomised to BKZ or placebo. Distribution of enthesitis by tendons and swollen joints at baseline, CfB in DAPSA and DAPSA disease states over time, and associations of enthesitis or arthritis resolution with improvements in clinical outcomes are reported as observed case. For mean CfB in MASES and SJC, missing data were imputed using multiple imputation. Complete resolution of enthesitis or peripheral arthritis, simultaneous achievement of SJC=0 and TJC=0, and the proportion of patients achieving resolution of Achilles tendon enthesitis were reported using non-responder imputation; patients not enrolled in the OLE were imputed as non-responders.

P values comparing BKZ and placebo at week 16 are presented for least squares mean CfB in MASES, SJC and DAPSA, and proportions of patients achieving resolution of enthesitis (MASES), Achilles tendon enthesitis and peripheral arthritis (SJC and combined SJC and TJC). P values were calculated using analysis of covariance with treatment, region, baseline MRI/CRP classification (BE MOBILE 1) and prior tumour necrosis factor inhibitor exposure (BE MOBILE 2) as factors, and baseline MASES, SJC or DAPSA as covariate. Least squares mean and mean improvement from these adjusted models are reported. P values are nominal.

Results

Patient disposition and baseline characteristics

Of the patients originally randomised to BKZ or placebo in BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies, 79.1% (201/254) and 83.7% (278/332), respectively, entered the OLE. In total, 74.4% (189/254) of patients with nr-axSpA and 80.4% (267/332) of patients with r-axSpA remained in the OLE to week 104.

Among patients with nr-axSpA, 186/254 (73.2%) and 88/254 (34.6%) had MASES>0 and SJC>0 at baseline, respectively, compared with 199/332 (59.9%) and 66/332 (19.9%) of patients with r-axSpA. The baseline demographics and disease characteristics of patients with MASES>0 and SJC>0 at baseline were generally comparable between treatment arms (table 1), and were reflective of the wider axSpA population.20 A graphical summary of study results is provided in figure 1.

Improvements in peripheral manifestations of axSpA

Enthesitis

Among patients with enthesitis at baseline (MASES>0), mean baseline MASES was similar across BKZ-randomised and placebo-randomised patients with nr-axSpA (4.8 vs 4.9; overall mean: 4.8) and r-axSpA (4.2 vs 4.4; overall mean: 4.3). There was a larger least squares mean improvement in MASES (ie, larger mean decrease from baseline) for BKZ-randomised versus placebo-randomised patients at week 16 (nr-axSpA: −2.2 vs −1.1 (BKZ vs placebo p=0.0138); r-axSpA: −2.1 vs −1.0 (BKZ vs placebo p=0.0027)). Figure 3A shows mean changes in MASES to week 104; values are as observed, rather than the least squares mean improvements described above (from adjusted models). Among both patients randomised to BKZ and those switching from placebo to BKZ, improvements continued to week 52 and were sustained to week 104, with similar CfB across the full disease spectrum of axSpA.

Improvements in both randomisation groups were reflected in the proportions of patients achieving complete resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0; figure 3B). At week 16, a higher proportion of BKZ-randomised versus placebo-randomised patients achieved MASES=0 in both studies (nr-axSpA: 51.1% (48/94) vs 23.9% (22/92); r-axSpA: 51.5% (68/132) vs 32.8% (22/67)). The OR (95% CI) for BKZ versus placebo at week 16 for nr-axSpA was 3.49 (1.84 to 6.62; p<0.001), and 2.47 (1.30 to 4.68; p=0.006) for r-axSpA. After this timepoint, the proportion of BKZ-randomised patients reporting resolution of enthesitis was maintained to week 52 (nr-axSpA: 54.3% (51/94); r-axSpA: 50.8% (67/132)), while the proportion of placebo-randomised patients rapidly increased after switching to BKZ (nr-axSpA: 44.6% (41/92); r-axSpA: 46.3% (31/67)). Fewer patients with nr-axSpA (BKZ-randomised and placebo-randomised) reported resolution of enthesitis at week 104 than week 52 (41.9% (78/186)), although the proportion of r-axSpA patients reporting resolution at this timepoint was maintained (53.3% (106/199)).

In total, 69/254 patients with nr-axSpA had Achilles tendon enthesitis at baseline (33 BKZ-randomised; 36 placebo-randomised) and 58/332 with r-axSpA (43 BKZ-randomised; 15 placebo-randomised). The BKZ versus placebo OR (95% CI) for achieving complete resolution of Achilles tendon enthesitis at week 16 was 4.71 (1.60 to 13.89; p=0.005) for nr-axSpA and 1.04 (0.27 to 3.94; p=0.9536) for r-axSpA. The proportions of patients with nr-axSpA and r-axSpA achieving complete resolution of Achilles tendon enthesitis at week 52, after all patients had received BKZ for at least 36 weeks, were similar (65.2% (45/69) and 63.8% (37/58), respectively). A smaller proportion of patients with nr-axSpA reported resolution of Achilles tendon enthesitis at week 104 (56.5% (39/69)) compared with patients with r-axSpA (69.0% (40/58)).

Peripheral arthritis

In total, 88/254 patients with nr-axSpA had peripheral arthritis (SJC>0) at baseline (45 BKZ-randomised and 43 placebo-randomised) and 66/332 with r-axSpA (44 BKZ-randomised and 22 placebo-randomised). Among these patients, mean baseline SJC scores were similar across BKZ-randomised and placebo-randomised patients with nr-axSpA (4.2 vs 3.8; overall: 4.0) and r-axSpA (4.7 vs 3.9; overall: 4.5).

At week 16, change in least squares mean SJC was significantly larger for BKZ-randomised versus placebo-randomised nr-axSpA patients (−3.0 vs −1.5 (BKZ vs placebo p=0.0065)) but non-significantly larger for r-axSpA patients (−3.3 vs −2.3; (BKZ vs placebo p=0.0738)). BKZ-randomised patients with nr-axSpA experienced a slight increase in mean SJC to week 52. SJC counts were then generally sustained to week 104 for both nr-axSpA and r-axSpA (pooled BKZ-randomised and placebo/BKZ-randomised patients CfB: nr-axSpA −3.1; r-axSpA −3.9).

There was also a larger improvement in least squares mean DAPSA score for BKZ-randomised versus placebo-randomised patients with SJC>0 at baseline at week 16 (nr-axSpA: −15.6 vs −7.5; BKZ vs placebo p=0.002; r-axSpA: −15.1 vs −11.3; BKZ vs placebo p=0.143). From week 16 onwards, placebo-randomised patients showed rapid improvements in DAPSA score, with improvements sustained in both treatment groups to week 52 and week 104 (figure 4B).

Improvement in mean DAPSA score with BKZ was reflected in DAPSA disease activity states to week 104 (figure 5). Almost all patients had HDA or MDA at baseline, and at week 16 more patients had achieved DAPSA score ≤14 (equivalent to REM or LDA as a combined category) in the BKZ-randomised versus placebo-randomised group. This was statistically significant for nr-axSpA (OR (95% CI) for achieving REM/LDA at week 16 rather than MDA/HDA, BKZ vs placebo, 8.40 (2.78 to 25.38); p<0.001). The r-axSpA OR (95% CI) for achieving REM/LDA rather than MDA/HDA at week 16, BKZ versus placebo, was 2.52 (0.70 to 8.99; p=0.156). Proportions of all patients achieving LDA or REM increased further at week 52 and were sustained to 2 years, with 75.8% of patients with nr-axSpA and 86.8% of patients with r-axSpA who remained in the OLE at week 104 achieving a DAPSA score ≤14.

Discussion

This analysis represents the first long-term study investigating the efficacy of dual inhibition of IL-17F in addition to IL-17A in the treatment of enthesitis and peripheral arthritis across the full disease spectrum of axSpA. Treatment with BKZ led to rapid and substantial improvements in peripheral manifestations of axSpA, which were sustained to week 104. Over 40% of patients achieved complete resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0) at week 104, and around 60% achieved complete resolution of peripheral arthritis (SJC=0).

Peripheral manifestations of axSpA can be hugely impactful on the quality of life experienced by patients due to their associations with pain and impaired physical function, and are reported by up to 64% of patients.1 5 6 21 In particular, enthesitis and peripheral arthritis have been shown to be significantly associated with depression and work impairment, highlighting the real-world effects of reduced daily functioning for patients.2224 Peripheral manifestations of axSpA are highly prevalent for patients with both nr-axSpA and r-axSpA, hence it is critical to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of new axSpA treatments to ensure they are effective in treating peripheral manifestations, as well as achieving standard clinical efficacy outcomes (ie, ASAS response and Axial Spondyloarthritis Disease Activity Score).

Our analysis showed that complete resolution of enthesitis at week 104 in patients with r-axSpA and enthesitis at baseline was associated with larger improvements in other clinical outcomes, including spinal pain, indicating how treatment of peripheral manifestations can have a holistic, widespread impact on treatment efficacy. Despite this and recent updates to the ASAS axSpA core outcome set to include enthesitis, arthritis and dactylitis in the mandatory domain of peripheral manifestations, they have historically not been prioritised as clinical outcomes and are rarely explored in-depth in randomised clinical trials.

For example, in the ACHILLES trial, a higher (but statistically non-significant) proportion of patients treated with secukinumab reported resolution of Achilles tendon enthesitis versus placebo at week 24 (42.2% vs 31.4%).25 From the pooled MEASURE 1–4 trials, more than one-third of patients treated with secukinumab achieved complete resolution of enthesitis at week 16, rising to over one-half by week 52.26 Improvements in MASES over time to week 16 were also shown with secukinumab treatment versus placebo (−2.9 (secukinumab 300 mg) and −2.4 (secukinumab 150 mg) vs −1.9 (placebo)).26 However, longer-term data from these trials past 52 weeks have not been reported, and the studies were limited to only patients with r-axSpA. Moreover, data on other peripheral manifestations such as peripheral arthritis, including SJC and TJC, have not been reported. In contrast, the sustained long-term improvements in enthesitis and peripheral arthritis demonstrated here (across various measures) following treatment with BKZ add to the existing published data for BKZ which has shown its efficacy and safety to 2 years.16

We report a wide range of measures to assess holistically the effect of BKZ treatment on various peripheral manifestations, including DAPSA. To date, DAPSA has largely been limited to assessing disease activity for patients with psoriatic arthritis (PsA), but has recently been shown to have good construct validity and discriminatory ability for patients with SpA, including both axSpA and peripheral SpA.18 19 The present analysis is among the first to use the DAPSA score to assess disease activity in patients with axSpA, and the first to use it in a randomised controlled study. As there are no currently defined categorical cut-offs for axSpA, here we use the same as those used for PsA. As a result, the DAPSA scores presented in this analysis are limited, exploratory and should only be considered as indications of treatment efficacy.

A key strength of this work is that we present data up to 2 years across both nr-axSpA and r-axSpA, facilitated by similar parallel feeder study designs and a cross-over screening approach. We are therefore able to compare the long-term effect of BKZ treatment across the full axSpA disease spectrum.

This post hoc analysis is limited by the BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies being powered for the predefined primary and key secondary endpoints; therefore, the outcomes reported here should be considered exploratory and interpreted with caution. MASES and SJC are recommended measures from the axSpA core outcome set; however, both require patients to have a manifestation at baseline. In this analysis, there was only a small subset of patients in these trials with enthesitis and SJC>0 at baseline, which restricted the analysis sample size and was a limitation. This was particularly evident in the analysis of associations between resolution of peripheral arthritis at week 104 and CfB in clinical outcomes; therefore, these findings should only be regarded as preliminary insights. Ultrasound and MRI imaging were also not used to confirm the presence of enthesitis in these studies, so subclinical inflammation visible via advanced imaging may not have been detected. This means that some patients with enthesitis may not have been included, and others may be inaccurately included. Nonetheless, sample sizes were sufficient to detect nominal but significant treatment effects across CfB and resolution of enthesitis (both nr-axSpA and r-axSpA), CfB in SJC and CfB in DAPSA (nr-axSpA only). Finally, data relating to dactylitis was not collected during the BE MOBILE 1 and 2 studies, so it was not possible to evaluate the effect of BKZ on all peripheral manifestations of axSpA. However, due to the low incidence of dactylitis in patients with axSpA, sample sizes of patients experiencing this manifestation would likely have been too low to conduct meaningful analyses.3

In conclusion, inhibition of IL-17F in addition to IL-17A with BKZ resulted in sustained improvements in peripheral manifestations to week 104 for patients with both nr-axSpA and r-axSpA. Complete resolution of enthesitis (MASES=0) was achieved by nearly half of BKZ-treated patients with axSpA by week 52, which was maintained to week 104. A similar pattern was observed in peripheral arthritis, with more than 60% of patients with axSpA achieving resolution (SJC=0) by week 52. These results support the benefit of BKZ for peripheral manifestations of axSpA which have a significant impact on the quality of life of patients.

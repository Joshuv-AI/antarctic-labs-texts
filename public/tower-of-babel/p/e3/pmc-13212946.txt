An enhanced escape algorithm with comprehensive learning and Cauchy–Gaussian mutation for reservoir optimization
Authors: Hongkui Chen, Xiaomin Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlobal optimization of complex, high-dimensional landscapes remains a fundamental challenge in scientific and engineering domains. To mitigate the inherent limitations of premature convergence and diversity loss, this paper proposes CLGMESC, an enhanced variant of the Escape Algorithm (ESC). The proposed algorithm integrates a dimension-wise comprehensive learning (CL) strategy with a hybrid Cauchy-Gaussian mutation (HCGM) operator. The CL strategy reconfigures the learning paradigm for stagnant individuals, enabling them to construct exemplars from multiple high-quality peers and thereby restore population diversity. Synergistically, the HCGM operator utilizes an adaptive weighting mechanism to dynamically balance heavy-tailed Cauchy mutations for global exploration and thin-tailed Gaussian mutations for local refinement, effectively facilitating escapes from local optima. Comprehensive evaluations on the CEC2017 benchmark suite demonstrate that CLGMESC achieves the top rank among ten advanced metaheuristics (including SBO, BBO, PO, DE, PSO, SMA, CPA, and MGO), with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirming its statistical superiority () across the majority of test functions. Furthermore, the practical efficacy of CLGMESC was validated through a reservoir production optimization problem using the three-dimensional Egg Model ( grid). In determining optimal well controls, CLGMESC achieved the highest Net Present Value ( USD) with the lowest standard deviation, thus substantiating its reliability and robustness in solving computationally intensive real-world engineering problems. The consistently high rankings across diverse benchmarks and the substantial economic gains in the reservoir simulation underscore the algorithm’s pronounced capability to maintain a robust exploration-exploitation balance and dynamically escape local optima in demanding parameter spaces.Keywords: Escape algorithm, Global optimization, Metaheuristic, Comprehensive learning, Cauchy-Gaussian mutation, Algorithm robustness, Benchmark functionsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The pursuit of optimal performance is a fundamental driver across scientific discovery and engineering design, underpinning advancements in fields ranging from aerospace and manufacturing to finance and energy systems1–3. This endeavor, formalized as optimization, seeks to identify the best possible configuration or decision from a vast set of alternatives, balancing competing objectives under defined constraints4. However, many critical real-world optimization problems present formidable challenges, characterized by complex, high-dimensional search spaces, nonlinear interactions between variables, and multimodal landscapes where numerous local optima can easily trap conventional solution methods5–7. To address these challenges, a rich body of optimization theory has been developed, initially dominated by classical deterministic techniques8.

Classical deterministic methods, such as steepest descent, Newton-based algorithms, and the Nelder-Mead simplex approach, form the foundational pillars of optimization9. Their principal strength lies in providing efficient, often theoretically guaranteed convergence to an optimum—but crucially, only under highly idealized conditions. These algorithms are predominantly designed for problems where the objective function is smooth, convex, low-dimensional, and analytically tractable8. Their applicability becomes severely limited when confronted with the intricate realities of modern engineering and scientific problems. First, these methods typically require explicit gradient information or strong assumptions about the function’s structure (e.g., convexity), which are seldom available in complex simulation-based or data-driven settings10,11. Second, their deterministic search logic renders them prone to becoming trapped in local optima when faced with nonconvex, multimodal landscapes, failing to explore the broader search space effectively12. Third, their computational cost frequently scales poorly with problem dimensionality, a phenomenon known as the curse of dimensionality, rendering them impractical for high-dimensional decision spaces13. These fundamental limitations create a significant methodological gap between the tractability of classical approaches and the demanding nature of contemporary optimization problems.

To bridge this gap, metaheuristic algorithms have emerged as a powerful and versatile class of optimization strategies14,15. Unlike their classical counterparts, metaheuristics do not rely on restrictive mathematical properties of the problem. Instead, they orchestrate high-level, often nature-inspired search principles to navigate complex landscapes16. Their core strength lies in a favorable balance between exploration, which involves investigating diverse regions of the search space, and exploitation, which refines promising solutions17. This balance enables them to effectively escape local optima and progressively converge towards near-global optimal solutions, making metaheuristics particularly well-suited for intricate, “black-box” optimization problems18.

Based on their underlying sources of inspiration, metaheuristics can be systematically classified into two predominant streams: Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) and Swarm Intelligence (SI) algorithms19. EAs are grounded in metaphors of biological evolution, iteratively improving a population of candidate solutions through simulated processes of selection, recombination (crossover), and mutation20. Prominent representatives include the Genetic Algorithm (GA), which operates on binary or real-valued chromosomes, and Differential Evolution (DE), renowned for its efficient perturbation strategy based on vector differences. In contrast, SI algorithms model the collective, decentralized intelligence emerging from the interactions of simple agents21. Key examples include Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)22,23, which simulates the social foraging behavior of bird flocks by updating particle velocities based on personal and communal best experiences, and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)24, inspired by the pheromone-mediated foraging behavior of ants for combinatorial problems. This taxonomy highlights the distinct yet complementary search philosophies—evolutionary pressure versus collective cooperation—that equip these algorithms to tackle a broad spectrum of challenges.

The remarkable diversity of metaheuristic algorithms, however, leads to a fundamental theoretical and practical question: is there a single, universally superior algorithm capable of optimally solving all types of optimization problems? This question finds its definitive answer in the No Free Lunch (NFL) theorem for optimization25. The theorem demonstrates, under a regex rigorous mathematical framework, that when averaged over the space of all possible problems, the performance of any two search algorithms is identical26. In practical terms, no single algorithm can be declared the best across every conceivable problem domain; superior performance on one class of problems is necessarily offset by poorer performance on another. This result provides the crucial theoretical motivation for the perpetual development and refinement of new algorithms. It underscores that to achieve high performance on a specific class of challenging problems, one must design or tailor algorithms whose search biases align with the underlying structure of that problem domain. Consequently, continuous innovation in metaheuristic research is intrinsically justified, driven by the goal of crafting specialized, high-performance solvers for complex, real-world landscapes that defy universal solution strategies27.

Building upon this context, the recently proposed Escape Algorithm (ESC) represents a novel bio-social metaheuristic inspired by behavioral dynamics during crowd evacuations28. Its core innovation lies in a structured, dual-phase search mechanism. An initial exploration phase divides the population into three distinct behavioral groups—Calm, Herding, and Panic—to simulate diverse search strategies. This is followed by an exploitation phase where all individuals adopt the Calm behavior for focused convergence. The transition is governed by an adaptive Panic Index, P(t), and a Lévy distribution for step sizing, while an Elite Pool persistently guides the population. This metaphor provides a robust framework for navigating complex landscapes by explicitly modeling diverse decision-making processes under pressure.

Despite its promising framework, the ESC algorithm exhibits specific structural limitations that hinder its performance, particularly in later search stages and on highly multimodal problems29. The primary weakness stems from its learning mechanism. During exploitation, all individuals are attracted towards the center of the Calm group or elite solutions, creating a homogeneous attraction pull. This lack of diverse information sources, coupled with fixed behavioral group proportions, leads to a rapid decline in population diversity. Consequently, the search trajectory becomes overly uniform, significantly increasing the risk of premature convergence to local optima because individuals cluster around a limited set of attractors without effective mechanisms to break stagnation or explore alternative promising regions30.

The primary motivation of this paper is to effectively address these limitations by tackling the ubiquitous dilemma of premature convergence versus diversity loss in metaheuristic optimization. We aim to construct an optimizer capable of both extensive exploration in early stages and fine-grained, stagnation-resistant exploitation in complex landscapes. To achieve this, this paper proposes an Enhanced Escape Algorithm (CLGMESC). The central improvement integrates two synergistic strategies designed to overhaul the learning paradigm and sustain constructive diversity throughout the search process. First, a dimension-wise comprehensive learning (CL) strategy is embedded into the position update rule for stagnant individuals. Unlike traditional improvement strategies that rely heavily on a single global or personal best solution to mitigate stagnation, the CL strategy constructs a unique exemplar vector for each dimension by learning from multiple high-quality peers selected via a tournament mechanism. This fundamentally disrupts homogeneous attraction by providing multi-source, dimension-specific guidance. Second, a hybrid Cauchy-Gaussian mutation (HCGM) strategy is applied as a post-update perturbation operator on promising solutions. It combines the heavy-tailed Cauchy distribution, which facilitates long-range exploratory jumps, with the thin-tailed Gaussian distribution, which allows for fine-tuning exploitation. Recent advancements emphasize the critical importance of such adaptive, optimization-driven learning and hybridization mechanisms for balancing exploration and exploitation effectively in complex engineering spaces31–33. The influence of each distribution is dynamically weighted across iterations to align with the search phase, from initial exploration to final exploitation. These two mechanisms operate highly synergistically: the CL strategy diversifies stagnant individuals, injecting fresh genetic material into the population, while HCGM then refines and perturbs the population at the elite level to prevent the entire swarm from collapsing into a singular local attractor.

Original algorithm overview: Escape Algorithm (ESC)

The Escape Algorithm (ESC), proposed by OuYang et al. in 2024, is a population-based metaheuristic optimizer inspired by the dynamics of crowd behavior during emergency evacuations28. Its core principle lies in emulating the diverse reactions of individuals—categorized into Calm, Herding, and Panic groups—to balance exploration and exploitation. The algorithm operates in two distinct phases: an initial exploration phase where the population is divided into these three behavioral groups, and a subsequent exploitation phase where all individuals are treated as calm, converging towards the best-found solutions stored in an Elite Pool. An adaptive Panic Index modulates the influence of random behaviors, while adaptive Lévy weights control step sizes, ensuring a smooth transition from wide-ranging search to focused refinement.

Comprehensive learning strategy

To circumvent premature convergence and enhance global exploration, the algorithm incorporates a Comprehensive Learning (CL) strategy. This strategy addresses particle stagnation by enabling each individual to learn from multiple high-quality peers on a dimension-by-dimension basis, rather than relying on a single global or personal best solution.

The mechanism is conditionally triggered for individuals whose personal best has not improved over a predefined number of iterations. Specifically, a stagnation counter for each individual i is initialized to zero and incremented whenever its current position fails to improve upon its historical best fitness pfit(i). Performance comparison with other algorithms

This section presents a quantitative analysis of the performance, statistical significance, and convergence behavior of the proposed method. CLGMESC is benchmarked against the original Escape Algorithm (ESC) and eight established metaheuristics: Status-based Optimization (SBO), Biogeography-Based Optimization (BBO), Parrot Optimizer (PO), Differential Evolution (DE), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Slime Mould Algorithm (SMA), Colony Predation Algorithm (CPA), and Mountain Gazelle Optimizer (MGO). To ensure fair comparability, all algorithms were executed under identical population sizes, maximum function evaluation limits, and independent run counts.

The comparative analysis in Table 2 demonstrates the superior optimization capability of the proposed CLGMESC. The algorithm achieves the top overall ranking among the ten comparators, surpassing the baseline ESC (Rank 2) and SBO (Rank 3). Specifically, Table 2 shows that CLGMESC yields the minimal mean fitness values on a broad spectrum of problems, ranging from unimodal (F1, F3) to complex hybrid (F11, F17) and composition functions (F25, F28). This performance advantage implies that the dimension-wise comprehensive learning strategy effectively restores population diversity, thereby mitigating the premature convergence issues prevalent in the original ESC framework. However, it is important to moderate these superiority claims: no single algorithm universally dominates. For instance, on the multimodal function F4 and certain composition cases, the original ESC and DE algorithms yield competitive or marginally superior average results. This underscores the classic No Free Lunch theorem, suggesting that while CLGMESC offers robust generalized performance, specialized structures in specific landscapes may occasionally favor alternative search paradigms.

Application to reservoir production optimization

Reservoir production optimization represents a formidable engineering challenge, aimed at determining operational strategies that maximize the economic recovery of hydrocarbons, typically quantified by the Net Present Value (NPV)34. This problem is inherently high-dimensional and non-deterministic (NP-hard), as the search space is defined by multiple control variables (e.g., well production rates or bottom-hole pressures) across discrete management periods. The resulting complex, nonconvex, and computationally intensive objective landscape makes it particularly well-suited for population-based metaheuristics, which navigate such terrains without requiring gradient information.

A high-fidelity synthetic reservoir model was implemented using the Eclipse simulator, which served as the forward model. Conclusions

This paper proposes CLGMESC, an enhanced variant of the Escape Algorithm, designed to overcome the limitations of premature convergence and population diversity loss that are inherent in the original framework. The enhancement is realized through the synergistic integration of a dimension-wise comprehensive learning (CL) strategy and a hybrid Cauchy-Gaussian mutation (CGM) operator.

The rationale behind these improvements addresses distinct phases of the optimization process. The CL strategy reconstructs the learning paradigm for stagnant individuals, allowing them to extract information from multiple high-quality exemplars rather than a single attractor. This mechanism fundamentally restores constructive diversity and prevents homogenization. Complementing this, the CGM operator introduces an adaptive perturbation mechanism that acts as a stochastic engine for escaping local optima. By dynamically balancing heavy-tailed Cauchy mutations for global exploration and thin-tailed Gaussian mutations for local refinement, the algorithm maintains a robust equilibrium between search breadth and depth.

Comprehensive empirical validation on the CEC2017 benchmark suite confirmed the statistical superiority of CLGMESC, which achieved the top rank among ten advanced metaheuristics (Rank 1 overall with a mean rank of 2.31) and demonstrated significant performance advantages (Wilcoxon ) on complex multimodal functions. Furthermore, the practical efficacy of the algorithm was verified through its application to a high-dimensional reservoir production optimization problem. CLGMESC achieved the highest Net Present Value of USD with the lowest variance ( USD), proving its capability to solve computation-intensive, real-world engineering challenges reliably.

Future work will focus on extending the adaptability of CLGMESC to multi-objective and constrained optimization domains. Additionally, investigating the algorithm’s scalability on ultra-large-scale problems (e.g., thousands of decision variables) remains a critical necessity, as the current evaluation is primarily delimited to 30-dimensional spaces. The integration of advanced surrogate modeling or parallel computing architectures also presents a promising direction to further mitigate the computational burden in industrial applications.

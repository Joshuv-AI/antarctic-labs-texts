HypoxamiRs in pancreatic cancer: master regulators of the hypoxic tumor microenvironment
Authors: Pooria Salehi-Sangani, Amir Hosein Maharati, Bahareh Payami, Mohsen Aliakbarian, Mohammad Reza Abbaszadegan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Pancreatic cancer (PC) is characterized by aggressive progression, chemoresistance, and immune evasion, largely driven by its hypoxic tumor microenvironment (TME). The extensive desmoplastic reaction in PC, characterized by dense stromal fibrosis, exacerbates hypoxia by impairing blood flow, creating a hostile environment that limits therapeutic efficacy. Hypoxia-induced microRNAs (HypoxamiRs) have emerged as critical regulators of these processes, modulating cellular pathways that promote tumor survival and therapy resistance. HypoxamiRs exert their effects by interacting with hypoxia-inducible factors, including HIF-1α and HIF-2α. Beyond their direct impact on tumor cells, HypoxamiRs modulate the behavior of immune and stromal cells within the hypoxic TME. Key contributors to hypoxia, such as cancer-associated fibroblasts, pancreatic stellate cells, macrophages, and natural killer (NK) cells, facilitate HypoxamiR transfer via exosomes. This review explores the multifaceted roles of HypoxamiRs in key processes such as immune modulation, chemoresistance, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), autophagy, angiogenesis, and apoptosis in pancreatic cancer. We also highlight the complex interplay between HypoxamiRs and other non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in regulating hypoxia-driven pathways. Investigating these complex interactions provides critical insights for developing novel therapeutic strategies to overcome chemoresistance and immune evasion in pancreatic cancer.

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is an extremely lethal disease and ranks as the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, according to GLOBOCAN 2022 (Bray et al. 2024). Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the most common form of pancreatic cancer, accounting for approximately 90% of cases (Chen et al. 2019a). Despite advances in therapeutic approaches, PDAC patients continue to face poor prognoses, primarily due to the tumor’s complex microenvironment, characterized by extensive desmoplasia and hypoxia. Most PDAC tumors are considered unresectable at diagnosis due to widespread metastasis and local invasion into surrounding tissues (Ryan et al. 2014; Vincent et al. 2011). Pancreatic cancer is often asymptomatic in its early stages, leading to late diagnosis and poor prognosis. Despite advancements in novel therapeutic approaches, chemotherapy remains the primary treatment option for metastatic pancreatic cancer. However, chemoresistance poses a significant challenge in managing this disease (Manoharan et al. 2021; Wood et al. 2022). Furthermore, recent immunotherapies have shown limited success, largely due to the complex interactions between tumor and immune cells, leading to immune evasion or exhaustion of effector immune cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME)(Bear et al. 2020). Given these challenges, investigating the TME of pancreatic cancer, with a specific focus on hypoxia as a critical factor, is crucial for the development of more effective therapeutic strategies.

MicroRNAs (miRs) are involved in various physiological and pathological processes. MicroRNAs play a crucial role in forming the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), where they bind to the 3' untranslated region (UTR) of target genes to regulate their expression (Garzon et al. 2009; Iorio and Croce 2009; Lee et al. 2004). Dysregulation of miRs has been implicated in the development of various types of cancers (10). MicroRNAs have been identified as critical modulators of tumor response to hypoxia. These specific hypoxia-related miRNAs, known as HypoxamiRs, regulate the TME (Gee et al. 2014). HypoxamiRs can be altered in response to hypoxia in various cell types within the TME and also regulate cellular functions under hypoxic conditions (Greco and Martelli 2014). These small molecules can function as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors, depending on their regulatory effects on different tumoral processes (Johnson et al. 2005; Cimmino et al. 2005; He et al. 2005). The microenvironment of pancreatic cancer is characterized by extensive hypoxia, where microRNAs play a vital role in regulating tumor chemoresistance, metastasis, and progression, while also serving as potential prognostic markers (Liu et al. 2021a; Negoi et al. 2017; Liang et al. 2020a). Consequently, investigating the role of HypoxamiRs in hypoxia-mediated responses, as well as their involvement in inducing hypoxia, can elucidate their critical functions and identify novel therapeutic, prognostic, or diagnostic targets.

Hypoxia, defined by an oxygen pressure of less than 5–10 mmHg, is a critical promoter of tumor metastasis (Lu and Kang 2010). Hypoxia influences the cancer microenvironment primarily by enhancing and stabilizing the expression of HIF-1α and HIF-2α, along with their downstream effectors (Löfstedt et al. 2007; Semenza 2010a). Hypoxia in the tumor microenvironment upregulated HIF-1α, which in turn regulated its downstream effectors (Zhao et al. 2020; Shi et al. 2021). HIF-1 is a heterodimeric transcription factor crucial for the cellular response to low oxygen conditions (Shan et al. 2013; Koda et al. 2007). The α subunit of HIF-1 is the dominant regulator of downstream gene expression under hypoxia, binding to hypoxia response elements (HREs) in the promoter regions of target genes while transcriptionally inhibiting them via acquisition of HDACs (Gómez-Maldonado et al. 2015; Xue et al. 2014; Gomez-Roman et al. 2016; Wang et al. 2016a; Kong et al. 2006; Qian et al. 2006; Zhang et al. 2010). Hypoxia triggers the transition of quiescent pancreatic stellate cells (qPSCs) to activated PSCs through sonic hedgehog (Hh) signaling pathway. This transition induces desmoplasia, promoting HIF-1α expression, which drives the malignant and aggressive behavior of PDAC TME. Inhibition of HIF-1α disrupts this loop by downregulating Hh signaling.(Spivak-Kroizman et al. 2013). Within the TME of PDAC, hypoxic stress predominantly stimulates the upregulation of HIF-1α, initiating pro-angiogenic signaling pathways and contributing to angiogenesis and subsequent partial alleviation of oxygen deprivation (Pugh and Ratcliffe 2003). However, HIF-2α exhibits more sustained activation under moderate-level oxygen conditions (Uchida et al. 2004). During prolonged hypoxia, HIF-2α expression increases gradually and induces EMT (Zhang et al. 2017). HIF-2α overexpression, particularly in CAFs, results in immunosuppressive TME of PDAC, while its suppression reduced tumor progression and fibrosis. Moreover, silencing HIF-2α reduces activation of M2-polarized macrophages and regulatory T cells, while promoting the anti-tumoral effect of immune checkpoint blockade in PDAC (Garcia Garcia et al. 2022). HIF-2α exerts its tumor-promoting effects mainly through non-canonical glutamine metabolism, mediated by the PI3K/mTORC2/GOT1 axis. HIF-2α inhibition prevents this metabolic axis and reduces tumor growth (Li et al. 2017a). Collectively, the complementary roles of HIF-1α and HIF-2α regulate the hypoxic PDAC microenvironment, via angiogenic, metabolic, stromal, and immune-modulatory pathways.

HypoxamiRs have been identified as critical regulators of HIF-1α in pancreatic cancer. For instance, one study demonstrated that sponging miR-143 led to the upregulation of HIF-1α, increasing its transcriptional activity, which subsequently promoted angiogenesis and gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic cancer (Liu et al. 2021b). Similarly, miR-301a suppressed TAp63 expression, resulting in enhanced HIF-1α activity and the induction of gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic cancer cells (Luo et al. 2018). Additionally, miR-224 has been shown to inhibit TXNIP, a key factor that destabilizes HIF-1α, thereby leading to its stabilization within the nucleus (Zhu et al. 2018). Conversely, HIF-1α plays a vital role in regulating the expression of various HypoxamiRs as a transcription factor under hypoxic conditions. It binds to HREs in the promoter regions of target HypoxamiRs, modulating their expression and downstream effects. For example, HIF-1α induces the expression of miR-210, which inhibits HOXA9 and promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in pancreatic cancer (Ni et al. 2019). Furthermore, HIF-1α promotes angiogenesis and apoptosis by upregulating miR-21 in pancreatic cancer cells (Bao et al. 2012). Moreover, HIF-1α-mediated overexpression of miR-191 and miR-212 has been associated with poor prognosis in pancreatic cancer patients (Song et al. 2014; Yue et al. 2019).

In the PDAC TME, hypoxia drives cancer progression, drug resistance, and metastasis(Yamasaki et al. 2020). While HIF-mediated transcription and downstream genes like VEGF are well characterized across tumors, their value as therapeutic targets remains limited (Skorupan et al. 2022; Kindler et al. 2010; Cutsem et al. 2009; Seo et al. 2000; Itakura et al. 1997). This limitation underscores the need to explore broader gene regulatory networks, including miRNAs, in the hypoxic TME. This review summarizes current advances on HypoxamiRs in PDAC, emphasizing their regulation of key hypoxia-driven hallmarks—immune evasion, chemoresistance, EMT, autophagy, and angiogenesis—and their clinical potential as therapeutic targets, diagnostic tools, and prognostic markers. Role of hypoxamirs in immune modulation of PDAC

Pancreatic cancer is characterized by a highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, enabling immune evasion and contributing to disease progression and resistance to therapies. Hypoxia, a hallmark of pancreatic tumors, further exacerbates immune evasion by altering immune cell function, promoting the recruitment of regulatory T cells, and inhibiting cytotoxic T cell activity through various signaling pathways (Watson et al. 2021; Angelin et al. 2017). In this context, HypoxamiRs have emerged as key modulators of immune evasion (Greco and Martelli 2014). These microRNAs influence the immune response by targeting genes involved in immune suppression under hypoxic conditions, presenting potential therapeutic targets to enhance immune surveillance in pancreatic cancer.

Natural killer (NK) cells are essential components of innate immunity, playing a critical role through direct cytotoxicity, antibody-mediated cytotoxicity, and regulation of other immune cells. NK cells exert their cytotoxic effects by recognizing specific ligands on the surface of target cells. However, impaired killing capacity of NK cells has been observed in several cancers (Fildes et al. 2008; Farag and Caligiuri 2006; Iannello et al. 2016; Wu et al. 2020). Hypoxia is one of the key factors that impair NK cell cytotoxicity. The hypoxic TME affects NK cell function by dysregulating CD16, NKp30, and NKG2D (Balsamo et al. 2013). AXIN2 is an oncogene that modulates cell proliferation, migration, differentiation, and apoptosis, primarily through its inhibitory effects on the Wnt/β-catenin pathway (Gu et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2014). There was a downregulation of miR-1275 under the hypoxic TME of pancreatic cancer, which normally suppresses AXIN2 expression in NK cells. Consequently, overexpression of AXIN2 decreased the perforin, IFN-γ and TNF-α expression levels, impairing NK cell activity and promoting the immune escape of PDAC cells under hypoxia (Ou et al. 2023). The interaction between membrane MHC class I-related molecule A (mMICA) and NKG2D complexes on NK cells is a crucial component of immune surveillance, and the downregulation of mMICA or degradation of NKG2D plays a significant role in the immune escape of tumor cells (Barsoum et al. 2011). Circ-0000977 sponged miR-153, reducing the miR-153-mediated HIF-1α and ADAM10 downregulation. Subsequently, ADAM10 decreased the mMICA and increased soluble MICA (sMICA). sMICA binding to the NKG2D promoted NKG2D degradation, resulting in reduced responsiveness of NK cells. Thus, circ_0000997-mediated miR-153 downregulation led to HIF-1α-mediated immune evasion in the hypoxic TME of PDAC (Ou et al. 2019a).

Macrophages can be polarized into M1 (classical) and M2 (alternative) macrophages in the TME. Proinflammatory M1 macrophages express inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and exert tumor-suppressive functions, while M2 macrophages express anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10 and arginase-1 (Arg1), which have oncogenic roles. M2 macrophages have been shown to promote pancreatic cancer cell migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) (Singh et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2015; Yin et al. 2016). Exosomes are small, cell-derived particles ranging from 30 to 140 nm in size, characterized by a bilayer membrane structure. These vesicles can encapsulate various molecules, including microRNAs, and play a crucial role in cell–cell communication (Whiteside 2018). Exosomal miR-301a-3p has been found to stimulate M2 polarization of macrophages in the hypoxic microenvironment of pancreatic cancer cells via activation of the PTEN/PI3Kγ pathway, thereby increasing cell migration and invasion in pancreatic cancer cells. Additionally, exosomal miR-301a-3p levels were correlated with TNM stage and poor outcomes in pancreatic cancer tissues (Wang et al. 2018). In addition, miR-21-5p has been found to be upregulated in myeloid cells of PDAC (Mühlberg et al. 2016). Exosomal miR-21-5p derived from M2 macrophages regulated KLF3, enhancing the functions of pancreatic cancer stem cells (Chang et al. 2022). Moreover, miR-210 is one of the key hypoxamiRs involved in TME of PDAC (Mortoglou et al. 2024). Exosomal miR-210 derived from pancreatic cancer stem cells induced polarization of macrophages toward M2-type via FGFRL1 suppression, resulting in decreased sensitivity to gemcitabine via -PI3K/p-AKT/p-mTOR axis (Guo et al. 2024).

T cells play a pivotal role in modulating the immune microenvironment of PDAC. An increased proportion of CD4⁺ T cells and a reduced infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells are associated with unfavorable outcomes in PDAC (Goulart et al. 2021; Johnson et al. 2017). Within the TME of PDAC, CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells are not only less abundant but are often sequestered at the tumor margins, whereas CD4+ FOXP3+ T cells (Tregs) exhibited greater intratumoral infiltration, potentially explaining the attenuated response of PDAC patients to immunotherapy (Goulart et al. 2021; Diaz-Riascos et al. 2019). Hypoxia plays a significant role in shaping this immunosuppressive TME through repressing antigen presentation to CD8+ T cells, while also recruiting Tregs to the TME (Garcia Garcia et al. 2022; Abdo et al. 2025). It has been shown that lncRNA LINC00460 is upregulated by HIF-1α in hypoxic TME of PDAC (Zhang et al. 2023). Silencing LINC00460 promoted the sensitivity of PDAC cell lines toward CD8+ T cells, which was further reversed by miR-503-5p downregulation or ANLN upregulation, representing the LINC00460/miR-503-5p/ANLN axis as a hypoxia-inducible pathway, leading to immunomodulatory effects on CD8+ T cells (Yao et al. 2022). MiR-21 is one of the key hypoxamiRs playing a significant role in the hypoxic TME of PDAC (Bao et al. 2012; Deng et al. 2024; Sun et al. 2019a; Mace et al. 2013). MiR-21-3p and miR-21-5p were overexpressed in myeloid cells of PDAC TME (Mühlberg et al. 2016). It has been found that miR-21 targets, including CCL-3/MIP-1α (miR-21-3p) and CXCL-10/IP-10 (miR-21-5p), could induce recruitment of CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and other immune cells (Kühnemuth et al. 2015; Bernardini et al. 2008). MiR-21-3p and miR-21-5p overexpression reduced the migration of CD8+ T cells in vitro, suggesting the potential inhibitory role of miR-21-3p and miR-21-5p in PDAC-associated myeloid cells in driving cytotoxic T cells into the PDAC TME (Mühlberg et al. 2016). Moreover, hypoxia induced the expression of miR-155 in PDAC (Bruning et al. 2011). Furthermore, it was found that exosomal miR-155-5p is functionally implicated in suppressing CD8+ T cells, via miR-155-5p/EHF/Akt/NF-κB axis, suggesting the potential role of miR-155-5p as a regulator of immune evasion in TME of PDAC (Wang and Gao 2021).

MDSCs are a heterogeneous group of immune cells typically seen as an adapted response of hematopoietic myeloid cells to chronic inflammation in conditions such as cancer or infection (Li et al. 2020a). Hypoxic condition triggers secretions of various chemokines, such as CXCL12 and granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) by CAFs and tumor cells, respectively, which in turn induce MDSC infiltration into the TME (Xu et al. 2015; Casazza et al. 2014; Bayne et al. 2012). In PDAC, MDSCs, though present at relatively low frequencies within the TME, exert disproportionate immunoregulatory influence. They suppress cytotoxic CD8⁺ T-cell effector functions while facilitating the recruitment and expansion of Tregs. Mechanistically, these effects are driven by the depletion of critical amino acids and metabolic reprogramming, which collectively impair T-cell chemotactic responsiveness, reconfigure cytokine secretion networks, and induce elevated PD-L1 expression on immune and tumor cells (Mota Reyes et al. 2022; Cheng, et al. 2021; Stromnes et al. 2014; Ostrand-Rosenberg 2010). Hypoxia induced HIF-1α-mediated miR-21 expression, preventing apoptosis in PDAC cells (Mace et al. 2013). Both miR-21-5p and miR-21-3p were found to be overexpressed in PDAC and PanIN-associated myeloid cells, resulting in diminished cytokine production. Specifically, miR-21-5p mediated PDCD4-dependent downregulation of CXCL10, whereas miR-21-3p induced PDCD4-independent downregulation of CCL3L. These effects attenuated the recruitment of lymphocytes into the PDAC TME. Collectively, these results suggested a hypoxia-induced expression of miR-21 in myeloid cells could potentially contribute to immune evasion and tumor growth in PDAC. In PDAC, miR-494 has been found to be downregulated by hypoxia (Jin et al. 2025). Exosomal miR-494-3p increased calcium flux in MDSCs in a SMAD4-dependent manner, thereby contributing to the establishment of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment by shifting T cells composition toward less CD8+ T cells, while more Tregs (Basso et al. 2017).

Immune checkpoints Such as programmed death Ligand 1 (PD-L1) are expressed by different tumor cells, which bind to the PD-1 receptor and activate the PD-L1/PD-1 signaling pathway, leading to immune evasion by T cells (Lin et al. 2015; Francisco et al. 2010). MiR-519 was downregulated under hypoxia in pancreatic cancer. Ectopic expression of miR-519 has been shown to decrease invasion and induce apoptosis by inhibiting PD-L1 in pancreatic cancer cells. Therefore, downregulation of MiR-519 under hypoxic conditions results in PD-L1 expression, leading to T-cell-mediated immune evasion(Nong et al. 2020).

These findings highlight the potential of targeting HypoxamiRs as a therapeutic strategy to overcome immune escape and improve the efficacy of cancer treatments in hypoxic environments. Further research into these miRNAs and also other immune cells such as T cells and B cells could pave the way for novel approaches to modulate the immune landscape of pancreatic cancer (Fig. 1). Moreover, the immunosuppressive effects driven by HypoxamiRs often intersect with their role in mediating chemoresistance. Role of hypoxamirs in gemcitabine resistance

Chemotherapy remains the main treatment for PDAC due to its non-resectable and invasive nature. Gemcitabine (GEM) forms the backbone of first-line chemotherapy regimens used for PDAC. However, gemcitabine resistance is a significant challenge during PDAC therapy, leading to increased mortality associated with the disease (Sarvepalli et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2021). Hypoxia is a characteristic feature of the microenvironment in several solid tumors and is closely associated with chemoresistance in these cancers (Rohwer and Cramer 2011). Under hypoxic conditions, tumor cells upregulate hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), which serve as key regulators of downstream genes (Nakazawa et al. 2016). Moreover, the significance of dysregulated HIF-1α and its associated genes in conferring gemcitabine resistance has recently been highlighted (Shukla et al. 2017). The interaction between microRNAs and hypoxia-related pathways, including key molecules such as hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), plays a crucial role in mediating chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer. Here, we review the role of HypoxamiRs in the regulating gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic cancer (Fig. 2).Fig. 2The HypoxamiRs mediated gemcitabine resistance in PDAC. This figure highlights the molecular pathways through which HypoxamiRs influence metabolism, DNA damage repair, and apoptosis, leading to reduced sensitivity of PDAC cells to gemcitabine

It has been shown that miR-221, miR-21, and miR-155 downregulation, and upregulation of miR-34 increased the growth and chemoresistance of hypoxic PDAC cell lines. The expression levels of miR-181a and let-7e were also correlated with increased chemoresistance in PDAC cell lines (Al-Sisan et al. 2023). Despite previous studies identifying miR-21 as an oncogenic molecule in PDAC, evidence suggests that it can also act as a tumor-suppressive miRNA in the context of drug resistance.

VMP1 (Vacuole Membrane Protein 1) is a multifunctional protein involved in several processes of tumor cells, including autophagy, apoptosis, and chemoresistance (Wei et al. 2022; Qian et al. 2014). The VMP1-mediated autophagy has been introduced as a pivotal regulator of gemcitabine resistance in PDAC cells, harboring a KRAS mutation (Ropolo et al. 2020). There was lncPVT1 overexpression and miR-143 downregulation in PDAC which enhanced gemcitabine resistance in PDAC cells. LncPVT1 modulated HIF-1α expression by sponging miR-143. Silencing PVT1 reduced gemcitabine resistance through the miR-143/HIF-1α/VMP1 axis in PDAC. Furthermore, miR-143 suppression reversed the effects of PVT1 silencing on gemcitabine sensitivity (Liu et al. 2021b). Additionally, given the inhibitory role of miR-519c on HIF-1α, co-transfection of miR-519c with gemcitabine has been demonstrated to reduce tumor growth and progression in PDAC cells (Xin et al. 2020).

HIF-1α stabilization induces glycolysis in tumor cells, even in a hypoxia-independent manner. HIF-1α upregulates glucose transporters, glycolytic enzymes, and glycolytic protein isoforms. It has been observed that inhibiting glycolysis reduces hypoxia-mediated chemoresistance in tumor cells (Robey et al. 2005; Semenza 2010b; Xu et al. 2005). Normoxic PDAC cells-derived exosomal circZNF91 acted as a sponge for miR-23b-3p, leading to the upregulation of the deacetylase Sirtuin1 (SIRT1) in hypoxic PDAC cells. SIRT1 stabilized the HIF-1α protein, which in turn promoted glycolysis and increased GEM resistance in hypoxic PDAC cells. Additionally, HIF-1α reciprocally regulated circZNF91 at the transcriptional level under hypoxic conditions (Zeng et al. 2021). It has been reported that miRNA-3662 overexpression also decreased the aerobic glycolysis and Gem-resistance in PDAC. Mir-3656 exerted its tumor suppressive role via directly targeting HIF-1α (Liu et al. 2021c).

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), key stromal cells within the TME of pancreatic cancer, are particularly abundant in their inflammatory subtypes (iCAFs) within hypoxic regions (Schwörer et al. 2023). Recent studies have indicated that hypoxia induces gemcitabine resistance through the activation of the PI3K/Akt and ERK signaling pathways (Yokoi and Fidler 2004). The AKT and ERK pathways are crucial regulators of cancer stemness and chemoresistance, as demonstrated in breast cancer and glioblastoma (Cheng et al. 2021; Tsai et al. 2019). In pancreatic cancer, hypoxia stimulates iCAFs to secrete large amounts of exosomal miR-21 via the HIF-1α/miR-21 axis, which subsequently activates the RAS/AKT/ERK signaling pathway. This activation enhances gemcitabine resistance and supports stemness maintenance in PDAC cells (Deng et al. 2024). TAp63, a homologue of p53, modulates various cellular processes, including metastasis, cell metabolism, cell cycle regulation, and apoptosis (Chen et al. 2018; Su et al. 2017; Su et al. 2012). Hypoxia led to the downregulation of TAp63 and PTEN via miR-301a. The inhibition of Tap63 led to increasing HIF-1α levels and downregulation of PTEN tumor suppressor enhanced Akt phosphorylation, followed by gemcitabine resistance in PDAC cells (Luo et al. 2018).

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of programmed cell death. The solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is a crucial regulator of ferroptosis and a key component of the cystine/glutamate transporter. SLC7A11 inhibits ferroptosis by increasing the transport of cystine into the cell, which in turn enhances glutathione synthesis, leading to the elimination of reactive oxygen species (Koppula et al. 2021). SRY-Box Transcription Factor 9 (SOX9), a cancer stem cell marker in pancreatic cancer, is involved in maintaining the stemness of tumor cells (Liang et al. 2022). Under hypoxic TME, DNA-methyltransferase 3 beta (DNMT3B) hypermethylated the miR-485-3p promoter, resulting in the upregulation of SOX9 and SLC7A11, thereby suppressing the ferroptosis process. The downregulation of miR-485-3p has been associated with poor prognosis in pancreatic cancer. MiR-485-3p exhibited tumor-suppressive functions and reduced GEM resistance in PDAC cells (Liu et al. 2024).

Overall, the interaction between hypoxia and microRNAs significantly influences gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic cancer. Recent advancements in high-throughput technologies, enabling the design of large cohorts and studies on patients before and after treatment at the single-cell level, offer a more precise understanding of the role of miRNAs in tumor cell chemoresistance. Understanding these molecular mechanisms provides valuable insights for developing targeted therapies to overcome chemoresistance in PDAC.

Role of hypoxamirs in apoptosis, autophagy and angiogenesis

Autophagy is a cellular process in which cells degrade and recycle their own dysfunctional cytosolic organelles to conserve energy (Palikaras and Tavernarakis 2014). Dysregulation of autophagy has been implicated in various diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and cancers (Piffoux et al. 2021; Meng et al. 2019; Guo et al. 2018). The autophagy is a complicated mechanism during pancreatic cancer. During the early stages, autophagy supports the proliferation of PDAC cells by supplying them with energy. However, excessive autophagy triggers apoptosis and degrades essential organelles within tumor cells (Lim et al. 2021). Recent studies have revealed that microRNAs are critical regulators of autophagy and apoptosis (Tolue Ghasaban et al. 2023). Hypoxia and nutritional starvation have been shown to induce autophagy in pancreatic tumor cells (Li et al. 2016a; Mizushima et al. 2010). The translocation of the nuclear protein SIRT1 to the cytoplasm has been associated with increased sensitivity to apoptosis under conditions of glucose and oxygen deprivation (Palmirotta et al. 2016). SIRT1 forms a complex with FoxO1 to regulate autophagy in the hypoxic environment of pancreatic cancer. Deacetylation of FoxO1 enhances the expression of autophagy-related genes, including Rab7 (Guerra and Bucci 2016). A negative correlation has been identified between miR-138-5p expression and autophagy in pancreatic cancer tissues. Overexpression of miR-138-5p suppressed the SIRT1/FoxO1/Rab7 pathway, thereby reducing autophagy in PDAC cells under hypoxia (Tian et al. 2017).

In the hypoxic microenvironment of pancreatic cancer, the upregulation of HIF-1α induces the expression of miR-21, which subsequently reduces apoptosis in PDAC cells. Conversely, suppression of miR-21 has been shown to increase the sensitivity of PDAC cells to hypoxia (Mace et al. 2013). Autophagy-related Gene 7 (ATG7) is critical in this process, playing a key role in the ATG12-ATG5 conjugation system (Qiang et al. 2017). Hypoxia increased the circATG7 level in the PDAC cells, promoting autophagy, tumor proliferation, and metastasis through the miR-766-5p/ATG7 axis. Vacuole Membrane Protein 1 (VMP1) is a critical modulator of autophagy in mammalian cells, particularly involved in the detachment of autophagosomes from the endoplasmic reticulum. Its function is notably significant in the cellular response to acute pancreatitis (Ropolo et al. 2007; Molejon et al. 2013; Zhao and Zhang 2018; Grasso et al. 2011). The overexpression of lncPVT1 and mir-143 downregulation was detected in pancreatic cancer. Moreover, silencing of lncPVT1 and induced expression of mir-143 decreased autophagy in PDAC cells. PVT1 acted as a ceRNA and sponged mir-143 to modulate the expression of HIF-1α. PVT1 regulated the autophagy mechanism in PDAC cells through miR-143/HIF-1α/VMP1 axis (Liu et al. 2021b).

Angiogenesis is essential for tumor progression, as it supplies oxygen and nutrients through newly formed blood vessels, enabling the survival and growth of cancer cells. In tumors, hypoxia frequently stimulates angiogenesis to counteract oxygen deprivation, thereby promoting more aggressive tumor behaviors (Carmeliet and Jain 2000). In pancreatic cancer, angiogenesis plays a particularly significant role due to the tumor's dense stroma and hypoxic microenvironment, both of which complicate treatment efforts. The hypoxic conditions drive the expression of pro-angiogenic factors, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which further supports cancer cell survival, invasion, and chemoresistance (Ren et al. 2018). Studies have shown that hypoxia-induced factors enhance the angiogenic potential of pancreatic cancer cells, exacerbating the challenges associated with current therapeutic approaches (Zhang et al. 2018). MicroRNAs are recognized as key regulators of tumor angiogenesis (Fanoodi et al. 2023).

Hypoxia-induced overexpression of miR-21 has been correlated with the upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and stem cell-related markers, underscoring miR-21's pivotal role in promoting tumor growth and angiogenesis in pancreatic cancer (Bao et al. 2012). Thioredoxin-interacting protein (TXNIP) has been identified as an inhibitor of the tumor promoter thioredoxin, which is involved in the angiogenesis and apoptosis of tumor cells (Nishiyama et al. 1999; Welsh et al. 2002; Saitoh 1998). As a well-known tumor suppressor, TXNIP exerts its effects through multiple signaling pathways (Chen et al. 2015; Waldhart et al. 2017; Spindel et al. 2012). Additionally, TXNIP has been reported to suppress HIF-mediated functions in various tumors (Shin et al. 2008; Wong and Hagen 2013). It has been found that there was upregulation of miR-125b in pancreatic cancer, which suppressed the TXNIP and facilitated the stabilization and nuclear translocation of HIF-1α, playing a crucial role in regulating pancreatic cancer angiogenesis and apoptosis under hypoxic conditions (Wang et al. 2021a).

The VEGF-C/VEGFR-3 has been detected as the main regulator of initiating lymphangiogenesis in tumor cells (Choi et al. 2017). BANCR sponged miR-143-5p in PDAC cells to regulate the HIF-1α/VEGF-C/VEGFR-3 axis, promoting the lymphangiogenesis and lymph node metastasis in pancreatic cancer (Hao et al. 2024). It also has been reported that mir-142 targeted HIF-1α to regulate the expression of VEGF-C in hypoxic PDAC cells (Lu et al. 2017). Moreover, there was downregulation of miR-221, miR-21, miR-155, and upregulation of miR-34 in hypoxic PDAC cells, resulting in elevation of angiogenesis and apoptosis(Al-Sisan et al. 2023).

The GJA1 Gene, encoding Connexin 43, a member of the cell junction protein family, is involved in regulating drug efflux and nucleic acid transfer (Huang et al. 2001; Valiunas et al. 2005). It has been shown that exosomal miR-30b-5p, derived from hypoxic pancreatic cancer cells, downregulated GJA1 gene in endothelial cells, enhancing tube formation, endothelial cell migration, and angiogenesis. Additionally, plasma exosomal miR-30b-5p has been identified as a diagnostic marker for pancreatic cancer detection (Chen et al. 2022). AMOTL2, a member of the angiomotin (Amot) membrane-associated scaffold proteins, is known to promote tube formation, polarity, and proliferation through the upregulation of the MAPK/ERK1/2 signaling pathway in vascular endothelial cells (Wang et al. 2011a; Bratt et al. 2002). The UCA1 lncRNA acted as a sponge for miR-96-5p, regulating the expression of AMOTL2. Exosomal UCA1 derived from tumor cells under hypoxic conditions promoted angiogenesis by modulating the miR-96-5p/AMOTL2/ERK1/2 axis (Guo et al. 2020). Moreover, The EFNA3 tumor suppressor is regulated by mir-210 via binding to its 7mer-m8 molecular binding site(Hu et al. 2016). The significant upregulation of exosomal mir-210 has been reported in pancreatic cancer which was involved in the angiogenesis, cell invasion, and proliferation. Mir-210 downregulated the EFNA3 to enhance the Wnt/Β-catenin/RHOA or PI3K/AKT/VEGFA pathways, thus increasing cellular permeability and tumor angiogenesis (Wu et al. 2022).

In conclusion, HypoxamiRs play a pivotal role in regulating both tumor autophagy and angiogenesis in pancreatic cancer. Although poor vascularization presents a challenge for effective drug delivery, the process of angiogenesis, even when resulting in dysfunctional blood vessels, continues to support tumor growth and metastasis, making it a critical target for therapeutic intervention. Future studies should focus on validating these findings in larger cohorts and exploring the therapeutic potential of modulating HypoxamiRs in combination with other treatment modalities. These cellular processes are closely linked to the acquisition of invasive traits, as HypoxamiRs can also promote epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), facilitating tumor cell dissemination under hypoxic stress.

Role of hypoxamiRs In EMT

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a critical process induced by hypoxia in various malignancies. This mechanism involves the loss of cell polarity and epithelial markers (E-cadherin, β-catenin), while acquiring mesenchymal markers (vimentin and N-cadherin) along with invasive features (Diepenbruck and Christofori 2016; Azimi et al. 2016; Ibrahim et al. 2017; Lin et al. 2016). Hypoxia and its effect on the induction of EMT are pivotal factors in pancreatic cancer development and metastasis. These processes are regulated by the activation of several key signaling pathways, including PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling pathways (Spivak-Kroizman et al. 2013; Cao et al. 2016; Du et al. 2014; Sahlgren et al. 2008). Overexpression of HIF-1a under hypoxic condition is associated with invasive phenotype, chemoresistance and poor prognosis (Zhu et al. 2017; Harris 2002). Hypoxia promotes EMT by HIF-1a to promote tumor invasion and chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer (Cheng et al. 2011). Vimentin and N-cadherin are critical mesenchymal markers that serve as master regulators of EMT and metastasis (Thiery et al. 2009; Mendez et al. 2010). Vimentin is upregulated in various cancers and plays an oncogenic role in metastatic tumor cells and enhancing the motility of mesenchymal cells and exhibiting cytoskeletal activity (Rathje et al. 2014; Zeisberg and Neilson 2009). In pancreatic cancer, Vimentin has been detected in metastatic sites, including peritoneal-washing cytology, portal vein invasion, and lymph node metastasis, making it a prognostic marker for poor outcomes and chemoresistance (Yamada et al. 2013). Histone deacetylase 1 (HDAC1) can interact with HIF-1α, forming a complex that downregulates downstream targets of HIF1-a (Liu et al. 2011). Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-1α–HDAC1 complex suppressed the transcription of miR-548an, leading to Vimentin upregulation and the subsequent activation of EMT and tumorigenesis (Zhu et al. 2016). The HIFs molecules, particularly HIF-1α and HIF-2α, drive EMT under hypoxic conditions by regulating key transcription factors such as ZEB1, TWIST1, and SNAIL (Mak et al. 2010; Yang et al. 2008; Joseph et al. 2015). MiR-21 significantly increased the levels of EMT mesenchymal markers ZEB1 and Vimentin, and decreased the epithelial marker E-cadherin in the PDAC cells under hypoxic conditions (Bao et al. 2012). Hypoxia induced RhoA expression and its downstream effector, ROCK1, which subsequently promoted cell metastasis(Gulhati et al. 2011). RhoA overexpression in hypoxic TME of PDAC was mediated through lncRNA NORAD/miR-125a-3p axis (Li et al. 2017b). Additionally, exosomal mir-301a induced M2 polarization of macrophages via the PTEN/PI3Kg pathway activation which subsequently enhanced EMT in PDAC cells (Wang et al. 2018).

In addition, the MiR-142/HIF1a axis regulated cell proliferation and invasion via targeting E-cadharein and vimentin expression in the hypoxic TME of PDAC, ultimately resulting in suppression of hypoxia-induced EMT. (Lu et al. 2017). Mir-210 also inactivated the HOXA9 to induce the NF-κB pathway which upregulated hypoxia/HIF-1a-induced EMT in PDAC cells (Ni et al. 2019). TP63 transcription factor, a member of the p53 family, plays a crucial role in regulating epithelial cells and the downstream targets of p53 (Barbieri and Pietenpol 2006). MiR-301a was found to be upregulated, transcriptionally regulated by HIF-2α in PDAC cells. The overexpression of miR-301a further enhanced HIF-1α expression, promoting hypoxia-mediated EMT in PDAC cells. Additionally, TP63 was significantly downregulated by miR-301a under hypoxic conditions, suggesting that the HIF-2α/miR-301a/TP63 signaling axis plays a critical role in regulating EMT in hypoxic PDAC cells (Zhang et al. 2020a).

pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs) are critical stromal cells that cause desmoplastic reaction and hypoxia in the TME of pancreatic cancer (Apte et al. 2004; Apte et al. 2013; Hwang et al. 2008; Masamune and Shimosegawa 2009, 2013; Masamune et al. 2009; Erkan et al. 2012). It has been revealed that pancreatic stellate cell (PSC)-derived exosomes induce oncogenic pathways in PDAC cells by transporting proteins, mRNAs, and non-coding RNAs (Ali et al. 2015; Li et al. 2020b; Lugea and Waldron 2017; Takikawa et al. 2017). PTEN, a pivotal tumor suppressor that regulates the PI3K/AKT pathway in various cancers, was significantly downregulated in PDAC cells exposed to hypoxic PSC-derived exosomes (Zhang et al. 2020b; Blanco-Aparicio et al. 2007). The miR-4465 and miR-616-3p were transferred to PDAC cells via these exosomes under hypoxic TME conditions, leading to increased growth and invasion by inhibiting PTEN and activating the AKT pathway that potentially induces EMT. Additionally, significant upregulation of these two HypoxamiRs was observed in the plasma samples of pancreatic cancer patients (Cao et al. 2021). Moreover, PSCs promoted miR-210 expression and subsequent tumor migration via EMT in PDAC cells (Takikawa et al. 2013).

In conclusion HypoxamiRs play a pivotal role in driving EMT in pancreatic cancer, significantly contributing to tumor progression and metastasis. These HypoxamiRs regulate key signaling pathways and modulate the expression of crucial transcription factors and markers, reinforcing the aggressive nature of pancreatic cancer under hypoxic conditions. However, further research is essential to fully elucidate the complex interactions between HypoxamiRs and the EMT process. Detailed studies focusing on the mechanistic roles of HypoxamiRs are needed to develop effective targeted therapies. In addition, HypoxamiRs act within broader regulatory networks involving other non-coding RNAs, forming axes that are largely orchestrated through HIF-1α–dependent hypoxic signaling pathways.

Role of ncRNAs/HypoxamiRs axes in hypoxia

Approximately 98% of the human genome consists of non-coding regions. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) are a diverse group of transcripts that regulate various cellular processes, often through epigenetic mechanisms. Among these, circular RNAs (circRNAs), long non-coding RNAs, and microRNAs are well-recognized for their critical roles in cancer development and progression (Slack and Chinnaiyan 2019). In the hypoxic microenvironment of pancreatic cancer, lncRNAs and circRNAs can act as sponges for miRNAs, thereby modulating their activity. This interaction indirectly influences the nuclear stabilization and activation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α), a key transcription factor in hypoxia response. HIF-1α, in turn, binds to hypoxia-responsive elements (HREs) within the promoter regions of its target genes, modulating their expression. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are long transcripts that do not code for proteins, have been increasingly recognized for their role in the epigenetic regulation of gene expression and their contribution to tumor progression (Chi et al. 2019; Sanchez Calle et al. 2018; Quinn and Chang 2016). lncRNAs play a critical role in various aspects of cellular biology, including the regulation of gene transcription, modulation of protein and transcription factor activity, recruitment of chromatin-modifying enzymes, and the assembly of ribonucleoprotein complexes (Wang and Chang 2011). lncRNAs can function as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), acting as sponges for miRNAs and inhibiting their regulatory roles on target genes (Cesana et al. 2011; Tay et al. 2014).

TEAD1 is a pivotal part of the Hippo signaling pathway and plays a regulatory role in the YAP. TAED1 is implicated in different biological processes of cancer cells including cell growth, migration, EMT, and chemoresistance (Wei et al. 2023; Li et al. 2022a; Sun et al. 2021; Zhao et al. 2008). HIF-1α overexpressed the MKLN1-AS lncRNA by binding to its HRE region, suppressing its regulatory role on miR-185-5p which led to TEAD1 upregulation. Therefore, the HIF-1α/MKLN1-AS/miR-185-5p/TEAD1 led to the tumor growth and migration under hypoxia (Chen et al. 2024).

Ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme 2 variant 1 (UBE2V1) and its co-factor UBC13 are involved in protein polymer production through K63-mediated ubiquitination, while ubiquitin-specific peptidase 10 (USP10) is a deubiquitinating enzyme implicated in tumorigenesis (Xu et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2020a). HIF-1α enhanced the expression of LINC00460 under hypoxic conditions, which sponged miR-4689, leading to the activation of UBE2V1 and also this lncRNA sequestrated the USP10. UBE2V1 and USP10 regulated the proliferation and metastasis of pancreatic cancer cells by stabilizing p53 (Zhang et al. 2023). Hypoxia increased the HIF-1 binding to the HRE regions of miR-191 and miR-212(Song et al. 2014). In addition, miR-212 overexpression in PDAC was correlated with poor prognosis in patients (Yue et al. 2019).

Stromal cell-derived-factor-1 (SDF-1) ligand and its receptor CXCR4 have been introduced as a prognostic marker for pancreatic cancer patients (Oh et al. 2012; Sachdeva and Mo 2010). It has been discovered that hypoxia downregulated the miRNA-150 expression in PDAC cells, increasing CXCR4 and SDF-1 expression level, led to tumor invasion and migration (Sun et al. 2015). RAS is a highly mutated oncogene and a key regulator in various malignancies, including pancreatic, thyroid, colorectal, and lung carcinomas (Kranenburg 2005; Tam et al. 2006; Kong et al. 2013). Under hypoxic conditions, the expression of lncRNA-NUTF2P3-001 was upregulated, leading to the downregulation of miR-3923. This miRNA typically inhibits KRAS expression; thus, its repression by lncRNA-NUTF2P3-001 resulted in the activation of the KRAS pathway, promoting tumorigenesis in pancreatic cancer (Li et al. 2016b). Moreover, a significant upregulation of LSAMP-AS1 and S100A2 has been observed in PDAC cells and tissues. The LSAMP-AS1/hsa-miR-129-5p/S100A2 axis plays a crucial role in regulating the hypoxic microenvironment and facilitating metastasis in pancreatic cancer (Li et al. 2023). Additionally, NORAD lncRNA sponges miR-125a-3p to upregulate RhoA, enhancing cell metastasis and EMT in PDAC cells(Li et al. 2017b).

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are critical regulators of TME in hypoxic pancreatic cancer which communicate with tumor cells via cancer-derived exosomes, containing non-coding RNAs such as lncRNAs and miRNAs (Ilkhani et al. 2021; Masoudi-Khoram et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2019). Desmoplasia is a characteristic of tumor microenvironment in pancreatic cancer that induce hypovascularity and poor blood supply, ultimately resulting in hypoxia (Liu et al. 2012a). CAFs are the primary stromal cells within the TME of pancreatic cancer, playing a crucial role in tumor progression and desmoplastic reaction by transporting exosomes to other cells within the TME (Nurmik et al. 2020; Biffi and Tuveson 2021). CAF-derived exosomal lncRNA NNT-AS1 was transported to the PDAC cells and sponged miR-889-3p to upregulate the HIF-1 expression, promoting cell growth, migration and glucose metabolism (Zhang et al. 2024). Sirtuin-3 (SIRT3), a mitochondrial gene belonging to the sirtuin family, is involved in maintaining glucose stability, suppressing cellular transformation, stabilizing the genome, and deacetylating histones H3K9 and H3K56 (Zhai et al. 2017; Pacifici et al. 2019). SIRT3 is known to negatively regulate HIF-1α and inhibit tumor proliferation by suppressing reactive oxygen species (ROS) formation (Bell et al. 2011). However, in the hypoxic environment of pancreatic cancer, miR-421 was transferred to PDAC cells via CAF-derived exosomes, which decreased the SIRT3-mediated inhibition of HIF-1α, thereby promoting tumor progression (Zhou et al. 2022).

Additionally, PCED1B-AS1 lncRNA upregulated the HIF-1α expression by sequestrating miR-411-3p in hypoxic PDAC cells (Zhang et al. 2021). Furthermore, PVT1 lncRNA induced the expression of HIF-1α by sponging miR-519d-3p, followed by increased glycolysis, growth and invasion in PDAC cells under hypoxia (Sun et al. 2020). FEZF1‐AS1 also regulated the miR‐142/HIF‐1α to promote PDAC cell proliferation and invasion under hypoxic condition (Ou et al. 2019b). The TXNIP knockdown is associated with progressive bladder breast, colorectal and gastric cancers (Spindel et al. 2012; Shin et al. 2008; Nishizawa et al. 2011; Woolston et al. 2013). It has been reported that TXNIP upregulation suppressed the HIF1α activity in different cancers (Chen et al. 2015; Wong and Hagen 2013). TXNIP binds to the the ubiquitin ligase pVHL and HIF1α to form a complex which results in the degradation of HIF1α (Bai et al. 2017). MiR-224 downregulated the TXNIP expression level, followed by HIF1α upregulation which promoted cancer metastasis and progression under hypoxic environment of PDAC (Zhu et al. 2018). Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) is a protein that plays a critical role in the ubiquitin ligase E3 complex, which targets HIF-1α for degradation under normoxic conditions. VHL recognizes and binds to hydroxylated HIF-1α, leading to its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation (Paulsen 2001). Silencing miR-21 in hypoxic pancreatic cancer cells suppressed tumor proliferation, invasion, and migration by modulating the VHL/HIF-1α/VEGF and MMP-2/−9 signaling pathways (Sun et al. 2019b). The migration and invasion inhibitory protein (MIIP) is tumor Suppressor that is involved in various biological process of tumor cells and is located on 1p36.22 as a frequently deleted region in different cancers (Wang et al. 2011b; Wang et al. 2016b; Sun et al. 2017). MIIP inhibited the histone deacetylase 6 (HDAC6) to increase acetylation and degradation of HIF-1α and decrease its nuclear accumulation. HIF-1α downregulated the MIIP expression via inducing the miR-646 expression in the hypoxic condition, resulting in tumor development, metastasis and poor prognosis in PDAC cells (Niu et al. 2018). MiR-124 also suppressed the activity of HIF-α via MCT1 regulation. MiR-124 modulated the expression of MCT1 as a lactate transporter and inhibited lactic acid efflux, decreasing cell proliferation and migration (Wu et al. 2018).

Mutations in the 3′ untranslated region (UTR) of genes targeted by miRNAs can significantly impact the miRNA-mRNA interaction, leading to altered gene expression (Ryan et al. 2010). Specifically, the single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs2057482, located within the 3′ UTR of the HIF1A gene, has been shown to reduce the inhibitory effect of miR-199a on HIF1A. This diminished regulation results in increased HIF1A expression and has been associated with a worse prognosis in pancreatic cancer patients (Wang et al. 2016a).

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are non-coding RNAs involved in various aspects of cancer biology (Yang et al. 2021a; Yang et al. 2021b). CircRNAs are a class of non-coding RNAs produced through alternative splicing that form covalently closed loop structures, rendering them resistant to exonuclease-mediated degradation. This stability makes circRNAs abundant in tumor cells and exosomes, highlighting their potential as robust biomarkers for cancer detection. Functionally, circRNAs regulate gene expression by acting as molecular sponges for miRNAs or serving as scaffolds for protein complexes, thereby influencing various cellular processes associated with tumorigenesis (Kristensen et al. 2019; Chen et al. 2019b; Li et al. 2022b; Wang et al. 2021b; Chen 2020). One of the well-known metabolic adaptations in cancer cells is the Warburg effect, where cells preferentially utilize glucose and produce lactate rather than relying on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (Riera Leal et al. 2020; Cassim et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2020b). This metabolic shift, although less efficient in energy production, enhances the metastatic potential of tumor cells, particularly under hypoxic conditions (Dai et al. 2020; Liang et al. 2020b). Circ_03955 expression has been found to be elevated in PDAC tissue samples and cell lines. Circ_03955 functioned as a molecular sponge for miR-3662, leading to the upregulation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α). This upregulation contributed to tumor progression, highlighting the critical role of circ_03955 in pancreatic cancer development under hypoxia (Liu and Xu 2021). Moreover, c-Myc is a critical transcription factor in pancreatic cancer, exerting its oncogenic effects through bromodomain PHD finger transcription factor (BPTF). The protein BIN1 acts as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting c-Myc activity, thereby reducing tumor development in pancreatic cancer (Richart et al. 2016; Ge et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2017). Exosomal circPDK1 was upregulated in pancreatic cancer tissues and serum, correlating with poor clinical outcomes and promoting cell motility, invasiveness, and glycolysis in pancreatic cancer cells. Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-1α significantly increases the expression of circPDK1, leading to the upregulation of BPTF and degradation of BIN1, which is mediated by the downregulation of miR-628-3p. This results in enhanced migration and progression of pancreatic cancer cells through the circPDK1/miR-628-3p/BPTF/c-Myc signaling axis (Lin et al. 2022) Additionally, circATG7 sponged the miR-766-5p to induce the expression of ATG7 and increased cell proliferation, migration, and autophagy in hypoxic PDAC cells (He et al. 2022). Pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2) is a key regulator of dysregulated glycolysis in cancer. As a protein kinase, PKM2 transactivates the downstream targets of HIF-1α, playing a crucial role in the metabolic reprogramming of cancer cells. PKM2 enhances cellular activity by stabilizing the insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) precursor via Heat shock protein 90 (HSP90) (Luo et al. 2011; Koo et al. 2021; Zahra et al. 2020). It has been shown that the circular RNA circR3HCC1L upregulated PKM2 expression by repressing miR-873-5p. Hypoxia-induced exosomal circR3HCC1L was implicated in promoting tumor development, malignant transformation, and glycolysis through the miR-873-5p/PKM2 axis in PDAC cells (Wang et al. 2024). Hypoxia-induced exosomal circular RNA circR3HCC1L upregulated PKM2 through miR-873-5p suppression, consequently promoting tumor development, malignant transformation, and glycolysis (Wang et al. 2024).

Therefore, the activation of hypoxia-inducible factor HIF-1α drive the expression of several non-coding RNAs, including miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs, which play crucial roles in promoting tumorigenesis, metastasis, and chemoresistance (Table 2). HypoxamiRs, in turn, play a crucial role in regulating HIF activity, a central factor in cellular adaptation to low oxygen environments. These hypoxia-induced ncRNAs are vital in controlling the expression of genes involved in tumor progression, angiogenesis, and metabolism under hypoxic conditions. Understanding the role of ncRNAs/HypoxamiRs axes in hypoxia could lead to new strategies for targeting hypoxia-driven processes in pancreatic cancer, underscoring their significance in tumor biology. Clinical implications of HypoxamiRs

HypoxamiRs are potentially recognized as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in PDAC. Circulating and exosome-associated HypoxamiRs can be detected in patient plasma or serum, where their expression profiles correlate with intratumoral hypoxia, clinicopathological features, and patient outcomes. Their inherent stability in biofluids and capacity to reflect the tumor microenvironment make them attractive candidates for non-invasive disease monitoring and for guiding treatment stratification (Wnuk et al. 2023). For instance, several HypoxamiRs, including miR-21 (Kawaguchi et al. 2013), miR-212 (Cote et al. 2014), miR-155 (Cote et al. 2014), and miR-210 (Yu et al. 2017; Liu et al. 2012b; Wang et al. 2009), have been reported to be upregulated in the plasma or serum of PDAC patients across stages I–IV, whereas others, such as miR-1275 (Aita et al. 2021) and miR-30b-5p (Khan et al. 2021), found to be downregulated.

Also, HypoxamiRs have been evaluated for their prognostic relevance in PDAC. Circulating miR-125b-5p was reported to be upregulated in plasma and associated with improved overall survival (OS) (Johansen et al. 2016). Conversely, multiple independent investigations have linked elevated plasma or serum miR-21 to adverse prognosis, with significantly shorter OS in high-expression groups (Abue et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2013; Liu et al. 2012c; Ali et al. 2010). Plasma miR-210, another hypoxia-inducible miRNA, has been associated with better prognosis, with patients exhibiting high expression demonstrating significantly longer OS compared to those with low levels (Yu et al. 2017).

In preclinical PDAC models, hypoxamiR modulation counteracts hypoxia-induced cancer hallmarks such as chemoresistance, EMT, immune suppression, and angiogenesis (Sadozai et al. 2024). Although delivery platforms such as lipid nanoparticles and bacterially derived minicells have shown feasibility for miRNA therapeutics, no clinical trials have yet evaluated HypoxamiRs-targeted approaches in PDAC (Ali et al. 2020; Lee et al. 2019). The functional role of HypoxamiRs in tumor progression warrants the need for targeted delivery systems and early-phase combination trials with pharmacodynamic endpoints.

Limitations and future perspectives

The existing research highlights the complex functional roles of HypoxamiRs in the progression of PDAC and its chemoresistance through various cancer hallmarks. However, several important limitations must be considered. Firstly, much of the current evidence is derived from in vitro studies and animal models. While these approaches provide valuable insights, they may not fully capture the complex nature of the human PDAC TME. Secondly, many of the mechanistic findings are based on studies focusing on individual miRNAs. However, in the TME, the regulation of these molecules involves interconnected interactions among various ncRNAs, transcription factors, and signaling pathways, which are not adequately represented in single-miRNA investigations. Lastly, there is a palpable lack of clinical data that directly links the expression of HypoxamiRs to patient outcomes in PDAC. To better understand their potential as diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic tools, large-scale and prospective clinical studies are essential. Addressing these gaps in knowledge will be crucial for translating preclinical findings into effective treatments for patients with PDAC. This study offers novel insights into the functional roles of these microRNAs in PDAC, and future research may explore their potential as therapeutic, prognostic and diagnostic panels.

Conclusion

HypoxamiRs are key regulators of PDAC progression. Through bidirectional regulation with HIF signaling and interactions with ncRNA, they contribute to a wide network of pathways, leading to potentiation of hypoxic TME of PDAC. In this review we summarized and categorized the HypoxamiRs functionally and mechanistically (Table 1). The detection of these immune, chemoresistance, EMT, angiogenesis, autophagy, and apoptosis related HypoxamiRs in tissues, blood, and exosomes highlights potential as diagnostic and prognostic targets. Targeting the HypoxamiRs–HIF–ncRNA axis, specifically in combination with current available therapies, offers a promising strategy to counteract PDAC’s hypoxia-driven aggressiveness and treatment resistance.

How close is too close for singular mean curvature flows?
Authors: J M Daniels-Holgate, Or Hershkovits
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSuppose (Mti)t∈[0,T), i=1,2, are two mean curvature flows in Rn+1 encountering a multiplicity one compact singularity at time T, in such a manner that for every k, the Hausdorff distance between the two flows, dH, satisfies dH(Mt1,Mt2)/(T-t)k→0. We demonstrate that Mt1=Mt2 for every t. This generalizes a result of Martin-Hagemayer and Sesum, who proved the case where Mt1 is itself a self-similarly shrinking flow.Mathematics Subject Classification: 35K93, 53E10

Introduction

Singularity formation is a central area of study in Geometric Analysis. An important consequence of Huisken’s monotoncity formula for mean curvature flow (MCF) is that a tangent flow at a singular point in an MCF is given by a self-shrinking mean curvature flow,

It was shown by Schulze, [14], that when a tangent flow is given by a multiplicity one compact self-shrinker, the tangent flow is in fact unique. Entwined with the question of uniqueness of tangents is the question of minimal rate of convergence to the tangent (cf. [1, 4, 16]). More generally, one can ask at which rates can the flow converge to the self-shrinker?

For the case of spherical singularities, the minimal and maximal rates of convergence were obtained by Sesum [15] and Strehlke [17], the latter showing that a solution to the MCF converging super polynomially to the family of shrinking spheres must itself be a family of shrinking spheres. Strehlke’s result was recently generalized by Martin-Hagemayer and Sesum, [13] (following a similar result for Ricci flow by Kotschwar [11]) to apply to a flow converging super polynomially to any self shrinking compact singularity. Importantly, obtaining a polynomial bound on the decay allows one to perform secondary blow-up arguments resulting in a non zero solution to the linearized equation.

Note that if Mt1 and Mt2 are two distinct MCFs with the same compact tangent, [13, 17] give no lower bound as to how close they might be, unless one of them is the shrinking flow itself. The purpose of this note is to provide such sharp lower bound.

Remark 1.2

It is natural to try linearizing both Nτ1 and Nτ2 over the static flow Σ, and consider the evolution of height difference over that fixed shrinker. Note however that in this case, the error term in (9) would have a free |∇2u| term on the right hand side (with a time integrable coefficient). Such errors are not easily amenable for frequency analysis (see [2, 5, 10]). A similar challenge is likely to appear if one tries to obtain an analogous result to Theorem 1.1 for the Ricci flow, using the ideas of [11], or if one tries to use the two shrinker quantities on Nτ1 and Mτ2 to derive Theorem 1.1 in a manner more similar to [13].

The organization of the paper is as follows: In Section (2) we derive Theorem 2.1, showing that solution u to (8) with errors satisfying morally (9) over a RMCF converging to compact self shrinkers can not converge to zero super-exponentially. In Section 3 we show how Theorem 1.1 reduces to Theorem 2.1. The Appendix derives the RMCF equation in normal graphical gauge over another RMCF, from which the estimate (8) and (9) follow.

Ayurvedic management of Haglund's deformity diagnosed as Vatakantaka - A case report
Authors: Priyanka A. Dorage, Ashish A. Thatere, Rohini S. Prabhale
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Haglund's deformity is one of the orthopedic problematic conditions manifest as a painful plantar heel. Haglund's deformity is a posterosuperior calcaneal projection of bony overgrowth from calcaneum bone which occurs when the foot is exposed to constant strain leading to calcium deposition in the heel bone, can be correlated with Vatakantaka in Ayurveda. It can be treated with a combination of Viddhakarma (therapeutic needle-piercing therapy), and Agnikarma (therapeutic heat therapy) with oral Ayurvedic medications which cure disease and provide long-term relief. A 48-year-old female came with complaints of difficulty in walking, pain in the left heel region which aggravates after awakening in the morning, standing long time, and after excessive walking. She had taken analgesics, and steroids drug for 8 months but didn't get relief. So, she opted for an Ayurvedic medication for further treatment. With the help of seven settings of Viddhakarma, and Agnikarma there was a significant improvement in clinical features, assessment scales of pain, function, swelling, tenderness, and in bony outgrowth. Viddhakarma and Agnikarma an easy, affordable, and straightforward techniques that may be carried out at the OPD level with basic facilities. It can be inferred from this case study that the Combination of Viddhakarma, Agnikarma with oral Ayurvedic drugs is an effective treatment modality for Haglund's deformity.

Introduction

An unpleasant sensory and emotional experience connected to prospective tissue damage known as pain, interferes with daily activities for the individual [1]. Despite being an essential component of daily activities and mobility, feet are frequently the body part that receives the least attention. Ignoring them might result in painful problems like calcaneal spurs and related disorders. It is widely acknowledged that chronic pain is a serious global health issue that impacts physical, psychological (sleep, fear, anxiety, Depression), and social (work, family, social network, finance) quality of life.

The bony posterosuperior calcaneal projections (at Achilles tendon insertion) known as Haglund's deformity develop around the largest, strongest, longest, and most prominent calcaneal bone that forms the prominence of the heel [2]. It gives rise to symptoms of pain and swelling with high heels impinge on the soft tissues overlying the prominence [3]. The calcaneum serves as a lever for the calf muscles and bears the weight of the body in both static and dynamic weight-bearing positions. Heel discomfort or talalgia can have various origins, Haglund's deformity is one of the significant causes of such orthopedic problematic condition.

Patrick Haglund initially introduced Haglund's deformity in 1927. It is also known as pump bump, Mulholland deformity, and retrocalcaneal exostosis. The etiology is not well known, but some theories have been proposed like a high arch of the foot, tight Achilles tendon, altered biomechanics of foot joints, poorly or tight fitting shoes, obesity, degeneration, and heredity. Haglund's syndrome manifests with a combination of pathology including calcaneal tendon bursitis, retrocalcaneal bursitis, inflammation, and thickening of the calcaneal tendon [4].

It is estimated that one in ten people may experience heel soreness at some point in their lives. There is an urgent need to find an alternative therapeutic model to overcome the therapeutic limitations of conventional treatments like significant recurrence rates, Dependency on non-steroidal anti-inflammatory medications, steroids, surgical procedures [5], etc. Ayurveda can be a ray of hope in a world where everyone is searching for ways to reduce and eliminate pain.

Haglund's deformity can be correlated with Vatakantaka or Raktavritta Vata (a stage where channels are blocked by blood) in Ayurveda. Vatakantaka is prick by thorns like pain in Gulfa Sandhi (ankle joint) due to Avarrita Vata [6]. Bhaishjyaratnavali, Bhavprakasha and Vangsen have stated Daha with Soochi and Raktamoshana as a line of treatment of Vatakantaka which can be considered as Agnikarma (Therapeutic heat therapy) and Viddhakarma (Needle-piercing therapy) respectively with Pana of Eranda Taila. [[7], [8], [9]] Sushruta also documented Siravedha as a line of treatment in Vatakantaka 2 Angula (approx. 3.90 cm) above Kshipramarma (∼Vital point of body located between the first and second toes) using Vreehimukha Shastra. [10].

Patient information

A 48-year-old female came to OPD complaining of pain in the left heel region, difficulty in walking, worsening pain after awakening in the morning, standing for a long time, and excessive walking. Patients had been advised for a surgical procedure and had taken NSAID, and steroids in conventional medication for 8 months, but did not get relief. She was experiencing worsening pain with every movement. Therefore, she opted for Ayurveda management. She was a housewife. There was no history of any past illness like rheumatoid arthritis, gout, or seronegative spondyloarthropathies, past hospitalization, family history, or surgical illness.

Clinical findings

On examination, her vitals were found to be within normal limits. Pallor, Icterus, Lymphadenopathy, and Clubbing were absent. Nadi (pulse) was Vata Kaphaja, 87/min, Regular, Mala(bowel) was Asamyak and Aam Lakshana Yukata, Frequent micturition (7–8 times/day) was present, Jivha (tongue) was Saam (coated), Shabda (voice) was Spashta, Sparsha (touch) was Samshitoshna. B/L eye vision was normal. The patient was Sthula (obsess) with a BMI- 28.7. Cardiovascular, respiratory, and abdominal examinations were within normal limits. Thompson's test was positive while Mate's test was negative.

Diagnostic assessment

The patient was diagnosed with Haglund's deformity(∼Vatakantaka) based on clinical presentation, and X-ray left foot lateral view [figure-1]. Pain, swelling, and tenderness were assessed. The pain was assessed on the visual analogue scale(VAS), Wong-Baker Face Scale, where "0″ is no pain and "10″ is severe pain. Function outcomes were assessed on American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society (AOFAS) scores [11] and lower extremity functional scale [12]. Local swelling and tenderness were graded where grade "4″ indicates severe swelling, not allowing to touch, and grade "0″ indicates no swelling, no tenderness respectively. The Viddhakarma and Agnikarma procedure involved Purva Karma (pre procedure steps), Pradhana Karma (procedure steps), and Paschata Karma (post procedure steps).

Purva Karma: The written informed consent was taken from the patient with an explanation of the pre-procedure and Investigations including CBC, BSL, HIV, bleeding time, and clotting time were carried out. Patient was asked to lie comfortably in a supine position on the examination table. The vitals of the patient were evaluated. Spirit gauze was used to clean a particular site. In diet patient was given Yavagu (gruel) prior to the Viddhakarma while before Agnikarma rice with curd was given.

Pradhana Karma: 1. During the Viddhakarma procedure, a sterile needle of number 26 was used. The point 2 Angula above Kshipramarma of the left foot was selected, and the sterile hollow needle was pierced at 90°at that tender point up to 30 s.

2. During the procedure of Agnikarma, the most tender point was identified. Cleaned the affected part with spirit gauze. Applied heat to Tamra Shalaka by indirect method by candle, then heat given at tender point with tip of Shalaka until Samyak Dagdha Lakshana appears.

Paschata Karma: During the post procedure phase, the vitals of the patient were measured. The disposable needle is appropriately discarded and the blood coming out of the punctured site was cleaned with dry cotton gauze after the Viddhakarma while alovera bulk was applied at the site after the Agnikarma.

Discussion

The tuberosity produces a retrocalcaneal exostosis called Haglund's deformity, confirmed by the parallel pitch lines and Fowler's angle on a lateral view radiograph [2]. Although the etiology is unclear yet now, according to some theories, when a foot is subjected to continuous strain leads to injury to the Achilles tendon, and Calcium deposits accumulate at the insertion point of the tendon on the heel bone [13]. In Ayurveda, the condition is understood under the heading Abhighataja Agantuja Vyadhi (due to exogenous cause), Vatavyadhi, and exclusively can considered as Vatakantaka. The etiology explained in classics is due to Vata Prakopa (aggravation of Vata Dosha), Rakta, and Kapha Dushti (vitiation of Kapha, and Rakta) at the heel region, resulting in pain, tenderness, and inflammation.

Modern science research states that although various therapeutic modalities have been documented in the literature till now, no consensus over a surgical treatment has been established [14]. To overcome the limitations of conventional therapy like the risk of rupture of the Achilles tendon due to corticosteroid injection, there is a necessity to find alternative therapy [15].

As the patient was overweight, there may be a collapse of the truss's integrity in response to forces of vertical and ground reaction [16]. To correct this etiological factor, fat-alleviating drugs i.e. Yograja Guggul, Methi Ghana Vati used after the correction of gut flora with appetizer Lashunadi Vati and the correction of direction of flow of Vata by laxatives Shunti Siddha Eranda Sneha.

In Vitro study conducted evaluated the potent anti-inflammatory action of Yograja Guggul against major enzymes cyclooxygenase- 1,2 and 5-lipoxygenase involved in inflammatory cascades [17]. Methi (Fenugreek) possesses pharmacological anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anticholesterolemic which was required in this case [18]. As bony exocytosis is not possible without Vata according to Ayurveda, Hence Cap. Burcalvin (a combination of potent Vata-alleviating herbal drugs) was used.

The procedure of Agnikarma acts against vitiated Vata and Kapha by relieving pain and inflammation at that site by its Sukshama (minute), Ushna (hot), Vyavayi (quick acting), Teekshna (penetrating), Laghu ( small), and Vikashi (quickly spreading) properties. The thermodynamics theory explains the pain management action of Agnikarma, where thermal energy is transferred from the instrument to the tissue. It stimulates the thermostatic center of the body and increases the blood circulation by vasodilation. This increased circulation helps to flush out exosomes from blood and causes efficient tissue perfusion [19] and increased temperature reduces nerve reflexes resulting in the relaxation of muscle thereby causing a reduction of stiffness [20].

Viddhakarma and Agnikarma may work as a gate control therapy of pain management by stimulating descending pain inhibitory fibers causing the release of endogenous opioid peptide, which blocks the transmission of pain pathway. Viddhakarma is a modified Siravedha (bloodletting) procedure that helps to reduce pain by eliminating vitiated Doshas, Dushta Rakta i.e. accumulated toxic compounds, damaged cells, excessive calcium.

Conclusion

A combination of Viddhakarma, Agnikarma, and oral Ayurvedic Medicines can be used as an efficient treatment in managing pain and inflammation associated with Haglund's deformity. Viddhakarma and Agnikarma are simple, easy, and economical with minimum or no side effects treatment modalities that can be performed at the OPD level with basic facilities. However, this was a single case report, better scientific outcomes can be achieved by considering this therapy in a larger number of similar patients.

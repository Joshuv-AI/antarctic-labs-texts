Analysis of treatment-free survival of patients with advanced melanoma receiving nivolumab as monotherapy or in combination with relatlimab in RELATIVITY-047
Authors: Meredith M Regan, Paolo A Ascierto, Evan J Lipson, Jennell Palaia, Andriy Moshyk, Abraham Selvan, Christopher D Lao, Michael B Atkins, David F McDermott, Ravi Potluri, Sandip Ranjan, Sandeep Bilthare, Georgina V Long, F Stephen Hodi, Hussein Tawbi, Dirk Schadendorf
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTreatment-free survival (TFS; time spent free of systemic anticancer therapy) is increasingly used to support traditional endpoints. TFS was previously evaluated in patients with advanced melanoma treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab. This analysis compared TFS for nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab monotherapy in patients with advanced melanoma.MethodsData were from 714 patients in the phase 2/3 RELATIVITY-047 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03470922). TFS was defined as the difference in restricted mean event times between the Kaplan-Meier curves for time to protocol therapy cessation and time to subsequent systemic anticancer therapy initiation or death. TFS was further partitioned into time with and time without grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Subgroup analysis based on tumor BRAF mutational status and tumor programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression level was conducted. Between-treatment group differences were calculated with bootstrapped 95% CIs.ResultsAt 48 months from randomization, Kaplan-Meier estimates of overall survival were 52% and 43% for patients in the nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab groups, respectively; 38% and 33% of patients in these respective groups were free of subsequent systemic therapy. The 48-month mean TFS was 2.9 months (95% CI 1.0 to 4.9) longer with nivolumab plus relatlimab than with nivolumab (9.7 vs 6.8 months, respectively). Mean TFS represented 20% and 14% of the 48-month period after initiating nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab, respectively. Considering only time without grade ≥3 TRAEs, the 48-month mean TFS was 2.6 months (95% CI 0.8 to 4.5) longer with nivolumab plus relatlimab than with nivolumab (9.1 vs 6.5 months, respectively). The 48-month mean total TFS was consistently longer with nivolumab plus relatlimab than with nivolumab in the BRAF mutant (9.4 vs 6.5 months), BRAF wild-type (9.9 vs 6.9 months), PD-L1 ≥1% (12.3 vs 7.7 months), and PD-L1<1% (7.9 vs 6.2 months) subgroups.ConclusionsThis analysis demonstrated TFS benefit during the 48 months since initiating nivolumab plus relatlimab compared with nivolumab alone in patients with advanced melanoma. A direct comparison between nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab plus ipilimumab is needed to determine the differences between the regimens in TFS and those in traditional endpoints.

Introduction

Advances in the understanding of cancer immunology and molecular pathogenesis during the past decade have dramatically transformed the treatment landscape of melanoma, with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) representing key therapeutic options. These agents are recommended as first-line treatments for advanced melanoma, offering durable immune-mediated responses and unique patterns of antitumor activity. ICIs indicated for advanced melanoma include ipilimumab (a cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen inhibitor), nivolumab (a programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor) administered alone or in combination with ipilimumab, pembrolizumab (a PD-1 inhibitor), and the more recently developed relatlimab (a lymphocyte-activation gene 3 inhibitor) administered in combination with nivolumab.1 2

The ICI combination of nivolumab plus relatlimab has been approved for the treatment of adult and adolescent patients aged 12 years and older with advanced (unresectable or metastatic) melanoma by the US Food and Drug Administration3 and by the European Medicines Agency (first-line setting with a tumor programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) level <1%)4 based on results from the phase 2/3 RELATIVITY-047 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03470922).5 At 2-year and 3-year follow-ups, nivolumab plus relatlimab continued to offer benefits over nivolumab monotherapy in terms of the primary endpoint (progression-free survival (PFS)) and the secondary endpoints (overall survival (OS), objective response rate, and melanoma-specific survival (MSS)), with sustained improvements in OS and MSS.6 7 At a median follow-up of 33.8 months, median PFS was 10.2 months with nivolumab plus relatlimab and 4.6 months with nivolumab alone (HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.95)).8 Similarly, median OS was 51.0 months for the combination and 34.1 months for nivolumab alone (HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.99)).8 Although this OS benefit did not reach statistical significance, it was deemed to be clinically meaningful. PFS at 48 months was 30% and 22% with the combination and nivolumab alone, respectively; OS at 60 months was 49% and 39%, respectively.8

Although assessment of PFS and OS has historically guided regulatory approvals and clinical decisions, these conventional endpoints may not comprehensively assess long-term benefits associated with ICIs. Notably, some patients who discontinue ICI treatment continue to experience prolonged remission or durable disease control without the need for subsequent systemic anticancer therapy.9 An outcome measure that aptly characterizes these periods in which patients remain off therapy is treatment-free survival (TFS), which was introduced to evaluate another ICI combination—nivolumab plus ipilimumab.9 10 TFS is defined by the area between Kaplan-Meier curves for time to discontinuation of protocol therapy and time to initiation of subsequent anticancer therapy or death.9 10 TFS is part of a partitioned survival analysis in which the area under the OS curve is divided into the following three distinct survival states: time on ICI protocol therapy, TFS, and survival after initiation of subsequent systemic anticancer therapy.9 10 TFS provides a meaningful reflection of the durability of treatment benefit, capturing both the absence of ongoing maintenance therapy and the lack of immediate need for subsequent systemic anticancer therapy, while also accounting for the presence or absence of persistent adverse events (AEs) resulting from the initial therapy.10

These findings hold significant implications, as patient-centered endpoints like TFS offer valuable insights into patient experiences that are not fully captured by traditional clinical endpoints. The aim of this study was to evaluate and compare TFS for nivolumab plus relatlimab with nivolumab monotherapy using data from the RELATIVITY-047 trial.

Methods

The study was based on the analysis of data from RELATIVITY-047, a phase 2/3, global, double-blind, randomized trial involving 714 patients with previously untreated metastatic or unresectable melanoma, including 355 patients treated with nivolumab plus relatlimab and 359 patients treated with nivolumab monotherapy.5 Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) in the trial to receive either nivolumab 480 mg plus relatlimab 160 mg as a fixed-dose combination every 4 weeks (Q4W) or nivolumab 480 mg monotherapy Q4W. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity or AEs, or withdrawal of consent. There was no set date for cessation of treatment. Information on subsequent systemic anticancer therapy use was collected, except in cases of withdrawal of consent. AEs were assessed continuously throughout the trial and for at least 100 days after treatment was discontinued and were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, V.5.0.

The TFS analysis included all 714 randomized patients who initiated therapy in the trial. Although this analysis was based on the 3-year database lock (median follow-up, 33.8 months), the maximum follow-up was 64.2 months (due to the gated phase 2/3 design, which had an enrollment pause during the transition from phase 2 to phase 3). For this analysis, follow-up was restricted at 48 months, at which time there were approximately 30% of patients still in follow-up (117 in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group and 90 in the nivolumab group).

As previously described,9 the area under the OS curve was partitioned into the following three distinct survival states: time on ICI protocol therapy, TFS, and survival after subsequent systemic anticancer therapy initiation. This partitioning was based on the following two other time-to-event curves: time to protocol therapy cessation and time to subsequent systemic anticancer therapy initiation or death (figure 1). All endpoints were defined from the date of patient randomization and censored at the date last known to be event-free. Each health state was characterized as an area between Kaplan-Meier curves and estimated as differences between restricted mean event times (RMSTs), in months, of the time-to-event curves flanking the health state, during a maximum follow-up of 48 months. The RMST is a measure of average survival from time 0 to a specified time point and may be estimated as the area under the Kaplan-Meier curve up to that time point. RMST was also estimated during intermediate 12-month landmarks (ie, 12, 24, and 36 months).

TFS, treatment-free survival.

TFS was defined as the difference in RMST between the curves for time to protocol therapy cessation (figure 1, line A) and time to subsequent systemic anticancer therapy initiation or death (figure 1, line C). Differences in RMST between treatment groups were calculated along with bootstrapped 95% CIs for each survival state.

TFS was further partitioned into two regions representing the TFS periods with or without toxicity, to account for the fact that AEs with ICIs may persist beyond or emerge after treatment discontinuation.9 For this purpose, toxicity was defined as follows: grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) that occurred before the discontinuation of study drug or reported after the discontinuation of study drug but before the initiation of subsequent systemic anticancer therapy.11 12 Relevant AEs reported from randomization to the initiation of subsequent systemic anticancer therapy—including those newly reported after the end of protocol therapy—were considered in the analysis. The total number of unique days with one or more reported AEs during this period was calculated. For AEs that began during protocol therapy and persisted afterward, the full duration was attributed to the relevant period. If an AE remained unresolved at the start of subsequent systemic anticancer therapy, the duration was truncated at that point. AEs occurring on the same day were not double-counted, and the total number of unique days was aggregated. These durations were summarized and visually represented as contiguous days, although they may not have occurred consecutively.10 In addition, time on protocol therapy was also divided into two health states based on toxicity using a similar approach to the partitioning of the TFS period (figure 1).

Sensitivity analyses considering two alternate definitions of toxicity were also conducted for the following: grade ≥2 TRAEs and immune modulating medication (IMM) use (defined as duration of IMM use for any-grade TRAEs, excluding oral corticosteroids used as hormone replacement therapy for pituitary and adrenal insufficiency). TFS was further analyzed in subgroups by tumor BRAF mutational status (mutant (n=275) or wild-type (n=439)) and tumor programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression level (≥1% (n=293) or <1% (n=421)).

Results

Total TFS analysis

In RELATIVITY-047, treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity or AEs, or withdrawal of consent.5 At 48 months from randomization, Kaplan-Meier estimates of OS were 52% and 43% for patients in the nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab groups, respectively8; 38% and 33% of patients in these respective treatment arms were free of subsequent systemic anticancer therapy or death, while 13% and 11% of the total patients continued to receive protocol-assigned study medication at 48 months. Thus, 25% and 22% of patients in the nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab groups, respectively, were surviving treatment-free at 48 months.

Patients treated with nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab alone had a mean TFS during the 48-month period of 9.7 and 6.8 months, respectively (difference, 2.9 months (95% CI 1.0 to 4.9); table 1; online supplemental figure S1). Mean TFS represented 20% and 14% of the 48-month period after initiating nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab, respectively (figure 2). The longer mean TFS resulted from a longer mean time to subsequent anticancer systemic therapy in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group than in the nivolumab group (25.2 and 22.5 months, respectively; difference, 2.7 months (95% CI −0.2 to 5.6); online supplemental table S1) and a slightly shorter mean time to discontinuation at 48 months (15.4 and 15.7 months, respectively; difference, −0.2 months (95% CI −2.6 to 2.1)).

The mean TFS time with grade ≥3 TRAEs for the 48-month period for nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab alone was 0.6 and 0.3 months, respectively (difference, 0.3 months (95% CI −0.2 to 0.8)), representing 1% of the time period for each treatment group (figure 2; online supplemental table S1 and figure S1). The difference in the mean TFS between nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab increased as the follow-up duration increased, from 0.3 months (95% CI −0.1 to 0.7) during 12 months to 1.2 months (95% CI 0.4 to 2.1) during 24 months to 2.2 months (95% CI 0.9 to 3.6) during 36 months and to 2.9 months (95% CI 1.0 to 4.9) during 48 months (figure 3, online supplemental table S1 and figure S1).

The proportion of time spent alive and free of treatment increased with follow-up in both treatment groups. In the nivolumab plus relatlimab group, the proportion of time spent free of treatment increased from 12% during 12 months to 16% during 24 months to 19% during 36 months and to 20% during 48 months. In the nivolumab group, the corresponding increase in the proportion of time spent free of treatment increased from 9%–11% to 13%–14% (online supplemental table S1).

Sensitivity analysis of TFS using alternate definitions of toxicity

When defining toxicity based on grade ≥2 TRAEs (instead of grade ≥3 TRAEs used in the base case), patients treated with nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab spent 3.9 and 2.2 months of the 48-month period, respectively, in TFS with toxicity, and 5.9 and 4.6 months of the 48-month period, respectively, in TFS without toxicity (online supplemental table S2 and figure S2). When defining toxicity based on use of systemic or topical IMMs for any-grade TRAEs, patients treated with nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab alone spent 3.1 and 1.3 months of the 48-month period, respectively, in TFS with toxicity, and 6.7 and 5.5 months of the 48-month period, respectively, in TFS without toxicity (online supplemental table S3 and figure S2).

Comparison of treatment endpoints by treatment group according to tumor BRAF mutational status

The mean total TFS during 48 months with nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab alone was 9.4 months and 6.5 months, respectively (difference, 2.9 months (95% CI −0.1 to 5.9)) in the BRAF mutant subgroup, and 9.9 and 6.9 months, respectively (difference, 3.0 months (95% CI 0.6 to 5.4)) in the BRAF wild-type subgroup (table 2; online supplemental table S4 and figure S3). The mean incremental total TFS and TFS without toxicity with nivolumab plus relatlimab compared with nivolumab increased during time for both the BRAF mutant and wild-type subgroups (online supplemental table S4 and figure S3).

Discussion

This analysis of TFS, a patient-centered endpoint that characterizes the time spent free of systemic anticancer therapy,9 reinforces the clinical benefit of the ICI combination of nivolumab plus relatlimab in patients with advanced melanoma, as demonstrated in the RELATIVITY-047 trial.58 The statistically significant PFS and clinically meaningful OS benefit with nivolumab plus relatlimab compared with nivolumab monotherapy was complemented by longer TFS. On average, TFS was 2.9 months longer with nivolumab plus relatlimab than with nivolumab (9.7 vs 6.8 months, respectively), and patients in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group were treatment-free for 20% of the 4-year follow-up time since randomization, compared with 14% in the nivolumab group. Of note, mean time on protocol treatment was similar between the treatment groups (15.4 and 15.7 months for nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab, respectively) during the 48-month period. Thus, the observed TFS differences can be attributed mainly to the differences in time to subsequent systemic anticancer therapy initiation or death. TFS benefits observed in the combination group persisted during the entire 48-month follow-up period, demonstrating sustained benefits in treatment outcomes and highlighting the potential for improved quality of life in patients with advanced melanoma with nivolumab plus relatlimab.

It was also encouraging to note that the quality of TFS as represented by TFS without toxicity was consistently higher in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group than in the nivolumab group, regardless of the definition of toxicity. The mean TFS with nivolumab plus relatlimab was almost entirely devoid of high-grade toxicity because the incremental mean TFS without grade ≥3 TRAEs was 2.6 months greater in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group than in the nivolumab group (9.1 vs 6.5 months), representing 19% and 14% of the 48-month period, respectively, similar to results for the total TFS. Further, as the vast majority of TFS was spent free of severe toxicity (as defined by grade ≥3 TRAEs), it can be reasoned that toxicities resolved quickly. Notably, the incremental TFS without toxicity with nivolumab plus relatlimab consistently increased as the follow-up increased, highlighting sustained benefits of nivolumab plus relatlimab during longer follow-up.

TFS with toxicity was also longer in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group than in the nivolumab group. Nivolumab plus relatlimab is associated with more toxicity than nivolumab monotherapy,8 and increased toxicity for the combination compared with nivolumab alone may lead to longer total TFS and TFS with toxicity. These results are consistent with those from the phase 3 CheckMate 067 trial in which patients with advanced melanoma receiving combination nivolumab plus ipilimumab demonstrated longer total TFS and TFS with toxicity than those receiving nivolumab alone.10

TFS benefit consistently favored nivolumab plus relatlimab over nivolumab across subgroups defined by tumor BRAF mutational status and tumor PD-L1 expression level, aligning with the overall clinical efficacy results of RELATIVITY-047.8 However, the mean TFS and the incremental TFS gain with nivolumab plus relatlimab was more pronounced in patients with tumor PD-L1 expression ≥1%, suggesting that this subgroup may derive more sustained benefit from the combination therapy. This finding was unexpected because PFS benefit with nivolumab plus relatlimab was less pronounced with tumor PD-L1 expression ≥1% than with PD-L1 <1% in RELATIVITY-047.8

These findings align with prior analyses evaluating TFS in patients with advanced melanoma in the CheckMate 067 trial and the phase 2 CheckMate 069 trial who received nivolumab plus ipilimumab,9 10 underscoring the consistency of this outcome across different ICI regimens. These trials showed that the restricted mean TFS was consistently longer with combination ICI therapy than with nivolumab monotherapy, even among patient subgroups according to lactate dehydrogenase level, tumor PD-L1 expression, tumor BRAF mutational status, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, and sex.13 Furthermore, in both the RELATIVITY-047 and CheckMate 067 trials, an evolving plateau in time to subsequent therapy initiation or death was noted after 36 months, which represented the upper bound of the TFS time. However, the difference in mean TFS between nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab increased from 2.2 months at 36 months to 2.9 months at 48 months in RELATIVITY-047, whereas the difference in mean TFS between nivolumab plus ipilimumab and nivolumab increased from 6.1 to 7.9 months for the same period in CheckMate 067.10 The shorter TFS results with nivolumab plus relatlimab compared with nivolumab plus ipilimumab were not unexpected, as there were variations in the patterns of therapy cessation between the two trials over time.10 14 This finding may likely be related to a lower frequency of treatment discontinuation due to toxicity in patients receiving nivolumab plus relatlimab, which allowed patients to remain on therapy longer. In addition, patients experiencing treatment-related toxicity were not allowed to resume maintenance nivolumab treatment in CheckMate 067. Although disease progression was the main reason for stopping protocol therapy in both trials, there were considerable differences in the proportion of patients discontinuing treatment because of toxicity. Study drug toxicity resulted in treatment discontinuation in 18% of patients in the nivolumab plus relatlimab group in RELATIVITY-047 (minimum follow-up, 33 months),8 compared with 39% of patients in the nivolumab plus ipilimumab group in CheckMate 067 (minimum follow-up, 36 months),15 although discontinuation rates due to toxicity may be lower in more recent studies given that CheckMate 067 was the first phase 3 trial with this combination and the study investigators were less experienced with managing toxicity. It is also possible that the longer TFS with nivolumab plus ipilimumab compared with nivolumab plus relatlimab was related to a difference in treatment response, although the two regimens appeared to have similar efficacy in an indirect treatment comparison involving patient-level data from RELATIVITY-047 and CheckMate 067.16 In this context, it is also noteworthy to highlight that TFS data for nivolumab were consistent and reproducible between RELATIVITY-047 and CheckMate 067 at 48 weeks (6.8 and 7.1 months, respectively).10 Cross-trial comparison of TFS for nivolumab plus relatlimab from RELATIVITY-047 with TFS for nivolumab plus ipilimumab from CheckMate 067 should be made with caution given the different toxicity profiles for these regimens and the varying therapy rules within the respective protocols. To determine the differences between the regimens in TFS and those in traditional endpoints, a direct comparison is needed.

A notable strength of this study is its reliance on well-established, widely recommended methods developed to assess quality-adjusted survival,1719 which is an important burden in oncology. Additionally, the analysis included all patients rather than being limited to a subset who discontinued treatment, ensuring a more comprehensive evaluation from the point of therapy initiation. By quantifying the areas under the Kaplan-Meier curves and using RMST for TFS estimation to compare ICI treatments, the study provides a better understanding of time-to-event endpoint distributions for these regimens than the median or milestone estimates of traditional endpoints.

The study had certain limitations. The resolution of some TRAEs may not have been fully captured, potentially leading to an overestimation of TFS with toxicity. Future clinical trials should prioritize the systematic collection of data for AEs, toxicity resolution, and health-related quality of life beyond the discontinuation of index treatment. Moreover, careful consideration of treatment duration, tolerability, and efficacy will be essential in determining the optimal timing for TFS analyses.

In summary, this analysis using data from the RELATIVITY-047 trial58 demonstrated a sustained benefit in overall TFS and TFS without toxicity with nivolumab plus relatlimab compared with nivolumab alone in patients with advanced melanoma during 48 months. TFS without toxicity was longer with nivolumab plus relatlimab than with nivolumab alone regardless of how toxicity was defined, reflecting an improved balance between efficacy and tolerability, while offering patients a longer duration of quality of life free from treatment-related complications. This partitioned survival analysis may help in treatment decision-making by providing further insight into how patients with advanced melanoma initiating nivolumab plus relatlimab or nivolumab monotherapy spend survival time, on and off initial therapy as well as with and without the toxicity of treatment. TFS may detect additional efficacy benefits not captured by traditional endpoints that may be of value in planning trials that seek to optimize ICI combination therapy and determine when to cease treatment after a clinical milestone. Furthermore, TFS as an outcome measure captures durable treatment benefits and potential quality-of-life improvements, supporting its inclusion in future ICI trials. Clinical trials may be strengthened by the use of TFS,9 10 14 in addition to the use of essential biomarkers for immunotherapy.20 A direct comparison between nivolumab plus relatlimab and nivolumab plus ipilimumab is needed to determine the differences between the regimens in TFS and those in traditional endpoints.

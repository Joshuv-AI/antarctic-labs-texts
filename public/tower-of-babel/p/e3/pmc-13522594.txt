Safe causal-graph primal-dual multi-agent scheduling for energy- and latency-constrained edge-assisted cognitive radio networks
Authors: T. Kannan, M. Lavanya, A. Ponraj, C. M. Nalayini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Edge-assisted cognitive radio networks require efficient scheduling mechanisms to jointly manage opportunistic spectrum access, task offloading, energy consumption, and latency constraints. Existing multi-agent scheduling approaches often rely on fixed penalty terms or average queue-based constraints, which may not effectively control service-level violations under uncertain spectrum availability and dynamic edge-resource contention. This work proposes SCOPE, a Safe Causal-Graph Primal–Dual multi-agent scheduling framework for energy- and latency-constrained edge-assisted cognitive radio networks. The major strength of SCOPE is its integrated design, where belief-state augmentation improves decision-making under imperfect spectrum sensing, dual-relational causal graph coordination separately models’ interference coupling and computation-resource contention, and CVaR-based primal–dual optimization regulates tail-risk violations of latency and energy constraints. The framework follows a centralized-training and decentralized-execution structure, enabling coordinated learning during training while supporting scalable decentralized scheduling during deployment. Simulation results under dynamic user mobility, stochastic task arrivals, and varying primary-user activity show that SCOPE improves latency, energy efficiency, service-level constraint satisfaction, and throughput compared with existing scheduling methods. Ablation analysis further confirms the individual contribution of belief modeling, graph coordination, and risk-sensitive constraint enforcement.

Introduction

The development of wireless networks to edge-integrated networks has created a form of new scheduling problem whereby opportunistic spectrum access, and computation offloading need to be optimized together under tight quality-of-service demands. The rapid growth of wireless and edge-integrated networks has intensified the demand for efficient spectrum utilization, adaptive resource allocation, and delay-aware computation scheduling. Cognitive radio networks (CRNs) have emerged as an effective solution for spectrum scarcity by allowing secondary users to opportunistically access underutilized licensed spectrum without causing harmful interference to primary users.

Recent advances in cognitive radio networks have significantly improved dynamic spectrum utilization through intelligent spectrum sensing and access mechanisms. Tan et al.1 formulated distributed dynamic spectrum access as a cooperative multi-agent learning problem and introduced a centralized-training and distributed-execution framework to improve spectrum utilization while reducing coordination overhead among cognitive users. Liu et al.2 investigated dynamic multichannel sensing strategies and demonstrated that reinforcement-learning-based sensing can effectively improve spectrum access opportunities under uncertain channel conditions. Keshavamurthy and Michelusi3 developed a learning-based spectrum sensing and access framework using partially observable Markov decision processes, enabling cognitive users to exploit temporal spectrum occupancy correlations and improve access reliability under sensing uncertainty.

More recently, deep reinforcement learning approaches have been employed to jointly optimize spectrum access and resource allocation decisions, allowing secondary users to adapt their transmission strategies according to changing wireless environments4. These studies have substantially advanced spectrum sensing, dynamic spectrum access, and distributed spectrum coordination. Nevertheless, existing approaches primarily concentrate on communication-layer spectrum management and rarely consider the joint interaction among spectrum uncertainty, computation offloading, edge-resource contention, and latency-energy reliability requirements. This motivates the development of the proposed SCOPE framework, which integrates belief-aware spectrum modeling, graph-based coordination, and risk-sensitive scheduling within a unified edge-assisted cognitive radio architecture.

The cognitive radio technology allows secondary devices to use spectral gaps in between primary-user transmissions to enhance the spectral use without necessarily licensing the spectrum. In cases where the secondary users also have to perform computation tasks that can be computed either locally or offloaded to shared edge servers, the scheduling problem is a closely coupled optimization between channel selection, transmission power, offloading fraction, and server allocation5. This is a multi-dimensional decision problem of fundamental interaction of each variable with the other in terms of interference relationships, server contention, and energy consumption.

This difficulty is exacerbated by two structural sources of uncertainty that the current approaches help address partially. Spectrum sensing can only give channel occupancy estimates which are probabilistic, instantaneous binary sensing outputs are susceptible to false alarms and false detections and can lead to interference with primary users or unnecessarily leaving available spectrum unused. Moreover, these networks are heterogeneous in terms of inter-device relationships which are physically organized. Interference between secondary users in the same frequency bands and competition due to limited processing ability are experienced between users sharing the same edge server. These two kinds of coupling have different physical basis, time dynamics, and effects on scheduling decision and must be represented differently, not lumped together in one representation of dependency.

Current approaches deal with sections of these problems but have critical gaps. The queue-stabilization techniques offer long-run average guarantees that are not able to constrain the possibility of single scheduling-slot constraint leakage6. The variable of multi-agent reinforcement learners normally assumes constraints by using fixed reward penalty coefficients that are unable to respond to the observed violation rates and they offer no tail-risk guarantees7. Coordination of wireless resources allocation with graph-neural networks has made use, predominantly, of homogeneous, single-relation graphs that combine interference and computational coupling on a single type of edge, at the expense of more relation-specific modeling8. The rule-based and federated scheduling are equally introduced to present efficiency of communication or interpretability, respectively, but neither possess principled mechanisms to enforce probabilistic service guarantees9,10.

The paper closes these gaps by considering SCOPE which is a Safe Causal-Graph Primal-Dual multi-agent scheduling model that jointly reasons about spectrum uncertainty, heterogeneous inter-agent coupling and tail-risk constraint enforcement. The framework combines belief-state augmentation based on sensing history, dual-relational causal graph between interference and computation contention as two distinct types of relations, and conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) constrained primal-dual optimization scheme in a centralized-training decentralized-execution (CTDE) architecture. Combining these aspects produces a scheduling policy that is well-posed to spectrum uncertainty, stochastic to structured patterns of coupling, and that is regulated in terms of tailing the violation of latency and energy limits11–13.

The main technical challenges addressed in this work are: imperfect primary-user spectrum sensing, coupled spectrum-computation scheduling, heterogeneous interaction among secondary users, latency-energy service-level violations, and scalable multi-agent coordination. Related work

A much more frequent solution to spectrum scarcity has involved the cognitive radio network, which allows secondary users to take advantage of temporal and spatial spectrum holes generated by primary users in order to enhance the overall spectrum efficiency. Cosensing, where the sensor reports of multiple secondary users have been summed, has been demonstrated to lower both the false-alarm and missed-detection rates in comparison with a single sensing, and has thus been shown to increase the accuracy of channel availability estimates. Spectrum-sharing control and interference management have been developed as optimization and deep learning problems and considerable progress has been made in minimizing the interference to primary receivers and maximizing secondary throughput20. Deep reinforcement learning of dynamical spectrum access has the capability of adaptive policies responding to time-varying primary user traffic dynamics without explicit information about the occupancy model21. Belief tracking in channel occupancy models with Bayesian filters and hidden Markov models of channel occupancy dynamics models temporal correlation in primary-user activity, which is more accurate compared to memoryless counterparts. In spite of this development, most of the spectrum access techniques fail to handle concurrently the edge computation resource management, thus the coupled scheduling problem remains unsolved fully.

Recent advances in cognitive radio and spectrum-sharing networks have focused on improving spectrum utilization, information freshness, and communication efficiency under dynamic wireless environments. The investigation on Age of Information (AoI) minimization in ambient backscatter-assisted energy harvesting is presented in22 for cognitive radio networks through cooperative spectrum sensing and a hybrid action-energy policy based on deep reinforcement learning. Their framework improves information freshness by jointly optimizing sensing, energy allocation, and spectrum access decisions under uncertain channel conditions. Further in23 throughput maximization in cell-free symbiotic radio networks is presented using a hybrid conventional symbiotic radio and parasitic symbiotic radio (CSR-PSR) architecture. The proposed optimization framework jointly determines time allocation, beamforming, and reflection coefficients to improve backscatter communication throughput. Further throughput maximization in STAR-RIS-aided symbiotic radio networks is analyzed in24 by optimizing active beamforming, passive beamforming, user pairing, and decoding strategies under spectrum-sharing constraints. A distributed DDPG-based resource allocation framework is presented in25 for wireless-powered IoT networks to minimize AoI through joint optimization of transmission duration, power allocation, and communication-node selection. Although these studies significantly improve spectrum access, information freshness, throughput optimization, and wireless resource management, they mainly focus on communication-layer optimization problems. In contrast, the proposed SCOPE framework jointly addresses spectrum uncertainty, computation offloading, edge-server contention, heterogeneous inter-user interactions, and latency-energy reliability constraints through belief-state modeling, dual-relational graph coordination, and CVaR-based safe multi-agent scheduling.

Mobile edge computing spreads the calculation power to the network edges, significantly decreasing the time delay in completing tasks which are perhaps delay sensitive5. Task offloading formulations are the formulations of offloading in the form of a continuous variable that is optimized together with transmission power and server placement and deep reinforcement learning can show substantial latency savings in the presence of dynamic task arrival and time-varying channel26,27. Multi-objective offloading models explicitly balance delay vs. energy consumption because it has been acknowledged that maximizing either of the two can be associated with unacceptable costs28. Simultaneously optimizing radio and computation resource allocation in heterogeneous edge network under delay constraints shows that this problem is significantly improved when optimized simultaneously on two dimensions compared to sequential or decoupled methods29. The constrained management of energy efficient resource under interference constraints has been developed as a constrained optimization problem but the results have been restricted to the assumption of the static channels. The Spectrum access uncertainty provided by computation offloading in cognitive radio settings is not provided in standard MEC formulations. Multi-user environment would present server contention between co-located users, which is an incentive to the multi-agent formulation and the construction of the contention graph adopted in SCOPE.

Multi-agent reinforcement learning has been used in numerous problems in wireless resource allocation such as channel access, power control and joint scheduling30. The independent agent schemes are affected by non-stationarity due to simultaneous updating of peer policies7. CTDE architectures alleviate non-stationarity with the help of a common global critic in training and limited execution-time policies to locally accessible information, which significantly enhances the quality of coordination. Actor-critic models which use Lyapunov-guided virtual queue constraints are able to stabilize time-averaged cost measures in edge-assisted cognitive radio models but have no ability to bound the probability of constraint violations per-episode. The formulations of deep Q-network coordinate scheduling by using fixed terms of penalty rewards, which do not adjust to changes in the violation rates. Value function Value function factorization methods break the joint value estimation into monotonic components, which are assigned per-agent, allowing one to do credit assignment in a scalable way. Multi-agent frameworks that are communication-efficient create overhead reduction by gating messages selectively which is important in environments with limited bandwidth31. SCOPE extends the CTDE architectures and factorized critics and introduces dual-relational graph coordination and CVaR constraints enforcement which overcome the limitations that remain in the existing literature.

GNNs offer a natural database of encoding the structure of spatial interaction into the policies of wireless resource allocation, and it has been demonstrated that GNNs achieve better spectrum reuse than fully connected and independent ones. The attention-based graph aggregation enables the selective weighting of the neighboring information by the agents according to the strength of interaction to enhance the coordination efficiency of heterogeneous user populations32. Knowledge representation and recommendation systems have been analyzed using heterogeneous GNN architectures, which utilize multiple types of edges, which leads to applying the same architecture to wireless networks where interaction types are physically different. The multi-relational GNN wireless channel assignment models have shown that type-specific aggregation does not blur the structure and does not result in excessive over-smoothing that happens when the qualitatively different types of coupling are combined under a common relation33. SCOPE generalizes the joint edge-computation and spectrum-access scheduling problem, building distinct dynamic graphs of each data point of interest of the interference coupling and computation contention at the individual time steps, and combining the two representations by independent aggregation.

Safety constraints are explicit optimization goals in constrained Markov decision process formulations, which use safety constraints as an alternative to unconstrained reward shaping, and offers a theoretically-based alternative to unconstrained reward shaping. Lagrangian relaxation and primal-dual approaches to CMDPs are used to optimize the policy parameters in an iterative way to minimize the cost with the dual variables punishing the violations and convergence being ensured by convexity. Through these mechanisms, safe reinforcement learning has been proved successful in robotic control, autonomous driving and network resource control, and it is now more reliable than penalty-based baselines34. The CVaR as a risk metric has an immediate relationship to the expected cost on the worst case outcome portion, which makes it more suitable than expectation-based constraints in cases where tail risks are disastrous. Markov decision process CVaR optimization has been solved using gradient-based approaches to estimate the CVaR using sample means and use it to form part of the policy gradient objective. Distributional reinforcement learning builds on the concept of tail-risk management by using more information to characterize the worst-case outcomes, as it models the entire distribution of returns35. The addition of CVaR constraints to primal-dual MARL has not been studied previously in wireless scheduling; currently constrained MARL based formulations rely on expectation-based surrogates which do not constrain extreme violation events. SCOPE bridges this gap by including CVaR-based dual variable updates within the CTDE training loop.

The proper Occupancy estimation of primary-user channel is the key to safe and efficient cognitive radio operation. The Bayesian filtering methods carry a probabilistic belief about the occupancy state of the channel, and they update the belief with likelihood ratios obtained based on the sensing readings to offer a sensible way of dealing with uncertainty over time. Channel occupancy estimation through hidden Markov model captures temporal correlation of PU activity and is better at prediction than the memoryless models36. Sensing cooperatives combine distributed sensing reports of several secondary users in order to enhance detection reliability in the face of shadowing and fading. It has been demonstrated that belief-state representations outperform conventional reinforcement learning state spaces by a significant margin in terms of policy robustness in imperfect sensing in single-user environments, which led to the belief-augmented design in SCOPE. Belief-state modeling of multi-agent coordination and risk-sensitive constraint enforcement of edge-assisted cognitive radio setting has not been considered.

Optimization objective and reliability constraints

The instantaneous per-user scheduling cost weighs latency and energy consumption according to operator-specified coefficients:14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{C}_{i}\left(t\right)=\alpha\:{D}_{i}\left(t\right)+\beta\:{E}_{i}\left(t\right)$$\end{document}

where α, β ≥ 0 are tunable weights satisfying \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:\:+\:\beta\:\:=\:1$$\end{document}. The primary scheduling objective is to find a joint decentralized policy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pi\:={{\pi\:}_{i}}_{i=1}^{N}$$\end{document} that minimizes the discounted expected cumulative cost:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:mi{n}_{\pi\:}{E}_{\pi\:}\left[{\varSigma\:}_{t=0}^{\infty\:}{\gamma\:}^{t}\left(1/N\right){\varSigma\:}_{i=1}^{N}{C}_{i}\left(t\right)\right]$$\end{document}

where γ ∈ (0,1) is the discount factor. Nonetheless, expectation minimization does not suffice to the service level assurance: an average cost policy can have catastrophic individual deadline failures. Hard service-level requirements are thus stated to be probabilistic constraints:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:P\left({D}_{i}\left(t\right)>{d}_{i}^{max}\left(t\right)\right)\le\:{\epsilon\:}_{D}$$\end{document}17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:P\left({E}_{i}\left(t\right)>{E}_{i}^{max}\right)\le\:{\epsilon\:}_{E}$$\end{document}

The chance constraints (16)-(17) are directly incomputable in sequential decision-making problems. Rather, these limitations are re-formulated based on CVaR, a coherent risk measure14. and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Va{R}_{\rho\:}\left(X\right)=infx:P\left(X>x\right)\le\:1-\rho\:$$\end{document} is the Value-at-Risk at 0 level. CVaR 0 (X) = -1 (X) is the expectation of X in the worst - portion of its realizations, offers a much narrower and more manageable bound than the probability constraint on its own. The reformulation of the reliability conditions (16)-(17) is in the form:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:CVa{R}_{\rho\:}\left({D}_{i}\left(t\right)\right)\le\:{d}_{i}^{m}ax\left(t\right)$$\end{document}20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:CVa{R}_{\rho\:}\left({E}_{i}\left(t\right)\right)\le\:{E}_{i}^{m}ax$$\end{document}

The full constrained scheduling problem is: optimization (15) under the server capacity constraint (1), the PU interference constraint (6), the power budget constraint, and the CVaR reliability constraints (19)-(20) about the entire set of decentralized policies π operating under spectrum uncertainty in the form of the belief states in Eqs. (3)-(4).

From the above formulation, it can be observed that the considered scheduling problem is a highly coupled and constrained multi-agent optimization problem. The channel selection decision directly affects spectrum availability and interference to primary users, while transmission power influences both achievable data rate and energy consumption. Similarly, the offloading ratio and edge-server selection jointly determine local computation delay, transmission delay, edge-execution delay, and server-resource contention. In addition, imperfect spectrum sensing introduces uncertainty in channel availability, and stochastic task arrivals and user mobility make the scheduling environment time-varying. Therefore, conventional independent scheduling, fixed-penalty reinforcement learning, or average queue-based methods may not be sufficient to ensure reliable latency and energy constraint satisfaction. This analysis motivates the proposed SCOPE framework, which is designed to jointly address spectrum uncertainty, heterogeneous inter-user coupling, and tail-risk-aware latency-energy constraint enforcement.

The SCOPE framework

SCOPE unites three coordinated mechanisms in one multi-agent scheduling framework belief-augmented state modeling, dual-relational causal graph coordination, and CVaR primal-dual constraint enforcement. The system architecture as a whole is depicted in Fig. 2 which depicts the flow of information of the cognitive radio environment to all the modules to the final decentralized scheduling policy.

Fig. 2Overall SCOPE system architecture: information flow across all framework components.

Overall SCOPE system architecture: information flow across all framework components.

Figure 2 illustrates the overall information flow of the proposed SCOPE framework. The cognitive radio environment provides sensing observations, task arrivals, channel conditions, energy status, and edge-server resource information. These inputs are converted into belief-augmented state representations to handle uncertainty in primary-user channel occupancy. The dual-relational causal graph module then separately models two types of interactions among secondary users: interference coupling caused by shared spectrum access and computation contention caused by shared edge-server resources. Relation-specific attention aggregation combines these interaction features and generates coordination-aware embeddings for each agent. Based on these embeddings, the CVaR primal–dual optimization module updates the scheduling policy while controlling latency and energy violation risks. Finally, under the CTDE structure, the trained policy enables each secondary user to make decentralized scheduling decisions such as channel selection, server allocation, offloading ratio, transmission power, and CPU frequency allocation.

SCOPE algorithm

Algorithm 1 presents the complete SCOPE learning procedure. The outer loop iterates over training episodes; the inner loop steps through \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{T}_{slot}$$\end{document} decision steps per episode. At each step, belief states are updated from new sensing observations, both relational graphs are reconstructed, relation-specific attention aggregation produces fused embeddings, decentralized actions are sampled, environment outcomes are observed, CVaR constraint surrogates are estimated, and parameter updates are performed for the critic, policies, and dual variables. The algorithm terminates after \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{T}_{ep}$$\end{document} episodes and returns the learned decentralized policies.

Algorithm 1SCOPE learning procedure.

SCOPE learning procedure.

The main idea of the proposed SCOPE algorithm is to learn a safe and coordinated multi-agent scheduling policy for edge-assisted cognitive radio networks under uncertain spectrum availability and strict latency-energy constraints. At each time slot, every secondary user first updates the channel occupancy belief using sensing observations and Bayesian filtering. Then, two dynamic relational graphs are constructed to separately represent interference coupling among co-channel users and computation-resource contention among users sharing the same edge server. Relation-specific attention aggregation is used to extract coordination-aware embeddings from these two graphs. Based on these embeddings, each agent selects its scheduling action, including channel selection, edge-server association, offloading ratio, transmission power, and CPU frequency. The critic network is trained using the augmented reward, while the dual variables are updated according to CVaR-based latency and energy constraint violations.

The major strength of the proposed algorithm is that it jointly addresses three important challenges in edge-assisted CRNs. First, the belief-state update improves scheduling reliability under imperfect spectrum sensing by using historical sensing information instead of relying only on instantaneous observations. Second, the dual-relational graph structure avoids mixing different physical interactions by separately modeling wireless interference and edge-server contention. Third, the CVaR-based primal–dual update provides risk-sensitive constraint regulation, which helps reduce extreme latency and energy violations instead of only optimizing average performance. In addition, the centralized-training decentralized-execution structure supports coordinated learning during training while allowing each user to make local scheduling decisions during deployment.

The computational complexity of SCOPE mainly depends on belief updating, graph construction, relation-specific attention aggregation, and neural-network parameter updates. Simulation setup

Environment and terminology

All experiments are conducted in a custom Python simulation implementing the channel, computation, and energy models defined in Section III. The x-axis for all training convergence and metric evolution plots is the training episode, where each episode consists of 200 sequential decision steps (time slots). This is the standard unit for episodic MARL convergence reporting: at the end of each episode the environment resets, belief states are re-initialized, and user positions are redrawn according to the mobility model. Reported metrics evaluated during training are episode averages, and final performance metrics are averages over 200 held-out evaluation episodes after training completes. The use of episodes rather than individual decision steps or parameter update iterations as the x-axis provides a physically meaningful measure of the number of independent environment interactions completed. The baseline methods were selected to represent different classes of scheduling strategies in edge-assisted cognitive radio networks. The greedy baseline was included as a non-learning reference method to indicate the performance limitation of instantaneous channel/server selection without long-term learning or constraint control. DQN-MA represents fixed-penalty multi-agent reinforcement learning, while LyDROO represents Lyapunov-guided constrained offloading and scheduling. REGNN was selected as a graph-based resource coordination baseline because it uses graph neural-network-based interaction modeling, but it relies on a homogeneous graph structure and does not separately model interference and computation contention. FedAvg-Sched was included to represent distributed/federated scheduling with periodic policy aggregation. Therefore, the comparison covers heuristic, reinforcement learning, constrained learning, graph-based learning, and distributed learning schemes.

Although some methods such as greedy scheduling are less competitive, they are useful for establishing a lower-bound reference and for showing the benefit of adaptive learning. Similarly, REGNN is retained not as a weak baseline, but as a relevant graph-based comparison method that helps isolate the advantage of the proposed dual-relational graph coordination. All comparison schemes were evaluated under identical network size, channel setting, mobility model, task-arrival distribution, energy budget, latency deadline, training episodes, and evaluation episodes. Hence, the reported improvements of SCOPE reflect its ability to jointly handle spectrum uncertainty, heterogeneous inter-user coupling, and risk-sensitive latency-energy constraint regulation under the same experimental conditions.

Evaluation metrics

Performance is reported in 6 main metrics: average task latency in seconds; average energy use in joules; latency violation of SLA as a percentage of tasks above d i max; energy violation of SLA as a percentage of tasks above E i max; average system throughput in Mbps; and cumulative discounted reward. Secondary metrics are per training episode metric evolution, dual variable curves, task offload ratio distribution, edge server usage and inference time per decision step.

Experimental results and analysis

SCOPE standalone performance characterization

This sub section completely describes the behavior of SCOPE in its own right then comparative analysis is brought in. It is analyzed in terms of training convergence, development of per-episode metrics, dual variable dynamics, learned scheduling behavior and energy-latency trade-off surface.

Training convergence

Figure 8 shows the average cumulative reward of the methods used by all the methods against the training episode. SCOPE attains 95% of its asymptotic reward at episode 820, before any of the bases. The accelerated convergence is due to two compounding factors: the graph-coordinated embedding gives the agent more gradient information per episode by embedding the structured neighbor information into the policy update of each agent and the CVaR dual variable algorithm eliminates the oscillatory reward fluctuations of a fixed-coefficient model with a sudden change of constraint penalty. SCOPE has the smallest reward variance across the five training seeds which validates the idea that belief-state augmentation lowers the unproductive exploration of highly occupied channels.

Figure 9 shows three training performance metrics in the 2,000 training episodes, in between every 100-episode step. Each of the data points will be the mean metric value of the 200 decision steps in that episode. Task latency (Fig. 9a) decreases exponentially between 0.741 s episode 100 and 0.318 s episode 2,000 with the steepest fall between episode 200 and 800 as the policy quickly finds efficient combinations of channel and server. Dual variable dynamics

Figure 10 presents the plot of the dual variables of latency and energy λD and 2E over the training episodes. The two variables increase in the initial 700 episodes when the policy searches through suboptimal areas and violates the constraints with a high frequency before leveling and decreasing gradually towards the near-feasibility solution. The presence of non-zero steady-state values finalizes that both CVaR constraints are bound at convergence not corner values and that SCOPE is operating at the efficient boundary of the feasibility region as opposed to meeting constraints with unwarranted wigle. The energy dual stabilizes slightly below the latency dual, as is also observed with energy budgets which are somewhat easier to meet with over the available power control range.

Conclusion

This paper presented SCOPE, a Safe Causal-Graph Primal–Dual multi-agent scheduling framework for edge-assisted cognitive radio networks addressing the coupled challenges of spectrum uncertainty, heterogeneous inter-agent coupling, and probabilistic service constraint enforcement. Three integrated design elements constitute the framework: belief-state augmentation derived from Bayesian filtering on sensing history for uncertainty-aware channel modeling; a dual-relational causal graph distinguishing interference coupling from computation contention as separate dynamic graph types with relation-specific attention aggregation; and a CVaR-based primal–dual optimization mechanism that enforces tail-risk latency and energy constraints through adaptive dual variables. A CTDE architecture with factorized critics supports scalable training while enabling fully decentralized deployment without inter-agent communication channels.

Experiments across 2,000 training episodes under dynamic mobility, stochastic task arrivals, and variable primary-user activity yield consistent advantages over six competitive baselines. Average task latency is reduced by up to 45.3% and energy consumption by up to 48.2% compared to evaluated methods. SLA violation rates are lowered by 51.7–56.5% relative to the strongest learning-based competitor. The results of ablation support that belief augmentation, dual-relational graph building, and CVaR enforcement give independent and non-redundant performance improvements. Scalability analysis demonstrates sub-quadratic growth inference time and robustness experiments demonstrate that there is retained performance advantages in high-mobility and high uncertainty operating conditions.

Future research will involve the extension of the belief model to include spatial correlations between PU activities when using multiple channels, the incorporation of workload arrival prediction to make proactive schedules, formal guarantees of convergence in CVaR primal dual updates in non-convex multi-agent systems, and the generalization of the model to multi-tier heterogeneous edge architecture. Software-defined radio testbeds would also be an experimental validation of the practicability of the proposed scheduling strategy.

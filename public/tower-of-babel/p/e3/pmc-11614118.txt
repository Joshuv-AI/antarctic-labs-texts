Impact of prosthodontic rehabilitation on psychological status and quality of life in maxillectomy patients of coronavirus disease 2019-associated mucormycosis at a tertiary care center: A prospective clinical study
Authors: P. Vijayabharathi, Surabhi Rambhau Somkuwar, Santhosh Rao, Virat Galhotra, Uvashri Selvaraj
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aims:The study was aimed to evaluate the effect of prosthodontic rehabilitation on psychological status (PS) and quality of life (QoL) in maxillectomy patients of coronavirus disease 2019-associated mucormycosis (CAM).Settings and Design:The study was designed as a prospective and clinical study.Materials and Methods:Twenty-four CAM patients undergoing maxillectomy and subsequent prosthetic rehabilitation were included. The treatment involved surgical, intermediate, and definitive obturator delivery at various phases of healing. PS was assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), QOL using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer QoL Questionnaire-Head and Neck Module (EORTC QLQ-HandN35), and the functioning of obturator was assessed utilising the Obturator Functioning Scale (OFS) at various time points: before maxillectomy (T1), 2 weeks after maxillectomy (T2), 2 weeks upon usage of intermediate obturator (T3), just before delivery of definitive obturator (T4), and 12 weeks after the usage of definitive obturator (T5).Statistical Analysis Used:Data were analyzed using a social science statistical analysis program (Stata 14.0; StataCorp LLC, Texas, USA). The significance level was chosen <0.05. Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of quantitative data. Student’s t-test was employed (α =0.05) to evaluate if the patient’s PS, QOL, and obturator function had changed over time. The Spearman correlation coefficient (α =0.05) was utilized to evaluate the correlation coefficient between PS, QOL, and obturator function seen at T3 and T5.Results:Out of 24 enrolled patients, nine were dropped out. HADS-anxiety score was the highest at T1 (18.6 ± 1.2) and HADS-depression score was the highest at T2 (18.8 ± 1.6). HADS scores decreased significantly (P = 0.001) after prosthetic rehabilitation. EORTC QLQ-HandN35 score was the highest at T2 (105.8 ± 29.5), with statistical significance (P = 0.001) after rehabilitation. OFS was the highest at T3 (51.9 ± 3.9) and lowest at T5 (25.4 ± 2.8).Conclusion:CAM and maxillectomy deliberately affects the PS and QOL. Prosthetic rehabilitation, psychological motivation, and education create greater improvement in PS and QOL. The progress in parameters showed an upward trend with good obturator quality over a period of time.

INTRODUCTION

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), a devastating pandemic, stunned the entire world by causing various severe illnesses in millions and millions of population. In this particular context, mucormycosis has become more common among COVID-19 patients, particularly in low- and middle-income nations, and it carries a high-mortality rate of 50%–90%.[12] Prior to the pandemic, the worldwide infection rate varied between 0.005 and 1.7 per million people. Following the pandemic, India experienced an incidence rate of 0.14/1000 people, which is approximately 80 times greater.[34] Ninety percentage of cases in India during the pandemic presented as rhinocerebral mucormycosis, with <10% presenting as pulmonary or disseminated forms among the various other clinical presentations of mucormycosis, which include gastrointestinal, cutaneous, disseminated, and other rare forms.[13] The fungus rhizopus, mucor of the order mucorales, class phycomycetes, is to blame for this. An absolute hypoxic environment, high glucose concentrations, the presence of an acidic medium, high iron levels, and impaired neutrophil response due to immunosuppression, either steroid-or SARS-CoV-2-mediated, were the factors that elevated the prevalence of mucormycosis in COVID-19 patients.[56]

The direct consequence of COVID-19-associated mucormycosis (CAM) itself and its therapeutic intervention, had led to significant facial disfigurement along with problems in mastication, speech, and swallowing which may impact the patient’s quality of life (QoL).[78] Prolonged hospitalization and isolation, as well as the effects of maxillectomy-such as intraoral changes in the size, symmetry, and form of the maxilla, changes in the facial profile due to unsupported facial tissues, collapsed facial height and drooping esthetics, difficulty chewing, speech disorders, and nasal regurgitation, all have a significant negative impact on the patient’s physical and mental health.[91011] A potentially fatal fungal infection on the face that requires surgery will cause mental anguish and increase the risk of posttraumatic stress disorder, anxiety, and depression. The patient’s overall QOL will be impacted by the combined effects of all these factors on their psychological status (PS).[12131415]

Although the physical and functional impacts of mucormycosis are documented, the psychological aspect is frequently overlooked. More thorough research and integrated care strategies that address the physical and mental health aspects of this serious illness are desperately needed. Therefore, following the removal of the affected tissues, the most crucial part of treatment is reconstructing or rehabilitating the lost structures, as well as restoring oronasal functions and facial contours that support psychological well-being.[1617]

Prosthetic rehabilitation with obturator prosthesis restores oral function, improve facial esthetics, support maxillofacial structures, and enhance psychosocial well-being, ultimately improving the PS and QOL with an added benefit of examining the surgical areas in case of recurrence at an earlier stage.[1819] Furthermore, when factors such as the extent of defect, availability of hard and soft tissues in the defect area to provide support for the prosthesis, proximity to vital structures, patient’s attitude, socioeconomic status, psychological, systemic conditions and the patient’s ability to adapt to the prosthesis have been considered pertinent, obturators have proved to be an extremely positive rehabilitation technique for those who have undergone maxillectomy.[1620212223]

There is a general consensus in the research community that prosthetic rehabilitation with obturators, significantly improves the PS and QOL in maxillectomy patients following head-and-neck cancer.[82425262728] With the continuing rise in CAM cases in India, there is a need for a comprehensive study on the evaluation of effect of prosthodontic rehabilitation on maxillectomy patients of CAM in an Indian population.

The aim of this clinical study was to evaluate and to highlight the impact of prosthodontic intervention on PS and QoL in maxillectomy patients of CAM. The study evaluated and compared the association between PS, QoL and obturator functioning over different periods of time. The null hypothesis was that prosthodontic intervention with obturators creates no significant difference in PS and QoL of maxillectomy patients of CAM.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This clinical study was carried out between July 2021 and July 2022 on individuals with CAM, undergoing maxillectomy followed by prosthetic rehabilitation in dental outpatient department. Convenience sampling was chosen in this study. An ethical clearance has been obtained from the Ethical Committee of the institution (Ref. No. IEC/2021/893). Registration of the study was made with Clinical Trial Registry, India (CTRI) (Reg. No. CTRI/2021/09/036553). Participant’s selection was not limited by age, gender, and resection site. Participants with negative COVID-19 history, history of pulmonary diseases, complete edentulism, bilateral maxillary resection, mid-facial defects, defects of mandible, and isolated soft palate defects were excluded from the study. Twenty-four subjects were enrolled as per the inclusion and exclusion criteria. An informed consent was obtained from all the participants before the study. All enrolled participants were educated on surgical outcomes, exercises to overcome trismus, use of trismus screws, and various treatment procedures of prosthetic rehabilitation. The nonresected region of the maxillary and mandibular arch of the participants had almost entire complement of teeth. The treatment procedure involved surgical, intermediate, and definitive obturator prosthesis delivery at various phases of healing.

PS and QoL of the participants were assessed before maxillectomy (T1), 2 weeks after maxillectomy (T2), 2 weeks upon usage of intermediate obturator (T3), just before delivery of definitive obturator (T4), and 12 weeks after the usage of definitive obturator (T5). Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) was used to assess the PS.[29] The HADS questionnaire’s domain for anxiety and depression consists of seven questions. The lowest possible score for each question is zero, which signifies the lack of abnormalities. The highest possible score for each question is three, which corresponds to the highest possible levels of anxiety or depression. Hence, the highest possible score in every domain is 21, and the lowest number that is feasible is zero. QOL was assessed using European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer QoL Questionnaire-Head and Neck module (EORTC QLQ-HandN35) questionnaires. EORTC QLQ-HandN35 consists of 35 questions, which evaluates oral functioning, social activity, body image, and sexuality as well as the signs and consequences of cancer therapy. Highest score indicates poor QoL.[82830]

Evaluation of obturator function using Obturator Functioning Scale (OFS) were performed 2 weeks after the usage of intermediate obturator (T3), just before delivery of definitive obturator (T4), and 12 weeks after the usage of definitive obturator (T5).[24] OFS evaluates the speech, eating capability, and esthetic satisfaction using a questionnaire containing 15 questions, answered using a 5-point Likert Scale.

From 24 participants who were enrolled as per the inclusion and exclusion criteria, nine participants were dropped from the study. Among them, five participants were excluded because of primary surgical closure, two participants have become completely edentulous and in two participants resection was extended to the face because of recurrence and secondary surgery.

The gathered data were compiled in an Excel spreadsheet and analyzed with a social science statistical analysis programme (Stata 14.0; StataCorp LLC, Texas, USA). All data were shown as mean ± standard error using descriptive statistics; the Shapiro–Wilk test was used to evaluate the normality of quantitative data. Student’s t-test was employed (α =0.05) to evaluate if the patient’s PS, QOL and obturator function had changed over time. The Spearman Correlation Coefficient (α =0.05) was used to calculate the correlation coefficient between the change in QOL, PS, and OFS seen at T3 and T5. In all analysis, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

CAM profoundly impacts the PS and QoL of the affected individuals by the invasive nature of the disease itself, fear of mortality, complex treatment process, resulting potential functional and esthetic disfigurement, and the challenges in day-to-day activities. Thus, to address the holistic impact of CAM on patients, a multidisciplinary approach involving various specialties is essential. Prosthetic rehabilitation with obturators, creates an anatomical barrier between the nasal and oral cavity, thereby enhances the masticatory function, allows speech to be articulated properly, and monitors the healing of surgically ablated tissues.[3132]

Various studies exist that address the challenges and outcomes of prosthetic intervention on QoL and PS of cancer patients who had undergone maxillectomy.[82425262728] However, the field of prosthetic rehabilitation for CAM patients assessing the PS and QOL is indeed relatively under-explored in terms of research. A number of factors, including the rapid progressive nature of infection course, the short time between the onset of symptoms and receiving effective treatment, lack of awareness in public, the length of time it takes to recover from COVID-19, extended hospital stays or readmissions, limited availability of Amphotericin B, apprehension about undergoing surgery, and the generally poor prognosis of mucormycosis, biologic cause such as the hyperinflammatory state explains the high level of psychological distress experienced in CAM patients.[33] Hence, this study was designed to evaluate the impact of prosthetic rehabilitation on the functional and psychological aspects of patients recovering from CAM.

All research participants received immediate surgical obturators as part of their prosthetic rehabilitation, which aided in their initial healing and made oral feeding easier. Since the prosthesis is removable, it was easier to detect recurrences because mucormycosis has the highest recurrence rate and also the definite impact of COVID-19 on mucormycosis is not evident. The material used in this study, for the fabrication of various types of obturator is the heat-curable acrylic resin, which is a biocompatible material.

The results of the study reject the null hypothesis. HADS and EORTC QLQ-HandN35 scores before and after maxillectomy and after rehabilitation with intermediate and definitive obturator prosthesis showed statistically significant improvement. Also the functioning of intermediate obturator after 2 weeks of insertion, just before insertion of definitive obturator and the functioning of definitive obturator after 12 weeks of usage showed statistically significant difference.

Preoperatively, the anxiety scores on HADS-A were the highest, apparently due to consternation associated with treatment and surgery. It was observed that the HADS-D score was at its maximum at T2, implying that the psychological state of depression was at its peak after surgery. This is similar to the findings from other research studies, in which patients perceive a part of their bodies as being lost.[243435] Anxiety and depression scores decreased over time from T3, T4, and T5, the possible reasons could be that the obturator restored the function to near normal. This may also be explained by enhanced prosthetic design and quality along with the phase of adaptation.

The evaluation of QoL using EORTC QLQ-HandN35 Questionnaire showed that the participants enrolled in the study had poor QoL before and after maxillectomy, which was improved following rehabilitation with an obturator prosthesis, suggesting improved oral function. With the exception of questions related to sexual wellness, all other questions showed better response. Due to the time needed to acclimatize and become accustomed to the prosthesis, improvement in speech, swallowing, and mastication and the overall improvement in QOL was at its highest after 12 weeks of obturator use. Similar findings were reported by Dutta et al. who found that the oral health related QoL was improved from 34.79% to 59.86% among patients with post-COVID-19 mucormycosis.[36] Various other studies are also in agreement with the above results.[82425262728]

On checking the intermediate obturator function at 2 weeks following insertion (T3), a high OFS score was seen. However, comparable score reductions at T4 and T5 may be attributable to the patient’s greater acclimation and compliance to the prosthesis throughout the 12-week observation period. Improvements in appearance, mastication, speech, and decreased pain perception were due to the cumulative impact.[37]

Changes in EORTC QLQ-HandN35, OFS, and HADS anxiety and depression scores were positively and linearly correlated. Improvement in obturator function results in greater improvement in QoL, since the oral function has been satisfactorily restored. Also significant reduction in depression and stress levels impacted the QoL of the participants, maybe because of the feeling of improved capability in handling life’s obstacles on their own. After therapy, the subjects generally reported feeling good.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the only study to date to be conducted on the evaluation of impact of prosthetic rehabilitation on PS and QoL on maxillectomy patients of CAM. The small sample size is one of the limitations of the study. A number of other variables are also left out of the study, such as size of the defect, the number and periodontal health of the remaining teeth, and socioeconomic status of the participant. In future, larger clinical trials with longer follow-up period and considering all the above-mentioned factors are to be considered.

CONCLUSION

Life-threatening illnesses such as CAM and its surgical intervention most frequently impact PS and QoL in terms of physical and mental health. Maxillofacial prosthodontists are crucial in these situations because they can diagnose and treat patients at an early stage. Greater improvements in PS and QOL are produced by psychological motivation, prosthetic rehabilitation, and education. Over time, the parameters progress demonstrated an upward trend with good obturator quality.

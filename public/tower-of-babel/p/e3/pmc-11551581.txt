Comment on ‘Large Language Models in Patient Queries on Gingival and Endodontic Health’
Authors: Rujittika Mungmunpuntipantip, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2024

Dear Editor

‘Comprehensiveness of Large Language Models in Patient Queries on Gingival and Endodontic Health.’ is an interesting study.1 This study sought to assess the ability of two large language models (LLMs), ChatGPT-3.5 and ChatGPT-4, to deliver comprehensive replies to frequent questions concerning gum and root canal health in both English and Chinese contexts. To accomplish this purpose, the researchers compiled a dataset of 33 questions, comprising 17 common sense questions and 16 expert-level questions. Three dental specialists graded these questions on a 5-point Likert scale to assess the quality of the LLMs' responses. The results showed clear disparities in performance by language, with English answers scoring higher on average than Chinese responses, as well as differences in performance across question categories.

One obvious flaw in this study is the subjective character of the assessment procedure. Relying on a small group of three experts may add bias because their expertise and personal expectations influence their scores. Furthermore, the comprehensiveness criteria were not standardised, which may have an impact on overall assessment consistency. Furthermore, the small sample size of 33 questions may not adequately represent the variety of inquiries faced in dental clinics, limiting the generalisability of the findings.

To address these difficulties in future study, the panel of experts could be expanded to eliminate bias and improve evaluation reliability. Future studies might use a broader and more diversified collection of questions that cover areas of dental health other than gingivitis and root canals, as well as patient-reported outcomes, to further determine how successfully LLMs handle real-world difficulties. A hybrid method could also provide further information by combining quantitative expert evaluations with qualitative feedback on the perceived clarity and usability of LLM responses. To expand on this area of innovation, researchers should consider incorporating domain-specific knowledge bases into LLMs, which could improve contextual understanding of oral health issues.

Furthermore, studying the effectiveness of LLMs in a conversational style, which resembles a patient-provider dialog, could aid in understanding their utility in a therapeutic context. Using user interaction data to improve LLM responses, highlighting the importance of tailoring information to a patient's knowledge level, and investigating cultural differences in healthcare inquiry may reveal new dimensions in the application of LLM technology in the dental sector.

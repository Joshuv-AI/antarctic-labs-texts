Protective effects of Lactobacillus acidophilus LM1060 on LPS/H2O2-induced cytotoxicity in SK-N-SH cells and scopolamine-induced memory impairment in mice
Authors: Yunjung Lee, Jiyoung Hwang, Tae-Rahk Kim, Minn Sohn, Eunju Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND/OBJECTIVESThis study examined the neuroprotective effects of Lactobacillus acidophilus LM1060 against scopolamine-induced memory impairment to elucidate its underlying mechanisms.MATERIALS/METHODSLM1060 (1 × 109 CFU/day) or donepezil (DO, 2 mg/kg) was administered to Institute of Cancer Research mice by oral gavage for 14 days (days 1–14). Memory impairment was induced by scopolamine (1 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection) for 6 days (days 9–14). Behavioral tests, including the Y-maze and novel object recognition tests, were performed to evaluate the cognitive function. The effects of LM1060 on the acetylcholine (ACh), acetylcholinesterase (AChE), and choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) levels in the hippocampus were examined. The β-secretase activity, β-amyloid levels, and DNA damage in brain tissue were also assessed. In vitro studies were conducted using SK-N-SH neuroblastoma cells to evaluate the cell viability, antioxidant capacity, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, AChE activity, and inflammatory cytokine gene expression.RESULTSLM1060 showed a protective effect against scopolamine-induced cognitive dysfunction, as confirmed by behavioral tests including the Y-maze and novel object recognition tests. Oral administration of LM1060 restored the ACh and ChAT activity reduced by scopolamine. The β-secretase activity and β-amyloid levels decreased after the LM1060 and DO treatment. The LM1060 administration group exhibited a higher protective effect against scopolamine-induced DNA damage in brain tissue than the scopolamine-treated group. In vitro studies showed that LM1060 significantly enhanced the cell viability and antioxidant capacity, reduced LDH release and AChE activity, and modulated inflammatory cytokine gene expression by downregulating interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-6 and upregulating IL-10.CONCLUSIONL. acidophilus LM1060 exerts potent neuroprotective effects through anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and cholinergic mechanisms, highlighting its potential as a functional ingredient for cognitive health.

INTRODUCTION

Dementia is a growing global health concern, with a prevalence rising in older populations [1]. Alzheimer’s disease (AD), a progressive neurodegenerative disorder, is the most common cause of dementia worldwide [1]. AD is characterized by progressive deficits in memory, cognition, and behavior, ultimately leading to dementia. The pathology of AD is characterized by extracellular amyloid-β (Aβ) deposits and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein [2].

Scopolamine, a non-selective muscarinic antagonist, impairs central cholinergic transmission and disrupts learning and short-term memory in animals and humans [3456]. This non-degenerative model of cholinergic dysfunction lacks the comparable alternatives and is used widely in rodents to screen candidate treatments for amnesia and dementia [78910].

The hippocampus and cerebral cortex are key regions involved in the maintenance and regulation of memory. AD is a progressive disease that leads to the irreversible loss of neurons in these brain regions, with the hippocampus being particularly associated with scopolamine-induced memory deficits [11]. Scopolamine increases the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) activity in the hippocampus [312].

Oxidative stress and neuroinflammation are also considered major contributors to neuronal dysfunction and cognitive decline in AD. In this context, lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and H2O2 are commonly used to induce inflammatory and oxidative cytotoxicity in neuronal cell models, enabling an evaluation of the direct neuroprotective effects at the cellular level.

Probiotics are microorganisms that contribute to gut health by adjusting the gastrointestinal pH, maintaining epithelial integrity, functioning as natural antimicrobial substances, and modulating brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) expression [13]. Neurotrophic factors are essential for neuronal survival and differentiation, playing essential roles in brain growth and function. Deficiencies in these factors can lead to learning disabilities and memory impairments [14]. In addition to regulating BDNF, probiotics help improve memory loss and psychiatric disturbances by modulating neurotransmitters, including serotonin, γ-aminobutyric acid, and dopamine [15]. Although previous studies have suggested that probiotics may influence BDNF signaling and neurotransmitter regulation, the mechanisms were not directly evaluated in the present study and are discussed only as potential pathways. Their beneficial actions on the central nervous system (CNS) are largely mediated by reshaping the gut microbiota, increasing the diversity and population of beneficial bacteria, thereby supporting neural functions. Furthermore, probiotics help re-establish microbial homeostasis and slow the progression of AD by reducing oxidative stress and inflammatory processes, ultimately protecting against cognitive deterioration [16]. Commonly used probiotic strains belong to the genera Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium, which do not induce inflammation upon ingestion because of the absence of lipopolysaccharides [17].

Lactobacillus acidophilus LM1060 was isolated from the feces of healthy adults, and its anti-wrinkle and anti-melanogenic properties have been reported in previous patent-based studies (WO2024185983A1; KR102657370B1) [1819]. Hence, LM1060 may possess bioactive properties, supporting further investigations of its potential effects in other physiological systems. Therefore, this study examined the neuroprotective effects of LM1060 in SK-N-SH neuronal cells exposed to LPS/H2O2-induced cytotoxicity, and evaluated whether LM1060 could alleviate scopolamine-induced cognitive impairment in a mouse model. This integrated approach was designed as an initial exploratory study by combining in vitro and in vivo models to examine direct cellular protection and indirect behavioral outcomes related to cognitive function. The protective effects of LM1060 against DNA damage in brain tissue were also examined.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Preparations of bacterial samples

L. acidophilus LM1060 was provided by Lactomason (Jinju, Korea), and its manufacturing information is confidential.

Cell culture

Human SK-N-SH neuroblastoma cells (ATCC, Rockville, MD, USA) were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37°C in 5% CO2. The culture medium was replaced every 2 to 3 days.

Cell viability assay

The cell viability was assessed using an MTT [3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide] assay. SK-N-SH cells (5 × 104/mL) were cultured overnight in 96-well plates and preincubated with LM1060 (1 × 107, 1 × 108, or 1 × 109 CFU) for 24 h before exposure to H2O2 (200 µM). An MTT solution (250 µg/mL) was added to each well, and formazan crystals were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide. The absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (Epoch; BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA).

Cellular antioxidant capacity (CAC assay)

SK-N-SH cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well on a 96-well plate to assess the CAC of LM1060. After 24 h incubation, the medium was replaced with Hanks' balanced salt solution (HBSS), and LM1060 was added to each well at final concentrations of 1 × 107, 1 × 108, or 1 × 109 CFU, followed by 30 min incubation. The wells were then washed with HBSS, and 80 mM AAPH was added to all samples except the negative control (NC). After a further 30 min incubation, 40 mM DCFH-DA was added to each well and incubated for 30 min in the dark. The fluorescence was measured at 485 nm excitation and 535 nm emission using an OPTIMA microplate reader (BMG LABTECH, Ortenberg, Germany).

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) cytotoxicity assay

An LDH release assay was performed to assess the loss of membrane integrity associated with cell death. SK-N-SH cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well in 96-well plates and cultured in DMEM with LM1060 (1 × 109 CFU) for 24 h. LDH release into the medium was quantified using a Cytotoxicity LDH Assay Kit-WST (Dojindo Laboratories, Kumamoto, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The cells were pretreated with LM1060 for 2 h before 24 h exposure to H2O2 (200 µM). Subsequently, 50 µL of medium from each well was transferred to a new plate, mixed with 50 µL of working solution, and blanks contained 100 µL of working solution. Plates were incubated for 40 min at room temperature in the dark, and the absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader (Epoch; BioTek).

AChE inhibition assay

The AChE inhibitory activity was measured using a modified version of the Ellman method [20]. Briefly, 25 μL of an AChE solution (2.36 U/mL), 50 μL of 0.1 M phosphate buffer (pH 8.0), and 5 μL of LM1060 (1 × 109 CFU) were mixed in a cuvette and incubated at 37 °C for 20 min. After incubation, 125 μL of 3 mM DTNB and 25 μL of 0.075 M acetylcholine (ACh) iodide were added, and the absorbance was read at 412 nm using a microplate reader (Epoch; BioTek).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis

SK-N-SH cells (5 × 105 cells/mL) were cultured overnight in 24-well plates, with or without LM1060 (1 × 109 CFU) preincubation for 24 h before exposure to H2O2 (200 µM) or LPS (1 µg/mL). The total RNA from SK-N-SH cells was isolated with TRIzol (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA), and cDNA was synthesized using M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). qRT-PCR was conducted with SYBR Green Master Mix (QuantiSpeed SYBR No-ROX; PhileKorea, Seoul, Korea) using a CFX Duet real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The PCR conditions were 95°C for 10 min, followed by 50 cycles of 95°C for 10 s, 55°C for 20 s, and 72°C for 20 s. The sequences of the sense and antisense primers for interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-4, IL-6, and IL-10 used for amplification were as follows: IL-1β, sense, 5′-gttgacggaccccaaaagat-3′ and antisense, 5′-aaggtccacgggaaagacac-3′; IL-6, sense, 5′-ccttcctaccccaatttcca-3′ and antisense, 5′-cgcactaggtttgccgagta-3′; IL-10, sense, 5′-taaggctggccacacttgac-3′ and antisense, 5′-agttttcagggatgaagcgg-3′; β-actin, sense, 5′-gattactgctctggctccta-3′ and antisense, 5′-atcgtactcctgcttgct-3′. The mRNA levels of each gene were normalized to β-actin. The relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method and presented as the fold change relative to the control.

Mice and experimental design

Seven-week-old male Institute of Cancer Research mice (Koatech, Seoul, Korea) were maintained at 25 ± 2°C and 50 ± 20% humidity, under a 12-h light/dark cycle. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Kyungnam University and were performed in accordance with the established regulations (approval number: KUICA-22-06). After one week of acclimatization, the animals were assigned randomly to 4 groups (n = 10) as follows: 1) normal saline, intraperitoneal (i.p.) and per os (p.o.) (NC), 2) scopolamine 1 mg/kg i.p. and normal saline p.o. (positive control; PC), 3) scopolamine 1 mg/kg i.p. and LM1060 1 × 109 CFU/day p.o. (LM1060), and 4) scopolamine 1 mg/kg i.p. and donepezil (DO) 2 mg/kg p.o. Memory impairment was induced by injecting scopolamine and administering LM1060 or DO (AChE inhibitor) for 2 weeks. Scopolamine (dissolved in deionized water) was administered intraperitoneally on days 7–14. LM1060 was administered 60 min before each trial, followed by scopolamine 30 min prior. After 2 weeks, the mice were fasted for 12 h and anesthetized with isoflurane (4 mL/kg). Blood was collected and centrifuged at 2,000 × g for 30 min to obtain serum. The organs were harvested, and the hippocampus was carefully dissected on ice. All samples were stored at −80°C until further analysis. Fig. 1 presents the experimental design.

Beginning on day 8, scopolamine was also administered for 7 days, and the Y-maze test and NORT test were used to assess behavior during this period (days 8–14). Scopolamine 1 mg/kg was i.p. injected 1 h after sample administration. The behavioral test was performed 30 min after scopolamine injection.

Behavioral tests

A behavioral test and the novel object recognition test (NORT) and Y-maze test were used to assess the learning abilities and memory, respectively. On the first day, 2 objects (A1 + A2) were placed in a box, adapted for 3 min, and the number of times the experimental animal touched the 2 objects was recorded for 5 min. The next day, object B was placed instead of object A1, and the number of times the animal touched the 2 objects was recorded for 5 min to determine the recognition index (time spent with new objects) and object cognition ability (number of touches for new objects). The time spent exploring each object was recorded (familiar object, N1; novel object, N2). The recognition index (%) was calculated as N2/(N1 + N2) × 100.

The Y-maze test was used to assess the spatial working memory, a type of short-term memory. The apparatus was constructed from black polyvinyl plastic and consisted of 3 identical arms (40 cm in length, 3 cm in width, and 12 cm in height) arranged at 120°. Each mouse was initially placed at the end of one arm, and its sequence of entries (e.g., ABCCAB, with letters indicating arm codes) and the total number of arm entries were recorded manually during an 8-min trial. An alternation was defined as consecutive entries into all 3 different arms (e.g., ABC, BCA, or CAB, but not BAB). The mice were orally administered LM1060 (1 × 109 CFU) or DO (2 mg/kg) 1.5 h before testing. Memory impairment was induced by an intraperitoneal injection of scopolamine (1 mg/kg) 30 min after treatment with LM1060, DO, or saline. Between the trials, maze arms were thoroughly cleaned to eliminate residual odors. Spontaneous alternation (%) was calculated as [Number of Alternations/(Total Arm Entries − 2)] × 100. The total arm entries were recorded as a measure of the locomotor activity, serving as an indicator of overall behavioral performance.

Hippocampal tissues were homogenized in cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS; 4×, pH 7.4) on ice and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 5 min to obtain the supernatants, which were used to assess the ACh level, AChE, choline acetyltransferase (ChAT), and β-secretase activities. For β-amyloid 42 analysis, the hippocampal tissues were homogenized in 5 M guanidine-HCl/50 mM Tris buffer (8×, pH 8.0), incubated at room temperature for 3–4 h, mixed with 10× cold BSAT-PBS, and centrifuged at 16,000 × g for 20 min. The supernatants were analyzed using commercial kits: ACh (EnzyChrom™; Bioassay Systems, Hayward, CA, USA), AChE (QuantiChrom™; Bioassay Systems), ChAT (MyBiosource, San Diego, CA, USA), β-Secretase (Mouse Bace1 ELISA; Express Biotech International, Frederick, MD, USA), and β-amyloid 42 (Mouse amyloid beta 42 ELISA; BioVendor R&D, Brno, Czech Republic), according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Comet assay

Brain cells (2 × 106 cells/mL) were prepared for the comet assay and suspended in 0.7% low-melting agarose, then layered onto slides precoated with 1% normal-melting agarose. The slides were lysed in cold alkaline buffer (100 mM Na2EDTA, 2.5 M NaCl, 10 mM Tris, 1% sodium lauroyl sarcosine, 1% Triton X-100, 10% DMSO) for 1 h and placed in an electrophoresis chamber containing cold electrophoresis buffer (300 mM NaOH, 10 mM Na2EDTA, pH 13.0) for 20 min. Electrophoresis was performed at 25 V, 300 mA for 20 min. The slides were then washed, fixed in cold ethanol for 5 min, air-dried, and stained with 20 μg/mL ethidium bromide. DNA damage was visualized using fluorescence microscopy (LEICA DMLB, Wetzlar, Germany) with a CCD camera (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) and quantified using the Komet 5.0 analysis system (Kinetic Imaging). The tail DNA was measured in 50 cells from 2 replicate slides.

Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as mean ± SE. The differences among the groups were analyzed using 1-way analysis of variance followed by a Duncan’s multiple range test. The statistical significance was set to P < 0.05. The analyses were performed using SPSS software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

Effect of LM1060 on cell viability and antioxidant activity

LM1060 enhanced the viability of SK-N-SH cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2A). Cells treated with increasing concentrations of LM1060 (1 × 107, 1 × 108, and 1 × 109 CFU) showed significantly higher viability than the oxidative stress-induced control groups. At 1 × 108 CFU and 1 × 109 CFU, LM1060 showed a similar protective effect to that of the NC. As shown in Fig. 2B, LM1060 also enhanced the CAC against 2,2′-azobis (2-amidinopropane) dihydrochloride -induced oxidative stress. The antioxidant activity increased at higher LM1060 doses, and significant differences were observed between the treatment groups (P < 0.05), indicating that LM1060 had a dose-dependent antioxidant effect.

Effect of LM1060 on cytotoxicity and AChE inhibition

Treatment with LM1060 significantly reduced the release of LDH from SK-N-SH cells (Table 1), indicating decreased cytotoxicity. The LDH level decreased from 99.94 ± 7.57% in the NC to 63.60 ± 2.16% after the LM1060 treatment (P < 0.05). In addition, LM1060 significantly inhibited the AChE activity, reducing the inhibition rate from 102.99 ± 11.09% to 49.75 ± 3.26% (P < 0.01). Hence, LM1060 protects against neuronal injury and cholinergic impairment.

Effects of LM1060 on inflammatory cytokine gene expression

The anti-inflammatory activity of LM1060 was evaluated by quantifying the expression of IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-10 (Fig. 3). LM1060 significantly suppressed the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 compared with the PC treated with H2O2 or LPS (P < 0.05). By contrast, the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 was significantly upregulated. These findings suggest that LM1060 exerts anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects by regulating cytokine gene expression.

Effect of LM1060 on learning and memory improvement in behavioral tests

The NORT is used widely to evaluate the learning and memory functions in rodents. The mice in the scopolamine-treated group (PC) spent significantly more time exploring the familiar object (113.9 ± 10.4 s) than the novel object (33.7 ± 5.7 s) (Fig. 4), indicating impaired recognition memory. The LM1060 treatment significantly decreased the time spent on familiar objects and significantly increased the time spent on new objects compared with the PC group. The scopolamine-treated mice exhibited a significantly lower recognition index than the NCs (31.4 ± 2.4% vs. 61.4 ± 1.9%). The LM1060 group (55.9 ± 8.3%) showed a markedly increased recognition index than the PC group, indicating improved cognitive function. In addition, the DO group showed results (56.7 ± 4.6%) similar to those of the LM1060 group, further supporting the cognitive benefits of the LM1060 treatment.

Effect of LM1060 on cholinergic activity in the hippocampus

The hippocampal ACh levels were significantly lower in the PC group (31.4 ± 5.2 μM) than in the NC group (56.2 ± 3.9 μM) (Fig. 6), showing cholinergic dysfunction caused by scopolamine. On the other hand, treatment with LM1060 significantly increased the ACh levels (45.4 ± 2.8 μM) compared with the PC group, suggesting a protective effect on the cholinergic function. Similarly, the DO group showed a further increase in ACh levels (50.3 ± 1.3 μM), comparable to the LM1060 group.

The AChE activity was significantly elevated in the PC group (147.9 ± 18.8%) compared to the NC group (100.0 ± 3.5%), reflecting increased ACh breakdown. The LM1060 treatment reduced the AChE activity (133.6 ± 13.5%) compared to the PC group, indicating an inhibitory effect on ACh degradation. The DO group also exhibited reduced AChE activity (117.1 ± 7.0%), indicating an effect similar to that of LM1060.

The ChAT activity, which is essential for ACh synthesis, was significantly lower in the PC group (13.5 ± 3.3 U/g fresh weight) than in the NC group (35.2 ± 4.5 U/g fresh weight). The LM1060 treatment significantly restored the ChAT activity (28.0 U/g fresh weight) compared to the PC group, suggesting its role in enhancing ACh synthesis. The DO group also showed an improvement in ChAT activity (31.2 ± 4.9 U/g fresh weight), similar to the effect observed in the LM1060 group. These results suggest that LM1060 can effectively modulate the cholinergic activity in the brain, thereby alleviating the scopolamine-induced cognitive impairment.

The β-secretase activity was significantly higher in the PC group (96.7 ± 8.2 ng/mL) than in the NC group (45.7 ± 0.8 ng/mL) (Fig. 7), indicating enhanced amyloidogenic processing. In contrast, the LM1060 treatment significantly reduced the β-secretase activity (68.7 ± 7.3 ng/mL) compared with the PC group, suggesting its potential to inhibit the amyloidogenic pathway. Similarly, the DO treatment also reduced the β-secretase activity (59.9 ± 5.2 ng/mL), comparable to that of LM1060.

DISCUSSION

In vitro studies with SK-N-SH neuroblastoma cells showed that LM1060 enhanced the cell viability and antioxidant capacity and reduced LDH release and AChE activity. In addition, LM1060 modulated cytokine expression by downregulating IL-1β and IL-6 while upregulating IL-10 (Table 1, Figs. 1 and 2). These findings suggest that LM1060 exerts protective effects at the cellular level by counteracting oxidative stress, cholinergic dysfunction, and neuroinflammation—key mechanisms associated with AD progression.

Scopolamine, a muscarinic receptor blocker, disrupts memory in animals and humans, mainly impairing learning and short-term recall [49]. Therefore, scopolamine is used widely as a standard reference drug for inducing dementia-related cognitive impairment in humans and animals [35921].

This study assessed the cognitive effects of LM1060 in a scopolamine-induced amnesia mouse model through behavioral analyses, including the Y-maze and NORT. The Y-maze test is a behavioral assay that evaluates the exploratory behavior in rodents, specifically measuring the spatial working memory [22]. This test leverages the natural tendency of rodents to explore novel environments, leading them to prefer entering a new arm of the maze rather than revisiting a previously explored one [22]. An increase in spontaneous alternation reflects improved learning and memory function [22]. In the Y-maze test, the LM1060 treatment significantly increased spontaneous alternation in mice, counteracting the reduction caused by scopolamine. This outcome was similar to that observed with DO [92324]. NORT is a tool to assess cognition, particularly recognition memory, in rodent models of CNS disorders [925]. In this study, the scopolamine-treated mice exhibited a significant decrease in exploration time for the novel object, indicating an impairment in object recognition memory. By contrast, the LM1060-treated mice displayed a clear preference for the novel object, spending more time exploring it than the familiar one.

Dementia, commonly linked to aging and AD, is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that poses a major medical challenge and a critical societal issue [26]. Among its symptoms, memory loss appears first and most prominently, primarily involving the hippocampus, a brain region essential for learning and cognition. Cholinergic deficits in the hippocampus are strongly linked to memory dysfunction because ACh is a key neurotransmitter involved in memory regulation [2728]. In patients with dementia, choline uptake and ACh synthesis are reduced in the hippocampus, while AChE, the enzyme responsible for ACh degradation, is upregulated [2930]. Previous studies have shown that patients with dementia exhibit decreased choline uptake and ACh synthesis [2931], accompanied by elevated AChE activity. In addition, ChAT, the key enzyme responsible for ACh synthesis, plays an essential role in sustaining fundamental brain functions. Decreased ChAT activity, particularly in the hippocampus, has been observed in patients with dementia [23]. Thus, measuring the ACh, AChE, and ChAT levels in the hippocampus is essential for understanding the mechanisms underlying memory impairment in dementia and for evaluating potential therapeutic strategies.

In the present study, scopolamine administration reduced the ACh levels, elevated AChE activity, and decreased ChAT activity in the hippocampus, mirroring the cholinergic deficits observed in dementia. Conversely, treatment with LM1060 increased the ACh levels, inhibited AChE activity, and enhanced the ChAT activity in the hippocampus. Hence, LM1060 effectively counteracts the scopolamine-induced cholinergic deficits in a manner similar to DO, a commonly used PC. The effects of LM1060 on the ACh levels and ChAT activity, comparable to those of DO, highlight its potential as a therapeutic agent for mitigating cholinergic dysfunction and memory impairment associated with dementia.

AD is a representative neurodegenerative disorder marked by progressive cognitive and physical decline [32]. Although its precise etiology remains unresolved, major symptoms include short-term memory loss, cognitive dysfunction, impaired reasoning, and communication difficulties [33]. A defining pathological hallmark of AD is the extracellular deposition of Aβ peptides [34]. Aβ aggregation and oxidative stress are widely regarded as central drivers of AD pathogenesis. In addition, Aβ accumulation triggers neuroinflammation and mitochondrial apoptotic pathways, ultimately leading to synaptic dysfunction.

Aβ is a peptide derived from amyloid precursor protein (APP) through proteolytic cleavage. The generation of neurotoxic Aβ peptides, especially Aβ40 and Aβ42, and their aggregation into insoluble plaques are defining neuropathological hallmarks of AD [35]. The activity of β-secretase plays a crucial role in modulating APP cleavage, leading to the formation of Aβ peptides and their aggregation into plaques. Scopolamine has been reported to elevate Aβ formation and oxidative stress in rodent brains. The main sites of Aβ accumulation are brain regions linked to memory and learning, largely mediated by cholinergic pathways [2436].

In the present study, the LM1060 treatment reduced β-secretase activity and Aβ production in a scopolamine-induced model, suggesting a similar potential therapeutic effect to that of DO. These findings suggest that LM1060 may alleviate amyloid pathology and oxidative stress associated with AD by modulating the key pathways involved in Aβ production and degradation.

Oxidative stress-mediated cellular injury has been implicated in the pathogenesis of several neurodegenerative disorders, including AD, Parkinson’s disease, and cerebral stroke [30]. Scopolamine induces neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, contributing to the degeneration of cholinergic neurons in the CNS, which is strongly linked to the cognitive and functional decline observed in AD and senile dementia. Cognitive impairment has been linked to DNA damage arising from aging or environmental stressors. In the present study, scopolamine increased the genotoxicity of brain tissue, whereas LM1060 protected against scopolamine-induced DNA damage.

This study showed that LM1060 administration significantly alleviated scopolamine-induced cognitive deficits by improving the learning and memory performance and reducing Aβ accumulation. In addition, LM1060 modulated the neurotransmitter levels and protected against DNA damage in brain tissue. Importantly, the combined use of in vitro and in vivo models in this study provides complementary evidence, allowing the identification of the direct neuroprotective effects at the cellular level and indirect cognitive benefits at the behavioral level. These beneficial effects may be attributed to its ability to regulate neurotransmission, potentially influence synaptic function, inhibit Aβ accumulation, and prevent neuronal DNA damage. In conclusion, LM1060 may represent a potential candidate for cognitive support. Nevertheless, further studies, including clinical trials, will be needed to validate its mechanisms, functional efficacy, and safety.

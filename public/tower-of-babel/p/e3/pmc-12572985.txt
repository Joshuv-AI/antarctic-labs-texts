Synthesis and Fluorescent Properties of Alkynylated 3‑Methylidenephthalides with Extended π‑Systems
Authors: Alexandra A. Vidyakina, Mariia M. Efremova, Kirill V. Korolev-Zelenyi, Natalia A. Danilkina, Vladimir N. Mikhaylov, Julia S. Okuneva, Alexander V. Ponomarev, Irina A. Balova
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The iodocyclization of (iodoethynyl)­benzoate via the 5-exo-dig pathway affords (diiodomethylene)­isobenzofuran-1­(3H)-one. The Sonogashira cross-coupling of the diiodide proceeds stereoselectively by the substitution of one iodine atom and protodeiodination of the second one, giving Z-isomers of alkynylated derivatives. The arylacetylenes coupling products exhibit fluorescence with an aggregation-induced emission enhancement. The coupling products of Z-3-(TMS-ethynyl)­methylenephthalid and 1,4-diiodo-2,5-bis­(octyloxy)­benzene with extended π-systems underwent fast isomerization, giving equilibrium mixtures of isomers and exhibiting strong fluorescence in nonaggregating solvents.

Introduction

Phthalide is a frequently found structural moiety in naturally occurring compounds isolated from plants, fungi, bacteria, and liverworts. Because of the wide range of pharmacophore activities, phthalide-containing bioactive compounds have gained worldwide popularity. Therefore, various kinds of synthetic approaches for their preparation have been developed over the years. However, searching for new methods for the synthesis of phthalide-based structures is still an attractive research area. , Among the methods of phthalide synthesis, one can single out the cyclization of o-ethynylbenzoates, which can proceed with varying regioselectivities along the exo- or endo-pathway, yielding an isocoumarin or phthalide ring, correspondingly. The investigation and systematization of the patterns of such reactions continues to arouse great interest. Baldwin’s rules can be used to consider possible directions of alkyne cyclization. − According to these rules, both exo- and endo-pathways are allowed for alkynes during the formation of either five- or six-membered rings. The preference of a certain pathway is determined by two main impacts: intrinsic stereoelectronics that arise from the differences in the orbital overlap patterns and activation barriers that combine intrinsic barriers with thermodynamic factors. For alkynes, the effect of these factors varies depending on the type of cyclization: anionic, cationic, or radical. For electrophilic cyclization, regioselectivity depends on the influence of substituents on cation stability and the nature of the nucleophile.

In particular, electrophile-promoted cyclizations have been reported for various 2-ethynylbenzoates under the action of different types of electrophiles. In most cases, reactions proceed as a 6-endo-dig process and lead to the formation of isocoumarin derivatives. − The formation of 5-exo-dig-cyclization products has been described only for the unsubstituted at the triple bond and trimethylsilyl-substituted methyl 2-ethynylbenzoate. − The authors suggest that the change in cyclization regioselectivity is associated with the formation of a benzyl-type cation despite the presence of an acceptor ester group in the aromatic ring. Electrophile-promoted cyclizations of halogenated 2-ethynylbenzoates have not been previously described.

Several examples of functionalized iodoalkyne iodocyclization have been reported, which can proceed as an exo- − or endo-process, − yielding cycles with diiodomethylidene fragment or vic-diiodoheteroindenes. Subsequent modifications of the resulting diiodides by Sonogashira cross-coupling give conjugated enyne systems (Scheme A) or enediynes (Scheme B). At the same time, valuable biological properties of lactones with a gemdihalomethylene group are described, as well as the possibility of their use in the synthesis of biologically active substances.

The Sonogashira cross-coupling between an aryl halide (or other cross-coupling participants such as triflate, diazonium salt, or sulfonium salts) and terminal alkynes − is a common approach to the preparation of π-conjugated systems that exhibit fluorescence properties. These systems contain acetylene spacers such as oligo­(arylene ethynylene)­s (OAEs), which have gained significant attention due to their wide range of applications in optoelectronics − and fluorogenic probes for bioconjugation and metal cation sensors. Arylethynyl derivatives of aromatic carbocycles and heterocycles have also held interest due to their valuable photophysical properties. Thus, aggregation-induced emission (AIE) − of such compounds is a useful tool for the design of new fluorescent probes. −

This study describes the iodocyclization of (iodoethynyl)­benzoate, which proceeds regioselectively via the 5-exo-dig pathway to form 3-(diiodomethylene)­isobenzofuran-1­(3H)-one (diiodomethylidenephthalide) (Scheme C). The Sonogashira cross-coupling of (diiodomethylidene)­isobenzofuran-1­(3H)-one with terminal alkynes occurs stereoselectively, giving Z-isomers of the corresponding enynes as the main product, accompanied by the protodeiodination of the second iodine atom. In the case of aryl-substituted enynes, products exhibited fluorescence with aggregation-induced emission enhancement. Coupling products containing either one or two phthalide units with an extended π-system exhibited strong fluorescence in nonaggregating solvents. Furthermore, unlike monoethynylated phthalides, they underwent Z/E isomerization.

Moreover, the 3a -d dimerization product corresponding to diphenylbutadiyne 3′ was isolated with a yield of 80%. This observation indicates that the source of hydrogen in the deiodination process is the starting acetylene.

Cross-Coupling of Diiodomethylidenephthalide 2 with Phenylacetylenes 3a–c

To rationalize the transformation under consideration, we proposed the following mechanism of the Pd/Cu-mediated transformation of the geminal diiodide 2 into the mono- and bis-alkynylated methylenephthalids 4 and 5 (Scheme ). Insertion of Pd(0) into one C–I bond first gives the palladium­(II) intermediate 2A. Both electronic and steric arguments favor the activation of the C–I bond in the anti-position to the oxygen atom. It was shown recently that the Stille couplings of 5-(dibromomethylene)­furan-2­(5H)­one with trans-PhCHCHSnBu3 or PhSnBu3 proceed with strict E-selectivity, with the C–Br bond trans to the oxygen atom being replaced first. The Suzuki coupling of gem-dichlorometylenefuranone with phenyl boronic acid also proceed E-selectively. Accordingly, in our gem-diiodomethylidenephthalide system (compound 2), the antioriented C–I bond is most likely the kinetically preferred site for the initial oxidative addition to Pd(0). Moreover, the isolation of the side product 6, whose structure preserves the syn C–I bond, further corroborates that the primary oxidative addition of Pd(0) occurs at the more reactive anti-C–I site. The Cu-acetylide transfer led to intermediate 2B and yielded E-monoalkynylated iodomethylidenephthalide 2C after reductive elimination. However, the product 2C after the first Sonogashira reaction was not detected under any of the conditions studied due to the pronounced activating effect of the ethynyl substituent on the remaining C–I bond, which accelerates the subsequent Sonogashira reaction, affording bis-alkynylated methylidenephthalide 5.

Based on the obtained data, we assume that competing protodeiodination at the C–I antibond occurs in parallel with the formation of compound 5 (see Table ). The formation of intermediate 6 can be rationalized by proto-/deuterodepalladation of the vinyl-Pd­(II) complex 2A generated after oxidative addition. In this redox-neutral pathway, a Brønsted acid resident in the reaction medium is typically the conjugate acid of the base ([Base–H]+) or a terminal alkyne (HCCR). The observed deuterium incorporation is best explained by the rapid formation of the deuterated conjugate acid [Base–D]+ from the deuterated alkyne.

As Sunwoo Lee recently demonstrated that the Pd-catalyzed decarboxylative alkynylation of 1-fluoro-2,2-diiodovinylarenes in DBU/DMSO led exclusively to Z-monoalkynyl fluoroalkenes. The authors attribute this selectivity to an initial deiodination that cleaves the C–I bond anti to the fluorine substituent before the coupling event. The E-selective reductive dehalogenation of a gem-dichlorofuranone has also been reported. ,, These findings are fully consistent with our observations. The intermediate 6 then enters the catalytic cycle (Scheme , right): Pd(0) inserts into the syn C–I bond, giving the σ-iodo palladium­(II) complex 6A; subsequent transmetalation with the copper acetylide generates the σ-alkynyl palladium species 6B, from which reductive elimination leads to the product 4. The product 4 predominates because protodeiodination of intermediate 2A to 6 proceeds more rapidly than the competing transmetalation of 2A to 2B under the present reaction conditions.

Proposed Mechanism for Cross-Coupling with Deuterodepalladation

The requirement for an excess of acetylene and the sizable amount of diyne can be rationalized by a facile proto-/deuterodepalladation of the vinyl–Pd­(II) intermediate 2A, which continuously feeds Pd­(II) into the reaction mixture. Reactivation of palladium then proceeds through oxidative homocoupling at palladium: two alkynyl fragments delivered (via Cu acetylides) to Pd­(II) form a bis­(ethynyl) Pd­(II) species that undergoes reductive elimination to the diyne, thereby regenerating Pd(0). Consequently, acetylene is consumed in two roles, as a coupling partner and as a sacrificial reagent that reactivates palladium, which naturally requires a higher alkyne concentration and leads to the accumulation of diyne under these conditions.

For reactions with substituted derivatives of arylacetylenes, optimization of reaction conditions was carried out using the reaction with 4-ethynylbenzonitrile 3d and its TMS derivative 3′d (Table S2 and Scheme S3). We found that with potassium fluoride, the coupling of the TMS derivative 3′d led to the product 4d with the same yield as in the case of 3d (Scheme a). A modified procedure involving sequential desilylation of TMS derivatives 3′d–f of terminal alkynes under the action of potassium fluoride and Sonogashira cross-coupling was used to obtain enynes 4d–f. In this case, deiodinated monoalkynyl products were also formed predominantly. For the donor-substituted derivative 4f, a slightly lower yield was observed compared to acceptor-substituted derivatives 4d and 4e. In addition, in the case of donor-substituted alkyne 4f, the formation of (Z)-3-(iodomethylene)­isobenzofuran-1­(3H)-one 6 as a by-product was observed in 20% yield. To further confirm the double-bond configuration, data from the NOESY spectra were used (SI, Section 2.4.2). The spectra showed cross peaks corresponding to the proton at the double bond and the proton of the aromatic ring in the heterocycle (highlighted in red on Scheme , for more details see the Supporting Information).

Arylethynylenes are prospective substrates for the investigation of aggregation-induced emission (AIE). − We investigated the luminescent properties of compounds synthesized and the influence of the fraction of water as an aggregating solvent on the emission intensity for 4a (see SI, Figure S19). Indeed, the luminescence intensity significantly depends on the proportion of water in the solution. The maximum luminescence intensity was observed for a solution containing 90% water. A further increase in the proportion of water to 99% leads to a slight decrease in the luminescence intensity. Using the dynamic light scattering method, the presence of 91.4–114.5 nm particles in a THF:H2O 1:99 solution was established. All of the Sonogashira cross-coupling products were investigated by absorption and emission spectroscopy in THF:H2O 1:99 solutions. Aryl-substituted compounds 4a and 4d–f show luminescent properties, and the emission maxima for these compounds are in the range of 443–499 nm (Scheme b). Compounds 4b, 4c, and 5a do not show significant luminescent properties (see SI, Figures S26–S28), which is probably due to a reduction in the conjugation system in the case of 4b and 4c and the absence of aryl moieties, which are likely important for aggregation. In compound 5a, on the contrary, the additional aryl fragment may create steric hindrance to the formation of aggregates and interfere with AIE.

Taking into account that oligoarylenethynylene containing bis­(alkoxy)­aryl units are widely used in the design of fluorescent oligomers, , we decided to synthesize additional examples of alkynylated methylidenephthalides bearing a bis­(octyloxy)­phenyl moiety. We tried two approaches to synthesize 7. First, we obtained 1,4-diethynyl-2,5-bis­(octyloxy)­benzene 9 using the Sonogashira cross-coupling between 1,4-diiodo-2,5-bis­(octyloxy)­benzene 10 and TMS acetylene, followed by the removal of the TMS group (see SI, Scheme S4) and the subsequent Sonogashira cross-coupling of the bis-alkyne 9 with heterocycle 2. However, it did not allow us to obtain the desired product due to the formation of a complex mixture. Therefore, an alternative route was used: compound 4c was introduced in the Sonogashira cross-coupling with diiodide 10, which allows us to obtain the target compound with a yield of 52% as a mixture of three isomers ZZ:EE:ZE (or three stable conformers) in the ratio 1:1:1.3 (Scheme a). To check whether the obtained product is really a mixture of conformers at room temperature (s-cis, s-cis; s-trans, s-trans; s-cis, s-trans), we decided to measure the NMR at 50 °C to achieve signal coalescence. No coalescence was observed; only the relative intensities of the methylidene H signals changed. It was found that when heated, isomers can transform into each other: the ratio ZZ:EE:ZE was 1:3:2.5 when shooting the spectrum at 50 °C and 1:6:5 when shooting the spectrum at rt after stirring the sample at 50 °C during 1 h (for details see SI). Based on the observed changes, the proportion of the EE-isomer increased, suggesting that it is more stable. The ZZ-isomer is less stable and transforms into the other two isomers.

To obtain the additional compound with an extended π-system 8, the Sonogashira cross-coupling of bisacetylene 11 with heterocycle 2 was used. Compound 8 was obtained as a mixture of geometric isomers with a ratio E/Z 3.7/1 in 47% yield (Scheme b). To determine the configuration of double bonds in geometric isomers, the NOESY spectrum was used (see SI, Figure S11). The cross-peak of the double-bond proton and the aryl proton was observed for the Z-isomer.

Compounds 7 and 8 also possess luminescent properties. However, it was found that the luminescence properties of 8 are significantly stronger in THF than in a mixture of aggregating solvents (THF/H2O, 1:99). Thus, it can be concluded that aggregation does not enhance the luminescent properties of compound 8 (Scheme c). Additionally, absolute quantum yields in a THF:H2O 1:99 solution were measured for compounds demonstrating luminescent properties. Compounds 4a and 4d–f show quantum yields from 4.1 to 5.9%. The maximum quantum yield was observed for compound 8 and amounted to 6.4%. For compounds 7 and 8, which possess more intense luminescence in THF, quantum yields for THF solutions were 75.2 and 60%, respectively (see SI, Table S4).

Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated that the iodine-promoted cyclization of 2-(iodoethynyl)­benzoate occurs through the 5-exo-dig pathway, leading to the formation of diiodomethylidenephthalide 2. We observed the nontrivial behavior of diiodomethylidenephthalide 2 under the Sonogashira cross-coupling conditions with various monoacetylenes. In each case, the main product was the monoethynylated methylidenephthalide with a Z-configuration of the double bond, lacking the second iodine atom. We found that the source of the hydrogen in the protodeiodinated Sonogashira cross-coupling products is the starting alkyne, which is converted to the homocoupled diacetylene. In the case of π-extended Sonogashira cross-coupling, products are a mixture of geometric isomers that can interconvert. The arylethynylated methylidenephthalides have fluorescence properties in water that are of an AIE nature. At the same time, methylidenephthalides with extended π-conjugation fluoresce in a nonaggregating solvent with high quantum yields. This means that both types of ethynylated methylidenephthalide have potential for further development of fluorescent materials for a variety of applications.

General Procedure for Sonogashira Cross-Coupling with the Preparative Isolation of the Target Products

To a stirred solution of the diiodide 2 (1.00 equiv) in DMFabs (c = 0.15 M) were added Pd­(PPh3)4 (5.00 mol %), CuI (15.0 mol %), and DIPA (4.00 equiv) or KF (5.00 equiv) for the TMS-protected alkyne. The reaction vial was degassed using a freeze–pump–thaw technique over three degassing cycles. After that, alkyne or TMS-protected alkyne (4.00 equiv) was added in one portion. The reaction mixture was stirred at 45 °C for 2.5 h (TLC control). After the completion of the reaction, the reaction mixture was cooled, poured into a saturated aqueous solution of NH4Cl, and extracted with ethyl acetate two times. The combined organic layers were washed with a saturated solution of NH4Cl and two times with brine, dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, and concentrated under reduced pressure to give the crude product.

Compact accelerator-based production of carrier-free 177Lu from 18 MeV D+ on [176Yb]Yb2O3
Authors: Austin A Morris, Tianhao Wei, Zhi Wang, Ying Xia, Meiyun Han, Yuanrong Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecent EMA and FDA approvals of Lu-DOTATATE and Lu-PSMA-617 have led to increased demand for radiotherapeutic 177Lu, due to its promising potential to treat castration-resistant neuroendocrine cancers. Conventional reactor production methods pose challenges related to cost, waste management, and local availability. In comparison, accelerators produce less waste, have lower maintenance costs, and can be directly integrated into hospital settings. In this study, we evaluate the production of radiotherapeutic 177Lu using a 10 mA, 18 MeV D+ compact linear accelerator design. The design consists of a single radio-frequency quadrupole (RFQ) and seven drift tube linacs (DTLs) that achieve a beam efficiency of 98.5% over a total length of 12m. Deuteron activations on a 99% enriched [176Yb]Yb2O3 target are estimated using experimental and simulated excitation functions.ResultsA circular target with a radius of 1 cm and 0.36 mm thickness is selected to optimize the yield of 177Lu while minimizing the production of undesirable radioisotopes, including 174g+mLu and 177mLu. Model calculations indicate that the accelerator design can produce 11.3 μg of 177Lu per hour. A 5-day irradiation is expected to yield approximately 1.07 mg of 177Lu (4.4 TBq), while a 12-day irradiation can produce up to 1.9 mg (7.8 TBq). Following a 2-day processing period, the specific activity of the 5-day irradiated sample is projected to approach 0.6 TBq/mg, with a radiopurity of approximately 99.8%. The minimal burn-up of the Yb2O3 target suggests it may be recycled and reused over multiple irradiations.ConclusionsThe study confirms the feasibility of accelerator-based 177Lu production as an alternative to existing reactor-based methods. The 10 mA, 18 MeV D+ RFQ-DTL design achieves an exceptionally high 177Lu radiopurity and a competitive overall yield, which can meet the dose requirements of thousands of patients.Keywords: Lutetium(177Lu) production, Ytterbium(176Yb) target, Linear accelerator, Radiopharmaceutical, Neuroendocrine cancer

Introduction

Several studies have reported on the potential of 177Lu as an in vivo radioisotope useful for the treatment of metastatic lesions and neuroendocrine cancers (Dash et al. 2015; Nagai et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2023). 177Lu is a desirable radioisotope due to its intermediate half-life (t1/2 = 6.65 days) and moderate energy release. It beta decays to the stable ground state of 177Hf (78.6% of the time), a 0.25 MeV excited state (9.2%), or a 0.32 MeV excited state (12.2%). Compared to other therapeutic radioisotopes (such as 90Y, 131I, and 186Re), the localized beta particles produced by 177Lu have a smaller mean penetration range in tissue of approximately 0.62mm (Patell et al. 2023). Since the excited states also produce low-energy gamma rays—113 keV (6.6%) and 208 keV (11%)—SPECT/CT image-guided, theranostic (therapy and diagnosis) treatments are possible (Praena 2020; Alliot et al. 2015; Vogel et al. 2021; Morgan et al. 2023). Furthermore, 177Lu can be combined with other radionuclides, such as 67Cu and 90Y, for tandem use (Shao 2023).

For targeted radiotherapy, lutetium can be added to a variety of molecular carriers, antibodies, and peptides to preferentially accumulate in cell receptors overexpressed on tumor surfaces (Dash et al. 2015; Alliot et al. 2015; Ladrière et al. 2023). Examples include prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) and peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT). An amino acid transporter protein, PSMA is expressed 100 to 1000 times more in prostate cancer than in normal tissue (Ribes et al. 2024; Patell et al. 2023; Sartor et al. 2021; Mendez et al. 2023; Ferdinandus et al. 2018). Successful treatments of patients with metastatic lesions and prostate cancer have led to recent approval by the EMA and FDA of 177Lu-DOTATATE and 177Lu-PSMA-617 for neuroendocrine cancers and PSMA-positive metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) (Shao 2023; Kong et al. 2024; Ladrière et al. 2023; Bhardwaj et al. 2017; Ribes et al. 2024; Sartor et al. 2021; Thaiss et al. 2023; Ahmadzadehfar et al. 2024; Bokhout et al. 2025). Early results indicate that 177Lu-based drugs have limited side effects and a better toxicity profile compared to other radionuclides (Mendez et al. 2023; Thaiss et al. 2023; Ferdinandus et al. 2018). Consequently, the demand for 177Lu has increased in recent years, with a particular focus on developing production facilities that are better equipped to meet treatment requirements.

In this study, we present the general design of an 18 MeV D+ linear accelerator (linac) optimized for the production of high-purity 177Lu. We develop a model based on experimental (Nagai et al. 2022; Hermanne et al. 2006; Manenti et al. 2011) and simulated (Koning et al. 2023) excitation functions to optimize the target width and yield of deuteron activations from a Yb2O3 target enriched to 99% 176Yb. Based on our accelerator and target parameters, the expected radiopurity of carrier-free 177Lu is estimated to exceed 99.8% after a 5-day irradiation. Longer irradiations offer similar radiopurity and produce 1 to 2 mg of 177Lu. Compared with previous studies using reactor-based production, we show that accelerator-based production generates less target burn-up, reduced waste, higher radiopurity, and competitive overall yield. Since an accelerator facility is generally safer and easier to maintain than a high-flux reactor, the design is expected to better meet therapeutic production demand.

Methods

Accelerator design

Currently, 177Lu is most commonly produced by neutron activation in a high-flux reactor (Dash et al. 2015; Knapp et al. 2005). This involves either irradiating an ordinary lutetium target or an enriched 176Yb target (12.9% natural abundance), giving the reactions

In the indirect route, 177Yb (t1/2 = 1.91 h) is produced, which quickly beta-decays into 177Lu. The drawbacks of reactor-based production include the high thermal neutron flux required (1014 n/cm2/s) as well as the 2.6% natural abundance of 176Lu (t1/2∼4×1010 y). For both direct and indirect production routes, high neutron activation cross sections lead to significant target burn-up and radioactive waste, which also reduce the specific activity of 177Lu (Bhardwaj et al. 2017; Zhernosekov et al. 2008). The waste products include 174gLu (t1/2=3.31 y), 174mLu (t1/2=120.9 d), 176mLu (t1/2=3.66 d), and 177mLu (t1/2=160.4 d) (Koning et al. 2019). Due to these contaminants, target waste and patient urine must be disposed of in a dedicated waste management facility (Praena 2020). Large amounts of stable 175Lu in the target also reduce the specific activity of the desired 177Lu product.

By comparison, accelerator-based production methods generate one-tenth as much nuclear waste, require lower maintenance costs (including decommissioning), and have a better safety profile (Wang et al. 2022). Proton beams are inadequate for lutetium production as a result of small Yb/Lu activation cross sections. However, deuteron beams, with moderate stopping power and high neutron-stripping cross sections, provide an ideal choice (Tárkányi et al. 2020).

For higher deuteron energies, 174g+mLu production cross sections increase (Shao 2023). Hence, a higher purity of 177Lu is obtained for deuteron beam energies below 20 MeV (Nagai et al. 2022). In this range, metastable 176mLu is produced but decays rapidly, while the production of longer-lived 177mLu is negligible due to its high spin value Jπ=23/2- (Tárkányi et al. 2013).

To produce 177Lu with high purity and high yield, we designed a 10 mA, 17.9 MeV D+ linear accelerator (linac) (Xia et al. 2025). Our design consists of a 0.02 MeV injector, 3.8 m radio-frequency quadrupole (RFQ) accelerator, and seven drift tube linacs (DTLs). For the RFQ, a 2.1 MeV beam is generated in continuous wave (CW) mode with a simulated transmission efficiency of 98.63% (Wei et al. 2023). The RFQ requires four 1200 L/s vacuum pumps and one 440 L/s ion pump to maintain a vacuum pressure of 10-6 Pa. For the high-energy beam section, each DTL consists of a power coupler and six tuners (Wei et al. 2024). A crossbar H-mode (CH) cavity increases the ion energy across all seven DTLs from 2.11 MeV to 17.92 MeV over 8.17 m. The transverse focusing between cavities is achieved using quadrupole doublet and triplet magnets depicted in Fig. 2. The beam transmission in each CH-DTL is nearly 100%, while the entire line efficiency is 98.5%, due mostly to particles lost at low energy in the RFQ bunching section. The RFQ-DTL system operates in CW mode at a frequency of 162.5 MHz, for the parameters given in Tables 1 and 2.

Target optimization

For an ytterbium target, metallic 176Yb (6.9 g/cm3) is separated from silicate mineral gadolinite and synthesized into crystalline Yb2O3 (9.2 g/cm3) (Haynes 2016). Yb2O3 is used as a target instead of pure Yb due to its better chemical and thermal stability, along with its higher melting point (2,355 ∘C compared to 819 ∘C) (Praena 2020; Haynes 2016). Although natural ytterbium contains only 12.6% 176Yb, it may be enriched to more than 97% (Dash et al. 2015; Knapp et al. 2005). A recent study by Yang et al. reported pilot-scale production of 177Lu using a 99.33% [176Yb]Yb2O3 enriched target acquired from Rosatom (Yang et al. 2023). Following irradiation, a Yb2O3 target can be dissolved in dilute mineral acid. Since Yb ions exist in a +2 oxidation state, forming an insoluble sulfate, micro amounts of +3 Lu may be separated from macro amounts of Yb, and synthesized into water-soluble LuCl3 (Dash et al. 2015; Haynes 2016). Therefore, the 17.9 MeV D+ design is well suited for optimal 177Lu production. For a 0.36 mm Yb2O3 target, most ions are expected to deposit around 10 MeV of their initial kinetic energy. As a result, the target will be cooler than if the full 17.9 MeV was deposited, while the production losses will remain negligible. To further enhance target cooling, a water-cooled copper-backed plate—approximately 1 mm thick—can be incorporated into the assembly. A related study by Praena et al. examined water circulation speeds ranging from 5 to 15 m/s, which are also expected to be suitable for our device (Praena 2020).

Discussion

Although the main advantage of the accelerator-based production method is its higher radiopurity, its overall yield is also competitive with reactor-based methods. Here, the lower production cross section for the deuterons is offset by their higher flux—1.99×1016 d/cm2/s on the target compared to 2×1015 n/cm2/s for the Oak Ridge High Flux Isotope Reactor (ORNL HFIR) and 1×1014 n/cm2/s for the Missouri University Research Reactor (MURR). For our result, x-markers represent target burn-up given by equation 19, which results in only a marginal difference.

To determine the radiopurity of an isotope I, we divide its activity αI(t)=λINI(t) by the sum of all activities of the same nuclide. Although the radiopurity of 176mLu initially exceeds that of 177Lu–-67.14% compared to 32.82%—it is quickly surpassed. After a 2-day processing time, the radiopurity of 177Lu is 99.81%, while that of 176mLu is just 0.028%. For a 12-day irradiation and 2-day processing time, the radiopurity of each isotope is nearly the same. For longer processing times, the radiopurity of 174g+mLu and 177mLu increase since their half-lives are longer than that of 177Lu. However, the activity of these longer-lived isotopes is generally negligible within the time frame during which the product can be used. After one half-life of 177Lu, or 6.647 days following a 5-day irradiation, the radiopurity of 177Lu remains above 99.73%.

Producing 1.9 mg of 177Lu—77% more than the initial amount—requires an additional 7 days of irradiation, a 140% increase in irradiation time. Optimal irradiation times are thus left to be determined by target processing costs. Due to saturation, the concentrations of 177Yb and 176mLu are not affected by longer irradiations, while the production of 174g+mLu and 177mLu is insignificant.

For direct 177Lu production by irradiating an 82% [176Lu]LuCl3 with thermal neutrons (ϕn=1.2×1014 n/cm2/s), Sairanbayev et al. estimate activities ranging from 364 to 537 GBq/mg for irradiations lasting between 6.25 and 17 days (Sairanbayev et al. 2021). The corresponding ratio of 177mLu to 177Lu activities was found to be less than 0.025. In our study, this ratio is 1.03×10-3 for 5-day and 1.37×10-3 for 12-day irradiations. After a 5-day irradiation and 2-day processing time, the specific activity of 177Lu, averaged over the mass of all radionuclides, is 3.75 TBq/mg. Considering that long-lived 176Lu and stable 175Lu are also produced by (d, 2n) and (d, 3n) reactions, the specific activity averaged over the total lutetium mass is found to be 594 GBq/mg.

After irradiation, the Yb2O3 target can be dissolved in a 4 M solution of hydrochloric acid (Kuznetsov et al. 2019). Macro amounts of Yb are separated from the solution to isolate the 177Lu product, which is ultimately precipitated into LuCl3. Between 84–95% of Lu can be retained in this process (Yang et al. 2023) with Yb impurities less than 10-4% (Kuznetsov et al. 2019). As the overall processing only takes several hours, our design is expected to meet the requirement that radiochemical purity exceed 99%.

Conclusion

Currently, 177Lu treatments cost around $14,500 per dose and require at least 4 doses in total (Vogel et al. 2021; Morgan et al. 2023). Given that these doses are administered every 6 to 8 weeks, it is necessary to localize production to ensure a reliable supply. Compared to reactor-based production methods, accelerators offer several advantages, such as easier supervision, enhanced safety, and lower maintenance costs (Wang et al. 2022). In addition, accelerators produce less nuclear waste and are capable of yielding a greater radiopurity of the desired isotope.

In this study, we demonstrated the feasibility of accelerator-based 177Lu production and introduced new methods, based on experimental and simulated excitation functions, that can be used to optimize future accelerator designs. This includes the calculation of the target-averaged cross section ⟨σ⟩T and its corresponding relations, as provided in Table 4.

In conclusion, we presented the design of a 10 mA, 17.9 MeV D+ linac intended to irradiate a 99% enriched [176Yb]Yb2O3 target. We showed that our design is capable of producing 4.4 TBq of 177Lu after 5 days and 6.4 TBq after 12 days, which meets the dose requirements for thousands of patients. The minimal burn-up of the target suggests that remaining Yb2O3 may be recycled and reused in multiple irradiations. Compared to traditional neutron-based production methods, our design achieves a competitive 177Lu yield (1 mg in the first 5 days) and exceptionally high radiopurity (>99.8%). This design is expected to play an integral role in the future production of 177Lu and other radioisotopes for the treatment of metastatic lesions and neuroendocrine cancers.

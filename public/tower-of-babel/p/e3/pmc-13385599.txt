Tumors >2 cm in node-negative, HER2-positive breast cancer: comparing neoadjuvant dual-targeted versus adjuvant single-targeted treatment pathways in real-world practice
Authors: Xiaoduo Li, Kexuan Feng, Junjie Liu, Xiaoqian Li, Rui Zhang, Huimin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:The optimal systemic treatment for node-negative, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer with tumors >2 cm remains unclear. De-escalation is commonly used for smaller tumors, but evidence supporting the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway in high tumor burden disease is limited.Objectives:To compare pathological response and exploratory disease-free survival (DFS) between the neoadjuvant dual-targeted and adjuvant single-targeted treatment pathways, focusing on tumors >2 cm.Design:Retrospective, single-center cohort study.Methods:We identified 518 consecutive patients treated from 2015 to 2023 at a tertiary academic center. In a guideline-recommended cohort (N = 426), patients managed via the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway were compared with those managed via the adjuvant single-targeted pathway. Propensity scores were estimated using logistic regression and applied via overlap weighting to balance covariates. DFS was assessed using overlap-weighted Cox regression, including a tumor-size interaction (⩽2 vs >2 cm). Sensitivity analyses addressed calendar-time confounding, truncation at 24 months with bootstrapped risk differences, and surgery-date landmark analysis. Reporting followed Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.Results:Overlap weighting achieved good balance (all standardized mean differences <0.10). In the guideline-recommended cohort (18 DFS events), the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway was not significantly associated with DFS (hazard ratio (HR) 0.26; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.04–1.55; p = 0.138), and the size-by-treatment interaction was not significant (p = 0.123). In tumors >2 cm (N = 247), the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway was associated with a pathological complete response rate of 56.9% (58/102) and an exploratory DFS signal (HR 0.08; 95% CI: 0.01–0.59). At 24 months, 0 versus 5 DFS events resulted in an absolute risk difference of 7.0% (95% CI: 1.1%–14.0%). Sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results, though estimates for tumors ⩽2 cm were imprecise.Conclusion:In this exploratory real-world cohort, the neoadjuvant dual-targeted treatment pathway for node-negative, HER2-positive breast cancer with tumors >2 cm was associated with high pathological response rates and an exploratory DFS signal, though sparse events and observational biases limit survival conclusions. Size-informed, risk-adapted strategies are needed.Trial registration: Not applicable.Keywords: HER2-positive breast cancer, neoadjuvant therapy, node-negative, pertuzumab, trastuzumab, tumor size

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy in women worldwide, 1 and the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive sub-type accounts for approximately 15%–20% of cases. 2 HER2-positive disease is characterized by amplification and over-expression of the HER2 gene, which activates downstream signaling pathways and drives aggressive tumor proliferation.3,4 Although this sub-type was historically associated with poor outcomes, HER2-targeted therapy, beginning with trastuzumab, has markedly improved prognosis and extended survival.2,5,6

In the setting of precision medicine, treatment of early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, particularly node-negative disease, has tended to follow two paths: de-escalation for lower-risk tumors and escalation for higher-risk presentations. 7 The APT trial defined a standard option for small node-negative tumors (T1N0), showing that adjuvant weekly paclitaxel plus trastuzumab achieved a 10-year invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) of 91.3%.8,9 For patients considered higher risk, adding pertuzumab to chemotherapy as escalation with neoadjuvant dual-targeted strategies increases pathological complete response (pCR) in the neoadjuvant setting10,11 and improves iDFS in the adjuvant setting. 12 However, long-term follow-up from APHINITY suggests that the iDFS benefit of adjuvant dual-targeted pathway is concentrated in node-positive disease, with no statistically significant benefit in the overall node-negative population.13,14

This contrast has left uncertainty about how intensively to treat node-negative patients with larger primary tumors, particularly those larger than 2 cm. Clinical guidelines, including NCCN and ESMO, generally support de-escalated strategies for lower-risk node-negative disease,15,16 but tumor size beyond 2 cm often leads clinicians to choose more intensive therapy in routine practice. In many centers, escalation means neoadjuvant chemotherapy with dual HER2 blockade, aiming to achieve pCR and potentially improve long-term outcomes.10,11,17,18 The supporting evidence, however, largely comes from trials enriched for higher-risk or node-positive populations rather than from node-negative cohorts. Against this backdrop, treatment patterns in real-world practice have shifted quickly. The growing use of neoadjuvant pertuzumab has occurred at different times across settings, creating non-concurrent changes in practice and widening variation in risk-adapted treatment selection.19,20

These evolving real-world treatment patterns may influence early outcomes, so it is important to assess their clinical impact. Comparisons between upfront surgery followed by de-escalated therapy and neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway are challenging in observational data because of staging asymmetry (pathological vs clinical node negativity) and era-related changes in practice. We therefore conducted a real-world cohort study that framed these strategies as alternative clinical decision pathways rather than simply different drug regimens. We used propensity score overlap weighting to reduce selection bias and focus the analysis on patients for whom clinical equipoise was most plausible. In this weighted cohort, we evaluated whether the high pathological response rates observed with the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway were accompanied by a biologically plausible, exploratory survival pattern. We also assessed whether pathway-specific outcomes varied by tumor burden and provide context-specific real-world evidence to inform risk-stratified discussions on treatment pathways in early-stage, HER2-positive breast cancer.

Methods

Study design and patient population

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University. We reviewed electronic medical records of female patients diagnosed with primary invasive HER2-positive breast cancer from January 2015 through December 2023. HER2-positive status was defined as an immunohistochemistry score of 3+ or a positive in situ hybridization result, in accordance with ASCO/CAP guidelines. 21

We included patients who were clinically node-negative (cN0) at diagnosis and received perioperative taxane-based chemotherapy. Clinical node-negative status was determined at the initial treatment decision using documented axillary assessment, including physical examination, axillary imaging (ultrasound with or without MRI), and, when performed, a negative needle biopsy of suspicious nodes. The analytic cohort was defined using sequential exclusions (Figure 1). Patients were excluded if lymph node involvement or distant metastasis was present at diagnosis, key clinicopathological data were missing, non-taxane-based regimens were used, fewer than four cycles of perioperative chemotherapy were given, or follow-up was insufficient. The final analytic cohort included 518 patients. The Institutional Review Board approved the study and waived informed consent because of the retrospective design (approval no. XJTU1AF2026LSYY-0108). The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement 22 (Supplemental File 2).

Within the primary analysis cohort, patients were categorized into the adjuvant single-targeted pathway (AC–Single) and neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway (NAC–Dual) subgroups.

Treatment cohorts and definitions

Patients were grouped based on whether treatment aligned with contemporary clinical guidelines.15,16,23 The primary analysis included the guideline-recommended cohort (N = 426), which reflected two clinical management pathways.

In the adjuvant single-targeted pathway (AC–Single; N = 308), patients underwent primary surgery and then received adjuvant taxane-based chemotherapy with single-agent targeted therapy. This pathway was restricted to patients with pathologically node-negative (pN0) disease, consistent with the eligibility criteria of the APT trial.8,9 In the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway (NAC–Dual; N = 118), patients received neoadjuvant taxane-based chemotherapy with dual-targeted therapy followed by surgery. Eligibility required cN0 status at presentation.

Tumor burden was dichotomized at 2 cm to define an exploratory subgroup with higher tumor burden (>2 cm). A separate non-guideline cohort (N = 92) was retained for supplementary analyses.

Data collection and outcomes

We extracted baseline information from the medical records, including age, year of diagnosis, clinical tumor stage (cT), estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) status, 24 the Ki-67 index, 25 and the chemotherapy backbone. We also recorded details of surgical axillary evaluation and post-treatment nodal status (ypN).

The primary endpoint was disease-free survival (DFS), defined as the time from initiation of systemic treatment to the first locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis, contralateral invasive breast cancer, or death from any cause. Because comparisons between neoadjuvant and adjuvant pathways can introduce immortal time bias, we prespecified a landmark sensitivity analysis in which time zero was set to the date of surgery. 26

Secondary endpoints included pCR, defined as no residual invasive cancer in the breast or axillary lymph nodes (ypT0/is ypN0) after neoadjuvant therapy. 18

Statistical analysis

We estimated propensity scores for management under the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway using multivariable logistic regression, with age, clinical T stage, ER and PR status, the Ki-67 index, year of diagnosis modeled continuously, and the chemotherapy backbone as covariates. 27 We then applied overlap weighting (ATO) to improve baseline balance. Balance was assessed using absolute standardized mean differences (ASMD), with values below 0.10 indicating adequate balance. 28 Survival curves were estimated with overlap-weighted Kaplan–Meier methods, and hazard ratios (HR) were obtained from ATO-weighted Cox proportional hazards models with robust sandwich standard errors. 29 Effect modification by clinical tumor size (⩽2 vs >2 cm) was evaluated using a prespecified treatment-by-size interaction term.

Given extreme event sparsity, we prespecified unweighted Firth penalized likelihood regression for exploratory subgroup analyses to reduce monotone likelihood.30,31 We also conducted three prespecified sensitivity analyses within the high tumor burden subgroup (>2 cm) to address era-related confounding and immortal time bias. Analyses were first restricted to overlapping diagnosis years (2019–2023), with propensity scores and ATO weights re-estimated to reduce calendar-time confounding. Follow-up was then administratively truncated at 24 months, with statistical differences in the truncated survival distributions evaluated using a log-rank test. The 24-month absolute risk differences (ARD) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated by stratified bootstrap resampling (2000 replicates), with propensity scores and ATO weights re-estimated within each replicate. 32 Finally, we performed a landmark analysis that set time zero to the date of definitive surgery to mitigate immortal time bias.33,34 To further address potential residual confounding under extreme event sparsity, additional prespecified unweighted Firth models were evaluated, adjusting individually for baseline Ki-67 index and year of diagnosis modeled continuously.

All analyses were performed in R (version 4.4.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Tests were two-sided. Point estimates and p values were interpreted descriptively as hypothesis-generating signals of comparative treatment.

Results

Baseline characteristics and propensity score weighting

This study included 518 female patients with HER2-positive primary invasive breast cancer who were cN0 at diagnosis and received perioperative taxane-based chemotherapy (Figure 1). The guideline-recommended cohort comprised 426 patients, including 308 patients managed under the adjuvant single-targeted pathway (AC–Single) and 118 patients managed under the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway (NAC–Dual).

Baseline characteristics differed between groups before weighting (Table 1). The NAC–Dual group had a later year of diagnosis than the AC–Single group (mean 2021.9 vs 2019.3; ASMD = 1.439) and a higher proportion of tumors larger than 2 cm (86.4% vs 47.1%; ASMD = 0.920). The chemotherapy backbone also varied: platinum-containing regimens were used in 70.3% of the NAC–Dual group, whereas the AC–Single group received platinum-containing (46.8%) or anthracycline-containing (30.2%) regimens. Median follow-up was 25.0 months in the NAC–Dual group and 43.0 months in the AC–Single group.

Heterogeneity of treatment effect and tumor burden

In the guideline-recommended cohort (N = 426; 18 events), the overlap-weighted Cox model estimated an HR for DFS of 0.26 (95% CI: 0.04–1.55; p = 0.138) for the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway (NAC–Dual) versus the adjuvant single-targeted pathway (AC–Single; Figure 2(a)). We assessed effect modification by tumor size (⩽2 vs >2 cm) using a prespecified treatment-by-size interaction term (Figure 2(b)). The primary overlap-weighted Cox model did not show statistically significant evidence of effect modification by tumor size (interaction p = 0.123). In the prespecified exploratory sensitivity analysis using an unweighted Firth penalized model to account for sparse events, a potential interaction was observed (interaction p = 0.035), this discrepancy designates subsequent subgroup-specific evaluations as exploratory and non-confirmatory. In a descriptive, unweighted analysis encompassing all four treatment pathways within the analytic cohort (N = 518), patients managed under dual-targeted pathways (NAC–Dual and AC–Dual) exhibited a numerically favorable DFS trajectory compared with those receiving single-targeted pathways (NAC–Single and AC–Single; Figure S2).

For descriptive purposes under the prespecified stratification plan, subgroup-specific estimates were evaluated (Figure 2(b)). In the >2-cm subgroup (N = 247; 13 events), the descriptive HR was 0.08 (95% CI: 0.01–0.59; p = 0.014). In the ⩽2cm subgroup (N = 179; 5 events), the estimate was highly imprecise (HR = 1.59; 95% CI: 0.17–14.71; p = 0.685). Because the primary interaction test did not reach statistical significance, these subgroup-specific estimates are presented as descriptive and non-confirmatory. In accordance with the prespecified analysis plan, subsequent exploratory efficacy and sensitivity analyses focused on the >2cm subgroup.

Exploratory efficacy in the high tumor burden subgroup

Sensitivity analyses in the high tumor burden (>2 cm) subgroup

We conducted prespecified sensitivity analyses within the >2cm subgroup (Table 3). Restricting the cohort to overlapping diagnosis years (2019–2023; N = 175, 6 events), the re-estimated overlap-weighted Cox model gave an HR of 0.08 (95% CI: 0.01–0.63; p = 0.016) for NAC–Dual versus AC–Single (Supplemental Table 1). When follow-up was administratively truncated at 24 months, no DFS events occurred among 102 patients managed under the NAC–Dual pathway, whereas 5 events occurred among 145 patients managed under the AC–Single pathway (Figure 3(b)), corresponding to a 24-month ARD of 7.0% (95% CI: 1.1%–14.0%; log-rank p = 0.06; Table 3, Panel B).

In a landmark analysis that set time zero to the date of surgery, the unweighted Firth model estimated an HR of 0.25 (95% CI: 0.03–1.07; p = 0.063; Table 3, Panel C). In additional prespecified unweighted Firth models, the HR was 0.22 (95% CI: 0.02–0.95; p = 0.041) after adjustment for baseline Ki-67 index and 0.09 (95% CI: 0.01–0.48; p = 0.004) after adjustment for year of diagnosis modeled continuously (Table 3, Panel C).

Discussion

We used propensity score overlap weighting to estimate the average treatment effect in the overlap population (ATO) and to examine real-world treatment strategy selection in cN0, HER2-positive breast cancer. In the guideline-recommended cohort, overlap-weighted estimates of DFS did not reach conventional levels of statistical significance, and the prespecified treatment-by-size interaction term did not show clear evidence of heterogeneity (interaction p = 0.123). In a separate prespecified exploratory analysis, the Firth model suggested possible effect modification (interaction p = 0.035). However, this divergence between the primary and exploratory models likely reflects the inherent sensitivity of rare-event data to statistical specifications under clinical equipoise, thereby designating any subsequent subgroup evaluations as strictly exploratory and non-confirmatory.

In descriptive analyses of patients with high tumor burden (>2 cm), early events were less frequent with the NAC–Dual pathway, with crude incidence rates of 0.32 versus 2.17 events per 100 person-years and a 24-month administratively truncated ARD of 7.0%. In contrast, estimates for tumors ⩽2 cm were imprecise and consistent with no clear early association. Because these descriptive subgroup patterns were based on few events, specifically 13 DFS events in the >2 cm subgroup and 1 event in the NAC–Dual cohort, the resulting hazard estimates are susceptible to random variation. Although the observational design and event sparsity preclude definitive conclusions regarding comparative effectiveness, these exploratory observations provide a tumor-burden-informed, hypothesis-generating framework that may support future prospective evaluation.

The exploratory early event pattern in the high tumor burden subgroup should be viewed alongside randomized evidence, including APHINITY, which did not show a significant improvement in DFS in the overall node-negative cohort.13,14 Differences in study design and risk mix may help explain this contrast. For example, the node-negative population in APHINITY likely included many low-risk T1 tumors, which could dilute any benefit when assessed at the cohort level.8,9 In the present cohort, tumors larger than 2 cm may therefore represent a higher-risk subset within node-negative disease that warrants further study rather than immediate changes in practice.

This interpretation is consistent with neoadjuvant evidence from NeoSphere10,11 and TRYPHAENA, 35 in which dual HER2 blockade increased pCR rates. In the present cohort, the pCR rate in the NAC–Dual pathway was 56.9%. Although higher pCR rates have been associated with more favorable long-term outcomes in prior studies,18,36 pCR does not replace survival endpoints in an observational comparison. Importantly, prognosis after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and surgery may be influenced by tumor response-related factors beyond pCR status. Recent evidence suggests that, among patients with residual disease, the histopathologic regression pattern of the primary tumor, including scattered versus circumscribed residual disease, may serve as an independent predictor of long-term oncologic outcomes. 37 The pathological response therefore supports biological plausibility for exploring early event differences, but residual confounding and chance findings cannot be ruled out.

In the low tumor burden subgroup (tumor size ⩽2 cm), escalation to the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway was not associated with a clear early benefit. The HR point estimate was above 1 and imprecise, reflecting the small number of events. This pattern is consistent with de-escalation evidence from APT8,9 and WSG-ADAPT-HER2+/HR−, 38 and with current guideline frameworks,15,16 which generally favor less intensive anti-HER2 strategies for small, node-negative tumors with low baseline recurrence risk. The present results therefore do not support routine use of dual-targeted strategies in this subgroup, where any incremental benefit may be limited relative to added toxicity and cost.39,40 These subgroup estimates remain descriptive and should not be taken as definitive comparative effectiveness evidence.

Median follow-up was shorter in the NAC–Dual pathway (25 months), reflecting an era effect linked to the more recent uptake of neoadjuvant pertuzumab. 23 Differential follow-up in non-concurrent cohorts can bias comparisons through asymmetric censoring and differences in time at risk. 41 This treatment-era difference reflects a non-concurrent clinical timeline and may introduce confounding related to broader improvements in the management of HER2-positive breast cancer over time. These changes include optimization of systemic chemotherapy backbones, advances in supportive care, more sensitive staging imaging, refinements in surgical techniques, and more standardized multidisciplinary management. Therefore, the more favorable outcomes observed in the NAC–Dual group may partly reflect secular improvements in clinical care rather than the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway itself. In addition, the median follow-up of 25 months primarily captures early events and does not allow a mature oncologic comparison. This limitation is particularly relevant in hormone receptor-positive/HER2-positive disease, in which late recurrence is well documented.13,14 Accordingly, these preliminary observations may change with longer follow-up.

Comparisons between neoadjuvant and adjuvant pathways can also introduce immortal time bias when time zero is not aligned across groups. 34 When time zero was set to the date of surgery in the landmark analysis, statistical significance was attenuated (p = 0.063), consistent with conditioning inference on survival to a prespecified time point under the landmark framework. 42 Overall, inference remains limited by sparse events and potential residual time-related biases.

In addition to imbalances in treatment era and follow-up, comparisons between these treatment pathways are limited by a structural asymmetry in nodal staging. In the upfront-surgery cohort, pN0 disease was confirmed after surgery, resulting in a selected population with surgically verified node-negative status. By contrast, the neoadjuvant cohort was classified as cN0 before systemic therapy. Because clinical nodal staging is less precise than pathological assessment, baseline non-equivalence may have been present; historically, approximately 15%–20% of patients classified as cN0 harbor occult nodal metastases.1,7 In the neoadjuvant pathway, such occult nodal disease could have been eradicated or downstaged to ypN0 after systemic therapy. As a result, some patients with initially higher-risk disease could have remained eligible for the NAC–Dual group, creating a structural baseline risk asymmetry between pathways that cannot be fully addressed by statistical adjustment.

The observed associations are consistent with the complementary mechanisms of trastuzumab and pertuzumab2,4,43 and with neoadjuvant trials showing higher pathological response rates with dual HER2 blockade.10,11,35 In this cohort, most patients in the NAC–Dual pathway received non-anthracycline regimens (e.g., TCbHP), consistent with randomized data comparing anthracycline-free and anthracycline-containing approaches in HER2-positive disease.5,39 Because chemotherapy backbone and calendar time were closely tied to pathway assignment, we included regimen categories (anthracycline-based, platinum-based, taxane-only) in the propensity score model and improved balance in measured backbone distributions after overlap weighting.

Even with these adjustments, the analyses cannot isolate the incremental contribution of the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway from regimen choice, era-related practice changes, or other unmeasured factors. Because chemotherapy backbone, treatment timing, and targeted therapy are clinically bundled in routine practice, these findings should be interpreted as comparisons of broader multimodal treatment strategies rather than as drug-specific therapeutic effects. Although this design does not allow the efficacy of a single drug component to be isolated, evaluation of these combined clinical pathways reflects the real-world trade-offs faced by clinicians when selecting among perioperative treatment strategies. The associations should therefore be interpreted as comparative strategy signals rather than drug-specific effects. The direction of the findings is consistent with recent institutional real-world analyses reporting favorable outcomes with neoadjuvant dual blockade,20,44 although differences in patient selection, follow-up, and endpoint definitions limit direct comparisons across studies.

Several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, structural asymmetry between clinical node-negative status (cN0) and pathologically confirmed node-negative status (pN0) may create a baseline risk imbalance between treatment pathways that cannot be fully resolved statistically. Second, non-concurrent treatment eras may introduce residual confounding related to changes in HER2-positive breast cancer management over the past decade. Third, follow-up in the NAC–Dual cohort remains relatively short, capturing early events rather than a mature oncologic comparison, particularly in HR-positive disease prone to late recurrence. Fourth, statistical inference is limited by sparse events, including only 13 DFS events in the >2cm subgroup, making hazard-based estimates unstable. Finally, overlap weighting cannot eliminate unmeasured confounding inherent to a retrospective design, and the ATO limits generalizability to the overlap population with clinical equipoise. Collectively, these limitations indicate that our findings should be interpreted as a hypothesis-generating signal rather than definitive comparative effectiveness evidence.

Conclusion

In this real-world cohort of cN0, HER2-positive breast cancer, overlap-weighted analyses suggested a descriptive, hypothesis-generating exploratory DFS signal associated with the neoadjuvant dual-targeted pathway among tumors larger than 2 cm. Although the observational design and sparse number of events preclude definitive conclusions regarding comparative effectiveness, this study provides real-world, signal-generating evidence in node-negative disease with high tumor burden. These exploratory observations should not direct routine practice, but they may inform prospective evaluation of risk-adapted, size-informed strategies.

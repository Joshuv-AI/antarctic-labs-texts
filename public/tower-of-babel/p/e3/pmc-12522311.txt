miR-1287-5p suppresses proliferation, migration, invasion and promotes apoptosis in gastric cancer cells through targeting LAMTOR3
Authors: Jinjia Zhang, Jinxin Zhang, Min Zhang, Wenwen Zhao, Huadong Wu, Rongying Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis research attempts to clarify the function of miR-1287-5p in gastric cancer (GC) and the mechanisms that underlie it.MethodsMiR-1287-5p and LAMTOR3 mRNA levels were determined using qRT-PCR.Western blot analysis was used to identify LAMTOR3 protein expression.The GC cells were assessed for proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis using the CCK-8, wound-healing, transwell invasion assay, and flow cytometry tests, respectively.The dual-luciferase reporter assay was used to validate the miR-1287-5p target.ResultsGC had lower miR-1287-5p levels than normal cells or tissues.MiR-1287-5p over-expression dramatically reduced GC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and accelerated apoptosis in contrast to the impact of miR-1287-5p knockdown.The dual-luciferase reporter experiment confirmed the direct targeting relationship between miR-1287-5p and LAMTOR3.Rescue experiments showed that GC cells proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis caused by miR-1287-5p were all reversed by concurrent over-expression or down-regulation of LAMTOR3.ConclusionsThis current study found that miR-1287-5p suppressed GC cells proliferation, migration, and invasion while increasing apoptosis by decreasing LAMTOR3 expression. This revealed that miR-1287-5p may function as a plausible therapeutic target or biomarker for GC.Keywords: Gastric cancer, MiRNA-1287-5p, LAMTOR3, Proliferation, Migration, Invasion

Introduction

GC represents a major global health challenge, ranking as the fifth most prevalent malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1, 2]. Despite advancements in surgical techniques and systemic therapies (e.g., platinum-based chemotherapy and immunotherapy), the 5-year survival rate for advanced GC remains below 30% due to frequent metastasis, therapy resistance, and recurrence [3]. Notably, Helicobacter pylori infection–detected in about 50% of the world’s population–is a primary etiological driver that promotes carcinogenesis through chronic inflammation, genomic instability, and dysregulation of signaling pathways [4]. This pathogen-induced microenvironment further accelerates cancer cell migration and invasion, correlating with poor clinical outcomes [5].

The pathogenesis of GC involves complex interactions between oncogenic signaling cascades and epigenetic regulators, particularly microRNAs (miRNAs) [6].MiRNA is a kind of endogenous small non-coding RNA with 18–25 bases and widely exists in eukaryotic cells.By binding to the 3’UTR of mRNA of target gene, miRNA can inhibit mRNA translation or promote RNA degradation [7, 8].According to several reports, the development and metastasis of GC are linked to the deregulation of miRNA expression in GC. The MUC1 gene, for instance, is negatively regulated by miR-206, which prevents GC cells from proliferation, migration, and invasion [9].By inhibiting MYO6, miR-143 and miR-145 prevent GC cells migrating and metastasis [10]. MiR-148a interacts to the 3’untranslated regions of p27 to suppress the expression of p27, thus promoting the proliferation of GC cells [11].Despite extensive characterization of miRNAs in GC, miR-1287-5p remains unexplored in this malignancy, though it demonstrates tumor-suppressive functions in colorectal and non-small cell lung cancers [12, 13].

Building on established biomarker discovery paradigms, recent pan-cancer analyses demonstrate how integrated omics approaches, particularly single-cell and bulk RNA sequencing, enable robust identification of molecular targets with therapeutic potential. As exemplified by Liu et al., such high-resolution profiling must be coupled with mechanistic validation to establish biological relevance [14].Following the proven methodology[15, 16], our study examined the levels of miR-1287-5p in GC cells and tissues and the effects of miR-1287-5p overexpression or knockdown on transfected GC cell’ proliferation, migration, invasion and apoptosis were further illustrated. Furthermore, we investigated the direct target of miR-1287-5p, which will help us better understand the molecular mechanism of GC. Ultimately, molecular discoveries can be linked to clinical prospects and are of great significance for potential treatment methods [17].

Materials and methods

Patient tissue samples

From September 2024 to December 2024, 60 GC patients who received surgery at the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University had their paired tumor tissues and nearby normal tissues (three to four centimeters away from the tumor tissue) gathered.Following surgical excision, all tissue samples were taken right away and kept at −80 °C. None of the trial participants received anticancer treatments prior to surgery.We got written informed permission from each subject.For this investigation, ethical authorization was given by the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University’s ethics committee(2024-R445).

Cell culture and transfection

A non-tumourigenic gastric mucosal epithelial cell line (GES-1) and human GC cell lines (MKN45,AGS) were supplied by the Chinese Academy of Sciences cell bank in Shanghai, China.These cells were cultivated at 37 °C in a medium that included 10% fetal bovine serum in 5% CO2 while being kept in liquid nitrogen.For genetic manipulations, knockdown (KD) refers to suppression of endogenous miR-1287-5p using specific inhibitors, while overexpression (OE) denotes enhancement achieved through mimic transfection.Using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA), the inhibitor, mimics and negative control (NC) of miR-1287-5p synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) were transfected into the aforementioned cell lines as directed to achieve KD or OE of miR-1287-5p.

Real-time quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain Reaction(RT-qPCR)

Total RNA from frozen tumor samples and cell lines was extracted using the Trizol reagent (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The RNAs were then reverse-transcribed into synthesis of complementary DNAs (cDNAs) by Reverse Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).The SYBR Select Master Mix was then used for RT-qPCR.As an internal control, U6 or GAPDH was employed.The LAMTOR3 and miR-1287-5p expression levels were normalized by GAPDH and U6, respectively, yielding a 2−ΔΔCt value. In this study, the RT-qPCR primer sequences were as follows:

miR-1287-5P-F: 5′-TGCTGGATCAGTGGTTCGAGTC-3′;

miR-1287-5P-R : 5′-TGGTGTCGTGGAGTCG-3′;

U6-F: 5′-CAAATTCGTGAAGCGTT-3′;

U6-R: 5′-TCACTGGTGTCGTGG-3′；

LAMTOR3-F:5′-TCCTGCGAGAGAAGGGGGTT-3′;

LAMTOR3-R:5′-TGCTCTGGAGCATTGTCATTTGCC-3′;

GAPDH-F:5′-GACCTGACCTGCCGTCTA-3′;

GAPDH-R:5′-AGGAGTGGGTGTCGCTGT-3′

Western blot

Western blotting was performed following established protocols for detecting target protein expression changes, as demonstrated in some studies [18, 19].This approach was widely adopted for its sensitivity in detecting biologically relevant protein changes.Using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), total proteins were extracted from tissues and cells. A BCA kit (Beyotime) was used to measure the protein concentration.Then following SDS-PAGE separation, the proteins were placed onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). As directed by the manufacturer, membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for an hour at 37 °C after proteins were treated with primary antibodies at 4 °C for a whole night, as instructed by the manufacturer. Finally, ECL reagents (Beyotime) were used to observe protein bands.

CCK-8, wound-healing and transwell invasion assay

To measure GC cell proliferation, the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) test was used. Following stable transfection,96-well plates were seeded with GC cells. A total of 2000–3000 cells with 100 µl fresh medium were seeded per well for testing. Then the cell medium was swapped out for one that included the CCK-8 reagent (1:10), and the mixture was incubated for two hours. We measured the absorbance at 450 nm. Data were collected from triplicate samples in three independent experiments.

To assess cell migration, wound-healing assay was used.After being moved to a 6-well plate, the appropriate cells were co-transfected with miR-1287-5p inhibitor or mimics. A 10-µl pipette tip was used to form a scraped line once the cell fusion reached 100%.The exfoliated cells were washed using sterile phosphate buffered saline and the residual cells were then cultured for twenty-four hours in serum-free media.Following scratching, the migratory cells were lastly photographed under a microscope at 0 and 24 h.

The cell invasion was assessed using the Transwell Invasion Assay.The cell solution (1 × 105) was put onto the upper part of a 24-well Transwell plate with a membrane precoated with Matrigel (Corning, NY, USA).In the meantime, 20% FBS was introduced to the lower transwell chamber, and incubated for 48 h at 37 °C.After 48 h of incubation, the cells that penetrated the chamber’s lower membrane were examined under an optical microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).The cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet dye after being fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde.These methods are established for evaluating cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion consistent with protocols used in oncopharmacological research [20].

Flow cytometry

To identify cell apoptosis, the annexin V-FITC/PI method was employed.After chondrocytes were collected using trypsin following various treatments, they were resuspended in 500 µL of binding buffer.In accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol, after adding Annexin V-FITC to the flow tube, the mixture was incubated for 15 min in the dark at room temperature. Apoptotic cells were then identified and examined in a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The miR-1287-5p and LAMTOR3 binding sites were predicted by TargetScan.The luciferase reporter vector was created by inserting the wild-type or mutant LAMTOR3 binding sites into the 3’UTR of pmirGLO vector.Then, either miR-1287-5p mimic or miR-1287-5p inhibitor were co-transfected into GC cells together with luciferase reporter plasmids. Luciferase activities were detected 48 h after transfection using the Dual Luciferase kit (Promega, USA).

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 23.0 (SPSS, USA).All data were displayed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and the t-test was employed for analysis. The expression of miR-1287-5p and the levels of LAMTOR3 mRNA were compared using Pearson’s correlation analysis; a difference of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

MiR-1287-5p was down-regulated in GC

The GC cell lines (MKN45 and AGS) had lower levels of miR-1287-5p expression when compared to GES-1(Fig. 1A).We also compared the miR-1287-5p levels in clinical GC tissues to those in comparable normal tissues (n = 60). GC tissues had much lower levels of miR-1287-5p than normal tissues (Fig. 1B). In addition, GC patients with metastases had lower miR-1287-5p levels than those without (Fig. 1C). A number of clinicopathological traits were also examined in relation to the miR-1287-5p levels of GC patients. TNM stage and lymph node metastases were shown to be significantly correlated (P< 0.05) with the decrease in miR-1287-5p expression levels (Table 1).

MiR-1287-5p functioned as a tumour suppressor in GC cells

Gain and loss of function tests were conducted in MKN45 and AGS cell lines, which contained varying amounts of miR-1287-5p, in order to investigate the functional role of miR-1287-5p in GC.Transfection of miR-1287-5p mimics efficiently boosted miR-1287-5p expression in MKN45 cells, as revealed by the qRT-PCR experiment, whereas transfection of miR-1287-5p inhibitor resulted in miR-1287-5p expression downregulation in AGS cells (Fig. 2A).

miR-1287-5p inhibited tumorigenesis in GC cell lines.(A) Expression of miR-1287-5p in MKN45 and AGS cells that were transfected with miR-1287-5p mimics or inhibitor was determined by qRT-PCR (B) CCK-8 assays were performed to detect cell proliferation (C) Wound-healing assays were performed to detect cell migration.(Scale bar:100 μm) (D) Cell invasion abilities were evaluated by transwell invasion assays.(Scale bar: 100 μm) (E) Cell apoptosis were analyzed by flow cytometry Data were from three biological replicates (each with n = 3 technical replicates).The obtained data are expressed as mean ± SD.Comparisons between two groups were analyzed by two-tailed Student’s t-test.*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001

Cell proliferation was markedly reduced by miR-1287-5p overexpression in MKN45 cells and boosted by miR-1287-5p knockdown in AGS cells on the basis of CCK-8 assays (Fig. 2B).Wound-healing assay indicated that miR-1287-5p overexpression significantly reduced MKN45 cell migration, while miR-1287-5p knockdown significantly enhanced AGS cell migration (Fig. 2C).According to the transwell invasion assay results, miR-1287-5p over-expression significantly reduced MKN45 cell invasion, while miR-1287-5p knockdown significantly enhanced AGS cell invasion (Fig. 2D).Additionally, according to flow cytometry, miR-1287-5p overexpression markedly elevated MKN45 apoptosis, while miR-1287-5p knockdown significantly reduced AGS cell apoptosis (Fig. 2E).

LAMTOR3 was discovered to be one of miR-1287-5p’s targets

TargetScan, mirDIP, miRDB, miRTarBase, and miRWalk were five internet databases used to identify the target genes for miR-1287-5p in order to better understand the underlying mechanisms by which it exerts its functional effects on GC cells. LAMTOR3 and RFX7 were found for common involvements among these five tools (Fig. 3A). It has gradually become clear how LAMTOR3 contributes to cancer.The function of LAMTOR3 in GC, however, is still entirely unclear.Therefore, we concentrated on LAMTOR3 in GC in our subsequent analyses.

(A) Venn diagram of potential targets of miR-1287-5p (B) LAMTOR3 mRNA levels were measured by RT-qPCR in MKN45 and AGS cells transfected with miR-1287-5p mimics or inhibitor and respective controls (C) LAMTOR3 protein expression were measured by Western blot in MKN45 and AGS cells transfected with miR-1287-5p mimics or inhibitor and respective controls (D) The TargetScan database analysis predicted LAMTOR3 as the miR-1287-5p target site. In MKN45 cells, co-transfection with miR-1287-5p mimics may considerably reduce LAMTOR3 mRNA and protein levels, whereas in AGS cells, co-transfection with miR-1287-5p inhibitor may greatly raise LAMTOR3 mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 3B, C).Utilizing constructs with the expected target sequence (LAMTOR3-wt) and the altered target sequence (LAMTOR3-mut), a luciferase reporter gene experiment was conducted to further validate that LAMTOR3 is a direct target of miR-1287-5p (Fig. 3D).Luciferase activity was found to be lower in MKN45 cells when miR-1287-5p mimics and LAMTOR3-wt were co-transfected than in the scrambled control, and higher in AGS cells when miR-1287-5p inhibitor and LAMTOR3-wt were co-transfected than in the scrambled control (Fig. 3E). However, after modifying the expression of miR-1287-5p, no discernible alteration in the luciferase activity was observed with the mutant construct of LAMTOR3 3′-UTR (Fig. 3E).Importantly, we discovered that the expression of miR-1287-5p and the levels of LAMTOR3 mRNA in GC tissues were negatively correlated (Fig. 3F). According to these results, miR-1287-5p can bind to LAMTOR3’s 3′UTR and function as a regulator of gene expression.

LAMTOR3 reversed miR-1287-5p’s effects on GC cells

To further show that miR-1287-5p worked by targeting LAMTOR3, rescue experiments were conducted.MiR-1287-5p and LAMTOR3 were over-expressed individually or simultaneously in MKN45 cells. And miR-1287-5p and LAMTOR3 were downregulated individually or simultaneously in AGS cells.The following cotransfections were performed on MKN45 cells: miR-1287-5p mimic + OE-LAMTOR3, miR-1287-5p mimic + OE-LAMTOR3 NC, miR-1287-5p mimic NC + OE-LAMTOR3, or miR-1287-5p mimic NC + OE-LAMTOR3 NC. Then, miR-1287-5p inhibitor + si-LAMTOR3, miR-1287-5p inhibitor + si-LAMTOR3 NC, miR-1287-5p inhibitor NC + si-LAMTOR3,or miR-1287-5p inhibitor NC + si- LAMTOR3 NC were cotransfected into AGS cells.The over-expression and knockdown efficiencies were confirmed by Western blotting (Fig. 4A).

Cell proliferation was markedly boosted by OE-LAMTOR3 in MKN45 cells and reduced by KD-LAMTOR3 in AGS cells on the basis of CCK-8 assays (Fig. 4A). Wound-healing assay indicated that OE-LAMTOR3 significantly enhanced MKN45 cell migration, while KD-LAMTOR3 significantly reduced AGS cell migration (Fig. 4C). According to the transwell invasion assay results, OE-LAMTOR3 significantly enhanced MKN45 cell invasion, while KD-LAMTOR3 significantly reduced AGS cell invasion (Fig. 4D).Additionally, according to flow cytometry, OE-LAMTOR3 markedly reduced MKN45 apoptosis, while KD-LAMTOR3 significantly elevated AGS cell apoptosis (Fig. 4E).Collectively, these findings establish LAMTOR3 as a potent oncogenic driver in GC.

The inhibition of cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in MKN45 cells induced by miR-1287-5p was restored by co-overexpression of LAMTOR3, according to the CCK-8, wound-healing, and transwell invasion experiments (Fig. 4B-D).Consistently, overexpression of LAMTOR3 also partially overturned miR145-5p-induced apoptosis (Fig. 4E).On the other hand, the stimulating action of miR-1287-5p loss on AGS cells was reversed when LAMTOR3 expression was silenced (Fig. 4B-E).These findings suggest that by specifically targeting LAMTOR3,miR-1287-5p can restrain the malignant biological behaviour of GC cells.

Discussion

We initially showed in this study that miR-1287-5p was down-regulated in GC compared to normal tissues or cells.Functional investigation revealed that miR-1287-5p can reduce GC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while enhancing apoptosis.Furthermore, we demonstrated that miR-1287-5p controls LAMTOR3 expression negatively, thereby exerting a tumor suppressor effect.All of these findings could shed light on the mechanisms underlying GC carcinogenesis.

According to our research, miR-1287-5p acts as a tumour suppressor in GC cells. This was in agreement with findings of other relevant studies.Wang et al. found that the miR-1287-5p/SLC7A11 axis increased cell ferroptosis while decreasing colorectal cancer cell migration and proliferation [21].MiR-1287-5p/PFN2 signaling reduced invasion and proliferation of non-small cell lung cancer cells [22].Additionally, overexpression of miR-1287-5p inhibited ovarian cancer cells proliferation and enhanced carboplatin sensitivity [23].Zhang and colleagues discovered that mir-1287-5p inhibitor prevented pancreatic cancer cells from proliferating and invading [12].By specifically targeting PIK3R3, miR-1287 inhibited hepatocellular carcinoma growth, invasion, and migration [13].MiR-1287-5p targets PIK3CB in breast cancer to prevent the growth of triple negative breast cancer [24].These all demonstrated the function of miR-1287-5p in tumor inhibition.

The molecular pathways that underlie the inhibition of GC development by miR-1287-5p were identified.We revealed that LAMTOR3 is a predominant functioning target of miR-1287-5p through bioinformatics analysis, luciferase reporter gene assay, and rescue experiments in GC cells.The protein-encoding gene LAMTOR3, often referred to as MP1 or MAPKSP1, is found on human chromosome 4q22.3 and codes for a scaffold protein [25].This scaffold protein is mainly located in the nucleus of the cell during the late stage of mitosis in the form of a protein complex [25]. LAMTOR3 could facilitate the interaction between MEK and ERK, and hyperphosphorylate MEK and ERK, thereby regulating the MAPK signaling pathway [26].Moreover, LAMTOR3 has been identified as a mTOR signaling regulator [27].

Evidence currently available indicated that LAMTOR3 was aberrantly expressed in a number of malignant tumors, especially breast cancer [28], pancreatic cancer [29], kidney renal clear cell carcinoma [30], and prostate cancer [31]. This was strongly linked to the development, incidence, and apoptosis of malignancies.LAMTOR3 played a crucial role in the survival of ER-positive breast cancer cells by facilitating pro-survival signaling through the PI3K/Akt pathway [28].LAMTOR3 was marked upregulated in pancreatic cells and human pancreatic tissues, and PAF-induced LAMTOR3 transactivation contributed to pancreatic cancer cell proliferation by activating MAPK signaling [29].And in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma, LAMTOR3 has been demonstrated to be a prognostic biomarker [30].Additionally, LAMTOR3 can regulate the key factor of tumor migration, p21 activated kinases (PAK), thereby inhibiting the migration of prostate cancer cell [31].Furthermore, LAMTOR3/MAPK has been demonstrated that to by activating BCL-2, it contributes to the chemotherapy resistance of lung cancer cells [32].

Non-coding RNAs have grown in significance in the control of LAMTOR3 in recent years.The circRNA-miRNA-mRNA CeRNA network proved essential in controlling the occurrence and progression of cancer [33].Through miR-1287-5p/LAMTOR3 signaling, circ_0075829 has been found to promote the development of pancreatic cancer [12].MiR-20a may suppress the growth and promote apoptosis of breast cancer cells by targeting LAMTOR3 gene [34].Brucea javanica Oil Emulsion controlled the miR-8485/LAMTOR3 signaling pathway, which in turn stimulated autophagy in ovarian cancer cells [25].These above studies suggested that LAMTOR3 may be a potential tumor regulator that was essential to the genesis and progression of malignancies.Our research shows that LAMTOR3 may be a potent oncogenic driver that promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion while suppressing apoptosis in gastric cancer cells. Considering that MAPK pathway activation occurs in the majority of gastric cancer cases [35, 36],and LAMTOR3 can regulate the MAPK signaling pathway, we will further investigate whether LAMTOR3 affects the tumor progression of gastric cancer through the MAPK signaling pathway.

Our findings highlight the potential of targeting the miRNA-LAMTOR3 signaling axis as a therapeutic strategy for gastric cancer.By comparing our results to other studies [37, 38], our findings highlight the broad relevance of targeting specific signaling pathways in cancer. While previous studies have focused on alternative approaches, such as targeting cancer cells or their microenvironment, our work suggested that modulating the miRNA-LAMTOR3 axis could provide a novel avenue for intervention. This approach aligns with the growing interest in precision medicine, where targeting specific molecular pathways could enhance therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.

It is important to place this interaction of the miR-1287-5p/LAMTOR3 axis within the broader paradigm of miRNA regulatory networks. Pan-cancer analyses of coordinated gene expression patterns—exemplified by studies of cuproptosis regulators [39, 40], disulfidptosis pathways [41], and mitotic checkpoint kinases [42]---consistently demonstrate that miRNAs function as network orchestrators rather than isolated target silencers. These investigations reveal that individual miRNAs typically regulate functionally clustered gene sets governing cohesive biological processes. While our validation confirmed LAMTOR3 as a direct and functionally significant target, miR-1287-5p likely coordinates additional effectors within the mTOR-MAPK signaling ecosystem.

Recent work by Ou L et al. [5] demonstrates that H. pylori infection promotes gastric cancer cell migration and invasion through immunity and drug sensitivity, with phenotypes consistent with the miR-1287-5p inhibition that enhances metastatic potential that we observed.Considering that chronic H. pylori infection frequently downregulates tumor-suppressive miRNAs, including downregulation of tumor-suppressive miR-7 and miR-153 which promote carcinogenesis [43], it is plausible that the reduced miR-1287-5p expression observed in our clinical specimens may partially reflect this infectious etiology. Importantly, since LAMTOR3 serves as a convergence point for multiple oncogenic pathways, including mTOR signaling known to be hyperactivated by H. pylori’s CagA effector [44, 45], our demonstration that miR-1287-5p directly targets LAMTOR3 suggests potential therapeutic utility in infection-associated carcinogenesis. Future studies should therefore explore whether H. pylori status correlates with miR-1287-5p expression in patient cohorts.

There were some limitations in this research. Most critically, the exclusive reliance on in vitro models without complementary in vivo validation restricts clinical translatability and fails to capture systemic tumor-microenvironment interactions. Additionally, the patient sample size was small, and the absence of clinical follow-up data precludes survival analysis that would establish miR-1287-5p/LAMTOR3 as prognostic biomarkers. Mechanistically, though LAMTOR3 targeting was confirmed, downstream pathway consequences remain unquantified. Future efforts will therefore prioritize patient-derived xenograft models with integrated H. pylori challenge systems to evaluate miR-1287-5p agomirs in vivo, coupled with multi-center cohorts linking RNA-seq profiles to longitudinal outcomes for robust biomarker assessment. Comprehensive signaling dissection through multiplexed phosphoproteomics will further resolve mTOR-MAPK-immune checkpoint crosstalk. Critically, these steps remain consistent with the emerging precision oncology paradigm[46], in which successful gastric cancer treatment requires dual regulation of tumor intrinsic signaling and microenvironmental immunosuppression. We consequently envision nanoparticle-delivered, sequence-optimized agomirs that concurrently silence LAMTOR3 scaffolds in cancer cells and reprogram tumor-associated macrophages via exosomal transfer. This integrated approach exemplifies the next evolution in RNA-based precision therapy for molecularly stratified gastric cancer subsets.

Conclusions

This current study found that miR-1287-5p suppressed GC cells proliferation, migration, and invasion while increasing apoptosis by decreasing LAMTOR3 expression. This revealed that miR-1287-5p may function as a plausible therapeutic target or biomarker for GC.

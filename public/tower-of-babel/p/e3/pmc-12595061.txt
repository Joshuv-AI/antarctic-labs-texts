External information environment required for cross disciplinary thinking in large language models based on interdisciplinary lay summary writing
Authors: Mei Chen, Yijun Su, Junyuan Guo, Xiaoying Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractExternal information stimuli are crucial for humans to reintegrate knowledge and activate cross-disciplinary thinking. This study investigates how the external information environment affects LLMs’ cross-disciplinary thinking through analyzing generated interdisciplinary lay summaries. The interdisciplinary lay summary, building on traditional lay summaries for non-specialists, integrates intersection interpretation and cross-inspiration to assess convergent and divergent thinking. This study utilizes general abstracts and lay summaries to create contexts of deep knowledge and broad knowledge. By comparing intersection interpretations and cross-inspirations generated by LLMs under different information input combinations, we explore the external information environment’s influence on cross-disciplinary thinking. ChatGPT-4.0 and Claude3 are used in this study. Findings indicate that simultaneous access to deep and broad knowledge enhances LLMs’ convergent thinking, while intersection interpretations foster divergent thinking. Furthermore, a modest correlation exists between the external information environment and divergent thinking. This study advances the LLMs’ application in cross-disciplinary research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-27107-5.Subject terms: Literature mining, Information technology

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have great potential for application in interdisciplinary fields. Since late 2022, LLMs, such as ChatGPT, have demonstrated remarkable performance in various academic task scenarios1–4. Several studies have shown that LLMs surpass humans in terms of creative thinking abilities5–7.The exceptional creative thinking capabilities of LLMs, combined with their vast world knowledge, suggest significant potential for application in interdisciplinary fields.

For scientists, although they possess extensive world knowledge, generating cross-disciplinary thinking is still closely related to the external information environment. For instance, George de Mestral invented Velcro after noticing burrs sticking to his dog’s fur during a walk. This example illustrates the important role of the external information environment in activating cross-disciplinary thinking. The external information environment encompasses all information and data in the external world that can be perceived, acquired, or utilized by individuals. The emergence of cross-disciplinary thinking in humans is closely linked to the external information environment. It provides stimuli that profoundly influence the reconstruction of individual knowledge, thereby promoting the formation and development of cross-disciplinary thinking.

For LLMs, the external information environment refers to all the information provided to them, playing an equally important role in stimulating their cross-disciplinary thinking. Existing research shows that prompt engineering can significantly influence the output of LLMs8. Prompts provide a foundational external information environment for the model, effectively activating its thinking process. However, prompt engineering mainly focuses on optimizing form and structure. In contrast, the formation of cross-disciplinary thinking often requires establishing distant connections between different knowledge domains, placing greater demands on the composition of knowledge content in the external information environment. Therefore, exploring the external information environment that promotes cross-disciplinary thinking in LLMs is crucial for fully unlocking their potential in interdisciplinary fields.

Both the depth and breadth of knowledge play crucial roles in cross-disciplinary thinking. To explore the impact of knowledge depth and breadth in the external information environment on the cross-disciplinary thinking of LLMs, this study introduces an innovative task to generate lay summaries for interdisciplinary papers as a probe. In academia, scientific summaries and lay summaries serve distinct purposes. Specifically, a scientific summary, which is the abstract of the paper itself, serves as a concise expression of the academic paper and uses highly specialized terminology. Its primary target audience is readers with corresponding professional background knowledge, representing the embodiment of knowledge depth. In contrast, a lay summary aims to present scientific research results to a broad audience without specific professional knowledge, using clear and accessible language, thus reflecting the breadth of knowledge9. Building on existing formats, our interdisciplinary lay summary builds on the scientific summary of an interdisciplinary paper and includes three structured components (as illustrated in Fig. 1a:1 The lay summary translates complex academic content into accessible language, helping readers without specialized knowledge to understand the core information, main findings, and practical significance of the research2. Intersection interpretation explains the connections between disciplines, helping readers quickly grasp their internal relationships and interactions3. Cross-inspiration sparks readers’ creativity, encouraging deeper associations and innovative thinking through additional examples. Therefore, by formally defining the interdisciplinary lay summary based on the characteristics of interdisciplinary research, this study not only provides a framework to enhance public comprehension and stimulate innovative associations but also establishes a well-suited probe for our investigative task.

During the generation of interdisciplinary lay summaries, intersection interpretation and cross-inspiration represent two key aspects of interdisciplinary thinking:1 Intersection interpretation integrates disparate knowledge into a coherent framework by detailing the logical relationships and interactions between different disciplines, thereby representing convergent thinking;2 Cross-inspiration fosters free exploration and creativity based on existing knowledge, thereby representing divergent thinking.

This study decomposes the task of generating interdisciplinary lay summaries into three sequential steps: lay summary generation, intersection interpretation generation, and cross-inspiration generation. The tasks for each step are outlined as follows:1 Lay summary generation: In this stage, the LLM learns several scientific summaries and corresponding manual lay summaries, and then generates prompts for lay summary generation. After that, the prompts is used to produce lay summaries;2 Intersection interpretation generation (Fig. 1b): This stage involves three input scenarios: scientific summary only, lay summary only, and simultaneous input of both scientific and lay summaries. The model generates intersection interpretations in these three scenarios. By comparing and analyzing the quality of the generated intersection interpretations, it reveals how the depth and breadth of knowledge in the external information environment affect the model’s convergent thinking in interdisciplinary knowledge integration;3 Cross-inspiration generation (Fig. 1c): This stage designs seven different input combinations, including separate provision of intersection interpretations, scientific summaries, lay summaries, and multiple combinations of these pieces of information. The model generates cross-inspirations based on these conditions. By analyzing the quality and disciplinary breadth of the generated cross-inspirations, it examines the impact of external knowledge on divergent thinking.

For the three steps—lay summary generation, intersection interpretation generation, and cross-inspiration generation—this paper adopts a collaborative approach involving a ‘generating LLM - correcting LLM’ framework. The generating LLM is used to complete the task of this step. The correcting LLM checks the generating model’s output according to the step’s purpose and proposes targeted modifications. The generating model then adjusts and optimizes its output based on feedback from the correction model to finalize the content. This approach leverages the capabilities of both LLMs to enhance the logical rigor, information accuracy, and clarity of expression.

Results

Lay summary generation

Building upon the prompt agent concept10, we developed a novel self-learning methodology for prompt generation to facilitate lay summary creation using a LLM. The comprehensive experimental results are documented in the supplementary materials (Table S1, S2). To validate the effectiveness of self-learning prompt generation, we compared our self-learning prompts generated by a LLM with human expert-designed prompt. The human expert-designed prompt was proposed, and its effectiveness matches or even surpasses that of expert-written lay summaries11. Table 1 compares the expert-written lay summaries, the self-learning prompts from a LLM, and the lay summaries generated using human expert-designed prompts, particularly in zero-shot or few-shot scenarios. The LLM’s self-learning prompts slightly outperform human expert-designed prompts in relevance and are comparable in readability. This indicates that the lay summaries generated using the LLM’s self-learning prompts in this study are of high quality. From the results in Table 1, it is evident that the readability of lay summaries generated by the LLM’s self-learning prompt under various conditions exceeds that of those written by human experts. Therefore, we selected the lay summaries generated under few-shot conditions, which exhibited the best correlation, for further experiments.

Intersection interpretation and intersection inspiration generation

In this step, the intersection interpretations and cross-inspirations were generated by LLM from different input combinations, and their quality was assessed through ranking. The evaluation of intersection interpretation encompasses three dimensions:1 Interdisciplinarity: Assess whether the intersections between different disciplines are thoroughly explained2. Relevance: Evaluate whether the intersection interpretations align with the original material and are consistent without fabrication3. Readability: Assess whether the text is easy to understand, flows smoothly, and is logically coherent. The evaluation of cross-inspiration similarly includes three dimensions:1 Interdisciplinarity: Independently assess each inspiration’s ability to establish connections and communication between different disciplines2. Innovativeness: Independently evaluate the innovative aspects of each inspiration in its respective field, including new ideas or methods3. Feasibility༚Independently assess the potential of each inspiration to transition from theory to practice, including any possible implementation difficulties and practical operability.

The evaluation results are shown in Fig. 2. For intersection interpretation, Fig. 2a shows that the ranking of the generated intersection interpretation in terms of relevance and readability decreases only slightly when the input is lay summaries compared to scientific summaries. However, the ranking of interdisciplinarity declines significantly. This result indicates that although lay summaries use accessible language, they do not significantly impact the relevance and readability of the intersection interpretation. However, this conversion process results in the loss of some professional information, causing the model to lack a sufficiently precise information base for generating intersection interpretations, thereby affecting their interdisciplinary quality. This result highlights the indispensable role of knowledge depth in convergent thinking. When both scientific summaries and lay summaries are used as input, compared to using only scientific summaries, the generated intersection interpretations show substantial improvements in both interdisciplinarity and readability, reaching the highest points in the experiment. This suggests that the professionalism and precision of scientific summaries provide a deep information base for generating intersections. Meanwhile, lay summaries offer a broader and more accessible perspective, enabling the LLM to efficiently explore discipline intersections within a wider knowledge landscape and significantly enhance its interdisciplinary convergent ability. Although this enhancement slightly negatively impacts relevance, it does not significantly weaken the overall relevance between the intersection interpretation and the original text. In summary, for the task of generating intersection interpretations, using both scientific summaries and lay summaries as input can significantly improve interdisciplinarity and readability while maintaining relevance, and generate high-quality intersection interpretations. This finding suggests that in the process of convergent thinking, the depth and breadth of knowledge are complementary and synergistic. Only by balancing both depth and breadth can the convergent thinking ability of LLMs be more effectively promoted, thus achieving interdisciplinary integration and innovation of knowledge.

For cross-inspiration, as shown in Fig. 2b, the cross-inspirations generated from seven different inputs can be clearly divided into three categories based on the LLM’s evaluation results. The first category is cross-inspirations generated by intersection interpretations, which exhibits the best performance in terms of interdisciplinarity and innovation, but performs the worst in feasibility. The second category of cross-inspiration derives from various summary types, including scientific summaries, lay summaries, and their combined use. All exhibit intermediate levels of interdisciplinarity, innovativeness, and feasibility, with no significant differences across these dimensions among the cross- inspirations generated by these inputs.

The third category of cross-inspiration arises from the combined use of various summaries and intersection interpretations, including scientific summaries with intersection interpretations, lay summaries with intersection interpretations, and the simultaneous use of both summaries and intersection interpretations. This category performs best in feasibility but worst in innovativeness and interdisciplinarity. The above phenomena reveal:1 The rankings of innovativeness and interdisciplinarity exhibit a high degree of convergence, while the ranking of feasibility shows an opposite trend compared to the other two. This phenomenon indicates a close correlation between innovation and interdisciplinarity, but an increase in innovation often comes with an increase in the difficulty of implementing the inspiration. Therefore, it is essential to deliberately construct external information environments tailored to the needs2. Lay summaries and scientific summaries exhibit no significant differences in activating divergent thinking, suggesting that both specialized and popularized contexts equally stimulate divergent thinking. This indicates that the information context does not have a decisive influence on divergent thinking in LLMs, and what is more crucial is the information combination3. Intersection interpretations that reveal how connections are established between disciplines, i.e., cross-examples, play a significant role in LLMs’ understanding of intersections and in promoting divergent thinking. On the one hand, cross-inspirations based on intersection interpretations exhibit excellent performance in innovativeness and interdisciplinarity. On the other hand, when intersection interpretations are added to summary information, the generated cross-inspirations show a significant improvement in feasibility. This clearly demonstrates that cross-examples significantly impact the interdisciplinarity thinking of LLMs. Regardless of the focus dimension, constructing appropriate cross-examples or intersection interpretations is crucial4. A larger amount of information helps improve feasibility. Therefore, adding relevant information based on intersection interpretations aids the model’s in information integration and enhances feasibility.

Evaluation of cross-inspiration breadth

Based on the results shown in Fig. 2b, the cross-inspirations stimulated by intersection interpretations exhibit the highest levels of interdisciplinarity and innovativeness. Therefore, this section analyzed the breadth of cross-inspirations stimulated by intersection interpretations by isolating individual cross-inspirations and conducting a breadth analysis based on discipline breadth and embedding.

As shown in Fig. 3, in terms of disciplinary breadth, compared to the disciplines involved in the intersection interpretations that stimulate them, 21% of cross-inspirations do not break through the disciplinary constraints imposed by those intersection interpretations, while 79% do. This result indicates that the LLM possesses the ability to transcend the disciplinary limits imposed by the examples and demonstrates strong disciplinary divergence.

Analysis results of discipline breadth. Close-distance intersections refer to cross-inspirations that include only disciplines present in the intersection interpretations, while far-distance intersections refer to cross-inspirations that incorporate disciplines not covered by the intersection interpretations.

Regarding embedding, as shown in Fig. 4a, the K-means clustering results show that the cross-inspirations corresponding to each intersection interpretation can be divided into an average of 2.62 categories. Additionally, based on the silhouette coefficient clustering results, as illustrated in Fig. 4b, they are divided into an average of 1.02 categories. Furthermore, this study calculates the cosine similarity and Mahalanobis similarity between each intersection interpretation and all the individual cross-inspirations it stimulates. Figure 4c,d show the distribution of similarity scores between intersection interpretations and cross-inspirations. For cosine similarity, the similarity scores between cross-inspirations and intersection interpretations is concentrated in the interval [0.4, 0.6]. For Mahalanobis similarity, the scores lie in the interval [1.5, 2.5]. These clustering and distance results suggest a moderate correlation between cross-inspirations and their corresponding intersection interpretations—indicating neither high consistency nor complete independence. This implies that they share certain key features or concepts, but still exhibit significant differences and variations.

Conclusion and discussion

By analyzing the interdisciplinary lay summaries produced by LLMs under various inputs, this study demonstrates a significant correlation between the external information environment and the cross-disciplinary thinking processes of LLMs. This correlation is primarily manifested in the following three dimensions: Firstly, the synergistic effect of deep and broad knowledge significantly enhances the convergent thinking capabilities of LLMs.

Secondly, intersection interpretations, i.e., cross-examples, play a pivotal role in facilitating divergent thinking. Lastly, there exists a moderate correlation between the external information environment and divergent thinking, as both share key features and concepts, yet maintain significant variability. This finding offers significant methodological insights for optimizing the utilization of LLMs in interdisciplinary research.

Drawing upon the research conclusions presented in this paper, the establishment of external information environments aimed at providing diverse thinking stimuli for LLMs is anticipated to encourage multifaceted thinking. Consequently, this integration of human creativity with the extensive knowledge possessed by LLMs is poised to yield richer, more expansive, and innovative thinking outcomes. Such an approach not only mitigates the potential for LLMs to stifle human creativity but also unlocks boundless opportunities for human-computer collaboration.

Although the generation of interdisciplinary lay summaries serves primarily as a probe for assessing the cross-disciplinary thinking abilities of LLMs in this study, it simultaneously offers an effective method for crafting accessible summaries of interdisciplinary research. This method not only generates traditional lay summaries but also produces high-quality interdisciplinary interpretations and cross-disciplinary inspirations, thereby contributing significantly to the dissemination and progression of interdisciplinary research. Thus, this study not only introduces a novel perspective and methodology for exploring cross-disciplinary thinking in LLMs but also provides invaluable insights for the progression of interdisciplinary fields.

This study provides preliminary insights into stimulating cross-thinking capabilities in LLMs, but several limitations should be noted. First, the generalizability of the findings has not been validated across a wider range of model architectures. Although GPT-4 was used as the generative model and Claude-opus as the evaluator, systematic testing on other mainstream models such as LLaMA and Qwen was not conducted due to time and resource constraints. While the Transformer architecture common to most LLMs offers some basis for extrapolation, broader validation is needed to assess robustness. Second, the evaluation methodology requires further refinement. This study combined automated LLM-based evaluation with sampled manual verification, and the manual assessments were conducted by external bioinformatics researchers with relevant interdisciplinary expertise, which helped ensure professionalism and reduce potential bias. Although manual checks supported the credibility of the results, prior work highlights risks of bias in LLM-based evaluation12. Future research should therefore focus on bias mitigation and the development of more robust cross-domain evaluation frameworks.

Materials and methods

Dataset construction

Based on relevant materials from the BioNLP conference13, this study initially conducted LLM screening and subsequent manual organization of 28,000 records from the lay summary datasets of eLife and PLOS14. Ultimately, this procedure resulted in the compilation of 101 interdisciplinary documents, each representing research that intersects biology and information science. This comprehensive dataset is openly accessible via the Hugging Face repository, located at https://huggingface.co/datasets/sulovexin/laysummary.

Model selection

The Python version utilized is 3.8. The model configurations are as follows: In both the prompt generation phase and the stages involving intersection interpretation and cross-inspiration generation, the GPT-4.0 Turbo model is employed as the generating model. For correction, the Claude3-opus model is utilized. During the generation phase, the temperature parameter is set to 1.0, whereas in the correction phase, it is adjusted to 0.0.

Evaluation metrics for lay summaries

During initial evaluation, we randomly sampled 10% of the lay summaries for manual assessment. The reviewers found that both types of summaries demonstrated good readability with no perceptible subjective differences between them. Based on these findings, we employed automated evaluation metrics to capture more nuanced, fine-grained distinctions between the two summary types. The evaluation of lay summaries uses two key types of indicators: relevance, which assesses how pertinent the summaries are to the original texts, and readability, which measures how easily they can be understood by laypersons.

Readability indicators

The readability indicators adopted are FKGL15, CLI16, and DCRS17.

①FKGL is an indicator that assesses the readability of a text based on sentence length and vocabulary complexity. Its values range from 4 to 18, representing the grade level of reading ability required to understand the text. Specifically, grades 4 to 6 correspond to elementary school, grades 7 to 9 to middle school, grades 10 to 12 to high school, and grades 13 to 18 to college and above.

②CLI is another readability measure that considers sentence length, vocabulary frequency, and syntactic complexity. A higher CLI score indicates that the text is more difficult to read, whereas a lower score indicates that the text is easier to read. Generally, CLI scores within the range of 5 to 17 are considered reasonable. A score of 5 represents a level suitable for elementary school students, and a score of 17 indicates a reading level appropriate for those with a bachelor’s degree or higher.

③Compared to FKGL and CLI, DCRS is a more comprehensive evaluation method. It calculates readability by considering sentence length, vocabulary complexity, contextual logic, and semantic consistency.

Relevance indicator

The relevance indicators used are ROUGE-L18 and BERTScore19.

①The ROUGE-L (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation) indicator measures the similarity between two documents using the Longest Common Subsequence (LCS). A higher ROUGE-L score indicates that the document being evaluated is more similar to the reference document.

②BERTScore, on the other hand, calculates the similarity between the document being evaluated and the reference document by analyzing the similarity of word embeddings. A higher BERTScore also indicates a higher degree of similarity between the two documents.

Evaluation methods for intersection interpretation and Cross-Inspiration

The evaluation of intersection interpretations and cross-inspirations employs LLMs. To verify the quality of the LLM evaluations, 20% of the samples were randomly selected for manual assessment. The human evaluators, external researchers in bioinformatics with foundational expertise spanning computer science and the life sciences, were able to ensure professionalism while minimizing potential bias associated with the research team. The results of the manual assessment, which confirm the reliability of the LLM evaluations, are available in the supplementary materials (Figure S1).

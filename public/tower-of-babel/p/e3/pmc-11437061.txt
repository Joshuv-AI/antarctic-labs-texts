High resolution 3-D seismic and sequence stratigraphy for reservoir prediction in ‘Stephi’ field, offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria
Authors: Oluwatoyin Abosede Oluwadare, Adetayo Femi Folorunso, Olusola Raheemat Ashiru, Stephanie Imabong Otoabasi-Akpan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria, stands as a dynamic geological marvel, known for its intricate processes and extensive hydrocarbon reservoirs. This study employed an integrated approach, utilizing 3D seismic data and well logs, to conduct a thorough analysis of sequence stratigraphy and reservoir characterization in the pursuit of optimizing hydrocarbon exploration in the region. The study focused on the NW–SE trending Miocene depocenters, which predominantly comprises alternating sandstone and thick shale layers within the Agbada Formation. These reservoir units showcased stacked shallow marine fluvial–deltaic sediments, separated by significant marine shale units. Within the study area, two hydrocarbon-bearing reservoirs were identified and named: R1 and R2. Petrophysical analysis identified R2 as the most promising reservoir, with a permeability of 1184 × 10− 3 µm2, 85% hydrocarbon saturation, porosity of 0.30, and effective porosity of 0.27. Fault structural analysis uncovered that hydrocarbons are trapped within a network of growth faults within the wave-dominated Niger Delta depositional system. From the sequence stratigraphic interpretation, four depositional sequences were delineated between the depths of 2030–3417 m, and are bounded by five sequence boundaries. Integrated seismic facies analysis revealed high-energy feeder systems likely supplying sediments from river sources to offshore locations. These integrated findings provide essential insights to inform resource management, exploration strategies, understanding of reservoir distribution, and structural intricacies within the complex offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria, providing valuable information. The depositional environment helped in the understanding of the stratigraphic traps which are prospects in the study area. Introduction

The exploration for hydrocarbons in the Niger Delta, Nigeria, has recently expanded into the ultra-deep offshore sector, driven by the increasing energy demands of the nation and the pursuit of untapped reservoirs beneath the seabed. The offshore Niger Delta, characterized by its intricate geological structures and substantial hydrocarbon potential, has emerged as a new frontier for the Nigerian energy industry. As technology advances and exploration techniques improve, the ultra-deep waters of the Niger Delta play a crucial role in meeting Nigeria’s future energy needs and solidify its position as a major player in the global energy market. However, the prevalent complexities necessitate the characterization of reservoirs and prediction of depositional environments to reduce exploration uncertainties and optimize resource extraction1.

Reservoir characterization, as defined by Ref.2, is the process of integrating diverse data to describe reservoir properties. This is essential for deciphering the complex geological landscape in the offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria. Within this framework, the application of sequence stratigraphic concepts, which focuses on the order of deposition of strata in available space, becomes crucial for understanding reservoir origin and potential in offshore settings3. Previous studies from researchers like4–12 have demonstrated the efficacy of sequence stratigraphy in refining the prediction of hydrocarbon habitats and correlating reservoir properties in the Niger Delta Basin. However, there is a need for further investigation to address the specific challenges and uncertainties associated with offshore exploration in this region.

The objective of this study is to integrate 3D seismic data and well logs to characterize the reservoir, and predict the depositional environment within the stratigraphic interval of the Stephi Field, located in the Offshore west, Niger Delta, Nigeria (Fig. 1). By applying sequence stratigraphic principles and reservoir characterization techniques, this study addressed the following research questions: (1) How can sequence stratigraphy and reservoir characterization techniques be effectively applied in the Offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria? (2) What is the distribution of hydrocarbon-bearing reservoirs within the Stephi field? (3) How do the structural framework and depositional environment influence reservoir properties in the study area?

Geology of the study area

The Niger Delta, situated in the Gulf of Guinea on the western coast of Nigeria, represents a complex sedimentary basin shaped by a combination of tectonic events, lithological diversity, and unique sedimentary characteristics. The geological evolution of this region is intricately linked to a series of processes that have significantly impacted the geology of the Niger Delta. Positioned at the junction of the African and South American tectonic plates, the evolution of the Niger Delta Basin has been heavily influenced by rifting, subsidence, and subsequent sedimentation13,14. The separation of these plates during the Cretaceous Era led to the formation of the Benue Trough, followed by the establishment of the Niger Delta sedimentary depocenter in the Paleogene, as detailed by Ref.15.

The subsurface sedimentary sequence of the Niger Delta reflects a dynamic interplay of transgressive and regressive events, resulting in complex stratigraphic layering. This geological complexity is evident in three primary formations (Fig. 2) that characterize the Niger Delta, each representing prograding depositional facies with varying proportions of sandstone and shale16. The Akata Formation, characterized by shale predominance with minor sand intercalations representing prodelta facies, is the oldest of these formations. This is followed by the Agbada Formation, which displays intercalations of sand and shale that represents sediments of the transitional environment, the coastal barrier and fluviomarine realms. The youngest litho-unit is the Benin Formation, primarily comprising continental sands and sandstones of high sand percentage, spanning from southern Nigerian to the Gulf of Guinea offshore Nigeria14,17. The structural configuration of the basin is influenced by basin geometry, sedimentation rates, and the progradation of sandy deposits over under-compacted delta marine shale. This structural interplay is characterized by various syn-depositional structural features associated with gravity tectonics18.

The reservoirs within the Stephi field, within Latitude: 3°58’48.25” N and Longitude: 2°28’18.27’’E, are strategically located within Miocene depocenters trending NW–SE, embedded in a wave-dominated depositional system of the Niger Delta. These reservoir units, integral to the Agbada Formation, exhibit a unique composition of stacked shallow marine fluvial–deltaic sediments interspersed with significant marine shale units, as highlighted by Ref.19. The deposition of sands in these reservoirs occurred in middle to upper shore face environments, strongly influenced by wave dynamics. Additionally, the hydrocarbons present in these reservoirs are believed to have originated from the underlying Eocene–Oligocene Akata marine shales, serving as probable sources for hydrocarbon accumulation in the reservoirs.

Methodology

The study employed an integrated approach utilizing 3D seismic data and well log data from the Stephi Field in the offshore Niger Delta region of Nigeria. It seamlessly integrated 3D seismic reflection and wireline log data, augmented by supporting information such as Checkshot survey data and formation well tops, for a quantitative assessment of crucial reservoir properties. The essential materials used included a base map for geographic reference, high-quality 3D seismic data for structural and depositional environment analysis, and well logs from strategically located boreholes providing subsurface measurements like resistivity, porosity, density, and sonic velocities. Well logs, capturing diverse borehole parameters against depth, underwent petrophysical analyses and litho-stratigraphic correlation, revealing the distribution and behavior of lithological units across different wells.

The methodology followed a systematic approach, as shown in Fig. 3. It began with thorough data quality checks before importing well data. Lithologies were delineated from gamma-ray log signatures based on API values, using the sequence stratigraphic interpretation techniques20–22. Utilizing measured log parameters—gamma radiation, natural spontaneous electrical potential, resistivity, density, neutron, sonic, among others—facilitated the identification of porous and permeable litho-units saturated with hydrocarbons, distinguishing them as reservoirs.

Flowchart of Research Methodology.

Derived reservoir parameters, including reservoir thickness, Net-To-Gross (NTG), volume of shale, effective porosity, hydrocarbon saturation, and facies distribution, were extracted from well-log data to estimate the hydrocarbon potential of the Stephi field. The 3D seismic reflection data, comprising in-lines and cross-line seismic sections, underwent meticulous analysis for horizon mapping and structural interpretation. For the seismic signal, the dominant frequency differs, mostly between the range of 50- 20HZ and reduces with depth but for this study, the dominant frequency was 25 Hz. Structural mapping was done which involved identifying reflection events across fault planes, discerning the nature of faults—whether normal or reverse dip slip. Hydrocarbon-bearing formations, recognized from well-log signatures, were mapped through seismic to-well-tie with synthetic seismograms23. Horizon mapping involved tracing target horizons across in- and cross-line sections using the 3D auto track tool in Petrel interpretation software. Two horizons were meticulously traced, generating surface maps indicating the spatial distribution of formations in seismic time (2-way time). Time structure maps, incorporating fault polygons and employing the layer cake velocity model, were derived from the horizon surface maps and subsequently converted to depth structure maps using sonic-calibrated Checkshot data24,25.

Results and discussion

The sequence stratigraphic analysis and reservoir characterization undertaken in this study demonstrated a strong grounding in established principles and methodologies in the field. The integration of multiple data sources, including well logs, seismic data, and geological models, reflected the holistic approach advocated by previous researchers like5,26–28. This comprehensive data integration is crucial for robustly understanding reservoir properties, facies distribution, and heterogeneity in complex geological settings like the Niger Delta. The study leverages extensive literature on the region’s geology, stratigraphy, and petroleum systems, ensuring a solid interpretive foundation rooted in established knowledge of the Niger Delta’s tectonic structure, formations (Akata, Agbada, and Benin), and depobelts29,30. Furthermore, applying well-established petrophysical principles strengthens the credibility of the reservoir property calculations and interpretations.

Lithology interpretation

The lithology interpretation from the four wells (STEPHI 1, STEPHI 2, STEPHI 3, STEPHI 4) revealed alternating layers of sandstone (reservoirs) and shale (seals), characteristic of the Niger Delta depositional environment. In STEPHI well1 (1131–4178 m), the shale layer thickness increased while the sandstone layer thickness decreased with increasing depth. The reservoirs R1 and R2 are located at depths of 2726 m and 2792 m, respectively. A similar lithological pattern was observed in STEPHI Well 2 (2306–3462 m), STEPHI Well 3 (1768–3467 m), and STEPHI Well 4 (1717–2969 m), with R1 and R2 reservoirs encountered at varying depths across the wells as seen in Figs. 4, 5, 6 and 7. Figure 8 show the well log correlation panel, trending from north to south, of the four wells (STEPHI 1 to STEPHI 4). Both the R1 and R2 reservoirs extend across all four wells along this panel, suggesting efficient drainage and recovery potential given favorable properties.

Computation of petrophysical parameters

Tables 1 and 2 detailed the computed petrophysical parameters for Reservoir 1 (R1) and Reservoir 2 (R2) across the wells. Table 3 showed the average computed petrophysical parameters across the two delineated reservoirs. Reservoir 1 (R1) has an average gross thickness of 34.26 m, net sandstone thickness of 24.31 m, net-to-gross ratio of 0.71, shale volume of 0.15, porosity of 0.24, effective porosity of 0.21, water saturation of 43%, hydrocarbon saturation of 57%, and permeability of 651 × 10^-15 m^2. Reservoir 2 (R2) exhibited more favorable petrophysical properties, with an average gross thickness of 24.31 m, net sandstone thickness of 23.35 m, net-to-gross ratio of 0.86, shale volume of 0.11, porosity of 0.30, effective porosity of 0.27, water saturation of 15%, hydrocarbon saturation of 85%, and permeability of 1184 × 10^-15 m^2. This is indicative of good inter-pore connectivity for fluid flow.

Sequence stratigraphic ınterpretation

The identification of depositional sequences, systems tracts, and key stratigraphic surfaces follows widely accepted models proposed in seminal works, aligning with concepts from researchers like6,22,31–34. The sequence stratigraphic analysis identified four depositional sequences (Supplementary Fig. 1) within the depth interval of 2030–3417 m, bounded by five sequence boundaries. This provides a predictive framework for spatial facies distribution and stacking patterns, to inform well placement, field strategies, and exploration targets.

The oldest sequence, Sequence 1, has a thickness of approximately 249 m and comprises a transgressive system tract, a maximum flooding surface, and a thin reservoir unit. Supplementary Fig. 2 shows the chronostratigraphic correlation of the studied wells. This study’s emphasis on applying high-resolution sequence stratigraphy to complex reservoirs in the Niger Delta offshore field is supported by recent efforts such as13 work on the Patao gas reservoir, highlighting the growing recognition of sequence stratigraphy’s value in unraveling intricate sedimentary architecture and stratigraphic organization to enhance reservoir management strategies.

Seismic sequence analysis

Seismic interpretations, including faults, structural highs, and facies, contribute an integrated structural-stratigraphic understanding of hydrocarbon entrapment and migration, with time-depth structure maps delineating areas of interest. The synthetic seismogram generated from the STEPHI 1 well (Supplementary Fig. 3) ties the top of the R1 reservoir at 2752 m depth to a trough at two-way time of 3151 ms, while the top of the R2 reservoir at 2818 m depth corresponds to a trough at two-way time of 3205 ms. The seismic structural interpretation (Supplementary Fig. 4) revealed antithetic and syntetic faults. A few of the faults are throwing towards the south while the other faults are throwing towards the north.

The time structure maps (Supplementary Figs. 5 and 6) exhibit faulted anticlinal closures, and the depth structure maps (Supplementary Figs. 7 and 8) confirm the presence of these structures. Four seismic sequences were delineated (Supplementary Fig. 9): Sequence 1 displays parallel, high-amplitude discontinuous reflections, interpreted as sheet sandstones or weakly confined channels.

Sequence 2 shows erosional truncation at the top and high-amplitude discontinuous facies, interpreted as channels. Sequence 3 is chaotic, with variable amplitudes and frequencies. Sequence 4, the uppermost sequence, exhibits low-to-medium amplitude continuous reflections, suggesting shale-prone intervals, as seen in Supplementary Fig. 10.

Conclusion

The study relied on welldata from only four wells (STEPHI1, STEPHI 2, STEPHI 3and STEPHI4) within the STEPHI field.While these well data provided valuable insights, they do not fully capture the lateral variability of reservoir properties across the entire field. Therefore, the integrated application of sequence stratigraphy and reservoir characterization techniques in this study successfully elucidated the distribution of hydrocarbon-bearing reservoirs, the influence of structural framework and depositional environment on reservoir properties within the Stephi field, offshore Niger Delta, Nigeria. Collectively, these geological, petrophysical, and geophysical results formed a comprehensive reservoir characterization framework aligning with industry practices to guide field appraisal, development, and production optimization decisions. However, inherent uncertainties and limitations based on the specific dataset and methodologies should be considered when extrapolating findings beyond this study area within or outside the Niger Delta basin. The petrophysical analysis and facies interpretations in this study relied primarily on well logs and seismic data. The lack of core data integration represented a significant limitation, as cores provide direct observations of reservoir rock properties, sedimentary structures and depositional fabrics. Continuous refinement, validation, and integration of new data sources and advanced technologies will be essential to further enhance complex reservoir understanding and optimize hydrocarbon exploration and production strategies going forward. In the broader context, this research has contributed to the growing body of scientific understanding and practical applications in the field of petroleum geosciences, by building upon established concepts that can guide future exploration efforts, enhance resource recovery, as well as, promote sustainable energy development in Nigeria.

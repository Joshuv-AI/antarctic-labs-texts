Deep learning for dynamic tactical formation recognition in professional football
Authors: YuDong Wang, QianCheng ShangGuan, GuoQiang Chen, YuChen Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecognizing tactical formations is a critical task in professional football performance analytics. However, existing methods often fail to capture the dynamic and continuously evolving nature of modern tactical systems within conventional classification frameworks. Current deep-learning models typically rely on snapshot-based recognition and struggle to capture continuous adaptations, role-switching, and multi-scale temporal dependencies characteristic of elite-level play. To address these limitations, this paper proposes the Hierarchical Dual stream Spatiotemporal Graph Transformer (HDS-SGT) as a new deep-learning architecture specifically designed to recognize dynamic tactical formations. The proposed model is a combination of a spatiotemporal graph attention network stream (equivalent to player-level relational dynamics) and a parallel temporal transformer stream (equivalent to hierarchical phase-transition patterns at varying temporal resolutions). A key innovation is the Dynamic Role Assignment Module, which uses learnable role embeddings along with cross-attention mechanisms and thus enables the recognition of fluid positional replacement during attacking and defensive transitions. Wide-ranging experiments on three professional-level datasets comprising 847 matches showed that HDS-SGT achieves a formation classification accuracy of 94.7% and a tactical transition detection F1-score of 0.912, which is 8.3 per cent and 11.6 per cent better than the state-of-the-art baselines, respectively. Importantly, real-time inference is sustained at 23.4 frames per second (including preprocessing and sliding-window buffering) on GPU-enabled hardware (NVIDIA RTX 3090), enabling deployment in live match-analysis applications. Ablation studies verify that the synergistic action of dual-stream fusion and multi-scale temporal modelling contributes to the overall performance improvements. These results represent a significant advance in understanding tactical dynamics in the team sports that has direct implications to coaching decision support systems, opponent analysis systems, and broadcast augmentation systems. However, the study relies on elite-level professional tracking data, which may limit generalizability to other contexts, and real-time performance was validated on specific GPU-enabled hardware configurations.Keywords: Deep learning, Football analytics, Graph neural networks, Multi-agent coordination, Real-time inference, Spatiotemporal modeling, Tactical formation recognitionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Association football, or soccer, is the world’s most popular sport, engaging billions of fans and generating significant economic activity within professional leagues globally. The sport’s complexity stems from its dynamic multi-agent nature. Twenty-two players interact continuously within a spatiotemporal framework, implementing strategies and tactics that ultimately determine match outcomes. Understanding these tactical dynamics is key to competitive success, although the historical reliance on subjective observational assessment has proven insufficient for comprehending the swift and subtle trends specific to elite-level play1,2. With the introduction of new propriety tracking systems and methods of computation, a paradigm shift has occurred toward data-driven tactical analysis, thus, providing greater opportunities to unravel the strategic architecture behind team performance like never before3,4.

The use of machine learning in football analytics has grown exponentially, with systematic reviews highlighting various applications over the past decade, including performance prediction, injury risk assessment, and tactical pattern recognition5. Similar data-driven approaches have demonstrated effectiveness in basketball analytics, where machine learning has been applied to player performance pattern mining, clutch performance analysis, and injury impact assessment6–8. One of the key principles of tactical analysis is team formation, which establishes player positioning in space and serves as the blueprint for organized offensive and defensive actions9. Modern studies have established that positional tracking data, obtained using GPS, semi-automatic camera systems, and local positioning systems, are crucial for quantitative tactical investigation10. Comparative analysis of two formations, for example, the 4-2-3-1 and 3–5-2, has shown that spatial coverage, pressing intensity, and transitional behavior can be markedly different, pressing intensity and transitional behavior could be markedly different, which establishes the tactical importance of the formation choice11. However, these studies have largely been carried out using more or less fixed analytic models which cannot account for the continuous nature of in-game tactical modifications12,13. The initial methods of computational formation identification made use of unsupervised learning methods, which extracted pattern of structures out of the spatiotemporal coordinates. Early work introducing role-based representations from tracking data demonstrated that these techniques could effectively distinguish team playing styles across seasons14, and the later studies used these techniques to identify latent team structures that are not limited to nominal positional assignments15. Clustering has been used to detect discrete formation states using continuous stream of positions16, relative movement pattern analysis has been used to detect coordinated spatial behaviors that are independent of absolute location of the pitch17. The tactical knowledge has also been enriched with the classification of discrete events especially passage sequences, which have a phenomenological value in terms of how many times a strategy has been performed over the larger team formation18. It has augmented these features to dynamic danger evaluation in live match action with real-time applications19. However, both methodologies possess fundamental constraints: they operate on aggregated time scales, rely on quasi-static formation assumptions, and struggle to measure the continuous transitions that define contemporary tactical systems20,21. Modeling football using graphs has proven to be a promising paradigm for capturing relational dynamics among players. Graph structures are inherently used to encode the relationship structure of team play, where each node is a single player and each edge is the passing network or spatial proximity or tactical dependency22. Graph-based machine-learned pipelines have been shown to be more predictive on complex tactical tasks like attack zone entry23. The addition of deep-learning architectures has also increased the sophistication of methods, with extreme learning machines providing end-to-end tactics estimation on video input24 and with techniques like Pass2vec showing latent stylistic hints on passing behavior25. Convolutional neural networks are already used to perform tactical analysis in women football26 and systems of intelligent motion recognition27, whereas the recent TacticAI system can be viewed as a milestone in the development of AI-assisted tactical recommendation used by professional teams28. The ability to recognize activities using wearable sensors was also complemented, and the suitability of deep learning in extraction of temporal patterns was proven29, as well as complex architectures can be used to measure space-control efficacy with its direct tactical effects30. Explainable AI methodologies have been used to model defensive performance quickly prediction31, and explanatory methodologies are more frequently used to support coaching choices affecting team building and participant recruitment and choice32,33. However, recent transformer-based and graph-augmented retrieval approaches in sports analytics, while promising, often struggle with the computational demands of real-time deployment and lack mechanisms for handling dynamic role permutations inherent in modern tactical systems.

Despite recent progress, significant gaps remain. Existing methods typically achieve formation classification accuracies below 90% and struggle with transition detection; state-of-the-art approaches report F1-scores below 0.82 on benchmark datasets. The current methods of formation recognition are mostly based on the rigid classification paradigms of giving discrete formation labels to the fully aggregated position snapshots over time thus not defining the fluid, continuous movement adaptation that elite teams undertake when playing a match. The dynamism of modern football, characterized by positional switching, depending on phases of roles and playing, as well as the temporal dependence occurring multi-scale, between individual activity and entire strategies, is not sufficiently modeled by traditional architectures. Moreover, even though graph neural networks and transformer-based models have shown personal advantages in the spatial relational modeling and learning place potential dependencies across long geographic scales respectively, no framework has yet combined these two complementary strengths into a unified model for dynamic formation perception. The complexity of existing methods of deep-learning also rules out real-time implementation, making practical consideration of live-coaching assistance and broadcast enhancement. All these constraints encourage the creation of the new architectural innovation directly aimed at the specific issues of active tactical analysis.

The proposed work presents the Hierarchical Dual Stream Spatio Temporal Graph Transformer (HDS-SGT) a new deep-learning model that overcomes the mentioned limitations with several major innovations. To start with, the proposed structure adopted a dual-stream setup that will run simultaneously: one spatiotemporal graph attention network stream will learn relational dynamics at the player level and spatial coordination patterns, whereas another temporal transformer stream will learn hierarchical phase-transition behavior across various temporal scales. Second, a Dynamic Role Assignment Module with learnable role embeddings and cross-attention mechanisms adapts the dynamic role swapping of the modern tactical systems, and thus provides the opportunity to represent roles continuously instead of discretely. Third, the multi-scale temporal modelling explicitly models dependencies between instantaneous coordination of movement on one extreme and long-term strategic adaptation on the other extreme. Fourth, architectural optimizations ensure the capability to make inferences in real-time which is useful to make deployment in real life matches analysis scenarios practical. A full experiment on professional league data shows that HDS-SGT shows substantial improvement over state-of-the-art baselines in accurate formation recognition as well as in tactical transition recognition, and thus provides a new paradigm to understand dynamic tactical behaviors in team sports.

The rest of this paper is structured in the following way. Section 2 outlines the suggested HDSSGT architecture, constituent of dual-stream design, role assignment mechanism which is dynamic and multi-scaling temporal modelling. Section 3 explains how the experiment was done covering data characteristics, evaluation procedures, and implementation specifications. Section 4 provides the results of an experiment and the comparative analysis of the results with the baseline methods and ablation analysis investigating the contribution of each component to the results. Section 5 is the implications of the findings to practical uses and gives the directions on future research. Section 6 brings the article to an end with the contribution summarization and its importance to the entire field of sports analytics.

Proposed methodology

System overview and architectural design

Hierarchical Dual-Stream Spatiotemporal Graph Transformer (HDS-SGT) has been consciously constructed with the aim of addressing the fundamental issue which is the identification of the dynamic tactical formations of professional football. This sport features rapid player motion and fluid positional changes, requiring an advanced model that can simultaneously capture spatial relationships and temporal dependencies. HDS-SGT uses raw positional tracking data from a match as its input, which generates continuous formation classification and tactical transitions detection as outputs that can be used in coaching during a match and analyzing performance.

The conceptual foundation of HDS-SGT is the hierarchical nature of football team formations across spatial and temporal scales. On a micro-scale, the movement of individual players and the interaction between any two players characterizes instantaneous spatial forms. At the meso-level, organized subgroup actions among defensive lines, midfield units, and attacking combinations form functional tactical units. On the macro-level, the overall cooperation of all the outfield in creating a recognizable pattern of formation that coaches and analysts strive to define and follow during a game. Current methods have primarily focused on single-scale analysis, thereby neglecting fine-grained temporal dynamics in favor of large-scale formation patterns14,15. HDS-SGT clearly mitigates this inadequacy by using a hierarchical multi-scale.

The entire system architecture, as shown in Fig. 1, consists of four main components that work within an integrated processing pipeline. Input Processing Module takes pose tracking data (as raw) and does necessary preprocessing, such as coordinate normalization, time coordinate synchronization and feature augmentation. The Dual-Stream Encoding Module comprises two parallel processing streams: a Spatiotemporal Graph Attention Network (ST-GAT) stream, which establishes relational dynamics of players through the combination of graph-based message transmission, and a Temporal Transformer (T-Former) stream, which approximates long-range temporal dependencies with the aid of self-attention mechanisms at a variety of temporal scales. Dynamic Role Assignment Module proposes learnable role embeddings that allow fluid role change through positional interchange throughout tactical transitions allowing the model to behave in a consistent manner to represent players as their roles keep changing. Lastly, there is the Hierarchical Fusion and Classification Module which combines representations in the two processing streams via attention fusion and generates formation classification outputs together with an estimate of a tactical transition probability. Input representation and preprocessing

The HDS-SGT system input has data of positional tracking which encodes the two-dimensional coordinates of all the players in pitch at discrete time steps. Sampling rates of professional tracking systems are usually in the range of 10–25 Hz and as a result, big data volumes are produced and thus effective processing strategies are required6,7. To ensure consistency across datasets, all data was resampled to a unified 10 Hz rate: the 25 Hz datasets were downsampled using a low-pass Butterworth filter (cutoff frequency 4 Hz) followed by decimation to prevent aliasing, while the native 10 Hz dataset required no resampling. Preliminary experiments comparing native 25 Hz processing with 10 Hz downsampled data showed minimal performance difference (< 0.3% accuracy), indicating that 10 Hz adequately captures tactical dynamics while reducing computational overhead. Since the input information is represented by raw data (coordinate pair) in detail at every time step, the raw data supplied to a single team, which has ten outfield players (not counting the goalkeeper, whose behaviors at each time is governed by different patterns not directly related to formation classification), will be represented by a set of coordinate pairs, which need to be processed into representations that can be evaluated by a deep-learning model. Note that HDS-SGT models one team at a time; for match analysis involving both teams, the model is applied independently to each team’s tracking data. This design choice allows for clearer formation attribution and simplifies the learning task while still enabling comparative tactical analysis.

The preprocessing pipeline applies key transformations to enhance model robustness and learning efficiency. To address formation identification across different pitch locations, we perform coordinate normalization by transforming positions relative to the team centroid and applying standardized scaling based on observed inter-player distances in professional football12,13. This normalization ensures that formations are identified consistently regardless of their location on the pitch and this is a limit inherent in earlier formation recognition studies16,17. In multi-source tracking data, temporal synchronization reduces possible time differences between multi-source tracking data with interpolation methods that introduced homogeneous time sampling in all player paths.

Feature augmentation enriches the positional representation with derived measures that encode important tactical information. Finite differencing of consecutive positions computes velocity vectors which reflect dynamics of motion needed to distinguish between static creation of form and dynamic tactical transitions of form. Discriminative information on the purposefulness and strength of positional movements is further offered in the form of acceleration features. The pairwise spatial relationship which determines formation geometry is encoded in inter-player distance matrices and coordinated movement patterns that are typical of organized tactical behaviors are encoded in relative velocity features19,20. The mean augmented feature representation of every gamer at each time is therefore made up of positional features, velocity features, acceleration features and context characteristics obtained through team member spatial conformity.

Spatiotemporal graph attention network stream

The ST-GAT stream models the football team as a dynamic graph, where each player is represented as a node and each tactical or spatial relationship between two players as an edge. This graphic model is intrinsically structured around the relationship nature of team sports the behaviors of individuals is essentially conditioned about the positions and motions of teammates and adversaries22,23. The graph structure is not static, but changes with movement and interaction of the players and needs specialized architectural elements in order to deal with time-dependent topological relationships.

The graph building is based on a hybrid approach which incorporates a distance-based approach to make the connections and learned attention-based edge weighting. The attention mechanism then modulates edge weights based on learned player state representations, enabling the network to dynamically prioritize tactically salient relations while suppressing irrelevant ones. The message-passing step of the ST-GAT stream has multi-head attention to graph data to sum up the information of the neighbor nodes without considering the learned weights. The attention heads compute the compatibility scores between representations of the players, normalize the softmax which results in attention weights, and aggregate representations of neighbor players by weighted attention coefficients. In the multi-head model, there is the possibility of modelling a variety of different types of relationships, including defensive partnership, midfield triangles and attacking compositions, in a single computational environment. The deep graph network employs layer normalization and residual connections to stabilize training and enable the extraction of hierarchical spatial features through repeated message-passing.

The ST-GAT stream has temporal modelling models gated recurrent units that maintain hidden states, which represents the change of player representations over time. The recurring structure helps the network to acquire typical time-dependent behavior patterns of different formation states and transitions like hand-in-hand backward movement of defensive lines when facing a pressing condition or forward motion of players when in an attacking scenario24,25. Spatial graph attention combined with temporal recurrence in one stream generates representations that encode both an immediate relational structure and dynamic behavioral pattern.

Temporal transformer stream

The T-Former stream makes use of self-attention methods which are directly aimed at capturing long-range temporal dependencies that can exist between the lengthy match sequencing. Although ST-GAT stream is fine-grained to model the fine-grained dynamisms of spatiotemporal detailing via recurrent processing, the sequential character of recurrent designs restricts their ability to explicitly define the connection among remote time steps26,27. This is overcome by the transformer architecture which incorporates global self-attention operations to allow information to be directly shared between any two time-positioned points, irrespective of the temporal distance between them.

The input of the T-Former stream is a sequence of time-aggregated team-level characteristics based on personal player features. The operations in aggregation are mean pooling to encode central tendencies of positioning in the team, variance pooling to encode spreads and compactness in formation, and extremal pooling to determine positions of boundaries that characterize formation shape. These complementary aggregation techniques are such that substantiation of the team-level representation contains adequate information to do the formation discrimination and the dimensionality reduction that would guarantee efficient transformer processing.

The positional encoding tackles the invariance of self-attention with regard to permutation by infusing temporal order-related information in the input representations. The encoding scheme uses learnable positional encoding and allows the model to learn the best temporal representations via end-to-end learning instead of designing them to have sinusoidal forms that might not correspond to the natural scale of the real time of a football tactical behavior28. The formula that can be learnt offers this flexibility to cover different match times, and different team tactical beats in different competition teams. Multi-scale temporal modelling is a key architectural innovation of the T-Former stream. Instead of operating the temporal sequence processing at one place, the architecture employs parallel attention pathways working at varying temporal granularities. Fine-scale attention is a response that represents rapid tactical adjustments that occur over periods on the order of seconds (e.g., positional varying to ball movement or striking trigger). Medium-scale attention models wrought on tens of second durations e.g. sustained possession durations or organized defensive sequences. Coarse scale attention is a focus on strategic patterns on a scale of minutes like formation changes to counter game state or opponent tactical change. The scale outputs are then combined by learned fusion weights which allow dynamic ways of emphasis of various temporal resolutions depending on the prevailing tactical conditions. The specific temporal window sizes (seconds, tens of seconds, minutes) were initially selected based on domain knowledge of tactical dynamics, then validated through preliminary experiments comparing alternative configurations; Sect. 4.5 provides empirical analysis of the learned attention distributions across scales.

Dynamic Role Assignment Module

Dynamic Role Assignment Module, in turn, does not solve the basic problem of formation recognition as a continuous exchange of player positions and tactical roles, which are inherent to the modern football systems. Traditional methods usually assign rigid roles based on nominal roles; that is not true in the situations when fullbacks start moving forward to play as midfielders, when the central midfielders move to the defensive side or when the forwards start moving to join in a pressing strategy11,14. The suggested module uses learnable representations of roles, which dynamically change based on player behaviors and tactical context, which allows learnable formation recognition when there is fluid positional change.

The module maintains a set of learnable role embeddings; each associated with established tactical positions in common formations. Such embeddings do not represent fixed positions, but instead encode prototypical behaviors patterns in each tactical role. Cross-attention mechanisms operate during inference and calculate scores of compatibilities of player representation and role embedding, which results in soft role assignments indicating the extent to which the current behaviors of any player match any tactical role. In this soft-assignment formulation positional ambiguity is easily accommodated and there are smooth transition mechanisms as positions are swapped between players as tactical adjustments occur. We acknowledge that these architectural choices represent empirically-motivated heuristics rather than theoretically derived optimums; the ablation studies in Sect. 4.4 provide empirical validation of their effectiveness. The cross-attention calculation is bidirectional which allows role embeddings to be updated on the behaviors of observed players and at the same time makes player representations informed with role-specific contextual information. This two-way process will keep the definition of roles based on realistic match behaviors instead of being abstract concepts and the player representations are informed by the contextual precedent that canonical role expectations present them with. Results of the integration are role-conscious representations of players that maintain the same semantic meaning in the face of changes in physical position, and solve directly the role fluidity problem that prior methods based on formation recognition have been so restricted by15,17.

Temporal consistency regularization promotes smooth dynamism in role assignments over time-steps, it is a natural belief that changes in tactical roles do not happen immediately, they tend to happen gradually. The regularization mechanism imposes penalties on sudden shifts in the probabilities of role-assignment and this would make the model explore consistent role representations though it should remain flexible to the occurrence of real role transition. The balance between stability and adaptability is regulated by a learnable temperature parameter that can be used to adjust the sharpness of role-assignment distributions.

Hierarchical Fusion and Classification Module

This module combines the representations of the two streams of processing information to provide the ultimate formation classification outputs. The fusion strategy uses weighting by attention which defines how various streams are learned to weight them based on how informative they are to the current tactical situation. The T-Former stream would also give more reliable connotations in periods of stable formation maintenance by its long-range temporal modelling performance. Under such high-speed tactical transitions, the ST-GAT stream can have an enhanced resolution as it provides fine-grained spatiotemporal resolution. This is achieved through the attention-based fusion which allows adaptation to stream weights to maximize the classification in different tactical situations.

The classification head enforces a hierarchical relation of output in the recognition of the levels of formation description. The main output produces classification probabilities on a taxonomy of standard formation types, such as 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 3–5-2, and 4-2-3-1 among others used commonly in professional football. Secondary outputs give more detailed characterization of formation options e.g., flat vs. diamond midfield formations or narrow vs. wide defensive formations. The tertiary outputs provide an approximation of tactical transition probabilities which would be the chance that the team is at the moment moving between rather than at a fixed team formation. This hierarchical output structure provides coaches and analysts with multiresolution tactical information to be used in a variety of tasks such as analysis. Training objectives and optimization

This training process follows a multi-task learning approach which optimize the classification of formations, detection of transitions and auxiliary goals which are set in order to enhance the quality of the learnt representations. The main classification loss is applied to the categories of the formation in the form of cross-entropy, and label smoothing is used to increase generalization and calibration. The transition detection loss is implemented as binary cross-entropy on the estimated transition probabilities, with temporal boundaries determined either by expert annotation or automated detection of transitions between formation labels.

Auxiliary losses give more supervisory information which helps to develop richer intermediate representations. The role consistency loss promotes the Dynamic Role Assignment Module to produce a temporally smooth assignment of roles that conform to expert-vetted positional tags (where these exist). Contrastive loss on the ST-GAT representations facilitates similarity among the representations of the same formation and dissimilarity among representation of different types of formation. Reconstruction loss imposed on the T-Former outputs encourages retaining enough information to reconstruct the input positional sequences and therefore, avoiding overly much information being lost during temporal aggregation.

The optimization process uses the AdamW algorithm using a learning-rate warm-up process followed by cosine annealing. Gradient clipping is deployed in order to alleviate training instability potentially introduced by potentially infrequent yet extreme gradients in the graph-attention computations. Dropout regularization is used regularly in each component of the network to curb the problem of over-fitting, and dropout rates are tuned to optimal value by use of performance on the validation-set. To optimize GPU memory utilization, mixed-precision training is used, and thereby the numerical stability is ensured by using loss-scaling. Experimental methodology

Dataset description and characteristics

The experimental analysis of HDS-SGT model is based on three extensive datasets obtained in relation to professional football matches which in their combination give a wide range of tactical situations and adequate volume to realize the tangible evaluation of the performance. The main data is the positional tracking statistics of 412 games of a leading European league of two full seasons of data collection by optical tracking. The correlating data is made of 285 games of a high-end global event where exposure is given to a variety of different tactical ideologies in various football cultures. The secondary dataset contains 150 matches from a second-tier European league which provides the opportunity to evaluate the performance of generalization at different levels of competition. The combined data includes 847 matches, comprising approximately 1,270.5 h of match footage that possesses prolonged positional monitoring of all players. The three datasets contain varying numbers of formation classes (Dataset A: 12 classes, Dataset B: 15 classes, Dataset C: 10 classes) due to differences in tactical diversity across competitions. During training, we use a unified label space of 15 formation classes with zero-padding for missing classes; during testing, evaluation is restricted to the formations present in each specific dataset to ensure fair comparison.

The ground-truth formation labeling process followed a rigorous, multi-stage annotation procedure which was created aiming to provide the highest accuracy and resulting consistency. Experienced annotators with backgrounds as professional coaches determined the formation states at 30-second intervals during each match, and whether they disagreed or not was decided by consensus conference. For transition boundaries specifically, annotators marked the precise timestamps (at 1-second resolution) when formation changes initiated and completed; transition start was defined as the first frame showing coordinated positional movement away from the current formation, and transition end as the first frame where the new formation stabilized. The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for transition detection was computed as the absolute difference between predicted and ground-truth transition midpoints, averaged across all transition events in the test set. The annotation protocol differentiated between stable and transitioning phases, whereby teams were capable of stable spatial arrangement and transition periods where teams actively changed among formation states. Inter-annotator reliability measured using Cohen’s kappa coefficient exceeded 0.85 in all of the datasets, which is high agreement as per the existing norms of sports performance annotation1,2. The taxonomy of annotations included 12 main formation categories and 28 secondary variants that comprehensively represent modern professional football as they represent the variety of the tactics used by the teams. Data stratification and partitioning followed a match-level split scheme, ensuring that complete matches appear only in the training, validation, or test partition, and transition events equally represent the formation categories as well as transition events across partition. Specifically, the training set consists of 70% of matches (593 matches), the validation set 15% (127 matches), and the test set 15% (127 matches). Evaluation metrics and procedures

Performance assessment uses an elaborate set of metrics that are aimed at measuring various elements of formation recognition capability. Classification accuracy is a metric used to measure the relative performance of recognition using the sum total of correctly classified formation states in all test-set timesteps. Specifically, all metrics are computed at the frame level (10 Hz temporal resolution) and then aggregated; to address potential inflation from long stable periods, we additionally report segment-level accuracy computed per stable/transition phase, where each continuous formation segment receives equal weight regardless of duration (see Table 3 for both frame-level and segment-level results). The macro-averaged F1-score also deals with the possibility of having an imbalance among different classes, where the harmonic mean of both the recall and precision of the different formation category is first computed before averaging the results, so that the performance on rare formation can be equally evaluated as well. Weighted F1-score employs class-frequency weighting, which indicates the practical significance of true recognition to frequently used structures.

The performance in transition detection is evaluated using the measures that are modified in terms of time-based action detection in video comprehension. Transition precision is a measurement to assess how much transitions that are being predicted are accurately transitions to actual formation change, and downgrading false-positive predictions that would cause the display of spurious tactical notifications. Transition recall gives the ratio of actual transitions that are adequately identified with a punishment on missed identifications that would not notify the coaches of plans to change. Transition F1-score gives harmonic mean of accuracy and recall, which shows a one-way quality assessment of transition detection ability. Temporal localization accuracy is the accuracy of the transition timing, which is determined by the mean of absolute differences between the predicted and ground-truth transition boundaries.

The real-time working practical performance metrics of the HDS-SGT framework of performance in utilizing the live match analysis applications are determined. The inference latency is a measurement of time taken to process each frame of input and generate results on the classification and the ideal target value is 100 milliseconds (enabling 10 Hz processing) set on calculation of the inference latency considering the requirements of the real-time coaching feedback system. Throughput is a measure with respect to the maximum number of frames that can be serviced per second in a continuous operation. The use of the GPU memory can estimate the needs and requirements of hardware resources to apply it to deployment on various computing platforms.

Baseline methods and comparative analysis

The comparative assessment compares HDS-SGT performance to a wide range of baseline techniques describing the state-of-art performance in the formation recognition and other tactical analysis problems. Examples of traditional machine-learned baseline classifiers are the Random Forest classifiers using hand-crafted spatial features20, Support Vector Machines using radial basis function kernels5, the Gaussian Mixture Models organizing formation clustering16. By providing a set of performance benchmarks on the non-deep-learning methods, these baselines allow the evaluation of the marginal contribution of the deep-learning components.

Convolutional Neural Network based deep-learning baselines are capable of working on rasterized pitch representations26,27, with sequential positional feature based Long Short-term memory networks24, and simple Graph neural networks that do not include temporal modeling elements22. The baselines allow separating the contributions of individual HDS-SGT architectural innovations. More specifically, the Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN), which was initially designed to identify actions using a skeleton but can be used to analyze team sports25, is one of the most relevant baselines since the network combines spatial modeling with the use of graph-based models and temporal convolutions.

With strategies of data augmentation, there was an increase in the model strength and overfitting was avoided. Horizontal mirroring (180° rotation about the pitch centerline) was applied to account for left-to-right symmetry in formation labels, while full 360° rotation was deliberately excluded due to the asymmetric nature of football: attacking direction and touchline constraints break rotational invariance. Ablation study design

Isolation of the role of each architectural element on system performance is done in systematic ablation studies. Ablation methodology adheres to the best practices in that it blocks or substitutes certain parts and keeps the other factors the same so that it can be attributed that the differences in performance are related to some particular design decisions25,26. The ablation variants were trained with the use of the same protocols and tested on the same test set to make them comparable. The experimental protocol and evaluation pipeline are illustrated in Fig. 4a-e.

e. Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Pipeline.

The ablation configurations include: (1) whether to remove the ST-GAT stream and only keep T-Former processing to determine the contribution of the graph-based spatial modeling; (2) whether to remove T-Former stream and only keep the ST-GAT processing to determine the contribution of the transformer-based temporal modeling; (3) whether to remove multi-scale temporal attention and keep single-scale processing to determine the contribution of the learned stream integration to the representation quality; (4) whether to remove the Dynamic Role Assignment Module and replace it with fixed positional features to determine the contribution of adaptive role representation; (5) whether to replace attention-based fusion with simple concatenation to determine the contribution of learned stream integration; (6) whether to remove auxiliary losses and train with only the primary classification objective to determine the contribution of multi-task learning.

Statistical analysis and significance testing

The differences in performance changes were statistically significant in the sense of making use of rigorous hypothesis testing techniques that are appropriate to assess the change of machine-learning models. Bootstrap resampling of 1,000 iterations is performed to build 95% confidence intervals of the all-performance measures to allow the evaluation of the uncertainty in estimation (see references of28,29. Paired t-tests were used to test the standard of the HDS-SGT model against each baseline method and a Bonferroni correction was used to limit the family-wise error rate commitment of the various comparisons. The estimation of effects was done using Cohen’s d to understand the quantitative extent of the observed differences in performances other than the statistical significance.

Experiments of cross-validation were also an extension of the first train-validation-test regimen, as it enables the measurement of the stability of results in dissimilar data partitions. Based on HDS-SGT and the principal baseline methods, five-fold cross-validation on match level was done and results of the mean and standard deviation of each measure of performance is reported. The fact that the same relative performance rankings are made across the folds continues to strengthen the confidence of the soundness of the comparative conclusions.

Transition detection performance

The analysis of tactical transition detectors capabilities solves an essential need of the application, or more precisely, the real-time coaching service, where the ability to detect the change of formations in time would allow to act in response to tactic promptly. A detailed analysis of the transition detection, that is, a temporal precision evaluation, detection latency characterization, and a performance difference across the various types of transitions is displayed in Fig. 7.

Transition detection performance analysis.

The analysis of the HDS-SGT performance on the temporal localization shows that the mean absolute error of HDS-SGT is 1.73 s in detecting transition boundaries, whereas, the Transformer-Traj baseline takes 4.21 s, representing a 59% improvement. This improved temporal accuracy stems from the multi-scale attention mechanism, which captures transition signatures at different granularities and combines these multi-resolution signals to accurately localize transition boundaries. The detection-latency distribution, the duration between when the ground-truth transition has happened and the model detects transition, shows that HDS-SGT responds to transitions within 3 s, which is 87.3 per cent as compared to 62.8 per cent of transitions being detected by the baseline, and allows much more responsive coaching intervention.

Differentiating the performance patterns based on the transition type categories with significant practical consequences is presented by the analysis of these categories. Defensive-to-attacking transitions: Forward mobility of defensive line and midfield enlargement is the type of transition with the largest detection F1-score of 0.934 since it leads to unique pattern of spaces that can be identified during a transition of this type. Attacking-to-defensive shifts achieve F1-score of 0.918, although the performance is a bit lower, which is attributable to the fact that defensive consolidation is significantly slower than attacking expansion. The frequency of formation restructuring transitions, which entails corresponding changes made at several positional lines at the same time, achieves an F1-score of 0.889, which is the most difficult category since the positional changes are complex and distributed. Notably, it is specifically the most challenging category to HDS-SGT that saw the most significant relative improvement with an 18.3% increase over baseline compared to 9.2% and 10.7% improvements in the other two types of transition.

Multi-scale temporal analysis

The exploration of a multi-scale temporal modelling provides the observation of the role of individual temporal resolutions in the performance of formation recognition. Figure 9 shows attention weight distributions across temporal scales: coarse-scale (60s) receives highest weight (0.47) during stable formation periods, while fine-scale (2s) dominates (0.52) during transitions, demonstrating automatic context-dependent allocation.

The analysis of the distribution of the attention proves that the relative significance of the temporal scales depends systematically on the stage of tactic. The highest average weight of (0.47) is obtained by coarse scale attention (60 s windows) during periods of maintenance of formation which represent the importance of extended temporal context in verifying the maintenance of formation. At active transition times, the most abundant attention (2-seconds windows) is the fine-scale attention (0.52), which allows the fine-tuning of the rapid adjustment of position in characterizing a formation change. In mid-scale attention, the significance remains rather constant across the stages (0.28–0.34), which creates a consistent intermediate feature between fine and coarse time characteristics.

Functional specialization is detected by the scale-specific feature importance analysis in terms of temporal resolutions. The fine-scale attention is mainly concerned with velocity and acceleration (importance ratio 2.3:1 in comparison with positional features) which reflects the dynamic motion of speed which is indicative of tactical changes. The coarse-scale attention focuses on the position features (the aspect of importance is 1.8:1 in comparison with the velocity), the consideration of the persistence of spatial structures that determines formation identity. This automatic end-to-end training brings about this emergent specialization and confirms the ability of the multi-scale design to find the right interventions in the form of representation of temporal features, thus showing that the pool of temporal features representations does not rely on an explicit architectural restriction.

Discussion

Interpretation of key findings

The experimental results substantiate that HDS-SGT framework represents an important breakthrough in recognizing dynamic tactical formations, and the performance indicators mark the effectiveness of every of the suggested architectural innovations. The 8.3% relative increase in the classification accuracy and the 11.6% relative increase in transition-detection F1-score is a significant gain that can be reflected into a practical increase in utility to a coaching application. Examining the underlying architectural design decisions provides insight into the mechanisms responsible for the performance improvements. Regarding the bias-variance tradeoff, HDS-SGT deliberately adopts higher model complexity (12.7 M parameters, dual-stream architecture) to reduce bias in capturing intricate spatiotemporal tactical patterns; the learning curves and cross-dataset generalization results (Sect. 4.3) demonstrate that this complexity is justified, as the model achieves low bias without excessive variance (i.e., overfitting). The close alignment of training and validation performance (gap < 1.5%), combined with strong cross-dataset transfer (87.3–89.1% accuracy), suggests the model operates in a favorable region of the bias-variance tradeoff for this task domain. The dual-stream structure achieves effectiveness through the complementary specialization of the ST-GAT and T-Former processing streams. Analysis of the ablation shows that spatial and temporal streams played equally in terms of the performance and neither of the streams interprets the representation. This balanced contribution indicates an effective architectural design that leverages the complementary strengths of graph-based spatial modeling and transformer-based temporal modeling without unnecessary computational overhead. There is also great positive interaction effect revealed during the factorial ablation analysis, which confirms once again the streams are not independent but work through each other, there are spatial representations that enhance the time modelling as well as the reverse.

The Dynamic Role Assignment Module solves one of the basic weaknesses of the previous methods based on the deterministic positional representations. The substantially greater performance improvements on rare and complex formations provide direct evidence of the module’s capability, which permits non-rigid role embodiment that allows positional fluidity of modern tactical systems8,11. Per-formation analysis shows that the magnitude of the improvement is also negatively correlated with the frequency of formation in training data (Pearson r = −0.73, p < 0.01): the specific formation that module adds value to is the one where the few training examples cannot support the acquisition of strong fixed-form representations.

The multi-scale temporal attention mechanism displays a complex adaptive behavior that is consistent with intuitively-defined concept in the tactical dynamics. End-to-end training without any direct supervision gives rise to automatic modulation of the scale-specific weights of attention according to tactical phase: fine-scale attention during the transition and coarse-scale attention during the steady-state. This emergent behavior validates the architecture’s ability to identify appropriate temporal modelling strategies and proposes that the multi-scale design manages effectively to address the hierarchical nature of the temporal structure of the tactical behavior inherent in football3,10. Despite these positive findings, we acknowledge that the interpretation remains primarily performance-centric; the learned representations lack direct correspondence to established tactical concepts used by coaches, and the model’s decision-making process remains partially opaque, limiting its utility for detailed tactical explanation.

Comparison with existing approaches

The performance advantages of HDS-SGT over existing methods stem from specific architectural innovations that address limitations in prior approaches. The conventional machine-learning procedures, such as Random Forest and SVM classifiers5,17, use features predefined by humans and capture a restricted range of exercises in spatial type, and are unable to learn end-to-end characterizations. The warrant on learned representations is verified by the 20-percentage-point accuracy gain over these schemes even in the representation of the more complicated spatial and temporal configurations otherwise inaccessible by manual feature engineering.

Deep-learning baselines, such as CNN, LSTM, and standard GNN methods19,21,23, have intermediate levels of performance although are limited due to the architecture of the approach which limits modelling capabilities. The CNN techniques work on rasterized forms of pitch representations lost in the relational structure intrinsically represented as graph formulations. LSTM based model LSTM modeling of temporal dependencies is both sequential and has restricted the ability to process and model long-range dependencies in parallel. Conventional GNN approaches are able to integrate spatial relationships but do not integrate well on the time aspect. ST-GCN baseline22 that combines spatial graph convolution with temporal convolution has the highest baseline performance of the previous approaches and is still confined to fixed graph architecture and convolutional temporal modeling that restrict representational flexibility.

When compared to transformer-based baselines25, attention mechanisms, albeit strong in terms of temporal modelling lack much to say unless they are combined with graph based spatial processing. The 7.4-percentage-point accuracy gain compared to Transformer-Traj shows that the hybrid architecture has access to complementary information which the attention-only methods could not access. This observation is consistent with recent results of the multi-agent trajectories prediction that team-sports modelling relies on graph structures as fundamental relational inductive biases19,20.

Practical implications

The real-time inference capability of HDS-SGT supports the practical implementation of applications that were originally impractical due to computation performance limitations of the deep learning models. It is latency performance of 32.1 ms on a typical GPU-based system (bigger numbers can be achieved with half-precision optimization) support the fact sheet because it can be incorporated into live match analysis pipelines with response times of less than 100 ms making it possible to affect the game’s result in a meaningful way4,25. This practical deployability is a significant step forward on this critical development of the earlier research-centric versions of the implementation that made accuracy a primary concern, as compared to computational efficiency.

Its application implications can be found in various fields of football analytics and football coaching. In the case of in-match tactics analysis, formation recognition allows real-time recognition of any opponent tactical changes in order to assist the coaching team in creating responsive tactical changes. Continuous formation tracking, to analyze the post-match, offers an unparalleled level of fine-granularity in inspecting the tactical patterns and allows finding the effective and ineffective tactical set-ups of a match-series. In the case of optimization of training, formation recognition and physical load monitoring are used to assist in evaluating tactical implementation during training activities9,10.

Broadcast augmentation application is a developing field where formation recognition assists in increasing the viewer experience via automated support of tactical graphics and commentator. The presented accuracy of 94.7% is near to the extent of autonomous deployment, whereas the transition-detection feature allows triggering tactical analysis overlays automatically when formations change significantly. Hierarchical classification structure with primary identification of formations and secondary characterization of variants and estimation of transition probabilities will provide multi-resolution tactical information that is highly useful in a wide array of broadcast and analysis tasks. However, practical deployment involves additional considerations including computational infrastructure costs, scalability challenges when processing multiple simultaneous matches, data governance requirements for handling proprietary tracking information, and the need for human oversight in high-stakes coaching decisions.

Limitations and future directions

Even though impressive progress has been made, there are some limitations that should be mentioned and indicate the way the research may be conducted in the future. A primary limitation concerns reproducibility: the reliance on proprietary tracking datasets from professional leagues restricts independent replication of our results, and we encourage future work to validate these findings on publicly available datasets as they become accessible. Moreover, all three datasets represent elite-level professional football, which may introduce dataset bias; the generalizability of these results to amateur, youth, or lower-division football remains unvalidated. The existing model concentrates only on the single-team formation identification, and the real-life analysis of tactics would entail the joint attention to both the structure, as well as to the interaction between the teams. Generalization through the joint modelling of adversary team formation, the adversarial nature of the tactical decision-making process, is another obvious direction to further interaction4,26.

The use of positional tracking data restricts to the application to the scenarios having positional tracking information but not in the context of amateur football or analyzing historical footage where no video information is available. It would be possible to extend the applicability of the framework by integrating with computer-vision pipelines to determine the positions of players automatically and track them in various ways, but the integration of additional error sources could spread to the formation recognition performance27,30. Furthermore, the current evaluation assumes high-quality tracking data; systematic robustness testing against noise, missing player detections, occlusions, and tracking inaccuracies remains an important direction for future validation.

The analysis aims at the classification of formations and transition identification, and the complete tactical analysis would involve the efforts at the areas of predicting tactical intent, evaluating the effectiveness of a specific formation, and generating tactical recommendations. The TacticAI model28 shows the possibility of creating AI-based tactical recommendation, which proposes recommendations on the further enhancements of formation recognition to guide actionable coaching.

Although the interpretability of the learned representations is improved with the help of attention visualization and ablation analysis, it is poor in comparison to tactical characterizations made by experts. The next generation of work that involves tactical concepts (i.e., pressing intensity, defensive compactness, or attacking width) would improve the practical use of the results of the formation recognition in coaching. Combining with knowledge in the domain by hybrid neuro symbolic would be an encouraging way forward in the attainment of recognition accuracy as well as semantic interpretability31,32. Additionally, while Table 1 reports the selected hyperparameters, a comprehensive sensitivity analysis examining model stability across varying hyperparameter configurations was not conducted; future work should systematically evaluate performance variance under different learning rates, attention heads, and embedding dimensions. Finally, ethical considerations warrant attention: automated tactical analysis raises questions about fairness in competitive balance if access remains limited to well-resourced clubs, data governance for player tracking information, and the appropriate role of AI in coaching decisions that affect player careers and team outcomes.

Conclusion

This paper proposed a new deep-learning model called Hierarchical Dual-Stream Spatiotemporal Graph Transformer (HDS-SGT) whose main purpose is to identify dynamic tactical formations in professional football. The limitations of the current strategies are tackled by the proposed framework, which connects two streams (processing stack) to represent a combination of spatiotemporal graph attention with a temporal transformer inputs, introduces dynamic Role Assignment Module to flexibly represent positional appearance, and uses multi-scale temporal modelling to address hierarchical tactical interdependencies with estimated outcomes, and implements architecture optimizations that ensure real-time inference functionality.

Comprehensive experimental evaluation on 847 professional football matches demonstrates that HDS-SGT has a classification accuracy of 94.7% and transition detecting an F1-score of 0.912, and 8.3 and 11.6% higher than the current state-of-the-art baseline systems respectively. Studies of ablation established synergy in the contribution of all architectural elements with the dual-stream design and Dynamic Role Assignment Module making the greatest contribution individually. The framework can keep the real-time inference capability at 32.1ms latency, making it viable to implement it in the live match analysis scenarios.

The contributions of this work are not confined to the particular area of formation recognition to the wider meanings on multi-agent spatiotemporal analysis. A graph-based relational modelling system using transformer-based temporal processing successfully leads to a successful integration and offers an architecture template to various team sports and multi-agent coordination environments. Dynamic Role Assignment paradigm provides a broad-based solution to situations in which there is a temporal fluidity in the agent identities or even roles. Multi-scale temporal modelling strategy is used to deal with hierarchical temporal structure which is the nature of most complex behavioral systems.

There is a large amount of practical implication of the research to football analytics and coaching. Real-time formation recognition enables automated tactical observation and monitoring, reducing reliance on manual expert analysis. The hierarchical classification architecture provides multi-resolution tactical insights, supporting analytical goals ranging from in-match intervention to long-term tactical evolution. The exhibited cross-dataset generalization implies a strong application in various competitions and in tactical situations.

Future research should prioritize three directions: (1) joint adversarial formation modeling, where simultaneous analysis of both teams’ tactical structures could enable predictive opponent analysis. We estimate this would require extending the graph architecture to 22-node representations with inter-team edge modeling; (2) integration with computer vision pipelines, specifically targeting sub-meter tracking accuracy from broadcast video to democratize access beyond elite clubs with dedicated tracking systems; and (3) tactical intent prediction, moving from descriptive formation labels to prescriptive recommendations with estimated 6–12 month development timeline given current architectural foundations. These focused extensions would advance HDS-SGT toward comprehensive AI-assisted tactical analysis. Among these, vision-based tracking integration carries the highest methodological risk due to error propagation from detection inaccuracies, while adversarial formation modeling is most feasible given the existing architectural foundations.

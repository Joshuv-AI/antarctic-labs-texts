Microwave-assisted eco-friendly synthesis of carbon quantum dots derived Cichorium intybus for fluorometric detection of cefdinir
Authors: El-Sayed Khafagy, Amr Selim Abu Lila, Ashraf M Ashmawy, Ahmed Al Saqr, Mohammed F Aldawsari, Ahmed M Abdel-Raoof
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGreen carbon quantum dots (CQDs) with minimal environmental damage can be used as a fluorescent probe. Owing to their non-toxicity and high solubility in water, they can play a significant role in environmental monitoring and water safety. Here, a straightforward hydrothermal process using a home microwave was used to create CQDs using chicory leaves known as Cichorium Intybus. The probe was used for the determination of cefdinir (CFD) in various samples such as biological and pharmaceuticals to verify the practicality of the proposed method. The CQDs showed a fluorescence with the wavelength varying from 375 to 500 nm and the maximum emission wavelength being at 419 nm. The linear range was 10 nmol L−1 to 700 nmol L−1 with a detection limit of CFD of 3.00 nmol L−1. Based on the ideas of click chemistry principles, the “Click Analytical Chemistry Index” (CACI) is a tool for evaluating and contrasting the usefulness and application of analytical techniques. This work broadens the use of CQDs in pharmaceutical analysis, spiked plasma and synthetic urine samples. Introduction

Cefdinir (CFD) represents a broad-spectrum, third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic. It is used for the urinary tract infections treatment, as well as upper and lower respiratory tracts infection1. Given the significant therapeutic relevance of this pharmaceutical agent, a variety of analytical methodologies have been proposed for the determination of CFD in biological fluids and pharmaceutical formulations, including chromatographic techniques2–7, spectrophotometric8,9, spectrofluorimetric methods10–12 and electrochemical13, however, these reported methods lacking selectivity with time-consuming and tedious techniques. The clinical and pharmaceutical relevance of CFD as a third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, emphasizing its widespread therapeutic use and the necessity for sensitive analytical methods for its quantification in biological matrices.

The green chemistry concept is a reaction to the imperative for enhanced sustainability in industrial methodologies, particularly within the field of organic synthesis, which subsequently catalyzed the subsequent evolution of green analytical chemistry (GAC)14. While the discipline of green chemistry predominantly concentrated on mitigating the ecological repercussions of extensive organic methodologies, the concept of GAC was developed to fulfill the demand for sustainable practices within the scope of analytical laboratories15. CACI seeks for the field of chemical analysis, applying the same concepts of simplicity, effectiveness, and practical consideration with just a click and transforming synthetic techniques with its dependability and simplicity. By decreasing resource consumption, lowering effort, and increasing analysis speed and efficiency, CACI is intended to optimize analytical operations. The term “CACI” was established to study a clear number of concepts focusing on simplicity, speed, and efficiency, which are partially overlooked.

Chicory (Cichorium intybus L.) is belonging to the genus Cichorium and the family Asteraceae of a perennial herbaceous plant, cultivated on a global scale. It has a wide application such as food production and dietary supplementation. Various bioactive compounds can be present in chicory plant, such as polyphenols, oligofructose, inulin, and sesquiterpene lactones16. It is widely recognized in Egypt as an inexpensive, locally available medicinal herb used to manage illnesses like fatty liver and as a popular coffee replacement. leaves are a nutrient-dense, 92% water-based leafy green containing bioactive compounds like sesquiterpene lactones and phenolic acids17. The role of chicory is strictly as a biomass precursor undergoing microwave-assisted pyrolysis, leading to carbonization and formation of CQDs. During this process, original phytochemical constituents are thermally decomposed and structurally transformed into carbonaceous nanostructures. The synthesis protocol includes purification steps (e.g., filtration and dilution) that remove residual low-molecular-weight by-products to minimize generating any persistent toxic by-products.

Biomass-derived CQDs are advantageous due to their low cost, environmental sustainability, facile synthesis, and favorable optical properties (e.g., excitation-dependent fluorescence, high photostability, and tunable surface functionalities) and as a competitive and sustainable alternative to conventional semiconductor quantum dots, particularly in GAC18. Upon converting waste product into a high value of CQDs biomass, it aided in reducing resource waste and promoting sustainability development19. The synthesis of CQDs from plentiful and inexpensive precursors is known as biomass-based CQDs, and although the process is somewhat complicated but less time-consuming, it also offers the advantages of stability and a comparatively high quantum yield (QY).

The present work created a new environmentally friendly microwave-assisted synthesis of CQDs for CFD fluorometric analysis. For the first time, mashed chicory leaves paste was microwave-heated in a single step to create CQDs in a very short amount of time as a green carbon source. Additionally, the goal of CACI, to offer a metric framework for method evaluation and for assessing analytical techniques. The study objective was the development and validation of a green, fluorescence-based analytical method using biomass-derived carbon quantum dots (CQDs) for the sensitive and selective determination of CFD in pharmaceutical formulations and biological samples.

Experimental

Materials and reagents and instruments, see supplementary file

Procedures

Green synthesis of CQDs with the aid of microwave

About 30 gm of chicory leaves were mashed in a domestic blender to obtain it as a suitable paste. The prepared chicory leaves paste was subjected to microwave irradiation in a domestic microwave oven (900 W) for 6 min. After cooling to room temperature, the obtained carbonized product was dispersed in 50 mL of deionized water, followed by appropriate dilution to volume to obtain the CQDs solution. The CQDs can be obtained in a dry powder through a freeze-drying process for further characterization.

Fluorescence quantum yield calculation of the chicory leaves CQDs

The relative quantum yield (Ф) calculation for the synthesized CQDs was studied using the methodology framework presented in more detail20 in the supplementary material.

CQDs-based probe for CFD determination

Different quantities of CFD working solutions were mixed with 500 μL of CQDs at a concentration of 500 μg/mL, then the total volumes were completed with Britton–Robinson (BR) of pH 7 for each to 10 mL to obtain a final concentration range within 0.0 to 700 nM. The fluorescence intensity was determined at λem equal 419 nm after appropriate excitation at λex equal 286 nm. The entire experiment was replicated five times and conducted at ambient temperature.

CFD determination in synthetic urine, plasma samples, and pharmaceutical formulations

Five CFD 300 mg per capsule were carefully weighed and finely ground. A bout 19.77 mg of CFD was transferred into a 50 mL volumetric flask and dissolved in 30 mL of methanol, then shaken and sonicated for 15 min. The prepared solution was further diluted with BR pH 7 to reach a final concentration of 1.0 µM. The general analytical procedure described above Sect. “CQDs-based probe for CFD determination” was applied using aliquots of CFD standard solutions at varying concentrations within the specified calibration range.

Given the high sensitivity of this procedure, synthetic urine samples were spiked with known concentrations of CFD prior to dilution to minimize matrix effects while maintaining analyte detectability, resulting in a final CFD concentration between 0.1 and 7.0 µM, where the mean values for synthetic urine controls were considered. Concentration aliquots of the drug solutions achieving the concentration range of 10–700 nM was used to repeat the general process. Urine can be used to measure CFD directly; no sample pretreatment is required.

A suitable aliquot from the stock solution in our recovery was incorporated into 5 ml of plasma, followed by 10 ml of acetonitrile as a solvent facilitates both protein denaturation and CFD extraction then agitation for 2 min. Upon the centrifugation the supernatant is separated by centrifugation for 15 min at 4000 rpm evaporated by rotary evaporator. The drug residue was dissolved in BR pH 7, and the general procedure was repeated using concentration aliquots of the drug solutions covering the concentration range.

Results and discussion

Structural and surface characterization of CQDs

Using local agricultural waste to minimize the requirement for expensive or harmful precursors is a good idea, like chicory for CQD manufacturing that offers a green alternative to typical top-down chemical synthesis processes. The selection of the carbon source is the key element for the successful synthesis of CQDs. Upon carbonization of the chicory leaves in the domestic microwave, the fluorescence QY of CQDs was found to be 39.29%, which is higher than the values reported for many other CQDs derived from other biomass waste byproducts21–23. CQDs are a class of zero‑dimensional carbon‑based nanomaterials that are widely reported to exhibit good water solubility, tunable fluorescence, low toxicity and favorable biocompatibility. These characteristics have made CQDs attractive platforms for sensing and bioanalytical applications in general24. In this study, we prepared water‑soluble CQDs from chicory leaves and evaluated their fluorescence behavior and analytical performance as a probe for CFD in pharmaceutical formulations and biological matrices.

The vibrational modes at 1625 cm−1 and 1425 cm−1 are observed at aromatic ring structures presence within the CQDs with a C = C bonds. The vibrational modes ascribed to 2924 cm−1 and 3427 cm−1 suggesting the presence of methylidene groups within the CQDs in conjunction with hydroxyl groups. The identification of C-H bonds, indicated by the band at 2924 cm−1, reinforces the carbon-based framework of CQDs25. The doublet peaks at approximately 2361 cm⁻1 and 2319 cm⁻1 in the FTIR spectrum are owing to characteristic of atmospheric carbon dioxide. The FT-IR spectra of CQDs powder exhibit three characteristic peaks at 2851, 1625, and 1241 cm−1, which can be ascribed to the vibrations of asymmetric CH2, carboxyl/carbonyl C = O, and C-O bending, respectively26.

Optical properties and quenching mechanism

CQDs-based probe for CFD sensing mechanism

An essential criterion for assessing fluorescent materials is their optical characteristics. PL (photoluminescence) measurements and UV–visible absorption spectroscopy are used to characterize the optical characteristics of the produced CQDs. When exposed to visible light, the produced CQDs aqueous solution takes on a brownish-yellow color. Under ultraviolet absorption impact, CQDs are seen to show two separate absorption peaks at 284 nm and 317 nm Fig. S2. Also, CFD has a little UV absorbance at 316 nm excitation wavelength, which confirm the presence of inner filter effect for quenching CQDs by CFD. Additionally, the mixture spectrum is essentially the superposition of the two components and shows clear spectral overlap with the CQDs excitation/emission region where no new absorption band appears in the mixture beyond what is already present in CFD and in CQDs. The synthesized CQDs’ excitation spectrum and CFD’s absorption spectrum (at pH 7.0) substantially overlapped, as shown in Fig. 4. However, CFD interferes with the CQDs’ emission spectrum to some extent. Therefore, the inner filter effect (IFE) has a significant effect on the fluorescence quenching and should be calculated and omitted to determine the corrected fluorescence to validate the suggested mechanism28.

CFD determination condition optimization

Although carbon quantum dots often show satisfactory stability over time and in a variety of pH situations, exposure duration and pH can have an impact on their fluorescence properties. Depending on the pH level, various CQDs may exhibit increased or decreased emission, causing variations in fluorescence intensity. The fluorescence probe showed a stability reading for CQDs intensity over the pH range 4–8 Fig. S6a for seven days (Fig. S6b) where the optimum pH for determination of CFD was pH 7. The reaction between CFD and CQDs for quenching occurred at once and was stable for a long time (Fig. S6c). Various buffers were utilized to obtain the most optimum one for quenching study, and BR was the more appropriate one for the interaction between the emitter and CFD (Fig. S6d).

Analytical performance and method validation

The proposed methodology for quenching utilizing quantum dots, was validated according to the guidelines established by the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)30. A linear calibration curve was derived by plotting the ratio of F0 / F obtained through spectrofluorimetric analysis versus varying concentrations of CFD (see Supplementary materials Fig. 5. The calibration range was established between 10.0 and 700 nM (Table 2). The slope and standard deviation (ϭ) of this calibration curve were determined to calculate the limits of detection (LOD) as 3.3ϭ/slope, while the LOQ was calculated to be 10ϭ/slope. The higher sensitivity of the proposed technique is confirmed by the lower values LOQ and LOD, which were found to be 0.009 and 0.003 µM, respectively (see Table 2). Intra-day (repeatability) and inter-day precision assessments were conducted using three distinct CFD concentrations of 20.0, 250.0, and 600.0 nM, with three measurements taken on a single day and on three separate days. The results showed the high precision of the proposed approach. Table 2. The calculated %R values for five different concentration measurements at three levels, which ranged from 98.25% to 101.67%, showed that the proposed method with higher accuracy Table 2. Robustness was evaluated upon minor changes to some of the method parameters, such as the volume of CQDs, the buffer volume, pH, and the excitation wavelength. Those little adjustments had no discernible effect on the CFD determination’s recovery findings Table 2. The benefits of the proposed (CQD)-based fluorescence probe are also examined, and Table 3 compares it to other published approaches and other analytical techniques for measuring CFD. The proposed one offers several advantages in comparison to these previously published methods. With minimal sample preparation, the CQD fluorescence probe provides a quick, useful, and eco-friendly alternative to HPLC method4, which sometimes call for costly equipment, lengthy analysis periods, and substantial sample preparation. Furthermore, compared to spectrophotometric techniques8,9, it has superior stability, repeatability, and multiple matrices and green solvents Table 3. Lastly, when compared to the other spectrofluorimetric methods10–12, this method offers several advantages, including greater environmental friendliness, applicability in more than two matrices, and lower cost and higher sensitivity with lowest limit of detection level. Method greenness assessment

CACI metric has definite benefits over the following tools: AGREE31, AGREEprep32, RGB1233, the Analytical Eco-scale34 , ComplexMoGAPI35, MoGAPI36, and BAGI37. AGREE, AGREEprep, MoGAPI, ComplexMoGAPI and the Analytical Eco-scale38,39, for instance, the evaluation of the method’s greenness is the focus of CACI on practical issues and offers an unbiased assessment of the method’s efficiency and real-world applicability, whereas RGB1233 takes a multifaceted approach that does not specifically focus on the method’s practicality. Even so, BAGI evaluates how useful analytical techniques are. The BAGI measure ignores several characteristics that CACI takes into account. For instance, the cost of analysis and the method’s suitability for various matrices were not taken into account by the BAGI. Furthermore, semi-quantitative approaches and their evaluation were not covered by BAGI. When evaluating the practicality of approaches, several characteristics are essential. For example, very costly methods cannot be considered viable. However, techniques that can be used with several matrices are more useful. Although there are several reliable indicators for evaluating the environmental friendliness of analytical techniques, there are currently few tools available to assess their applicability. In the present study, the proposed methodology employed a small sample size, thereby facilitating the enhancement of efficiency, sustainability, and precision. The availability of requisite resources, the accessibility of essential chemicals and reagents, laboratory infrastructure, and cost-effectiveness assure the feasibility of the method. Furthermore, due to the accessible spectrofluorimetric apparatus and low cost of less than $10 per sample, the proposed analytical methodology is highly feasible and applicable. The proposed methodology is expanding the utility and scope of the analytical method owing to its applicability for the quantitative analysis of CFD across multiple matrices. The method exhibits high sensitivity and a rapid analysis time for each sample. According to the result obtained in Fig. 7, our suggested CQDs fluorescence probe achieved a CACI metric score of 78, which is a strong practical value, and is deemed practically acceptable.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the extremely rapid carbon quantum dots (CQDs) synthesis from chicory leaves represents a promising development path for fluorescent probes. There is no need for organic solvents or harsh chemicals because the microwave-assisted synthesis is completed in 6 min at an output of 900 W. The successful application of CQDs as turn-off fluorescence probes for measuring CFD in spiked plasma, synthetic urine samples, and pharmaceutical formulations demonstrates their enormous potential in the field of analytical chemistry. The current high recoveries and robust sample preparation suggest minimal matrix interference and explicit demonstration of specificity through interference and selectivity assessments. The creation of the CACI

offers a standardized tool for evaluating and contrasting analytical approaches, encouraging the use of strategies that are not only viable but also useful and efficient. The approach has a total score of 78 on the CACI criteria, making it extremely practical.

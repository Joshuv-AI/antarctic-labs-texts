Economic evaluation of the second booster dose of diphtheria-tetanus-acellular pertussis vaccine for children aged 6 years in Zhejiang Province: A cost-utility analysis
Authors: Ying Su, Yang Zhou, Feng Luo, Shuhan Zheng, Hanqing He, Yao Zhu, Xuewen Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDespite increasing pertussis breakthroughs and waning immunity, this study evaluates the cost-effectiveness of replacing the DT booster with DTaP in 6-year-olds in Zhejiang, China. While implemented in high-income regions, this strategy’s cost-effectiveness remains unassessed in diverse Chinese settings. We address this gap using local data.Using surveillance data from the Zhejiang Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention, literature reviews, and a 10-year simulation timeframe, a decision tree-Markov model was constructed from a societal perspective. The study cohort was 6-year-old children in Zhejiang Province in 2022. Comprehensive evaluation indicators, including Incremental Cost Effectiveness Ratio (ICER) and Incremental Cost-Utility Ratio (ICUR), were used. Robustness was assessed via Deterministic Sensitivity Analysis (DSA) and Probabilistic Sensitivity Analysis (PSA). Simulating 10 years after implementing the DTaP strategy for 628,619 children in 2022, under lower background incidence, DTaP could prevent 1,886 pertussis cases, reduce costs by 1.7 billion CNY, and gain 0.09 QALYs (ICUR = 3,096.15 CNY/QALY). Under higher incidence, it could prevent 8,743 cases, reduce costs by 1.6 billion CNY, and gain 0.084 QALYs (ICUR = 3,081.86 CNY/QALY). Both ICUR values are significantly lower than Zhejiang’s per capita GDP (118,496 CNY). Our findings indicate that administering a second booster dose of DTaP to 6-year-old children is cost-effective, particularly with high incidence of pertussis.

Introduction

Whooping cough (pertussis), cause by Bordetella pertussis, is an acute respiratory infectious disease transmitted through droplets to susceptible individuals.1 Before the era of vaccinations, the global burden of pertussis was high,2 making it a significant cause of infant mortality worldwide.3 Large-scale childhood vaccination programs have successfully reduced the incidence of pertussis and the mortality rate of patients with the disease. However, Countries with high vaccination rates, such as the United States, the United Kingdom, and several in Europe, have reported a significant resurgence of pertussis in recent years.4 According to the World Health Organization (WHO) statistics, the global incidence of pertussis in 2023 was approximately 3.2 times that in 2022.5 The shifting epidemiology of whooping cough is the result of multiple interconnected factors. On one hand, since the COVID-19 pandemic, public awareness of respiratory infections has increased significantly. At the same time, nucleic acid detection technologies such as PCR – valued for their high sensitivity and specificity – have been widely adopted and were officially incorporated into the national diagnostic criteria for whooping cough by the end of 2023. On the other hand, during the pandemic, reduced social contact and temporary disruptions in vaccination efforts led to a growing pool of susceptible individuals, which has also contributed significantly to the recent surge in whooping cough cases.6 In the first quarter of 2024, China reported over 58,990 cases of pertussis, a tenfold increase compared to the same period in the previous year.7 The incidence rate of pertussis is progressing to levels comparable to those observed in the late 1980s.8

Neither vaccination nor natural infection confers lifelong immunity against pertussis, as both types of protection wane over time,9 vaccines containing pertussis components have been reinforced for preschool children in most developed countries by incorporating five doses as part of their routine vaccination program.10 Among the 194 WHO member states, 65 (33.51%) have adopted acellular pertussis (aP) vaccines in their immunization programs, with 29 (44.62%) administering a fifth dose of the vaccine. Furthermore, 33 states (28.21%) have incorporated a fifth dose for children aged 3–6 years.11 Currently, the immunization program in China includes administering four doses of pertussis vaccines and diphtheria-tetanus (DT) vaccines to children aged 3, 4, 5, and 18–24 months. At the age of 6, another dose of DT combination vaccine is administered.12 In a cross-sectional serological survey conducted in Zhejiang Province in 2020, >50% of the participants aged ≥7 years had undetectable anti-pertussis toxin Immunoglobulin G antibodies ( <5 IU/mL), primarily due to the waning of vaccine-cause immunity.9 Therefore, implementing booster immunizations for pertussis is urgent and essential in China.

Pertussis imposes a significant economic burden compared with other vaccine-preventable illnesses. In a study conducted in China, the average direct medical costs for pertussis and the average costs per hospitalization were reported to be 1878.70 Chinese CNY (CNY) ($279.33) and 7104.08 CNY ($1056.23), respectively.13 Health economic evaluation can effectively offer evidence to policymakers and public health officials. However, reports on health economic evaluation of pertussis vaccine booster strategies in China are lacking.

In this study, we analyze the cost-effectiveness of pertussis booster vaccination in 6-year-old children in Zhejiang Province using a Markov model. While international studies have conducted health economic evaluations of pertussis boosters, their results cannot be reliably extrapolated to China due to differences in immunization policies, low birth rates, vaccine costs, and vaccination coverage. Although Shanghai pioneered the first domestic evaluation, variations in model structures, parameter sources, data quality, and cost-calculation methods – combined with inter-provincial differences in disease incidence and surveillance systems – may yield divergent results. Our province-specific assessment not only clarifies the importance of the 6-year-old booster within China’s immunization strategy but also contributes unique evidence to global pertussis control efforts by identifying key cost-effectiveness determinants in this understudied context.

Methods

Decision tree – Markov model

To compare the cost-effectiveness of vaccinating versus not vaccinating 6-year-old children with vaccines containing pertussis components, we examined two strategies. (1) Strategy 1 (Current Strategy): Vaccinating 6-year-old children with a Diphtheria and Tetanus (DT) vaccine (maintaining the current national immunization strategy involving four doses of primary immunization); (2) Strategy 2 (Proposed Strategy): Vaccinating 6-year-old children with a Diphtheria, Tetanus, and Acellular Pertussis (DTaP) vaccine containing pertussis components.

A decision tree Markov model was constructed using TreeAge Pro Healthcare 2023 (TreeAge Software, Inc., Williamstown, MA, USA) to simulate the outcomes of the two strategies. The model incorporated key parameters such as pertussis-related disease history, health outcomes, vaccine efficacy, vaccine coverage, vaccine costs, and medical costs. The analysis was conducted from a societal perspective, and a cost-effectiveness analysis was performed, with all costs discounted at a rate of 3%.14 The results were reported in accordance with the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS), providing insights into the comparative health and economic impacts of vaccinating 6-year-old children with pertussis-containing vaccines (Figure 1). Figure 1.Decision tree-Markov model construction.

Decision tree-Markov model construction.

The Markov model simulates the pertussis disease process based on the natural history of pertussis and vaccine-induced immunity status. The model begins with individuals in one of three possible health states: (i) acute pertussis infection, (ii) vaccine-induced immunity following vaccination, or (iii) natural immunity following infection. For vaccinated individuals, those who develop complete immunity transition to a “vaccine immune” absorbing state, while those with partial or no immunity transition to a “susceptible” state. Similarly, naturally infected individuals who develop complete immunity transition to a “naturally immune” state, while those with partial or no immunity transition to a “susceptible” state.

Both strategies involve expenses related to vaccine management fees, cold chain costs, and preventive vaccination clinic management fees. The vaccine prices are based on the government’s unified procurement prices, with the DTaP vaccine priced at 5.74 CNY per dose and the DT vaccine at 2.56 CNY per dose. Vaccine administration rate and disease incidence data

The vaccination coverage and disease incidence data were sourced from the immunization program data monitoring platform or epidemiological surveys of the Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Additionally, pertussis incidence rates under active surveillance were obtained through community-based fixed-point monitoring surveys conducted under an active surveillance model. The DTaP vaccination coverage data for Zhejiang Province, reflecting full 4-dose series completion, were obtained from the World Health Organization’s official website, with a reported coverage rate of 95.04% (90.67–99.41%).14 The pertussis incidence rates were based on three years of data from Zhejiang Province (2019–2022). The average incidence rates from 2019 to 2021 were relatively low, while the average incidence rate in 2022 was higher. Another set of incidence data was obtained from an active epidemiological study of pertussis in Zhejiang Province, which was estimated based on the active surveillance model for pertussis in Tianjin.23 The natural mortality rate was sourced from the chronic disease surveillance data of the Zhejiang Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention (Table 1).

Disease economic burden investigation

In this study, the collection of disease burden data was based on pertussis cases that met the inclusion criteria. Using the reported incidence rates in Zhejiang Province, stratified sampling was conducted across hospitals at the provincial, municipal, and county levels. The investigation was primarily carried out in counties and cities such as Yiwu and Wenzhou, Zhejiang Province. Data on direct and indirect medical expenses were collected through the medical information systems of healthcare institutions and patient questionnaires. Direct medical expenses included costs for outpatient/inpatient treatment, examinations, and medications. Indirect medical expenses covered losses of work time and corresponding wage reductions due to vaccination or outpatient/inpatient visits. The assessment of indirect burden was conducted solely from a societal perspective.

Utility values

Quality Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) were used to evaluate the health outcomes. QALYs comprehensively reflect the impact of interventions on the quantity and quality of life. Health utility represents the preference for the health outcomes resulting from the intervention and is indicated using numerical values between 0 and 1 (1 indicates perfect health, whereas 0 indicates death).This study requires key utility parameters, including health utility values for pertussis, hospitalization for pertussis, treatment with mechanical ventilation, and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO). Currently, there are no publicly available utility values for these conditions in China. Therefore, the utility parameters used in our model were sourced from previous studies. Sensitivity analysis

Owing to the uncertainty associated with the parameters in our model, we employed the deterministic sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) to assess the impact of the parameters on our results and investigate the stability of our findings. The deterministic sensitivity analysis was conducted to explore the impact of varying parameters on the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICUR). A tornado diagram was used to visually present the influence of each parameter. In the deterministic sensitivity analysis, tornado diagrams were used to visually represent variables such as the incidence rates of pertussis, costs, and pertussis-related utility values. PSA was performed using Monte Carlo simulations, and visualization was achieved by plotting cost-effectiveness planes.

Results

Cost-utility analysis

From a societal perspective, the analysis was conducted based on the incidence rates under two surveillance modes: routine surveillance and active surveillance. Under a 3% discount rate, the costs for DTaP and DT vaccines were 3,270.49 CNY($456.21)and 3,542.3 CNY($494.03), respectively, with an incremental cost of 271.84 CNY($37.91). The utility values (QALYs) for DTaP and DT were 19.088437 QALY and 19.000639 QALY, respectively, with an incremental utility of 0.087798 QALY. The incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR) for DTaP compared to DT was 3,096.15 CNY/QALY.

In the active surveillance mode, the costs for DTaP and DT were 3,140.35 CNY($437.96) and 3,400.66 CNY($474.25), respectively, with an incremental cost of 260.31 CNY($36.30). The utility values (QALYs) for DTaP and DT were 19.094462 QALY and 19.009996 QALY, respectively, with an incremental utility of 0.084466 QALY. Cost-utility acceptability curve

In this study, the cost-utility acceptability curve indicates that when the willingness-to-pay threshold is reached (1–3 times the per capita GDP), the probability of the DTaP vaccination being cost-effective is 100%. This suggests that, at this threshold, the cost-effectiveness of the intervention is fully supported economically. Specifically, as the willingness-to-pay increases, the incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR) of the DTaP vaccine gradually decreases, ultimately falling entirely within the economically acceptable range at the threshold level (Figure 3). Discussion

Despite the existence of effective vaccines, there has been an upward trend in the number of pertussis cases among children aged 5–9 years, with a concurrent downward trend in the age of those affected by pertussis in China.24 This epidemiological shift prompted a significant revision to China’s national immunization program effective January 1, 2025, transitioning from the previous schedule (DTP at 3, 4, 5, and 18 months plus DT at 6 years) to an enhanced schedule featuring DTaP vaccination at 2, 4, 6, and 18 months with an additional booster at 6 years. These programmatic changes the urgent need to reassess current immunization strategies, and our study provides a timely framework for evaluating the potential health benefits of such booster programs against their implementation costs within this new policy context.

In the present study, data were derived from field surveys using real-world pertussis economic burden investigations in Zhejiang province, China. Vaccine costs were obtained from government procurement prices, and the actual incidence rates of pertussis reflected monitoring data specific to Zhejiang province, making it highly representative. In the current study, the health economics model showed that under a low-incidence scenario, the DTaP booster strategy at age 6 prevented 1,886 pertussis-related illnesses, resulted in 0.087798 additional QALYs, and saved 1.7 billion CNY, with an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of 3,096.15 CNY per QALY. Under a high-incidence scenario, the strategy prevented 8,743 pertussis-related illnesses, resulted in 0.084466 additional QALYs, and saved 1.6 billion CNY, with an ICER of 3,081.86 CNY per QALY. Both ICER values are significantly lower than Zhejiang Province’s per capita GDP (118,496 CNY), indicating that the DTaP booster strategy is highly cost-effective and represents a significant economic value. These findings align with a study from Shanghai, which also supported the cost-effectiveness of pertussis booster vaccination.15

However, the results of this study contrast with findings from Japan.19 A Japanese study reported an ICER of 71,605,491 JPY per QALY (approximately 656,931 USD) for achieving herd immunity in unvaccinated infants by increasing booster vaccination in 6-year-old children. This suggests that such a strategy might not be cost-effective in Japan. Conversely, in high-incidence settings, replacing DT with DTaP for adolescents aged 11 and older was found to be cost-effective in Japan. These differences likely stem from variations in vaccine prices, cost policies, and disease prevalence across countries. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of contextual factors in evaluating the cost-effectiveness of vaccination strategies and underscore the need.

Despite the contrasting findings with Japanese studies, Japanese research also indicates that adolescent vaccination is cost-effective from both a societal and healthcare payer perspective.25 In China, however, there is currently a lack of health economic evaluations for pertussis booster strategies targeting adolescents. This gap in research evidence needs to be addressed to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the economic value of such strategies. Future studies should consider China’s disease prevalence and economic environment to explore the cost-effectiveness of pertussis booster immunization in other age groups. This will help optimize vaccination policies and provide robust scientific evidence for decision-making.

In our univariate sensitivity analysis, the pertussis incidence rate was identified as the most significant factor affecting the incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR). This is because incidence directly determines the extent and severity of disease transmission in the population, which in turn affects the disease prevention benefits of vaccination. When incidence increases, the number of cases avoided through vaccination rises, making the vaccination strategy more cost-effective. Conversely, lower incidence may reduce the relative benefits of vaccination, leading to an increase in ICUR. Additionally, vaccine cost plays a critical role in ICUR. Higher vaccine costs directly increase the total cost of the vaccination strategy. If costs rise significantly without a corresponding improvement in disease prevention, ICUR will increase, potentially rendering the strategy economically inefficient. Other parameters, such as hospitalization utility, ECOM utility, hospitalization rate, and ventilator treatment utility, also influence ICUR, particularly in severe cases. Parameters like discount rate and home treatment utility have relatively minor impacts on ICUR. In practical decision-making, it is essential to focus on the accuracy and stability of these key parameters while considering the potential effects of other parameters. This comprehensive approach will ensure a more reliable and robust health economic evaluation of pertussis booster vaccination for the 6-year-old age group, providing scientific support for immunization strategy development.

In our probabilistic sensitivity analysis, we conducted 1,000 iterations using Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate the health economic value of the pertussis booster strategy under different surveillance models. The results showed that regardless of whether the surveillance model was active (high incidence) or routine, the probability of the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) being below one times the per capita GDP of Zhejiang Province (the willingness-to-pay threshold of 118,496 CNY) was 100%. This indicates that the booster strategy is highly cost-effective across all surveillance models. Further analysis of the incremental cost-utility scatter plot revealed that all data points fell within the fourth quadrant, with ICER values ranging from 3,287.62 to 3,501.01 CNY per QALY, which is significantly lower than the set threshold. Additionally, the cost-utility acceptability curve demonstrated that the probability of the strategy being cost-effective reached 100% when the willingness-to-pay threshold was met. These findings confirm that in high-incidence settings, the booster strategy not only effectively reduces the disease burden of pertussis but also demonstrates significant economic feasibility.

Despite the robust results, this study has certain limitations. Due to the lack of relevant data in China, some parameters used in the model were sourced from international studies. To assess the stability of the model and parameters, we conducted sensitivity analyses and model validation, including external validation and cross-validation, which ensured high credibility of the results. However, the study did not consider the impact of herd immunity, as this would require more complex data and model development. Future research should address this gap by conducting additional modeling studies to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of pertussis vaccination strategies in different scenarios. These efforts will contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the economic value of pertussis immunization and provide stronger evidence for policy optimization.

Conclusion

This study suggests that substituting DT with DTaP vaccines for 6-year-old preschoolers in Zhejiang Province may be a cost-saving immunization strategy. I replicated and validated in larger scale studies, this substitution strategy can help maintain protection against diphtheria and tetanus as well as enhance immunity to significantly reduce pertussis incidence among school-aged children while alleviating the associated financial burdens on families.

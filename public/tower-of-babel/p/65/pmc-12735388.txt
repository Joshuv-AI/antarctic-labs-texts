Prevalence of Insomnia among the Students of Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) Enrolled in 2023
Authors: Biplab Jamatia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Insomnia is defined as a condition involving insufficient or poor-quality sleep, marked by one or more symptoms such as difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep, early morning awakening, or non-restorative sleep. Sleep plays a critical role in supporting mental functions and, consequently, overall performance. Inadequate sleep reduces general alertness, impairs attention, and slows cognitive processing.Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted among students enrolled at Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) in 2023. The Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) scale was employed to assess the prevalence of insomnia within this group. Data were collected via an online Google Form from November 2023 to June 2024.Results:A total of 17,172 students responded to the study. The prevalence of insomnia was 42.9% (95% CI: 42.2%–43.6%). The prevalence of subthreshold insomnia, clinical insomnia of moderate severity, and clinical insomnia of severe severity were 30.6% (95% CI: 29.9%–31.3%), 10.2% (95% CI: 9.7%–10.6%), and 2.1% (95% CI: 1.9%–2.3%), respectively. More than half of the students, 57.1% (95% CI: 56.4%–57.8%), did not have insomnia. The prevalence of insomnia among female students was 44.8%, compared with 40.6% among male students, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001). A significant difference (P < 0.001) was also found in the prevalence of insomnia between students living in rural areas (37.1%) and those living in urban areas (46.5%).Conclusion:Approximately two out of every five students at IGNOU experience insomnia.

INTRODUCTION

Sleep was central to health and well-being.[1] Representing one-third of our life span, sleep was considered to be an important biological and behavioral constituent of human physiology.[2] As such, metabolic and energy rebooting for proper brain function during the active hours could occur during our sleep.[3] For older adults between 46 and 60 years of age, the recommended sleep duration was 7 hours or more per night on a regular basis. In comparison, for younger adults between 18 and 45 years of age, an even longer duration (>9 hours) was considered appropriate.[4] Sleep greatly influences mental functions, and thus, affects performance. Insufficient sleep decreases general alertness, impairs attention, and slows cognitive processing.[56]

Insomnia was defined as an experience of insufficient or poor-quality sleep, characterized by one or more of the following symptoms such as difficulty in initiating or maintaining sleep, early awakening, and poor restorative sleep.[7]

Worldwide reports suggest that around one-third of the adult population complains of insomnia symptoms.[8] Insomnia affects 33% of adults in the United States,[9] whereas in Europe the prevalence of severe insomnia was between 4% and 22%.[1011]

The objective of this study was to measure the prevalence of insomnia in the students of Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) enrolled in 2023.

METHODOLOGY

This cross-sectional descriptive study was carried out among the students of IGNOU enrolled in 2023 (during January and July cycle). The students of Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) were studying through open and distance learning mode/blended mode. Students were enrolled in 316 academic educational program of the university. IGNOU was offering four awareness and appreciation programs, 81 certificate programs, 14 postgraduate certificate programs, 29 diploma programs, 66 postgraduate diploma programs, 77 master programs, and 45 graduate programs. Students of IGNOU were located both within India and outside the country. Those residing outside India were designated as international students.

The sample size was calculated based on the study conducted by Shah F Autism et al.,[12] which reported a prevalence rate of insomnia of 33%. It was computed using the formula N = 4pq/r² =4p (1–p)/d² = (4 × 33 × 67)/(4 × 4) =553, where P represented the prevalence of insomnia (33%), q was 1–p, and d denoted the precision (corresponding to effect size). The confidence level adopted was 95%, in line with the standard practice of most researchers, who present their results with a 95% confidence interval (CI). As the response rate from study participants was unknown, it was decided to send the questionnaires to all students.

The prevalence of insomnia was assessed using the Insomnia Severity Index scale.[13] This scale comprised seven questions, each scored on a scale from zero to four. The scores from all seven items were summed to obtain a total score, which was classified as follows: 0–7 = no clinically significant insomnia, 8–14 = subthreshold insomnia, 15–21 = clinical insomnia (moderate severity), and 22–28 = clinical insomnia (severe).

Data were collected via an online Google Form. The questionnaire was made available in both English and Hindi. Permission to conduct the study among IGNOU students was obtained from the university authorities. All students enrolled in 2023 (both January and July admission cycles) were sent emails containing the Google Form link, along with a request to complete it. Responses were automatically saved to Google Drive upon submission. Data collection took place from November 2023 to June 2024.

The data were analyzed using Epi Info version 7.2.5.0. Descriptive statistics, including frequency and percentage, were used to summarize the findings. The Chi-square test was employed to examine associations between insomnia and various factors, with statistical significance set at P ≤ 0.05.

RESULT

A total of 17172 students comprised the cohort. It comprised of 12,743 students (74.2% of total participants) from 28 states, 4,191 students (24.4% of total participants) from eight union territories, and 238 students (1.4% of total participants) from 26 countries (Afghanistan, Argentina, Bahrain, Egypt, France, Gambia, Ghana, Kenya, Kingdom of United Arab, Kuwait, Malawi, Mali, Mauritius, Mozambique, Nepal, Niger State, Nigeria, Oman, Qatar, Sierra Leone, Somalia, South Sudan, United Arab Emirates, Uganda, Zambia). Majority of the international students belongs to the general category (74.79%).

Around half of the students belonged to the general category [50.4% (95% CI: 49.7%–51.2%)], followed by OBC, SC, and ST categories.

Demographic profile of the students

Most of the students were graduates [39.5% (38.7%–40.2%)], followed by undergraduates [33% (95% CI: 32.3%–33.7%)], and master degree or above [27.5% (95% CI: 26.8%–28.2)]. A majority of the students were from urban areas [61.79% (95% CI: 61.1%–62.5%)], as compared to those from rural areas [38.21% (95% CI: 37.5%–38.9%)]. Around three fourth of the students practice Hindu religion [76.3% (95% CI: 75.7%–77%)], followed by Islam and Christianity. More than half of these students were living in a nuclear family [52.8% (95% CI: 52.1%–53.6%)], followed by joint family [32.2% (95% CI: 31.5%–32.9%)], and those staying away from family [14.9% (95% CI: 14.4%–15.5%)]. A majority of these student did not had hypertension [85.9% (95% CI: 85.5%–86.4%)], diabetes mellitus [94.9% (95% CI: 94.6%–95.3%)], and other chronic diseases (excluding hypertension and diabetes) [92.4% (95% CI: 91.9%–92.8%)].

The mean age of students was 25.6 ± 7.2 SD. The mean ages of male and female students were 26.2 ± 7.6 SD and 25.1 ± 6.7 SD, respectively, and P value was < 0.0001. The mean age of students staying in rural and urban areas was 25.3 ± 6.4 SD and 25.8 ± 7.6 SD, respectively, and P value was < 0.0001.

The prevalence of insomnia was more among students who had hypertension (66.4%), as compared to the students who did not had hypertension (39.7%) and who did not know the status of their hypertension (60.9%) and difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001).

The prevalence of insomnia was more among the students who had diabetes mellitus (48.9%) as compared to the students who did not had diabetes mellitus (42.1%). Prevalence of insomnia among all international students (36.1%) was less, compared to the students coming from states (42.05%), and union territories (45.9%). DISCUSSION

There were a total of 7,30,177 students enrolled in the University in the academic year of 2023. An email was sent to all students during November 2023 and April 2024. A total of 17,172 response were received from the students by 30th April 2024, effectively resulting in 2.3% students participating in this study.

The prevalence of insomnia was 42.9% (95% CI: 42.2%–43.6%) among the students of IGNOU. This prevalence was higher than the medical students of Delhi (30.3%),[14] general population of Bathinda (33%),[12] and medical students of Bengaluru (39.4%).[15] This prevalence of insomnia among university students in South Asian Region through the systematic review of studies indicates that the prevalence rate of insomnia in the seven studies ranged between 35.4% (95% CI: 32.4–38.5%) and 70% (95% CI: 65.7–74.1%). The pooled prevalence of insomnia among university students was 52.1% (95% CI: 41.1–63.1%).[16] The high prevalence of the insomnia among the students of IGNOU as compared to the students of other universities might be due to nature of the course work of IGNOU. Most of the students of IGNOU were not full-time students like other universities, and were part of the working population.

The prevalence of insomnia among the international students of IGNOU was 36.1%, and it was also higher than the Medical and Paramedical Students in Jordan (26.0%),[3] and medical students enrolled in the Faculty of Medicine, King Abdulaziz University (KAU), Jeddah, KSA (33%).[17]

Prevalence of subthreshold insomnia, clinical insomnia (moderate severity) and clinical insomnia (severe) of IGNOU students was 30.6% (95% CI: 29.9%–31.3%), 10.2% (95% CI: 9.7%–10.6%), and 2.1% (95% CI: 1.9%–2.3%), respectively. This prevalence was higher as compared to the undergraduate students of (Medicine, Dentistry, Nursing, Pharmacy, Law, Hospital Administration, Business Administration and Agriculture) Siksha ‘O’ Anusandhan University, Odisha [mild insomnia (29.3%), moderate insomnia (8.4%), and severe insomnia (0.7%)].[18]

The current study indicates that the prevalence of insomnia was higher among the female students (44.8%) as compared to the male students (40.8%). However, the prevalence of insomnia was higher among males than among females among the medical students of Delhi.[14] It was further observed that there was no difference in the prevalence of insomnia among male and female medical students in Bengaluru, India.[15]

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the prevalence of insomnia among students of Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) was found to be 42.9%, indicating that approximately two out of every five students at IGNOU experiences insomnia. It was recommended that universities implement programs for screening sleep quality and mental health conditions among students. Furthermore, conducting interventional and longitudinal studies would enhance the understanding of insomnia’s nature and its impact on students.

Dynamic fog node placement optimization using adaptive dynamic pufferfish optimization for real-time IoT networks
Authors: Ashraf A. Abu-Ein, Obaida M. Al-Hazaimeh, Mohammed Tawfik, Islam S. Fathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The exponential growth of Internet of Things (IoT) deployments demands fog computing architectures that position computational resources at the network edge to ensure low-latency processing. However, optimal fog node placement in dynamic environments remains a significant challenge due to node mobility, random failures, and fluctuating traffic patterns, which require continuous adaptation to maintain service quality. This paper introduces the Dynamic Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (D-POA), a bio-inspired metaheuristic that leverages behavioral strategies observed in pufferfish species to adaptively reposition fog nodes in real-time, balancing exploration of the solution space with refinement of promising configurations. D-POA formulates the placement problem as a continuous multi-objective optimization that simultaneously addresses network connectivity, area coverage, and movement costs to minimize service disruption during reconfiguration. Comprehensive experimental evaluation across five dynamic scenarios demonstrates that D-POA achieves 97.8% connectivity and 98.4% coverage while reducing movement costs by 38–57% compared to baseline algorithms, with near-linear scalability maintaining over 96% solution quality across networks ranging from 50 to 1000 nodes.

Introduction

The exponential growth of Internet of Things (IoT) deployments has fundamentally transformed the landscape of distributed computing. Billions of interconnected devices now generate unprecedented volumes of time-sensitive data across diverse application domains including smart cities, autonomous vehicles, industrial automation, and healthcare monitoring1,2. Traditional cloud computing architectures, while offering substantial computational resources and scalability, introduce inherent latency constraints due to geographical distance between edge devices and centralized data centers. This renders them inadequate for latency-critical IoT applications that demand real-time processing and decision-making capabilities. This computational gap has catalyzed the emergence of fog computing as an intermediary paradigm. Fog computing strategically positions computational resources at the network edge, thereby bridging the divide between resource-constrained IoT devices and distant cloud infrastructure3.

Fog computing extends cloud capabilities to the network edge by deploying distributed computing nodes in proximity to data sources. Consequently, it enables localized data processing, reduced transmission latency, bandwidth optimization, and enhanced privacy preservation4. Recent studies have demonstrated fog computing’s effectiveness across various domains. The dynamic allocation of fog resources based on processing demands significantly optimizes latency and resource utilization in real-time IoT systems. Furthermore, research on energy-efficient implementations for 6G-enabled massive IoT highlights fog computing’s vital role in supporting next-generation network applications5. However, the practical deployment of fog computing systems introduces complex optimization challenges that extend beyond traditional cloud resource allocation problems. These challenges are particularly pronounced in dynamic environments characterized by node mobility, unpredictable failures, fluctuating traffic patterns, and evolving network topologies6.

The service placement problem in fog computing determining optimal allocation of computational tasks to geographically distributed fog nodes has been proven NP-complete with exponential computational complexity. This complexity necessitates sophisticated optimization approaches that balance multiple conflicting objectives including latency minimization, energy efficiency, network connectivity, coverage maximization, and operational costs7. Contemporary research has explored diverse methodological frameworks for fog computing optimization. These include hierarchical architectures employing clustering techniques for scalable management of large-scale deployments, metaheuristic algorithms including genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, and nature-inspired approaches for near-optimal solution discovery, and fuzzy logic systems for handling uncertainty in resource allocation decisions8. Additionally, hybrid frameworks combining multiple optimization techniques have been developed to leverage complementary strengths. Recent advances have incorporated machine learning techniques into fog computing resource management. Approaches utilizing deep reinforcement learning, in particular, demonstrate adaptive learning capabilities for exploring optimal placement strategies in dynamic environments. Despite significant progress, existing approaches exhibit critical limitations when confronted with highly dynamic operational scenarios. Static or semi-static placement strategies optimize for instantaneous network conditions but fail to maintain performance as environmental parameters evolve over time9. Many algorithms demonstrate prohibitive computational overhead that precludes real-time reconfiguration. Conversely, others achieve rapid convergence at the expense of solution quality or incur excessive resource consumption through unnecessary node repositioning. The fundamental challenge lies in developing adaptive optimization frameworks capable of continuous network monitoring and intelligent detection of performance degradation10. Additionally, these frameworks must implement efficient reconfiguration mechanisms that minimize operational disruption while maintaining service quality guarantees. These frameworks must operate effectively across diverse dynamic conditions including node mobility, random failures, traffic variations, and progressive network expansion11.

This paper addresses these challenges through a novel Dynamic Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (D-POA) specifically designed for real-time fog node placement in highly dynamic IoT networks. D-POA draws inspiration from the dual behavioral strategies of pufferfish, including macro predator targeting for global exploration and defensive inflation mechanisms for local refinement12. The algorithm integrates these bio-inspired optimization principles with adaptive mechanisms tailored to dynamic network environments. The algorithm introduces three key innovations: warm-start initialization that leverages historical optimal configurations to accelerate convergence during reconfiguration cycles, adaptive iteration control that dynamically adjusts computational effort based on the severity of environmental changes, and intelligent triggering mechanisms that balance proactive reconfiguration against operational stability. These innovations collectively enhance the algorithm’s efficiency and responsiveness in dynamic environments. Unlike conventional approaches that treat fog node placement as a static optimization problem, D-POA formulates it as a continuous multi-objective optimization process over time. The algorithm explicitly accounts for movement costs, reconfiguration frequency, and performance stability alongside traditional connectivity and coverage objectives.

This work builds upon and extends existing approaches in two fundamental ways. Compared to the original Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (POA), we add three critical adaptive mechanisms specifically designed for dynamic environments: (i) warm-start initialization that leverages previously optimal configurations to accelerate convergence during reconfiguration cycles, (ii) adaptive iteration control that dynamically adjusts computational effort based on the magnitude of environmental changes, and (iii) intelligent triggering mechanisms that determine when reconfiguration is necessary rather than applying fixed optimization schedules. Compared to existing dynamic fog placement methods, we add an integrated framework that simultaneously addresses multiple dynamic challenges. While prior trigger-based reconfiguration approaches respond reactively to network degradation, we add proactive monitoring with threshold-based triggers that balance timely adaptation against operational overhead. While existing warm-start techniques initialize from historical data, we add a perturbation-based strategy that maintains solution diversity to prevent premature convergence in rapidly changing environments. While conventional methods use fixed iteration counts regardless of change severity, we add an adaptive iteration mechanism that scales computational resources proportionally to environmental dynamics, reducing unnecessary computation during minor changes while ensuring thorough optimization during major disruptions. These combined innovations enable D-POA to maintain superior network quality while minimizing movement costs and reconfiguration frequency across diverse dynamic scenarios.

Related works

The proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and latency-sensitive applications has necessitated the development of fog computing paradigms to bridge the gap between cloud infrastructure and edge devices. Recent research has focused extensively on optimizing service placement and task scheduling in fog computing environments, addressing challenges such as latency minimization, energy efficiency, resource utilization, and quality of service (QoS) guarantees. This section reviews contemporary approaches categorized by their methodological frameworks and optimization strategies.

Hierarchical architectures have emerged as effective solutions for managing large-scale fog deployments. Shaik and Baskiyar13 introduced HAFA (Hierarchical and Autonomous Fog Architecture), which organizes 1,073 fog nodes into hierarchical structures using Agglomerative Clustering, followed by an iterative orchestrator algorithm for distributed service placement. Their evaluation on the Chicago city dataset demonstrated near-optimal cost efficiency while examining only 16–23 nodes compared to 1,074 in centralized approaches, achieving zero task failures for Augmented Reality applications. Similarly, Šatkauskas and Venčkauskas14 proposed a multi-agent dynamic fog service placement orchestrator that combines Improved Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization with Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) for final placement decisions. Their distributed architecture achieved service redistribution within 1.5 s while eliminating single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities inherent to centralized systems.

Dynamic placement strategies address the challenge of real-time service deployment in response to changing network conditions and application requirements. Trabelsi et al.15 formulated dynamic service placement as an Integer Linear Programming (ILP) problem to minimize response time while meeting application deadlines. Testing across hierarchical fog infrastructure with eight IoT applications revealed that their DSP algorithm achieved a 99.92% fog placement rate and successfully met al.l application deadlines, outperforming First-Fit, Random, and Cloud-Only strategies. Lin et al.16 extended this work through their ODSPS framework, which employs a Political Optimizer (PO) algorithm combined with Warshall algorithm-based dependency management. Their approach achieved 100% fog utilization and 88.8% response time reduction compared to CSA-FSPP, demonstrating convergence 39.6% faster than competing methods.

Multi-objective optimization has gained prominence for balancing conflicting metrics such as latency, energy consumption, cost, and resource utilization. Saif et al.17 developed a Multi-Objective Grey Wolf Optimizer (MGWO) algorithm that employs queue theory models (M/M/1, M/M/C, M/M/∞), non-dominated Pareto sorting, and inverse mutation operators for task scheduling in cloud-fog environments. Their simulations demonstrated superior performance over NSGA-II and MOPSO algorithms with linear scalability across workloads ranging from 30 to 200 tasks. Mokni et al.18 proposed a multi-objective fuzzy approach integrating Multi-Agent Systems with Genetic Algorithm for workflow scheduling, achieving 48% makespan reduction compared to EM-MOO through workflow partitioning based on execution cost and fuzzy inference system modeling of budget-deadline constraints.

Fuzzy logic approaches have proven effective for handling uncertainty and imprecision in fog computing environments. Javanmardi et al.19 introduced F-DTA, a lightweight fuzzy-based task allocation method specifically designed for Fog-enabled Internet of Drones. Using Mamdani fuzzy inference with O(k·m) time complexity, F-DTA achieved 4.18 s execution time for 200 tasks compared to PSO’s 6031 s, demonstrating the impracticality of metaheuristics for time-sensitive applications. Jin and Rezaeipanah8 developed DTA-FLE, employing a Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System with 243 rules processing five input parameters (CPU utilization, storage utilization, bandwidth utilization, network latency, task deadline) to categorically distribute tasks across Low-Capacity Fog Layer, High-Capacity Fog Layer, and Cloud Layer.

Hybrid approaches combining multiple optimization techniques have demonstrated enhanced performance across various metrics. Ben Rjeb et al.20 proposed a hybrid recommendation system integrating collaborative filtering and content-based filtering with the Imperialist Competitive Algorithm (ICA) metaheuristic. Validated using Internet of Drones scenarios with EPFL mobility dataset, their system achieved 17.47% improved fog utilization while considering user mobility and real-time context awareness. Tripathy et al.21 developed GA-COA, combining Genetic Algorithm with Crayfish Optimization Algorithm for task offloading, achieving 78% energy efficiency and 32% reduction in energy consumption compared to PSO through vertical crossover for global search and biologically-inspired behavioral phases for local exploitation.

The integration of machine learning techniques represents an emerging direction in fog computing optimization. Singh et al.22 presented ORA-DL, a framework integrating deep neural networks and reinforcement learning for smart grid optimization. Their four-layer DNN achieved 93.38% energy demand prediction accuracy and 96.25% grid stability, demonstrating 22.96% operational cost reduction through real-time data preprocessing and periodic retraining for adaptation to evolving conditions.

Comprehensive surveys provide valuable insights into the current state and challenges of fog service placement. Apat et al.23 presented a thorough examination of optimization approaches, proving the Service Placement Problem (SPP) as NP-complete with O (2^n−1) complexity. Their taxonomy classifies IoT applications by architecture, granularity, and computation-to-communication ratio, while analyzing single-objective and multi-objective optimization techniques including heuristic algorithms (GA, PSO, ACO), metaheuristics (Firefly, Jellyfish, Cuckoo Search), and mathematical programming (ILP, MILP). The survey identifies critical challenges encompassing interoperability, diverse constraints, effective resource management, and SLA/QoS compliance in distributed fog-cloud environments. While existing research has made significant advances in fog service placement and task scheduling, several challenges remain. The trade-off between solution optimality and computational complexity continues to be a critical concern, particularly for time-sensitive applications. Additionally, the integration of mobility awareness, security considerations, and energy efficiency within unified frameworks requires further investigation. The increasing heterogeneity of IoT devices and the dynamic nature of fog environments necessitate adaptive algorithms capable of real-time decision-making while maintaining acceptable computational overhead. Apat et al.24 present a comprehensive survey on fog service placement optimization in IoT environments, addressing the NP-complete challenge of mapping application services to heterogeneous fog-cloud resources. The authors propose three-layer architecture with a Fog Control Manager and categorize solution approaches into exact methods (Integer Linear Programming, Mixed-Integer Linear Programming) and approximate techniques including metaheuristic algorithms (Genetic Algorithm, Particle Swarm Optimization, NSGA-II, MOEA/D). Their systematic classification of IoT applications uses Communication-to-Computation Ratio (CCR) to distinguish latency-sensitive from compute-intensive workloads and employs Directed Acyclic Graphs for workflow dependencies. Javanmardi et al.25 present the first survey examining integrated security, privacy, and resource efficiency in IoT-Fog networks. The authors categorize solutions into three frameworks: secure architectures using Fog computing, SDN, and Blockchain; secure routing protocols with trust-based authentication; and secure Resource Management Systems. Analyzing 50 + works (2018–2024), results show 35% cost reduction and 27% latency improvement. Critical gaps include lack of real-world integrated datasets and scalability challenges in heterogeneous environments.

Maiti et al.26 address latency minimization in fog computing through optimal Fog Smart Gateway (FSG) deployment for IoT services, formulating it as an NP-hard Un-capacitated Facility Location problem. The authors employ five optimization techniques: randomized selection, greedy method, k-Median, k-Means with ten initial centroid strategies (Forgy, Partition Mean, Kauffman, Split Tree, Forward Difference, and their sorted variants), and Simulated Annealing. Simulations in MATLAB R2017b tested 32 to 1,024 randomly distributed gateways with 1 to 10 fog nodes and packet sizes of 34–65,550 bytes. Results show Simulated Annealing consistently achieved minimum latency across all configurations, while Partition Mean provided optimal k-Means initialization. The study demonstrates that strategic fog node placement significantly reduces latency compared to cloud-only processing, with performance improving as fog node count increases, enabling compliance with stringent Service Level Agreement requirements. Maiti et al.27 address the NP-hard fog node placement (FNP) problem in IoT networks to minimize latency, with time complexity O (2^n). The authors propose five meta-heuristic algorithms: FNP_SA (Simulated Annealing), FNP_GA (Genetic Algorithm), FNP_PSO (Particle Swarm Optimization), and two hybrid approaches FNP_GASA (GA with SA) and FNP_GAPSO (GA with PSO). The network is modeled as graph G = (V, E) optimizing Gateway-to-Fog (G2F) and Fog-to-Fog (F2F) latencies. MATLAB R2017b simulations tested 32–256 gateways in a 100 × 100 km area with sparse (r = 0.3) and dense (r = 0.9) network densities. Parameters included population size 40, crossover probability 0.6, mutation probability 0.07, and inertia weights 0.4–0.9 over 100 iterations. Results show FNP_GASA consistently outperforms all baselines, achieving mean latencies of 21.32s (r = 0.3) and 17.95s (r = 0.9) for 32 gateways versus random placement at 34.27s and 46.75s, with superior performance maintained across all topologies up to 256 gateways (25.30s and 23.29s respectively).

The proposed dynamic pufferfish optimization algorithm (D-POA)

Algorithm overview

The Dynamic Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (D-POA) addresses the dynamic fog node placement problem through a continuous monitoring and adaptive reconfiguration framework. The algorithm operates in two main phases:

Phase 1: Initial deployment - Establishes baseline network configuration.

Phase 2: Continuous adaptation - Monitors network state and triggers intelligent reconfigurations.

D-POA is inspired by pufferfish behavior, which exhibits two distinct response strategies to environmental threats, naturally mapping to network optimization needs:

Macro predator targeting (global search): When faced with large predators, pufferfish identify and move toward safer positions - analogous to global topology optimization.Defense mechanism (local refinement): When cornered, pufferfish inflate and make defensive adjustments - analogous to local optimization.

Base POA operations

Belonging to the family Tetraodontidae and order Tetraodontiformes, pufferfish are primarily found in marine and estuarine environments28. These fish share certain physical characteristics with porcupine fish, most notably the presence of large spines. What sets pufferfish apart from many other fish species is their lack of several anatomical features, including ribs, a pelvic structure, and pectoral fins. When threatened, pufferfish employ a distinctive defensive mechanism by drawing water into their oral cavity, which results in significant changes to their fin and skeletal structure. Before detailing adaptive mechanisms, we briefly review base POA operations.

Computational complexity analysis

To assess the scalability of D-POA, we analyze its computational complexity for a population of size P optimizing N fog nodes serving M edge devices.

Time complexity

Each iteration involves two stages: (1) Macro Predator Targeting requires fitness evaluation O(P × N × M) and solution comparison O(P²), and (2) Defense Mechanism performs local refinement O(P × N × M). For T iterations, the total complexity is O (T × (P × N × M + P²)). In typical scenarios where N and M significantly exceed P, this simplifies to O (T × P × N × M), demonstrating near-linear scaling with network size.

The adaptive mechanisms reduce computational overhead significantly. Warm-start initialization decreases effective iterations from T_full to T_adaptive (40–60% reduction), while intelligent triggering eliminates 60–75% of unnecessary reconfigurations. The monitoring overhead per time step is O(N × M) for connectivity and coverage evaluation.

Space complexity

Memory requirements include population storage O(P × N × d), network state O(N + M), connectivity matrix O(N²) (or O(N × k_avg) for sparse networks), and historical data O(N × d). Total space complexity is O(P × N × d + N² + M), which reduces to O(P × N × d + N × k_avg + M) for sparse topologies.

Scalability assessment

Empirical results validate theoretical predictions: execution time increases from 35.2 s (50 nodes) to 612.8 s (1000 nodes), demonstrating approximately linear scaling for 20× network expansion. Compared to baseline algorithms with similar O(T × P × N × M) complexity, D-POA achieves superior solution quality (97.8% connectivity, 98.4% coverage) while maintaining competitive execution times through adaptive iteration control. The algorithm’s linear memory footprint and efficient core operations enable deployment on standard fog computing hardware for networks ranging from small-scale edge deployments to metropolitan-scale infrastructures (1000 + nodes).

Experimental results

Experimental configuration and fair comparison framework

The experiment was performed in MATLAB utilizing an artificially created system comprising cloud computing, fog computing, and Internet of Things elements. Additionally, the results from each experiment are presented as averages calculated from 50 independent runs. To demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed model, we conducted a comparative analysis against three well-established contemporary methods that function as baseline standards. The specific characteristics of these comparison approaches are: Service Placement Problem-Differential Evolution Algorithm (SPP-DEA)29, Cuckoo Search Algorithm-Fog Service Placement Problem (CSA-FSPP)30, and Service Placement Problem-Teaching Learning- Based Optimization (SPP-TLBO)31.

All algorithms employed uniform population sizes of 50 individuals and maximum iteration limits of 200 iterations to ensure equitable computational resource allocation. The proposed D-POA algorithm utilized warm-start ratio ρ_warm = 0.7, adaptive iteration factor α = 0.5, and reconfiguration thresholds θ_conn = θ_cov = 0.05. For baseline algorithms, SPP-TLBO used teaching factor TF randomly selected from {1, 2} following the original formulation. CSA-FSPP employed discovery rate p_a = 0.25 and Lévy flight parameter β = 1.5 as specified in the source publication. SPP-DEA utilized scaling factor F = 0.8 and crossover probability CR = 0.9 based on recommended settings. These algorithm-specific parameters were selected from preliminary sensitivity analysis and original publications while maintaining identical population sizes and iteration budgets across all methods.

Unified stopping criteria were applied to all algorithms: termination occurred upon reaching 200 iterations or when fitness improvement fell below 10⁻⁶ for 20 consecutive iterations. For dynamic scenarios, identical reconfiguration triggers were used across all methods, initiating network adaptation when connectivity or coverage degradation exceeded 5% or upon node failure or addition events. The network simulation employed a 1000 m × 1000 m deployment area with fog node communication ranges of 100–150 m and IoT device sensing ranges of 50 m. Mobility models included Random Waypoint (1–5 m/s velocities), Gauss-Markov (correlation coefficients 0.7–0.8), and realistic vehicular traces. Failure characteristics followed Poisson distributions with λ = 0.001, temporary failure probability 0.7, and recovery times of 30–120 s. The multi-objective fitness function weights were ω_conn = 0.4, ω_cov = 0.4, and ω_stab = 0.2, with unit movement cost c_i = 1.0 and maximum movement constraint d_max = 100 m. This comprehensive framework, characterized by identical computational budgets, unified stopping criteria, and consistent environmental parameters, ensures that observed performance differences reflect genuine algorithmic superiority rather than implementation bias or unfair resource allocation. Limitations and future directions

While D-POA demonstrates superior performance across multiple dynamic scenarios, several limitations warrant acknowledgment and suggest directions for future research.

Computational and architectural constraints

The algorithm’s O(T × P × N × M) complexity may pose challenges for extremely large-scale deployments (> 5000 nodes) requiring sub-second reconfiguration responses. The current centralized implementation introduces a single point of failure and potential communication bottlenecks in geographically dispersed networks, suggesting the need for distributed optimization variants. Additionally, D-POA assumes reliable communication channels and uniform communication ranges, neglecting environmental factors such as physical obstacles, interference, and node heterogeneity in computational capabilities that commonly affect real-world deployments.

Movement and energy modeling

The physical movement model assumes unrestricted mobility within deployment boundaries, overlooking real-world constraints including road networks, building structures, and regulatory restrictions. The linear movement cost function does not capture nonlinear energy consumption patterns, mechanical wear, or collision avoidance requirements when repositioning multiple nodes simultaneously. Furthermore, while movement costs proxy energy consumption, D-POA lacks explicit modeling of battery depletion, energy harvesting dynamics and heterogeneous energy budgets across fog nodes—particularly significant for solar-powered and battery-operated platforms where energy availability varies temporally and spatially.

Operational and validation limitations

Certain operational conditions remain unexplored, including adversarial scenarios with deliberate attacks, catastrophic failures affecting > 50% of nodes, flash crowd events causing sudden traffic spikes, and cascading failures. The fitness function employs fixed weighting parameters (α_conn, α_move) requiring manual tuning rather than adaptive adjustment to changing operational priorities. The experimental evaluation relies on simulation-based assessment rather than physical testbed deployment, which cannot fully capture real-world complexity including hardware failures, network protocol overhead, and human operator interactions. Field trials in operational IoT networks would provide valuable insights into practical deployment challenges and algorithm robustness. These limitations indicate clear research directions: distributed optimization for ultra-large networks, integration of realistic mobility constraints using geographic information systems, energy-aware placement incorporating harvesting models, security-aware optimization for adversarial scenarios, and validation through physical testbed deployments in operational fog computing infrastructures.

Conclusion

This paper addressed dynamic fog node placement optimization in IoT networks through the Dynamic Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (D-POA). Traditional static placement strategies demonstrate inadequate performance in environments characterized by node mobility, random failures, and fluctuating traffic patterns. D-POA integrates bio-inspired optimization principles with three adaptive mechanisms: warm-start initialization leveraging historical configurations, adaptive iteration control adjusting computational effort based on change severity, and intelligent triggering mechanisms balancing reconfiguration frequency against operational stability. Extensive experimental evaluation across five dynamic scenarios demonstrated D-POA’s superior performance compared to baseline algorithms (SPP-TLBO, CSA-FSPP, SPP-DEA). The algorithm achieved 97.8% connectivity and 98.4% coverage while reducing movement costs by 38–57%. Under mobile fog node conditions, recovery time reached 72.4 s, outperforming competitors by 26–37%. Service disruption duration in failure scenarios was reduced to 12.5 s, representing 58–73% improvement. Scalability analysis revealed near-linear computational scaling with solution quality exceeding 96% across networks of 50–1000 nodes. Future research directions include integrating machine learning for predictive reconfiguration, extending to multi-tier hierarchical architectures, incorporating energy consumption models, and developing distributed optimization variants. D-POA establishes a practical framework for adaptive fog computing resource management in latency-critical IoT applications.

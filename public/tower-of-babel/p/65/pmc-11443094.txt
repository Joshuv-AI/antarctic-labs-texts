ROAD value-based detection for GAE enhanced interference mitigation in LDACS
Authors: Miziya Keshkar, Raja Muthalagu, Abdul Rajak
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The increasing volume of air traffic has placed significant stress on the current Air Traffic Management (ATM) systems, especially concerning the use of Very High Frequency (VHF) communication bands. As air traffic continues to grow, the limitations of the existing spectrum and infrastructure necessitate significant upgrades to ensure safety, efficiency, and capacity. The modernization of air traffic management systems has led to the development and introduction of the L-band Digital Aeronautical Communication System (LDACS), a new communication protocol. LDACS is designed to operate alongside existing L-band systems, ensuring compatibility with legacy users. The coexistence of LDACS with legacy systems poses significant interference challenges, as any disruption in data retrieval can critically impact flight safety. This paper proposes four potential interference mitigation techniques that LDACS can employ to detect and reduce the primary source of interference: Distance Measuring Equipment (DME). The authors introduce a prototype LDACS receiver that uses Rank-Ordered Absolute Differences (ROAD) statistics for effective interference sensing and employs GAE-enhanced pulse peak processors to mitigate Distance Measuring Equipment (DME) interference. Unlike the current GAE-enhanced pulse peak processors, the proposed methods use ROAD value-based detection for identifying DME interference. The performance of the proposed methods - ROAD GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Attenuator (RGPPA), ROAD GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Limiter (RGPPL), ROAD joint GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Attenuator (RJGPPA), and ROAD joint GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Limiter (RJGPPL) is analyzed across different threshold ROAD values to determine their efficacy in various signal conditions. Moreover, the performance of the proposed methods is compared to existing methods such as conventional pulse blanking and GAE-enhanced nonlinear devices, which use the amplitude of the received signal for the detection of interference. Furthermore, the proposed method’s performance is compared to another method, ROAD PB, which uses ROAD statistics to detect DME interference and pulse blanking for DME mitigation. The comparative results show that the proposed methods outperformed conventional pulse blanking and ROADPB. Besides, these methods outperformed existing GAE-enhanced methods for their optimum threshold ROAD value.

Introduction

Air traffic must increase by 4.3% per year on average over the next 20 years to keep up with the aviation industry’s consistent growth1. Air traffic is expected to grow at a 6% annual rate by 20352. Furthermore, recent recovery projections from the Statistics and Forecast Service (STATFOR) indicate that air traffic is expected to rebound to 2019 levels by 2024. Eurocontrol expects that if this surge in traffic occurs, the capacity crisis experienced in 2018/19 will reemerge with the same intensity3,4. To accommodate this forecast growth, far-reaching upgrades and efficiencies for airports and Air Traffic Management (ATM) need to be innovated.

Presently, Airline Operational Control (AOC) and Air Traffic Control (ATC) connect to aircraft via both voice and data communication links. While analog VHF (Very High Frequency) using Double Sideband Amplitude Modulation (DSB-AM) offers reliable and secure voice transmission with low-cost equipment, it falls short for sophisticated ATM applications, such as flight-centric operations requiring point-to-point communications. Ground-based equipment utilizing HF (High Frequency) or VHF radio channels provides data connections to the cockpit. However, these narrow-band radio channels limit data throughput to only a few kilobits per second. This bandwidth constraint prevents the current data links from delivering broadband services or supporting the new ATM applications due to the scarcity of available spectrum. As a result, the existing system will continue to be inadequate until it is replaced by a new infrastructure that is both spectrum-efficient and capable of meeting modern demands for reliability5.

In the meantime, spectrum occupancy studies, contrary to common perception, provide a startling insight into the availability and usage of spectral resources. Global surveys and extensive research measuring wireless activity across various application spectrum blocks have revealed surprising insights into their actual usage. The findings from these studies6–9 indicate that the available spectral resources are not as limited as previously thought. There are large swaths of the spectrum that are rarely used. Furthermore, large portions of the radio spectrum are underutilized; the usage rate is even lower than 10%6,7. The aeronautical frequency band, primarily used for A/G communications, is one of the most underutilized (less than 12.5%)10 while also predicting significant spectrum scarcity. Therefore, spectrum utilization rather than spectrum scarcity is the issue to accept new ATM applications or ATMS powerful enough to handle heavy air traffic.

Therefore, the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) has recommended upgrading the current communication links to a more efficient protocol. This modernization initiative aims to establish a secure and spectrum-efficient communication framework. As a result, L-band Digital Aeronautical Communication Systems (LDACS) were developed to meet these evolving needs5,11.

The specification of LDACS was decided in 2009 and the prototype was demonstrated in 2014. For further studies and expansion, ICAO propounded two potential standards: LDACS1 and LDACS2. LDACS1 is based on the IEEE 802.16 wireless system12, while LDACS2 is derived from the Global System for Mobile Communication (GSM)13. ICAO has foreseen any of these subsystems of LDACS as a solution to all the issues related to current air traffic management systems.

Some legacy users in L-band include Joint Tactical Information Distribution System (JTIDS), military tactical air navigation (TACAN) system, Distance Measuring Equipment (DME), Secondary Surveillance Radar (SSR), Universal Access Transceiver (UAT) at 978 MHz, and airborne collision avoidance systems at 1030 and 1090 MHz12,13. As the studies about spectrum occupancy brought to light the underutilization and vacant frequency range in the L-band12,13, LDACS is proposed as a coexisting system in the L-band. LDACS1 is expected to be implemented as an inlay system within the 1 MHz spectral gap of legacy users and LDACS2 (960-975 MHz) as an overlay system in the available vacant spectrum14 (Fig. 1). Though the overlay method is comparatively less complex, it faces a significant challenge of spectrum scarcity12,13. In contrast, the inlay method is anticipated to overcome spectrum scarcity by leveraging the spectral gap, thereby improving spectrum utilization. Moreover, detailed comparative studies between LDACS1 and LDACS2 have identified LDACS1 as the preferred option over LDACS2. Further, LDACS1 is referred to as LDACS and is recognized as the first Communication, Navigation, and Surveillance (CNS) system by ICAO5,15. As a result, the work presented in this paper concentrates on the LDACS1 standard, which will be referred to as LDACS from now on.Fig. 1L-Band spectrum occupancy14.

L-Band spectrum occupancy14.

LDACS incorporates two-way communication: a Forward link (FL) (962-1213 MHz), from the ground station (GS) to the air station (AS) and a Reverse link RL (1025-1150 MHz)from AS to GS. A frequency separation of 63 MHz between FL and RL enables LDACS to provide Frequency Division Duplexing (FDD). However, deploying LDACS introduces interference between DME and LDACS systems. Out-of-band radiation reduction techniques are used to avoid interference from LDACS to legacy systems. Interference mitigation schemes alleviate the effect of signals from legacy users to LDACS. Related work

The literature extensively covers a variety of strategies and techniques for mitigating interference and minimizing out-of-band radiation in LDACS systems. This section briefly discusses several proposed schemes aimed at minimizing DME noise. In 2009, it was addressed how clipping or Pulse Blanking (PB) can reduce interference in LDACS 117. Even though the technique is simple in principle and practice, pulse blanking has limits. When using a simple amplitude-based detection method with a threshold value, setting the threshold too low can cause peaks within the usable OFDM signal to be mistakenly identified as DME interference. Conversely, if the threshold is set too high, it may result in missing some DME pulse pairs. Thus, selecting an appropriate threshold is crucial to strike a balance between minimizing signal loss and effectively reducing interference. Even though the interference detection is accurate, the PB method can still cause more interference since it greatly blanks mixed signals that include small amounts of significant LDACS components. This results in impulsive interference with negative amplitudes when PB blanks usable signals, thereby altering the spectrum of OFDM subcarriers, referred as inter-carrier interference (ICI)18. Raja14 put forward a method for removing ICI that is based on decision-directed noise estimates. Despite its effectiveness in eliminating inter-carrier interference, this approach has drawbacks. The cyclic prefix used in the method slows down transmission throughput and consumes additional power.

In 2011, two techniques for the detection and mitigation of DME interference were proposed. The techniques use the repeating structure and spectral contour of the DME signal in detection and mitigation. This method has the benefit of being of simple, but due to pulse blanking, there is a chance that some of the target signals will be missed19. In 2012, a mitigation scheme combining the benefits of pulse blanking and notch filtering to effectively process signals in both the time and frequency domains was proposed. This scheme was deemed safe and efficient for B2 band (1900 MHz) signals20.

In21; authors propose a method , which reconstruct and subtract ICI so that the influence of PB is compensated. The PB window is utilized to determine the form of the subcarrier spectra that are required. An iterative receiver structure is suggested for estimating transmitted data and subcarrier channel coefficients. Future research could improve the estimation of data symbols by integrating soft values from the output of the decoder and applying weights to both estimated and discarded data symbols based on their reliability.

Khodr A. Saaifan etal. suggested a precoding technique based on lattice signal sets. This technique alters the form of spectrum of DME signal . Additionally, they employed basic clipping to mitigate DME interference22. Further, Yun Bai et al. put forwarded another method for DME mitigation, which utilizes Complementary Code Keying (CCK) to achieve improved performance. However, the drawbacks of this scheme include high latency and long acquisition times23.

Q. Li et al. in 2014 utilized the Turbo concept in proposing an iterative receiver design24. In25, a block-based selective pulse blanking is used to reduce DME interference. In 2016, Douzhe Li and colleagues introduced a method aimed at identifying distorted pulse pairs and then removing them from the original signal26.

In27 an extension of the existing blanking non-linearity, which uses optimization approaches to combine the blanked signal and the original received signal, is proposed.

The study in28 examines strategies for estimating aeronautical channel scenarios using a pilot and data-aided approach. In addition, the research proposes a low-complexity iterative estimating approach that does not include channel decoding. The proposed method offers a promising balance of accuracy and complexity. In29, a power detection system is proposed for two frequency selective interference mitigation approaches. The study also examines optimal thresholds for detecting and mitigating DME at an LDACS receiver. Furthermore, the best parameters for these two mitigation strategies are examined.

In30, OFDM in LDACS is based on discrete wavelet transform. The method leverages the inherent characteristics of DME signals by selectively transmitting the DME-affected signal through the quadrature channel. Through the strategic application of the direct sequence spread spectrum, the approach aims to achieve transmission free from DME interference. The scheme’s shortcomings are its computational complexity and resource requirements. In 2021, DME interference sensing based on Nested RF upconversion is proposed31. Deep clipping and joint clipping/blanking are two double threshold-based detection techniques suggested in 202132. In 2022, different types of pulse peak attenuators and limiters, which use the knowledge from the Optimum Baysien estimator are proposed33. The method could reduce the data loss caused due to blanking. The authors made use of single and double-threshold-based noise detection in recognizing the noisy sub-carriers and 2-GMM estimation to design nonlinear scaling parameters to reduce the effect of DME. This thorough review of current interference mitigation techniques in LDACS highlights the necessity for more in-depth research in this field.

The Genie-aided estimator (GAE) uses numerical data from auxiliary sources to develop design parameters that aim to reduce the bit error rate (BER) at the receiver34. Achieving this lower limit performance requires incorporating side information elements. Additionally, there is a relationship between impulsive noise and frequency in terms of the timing of impulsive noise arrivals35. In 2022, different types of pulse peak attenuators and limiters that use the knowledge of GAE are proposed for the reduction of DME interference in LDACS33. The 2-GMM estimation of the received signal is effectively used to calculate the nonlinear scaling factors needed to suppress noise from the already identified noisy subcarriers.

In 2005, the ROAD value based impulse detection was proposed for detecting impulse pixels in images. This concept can be effectively used to eliminate mixtures of impulse and Gaussian noise from images36. In37, received signal’s ROAD values are used to train a deep neural network aimed at detecting signal corruption caused by impulse noise. Periodically, subcarriers with the most severely disturbed or least appropriate data are pinpointed by identifying significant power differences from adjacent subcarriers. Therefore, ROAD statistics are effectively utilized to pinpoint subcarriers affected by impulse noise.

In38, the authors reported a new method named ROADPB which uses ROAD values to detect DME noise and pulse blanking to remove it. The effectiveness of pulse blanking significantly improves when incorporating ROAD statistics-based detection. The paper emphasizes the benefits of using ROAD values for impulse noise detection compared to traditional amplitude-based sensing methods. The method claims to achieve more accuracy in sensing by the reduction of false alarms, especially at low SNR regions. The ROAD statistics based sensing method is identified as a comparatively stable method to sense DME interference as the performance does not vary much with different transmit SNR values. However, the method holds the adverse effect of loss of preferred data signal caused due to blanking, especially at threshold values less than the optimum threshold value. It is possible to reduce the data loss due to blanking by using any of the data estimation methods. Moreover, it is feasible to enhance noise detection using double-threshold (ROAD values) detection compared to single-threshold detection. Expansion of this thought resulted in proposing new interference mitigation for LDACS.

In this paper, the combination of ROAD-based DME noise detection38 and GAE-enhanced pulse peak processing33 for DME noise suppression leads to the proposal of four new methods: ROAD value based GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Attenuator (RGPPA);ROAD value based GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Limiter (RGPPL);ROAD value based Joint GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Attenuator (RJGPPA)ROAD value based Joint GAE enhanced Pulse Peak Limiter (RJGPPL)

Among the proposed methods, RGPPA and RGPPL utilize a single threshold ROAD value to separate noisy subcarriers, whereas RJGPPA and RJGPPL employ double threshold ROAD values. In RGPPA and RJGPPA, noise mitigation is achieved through GAE-enhanced pulse peak attenuation, while in RGPPL and RJGPPL, it is achieved through GAE-enhanced pulse peak limiting. These methods are anticipated to surpass existing GAE-enhanced pulse peak processors due to their utilization of ROAD value-based detection instead of amplitude-based detection. They may also outperform ROADPB by reducing inter-carrier interference, which can occur when informative signals are blanked. The proposed methods are expected to outperform pulse blanking techniques, as the performance of ROADPB is superior to standard pulse blanking. System and noise model

System model

The system model consists of an LDACS ground station transmitter, an LDACS airborne station receiver, and a channel that introduces DME noise and Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN). The block diagram of the LDACS GS transmitter is depicted in Fig. 3.Fig. 3LDACS ground station transmitter block diagram.

LDACS ground station transmitter block diagram.

In this system, a Reed-Solomon (RS) coder first processes the incoming random data by including additional data. After the RS coder adds extra bits, zero padding is applied to its output before it is passed to the convolutional coder for additional processing. The permutation interleaver takes the output from the convolutional coder and applies interleaving to mitigate burst errors. The interleaved data then undergoes symbol mapping and modulation using Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK). The resulting complex data values are further rearranged into the LDACS data format (F’) in the frame composer block.

Nonlinear devices

The nonlinear devices discussed in this section are designed to sense and suppress the DME signal that interferes with the transmitted data. This operation is applied to the received signal R before performing the FFT to prevent the spreading of sparse time-domain impulses across all OFDM carriers. The authors propose four types of nonlinear methods: RGPPA, RGPPL, RJGPPA, and RJGPPL. The methods are compared with existing interference mitigation methods such as GAE Enhanced pulse peak processors, ROAD PB and pulse blanking33,38. The detailed operation of the nonlinear devices, including their mathematical expressions and significance, is discussed below.

These advanced pulse peak processors outperform traditional pulse blanking techniques because they employ genie-aided estimation to recover the DME noise-affected subcarriers in the received signals.

Computational Complexity

The additional computational complexity introduced by ROAD statistics-based detection for GAE-enhanced methods compared to previous studies is attributed to the steps required to compute the ROAD value, as detailed in (8–10). Since these steps do not involve complex multiplications, it is clear that the ROAD statistics-based detection does not add any extra complex multiplications. Therefore, the number of complex multiplications remains the same as in the LDACS OFDM receiver (without any mitigation) or with pulse blanking or with existing GAE enhanced methods. Given that the LDACS OFDM receiver utilizes FFT, the total number of complex multiplications involved is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(N/2){\cdot } log_2(N)$$\end{document}, where N represents the number of subcarriers in the OFDM signal.

The total number of complex additions in the LDACS OFDM receiver with the proposed methods is given by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N\cdot log_2N + N\cdot (N-1)$$\end{document}. The number of real-time additions required for sorting depends on the specific sorting algorithm employed. Conclusion

In this paper, four types of ROAD value-based GAE enhanced pulse peak processors are proposed.

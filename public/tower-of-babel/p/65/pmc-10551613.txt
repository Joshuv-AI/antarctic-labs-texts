A case report on POEMS syndrome with high parathyroid hormone and repeated surgery, a rare presentation of a rare disease
Authors: Aram Yaseri, Shima Ghannadi, Lida Perseh, Zahra Hoseini Tavassol, Shirin Hasani-Ranjbar
Published: 2023

Introduction

Polyneuropathy, Organomegaly, Endocrinopathy, Monoclonal protein and Skin changes (POEMS) syndrome is a rare multisystemic disease usually presented in the fifth and sixth decades [1]. The physiopathology is not well understood; however, it seems that it is related to an imbalance of proinflammatory cytokines. Endocrinopathy in POEMS syndrome is an enigma and the most recognized endocrinopathies are hypogonadism, hypothyroidism, abnormal glucose metabolism, and adrenal insufficiency [2,3]. Yet in the current study, we reported a case of POEMS syndrome with a rare endocrinopathy presentation, a patient presented with high parathyroid hormone (PTH), hypercalcemia, and multiple bony lesions. This case report has been reported in line with scare guidelines [4].

Case presentation

The case we present is a 39-year-old man with a positive past medical history of kidney stones and bell's palsy referred for an endocrine consult for a third parathyroid surgery. Diagnosed with Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS), he was hospitalized in April 2018 due to progressive bilateral upper and lower extremities, weakness and numbness, and paresthesia of the face. During hospitalization, the case underwent 12 sessions of plasmapheresis. Meanwhile, blood sugar increases were controlled by insulin administration.

Discharged from the hospital, treatment continued with pregabalin 75 mg and carbamazepine CR 400 mg daily besides insulin. The former two medications were later substituted with duloxetine 60 mg daily as a result of which paresthesia and the feet muscles force reduction improved, though some numbness and tingling persisted in the hands and face.

Due to the slow recovery process and persistence of the neurological symptoms, he underwent new laboratory tests. The results demonstrated a normal phosphorus level (3.9 mg/dl) but increased levels of calcium (19.3 mg/dl) and PTH (185 pg/ml) together with vitamin D deficiency (14.2 ng/ml) and the presence of hematuria in urine.

Initially, serum and urine protein electrophoresis, as well as bone densitometry test, were run with normal results. After that, the 99mTc-sestamibi parathyroid single-photon emission computed tomography/ computed tomography (SPECT-CT) and thyroid uptake scan revealed the thyroid nodule without parathyroid adenoma. Ultrasound-Guided fine needle aspiration (FNA) from the thyroid nodule was performed, which was reported as a benign follicular nodule.

The parathyroid glands diagnosed with hyperparathyroidism due to parathyroid hyperplasia were operated on twice in June and August 2018 in two separate centers; in both surgeries, the parathyroid tissue abnormality was not found in pathology. Afterward, treatment was continued with the tablet Cinacalcet 30 mg, and Calcitonin 200 Nasal spray.

In further assessment, the result of MIBI Parathyroid Scintigraphy and Parathyroid SPECT Scan were reported as negative for typical parathyroid adenoma in September 2018, but despite normal levels of vitamin D and calcium in a blood test, the PTH levels remained high (constantly more than 100 pg/ml).

The possibility of rheumatic disorders (Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), vasculitis), antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), and hepatitis as underlying causes came to mind due to the persistence of hematuria (3+), proteinuria (24-hour urine protein above 1.2 g), high creatinine (2.9 mg/dl) level, alongside patient's other symptoms but all of them were ruled out. A needle biopsy was done on the left kidney. The pathology results indicated extensive effacement of visceral foot processes without obvious deposit (minimal change disease and unsampled focal & segmental glomerulosclerosis were in differential diagnosis). Minor glomerular lesions at a light microscopic examination level were considered as a possible diagnosis. To manage the situation, injections of Cyclophosphamide 200 mg and Methylprednisolone 500 mg were prescribed which was later tapered to Prednisolone 5 mg daily within three months. As result, the patient's symptoms improved and the blood creatinine level decreased. Except for the detection of a 20.4 mm right inguinal lymphadenopathy the abdomen and pelvis sonography revealed neither abnormalities in the renal or urinary tract nor organomegaly, and the case was discharged from the hospital. Four months later, however, in March 2019 the patient was again committed to the hospital with severe and progressive pain in the feet and hands, paresthesia, difficulty walking, and peripheral edema.

During a series of assessments with a Thoracic spiral and coronal CT scan and Thoracic and Hip MRI scan, bilateral femoral heads' AVN (Avascular Necrosis) was diagnosed which was later treated by surgery and a well-defined enhancing mass lesion in the left paravertebral at T11-T12 level measuring 55*50 mm was reported too. Following the surgical removal, the pathology report was in favor of Ganglioneuroma. Another finding in whole-body scintigraphy was a lucency in symphysis pubis and right ischium indicating bone marrow fibrosis and plasma cell-rich infiltration.

In light of the new data, the patient's diagnosis and treatment course took a new turn and the patient's parathyroid disorder was re-evaluated in our center. He had normocalcemic hyperparathyroidism. A parathyroid assessment with SPECT and sonography of the thyroid and neck showed normal thyroid and one mildly enlarged parathyroid with two isoechoic nodules on both sides of the thyroid; therefore, a Rocaltrol test was done (decrease in PTH level and no increase in calcium level). The patient's treatment began with multiple sessions of radiotherapy and Lenalidomide over four months. In the second month of treatment as the patient was hospitalized for corona-related complications, the treatment was stopped. In two months, he returned to the hospital again, this time for gastrointestinal problems: epigastric pain, heartburn, dysphagia, as well as an inability to walk, and shooting pain in both inguinal regions. The result of a biopsy from an esophageal ulcer taken during an upper gastrointestinal Endoscopy was in favor of Multiple myeloma besides Positive CD138 with Kappa/lambda positive ratio of 6/1 and KI67 positive in about 30 % of nuclei in his IHC report. His symptoms were controlled by prescribing Sucralfate 1 g and omeprazole 40 mg.

The treatment continued with cyclophosphamide and Lenalidomide 5 mg. However, due to the patient's lack of response to the treatment, VAD (Vincristine, Dexamethasone, Adriamycin) diet therapy was started for 2 months (twice a day, 4 days a week). Unfortunately, there was no response to this treatment either. Eventually, Pomalidomide 2 mg was administered with multiple acupuncture into the inflammatory inguinal region treatment and autologous stem cell transplantation.

In May 2021, with most of his symptoms improved, his kidney and parathyroid functions all became normal and the laboratory test results, including PTH (74 pg/ml) and calcium (9.3 mg/dl) levels normalized. All biochemical data are included in [Supplementary Table 1]. The case is currently undergoing follow-up and his condition remains stable. However, he still complains about mild back pain, weakness, fasciculation especially during sleep, and mild walking impairment.

Discussion

Given the multisystem nature of POEMS syndrome and variable clinical presentation, an early diagnosis is challenging [5], and also since the syndrome is rare it can easily be mistaken for other disorders, such as monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance or immunoglobulin light chain amyloid neuropathy and multiple myeloma [2]. However, the criteria that are used to distinguish it from other similar syndromes make the diagnosis less challenging; mandatory major criteria (polyneuropathy and monoclonal plasma cell proliferative disorder), major criteria (Castleman disease, osteosclerotic lesions, elevated serum levels of plasma vascular endothelial growth factor) and minor criteria (organomegaly, endocrinopathy, characteristic skin changes, papilledema, extravascular volume overload, and thrombocytosis) [6]. However, not all the features are required to make the diagnosis [7]. A diagnosis of POEMS syndrome is confirmed when patients demonstrate both mandatory major criteria, one of the three other major criteria, and one of the six minor criteria [8].

In this case, POEMS syndrome was diagnosed according to typical multiple peripheral neuropathies and monoclonal plasma cell proliferative disorder, the two mandatory major criteria, along with multiple bony lesions (right femoral head and neck, L5 vertebra, right ischium, and inferior pubic ramous), one of the three major criteria of the POEMS syndrome, multiple peripheral neuropathies and monoclonal plasma cell proliferative disorder as minor criteria, and also mildly hypocellular marrow with polymorphic population and trilineage hematopoiesis and progressive maturation less than 5 % plasma cell in bone marrow biopsy and aspiration. While we witness the occurrence of Ganglioneuroma with POEMS syndrome in this patient, there isn't any evidence that these two diseases are somehow related nor any similar case has been reported.

Numerous endocrine abnormalities have been described in POEMS syndrome. Based on the largest published series from Mayo Clinic, which included 64 patients with endocrine data, hypogonadism (primary or secondary) was the most common finding (79 %). Other frequent endocrinopathies include hypothyroidism (58 %) and abnormalities in glucose metabolism (48 %). Also, about 54 % of patients had multiple endocrinopathies in the 4 major endocrine axes (gonadal, thyroid, glucose, and adrenal) [9]; however, due to the high prevalence of these disorders in the general population, those are not sufficient to satisfy minor criteria for POEMS syndrome [10]. The endocrine disorders are seen in our case, i.e. hyperglycemia along with hypercalcemia and high PTH, not the most common forms of endocrinopathies in POEMS patients [9]. In this case, the presence of hypercalcemia was yet another issue to make an early diagnosis even more challenging. In contrast to multiple myeloma this syndrome is rarely associated with hypercalcemia [11] and is more likely to be presented with hypocalcemia; based on published series from Mayo Clinic 27 % of patients tested had hypocalcemia [9]. So, we reported this atypical case of POEMS syndrome to highlight the importance of considering POEMS syndrome in the differential diagnosis of peripheral neuropathy in association with different types of endocrine abnormalities and to emphasize the fact that in these unusual cases the diagnosis of POEMS syndrome should be judged based on the patient's abnormalities as a whole.

Even though the high amount of calcium levels in the initial examination could have had multiple reasons, as we can see the possibility of primary hyperparathyroidism related to a parathyroid adenoma was considered unlikely in an account of postsurgical pathology. So instead of performing two surgeries for parathyroid adenoma removal, the patient should have been examined more carefully and other possibilities such as the excessive release of calcium leading to hypercalcemia as a result of excessive bone resorption in Myeloma bone disease should have been taken into consideration.

There are a few possible explanations for the presentation of high PTH in this patient. Due to the negative result of the pathology of the adenoma, the possibility of both parathyroid hyperplasia and vitamin D deficiency should be assessed. Compare to the second diagnosis, the importance of parathyroid hyperplasia pales significantly since we witnessed the normalization of calcium and PTH levels as well as the patient's condition during treatment. However, after performing a Calcitriol test, decreasing levels of PTH, and no increase in calcium level make the second diagnosis, vitamin D deficiency, more reliable and possible as an underlying cause. Also, we suppose that treatment of underlined gammopathy and a decrease in autoantibodies could affect PTH level and activity in this case.

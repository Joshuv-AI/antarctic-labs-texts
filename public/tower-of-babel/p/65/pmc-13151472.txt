Cause of chronic diarrhoea
Authors: Tingting Cao, Honggang Wang
Published: 2026

Clinical presentation

A 52-year-old man presented with a 6-month history of diarrhoea and unintentional weight loss of 20 kg. During this period, he reported 2–3 daily episodes of watery stools with intermittent epigastric distension. On admission, his body mass index (BMI) was 14.9 kg/m² (height 1.65 m; weight 40.5 kg). He was not on any chronic medications. Laboratory investigations revealed haemoglobin of 90 g/L, serum total protein of 47.1 g/L and albumin of 22.6 g/L. C reactive protein was 73.3 mg/L. Immunoglobulin M (IgM) was 34.5 g/L (normal range: 0.5–2.2 g/L) and β2-microglobulin was 4949 ng/mL (normal range: 955–2157 ng/mL). White blood cell count, platelets, coagulation profile, renal function and lactate dehydrogenase were unremarkable. Anti-nuclear antibody, extractable nuclear antigen panel and the interferon-gamma release assay were negative. Urinalysis was negative for protein. Stool tests showed calprotectin 110 µg/g (normal range: 0–75 µg/g), positive occult blood and negative cultures. The abdominal CT scan showed no significant abnormalities. Gastroscopy revealed diffuse white patch-like lesions in the duodenum (figure 1A). Colonoscopy showed diffuse whitish nodularities in the terminal ileum (figure 1B). Capsule endoscopy showed similar lesions in the jejunum and ileum as seen in the duodenum and terminal ileum (figure 1C,D).

(A) Gastroscopic view of the duodenum. (B) Colonoscopic view of the terminal ileum. (C and D) Capsule endoscopic findings of the jejunum and ileum.

Question

What is your diagnosis?

Answer

Histology revealed marked villous blunting in the terminal ileum, alongside dilated lymphatic vessels, confirmed by D2-40 immunohistochemistry, filled with eosinophilic, hyaline material (figure 2). Bone marrow examination indicated a 22.84% infiltration by clonal lymphoplasmacytic cells expressing IgM. Immunophenotyping of the abnormal mature B-lymphocytes revealed expression of IgM, Cluster of Differentiation (CD) 19, CD20, CD22 and CD45, no expression of CD5 and CD23. Testing identified an MYD88 L265P mutation alongside a normal karyotype. The final diagnosis was Waldenström’s macroglobulinaemia (WM) with intestinal lymphangiectasia. Following four cycles of the RCD regimen (rituximab, cyclophosphamide and dexamethasone), a partial response was achieved: IgM fell to 8.31 g/L, diarrhoea and epigastric distension resolved, and nutritional status improved (BMI 19.9 kg/m²). Follow-up endoscopy showed marked regression of the duodenal and terminal ileal lesions compared with baseline (figure 3).

WM is a lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma defined by an IgM monoclonal protein and ≥10% bone marrow infiltration by clonal lymphoplasmacytic cells. Common clinical manifestations include anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly and lymphadenopathy, with hyperviscosity being a rare but serious complication.1 Intestinal lymphangiectasia occurs in an estimated 1–3% of WM cases.2 The pathogenesis entails IgM-phospholipid deposition in the intestinal lamina propria and lacteals, leading to lymphatic obstruction and gastrointestinal manifestations.3

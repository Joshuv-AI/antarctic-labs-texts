Hypergraph Learning with Hyperedge Gating and Multiscale Topology Feature Learning for Predicting Disease-Related circRNAs
Authors: Ping Xuan, Runguang Zheng, Hui Cui, Fengjiao Sun, Fei Wang, Tiangang Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
As circular RNAs (circRNAs) play a crucial role in the onset and progression of diseases, identification of disease-related circRNAs can provide deep insights into the pathogenesis of complex diseases. Constructing graph learning-based models is a widely used advanced approach to infer the associations between circRNAs and diseases. The biological correlations among multiple circRNAs, diseases, and miRNAs are complex and diverse. However, existing methods cannot sufficiently represent and encode those correlations. In addition, each circRNA (disease) node has its specific semantic within a meta-path, which is often neglected by the previous methods. To address these limitations, we propose a circRNA–disease association prediction model, HSCDA, which can encode the biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases, learn the semantics of meta-path neighbors, and integrate the multiscale topology structure within a heterogeneous network. First, we construct a hypergraph comprising circRNA and disease nodes to uncover the intricate biological correlations among multiple circRNA and disease nodes. The hypergraph has learnable hyperedges so that HSCDA can dynamically refine the hypergraph topology to reflect multiperspective correlations. Second, we propose hypergraph convolution with a hyperedge gating mechanism (HGHG). HGHG adaptively estimates the hyperedge importance by leveraging the contributions of circRNA, disease, and miRNA nodes contained within each hyperedge. Third, a semantic-enhanced graph convolutional network (SGCN) is proposed to integrate the diverse semantics between circRNA and disease nodes within each meta-path. SGCN is able to generate semantic representations for each circRNA and disease node, derived from integrating the semantic features of meta-path neighbors and the node features. Finally, a multiscale topology transformer (MST) is introduced to effectively learn the structural characteristics of nodes by analyzing their multiscale neighbor topologies in a heterogeneous network. MST can adaptively form topological features by integrating information across various scales and encoding the close relationships among them. Experimental results demonstrate that HSCDA outperforms six comparing methods in predicting circRNA–disease associations. Ablation studies further demonstrate the effectiveness of HGHG, SGCN, and MST, while case studies on three diseases highlight HSCDA’s ability in discovering potential circRNA candidates related to the diseases.

Introduction

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are single-stranded, circular, noncoding RNAs characterized by their closed-loop structure, lacking 5′ caps and 3′ polyadenylated tails. In recent years, many studies have highlighted the involvement of circRNAs in essential biological functions, such as acting as miRNA sponges or serving as carriers for specific RNA-binding proteins. Additionally, a growing body of research findings indicates that the expression of circRNAs is often dysregulated in the development and progression of many diseases, such as cancers , and cardiovascular diseases. Therefore, identifying disease-related circRNAs can assist biologists in comprehending disease mechanisms while also facilitating the diagnosis and treatment of these conditions.

In recent years, a growing body of evidence from researchers like Wang et al. and Zhu et al. has indicated that noncoding RNAs, or ncRNAs, such as circular RNAs, known as circRNAs, play a crucial role in the development and progression of various human diseases, ranging from cancer to inflammatory conditions. These molecules do not act in isolation; instead, as shown by Liu et al., they form a complex regulatory network interwoven with various molecules like lncRNAs, circRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs. For example, in studies of diseases such as nasopharyngeal carcinoma, scientists including Liu et al. and Zhu et al. have identified specific circRNA regulatory networks, revealing their complex mechanisms in pathological processes. However, fully elucidating the complex interactions within these networks and accurately predicting key disease-related circRNAs remain significant challenges. To address these challenges, deep learning techniques, particularly complex frameworks capable of integrating multisource and multidimensional information, have become a frontier in bioinformatics research. These advanced models can not only process complex biological data, for instance, work by Wang et al. on predicting RNA secondary structures or research by Zhang et al. on identifying transcriptional regulatory factors, but also have the potential to uncover causal relationships that go beyond simple associations. This aligns with the current goal in genetic research of exploring causal links in diseases, a point emphasized by Han et al. These cutting-edge advancements highlight the urgent need to develop novel computational models capable of effectively integrating heterogeneous biological data to more accurately identify disease-related circRNAs.

Traditional biological experiments for identifying potential disease-related candidate circRNAs are time-consuming and costly. Researchers have developed many computational approaches to enhance experimental efficiency, which fall into three categories. The first category focuses on information propagation within the network. KATZHCDA, designed by Fan et al., integrates Gaussian interaction profile (GIP) kernel similarity, circRNA expression similarity, and disease phenotype similarity to construct a heterogeneous graph. It employs the KATZ algorithm to identify potential circRNA–disease associations. Deng et al. proposed a similar approach, in which they constructed a heterogeneous graph using multisource data and predicted potential associations between circRNAs and diseases based on an improved KATZ algorithm. Lei et al. proposed the PWCDA model, which calculates association scores for each circRNA–disease pair by traversing multiple weighted paths connecting circRNAs and diseases while employing a decay function to diminish the influence of longer paths. Li et al. developed DWNCPA to extract node embeddings from the association network between circRNAs and diseases using DeepWalk. The approach uses these embeddings to calculate circRNA and disease similarities and then integrates network consistency projection to predict associations between circRNAs and diseases. Although these models have shown promising effectiveness in predicting these associations, most rely on existing circRNA–disease association data to construct networks and compute similarities. If the initial data are biased or insufficient, the predictive accuracy of the models may be severely compromised.

The second category of methods employs machine learning to predict circRNA–disease associations. Lei and Bian. introduced RWRKnn, an approach for predicting circRNA–disease associations. This approach utilizes the random walk with a restart algorithm to weight features incorporating global network topology information. It employs the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm for classification based on these features. Xiao et al. developed the ICDA-CMG model, which adopts a graph learning approach incorporating collective matrix completion to predict potential circRNAs associated with diseases. However, these methods predominantly rely on shallow models such as the random walk with restart (RWR) or matrix factorization. These linear approaches may struggle to capture complex nonlinear relationships in multisource data.

In recent years, deep learning has emerged as a predominant methodology for link prediction in bioinformatics, including microbe–drug and microRNA–disease association prediction. The third category involves deep learning-based approaches for circRNA–disease association prediction, where Wang et al. and Fan et al. employed convolutional neural networks (CNNs) integrated with multisource biological data. Notably, graph neural networks (GNNs) have also been applied to this task. As demonstrated by Wang et al. in their GCNCDA framework, which constructs a heterogeneous graph by integrating both known circRNA–disease relationships and their intrinsic similarities, this approach employs graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to predict novel associations. Dai et al. proposed a novel approach, GraphCDA, which combines GCNs and graph attention networks (GATs) to capture both local and global information on nodes. However, these methods do not capture biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases. They overlook the rich semantic information within the heterogeneous graphs formed by circRNAs and diseases.

In recent years, while GNNs have been widely applied to association prediction in bioinformatics, their “black-box” nature has drawn increasing attention. To enhance model trustworthiness, improving the interpretability of GNNs has become a cutting-edge research direction. Particularly in fields about circRNA function prediction, the researchers have developed excellent models that prioritize interpretability as a core design principle. A prime example is the MiGNN2CDS model recently proposed by Niu et al., which is specifically designed to predict the associations between circRNAs and drug sensitivity. Through an interpretable multiscale attention network, it not only achieves high-precision predictions but can also identify the meta-path instances that contribute most to the prediction results, thereby providing biologically interpretable pathways for the model’s decisions. More broadly, attention-based methods are a mainstream approach for enhancing model interpretability. For instance, Feng et al. utilized a multihead attention network in their drug repositioning model to explain key drug–disease associations. The versatility of this technique is also demonstrated in the work of Yin et al., who employed a Transformer-based attention model to understand the image feature extraction process. Beyond the attention mechanism, a more cutting-edge direction is the exploration of causal inference, which aims to obtain more robust and profound explanations. A typical example in this area is the research by Chen et al., who explored causal representation learning to uncover the fundamental causal relationships behind the data. These methods have made significant progress in improving model transparency. Compared with these methods that focus on the intrinsic interpretability of the model, our work, HSCDA, places its design emphasis on maximizing predictive performance by integrating complex biological features from multiple sources and dimensions. Although HSCDA does not currently incorporate a dedicated interpretability module, some of its components offer the potential for future explainability analysis. For example, the gating values in our HGHG module can be used to infer the importance of different learnable hyperedges in the predictions; the attention scores in the MST module can also reflect the strength of topological dependencies between nodes. Therefore, our work is complementary to current research on interpretability. A future research direction is to apply mature interpretability techniques from related fields to HSCDA, aiming to further open the model’s black box while maintaining its powerful predictive performance.

This study introduces a novel method for predicting associations based on hypergraph convolution with hyperedge gating and a semantic-enhanced graph convolutional network for circRNA-disease association prediction (HSCDA). Our method encodes the biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases, exploits semantic information through meta-paths, and learns the topological representation of nodes. The contributions of HSCDA are as follows.

First, a circRNA may be implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple diseases, while multiple circRNAs may simultaneously participate in the development of a single disease. Therefore, there are complex biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases. We designed learnable hyperedges to characterize these correlations, and the correlations can be reflected from various perspectives by constructing multiple hyperedges. The learnable hyperedges enable the model to dynamically adapt the hypergraph topology, which helps to more accurately describe the correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases.

Second, the hyperedge contains multiple circRNA, disease, and miRNA nodes, and these nodes play an important role in assessing the hyperedge’s importance. Traditional hypergraph convolutional methods often overlook the influence of nodes within a hyperedge on the hyperedge itself. Therefore, we designed the hypergraph convolution with hyperedge gating (HGHG) to leverage the nodes within the hyperedge to help measure the importance of the hyperedge, thereby more effectively encoding the biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases.

Third, in the heterogeneous graph, the circRNA nodes and disease nodes are interconnected through both association and similarity connections, which encapsulate diverse semantics. We construct multiple meta-paths and obtain the semantic features for each circRNA (disease) node by learning the semantics among meta-path neighbors. A semantic-enhanced graph convolutional network (SGCN) is proposed to comprehensively integrate the semantics from meta-path neighbors and the features of all of the circRNA (disease) nodes, thereby forming the semantic representation of each node.

Finally, the multiscale neighborhood topology of a node can reveal its structural characteristics across different neighborhood ranges. To effectively capture these characteristics, we design a multiscale topology transformer (MST), which can adaptively form topological features by integrating the topological information from multiple scales and encode the close relationships among these features. The comparisons with six advanced prediction methods and case studies demonstrate that HSCDA achieves superior performance in circRNA–disease association prediction and effectively screens potential circRNAs for the interested diseases.

Materials and Methods

We introduce a novel model called HSCDA (Figure ) for predicting circRNAs associated with specific diseases. The model uses a heterogeneous graph to represent interactions, similarities, and associations among circRNAs, diseases, and microRNAs. It consists of three components, which encode the biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases, the semantic representations, and the multiscale topological features of nodes. We construct a dynamic hypergraph to represent the complex relationships among multiple circRNAs and diseases and propose an HGHG to capture the biological correlations among multiple circRNA and disease nodes. A novel MST integrates multiscale neighbor topological node features and learns their dependency patterns. The SGCN extracts the semantic representations of the target circRNA (disease) nodes. The features extracted by these three components are integrated to assess the probabilities of the circRNA–disease associations.

Data Set

The data sets utilized in this work derive from a previous study. The raw data were obtained from verified and publicly available biological databases, including the circR2Cancer database, the circad database, and the circRNADisease database. After removing duplicates, 989 pairs of disease–circRNA associations, 902 pairs of circRNA–microRNA interactions, and 837 pairs of microRNA–disease associations are from these databases. We collected 834 circRNAs, 138 diseases, and 555 microRNAs from these processed results.

Construction of circRNA–Disease–miRNA Heterogeneous Graph

We constructed a circRNA–disease–miRNA heterogeneous graph G=(V,E). V represents the set of nodes comprising a circRNA nodes subset V cir, disease nodes subset V dis, and miRNA nodes subset V mir. The edge connecting the nodes v i , v j ∈ V is denoted as e i,j ∈ E. The connection relationships among different node types can be represented by an association matrix M, which includes the circRNA–disease association matrix M c_d , a microRNA–disease association matrix M m_d , and a circRNA–microRNA interaction matrix M c_m .

The association matrix M in the heterogeneous graph is defined as 1 M={Mc_d∈RNc×NdMm_d∈RNm×NdMc_m∈RNc×Nm where N c , N d , and N m denote the numbers of circRNA nodes, disease nodes, and microRNA nodes, respectively, in the heterogeneous graph. If the i-th circRNA­(microRNA) node c i (m i ) is associated with the j-th disease node, the association is denoted as Mi,jc_d=1 ( Mi,jm_d=1 ). Conversely, Mi,jc_d=0 ( Mi,jm_d=0 ) indicates that no association has been discovered between these two nodes. Similarly, in the interaction matrix M c_m , Mi,jc_m=1 represents that an interaction has been identified between the c i and the m j . Otherwise, Mi,jc_m=0 .

Wang et al. calculated the semantic similarity between disease nodes by constructing a directed acyclic graph (DAG) for diseases. Our method also adopts this approach to compute the similarity matrix for disease nodes. Based on existing circRNA–disease (microRNA–disease) association data, we calculate the similarity between circRNA (microRNA) nodes following the methods proposed by Wang et al. and Chen et al. This calculation evaluates the similarity between the sets of diseases associated with two circRNA (microRNA), which we then use as the similarity between the two circRNA (microRNA) nodes. For example, circRNA node c 1 is associated with a set of diseases {d 1,d 2,d 5}, and c 2 is associated with another set of diseases {d 3,d 4}. The similarity between the sets {d 1,d 2,d 5} and {d 3,d 4} is calculated and used as the similarity between c 1 and c 2.

Given S and M, the adjacency matrix of M is defined as 3 Ahete=[ScirMc_dMc_mMc_dTSdisMm_dTMc_mTMm_dSmic] where Ahete∈R(Nc+Nd+Nm)×(Nc+Nd+Nm) . M T denotes the transpose of M. Encoding Correlations among Multiple circRNAs and Diseases by HGHG

A circRNA is often involved in the functional processes of multiple diseases, and several circRNAs may simultaneously participate in the pathogenesis of the same disease. Therefore, diverse biological relationships exist between circRNAs and diseases. In the prediction of circRNA–disease associations, the biological connections among circRNAs, diseases, and miRNAs often exhibit complex, high-order interaction characteristics. A hypergraph is a powerful tool for this purpose, as a single hyperedge can simultaneously connect multiple nodes, directly representing these multiparty synergistic relationships. Each hyperedge can reflect a specific biological correlation between circRNAs and diseases. For instance, in Figure a, the hyperedge e 1 denotes the involvement of circRNA c 1 in the disease pathogenesis of d 1 and d 2. The hyperedge e 2 represents the collaborative involvement of circRNA c 2 and c 3 in the biological processes of disease d 3.

The core motivation for making these hyperedges learnable is to empower the model to automatically discover and construct a hypergraph topology that is optimally suited for the prediction task directly from the data. Unlike static methods that rely on fixed, incomplete prior knowledge, our approach allows the hypergraph structure to dynamically evolve and be optimized during training. This enables the model to capture latent and nonexplicit high-order associations, serving the downstream prediction task more accurately. We formally define the i-th hyperedge as a learnable embedding vector ei∈RNc+Nd+Nm , which can be viewed as a prototype for a specific biological association pattern. All B hyperedge prototypes are combined into a learnable hyperedge embedding matrix Eh∈RB×(Nc+Nd+Nm) , where B denotes the number of hyperedges. E h is randomly initialized and dynamically updated throughout the learning process, enabling the hypergraph to evolve its topological structure. By integrating this hyperedge embedding matrix E h with the node attributes X∈R(Nc+Nd+Nm)×(Nc+Nd+Nm) , we construct a dynamic hypergraph. Its corresponding incidence matrix H∈R(Nc+Nd+Nm)×B 4 H=XEh where h i,j ∈ H is the result of the dot product h ij = x i e j T . This operation measures the similarity between the feature vector of node i and the “association pattern prototype” represented by hyperedge j. A higher value for h i,j indicates a stronger affiliation of the node with the hyperedge. It is crucial to note that the hyperedge embedding matrix E h is a learnable parameter that is randomly initialized and can be dynamically updated during training. As an integral part of the overall model, which is trained end-to-end using the Adam optimizer and the cross-entropy loss function, E h is updated via a standard back-propagation algorithm.

Multiple hyperedges contribute differently to the learning of the biological correlation feature vectors of each node. Therefore, we propose a hyperedge gating mechanism to adjust the contribution weight of each hyperedge adaptively. Specifically, it aggregates the node features within each hyperedge to generate the aggregated feature matrix Fe∈RB×(Nc+Nd+Nm) 5 Fe=HTX and then, we employ a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) to process F e , transforming it into the gating scores for each hyperedge. A Sigmoid activation function follows these scores to compute the gating values for each hyperedge. The calculation method is as follows: 6 Ge=sigmoid(WgFe+bg) where Ge∈RB×B represents the hyperedge gating value matrix, the values on the diagonal are the gating values of each hyperedge, which serve to provide a weight for each hyperedge. W g and b g denote the weight matrix and bias vector, respectively. sigmoid(·) denotes the activation function, which maps the input values to the interval of [0,1], thereby providing a gating value between [0,1] for each hyperedge. The hyperedge gating mechanism quantifies each hyperedge’s contribution to the biological correlation features of the nodes through the learned gating values. A higher gating value indicates a higher contribution of the hyperedge, while smaller gating values suggest a lower contribution.

Multiscale Topological Representation Learning by MST

In a heterogeneous graph, the interactions among circRNA, diseases, and miRNA form a complex and highly connected topological network. The topological structure of a node’s neighbors at different scales reflects its different roles and functional information within the network, which provides a valuable perspective for predicting circRNA–disease associations. Specifically, the one-hop (1-hop) neighborhood primarily captures direct relationships between nodes such as known associations, interactions, or similarities. Higher-order neighborhoods (such as 2-hop, 3-hop) can reveal more complex and indirect potential relationships through intermediate nodes. For example, a 2-hop path like circRNA_A → disease_X ← circRNA_B reveals that circRNA_A and circRNA_B are connected because they are both involved in the process of disease_X. Based on the biological hypothesis that molecules involved in the same disease process are likely to be functionally synergistic, this path provides an important clue for potential functional synergy between the two circRNAs, even if no direct similarity or association exists between them.

In the field of graph learning, GCN is used to capture multihop neighborhood information. However, as the number of GCN layers increases, node representations tend to converge, leading to the oversmoothing phenomenon. This makes it difficult to distinguish and utilize specific topological information from higher-order neighborhoods. To effectively utilize multiscale neighborhood information while avoiding the oversmoothing problem, we designed a multiscale topological feature learning mechanism. This mechanism can effectively capture topological neighborhood information on different orders. Its role is to maintain the stability of the feature scale and to prevent gradient explosion or vanishing. Âj contains the j-th order topological information on the nodes. The multiscale topological features capture a broader range of topological information but may potentially obscure some direct and critical relationships from the original adjacency matrix during the fusion process. We preserve these essential topological relationships by combining the original topological features with the learned higher-order neighborhood topological features. By introducing a learnable parameter matrix β, we adaptively adjust the importance of the original topological features, thereby deriving the multiscale topological embedding X topo of the nodes, 12 Xtopo=T+βAhete where β is a learnable parameter matrix, which is randomly initialized and dynamically updated via the back-propagation algorithm during the model’s training process to adaptively adjust the weights of the original topological features.

After we extract the rich multiscale topological features described above for each circRNA, disease, and miRNA node, the key task is to model the global dependencies between these node topologies. Our core hypothesis is that two nodes playing similar topological roles in the network are more likely to be functionally related. The Transformer is an ideal tool to achieve this goal. Thus, we employ a multihead self-attention mechanism within our MST module. This approach allows the model to calculate attention from multiple perspectives, thereby reducing bias during the learning process. Using the multiscale topological embeddings X topo as input, the MST evaluates the relationship between any two nodes by computing attention scores, which quantify the importance of one node relative to another. Subsequently, we normalize XQmXKmT and perform its multiplication with X V m to generate the topological representations for the m-th attention head, 14 Em=softmax(XQmXKmTd1)XVm by integrating the outputs from all attention heads. We then obtain the final output, 15 E=[E1∥E2∥···∥EM] where ∥ denotes the concatenation operation. The topological representations E i circ and E j dise of v i circ and v j dise, respectively, are stacked to generate the topological representation E i,j tpr for the node pair, 16 Ei,jtpr=[EicircEjcirc]

The complexity of the MST module consists of two main parts: multiscale feature fusion and the multihead self-attention mechanism. Let N = N c + N d + N m be the total number of nodes in the heterogeneous graph, J be the total number of topological scales, and d 2 be the dimension of the topological embeddings. For time complexity, the analysis is as follows. First, the main computational cost of multiscale feature fusion lies in computing the powers of the adjacency matrix. In the worst-case scenario of a dense graph, this has a time complexity of O(N 3). However, since the heterogeneous graph constructed in this study is sparse, the actual computational cost is significantly lower than the theoretical upper bound. Second, the complexity of the self-attention mechanism is dominated by matrix multiplications, with a time complexity of O(N 2·d 2). Therefore, the total time complexity of the MST module is bounded by O(N 3+N 2·d 2). For space complexity, the primary overhead comes from storing the powers of the adjacency matrix for different scales and attention score matrices. This results in a total space complexity of O(J·N 2).

Semantic Feature Learning by SGCN

The heterogeneous graph G has many nodes linked through various paths that contain a wealth of semantic information. These paths are denoted as meta-paths. The definition of meta-path ρ is as follows: 17 u1→r1u2→r2···→rlul+1 where u 1···u l+1 denote the node types and r 1···r l represent the relationship types. We define an instance of a meta-path as a sequence of nodes, and the instance follows the definition ρ. For instance, c→r1c→r2d is a meta-path, where c and d represent circRNA node type and disease node type, respectively. r 1 represents the circRNA similarity, and r 2 represents the circRNA–disease association. c 1 → c 2 → d 1 is one of its instances.

For circRNA, we established three metapaths, c → c, c → c → c, and c → d → c. Two circRNAs have a higher probability of associating with similar diseases if their functions are more similar. Based on this biological premise, we construct the first meta-path, c → c. The second meta-path c → c → c captures the two-hop neighbors of the target circRNA node, which are likely to have functions similar to those of the target circRNA. This meta-path is constructed based on the biological premise of the first meta-path and includes two connections that reflect the functional similarities among circRNAs. If two circRNAs are associated with the same disease, we can infer that these two circRNAs may have similar functions. Therefore, the third metapath c → d → c is designed with its semantics reflecting the association between circRNA and disease. We also design three meta-paths for disease d → d, d → d → d, and d → c → d.

The circRNA­(disease) node possesses multiple meta-paths, each with specific semantics. Therefore, we design independent learning modules for each meta-path to learn their distinctive semantic features. Since the semantic encoding process of circRNAs is similar to that of diseases, we use a meta-path of circRNA, specifically, c → c → c as a case to explain this process. c 1 → c 2 → c 4 is an instance of this meta-path, where a semantic similarity exists between c 1 and c 2 and between c 2 and c 4. Therefore, a semantic relationship exists between the meta-path neighbors c 1 and c 4. To fully utilize the semantic information among meta-path neighbor nodes, we first construct a meta-path-based neighbor adjacency matrix to represent the relationships between meta-path neighbor nodes. Taking the meta-path c → c → c as an example, we construct the adjacency matrix for this meta-path through matrix multiplication S circ S circ, 18 Accc=ScirScir Each element A ccc i,j in the matrix represents the semantic similarity of node c i reaching node c j through a 2-hop path of the same node type. The purpose of this step is to transform the abstract concept of a meta-path into a computable adjacency matrix that quantifies the strength of specific semantic relationships between nodes.

To introduce richer contextual information into the feature space, enabling the model to discover potential connections between circRNAs from different perspectives and levels, we map A ccc to a high-dimensional space. This enables the extraction of richer and more fine-grained semantic features. The original node feature matrix contains the inherent attribute information on each node, while the node semantic features encode the semantic similarity between the target node and its meta-path neighbors. By concatenating the original node features with the node semantic features, we can incorporate the individual features of the nodes while preserving the meta-path semantic information, thus constructing a more comprehensive feature representation. We concatenate the neighbor semantic features H ccc obtained in the previous step with the original attributes of the nodes X cir to get the semantic-enhanced node attribute matrix Z ccc , 21 Zccc=[Xcir∥Hccc] where X cir denotes the node attribute of circRNA, and ∥ represents the concatenation operation. Then, we design a semantic-enhanced graph convolutional network (SGCN) (as shown in Figure ) and input this enhanced node attribute matrix Z ccc and the meta-path adjacency matrix A ccc into the SGCN together. W ccc l denotes the learnable parameter matrix at the l–th layer, and L sgcn is the number of layers in the SGCN. When l = 0, Z̃ccc0=Zccc .

Similarly, we derive the semantic representations for diseases under different meta-paths, denoted as Z̃dd , Z̃dcd , and Z̃ddd . Fusion of Multiple Representations and Training

The biological correlation representation X i,j bcr, topological representation E i,j tpr, and semantic representation Z i,j nsr of nodes v i circ and v j dise are integrated to derive the final representation F i,j rep of the node pair: 25 Fi,jrep=[Xi,jbcr,Zi,jnsr,Ei,jtpr] F i,j rep is then fed into k-th convolution and max-pooling layers (Figure d), which fuse F i,j rep to form the feature map F i,j rep,k 26 Fi,jrep,k=pool(σ(Wconvk*Fi,jrep,k−1+bk)),k=1,2. where * denotes the convolution operator, the max-pooling and activation function are represented by pool(·) and σ(·), and F i,j rep,0 = F i,j rep. W conv k and b k denote the filter weights and bias vector in the k-th convolutional layer, respectively. The output generated by the final convolution and max-pooling layer is denoted as F̃i,jrep , which helps predict the scores e associated with v i circ and v j dise after passing the fully connected layer: 27 e=softmax(WscoreF̃i,jrep+bscore), where W score and b score represent its learnable weight matrix and bias vector, respectively. The softmax activation function transforms the output of the NP into a probability distribution. Specifically, e = [e 0,e 1], where e 0 represents the probability of no association between the v i circ and v j dise, while e 1 denotes the probability of an association between the pair of nodes. The cross-entropy loss function calculates the loss between the predicted scores e and the true labels y l 28 loss=−∑t=1T[yl×log(e1)+(1−yl)×log(1−e1)] where T is the batch size of training samples.

Results and Discussion

Parameter Settings

HSCDA uses the PyTorch framework on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU. We employed the Adam optimizer to optimize the model and trained it for 70 epochs, with the learning rate set to 0.001 and a batch size of 16. There are two layers in HGHG, with the output feature dimension of the first layer being 600 and that of the second layer being 300. Additionally, we established 1527 hyperedges to capture the intricate associations among the nodes. SGCN comprises two layers, where the output feature dimensions are 900 for the first layer and 600 for the second layer. d 3 is the size of the output dimension for the second layer. In the MST module, the scale of the topological neighborhood j is set to 3, with a single attention head employed. The feature dimensions d 1 and d 2 are 64 and 300, respectively. Fusing multiple representations consists of 2 convolutional layers with 32 filters of size 2 × 13 and 64 filters of size 1 × 4, along with two pooling layers with filter sizes of 1 × 5 and 1 × 10.

Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate the predictive performance of HSCDA and comparison methods using 5-fold cross-validation. The confirmed circRNA–disease associations are randomly split into five equal subsets, termed positive samples, while the significantly larger pool of unconfirmed associations provides the negative samples. We combined four partitions of positive samples with an equal number of randomly selected negative samples to form the training set. The test set consists of the remaining negative samples and the leftover positive samples.

We used the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and the area under the precision–recall curve (AUPR) to evaluate the model. In each fold of cross-validation, the AUC and AUPR values are independently computed and then averaged to obtain the final scores for the model. Considering that biologists might select the top-ranked candidates for further validation, we computed the recall rate of the top-k candidates.

Ablation Experiments

Ablation experiments were conducted to systematically evaluate the individual contributions of our model’s key components and design choices. The results, summarized in Table , demonstrate the effectiveness of each part of the HSCDA framework. The exclusion of the three main modules, HGHG, SGCN, and MST, resulted in significant performance degradation, underscoring their foundational importance. The exclusion of HGHG led to a 3.3% reduction in AUC and a 3.4% decline in AUPR when compared to the complete model, highlighting the importance of encoding the complex correlations between circRNAs and diseases in enhancing the performance of the circRNA–disease association prediction. Without semantic representation learning by SGCN, the AUC decreased by 3.0% and the AUPR decreased by 3.2% compared with the final model. This result highlights the importance of leveraging the semantic information encoded in meta-paths to achieve better performance in circRNA–disease association prediction. When we remove MST, the AUC and AUPR of HSCDA diminished by 2.8% and 2.3%, respectively. These results suggest that incorporating multiscale topological information improves circRNA–disease association prediction.

Ablation Experimental Results on Our Method

To further dissect the model’s architecture, we performed more granular ablations. Within the HGHG module, removing the hyperedge gating mechanism resulted in a 2.4% drop in the AUC and a 1.1% drop in the AUPR. This demonstrates that the hyperedge gating mechanism provides a clear advantage by effectively learning to weigh the contributions of different hyperedges. Similarly, replacing the learnable hypergraph with a static one in the variant “HSCDA-Static” caused a 1.7% decrease in the AUC and a 1.8% decrease in the AUPR, validating the effectiveness of allowing the model to adapt the hypergraph topology during training. We applied the same detailed analysis to the MST module. The importance of integrating diverse neighborhood information was confirmed by the “HSCDA-SingleScale” variant; restricting the model to a single topological scale led to a 2.5% drop in the AUC and a 2.6% drop in the AUPR. To isolate the contribution of the Transformer, we tested the “HSCDA w/o Transformer” variant. This led to a 1.7% decrease in the AUC and a 1.5% decrease in the AUPR, confirming that the Transformer is key to effectively modeling dependencies among topological features. Finally, excluding miRNA data from the initial graph construction caused the AUC to fall by 3.0% and the AUPR to fall by 2.3%, which confirms that these interactions provide a valuable biological context for the model.

In summary, the outcomes of these ablation experiments suggest that while HGHG makes the most significant contribution, primarily due to the hypergraph’s ability to effectively represent the diverse correlations between multiple circRNAs and diseases, each component from the high-level modules down to specific design choices like gating, learnable topology, and multiscale analysisprovides a clear and quantifiable improvement to the model’s predictive power.

Comparison with Other Methods

We compared six advanced circRNA–disease association prediction methods with HSCDA, including SGFCCDA, Bi-SGTAR, MLNGCF, MPCLCDA, GraphCDA, MDGF-MCEC, and Circ2DGNN. HSCDA and the six comparison methods utilized the same data set for training and testing in cross-validation. A brief introduction of the comparative methods appears below.

Circ2DGNN: This method employs a Transformer-based Graph Neural Network (GNN) that operates directly on a comprehensive heterogeneous network containing circRNAs, diseases, and various other biomolecules. The model learns node embeddings by calculating heterogeneous attention scores and assigning unique parameter matrices for different types of meta-relationships to predict associations between circRNAs and diseases.

Bi-SGTAR: This model uses only known circRNA–disease associations, applying dual-view sparse gating and true association regression to enhance prediction accuracy through an encoding–reconstruction–regression framework.

MLNGCF: This method employs an autoencoder to generate initial features, constructs a heterogeneous graph of diseases and circRNAs, and applies a multilayer attention mechanism to obtain higher-order features.

MPCLCDA: This model constructs a heterogeneous graph by combining circRNA and disease similarity matrices with known associations. It extracts multihop connections via meta-paths and optimizes node features through contrastive learning.

GraphCDA: It constructs circRNA and disease similarity networks and alternately stacks GCN and GAT layers to capture multilevel features of circRNAs and diseases.

MDGF-MCEC: It builds relation graphs between diseases and circRNAs using various similarity metrics, applies dual attention to optimize feature weights, and predicts associations through a cooperative ensemble classifier with multiple views.

SGFCCDA: This method uses Scale Graph Convolutional Networks (ScaleGCN) to extract topological and attribute information and employs CNNs for feature convolution to enhance feature representation.

As shown in Table , HSCDA not only achieves the highest average AUC (0.949) and AUPR (0.246) scores but also demonstrates stable performance across the various folds of cross-validation. Figure and Table show that HSCDA achieved the highest average AUC value of 0.949, outperforming SGFCCDA by 13.7%, Bi-SGTAR by 13.9%, MLNGCF by 13.6%, MDGF-MCEC by 19.6%, GraphCDA by 22.4%, MPCLCDA by 15.0%, and Circ2DGNN by 14.6%. Conclusions

We propose a method to encode the biological correlations among multiple circRNAs and diseases, integrating the similarity and association semantics within the meta-paths, along with multiscale neighbor topologies for predicting disease-related candidate circRNAs. The constructed hyperedges help to reveal the involvement of multiple circRNAs in the pathological processes of multiple diseases from various perspectives. The designed hypergraph convolutional network with hyperedge gating can dynamically evolve the hypergraph topology and assign higher weights to the informative hyperedges. A semantic-enhanced graph convolutional network (SGCN) is developed to generate the semantic features of each node in a specific meta-path context and learn the semantic representations of nodes. SGCN explores the diverse semantic relationships among meta-path neighbors. The multiscale topology transformer is designed to embed the multiscale topological structures and integrate the close relationships among the topological features of multiple nodes. The results of the comparison experiments demonstrate that HSCDA achieves higher AUC and AUPR than the six compared methods. Additionally, ablation experiments further confirm the effectiveness of the major innovations of HSCDA. The recall rates for the top-ranked candidates and the case studies on three diseases highlight HSCDA’s capability to retrieve reliable disease-related candidate circRNAs.

For the future work, a direction is to integrate Large Language Models­(LLMs) with advanced graph learning techniques to improve the model’s performance. This approach would allow us to leverage the rich textual information available in biological databases, such as descriptions of circRNA functions, their roles as miRNA sponges, and the molecular pathways of associated diseases, which are often neglected by current graph models. Specifically, LLMs could be used to process scientific literature to generate semantically rich features for nodes or to automatically extract relationships to build more comprehensive knowledge graphs. This integration of graph and text data is expected to significantly enhance both the predictive performance and the interpretability of our results.

Soft sensor modeling method and application based on TSECIT2FNN-LSTM
Authors: Huangtao Dai, Taoyan Zhao, Jiangtao Cao, Ping Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To address the issue of low accuracy in soft sensor modeling of key variables caused by multi-variable coupling and parameter sensitivity in complex processes, this paper introduces a TSK-type-based self-evolving compensatory interval type-2 fuzzy Long short-term memory (LSTM) neural network (TSECIT2FNN-LSTM) soft sensor model. The proposed TSECIT2FNN-LSTM integrates the LSTM neural network with the interval type-2 fuzzy inference system to address long-term dependencies in sequence data by utilizing the gate mechanism of the LSTM neural network. The TSECIT2FNN-LSTM structure learning algorithm uses the firing strength of the network rule antecedent to decide whether to generate new rules to improve the rationality of the network structure. TSECIT2FNN-LSTM parameter learning utilizes the gradient descent method to optimize network parameters. However, unlike other interval type-2 fuzzy neural network gradient calculation processes, the error term in the LSTM node parameter gradient of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM is propagated backwards in the time dimension. Additionally, the error term is simultaneously transferred to the upper layer network to enhance network prediction accuracy and memory capabilities. The TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensor model is utilized to predict the alcohol concentration in wine and the nitrogen oxide emission in gas turbines. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensing model achieves higher prediction accuracy compared to other models.

Introduction

In modern industrial production processes, certain key variables are challenging or impossible to measure online due to economic and technological limitations. These variables are typically measured offline in the laboratory or using online analytical instruments. However, offline measurements often have a significant time delay, while online instruments are expensive and difficult to maintain. This poses challenges in meeting production requirements and may hinder the implementation of optimization control schemes in the future. To address the challenge of measuring certain variables, it is necessary to employ a technology that can monitor and measure these variables in real-time, as they have a significant impact on the economic benefits and production safety of enterprises. Soft sensor technology has emerged as a widely used solution. The underlying concept of Soft sensor involves integrating self-control theory with production practice, utilizing computer technology, and selecting easily measurable variables (auxiliary variables) to estimate difficult or unmeasurable key variables (dominant variables) through a mathematical relationship. Soft sensor technology offers several advantages over traditional hardware sensor measurement methods, including its cost-effectiveness, intelligence, reliability, and fast dynamic response. As a result, since the 1990s, soft sensor technology has found extensive application in the production processes of various enterprises.

The soft sensor modeling technology primarily comprises three components: mechanism analysis and auxiliary variable selection, data collection and processing, and the establishment of soft sensor models. Among these, the establishment of soft sensor models is the most crucial. Fuzzy neural network (FNN) is a commonly employed model in soft sensor that integrates the benefits of fuzzy logic systems and neural networks1. It offers a wider processing range and faster information processing speed, making it extensively utilized as a soft sensor model. Ding et al.2 proposed a self-organizing recursive FNN to improve the network’s dynamic characteristics. They achieved this by establishing internal feedback nodes and applied the network to predict the biochemical oxygen demand of effluent. Xu et al.3 proposed a generalized dynamic FNN soft sensor model. They improved the global or local learning rate through a parameter hierarchical learning mechanism. Experimental results demonstrated that the model has high accuracy and strong real-time performance. Yuan4 proposed an SVI soft sensor model based on a self-organizing fuzzy Elman neural network. This model achieves high-precision prediction of parameters without requiring an understanding of the relationships between variables in the system. Fang et al.5 developed a soft sensor model based on a rough set dynamic FNN. They applied it to study the average particle size soft sensor of polyvinyl chloride, and the results showed excellent predictive performance. Wang et al.6 utilized the fuzzy transfer method to dynamically reconstruct the soft sensor model of a dynamic FNN. They then applied this method to the modeling problem of conversion rate in the aggregation process. The prediction accuracy of this approach was proven by the experimental results. Compared to traditional FNNs, interval type-2 fuzzy neural networks (IT2FNNs) utilize type-2 fuzzy logic systems7, which offer enhanced capabilities in handling uncertainty and anti-interference problems. These networks have been effectively employed in nonlinear system modeling8–10. Qiao et al.11 proposed a soft sensor model for predicting PM2.5 concentration in the atmosphere. They utilized a T-S IT2FNN and applied it to practical environments. The experimental results demonstrated that this method can achieve real-time prediction of PM2.5. In comparison to other methods, the PM2.5 prediction method based on T-S IT2FNN exhibited better training effectiveness and higher prediction accuracy. Han et al.12 developed a soft sensor model using an IT2FNN. They updated the model’s relevant parameters through the gradient descent method, thereby improving its accuracy. This model successfully addressed the issue of predicting ammonia nitrogen in wastewater treatment process effluent. Feng13 introduced a soft sensor modeling method called regularized AdaBound based on IT2FNN (RAIT2FNN) and applied it to predict tail oxygen concentration in the non-catalytic oxidation process of cyclohexane. The model provides the benefits of accurate and prompt prediction. Guo et al.14 introduced a soft sensor method for predicting total phosphorus in effluent using a IT2FNN. They integrated this method into the sewage treatment operation system and conducted experiments to validate its effectiveness. The results demonstrated that the method can accurately predict total phosphorus in real-time. Corissa15 suggested a bone pH soft sensor method using IT2FNN. The findings from the conducted experiments illustrate that the model exhibited strong learning and generalization capabilities, significantly improving measurement accuracy and enabling online detection of bone pH. Song et al.16 developed a T-S IT2FNN soft sensor model with a recursive link and applied it to the clarification process in a sugar factory. The experimental results highlighted the superior performance of the model, which achieved the global optimal model for a nonlinear system. Dong et al.17 proposed a wind speed soft sensor method based on an adaptive IT2FNN. Simulation results demonstrated that the estimated wind speed accurately tracked the changes in effective wind speed and exhibited high accuracy.

However, the aforementioned studies on FNN and IT2FNN are all based on feedforward network structures and lack the ability to process historical information. This limitation can potentially impact the accuracy of identification and prediction in the model. To address this issue, this article proposes the integration of long short-term memory networks (LSTM) into IT2FNNs. LSTM, initially introduced by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, is designed to effectively handle temporal data18,19. In contrast to traditional recurrent neural networks (RNN), LSTM incorporates a memory unit and three control unit state switches in its structure-the input gate, forget gate, and output gate. By utilizing storage units to manage correlations in time series, the LSTM model effectively addresses both short-term and long-term time dependency issues. For instance, Choi et al.20 introduced a novel LSTM ensemble prediction algorithm, which efficiently integrates various prediction outcomes from a group of individual LSTM networks. Additionally, LSTM, being a recurrent structure of neural networks, has been applied in various domains such as control fields21, event forecasting22, and natural language acquisition23. Additionally, recent years have seen the emergence of variants of LSTM, such as bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM)24 and gated recurrent unit (GRU)25.

In summary, this paper proposes a TSK-type-based self-evolving compensatory interval type-2 fuzzy LSTM neural network (TSECIT2FNN-LSTM) and applies it to industrial soft sensing modeling problems. The self-evolutionary characteristics of the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensor model structure learning algorithm enable it to autonomously adapt the necessary network structure (including the number of rules and initial fuzzy set shape) and parameters using the provided training data samples. Moreover, integrating LSTM network into IT2FNN equips the network with the ability to process historical information, effectively addressing long-term dependency issues without being constrained by the technical limitations of feedforward network structures. Simultaneously, it addresses the challenge of gradient disappearance faced by traditional recursive fuzzy neural systems and improves the inference capability of sequence data containing long-term dependency characteristics. Additionally, a fully recursive structure in terms of the time dimension has been implemented to ensure improved network prediction accuracy and memory building capability. To attain the utmost type-2 fuzzy logic reasoning and determine the most suitable type-2 fuzzy operation, this research paper presents the incorporation of a compensatory element. The utilization of compensation techniques facilitates the mapping of both pessimistic input ×1 and optimistic input ×2, providing a relative middle ground between superior and inferior scenarios. As a result, the proposed TSECIT2FNN-LSTM is capable of adaptively adjusting the rule firing strength. Additionally, an interval type-reduction technique is proposed to dynamically determine the weights of the upper and lower firing strength after compensatory operations, effectively optimizing fuzzy operations. This ensures that the adaptability of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM is not limited and contributes to improving the network’s accuracy. Moving on to parameter learning for the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model, the gradient descent method is employed to optimize the network parameters. In gradient calculation, apart from the error term transmitted to the previous layer of the network, there is also the reverse transmission of the error term in the time dimension. This distinguishes LSTM node gradient calculation from other IT2FNNs. Finally, the application of the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensor model was utilized to forecast the precision of wine and the concentration of nitrogen oxide in gas turbines. The experimental results showed that the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensor model has high prediction accuracy.

The subsequent parts of this article are as listed: Sect “TSECIT2FNN-LSTM structure” presents the configuration of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM, a fusion of compensation operations, interval reduction techniques, and long short-term memory neural networks. The Sect "The learning algorithm of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM" outlines the algorithms for structure learning and parameter learning in TSECIT2FNN-LSTM. The Sect "Simulation examples" encompasses two simulation instances, namely wine alcohol prediction and gas turbine nitrogen oxide prediction. Finally, the Sect "Conclusion" concludes this article.

The objective of this method is to enhance the precision of network approximation, while maintaining the speed and robustness achieved by the Nie Tan approach. The learning algorithm of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM

TSECIT2FNN-LSTM employs simultaneous structure and parameter learning. In the context of offline learning, structure learning is initially conducted on the entire training dataset, followed by parameter learning on the established and fixed network structure. In contrast, simultaneous learning entails the execution of both structure and parameter learning algorithms for each incoming data instance. During practical implementation, structure learning is performed first, followed by parameter learning for each new piece of data. This means that parameter learning is conducted online on the newly evolved network structure. The TSECIT2FNN-LSTM begins without any predefined rules. It utilizes an efficient algorithm for rule and fuzzy set generation, which operates online as learning progresses with the receipt of training data. Once a recurrent rule is generated, its free parameters are adjusted using a parameter learning algorithm. The subsequent sections will detail the structure and parameter learning algorithms.

Structure learning of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM

The significance of structural learning algorithms lies in their ability to determine whether to extract and add a new rule from training data, thereby correcting the network structure of TSECIT2FNN-LSTM. Soft sensor modeling of nitrogen oxide emissions from gas turbines

A gas turbine, which is a type of rotating impeller thermal engine, is an internal combustion engine that uses continuously flowing gas as the working fluid to convert the energy of fuel into useful work. However, gas turbines emit a significant amount of pollutants during their operation. As environmental protection standards become increasingly strict, research on gas turbine emissions is gaining importance. The pollutants emitted by natural gas fueled gas turbines include NOX, CO, unburned hydrocarbons (UHC), SOX, and dust. Generally, the CO, UHC, SOX, and dust levels generated during stable operation of the unit are very low, while NOX emissions are high. Therefore, this article focuses on nine input variables: intake temperature, intake pressure, intake humidity, air filter pressure difference, gas turbine exhaust pressure, turbine inlet temperature, turbine post turbine temperature, compressor exhaust pressure, and turbine energy yield. Ablation study

The TSECIT2FN-LSTM model proposed in this article consists of seven layers. The first three and seventh layers are fixed structures of interval type-2 fuzzy neural networks. Therefore, only the fourth to sixth layers require ablation experiments, and one layer needs to be removed in each experiment. The experimental results are shown in Tables 5 and 6. Table 5 shows the ablation experiment of alcohol concentration. It can be seen that compared with Model 4 (TSECIT2FNN-LSTM), Model 1 without the sixth regression layer has significantly reduced model complexity, reduced model running time by 32.2%, increased training and testing RMSE to 0.08126 and 0.07493, respectively, and decreased training and testing R2 value by 3.39% and 2.23%, respectively. Compared with Model 4, Model 2, which lacks the fifth interval reduction layer, has almost no change in model complexity, only reduces model running time by 3.6%, increases training and testing RMSE to 0.03316 and 0.04189, respectively, and decreases training and testing R2 value by 1.01% and 1.04%, respectively. Compared with Model 4, Model 3, which lacks the fourth layer of incentive compensation, has slightly reduced model complexity, reduced model running time by 19.8%, increased training and testing RMSE to 0.03554 and 0.04812, respectively, and decreased training and testing R2 value by 0.97% and 0.075%, respectively. In summary, the absence of the sixth regression layer (i.e. LSTM layer) has the greatest impact on the running time and various performance indicators of the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model; The absence of the fifth interval descent layer has a relatively small impact on the performance of the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model; The absence of the fourth compensation excitation layer has a significant impact on the running time of the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model, but has minimal effects on the training and testing RMSE values and training and testing R values.

Conclusion

A TSECIT2FNN-LSTM soft sensor modeling method is proposed to address the issue of low accuracy in monitoring variables caused by multivariable coupling and parameter sensitivity in complex industrial processes. This article applies the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model to the prediction of wine alcohol concentration and nitrogen oxide emissions. The experimental results show that the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model has a relatively short running time, indicating its good real-time prediction performance. The lower RMSE value and higher R2 value indicate that the TSECIT2FNN-LSTM model has higher prediction accuracy. In the ablation experiment, the network structures of the fourth, fifth, and sixth layers of the TSECIT2FN-LSTM model all contribute to the performance improvement of the model. Although the sixth layer affects the real-time performance of the model to some extent, it plays a relatively significant role in the predictive ability of the model; The fourth and fifth layers slightly reduced the real-time performance of the model, which slightly helped improve the predictive performance of the model but was much lower than the sixth layer. The contribution of this article is as follows: (1) An optimal type-2 fuzzy logic reasoning can be achieved by introducing a compensation operator, which enables adaptive adjustment of the membership function and effective optimization of the fuzzy operator. In addition, the selection of the appropriate type-2 fuzzy operation plays a crucial role in this context. (2) To ensure adaptability, the interval reduction technique can be employed to select the weights of the upper and lower excitation intensities. These weights are adjusted by the compensation operator for each rule, enhancing the overall performance of the system. (3) The method utilizes a long short-term memory mechanism to implement a recursive structure, which helps to avoid the gradient vanishing problem commonly encountered in classical recursive FNNs. This enhancement significantly improves the inference performance of sequence data exhibiting long-term dependency features. While the LSTM mechanism has shown promising results in learning long-term dependent data, its internal structure is quite intricate. Exploring ways to simplify this cyclic structure by incorporating memory capabilities and gate mechanisms in fuzzy neural systems could be a valuable area for future research.

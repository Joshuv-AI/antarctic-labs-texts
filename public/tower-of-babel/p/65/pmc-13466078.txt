Comments on “Impact of delayed and early umbilical cord clamping on maternal and neonatal outcome – A prospective observational study”
Authors: V Revathi
Published: 2026

To,

The Editor,

Journal of Family Medicine and Primary Care

Subject: Comments on “Impact of delayed and early umbilical cord clamping on maternal and neonatal outcome – A prospective observational study”

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the study by Prasad et al. on early versus delayed umbilical cord clamping (ECC vs. DCC). While the topic is highly relevant, we would like to highlight several concerns regarding the study design and interpretation of results.

First, as a prospective observational study without randomization, there is a notable risk of selection bias. Indeed, baseline differences in maternal age and body mass index between groups may have influenced neonatal outcomes such as birth weight, hemoglobin, and hematocrit.

Second, the timing of cord clamping, particularly in the DCC group (1–3 minutes), was not standardized. Variability in practice between clinicians may confound the observed effects.

Third, important potential confounders—maternal nutrition, neonatal gender, and subtle intrapartum complications—were not controlled for, limiting the ability to attribute differences solely to cord clamping.

Fourth, while statistical significance was reported for several outcomes, the wide variability in hemoglobin and hematocrit, along with small differences in 5-minute Apgar scores, raises questions about clinical relevance.

Finally, the modest sample size and low number of adverse events restrict generalizability and limit conclusions regarding rare but important outcomes like polycythemia or hyperbilirubinemia.

In conclusion, while the study supports the benefits of delayed clamping, these methodological limitations suggest caution in interpreting the findings. Randomized controlled trials with standardized protocols and adjustment for confounders would provide more robust evidence.

Sincerely,

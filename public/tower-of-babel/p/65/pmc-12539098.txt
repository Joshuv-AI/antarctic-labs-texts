A case report on SMARCA4 deficient cervical adenocarcinoma with high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion
Authors: Liang Xu, Shubin Huang, Haoyu Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SMARCA4-deficient cervical adenocarcinoma is an exceedingly rare and aggressive subtype of cervical malignancy that presents with clinicopathological features mimicking other types of cervical cancer, leaving no established treatment protocols available. This report describes a case of a 50-year-old woman in perimenopause who presented with an increase in vaginal discharge, discomfort in the external genitalia, and bleeding after intercourse. The imaging examination revealed a cervical mass accompanied by enlarged lymph nodes in the pelvic cavity. A cervical biopsy confirmed adenocarcinoma, with an initial clinical stage classified as International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018 stage IIA1. The patient underwent a radical hysterectomy and pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND), during which it was found that the tumor had involved pelvic lymph nodes. The revised staging was Stage IIIC1. According to the postoperative pathologic analysis, the woman was diagnosed as adenocarcinoma with a poorly differentiated grade and with myoepithelial differentiation features. Immunohistochemical analysis supported the diagnosis of the SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma that was accompanied by high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) in its surroundings, indicating the presence of two distinct types of lesions. Postoperative review after one month revealed multiple lymph node metastases in the left neck. Pathological examination confirmed it as distant metastasis from cervical adenocarcinoma, ultimately leading to a diagnosis of pT1N1M1 (IVB stage) cervical cancer. Following a six-cycle treatment regimen with cadonilimab, paclitaxel, and cisplatin, the lymph nodes in the neck demonstrated a significant reduction, indicating a preliminary positive response. In this case, SMARCA4-deficient cervical adenocarcinoma was characterized by significantly high invasive potential, early metastasis, and heterogeneity, indicating the significance of early detection and molecular pathological diagnosis in guiding personalized treatment strategies. Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) ranks as the fourth most common type of cancer in women globally, serving as the leading cause of incidence and mortality [1–3]. According to statistics by the World Health Organization (WHO), in 2022, over 660,000 new cases of CC were recorded globally, with approximately 350,000 deaths [3, 4]. CC is primarily classified into two distinct histological types, including squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma. Compared to squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma has a rising incidence rate, posing greater challenges in achieving good prognoses [5].

The brahma-related gene 1 (BRG1) protein encoded by the SMARCA4 gene is a key component of the SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex, involved in various important biological processes such as gene transcription regulation, DNA repair, and cell cycle regulation [6]. SMARCA4 deficiency (including mutations, intermittent silencing, or loss of protein expression) has been reported in various malignant tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), ovarian cancer, gastric cancer, sinus cancer, and gallbladder cancer [7–11]. SMARCA4-deficient tumors typically have high invasiveness and poor response to traditional treatment methods, and are closely associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis [12, 13]. In CC, since SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinomas are exceedingly rare, their clinical presentations and pathological characteristics remain largely uncharted and underexplored. In addition, high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) is an intermediate stage between normal cervical epithelium and CC, often associated with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infection [14, 15]. In CC with HPV infection, P16 protein is often overexpressed, serving as an alternative biomarker for HPV-related lesions [16, 17]. However, current studies on SMARCA4-deficient head and neck tumors, sinus cancer, and primary undifferentiated malignant tumors of the skin all show negative expression of P16, and there are no reports of P16-positive cases [18–20]. SMARCA4-deficient tumors usually present as P16-negative, indicating that they do not belong to HPV driven tumors. This work reported an unusual case of a SMARCA4-deficient cervical adenocarcinoma with HSIL, aiming to explore the coexistence phenomenon of these two disorders in a patient, analyze their molecular characteristics, and potential pathogenic mechanisms, thus enhancing the awareness of this rare type of malignancy and guiding the development of clinical diagnostic and treatment strategies.

Case

A perimenopausal woman aged fifty reported an increase in vaginal secretions over the past half-year, accompanied by discomfort and an unpleasant odor. Approximately one month ago, mild bleeding following intercourse occurred, which ceased after one day. Due to an excessive secretion and persistent discomfort in the perineal region, she sought consultation at our Department of Gynecology on July 8, 2024. Upon examination, a roughly 4 cm-sized cauliflower-like neoplasm was observed on the cervical surface, with a somewhat brittle texture and obvious bleeding upon palpation. It affected both the left side and the posterior fornix, leading to an initial diagnosis of CC upon outpatient consultation. Subsequently, hospitalization was recommended for further treatment.

The diagnostic process unfolded as follows. Upon admission, five tumor marker tests revealed the following results: carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), 1000.00 ng/mL; alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), 0.85 ng/mL; carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA-125), 108.28 U/mL; carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9), 1.00 U/mL; and carbohydrate antigen 15 − 3 (CA15-3), 15.84 U/mL. A computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen on July 10 revealed a full cervical canal, soft tissue mass in the left lateral cervical region, with unclear contour, and multiple enlarged lymph nodes with rough edges in the right pelvis and retroperitoneum (Fig. 1A). However, the short diameter of the lymph nodes mentioned above did not exceed 1 cm, and the imaging results did not meet the clear criteria for metastasis. The radiologist did not consider it a metastatic lesion. A pelvic MRI revealed a tumor mass in the posterior lip of the cervix, confined to the cervix and not involving the vagina or parametrial structures. The CC was considered. Enlarged lymph nodes were observed near the bilateral iliac vessels (Fig. 1B). The cervical biopsy histopathological diagnosis on July 11 turned out to be adenocarcinoma of the cervix, with an initial clinical stage classified as International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018 stage IIA1. The HPV examination revealed a positive result for HPV-16 (high-risk type).

Following meticulous preoperative preparation, a surgical plan was devised. Due to the inability of preoperative imaging examination to determine whether there was lymph node metastasis, a preliminary surgical plan was proposed on July 12 as “abdominal total hysterectomy + bilateral oophorectomy (high ligation of ovarian arteries and veins) + pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND)”. During the operation, multiple enlarged lymph nodes with hard textures and unclear boundaries were found in the left pelvic cavity. Therefore, rapid frozen pathological examination was conducted, indicating lymph node metastasis of adenocarcinoma. The surgical procedure was then adjusted to “radical hysterectomy + bilateral oophorectomy + PLND”. During the surgery, the staging was revised to FIGO 2018 stage ⅢC1. Following surgery, prophylactic administration was given with cefuroxime (0.75 g IV q8h for 2 days) and metronidazole (0.5 g IV q8h for 3 days) to prevent infection, resulting in a smooth recovery process. On July 21, the results of immunohistochemical markers were as follows: cancer cells P16 (-), CKpan (+), EMA (+), Vimentin (-), HMB45 (-), Melan-A (-), S-100 (-), TFE3 (-), INI-1 (+), BRG1(-), Syn (-), INSM1 (-), Ki-67 (70%+), and MyoD1 (-). The pathological results showed that (1) a raised tumor was visible at the cervical internal opening, with a histopathological type of adenocarcinoma and a low degree of differentiation. The cancer tissue exhibited striated muscle-like differentiation characteristics with obvious nucleoli. Combined with immunohistochemical labeling results, the SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma was indicated. (2) The tumor measured 3.3 * 2.2 * 1.3 cm, with a depth of infiltration into the cervical fibrous muscle layer exceeding 2/3. The tumor was classified as C type according to the Silva system, with vascular invasion (+) and no invasion of endometrial glandular tissue. (3) HSIL that extends to the glandular structures was observed around the tumor. The uterine lining exhibited proliferative changes, with no evidence of cancerous tissue invasion detected in bilateral parametria, vaginal walls, bilateral ovaries, or fallopian tubes (Fig. 2). The detection of bilateral pelvic lymph nodes revealed that in the left pelvis, low-grade carcinoma metastasis was observed (2/7), but no metastasis was detected in the right pelvis (0/5). The postoperative recovery was favorable, allowing for the discharge on July 26. It was recommended to rest for 4 weeks after surgery before going to the oncology department for further evaluation and treatment with adjuvant chemotherapy.

However, one month after surgery, a palpable mass was noted in the left neck. On August 10, 2024, a review was conducted in an external hospital with B-ultrasonics and chest CT scans, which showed multiple enlarged lymph node shadows on the left side of the neck (Fig. 3A). Following further examination within our facility, the biopsy smear of the left cervical lymph node identified the nodes as metastatic low-grade adenocarcinoma. Coupled with the original cervical histopathological features, the nodes were observed to be induced by the metastasis of cervical adenocarcinoma. A diagnosis of postoperative CC pT1N1M1 (IVB stage) was finally achieved. On August 14, a squamous cell carcinoma-associated antigen (SCC Ag) test was conducted, and the result was 0.63 ng/ml, within the normal range. Considering the low sensitivity of SCC-Ag to adenocarcinoma, its clinical reference value was limited. After multidisciplinary consultation, the treatment plan was preliminarily determined based on the CSCO cervical cancer diagnosis and treatment guidelines and the COMPASSION-13 (Phase II AK104-210 study) of Clinical Application Guidelines for Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Gynecological Tumors (2023 Edition). After sufficient communication with the family, we decided to adopt an immunotherapy combined chemotherapy regimen (cadonilimab in conjunction with TP). Thus, on August 16 (first chemotherapy), treatment began, consisting of cadonilimab 500 mg on day 1 + paclitaxel 200 mg on day 1 + cisplatin 30 mg over days 1–3, with no significant adverse reactions observed. Following treatment, the left cervical lymph nodes showed a marked reduction in size compared to before, and the tumor markers exhibited a substantial decrease (5 tumor markers: CEA 429.87 ng/mL, AFP 1.21 ng/mL, CA-125 8.61 U/mL, CA19-9 1.00 U/mL, CA15-3 15.44 U/mL). A preliminary assessment suggested that the treatment was effective. The subsequent treatment methods are outlined in Attachment 1. On December 3, 2024, after six cycles, CT re-evaluation showed multiple slightly larger lymph nodes in the left neck root and subclavian fossa, which had shrunk significantly compared to before (Fig. 3B). The treatment yielded satisfactory results. As of March 17, 2025, outpatient follow-up and cervical ultrasound revealed no enlarged lymph nodes on either side, with regular shapes (Fig. 4A and C). A CT scan of the abdomen revealed no significant mass or abnormal enhancement in the surgical site after a hysterectomy and bilateral adnexectomy (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma is a rare and highly invasive entity characterized by histological patterns of low or undifferentiated carcinoma. It typically displays striated muscle-like features and is often accompanied by BRG1 protein deficiency [12, 21]. Previous research has primarily focused on SMARCA4-deficient tumors in locations such as lungs, stomachs, ovaries, gastrointestinal tracts, and bladders [12, 22, 23]. However, the primary uterine cervical SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma is exceedingly rare, with no large-scale epidemiological data currently available. In cases of cervical low-grade adenocarcinoma, if immunohistochemical staining suggests a lack of BRG1, heightened vigilance should be exercised regarding this subtype. In this study, lymph node metastasis was detected in the left neck even one month post-surgery, indicating rapid disease progression, high malignancy, and poor prognosis. SMARCA4-deficient tumors often exhibit an exceedingly aggressive and early distant metastatic tendency, with dissemination to sites including the hilar and para-aortic lymph nodes, retroperitoneum, and extracranial lymph nodes [23, 24]. The presence of neck lymph node metastasis further supported this conclusion, emphasizing the clinical need to increase vigilance for SMARCA4-deficient tumors’ potential for distant metastasis.

In this case, the individual is a perimenopausal female who presented with classical CC symptoms. The histopathological type of cervical mass is adenocarcinoma, with low differentiation and high expression of Ki-67. The immunohistochemical results showed P16 negative and BRG1 deficiency, confirming the diagnosis of SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma. The tumor exhibits highly malignant and invasive biological behavior. It is noteworthy that the patient’s cervical periphery harbors HSIL with the involvement of glands. The two ailments differ fundamentally in their causative mechanisms and molecular backgrounds. SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma typically presents with negative P16, suggesting that it does not belong to HPV-driven CC[25]. However, HSIL is a typical HPV-related change, commonly characterized by diffuse and strong positivity for P16 and originates from squamous epithelium in the transformation zone of the cervix, serving as an important precursor lesion of CC[16, 26, 27]. In a relevant study, 100% of penile squamous cell carcinomas are reported as HPV-positive, with 96% being associated with high-risk HPV infections. Approximately 30% of cases exhibit a lack of SMARCA4[28]. In this case, non-HPV-related SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma coexists with HPV (P16 positive) - related HSIL lesions, suggesting that the two lesions develop independently pathologically. This reflects the highly complex and heterogeneous nature of cervical tumorigenesis at the local level, which should be given due consideration in clinical practice. A comprehensive assessment should be formulated to tailor a personalized treatment plan.

In addition, Sirák et al..[23] proposed that SMARCA4-deficient tumors are relatively resistant to conventional chemotherapy, and there is no widely accepted consensus on using cisplatin in combination with etoposide as an effective treatment for the small cell carcinoma of hypercalcemic type. Existing studies have shown that SMARCA4-deficient tumors often co-occur with mutations in other subunits of the SWI/SNF complex, and are associated with immunotherapy responsiveness [29, 30]. Therefore, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) may prove to be a promising therapeutic approach. According to recent reports, SMARCA4-deficient non-small cell lung cancer responds significantly to ICI therapy, leading to a significant extension of progression-free survival and overall survival. In some cases, the disease can be controlled for over 14 months [31]. In this case, after receiving the combination therapy of cadonilimab with TP (paclitaxel plus cisplatin), the lymph nodes in the posterior neck showed significant shrinkage, indicating the potential efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in such tumors that warrant further clinical exploration. Unfortunately, the patient did not undergo whole-exome gene testing, so it cannot be determined whether SMARCA4 deletion is accompanied by other driver gene mutations, methylation modification abnormalities, or specific immune microenvironment features. We performed immunohistochemical detection on the mismatch repair (MMR) protein, finding that the expression of MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2 was normal, indicating unimpaired DNA mismatch repair function (Supplementary Fig. 1). However, the lack of PD-L1 expression and tumor mutation burden (TMB) detection has limited further understanding of the molecular characteristics and biological behavior of this tumor. In the future, with the aid of high-throughput sequencing and multi-omics analysis, it is hoped that we will discover the pathogenic mechanism, immune evasion pathways, and potential therapeutic targets in SMARCA4-deficient tumors, thereby providing more compelling evidence for personalized therapy.

In summary, primary SMARCA4-deficient cervical adenocarcinoma is exceedingly rare, with diagnosis relying on histopathological features and immunohistochemical characteristics. In this case, the coexistence of SMARCA4-deficient adenocarcinoma with HPV-related HSIL reflects significant pathological heterogeneity in the cervical region, underscoring the need for a multi-modal diagnostic approach and personalized treatment. Early molecular pathological testing is conducive to optimizing treatment plans and prognosis assessment.

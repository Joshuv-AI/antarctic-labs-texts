A clinical study on current patterns of epidemic conjunctivitis in a tertiary care hospital of Assam, North-East India
Authors: Tapan Gogoi, Tashrifa Naznin, Krisna Nath, Neelanjana Sarmah, Biswajyoti Borkakoty
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Pathogen-associated conjunctivitis can be either viral or bacterial with viral conjunctivitis accounting for up to 75% of the total conjunctivitis cases.[1] Enterovirus is profoundly associated with acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis (AHC), which is highly contagious and can be identified by distinct symptoms including photophobia, watery eyes, eyelid edema, conjunctival hemorrhages, and superficial punctate keratitis.[2]

The present study investigated the causative agent responsible for the conjunctivitis outbreak that occurred in August 2023 in a tertiary care hospital, in Assam, North-East India. The study was an observational cross-sectional study based on clinical examination and molecular characterization. The samples (conjunctival swab and throat swab) from 22 suspected cases of conjunctivitis were collected from both eyes.

For screening of the responsible pathogen, all the throat swab and conjunctival swab samples were processed for nucleic acid extraction using MagRNA-II automatic nucleic acid extraction kit (GENES2ME PRIVATE LIMITED, India), followed by real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT PCR). The PCR reaction was set up in a total volume of 20 µl using enterovirus-specific primer pairs and an enterovirus-specific probe (1 µl each of the primers and 0.5 µl of the probe), one step real-time RT PCR master mix (5 µl), nuclease free water (5.5 µl), and RNA samples (7 µl). The PCR cycling conditions were as follows: holding stage at 50◦C for 5 minutes, 95◦C for 20 seconds, and cycling stage of 45 cycles with 95◦C for 5 seconds, followed by 58◦C for 30 seconds.

All the enterovirus-positive samples were further sequenced to target the 436 bp region of the 5’ untranslated region (5’UTR) of enterovirus using the procedure of the Ilumina COVIDSeq Test kit and the enterovirus primers as published previously.[3] The experiment was set up on an Illumina MiSeq platform with a 2 × 75 paired-end v3 Flow Cell. Post-sequencing data analysis was performed using the Linux Command Line Interface (CLI). The obtained sequences were aligned to the reference genome (Enterovirus C accession No. NC_002058.3 and Coxsackievirus A24 accession No. DQ443001.1). To characterize the causative enterovirus species of the outbreak, phylogenetic analysis was performed by comparing partial 5’UTR sequences of the enterovirus with nearly related reference sequences available in NCBI GenBank. Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST) was used for the identification of the nearest available sequences of the target sequences. Furthermore, all the positive partially sequenced enteroviral sequences (approximately 436 base pairs of the partial 5’UTR region) were aligned with reference sequences from enterovirus types A to F. For the multiple sequence alignment, the Clustal W alignment program was used available in MEGA X software. Phylogenetic analysis was carried out using the best fit model. The representative nucleotide sequences of the present study were submitted to GenBank, and the accession numbers of the representative sequences are OR554929, OR554930, OR554931, OR554932, OR554933, OR554934, OR554935, OR554936 OR554937, OR554938, OR554939, OR554940, OR554941, OR554942 OR554943, OR554944, OR554945, OR554946, OR554947, OR554948, OR554949, and OR554950.

Enterovirus positivity in acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis patients

Overall, 19/22 samples were positive for enterovirus with a positive test rate of 86.36%. Interestingly, among the enterovirus positive samples, 6/19 samples were enterovirus positive only in throat swabs and 5/19 samples were positive of enterovirus in both throat and conjunctival swabs [Table 1]. All the patients possessed mostly bilateral ocular involvement with redness, mild photophobia, foreign body sensation, and subconjunctival hemorrhages, which can be linked to the symptoms related to enteroviral conjunctivitis as presented in Fig. 1. Partial sequencing of the samples showed 97%–99% nucleotide similarity with the human Coxsackievirus A24, a member of enterovirus group C. Hence, it could be stated that the outbreak was associated with the infection of Coxsackievirus A24 (enterovirus C group). This finding was in accordance with the earlier findings from India in 2002 and 2007 that had also reported the Coxsackievirus A-24 (CA-24)-associated conjunctival outbreak.[45] The surge of the present conjunctival outbreak was witnessed in August 2023 when the weather of the study region was hot with high humidity, and that could have served as a favorable environment for the spread of enterovirus. Our contention on the fact that conjunctivitis outbreak is seasonally influenced was further supported by a previously published study where authors reported that viral conjunctivitis most commonly occurs in summer.[6] Apart from enterovirus and adenovirus, human influenza virus and SARS-CoV-2 have also acquired the potential to cause conjunctivitis. Harnessing that scenario, the conjunctivitis outbreak samples from the present study were also screened for SARS-CoV-2 and influenza virus positivity.

In conclusion, the enteroviral strain responsible for this outbreak was the Coxsackievirus A-24 (CA-24) from the enterovirus C family and the disease was characterized by rapid onset, usually benign, and resolved approximately within 7–14 days as per the clinical data. Furthermore, dual sample collection from both conjunctival and throat swabs may improve the sensitivity of enterovirus detection in cases of conjunctivitis.

Intravenous immunotherapy with Der p 1-containing nanoparticles alleviates the allergic responses in a murine allergic rhinitis model
Authors: Yu-Mi Park, Hana Kim, Ji An Kim, Hyun Jong Lee, Yoon Hong Chun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSubcutaneous immunotherapy has several limitations, including the long dose escalation period, frequent injections and risk of adverse reactions. Crude extracts used for immunotherapy can contain unrecognized immunostimulatory components or induce neosensitization. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to develop a new efficient method to induce allergen-specific immune tolerance using Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus (Der p) 1-containing nanoparticles. We developed two different types of allergen-containing poly(lactic-co-glycolic) [PLGA] nanoparticle vaccines: Der p 1-encapsulated and Der p 1-conjugated vaccines. House dust mite (HDM)-sensitized mice were administered positive control (PC), PLGA (vehicle control), encapsulated vaccine (EV), and conjugated vaccine (CV) via intravenous injections one week after sensitization. Subcutaneous immunotherapy group received three subcutaneous HDM extract injections at two-day intervals. After challenge, we compared the rhinitis symptoms, immunoglobulin (Ig) levels, and histopathology of the nasal mucosa and analyzed splenocyte cultures for cytokine production and regulatory T cell activation. EV and CV groups exhibited improved nasal symptoms and lower ear swelling and inflammation in the nasopharyngeal lavage and nasal tissues than PC group. Serum HDM-specific IgG1 levels were higher in EV and CV groups than in PC group. No changes were observed in the total and HDM-specific IgE levels in EV group. EV group exhibited lower interleukin-4, −5, −13, and −17 levels and higher interleukin-10 and interferon-γ levels than PC group. The two vaccinated groups exhibited significantly higher regulatory T cell activation than PC group. Overall, intravenously administered Der p 1-containing nanoparticles alleviated the allergic responses and induced tolerance in a murine allergic rhinitis model.

Introduction

Allergen immunotherapy (AIT) involves the regular injection of gradually increasing allergen extract doses over a long period of time to induce immune tolerance and fundamentally treat the allergy symptoms of patients.1,2 However, despite being the only causative treatment for allergic diseases for over 110 years, AIT is not widely used owing to several limitations, including the long dose escalation period, need for frequent injections, and long treatment duration.3–5 Immunotherapy discontinuation rate varies widely based on the study and follow-up period, ranging from 6 to 84% for subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT) and 21 to 93% for sublingual immunotherapy.6 However, a recent systematic review indicated that the real-world SCIT adherence rate is less than 80%.5 SCIT also exerts various side effects, including local and systemic reactions. Although fatal and near-fatal systemic reactions are very rare (approximately 0.01%), SCIT poses a risk of systemic allergic reactions, which is a cause of treatment discontinuation.3,7 Moreover, crude extracts used for immunotherapy can contain unrecognized immunostimulatory components and may induce new sensitization, whereby patients undergoing SCIT can be newly sensitized to previously unrecognized allergen components contained in the therapeutic extract.8–10 Although it was a small-scale cohort study, a recent study reported that 12 (24%) of 51 SCIT cases exhibited neosensitization (new sensitization) to the allergen components used for treatment.8 Due to the risk of new sensitization, personalized intervention strategies using recombinant vaccines are urgently needed for immunotherapy.10,11

Various studies have investigated new drug delivery systems to efficiently and safely deliver allergens and induce immune tolerance in patients. For example, a vaccine system delivering antigens and adjuvants using poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) particles has been widely studied for the easy and safe delivery of large amounts of allergens.12–14 A study reported the alleviation of allergic airway inflammation via intravenous injection of biodegradable PLGA nanoparticles with large amounts of ovalbumin, indicating a safe treatment method with a new administration route.14

In this study, we investigated whether immunotherapy using recombinant Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus (Der p)-1-containing PLGA inhibits immunoglobulin (Ig)-E neosensitization, alleviates inflammation, and induces allergen-specific immune tolerance using house dust mite (HDM)-induced allergic rhinitis model mice. We also evaluated the safety and efficiency of the intravenous administration of biodegradable allergen-associated PLGA nanoparticles as a new immunotherapy method.

Materials and methods

Study design

Six-week-old female BALB/C mice (OrientBio, Seongnam, Korea) were acclimated to an environment of 20 ± 2°C with 40–60% humidity for one week and then randomly allocated into positive control (PC), PLGA vehicle control (VC), HDM-SCIT, Der P 1 allergen-encapsulated poly (encapsulated vaccine [EV]), and Der P 1 allergen-conjugated poly (conjugated vaccine [CV]) groups (N = 9/group; Table 1). In each group, nine animals underwent assessments including ear swelling test, clinical rhinitis scoring, nasopharyngeal lavage, and nasal mucosa histological evaluation. Cytokine profiling and flow cytometric analysis of splenocytes were performed on five animals per group, while detection of serum HDM-specific antibodies was conducted in six animals per group. For sensitization, all experimental groups received intraperitoneal injections (on Days 0, 7, and 14) containing 5 µg HDM antigen (Der p; GREER laboratories, Lenoir, NC, USA) plus 20 µL Imject Alum (Thermo Scientific, Rockford, IL, USA) in 0.1 mL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (Figure 1). Animals in the SCIT group received subcutaneous injections (on Days 21, 23, and 25) of 250 µg HDM antigen in 0.1 mL PBS. In the groups treated with nanoparticle vaccines, a mixture containing 4 µg Der p 1 allergen in 20 μL PLGA solution was administered intravenously (once, on Day 28) via the retro-orbital plexus. The PC and VC groups were given intravenous administrations of equal volumes of PBS and PLGA, respectively. To establish allergic rhinitis symptoms, all groups received intranasal administrations of 25 µg HDM antigen in 30 µL PBS daily for five consecutive days (Days 39–43). After 24 hours following the final intranasal challenge, mice were anesthetized and euthanized by armpit vessel incision, followed by the collection of blood and tissue samples. All procedures were performed according to the National Research Council Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Catholic University of Korea (approval number: CIMH-2022–003). PLGA nanoparticle synthesis

Preparation of PEMA solutions

Poly (ethylene-alt-maleic acid) (PEMA) aqueous solutions were freshly prepared before nanoparticle synthesis. To make a 1% (w/v) PEMA solution, 0.04 g of PEMA was added to 4 mL of distilled water and stirred at 80°C for 8 hours. To prepare a 0.5% (w/v) solution, 1 g of PEMA was dissolved in 200 mL of distilled water under stirring at 80°C for 8 hours.

Empty PLGA-PEMA nanoparticles

Empty nanoparticles were produced via a single emulsion/solvent evaporation protocol. In brief, 2 mL of 20% (w/v) PLGA dissolved in dichloromethane (DCM) was introduced dropwise into 4 mL of 1% (w/v) PEMA aqueous solution. The combined solution underwent sonication at 100% amplitude for 30 seconds using a probe sonicator. The resulting emulsion was promptly transferred into 200 mL of 0.5% (w/v) PEMA aqueous solution under continuous stirring. This mixture was maintained under agitation in a fume hood for 12 hours to ensure complete evaporation of the organic solvent. Nanoparticles were isolated by centrifuging at 3000 × g for 5 minutes, washed three times with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and lyophilized over 3 days for long-term storage.

Der p1-encapsulated PLGA-PEMA nanoparticles

Der p1-encapsulated nanoparticles were generated using a double emulsion/solvent evaporation technique. A solution of Der p1 protein was added dropwise (100 μL) into 2 mL of 20% (w/v) PLGA in DCM and immediately subjected to sonication at 100% amplitude for 30 seconds. Then, 4 mL of 1% (w/v) PEMA solution was slowly introduced into the primary emulsion and sonicated again at 100% amplitude for 30 seconds. The resulting double emulsion was transferred into 200 mL of 0.5% (w/v) PEMA aqueous solution. This suspension was stirred in a fume hood for 12 hours to allow for evaporation of the organic phase. Nanoparticles were separated by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 5 minutes, rinsed three times with sterile PBS, lyophilized for 3 days, and then resuspended in sterile PBS to reach a concentration of 200 μg Der p1/mL. This allowed a 20 μL injection volume to deliver 4 μg Der p1 per mouse.

Der p1 conjugation to PLGA-PEMA nanoparticles

Pre-synthesized empty PLGA-PEMA nanoparticles (1.25 mg) were resuspended in 200 μL PBS and mixed thoroughly by pipetting. The suspension was washed three times by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 5 minutes to eliminate residual PEMA. Following these washes, the nanoparticles were resuspended and briefly vortexed.

For surface conjugation, 1-ethyl-3-(3′-dimethylaminopropyl)-carbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC) was dissolved in PBS to a concentration of 200 mg/mL, and 1.6 μL of this reagent was added to the nanoparticle suspension followed by 5 seconds of vortexing. The appropriate volume of Der p1 protein was introduced to attain the intended final concentration. This preparation was incubated at 25°C with stirring at 750 rpm for 1 hour, with brief 5-second vortexes every 10 minutes to promote homogenous mixing. After the conjugation procedure, nanoparticles were washed by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 5 minutes, then resuspended in sterile PBS to achieve a final level of 200 μg Der p1/mL, so that each 20 μL injection delivers 4 μg Der p1 per mouse.

Ear swelling test and rhinitis clinical scores

On day 32, an ear swelling test was conducted.6 Mice were anesthetized using inhaled isoflurane (1.5–2%), followed by intradermal injection with an insulin syringe: the right ear received 0.5 µg HDM antigen in 10 µL PBS, while the left ear received an equivalent volume of PBS. After 1 h, ear thickness was measured using a micrometer (Mitutoyo, Kanagawa, Japan), and the thickness difference between both ears was determined. On day 43, a final dose of HDM antigen was administered into the nasal cavity, after which nose rubbing and sneezing episodes were recorded for 10 min to compare symptom scores.

Nasopharyngeal lavage and histologic test15

The chest cavity of the mouse was opened, and a 24-gauge venous catheter was inserted through a tracheal incision and advanced toward the posterior naris. Following the instillation of 0.2 mL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the nasopharyngeal lavage fluid was collected into a 1.5 mL microtube and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 minutes using a Cyto-Spin centrifuge (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The cells fixed onto glass slides were stained with Diff-Quik (Sysmex Corporation, Kobe, Japan) and scanned using a digital slide scanner (Grundium Ocus®, Tampere, Finland) for enumeration of total eosinophil counts.

For histological examination of the nasal mucosa, the hypopharyngeal region, after removal of skin and muscle, was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 hours, decalcified in RDO Gold solution (Apex Engineering Products Corporation, Aurora, IL, USA) for 6 hours, and embedded in paraffin. The paraffin blocks were coronally sectioned at a thickness of 4–5 µm. Eosinophils, goblet cells, and mast cells were visualized by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), periodic acid – Schiff (PAS), and toluidine blue staining, respectively. Whole-slide images were acquired using a digital slide scanner, and four representative areas were selected at 400× magnification for cell counting and quantification.

Measurements of cytokines in splenocyte culture media15

Mouse spleens were harvested and mechanically dissociated through a 70 µm cell strainer (BD Falcon, Bedford, MA, USA) to obtain single-cell suspensions. Erythrocytes were lysed using RBC lysis buffer (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA), and the resulting suspensions were centrifuged at 350×g for 5 minutes. The cell pellets were resuspended and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere.

For cytokine measurement, 500 µL of splenocyte suspension (1 × 107 cells/mL) containing 100 µg/mL house dust mite (HDM) antigen was plated in each well of a 24-well culture plate and incubated for 72 hours. After incubation, the cultures were centrifuged at 350×g for 5 minutes, and the supernatants were collected for cytokine analysis. The concentrations of interleukin (IL)-4, IL-5, IL-10, IL-13, IL-17, and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) were quantified based on median fluorescence intensity using the Mouse Premixed Multi-Analyte Kit (R&D Systems, Inc., Minneapolis, MN, USA) with a Luminex Analyzer (Luminex Corporation, Austin, TX, USA).

Measurement of serum HDM-specific antibody

On day 44, serum samples were collected for the evaluation of antibody responses. The concentrations of total IgE, HDM-specific IgE, HDM-specific IgG1, and HDM-specific IgG2a were measured using ELISA kits (Chondrex, Inc., Woodinville, WA, USA), with the HDM-specific assays employing Der p extract as the coating antigen.

Flow cytometry15

To investigate regulatory T cells, 1000 µL of splenocyte suspension (1 × 107 cells/mL) containing 100 µg/mL HDM antigen was added to each well of a 24-well culture plate and incubated for 72 hours. Subsequently, 1 × 106 cells were harvested for further analysis. Following Fc receptor blocking with Mouse BD Fc Block™ (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), the cells were surface-stained with anti-mouse CD4 and anti-mouse CD25 antibodies (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). The surface-stained cells were then fixed and permeabilized using a Transcription Factor Buffer Set (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), followed by intracellular staining with anti-mouse Foxp3 antibody (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Finally, the stained cells were analyzed using a flow cytometer (FACS Canto™ II, Becton Dickinson, San Jose, CA, USA).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.1.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Body temperature data were analyzed using a repeated-measures ANOVA model. Differences between experimental groups were evaluated using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. A P-value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characterization of allergen-containing PLGA vaccines

To determine whether EV and CV cause anaphylaxis, rectal temperatures of mice were measured after intravenous injections. Ten minutes after intravenous injection under isoflurane anesthesia, temperature first decreased equally due to the effect of the anesthetic and then recovered equally in all groups. Notably, no significant differences in temperatures 10, 20, 40, and 60 min after intravenous injection were observed among all groups (Figure 2). Rhinitis clinical scores and ear swelling test

Number of sneezes was counted on day 43 after final HDM antigen stimulation of the nasal cavity. HDM-SCIT (21.78 ± 2.93; P < .0001), EV (12.00 ± 2.90; P < .0001), and CV (15.00 ± 2.74; P < .0001) groups exhibited significantly lower numbers of sneezes than the PC group (38.11 ± 5.95; Figure 3A). Number of nose rubs was also significantly lower in the HDM-SCIT (30.67 ± 8.44; P < .01), EV (24.78 ± 5.53; P < .0001), and CV (27.33 ± 4.32; P < .001) groups than in the PC group (50.11 ± 11.51; Figure 3B). Ear swelling test on day 32 after the intradermal injection of HDM antigen revealed significantly lesser swelling in the HDM-SCIT (0.05 ± 0.01 µm; P < .0001), EV (0.05 ± 0.01 µm; P < .0001), and CV (0.05 ± 0.02 µm; P < .0001) groups than in the PC group (0.13 ± 0.03 µm; Figure 3C). Immune cell findings in nasopharyngeal lavage samples and nasal mucosal tissues

In the nasopharyngeal lavage samples, HDM-SCIT (22.44 ± 6.84; P < .01), EV (11.78 ± 7.91; P < .0001), and CV (13.44 ± 6.65; P < .0001) groups exhibited significantly lower eosinophil counts than the PC group (52.93 ± 18.06; Figure 4A). Consistently, eosinophil infiltration in the nasal mucosal tissues, as determined via hematoxylin and eosin staining, was significantly lower in the HDM-SCIT (36.56 ± 5.75; P < .0001), EV (29.44 ± 5.31; P < .0001), and CV (32.89 ± 9.14; P < .0001) groups than in the PC group (59.89 ± 10.32; Figure 4B). Periodic acid-Schiff staining revealed significantly lower numbers of goblet cells in all immunotherapy groups (HDM-SCIT: 42.78 ± 8.20 [P < .001], EV: 44.33 ± 10.06 [P < .001], and CV: 48.78 ± 14.52 [P < .01]) than in the PC group (70.67 ± 8.44; Figure 4C). Toluidine blue staining revealed significantly higher number of mast cells in the PC group (54.44 ± 17.96) than in the HDM-SCIT (33.11 ± 4.50; P < .01), EV (31.67 ± 6.42; P < .01), and CV (31.67 ± 9.88; P < .01) groups (Figure 4D). Serum HDM-specific antibodies

HDM-SCIT group (16974.00 ± 1905.84 ng/mL) showed significantly higher serum levels of total IgE than the PC (6937.00 ± 1810.55 ng/mL; P < .0001), EV (8059.00 ± 2068.92 ng/mL; P < .0001), and CV (8494.00 ± 981.29 ng/mL; P < .0001) groups (Figure 5A). Moreover, HDM-specific IgE levels were significantly higher in the HDM-SCIT (370.50 ± 46.25 ng/mL; P < .0001) and CV (283.10 ± 96.29 ng/mL; P < .01) groups than in the PC group (135.80 ± 26.36 ng/mL). However, no difference in HDM-specific IgE levels was observed between the EV and PC groups (Figure 5B). HDM-specific IgG1 levels were higher in the HDM-SCIT (41917.00 ± 5917.16 ng/mL; P < .01), EV (42342.00 ± 3958.20 ng/mL; P < .01), and CV (41272.00 ± 13259.61 ng/mL; P < .01) groups than in the PC group (20827.00 ± 5164.54 ng/mL; Figure 5C). HDM-specific IgG2a levels were higher in all immunotherapy groups than in the PC group; however, the difference was not statistically significant (Figure 5D). Cytokine secretion by splenocytes

Supernatants of splenocyte cultures were analyzed 72 h after HDM antigen stimulation. Among all Th2 cytokines, interleukin (IL)-4, IL-5, and IL-13 levels were compared in all groups. IL-4 levels were significantly lower in the HDM-SCIT (95.94 ± 8.98; P < .001), EV (104.90 ± 2059; P < .001), and CV (124.60 ± 58.15; P < .05) groups than in the PC group (237.90 ± 50.46; Figure 6A). Notably, IL-5 levels were significantly reduced only in the EV group compared to those in the PC group (1316.00 ± 218.94 vs. 815.30 ± 91.81; P < .05; Figure 6B). IL-13 levels were also significantly lower in the EV group (2995.00 ± 624.79; P < .05) than in the PC group (5852.00 ± 1890.08; Figure 6C). Levels of IL-10, which is involved in anti-inflammatory activity, were significantly higher in the HDM-SCIT (1454.00 ± 95.03; P < .0001), EV (1028.00 ± 237.19; P < .001), and CV (975.80 ± 91.41; P < .01) groups than in the PC group (503.50 ± 122.29; Figure 6D). IL-17 levels were significantly lower in all immunotherapy groups (HDM-SCIT: 67.46 ± 35.27 [P < .01], EV: 66.88 ± 9.69 [P < .01], and CV: 87.14 ± 15.01 [P < .01]) than in the PC group (260.00 ± 94.00; Figure 6E). Levels of interferon (IFN)-γ, a Th1 type cytokine, were significantly higher in the HDM-SCIT (487.60 ± 85.70; P < .001), EV (353.10 ± 111.45; P < .05), and CV (432.00 ± 117.67; P < .001) groups than in the PC group (141.60 ± 31.16; Figure 6F). Discussion

This study underscores the potential utility of antigen-associated nanoparticles, which have been extensively explored for novel therapies targeting autoimmune and allergic diseases,14 as a safe and effective strategy for delivering substantial amounts of Der p1 into the human body. In the groups treated with the recombinant Der p1 allergen for immunotherapy, a reduction in Th2 cytokines and increases in HDM-specific IgG1 and regulatory T cells were observed. Therefore, the current research demonstrated the therapeutic efficacy of a novel immunotherapy approach utilizing recombinant Der p1 allergen in a murine allergic rhinitis model. In this context, we examined whether immunotherapy incorporating recombinant Der p 1-loaded PLGA may limit an initial elevation in total IgE levels during the early phase of immunotherapy, attenuate allergic rhinitis inflammation, and induce allergen-specific immune tolerance in a mouse model of HDM-induced allergic rhinitis. Additionally, we sought to determine whether intravenous administration of biodegradable allergen-conjugated PLGA nanoparticles could serve as a safe and efficient new immunotherapeutic modality.

In a German birth cohort study, more than 50% of individuals sensitized to Der p exhibited positive IgE responses exclusively to Der p1 and Der p2, out of 12 Der p allergenic molecules, by the age of 20.16 Previous research has shown that over 80% of patients with HDM allergies have elevated serum anti-Der p1 IgE titers,17,18 indicating that Der p1 is an optimal target for developing novel epitope vaccines for HDM allergy immunotherapy. A study utilizing only purified natural group 1 and 2 Der p allergens demonstrated that SCIT was effective in reducing airway hyperresponsiveness and suppressing allergic inflammation in a mouse model of allergic asthma.10 Based on these findings, we selected both the traditionally used crude extract and the recombinant Der p1 antigen for direct comparison of HDM immunotherapy efficacy in this study. In both the HDM – SCIT group, which received immunotherapy with the crude extract, and in the EV and CV groups, which received immunotherapy with the recombinant Der p1 allergen, reductions were observed in Th2 cytokines, IL-17, and other inflammatory cytokines, as well as in allergic inflammation within the nasal mucosa. Conversely, increases were noted in IL-10, IFN-γ, HDM-specific IgG1, and Treg cell counts. These findings confirm that immunotherapy utilizing recombinant Der p1 achieves efficacy comparable to that of conventional immunotherapy.

Another significant observation of this study is that, in contrast to the HDM – SCIT group, immunotherapy using only recombinant Der p1 did not result in an initial elevation in total IgE levels, which is commonly noted during the early stages of immunotherapy. These results suggest that employing recombinant Der p1 alone for immunotherapy may reduce the likelihood of new sensitization, a risk often associated with immunotherapy using crude extracts. This finding is consistent with the increasing emphasis on purified or recombinant allergens to prevent new sensitization that may arise during AIT with crude extracts, as previously reported.8 Consequently, current research investigating purified or recombinant allergens9,10 is aligned with the context and purpose of our study. In addition, the use of purified or recombinant allergens in immunotherapy enables the targeting of specific allergens relevant to an individual’s sensitization profile, which facilitates a more personalized therapeutic strategy. Moreover, administration of this hypoallergenic combination vaccine restricted IgE and T cell reactivity while preserving the therapeutic efficacy seen with conventional immunotherapy, and resulted in fewer adverse effects such as anaphylaxis.11 Therefore, as illustrated in this study, immunotherapy using purified or recombinant hypoallergenic allergens represents a promising approach. Nevertheless, additional investigations are necessary to refine the administration protocol – including optimal delivery systems, precise selection of allergenic components, and the possibility of adjuvant co-administration – as well as to confirm its safety and efficacy.19,20

PLGA, the nanoparticle used for allergen delivery, is biodegradable and has been shown to be safe for use in immunotherapy in animal studies.13,19 The mechanism of action of nanoparticles involves their uptake by antigen-presenting cells (APCs), resulting in the activation of CD4+ T cells. This activation then shifts the immune response from a Th2-dominant profile toward a Th1 or regulatory T cell (Treg)-mediated response. This immunologic shift is marked by increased levels of Th1 and Treg cytokines, along with reduced Th2 cytokines, which ultimately suppresses IgE and eosinophil activation in allergic reactions and enhances allergen-specific immune tolerance.21 The encapsulated-PLGA preparation demonstrated greater stability compared to the conjugated-PLGA formulation and was effective in suppressing Th2 responses without causing adverse reactions such as anaphylaxis.14 The differing immune outcomes between the EV and CV groups appear to result from the distinct Der p 1 conjugation methods. Encapsulated Der p 1 is shielded within the PLG matrix, preventing premature allergen exposure and allowing efficient splenic processing, whereas surface-conjugated Der p 1 remains externally exposed. This early exposure likely led to the increased HDM-specific IgE observed in the conjugated group. In contrast, encapsulated allergens more effectively limited Th2 responses and promoted tolerance, while conjugated forms showed only partial suppression. These findings underscore the importance of antigen presentation and conjugation strategy in shaping immunoregulatory mechanisms during immunotherapy. Nanoparticles like PLGA allow the delivery of higher allergen concentrations and provide a prolonged immune response due to their slow-release characteristics. Sustained antigen exposure contributes to long-term immunomodulatory effects.13,14 Building on these findings, this study utilized a PLGA-based delivery platform containing both encapsulated and conjugated forms. Although the outcomes for EV and CV converged to a similar tolerogenic immune profile in this study, additional investigations will be necessary to clarify potential kinetic differences between the two formulations. Future longitudinal studies with serial sampling at multiple time points after injection will help delineate how each formulation shapes modulates the immune response over time.

In addition, whereas HDM-SCIT was administered subcutaneously three times over five days with two-day intervals, a single intravenous administration of Der p1-loaded PLGA nanoparticles produced a comparable immune response with effective suppression of Th2 cytokines. These outcomes indicate that the use of encapsulated and conjugated allergens supports extended immune activation by enabling controlled and sustained release of allergenic proteins. While previous studies have shown that encapsulated formulations allow the gradual release of allergen over five days in animal models,14 further investigation is necessary to determine optimal dosing intervals in human subjects. Nonetheless, this strategy may help to mitigate one of the main limitations of immunotherapy – frequent injections – which is widely recognized as the principal cause of discontinuation.3 Therefore, this approach could enhance access to treatment, particularly for pediatric patients apprehensive about repeated injections.

This study has several notable strengths. First, we employed intravenous administration of allergen-loaded nanoparticles, a route that enables rapid engagement of tolerogenic antigen-presenting cells and likely contributed to the robust IL-10 and Treg induction observed. Second, the use of a single recombinant major allergen (Der p 1) showed similar therapeutic effects to SCIT using crude extracts, while maintaining stable total and HDM-specific IgE levels. Third, by evaluating both EV and CV nanoparticle platforms in parallel, we demonstrated that distinct antigen-presentation strategies can nevertheless converge to potent suppression of Th2/Th17 inflammation and strong enhancement of regulatory responses. However, certain limitations must be addressed before clinical application, such as determining the optimal nanoparticle size for allergen loading, establishing the necessary antigen concentration to elicit immune responses in humans, identifying the minimum effective dosing interval, and safety within the human body. In addition, PLGA is a safety, biodegradable polymer approved by both the FDA and European regulatory agencies, and its primary role in this platform is to deliver allergenic molecules to the immune system. However, unlike SCIT, which relies on subcutaneous administration, these nanoparticles are administered intravenously, and the mechanistic implications of this alternative delivery route require further investigation. Moreover, there is still limited evidence regarding the exact duration required for PLGA’s complete degradation in vivo.20 These considerations highlight the need for continued research in this area.

Conclusion

Despite these challenges, this strategy holds promise to preserve immunotherapeutic efficacy while reducing the early adverse reactions and the inconveniences linked to immunotherapy, indicating its potential for future clinical implementation.

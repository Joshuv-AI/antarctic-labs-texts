YTHDF3-associated m6A regulation and cuproptosis-related gene expression in steroid-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head
Authors: Yong Cui, Aikeremujiang Alken, Wu Wang, Tao Huang, Zhongwei Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to explore the role of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA modification in regulating Cuproptosis in steroid-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head (SONFH), providing insights into its underlying mechanisms and therapeutic targets. Gene expression profiles from both human (GSE123568; 30 SONFH patients and 10 controls) and rat femoral head samples (GSE26316; 3 SONFH and 3 controls) were analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and enriched pathways. In a matched case-control study, femoral head tissues from SONFH patients and non-SONFH controls were collected. The expression of YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA binding protein 3 (YTHDF3) and dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase (DLD) was measured using qRT-PCR and Western blotting. Functional validation was performed in human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) via siRNA-mediated knockdown of YTHDF3 and treatment with elesclomol, a cuproptosis inducer. In the GSE26316 and GSE123568 datasets, we identified 708 common DEGs. Cuproptosis were activated in SONFH. The expression levels of YTHDF3 and DLD were elevated in SONFH tissues. Knockdown of YTHDF3 reduced the DLD expression and mitigated the inhibitory effects of elesclomol on BMSC proliferation and osteogenesis. YTHDF3 may contribute to SONFH progression by regulating DLD expression and cuproptosis, offering a potential important molecular target for novel therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10735-025-10544-x.Keywords: Steroid-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head, Cuproptosis, m6A modification, YTHDF3, DLD

Background

Osteonecrosis, also known as avascular necrosis, is a condition characterized by the death of bone tissue due to insufficient blood supply. This condition can affect any bone, but is particularly common in the hip, knee, and shoulder joints (Pavelka and Osteonecrosis 2000). Steroid-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head (SONFH) is caused by long-term or high-dose use of glucocorticoids, characterized by blood flow disturbances in the femoral head leading to the death of bone cells and collapse of bone tissue. Epidemiological data show that SONFH primarily affects middle-aged and young adults, especially those with autoimmune diseases, inflammatory diseases, or organ transplants who use glucocorticoids long-term (Chen et al. 2020; Huang et al. 2021). Genetic factors also play a role in the development of SONFH, with studies like those by Lee et al. (2022) revealing multiple gene mutations associated with SONFH through systematic reviews and meta-analyses, offering possibilities for risk assessment and the development of personalized treatment strategies.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most common modification on eukaryotic mRNA and is involved in various biological processes such as RNA metabolism, post-transcriptional modifications, and translation regulation (Yang et al. 2020). Abnormal regulation of m6A modification is closely associated with various disease states, including cancer (Wei et al. 2023), neurodegenerative diseases (Zhang et al. 2022), cardiovascular diseases (Zhang et al. 2023), and inflammatory responses (Luo et al. 2021). In research on metabolic bone diseases, m6A modification is particularly important; its regulatory role in bone development and osteogenesis is closely related to the pathogenesis of diseases such as osteoporosis and osteosarcoma (Gu et al. 2024).

Cuproptosis is a recently discovered form of cell death closely related to the metabolic activities of copper ions within cells, especially the direct interaction between copper ions and specific proteins inside cells, leading to protein aggregation and inactivation, thereby inducing cell death (Jiang et al. 2022; Springer et al. 2024). Cuproptosis has been known to play a role in various metabolic bone diseases (Li et al. 2023a, b). YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA binding protein 3 (YTHDF3) is a key reader protein that recognizes and binds to m6A-modified RNA(Zheng et al. 2025). Recent studies have shown that YTHDF3 influences BMSC proliferation and osteogenic differentiation (Feng et al. 2024). Dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase (DLD) is a mitochondrial enzyme and plays a pivotal role in mitochondrial energy metabolism and redox homeostasis (Lv et al. 2024). In bone metabolism, DLD activity is essential for osteoblast energy supply and mitochondrial function, which are crucial for osteogenesis and bone remodeling (Li et al. 2023a, b).

Given that SONFH is a complex pathological process involving multiple cell types and molecular pathways, conducting further research to deeply understand the roles of m6A modification and cuproptosis in SONFH is particularly important, exploring their potential as therapeutic targets.

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Gene expression data of GSE26316 and GSE123568 datasets were collected from the GEO database. GSE26316 including gene expression data of femoral heads from three rats with SONFH and three control rats. GSE123568 contained gene expression data of peripheral blood samples from 30 SONFH patients and 10 non-SONFH patients. The raw data were background corrected and normalized using the RMA algorithm. Differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma package, defining a significance threshold of p-value less than 0.05 and |logFC| >1, thereby identifying significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between SONFH and controls. DEGs were analyzed for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways using the clusterProfiler package. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) method was used to assess changes in the activity of programmed cell death in different samples. Programmed cell death-related markers as shown in Table S1. The importance of genes in diagnosing SONFH was analyzed using random forest analysis. Pearson correlation was used for correlation analysis.

Clinical sample collection

A matched case-control study was conducted, where femoral head samples from 10 SONFH patients treated at the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University were randomly collected from January to December 2019. Concurrently, femoral head samples from patients undergoing total hip replacement due to femoral neck fractures were collected as controls. Inclusion criteria for SONFH patients: (Pavelka and Osteonecrosis 2000) aged between 18 and 65; (Chen et al. 2020) diagnosed with SONFH based on clinical and imaging examinations. Exclusion criteria: (Pavelka and Osteonecrosis 2000) patients with severe heart, liver, kidney diseases, or other significant chronic diseases; (Chen et al. 2020) patients who had major surgery or severe trauma in the past six months; (Huang et al. 2021) pregnant or lactating women; (Lee et al. 2022) unclear osteonecrosis lesions or bone lesions caused by other reasons. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (XJDWFYLS-2019-06) and was based on the Helsinki Declaration on Ethical Principles for Medical Research Involving Human Subjects. All patients signed an informed consent form.

Cell culture, transfection, and cell proliferation assay

Human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) were obtained from Bioworld (Shanghai, China) and cultured in α-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C and 5% CO2. For gene knockdown experiments, siRNAs targeting YTHDF3 and a negative control (NC) siRNA (Gemma, Shanghai, China) were transfected into BMSCs using Lipofectamine® 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, MA, USA), according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Additionally, to construct the cuproptosis model, 5 × 106 BMSCs were treated with elesclomol (Selleck Chemicals, China; 200 nM).

BMSCs (2 × 103/well) were seeded into a 96-well plate and cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h. Then, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the culture was continued for another 4 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

qRT-PCR

Femoral head tissues were ground in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA from tissues and BMSCs was extracted using Trizol (Invitrogen, USA). Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT kit (Takara, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. PCR was then carried out using the SYBR Green PCR kit (Invitrogen, USA). β-actin was used as the internal reference gene, and relative mRNA levels of genes were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Specific primer sequences are listed in Table S2.

Western blot

Proteins from femoral head tissues and BMSCs were extracted using RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Equal amounts of protein samples and molecular weight markers were loaded onto polyacrylamide gels for electrophoresis. Proteins were then transferred to a PVDF membrane. The transferred membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h. The membrane was incubated overnight at 4 °C with blocking solution containing primary antibodies against YTHDF3 (Abclonal, A8395; 1:1200), DLD (Abclonal, A5220; 1:1200), and β-actin (Abclonal, AC026; 1:1200), followed by washing with TBST three times. The membrane was then incubated with corresponding HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Abclonal, AS014; 1:5000) at room temperature for 1 h, followed by three washes with TBST. Protein bands were visualized using ECL reagent under a Western blot imaging system. β-actin was used as the loading control, and densitometric analysis was performed using ImageJ software.

Osteogenic and adipogenic assay

For alizarin red S staining, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for one hour, and samples were stained in 2% alizarin red solution for 20 min. Samples were dehydrated sequentially in 70%, 95%, and 100% ethanol series, each step lasting 5 min. Slides were then mounted using a coverslip.

For oil red O staining, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and samples were treated with 60% isopropanol for 5 min. Samples were stained in oil red O working solution for 10 min. They were washed briefly with 60% isopropanol, followed by a gentle water rinse. Slides were mounted with a coverslip. The cells were observed using a microscope (Leica, Germany).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using R software and GraphPad. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Changes in gene expression and signaling pathways in SONFH

In the GSE26316 and GSE123568 datasets, differential analyses between SONFH and controls identified 1283 DEGs (Fig. 1A) and 7453 DEGs (Fig. 1B), respectively. Through intersection analysis, we identified 708 common DEGs (Fig. 1C).

(B) Volcano plot of differentially expressed genes in GSE123568 dataset. (C) Screening common differentially expressed genes between GSE26316 and GSE123568 datasets through intersection analysis. (D) Enrichment analysis for common differentially expressed genes in SONFH. (E) GSVA evaluated the changes of signaling pathways in SONFH

Enrichment analysis of these common DEGs revealed significant enrichment in pathways such as ferroptosis, protein export, and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway (Fig. 1D). Additionally, GSVA results revealed that apoptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, cuproptosis, entotic cell death, lysosome-dependent cell death, and alkaliptosis were significantly activated in SONFH, while netotic cell death and oxxiptosis pathways were significantly suppressed (Fig. 1E).

Correlation between m6A and cuproptosis

To identify the expression of m6A-related genes in SONFH, differential analyses were performed between the SONFH and control group. Compared to the control group, the SONFH group had higher expression levels of ALKBH5, CBLL1, FMR1, HNRNPA2B1, KIAA1429, METTL3, WTAP, YTHDC2, YTHDF1, YTHDF2, and YTHDF3, and lower levels of IGF2BP2 and IGF2BP3 (Fig. 2A, B).

Expression of m6A-related genes in SONFH. (A) Heatmap of m6A-related gene expression. (B) Box plots of m6A-related gene expression. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01

Random forest analysis revealed that among m6A-related genes, YTHDF3 showed the highest importance in relation to SONFN, followed by YTHDF2, IGF2BP2, and others (Fig. 3A). Correlation analysis between different cell death pathways and m6A-related genes indicated strong associations of FMR1, WTAP, METTL14, METTL3, YTHDF3, etc., with cuproptosis (Fig. 3B). The analysis of correlations between m6A-related genes and cuproptosis-related genes showed that the strongest association was between DLD and YTHDF3, followed by ATP7A, MTF, etc. (Fig. 3C). Differential analysis showed that in SONFN patients, levels of ATP7A, DLD, GLS, LIPT1, MTF1 were higher than controls, while FDX1 was lower (Fig. 3D).

Expression of YTHDF3 and DLD

Results of qRT-PCR showed that the mRNA levels of YTHDF3 and DLD were higher in the SONFH patients compared to the controls (Fig. 4A). Western-blot results indicated that protein levels of YTHDF3 and DLD were also higher in the SONFH patients than that in controls (Fig. 4B).

Differences in YTHDF3 and DLD expression levels between SONFH and controls. (A) YTHDF3 and DLD mRNA levels detected by qRT-PCR. (B) YTHDF3 and DLD protein expression detected by Western-blot. ***P < 0.001

Additionally, qRT-PCR (Figure S1A) and Western-blot (Figure S1B) results showed that the knockdown efficiency of YTHDF3-Homo-1738 was the highest, and it was used for subsequent experiments. CCK-8 assays found that compared to the BMSCs group, cell proliferation was significantly reduced in the BMSCs + elesclomol group; compared to the BMSCs + elesclomol group, cell proliferation was significantly increased in the BMSC + elesclomol + si-YTHDF3 group (Figure S2).

Then, qRT-PCR analysis revealed that treated with elesclomol significantly increased mRNA levels of YTHDF3 and DLD in BMSCs, while transfection with si-YTHDF3 significantly decreased mRNA levels of YTHDF3 and DLD (Fig. 5A). Western-blot results (Fig. 5B) also showed that compared to the BMSCs group, YTHDF3 and DLD levels were significantly increased in both the BMSCs + elesclomol group and the BMSCs + elesclomol + YTHDF3 NC group. Compared to the BMSCs + elesclomol group, the expression of YTHDF3 and DLD was significantly reduced in the BMSCs + elesclomol + si-YTHDF3 group.

Differences in expression levels of YTHDF3 and DLD in BMSCs. (A) YTHDF3 and DLD mRNA levels detected by qRT-PCR. (B) YTHDF3 and DLD protein expression detected by Western-blot. Compared to BMSCs group, ***P < 0.001; compared to BMSCs + elesclomol group, ###P < 0.001

Alizarin red and oil red O staining

The results of the alizarin red staining (Fig. 6A) showed that the BMSCs group had almost no osteogenic formation. After treatment with elesclomol combined with si-YTHDF3, alizarin red staining was significantly increased than BMSCs + elesclomol group, suggesting that si-YTHDF3 significantly promotes osteogenic differentiation. The oil red O staining showed that the BMSCs group had almost no adipogenic formation. After induction with elesclomol, there was an increase in oil red O staining in the field of view, indicating significant adipogenic formation. After treatment with elesclomol combined with si-YTHDF3, there was a relative decrease in oil red O staining, suggesting that si-YTHDF3 significantly inhibits adipogenic differentiation (Fig. 6B).

Discussion

SONFH is a severe bone disease that significantly impacts patients’ quality of life. Treatment for SONFH may involve long-term medical interventions and costly surgical procedures, such as joint replacements, which further exacerbate the economic burden and reduce the quality of life for patients (Li et al. 2023a, b). Therefore, in-depth research on SONFH is of significant scientific and clinical importance.

This research identified 708 common DEGs by conducting differential analysis between SONFH and controls in datasets GSE26316 and GSE123568. Further investigations revealed that in SONFH, most cell death pathways were significantly activated, these point to biological processes potentially involved in SONFH, providing valuable molecular targets for future therapeutic strategies for SONFH. Zhao et al. (2022) identified biomarkers for SONFH closely associated with immune cell infiltration using bioinformatics methods. Liang et al. (Liang et al. 2022) further revealed the role of autophagy in SONFH through bioinformatics analysis. Chai et al. (2023) evaluated the role of Pyroptosis in the pathological process of SONFH by screening and validating potential inflammatory-related genes for SONFH diagnosis. Additionally, Qi et al. (2023) revealed abnormalities in immune cell infiltration in SONFH.

Recent studies have found that cell death pathways may be closely related to the pathogenesis of SONFH. For example, Cai et al. (2023) found that erythropoietin could reduce osteoblast apoptosis in a rat model of SANFH by inhibiting the STAT1-caspase 3 signaling pathway. Furthermore, Xiao et al. (2023) found that a reduction in 4-hydroxynonenal could be a key biomarker in the progression of SONFH. Sun et al. (2023) found that Tanshinone I alleviated SONFH by promoting angiogenesis and regulating SOX11 expression. Liu et al. (2023) identified key genes related to Ferroptosis as potential therapeutic targets for SONFH using machine learning algorithms. These studies further emphasize the importance of considering various cell death pathways in the pathological process of SONFH and provide directions for finding new methods to treat SONFH.

Through the validation of clinical and cellular experiments, we discovered that the expression levels of YTHDF3 and DLD were significantly altered in SONFH. Given its role in regulating mRNA stability and translation, YTHDF3 may influence osteogenic differentiation through m6A-dependent post-transcriptional mechanisms (Chen et al. 2022). Dysfunction of DLD could lead to metabolic disorders, potentially affecting the survival and differentiation of bone cells, a major characteristic in SONFH where the primary manifestation is the destruction of bone tissue (Yang et al. 2023; Yan and Wang 2023). Knockdown of YTHDF3 led to decreased DLD expression, suggesting a potential regulatory axis between m6A signaling and cuproptosis. Our data suggest that under cuproptosis stress, YTHDF3 facilitates DLD expression, thereby modulating BMSC fate decisions.

The Alizarin Red staining results demonstrated that elesclomol significantly suppressed osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs, while knockdown of YTHDF3 restored mineralization capacity. This suggests that YTHDF3 plays a regulatory role in bone matrix formation under cuproptosis-inducing conditions. On the other hand, Oil Red O staining revealed increased lipid accumulation following elesclomol treatment, indicative of enhanced adipogenic differentiation. YTHDF3 knockdown reversed this trend, highlighting its potential involvement in lineage commitment of mesenchymal stem cells. These findings suggest the notion that YTHDF3 may promote adipogenesis at the expense of osteogenesis, contributing to the pathophysiological imbalance seen in SONFH.

In SONFH, changes in the microenvironment due to insufficient blood flow may promote the abnormal accumulation of copper ions, thereby triggering cuproptosis, which affects the survival and function of bone cells (Huang et al. 2021). Regulation of cuproptosis not only affects the balance of copper within cells but may also influence bone health by activating or inhibiting specific signaling pathways, such as antioxidant pathways and apoptosis pathways(Xiang et al. 2025). Additionally, the use of copper chelators or copper modulators to control copper levels may help alleviate cellular damage caused by excess copper, thereby protecting bone tissue from further degradation (Niu and Xu 2021). The study of the cuproptosis pathway offers us a new perspective to understand the potential mechanisms of cell damage and death occurring in SONFH.

This study has some limitations. First, despite the observed upregulation of DLD and increased GSVA scores for cuproptosis, the current study did not validate essential cuproptosis regulators such as FDX1, DLAT, or CDKN2A. Therefore, while our findings suggest a possible involvement of cuproptosis-related mechanisms, we cannot definitively confirm its occurrence. Second, GSVA revealed concurrent activation of various cell death pathways, including apoptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis, indicating that SONFH may involve multiple overlapping cell death mechanisms. This complexity limits the ability to prioritize cuproptosis as a predominant pathway in the disease process. Future studies should include direct assessments of specific cuproptosis markers and compare them to other cell death forms to clarify their respective contributions. Moreover, the conclusions drawn from the current study are based primarily on transcriptomic analysis and in vitro assays using BMSCs; thus, the physiological relevance in vivo remains to be verified. Finally, although YTHDF3 knockdown was shown to reduce DLD expression, it remains unclear whether this regulation is mediated through direct m6A modification of DLD mRNA. Further studies involving MeRIP-seq or dual-luciferase reporter assays are necessary to clarify this mechanistic relationship.

Conclusion

This study explored the potential role of YTHDF3 in SONFH and provided a scientific basis for further investigating its potential as a therapeutic target. A deeper investigation into m6A modification and cuproptosis mechanisms not only enhances our understanding of the pathogenesis of SONFH but may also reveal new therapeutic targets, offering new directions for developing more effective treatment strategies.

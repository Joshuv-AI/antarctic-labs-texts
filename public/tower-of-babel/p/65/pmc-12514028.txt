Exergy and energy analysis for dimple tube solar water heater using deionized water zinc oxide based nanofluid
Authors: M. Arun, Debabrata Barik, Kapura Tudu, Seepana Praveenkumar, A. Saravanan, Dana Mohammad Khidhir, Syed Waheedullah Ghori, T. M. Yunus Khan, Manzoore Elahi M. Soudagar, Nasim Hasan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study examines the thermodynamics of a solar water heating (SWH) system utilizing a parabolic trough with an enhanced zinc oxide (EZnO)-based nanofluid (NF). The examination includes energy and exergy studies. The research examines the thermal performance of systems with plain and dimpled absorber tubes at flow rates ranging from 1.5 to 3.5 kg/min. This research comprehensively examines how dimple-induced flow interruption and nanoparticle-enhanced thermal conductivity enhance heat transfer and mitigate entropy formation. The trials show that dimpled tubes outperform ordinary tubes in thermal and exergy efficiency. Most likely owing to greater surface area and enhanced turbulent fluid circulation. EZnO-NF enhances thermal conductivity and reduces viscosity, thereby improving system performance and the convective heat transfer coefficient. A maximal exergy production of 496 W and a thermal efficiency of 98.2% yielded 480 W of energy gain and 93.7% of thermal energy at 2.5 kg/min. The thermal efficiency of the system was good. Thermal power generation was 530 W, and the entropy was substantially lower than that of the baseline (deionized water with a simple tube). According to the results, structured absorber geometries and EZnO nanofluids work synergistically to enhance the thermodynamic efficiency of future solar thermal systems, indicating a route to success.

Result and discussion

This experiment tests zinc oxide-based NF in a parabolic trough SWH system with plain and dimple tubes. Energy and exergy studies were utilized to compare the two systems. Zinc oxide-based NFs boost thermal efficiency and dimple tube collector exergy destruction. Instead of plain tubes, dimpled tubes enhance system heat efficiency. These studies suggest many advantages of using the EZNO-NF in SWH systems. By accurately separating the acquired thermal signals into their dominant mode components, the MSVMD approach allowed for the separation of useful thermal response patterns from background noise and non-stationary fluctuations. Temperature increase, entropy change, and heat flux distribution throughout the absorber tube were all identified as better trends as a result of the improved signal clarity. A more reliable categorization of thermal performance stages under varying fluid conditions and flow rates was achieved by including these clean properties into the SVM-DSET model. To improve model generalization and reduce overfitting, the DSET version differed from typical SVM models by training on datasets with higher informational value. The dimple tube containing EZnO-NF outperformed the plain tube containing water at the optimal flow rate of 2.5 kg/min, with a thermal output of 530 W and a thermal efficiency of 98.2%. This significant enhancement is due to the combined effects of dimple-induced secondary flows, which accelerate convective heat transfer by disrupting the boundary layer and promoting mixing, and nanoparticle-enhanced thermal conductivity, measured at 0.9 W/m·K for EZnO-NF. Conclusion

This investigation performed a detailed energy and exergy analysis of a parabolic trough SWH system incorporating EZnO-NF and evaluated the performance of plain versus dimpled absorber tubes under varying flow conditions. The experimental findings establish that the dimple tube configuration significantly outperforms the plain tube in terms of thermal efficiency, exergy efficiency, and heat transfer enhancement. These gains are attributed to the combined effects of enhanced surface-induced turbulence from the dimples and the superior thermal properties of the nanofluid. Implications

One practical approach to improving the thermal efficiency of solar collectors is to combine designed surface shapes, such as dimples, with nanofluids. This approach is particularly relevant for high-efficiency water heating in residential, commercial, and industrial solar thermal installations. Moreover, the enhanced exergy efficiency suggests potential for hybrid thermal–electrical solar energy systems, where waste heat can be effectively recovered and utilized.

Limitations

Despite the promising results, several limitations must be acknowledged. The long-term stability, dispersion, and potential agglomeration of EZnO nanoparticles under cyclic thermal loading conditions remain unaddressed. Moreover, pressure drop penalties due to dimple-induced turbulence and nanoparticle interactions were not quantified in this study, and these factors could influence system design considerations. Additionally, the scope of the work was limited to steady-state, controlled laboratory conditions, which may not fully capture the dynamic variability of outdoor environments.

Further scope

To examine how factors affect the performance of a parabolic trough SWH system utilizing EZNO-NF, experiments that compare the energy and exergy of a plain tube and a dimpled tube may be extended. These factors include NF intake temperature, mass flow rate, parabolic trough tilt angle, absorber plate surface area, tube length, and others. This research might develop if many NFs and environmental characteristics like temperature and wind speed are included. In addition, complex numerical simulations may be used to evaluate the proposed EZNO-NF and improve its design.

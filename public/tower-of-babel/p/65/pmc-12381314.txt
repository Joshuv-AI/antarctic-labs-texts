Neoadjuvant therapy demonstrates both safety and clinical feasibility for renal cell carcinoma patients with tumor thrombus
Authors: Le Yu, Kewei Chen, Liyuan Ge, Fan Zhang, Shaohui Deng, Jianfei Ye, Ye Yan, Guoliang Wang, Shudong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile the applications of neoadjuvant therapies on patients diagnosed with renal cell carcinoma (RCC) with tumor thrombus (TT) is growing, the safety of preoperative therapies has yet to be clarified.Methods451 patients diagnosed with RCC with TT undergoing radical nephrectomy and thrombectomy were included. Propensity score-matched cohorts were used to investigate the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapies. We compared perioperative parameters and postoperative complications. Postoperative complications were assessed within 30 days of the operation and were graded using the Clavien-Dindo grading system. A Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier curves were used to assess the impact of neoadjuvant therapies on patients’ overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS).ResultsAfter 1:3 ratio propensity-score matching (PSM), 56 patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy and 153 patients receiving non-neoadjuvant therapy were included in the analysis. No significant difference was found between the two groups after PSM in terms of baseline information (P > 0.05). The rate of blood transfusion (71.4% vs. 52.3%, P = 0.01) was higher in neoadjuvant therapy. 8 patients (14.3%) in neoadjuvant therapy group experienced serious complications while 15 patients (14.9%) in non-neoadjuvant therapy group experienced serious complications. The perioperative parameters and postoperative complications did not show significant differences in preoperative-combined therapy group and non-preoperative-combined therapy group. No significant differences were found in OS or CSS between the neoadjuvant therapy group and non-neoadjuvant therapy group.ConclusionsNeoadjuvant therapies can be safely administered to RCC patients with TT. Neoadjuvant therapies recipients’ OS or CSS did not differ significantly from those who did not receive the treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03489-w.Keywords: Carcinoma, renal cell; Neoadjuvant therapy; Molecular targeted therapy; Immune checkpoint inhibitors; Risk management

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is one of the most common cancers in urology, accounting for approximately 3% of all cancers [1]. 4–10% of newly diagnosed RCC patients have tumor thrombus (TT) [2], indicating a worse prognosis. While patients undergoing radical nephrectomy and thrombectomy (RNAT) have a cancer-specific survival (CSS) of 40–60% at five years, untreated patients have a median survival of five months [3, 4]. However, operative management of RNAT, especially in patients with high-level TT, continues to pose challenges [5]. Intraoperative hemorrhage and TT-induced pulmonary embolism are two major concerns during thrombectomy, particularly when TT invades the right atrium [6]. In patients with advanced RCC, immune-checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have excellent therapeutic effects, and they show potential in reducing tumor volume and lowering inferior vena cava (IVC) thrombus levels to ease surgical difficulty [7]. Preoperative-targeted therapy can shorten TT and induce fibrosis, prompting further thrombectomy [8]. According to the first prospective study, NAXIVA [9], systemic therapy can effectively downstage all levels of venous tumor thrombus (VTT) and reduce the scope of surgery in patients with resectable clear cell RCC (ccRCC). The phase 2 study NEOTAX [10] preliminarily indicated that Toripalimab in combination with Axitinib downstages IVC-TT in a significant proportion of patients and the safety was verified. According to European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) guideline, there is no established role for neoadjuvant therapies [11]. Some have raised concerns that the applications of neoadjuvant therapy may significantly increase the prevalence and severity of intraoperative adhesions and delayed the wound healing [12–16]. There is no study focusing on the safety of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT. Therefore, based primarily on the perioperative parameters and postoperative complications, this study conducted a propensity-matched retrospective cohort analysis to assess the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT.

Methods

Study setting and study design

We conducted a retrospective review of the patients diagnosed with RCC with TT receiving RNAT in our medical center between January 2014 and July 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) postoperative pathology confirmed RCC with TT; (2) preoperative lab tests and cross-sectional imaging available.

The neoadjuvant therapy group was defined as patients who received only neoadjuvant therapy preoperatively. The preoperative-targeted therapy group was defined as patients who received only TKIs preoperatively. The preoperative-combined therapy group was defined as patients who received both TKIs and ICIs preoperatively. Non-neoadjuvant therapy group refers to the group of patients who did not use neoadjuvant therapy prior of surgery. For those with preoperative imaging suggesting distant metastasis, we performed adjuvant therapy after surgery. For patients without distant metastasis, we only performed surgery.

To determine the tumor volume and TT level, all patients received enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The TT level was categorized using the Mayo Clinic Classification [17]. Prior to surgery, imaging was also used to detect distant metastases, possible lymph node metastases, and adrenal metastases, which served as the foundation for the clinical TNM staging. The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status classification system was used to evaluate the patient’s surgical tolerance. The expert pathologist made a pathological diagnosis for each specimen. To evaluate the renal function of the patients, serum creatinine (Scr) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were used. Complications and hospital stays following surgery were recorded in the postoperative data. Within 30 days of the procedure, we assessed the postoperative complications and graded them using the Clavien-Dindo grading system [18, 19]. Serious complications were considered as Grades III or higher on the Clavien-Dindo grading system. The regimen used in the neoadjuvant setting is detailed in Supplementary Material 1.

The radical nephrectomy procedure was followed during the procedure for Mayo level 0 TT. For level I/II patients without IVC wall invasion, balloon catheter extraction is performed after establishing vascular control. When IVC wall invasion exists, the approach differs by tumor laterality. Left-sided tumors require left renal artery embolization first, followed by kidney mobilization, sequential clamping of distal IVC, right renal artery and vein, and proximal IVC before thrombectomy and IVC reconstruction. Right-sided tumors necessitate initial right renal artery ligation, then sequential control of distal IVC, left renal vein, and proximal IVC prior to thrombus removal. For level III TT, Vascular control begins with hepatic vein occlusion followed by proximal IVC clamping. The liver would be mobilized, and the opposing renal vein, proximal IVC, and distal vena cava would all be blocked one after the other. IVC removal was performed. After addressing supra-portal IVC thrombus with reconstruction, managing infra-portal components after releasing proximal clamps. For level IV TT, the central tendon of the diaphragm was cut around the vena cava or the diaphragm was directly cut and then TT was gently pushed into the IVC to change it to the lower diaphragm in order to further remove the TT. Cardiopulmonary bypass was used during an open thoracotomy to remove supradiaphragmatic TT (> 2 cm) that had entered the right atrium [20].

Both overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) were used to measure the oncologic outcomes. OS is the time from surgery to death from any cause, while CSS is the time from surgery to death from cancer.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were presented by the median/interquartile range (IQR), and categorical data were presented by numbers with percentages. The Wilcoxon sum rank test or unpaired t-test was used to evaluate differences between continuous data, and the Chi-square test or Chi-square test for trend was used to analyze differences between categorical data. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed with the matching package (MatchingIT) in R software (version 4.3.1 for Windows, Bell Laboratories), and 1:3 matching without replacement was performed with a caliper width equal to 0.02. The Kaplan-Meier method, along with the log-rank test, was used to conduct the survival analyses. The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26.0 (SPSS, Inc.). Significance was defined as P < 0.05.

After 1:3 PSM, 56 patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy and 153 patients receiving non-neoadjuvant therapy were included in the analysis. Age, Mayo level, surgical approach, and lymph node metastases were significantly different between the two groups prior to PSM. Discussion

Neoadjuvant therapy is frequently utilized in the treatment of several cancers, including locally advanced RCC [21, 22]. A higher percentage of patients underwent less invasive surgical treatment after receiving neoadjuvant-targeted therapy [23]. Roy A.M. reported that cabozantinib significantly reduced tumor size and even trigger a pathologic tumor response that making surgical resection possible [24]. However, the effect of TKIs on RCC patients with TT is still up for debate. The first phase II clinical trial of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT was reported in 2022 [9]. The safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy before thrombectomy in RCC patients are increasingly important due to its growing range of applications. Neoadjuvant therapy might cause necrosis and fibrosis of the tumor and tumor stroma [14, 15]. In RCC, nivolumab may result in immune-related pathologic reactions, such as wound healing with neovascularization and fibrosis [16]. The prevalence and severity of intraoperative adhesions may significantly increase in RCC patients who receive preoperative TKIs [12, 13]. Even though several studies concentrated on the patients’ oncologic prognosis, limited research has primarily focused on the postoperative complications [8, 9, 25, 26]. RCC with TT poses challenges in its surgical management [27]. The adverse event rates of IVC TT have been reported to be 18%-47%, with the rate rising as the TT level increased. Approximately 7%-25% of patients developed serious complications (> Clavien-Dindo Grade 2) [28, 29]. Consequently we evaluated the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT.

Several studies have revealed the impact of neoadjuvant therapy on postoperative complications in RCC. Gorin, M.A. demonstrated the safety of neoadjuvant nivolumab in patients with high-risk nonmetastatic RCC [16]. Karam, J.A. conducted a phase II trial, which indicated that no grade 4/5 treatment-related adverse events occuredin patients undergoing preoperative sitravatinib + nivolumab for locally advanced ccRCC [30]. Nicholas G Cost reported that patients who received sunitinib prior to surgery reduced RCC tumor burden and TT level [26]. The significance of monitoring the systemic side effects of preoperative-targeted therapies was also emphasized. In NAXIVA, 6 out of 17 patients experienced an intraoperative complication, and 6 patients experienced a postoperative complication (35.3%), none of which was considered to have been caused by preoperative TKIs [9]. Kazuhiko Yoshida indicated that no patients developed perioperative complications after preoperative combination therapy [31]. Nevertheless, the sample sizein previous studies was small, and some of these studies did not specifically analyze the RCC with the TT group. Thus, the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT remains uncertain.

According to our study, neoadjuvant therapy did not significantly affect postoperative complications, which is consistent with earlier research in RCC [9, 21, 22, 30]. Between the neoadjuvant and non-neoadjuvant groups, there were no significant differences in perioperative parameters or postoperative complications other than transfusion rates and Clavien-Dindo grade. We investigated plausible explanations for the differencein transfusion rates. When operating on patients who have TT, surgeons may need to exercise extra caution, especially if preoperative neoadjuvant therapy is used. Patients who received neoadjuvant therapy before surgery experienced more severe adhesions during surgery, according to the literature [12, 13] and our clinical experience. When there is a similar level of blood loss, surgeons may be more likely to adopt the blood transfusion strategy. We came to the conclusion that the difference in Clavien-Dindo grade was not clinically significant because the Clavien-Dindo grade was examined for within-group differences using a trend chi-square test, and there was no statistically significant difference between the proportion of complications and the proportion of severe complications. The Clavien-Dindo grade between the preoperative and non-preoperative-targeted therapy groups did not, in our opinion, differ clinically significantly. Additionally, we examined the oncologic results of the compared groups. Neither the preoperative-targeted group nor the neoadjuvant therapy group displayed any significant differences. Nevertheless, more than 30% of the enrolled patients had follow-up durations of less than 12 months. It is possible that a longer period of follow-up will be required to produce significant results.

Considering the expanding applications of ICIs, we also extended our research on combination therapy. Both the perioperative parameters indicated no significant differences between the preoperative-combined therapy group and the non-preoperative-combined therapy group.

Concerns have been raised that patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy may have worse tumor conditions despite having better baseline physical conditions. The neoadjuvant group and non-neoadjuvant group showed no discernible differences in baseline characteristics following PSM, making the two groups comparable. As the analysis and subgroup analysis revealed that there were no significant differences in postoperative complications and perioperative parameters, the safety and feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy in RCC patients with TT were demonstrated. Therefore, we came to the conclusion that it is safe to administer neoadjuvant therapy to RCC patients with TT.

This study has several limitations. First, our study is a non-randomized, retrospective, and single-center study. Despite the PSM we have conducted, there might still exist selection bias in the study. To provide higher-level evidence, a prospective cohort study or perhaps a randomized controlled study is required. One potential weakness is that we primarily focused on short-term complications. The long-term postoperative complications were not examined in the study. A longer follow-up is necessary to strengthen the findings of this study. Besides, since the majority of patients undergoing neoadjuvant therapy were hospitalized in the last two years, the follow-up time is short, and we will continue to follow up with these patients in order to provide more definitive conclusions for the prognosis. To draw more useful conclusions, we will expand our research capacity and increase the sample size. We will also examine long-term postoperative complications.

Conclusions

Neoadjuvant therapies can be safely and feasibly administered to RCC patients who have TT. Neoadjuvant therapies recipients’ overall survival or cancer-specific survival did not differ significantly from those who did not receive the treatment.

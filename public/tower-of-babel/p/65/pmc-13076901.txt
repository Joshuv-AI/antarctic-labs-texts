Diagnosis model of early malignant pulmonary nodules based on clinical laboratory data
Authors: Lisheng Liu, Hua Li, Yajun Miao, Weiwei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate differentiation of malignant from benign pulmonary nodules remains challenging. This study aimed to develop and validate machine learning models integrating seven autoantibodies (7-AABs) and routine laboratory parameters for lung cancer risk prediction. We retrospectively enrolled 310 patients with pulmonary nodules (142 early-stage malignant, 168 benign). LASSO regression was used for feature selection. Eleven machine learning algorithms were developed and validated. Model performance was assessed by AUC, calibration, and decision curve analysis. SHAP was applied for model interpretation. Twelve predictors were selected, including 7-AABs, gender, LYM, RDW, PAR, and fibrinogen (Fg). The random forest model demonstrated optimal performance. A simplified five-feature model (Fg, GBU4-5, SOX2, p53, MAGE A1) retained 95% of incremental discriminatory performance. SHAP identified Fg as the strongest contributor. Decision curve analysis confirmed clinical net benefit. A web-based calculator was developed to facilitate external validation. This proof-of-concept study presents an interpretable machine learning model integrating 7-AABs and routine laboratory parameters for pulmonary nodule risk stratification. The simplified model maintains robust performance while improving clinical practicality. Its current sensitivity (65.1%) precludes standalone screening use; rather, it serves as an auxiliary tool for identifying low-risk patients who may be candidates for conservative management. External and prospective validation are mandatory before clinical translation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42111-z.

Introduction

Lung cancer ranked as the leading cause of both cancer incidence and mortality worldwide, with rates of 32 and 45.4 per 100,000 people in 2020, respectively1. In China, a total of 4.8 million new cancer cases and over 3.2 million cancer-related deaths were reported in 2022, among which lung cancer exhibited the highest incidence and mortality2. Data from the United States indicate that approximately 48% of lung cancer cases are diagnosed at the stage of distant metastasis, which is associated with a five-year survival rate of only 8%. In stark contrast, patients diagnosed with localized or regional disease demonstrate significantly higher five-year survival rates of 62.8% and 34.8%, respectively3. These statistics underscore the critical importance of early and accurate diagnosis. Low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) is currently employed primarily for screening asymptomatic populations to improve early lung cancer detection4,5. However, LDCT demonstrates a false-positive rate as high as 96.4% in distinguishing early-stage lung cancers from benign nodules6. This high rate of misdiagnosis, coupled with the potential risk of false negatives7, and the fact that radiation exposure from CT scans may itself be an independent risk factor for lung cancer8, presents considerable clinical challenges. The differentiation between malignant pulmonary nodules (MPNs) and benign pulmonary nodules (BPNs) continues to rely heavily on clinicians’ subjective experience or specific CT imaging characteristics rather than objective and reliable diagnostic methods9. In clinical practice, misdiagnosis and over-treatment remain significant concerns, particularly among patients classified as having intermediate risk10.

Conventional serum biomarkers—including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cytokeratin 19 fragment (CYFRA 21 − 1), neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and pro-gastrin-releasing peptide (ProGRP)—exhibit limited sensitivity for early-stage lung cancer, rendering them unsuitable for lung cancer screening applications11. Auto-antibodies (AABs), as prospectively validated serum biomarkers for lung cancer screening, demonstrate the capability to detect lung cancer at asymptomatic stages up to five years earlier than CT12,13. The China Food and Drug Administration (CFDA) has recommended a panel of seven auto-antibodies (7ABBs, including p53, PGP9.5, SOX2, GAGE7, GBU4-5, MAGE, and CAGE) suitable for the Chinese population. Multiple studies14,15 have confirmed their favorable clinical utility with high diagnostic specificity. However, its clinical application is constrained by sub-optimal diagnostic sensitivity, which typically ranges from 20% to 50%. A critical challenge lies in enhancing the diagnostic sensitivity of this auto-antibody panel while maintaining its high specificity, which warrants further investigation.

Machine learning (ML), which operates on computational principles inspired by the human brain’s pattern recognition mechanisms, minimizes error functions by iteratively adjusting weights within hidden layers during training16. As a branch of artificial intelligence, ML offers powerful tools for addressing classification problems. It has demonstrated considerable potential in predicting clinical outcomes across various medical conditions17,18. The application of such algorithms could facilitate the development of valuable diagnostic models for patients with pulmonary nodules, thereby supporting clinicians in making well-informed decisions regarding treatment and management strategies. In recent years, explainable artificial intelligence has played a pivotal role in enhancing the transparency and trustworthiness of medical AI models. Particularly in the field of cancer detection, researchers are increasingly adopting methods such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to render the decision-making processes of complex models comprehensible to clinicians19,20. This study aims to develop and validate 11 machine learning models that integrate 7-AABs, age, sex, smoking history, and 18 laboratory parameters to predict lung cancer incidence in adult patients with pulmonary nodules. Furthermore, the SHAP method was employed to interpret the pulmonary nodule prediction model, providing an intuitive and quantitative analysis of feature contributions to the risk predictions. This approach is designed to aid clinical understanding and potentially facilitate subsequent translational applications.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study protocol was approved by the Local Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute (No. SDZLEC2024-171-03, approved on May 28th, 2024), and it was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and current hospital ethical guidelines.

This study enrolled inpatients diagnosed with lung cancer or BPN via histopathological examination between June 2024 and January 2025 from the Affiliated cancer Hospital of Shandong First Medical University.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients with pulmonary nodules suspected of malignancy and scheduled for surgical resection or biopsy; (2) Availability of detailed medical records, including but not limited to CT findings and pathological diagnoses; (3) All included positive cases were patients initially diagnosed with lung cancer, which was pathologically confirmed according to the World Health Organization (WHO) criteria; (4) Inclusion of patients with benign pulmonary nodules confirmed by pathological diagnosis, such as inflammatory nodules, hamartomas, or tuberculous nodules.

The exclusion criteria included: (1) Patients with autoimmune diseases, long-term use of immunosuppressive drugs, or regular consumption of immune-boosting foods or medications; (2) Patients with primary lung tumors of other malignant types or metastatic tumors from other sites; (3) Presence of benign or malignant tumors in other organs; (4) Patients with severely hemolyzed, lipemic, or icteric blood samples; (5) patients with data loss exceeding 5%.

A total of 310 patients were enrolled, including 142 patients with early-stage MPNs and 168 patients with BPNs.

Data collection and feature selection

The models included admission parameters of 310 enrolled participants, with 55 parameters incorporated into the study. Clinical characteristics were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system and Ruimei laboratory information system (version 6.0 rmlis, Huangpu District, Shanghai, China). This work was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Shandong First Medical University (No. SDTHEC202503012, approved on March 1st, 2025).

These parameters incorporated two categories:

(1) Basic characteristics: age, gender and smoke.

(2) Admission laboratory data: lymphocyte (LYM), neutrophil (NEU), monocyte (MON), red blood cell distribution width (RDW), platelet (PLT), platelet distribution width (PDW), prealbumin (PA), fibrinogen (Fg), albumin (ALB), CEA, CYFRA211, NSE, ProGRP.

Derived Inflammatory indices: neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), platelet-to-albumin ratio (PAR), and a panel of 7AABs.

All admission laboratory parameters, along with age and gender, were standardized and incorporated into this investigation. All variables, except for gender (a binary variable), were treated as continuous variables. Feature selection was performed using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), a widely utilized feature selection methodology. This process was conducted strictly within the training set. LASSO constrains the sum of the absolute values of regression coefficients to remain below a predetermined threshold, thereby driving certain coefficients to exact zero and producing a more parsimonious model. This approach reduced model complexity and variance, thereby enhancing the model’s generalization capability, by penalizing the magnitude of the coefficients (shrinkage). The final prediction model was then built based on the feature subset identified by LASSO and evaluated on the independent validation set.

Quantitation of 7 AABs and other laboratory indicators in blood

CEA, NSE, ProGRP and CYFRA211 were quantitatively measured in serum via electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) on a COBAS E801 immunoassay analyzer (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Mannheim, Germany). A panel of 7AABs were analyzed by chemiluminescence immunoassay (CLIA) using MT120 automated chemiluminescence immunoanalyzer (Shenzhen Jinrui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). Serum PA and ALB were assessed by immunoturbidimetry and colorimetry on a Beckman Coulter AU5800 biochemical analyzer (Beckman Coulter, CA, USA). Complete blood cell count performed on a Sysmex XN9000 automated hematology analyzer (Sysmex Corporation, Kobe, Japan), including WBC, LYM, NEU, MON, PLT, RDW, PDW and, derived indices, NLR (NEU/LYM), PLR (PLT/LYM), SII [(NEU × PLT)/LYM], LMR (LYM/MON), and PAR(PLT/ALB). Fib was determined by absorbance variation using an ACL TOP700 analyzer (Werfen, USA). All assays were performed followed manufacturers’ protocols.

Model building strategy

In training sets, we assessed multicollinearity among potential predictors selected through LASSO regression feature selection using variance inflation factor (VIF). Variables demonstrating high collinearity (VIF > 5) were excluded from subsequent analysis. The finally selected variables were incorporated into predictive modeling.

We developed and validated 11 machine learning algorithms, including Naïve Bayes (NB), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Decision Tree (DT), Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). All modeling procedures were implemented in Python (version 3.10). The machine learning algorithms were constructed using sklearn (v1.2.2), XGBoost (v1.7.4), and LightGBM (v4.0.0), with optimal hyperparameters determined via GridSearchCV (sklearn v1.2.2), the specific hyperparameter grids used are detailed in Supplementary Material-1. Model performance was compared across all algorithms based on the selected predictor variables to identify the optimal model.

Predictive accuracy was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and calibration curves. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess clinical utility and net benefit. Model performance was validated and compared using the independent validation set. To ensure the reliability of the models, we utilized eight evaluation metrics: AUC, sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and F1-score. Furthermore, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP, v0.44.0) values were applied to determine variable importance and visualize the association between predictors and lung cancer development.

Model simplification and outcomes

The results of best ML algorithm’s feature importance ranking were used to select the top 1, top 2, top 3, top 4, top 5……top 11 and top 12 features for constructing the simplified prediction models, in order to retain the characteristic of contributing 95% of cumulative predictive influence. Based on feature importance rankings from the optimal algorithm, we sequentially simplified the prediction model. The primary outcome was pathological diagnosis of lung cancer in patients with pulmonary nodules. Details of the survey design are displayed in Fig. 1.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA) and SPSS version 23.0 statistical software (SPSS, IL, USA) were used for statistical analysis. All continuous variables were assessed for normal distribution using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Mann-Whitney U tests were used to compare non-normally distributed data. Comparisons of normally distributed data were performed by independent samples t tests. Normally distributed data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data are reported as the median (25th percentile, 75th percentile). ROC analysis was performed separately on the training set and the test set, with sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV calculated accordingly. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The machine learning models were constructed using the MyLab + i-Research software. Variables with missing data comprising less than 5% of the total were imputed via multiple imputation methods.

Results

Patients’ characteristics and model description

A total of 142 patients with early-stage MPNs (all enrolled patients had a pathological confirmation of stage I disease) and 168 patients with BPNs were included in this study. Pathological diagnoses in the BPN group comprised 23 cases of hamartoma, 16 pulmonary adenoma, 32 granulomatous lesions, 3 fungal infections, 5 tuberculosis, and 89 cases of desmoplasia. The objective of this clinical trial was to evaluate the clinical performance of a seven auto-antibody panel for lung cancer, measured using chemiluminescence immunoassay. The results demonstrated that p53 (P < 0.001), GAGE7 (P = 0.01), PGP9.5 (P = 0.001), CAGE (P = 0.05), MAGE A1 (P = 0.003), SOX2 (P = 0.014), and GBU4-5 (P < 0.001) exhibited statistically significant differences between the BPN group and the early-stage MPN group. To improve upon the diagnostic performance of the auto-antibody panel alone, we incorporated additional clinical laboratory parameters. Univariate analysis identified significant differences between groups in gender (P < 0.001), smoking history (P = 0.013), NEU (P = 0.003), MON (P = 0.005), PLT (P = 0.008), ALB (P = 0.048), Fg (P < 0.001), NLR (P = 0.018), SII (P = 0.002), and PAR (P < 0.001). No significant differences were observed in age, LYM, RDW, PDW, PA, CEA, CYFRA211, NSE, ProGRP, PLR, or LMR. A comprehensive summary of patient characteristics is presented in Table 1.

Feature selection and ML model development

Following application of multicollinearity assessment (VIF < 5, 1 iteration) and LASSO regression, 12 predictors were retained for model construction: p53, GAGE7, PGP9.5, CAGE, MAGE A1, SOX2, GBU4-5, gender, LYM, RDW, PAR and Fg (Fig. 2A-B). The VIF values for all predictors have been included in the Supplementary Table 1. The excluded parameter is: smoke, age, NEU, MON, PLT, PDW, PA, ALB, CEA, CYFRA211, NSE, ProGRP, NLR, PLR, SII, LMR. It is noteworthy that some predictors significant in univariate analysis were not selected by LASSO. This is likely attributable to the regularization property of LASSO and its approach to handling multicollinearity: when a variable’s predictive information highly overlaps with that of other selected variables (collinearity), LASSO tends to exclude it to build a more parsimonious and generalizable model.

We developed and evaluated 11 distinct machine learning algorithms. In the training cohort, the top-performing models by AUC were: Random Forest (RF, 1.0), Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT, 1.0), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost, 1.0), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM, 0.95), and Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost, 0.88). In the independent validation cohort, the highest AUC values were achieved by Support Vector Machine (SVM, 0.84), RF (0.81), AdaBoost (0.81), LightGBM (0.80), and Naïve Bayes (NB, 0.80) (Fig. 2C-D).

Based on its consistent performance across both cohorts, the RF algorithm was selected as comparatively favorable model. Detailed performance metrics are summarized in Table 2. In the training set, RF, GBDT, and XGBoost demonstrated identical top-tier performance across multiple metrics, including sensitivity (99.0%), specificity (100%), accuracy (99.5%), and F1-score (0.995). In the validation set, RF achieved the highest specificity (90%) and accuracy (78.5%), with competitive sensitivity (65.1%) and F1-score (0.781).

Model interpretation and feature importance

SHAP analysis was applied to interpret the RF model and quantify feature contributions (Fig. 2E). Fg was identified as the predictor with the greatest contribution in this study, followed by GBU4-5, SOX2, p53, MAGE A1, CAGE, and gender.

Model simplification and validation

To enhance clinical practicality, we systematically simplified the model by incrementally incorporating features based on their SHAP-derived importance ranking. We evaluated models containing the top 1 through top 5 predictors (Table 3). The simplified model retained approximately 95% of the incremental discriminatory performance of the full model on the validation set. This demonstrates that feature simplification can be achieved with minimal compromise to the model’s generalizable discriminative ability, supporting its potential for more parsimonious and interpretable clinical application (Fig. 3A-B). This simplified model was therefore selected as the final recommended version.

Discussion

This study developed a diagnostic model for lung cancer based on a panel of 7AABs, in which the RF algorithm demonstrated robust performance in the validation cohort (AUC: 0.81, 95% CI 0.73–0.904 ). This model effectively preserves the high diagnostic specificity inherent to the 7AABs panel while compensating for its previously limited sensitivity. Utilizing SHAP for model interpretation, we identified a feature subset (Fg, GBU4-5, SOX2, p53, and MAGE A1) that contributed the most to the model’s predictions within our analytical framework. This SHAP-derived ranking provided the basis for developing a simplified yet performant model. The development of a web-based calculator provides a research tool to standardize predictions and facilitate future external validation studies. Its clinical translation remains contingent upon the successful outcomes of such validation. The epidemiological profile of lung cancer in China presents a considerable public health challenge, characterized by its leading incidence and mortality rates among all cancers. Distinct epidemiological trends have emerged, notably a relatively high prevalence among non-smokers and the predominance of adenocarcinoma as the most common histological subtype, accounting for 54.5% of cases in men and 66.6% in women2. Importantly, over 92% of lung cancers diagnosed in non-smokers are adenocarcinomas. These characteristics were clearly reflected in our study cohort, where adenocarcinoma constituted 92.25% of cases in the MPN group, with 58.45% of patients being non-smoking women.

Accurate differentiation between early-stage MPN and BPN is critical for the early diagnosis of lung cancer, as it dictates subsequent clinical management pathways (e.g., active surveillance vs. intervention. To address the limitations in early pulmonary nodule diagnosis, researchers have developed various novel biomarkers, including auto-antibody panels, circulating microRNAs, small non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), DNA methylation analysis, complement fragment detection, blood protein profiling, and plasma lipid markers derived from liposomes21. Among these, autoantibodies have demonstrated significant value in early lung cancer screening. However, multiple studies have indicated that while autoantibodies exhibit acceptable diagnostic specificity, their sensitivity remains suboptimal14,15. To explore the potential for enhanced diagnostic sensitivity, this study aimed to integrate additional parameters with autoantibodies. Consequently, we incorporated commonly available laboratory indicators, all of which have been empirically validated to exhibit varying degrees of association with tumor initiation and progression, and have demonstrated utility in cancer diagnosis. CYFRA 21 − 1, CEA, NSE, and ProGRP serve as established serum biomarkers for lung cancer, demonstrating significant value in diagnosis, prognostic stratification, and treatment monitoring22,23. Inflammatory cells constitute a critical component of the tumor microenvironment, actively promoting tumor initiation and progression. NLR, PLR, and SII serve as key indicators of systemic inflammatory response, demonstrating significant correlations with diagnosis and prognosis across various cancer types24,25. PA and ALB is regarded as a sensitive nutritional biomarker for evaluating the preoperative nutritional status of cancer patients26. Extensive research has focused on the role of this glycoprotein. In our model, we integrated established serum biomarkers (CYFRA 21 − 1, CEA, NSE, ProGRP) with indicators of systemic inflammation (NLR, PLR, SII) and nutritional status (PA, ALB), all of which have demonstrated associations with tumor pathogenesis and progression. Fg emerged as a particularly significant contributor in our model. Its elevated predictive importance may be attributable to its well-established biological roles in inflammatory microenvironment formation, angiogenesis, and malignant progression27,28, as reported in prior literature. The combination of fibrinogen β-chain and fibrinogen γ-chain demonstrated favorable sensitivity and specificity in discriminating between benign and malignant pulmonary nodules29. This association suggests a potential link worthy of further mechanistic investigation between fibrinogen-related pathways and the malignant phenotype in our cohort. Using multivariable-adjusted Cox regression models, Liao, Zufang et al. identified a significant association between Fg and lung cancer30.

Existing predictive models for lung cancer exhibit certain limitations: some serology-based models demonstrate suboptimal diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.768)30, while models relying solely on autoantibodies are characterized by consistently poor sensitivity14,15. Although integrated models incorporating both autoantibodies and multiple indicators have been developed, their construction has been limited to logistic regression methodologies (AUC = 0.884)3. Machine learning approaches offer a set of tools capable of modeling complex, non-linear relationships within high-dimensional data, which we aimed to explore for this prediction task. In the present study, we utilized 7-AABs along with readily accessible clinical parameters. By applying both LASSO regression and eleven distinct machine learning algorithms, we identified the RF algorithm as optimal. The RF approach was subsequently employed not only for prediction but also to interpret the model and rank feature contributions. To enhance clinical applicability, we systematically compared the predictive performance of various combinations of the top-ranked variables. This analytical process led us to recommend a refined model comprising the five most contributory features: Fg, GBU4-5, SOX2, p53, and MAGE A1. Consequently, leveraging commonly available laboratory indicators, we have constructed a predictive model and developed a web-based calculator. This integrated proof-of-concept work lays the groundwork for future validation. Its potential translation toward clinical utility, including any impact on resource utilization, is strictly dependent on successful external and prospective validation.

Based on these parameters, the machine learning algorithms developed in this study demonstrated excellent performance in terms of discrimination, calibration, and decision curve analysis. Our research is grounded in a study designed to evaluate the measurable panel of 7-AABs. By integrating 7-AABs with the routine laboratory parameter Fg, we constructed a predictive model and developed a complementary web-based calculator. A key contribution of our study is the development of a practical risk-stratification tool for pulmonary nodules, based on readily accessible parameters. It provides a quantifiable risk score (validation AUC: 0.80) to aid clinical decision-making, particularly in identifying cases where a more conservative management approach may be warranted, as suggested by its high specificity and decision curve analysis. The model’s current sensitivity (65.1%) limits its utility as a standalone rule-out diagnostic tool. Future work should focus on improving sensitivity by incorporating richer feature sets or algorithmic optimizations, or on clarifying its auxiliary role in contexts where high specificity is prioritized.

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design and the inclusion of patients from a single institution may limit the generalizability of our findings and the machine learning algorithms to broader, more diverse populations. Second, the laboratory data were obtained from initial routine tests upon admission. Incorporating dynamic, real-time patient data along with more potent predictive factors could enhance model performance. Third, the relatively limited sample size from a single center may affect the stability of our performance estimates. This limitation directly impacts the interpretation of our clinical utility assessment. While decision curve analysis suggested potential net benefit, these estimates are derived from internal validation and depend on model calibration, which may be optimistic in a smaller cohort. Therefore, claims of clinical utility should be considered preliminary. Fourth, our cohort consisted predominantly of lung adenocarcinoma (92.25%), with a small proportion of squamous cell carcinoma (4.93%) and other types. Consequently, we did not evaluate the model’s performance across different histological subtypes, which limits insight into its subtype-specific applicability. In summary, external validation in larger, multi-center, and prospective cohorts is essential to confirm the model’s robustness, calibration, and real-world net benefit before any clinical application can be considered. Furthermore, within such prospective studies, head-to-head comparisons against established clinical prediction models will be crucial to definitively clarify its relative performance and clinical incremental value. In summary, external validation in larger, multi-center, and prospective cohorts is essential to confirm the model’s robustness, calibration, real-world net benefit, and performance across histological subtypes before any clinical application can be considered.

Conclusion

Using the RF algorithm, we developed a predictive model for estimating lung cancer probability in patients with pulmonary nodules, based on a combination of a 7-AABs panel and the routine laboratory parameter fibrinogen (Fg). The model demonstrated robust discriminatory ability (AUC: 0.835) and was well-calibrated in our internal validation cohort. Decision curve analysis indicated its potential to provide a net benefit within a defined range of clinical threshold probabilities. Within the analytical framework of this model, Fg, GBU4-5, SOX2, p53, and MAGE A1 were ranked as the top contributors to the predictions, providing a basis for a simplified and interpretable feature set. In summary, this study presents a proof-of-concept model designed as a potential auxiliary tool for risk stratification in pulmonary nodule management. It is not intended for standalone screening or diagnosis. If the model’s effectiveness is confirmed through rigorous external and prospective validation, and its integration into clinical workflows is proven feasible, it could potentially aid in addressing the diagnostic challenge of indeterminate nodules. By helping to identify patients at lower risk, it might contribute to optimizing clinical management pathways and the efficient use of healthcare resources. These potential impacts on clinical practice and resource utilization require formal evaluation in future health economic and implementation studies.

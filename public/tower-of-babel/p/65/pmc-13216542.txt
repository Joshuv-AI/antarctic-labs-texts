Studies on electrospun PVDF/Silanized hyperbranched polyester of Gen-1 nanofibers as Piezo and Triboelectric nanogenerators
Authors: Indumathy Balaraman, Anand Prabu Arun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The growing demand for sustainable and wearable power sources demands the development of flexible energy harvesting system capable of converting ubiquitous biomechanical energy into feasible electrical energy. This study presents a novel approach to enhance the performance polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) based nanogenerators through the strategic incorporation of first-generation silane hyperbranched polyester (Si.HBP-G1). Varying percentages (0 to 40 wt%) of Si.HBP-G1, featuring a silane core and peripheral hydroxyl end groups were blended with PVDF to process nanofibrous mats through electrospinning. These nanofibers were utilized in fabricating piezoelectric nanogenerator (PENG) and triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) devices. The fabricated piezoelectric nanogenerator (PENG), employing PVDF/Si.HBP-G1 nanofibers as the active layer effectively converted mechanical stress into electrical energy through the piezoelectric effect. In parallel, a triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) was constructed using the PVDF/Si.HBP-G1 nanofiber mat as the tribonegative layer and a thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) cast film as the tribopositive layer, operating under vertical contact–separation mode. Among the compositions studied, the blend containing 30 wt% of Si.HBP-G1 exhibited superior electrical output voltages of 6.4 V for the PENG and 44.4 V for the TENG, which is almost threefold increase compared to 1.9 V and 12.9 V for neat PVDF. The optimized TENG device successfully powered nine LEDs and generated measurable output voltages when tested at various locations on the human body, highlighting its potential for renewable energy applications and smart health monitoring systems.These findings highlight the significant role of Si.HBP-G1 in enhancing the electromechanical performance of PVDF-based nanogenerators and underscores the potential of the developed devices for self-powered wearable electronics and smart health monitoring applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46833-y.

Introduction

The burgeoning demand for renewable, sustainable energy sources has stimulated remarkable research efforts towards developing flexible, wearable power systems capable of converting ambient mechanical energy into useful electrical output1–6. Biomechanical energy harvesting from human motions represents a promising approach as it enables the creation of self-powered wearable devices and sensors without relying on traditional batteries or external power sources7–15. This technology offers tremendous potential for real-time health monitoring, smart textiles and wearable electronics, addressing the growing demands for comfortable, lightweight, flexible and long-lasting power solutions16–18.

Among the various energy harvesting strategies, both Piezoelectric nanogenerators (PENGs)19–21 and Triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs)22–30 have emerged as leading candidates for converting mechanical deformation into electrical energy31–34. Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), a ferroelectric polymer, has attracted considerable attention as an active material for nanogenerator fabrication due to its mechanical flexibility, biocompatibility, ease of processing, and cost-effectiveness. It plays a pivotal role in improving the PENG/TENG device performance due to its inherent characteristics such as large difference in electronegativity between C-F bond (1.5; Pauling scale = 0.7 to 4.0), built-in polarization capability and a stable internal dipole configuration. Along with higher dielectric constant (around 10 at 1 kHz), PVDF exhibits superior elongation at break (200 ~ 800%), making it an ideal dielectric layer in PENG/TENG applications. Among the five known crystalline configurations of PVDF (α, β, γ, δ and ε), the polar β-phase is extensively studied for its superior electrical-responsive properties and thermal stability, making it well-suited for PENG/TENG devices. Moreover, PVDF can be readily used to fabricate devices along with materials such as polymers, nanomaterials, fillers, and carbon nanotubes35–38 to harvest energy through Piezoelectric and triboelectric mechanisms, with enhanced output performance compared to single-mode devices39–47.

Electrospinning(ES) has proven effective for producing PVDF nanofibers with enhanced electroactive β-phase content, which directly correlates with superior piezoelectric and triboelectric performance. The ES process offers the combined advantage of fiber alignment, mechanical stretching and controlled solvent evaporation, enabling the direct fabrication of uniform nanofiber (NF) on a collector. Under simultaneous poling and stretching during ES, the C–F dipoles in PVDF chain tend to orient in an all-trans (TTTT) conformation, leading to the formation of electroactive β-phase and a higher dipole moment in the resultant nanofibrous structure with the choice of materials39–47.

Despite recent advances, PVDF-based nanogenerators still face notable limitations in terms of electrical output and charge generation efficiency. A pivotal approach for overcoming these constraints involves the incorporation of functional nanofillers, and additives into the PVDF matrix to enhance interfacial polarization, by improving its electroactive phase fraction. Previous work has demonstrated that incorporating inorganic nanoparticles, such as ZnO@C, barium titanate (BaTiO₃) and lead-free halide perovskites, can substantially boost the electrical output of PVDF-based devices48–50. However, the development of novel organic-inorganic composite systems with improved performance remains an active research frontier.

In this context, hyperbranched polymers represent an emerging class of materials with unique structural advantages—including multiple functional end groups, globular architecture, and internal cavities—that can be strategically exploited to enhance the performance of energy harvesting systems. These materials have shown promise in modifying polymer properties through interfacial interactions and charge trapping mechanisms51,52. Silane molecules possess both organo-functional and hydrolysable alkoxy groups, enabling the formation of stable organic–inorganic hybrid networks within polymer matrices53–55.

Herein, we present a comprehensive investigation of PVDF-based nanogenerators enhanced through the incorporation of First-generation Silane Hyperbranched polyester (Si.HBP-G1). By systematically varying the Si.HBP-G1 loading (0–40-wt%), we fabricated electrospun nanofibrous mats and investigated their performance in both PENG and TENG configurations. The silane core and peripheral hydroxyl end groups of Si.HBP-G1 are designed to promote favorable interactions with the PVDF matrix, enhance crystalline phase development, and improve surface charge density during triboelectric contact. Our results demonstrate that an optimized composition containing 30-wt% Si.HBP-G1 yields remarkable threefold increases in electrical output compared to neat PVDF, making these devices highly promising for wearable electronics and real-time health monitoring applications. The detailed results are presented in the given below sections.

Experimental section

Materials

3-aminopropyl trimethoxy silane (γ-APTMS), dimethylol propionic acid (DMPA), and p-toluenesulfonic acid (p-TSA) were procured from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). Dimethylformamide (DMF) and acetone were sourced from Merck (India). PVDF (Kynar 761, Mw = 370,000) was obtained from Solvay, Korea. All these chemicals were used in their as-received form, without further purification or modification.

Synthesis of Si.HBP-G1

In this study, γ-APTMS (1 mol, as core), DMPA (3 moles, as monomer) and p-TSA (as acid catalyst, 0.05 wt.-% w.r.t. core and monomer content) were used to synthesize Si.HBP-G1 via one-pot melt-polycondensation process. The reactants were placed in a four-necked glass reactor equipped with stirrer (for uniform mixing of the reacants), Nitrogen inlet (to provide an inert atmosphere throughout the reaction) and guard tube (packed with CaCl2 to avoid external contaminants). One neck was closed with a lid, and used for collecting the reaction samples at regular intervals of time. The whole reaction setup is kept in an oil bath, with the temperature gradually raised from ambient to 130 °C, and maintained at that temperature for 18 h to facilitate the reaction. The by-product (MeOH) formed during the course of the reaction was removed under reduced pressure every 2 h. The final reaction mixture was collected in a Teflon tray in a highly viscous melt-state, cooled to ambient temperature, and stored in a moisture-controlled desiccator to eliminate residual humidity. Figure 1 depicts the reaction scheme of Si.HBP-G1.

Preparation of P-Si.HBP-G1 electrospun NF

P-Si.HBP-G1 blend homogeneous solutions (16 wt.-% in DMF: acetone solvent mixture of 6:4 ratio) with varying Si.HBP-G1 content (0 to 40 wt.-%) were prepared by stirring with magnetic pellet in a sterilized glass vial (25 ml) for 4 h. ES setup (ESPIN NANO V1-VH) with a single nozzle (10 ml syringe with 23G needle) was utilized to facilitate NF formation under applied positive voltage of 20kV to the needle tip, solution flow rate at 1.2 mL/h, needle tip-to-collector distance at 15 cm and collector roller speed at 60 rpm to optimize fiber morphology and collection efficiency. The optimized conditions (20 kV, 1.2 mL/h, 15 cm, and 60 rpm collector speed) were kept constant for all compositions. Maintaining identical electrospinning parameters ensures that differences in fiber morphology and device performance arise solely from compositional effects of Si.HBP-G1 rather than processing variations. Thickness measurements were taken at five different regions and averaged showed values ranging from 150 to 190 μm (± 2 μm). The as-prepared NFs were subsequently employed for the fabrication of PENGs and TENGs, as illustrated in Fig. 2(i-vi).

Fabrication of PENG/TENG devices

Figure 2(v) depicts the PENG device setup comprising a pair of Ni-Cu coated conducting fabrics (2 × 2 cm2) serving as electrodes. PENG device fabrication was completed using P-Si.HBP-G1 NFs as the piezoelectric component with dimensions slightly larger than the Ni-Cu fabric (0.2 × 0.2 cm2) to prevent short-circuiting. Figure 2(vi) shows the construction of TENG device using P-Si.HBP-G1 NFs and thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) film as tribonegative and tribopositive layer, respectively. Both the layers were adhered using an adhesive to the Ni-Cu electrodes by taking 2.0 × 2.0 cm² measurement and were aligned opposite to each other. In the final step, both the PENG/TENG devices were assembled by affixing copper leads to the conductive textile via precision soldering, enabling efficient charge transfer for both PENG/TENG configurations. Entire device is enveloped in PET film to insulate from environmental interference and to improve its durability.

Characterization

FTIR analysis was carried out using Thermo Nicolet iS50 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) spectrophotometer at 4000–400 cm− 1 region under 4 cm− 1 resolution and 32 scans to identify the functional groups. 1H-NMR spectra of Si.HBP-G1 were acquired with Ascend TM 400 MHz spectrometer (Bruker, Germany) to identify the structural units. Crystalline phase changes in ES NFs were analyzed using XRD (Bruker, Germany) in the scanning range of 2θ = 10–90° and scanning speed of 8.5°/min. Morphological features and elemental analysis of the blended NFs were observed using FE-SEM (Quanta250 FEG). The surface roughness was characterized using atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Nanosurf, Switzerland). Piezoelectric output signal was monitored using Piezo Film Lab Amplifier (Specialties Inc., USA) to ensure accurate voltage detection. connected to a dynamic pressure device applying a sinusoidal load of 1 kgf at 1 Hz. For TENG assessment, a custom-made oscilloscope setup generated triboelectric signals by applying a square pulse pressure of 1.0 Hz and 10 N. Physiological signal monitoring using the TENG was carried out with BIOPAC data acquisition system (MP150, USA) connected to Piezo Film Lab Amplifier, and configured with an input resistance of 100 MΩ and a gain of 0 dB for optimal signal fidelity.

Characterization of P-Si.HBP-G1 NFs

This proves that NFs produced using ES with 30 wt.-% Si.HBP-G1 and PVDF have increased β-crystalline phase than neat PVDF NFs, resulting from the alignment of C-F dipoles in PVDF towards the hydroxyl end-groups in Si.HBP-G1, as illustrated in Fig. 4(d). The enhancement in piezoelectric output correlates with the FTIR analysis demonstrating an increase in the absorption bands corresponding to the β-phase of PVDF, notably around 840 cm⁻¹ and 1275 cm⁻¹, which signify the all-trans planar zigzag conformation responsible for piezoelectricity. As Si.HBP-G1 content increases up to 30 wt.-%, these β-phase characteristic peaks intensify, indicating improved dipole orientation due to the nucleating effect of Si-HBP. Moreover, FTIR spectra reveal shifts and increased intensity of specific absorption bands associated with interfacial interactions between PVDF and Si.HBP-G1 functional groups. This suggests stronger interfacial polarization arising from dipole-dipole interactions at the polymer interface, which contributes to enhanced charge separation and storage. The spectroscopic evidence solidly supports the development of β-phase orientation and interfacial polarization responsible for the observed output voltage increase in the nanogenerators. At 40 wt.-% loading, frequent blockage of the electrospinning syringe occurs, indicating increased solution viscosity and instability in the polymer blend precursor. This physical evidence suggests aggregation or phase separation, preventing smooth fiber formation, which has a deleterious effect on electrospinning dynamics, fiber morphology, and consequently, the nanogenerator efficiency.

Surface morphology of all the PVDF NFs (0–40 wt.-%) were distinctly visualized through FE-SEM imaging as depicted in Fig. 5(a–k). In its pristine form, PVDF displays a fine fiber structure (Fig. 5(a)), while Fig. 5(b-e) depicts the FE-SEM micrographs of P-Si.HBP-G1 NFs at magnifications of 5μm, respectively, Incorporation of Si.HBP-G1(0–40 wt%) appears to enhance fiber uniformity and adheres effectively to the PVDF matrix, indicating miscibility and structural compatibility during ES. The FESEM micrographs confirm the formation of continuous, randomly oriented, and interconnected nanofibrous mats for all compositions. Pristine PVDF shows smooth and bead-free fibers with Davg of 321 nm. Upon incorporation of Si.HBP-G1, the fibers remain uniform and defect-free at 10, 20, and 30 wt% loadings, with a gradual reduction in average diameter to 272, 263, and 249 nm, respectively. This progressive thinning indicates improved jet stretching and electrospinning stability, due to favorable polymer–additive (PVDF -Si.HBP-G1(10–40 wt%) interactions. However, at 40 wt% loading, the morphology becomes less homogeneous, showing bead formation, occasional fiber fusion, and a further decrease in Davg to 229 nm suggesting jet instability and insufficient chain entanglement at higher concentrations. Such morphological defects can interrupt stress transfer and charge transport, thereby contributing to the observed reduction in piezoelectric and triboelectric performance. Figure 5(f, g) shows the elemental mapping and the corresponding EDS spectrum, confirming the homogeneous distribution of constituent elements in P-Si.HBP-G1 samples. Figure 5(h-k) displays the EDAX spectra, highlighting the presence of carbon (C), fluorine (F), oxygen (O) and silicon (Si) within the composite NFs.

Electrical measurements

PENG working principle and PENG device measurements

PENG is a device that converts mechanical energy, specifically pressure in this context, into electrical energy. This conversion utilizes the piezoelectric effect, a property exhibited by certain materials, where an applied mechanical stress generates an electrical voltage59. When external pressure is not applied to the material, electrical dipole moments are not induced and there will be no potential difference between the electrodes as shown in Fig. 6(i). On the other hand, when pressure is applied and released in the material, it undergoes mechanical deformation. This deformation disrupts the balanced position of the positive and negative charges within the structure, creating a potential difference across the electrodes attached to the material as shown in Fig. 6(ii) and (iii). The potential difference established across the electrodes drives the flow of electrons in an external circuit, generating an electrical current depending on the applied pressure, properties of the piezoelectric material and design of the nanogenerator. PENGs were fabricated using ES NFs of PVDF and P-Si.HBP-G1 blends in varying weight percentages (0–40 wt.-%).

The output performance of these devices evaluated under mechanical load of 1 kgf at frequency of 1 Hz is presented in Fig. 7(a–e). The piezoelectric output performance of all devices was evaluated under a periodic compressive load of 1 kgf (9.8 N) applied at a constant frequency of 1 Hz, as shown in Fig. 7(a–e). The applied force corresponds to a uniaxial normal compression mode. However, at 40 wt.-%, excessive Si.HBP-G1 content led to precursor instability and phase separation, adversely affecting fiber morphology and reducing piezoelectric efficiency. Under applied loads of 2 kgf and 3 kgf, the device produced 9.54 V and 15.5 V as shown in Fig. 7(i-j). Overall, the output voltage increased with both frequency (0.1–1 Hz) and applied load (1–3 kgf), indicating enhanced piezoelectric polarization under higher mechanical stimulation as illustrated in Fig. 7(k-l).

TENG working principle and TENG measurements

As illustrated in Fig. 8, the TENG device operation is based on contact-separation mode. In P-Si.HBP-G1/TPU combination, Si.HBP-G1 NFs acts as a tribo-negative material and TPU as tribopositive material based on the triboelectric sereies to elucidate its working mechanism as a sensor31,60. TENG is a novel and efficient energy harvesting device that converts mechanical energy into electrical output through the triboelectric effect, which is the generation of static electricity through the physical contact and separation of two dissimilar materials61. The working principle of TENG can be explained through a four-step charge generation and electricity generation process such as surface charge generation, charge separation, electrostatic induction and electricity generation62. In the original state (Figure 8(i)), since the layers did not come into contact, no charge transfer was observed, and both remained electrically uncharged. During the first step (Figure 8(ii)), surface charge generation occurs when two different materials with opposite triboelectric polarities come into contact, causing electrons to be transferred from one material to the other, resulting in the accumulation of positive and negative charges on the surfaces of the two materials. In the second step (Figure 8(iii)), charge separation occurs when the two materials are pulled apart, creating a potential difference between them and generating an electric field. In the third step (Figure 8(iv)), electrostatic induction occurs due to the potential difference caused by the separated charges, which induces an opposite charge on the surface of an electrode placed between the two triboelectric materials. Finally, in the fourth step (Figure 8(v)) of electricity generation, flow of induced charges through an external load generates an alternating current, enabling mechanical-to-electrical energy conversion As illustrated in Fig. 8(i)-(v), this continuous contact and separation mechanism consistently generates alternating current.

Working mechanism of TENG device.

Different compositions of Si.HBP-G1 and PVDF were tested under a load of 10 N and 1 Hz frequency. From the voltage output plotted as a function of time, we observed an increase in voltage values for P-Si.HBP-G1 blended NFs than the neat PVDF NFs (Fig. 9), which perfectly aligns with the fact that increase in β-crystalline phase plays an impeccable role in their enhanced electrical measurements. Specifically, TENG devices based on neat PVDF exhibited a 12.92 V, while the output voltage of blend based TENGs improved from 10 to 30 wt.-%. Further addition beyond 30 wt.-% decreased its electrical performance, which is attributed to the agglomeration effect during the ES process. TENG device based on P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU exhibited output voltage approximately four times higher than the neat PVDF/TPU based TENG as shown in Fig. 9(f). The improved electrical output observed in the P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU composite is likely due to the favorable positioning of PVDF and Si.HBP-G1 within the triboelectric series, which supports effective charge generation during contact and separation. The structured combination of these materials helps retain more of the generated charge, minimizing loss during friction. In comparison, PVDF/TPU blend tends to lose a greater portion of surface charges to the atmosphere, thereby reducing its voltage output. The measurable increase in output voltage in TENG containing 30 wt.-% Si.HBP-G1/TPU, compared to neat TPU and other Si.HBP-G1 content, provides direct evidence of improved electrical performance attributed to better charge storage. Hence the presence of Si.HBP-G1 appears to enhance charge storage efficiency, contributing to the increased electrical performance in the P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU sample. The operational stability of the P-Si.HBP-G1-30 TENG was evaluated over 1200 continuous working cycles under a load of 10 N and 1 Hz frequency as shown in Fig. 9(g). The device exhibited a stable voltage output during the process. The consistent signal amplitude indicates the durable performance of the fabricated TENG for energy harvesting applications. Based on these findings, this composition is chosen for further investigation for wearable and portable electronics applications. Application studies

Energy harvesting applications

A short-circuit current (Isc) measuring 1.12 µA is obtained for P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU, which is comparatively higher than neat PVDF/TPU (0.48 µA) as indicated in Fig. 10(a, b). Rectified voltage of 22.8 V is generated by P-Si.HBP-G1-30 which operates at a frequency of 1 Hz and load of 10 N as shown in Fig. 10(c). The circuit configuration used for LED application is illustrated in Fig. 10(d). As shown in Figs. 10(e, f), the P-Si.HBP-G1/TPU-based TENG successfully powered a series of 9 LEDs, demonstrating its capability to function as a direct mechanical-to-electrical energy converter. This result proves the efficiency of the TENG device in self-sustained power generation.

Wearable applications

In this study, a wearable TENG for gathering human motion data is explored. First, the sensor is attached to a piezo amplifier during measurements. Its output connects to the Biopac MP 150 system, which can wirelessly send data to a PC or mobile device using measurement acknowledgment software as shown in Fig. 11(a). By securing the P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU device on different areas of the body like the wrist, pocket, and shoe insole, various human motions can be detected effectively. The sensitivity of the P-Si.HBP-G1-30/TPU based TENG (2 × 2 cm²) was assessed as shown in Fig. 11(b) before testing its real-time wearable application. This device accurately detects finger tapping, bending and twisting motions with output voltages of 7.6 V, 5.1 V, and 2.8 V respectively. The sensor can also distinguish among multiple human activities. Apparently, it identifies leg movements when placed in a pocket and monitors knee flexion and actions at the shoe sole, as indicated in Figs. 11(c-f). The output voltages from these different motion activities are summarized in Table 2. Results clearly indicate that the P-Si.HBP-G1 based TENG device has potential applications in recognizing and differentiating various physical activities. It can help extract biomechanical energy from human motion environments efficiently (Table 2).

Conclusions

This study explores the practical feasibility of developing flexible PENGs and TENGs using ES P-Si.HBP-G1 NFs. ES process significantly enhances the intermolecular interaction between PVDF and Si.HBP–G1, resulting in remarkable improvement of β-phase of PVDF, validated through FTIR analysis and XRD measurements. Compared to neat PVDF, the performance of P-Si.HBP-G1-30 increased up to three-fold (6.4 V) in PENG and four-fold (44.4 V) in TENG devices. These findings highlight the influential role of Si.HBP-G1 in PVDF towards generating higher output voltage that efficiently converts human motion into electricity using various body movements such as tapping, twisting, folding and bending. The flexible TENGs developed in this study hold great promise for powering portable electronics, representing a significant stride towards practical and efficient energy harvesting solutions.

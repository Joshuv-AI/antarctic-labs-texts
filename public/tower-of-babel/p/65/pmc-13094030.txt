Effect of a transfer- and ambulation-focused early mobilization protocol on functional capacity after kidney transplantation: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Guo Li, Miao-wei Wang, Xia Zhang, Ao Wang, Yu-qi Wu, Li Zhang, Qiang Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly mobilization is recommended to improve postoperative recovery, yet implementation after kidney transplantation is often delayed or limited.ObjectivesTo determine whether a transfer- and ambulation-focused early mobilization protocol improves early postoperative exercise capacity and mobilization in kidney transplant recipients(KTRs), and to evaluate its safety and feasibility.MethodsSingle-center, randomized, parallel-group controlled trial conducted at West China Hospital, Sichuan University (Chengdu, China). Fifty-four adults undergoing kidney transplantation were randomized 1:1 postoperatively to usual care (control group) or usual care plus an early transfer- and ambulation-focused mobilization protocol (early mobilization group, EMG). The primary outcome was the 6-minute walk distance (6-MWD) on postoperative day(POD) 7; secondary outcomes included 6-MWD change from baseline, grip strength, grip strength change from baseline, laboratory indices, prespecified safety and feasibility endpoints, postoperative complications, and hospital length of stay.ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Compared with CG, EMG achieved shorter bed rest time, better 6-MWD (with a smaller postoperative decline from baseline where available), and lower white blood cell (WBC) count on POD7 (all p < 0.05). No participant in EMG was graded as “unable to complete” the mobilization protocol, and no protocol-related adverse events were observed.ConclusionsA transfer- and ambulation-focused early mobilization protocol improved early postoperative functional capacity and mobilization in KTRs and appeared safe and feasible.Trial registrationhttp://www.chictr.org.cn ChiCTR2400086546(07/04/2024).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-026-04855-8.Keywords: Early mobilization, Transferring, Ambulation, Exercise capacity, Kidney transplant recipients

Background

The burden of end-stage kidney disease continues to rise, and kidney transplantation is the preferred renal replacement therapy for improving long-term survival and quality of life [1]. However, the early postoperative period after kidney transplantation is still characterized by delayed functional recovery, postoperative complications, and prolonged hospitalization. Optimizing rehabilitation during this critical window is of clinical importance.

In recent years, enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) has been widely implemented in transplant surgery and has been associated with fewer complications, shorter hospital length of stay, and lower medical costs [2–4]. Multiple guidelines and consensus statements identify early mobilization as a core component of ERAS and recommend initiating activity within the first few postoperative days, while prolonged immobilization is consistently linked to adverse outcomes [5–7].

Within kidney transplantation, ERAS pathways commonly include multimodal analgesia, optimized fluid and nutrition management, early management of catheters and drains, and early out-of-bed activity. Early mobilization is considered beneficial for improving postoperative functional capacity such as the 6-minute walk distance (6-MWD), reducing complications and length of stay, and enhancing overall recovery quality [8–11]. National consensus documents in China likewise recommend initiating out-of-bed activity within 1 to 2 postoperative days following kidney transplantation [8].

Despite these recommendations, implementation in routine practice remains suboptimal. Recent reports describe prolonged bed rest and insufficient early out-of-bed ambulation among kidney transplant recipients (KTRs) [12, 13]. Barriers include the lack of standardized and quantifiable progression from bed mobility to transfers and corridor ambulation, uncertainty about safety thresholds and stopping criteria among clinical teams, and constraints related to staffing and workflow. These factors have created a persistent evidence to practice gap.

To address this need, we developed an early mobilization protocol that focuses on transfer and ambulation, with emphasis on early initiation, stepwise progression, objective monitoring, and safety oversight. We hypothesized that this protocol would enhance early postoperative functional capacity and mobilization in KTRs within an ERAS framework. We therefore conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate its effect on 6-MWD and to assess safety and feasibility, while also observing potential effects on postoperative complications and hospital length of stay.

Patients and methods

Study design

This was a single-center, prospective, open-label, randomized controlled trial conducted at West China Hospital, Sichuan University during the early postoperative inpatient period after kidney transplantation. In accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, this protocol was approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of West China Hospital, Sichuan University (approval No. 2023[769]) and was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400086546). The flowchart of study is presented in Fig. 1.

Flowchart of study

Participants

Patients were recruited at the Kidney Transplantation Center, West China Hospital, Sichuan University from June 2023 to December 2023. After confirmation eligibility and obtaining informed consent, participants were randomized to the early mobilization group(EMG) and the control group(CG) post kidney transplant surgery. Participants were eligible for enrollment if they met all the conditions: (1) adults with end-stage kidney disease undergoing kidney transplantation according to standard indications, (2) aged between 18 and 60, (3) had been on dialysis for 3 months or longer, (4) and graded 2 or lower by the American Society of Anesthesiologists Grading. And participants were excluded if they met any one of the conditions: (1) receiving kidney re-transplantation or multi-transplantation, (2) COVID-19 infected during the perioperative period, (3) serious diseases of vital organs or acute complications after surgery that are not suitable for early mobilization, (4) or unable to understand and cooperate with the assessment or exercise intervention.

Randomization and blinding

Participants were numbered with a random number formed by Microsoft Excel software after enrollment post transplant surgery, and were assigned to CG while numbered with an odd number or EMG while numbered with an even number. Blinding was not implemented cause the participants cannot be blinded while exercise intervention is performing in the same ward environment.

Intervention

Participants in both control group (CG) and early mobilization group (EMG) received routine postoperative care following kidney transplantation, including health education, early oral feeding, intravenous nutrition when required, exercise guidance by the nurses and drain management, as summarized in Table 1.

Other than the general encouragement to exercise during routine care, participants allocated to the EMG were required to follow a structured early mobilization protocol that focused on transfer training and progressive ambulation after surgery (Fig. 2). This protocol was developed in accordance with Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) recommendations and provided participants with specific daily mobility goals for each postoperative day.

The transfer- and ambulation-focused early mobilization protocol

The progression of these daily goals was based on the results of our previous investigation in this kidney transplantation centre [14], indicating that most participants are capable to achieve these functional milestones during the early postoperative period. The ultimate objective of the protocol was to ensure that participants regained independent walking ability prior to hospital discharge, and were able to resume safe, independent ambulation in the community upon returning home.

The early mobilization was performed once in the morning and once in the afternoon, with a score < 14 on a 20-point rating of perceived exertion which is recommend for a low intensity exercise training for patients after surgery [15]. All mobilization sessions were delivered and supervised by an experienced physiotherapist, who provided appropriate assistance, monitored participants` responses and adjusted the activities to ensure safety and adherence to the prescribed progression. The structured goals included exercise in bed, out-of-bed activities, transfer practice, and gradually increasing ambulation distance, with intensity maintained within a safe range based on patients` symptoms and vital signs(mobilization would be temporarily suspended if there is a moderate-to-severe pain, persistent heart rate > 130 beats/min, percutaneous oxygen saturation < 93%, dizziness, dyspnea, palpitations, profuse sweating, or any other condition judged unsafe). Besides, essential activities, such as turning over and sitting up in bed, toileting, and other daily activities were allowed and encouraged in all participants of both groups after surgery.

Outcomes

Physical function

6 min walk test distance(6-MWD) The 6 min walk test(6-MWT) is a practical, simple test of physical functional with excellent relative reliability in chronic kidney disease(CKD) patients [16] and is also used to assess the physical function in KTRs [10]. Participants are asked to walk as far as they can in 6 min at a brisk and comfortable pace along a 30-metre corridor. Participants are allowed to stop if necessary and continue walking when recovered. All participants perform the 6-MWT after enrollment before surgery as a baseline assessment and repeat the 6-MWT on postoperative day(POD) 7; a longer 6-MWD indicates better physical function. In this study, the 6-MWD on POD 7 is record as the primary outcome, and 6-MWD change from baseline (6-MWD change = 6-MWD on POD 7–6-MWD at baseline) as a second outcome which may indicates the postoperative functional restoration.

Grip strength Handgrip strength is a commonly used measure of skeletal muscle strength. The grip strength test is performed just before the 6-MWT after enrollment and POD 7. Participants are required to keep sitting with 90° elbow flexion, and grip strength test is performed with the participants` dominant hand through an electronic hand dynamometer, and is recorded in kilogram(kg). Grip strength will be tested twice with a break time of 1-minute each time, and the better score will be retained.

Feasibility and safety of the early mobilization protocol

Length of bed rest time after surgery The length of bed rest time is recorded in hours as the duration time since the participants returned the ward of kidney transplantation center after surgery to the first time when participants stand, steps or ambulate out of bed in the postoperative days. A prolonged bed rest time among the participants may indicate a lack of feasibility in the early mobilization protocol.

Completion rate of early mobilization protocol Participants in the EMG will be graded as “Completely completed” if they reached all the daily goals in the early mobilization protocol, “Partially completed” if they failed to reach the daily goals < 3 days, or will be graded as “Unable to complete”. And this protocol will be considered to be feasible for the participants if the proportion of participants who were graded as “unable to complete” is less than 20%.

Adverse events in early mobilization The adverse events in early mobilization including falls, catheter detachment, wound rupture, and other adverse events that require specialized doctors or nurses for further surgical management will be recorded as an outcome to assess the safety of this early mobilization protocol.

Laboratory results

Results of the postoperative laboratory tests including red blood cell(RBC) count, white blood cell(WBC) count, platelet count, haemoglobin(Hb), serum creatinine concentration, serum uric acid concentration, estimated glomerular filtration rate(eGFR) on POD 1 and POD 7 were collected from the electronic medical record system, and all the laboratory results come from the routine postoperative laboratory tests without an additional blood sample collection.

Postoperative complications and hospital stay

KTRs suffers a higher risk of complications after surgery, due to the poor health conditions in the candidate phase, complex transplant surgical procedures and immunosuppression post transplantation. The Clavien-Dindo classification [17] is applied to grade for the postoperative complications on the day when participants are discharged from hospital, and only postoperative complications graded III or higher will be recorded as a second outcome, cause complications such as mild or controllable infections, temporary gastrointestinal dysfunctions are very common in patients after surgery. And postoperative hospital stay is recorded as the length of stay from the operation day to the day participants are discharged from hospital.

Sample size

We assumed equal allocation, alpha = 0.05, power = 0.8 and effect size d = 0.796 which based on the results of a previous study [18], a total sample size of n = 42 was calculated by GPower 3.1 software as independent t-test was applied to examine the between-group difference. Considering to possibly dropout and biases due to kidney transplant surgery procedures other than previous study we based to evaluate the value of d, 54 participants were finally included in this study.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed by using SPSS 25.0 statistical software. Quantitative variables were presented as the mean± standard deviation (M ± SD) or the median and interquartile range[M(IQR)] depending on the normal distribution results examined by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Categorical variables were presented as the number(n) and percentage (%).

Independent t-test were applied to examine the between-group differences in 6-MWD, grip strength and laboratory results. Mann-Whitney U test were applied to examine the between-group differences in length of bed rest time after surgery 6-MWD change, grip strength change, and postoperative hospital stay. And a chi-square test or fisher’s exact test was applied to examine the between-group difference in postoperative complications. In all analyses, the significance level was set at P < 0.05.

Discussion

In this study, we noted that 13 participants in EMG were marked as “completely completed” the early mobilization protocol and the remaining 14 participants “partially completed” this protocol. In this study, no participant was “unable to complete” the protocol which indicates that participants in EMG reached most of the daily goals in the early mobilization protocol as we expected before the study. Besides, no adverse event was reported during implementation. Thus, we considering this early mobilization protocol focused transferring and ambulation post surgery may be feasible and safe for KTRs. And by providing participants with clearly defined and attainable daily exercise goals, and by assigning an experienced physiotherapist to offer individualized assistance, adherence to early mobilization appeared to be strengthened and more effectively implemented. Through the participants might usually report unpleasant experiences such as pain and fatigue during the early mobilizations, and some participant even experienced severe pain, weakness, tightness in breathing and dizzy that prevented completion of the daily goal, but those symptoms usually soon released after a bed rest without requiring additional interventions. In addition, we also noted that participants in the EMG demonstrated earlier mobilization after surgery, with all achieving out-of-bed activity within the first 2 postoperative days. This result is consistent with previous literature that indicated structured implementation of exercise intervention would not increase the risk of adverse event related to exercise [19, 20]. At the same time, we acknowledge that the 52% rate(14/27) of partial completion underscores the challenge of applying a standardized, progressive protocol in the immediate post-operative setting. This finding precisely highlights the need for individualized adjustment and supportive measures such as optimized pain management and early nutritional support [21, 22]. And we believe that the application of early mobilization post surgery in KTRs requires flexibility within a structured framework, rather than viewing partial completion as a failure of the protocol itself.

As prolonged bed rest is known to reduce maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max), with VO2max declining linearly over time [23]. Early mobilization, especially early exercise out of bed, may mitigate this declining of VO2max, and the upright position in the transferring and ambulation training also promotes sputum drainage and airway clearance [24]. Additionally, early exposure to transferring and ambulation training may reduce psychological barriers such as fear or anxiety associated with getting out of bed, which could improve performance in physical function tests such as the 6-MWT. At the same time, early mobilization might enhance the gastrointestinal functional recovery and earlier oral feeding post surgery [25, 26]. Collectively, reduced gastrointestinal discomfort during ambulation and improved postoperative nutritional intake may explain the superior 6-MWD observed in the EMG on postoperative day 7, indicating better cardiopulmonary capacity and physical functional restoration post surgery. However, even post transplantation, KTRs usually cannot fully reach the level of a healthy individual of the same age and gender in cardiopulmonary function and exercise capacity [27], cause they may generally have long-term reductions in activity and cardiopulmonary function during the course of CKD, especially during the dialysis phase and candidate phase [28]. Therefore, a short-term mobilization protocol alone is insufficient for full restoration.

Different from the 6-MWD, grip strength did not show a postoperative decline, and the between-group differences in grip strength on POD 7 and change in grip strength were not significant. We assume this may be attributable to rapid purification in the internal environment after transplantation, especially the clearance of toxins that may inhibited the neuromuscular function. And such physiological recovery may offset the negative effects of surgical trauma on muscle strength. However, a short-term early mobilization protocol without dedicated resistance training is unlikely to a significant improvement in grip strength.

Although exercise was reported to improve laboratory results of CKD patients and KTRs in previous studies [29, 30], the positive effect of exercise on the laboratory results of KTRs early post surgery was rarely reported. And the laboratory results we observed in this study may indicate the function and recovery of the graft, a short-term early mobilization intervention might be limited to an significant impact on these results. However, we noted a significant lower WBC count in EMG on POD 7, and we assume this might resulted in a more favorable inflammatory response, as exercise is regarded to reduce chronic systemic inflammation in the CKD population and early mobilization also have been proved to be a protective factor against postoperative infections and in previous reports [31, 32].

In recent years, modifications in surgical techniques and perioperative management have reduced severe complications and shortened hospital stays. In this study, 4 participants(1 participant in EMG and 3 participants in CG) were graded III or higher according to the classification of Clavien-Dindo after surgery, but the between-group difference was not significant through the fisher’s exact test, and there was no significant difference in postoperative hospital stay between the 2 groups. We assume these results may caused by most of the participants received a living kidney donation, and KTRs who received a living organ donation usually had a lower risk of complications and better outcome after surgery in previous studies [33, 34]. Besides, reduced postoperative complications and shorter hospitalization are resulted in not only the enhanced early mobilization, but also the other components of multidisciplinary perioperative care managements.

Limitations

We also noted some limitations in this study. Firstly, it is limited by a small sample with a single centre experience and there may be a lack of allocation concealment during the enrollment. Secondly, there is a lack of blinding in participants, therapist and assessors, which may constitute a significant source of potential measurement bias. Thirdly, as participants in CG are expected to receive encouragement and assistance to active exercise during the specific care after kidney transplant, this study actually compared “a structured mobilization” and the “less structured mobilization” rather than the early mobilization and immobility. Besides, there is a short observation duration of only 7 days and lack of long-term follow-up. Finally, most participants in this study received a living kidney donation, while the patients who were receiving a deceased kidney donation were usually scheduled for emergency surgery and lack of sufficient time for baseline assessment were excluded, potentially limiting generalizability.

Conclusion

We provided a transfer- and ambulation-focused early mobilization protocol to the KTRs after surgery, and we recognized the protocol could enhance KTRs to early mobilization, an improved postoperative functional capacity and restoration after kidney transplant surgery, as en earlier exercise out of bed, a better 6-WMD and a reduced decreasing in 6-MWD was observed in the EMG. And this early mobilization protocol might be feasible and safe for the KTRs post surgery as no participant was unable to complete the protocol in EMG and no adverse event was reported. We also observed a lower WBC count in EMG on POD 7, but the effects of this early mobilization protocol on grip strength, laboratory results except WBC, postoperative complications and hospital stay is insufficient and further study is needed.

Selection of third-party logistics in supply chain finance under probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy sets and distance measures
Authors: Shougi S. Abosuliman, Ijaz UR Rahman, Saleem Abdullah, Abbas Qadir
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Financial institutions attach great importance to the multiple attribute decision-making (MADM) problems of selecting an effective third-party logistics (3PL) service provider in supply chain finance. A suitable 3PL service provider can not only assist financial institutions in carrying out supply chain finance activity but can also replace financial institutions in supervising the operation of a target financing supply chain, therefore minimizing financial institutions' operating risks. However, there is no sufficient study on the selection of a 3PL service provider. Hence, the objective of this paper is to establish an innovative concept of the “probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy set” (PCHFS), which is a hybrid structure of the complex hesitant fuzzy set and the probabilistic fuzzy set to manage complex information in real-decision theory. This paper examines the periodicity of probabilistic fuzzy information and extends the range from [0,1] to the unit disc in the complex plane to provide more ability to describe the full meaning of the information in a mathematical model. Based on the internal structure of the set and to find the degree of discrimination between the pairs of PCHFSs, the generalized distance measures and modified generalized distance measures are defined. Several properties and their relationships between them are derived in detail. Also, several cases of the proposed measures are exposed, which reduces them to the existing studies. Furthermore, based on these proposed measures, a multiple-attribute decision-making (MADM) approach is established in an uncertain environment, and several numerical examples are given to examine the feasibility and validity of the explored measures. Finally, the modified and parameterized distance measures based on PCHFSs are verified by comparing them with some existing measures.

Introduction

Supply chain financing is a financial model in which banks connect core enterprises with both upstream and downstream partners to offer flexible financial services and products. Many of the world's top banks provide supply chain financing to enterprises, and several are actively looking to expand their businesses in this area. With the industry's growth and immense potential, various financial institutions primarily offer supply chain financial services [1]. Logistics serves as the main channel through which capital flows, making inventory pledge financing a core component of supply chain finance. Modalities such as accounts payable and prepayment in supply chain financing rely heavily on the flow of inventory. It is possible to say that logistics in a supply chain form the foundation for the development of the supply chain financial business. Logistics enterprises, as the primary coordinators of supply chain finance, may not only offer logistics and warehousing services to small and medium-sized businesses but also provide goods custody supervision services to financial institutions, thereby bridging cooperation between banks and enterprises. Preventing illicit transactions requires financial institutions to enlist experienced third-party logistics (3PL) service providers that help safeguard the financial system from potential collusion among these businesses. For instance, in China, numerous banks have engaged the services of the China Foreign Trade and Transportation Group to provide logistics supervision services to their clients. This enables banks to promptly grasp the actual logistics conditions within a supply chain, thereby mitigating credit risk [1]. Thus, financial institutions can engage in supply chain financing business with the assistance of a qualified third-party logistics (3PL) service provider. This provider can also take on the role of overseeing the targeted financing supply chain for financial institutions, thereby reducing the operational risks associated with them. Therefore, the selection of the most suitable third-party logistics supplier is of great importance, and numerous methods have been established to address the problems associated with selecting 3PL service providers [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. Evaluating the service quality of third-party logistics service providers in supply chain financing is a popular and challenging topic, as it requires consideration of both quantitative and qualitative factors.

The method of multiple attribute decision-making (MADM) involves looking at multiple possibilities and ranking them in the position where they are most reliable. Data must be complete and accurate for us to make decisions. However, in most cases, we are not able to determine the exact data. For example, we cannot use an exact number to describe how comfortable a chair is or what the weather is like at the moment. In these situations, the comfort of a chair and how we feel about the weather are vague impressions that often appear in our daily lives. To solve this situation, Zadeh proposed the concept of a fuzzy set (FS) [7], which consists of membership degrees ranging from 0 to 1. Zadeh also demonstrated how basic set operations, such as union and intersection, are defined on these ‘fuzzy sets’ and created a consistent framework for dealing with such problems. In the last few decades, fuzzy sets (FS) have gained significant attention, and researchers have applied them to solve decision-making (DM) [8], [9], pattern recognition [10], medical diagnosis [11], and other fields as well [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]. Wang [18] introduced the similarity measures of fuzzy sets and their elements.

Later, Atanassov proposed the well-known idea of an intuitionistic fuzzy set (IFS), which consists of two functions: membership and non-membership degrees [19]. He also discussed its condition that the sum of the function values is constrained to the range [0,1]. IFS is a useful extension of fuzzy set theory. Therefore, in the context of IFS, researchers have developed several operations [20], distance measures [21], [22], decision-making [23], [24], and their applications to pattern recognition [25] and probability spaces [26], etc. However, in some practical problems, the IFS condition fails if the sum of the membership and non-membership degrees is greater than 1. To this end, Yager [27] proposed a Pythagorean fuzzy set (PFS) to solve these scenarios, with the condition that the sum of the squares of the membership and non-membership functions should be equal to or less than 1. Later, researchers have developed numerous decision-making techniques such as the Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) methods [28], Failure Mode and Effect Analysis (FMEA)-based Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), MOORA technique [29], aggregation operators [30], [31], Multi-Attributive Border Approximation Area Comparison (MABAC) approach [32], Evaluation based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) method [33], and applications to internet finance soft power evaluation [8] and bibliometric analysis [34] in the PFS environments. For more details, see [35], [36], [37]. Kaushik and Sankar proposed Power partitioned neutral aggregation operators of T-spherical fuzzy sets to select H2 refueling [38]. Further Arijit et al. [39] proposed three-way decision-making with possibility dominance in an incomplete information system. To deal with uncertain information in real-world decision-making problems, researchers have proposed many decision-making techniques [40], [41], [42].

As a result of the development of this theory, Ramot et al. [43] investigated the Complex Fuzzy Set (CFS), which incorporates the membership grade as a complex number. The CFS has a range that extends beyond [0,1] to the unit circle in the complex plane. The membership function defined for the complex fuzzy set is z=rseιws(x), which comprises an amplitude term rs(x) and a phase term ws. The amplitude term retains the concept of fuzziness, while the phase term signifies the declaration of a complex fuzzy set, requiring a second dimension of membership. The complex fuzzy sets (CFS) can better manage two-dimensional data in decision-making problems. Nguyen et al. [44] developed the concept of the complex fuzzy set (CFS), which allows decision-makers a greater range of options and flexibility in handling complex data. Li and Chiang [45], [46] investigated function approximation and extended it to a complex neuro-fuzzy system. Yazdanbakhsh and Dick conducted a systematic review of Complex Fuzzy Sets (CFS) [47], [48]. Xin et al. [49] also discussed the representation of a complex number in cartesian form, where a=rscos(x) and b=rssin(x). The membership functions are usually difficult to determine accurately and one may argue that accurate or precise membership functions are necessary in reality. In discussing the characterization of an accurate membership function, Cai [50] emphasized the importance of precise membership functions due to the inherent difficulty in accurately determining fuzzy sets in reality. CFS has been used by many researchers in various domains such as operation characteristics and equations [51], distance and continuity [52], neurofuzzy ARIMA forecasting approach [53], and the aggregation operators [54], [55].

To deal with circumstances where people exhibit hesitation in their performance when making decisions about objects, Torra [56] proposed the hesitant fuzzy set (HFS) which is a generalization of FS. Many MADMs have been developed in HFS environments such as [57], [58], neural networks [59], clinical diagnosis [60], distance measure [61], [62] and market forecasts [63]. Arijit et al. [64] proposed linguistic hesitant-Z-number to express hesitancy, vagueness, and reliability of evaluation information. HFS can be used not only to handle situations where decision makers are not sure which evaluation values to represent, but also to assign different probabilities to the evaluated values. In the case of probabilistic fuzzy situations, probabilistic information can easily be lost. To address these challenges, Pang et al. [65] created a new probabilistic fuzzy linguistic term set. Later, Xu and Zhou defined a new concept of probabilistic hesitant fuzzy sets [66].

Motivation of paper

From the literature review, the authors found many tools and distance measures for solving decision-making problems but no such tools and implementations of the Probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy set (PCHFS) were defined. Therefore, inspired by the above discussion, the present work is motivated to propose a new tool Probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy Set (PCHFS) by extending the complex hesitant fuzzy set to the Probabilistic fuzzy set in decision-making problems.

The motivation for this study is given below.1)Recent studies on complex hesitant fuzzy sets and Probabilistic fuzzy sets are insufficient and their characteristics are not thoroughly investigated. Introducing a new concept such as the Probabilistic Complex Hesitant Fuzzy Set (PCHFS) is necessary to process the decision information more accurately. The PCHFS is a useful tool for dealing with difficult and sophisticated data in real-world decision-making situations compared to other existing tools such as FS, CFS, HFS, and CHFS.2)Defining basic operational laws for PCHFS is crucial for handling the MADM issue as it captures the relationship of different Probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy numbers. Existing operational laws are unable to handle this relationship within the Probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy set, necessitating an extension of the operational laws in the PCHFS framework.3)Developing a new distance measure is essential for finding the distance between two possible complex reluctance numbers. This paper focuses on using MADM techniques for the selection of a 3PL in supply chain finance.

The Probabilistic Complex Hesitant Fuzzy Set (PCHFS) is a versatile strategy for handling complex data in real-world decision-making situations. The PCHFS represents two-dimensional data in a single set. In PCHFS, the degree system uses polar coordinates to represent complex values. In PCHFS when we assume that the imaginary part is equal to 1, then the theory under study becomes HFS [56]. Similarly, when we consider PCHFS as a singletons set, then PCHFS becomes CFS [43]. Furthermore, if the PCHFs are assumed to be a singleton set with a zero imaginary portion, the PCHFS is changed to FS, as specified by Zadeh [7].

Distance measures under probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy sets

In this section, we put forward some distance measures because distance is a noteworthy and widely used measure of information for improving decision-making performance. However, various distance measures have been developed to address decision-making with different numerical results [22], [61], [62]. All of these play an important role in solving decision-making problems, but they only handle one-dimensional data. We study the above distance measures and tools to define distance measures specifically for PCHFS as follows.

Third-party logistics service providers selection for financial institutions

This section, presents a case study on choosing the best third-party logistics (3PL) service provider. The case study aims to help financial institutions develop their supply chain finance business and demonstrates the applicability of the proposed methodology.

Case description

Supply chain finance needs to focus on the entire supply chain operation, yet financial institutions may not understand supply chain operations, increasing operational risks. Finding the right 3PL service provider to collaborate with is critical at this time. Through collaboration with 3PL service providers, financial institutions can play a significant role in supply chain financing. Apart from offering standard logistics services like product storage and transportation, third-party logistics (3PL) service providers can offer financial institutions and small and medium-sized businesses extra services like quality evaluation, oversight, elimination, and credit assurance. This helps financial institutions attract more clients and increase their profits. In many circumstances, there is only one firm that requires finance, and this enterprise has no equivalent accounts receivable or credit guarantee from other enterprises in the supply chain. At this time, financial institutions may utilize the financing warehouse to provide credit to them. Financing a warehouse refers to a financing model in which an enterprise uses inventory as a pledge and financial institutions provide credit after passing the evaluation and certification of the 3PL service provider. Financial institutions should consider the stability of the enterprise's inventory, the presence of long-term cooperative trading partners, and the overall performance of the supply chain when reviewing an application for financing a warehousing business. Financial institutions may offer 3PL service providers credit lines based on their scale and operational capacity, and 3PL service providers are completely liable for the operation and risk management of financing business enterprise loans. As a result, finding a suitable 3PL service provider is critical for financial institutions.

As one of China's leading banks, U Enterprise must ultimately choose a suitable 3PL service provider to help it carry out supply chain finance operations. We assumed five alternatives (candidates) and four criteria in this case. The five candidates considered as alternatives, indicated by (A1, A2, A3, A4, and A5), are well-known comprehensive logistics enterprises in China that can provide 3PL services. The specific information of the four criteria (c1, c2, c3, c4) is listed below based on which we will select the best alternative.c1:Skill to offer value-added services.c2:Access to information, including timeliness and reliability of information gathered (it can assist financial institutions in monitoring the functioning of financing enterprises).c3:Flexibility, the capacity to adjust to changing environments and meet particular needs.c4:Financial stability, revealing service continuity and reliability (it may decrease financial institutions' risk in supply chain financing).

Discussion

From the above evaluation comparison results, we observe that the data is in PCHFS form, and using the existing CHFS distance measures and converting PCHFS to CHFS, the classification results are similar to the proposed method. Furthermore, if we assume the imaginary part to be zero, then the explored distance measures are reduced for the PHFS. Likewise, if we assume the PCHFS as a singleton set, then the explored methods are reduced for the PCFS. Also, if we consider PCHFS as a singleton set with a zero imaginary part, the discovered idea becomes PFS. Therefore, the proposed concepts are more general and capable of handling uncertainty more effectively than existing methods.

Conclusion

The MADM has the high potential and discipline process to improve and evaluate multiple conflicting criteria in all areas of DM to get more satisfactory and feasible DM results. In DM problems, the factual information about some facts is usually unknown, and this uncertainty makes the decision process more challenging and complex. This paper introduces new concepts of probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy sets, which are mathematical tools capable of efficiently handling uncertain and inaccurate knowledge. A novel parameterized distance measures and their special cases are developed to examine the differences between two PCHFSs. After this, the established measures are utilized in the environment of decision-making to examine the feasibility and validity of the proposed tool. Moreover, the numerical examples for established measures are solved to express the superiority and integrity and reliability of the explored work. Finally, to evaluate the credibility of the modified and parameterized distance measures based on PCHFS, they are verified by comparing with some existing measures.

This paper proposes the idea of probabilistic complex hesitant fuzzy sets, which are an efficient generalization, and they work effectively in processing uncertain and complex data. However, this theory cannot assign non-membership degrees to elements individually. This means that in PCHFSs, it is not possible to assign non-membership degrees to elements alone. To overcome the shortcomings of the proposed work, this work can be further extended to future studies by using fuzzy extensions, such as probabilistic complex intuitionistic hesitant fuzzy set, Interval-valued probabilistic complex intuitionistic hesitant fuzzy set, probabilistic complex Pythagorean hesitant fuzzy set and Interval-valued probabilistic complex Pythagorean hesitant fuzzy set for solving uncertainty in different MCGDM problems.

Implementability of multicomponent interventions in Primary Care: an overview of systematic reviews
Authors: E Renzi, A Covelli, G Abbatecola, M Innocenzio, E Parrinello, V Baccolini, C De Vito, C Marzuillo, P Villari, A Massimi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLiterature defines “implementability” as the likelihood that a given evidence-based intervention (EBI) will be adopted into routine practice. As time goes by, EBIs delivered in Primary Health Care (PHC) increasingly consist of multicomponent interventions aimed at promoting active ageing in the elderly. The aim of this review is to identify a conceptual framework that uses implementation outcomes (acceptability, fidelity, feasibility, implementation cost, appropriateness, and sustainability) to guide the implementability of multicomponent PHC interventions in the elderly population.MethodsAn overview of systematic reviews was conducted through PubMed, CINAHL, Scopus, Web of Science, PsycInfo, Conchrane Library including articles based on the following criteria: (1) individuals ≥65 years; (2) including multicomponent PHC interventions (3) including a framework to assess implementability. Methodological quality of systematic reviews was evaluated using the AMSTAR 2.ResultsOf the 14,984 articles identified, twenty-six reviews met the inclusion criteria. The characteristics of the identified conceptual frameworks included multicomponent PHC interventions based on the use of technology (n = 16) and aimed at preserving cognitive function (n = 12); The most considered implementation outcomes were: feasibility (n = 21) and implementation costs (n = 17). The RE-AIM and PRISM integrated framework were identified in five systematic reviews used to implement preventive interventions (cognitive function and physical activity) for community-dwelling older adults.ConclusionsRE-AIM and PRISM are a widely used model for assessing the impact of EBIs on public health and policy. The use of this framework occurs after EBI realization, so the next steps of our research will aim to test this framework on an elderly-focused health intervention delivered in Italy through the Next Generation EU fund and to develop a revised version for use in multicomponent PHC interventions.Key messages• Identifying a framework to help guide the implementation of multicomponent PHC interventions facilitates the application of evidence-based intervention in routine practice.• RE-AIM and PRISM are a widely used framework that may help evaluate implementation outcomes.

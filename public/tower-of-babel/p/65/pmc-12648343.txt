Control of Conical Intersection Dynamics in CF3I Photodissociation via Halogen Bonding
Authors: Dong Yan, Huimin Zhang, Fangfang Li, Mingjuan Yang, Jiaxing Liu, Yujie Ma, Ang Xu, Ti Zhou, Minghui Yang, Mingfei Zhou, Fengyan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The dynamics of conical intersections are critical in governing the diverse pathways of chemical reactions, especially in photoexcited polyatomic systems. However, the systematic control of conical intersection dynamics remains a formidable challenge in reaction dynamics. Here, we demonstrate that halogen bonding, a key type of noncovalent interaction between halogenated molecules and electron-rich species, can control the conical intersection dynamics of halogenated compounds. Using time-sliced ion velocity map imaging with high resolution, we investigate the ultraviolet photodissociation dynamics of CF3I upon interaction with N2, CO, and C2H4 in supersonic molecular beams. We observe significant changes in the speed and angular distributions of the I­(2P3/2) products, as well as in the product branching ratios of I­(2P3/2):(I­(2P3/2) + I*­(2P1/2)), which vary from 0.10 to 0.71. These changes arise from halogen bonding of CF3I with N2, CO, and C2H4 at ultraviolet wavelengths near the conical intersection region. Theoretical calculations reveal that halogen bonding elevates curve-crossing energies at the conical intersections from the 3Q0+ state to the 1Q1 state in a degree that directly correlates with the strength of the halogen bonding, whether in σ or π halogen bonding. This results in a reduction of product velocities and a controllable enhancement of curve-crossing probabilities to the I­(2P3/2) channel. These findings highlight the role of halogen bonding in controlling nonadiabatic transition dynamics and offer a ”soft chemical control” approach for regulating chemical reactions.

Introduction

Conical intersections (CIs) or curve crossings between potential energy surfaces (PESs) play a fundamental role in diverse chemical reaction dynamics, particularly in photoexcited polyatomic systems. − Their existence explains many energy transfer and nonadiabatic transition phenomena in photochemistry and photophysics, such as photosynthesis, vision, and photoprotection in biological systems, making them a cornerstone of modern theoretical and experimental research. The control of CIs would facilitate the design of chemical pathways, the optimization of energy conversion processes, and the advancement of our understanding of complex molecular systems. However, accurate control of curve-crossing probabilities near CIs in molecules is a formidable challenge. In the field of reaction dynamics, the role of CIs is commonly investigated by manipulating photon or collision energy conditions in system driven by CIs. − Concurrently, increasing attention is being directed toward understanding the effects of emerging noncovalent interactions on reactivity, specifically in systems such as hydrogen-bonded complexes and weakly bound van der Waals molecules. − Nevertheless, deciphering the interplay between reaction dynamics and halogen bonding in regulating the chemical reactivity of CIs remains elusive, primarily due to the recent recognition of such interactions and the inherent complexities involved in studying CI dynamics.

Halogen bonding has emerged as a versatile noncovalent interaction for a diverse array of anions, especially halogenated compounds. − Halogen bonding with CF3I molecules has attracted great attention due to the strong electron-withdrawing ability of the F atoms, which makes I effective as an acceptor of electrons from Lewis base X and leads to strong halogen bonds CF3I···X. − FTIR, Raman, and rotational spectroscopies, along with theoretical calculations, have provided evidence for the formation of halogen-bonded CF3I in σ-type or π-type halogen bonding. − , Halogen bonding provides stronger noncovalent interactions with Lewis bases compared to hydrogen bonding, owning to the high polarizability of the halogen atom bound to carbon. In the context of CI dynamics, halogen bonding can serve as “a small lever”, a subtle yet powerful factor that could alter the electronic state dynamics near CIs.

Numerous studies on the ultraviolet (UV) photodissociation of CF3Ian important halogenated system related to ozone depletion and global warminghave demonstrated the critical role of CI dynamics in the determination of pathways. ,− The UV absorption spectra of CF3I in the 220–350 nm range feature dipole-allowed transitions from the ground state, X̃1A1, to three repulsive excited electronic states, 3Q1(E in C3v symmetry), 3Q0+(A 1), and 1Q1(E) with the spin–orbit interaction, listed in order of increasing energy, as depicted in Figure A. The CI between the 3Q0+ and 1Q1 states is significantly modified by spin–orbit coupling, which facilitates transitions between states of different spin multiplicities. At small C–I distances, the 1Q1 state lies higher in energy than the 3Q0+ state, but at larger C–I distances, it converges to the I­(2P3/2) asymptote, 7603 cm–1 below the I­(2P1/2) limit of 3Q0+. The dissociation process along these three repulsive states occurs rapidly (approximately 300 fs), with energy disposal following an impulsive mechanism and angular distribution exhibiting strong anisotropy. The 3Q1 and 1Q1 states correlate with the ground-state I­(2P3/2) products (denoted simply as I), which feature a perpendicular angular distribution. In contrast, the 3Q0+ state correlates with the excited state I*­(2P1/2) products (denoted as I*), but it can also produce ground-state I­(2P3/2) products through curve crossing to the 1Q1 state via a CI, as indicated by the circle in the PESs in Figure A calculated in this study, resulting in a parallel angular distribution. The probability of curve crossing from the 3Q0+ to the 1Q1 state affects the angular distribution of the ground-state I products, as well as the branching ratio between I and I* products. −

In this study, we investigated the photodissociation dynamics of CF3I upon interaction with gas molecules such as N2, CO, and C2H4 through σ and π halogen bonding using the supersonic beam expansion and time-sliced ion velocity imaging techniques. Researchers have developed the time-sliced ion velocity imaging technique with high resolution in speed and angular distributions, eliminating the need for inverse Abel transformation, and enabling direct measurement of the product signal from clusters and isolated molecules with different recoiling velocities. − By combining slice imaging and the resonance-enhanced multiphoton ionization (REMPI) method, we experimentally obtained the high-resolution raw slice images and dynamics spectra of the photolysis product I­(2P3/2,1/2) from the halogen-bonded CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes in the gas phase. Our theoretical calculations clearly revealed significant differences between the CI positions of the CF3I molecule and CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes. The combined experimental and theoretical studies reported here provide unprecedented insights into the modulation of CI dynamics in the CF3I system, driven by halogen bonding with varying strengths.

Results and Discussion

Effects of Halogen Bonding on Spin–Orbit Split Iodine Products

The high-resolution raw slice images and dynamics spectra of the photolysis products I and I* from the CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes in a supersonic molecular beam were recorded using our time-sliced ion velocity map imaging setup. − Slice images of I products, shown in Figure B, were obtained at a photolysis wavelength of λ = 277.87 nm in a supersonic molecular beam containing pure He, pure O2, pure N2, pure CO and pure C2H4 as the carrier gases for CF3I, respectively, with the same wavelength used for the REMPI of I atoms (Table S1). Detailed methods are provided in the Supporting Information and Figure S1. Compared to the I and I* images obtained with pure He and O2, those recorded with N2, CO, and C2H4 showed the presence of an additional inner ring, indicating photodissociation from halogen-bonded CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes. These complexes were also detected along the molecular beam propagation direction in mass-gated (M = 224 g/mol) velocity map mode (Figure S2). Notably, the raw images reveal a drastic change in the angular distribution of I products: a strongly parallel distribution in CF3I photolysis, contrasting with a nearly isotropic or even perpendicular distribution in the photolysis of halogen-bonded complexes. This indicates that halogen bonding significantly influences the CI dynamics of the C–I bond.

Quantum mechanical calculations reveal that the CF3I···N2 and CF3I···CO complexes adopt C3v symmetry, with the C–I axis aligned linearly, and N2/CO acting as electron donors in σ halogen bonding. In contrast, the CF3I···C2H4 complex adopts a T-shaped configuration, with C2H4 serving as an electron donor in π halogen bonding, as shown in Figure C (Table S2 for detailed data). These structural findings are consistent with those of Herrebout, Legon, and their co-workers, who demonstrated the halogen bonding capabilities of unsaturated hydrocarbons, N2, and CO through infrared, Raman, or rotational spectroscopy combined with theoretical calculations. ,, The electrostatic potential maps reveal a region of positive potential along the C–I axis at the iodine end of the CF3I molecule, while negative potential regions are observed at the nitrogen end of the N2 molecule and at both ends of the CO molecule, indicating the presence of σ halogen bonds in the CF3I···N2/CO complexes (Figure S3). The interaction energies of the halogen bonds are ranked as follows with regard to their magnitudes: CF3I···C2H4 (−1094.73 cm–1) > CF3I···CO (−592.70 cm–1) > CF3I···N2 (−433.45 cm–1) > CF3I···OC (−281.61 cm–1). The more negative the interaction energy, the more stable the interaction is. Based on stability predictions, it is anticipated that the dominant structure of the CO-bonded complex is CF3I···CO, rather than CF3I···OC in the beam experiments. The magnitude of calculated interaction energy for the CF3I···O2 complex is significantly smaller than those of the CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes (Figure S4), which implies that the CF3I···O2 complex is unlikely to maintain stability for the photodissociation studies under the present experimental conditions.

The PESs of CF3I in Figure A conducted in this work reveal that the three states, X̃1A1, 3Q1, and 1Q1, are degenerate at the dissociation region and are associated with the ground state of I­(2P3/2). In contrast, in the PESs of CF3I···C2H4 complex in Figure A, it is noted that these initially degenerate states are split into two distinct groups: the lower-energy X̃1A1 state and the higher-energy 3Q1 and 1Q1 states. This splitting occurs because one component of iodine’s 5p orbital, specifically 5p z , is more closely associated with C2H4 than the other components, 5p x and 5py, which are the nonbonding orbitals of the complexes. The intermolecular interaction between iodine and C2H4 in the complexes and dissociation products results in a higher energy for the 5p z orbital relative to 5p x and 5p y (Tables S3 and S4). Consequently, this leads to the splitting of the degenerate states (Table S5). Similar splitting patterns are observed for CF3I···N2 and CF3I···CO complexes (Figure S5). Further analysis of the spin–orbit eigenvectors in the dissociation products of I···X (X = N2, CO and C2H4) (Figure S6 and Tables S6–S10), reveals that for the X̃1A1 state, the electronic configuration of iodine in the dissociation product is 5p x 2 5p y 2 5p z 1. In contrast, for the 3Q1 and 1Q1 states, the electron configurations are combinations of 5p x 1 5p y 2 5p z 2 and 5p x 2 5p y 1 5p z 2. The energy differences between the two groups are shown at the dissociation limit of fast C–I bond breaking in the halogen-bonded complex, which follow the order: CF3I···C2H4 (6026.94 cm–1) > CF3I···CO (1516.30 cm–1) > CF3I···N2 (546.23 cm–1) > CF3I···OC (366.66 cm–1). The dissociation of the halogen-bonding complex proceeds through two steps: the initial rapid cleavage of the C–I bond (I), followed by the subsequent collision dissociation of the halogen bonding interaction of I···X (II). The process II is driven by the significant kinetic energy release in process I along the repulsive states, ultimately generating the I and I* products, which are then detected.

Effects of Halogen Bonding as a Function of Excitation Wavelength

Images of I and I* products resulting from the photodissociation of CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes were recorded as a function of wavelength in the range of 272–282 nm, near the CI region, as shown in Supporting Information and Figure S7. The signal for the excited-state I* products from halogen-bonded complexes diminishes at longer wavelengths than 282 nm. The radius of the inner ring in the CF3I···C2H4 complex is smaller than those in the CF3I···N2 and CF3I···CO complexes at a fixed excitation wavelength. Notably, while the excited-state I* products from both CF3I and the halogen-bonded complexes maintain strong parallel angular distributions, the I products from the complexes undergo a significant transition in angular distribution from perpendicular to parallel as the wavelength decreases. These observations offer critical insights into the role of the CI region in modulating the photodissociation dynamics of halogen-bonded complexes, underscoring the sensitivity of the reaction pathways to both wavelength and molecular environment.

The speed distributions of I and I* products derived from the images at 273.77 and 273.53 nm are shown in Figure A and B. The speed distributions of I products for the carrier gases N2, CO, and C2H4 exhibit two prominent peaks: a fast peak at 1000 m/s, resulting from the photolysis of isolated CF3I, and a slower peak at 720 m/s for CF3I···N2/CO, and 600 m/s for CF3I···C2H4. Similarly, the speeds of I* products from the halogen-bonded complexes are also lower than those from CF3I, with the I* speed from the C2H4-bonded complex being lower than those from the N2 and CO-bonded complexes. Additional speed distributions of I and I* from the dissociation of CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) at various photolysis wavelengths are provided in Figure S8.

The energy required for the 3Q1/1Q1 repulsive states to dissociate the C–I bond in the I channel is represented by the C–I bond dissociation energy, D(C–I). Theoretical analyses yield the following values for D(C–I): CF3I···C2H4 (23322.97 cm–1) > CF3I···CO (20281.75 cm–1) > CF3I···N2 (18784.40 cm–1) > CF3I···OC (18531.31 cm–1) > CF3I (18046.63 cm–1). Upon C–I bond dissociation, the maximum total kinetic energy of the resulting products can be predicted using energy conservation and recoil momentum conservation (see Supporting Information for details). In the case of halogen-bonded C–I dissociation, the breaking of C–I bond drives the iodine atom toward N2/CO/C2H4, leading to the rupture of the halogen bond D(I···X, X = N2, CO, and C2H4). This process results in a kickback velocity to the iodine and the acceleration of N2/CO/C2H4. This kickback velocity can also be predicted based on energy and momentum conservation, with the constraint that the final velocity of N2/CO/C2H4 is equal to or exceeds that of the iodine products (see Supporting Information for details). The positions of maximum velocity of the iodine products resulting from the D(C–I) and D(I···X, X = N2, CO, and C2H4), as determined by our calculations using equations S1–9 in the Supporting Information, are indicated by the vertical dashed lines in Figures A and B. These positions are consistent with the observed photolysis signals, providing solid evidence for dissociation arising from the calculated complex pathways.

The angular distribution of products following photodissociation by linearly polarized laser is described by the equation 1 I(θ)=I0[1+βP2(cos⁡θ)] where θ is the angle between the polarization direction of the laser and the recoil direction of the product, and β is the anisotropy parameter, which weights the contribution from the second Legendre polynomial P 2(cos θ). The value of β, ranging from −1 to 2, provides insight into the relative contributions of parallel and perpendicular transitions. A β value of −1 corresponds to a purely perpendicular transition, while a β value of 2 indicates a purely parallel transition followed by rapid dissociation. The angular distributions of I and I* at λ = 273.77 and 273.53 nm are shown in Figure C and D. Notably, the β value for CF3I photolysis is relatively high at 1.0, but it decreases significantly for the halogen-bonded complexes: to 0.2 for N2, −0.1 for CO, and −0.2 for C2H4. This decrease in β values suggests a reduced contribution from the parallel transition of the 3Q0+ state (Additional angular distributions of I and I* from the dissociation of CF3I···X with X = N2, CO, and C2H4, along with β fits at various photolysis wavelengths, are provided in Figure S9 and Table S11).

The angular distribution of I* products in the halogenated complexes remains predominantly parallel and largely unaffected by collisions with N2, CO, or C2H4 molecules, similar to the distribution observed in CF3I. The preservation of the angular distribution of I* indicates that the collision between I* and N2, CO, or C2H4 have minimal impact on the angular distribution of the I* fragments. This observation can be attributed to the rapid dissociation of the C–I bond along the strong repulsive state, which occurs on a time scale too short for the second collision to impart substantial directional changes. As the C–I distance changes, the approach of I toward N2, CO, or C2H4 generates collision energy primarily along the C–I bond axis, leading to the rupture of the halogen bond in the same direction. This process subsequently causes the I/I* fragment to recoil with a relative velocity opposite along the initial flying direction. The observed reduction in the speed of I or I* fragments in the halogen-bonded complexes further supports this interpretation.

Effects of Halogen Bonding on CI Dynamics

The I:(I + I*) branching ratio from the photolysis of CF3I has been measured as approximately 0.1 in the wavelength range of 248–283 nm in earlier experiments. , In the current study, the relative I:(I + I*) branching ratios for the halogen-bonded complexes are determined by comparing the signals of I and I* between the complexes and isolated CF3I molecules, and using the I: I* branching ratio from the photolysis of CF3I as a scaling factor, as shown in Figure A (more details are shown in Supporting Information and Table S12). From Figure A, it is evident that halogen bonding leads to a notable enhancement in the I:(I + I*) branching ratio, particularly at longer wavelengths near CIs from 0.10 ± 0.01 to 0.71 ± 0.13 with a trend CF3I···C2H4 > CF3I···CO/OC > CF3I···N2 > CF3I. As the wavelength exceeds 280 nm, the branching ratio approaches 1, as the I* dissociation signal from the halogen-bonded complexes nearly disappears.

The β values for I products from CF3I and the halogen-bonded complexes are summarized in Figure B. The β values for CF3I are consistent with those reported in prior literatures. ,, The significantly smaller β­(I) values for the halogen-bonded complexes compared to those from CF3I are observed at UV excitation energies near the CIs, suggesting a change in CI dynamics due to halogen bonding. In contrast, the large positive β­(I*) values for all complexes and CF3I molecules indicate a purely parallel transition to the 3Q0+ state followed by rapid dissociation, implying a negligible contribution from the highest excited 1Q1 state in the studied energy range (Figure S9 and Table S11).

Within the present range of excitation energies, the electronic states 3Q1 and 3Q0+ are the only states that are both energetically accessible and fulfill the selection rule for optical transitions. Consequently, the ground-state I channel predominantly originates from the two electronic states, as illustrated by the PESs in Figure . The cross section to the 3Q1 state is denoted as σ­(3Q1), while the cross section to the higher excited 3Q0+ state is represented by σ­(3Q0+), with the curve crossing probability P for the transition 3Q0+ → 1Q1. The relative contributions from σ­(3Q1) and σ­(3Q0+), along with the probability P, can be directly derived from the measured I:(I + I*) branching ratios and β values from the photolysis of CF3I and halogen-bonded complexes, according to the equation (see Supporting Information for details), 2 Branch(I:I+I*)=(σ(Q31)+Pσ(Q30+))/(σ(Q31)+σ(Q30+)) 3 β(I)=(σ(Q31)β⊥+Pσ(Q30+)β(I*))/(σ(Q31)+Pσ(Q30+))

These values are plotted at various wavelengths in Figure C and D. The value of β⊥ is regarded as −1 for fast dissociation of C–I bond along the repulsive state of 3Q1. The cross-section ratio of the 3Q0+ state to the 3Q1 state, represented by σ­(3Q0+):σ­(3Q0+ + 3Q1), is dominant for CF3I, slightly decreased for the N2 and CO halogen-bonded complexes, and further reduced for CF3I···C2H4, which trend is particularly pronounced at longer wavelengths near 279 nm. In contrast, the curve crossing probability P(3Q0+ → 1Q1) is strongly enhanced in the presence of N2 and C2H4 compared to isolated CF3I, particularly at longer wavelengths near the CI. The presence of CO exhibits a significantly enhanced curve crossing probability at longer wavelengths, comparable to that of N2, while at shorter wavelengths, the curve crossing probability is markedly lower than that influenced by N2. This discrepancy may be attributed to the effects of the two halogen bonding modes associated with CO. Overall, the pronounced increase in P leads to a significantly greater contribution to the I channel from the 3Q0+ state, despite its relatively smaller cross section. These results underscore the critical role of noncovalent interactions in modulating CI dynamics and photodissociation pathways.

While calculating the full-dimensional PESs for the CF3I···X (X = N2, CO, and C2H4) complexes is challenging, one-dimensional spin–orbit coupled diabatic PESs provide valuable insights. The curve crossing probability, P(3Q0+ → 1Q1), can be estimated using the Landau–Zener model for diabatic states: 4 P=1−exp(−2πV122ℏ|ΔF|vcc) where V 12 is the coupling term between diabatic potentials (in this case the 3Q0+ and 1Q1 states), ΔF is the difference in the slopes of the PESs at the crossing point, and v cc is the radial velocity at the crossing point. A lower v cc generally leads to a higher P, as the system spends more time near the crossing point, increasing the likelihood of an adiabatic transition. Taking CF3I···C2H4 as an example, as indicated by the purple data in Figure D, decreasing λ increases v cc, thus reducing P. The highest curve-crossing probability was noted for all respective complexes at the longer wavelength of 279.79 nm, following the trend CF3I···C2H4 (0.35 ± 0.04) > CF3I···CO/OC (0.20 ± 0.03) > CF3I···N2 (0.18 ± 0.04) > CF3I (0.07 ± 0.04), as shown in Figure E. The halogen bonding increases the curve-crossing probabilities to the ground-state products by a factor of 3 to 5 compared to the isolated case. Meanwhile, for all the halogen-bonded complexes, despite minimal variation in RC–I at the crossing, the potential energy at the crossing point is significantly increased due to halogen bonding, as shown in Figure E, following the order: CF3I···C2H4 (35871.96 cm–1) > CF3I···CO (34180.95 cm–1) > CF3I···N2 (33961.78 cm–1) > CF3I···OC (33181.20 cm–1) > CF3I (31958.32 cm–1). Higher crossing energies correlate with lower velocity at the crossing point (v cc) and increased crossing probabilities (P), particularly at photon energies near the CI region. This relationship quantitatively explains the high crossing probabilities observed in the halogen-bonded complexes compared to CF3I across a range of photon energies, particularly at energies close to the crossing point. The data presented in Figure E demonstrates that the energy at the crossing point substantially increases with the strengthening of halogen bonding interactions, a trend that aligns with the observed increase in the curve-crossing probabilities and the I:(I + I*) branching ratios near CIs.

Conclusions

In summary, our study provides the first experimental and theoretical evidence that halogen bonding of varying strengths significantly influences conical intersection dynamics in the CF3I system, leading to substantial changes in product energy and angular distributions, as well as branching ratios. The understanding of CF3I photodissociation dynamics in the N2, CO, and C2H4 surrounding molecules has significant implications for environmental chemistry and atmospheric dynamics, particularly its impact on ozone interactions. Building on these insights, the present study introduces a novel approach to achieving “soft chemical control” in photochemistry and femtochemistry by utilizing halogen bonding interactionsboth σ-type and π-typebetween target molecules and Lewis bases. This strategy not only deepens our fundamental understanding of halogen bonding but also underscores its potential as a powerful tool for steering chemical processes at the molecular level.

Percutaneous endocardial alginate–hydrogel injection in the treatment of heart failure: First‐in‐human study
Authors: Bo Wang, Shuo Wang, Chao Gao, D Scott Lim, Rutao Wang, Xin Meng, Ying Liu, Cun‐jun Zhu, Yoshinobu Onuma, Yunbing Wang, Patrick WJC Serruys, Runlin Gao, Randall J Lee, Ling Tao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsDespite the potential of alginate hydrogel intramyocardial injections in the treatment of heart failure (HF), minimally invasive implantation techniques remain scarce. This study evaluated the safety and feasibility of percutaneous transcatheter endocardial alginate hydrogel injection (TEAi), facilitated by novel implants and a dedicated catheter‐based device, in patients with HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).Methods and ResultsThis first‐in‐human study enrolled HFrEF patients [New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III–IV and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤35%]. The primary endpoint was the incidence of procedure‐ or device‐related serious adverse events (SADEs) at 30 days. Secondary endpoints included the device success rate, HF hospitalization at 6 months, and change from baseline to 6 months post‐procedure in the following parameters: LVEF as assessed by MRI; NYHA functional class; 6 min walk test distance (6MWT); the quality of life assessed by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Heart Failure Questionnaire (KCCQ); and serum N‐terminal prohormone of B‐type natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP) level. Pre‐ and post‐procedural biomechanical analysis was also evaluated. Ten patients successfully underwent TEAi with no SADEs at 30 days. There was one death and two HF hospitalizations at 6 months. At 6 months, LVEF improved from 17.7% ± 3.8% to 24.9% ± 11.2% (P = 0.021), end‐systolic volume decreased from 297.5 ± 67.9 mL to 264.8 ± 101.4 mL (P = 0.029), and KCCQ scores increased from 49.7 ± 3.9 to 79.0 ± 8.07 (P = 0.008). No statistically significant changes were observed in end‐diastolic volume, NT‐proBNP and 6MWT at six months compared with the baseline. Biomechanical analysis revealed a reduction in peak left ventricular end‐diastolic wall stress (6.5 ± 1.1 kPa vs. 5.9 ± 1.3 kPa, P = 0.043).ConclusionsTEAi is feasible and safe for the treatment of HFrEF, warranting further randomized, efficacy clinical trials.Keywords: alginate–hydrogel, percutaneous intramyocardial injection, heart failure

Introduction

Intramyocardial injection of biomaterials has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of heart failure (HF) with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). 1 Although the therapeutic mechanism remains elusive, the primary contribution of biomaterials could be providing structural support to the thin left ventricular (LV) wall, thereby reducing wall stress and constraining the progressive chamber dilation. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Among the polymers evaluated as potential candidates in preclinical studies, 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 alginate hydrogels are promising biomaterials due to their bioinert, non‐thrombogenic and non‐immunogenic properties. 1 , 13 , 14

Despite encouraging findings from preclinical studies, the clinical application of alginate hydrogels in HFrEF patients has yielded mixed results. The PRESERVATION I trial evaluated the intracoronary delivery of an alginate–hydrogel mixture in patients with ST‐segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) following primary percutaneous coronary intervention but failed to demonstrate significant changes in LV geometry or reduction in major cardiovascular events. 15 In addition, an associated rise in stent thrombosis highlighted potential risks of the intracoronary approach. Conversely, the AUGMENT‐HF study, which examined the intramyocardial injection of Algisyl‐LVR™ hydrogel (LoneStar Heart, Inc., USA) via thoracotomy in patients with advanced HF, reported improved exercise capacity and clinical symptoms. 11 , 16 These results were confounded by concomitant coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and a surgical complication rate of 25%. Thus, a percutaneous transcatheter approach for endocardial alginate–hydrogel injection is an attractive alternative, offering potential advantages such as better patient tolerance, lower periprocedural risks and shorter recovery times. 13 To date, few alginate–hydrogel products have been evaluated for transcatheter delivery in either preclinical or clinical trials. This scarcity of options arises from inherent biomechanical characteristics such as rapid gelation and high viscosity, which challenge the feasibility of percutaneous delivery. Additionally, determining the most effective injection patterns for these treatments remains an unresolved issue. By utilizing computational cardiac models, numerous preclinical studies have demonstrated that injecting alginate–hydrogel into the mid‐LV wall in a sequential circumferential pattern acts as a constraining mechanism, mitigating adverse LV remodelling. 2 Despite these findings, the clinical application of this specific transcatheter endomyocardial alginate hydrogel injection (TEAi) approach within the limited structural space of the left ventricle remains to be explored in a clinical trial setting.

To address these challenges, we have developed XDROP™ alginate hydrogel, which has high stiffness and well shear‐thinning properties. 17 This hydrogel can be more easily delivered via catheter due to its reduced viscosity under increased thrust force. We also introduced a dedicated catheter‐based injection system, EndoWings™ (Hangzhou Deke MedTech Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China), featuring a stable base and multi‐directional adjustment capabilities. This system allows us to perform endocardial injections in a circumferential pattern within the LV mid‐wall. Our group has published a single case report on TEAi. 18 This study reports the procedural feasibility and safety results of the first‐in‐human clinical trial on TEAi in HFrEF patients. Additionally, we employ a three‐dimensional (3D) finite element (FE) model derived from the cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMRI) of HF patients as a personalized tool to assess the impact of this treatment on LV wall stress.

Methods

Study design and patient population

A prospective, non‐randomized, single‐arm, phase I trial (NCT04781660) was conducted at Xijing Hospital, China. This study aimed to investigate the safety and feasibility of multiple consecutive circumferential endomyocardial injections of XDROP™ alginate hydrogel into the mid‐LV by the dedicated catheter‐based system EndoWings™ (graphical abstract). The trial was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Xijing Ethics Committee (reference number KY20202120‐F‐1), and all patients provided written informed consent. Eligibility criteria for the study included HF patients with severely reduced ejection fraction (EF ≤ 35%, as determined by screening transthoracic echocardiography) and a New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class III or IV who were stable on guideline‐directed medical therapy for a minimum of 1 month before enrolment but with HF symptoms. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Supporting information methods.

Device

The mechanical characterization and design features of the XDROP™ alginate hydrogel and EndoWings™ (Deke MedTech, Hangzhou, China) have been previously published. 17

TEAi

Before the procedure, we assessed the thickness of the patients' LV wall using preoperative CT and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), as recommended in the literature. 18 The minimum thickness at the injection site was >6 mm at the end‐diastolic (ED) phase. The total number of injections for each patient depended on the individual patient's target area and avoided overlapping injection sites. The procedure was carried out under general anaesthesia. We used the papillary muscles as anatomical landmarks and performed injections under transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) guidance. After the procedure, patients were transferred to the cardiac care unit and continuously monitored for persistent ventricular arrhythmias via ECG. Additionally, 24 h Holter monitoring was conducted at the 6 month follow‐up.

Outcome

The primary endpoint was the incidence of serious adverse device events (SADE) within 30 days, defined as any device or procedure‐related complications, including, but not limited to, death, stroke, cardiac injury, vascular injury and cardiac or surgical intervention. The secondary endpoints included the incidence of HF hospitalization; device success rate, which was defined as the catheter reaching the intended implant location, injecting the hydrogel into the LV wall with 6–14 sites, and successful withdrawal of the catheter system; procedure success, which was defined as achieving device success and the absence of any complications related to TEAi both intraprocedural and within 30 days post‐procedure; and the change from baseline to 6 months post‐procedure in the following parameters: LV ejection fraction (LVEF) evaluated by CMRI; NYHA functional classification assessment; 6 min walk test distance (6MWT); the quality of life using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Heart Failure Questionnaire (KCCQ) score; and serum N‐terminal prohormone of B‐type natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP) level. An independent clinical event committee (CEC) adjudicated all events.

Statistical methods

All the safety and exploratory efficacy were summarized with descriptive statistics. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. Continuous variables were depicted as means with standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges. Paired Student's t‐tests and Wilcoxon signed‐rank tests were used to compare changes in continuous variables with or without normal distributions, respectively. Missing data were reported, and analyses were performed based on available data. Statistical significance was set at two‐sided P < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Baseline patient characteristics

Between January 2021 and April 2022, a total of 21 patients underwent screening for eligibility. Of these, 10 patients met the criteria and were subsequently enrolled in the study (Figure S2). The mean age of the enrolled patients was 49.5 ± 10.2 years, with four females and six males (Table 1). The aetiology of HF was non‐ischaemic cardiomyopathy in 7 patients (70%) and ischaemic cardiomyopathy in 3 patients (30%). On average, these patients had been experiencing HF for 6.9 years. All of them were classified as NYHA Class III or IV. The mean values for pre‐injection LVEF, LVEDV, and LVESV were 17.2 ± 3.9%, 352.6 ± 71.3 mL, and 296.2 ± 64.1 mL, respectively. Three out of 10 patients (30%) presented with severe functional mitral regurgitation (SFMR). All patients had an optimal medical therapy (OMT) score greater than 3, indicating their adherence to OMT for HFrEF. 19 The patients' baseline and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Periprocedural TEAi outcomes

The TEAi procedure achieved technical success in all patients (100%) without SADE (Table 2). The mean (SD) number of intramyocardial injections is 10 ± 1.8, and the mean (SD) total volume of alginate–hydrogel was 2.7 ± 0.5 mL (Table 1). Figure 1 illustrates a representative case of TEAi, demonstrating the administration of multiple consecutive circumferential endomyocardial injections of XDROP™ into the mid‐LV wall under fluoroscopy, along with corresponding TEE images. Subsequent laboratory examinations revealed elevated but not statistically or clinically significant troponin I at 7 days (Figure 2, panel A). All patients were discharged within 7 days of the procedure.

Safety outcomes

All patients completed the 30 day follow‐up without procedural or device‐related complications (Table 2). A 39 year‐old patient diagnosed with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) was in NYHA Class IV. This patient had a low baseline LVEF of 12.6% and concomitant SFMR. He experienced acute HF hospitalization due to pulmonary infection, which ultimately led to death at 6 months post‐injection. The cause of death was adjudicated by CEC to be worsening HF unrelated to TEAi.

Two patients experienced HF hospitalization during the 6 month follow‐up secondary to influenza pneumonia, which was successfully treated with intermittent administration of intravenous diuretics (Table 2). These events were not adjudicated as definitively related to the alginate–hydrogel or procedure.

No sustained ventricular arrhythmia was observed during follow‐up (Table 2). The numbers of non‐sustained ventricular arrhythmias (NSVT) did not significantly differ before and after TEAi (Figure 2, panel B).

Secondary outcomes

At 6 months, nine patients had completed the follow‐up, excluding the patient who died (Table 2). The CMRI results showed a statistically significant improvement in LVEF (17.7 ± 3.8% vs 24.9 ± 11.2%, P = 0.021, Figure 2, panel C) and a reduction in ESV (297.5 ± 67.9 mL vs. 264.8 ± 101.4 mL, P = 0.029, Figure 2, panel D) from baseline to 6 months, while EDV did not change significantly (359.5 ± 71.9 mL vs. 341.6 ± 67.9 mL, P = 0.086, Figure 2, panel E).

Between baseline and the 6 month follow‐up, the KCCQ score increased (49.7 ± 3.9 vs. 79.0 ± 8.07, P = 0.008, Figure 2, panel F). The 6MWT showed that the walk distance post‐injection was greater than at baseline with average changes of 30.7 m, but not statistically different (339.7 ± 37.6 m vs. 370.4 ± 64.8 m, P = 0.184, Figure 2, panel G). NT‐proBNP decreased by 31.2% at 6 months, although the change was not statistically significant [945.6 (750.5–1255) vs. 650.3 (495.7–1090), P = 0.314, Figure 2, panel H]. However, the NYHA cardiac functional assessment improved significantly at 6 months, as demonstrated by the nine patients with follow‐up classified as Class II (Figure 3).

Distribution of wall stress in the LV wall of patients with HF

We used an FE model to investigate the distribution of LV wall stress in 10 patients before and after treatment (Figure. 4). The results showed that the mid‐LV free wall exhibited the highest wall stress burden compared to the apical and basal regions before injection. Following injection, while there was no significant change in the average ED wall stress (3.1 ± 0.5 at baseline vs. 2.9 ± 0.7 at 6 months, P = 0.128), the change in peak ED wall stress demonstrated statistical significance (6.5 ± 1.1 kPa vs. 5.9 ± 1.3 kPa, P = 0.043).

Distribution of left ventricular end‐diastolic wall stress in patients at baseline and 6 months post‐treatment. Distribution of ventricular wall stress for 10 patients at baseline and nine patients at post‐treatment (the left side represents the epicardial surface, and the right side represents the endocardial surface). From the baseline images, areas of increased wall stress (red colour) can be observed in a circumferential distribution in the mid‐left ventricular wall beneath the endocardial region. Among the nine patients who completed the 6 month follow‐up, seven patients showed a reduction in wall stress, while two patients exhibited worsening wall stress.

Seven patients exhibited >5% improvement in EF from their baseline, with four patients (002, 003, 008 and 009) showing >15% reduction in ESV from baseline, accompanied by a reduction in wall stress. Of the two patients (003, 004) hospitalized for HF, patient 003 experienced symptom relief after short‐term intravenous diuretic treatment and improved CMRI parameters and wall stress at 6 months. However, another patient (004) showed progression of cardiac remodelling, as evidenced by CMRI parameter deterioration and increased wall stress. Additionally, patient 010 displayed improved EF (from 12.2% to 16.7%) after treatment but increased EDV and increased wall stress. A review of the medical histories of patients (004, 006 and 010) revealed a history of SFMR, relatively lower EF (15.8%, 12.6% and 12.2%, respectively), and higher baseline wall stress burden.

Discussion

This study represents the first clinical trial investigating percutaneous intramyocardial injections of alginate–hydrogel for the treatment of HF. Our findings provide supportive evidence for the preliminary safety and feasibility of mid‐LV wall intramyocardial injections of high‐stiffness XDROP™ alginate–hydrogel, administered in a circumferential distribution pattern by using dedicated EndoWings™ catheter‐based systems in HFrEF patients, confirmed by the procedural success in all patients and the absence of complications.

Previous study indicates that stiffer polymers more effectively reduce wall stress than softer hydrogels, emphasizing the role of mechanical support in stabilizing the weakened LV wall. 5 Meanwhile, preclinical studies also suggest that circumferential biomaterial placement in the mid‐ventricle better reduces myofiber stress and improves LV geometry than other injection patterns, potentially explaining the varied outcomes seen in different studies. 2 , 20 , 21 A recent Phase I study evaluated the safety and feasibility of transendocardial injection of an extracellular matrix hydrogel (VentriGel, Ventrix Bio, San Diego, CA, USA) in patients with LV dysfunction post‐myocardial infarction (MI). However, VentriGel's low elastic modulus, although beneficial for facilitating catheter delivery, limits its role as mechanical support for the LV wall. 22 The XDROP™ employed in this study exhibited a higher stiffness, ranging from 2 to 10 kPa, which was notably higher than that of VentriGel and comparable to Algisyl‐LVR™. Moreover, XDROP™ was gelled in vitro before injection, which ensured homogeneity of the physical properties of the hydrogel at each injection site and thus better performance of physical support and constraint of the ventricular wall.

In addition, this FIH study addresses several challenges associated with performing TEAi. First, we formulated XDROP™ hydrogel with well shear‐thinning properties, enabling a decrease in viscosity in response to increased injection force, facilitating transcatheter delivery. This enables efficient administration through EndoWings™, requiring less than 40 N of force, comparable to prior catheter‐based systems. 23 Second, we confirmed XDROP™'s mechanical property restoration post‐injection, with less than 3.00% change in both storage (G′) and loss modulus (G″) after injection via EndoWings™. 17 This confirms the excellent compatibility between the catheter and the polymer. Following the injection, we observed the rapid solidification of the hydrogel, a vital feature for maintaining its structural integrity and function within the dynamic cardiac environment. Importantly, no catheter occlusion occurred in vitro or clinically. Third, a circumferential distribution of the hydrogel along the mid‐LV free wall was achieved, which was mainly dependent on the excellent manoeuvrability of the EndoWings™.

Additionally, this study aimed to explore the number of injection sites and dosage for TEAi. According to our injection protocol, we took meticulous care to optimize the distribution of the hydrogel within the target area while avoiding the overlap of injection sites. Although our study revealed a lower average number of injection sites and a lower mean total dose compared with the AUGMENT‐HF study, we still observed improvements in some patients. This is consistent with previous studies that have indicated that shown even a minor fractional change in myocardial wall volume can have a substantial impact on cardiac mechanics, reducing wall stresses and enhancing cardiac function. 24 This study lays the foundational methodology for TEAi in the next phase of clinical research.

While this Phase 1 study was not explicitly designed to evaluate efficacy, we noted signs of improvement in most patients. These included enhancements in EF and ESV, as well as improved patient‐reported outcomes. Notably, patients with SFMR typically exhibit poor outcomes, and SFMR often signals advanced HF, with the resulting increased LV volume burden accelerating detrimental cardiac remodelling. Consequently, relying exclusively on TEAi poses a challenge in delaying or reversing the progression of HF in such cases. These observations are instrumental in identifying suitable candidates for future clinical studies.

Numerous FE models have been employed to investigate the impact of injectable materials on cardiac wall stress. 24 , 25 Using personalized wall stress analysis in this study, we found that the wall stress was predominantly concentrated in the LV‐mid wall. This finding substantiates our injection protocol, which strategically targets this area. Post‐injection observations revealed that while the increase in EF was modest for some patients, there was a notable decrease in wall stress. In addition, we observed that patients who experienced cardiogenic events had higher baseline ventricular wall stress and showed no improvement in wall stress post‐injection, a finding that contrasts sharply with those who experienced improvements in cardiac function. This implies that wall stress may serve as a valuable predictor of clinical outcomes and a tool for evaluating treatment efficacy.

Study limitations

The primary objective of this study was to assess the feasibility and safety of TEAi, not to provide definitive evidence of its efficacy in the treatment of HF. As such, this was an exploratory study that lacked the statistical power to rigorously evaluate efficacy endpoints. Therefore, caution should be exercised in interpreting the efficacy of treatment. It is important to note that the follow‐up period for this study was restricted to 6 months. The long‐term impact of TEAi on cardiac function remains uncertain, necessitating further research to assess its durability and sustained benefits over time. Although we reconstructed personalized 3D cardiac structures using cardiac MRI, we relied on population‐based cardiac fibre orientation and ED pressure assumptions due to a lack of precise physiological data. While this introduced some degree of uncertainty into personalized biomechanical analysis, the limitations are consistent with findings from previous studies.

Conclusions

Our study is the first to assess the feasibility and safety of TEAi in HFrEF patients, demonstrating that the EndoWings™ system could deliver alginate–hydrogel XDROP™ in a circumferential distribution pattern to the intended myocardial region and has the potential to improve patients' cardiac function, HF symptoms, quality of life and biomechanical metrics at 6 months. Larger‐scale clinical trials are warranted to determine the efficacy of this innovative treatment.

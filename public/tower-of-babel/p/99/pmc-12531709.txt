Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of l‐cystine produced with Escherichia coli DSM 34232 for all animal species (Wacker Chemie AG)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L Innocenti, Joana P Firmino
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of l‐cystine produced with a genetically modified strain of Escherichia coli K‐12 (DSM 34232) as a sensory feed additive (functional group: flavouring compounds) for all animal species. The l‐cystine manufactured by fermentation with E. coli DSM 34232 did not give rise to any safety concern regarding the genetic modification of the production strain. No viable cells or DNA of the production strain were detected in the final product. The use of l‐cystine produced by fermentation with E. coli DSM 34232 in feed was considered safe for the target species when supplemented at concentrations up to 25 mg/kg complete feed. The FEEDAP Panel considered that the use of l‐cystine in animal nutrition is safe for the consumers of products from animals receiving the additive, and for the environment. Regarding user safety, the additive was not considered a skin and eye irritant, nor a skin sensitiser. As l‐cystine is used in food as a flavouring compound, it is expected that it can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.Keywords: DSM 34232, efficacy, Escherichia coli K‐12, flavourings, l‐cystine, safety, sensory additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of a feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from Wacker Chemie AG 2 for the authorisation of the additive consisting of l‐cystine produced with Escherichia coli DSM 34232, when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: nutritional additives, functional group: aminoacids, their salts and analogues and category: sensory additives, functional group: flavouring compounds). During the assessment, the applicant withdrew the application as a nutritional additive. 3

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 28 June 2023, and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2023‐00436. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 10 June 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of l‐cystine produced with E. coli DSM 34232 when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.4 ).

Additional information

l‐Cystine produced with E. coli DSM 34232 has not been previously authorised as a feed additive in the European Union. l‐Cystine produced by hydrolysis of keratin from poultry feathers (3c391) 4 or by fermentation using a different production strain (3c392) 5 is currently authorised for its use in all animal species as a nutritional and/or sensory additive.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 6 in support of the authorisation request for the use of l‐cystine produced with Escherichia coli DSM 34232 as a feed additive.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 7 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 8 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on the pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 07 March to 28 March 2025, for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 03 July to 03 October 2024, for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of l‐cystine in animal feed. 9

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of l‐cystine produced with E. coli DSM 34232 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 10 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a); Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c); Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019); EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2021); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023); and Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

l‐Cystine (≥ 98.5% on an as is basis) produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain of E. coli K‐12 (DSM 34232) is intended to be used as a sensory additive (functional group: flavouring compounds) in feed for all animal species and categories.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the production microorganism

The production microorganism is a genetically modified derivative of E. coli strain K‐12 that is deposited in the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH (DSMZ) with accession number DSM 34232. 11

The taxonomic identification of the production strain DSM 34232 as E. coli was confirmed by average nucleotide identity (ANI) based on the analysis of its whole genome sequence (WGS) data. 12 The results of this analysis showed an ANI value of 99.96% with the reference strain E. coli K‐12 ■■■■■. The strain harbours one plasmid ■■■■■ integrated in the genome.

E. coli K‐12 is well characterised, its safety (non‐pathogenicity) has been documented (Kaper et al., 2004) and its ineffectiveness in colonising the human gut has been reported.

The susceptibility of the production strain to antimicrobials was tested by broth microdilution against the battery of antibiotics recommended by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018). 13 All the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were equal to or fell below the corresponding cut‐off values for ‘Enterobacteriaceae’ (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018), except for ■■■■■. Therefore, the strain is considered to be susceptible to all the tested antibiotics, but resistant to ■■■■■.

The WGS data of the production strain, including the plasmid, were interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes in the Resfinder and AMRFinder databases using EFSA thresholds (EFSA, 2021). The search identified two hits exceeding the EFSA thresholds: ■■■■■. 14 ■■■■■ (see Section 3.1.1.1 ). Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the strain harbours two acquired AMR genes.

The WGS data of the production strain were also interrogated for the presence of known toxins and virulence factor genes against the Virulence Factor database (VFDB). No genes of concern were identified. 15

Manufacturing process

l‐Cystine is produced by fermentation with E. coli DSM 34232 as the production strain, which produces l‐cysteine ■■■■■. 18 l‐Cysteine is exported via the cell membrane into the fermentation broth and oxidises to l‐cystine. ■■■■■ An antifoaming agent ■■■■■ 19 is added as needed.

After fermentation, the fermentation broth is inactivated by pasteurisation, and the l‐cystine crystals are separated by decantation. l‐Cystine crystals are then dissolved ■■■■■, filtrated ■■■■■, precipitated, centrifuged and dried.

The data provided showed compliance with the specifications set by the applicant. The total amount of identified material on an as is basis was > 99%. The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the amounts of the detected impurities do not raise safety concerns.

The presence of viable cells of the production strain was investigated in three batches of l‐cystine, in triplicate. 23 ■■■■■ A positive control was included. No colonies were detected on the plates. Therefore, it can be concluded that no viable cells of the production strain were found in the product.

The presence of DNA from the production strain was investigated in three batches of l‐cystine, in triplicate. 24 Samples ■■■■■ of l‐cystine were analysed in triplicate by polymerase chain reaction (PCR). ■■■■■ The analysis showed that no DNA of the production strain was detected in any of the batches.

The antimicrobial activity of the final product was tested with a bacterial growth inhibition assay using culture supernatant of the production strain and showed no inhibition of bacterial growth of Bacillus spizizenii (ATCC 6633). 25

Conditions of use

l‐Cystine is intended to be used as a sensory additive in complete feed for all animal species at a maximum recommended level of 25 mg/kg complete feed.

Safety

Safety of the production microorganism

The parental strain of the production strain E. coli DSM 34232 is K‐12 ■■■■■, which is considered to be safe. The genetic modifications to obtain the production strain were performed to increase the production of l‐cystine. The production strain carries acquired AMR genes for ■■■■■ and for ■■■■■ and is considered to be susceptible to all the tested antibiotics, but resistant to ■■■■■. However, no viable cells of the production strain nor its DNA were detected in the final product. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel considers that the additive does not raise any safety concerns regarding the genetic modification of the production strain.

Safety for the target species, consumers and the environment

The additive l‐cystine is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified E. coli K‐12 derivative (DSM 34232) and no safety concerns were identified for the production strain (see Section 3.2.1 ), the fermentation process and its residues/metabolites. Moreover, the additive l‐cystine is highly purified (≥ 98.5% l‐cystine and < 1% unidentified material on as is basis). Concerns on the use of the additive would not derive from the l‐cystine, which is considered safe, but may arise from residues of the fermentation process/production strain remaining in the final product. Since the levels proposed for the use of l‐cystine as a flavouring compound (25 mg/kg complete feed) are substantially lower than the animal requirements as nutrient, the FEEDAP Panel considers that l‐cystine produced with E. coli DSM 34232 is safe for the target species when used as a flavouring compound.

The endotoxin activity was below the limit of quantification (5 μg/g, which correlates with 50,000 IU/g). These values are very low when compared with ca. 1,000,000 IU/g commonly found in feedingstuffs (Cort et al., 1990).

The absorption and metabolic fate of l‐cystine in the organism is well known. The amino acid l‐cystine, supplemented to feed, will be incorporated into proteins of tissues and/or products of animal origin, and any of its potential excess will be metabolised and excreted. Therefore, the protein composition of tissues and products of animal origin will not be affected using l‐cystine in animal nutrition. Therefore, the Panel considers that the use of the additive in animal nutrition is safe for the consumer.

Neither E. coli DSM 34232 nor its recombinant DNA was detected in the final product. The final product does not pose any environmental safety concern associated with the genetic modification. The amino acid l‐cystine is a physiological and natural component of animals and plants. When consumed, it will be absorbed, and the non‐absorbed fraction will be incorporated into the intestinal microbial mass and excreted as such. The use of the additive l‐cystine in animal nutrition would not lead to any localised increase in the concentration in the environment. It is concluded that the use of l‐cystine as a feed additive does not represent a risk to the environment.

Safety for the user

Effect on respiratory system

Based on the highest dusting potential measured value (see Section 3.1.3 ), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

Users can suffer from occupational respiratory disease depending on the level of endotoxins in air and dust (Rylander, 1999; Thorn & Kerekes, 2001). Although no occupational exposure limits have been set in the EU for inhalable endotoxins, the Dutch Expert Committee on Occupational Safety recommended a health‐based occupational exposure limit for inhalable endotoxins of 90 EU/m3 (8‐hour time‐weighted average) (DECOS, 2010). To reduce the risk, the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of the users to bacterial endotoxins potentially present in the additive should be minimised.

Effect on eyes and skin

The applicant submitted a study on bovine corneal opacity and permeability, a study on acute eye irritation in rabbits, a lymph node assay, an in vitro skin corrosion study and acute dermal toxicity studies in rabbits and rats. The studies were performed with two different batches of l‐cystine produced with a different but related E. coli K‐12 derivative ■■■■■. The Panel considers that the test item is representative of the product under assessment.

The skin irritation potential of l‐cystine was tested in a study performed according to OECD Testing Guideline (TG) 404, 26 which showed that the additive is not a skin irritant (UN GHS No Category).

The skin corrosion potential of l‐cystine was tested in a study performed according to the OECD TG 431, 27 which showed that the additive is not corrosive to the skin (UN GHS No Category).

An acute dermal toxicity study with l‐cystine was performed according to the OECD Guideline 402. 28 The results showed that there were no signs of toxicity (UN GHS No Category).

The eye irritation potential of l‐cystine was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 405, 29 which showed that the additive is not an eye irritant (UN GHS No Category).

The eye irritation potential of l‐cystine was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 437, 30 which showed that l‐cystine is not corrosive/not a severe irritant to the eyes (UN GHS No Category).

The skin sensitisation potential of l‐cystine was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 429 and the Method B42 Skin sensitisation of Commission Regulation (EC) No 440/2008, 31 which showed that the additive is not a skin sensitiser (UN GHS No Category).

Conclusions on the safety for the user

Based on the information available, the additive l‐cystine should not be considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.

Efficacy

As l‐cystine is used in food as a flavouring compound, it is expected that it can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

CONCLUSIONS

The production strain E. coli DSM 34232 harbours two acquired AMR genes. However, no viable cells of the production strain or its DNA were detected in the final product. Therefore, the additive does not raise any safety concerns regarding the production strain.

The use of l‐cystine produced by fermentation with E. coli DSM 34232 in animal nutrition at the proposed conditions of use is considered safe for the target species, the consumers and for the environment.

Regarding user safety, the additive is not considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.

As l‐cystine is used in food as a flavouring compound, it is expected that it can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

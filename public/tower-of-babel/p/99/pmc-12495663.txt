Impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on tuberculosis patients’ healthcare-seeking behaviors and doctors’ diagnosis delays
Authors: Xinling Shi, Zhenpeng Guo, Qiao Liu, Qiang Zhang, Xiaolong Zhang, Qin Wen, Yuting Wang, Jianming Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSince the onset of the Corona Virus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, global healthcare systems have faced unprecedented challenges. To control the spread of COVID-19, China implemented multiple nonpharmaceutical interventions (NPIs). These measures have significantly altered people’s healthcare-seeking behaviors. This study aims to investigate the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on healthcare-seeking behaviors and doctor s’ diagnostic delays among pulmonary tuberculosis patients, providing systematic information for future responses to pandemics of other “X diseases”.MethodsWe selected Changzhou and Suzhou as research sites in eastern China. Pulmonary tuberculosis patients diagnosed from 2018 to 2022 were recruited as study subjects. Based on the occurrence of the COVID-19 pandemic and the measures implemented at the study sites from 2020 to 2022, the study period was classified into the prepandemic, initial outbreak, regular management, strict NPI implementation, regular management, and transition periods. Patient and diagnosis delays were calculated. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to estimate the risk of delay using Cox regression models.ResultsA total of 6,764 tuberculosis patients from Changzhou and 15,140 tuberculosis patients from Suzhou who were diagnosed from 2018 to 2022 were included in the analysis. Compared with the prepandemic stage (2018–2020), the risk of patient delay did not decrease significantly during the pandemic of COVID-19 (P > 0.05), but the risk of diagnosis delay showed different trends at different stages throughout the pandemic. The risk of diagnosis delay decreased in Changzhou (HR: 1.18, 95% CI: 1.12–1.24), but it increased in Suzhou (HR: 0.92, 95% CI: 0.88–0.96).ConclusionsThe impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on tuberculosis varied by region and population. It is necessary to respond in a timely and precise manner according to the scope of the disease pandemic, accessibility of medical resources, and population literacy to reduce the adverse impacts of other disease epidemics on tuberculosis prevention and control.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24621-z.Keywords: Tuberculosis, COVID-19, Patient delay, Diagnosis delay, Five periods

Introduction

Since the outbreak of the Corona Virus Disease 2019 (COVID-19), global healthcare systems have faced unprecedented challenges [1–4]. In response to this crisis, healthcare sectors promptly adjusted their service priorities, reallocating medical resources to prioritize the control and prevention of COVID-19. These urgent measures have significantly altered people’s healthcare-seeking behaviors and doctors’ diagnosis and treatment practices [5, 6]. A Canadian study reported significant changes in intensive care unit utilization during the COVID-19 lockdown, with reduced admission rates and shorter patient stays [7]. A study in the Democratic Republic of Congo described that outpatient visits for chronic conditions such as diabetes and hypertension significantly declined during the COVID-19 lockdown but rebounded after this lockdown was lifted [8]. An Indonesian study revealed that the number of tuberculosis (TB) patients undergoing routine check-ups decreased from 50,000 to approximately 3,000 in June 2020 due to concerns about the check-up process [9].

TB presented distinct healthcare challenges during the COVID-19 pandemic [10–14]. Patients hesitated to seek medical care due to the fear of infection and the reallocation of medical resources to combat COVID-19, which reduced the medical attention that was given to TB patients. Moreover, the implementation of nonpharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) imposed additional restrictions and inconveniences on the healthcare process, further influencing patients’ decisions to seek healthcare [15]. A study in Portugal demonstrated that during the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown, there was a significant delay in the diagnosis and treatment of TB patients [16]. Investigative studies show that the COVID-19 pandemic has affected the medical-seeking behavior of TB patients in China, causing various degrees of delays in diagnosis and follow-ups. However, research has also indicated that the rate of TB patient delay did not change significantly after 2019 [17, 18]. We assume that the reasons for the disparities in the above-mentioned findings are associated with the different control measures implemented and control durations during the disease outbreak, as well as the different impacts of the pandemic on the behaviors of TB patients and doctors in the same period.

To better address the direct and indirect effects of public health emergencies similar to COVID-19, we urgently need to deepen our understanding of the patterns of patient delay in seeking healthcare and doctors’ delays in diagnosis during the COVID-19 pandemic and take measures to ensure fairness in health and access to healthcare services.

Methods

Study areas and data collection

We selected Changzhou and Suzhou in Jiangsu Province, China, as the research areas. Both cities are located in the southern Jiangsu region and share similar levels of economic development, which helps control for potential economic influences on our study results. Pulmonary TB patients diagnosed in Changzhou and Suzhou from 2018 to 2022 were recruited as study subjects. We obtained TB patients’ data from the TB Information Management System, including sex, age, occupation, address, pathogen testing results, treatment history, local residents or migrant population status, time of symptom onset, time of seeking healthcare, and time of diagnosis. The information on the COVID-19 situation was sourced from the Jiangsu Provincial People’s Government website (https://www.jiangsu.gov.cn/).

Definition of delay

Patient delay was defined as the time between the onset of symptoms and the first medical visit, measured in days. A delay exceeding 14 days was classified as a patient delay [19]. Diagnosis delay was defined as the interval between the date of first presentation to a professional healthcare provider and the TB diagnosis, measured in days. A delay exceeding 14 days was classified as a diagnosis delay [20, 21].

Based on the occurrence of the COVID-19 pandemic and the prevention and control measures implemented at the study sites from 2020 to 2022, the study period was classified into different stages for each location as follows:

(1) Changzhou: prepandemic period (January 1, 2018 – January 21, 2020); period 1, initial outbreak (January 22, 2020 – March 31, 2020); period 2, regular prevention and control (April 1, 2020 – March 12, 2022); period 3, strict NPI implementation (March 13, 2022 – March 25, 2022); period 4, regular prevention and control (March 26, 2022 – December 7, 2022); and period 5, transition period (December 8, 2022 – December 31, 2022).

(2) Suzhou: prepandemic period (January 1, 2018 – January 21, 2020); period 1, initial outbreak (January 22, 2020 – March 31, 2020); period 2, regular prevention and control (April 1, 2020 – February 13, 2022); period 3, strict NPI implementation (February 14, 2022 – May 5, 2022); period 4, regular prevention and control (May 6, 2022 – December 7, 2022); and period 5, transition period (December 8, 2022 – December 31, 2022).

Statistics

Categorized variables are presented as frequencies and percentages, whereas nonnormally distributed continuous variables are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR). Group comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney test, Kruskal-Wallis H test, or chi-square test. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using univariate and multivariate Cox regression models. An HR > 1 indicated that the risk of delay decreased. Additionally, based on relevant literature, sensitivity analyses were conducted using different cutoff values for delays (Changzhou: 21 days for patient delay, 5 days for diagnosis delay; Suzhou: 8 days for both patient delay and diagnosis delay) [20, 22, 23]. The significance level was set at two-sided α = 0.05. All the statistical analyses were performed using R software for Windows version 4.2.0 (https://www.r-project.org/).

Results

The COVID-19 situation

Before the transition period, Changzhou reported 87 confirmed COVID-19 cases, and Suzhou reported 289 cases (Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Fig. 1).

General characteristics of the study subjects

As shown in Fig. 1, there was a decrease in TB notifications in Suzhou and Changzhou following the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The average annual number of reported TB cases in Changzhou during 2020–2022 decreased by 21.7% compared with that during 2018–2019, and in Suzhou, it decreased by 16.5% (Supplementary Table 2). From 2018 to 2022, there were 6764 newly diagnosed TB patients in Changzhou. Among these patients, 4866 (71.9%) were male, 4185 (61.9%) were under 60 years of age, 3735 (55.2%) had bacteriologically positive smear, 3501 (51.8%) were farmers, 6075 (89.8%) were newly diagnosed patients, and 1458 (21.6%) were from migrant populations. In Suzhou, 15,140 TB patients were reported during the study period. Among these patients, 10,618 (70.1%) were male, 11,218 (74.1%) were under 60 years of age, 8145 (53.8%) had bacteriologically positive smear, 4354 (28.8%) were farmers, 14,284 (94.3%) were newly diagnosed patients, and 7426 (49.0%) were from migrant populations (Table 1).

Patient and diagnosis delays in Changzhou during the COVID-19 pandemic

From 2018 to 2020, the median time to seek medical care for TB patients in Changzhou was 15.0 days (IQR: 4.0–36.0), with a median diagnosis time of 5 days (IQR: 1.0–9.0). During this period, 51.3% of patients experienced patient delay (over 14 days), and 15.6% experienced diagnosis delay (over 14 days). From 2020 to 2022, the median time to seek medical care remained at 15.0 days (IQR: 5.0–39.0), whereas the median diagnosis time decreased to 4 days (IQR: 0.0–7.0). During this period, 52.1% of patients experienced patient delay, and 9.7% experienced diagnosis delay. There was no significant difference in patient-related delays between 2018–2020 and 2020–2022 (P > 0.05), but the proportion of patients with diagnosis delay was significantly different (P < 0.05) (Table 2). Similar patterns could be observed in other time periods (Table 3).

Patient and diagnosis delays in Suzhou during the COVID-19 pandemic

From 2018 to 2019, the median time for TB patients to seek medical care was 8 days (IQR: 1.0–24.0), with a median diagnosis time of 8 days (IQR: 0.0–28.0). During this period, 35.8% of patients experienced patient delay, and 37.6% experienced diagnosis delay. From 2020 to 2022, the median time to seek medical care for TB patients was 7 days (IQR: 1.0–25.0), with a median diagnosis time of 10 days (IQR: 1.0–29.0). During this period, 36.1% of patients experienced patient delay, and 41.6% experienced diagnosis delay. There was no significant difference in the proportion of patients experiencing patient delay in Suzhou between 2018 and 2020 and 2020–2022 (P > 0.05). However, the proportion of patients experiencing a delay in diagnosis was significantly different (P < 0.05) (Table 4). Similarly, there was no significant difference in patient delay in other periods (P > 0.05), whereas diagnosis delay differed significantly (P < 0.05) (Table 5).

Impact of the pandemic on patient and diagnosis delay

Compared with the pre-pandemic stage (2018–2020), the risk of patient delay in Changzhou did not change significantly during the COVID-19 pandemic (P > 0.05), but the risk of diagnosis delay showed different trends at different stages throughout the pandemic. The HRs (95% CIs) of patient delay were 1.27 (1.01 ~ 1.58), 0.98 (0.91 ~ 1.05), 1.34 (0.85 ~ 2.11), 0.87 (0.78 ~ 0.97), and 0.85 (0.59 ~ 1.24) in periods 1–5, respectively. The HRs (95% CIs) of diagnosis delay were 1.02 (0.85 ~ 1.22), 1.18 (1.12 ~ 1.25), 1.34 (0.93 ~ 1.93), 1.50 (1.39 ~ 1.63), and 1.93 (1.49 ~ 2.49) in periods 1–5, respectively (Table 6). Multivariate Cox regression analysis yielded results consistent with the univariate analysis after adjusting for sex, age, occupation, bacteriological results, treatment type, and floating type (Table 6).

Impact of the pandemic on patient and diagnosis delays among TB patients in different groups

The results of the multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that the risk of patient delay among different groups of TB patients in Changzhou did not change significantly during the entire COVID-19 pandemic compared with that in the pre-pandemic stage (2018–2020) (P > 0.05), but different trends were observed in different stages of the pandemic. In the male, farmer, and age ≥ 60 years subgroups, there was no significant Change in patient delay across periods 1–5. In the female, nonfarmer, and age < 60 years subgroups, the risk of patient delay significantly increased in period 4. In contrast, the risk of patient delay significantly decreased in the nonfarmer subgroup during period 1 (P < 0.05). Diagnosis delay significantly decreased in the bacteriologically positive patient group in 2020–2022 and periods 3–5, as well as in the bacteriologically negative patient group in 2020–2022 and periods 2, 4, and 5 (P < 0.05) (Table 8).

Sensitivity analyses

We further conducted a sensitivity analysis, using 21 days and 5 days as the cutoff values for determining patient and diagnosis delays, respectively, in Changzhou and 8 days as the cutoff value for both patient and diagnosis delays in Suzhou. In Changzhou, except for the Change in patient delay risk in period 1, the findings were consistent with those of previous analyses that applied 14 days as the cutoff value (Supplementary Table 3). In Suzhou, the results were consistent with those of previous analyses in which 14 days was used as the cutoff value (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

Compared with the pre-pandemic stage, the risk of patient delay did not change significantly during the COVID-19 pandemic, but the diagnosis delay decreased in Changzhou and increased in Suzhou. This discrepancy might reflect differences in public health awareness and TB screening practices between the two regions. Additionally, Changzhou reported fewer COVID-19 cases and experienced a more substantial decrease in TB notifications (a 21.7% decrease) compared to Suzhou, which had more COVID-19 cases and a smaller reduction in TB notifications (a 16.5% decrease). These differences suggest that Changzhou may have implemented more effective COVID-19 control measures, which helped sustain TB diagnosis despite the challenges brought by the pandemic. Furthermore, our stratified analysis indicated that bacteriological results did not significantly impact the difference in diagnosis delay between the two regions. This implies that other factors, such as regional public health responses and screening practices, may play a more crucial role in affecting diagnosis delay.

During the five pandemic-control periods, Suzhou consistently showed no significant Changes in the risk of patient delay and diagnosis delay, except for an increase in diagnosis delay during period 2. In contrast, Changzhou exhibited more variability: risk of patient delay decreased in period 1, increased in period 4, and showed no significant Change in periods 2, 3, and 5. The risk of diagnosis delay in Changzhou decreased in periods 2, 4, and 5,with no significant Changes in periods 1 and 3. Through comparative analysis, we found differences in the patterns of delays in TB patients seeking medical services and obtaining diagnosis at different stages of the COVID-19 pandemic and in different regions.

During an epidemic, medical resources are limited, and various control measures are implemented, which affect the behavior of patients and doctors. Previous studies have shown that the COVID-19 pandemic increased patient delay [24–27]. Due to transportation restrictions, disruptions in TB services, and other factors, patients affected with TB may delay seeking healthcare services [17]. A nationwide survey revealed that patients without COVID-19 continued to delay seeking healthcare even when the incidence was low [28]. However, another study found no significant increase in TB patient delay since 2019, which is consistent with our findings [18]. The disparity may be attributed to the accessibility of medical resources in different regions during the pandemic period, the health literacy of the population, the severity of disease, or the implementation of NPIs. Thus, we cannot arbitrarily assume that a disease pandemic will affect or will not affect the diagnosis and treatment of TB without considering the regional context.

The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on patient delays may vary by time, region, or population. In the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, the risk of patient delay for TB patients was found to increase in early 2020 compared with the pre-COVID-19 period [29]. However, a study in Tianjin reported a decrease in patient delay [30]. Our findings indicate that in early 2020, the risk of patient delay decreased in Changzhou, whereas no significant change was observed in Suzhou. Additionally, we found that during the COVID-19 outbreak (period 3), there was no significant Change in patient and diagnosis delay for TB patients in either Changzhou or Suzhou. However, during period 4, patient delay increased in Changzhou but not in Suzhou. These differences may be influenced by regional public health policies, medical resource allocation, socioeconomic conditions, and the extent of the COVID-19 spread [17, 31]. The differences in the results between period 1 and period 4 in Changzhou may be attributed to differences in COVID-19 prevalence, public awareness, and control measures during these periods. The specific reasons for these differences warrant further investigation. Our study also revealed that in Changzhou, during period 1, patient delay primarily decreased in the nonfarmer subgroup, whereas in period 4, patient delay increased in the female, age < 60 years, and nonfarmer subgroups. This further confirms that the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on patient delay varies by population.

Previous studies have shown that the risk of diagnosis delay may vary during the COVID-19 pandemic. Some studies have indicated an increase in the diagnosis delay during the pandemic [32, 33], whereas others have not reported significant changes [34, 35]. A study that was conducted in Africa reported decreased TB diagnosis delay during the COVID-19 pandemic, which is consistent with our findings in Changzhou [36]. This result may have occurred due to pandemic-driven reforms such as streamlined diagnostic processes, enhanced TB awareness and screening, and better allocation of medical resources, which likely improved early detection and reduced diagnosis delays.

This study explores the patterns of delay among TB patients seeking medical services and obtaining diagnosis at different stages of the COVID-19 pandemic and in different regions. This study provides a longitudinal perspective on the pandemic’s impact on patient and diagnosis delays, helping to better understand the evolving challenges that were faced during the course of the pandemic. Notably, similar impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on healthcare seeking and diagnostic delays have been reported for other infectious and non-infectious diseases [37, 38], underscoring the importance of building resilient health systems capable of maintaining essential services during public health emergencies. However, our study has several Limitations. First, it was a retrospective analysis based on monitoring data. The number of undetected TB cases was much greater in the pandemic period, making the underestimated delay longer in 2020–2022 than in 2018–2020. Second, we did not evaluate the role of social and environmental factors, making it impossible for us to comprehensively estimate the impact of the disease pandemic on the diagnosis and treatment of TB.

Conclusions

In this study, we selected two cities in China as the study sites and analyzed the alterations in healthcare seeking and healthcare services for TB patients across different regions and stages of the COVID-19 pandemic. The results demonstrated that patient delay did not change significantly, but the diagnosis delay decreased in Changzhou and increased in Suzhou. Our study findings indicated that the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on TB patients differed by region and population. Therefore, it is essential to formulate timely responses based on the scope of the disease, the accessibility of medical resources, and the literacy of the population. Such measures are crucial for reducing the adverse impact of other disease epidemics on TB prevention and control.

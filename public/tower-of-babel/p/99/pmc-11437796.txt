A multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion lightweight network for Alzheimer’s disease diagnosis
Authors: Qiongmin Zhang, Ying Long, Hongshun Cai, Siyi Yu, Yin Shi, Xiaowei Tan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveAlzheimer’s disease (AD) is a type of neurological illness that significantly impacts individuals’ daily lives. In the intelligent diagnosis of AD, 3D networks require larger computational resources and storage space for training the models, leading to increased model complexity and training time. On the other hand, 2D slices analysis may overlook the 3D structural information of MRI and can result in information loss.ApproachWe propose a multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion lightweight network for automated AD diagnosis. It incorporates a multi-branch lightweight backbone to extract features from sagittal, axial, and coronal view of MRI, respectively. In addition, we introduce a novel multi-slice attention fusion module, which utilizes a combination of global and local channel attention mechanism to ensure consistent classification across multiple slices. Additionally, a multi-view personalized fusion module is tailored to assign appropriate weights to the three views, taking into account the varying significance of each view in achieving accurate classification results. To enhance the performance of the multi-view personalized fusion module, we utilize a label consistency loss to guide the model’s learning process. This encourages the acquisition of more consistent and stable representations across all three views.Main resultsThe suggested strategy is efficient in lowering the number of parameters and FLOPs, with only 3.75M and 4.45G respectively, and accuracy improved by 10.5% to 14% in three tasks. Moreover, in the classification tasks of AD vs. CN, AD vs. MCI and MCI vs. CN, the accuracy of the proposed method is 95.63%, 86.88% and 85.00%, respectively, which is superior to the existing methods.ConclusionsThe results show that the proposed approach not only excels in resource utilization, but also significantly outperforms the four comparison methods in terms of accuracy and sensitivity, particularly in detecting early-stage AD lesions. It can precisely capture and accurately identify subtle brain lesions, providing crucial technical support for early intervention and treatment.Keywords: Alzheimer’s Disease, sMRI, Lightweight CNN, Multi-slice attention fusion, Multi-view personalized fusion

Introduction

Currently lacking a viable treatment, Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a gradual and irreversible brain illness. AD is assumed to start with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) [1]. Every 6 s, a new patient is thought to advance from MCI to AD, and by 2030, there will be 78 million AD patients worldwide [2]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for an early and accurate method of detecting AD to enable timely interventions.

Since magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) offers several advantages over other imaging modalities, it is commonly accepted as the primary technique for AD screening. MRI stands out for its non-invasive nature, as it does not require the use of radioactive tracers, ensuring a safer procedure for patients. Additionally, MRI allows for the generation of diverse tomographic views, enabling a comprehensive analysis of the brain. Structural MRI (sMRI) is generally used to observe organ structure changes, such as atrophy and deformation [3]. At the present, numerous deep learning technologies used to accurately detection features relevant to the diagnosis [4–6].

In recent years, unsupervised learning, weakly supervised learning, and semi-supervised learning has been widely used in image analysis. Ren et al. [7] proposed a simple unsupervised representation learning model, employing data augmentation techniques to construct a triple-view architecture and introducing triple-view loss to effectively mitigate model collapse issues. To address the challenge of small sample in Histopathology whole slide images (WSIs) classification tasks, Zhang et al. [8] introduced pseudo-bags to augment bag sizes based on weakly supervised learning and utilized feature distillation based on instance probability derivation. Beyond medical image analysis, semi-supervised learning methods also widely applied on food category classification. A food quality assessment model based on data mining is proposed based on double layer rough-refinement optimization by Zhou et al. [9], to realize the standardization and consistency of food quality assessment. Despite various models or methods using weakly supervised learning mechanisms for image analysis, applying these methods in practical medical image analysis remains challenging. The main reason is that the distribution of medical data in the real world does not provide valuable data patterns to the models [10, 11].

Some researchers have employed supervised learning methods to diagnose AD by extracting 3D features from MRI images. A 3D convolutional neural network (CNN), developed by Hosseini et al. [12] was presented for the purpose of predicting AD, capable of capturing AD biomarkers and adapting to various datasets. Lam et al. [13] used a interpretable 3D grid-attention CNN to extract sMRI features, which accurately predict individual’s age and whether or not they have AD. And a deep 3D-dense connected convolutional networks is used to diagnose AD and MCI by Wang et al. [14]. These methods extract potential 3D features and capture anatomical brain changes from MRI images. Nevertheless, the extraction of high-level features from 3D MRI images is quite challenging and necessitates a huge number of parameters.

Many studies use sMRI slices instead of 3D MRI for AD recognition in order to overcome this restriction. Hon et al. [15] suggested a transfer learning-based approach, achieving high accuracy even when using a limited number of training images for AD detection from sMRI slices. A deep CNN based pipeline is proposed by Farooq et al. [16], for employing MRI slices to diagnose AD. Tanveer et al. [17]adopt VGG16 [18] architecture as the backbone for feature extraction from sagittal view of sMRI slices, achieving an accuracy of 85% for the CN vs. AD classification task on a small dataset comprising only 50 samples per class.

To address the challenge of inconsistent classification across multiple slices of the same subject, researchers have explored multi-slice networks. Zhang et al. [19] utilized the Concat method in the MFEFnet to fuse features from different modalities within each slice, while employing the Add method to fuse the weighted slice features for final classification prediction. However, concerns regarding increased computational complexity or potential information loss due to these straightforward concatenation or addition methods have been raised. Dai et al. [20] addressed this by utilizing the MS-CAM to obtain the weights for feature fusion across multiple branches. Similarly, Chen et al. [21] designed attention modules from channel and spatial respectively, in order to derive certain channel and spatial weights. Previous research findings indicate that the sagittal view highlights lateral components, the axial view provides a comprehensive image of all brain lobes, and the coronal view shows a portion of the anterior and posterior brain tissues [22]. Incorporate the significance of different views in the fusion process is crucial, as it enables multi-view feature fusion techniques to deliver a more comprehensive and accurate analysis. Mishra et al. [23] combined details from all viewpoints by applying fusion methods to features taken from three different views of the brain. Drawing inspiration from VGG16, Mehmood et al. [24] developed a SCNN model for classifying dementia stages. However, these methods treat different views equally, disregarding the variations in lesion size, shape, and appearance among different views. Therefore, during the fusion process, it is vital to take into account the significance of different views.

Earlier studies have demonstrated that using simple convolution models with fewer parameters and FLOPs (floating-point operations per second) can effectively address the challenges posed by limited labeled datasets [25, 26]. Additionally, these models can also be used with devices that have low processing power. In this study, we propose a multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion lightweight network for AD diagnosis. Our contributions are given below:

The multi-branch lightweight 2D model can effectively extract underlying representations in resource-constrained environments.

Methods

In clinical medical imaging diagnosis, the high dimensionality of sMRI combined with limited datasets, poses a significant challenge for CNNs to effectively extract features, this process also results in a significant rise in computational costs and the number of parameters. while also leading to a substantial increase in the number of parameters and computational costs. To solve these issues, we propose a lightweight network incorporating multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion. Illustration of the framework is shown in Fig. 1: (1) Multi-branch feature extraction module with Depthwise Convolution (DWConv) and Pointwise Grouped Convolution (PGConv), which is implemented to extract sagittal, axial, and coronal view information from sMRI. (2) Multi-slice attention fusion module, which merges features from multiple slices within the same view. (3) Multi-view personalized fusion module, which assigns appropriate weights to the multi-view data of each subject.

Framework of multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion lightweight network. The first module is multi-branch feature extraction module, where I(s), I(a), and I(c) respectively represent slices from sagittal, axial, and coronal views. These slices serve as inputs to the backbone and after processing through the feature extraction module, yield three results: f(s), f(a), and f(c). Then, f(s), f(a), and f(c) are passed through the second module: multi-slice attention fusion module, which uses attention mechanisms to integrate features from multiple slices of the same view. Finally, the third module is multi-view personalized fusion module. This module merges the predicted values obtained from each single view by applying different weights to them, and the results are fused again to produce the final diagnostic

Multi-branch feature extraction module

Multi-branch feature extraction module consists of three branches, corresponding to the sagittal, axial, and coronal views, respectively. In each branch, the same feature extraction backbone is employed, consisting of 2D Conv, Unit1, and Unit2. Initially, extract the initial feature from I(s), I(a), and I(c) using convolution with kernel size 3×3. Then, Unit1 and Unit2 are designed to further extract slice features. Finally, high-level features f(s), f(a), and f(c) are obtained by 2D Conv.

Specifically, Unit1 and Unit2 follow a sequential connection pattern (2, 3, 3, 3), where 2 represents one Unit1 and one Unit2 combination, and 3 represents one Unit1 and two Unit2 combinations. As shown in Fig. 2, both Unit1 and Unit2 use PGConv and DWConv operations. PGConv extracts the grouped features separately to reduce computational cost, while DWConv enhances the lightweight nature of the feature extraction backbone by replacing traditional convolution. Notably, to address the potential lack of interaction between channel information across different groups when DWConv follows PGConv, a channel shuffle (CS) is introduced between the two operations to evenly shuffle channels among different groups. Furthermore, concatenation and addition operations within Unit1 and Unit2 are employed to maintain model stability and mitigate degradation risks. These operations play a crucial role in reducing over-parameterization and mitigating the risk of over-fitting.

Units of feature extraction module, serving as the backbone network for each branch. Apart from the first and last layers which employ traditional convolutions, the middle section comprises combinations of Unit1 and Unit2. Each combination begins by processing the output from the previous stage through Unit1, which includes operations like PGConv, CS, and DConv (with stride = 1). Simultaneously, AvgPool is used to map input features to enhance feature learning. Subsequently, 1 or 2 instances of Unit2 are applied consecutively to further extract features from Unit1’s output. In the multi-view personalized fusion module, the network employs a decision-level feature fusion strategy to fuse the prediction results from the sagittal, axial, and coronal views. It does this by automatically assigning appropriate weights to the corresponding views of the same subject. This approach facilitates comprehensive utilization of multi-view information in sMRI.

Materials and experiments

Datasets and implementation details

The AD neuroimaging initiative database (adni.loni.usc.edu) provides the sMRI images used in this study. Subjects with AD, MCI, and Cognitive Normal (CN) are included in the database. The primary aim of database is to investigate the potential use of serial MRI, Positron Emission Tomography (PET), other biological markers, clinical, and neuropsychological evaluations in monitoring the development of MCI and AD.

The preparation methods included correcting the anterior commissure-posterior commissure (AC-PC), removing the skull, and aligning the sMRI data to the standard space using the colin27 template. In this study, we used sMRI slices from a cohort of 1200 individuals, with 400 subjects representing each of the following categories: AD, MCI, and CN. To minimize information loss, For every subject, 10 consecutive intermediate slices were chosen from the three views, sagittal, axial, and coronal view respectively. Therefore, each category has 12,000 slices, as shown in Table 1, and the size of all images is set to 128×128.

In this study, all experiments were conducted under the same settings, utilising the PyTorch framework on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 graphics card and an Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-10500 processor operating at 3.10GHz with 8 gigabytes of random access memory (RAM). The model underwent 100 epochs of end-to-end training with a sample size of 30, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer and warm-up cosine learning rate with an initial value of 0.001 was used, the weight decay with value of 0.01 is utilized to suppress over-fitting, and the data split for model training and testing is 80% for training and 20% for testing.

The neural network’s complexity is evaluated by measuring the number of parameters and FLOPs, where a smaller value indicates a more lightweight model. The model’s classification performance is evaluated using performance evaluation measures, with higher values indicating better performance, such as accuracy (ACC), sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Each method’s efficacy and efficiency may be assessed by contrasting these parameters.

Contrastive models

In this study, we conducted a comparative analysis between the experimental results of our method and 4 lightweight ways to confirm the efficacy of our approach, including VGG16, MobileNet, GhostNet, and ShuffleNet. To ensure the fairness and comparability of the experiment, we reset VGG16, MobileNet, GhostNet, and ShuffleNet to extract features from sagittal, axial, and coronal views, respectively. After that, we fuse slice information from each view and combine the predictions from the three views for classification. Note that all experiments used the same training strategy and sMRI slices.

VGG16 employs tiny convolutional kernels to decrease the amount of network parameters, hence enhancing model performance via increased network depth.

MobileNet [27] is a network model that is designed to be lightweight. It does this by using depth-wise separable convolutions instead of regular convolutions. which can effectively extract features while reducing the number of parameters and FLOPs.

GhostNet [28] generates more feature maps by using a linear feature mapping method, which effectively obtains more feature maps with fewer parameters.

ShuffleNet [29] modifies the ResNet by incorporating CS, PGConv, and DWConv. These modifications help maintain a lightweight network architecture.

Results of the ablation experiments

According to the results, the Sagittal, Axial, and Coronal views are all useful for diagnosing AD, although they provide slightly different information. Specifically, the sagittal view model results indicate that in AD vs. CN and AD vs. MCI tasks, there was a modest increase in ACC and F1-score, suggesting that diagnosing AD based on the sagittal view may be crucial for decision-making. Furthermore, the Multi-view approach exhibits better performance compared to single sMRI views. These findings indicate that various sMRI views include complimentary information, and combining these views may enhance diagnostic accuracy. By comparing experimental results with and without CS, it can be found that shuffling channels between different groups enhances the exchange and flow of information between different channel features. This effectively improves the model’s ACC by 0.37% to 2.38%.

We explored the performance of the proposed framework incorporating the multi-slice attention fusion module and multi-view personalized fusion module. By replacing simple addition with the multi-slice attention fusion module, we validated the effectiveness of multi-slice fusion, resulting in promising performance with the Multi-view with SA approach. Additionally, introducing the Multi-view with PW, base on Multi-view with SA, enhanced the model’s performance, indicating the importance of personalized weighting for different views. Furthermore, the performance of Ours was further improved by introducing label consistency loss.

The experimental results consistently show that integrating the multi-slice attention fusion module, multi-view personalized fusion module, and label consistency loss produces the best results across all three classification tasks. The ablation experiment results further confirm the effectiveness of our method.

Limitations

While our approach has shown promising results in the classification tasks of AD vs. CN, AD vs. MCI, and MCI vs. CN, there are still some limitations that need attention.

One limitation is related to the selection of consecutive intermediate slices from the three views of each patient as experimental data. This approach may introduce noise or result in the loss of important information. It is crucial to use suitable methods in future research to reduce noise and improve the signal-to-noise ratio, hence guaranteeing the quality of the input data.

The multi-slice attention fusion module involves iteratively applying a set of operations on the slice feature maps, resulting in an increase in the model’s parameters and FLOPs. In order to enhance the efficiency of our method, it is essential to delve further into lightweight strategies that may minimize the computational cost of the attention feature fusion module.

Both the attention feature fusion module and the feature extraction module have an impact on how well the multi-view tailored fusion module performs. Improving the robustness of the multi-view personalized fusion module is crucial to enhance the final classification performance. Further research and enhancements are needed in this regard.

Considering the limitation that most patients may never go with an MRI scan, relying solely on MRI data for diagnosing AD is not always feasible. Therefore, in future work, we will explore multimodal diagnostic approaches that combine heterogeneous data such as MRI, CSF biomarkers, genetic data, and cognitive assessment, to improve the early diagnosis of AD.

Additionally, the absence of an external test set in this study may constrain the effectiveness and generalizability of the results. To enhance the reliability and applicability of the findings, future research should consider incorporating an external test set for further validation.

Conclusions

In this study, we have proposed a multi-slice attention fusion and multi-view personalized fusion lightweight network for AD diagnosis using multi-view sMRI. The multi-branch feature extraction module combines PGConv and DWConv, enabling efficient feature extraction from sagittal, axial and coronal views. We introduce a novel multi-slice attention fusion module that effectively merges multi-slice features of the same subject through global and local channel attention mechanisms. Additionally, the multi-view personalized fusion module asymmetrically emphasizes multiple views from the same subject by considering their varying contribution, and label consistency loss is employed to limit the prediction of label consistency among three views. Our method outperforms 4 other state-of-the-art approaches and single-view models, according to the experimental findings. Furthermore, with accuracy of 95.63%, 86.88%, and 85.00% in the AD vs. CN, AD vs. MCI, and MCI vs. CN tasks, respectively, the results of the comparison and ablation studies show that our method attained the highest accuracy. Comparing our method to other competitive lightweight networks, these results demonstrate the method’s potential as a promising alternative for improving AD diagnosis while requiring fewer parameters and FLOPs. Overall, our proposed method shows great potential for enhancing AD intelligent diagnosis, especially on hardware with constrained processing power. By providing better performance and outcomes, our approach can contribute to advancing patient care in AD diagnosis.

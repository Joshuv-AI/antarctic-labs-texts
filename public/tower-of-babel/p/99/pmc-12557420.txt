To the Editor:

Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) is an opportunistic respiratory tract infection caused by the fungus Pneumocystis jirovecii. PCP remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality in those infected with HIV before immune reconstitution. An effective HIV treatment and PCP prophylaxis strategy has led to a significant decrease in incidence and mortality [1]. PCP in non-HIV patients carries a higher mortality rate, quoted as 30–60% [2]. PCP incidence in the non-HIV population has been rising in European countries [1, 3–5]. Older age, pre-existing lung disease, use of glucocorticoids and concomitant immunosuppressive therapies are all risk factors for PCP [6]. Interstitial lung disease (ILD) subtypes commonly managed with immunosuppressive strategies include connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (CTD-ILD), nonfibrotic hypersensitivity pneumonitis and nonspecific interstitial pneumonia. Multiple studies recommend the development of guidelines for PCP prophylaxis in high-risk groups [1, 4, 5]. The most common prophylaxis regime is trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (TMP/SMX) 480 mg daily or 960 mg three times weekly. TMP/SMX is effective in preventing PCP in both HIV and non-HIV patients [3]. Side-effects such as skin reactions and cytopenias could negate the intended benefits. We report the results of a national multicentre survey to evaluate current specialist opinion on PCP prophylaxis in ILD patients.

We invited specialist centres within the UK to participate in the survey through a lead contact. Consultants, SAS (specialist, associate speciality and speciality) respiratory physicians and rheumatologists with a subspecialty interest in ILD were e-mailed a single-stage survey. The survey was created using Microsoft Forms and designed through an iterative process of review by ILD physicians. We posed 10 clinical scenarios (figure 1) asking specialists to rate their agreement with giving PCP prophylaxis. We used a Likert scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). We calculated the mean Likert scale score. Participants could justify ratings with free text. The clinical vignettes were fictional and generalised. All responses were anonymised, in part to avoid social desirability bias. This study followed a clinical governance discussion about such patients at our National Health Service Trust as part of a service evaluation.

27 centres participated. There were 63 responses from 58 respiratory physicians and five rheumatologists. All responders completed 100% of the survey. Figure 1 illustrates the survey statements and results.

When considering an ILD patient for immunosuppression, most physicians agreed with giving prophylaxis if there was a history of PCP infection (mean 4.27, 85.7% agree or strongly agree) or when commencing cyclophosphamide for CTD-ILD (mean 3.92, 61.9% agree or strongly agree). In patients commencing on rituximab (27.0% disagreed, 31.8% neutral and 41.2% agreed) and intravenous methylprednisolone (34.9% disagreed, 25.4% neutral and 39.7% agreed), heterogeneity of practice was apparent, with no majority opinion. There was variation of practice for patients on glucocorticoids, depending on the intended dose. In patients commencing on prednisolone 30 mg once daily for >4 weeks, 34.9% disagreed, 14.3% were neutral and 50.8% agreed with prophylaxis. Those on prednisolone equivalent of 15 mg once daily for >4 weeks, most respondents disagreed with giving prophylaxis (mean 2.35, 73.0% disagree or strongly disagree). In patients commencing mycophenolate, physicians disagreed with giving prophylaxis (mean 2.21, 77.7% disagreed or strongly disagreed) unless there was evidence of PCP DNA on bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) (mean 3.75, 68.2% agreed or strongly agreed). Some questioned the significance of the BAL results and favoured multidisciplinary team opinion with consideration for active PCP treatment first.

Our survey sampled current opinion in the UK on PCP prophylaxis in ILD, from 63 subspecialists who commonly treat patients with ILD. In patients commencing immunosuppression for ILD with a history of PCP infection or when using cyclophosphamide in CTD-ILD, a clear majority agreed with prophylaxis.

European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) guidance recommends PCP prophylaxis should be considered in patients with autoimmune inflammatory rheumatic diseases (AIIRDs) on high-dose glucocorticoids, especially with concomitant immunosuppressants [6]. EULAR could not make recommendations for PCP prophylaxis in individual diseases aside from when commencing on cyclophosphamide for ANCA-associated vasculitis [7].

Systemic glucocorticoid use is known to increase the risk of PCP but there is a lack of relative risk data for PCP in patients being treated for chronic lung disease [2]. We can infer that surveyed specialists believe that prednisolone <15 mg once daily equivalent for >4 weeks in ILD does not justify prophylaxis but that higher doses are more contentious. Observational evidence in AIIRD suggests with daily doses of >15–30 mg prednisolone equivalent for >2–4 weeks, prophylaxis is beneficial [6]. The same retrospective study identified 90% of their PCP cases occurred when a patient received >15 mg·day−1 prednisolone, in line with previous studies [8]. The American Thoracic Society advises to consider prophylaxis where prednisolone daily dose exceeds 20 mg daily for 1 month, especially if the patient is receiving cytotoxic or anti-tumour necrosis factor (TNF) drugs [9]. Anti-TNF drugs are independently associated with an increased risk of developing PCP [9]. A retrospective study looking at rituximab use in haematological disease, rheumatic disease and pre/post-solid organ transplantation demonstrated a favourable risk–benefit analysis for prophylactic TMP/SMX [10].

While guidelines for PCP prophylaxis in patients with HIV are specific and widely followed, there is lack of consensus on prophylaxis in most non-HIV patients with an immunocompromised state [2]. The lack of an evidence base to inform specific guidance in ILD patients likely accounts for the significant variations in opinion seen in this survey.

A limitation of the study includes the use of generalised scenarios with a particular focus on therapeutic drug-induced immunosuppression. The decision on PCP prophylaxis should be specific to the patient and the potential benefit versus risk must be considered. Fundamental host-related risk factors such as advanced age and poor nutritional status need recognition. Advanced age appears to be associated with increased Pneumocystis colonisation in the respiratory tract, thought to be related to immunosenescence [11]. In addition, advanced age is associated with poorer outcomes in non-HIV PCP [12, 13]. Protein energy undernutrition can be associated with impaired cellular immune response and occurs amongst older patients with associated frailty and/or chronic disease [14]. The role of immunodeficiency secondary to malnutrition and risk of PCP, however, remains unclear.

Adverse events with TMP/SMX use such as nausea, headache and rash are common, affecting 20% of patients [5]. Severe adverse events requiring permanent discontinuation, including cytopenias and dermatological reactions, are reported to occur in 3.1% of patients [2]. We should question the safest and most effective PCP prophylaxis regimen. EULAR acknowledges several studies evidencing that reduced dose TMP/SMX prophylaxis may also be effective with fewer adverse events [6]. A more recent study suggested reduced-dose TMP-SMX treatment for PCP may reduce adverse effects whilst maintaining similar efficacy [15]. Drug interactions such as methotrexate and trimethoprim need consideration. Alternative prophylaxis includes pentamidine nebulisation, dapsone or atovaquone [2].

We conclude there is a lack of evidence for PCP prophylaxis in ILD patients commencing immunosuppression. This study shows there is substantial variation in practice but also some unified views, which is promising for the development of consensus statements on the role of PCP prophylaxis. These results can assist in the design of future research studies to address best practice.

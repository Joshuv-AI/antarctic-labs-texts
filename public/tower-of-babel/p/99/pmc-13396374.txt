Hsa-circ_0081481-miR 3960-FBXO24 regulatory axis in non-obstructive azoospermia identifies potential biomarkers of spermatogenic failure
Authors: Emad Babakhanzadeh, Ali Khodadadian, Forouzan Esmaeili Dahaj, Hormoz Solatzadeh, Majid Nazari, Mahya Mozhdeh, Serajoddin Vahidi Mehrgardi, Mohammad hassan jafari, Mohammadreza Dehghani, Soudeh Ghafouri-Fard, Nasrin Ghasemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe etiology of non-obstructive azoospermia (NOA) remains largely idiopathic, emphasizing the urgent need for molecular biomarkers to predict sperm retrieval outcomes. Here, we integrate bioinformatic analyses of public transcriptomic data with experimental expression profiles to investigate a potential regulatory network involving hsa-circ_0081481, miR-3960 and FBXO24 in NOA. Seven GEO datasets were analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes, and FBXO24 was found to be a consistently downregulated gene in NOA. Upstream regulators and interacting non-coding RNAs were predicted, leading to the selection of miR-3960 (a microRNA targeting FBXO24) and hsa-circ_0081481 (a circular RNA predicted to sponge miR-3960). Subsequently, FBXO24, miR-3960 and hsa-circ_0081481 expression levels were measured in the plasma of 60 men with NOA (categorized by histopathology and sperm retrieval outcome) and 40 fertile controls. FBXO24 and hsa-circ_0081481 were significantly downregulated in NOA (especially in hypospermatogenesis and maturation arrest), whereas miR-3960 was significantly upregulated in these subgroups. Of note, hsa-circ_0081481 achieved excellent diagnostic performance (AUC 0.920) in distinguishing NOA cases with successful and failed sperm retrieval, respectively. miR-3960 also showed high accuracy (AUC 0.861) after its inverse expression pattern was taken into account, and FBXO24 showed moderate predictive value. Our findings suggest that the hsa-circ_0081481/miR-3960/FBXO24 axis is dysregulated in NOA and may serve as a novel trio of non-invasive biomarkers. This study provides new insights into the molecular mechanisms of spermatogenic failure in NOA and provides a basis for improved diagnosis and personalized fertility treatment.Keywords: Non-obstructive azoospermia, Has-circ_0081481, miR-3960, FBXO24Subject terms: Biomarkers, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Genetics, Medical research, Molecular biology

Introduction

Non-obstructive azoospermia (NOA) is one of the most severe forms of male infertility and accounts for around 10–15% of all infertile men1. It is characterized by severely impaired or absent spermatogenesis, even though there are no physical obstacles in the reproductive tract2. The etiology of NOA is diverse and includes genetic mutations, chromosomal abnormalities, endocrine dysfunction and developmental disorders3. However, in almost 75% of NOA cases4, the underlying molecular mechanism is idiopathic, emphasizing the urgent need for more in-depth molecular investigations.

Current therapeutic interventions—such as testicular sperm extraction by micro-dissection (micro-TESE) and subsequent intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)—have limited success rates (often below 50%)5. This limitation is largely due to the lack of reliable molecular biomarkers for early diagnosis, prognosis and prediction of sperm retrieval outcomes. Furthermore, the heterogeneous histology of NOA testes and the presence of isolated foci of spermatogenesis make it difficult to predict the success of sperm retrieval preoperatively6. A non-invasive predictive biomarker approach could save couples the financial and psychological burden of unnecessary surgery while improving success rates through better patient selection and customized treatment strategies.

In recent years, non-coding RNAs—especially circular RNAs (circRNAs)—have emerged as critical regulators of gene expression at the post-transcriptional level. CircRNAs are endogenous RNAs characterized by a covalently closed circular structure formed by back-splicing (joining a downstream splice donor to an upstream splice acceptor)7. In contrast to linear mRNAs, circRNAs lack 5′ caps and 3′ poly(A) tails, which makes them resistant to degradation by exonuclease and very stable in cells and body fluids. Functionally, circRNAs can act as “sponges” for microRNAs (miRNAs) by modulating the activity of specific miRNAs and thereby influencing downstream gene expression8. Such circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory axes form complex networks that play a role in numerous biological processes, including spermatogenesis9. Dysregulation of these networks can contribute to impaired sperm production and male infertility.

One potential regulatory pathway relevant to spermatogenesis failure is the hsa-circ_0081481–miR-3960– FBXO24 axis. FBXO24, a member of the F-box protein family, is involved in the ubiquitination and degradation of proteins, processes that are essential for cell cycle regulation and testicular function10–12. Meanwhile, miR-3960 has been associated with germ cell differentiation and proliferation13. We hypothesize that hsa-circ_0081481 may modulate the expression of FBXO24 by acting as a sponge for miR-3960, thereby influencing the spermatogenic process through this competitive endogenous RNA network.

Importantly, this study provides the first assessment of the hsa-circ_0081481/miR-3960/FBXO24 axis in the blood plasma of men with NOA. This molecular signaling pathway has not previously been studied in NOA, highlighting the novelty and potential clinical significance of our approach. Our aim was to compare the expression levels of hsa-circ_0081481, miR-3960 and FBXO24 in NOA patients compared to fertile controls. Uncovering dysregulations in this axis may provide valuable insights into the molecular etiology of NOA and facilitate the development of new non-invasive biomarkers and therapeutic targets, thereby advancing diagnosis and personalized treatment strategies for male infertility.

Materials and methods

Selection of transcriptomic datasets

The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was searched for transcriptomic datasets analyzing differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in testicular tissue from males with impaired spermatogenesis (including NOA). To ensure consistency and minimize confounding factors, we applied the following inclusion criteria: (a) human samples only; (b) microarray-based expression profile data available for both healthy controls and infertile patients; (c) at least three samples per group. Of the ~ 35 available male infertility datasets, 7 datasets met these criteria and were selected for analysis (Table 1).

Identification of differentially expressed genes and functional enrichment

Differentially expressed genes were identified using the GEO2R tool for each selected dataset, applying a strict threshold of |log₂ fold change| > 2 and a p-value < 0.01. The overlapping DEGs between the studies were identified. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were then performed for the shared DEGs using the Enrichr platform.

Selection of FBXO24, has-circ_0081481, and miR-3960

Following these analyses (Fig. 1), FBXO24 was identified as a common gene significantly downregulated in NOA in all comparisons, making it an interesting candidate for further investigation. To explore potential upstream regulatory mechanisms of FBXO24, we used miRTarBase, TargetScan, miRBase and miRDB to predict miRNA candidates targeting FBXO24. miR-3960 emerged as a strong regulatory candidate for FBXO24 in this in silico analysis. In parallel, circBank, circBase, CircInteractome and circAtlas were interrogated to identify circular RNAs that may interact with miR-3960, leading to the identification of hsa-circ_0081481.

Venn diagram of significantly downregulated genes in NOA across datasets. FBXO24 appears at the intersection, indicating consistent downregulation in NOA.

Participants

A total of 60 men diagnosed with NOA were recruited from the Abortion Research Centre of the Reproductive Science Institute in Yazd to participate in this study. All patients underwent bilateral microdissection testicular sperm extraction (mTESE) as part of infertility treatment to retrieve sperm for ICSI. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences in Tehran (approval number IR.SBMU.MSP.REC.1403.331). All procedures were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations, and all patients gave their written informed consent. Before surgery, all patients underwent standard investigations, including karyotyping, screening for Y chromosome microdeletion, and measurement of serum levels of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinising hormone (LH) and testosterone. None of the participants had a history of cryptorchidism, previous TESE or hormone therapy, and all cases were primary infertility. Individuals with chromosomal abnormalities, cystic fibrosis or Y chromosome microdeletions were excluded.

During the operation, testicular tissue samples were taken for histopathological analysis (fixed in Bouin’s solution and stained with haematoxylin and eosin). An experienced pathologist classified the NOA samples into three histopathological subgroups: Sertoli cell-only syndrome (SCOS, n = 20), hypospermatogenesis (HYPO, n = 20) and maturation arrest (MA, n = 20). In addition, peripheral blood (≈ 3 ml) was taken from each patient for molecular analysis. Blood samples were also collected from 40 fertile men (partners of women with confirmed infertility by female factor) who served as a control group. All control participants were confirmed to have normal reproductive function, including normal semen analysis and hormone levels. No significant age difference was observed between NOA and control groups (age-matched subjects). Based on the mTESE results, NOA cases were further categorized into NOA+ (successful sperm retrieval; including SCOS n = 3, HYPO n = 16, MA n = 10) and NOA− (failed sperm retrieval; n = 31) (Table 2).

RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and RT-qPCR

After blood collection and plasma separation, total RNA was extracted from the plasma using the TRIzol reagent (manual protocol). RNA quantity and quality were assessed on a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (A260/280 ≈ 1.8–2.0 for all samples) and by Bioanalyzer (RNA Integrity Number > 7), and equal amounts of RNA were used for cDNA synthesis. To reduce costs and allow simultaneous profiling of miRNA, circRNA and mRNA targets, we used a novel two-step approach: first, miRNAs were polyadenylated, and then total cDNA was synthesized in a single reaction with both random hexamers and oligo(dT) primers.

For miRNA polyadenylation, a 20-µL reaction containing 2 µL poly(A) polymerase, 2 µL reaction buffer, 2 µL ATP, 8 µL RNA template, and 6 µL nuclease-free water was incubated at 40 °C for 35 min. Each 10 µL aliquot of polyadenylated RNA was then reverse transcribed in a 25 µL cDNA synthesis reaction containing 2 µL reverse transcriptase, 5 µL RT buffer, 1 µL dNTP mixture, 2.5 µL random hexamers, 1 µL oligo(dT) primer, 1.5 µL specific miRNA primer, 10 µL polyadenylated RNA template and 3 µL nuclease-free water. Reverse transcription was performed at 25 °C for 15 min, followed by 50 °C for 60 min, and the enzyme was inactivated at 80 °C for 6 min.

Expression of hsa-circ_0081481, miR-3960, and FBXO24 was quantified by real-time PCR (Rotor-Gene Q, Qiagen). Specific primers were designed for each target: for FBXO24 (forward 5′-AGCCAAGGATTTTGCCTCGGA-3′; reverse 5′-ACTGCAGATAGACCTCAACA-3′), for hsa-circ_0081481 (forward 5′-GCCAAGGATACACGAAGGGC-3′; reverse 5′-CCACGTAGTCAAGAATGAAGA-3′), and for hsa-miR-3960 (forward 5′-GGCGGCGGCGGAGGCGGGGG-3′; reverse 5′-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3′). U6 small nuclear RNA and B2M (β-2-microglobulin) were used as internal reference controls (primers for U6: forward 5′-CTGGCTACGGCAGCCCA-3′, reverse 5′-AACGATTCACGCATCTGCGT-3′; primers for B2M: forward 5′-AAGATGAGTATGCCTGCCGTG-3′, reverse 5′-CTGCTTACATGTCTCGATCCCAC-3′). Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2^(−ΔΔCt) method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism version 10. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by a Dunnett post hoc test was used to assess differences between groups. For comparisons between two independent groups (NOA + vs. NOA−), an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered an indicator of statistical significance. In addition, the diagnostic performance of selected biomarkers was assessed by analyzing the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to determine their discriminatory ability between NOA + and NOA− groups.

Results

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

Integration of the seven selected GEO datasets (Table 1) revealed a number of common DEGs that were consistently dysregulated in NOA. Notably, FBXO24 was among the most frequently downregulated genes in NOA (Fig. 1), justifying its selection for further investigation. To further investigate the potential functional roles of these common DEGs (and the context of FBXO24 downregulation), we performed GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses (Fig. 2). In the Biological Processes category (Fig. 2A), the enriched DEGs were predominantly involved in spermatid development, axoneme assembly, microtubule bundle formation, and other sperm-related or cilia-associated processes. These biological processes highlight the involvement of common genes in spermatogenesis and structural organization, which is crucial for the formation of motile cilia (flagella). In the Cellular Components category (Fig. 2B), structures such as the sperm flagellum, motile cilium, acrosomal vesicle and membrane-associated complexes such as the PAR polarity complex have been enriched with terms. These components are essential for sperm motility and fertilization capacity. Enrichment with KEGG pathways (Fig. 2C) revealed that DEGs were mainly involved in lysosome and endocytosis pathways, which play an important role in cell degradation, recycling and receptor internalization—processes that may contribute to germ cell maturation and differentiation. To complement these analyses, tissue-specific expression of FBXO24 was examined using the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) and GTEx datasets (Fig. 2D). Both sources showed highly specific expression of FBXO24 in the testis, with negligible levels in almost all other tissues, highlighting a testis-specific role of this gene.

Gene expression analysis of FBXO24, miR-3960, and hsa-circ_0081481 in NOA subtypes

To validate the findings of our bioinformatic analysis, we analyzed the expression profiles of FBXO24, miR-3960 and hsa-circ_0081481 in the plasma of patients of different NOA subtypes (HYPO, MA, SCOS) and healthy controls. As shown in Fig. 3a, FBXO24 mRNA expression was significantly downregulated in both the HYPO and MA groups compared to controls (p < 0.0001), while the SCOS group showed no significant reduction. This indicates a possible role of FBXO24 in the spermatogenesis process, especially in the phases of hypospermatogenesis and maturation arrest. In contrast, miR-3960 was significantly upregulated in the HYPO and MA groups (Fig. 3b), with expression levels significantly higher than in controls (p < 0.0001). This inverse pattern between FBXO24 and miR-3960 supports a possible negative regulatory relationship between the two. Furthermore, hsa-circ_0081481 levels (Fig. 3c) were significantly decreased in all three NOA subtypes compared to controls (p < 0.0001), underscoring the involvement of this circRNA in NOA pathogenesis. Overall, these findings accentuate a dysregulated molecular axis involving FBXO24, miR-3960 and hsa-circ_0081481 in NOA.

Association of the FBXO24, miR-3960, and hsa-circ_0081481 axis with sperm retrieval outcomes in NOA

To evaluate whether components of the proposed regulatory axis are associated with sperm retrieval outcomes, we compared expression levels between NOA patients with successful (NOA⁺) and unsuccessful (NOA−) micro-TESE. As illustrated in Fig. 4A, FBXO24 mRNA expression was markedly higher in the NOA⁺ group compared with NOA− patients (p < 0.0001), indicating a potential association between elevated FBXO24 levels and preserved spermatogenic activity. In contrast, miR-3960 expression was significantly increased in the NOA− group (Fig. 4B), consistent with its predicted inhibitory role on FBXO24. Similarly, the circular RNA hsa_circ_0081481, proposed as an upstream regulator of miR-3960, showed significantly higher expression in NOA⁺ patients compared to those with unsuccessful sperm retrieval (Fig. 4C, p < 0.0001). Collectively, these findings support a coordinated expression pattern in which upregulation of FBXO24 and hsa_circ_0081481, together with downregulation of miR-3960, is associated with favorable micro-TESE outcomes. This expression signature is consistent with a putative circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory axis and highlights the potential utility of these circulating transcripts as non-invasive biomarkers for predicting sperm retrieval success in patients with non-obstructive azoospermia.

Diagnostic Potential of FBXO24, miR-3960, and hsa-circ_0081481 in NOA

Next, we investigated the diagnostic potential of FBXO24, miR-3960 and hsa-circ_0081481 in distinguishing NOA+ from NOA− cases. ROC curve analysis was performed (Fig. 5). hsa-circ_0081481 showed the highest diagnostic performance with an AUC of 0.920 (95% CI 0.850–0.990), indicating excellent discrimination. miR-3960 also showed good diagnostic accuracy with an AUC of 0.861 (95% CI 0.765–0.957). In comparison, FBXO24 showed moderate diagnostic ability (AUC 0.779, 95% CI 0.664–0.895). These results suggest that hsa-circ_0081481 and miR-3960 may serve as promising non-invasive biomarkers for the prediction of sperm retrieval outcomes in NOA patients, especially in distinguishing between successful (NOA+) and unsuccessful (NOA−) mTESE cases.

ROC curve analysis for the diagnostic value of FBXO24, miR-3960, and hsa-circ_0081481 in predicting sperm retrieval success among NOA patients. hsa-circ_0081481 achieved the highest AUC (0.920), followed by miR-3960 (AUC 0.861) and FBXO24 (AUC 0.779). These results indicate the potential utility of this marker panel in preoperative prediction of mTESE outcomes. Since miR-3960 showed an inverse expression pattern with respect to sperm retrieval outcome (significantly lower in NOA + and higher in NOA−), the group designations were reversed in the ROC analysis to obtain a meaningful AUC > 0.5. Nonetheless, the clinical interpretation remains consistent: lower circulating miR-3960 levels are associated with a positive sperm retrieval outcome in NOA patients.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the expression profiles of FBXO24, hsa-circ_0081481 and miR-3960 in plasma samples from men with NOA with the aim of elucidating transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms underlying spermatogenic failure. By shifting the focus from testicular tissue to an easily accessible biofluid (blood plasma), we investigated the potential of these molecules as non-invasive biomarkers for NOA management. Identification of circulating biomarkers is particularly valuable because spermatogenesis in NOA testes is mosaic, patchy foci of sperm production that are difficult to detect prior to surgery. Our findings address this critical need by demonstrating coordinated dysregulation of a circRNA–miRNA–mRNA axis in NOA that may reflect the presence or absence of active spermatogenesis and thereby guide therapeutic strategies such as micro-TESE.

FBXO24

Our data showed that FBXO24 expression was significantly reduced in NOA subgroups—HYPO, MA— compared to healthy controls. From a clinical perspective, the pronounced reduction of FBXO24 in MA and HYPO patients is particularly remarkable. These two histological subtypes exhibit partial spermatogenic activity and contain spermatids or more differentiated germ cells, in contrast to SCOS where germ cells are not present. The downregulation of FBXO24 in MA and HYPO suggests a link between impaired late-stage spermatid development and deficits in ubiquitin-mediated degradation of regulatory proteins or RNA-binding factors that FBXO24 likely controls. The lack of a significant decrease in FBXO24 in SCOS (despite absent germ cells) may reflect uniformly low baseline levels and limited statistical power in this subgroup. Similarly, the absence of an FSH elevation in NOA patients (Table 3) might be due to cohort size or selection bias toward less extreme endocrine dysfunction.

FBXO24 plays a multifaceted role in spermiogenesis by regulating several processes essential for sperm development. It is involved in chromatin remodeling through facilitating histone-to-protamine replacement, a critical step for proper DNA condensation in elongating spermatids11. Reduced FBXO24 activity has been associated with histone retention, DNA damage, and compromised chromatin integrity, hallmarks of defective spermatogenesis. In addition, FBXO24 contributes to mitochondrial organization during sperm maturation, supporting the formation of a functional mitochondrial sheath required for motility14. It also participates in post-transcriptional regulation by interacting with splicing factors to control alternative splicing of transcripts important for chromatin packaging, mitochondrial function, and flagellar assembly12. Moreover, FBXO24 mediates ubiquitin-dependent degradation of key spermatogenic regulators, and its downregulation may disrupt RNA processing and protein turnover, ultimately contributing to spermatogenic arrest observed in NOA15.

Beyond its individual functions, FBXO24 likely operates as part of an SCF ubiquitin ligase complex, like other F-box proteins known to be essential for meiosis and spermatid development16. Consistent downregulation of FBXO24 in NOA patients therefore suggests a critical role in late-stage germ cell differentiation17. In this context, we propose a post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism in which hsa_circ_0081481 modulates FBXO24 expression by acting as a competing endogenous RNA for miR-3960. Elevated miR-3960 levels and reduced hsa_circ_0081481 expression in NOA may synergistically repress FBXO24, thereby impairing spermatogenesis. Similar circRNA–miRNA regulatory networks involving miR-3960 have been reported in other species13, indicating that such mechanisms may represent conserved regulatory modules in male germ cell development.

miR-3960

Similarly, we found a significant upregulation of circulating miR-3960 in NOA patients, especially in those with MA and HYPO histopathology. This pattern contrasts with the significantly lower miR-3960 levels observed in SCOS patients and healthy controls, and emphasizes the potential of miR-3960 as a non-invasive biomarker to distinguish different NOA phenotypes and predict the likelihood of successful sperm retrieval. miR-3960 is a regulatory microRNA involved in fundamental cellular processes such as differentiation, chromatin remodeling and mitochondrial dynamics13,20. Its elevated levels in MA and HYPO patients may indicate ongoing but incomplete spermatogenic activity in these subgroups. Despite the overall impaired spermatogenesis, HYPO and MA testes still harbors partial germ cell populations capable of initiating spermatogenic program—processes that could lead to the leakage or active release of miR-3960 into the bloodstream. In contrast, the very low miR-3960 levels in SCOS likely reflect the near complete absence of germ cells in this condition, emphasizing the specificity of miR-3960 as an indicator of residual spermatogenic potential.

From a clinical perspective, these subgroup-specific expression patterns suggest that miR-3960 in plasma could be used as a predictive tool for sperm retrieval outcomes in NOA. High miR-3960 expression—especially in MA and HYPO cases-could indicate the presence of isolated foci of active spermatogenesis and thus a higher probability of finding sperm by TESE. This information could be invaluable for pre-operative patient counselling, helping to manage expectations and potentially reduce the emotional and physical burden of unsuccessful exploratory surgery in cases where the likelihood of sperm retrieval is minimal.

In addition, the use of circulating miRNAs such as miR-3960 in the clinical setting offers a practical advantage: miRNAs are extremely stable in plasma (often protected in exosomes or Argonaute complexes) and can be easily measured by qPCR21–23. These properties make them ideal candidates for routine testing based on liquid biopsies. The diagnostic utility of miR-3960 could be further enhanced if it is combined with other molecular markers (e.g. circRNAs or mRNAs) or conventional parameters (hormone levels, testicular volume, etc.) to create a comprehensive predictive model for NOA outcomes.

Hsa-circ_0081481

In this study, we observed a consistent and statistically significant downregulation of hsa-circ_0081481 in the plasma of NOA patients in all major histopathological subgroups (MA, HYPO and SCOS) compared to healthy controls. This consistent decrease suggests that hsa-circ_0081481 plays a fundamental role in the maintenance of testicular homeostasis and spermatogenic integrity and that its disruption is a common molecular feature of NOA regardless of specific histology.

Circular RNAs are highly stable in the bloodstream due to their closed loop structure and are increasingly recognized as critical gene regulators—acting as miRNA sponges, transcriptional modulators or protein scaffolds24. The observed reduction of hsa-circ_0081481 in all NOA subtypes may be due to the loss of such regulatory capacity in the testicular environment. In particular, the consistent downregulation in different histology (from HYPO to SCOS) suggests a widespread involvement of hsa-circ_0081481 in pathways important for germ cell survival, communication between Sertoli and germ cells or epigenetic regulation of spermatogenesis. Functionally, bioinformatic predictions (and the suspected interaction with miR-3960) suggest that hsa-circ_0081481 may affect genes related to chromatin remodeling, cell cycle progression or apoptosis inhibition. Its downregulation could therefore exacerbate the pathological landscape of NOA by allowing uncontrolled miR-3960 activity, promoting germ cell apoptosis or disrupting supportive interactions within the seminiferous epithelium. These effects may help to explain the spectrum of spermatogenic failure observed: from partial arrest in MA to reduced germ cell density in HYPO to almost complete absence of germ cells in SCOS.

From a diagnostic point of view, hsa-circ_0081481 in plasma is an attractive biomarker candidate. Its frequency is significantly lower in all NOA patients compared to controls, suggesting that it could serve as a general indicator of testicular dysfunction (possibly useful in differentiating between non-obstructive and obstructive azoospermia). Furthermore, as hsa-circ_0081481 is a stable circRNA, it could be reliably detected in blood and, in combination with other markers such as miR-3960, improve diagnostic precision and stratification of patients according to underlying testicular pathology and sperm retrieval potential.

Furthermore, the combination of all three biomarkers (FBXO24, miR-3960 and hsa-circ_0081481) in one panel might have a better predictive power. It is noteworthy that the clinical success rates of sperm retrieval vary greatly depending on NOA histology: In hypospermatogenesis, where some spermatogenesis persists, micro-TESE retrieves sperm in ~ 60–80% of cases; in maturation arrest, success rates average ~ 40–50%; and in SCOS, the probability drops to below 20%25. Our results are consistent with these findings – patients with higher chances of sperm retrieval (HYPO and some MA) had higher FBXO24 and hsa-circ_0081481 levels and lower miR-3960 levels, while the worst prognosis cases (SCOS) showed the opposite expression profile. This correlation suggests that the hsa-circ_0081481/miR-3960/FBXO24 axis may serve as a molecular predictor of sperm retrieval success. In practice, a combined assessment of these three factors could help to identify NOA patients who are most likely to benefit from surgical sperm retrieval, thus improving decision-making and counselling.

A limitation of our study is the lack of direct functional validation of the hsa-circ_0081481/ miR-3960/FBXO24 interactions; our conclusions are based on correlative expression and bioinformatic prediction. The very small number of SCOS cases with successful sperm retrieval (only 3 patients) also limits subgroup analyses. We did not record smoking or alcohol status, and controls were defined by partner infertility status without extensive fertility testing, factors which could confound plasma RNA levels. Finally, our initial gene selection was based on testicular tissue data, whereas validation was done in blood plasma; further work is needed to confirm that plasma levels faithfully reflect testicular pathology.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study identifies a novel circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory axis (hsa-circ_0081481/miR-3960/FBXO24) in non-obstructive azoospermia. In NOA blood plasma, FBXO24 expression was significantly downregulated, accompanied by increased miR-3960 and reduced levels of its predicted circRNA sponge, hsa-circ_0081481. This expression pattern suggests that loss of hsa-circ_0081481 triggers miR-3960–mediated repression of FBXO24, possibly contributing to spermatogenesis failure. Moreover, ROC curve analysis revealed the strong diagnostic potential of hsa-circ_0081481 and miR-3960 to predict successful sperm retrieval in NOA. Overall, our findings highlight hsa-circ_0081481 and miR-3960 as promising biomarkers for sperm retrieval outcomes and underscore the clinical importance of the circ_0081481/miR-3960/FBXO24 axis in male infertility.

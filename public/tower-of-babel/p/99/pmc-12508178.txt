Comparative study and multi-objective optimization of solar stills using priority-based clustering and prediction modelling
Authors: Mohd Zaheen Khan, Osama Khan, Zeinebou Yahya, Aiyeshah Alhodaib, Taufique Ahamad, Md Kareemullah, Syed Waheedullah Ghori, Yohanis Dabesa Jelila
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rising scarcity of potable water, coupled with environmental concerns over conventional purification methods, has accelerated the global shift toward renewable energy-based clean water solutions. Solar stills, with their simplicity, sustainability, and low operating costs, present a promising option for decentralized water purification. This study examines three solar still designs—rectangular, conical, and hemispherical—under identical conditions to identify the most efficient configuration based on key performance outcomes. However, the intricate nonlinear relationships among the output parameters and the geometric complexities of the stills pose significant challenges to conventional optimization techniques. To overcome these limitations, a hybrid methodology is employed, incorporating a priority-based clustering model to systematically optimize and identify the ideal solar still design. The integrated analysis reveals strong thermal-performance relationships, with heat transfer coefficient (h) showing high correlations with Tw, Tg, and Ta (r > 0.77, Adj R2 ≈ 0.89), while Qew is strongly linked to Tg (r = 0.9284), Tw (r = 0.8941), and h (r = 0.9221); Mew exhibits an almost perfect correlation with Qew (r = 0.9998, Adj R2 ≈ 0.9971). The ANN model using the Gaussian membership function demonstrated the highest prediction accuracy with R2 = 0.997, RMSE ≈ 0.14%, and MSE ≈ 0.01%. Priority weighting favoured Mew at 39%, followed by η (28%), Qew (19%), and h (14%), improving clustering accuracy. K-means clustering pinpointed Trial 27 as optimal, with I(t) = 820 W/m2, Ta = 37 °C, Tw = 59 °C, and Tg = 53 °C, yielding the best thermal synergy for maximum solar still performance.Keywords: Solar still, Water distillation, Energy efficiency, Machine learning, Prediction modellingSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Access to clean water is essential for sustaining life, and its significance in our daily routines cannot be overstated. With the rapid increase in global population and the subsequent surge in water demand, the world now faces a critical shortage of drinking water1. Traditional freshwater sources are under immense pressure, while oceans cover a vast portion of the Earth’s surface and contain about 97% of the planet’s water, this water is saline and unsuitable for direct consumption2. Of the remaining 3% classified as freshwater, a significant portion is trapped in polar ice caps, glaciers, and deep underground reserves, rendering it inaccessible for direct human use. Ultimately, only about 0.014% of the Earth’s total water is readily available to meet the needs of humans and other organisms3. In this context, solar stills offer a promising and eco-friendly solution for addressing water scarcity, particularly in arid and remote regions. A solar still operates on a simple principle: it uses solar energy to evaporate brackish or impure water and then condenses the vapor to yield clean, drinkable water4. This technique mimics the natural hydrological cycle and is both cost-effective and sustainable, requiring minimal maintenance and no external energy source5. By harnessing the abundant energy of the sun, solar stills can transform saline, contaminated, or wastewater into potable water, thereby offering a viable solution to alleviate the global drinking water crisis, especially in areas where access to conventional purification systems is limited6,7.

Traditional solar still designs, while effective in converting brackish or wastewater into potable water, are often limited by their low yield and relatively poor thermal efficiency8. These conventional systems typically rely on single-slope basin configurations with passive heat collection, resulting in modest water production rates, especially under varying climatic conditions9. Hybrid solar stills incorporate advanced material, designs and multi-effect evaporation techniques to maximize solar energy absorption, extend the duration of evaporation-condensation cycles, and significantly increase water output10. Such systems not only boost the overall energy efficiency but also offer operational flexibility and adaptability. However, the transition from conventional to hybrid solar stills introduces considerable design and operational complexity11. These designs involve the interaction of multiple physical phenomena such as multi-phase heat transfer, variable fluid dynamics, non-linear thermal gradients, and radiation-convection coupling across irregular geometries and heterogeneous materials12. Due to these complexities, traditional modelling approaches fall short in accurately predicting system behavior and optimizing performance parameters. Therefore, hybrid modelling needs to be applied, which leverages the ability of neural networks to capture highly non-linear relationships and interactions between numerous input variables13. This can simulate, predict, and fine-tune complex solar still configurations with higher accuracy and computational efficiency compared to conventional methods14.

Priority-based machine learning clustering coupled with prediction modeling effectively addresses the complex design challenges of solar stills, which arise due to nonlinear interactions between geometric features, material properties, and fluctuating environmental conditions like solar irradiance and ambient temperature. Traditional optimization techniques often fail to capture these dynamic, multivariate dependencies, leading to suboptimal or inaccurate design evaluations. By integrating predictive modeling to quantify the relationship between input parameters and outcomes, and applying clustering to group trials based on performance priorities, this approach enables a data-driven comparison across diverse solar still configurations. It not only identifies the most efficient design under varied conditions but also surpasses traditional static methods by dynamically adapting to real-world complexities. This methodology enables accurate comparison of solar stills by capturing complex parameter-outcome relationships through predictive modeling. Clustering based on prioritized outcomes ensures that performance groupings reflect true operational efficiency. Validation through refinement techniques, further enhances result reliability, reducing errors in design evaluation.

Abu Hijleh and Rababa’h15 investigated the sponge cubes of varying sizes inside a solar still basin, eventually enhancing productivity by 20–70%. Abdel Rehim and Lasheen16 reported a 20% increase in productivity by incorporating a heat exchanger and parabolic concentrator in their solar desalination unit. Khan17 demonstrated that internal and external reflectors could boost the productivity of a basin-type solar still by 75–90%. Omara et al.18 observed a 125% increase in productivity for a stepped solar still using reflectors, along with a cost reduction of ₹1.17 per liter of distilled water. Khalifa19 emphasized the importance of adjusting the cover tilt angle of solar stills to match the latitude of the location, recommending larger angles in winter and smaller angles in summer for optimal output. Additionally, Omara et al.20 investigated different basin shapes and found that conventional, conical, and hemispherical solar stills varied in their daily yield efficiency.

A significant research gap exists as previous studies have seldom explored or compared multiple solar still designs comprehensively20. Even when comparisons were made, they often lacked robust validation techniques, resulting in high error margins and rendering the findings inefficient and ineffective for practical implementation21. Motivated by the lack of comprehensive and validated comparisons in existing literature, the authors aimed to systematically evaluate and compare multiple solar still designs based on unique outcome parameters. This study seeks to reduce error margins and enhance decision-making through a robust hybrid optimization approach. The novelty of the study lies in its use of uniquely structured solar still designs, evaluated under identical conditions for fair comparison. Unlike previous works, it integrates a hybrid methodology combining correlation modelling, priority-based clustering. This ensures high-accuracy validation and addresses the geometrical and atmospheric inefficiencies often overlooked in studies associated with solar stills21. The following objectives will be addressed in the current study:

The objectives of this study are to evaluate the experimental performance of different solar still designs—rectangular, hemispherical, and conical—under varying atmospheric conditions. It further aims to establish correlations between performance outcomes and input operating parameters to understand their interdependencies. Additionally, the study seeks to identify the optimal membership function for prediction models by minimizing errors between predicted and actual outcomes. Experimental trials are clustered to categorize performance, with the best-performing datasets identified based on optimized metrics. Finally, refinement techniques are applied to validate the clustering process, ensuring improved accuracy and reliability of the clustered data.

Materials equipment selection

Solar still selection and relevance for the analysis

Selecting the right geometry for a solar still is crucial because the shape directly influences heat absorption, internal convection patterns, surface area for evaporation and condensation, and thermal losses22,23. Optimal geometry enhances solar radiation capture, maximizes temperature gradients, and improves vapour condensation efficiency. The three designs shown in Fig. 1 were specifically chosen due to their unique thermal advantages: the hemispherical design offers uniform solar capture and minimal shadowing throughout the day while the conical design ensures multi-angle solar exposure and enhanced internal reflection. These geometries are known to improve thermal efficiency and distilled water yield under varied climatic conditions24,25.

Selected designs of solar still for analysis.

Increasing the efficiency and mass flow rate of solar still, can be achieved by decreasing the shadow effect, eventually leading to maximum utilisation of solar radiation26. In this study, three types of solar stills, namely rectangular (conventional), conical and hemispherical shaped stills are explored for different outcomes for varying operating conditions throughout the day27. All three solar stills—conventional, conical, and hemispherical—use a black iron tank filled with water. A black pipe connects the main tank to each still for water supply, while a transparent manometer adjusts the water depth inside. Temperature sensors are installed in each still to monitor the water, basin, glass, and internal air temperatures.

The conical solar still features a circular iron base with a basin area of 0.9 m2, a cone height of 30 cm, and a basin diameter of 90 cm. Its sides and basin are covered with a 0.6 mm thick layer, painted black to enhance solar absorptivity. Condensed water is collected through an inclined circular channel, while the condensing surface consists of a 4 mm thick acrylic cover. The cover is set at a 28° angle on the circular side, matching the latitude of New Delhi, India. For comparison, the conventional solar still also has a basin area of 0.9 m2, with a low sidewall height of 8 cm and a sidewall depth of 68 cm. Both the conical and hemispherical solar stills are constructed using the same materials as the conventional still. All models are mounted on a wooden frame, with the base and sides thermally insulated for improved efficiency.

Site selection and characteristics

In July 2024, a comprehensive experimental study was conducted in New Delhi, India (Latitude 28°38’41.2800’’ N, Longitude 77°13’0.1956’’ E) to assess the performance of three different solar still designs (Fig. 2). The experiments ran daily from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, aligning with peak daylight hours to ensure maximum solar energy utilization. Throughout the study, careful attention was given to maintaining consistent and clear weather conditions to eliminate environmental anomalies that could skew the results. The aim was to generate reliable, repeatable data under optimal solar exposure across all three solar still types—conical, hemispherical, and rectangular.

Schematic diagram of experimental setup for solar still analysis.

At the end of every hour, key parameters were meticulously recorded, including basin water temperature, intensity of solar radiation, glass surface temperature, ambient atmospheric temperature, and the quantity of distilled water produced. This high-frequency data collection allowed the researchers to track the performance dynamics of each still in near-real-time and across varying sunlight intensities. By focusing on cloud-free days, the study ensured that the measured values of solar intensity and distillate productivity directly reflected the design efficiency of each still. These detailed observations provided the foundation for evaluating and comparing the thermal behaviour and water yield of each geometry under consistent solar input.

Assumptions of the study

In any experimental study involving outdoor solar systems, it is essential to define certain assumptions to ensure consistent data interpretation and to simplify the complex interactions between environmental variables and system behaviour28. These assumptions help isolate the impact of system design (in this case, different solar still geometries) on performance outcomes, without being overly affected by random or uncontrollable factors such as transient cloud cover, wind fluctuations, or minor variations in water properties29. The study assumes near-uniform weather by conducting trials only on clear, sunny days, minimizing abrupt variations in irradiance. Hourly averages of solar radiation and ambient temperature were used to smooth out short-term fluctuations, ensuring stable input conditions. This approach allowed fair comparison of geometries, as performance trends reflected design response rather than transient atmospheric disturbances. For this solar still analysis, the assumptions are particularly relevant to maintain uniformity across measurements and ensure that comparisons among the rectangular, conical, and hemispherical stills are fair and scientifically valid30. Methodology

The methodology began with experimental trials on solar still geometries under identical atmospheric conditions. These outcomes were initially analyzed using Pearson correlation to establish the strength of relationships between input parameters and outputs, ensuring identification of the most influential factors driving performance. The correlated datasets were then processed through an ANN model to test different membership functions and identify the most suitable one for capturing the nonlinear thermo-geometric dynamics of solar stills. Finally, a weighted k-means clustering approach was applied for optimization, where outcome priorities guided centroid recalibration, producing robust clusters that enabled accurate comparison and optimization of solar still performance beyond the scope of traditional methods.

ANN prediction method application

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) form the core of machine learning and artificial intelligence. Modeled after the architecture and operation of the human brain, ANNs are made up of linked units known as neurons or nodes, organized in layers. The three main layers are the input layer, hidden layers, and the output layer31. Each connection between neurons has an associated weight, and the network learns through a process called training, where it adjusts these weights based on input data and desired output. ANNs excel at pattern recognition, classification, regression, and other tasks32. In this study, an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) prediction model will be applied to determine the most suitable membership function for evaluating the performance of different solar stills. The model will classify and assess the prediction accuracy based on statistical error metrics including R2, RMSE, and MSE33. Among the various tested membership functions, the one that yields the highest R2 value—indicating strong correlation—and the lowest RMSE and MSE—reflecting minimal prediction error—will be identified as the ideal function for capturing the relationship between input parameters and performance outcomes of the solar stills34. k-means clustering machine learning algorithm

K-means clustering is a robust hybrid clustering model well-suited for managing the nonlinear and multivariable complexities inherent in solar still systems, where performance is influenced by fluctuating atmospheric conditions, geometric design variations, and thermal interactions35. This method effectively segments the dataset into best, worst, and average performing clusters based on outcome parameters, enabling clear categorization of design efficiency36. However, due to the limitations of traditional optimization techniques—which often assume linearity and fail to capture the dynamic interdependencies in such systems the clustering results will be further refined using a more advanced technique capable of modelling probabilistic distributions and overlapping clusters, ensuring a more accurate and optimized classification of solar still performance. In this study, the initial centroids for k-means clustering were selected using outcome-based criteria, ensuring coverage of low, medium, and high ranges of outcome values from the experimental dataset. This stratified initialization prevented centroid overlap and reduced bias from random selection, improving cluster stability. Further, it allowed the algorithm to better capture nonlinear variations in solar still performance arising from geometry and fluctuating ambient conditions. Pearson’s r correlation helps quantify the linear relationship between input parameters and performance outcomes of solar stills, depicting key influencing variables. In this study, it supports identifying which thermal and environmental factors most directly affect productivity and efficiency as shown in Fig. 7. The heat transfer coefficient (h) demonstrates a strong positive correlation with key thermal inputs like water temperature (Tw), glass temperature (Tg), and ambient temperature (Ta), as evident from Pearson’s r values exceeding 0.77 and adjusted R2 values around 0.89. These strong correlations enable efficient convective and evaporative heat transfer in solar stills which relies on the temperature gradients between basin water, glass cover, and surrounding air. As the ambient and surface temperatures rise, they enhance the heat exchange rate, validating the strong interdependency observed37. The energy output (Qew) also exhibits high correlation with most thermal parameters, particularly with Tg (r = 0.9284, Adj R2 ≈ 0.8541), Tw (r = 0.8941), and h (r = 0.9221), indicating that the energy output is primarily driven by the absorbed solar radiation and effective heat transfer38. Evidently, a higher water and glass temperatures promote greater vaporization, thereby increasing latent heat gain, which is directly measured as energy output39. The solar irradiance (I(t)) showed a weaker correlation (r = 0.2957) due to its fluctuating nature over time, which affects energy output non-linearly40.

Pearson-r correlation analysis for performance outcomes of solar still.

The mass of water collected (Mew) correlates almost perfectly with Qew (r = 0.9998, Adj R2 ≈ 0.9971), confirming that higher energy input and better heat transfer directly result in higher water yield. Significant correlations with Tg, Tw, and h reinforce the fact that mass yield in a solar still is highly dependent on the thermodynamic response of the system to the ambient environment. This outcome is based on the principle that evaporative yield scales with the thermal gradient and energy conversion efficiency of the system. The efficiency (η) of the solar still shows moderate to strong correlations with most thermal parameters, especially with Mew (r = 0.8351), Qew (r = 0.8631), and Tg (r = 0.7741), suggesting that efficiency is an integrated outcome of thermal performance and system design. Efficiency is lower in correlation compared to other outcomes due to its sensitivity to both energy input and losses through radiation and conduction, which can vary unpredictably with ambient factors. These observations confirm that efficiency is a cumulative indicator influenced by all thermal dynamics within the solar still.

The priority analysis in this study was based solely on correlation strength between input parameters and outcomes, quantified through Pearson’s r and adjusted R2 values. This ensured that parameters most strongly influencing system performance, received higher weightage. Relying on correlation provided a statistically objective way to align clustering with the dominant thermo-geometric interactions of solar stills.

ANN prediction and statistical analysis

The correlation outcomes from the earlier analysis, which demonstrated strong interrelations between input parameters and performance indicators, were incorporated into the ANN model to identify the most suitable membership function as shown in Fig. 8. Among the various tested functions, the Gaussian membership function showed the highest prediction accuracy with an R2 value of 0.997, while maintaining the lowest RMSE and MSE values of approximately 0.14% and 0.01%, respectively. This superior performance is attributed to the Gaussian function’s smooth, continuous nature, which effectively models the nonlinear and dynamic characteristics of solar still operation influenced by ambient temperature, solar radiation, and internal thermal gradients. In the study, the ANN structure consisted of an input layer representing solar irradiance, ambient temperature, water, and glass temperatures, a hidden layer with optimized neurons to capture nonlinear thermal interactions, and an output layer predicting key outcomes. A single hidden layer with sufficient neurons was chosen to balance accuracy and computational efficiency.

Error prediction modelling for different membership functions.

With the ideal membership function established, the dataset can now be accurately clustered using the k-means method, followed by refinement through the k means clustering. The clustering’s probabilistic nature allows it to capture the underlying variance within the clustered data more effectively than traditional methods, especially in systems like solar stills that involve complex thermodynamic behaviour and fluctuating environmental inputs. This hybrid clustering approach ensures the most reliable grouping of data trials into best, average, and worst categories, optimizing performance comparison and system evaluation. The very low values indicate excellent predictive accuracy of the ANN model in capturing nonlinear solar still dynamics. These were validated using additional unseen datasets, where the model maintained consistent error levels, confirming robustness. This cross-validation ensured the model was not over fitted and could generalize well under varying thermo-environmental conditions.

Machine learning clustering analysis

The k-means algorithm is employed to optimize the operating conditions of the solar still, as it effectively clusters performance data based on key thermal and productivity parameters. This algorithm is particularly preferred over traditional optimization methods due to its ability to handle complex, nonlinear interactions inherent in solar stills, such as radiation-acceptance exchange dynamics, which vary significantly with design geometry and solar incidence angles. Unlike conventional techniques that often struggle with overlapping thermal influences from curved or multi-surface designs, k-means enables enhanced classification of operating states by grouping similar thermal behaviours, thereby facilitating more precise tuning of conditions for maximum efficiency.

The k-means algorithm initially segments the dataset from multiple solar still trials into preliminary clusters based on shared thermal and performance features. These clusters are then refined into final groupings using a priority-weighted approach derived from correlation analysis, which identifies the influence of each parameter on system output. As shown in.

Figure 9, the highest priority is assigned to Mew (39%) due to its direct representation of distilled water yield, which is the primary output metric. η (efficiency) follows at 28%, reflecting the system’s ability to convert input energy into useful output. Qew (evaporative heat loss) holds 19% weight, indicating its moderate role in controlling energy utilization. The lowest priority is given to h (convective heat transfer coefficient, 14%) as it acts more as a supporting parameter rather than a direct outcome. This priority-based cluster formation ensures more accurate classification by aligning cluster logic with the system’s performance-driving variables.

As a result, substantial improvements are seen in Cluster 1, where the errors for all parameters significantly reduced the misclassification, aligning the final centroids closer to actual performance behavior. For Clusters 2 and 3, the recalibrated centroids reduced deviation, especially for Qew, Mew, and η, which originally dominated the system’s variability. This approach makes clustering not only more accurate but also technically relevant, as it aligns the centroid update process with outcome-sensitive thermodynamic behavior of the hemispherical solar still system.

For each outcome variable (h, Qew, Mew, and η), the F-values are notably high (93.67 for Mew and 90.07 for Qew), indicating substantial variance between clusters relative to the variance within clusters. The extremely low p-values (< 0.0001) further affirm the statistical significance of these differences. This validates that the observed cluster distinctions are not due to random chance but reflect meaningful separations in the dataset based on performance parameters, thereby confirming the effectiveness and reliability of the clustering process.

Comparison with previous studies on different solar still designs

The current study is compared with previous similar solar still designs, as detailed in Table 5, highlighting variations in performance outcomes across different geometries and methodologies. For instance, Ismail41 observed reduced efficiency with increasing water depth in a hemispherical design (0.50 m2), while Nafey et al.42 reported a 15% productivity increase with a daily output of 3.4 L/m2 in a rectangular basin (0.25 m2). Al-Karaghouli & Alnaser43 achieved 43.8% efficiency in a double-decker still (0.45 m2), and Sahoo et al. [44] recorded 8.59% efficiency with high fluoride removal in a rectangular design (0.53 m2). In contrast, the present study with a 0.55 m2 hemispherical shows improved performance, achieving an enhanced daily yield of 5.9 L/m2 and maximum thermal efficiency of 32.1%, supported by enhanced energy output of 384 W/m2. The use of advanced priority Pearson based clustering along with statistical validation sets the study apart from other. These advanced methods incorporate variability in environmental conditions, providing a more reliable and adaptable performance evaluation, thereby offering a significant edge over previous conventional approaches. These findings are scalable to larger solar stills as the governing thermo-geometric interactions (heat transfer, evaporation, condensation) remain consistent across scales. The ANN–clustering framework can adapt to different climatic regions by retraining with local irradiance and ambient temperature datasets. This ensures the methodology captures regional variability while preserving predictive accuracy and optimization capability. The improved performance of conical and hemispherical stills justifies their higher manufacturing costs, as they achieve greater daily yield and higher efficiency than rectangular designs. Their geometry minimizes shadow effects, maximizes solar capture, and sustains output under varying conditions. Over time, the enhanced water productivity offsets the initial cost, making them more economically viable for long-term deployment.

Conclusion

The world is facing an urgent crisis of clean drinking water due to rapid industrialization, population growth, and contamination of freshwater sources, making it imperative to explore sustainable purification methods. Solar stills emerge as a highly effective renewable solution, leveraging abundant solar energy to desalinate and purify water without harmful emissions or high energy input. Unlike other purification technologies, solar stills are low-cost, easy to maintain, and ideal for off-grid or rural regions where access to electricity is limited. To determine the most efficient configuration, different solar still designs are compared in this study by evaluating their performance under varying operating conditions at different times of the day, capturing the dynamic nature of solar intensity and ambient conditions that influence water output.

The performance comparison of different solar still designs under varying operating conditions involves complex interdependencies between design parameters and dynamic environmental variables, making traditional optimization methods inadequate. To address this, the study employs advanced machine learning and optimization techniques. Initially, experimental outcomes from various solar still configurations under different operating conditions are evaluated. These results are used to develop a correlation model that captures the intricate relationships among performance indicators. Subsequently, a priority-based ranking of outcome parameters is established, which is then input into a clustering analysis to categorize the datasets into distinct performance groups. The following conclusions are obtained from the study:

Correlation results show that h has strong correlations with Tw, Tg, and Ta (r > 0.77, Adj R2 ≈ 0.89), Qew is highly correlated with Tg (r = 0.9284), Tw (r = 0.8941), and h (r = 0.9221), while Mew nearly perfectly correlates with Qew (r = 0.9998, Adj R2 ≈ 0.9971); efficiency (η) moderately correlates with Mew (r = 0.8351), Qew (r = 0.8631).

The study is limited by the number of solar still designs evaluated; additional configurations should be tested for broader validation. Furthermore, the effect of different coating materials on performance requires exploration using the same hybrid methodology. A possible study limitation does not include a sensitivity analysis accounting for variations in solar irradiance or ambient temperature. The limitation of the study is that it does not address thermal losses, which could significantly influence the overall performance of the solar stills. A selective solar-absorber coating such as black chrome on the basin would increase solar absorption while reducing heat losses, leading to higher water temperature, greater energy output, and improved efficiency. A super-hydrophilic anti-fog TiO₂ coating on the glass cover would promote smooth film-wise condensation, reduce droplet shading, maintain light transmission, and enhance condensation efficiency under varying sunlight conditions. Future work will involve expanding the analysis to include a wider range of solar still designs and structural modifications. Additionally, the methodology can be applied to assess the impact of various surface coatings on thermal performance and water yield.

Rapid Clinical and Renal Recovery after Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement

ABSTRACT

Severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is increasingly recognized as an independent predictor of morbidity and mortality in patients with heart failure.1 Chronic right-sided volume overload results in systemic venous congestion, leading to progressive hepatic and renal congestion and dysfunction.2 Despite its clinical impact, isolated surgical intervention for TR is often deferred because of high operative risk and late referral.3 Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) has emerged as a promising alternative for selected patients with severe TR who are considered too high risk for surgery. The Evoque system (Edwards Lifesciences) is a dedicated TTVR device designed to accommodate the complex anatomy of the tricuspid valve. We describe its use in the case of an elderly patient with torrential TR and end-organ dysfunction, demonstrating rapid clinical and renal improvement after TTVR.

History of presentation

We present the case of an 85-year-old White male. The patient was evaluated in our heart failure outpatient clinic given torrential TR with progressively worsening symptoms of right-sided heart failure, including peripheral edema, weight gain, shortness of breath (NYHA functional class III) and jugular venous distention. Over time the patient showed signs of worsening renal function, with creatinine levels rising from 0.97 mg/dL initially to 1.5 mg/dL, consistent with cardiorenal syndrome.4 As there were no significant recent effects that could have influenced the kidney function and no timely relation to pacemaker placement, renal resection, or medication changes, the deterioration of renal function was concluded to be related to worsening right-sided heart failure and renal congestion in the setting of torrential TR. Furthermore, the patient showed signs of constant hepatic congestion with elevated total bilirubin level between 1.7 and 1.9 mg/dL as well as elevated B-type natriuretic peptide (400-800 pg/mL).

Past medical history

The patient had a history of hypertension, atrial fibrillation, pacemaker implantation, chronic kidney disease (stage 2), prostate carcinoma with surgical removal and radio therapy, femur fracture, renal carcinoma with right-sided subtotal renal exportation, as well as previous history of nicotine abuse.

Investigations

Transthoracic and transesophageal echocardiography revealed torrential TR (proximal isovelocity surface area: 1 cm, vena contracta: 9 mm). The mechanism of the TR was a combination of mechanical impingement of the ventricular lead with reduced mobility of the posterior leaflet, as well as a dilation of the tricuspid annulus (anteroposterior diameter: 49 mm, superolateral diameter: 47 mm, perimeter: 15.9 cm). The maximum coaptation deficit was measured at 12 mm between the septal and posterior leaflet (concurrent with the position of the ventricular lead of the pacemaker) and 6 mm between the septal and the anterior leaflet (Figures 1A and 1B). In addition, there was severe biatrial dilation, as well as right ventricular dilation. Transesophageal echocardiography showed that the right atrium was severely dilated (right atrial volume index: 96 mL/m2). The right ventricle was also dilated (end-diastolic area: 29 cm2, 3-dimensional end-diastolic volume: 102 mL/m2). Furthermore, the fractional area change of the right ventricle was measured at 49%, and 3-dimensional ejection fraction was calculated at 46%, with normal longitudinal function (tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion: 19 mm, S′: 10 cm/s, strain: −26%). Given these findings, the TR was considered a mixed form between atrial/ventricular functional as well as lead related. The inferior vena cava was dilated (24 mm), with reduced respiratory collapsibility and elevated estimated systolic right ventricular pressure.

Management (medical/interventions)

Despite the severity of the patient's symptoms, given his advanced age and worsening renal function, he was deemed too high a risk for surgical intervention. At the time of the decision, the patient's TRI-SCORE was 7/12 with a 34% predicted in-hospital mortality rate after isolated tricuspid valve surgery. After a second multidisciplinary evaluation, he was considered a candidate for TTVR.

TTVR was performed using a 56-mm Evoque valve via right femoral vein under general anesthesia. Valve deployment was successful, with correct placement under transesophageal echocardiographic guidance (Figures 2A and 2B). There were no issues regarding the lead and deployment of the valve. Transthoracic echocardiography after valve positioning revealed only mild remaining TR (Figures 1C and 3A). The postprocedural phase was uncomplicated, including no vascular injury, conduction disturbances, valve embolization, or hemodynamic instability.

Outcome and follow-up

The patient demonstrated rapid clinical improvement, with significant reduction of clinical symptoms within days of the procedure. Functional status improved from NYHA class III to class I immediately (Figure 3B). B-type natriuretic peptide decreased within the first month postprocedure (Figure 3C). Furthermore, the patient decongested rapidly, losing a total of 8 kg from before to after TTVR (Figure 4A). The inferior vena cava returned to its normal caliber within few months postintervention and regained normal collapsibility shortly after (Figure 4B). Laboratory values, mirroring end-organ function, showed parallel improvement. Renal function improved significantly. Creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate returned to their baseline levels within few months (Figures 5A and 5B). Total bilirubin reduced substantially within few months (Figure 5C). These findings demonstrate reversal of venous congestion and cardiorenal dysfunction. Given the significant decongestion after TTVR and rapid improvement of renal function with good diuresis, the patient's furosemide dosage was significantly reduced postprocedure (Figure 6A). Losartan was also reduced by half postprocedure, while dosages for canrenone and dapagliflozin remained the same (except for a short interruption of dapagliflozin in the periprocedural period). On the other hand, hydrochlorothiazide had already been suspended before TTVR (Figures 6B to 6E).

Discussion

This case illustrates the significant impact of severe TR on heart failure symptoms as well as end-organ dysfunction. Effective correction of TR using a TTVR system resulted in rapid resolution of heart failure symptoms and marked improvement of renal and hepatic congestion despite the advanced age of the patient. The observed improvement in renal function highlights the central role of venous congestion in cardiorenal syndrome.4 Although long-term outcomes after TTVR continue to be evaluated, this case supports the growing evidence that transcatheter correction of severe TR can rapidly reverse heart failure symptoms and improve end-organ dysfunction, even in older patients considered too high risk for surgery.

Conclusions

Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement with the Edwards Evoque system was safe and effective in an older patient with severe symptomatic TR, resulting in rapid improvement of heart failure symptoms, venous congestion, and end-organ function.

Impact of tumor number on the efficacy of lenvatinib in borderline resectable hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Naokazu Chiba, Takumi Seichi, Masashi Nakagawa, Shigeto Ochiai, Takahiro Gunji, Toru Sano, Junichi Taira, Katsuya Kitamura, Shigeyuki Kawachi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe global incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is steadily rising, and the disease is associated with poor survival outcomes. As a multicentric cancer with a high recurrence rate, surgical resection alone is often not sufficient for a curative treatment. This study aimed to identify factors that predict its efficacy in patients with HCC classified as borderline resectable according to the BR-HCC Expert Consensus 2023.MethodsThis study retrospectively analyzed 30 patients with borderline resectable HCC who were treated with Lenvatinib as their initial therapy. The study retrospectively examined the predictive factors for the disease control rate (DCR) following Lenvatinib treatment. A secondary objective was to evaluate the impact of the treatment on overall survival and identify cases that showed a significant response.ResultsThe presence of a single tumor was found to be the only independent predictive factor for treatment response. The 5-year survival rate for patients in the stable disease (SD) and partial response (PR) groups was 33.3%, which was significantly higher than that of the progressive disease (PD) group. Of the six patients who achieved a PR with Lenvatinib, four had a single tumor with vascular or biliary invasion or extrahepatic metastasis. Similarly, the two patients who underwent radical resection after Lenvatinib treatment also had a single tumor with these characteristics.ConclusionsThe presence of a single tumor may be a crucial factor in the use of Lenvatinib for borderline HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00423-025-03867-6.

Introduction

Worldwide, the incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is steadily increasing, and HCC is associated with poor survival rates [1, 2]. Recently, the classification of borderline resectable tumors, based on oncological and anatomical factors, has become an important topic in hepatobiliary pancreatic cancer research. The Japanese expert consensus on oncological criteria for HCC resectability (BR-HCC Expert Consensus 2023) provides recommendations for the definition of resectable and borderline resectable HCC. HCC is a multicentric cancer with a high recurrence rate, and surgical resection alone is often insufficient for curative treatment [3].

Recent advances in drug therapy for HCC have been remarkable. Since the first report on the efficacy of sorafenib, several molecular-targeted therapies have been developed. More recently, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapies have shown promising results [4–6]. While numerous publications have examined the efficacy of chemotherapy with various drug combinations, the optimal patient selection criteria and drug choice remain unclear. Additionally, there are no established criteria for Lenvatinib.

Therefore, this study aims to identify predictive factors for its effectiveness in HCC classified as borderline resectable according to the BR-HCC Expert Consensus 2023. The results of this study may help determine the indications for NAC in HCC treatment.

Borderline Resectable 3 (BR2) refers to an oncological status in which the efficacy of surgery is indeterminate, requiring careful evaluation under standard multidisciplinary HCC management. Patients met at least one of the following criteria: multiple lesions with more than five nodules or a diameter exceeding 5 cm; major vascular invasion (Vp4, Vv3, or B4); or extrahepatic disease that does not meet the criteria for a localized factor classified as BR1.

Lenvatinib treatment and evaluation of response and toxicity

Patients received lenvatinib orally at a dose of 12 mg daily for those with a body weight of ≥ 60 kg or 8 mg daily for those with a body weight < 60 kg. Efficacy was assessed monthly using alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and protein induced by vitamin K absence-II (PIVKA-II) levels, while computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed every two months.

Tumor response was evaluated according to the modified response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (mRECIST). Responses were categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR or PR, while the disease control rate (DCR) included patients with CR, PR, and SD. Imaging assessments were performed by two experienced radiologists, with consensus reached in collaboration with the surgeon. The criteria for surgery after chemotherapy were sufficient remnant Liver function and technical feasibility. In addition, imaging tests were performed every four cycles of chemotherapy to determine the timing of surgery. Toxicities were evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

Study endpoint

The predictive factors for DCR after Lenvatinib treatment were retrospectively examined. As a secondary objective, the study also evaluated the impact on overall survival and identified cases with significant treatment responses.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using several statistical methods. To identify predictive factors for the disease control rate (DCR) after Lenvatinib treatment, we performed univariate analyses. The factors examined included age, sex, BMI, etiology, tumor markers (AFP and PIVKA-II), liver function scores (Child-Pugh score, PNI index, mALBI grade), tumor characteristics (number of tumors, maximum diameter, vascular invasion, extrahepatic spread), and other criteria (Up-to-7 and 5-5-500 criteria).

The relationships between categorical variables and DCR were assessed using Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were compared between groups using Welch’s t-test, Student’s t-test, or the Mann-Whitney U-test, as appropriate. For the analysis of overall survival, the Kaplan-Meier method was used to generate survival curves, and the log-rank test was used to compare survival rates between groups (SD/PR vs. PD). Cox hazard analysis was performed to determine independent predictive factors for survival. To evaluate the performance of our predictive models, we used the c-index, Akaike’s information criterion (AIC), receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, and the area under the curve (AUC). A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Discussion

Among the various treatment options available for Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), Liver resection remains the most curative treatment. However, long-term outcomes have been unsatisfactory. A nationwide cohort study in Japan showed a median survival advantage of 1.77 years in patients that underwent Liver resection compared to those that did not. Despite this, survival rates following Liver resection remain suboptimal, with 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates of 74.8%, 49.1%, and 39.1%, respectively [7]. In addition to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI), immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) have significantly advanced drug therapy for HCC. These developments have led to an ongoing debate regarding the definition of borderline for HCC. In this study, we investigated predictive factors for the efficacy of Lenvatinib in BR HCC. Our results suggest that Lenvatinib treatment may be very effective in patients with a single tumor, highlighting the importance of tumor burden when considering Lenvatinib.

The benefits of NAC for HCC have been reported in several studies. Liver resection after NAC has been shown to be feasible, with R0 resection achieved using NAC regimens that include cabozantinib and nivolumab for borderline or locally advanced HCC [8]. Additionally, neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 monotherapy in resectable HCC prior to liver resection has demonstrated a high rate of pathological tumor response [9]. Recent advancements in drug therapy for HCC have been remarkable, leading to increasing efforts to integrate these therapies into NAC. Although combination therapies, including ICI as NAC, have been reported, the underlying mechanism that enhance their antitumor effects remains unclear.

With the recent rise of ICIs, there are several reasons to choose Lenvatinib over ICIs for NAC. Most clinical trials have shown that ICIs do not significantly improve survival within the first year of treatment, making them potentially less suitable for NAC. For example, the IMbrave150 trial showed a median time to response of 12 weeks [10], and another study reported a CR in one patient, but the initial tumor response was lasted as long as 7 months after treatment initiation [11]. In contrast, the rapid onset of lenvatinib’s therapeutic effects suggests that it may be useful as NAC. The results of the REFLECT trial indicated that most patients experienced an initial tumor response within 8 weeks of Lenvatinib administration [12]. Another retrospective study reported that a PR was observed in most patients after only 2 weeks of Lenvatinib administration [13]. Additionally, Lenvatinib has a much shorter half-life compared to ICIs, which allows for a reduced discontinuation period before transitioning to surgery. Lenvatinib has a half-life of only 35.4 h, making a drug-free period of 1 week sufficient before liver resection [14].

Many prognostic factors have been reported for Lenvatinib treatment in HCC, ranging from morphological factors, such as tumor size and vascular invasion, to oncological factors, including the presence of extrahepatic metastases and tumor markers, as well as other factors like sarcopenia and ALBI scores [15–17]. The uniqueness of this study lies in its clear definition of borderline HCC and the identification of a single tumor as a simple yet predictive factor. Recently, ICI regimens, such as atezolizumab plus bevacizumab and durvalumab plus tremelimumab, have emerged, further highlighting the growing interest in NAC therapy. The significance of the predictive factors identified in this study with Lenvatinib is rooted in the notion that liver resection alone is often insufficient as a curative treatment for HCC. Therefore, NAC may have the advantage of preserving further treatment options after recurrence. Furthermore, in cases with multiple tumors, chemotherapy using an ICI regimen may be a more effective NAC.

This study has several Limitations, including its retrospective nature, the small number of cases, and variations in patient backgrounds and disease etiology. The most significant Limitation is patient selection bias. In May 2018, Lenvatinib became covered by insurance in Japan and became the first-line chemotherapy treatment for BR HCC. However, in April 2020, Atezolizumab and Bevacizumab also became covered by insurance. Both drugs were used without clear indications. These factors introduce the possibility of differences in treatment efficacy as a target therapy.

Conclusion

An important factor in the use of Lenvatinib for borderline HCC might be single tumor.

360-Degree Running Suture Technique in Robotic-Assisted Surgery for Bladder Neck Contracture
Authors: Daniel Melecchi de Oliveira Freitas, Vagner Menegotto Comin, Eduardo Rodrigues
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) affects a significant proportion of aging males, often requiring surgical intervention when conservative treatments fail.Case Description:This case report details the management of a 58-year-old male with severe lower urinary tract symptoms and a markedly enlarged prostate, presenting with bladder stones and persistent obstruction despite medication. The patient underwent an open simple prostatectomy but developed bladder neck contracture and recurrent urinary retention, necessitating a suprapubic cystostomy. Following this, a robotic-assisted approach was chosen to address the complex contracture. The surgical strategy involved a vertical posterior bladder incision, after guide wire was visualized a bladder stone was removed, resection of inflammatory tissue, and a novel 360-degree running suture with 3-0 thread for bladder neck reconstruction. This approach was followed by closure with a double-layer running suture using 3-0 V-Lock material. The patient showed significant improvement in urinary flow and symptom resolution postoperatively, with no residual contracture detected on follow-up imaging.Discussion:This report highlights the effectiveness of the robotic-assisted technique and the innovative use of the 360-degree running suture for managing challenging bladder neck contractures, marking a novel application in surgical practice.

INTRODUCTION

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a prevalent condition among aging males, with 2023 data showing that around 50% of men over 50 and up to 90% of men over 80 are affected.1,2 BPH, a noncancerous enlargement of the prostate, causes lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) like increased urinary frequency, nocturia, weak urine stream, difficulty initiating urination, and a sensation of incomplete bladder emptying.3,4 The condition is linked to age-related hormonal changes, particularly involving dihydrotestosterone (DHT) and estrogen.5 Management includes pharmacological treatments such as α-blockers and 5-α-reductase inhibitors, and surgical options for severe cases.4,5 Complications following simple prostatectomy, including urinary incontinence, infection, and urethral strictures, are significant concerns.6,7 Bladder neck contracture, characterized by the narrowing of the bladder neck, is a notable postoperative complication, leading to obstructive urinary symptoms and necessitating further intervention.8,9 In this context, we present a case where a novel approach was implemented: a 360-degree running suture technique, which, to the best of our knowledge, represents the first use of this method for bladder neck contracture. This innovative approach was employed following the failure of initial treatments, highlighting its potential to address complex contractures effectively and improve patient outcomes.

THE CASE

A 58-year-old male presented with severe LUTS characterized by increased urinary frequency, nocturia, weak urine stream, and a sensation of incomplete bladder emptying. Despite using combined oral medications, including α-blockers and 5-α-reductase inhibitors, his symptoms persisted and significantly affected his quality of life. Further evaluation with ultrasonography revealed a markedly enlarged prostate of 120 cc and the presence of 7 bladder stones. Given the severity of his symptoms and the failure of medical management, the patient was scheduled for an open simple prostatectomy. The surgery was performed without intraoperative complications, and a Foley catheter was placed intraoperatively to ensure urinary drainage.

The catheter was removed on postoperative day 7, and the patient was discharged with instructions for follow-up. Three weeks postoperatively, the patient presented to the emergency department with acute urinary retention and a complete obstruction of the urinary stream. Attempts to place another Foley catheter were unsuccessful, prompting the need for a suprapubic cystostomy to relieve the retention. Subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated a contracture of the prostatic bed with significant inflammatory tissue at the bladder neck (Figure 1).

Arrow demonstrates fibrous tissue contracting the bladder neck and prostatic capsule.

During the procedure, a council tip Foley catheter was used in conjunction with a Glidewire to navigate through the bladder neck contracture (Figure 2). This technique allowed for precise placement through the obstructed area, ensuring accurate surgical intervention. The inflammatory tissue at the bladder neck was carefully resected using a combination of robotic instruments, including fenestrated forceps for handling the tissue and robotic scissors for precise excision. The contracture was completely excised circumferentially, extending the resection down to the prostatic capsule to prevent recurrence of the contracture.

(A) Bladder vertical incision. (B) Bladder stone removal. (C) Guide wire placement. (D) Inflammatory tissue resection.

Given the complexity of the case and the patient's recurrent obstruction, a multidisciplinary team discussion was held. The patient opted for a minimally invasive robotic approach for the management of the bladder neck contracture. We performed a robotic resection of the inflammatory tissue and a 360-degree reconstruction of the bladder neck mucosa and urethra. The standard setup includes an optical port, usually placed slightly above the umbilicus, providing central visualization through the laparoscope with a zero-degree camera. Three robotic ports are arranged in a triangular pattern: the right lateral port located in the right lower quadrant about 8–10 cm from the umbilicus, the left lateral port similarly positioned on the left, and a midline assistant port, placed between the optical and lateral ports, typically between the left port and the umbilicus, which may be used for aspiration and hem-o-lock insertion. A third arm port was placed lateral to the right portal.

A single 360-degree running suture was applied using a 3-0 V-Loc suture. The suture was anchored at one point on the bladder neck and run circumferentially around the neck to achieve a tension-free reconstruction. The bladder mucosa was sutured directly to the prostatic capsule, ensuring a robust and secure connection to reduce the risk of recurrent contracture. After the suture, a 20 French silicone Foley catheter was inserted to ensure proper drainage, and the bladder wall was closed using a double-layer running suture with 3-0 V-Loc material for secure closure (Figure 3). No drain was placed.

(A and B) Placement of 3-0 V-Lock running suture. (C) Final aspect with Silicone catheter placement. (D) Closure of bladder wall.

The choice of a silicone catheter was intentional due to its biocompatibility and lower risk of causing urethral irritation compared to latex catheters. In our practice, silicone catheters have been beneficial in reducing the risk of contracture recurrence by minimizing inflammatory responses at the anastomosis site.

The robotic surgery was performed successfully, with precise resection of the contracture and reconstruction of the bladder neck. The patient had an uneventful postoperative course. He was discharged with a Foley catheter in place on day 2, and the catheter was removed 2 weeks postsurgery. Follow-up evaluations showed significant improvement in urinary flow and resolution of symptoms. At 3 months postoperatively, the patient remained recurrence-free, with significant improvement in urinary flow and no evidence of bladder neck contracture on imaging. At the time of this report, 12-month follow-up had not been completed, but no early signs of recurrence were noted in the initial recovery period.

DISCUSSION

BPH is a common condition in aging males that often requires surgical intervention when conservative treatments fail.1,2 Despite the availability of various surgical options, complications such as urinary incontinence, bladder neck contracture, and urethral stricture remain prevalent. Studies underscore the complexity of managing BPH and highlight the need for precise surgical techniques to improve patient outcomes.3,4 In this context, the robotic approach has emerged as a promising option, offering potential advantages over traditional methods.5,6 Given the challenges and complications associated with conventional prostatectomy techniques, we opted to employ a robotic approach for this case to enhance precision and recovery.

Bladder neck contracture, a common complication following prostatectomy, is characterized by the narrowing of the bladder neck, leading to obstructive urinary symptoms such as decreased flow, increased frequency, and urgency.7,8 To manage this condition, several surgical techniques are employed. Endoscopic incision, which involves making incisions via an endoscope, offers a minimally invasive solution for less severe contractures and provides immediate symptom relief.7 Endoscopic balloon dilation, another minimally invasive method, uses a balloon catheter to stretch the contracted area and is suitable for less severe cases.8 For more extensive or recurrent contractures, surgical reconstruction techniques such as Y-V plasty or the Rocco stitch may be necessary to restore a functional bladder neck.9

Y-V plasty, a well-established technique for managing bladder neck contracture, involves creating a V-shaped incision and advancing bladder tissue to reconstruct the neck. In comparison, our 360-degree running suture technique offers a more minimally invasive approach, especially in the context of robotic-assisted surgery, allowing for precise tissue handling and circumferential closure. Early results suggest this technique may offer similar efficacy in preventing recurrence, with potentially reduced morbidity due to the smaller incisions and shorter recovery time.

Additionally, robotic-assisted surgery provides enhanced precision and control for bladder neck reconstruction, potentially improving outcomes and reducing recovery times.10 These techniques offer a range of options to address bladder neck contracture, aiming to restore normal urinary function and enhance patient quality of life. The case report demonstrated a shift to robotic-assisted surgery after initial treatments failed, reflecting a modern approach that leverages enhanced precision and control to address complex contractures with a 360-degree bladder mucosa urethral suture.10

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the management of BPH and its complications, such as bladder neck contracture, presents ongoing challenges in urological practice. This case highlights the innovative use of a 360-degree running suture technique for addressing complex bladder neck contracture following the failure of conventional treatments. The transition to a robotic-assisted approach provided enhanced precision and control, which proved pivotal in achieving successful surgical outcomes and improving patient quality of life. While robotic surgery offers significant benefits, including reduced complications and shorter recovery times, it is important to balance these advantages with the higher associated costs.

This case also emphasizes the technical nuances that can improve surgical precision, such as using wristed robotic instruments for tissue manipulation and following a clockwise suturing pattern for better circumferential closure. Further studies and long-term follow-up are needed to fully assess the efficacy and cost-effectiveness of this approach, but the promising results from this case suggest that robotic-assisted surgery could play a crucial role in the future of BPH management and bladder neck reconstruction.

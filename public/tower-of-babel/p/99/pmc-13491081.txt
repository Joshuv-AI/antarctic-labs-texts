Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol Goal Attainment in Indian Patients With Coronary Artery Disease
Authors: Raman Puri, Manish Bansal, Kunal Mahajan, Rajeev Gupta, Vimal Mehta, Shamanna S Iyengar, Dorairaj Prabhakar, Rajeev Agarwala, Aziz Khan, Neil Bordoloi, Nagaraj Desai, Sheeba George, Biswajit Majumder, Smit Shrivastava, Vinod Vijan, Asha Mahilmaran, Dharmendra Jain, Sadanand R Shetty, P Barton Duell, Nathan D Wong, LAI-LCARE Study Investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPremature atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) remains a significant challenge in India, despite comparatively lower population-level low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels. The Lipid Association of India advocates for aggressive lipid-lowering therapy to achieve an LDL-C target <50 mg/dL in patients with established ASCVD.ObjectivesThis study aimed to provide contemporary data on LDL-C goal attainment in Indian patients with ASCVD.MethodsIn this largest Indian study to date, 122 cardiologists across India recruited 10,417 patients (9,658 included in analysis, mean age 60.7 ± 11.0 years, 7,427 [76.9%] men) with documented coronary artery disease who were on stable lipid-lowering therapy for at least 1 month before their most recent LDL-C measurement. Only directly measured LDL-C was considered.ResultsThe median treated LDL-C concentration was 66.1 mg/dL (IQR: 49.0-92.0) with only 27.0% (2,603 of 9,658, 95% CI: 26.1%-27.8%) having values ≤50 mg/dL. High-intensity statin therapy was used in 73.3% (7,047/9,615, 95% CI: 72.4%-74.2%) of subjects. Combination LDL-C lowering therapy was used in 11.9% (1,153/9,658, 95% CI: 11.3%-12.6%) of subjects [8.6% (830/9,658, 95% CI: 8.0%-9.2%) receiving two drugs, 3.3% (323/9,658, 95% CI: 3.0%-3.7%) receiving 3 drugs]. Among patients with LDL-C >50 mg/dL, the treating clinicians uptitrated the regimen in only 37.3% (2,552 of 6,833, 95% CI: 36.2%-38.5%) of subjects.ConclusionsThis study highlights a major gap in LDL-C control for secondary prevention of ASCVD in India. Despite high-intensity statin use, only one-quarter achieved target levels. The low utilization of combination therapy and the limited treatment intensification observed in patients with LDL-C above target represent important opportunities for improving lipid management and cardiovascular outcomes.Key Words: bempedoic acid, evolocumab, ezetimibe, inclisiran, Lipid Association of India, lipid-lowering therapy

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) is the leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for nearly one-fourth of all global deaths.1 India is among the worst affected nations with rising incidence and prevalence of ASCVD, premature onset of the disease, and a higher case fatality rate.2 Considering this enormous burden of ASCVD and the fact that a large part of the risk is preventable, aggressive efforts for its prevention are required on priority.

Effective lowering of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) is one of the most important strategies for ASCVD prevention. Data from numerous observational, interventional, and Mendelian randomization studies have unequivocally demonstrated the benefit of LDL-C reduction for prevention of ASCVD. The absolute ASCVD risk reduction is proportional to the baseline ASCVD risk and the magnitude of LDL-C lowering, with no LDL-C threshold below which there is no further cardiovascular benefit.3,4 Hence, all current guidelines recommend intensive LDL-C lowering as one of key therapeutic goals for secondary prevention of ASCVD, with LDL-C targets ranging from <70 mg/dL to <30 mg/dL, depending on the estimated risk and source of the guidelines.5, 6, 7

Unfortunately, despite universal recommendations for intensive LDL-C lowering for secondary prevention of ASCVD, LDL-C control remains suboptimal worldwide. This represents a major missed opportunity that needs urgent attention. This is particularly relevant for India where more intensive LDL-C lowering is essential to reduce the excess ASCVD related morbidity and mortality.8 However, to formulate policies for improving lipid management in clinical practice, contemporary data describing the patient profiles, prescribing patterns of various lipid-lowering therapies (LLTs), and the status of LDL-C control are needed. Most of the available data on this subject has been derived from the studies performed in the Western populations, with only limited information available from India. Hence, to bridge this knowledge gap, this nationwide study, the LAI-LCARE (Lipid Association of India LDL-Cholesterol optimization in Coronary ARtEry disease), was undertaken. This is one of the largest clinical audits conducted to this date of lipid management in real-world clinical practice. In this report, we describe the key findings from this comprehensive nationwide study.

Methods

Study design

The study was conducted under the direction of the Lipid Association of India (LAI). Practicing cardiologists were invited to participate in this study through direct email and outreach via social media cardiology networks to maximize awareness and participation. Those who confirmed to recruit at least 100 consecutive, eligible patients during the study period were included in the study on first-come-first-served basis. Patients with established ASCVD attending outpatient clinics under the care of participating cardiologists were recruited. Enrollment commenced in May 2024, with the initial 2 weeks serving as a preparatory phase to familiarize participating cardiologists with the study protocol. Regular recruitment began in June 2024 and continued steadily until the enrollment period concluded in December 2024. A standardized recording proforma was provided to all participants to ensure uniform data collection. This proforma was made available in both online and offline formats, allowing flexibility in data entry and facilitating broader participation across diverse healthcare settings. The enrollment per participating cardiologist was capped at 100 to avoid any recruitment bias.

Ethical approval

The Ethical Committee of the Eternal Heart Institute, Jaipur, India provided ethical oversight and approval on behalf of all participating centers (study reference no. 001-05/2024), ensuring compliance with ethical standards for the study. In addition, participating centers were encouraged to also obtain local institutional ethical committee approval, in accordance with their respective institutional requirements. Given the noninterventional design, use of deidentified data, and lack of additional risk, a waiver of informed consent was granted in line with national regulations and internationally accepted ethical standards.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

The study included patients aged 18 years and older with established ASCVD who had a direct LDL-C measurement performed within the preceding 3 months, with the most recent value considered for analysis. Eligible participants were those with angiographically proven coronary artery disease (CAD), defined as >50% stenosis in at least 1 major epicardial coronary artery, with angiography performed at least 1 month before the most recent LDL-C measurement. Patients with a history of prior percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting performed at least 1 month before their latest LDL-C assessment were also included. To ensure stability in lipid management, all patients were required to have been on a constant LLT regimen, including a statin, for at least 1 month before their most recent LDL-C measurement, without any dosage adjustments or medication changes.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded from the study if they were taking statins irregularly, defined as missing at least 1 dose per week for 2 or more weeks in the past month. In addition, individuals who had undergone any dose escalation or a change in their lipid-lowering medication within the 4 weeks preceding their most recent LDL-C measurement were not eligible. Patients whose LDL-C values were not directly measured but rather calculated from other lipid parameters were also excluded. Other exclusion criteria included known human immunodeficiency virus–positive status, ongoing treatment with immunosuppressants or cancer chemotherapy, and women who were pregnant or breastfeeding at the time of enrollment.

Data collection

For every patient, demographic details, details of their ASCVD and ASCVD risk factors, and ongoing LLT were collected. The latest lipid profile values and glycosylated hemoglobin and serum creatinine values were also recorded. The usage of other ASCVD risk reduction therapies was also recorded. Last, the clinicians were also asked whether they made changes in the patient’s LLT in response to the latest lipid profile findings. However, the reasons underlying these treatment decisions were not asked to minimize respondent burden and to maintain a nonintrusive, real-world data collection process for participating clinicians.

Definitions

An LDL-C level ≤50 mg/dL was considered the primary target for LLT. High-intensity statin therapy (HIST) was defined as atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg/d or rosuvastatin 20 to 40 mg/d. Combination LDL-lowering therapy was defined as any combination of statins with ezetimibe and/or bempedoic acid. Evolocumab and inclisiran were not included in this definition because very few patients (0.1% each) were receiving these therapies. Alirocumab is not available in India. Other lipid-lowering agents such as fenofibrate, icosapent ethyl, and saroglitazar were also not considered in the definition of LDL-C lowering combination therapy.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics and other descriptive variables were summarized using standard statistical tools such as mean ± SD, median with IQR, or counts and proportions as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, whereas quantitative variables were compared using the Student’s independent t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, or Kruskal-Wallis test. Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify factors associated with attainment of LDL-C goals. The outcome variable was attainment of LDL goal (yes/no). Logistic regression using the logit link function was used to estimate ORs with 95% CIs. Variables with P value <0.05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariable logistic regression model to adjust for potential confounding factors. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 20.0.

Results

Overall, 122 Indian cardiologists from 25 different states participated in this study (Supplemental Figure 1, Supplemental Appendix 1). A total of 10,417 subjects were enrolled in the study during the period May 2024 to Dec 2024. Of these, 759 (7.3%) entries were excluded due to data entry errors or incomplete lipid profile values (655, 6.3%) or incorrect patient enrollment (104, 1.0%). The remaining 9,658 patients were included in the final analysis. Supplemental Table 1 describes the clinical and laboratory characteristics of the subjects excluded from the analysis.

Mean age of the patients was 60.7 ± 11.0 years and 76.9% (7,427 of 9,658) were men. Diabetes mellitus and hypertension were present in nearly half of all individuals. Overall, 68.4% (6,609 of 9,658) of subjects had previously had a myocardial infarction and 71.1% (6,866 of 9,658) had undergone PCI. Most patients had presented with CAD alone, with only 2.1% (201 of 9,658 and 198 of 9,658) of patients having concomitant history of cerebrovascular accident or peripheral artery disease each (Table 1).

Lipid levels

The median LDL-C at the time of enrollment was 66.1 mg/dL (IQR: 49.0-92.0) with only 27.0% (2,603 of 9,658, 95% CI: 26.1%-27.8%) having LDL-C values ≤50 mg/dL and 55.2% (5,336 of 9,658, 95% CI: 54.3%-56.2%) having LDL-C ≤70 mg/dL (Table 1). The patients who had LDL-C below the target (≤50 mg/dL) were more likely to be males and have history of diabetes mellitus, previous myocardial infarction, and previous PCI. The patients with peripheral artery disease had paradoxically higher LDL-C levels. The angiographic extent of CAD did not have any relationship with the LDL-C levels (Table 1, Supplemental Table 2).

LLT and the attainment of the LDL-C goals

Rosuvastatin was used more often than atorvastatin (52.6% [5,079 of 9,658, 95% CI: 51.6%-53.6%] vs 47.0% [4,536 of 9,658, 95% CI: 46.0%-48.0%]). Statin type was not defined for 43 (0.45%, 95% CI: 0.31%-0.58%) patients. HIST was used in 73.3% (7,047/9,615, 95% CI: 72.4%-74.2%) of subjects. (Table 2). Combination LDL-C lowering therapy was used in 11.9% (1,153/9,658, 95% CI: 11.3%-12.6%) of subjects with 8.6% (830/9,658, 95% CI: 8.0%-9.2%) of subjects receiving two drugs and 3.3% (323/9,658, 95% CI: 3.0%-3.7%) receiving 3 drugs. Ezetimibe was the most used nonstatin drug, prescribed to 1,111 (11.5%, 95% CI: 10.9%-12.1%) subjects. Only 0.1%-0.2% of patients were on evolocumab, inclisiran, icosapent ethyl, or saroglitazar.

Differences in LLT use and LDL-C goal attainment were observed across geographic regions and clinical practice settings (Table 4). The use of ezetimibe and combination therapy was lower in the east and northeast zones compared with other regions and was also lower in public health care settings than in private centers. LDL-C goal attainment was largely comparable across geographic regions, except in the south zone, where a significantly lower proportion of patients achieved the LDL-C target (P < 0.001). In addition, LDL-C goal attainment was lower in the public health care system compared with the private health care system (P < 0.001).

Multivariable analysis

The multivariable analysis showed that male sex, diabetes mellitus, history of previous PCI, use of rosuvastatin, and treatment in a private health care setting were independently associated with greater achievement of LDL-C goal (≤50 mg/dL), whereas peripheral artery disease was inversely associated with it (Table 5).

Physician response to the lipid profile findings

Treatment modification data were available for 9,350 (96.8%, 95% CI: 96.5%-97.2%) patients. Among patients with LDL-C above goal (>50 mg/dL), the LDL-C lowering therapy was uptitrated in 37.3% (2,552 of 6,833, 95% CI: 36.2%-38.5%), whereas no change was made in 59.7% (4,080 of 6,833, 95% CI: 58.5%-60.9%) and downtitrated in 2.9% (201 of 6,833, 95% CI: 2.5%-3.3%). Conversely, among patients with LDL-C ≤50 mg/dL, the therapy was downtitrated in 9.5% (240 of 2,517, 95% CI: 8.4%-10.7%) of patients (Table 6).

Discussion

The LAI-LCARE study is the largest investigation to evaluate LLT-prescribing patterns and LDL-C goal attainment in patients with ASCVD in India. A total of 10,417 patients with angiographically documented ASCVD and stable LLT regimen were recruited from 122 cardiologists across India. The results of the study showed that LDL-C goals were achieved in only about one-quarter of patients, despite HIST reported in 73.3% patients and combination LDL-C lowering therapy in 11.9% of patients. Moreover, among the 73% of patients with LDL-C above goal, the LLT regimen was uptitrated in only about 40% of patients (Central Illustration). Hence, despite failure to achieve LDL-C goals in 73% of patients, and very low rates of treatment with combination therapy, no change in the treatment regimen was made in more than half of patients whose LDL-C results were above goal. This demonstrates a missed opportunity for improved LDL-C goal achievement and reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with ASCVD, even under the expert care of cardiologists in India.

Key Findings From the LAI-LCARE (Lipid Association of India LDL-Cholesterol Optimization in Coronary ARtEry Disease) Survey

Among 9,658 Indian patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) on stable lipid-lowering therapy (LLT), rosuvastatin (R) was used more commonly than atorvastatin (A), high-intensity statin therapy (HIST, defined as atorvastatin 40 or 80 mg/d or rosuvastatin 20 or 40 mg/d) in 73.3% and combination therapy (statin with ezetimibe and/or bempedoic acid [BA]) in 11.9% of patients. Only 27.0% of patients achieved the low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) goal of ≤50 mg/dL overall and only 29.8% and 46.3% of those on HIST and combination therapy. Among those with LDL-C >50 mg/dL, LDL-C lowering therapy was stepped up in only 37.3% of patients.

LDL-C reduction in clinical practice

Numerous experimental, epidemiological, Mendelian randomization, and interventional studies have clearly shown that LDL-C is a primary cause of ASCVD. Reducing LDL-C reduces ASCVD risk with the absolute risk reduction being proportional to the baseline ASCVD risk and the magnitude of LDL-C reduction. Furthermore, there is no apparent threshold below which there is no incremental benefit.3,4 The safety of ultra-low LDL-C levels has also been established.9, 10, 11 Accordingly, all major lipid management guidelines recommend aggressive LDL-C reduction in patients with established ASCVD. The 2018 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines recommend an LDL-C target of <70 mg/dL, whereas the 2019 European Society of Cardiology (ESC)/ European Atherosclerotic Society guidelines recommended an LDL-C target of <55 mg/dL for all patients with ASCVD and <40 mg/dL for those with recurrent vascular events.5,6 The LAI recommendations have been more stringent with a goal of <50 mg/dL for all patients with ASCVD and more intensive targets (<30 mg/dL or even 10-15 mg/dL) for patients with additional high-risk features such polyvascular disease or recurrent vascular events.7,12 Such aggressive LDL-C lowering goals are necessary for Indian individuals because of the higher incidence, premature onset, and more malignant course of ASCVD despite lower LDL-C compared with Western populations, along with the unique lipid pattern in Indian individuals characterized by atherogenic dyslipidemia.8

Unfortunately, despite clear recommendations for intensive LDL-C lowering for secondary prevention for ASCVD, LDL-C goal achievement has generally been suboptimal worldwide.13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 DA VINCI (EU-Wide Cross-Sectional Observational Study of Lipid-Modifying Therapy Use in Secondary and Primary Care) was a retrospective observational study that included 5,888 patients from 18 European countries.13 Of these, 2,888 patients had established ASCVD. Complete data were available for only 2,039 patients. HIST was used in 42% of subjects and statin-ezetimibe combination was used in 9.3% of subjects. The 2019 ESC recommended LDL-C goal <55 mg/dl was achieved in only 18% of subjects overall, and only 22% and 21% of those receiving HIST and statin-ezetimibe combination, respectively.

DYSIS II (Dyslipidemia International Study II) was a multinational, multicenter, prospective observational study that included 6,794 patients with stable CAD and 3,867 patients being discharged after an acute coronary syndrome (ACS).14 The patients were recruited during 2012-2013 from 18 countries in Asia, Europe, and the Middle East. At baseline, 93.8% of patients with CAD were receding LLT and 29.4% had LDL-C <70 mg/dL. Among patients with ACS, statin usage increased after the ACS event with 95.6% of patients receiving LLT at 120-day follow-up. The LDL-C was <70 mg/dL in only 37% of 1,071 patients for whom follow-up data were available.

A recent single center study from Singapore reported LDL-C and remnant cholesterol levels in 21,605 subjects. Of these, 68% had ASCVD and 82.5% were on statins. Among ASCVD patients, only 8.6% had LDL-C <55 mg/dL and 23.7% had LDL-C <70 mg/dL.15

A large study from Japan looked at the Medical Data Vision claims database for the period 2018 to 2021 to ascertain the status of lipid lowering.16 Overall, 183,145 subjects were recruited of whom 57,910 were in the secondary prevention group and 33,850 belonged to the high-risk secondary prevention category. Among those in the high-risk secondary prevention category, the LDL-C target was <70 mg/dL. Only 46.6% of individuals were on statins and only 6.6% were on statin-ezetimibe combination, whereas 46.5% were on no LLT. The LDL-C was below the target in 26.7% receiving statin therapy and 46.4% receiving statin-ezetimibe combination.

ICLPS (International ChoLesterol management Practice Study) was a physician survey conducted across 18 countries outside Western Europe.17 A total of 9,049 patients with an LDL-C measurement on stable LLT available within the previous 12 months were included. In the very high-risk group, which included 3,319 subjects with documented CAD, 98.8% of subjects were on statins with 32.5% on HIST and 9.1% on statin-ezetimibe combination. The LDL-C was at goal <70 mg/dL in only 28.5% of subjects.

More recently, a clinical audit— SURF CHD II (The Survey in Risk Factors in Coronary Heart Disease II)—was performed in 29 countries to assess the impact of education level on secondary prevention of CAD. A total of 13,884 patients were recruited from 2019 to 2022, of whom 10,900 had a recent LDL-C measurement available. Only 20.8% of individuals had LDL levels <55 mg/dL and 40.8% LDL <70 mg/dL.18

Very limited information is available about the usage of LLT among Indian subjects. In the SURF CHD II study described previously, fewer than 2,000 patients were recruited from India and the findings from Indian patients were not published separately.18 Recently, a study was published from a tertiary care center in India.20 The study included 1,275 consecutive subjects with newly diagnosed CAD who had undergone coronary revascularization in 2023. Rosuvastatin was used in 1,139 (89.3%) patients and atorvastatin in 124 (9.7%) patients. HIST was used in 90% of subjects and nonstatin drugs in 49 (3.8%) subjects. Despite high usage of HIST, only 29.9% of subjects overall and 32.1% of those on HIST reached the LDL-C target of <50 mg/dL after a median follow-up of 99 days (IQR: 91-109 days). It is notable that among patients receiving both rosuvastatin 40 mg/d and ezetimibe 10 mg/d, 46.7% achieved LDL-C <50 mg/dL.

Reasons for inadequate LDL-C lowering

Multiple factors contribute to inadequate LDL-C lowering in clinical practice, which include physician inertia, lack of awareness among patients, and sometimes physicians regarding the benefits of intensive LDL-C lowering, the cost of drug treatment and laboratory testing, concerns regarding possible side effects of medications, and other variables. For more than the past decade, LAI has been providing physician educational programs highlighting the need for intensive LDL-C lowering and offering strategies for achieving LDL-C goals. Multiple guidelines and consensus statements have also been published.7,8,12,21, 22, 23

Despite these efforts, the results of our study document low rates of LDL-C goal achievement and provide insights into the reasons for underachievement of LDL-C goals in clinical practice in India. Multiple factors likely contribute to inadequate LDL-C lowering in clinical practice. In the present study, treatment was not intensified in approximately 60% of patients with LDL-C levels above target. Because the reasons underlying treatment decisions were not collected, our findings cannot distinguish appropriate treatment non-intensification from clinician inertia or other patient-, physician-, or health-system-related factors. Nevertheless, the observed rates of treatment intensification suggest an important opportunity to further optimize LDL-C lowering in routine clinical practice. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies, such as a survey in Germany that showed that only 9.3% of subjects with CAD and 15.2% of subjects with CAD and diabetes achieved the LDL-C target of <70 mg/dL.24 Despite very low rates of goal achievement, more than 60% of the participating physicians were satisfied with the achieved LDL-C levels.

These findings highlight the need for increased awareness among cardiologists and general physicians regarding the necessity of prescribing adequate LDL-C lowering therapy to achieve LDL-C goals. Cost is not a primary constraint in India as statins and ezetimibe are quite inexpensive, and bempedoic acid is affordable for most patients. Injectable therapies such as evolocumab and inclisiran are unaffordable for most patients. Concerns about side effects are also unfounded as no major safety issues have been reported even with high-dose statin therapy or at very low LDL-C levels.9, 10, 11 Moreover, very few patients in our study were on combination LDL-C lowering therapy, which indicates a significant opportunity for adding nonstatin drugs to facilitate additional LDL-C lowering, without increasing the likelihood of statin-related muscle symptoms, the commonest statin-related side effect in clinical practice. Data from our recently published treatment study demonstrated high tolerability of the triple combination of rosuvastatin 40 mg, ezetimibe 10 mg, and bempedoic acid 180 mg daily, as well as high rates of LDL-C goal achievement among patients with ACS.25 The study included 122 statin-naïve patients who presented with an ACS. All patients were treated with this triple combination starting at the time of admission for ACS. At 2 weeks, 76.3% of the patients had LDL-C <50 mg/dL and 79.8% had an LDL-C <55 mg/dL. After 6 weeks of treatment 64.8% and 73.3% had achieved LDL-C <50 and <55 mg/dL, respectively. The combination therapy was well-tolerated with no apparent safety concerns, at least in the short term. These findings indicate that greater use of HIST, along with appropriate treatment intensification using ezetimibe and/or bempedoic acid in patients whose LDL-C levels remain above target, has the potential to improve LDL-C goal attainment among Indian patients.

Study Strengths and limitations

Our study has many strengths. It was a nationwide study with representation from almost every state in India. The sample size was large, making it one of the largest clinical audits assessing LLT usage in the world. Unlike most of the previous studies that relied on retrospective reviews of patient records, our study involved collection of information as part of real-world clinical practice, which helped to avoid errors in correlating LLT usage with LDL-C goal attainment. In addition, only directly measured LDL-C values were considered, which improves the accuracy of LDL-C quantification in the context of high rates of hypertriglyceridemia and low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol among Indian individuals. All the participating clinicians were practicing cardiologists who were expected to be using more intensive LLT as recommended by ESC and LAI guidelines. Last, this study provides the most contemporary data regarding LLT usage in clinical practice at a time when stringent guideline-recommended LDL-C goals (<50-55 mg/dL) have been available to clinicians for more than 4 years.

Our study has several important limitations that warrant consideration. First, although participating physicians were drawn from across the country, they were not randomly selected, introducing the potential for selection bias. In addition, all participating clinicians were cardiologists and most centers were located in urban or semi-urban settings. Also, though it was planned to recruit 100 patients per center, the numbers varied (median 101, IQR: 79-104) due to varying patient load. These factors limit the generalizability of our findings to broader clinical practice in India, particularly to rural settings, lower socioeconomic populations, and care provided by noncardiologists. Consequently, our results likely represent a best-case scenario, and the reported rates of LLT use and LDL-C goal attainment may overestimate what would be observed in the general population. Consistent with this, the population-based PURE (Prospective Urban Rural Epidemiology) study from India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh reported statin use in fewer than 5% of individuals with ASCVD, although these data are now almost 10 years old.26

Second, given the observational nature of the study, our findings were primarily descriptive, with the objective of characterizing real-world use of LLT and LDL-C goal attainment in routine clinical practice. We recognize that adjustment for additional factors such as socioeconomic status, disease duration, treatment adherence, and comorbidities could further enhance interpretation; however, the comprehensive and uniform collection of these variables was beyond the scope of a large, nationwide clinical audit. Consequently, our analyses were intentionally focused on describing overall prescribing patterns and LDL-C control, rather than on generating mechanistic or causal inferences. The multivariable analysis was also included only as a supportive, exploratory assessment and was not intended to provide mechanistic insight.

Last, participation in the study may have influenced physician behavior, potentially leading to optimization of LLT following data collection. This could have resulted in an overestimation of treatment intensification among patients not at LDL-C goal and may also have influenced baseline LLT patterns. Taken together, these considerations suggest that LLT utilization observed in this study reflects the upper bound of contemporary clinical practice rather than routine care across all health care settings.

Conclusions

This study reveals a critical gap in LDL-C management in secondary prevention of ASCVD in India. Despite widespread high-intensity statin use, only about one-quarter of patients attained guideline-recommended LDL-C levels. The strikingly low utilization of combination therapy and the limited treatment intensification observed in patients whose LDL-C remained above target represent important opportunities for improving lipid management and cardiovascular outcomes.

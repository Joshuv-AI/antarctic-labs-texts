Protocol to establish turkey oviductal organoids as an in vitro model
Authors: Pitchaya Santativongchai, Caitlin C Klaeui, Sunantha Kosonsiriluk, Milena Saqui-Salces, Kent M Reed, Ben W Wileman, Marissa M Studniski, Kahina S Boukherroub
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe study of reproductive function in turkey hens has been difficult due to the lack of a reliable, representative in vitro model for investigating profound physiological aspects. This article presents a protocol to establish turkey oviductal organoids, including steps for isolating turkey oviduct epithelial cells followed by seeding and maintaining 3D organoid cultures. We also detail procedures for organoid fixation for histological analysis. This organoid model could serve as a valuable in vitro tool for understanding the intricacies of turkey reproductive physiology.Subject areas: cell biology, organoids, tissue engineering

Before you begin

This protocol was optimized using magnum tissues of the oviduct obtained from turkey hens (30–33 weeks of age, 2nd to 5th week of lay) and modification of previously reported culture protocols for mouse and human oviductal and vaginal organoids,1,2,3 chicken reproductive tract cells,4,5,6 and turkey sperm-storage-tubule cells7 to develop methods for isolating turkey oviduct epithelial cells, establishing and maintaining 3D organoid cultures, and cryopreservation. All steps for the isolation of turkey oviduct epithelial cells must be carried out on ice. Prepare all reagents before starting the procedure and keep on ice or at 4°C. All procedures are performed under standard aseptic conditions using sterile equipment, sterile cell culture-grade reagents, and appropriate personal protective equipment.

Step-by-step method details

Isolation of turkey oviduct epithelial cells

Timing: 1–2 h

This step outlines the procedure for isolating turkey oviduct epithelial cells from a single turkey hen using enzymatic dissociation. The cell suspension is washed and centrifuged to generate a cell pellet. The pellet is subsequently resuspended for cell counting.

1.Dissection of oviduct tissues.a.Euthanize the turkey hen using CO2 administration at 23 L/min for 5 min.b.Before dissection, spray the ventral surface of the hen and surrounding area with 70% ethanol.i.Make an incision under the keel bone into the abdominal cavity.ii.Move the intestine laterally to reveal the reproductive tract located on the left side of the body.iii.Follow the uterus cranially to locate the oviduct.iv.Aseptically dissect the distal part of the oviduct (infundibulum and magnum (7–10 cm in length); see Figure 1).v.Wash the tissues briefly with ice-cold dissection wash buffer.vi.Place the tissue into ice-cold collection media in a collection cup.c.Transfer the samples on ice to continue the next step in a biosafety cabinet.

Establishment of turkey oviductal organoid cultures

Timing: 30–60 min

This section outlines the steps required to seed the turkey oviduct epithelial cells into the Matrigel domes to develop organoid cultures. An appropriate dilution of the cell isolate is resuspended in Matrigel, dispensed as gel domes onto the multiple-well plate, and overlaid with warm organoid culture media. The organoids grow and expand within these gel domes.

3.Seeding organoid cultures.a.Prewarm organoid culture media in a 37°C water bath, and place sterile 48-well tissue culture plates in a 37°C incubator at least 1 h before seeding. Thaw Matrigel on ice.Note: Prewarming of tissue culture plates and organoid culture media before seeding helps the Matrigel domes to solidify in the middle of wells without touching the sides, to ensure that the organoids develop in 3D cultures.b.Dilute cells with ice-cold DMEM/F12 into 1.6 × 104 viable cells/μL.Note: This step can be skipped if using isthmus and vagina tissues, to keep cell concentrations as high as possible to facilitate organoid development (final concentration of cells in Matrigel = 2.5 × 104 to 7.5 × 104 cells/μL).c.Add 80 μL of the cells to 80 μL of prewarmed organoid culture media and resuspend in 480 μL ice-cold Matrigel by carefully pipetting for 20–30 times (final concentration of cells = 2,000 cells/μL).CRITICAL: Cell suspension, organoid culture media, and Matrigel volumes should be adjusted to achieve a 1:3 cell/Matrigel ratio, depending on the number of wells to be plated.Note: Resuspend the cells slowly and carefully to avoid introducing air bubbles, as this can affect the growth of organoids. Plate as quickly as possible, as Matrigel can solidify and adhere to pipet tips and tubes.d.Plate 80 μL of the cell/Matrigel solution onto the center of wells in the prewarmed 48-well plates.e.Incubate the plates in a 37°C incubator for 15 min to allow the Matrigel to solidify.f.Once solidified, add 250 μL of prewarmed organoid culture media containing 1X RevitaCell supplement to each well. Incubate the cultures in a humidified 37°C incubator supplied with 5% CO2.

Expansion of turkey oviductal organoid cultures

Timing: 10–14 days

Organoids should become visible after 3–7 days in culture (Figure 3A) and should be passaged every 7–14 days (Figure 3B). Organoids are passaged using mechanical dissociation. Since the organoids tend to be sticky and adhere to the inside of pipet tips and tube walls, pre-wetting with DMEM/F12 or appropriate media helps prevent adherence and maintains organoid yields (see problem 1). Dissociated organoids, as small multicellular fragments, are then washed, resuspended in Matrigel, and seeded to culture.

4.Maintaining organoid cultures.a.Replace the organoid culture media (without the addition of RevitaCell supplement) every 2–3 days.b.Monitor organoid development under an inverted microscope. If organoids fail to develop within 14 days, discard the culture and reinitiate it from a new batch (see problem 2).

Expected outcomes

This study developed a protocol for isolation of turkey oviduct epithelial cells, establishment, and cryopreservation of 3D organoid cultures. Turkey oviductal organoids should appear within 3–7 days of culture as small, spherical structures with defined edges (Figure 3A). The organoids become large cystic balloon-like structures with a central lumen, consistent with reports on mouse and human oviductal organoids11,12 and were ready for passage after 10–14 days of culture (Figure 3B). The infundibulum (INF) and magnum (MAG) organoids developed using this protocol were passaged for at least 8 passages and continued to grow (Figure 3C), while other regions were able to be passaged for 2–3 passages, relevant to the highest growth rate of the distal region reported in mouse oviductal organoids.12,13 These organoids (INF and MAG) have also been recovered from cryopreservation, expanded, and re-cryopreserved for future use. The post-thaw cryopreserved organoids initially appear as fragments or intact small organoids in cultures (Figure 3D), and about 50% of the organoids continued expanding (Figures 3E and 3F). Depending on the experiment, either using each region of the oviduct, only the distal region (INF and MAG),13 proximal region (isthmus, uterus, and vagina),13 or the entire oviduct11,12,14 may be required.

This article describes an efficient method of organoid fixation for further paraffin embedding and histological analysis. Staining results showed that the organoids displayed expression of progesterone receptor alpha and sialic acid α2,3-galactose linked (avian influenza virus, AIV) receptors similar to that observed in the original oviduct epithelial tissues (Figure 4). The progesterone receptor was expressed in the cytoplasm of the organoid cells instead of the nucleus, suggesting that the receptors in organoids were not activated by the hormone and thus localized to the nucleus as observed in the tissues.15 Since oviductal organoids are amenable to many standard in vitro assays, including RNA and DNA extraction,13,16 immunohistochemistry,16 immunofluorescence,2,13 viral transduction,1 and bacterial infectivity assay,17 turkey oviductal organoid cultures could be used to investigate physiological processes related to progesterone pathways and AIV infectivity.

Interestingly, the organoids developed in this study did not present ciliated cells. Multiciliated cell differentiation may be stimulated by Notch inhibition through the use of selective γ-secretase inhibitor (Y0-01027) as suggested in previous studies.1,11,12 Before this study, oviductal organoids had only been developed from mice,12 humans,11,18 cattle,19 and equids.14 With further investigation and validation, turkey oviductal organoids represent a valuable tool to study turkey reproductive physiology in vitro and provide a complementary model to research in avian reproductive health and diseases.

Limitations

This protocol developed turkey oviductal organoids using crude epithelial cell isolation, which may inevitably result in stromal cell contamination. If contamination occurs, it is essential to monitor stromal cell growth and passage the cultures before they become overgrown. Note however that the stromal cells will be gradually washed away after a few passages. To minimize contamination, ensure all connective tissues are removed during dissection. If pure epithelial organoid cultures are required, fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) or differential attachment could be conducted prior to epithelial cell seeding to remove the stromal compartment. However, this process may impact the number and viability of cells.

While the INF and MAG organoids in this study demonstrated robust survival through passages and hold promise for long-term experiment use, organoids from other regions exhibited inferior growth and could only be maintained for a limited number of passages. Consequently, turkey oviductal organoids isolated from the isthmus, uterus, uterovaginal junction, and vagina using this protocol are suitable for short-term experiments. Further investigation for other specific organoid supplements required for development of organoids of these regions should be considered to establish effective cultures for long-term studies.

Problem 1

Organoids consistently adhere to pipet tips during passaging (step 5), cryopreservation (step 6), thawing (step 7), and organoid fixation (step 8) protocols. This organoid model exhibits sticky characteristics, leading to adherence to the plastic supplies.

Potential solution

•Pre-wetting the tips with PBS, basal media, or other appropriate media before use can help reduce the adherence.

Pre-wetting the tips with PBS, basal media, or other appropriate media before use can help reduce the adherence.

•If this measure proves insufficient, prewetting with ice-cold sterile FBS or 0.5%–1% BSA in DMEM/F12 solution20 may provide additional assistance.

Problem 2

Organoids fail to grow, or only a few small organoids appear after seeding, refer to step 4.

If cell viability exceeds 70%, the absence of organoid formation post-seeding is typically due to incorrect cell seeding density or problems with the media.

Potential solution

•Verify the correct seeding density by revising the method for cell counting and calculating cell dilutions.

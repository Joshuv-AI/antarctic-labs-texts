Novel locally nebulized indocyanine green for simultaneous identification of tumor margin and intersegmental plane
Authors: Ok Hwa Jeon, Kyungsu Kim, Chang Geun Kim, Byeong Hyeon Choi, Jun Hee Lee, Beop-Min Kim, Hyun Koo Kim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Segmentectomy, recommended for early-stage lung cancer or compromised lung function, demands precise tumor detection and intersegmental plane identification. While indocyanine green (ICG) commonly aids in these aspects using near-infrared imaging, its separate administrations through different routes and times can lead to complications and patient anxiety. This study aims to develop a lung-specific delivery method by nebulizing low-dose ICG to targeted lung segments, allowing simultaneous detection of lung tumors and intersegmental planes across diverse animal models.Methods:To optimizing the dose of ICG for lung tumor and interlobar fissure detection, different doses of ICG (0.25, 0.1, and 0.05 mg/kg) were nebulized to rabbit lung tumor models. The distribution of locally nebulized ICG in targeted segments was studied to evaluate the feasibility of detecting lung tumor and intersegmental planes in canine lung pseudotumor models.Results:Near-infrared fluorescence imaging demonstrated clear visualization of lung tumor margin and interlobar fissure using local nebulization of 0.1 mg/kg ICG for only 4 min during surgery in the rabbit models. In the canine model, the local nebulization of 0.05 mg/kg of ICG into the target segment enabled clear visualization of pseudotumor and intersegmental planes for 30 min.Conclusions:This innovative approach achieves a reduction in ICG dose and prolonged the visualization time of the intersegmental plane and effectively eliminates the need for the hurried marking of tumors and intersegmental planes. The authors anticipate that lung-specific delivery of ICG will prove valuable for image-guided limited resection of lung tumors in clinical practice.Keywords: intersegmental plane, locally nebulized, simultaneous identification, tumor margin

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide1,2. The widespread use of thoracic computed tomography (CT) has facilitated the detection of an increasing number of small pulmonary nodules preoperatively3,4. For early-stage nonsmall cell lung cancer patients, especially with compromised cardiopulmonary function, limited resection, such as segmentectomy and wedge resection, can better preserve pulmonary function better than extensive resection5,6. Among limited resection, segmentectomy is more effective than wedge resection because it provides safer surgical margins and lower local recurrence rates7. In segmentectomy, tumor margins and intersegmental planes need to be accurately identified. However, identifying lung tumors and the anatomical boundaries of intersegmental planes simultaneously during surgery, especially in robotic or video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery, can be challenging.

For identification lung tumor, invasive preoperative localization of pulmonary nodule such as hook-wire8,9, microcoil, lipiodol, indocyanine green (ICG)10,11, and methylene blue12 localization at the tumor site are widely used13–15. Our previous research also conducted a study using injection of ICG and lipiodol emulsions in preoperative CT for the detection of lung tumors16. However, these techniques only offer an image of the material and not the tumor itself, which presents a challenge in accurately identifying the resection margins17. In addition, these techniques requires material injection to the patients in the CT room before surgery, which not only burdens patients and medical staff, but also risks complications such as pneumothorax, hemorrhage, and pain18. In order to accurately detect the lung tumor margin during surgery, some researchers have adopted the method of intravenous injection of ICG, which was also conducted in our previous study19–23. However, ICG, which has a low quantum yield and is not a cancer-specific drug, results in a low TNR and cannot differentiate between cancer and inflammation24. Furthermore, to accurately detect lung tumors, 2 mg/kg or more of ICG is required, which may cause adverse side effects such as anaphylactic shock and hypotension25,26. To address these issues, in a previous study, we assessed and confirmed for the first time the efficacy of lung-specific inhalation delivery of low-dose ICG (0.25 mg/kg) for intraoperative visualization of lung tumor margins in animal models and patient tissues27. Subsequently, it was reported that ICG inhalation in lung cancer patients achieved intraoperative detection of lung cancer using near-infrared (NIR) fluorescence imaging, and even ground-glass nodules of lung could be detected28–30. Thus, lung-specific delivery of ICG proved to be accurate and effective in the detection of lung tumor even in ground-glass nodules.

To identify the intersegmental plane precisely, the traditional method is to determine the inflation/deflation of the target segment by clamping and unclamping the relevant bronchus. But, the inflated lung may obstruct the view of the target segment, especially in minimally invasive surgeries31. Many studies have reported the feasibility of using intravenous injection of ICG to detect the intersegmental plane32,33. However, in addition to the problem of systemic distribution of injected ICG, the short blood half-life of ICG requires additional electrocautery to label target fragments before it can be washed out32. Recently, transbronchial instillation of ICG for intersegmental plane identification have been carried out34–38. The method is lung-specific local instillation, which is considered to be simple and effective, and can reduce the instillation of ICG39. However, this local delivery method of instilling ICG into targeted lung segments hinders the detection of lung tumor margins.

In a prior study, we combined the use of ICG for preoperative localization of pulmonary nodules and intravenous injection to detect intersegmental planes, which effectively enabled the visualization of both lung tumors and intersegmental planes during segmentectomy40. Nevertheless, the simultaneous identification of these elements during surgery requires the administration of fluorescents via different routes and at different times, potentially leading to complications and increased patient anxiety. Therefore, it is necessary to establish a method that can simultaneously identify lung cancer and intersegmental planes more effectively and conveniently. In this study, we aimed to develop and evaluate the feasibility of a lung-specific local delivery method using nebulized low-dose ICG to target lung segments to simultaneously detect lung tumor margins and intersegmental planes in various animal models.

Materials and methods

Study design

To develop a lung-specific delivery method utilizing nebulization of low-dose ICG to targeted lung segments for simultaneous detection of lung tumor and intersegmental planes in various animal models. In order to optimizing the dose of ICG for lung tumor and interlobar fissure detection, different doses of ICG (0.25, 0.1, 0.05 mg/kg) were nebulized to rabbit lung tumor models (n=12). The distribution of locally nebulized ICG in targeted segments was studied to evaluate the feasibility of detecting lung tumor and intersegmental planes in canine lung pseudotumor models (nine lobes). Finally, to extend the lung cancer detection method utilizing ICG nebulization to diverse lung tissue conditions, we established a mouse model of lung cancer with inflammation or idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Subsequently, we evaluated whether ICG nebulization could effectively distinguish lung cancer models, including those with inflammation, and IPF (n=12).

Animals and animal care

All surgical procedures, including animal care and handling, were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of University (Korea-2021-0227). Twelve female New Zealand white rabbits (2.5–3 kg) were obtained from DooYeol Biotech (Seoul, Korea), three female miscellaneous dogs (25–30 kg) were obtained from Hana Biotech (Seoul, Korea). To assist the animals in adapting to their environment, rabbits and dogs were housed in individual cages with freely available food and water supplies for 1–2 weeks, in accordance with our humane animal care protocols. In this study, we included animals in which tumors were successfully established and excluded animals in which tumors were not established. The work has been reported in accordance with the Animals in Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) Guidelines (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/C573)41,42.

Dose optimization of lobal nebulized ICG for lung tumor and interlobar fissure identification

Twelve VX2 rabbit lung tumor models were divided into three groups according to the (Daiichi-Sankyo, Seoul, Korea) nebulization dose (0.05, 0.1, and 0.25 mg/kg; n=4 per group). ICG for inhalation was prepared at different concentrations (0.125, 0.25, and 0.75 mg/ml). The rabbits were anesthetized tiletamine-zolazepam (Zoletil 50; Virbac Korea Inc.) and 5 mg/kg xylazine (RompunTM; Bayer Korea Inc.) and were intubated via tracheostomy. A mechanical ventilator was connected to the tracheostomy site, and anesthesia was maintained with 2% isoflurane (Baxter Healthcare). Vital signs were monitored throughout the surgery. The rabbits were placed in a lateral decubitus position, and unilateral chest wall resection was performed to visualize the entire lung. An endotracheal tube (2.5 mm) was deeply inserted into the target lobar bronchus, and the nebulizer (Omron Healthcare) was positioned in the inspiratory limb of the ventilatory circuit. The rabbits were nebulized for 4 min (0.25 ml/min) with different doses of ICG. The distribution of ICG in the lungs was visualized in real-time using intraoperative color and fluorescence imaging system (ICFIS), and the target lobe where the tumor is located was surgically removed.

Local nebulized ICG to identify canine lung pseudotumor and intersegmental planes

Three miscellaneous female dogs (25–30 kg) were anesthetized by intramuscular injection of 10 mg/kg tiletamine/zolazepam and xylazine. Once under general anesthesia, the dogs were intubated and mechanically ventilated. This involved delivering a mix of 50% oxygen at a rate of 18 breaths per minute, with each breath having a tidal volume calculated at 10 ml/kg of the dog’s body weight. To ensure a steady and appropriate depth of anesthesia, a continuous flow of isoflurane, in the concentration range of 2–3%, was utilized. The dogs were placed in a lateral decubitus position, and unilateral chest wall resection was performed to obtain an image of the entire lung. Lung pseudotumor were created by direct injection of 0.3 ml of liquid agar mixed with lipiodol into the parenchyma of different lung lobes43. A bronchoscope (4 mm, Vathin) was inserted into the target segment, and a catheter (21G, 1.5 mm) was carefully inserted into the working channel of the bronchoscope. After ligating the pulmonary artery and vein of each segment, the end of the segmental bronchus, where the bronchoscope was located, was also ligated to prevent the atomized ICG distributing to other lung segments. Dogs were nebulized for 4 min (0.25 ml/min) with 0.1, 0.05 and 0.025 mg/kg of ICG. The distribution of ICG in the lung was visualized in real-time using a SPY Portable Handheld Imaging System (Stryker), and the lung tumor located in the target segment was removed.

The tumor margin detection efficiency (TMDE) is calculated by dividing the signal to noise ratio of the healthy tissue by that of the tumor tissue44. Two of surgeons reviewed images depicting different levels of tumor margin detection efficiency.

ICG nebulization to identify tumor in different types of mouse tumor models

Six-week-old female C57BL/6 mice (Orient Bio Laboratory) weighing 20–25 g were selected and used for tumor implantation. The lung tumor mouse model was established after 15 days of luciferase-expressing Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) (100 μl of 2×105 cells/ml) intravenous injection. Next day the mouse were treated intratracheally PBS, 1 mg/kg of Lipopolysaccharides (LPS), and 2 mg/kg of Bleomycin (TCI America). In the lung tumor mouse model that received inhalation of ICG at a dose of 0.25 mg/kg, the distribution of ICG was detected using the NIR imaging system. The NIR fluorescence signal was quantified by dividing the fluorescence SNR using the Image Studio software. All lungs of mice were excised, fixed in 10% formalin and embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded tissue samples were sectioned into 5 μm slice, and stained with Haematoxylin&Eosin (H&E) as well as Masson’s trichrome to investigate the degree of lung tissue inflammation and fibrosis, respectively.

Statistical analyses

We used means and SDs for normally distributed data and medians and ranges or interquartile ranges (IQRs) for data that are not normally distributed. One-way ANOVA was used to evaluate the differences in TMDE and targeted lobe or segment detection efficiency among different nebulized doses of ICG in rabbits (0.25, 0.1, and 0.05 mg/kg) and dogs (0.1, 0.05, and 0.025 mg/kg). One-way ANOVA was used to evaluate the differences in TMDE of tumors, tumors with inflammation, and tumors with IPF. Bar graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 10.1 (GraphPad Software).

Results

Dose optimization of lobal nebulized ICG for lung tumor margin and interlobar fissure identification

We attempted to determine the minimum dose of locally nebulized ICG for the accurate detection of tumor margins and interlobar fissure in a rabbit model. Various doses of ICG (0.25, 0.1, and 0.05 mg/kg) were locally nebulized to one lobe of the rabbit’s lungs, and the NIR fluorescence image of the lung was evaluated using ICFIS. NIR fluorescence imaging, coupled with signal analysis, demonstrated that tumor and interlobar fissures were distinctly visible when using ICG at doses of 0.1 mg/kg or higher (Fig. 1A-C). Therefore, at least 0.1 mg/kg of ICG should be locally nebulized for the detection of surgical safety margin precisely. The local nebulization of ICG for lung tumor margin detection reduced the inhaled dose by ≥2.5-fold compared to the dose required for whole-lung inhalation.

A) Colored, near-infrared images and merged of a lung tumor and interlobar fissure following nebulized indocyanine green (ICG) at different dosages (0.25, 0.1, and 0.05 mg/kg). The white dotted line indicates lung tumor margin and the yellow dotted line indicates interlobar fissure. B) Quantification of the tumor margin detection efficiency using lobal nebulization of ICG at different dosages. ***P<0.001 and ****P<0.0001, as determined by one-way ANOVA (n=4, mean±SD). C) Quantification of the targeted lobe detection efficiency using lobal nebulization of ICG at different dosages. ***P<0.001 and ****P<0.0001, as determined by one-way ANOVA (n=4, mean±SD). D) Microscopic images of the lung tumor margin in bright-field, near-infrared and merged in rabbits following lobal nebulization of ICG at 0.1 mg/kg.

Segmental nebulized ICG can identify canine lung pseudotumor and intersegmental planes

The clinical applicability of segmental nebulized low-dose ICG (0.1, 0.05, and 0.025 mg/kg) for the simultaneous detection of tumor margins and intersegmental planes was examined in a canine lung pseudotumor model. Simultaneously, the feasibility of segmental nebulized ICG was assessed for the identification of intersegmental planes. As shown in Figure 2, bronchoscopy was used to locally nebulize ICG to target lung segments, and NIR fluorescent image were observed to detect tumor margins and intersegmental planes (Supplementary Video 1, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JS9/C574).

Skim of novel locally nebulized indocyanine green for simultaneous identification of tumor margin and intersegmental plane.

Figure 3A shows that segmental nebulized with 0.1 and 0.05 mg/kg of ICG, lung pseudotumor and intersegmental planes could be precisely identified in canine lungs, and both TMDE (0.1 mg/kg, 2.8±0.2; 0.05 mg/kg, 2.3±0.1) and targeted segment detection efficiency (0.1 mg/kg, 3.4±0.3; 0.05 mg/kg, 2.6±0.1) were higher than 2.0. Therefore, local nebulized low-dose ICG to the target segment resulted in the localization of the ICG fluorescence signal mainly in the noncancerous tissue of the lung segment, which not only facilitated the identification of tumor margins but also intersegmental planes using NIR imaging. No fluorescent signal was detected in the rest of the lung tissue, except the target lung segment. Moreover, locally nebulized low-dose ICG remained in the lung tissue for a longer period, allowing intersegmental plane detection for up to 30 min.

Identification of lung tumors in lung cancer mouse models with inflammation or IPF using nebulized ICG.

To extend the lung cancer detection method of ICG nebulization to various lung tissue conditions, such as inflammation and fibroblasts, we evaluated whether ICG nebulization could effectively distinguish lung cancer in a lung cancer mouse model with inflammation or IPF. As depicted in Figure 4, nebulized ICG exhibited uniform distribution in healthy, inflammatory, and fibrotic tissues, highlighting a distinct contrast between lung cancer and other tissues. The TMDE of tumors (4.1±0.7), tumors with inflammation (4.7±0.6), and tumors with IPF (4.6±0.4) did not show any significant difference.

Discussion

Visualization of cancer by NIR fluorescence imaging is convenient for surgeons and improves surgical outcomes in patients by minimizing normal tissue resection. Therefore, many researchers have demonstrated the feasibility of ICG in the localization of pulmonary nodules45–49 and the identification of intersegmental planes2,33,50–54. However, this method requires CT guided direct injection of ICG to pulmonary nodule for tumor detection, and electronic labeling is necessary for intersegmental plane detection owing to the short blood half-life of ICG. Moreover, different methods and times are required to inject ICG.

In this study, we developed a lung-specific local delivery method, locally nebulized low-dose ICG, to simultaneous detect lung tumor margins and intersegmental planes intraoperatively for the first time. There are three key features of the lung-specific delivery method. First, it allows for a substantial reduction in the nebulized dose of ICG by more than five times compared to the dose for the whole lung nebulization. Second, ICG nebulization maintains its fluorescent signal of ICG in lung tissue for at least 30 min, resolving the problem posed by the short blood half-life of ICG in intersegmental plane detection. Lastly, delivering ICG to the target lung segment requiring resection prevented potential ICG damage to the normal lung tissue that could occur in whole-lung nebulization. Therefore, the locally nebulized low-dose ICG method can change the paradigm of image-guided lung cancer surgery using ICG.

In a previous study, we evaluated the possibility of effectively detecting lung tumor margins using inhaled ICG and demonstrated that lung-specific drug delivery in image-guided lung cancer surgery reduces the dose of ICG while improving tumor margin detection efficiency27. The basic metabolism and toxicity of intravenous ICG have been confirmed by many researchers25,26,55; however, its effect on lung tissue after inhalation remains unexplored. Therefore, the potential toxicity of inhaled ICG to lung tissue may be mild but still unpredictable. Consequently, it is necessary to reduce the dose of inhaled ICG and its distribution in lung tissue to prevent potential tissue damage caused by ICG. To address this concern, we developed a method of local nebulization of ICG that is exclusively delivered to the lung tissue targeted for resection. As shown in Figure 1, we attempted to nebulize a low-dose of ICG to the lung lobes where the lung tumor was located which was successfully detected in fluorescence imaging. Thus, local nebulization reduced the dose of ICG necessary for tumor visualization from 0.25 mg/kg with whole lung nebulization to 0.1 mg/kg with local nebulization.

After demonstrating the beneficial effect of local ICG nebulization on tumor margin detection in a rabbit model with lung tumor, due to the lack of canine cancer models, we assessed its clinical applicability for segmentectomy in a canine lung pseudotumor model (Fig. 2). Interestingly, locally nebulized optimal low-dose ICG to the target segment resulted in the accumulation of the ICG fluorescence signal mainly in the noncancerous tissue of the target lung segment, which facilitated the identification of tumor margins and intersegmental planes. Additionally, the locally nebulized ICG remained in the lung tissue for up to 30 min, allowing the detection of intersegmental lines for a longer period compared to those of intravenous injections. These results are consistent with our previous findings that nebulized ICG persists in the lung tissue and maintains its fluorescence signal for at least 1 h. While intravenously injected ICG has been applied by many researchers to identify the segmental plane of the lung33,50,51,53,54, its blood half-life is 4 min approximately. We also reported a method of identifying the segmental plane using ICG, but, due to the short blood half-life of ICG, additional electrocautery is required to mark the target segment before ICG is washed out40,56. For these reasons, the method of locally nebulized ICG is of great benefit to surgeons and patients as it provides sufficient time to identify the lung intersegment plane.

For segmentectomy, ICG was administered preoperatively percutaneously for the localization of pulmonary nodules in lung tumor detection, and intraoperatively via intravenous injection for the delineation of segmental anatomy, potentially leading to complications and increased patient anxiety. The method of locally nebulized ICG eliminates the complicated process of patients or medical staff rushing to mark tumors and intersegmental planes. Since local ICG nebulization was performed after surgical anesthesia, it can reduce the patient’s anxiety and burden. Although this technique involves the technical challenge of using a bronchoscope and accurately positioning the fiber into the targeted segment during the surgery, target segment can be easily found through 3D CT reconstruction57.

For clinical practice with ICG nebulization, due to the lung-specific local instillation method developed for intersegmental plane identification in lung cancer patients34–38, it can be readily applied if nebulization is used instead of the local instillation technique. In this manner, not only can the intersegmental plane be detected, but lung cancer can also be detected simultaneously. In addition, it is not expected to prolong surgery time considering that the nebulization only takes 4 min.

Lastly, considered to lung cancer patients accompanied by various lung condition, such as inflammation and fibrosis, we evaluated lung cancer detection efficiency of ICG nebulization in lung cancer mouse model with inflammation or IPF. From our results, we found that nebulized ICG uniformly distributed within healthy, inflammatory, and fibrotic tissues, resulting in a clear negative image of lung tumors (Fig. 4). This suggests that our study’s findings could offer promising strategies for intraoperative detection lung tumors using ICG nebulization in various lung cancer patients, even in cases of inflammation or fibrosis.

Despite local nebulization of ICG guided surgery is a feasible and effective intraoperative technique, it has several limitations. First, our research was limited to tumors with a depth of up to 1 cm from the pleural surface, considering that several researchers have reported that the NIR fluorescent image-guided surgery has depth limitation (5–10 mm). Secondly, nebulized ICG cannot be distributed to collapsed or closed lungs, such as atelectasis caused by various lung diseases such as inflammatory nodules, tuberculosis, asthma, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Lastly, evaluating the toxicity associated with low-dose ICG inhalation for lung-specific delivery is crucial before seeking Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for clinical applications. However, since the ICG distributed through nebulization in the target segment is removed through segmentectomy, the effect on the lungs is expected to be minimal, so entry into phase 1 clinical trials is expected to be easy. We are presently conducting toxicity assessments related to ICG inhalation and plan to pursue FDA approval in the future.

Conclusions

We developed a lung-specific local delivery method, consisting of locally nebulized optimal low-dose ICG to the target lung segment, to simultaneously detect lung tumor margins and intersegmental planes. This innovative approach achieves a reduction in the ICG dose to just 0.05 mg/kg and requires nebulization for only 4 min during the operation. Lung segmentectomy has emerged as a current trend, we believe that the local nebulized ICG method can change the paradigm of image-guided lung cancer surgery.

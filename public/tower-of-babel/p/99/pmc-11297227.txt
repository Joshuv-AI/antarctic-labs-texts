Bio-inspired machine-learning aided geo-magnetic field based AUV navigation system
Authors: Ananda Ramadass Gidugu, Bala Naga Jyothi Vandavasi, Vedachalam Narayanaswamy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The navigational accuracy of sea animals and trans-ocean birds provides inspiration in using geo-magnetic field (GMF) for realizing strategic truly autonomous underwater vehicles (AUV) capable of determining their absolute position on earth, without the aid of ship-referenced acoustic baseline systems. Supervised Machine Learning algorithms are applied on the GMF intensity data obtained from NOAA World Magnetic Model for a 900 km2 within the Indian mineral exploratory area in the Central Indian Ocean, with a resolution of 50 m, considering the sensitivity of commercially available magnetometers. It is identified that, for AUVs equipped with magnetometers with a detection sensitivity of 0.1 nT, the supervisory random forest regression and decision tree algorithm trained with priori GMF data, could provide trajectory guidance to AUVs with an absolute mean position accuracy in 2D plane, with reference to the last known position from Integrated Navigation system aided initially with GPS and with acoustic positioning in underwater. Circular Error Probable (CEP 50) of 53 m and 56 m, respectively. The scalar GMF anomaly navigation demonstrated to be a viable GPS-alternative navigation system could be extended to larger areas with inclination and declination vectors, as unique identifiers.

Introduction

Oceans covering 72% of the Earth’s surface houses immense living and non-living resources, and plays a key role in regulating the planet’s climate. Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUV) is one of the most efficient technological tools vital for effective exploration of the vast blue economic resources, spatiotemporal monitoring of the oceans for understanding the changing climate patterns, marine pollution monitoring, defense and early identification of the assets lost in the oceans. The technological advancements in underwater navigation sensors, processing electronics, underwater navigation algorithms, high-energy density battery storage, low-light cameras, high density buoyancy packs and lighter high-strength pressure casings supported by modern numerical modeling tools have enabled development of AUV capable of carrying out exploration in remote, hostile and challenging environments, from the world’s deepest hydrothermal vents to beneath the Polar ice shelves. The phenomenal development in the Integrated Navigation System technologies has resulted in positioning accuracy of better than 0.01% of distance travelled, and a heading accuracy of 0.01°sec Lat1.

True Autonomous Navigation (TAN), without the aid of ship-based acoustic positioning systems, map matching, Integrated Navigation system, Source Localisation and Mapping (SLAM) is one of the key requirements for strategic AUVs (Fig. 1) involved in challenging long endurance missions2. To achieve the objective of TAN, based on bio-inspiration, we developed a supervised Machine Learning (ML)-based magnetic navigation model for 30 km × 30 km, in which the model learns from the priori 3-axis Geo-Magnetic Field (GMF) data in x, y and z directions. The learnt-algorithm shall provide true navigation guidance to the AUV in following the defined trajectory within the defined geographic boundary.Figure 1Requirements of strategic AUV.

Bio-inspiration

Migratory movements of trans-ocean birds and animals tend to be extremely accurate towards geographic locations such as oceanic islands or offshore reproductive areas. For many species, migratory routes are not learned, but are innate, which requires them to possess internal orientation mechanisms enabling autonomous navigation to specific targets. They use magnetic, visual, olfactory and inertial clues to trace their way back home, when displaced several kilometres3. Several birds and ocean animals, including magneto-tactic bacteria to nematodes, crustaceans (spiny lobster), fishes (salmon), birds (Pigeon, Albatrosses, Manx Shearwater, Herring gulls and Arctic tern), mammals (whales, mole-rat) and reptiles (sea turtle) (Fig. 2), based on magneto-reception (MR), have evolved to acquire the ability to derive useful directional (compass) and/or positional information (map-sense) from the GMF.Figure 2Navigation aids for birds and sea animals.

Navigation aids for birds and sea animals.

Homing pigeons possess large-scale spatial cognition capabilities that help them establish idiosyncratic routes back to their home from distances > 1000 km based on MR and olfactory clues that they recapitulate when flying solo4. The regional homing of juvenile turtles raises the interesting possibility that such animals may diminish the effects of secular variation on natal homing accuracy by updating their knowledge of the GMF in their natal region long before their first reproductive migration. Salmons could imprint on both the inclination and intensity of the GMF, and use both elements as redundant markers of the natal area during their return. The fidelity of navigation of multi-decadal humpback whale migrating route despite oceanographic and GMF changes can be understood from the historic whaling records and 15 years of satellite-derived data. The Cetaceans, the animal family which whale belongs have bio-magnetite in the retinas of their eyes, help GMF support navigation during the migrations5. Laysan Albatrosses, Manx Shearwater and Herring gulls are believed to use GMF aided by olfactory clues when they cover enormous distances (> 15,000 km in a single trip over wide featureless ocean areas) for foraging6. Using data from 2 million captures of 152 land-bird species in North America (including European Robin, Sea Eagle) over 60 years, a strong association between disruption to the GMF and avian vagrancy during fall migration is proven7. Furthermore, published reports provide macro-ecological evidence in support of the widespread use and importance of MR in migratory birds, and the common reliance of birds on favorable solar and magnetic conditions to migrate accurately.

Challenges in using GMF anomaly for marine navigation

The entire Earth is polarized with magnetic field converging near the poles, a phenomenon driven by the motion of the Earth’s liquid core. The GMF systematically varies across the surface of the earth in polarity (direction), inclination (angle between the magnetic field lines and the earth surface) and the intensity (from ~ 20,000 nT at low latitudes to ~ 60,000 nT at high latitudes) (Fig. 3a,b), offering spatial and directional reference frame for orientation and navigation. Typically, each point on the near-earth space has a unique magnetic field vector with respect to the corresponding coordinate, while the GMF-based navigation is employed to provide a reliable navigation reference.Figure 3(a) Magnetic Field Distribution8 (b) Geo-magnetic field elements9.

(a) Magnetic Field Distribution8 (b) Geo-magnetic field elements9.

Even though, GMF is a reliable source of navigational information, especially, in the open-ocean, there are multiple challenges in practically using the information directly for reliable navigation. Even on a global map, however, the isolines of the two variables (intensity and inclination) do not at all run in parallel and west–east everywhere, but in some region they deviate from each other by up to 90° so that, in theory, they could be used there as two coordinates (Fig. 4). In most areas, however, they can hardly be used that way. On smaller regional and local scales, the geomagnetic topography is often so irregular and unpredictable that extrapolations from one position to another without local exploration are impossible. Further, fine-scale or local patterns of GMF contours are more complex than the general regional patterns because concentrations of magnetic minerals in the Earth’s crust often generate local field anomalies. Although these variations are typically less than 1% of the total field, their gradients (i.e. variation per distance) can be significantly greater than the gradients due to the main dipole field, and variation in directions.Figure 4Contour plot for field intensity across the global map.

Contour plot for field intensity across the global map.

Recently, Qiong Wang et al. reported a geomagnetic/INS based on integrated triangle matching navigation algorithm suitable for aircraft navigation in complicated geomagnetic environments10. Chinmaya Kaji et al. reported feed-forward neural network generated using the International Geomagnetic Reference Field (IGRF) model to estimate the geographical location of the vehicle by mapping magnetometer sensor readings (field intensity, inclination, and declination) to a unique location on the Earth’s surface11. In the space sector, Zhao Minhua et al. proposed a method to combine the radar altimeter and the geomagnetic navigation system to locate the satellite12. Raquet et al. proposed the use of GMF navigation models for satellite positioning and navigation, which uses the intensity value to determine the altitude and satellite orbit13. The key benefits of magnetic vector navigation over scalar magnetic navigation in achieving modestly improved navigation accuracy and improved platform attitude is described by Caciani et al.14. Hitherto, based on the characteristics of the GMF, various methods have been developed for GMF-based navigation. Electro Magnetic tracking applications are also used as surgical guidance in the medical field, by generating a defined EM field in which the EM micro- sensors are accurately and precisely tracked in hard-to-reach access areas15. The EM based guidance technique is proven in terrestrial and subsea, demonstrated in Ocean16,17, and the same is adopted with modern ML techniques for long range navigation application in dynamic ocean environment18. Radar systems have been increasingly used to monitor birds. To take full advantage of the large datasets provided by radars, researchers have implemented machine learning (ML) techniques that automatically read and attempt to classify targets19,20.

The principal idea uses priori GMF map with matching algorithms such as Mean Square Difference, Mean Absolute Difference, Iterative Closest Contour Point Algorithm, etc.21. However, the complexity of local magnetic contours suggests that any navigational strategies that exploit magnetic topography over these smaller spatial scales are likely to be site-specific, difficult to generalize, and inspired us in opening new avenue in AUV TAN with machine-learning capabilities. The data is trained and validated with widely used ML techniques which are detailed in the next chapter.

Materials (Data sets)

The total magnetic field (Mtotal) measured at any point in space over the Earth’s surface (including the oceans) is made up of multiple sources. Some sources are static, while others change over time. Two primary magnetic sources caused by the Earth are the Earth’s core magnetic field (Mcore) and the Earth’s crustal magnetic field (Mcrustal). Magnetic anomaly navigation uses a specific static component of the overall Earth magnetic field to navigate—the Earth’s crustal magnetic field. The crustal field is captured with “magnetic anomaly maps”. At each point over the Earth, the GMFis described by a vector M(Mx My Mz). The Mx, My, Mz are the components of the vector point in the North, East, Down directions of a local-level plane, respectively. The long-wavelength, slowly time-varying core field of the Earth is represented by global, satellite-based models such as the World Magnetic Model (WMM) and the International Geomagnetic Reference Field (IGRF). They provide excellent support for alternate navigation. We obtained the GMF data (including 3-axis magnetic fields) from NOAA WMM (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model) for a 30 km × 30 km location in the Central Indian Ocean Basin (CIOB) located at 13.2414°S, 75.57468°E with a resolution of 50 m. The location lies within the 75,000 km2 area allocated to India by International Seabed Authority for carrying our mineral exploration and demonstrative mineral mining. Dataset includes 0.36 million data points of respective 3-axis GMF, inclination of − 9.03°, and declination of − 44.7° at the given location (Latitude and Longitude). All the data are queried from the NOAA website which has data base of WMM in Python language with science kit libraries (Fig. 6). The data training system processor is a 128 GB RAM of high-end workstation with 16 cores Xeon processor. The training and validation of the 0.36 million points is intensive and took a turn-around time of ~ 1 h.Figure 6Location for which the datasets are collected.

ML training, validation and prediction

We have used supervised ML-based regression techniques including Linear Regression, KNN (k-Nearest Neighbours), Support Vector (SVR), Decision Tree, Random Forest that are used for indoor positioning and localization applications22,23. Out of the 0.36 million data sets, 70% is used for training and 30% datasets for validation of the 5 identified ML techniques. Performance evaluation metrics such as R2 (R2 (coefficient of determination) regression score function), MAE (Mean absolute error represents the average of the absolute difference between the actual and predicted values, that is the average of the residuals in the dataset, MSE (Mean Squared Error represents the average of the squared difference between the original and predicted values in the data set, measures the variance of the residuals. These evaluation indicators were obtained to choose the best ML technique and the best suitable ML model is chosen for prediction of the absolute 2D position in Geo-coordinates (Latitude and Longitude) with the given or measured 3-axis GMF.

Navigation performance analysis

For each predicted way point, optimum value with a circular error probability of 50% (CEP50) is obtained by performing Monte Carlo simulation for 100 iterations. Conclusion

Geomagnetic field-based navigation is a promising true alternate navigation system for marine applications, which overcomes the need for ship-based acoustic positioning systems and Inertial Navigation Systems, which drifts over time. Our simulations based on supervisory machine-learnt random forest regression and decision tree algorithm based on priori GMF intensity data and AUV-mounted 3-axis scalar magnetometer with detection sensitivities of 0.1 nT and 0.5 nT indicate that the possibility of achieving absolute position accuracies of 56 m and 526 m, 53 m and 534 m respectively, within the 900 km2 boundary. Even though the result shows confidence in extending the scalar GMF to wider geographic boundaries, with inclination and declination parameters as unique identifiers, the precision of the priori GMF anomaly map and use of high precision 3-axis flux gate magnetometers on-board AUV are challenging. By overcoming these challenges, GMF-based navigation will allow passive, world-wide, all-weather, all-time, and true navigation capability for surface vessels and autonomous underwater vehicles.

Enhancing full-thickness skin graft take in nipple reconstruction using PICO™ negative pressure therapy: A technical note
Authors: Marco Palmesano, Gabriele Storti, Irene Nunziata, Riccardo Foti, Carlo Cossi, Valerio Cervelli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNipple-areola complex (NAC) necrosis remains a challenging complication following nipple-sparing mastectomy, with significant emotional and aesthetic consequences for patients. Full-thickness skin grafting (FTSG) offers a viable reconstructive option in secondary procedures. We report our experience using PICO™ single-use negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT), applied directly over the breast, to enhance graft take in three consecutive cases of nipple reconstruction using groin-derived FTSG. The technique was effective, cost-conscious, and well-tolerated by patients during the recovery period.Keywords: Breast reconstruction, Nipple reconstruction, Skin Graft

Main text

Post-mastectomy NAC necrosis, especially after nipple-sparing procedures, is a distressing event for patients, compromising not only aesthetic outcomes but also emotional well-being and body image. Secondary reconstruction may involve tattooing, local flaps, or full-thickness skin grafts (FTSG).1 Among these, groin-derived FTSGs are particularly suited for nipple reconstruction due to favorable pigmentation and skin quality, though graft integration may be impaired by scarred or irradiated recipient beds.

We implemented a reconstructive approach combining groin-derived FTSGs and PICO™ NPWT in three female patients who had developed complete NAC necrosis following prosthetic breast reconstruction. The FTSG was sculpted into a dome-shaped neonipple and inset centrally on the breast. PICO™ NPWT dressings were applied directly over the graft at −80 mmHg for 7 days (Figure 1, Figure 2). Across all three cases, we observed: complete graft take, excellent preservation of shape and pigmentation, absence of hematoma, infection, or dehiscence, minimal disruption to daily life, allowing outpatient management and normal mobility.2,3 Compared to conventional NPWT systems, PICO™ proved to be more cost-effective,4 reduced the need for dressing changes, and offered a psychologically lighter burden during the early postoperative phase, avoiding bulky equipment or negative visual stimuli.

Discussion

The breast, due to its curvature and prior surgical manipulation, is a difficult site for graft stabilization. Traditional bolster dressings often fail to ensure uniform pressure.5 PICO™, with its low-profile design, transparent dressing, and consistent negative pressure, proved especially suited for this indication. It enhanced graft adherence while minimizing shear and fluid accumulation. The psychological benefit of offering a fast, clean, and outpatient-friendly solution was also non-negligible in our experience.

Conclusion

Secondary NAC necrosis represents not only a reconstructive challenge but also an emotional setback for breast cancer patients. In our three-case experience, the use of PICO™ NPWT over groin-derived FTSGs on the breast mound yielded consistent clinical success, high patient satisfaction, and minimal psychosocial disruption. This technique may serve as a valuable adjunct in nipple reconstruction strategies, especially in previously compromised surgical fields. Further studies should explore its role in larger cohorts.

Super learner model for classifying leukemia through gene expression monitoring
Authors: Sharanya Selvaraj, Alhuseen Omar Alsayed, Nor Azman Ismail, Balasubramanian Prabhu Kavin, Edeh Michael Onyema, Gan Hong Seng, Arinze Queen Uchechi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractLeukemia is a form of cancer that affects the bone marrow and lymphatic system, and it requires complex treatment strategies that vary with each subtype. Due to the subtle morphological differences among these types, monitoring gene expressions is crucial for accurate classification. Manual or pathological testing can be time-consuming and expensive. Therefore, data-driven methods and machine learning algorithms offer an efficient alternative for leukemia classification. This study introduced a novel super learning model that leverages heterogeneous machine learning models to analyze gene expression data and classify leukemia cells. The proposed approach incorporates an entropy-based feature importance technique to identify the gene profiles most significant to the labeling process. The strength of this super learning model lies in its final super learner, Random Forest, which effectively classifies cross-validated data from the candidate learners. Validation on a gene expression monitoring dataset demonstrates that this model outperforms other state-of-the-art models in predictive accuracy. The study contributes to the knowledge regarding the use of advanced machine learning techniques to improve the accuracy and reliability of leukemia classification using gene expression data, addressing the challenges of traditional methods that rely on clinical features and morphological examination.Keywords: Gene expressions, DNA microarray, Super learner, Random forest, Machine learning, Leukemia

Introduction

Leukemia is a kind of cancer that primarily affects the bone marrow, the soft tissue found beneath bones that is used to make blood cells. It involves the aberrant generation of white blood cells, considered vital for the defense against pathogens. Numerous health problems may result from these aberrant cells crowding out the usual blood cells. The type of leukemia depends on the blood cell that is affected and its pace of growth. The occurrence of leukemia is characterised by the changes in the genetic material of the blood cells. The DNA is responsible for controlling the growth and death of a cell. In leukemia, the mutations alter the genetic material so that the blood cells continue to grow by rapid division. When this happens, blood cell production becomes out of control. Over time, these abnormal cells can crowd out healthy blood cells in the bone marrow, leading to fewer healthy white blood cells, red blood cells and platelets, causing the signs and symptoms of leukemia. This form of cancer is characterised by abnormal or uncontrolled growth of immature blood cells which leads to massive production of White Blood Cells (WBC) [1]. Leukemia alters the normal functioning of healthy blood cells. Bone marrow produces different types of blood cells and stem cells commonly known as hematopoietic stem cells. Sometimes, the hematopoietic stem cells differentiate themselves into abnormal WBCs, crossing the healthy blood cells and disrupt their functions, thus causing Leukemia. Some of the Genetic risk factors of Leukemia include Klinefelter and Down syndromes, ataxia telangiectasia, Bloom syndrome, Fanconi anemia, dyskeratosis congenita, and Shwachman-Diamond syndrome [2]. Due to its high mortality rate, leukemia has garnered significant interest from clinical researchers aiming to improve diagnosis and treatment for patients. Traditional methods for classifying different types of leukemia include physical examinations, blood tests, bone marrow biopsies, and imaging tests. Common procedures encompass blood smear tests, complete blood counts, differential counts, flow cytometry, X-rays, CT scans, and positron emission tomography (PET) scans. The disease can be categorised into four main types namely: Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL), Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) and Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) [3]. Accurate diagnosis is crucial in leukemia treatment because it would guide medical professionals to determine the appropriate therapy for the patient. The classification criteria for the four major types of Leukemia are based on the part it affects and nature of progression. The acute type of leukemia progresses more aggressively and rapidly than the chronic types. Hence treating the acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL) and acute Myeloid leukemia (AML) should be done vigorously, as any delay in diagnosing them may accelerate the growth. The treatment for ALL and AML types are different and hence distinguishing between them place a pivotal role for planning for the treatment [4]. Identifying ALL and AML types of leukemia demands highly skilled resources and the process can be time consuming, tedious and expensive. One of the major concerns in treating leukemia is that the cells may appear morphologically similar but will respond differently to the therapy and cytotoxic drugs. These factors necessitated the need to develop a more advanced diagnostic methods using cutting edge technologies which leverages gene expression data to rightly classify the type of Leukemia. A DNA microarray is an important step in cancer cell classification because it can sequence and monitor thousands of genes at the same time. This method facilitates the comparison of gene expressions between the normal cells and cancer affected cells, thus improving the decision making process. A DNA microarray is an effective detection tool which harnesses the specificity of DNA binding with the nucleic acids of the complementary DNA sequences. The RNA extracted from the sample genes is processed and a solution prepared for DNA microarray is applied to perform the hybridization. A complementary probe to target nucleic acids are placed on DNA microarray which forms a double stranded structure along with complementary target nucleic acids present in sample solution. As the double strands cannot be examined and confirmed macroscopically, fluorescent dye is incorporated and subjected to the detector to identify the target genes. Literature survey

The recent advancements in computing technologies have reported widespread utilization Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning and Deep Learning (DL) techniques for effective diagnosis and classification of many type of clinical diseases [5]. Also, the introduction of digital pathology has further accelerated the classification of Leukemia by leveraging ML techniques for more accurate and precise classification and diagnosis [6]. Automating the diagnostic procedures by data driven intelligent techniques has changed the therapeutic landscape with more accurate, customised, and efficient treatment procedures.

As a forerunner, Golub et al. conducted a maiden study on automating the classification of leukemia by analysing the gene expressions [7]. The study confirm the presence of correlation between the gene expression pattern and the type of leukemia. Hence this work served as a benchmark for all the other intelligent, automated leukemia classifications. Literature witnesses many significant works based on artificial neural networks (ANN) and other deep neural architectures for classifying leukemia based on the gene expression data. The neural network architecture text gene expression profiles as input and the final output layer gives the probability of the gene belonging to a particular type of leukemia [8]. Another notable advancement made in the gene expression profile matching is the introduction of convolutional neural networks (CNN) for processing multidimensional digitised pathological DNA data using several kinds of filters to extract predominant features from the gene expression. CNNs are more successful in classifying the type of leukemia because of their ability to observe local spatial relations from the input array [9]. Deep CNN architecture was deployed by Arulselvam et al. to extract features augmented with gamma correction and guided filtering techniques [10]. This method prove to be effective in extracting essential features without loss of generality. Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are employed for modelling the gene expression data with state vector to integrate the current input and the previous values through the feedback connections to label the blood cell [11]. The success of RNN in modelling the gene profiles can be attributed to the fact that, they are capable of learning the correlations and dependencies in the gene expression data.

Transformer neural networks (TNN) has gained more popularity because of its self-attention mechanisms. Amin et al. developed a novel vision transformer with self-attention modules to act on image patches and capture the global context information. This transformer model was able to classify the leukemia cells but the promising accuracy of around 81.5% [12]. Identification of blast cells through Resnet architecture was done by Ahmad Badruzzaman et al. [13]. Comparative analysis is performed using using Resnet, Efficient net and ResNetXt and the performance assessment is done at both class and macro level. The results indicate that Resnet50 was able to converge faster with promising classification accuracy. Graph based neural networks (GNNs) learn the graphical representations from the presenter gene expression data and establisher expression topology by imbibing the inherent correlation among the genes [14]. The GNNs exhibit much wider scope in gene profile analysis especially in the classification of leukemia. A novel Gated Graph Attention (GGAT) network which integrates the logic of getting mechanism along with attention modules to realise 1 hop neighbourhood reasoning. This work also proposed a hybrid feature selection algorithm that selects the features which can effectively reduce the training time of the model [15]. Transfer learning approaches are also employed in prediction of cancer.

Bihter Das et al. digitised the genes using numerical mapping techniques Which are then examined using 1D and 2D spectrogram image [16]. The CNN model acts on these data and the feature vectors where formed using VGG 16 architecture. Later Support Vector Machine (SVM) is used as a classifier to label the cancer cells. A Committee Neural Network is used as a classifier for labelling leukemia [17]. The effectiveness of the work was further enhanced by filtering out non informative genes. The committee used majority voting scheme to give the final prediction by learning the DNA microarray. Under the assumption that gene sequence do not degenerate and there is no limitation on the network parameters, Pradeep kumar et al. proposed a deep five layered DNN classifier to categorise leukemia [18]. A detailed comparative study of various DL algorithms in classifying leukemia was conducted by Omar Ahmed et al. [19]. Among the CNN, RNN, DNN and improved DNN with dropouts, the later was found to be accurate in classifying the type of leukemia. Another noteworthy comparative analysis was done by Shadman Sakib et al. which assessed the effectiveness of various ML algorithms such as SVM, Logistic Regression (LR), Decision trees (DT), Random Forest (RF), K-Nearest Neighbours (K-NN) and Naïve Bayes after performing dimensionality reduction using Principle Component Analysis (PCA) [20]. The results indicate that almost all the models exhibited similar results.

A five layered convergent learning model was proposed by Pradeep Kumar Mallick et al. which considered extensive bone marrow expressions of obtained from 72 samples [18]. The predictive performance of the model was found superior to the ML models. Feature selection plays a pivotal role in the gene expression monitoring. Mahwish Ilyas et al.used Fisher’s linear discriminant analysis, to reduce the dimensions of the gene data without loss [24]. This method of feature selection was able to achieve good improvement in terms of prediction accuracy and computation time.

The detailed literature review indicates that the automated leukemia classification can be done with great accuracy and precision using data driven intelligent techniques. The main reason for this is the developments in the digital pathology. However, the works indicates that most of the models explore the data space from single perspective. Deployment of versatile and heterogeneous hybrid and ensemble models is an area which is left unexplored in the context of gene expression monitoring. Ensemble models learn the dataspace from different perspectives, which leads to the identification of latent features from the gene expressions. Also, the heterogeneous combination of base learners in this type of modelling exhibits complimentary behaviour, where the limitations of one model will be overcome by subsequent learning models. Hence, the present work focuses on developing a novel super learning model which harness the benefits of including the predictions of cross validation samples in the prediction vector.

Detailed methodology for development of super learning model for gene expression monitoring

The proposed methodology learns the patterns and trends in gene expression profile from the digitized pathological records to rightly classify the category of leukemia. The progression of the proposed work is done in two phases namely determining the significant gene properties that could contribute to the classification problem and development of super learning model.

(i)Determining contributing factors for leukemia type classificationThe gene expression profile contains digitized DNA data that helps to classify the type of leukemia through gene monitoring. However, not all the features of gene play crucial role in the prediction process. Hence, the work employs entropy based feature importance technique to rank the features which significantly impact the classification [21]. This aids the process of model building by improving precise model with reduced computational complexity, as the feature that does not hold any significance in distinguishing the type of leukemia will not considered for further processing. This process holds high importance in building ML models which proceeds by estimating the score for all the input features and analyses the influence of each feature to the final classification result. If the scores are higher, then the feature plays a prominent role in classifying the type of leukemia. The mathematical expression for calculating the Feature Importance (FI) is given as Eq. (1). Architecture of the proposed super learning model for leukemia classification

The functioning of the proposed super learning model is to rightly classify the leukemia cells into its proper class with minimum error or loss. The proposed model is a stacked generalization of out of fold classification of the candidate models. This is a unique feature of the proposed work. Unlike the other ensemble techniques, the proposed model considers the classification results of all the candidate learners including the out of fold predictions. The out of fold predictions are handled by Random Forests, which act as the super learner. The k-fold cross-validation is used to evaluate the performance of candidate learners. To facilitate this, the gene expression monitoring dataset is divided into k subsets. The proposed model uses strategic tenfold cross validation. Each of the candidate model has various hyper parameters which must be empirically tuned and the candidates explore the gene expression in their unique ways to construct a prediction matrix. The weighted combinations of the candidate algorithms are then indexed using appropriate weights which is determined by reducing the loss function. The algorithm converge with optimal set of weights at which the proposed model predicts the results with minimum loss.

The FI score of the gene expression profiles indicates that all the features positively contribute to the classification performance. Thus, the experimental analysis shows that the proposed super learning method exhibits good acclamation in almost all classification metrics. There is no significant performance deterioration even in multi-label classification. However, there are few challenges in real time implementation of the model in clinical purpose. The model’s performance is only as good as its dataset. Hence, noisy gene expression profile will significantly impact the efficacy of the model. In addition to this, the choice of k value in cross validation is a hyper parameter, whose value has been empirically estimated, which is time consuming. Integration of evolutionary algorithms for learning the hyperparameters would be a wise choice for optimising the model. The study would contribute to the enhancement of effective treatment and survival of patients, and would support previous efforts such as that of Siegel et al. Conclusions and future works

The advent of intelligent techniques suggests AI, ML and DL have brought a dramatic change in the operation of clinical disease diagnosis and the therapy. The introduction of digital pathology further facilitated the easy and timely diagnosis of acute diseases like Leukemia. The proposed work focuses on the development of a novel super learning model that explores the gene expression profiles different types of Leukemia cells to classify the type into ALL or AML for further Drug Administration. Though, various types of gene expressions are present in Leukemia DNA microarray, only few of them contribute to the final classification. The contributing gene expressions are determined by using entropy-based feature importance technique. The candidate models explored the gene expression array to give its individual prediction and the outcome was determined by making random forest as the Super learner which acts on the predictions of unseen cross validation data set of each candidate model. The experimental analysis and the performance assessment of the proposed model indicates that the model was able to exhibit better predictive power than the other state of art DL models. This work employs two different gene expression dataset namely ALL-AML and CuMiDa. Its supremacy in prediction can be attributed to the fact that each candidate model acts on the gene data unique way and learns the hidden patterns and trends in the gene expression. This work can be further extended by assigning adaptive weights by using evolutionary algorithms which explores the data space in a better manner. Also, employing evolutionary algorithms to determine the optimal value of hyperparameters would further improve the model. However, the model is limited by the size of the dataset for training the model which could be improved upon in future studies to expand its ability to perform and deal with more complexities.

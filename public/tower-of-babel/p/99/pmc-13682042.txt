Association of preoperative forced vital capacity with prolonged mechanical ventilation after coronary artery bypass grafting: a retrospective cohort Study
Authors: Meng He, Liaoming He, Jinwei Zhang, Yajing Xu, Xiwei Zhang, Xiaoting Li, Jing Nie, Yongqiang Lai, Changwei Ren, Hao Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProlonged mechanical ventilation (PMV) is a serious complication after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), yet the association between preoperative pulmonary function and PMV risk remains to be comprehensively evaluated.MethodsThis study included adult patients who underwent isolated CABG with preoperative pulmonary function testing. Predicted forced vital capacity (FVC), predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV₁), and the FEV₁/FVC ratio were assessed for their association with PMV.ResultsAmong 1,702 patients, 151 (8.9%) experienced PMV. Patients with PMV were older (65.1 ± 8.5 vs. 62.8 ± 8.8 years, P = 0.003), had higher rates of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (9.9% vs. 5.4%, P = 0.034) and on-pump CABG (8.6% vs. 1.2%, P < 0.001), lower left ventricular ejection fraction (57.1 ± 10.3% vs. 59.1 ± 8.7%, P = 0.007), lower predicted FVC (82.9 ± 14.0% vs. 88.1 ± 14.9%, P < 0.001), and lower predicted FEV₁ (88.7 ± 15.5% vs. 92.6 ± 16.2%, P = 0.007). They also had higher perioperative mortality (4.0% vs. 0.1%, P < 0.001), longer postoperative hospital stays (10.9 ± 4.2 vs. 8.0 ± 2.6 days, P < 0.001), and greater hospitalization costs (25,309.4 [22,119.4–33,359.3] vs. 18,155.4 [16,665.8–20,001.5] USD, P < 0.001). Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. With population aging and the increasing prevalence of cardiometabolic risk factors, the number of patients requiring coronary revascularization continues to rise in the United States, Europe, and China [1–3]. For patients with multivessel CAD, diabetes mellitus, or impaired left ventricular function, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) remains an important revascularization strategy [4].

Despite advances in surgical techniques, anesthesia, and perioperative care, prolonged mechanical ventilation (PMV) remains a clinically important complication after CABG. PMV has been associated with increased perioperative morbidity and mortality, longer intensive care unit and hospital stays, greater healthcare resource utilization, and higher hospitalization costs [5–10]. Therefore, early identification of patients at increased risk for PMV is important for perioperative risk stratification, respiratory optimization, fast-track extubation planning, and postoperative resource allocation.

Previous studies have reported that impaired preoperative pulmonary function, including reduced forced vital capacity (FVC) or forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1), is associated with prolonged ventilatory support or adverse postoperative outcomes after cardiac surgery [10–13]. However, these studies included heterogeneous cardiac surgical populations, evaluated different pulmonary function parameters and cutoff values, and applied varying definitions of prolonged ventilation. As a result, the association between preoperative pulmonary function and PMV remains insufficiently characterized in patients undergoing isolated CABG.

This issue is clinically relevant because smoking exposure is common among patients undergoing CABG and represents a shared risk factor for both CAD and impaired pulmonary function [14–16]. Clarifying the relationship between preoperative pulmonary function and PMV may therefore help improve perioperative risk assessment and postoperative respiratory management in this population.

Therefore, the objective of this study was to evaluate the association between preoperative pulmonary function, including predicted FVC, predicted FEV1, and the FEV1/FVC ratio, and the risk of PMV in patients undergoing isolated CABG.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Anzhen Hospital, with a waiver of informed consent. Consecutive adult patients (≥ 18 years) who underwent cardiac surgery between February 1 and November 30, 2025, were assessed for eligibility. Patients were included if they underwent isolated CABG and had available preoperative pulmonary function test results. Patients were excluded if they had no preoperative pulmonary function test, had prior cardiac surgery, underwent concomitant cardiac procedures such as valve repair/replacement or ventricular remodeling, or died within 48 h after surgery (Fig. 1). Relevant clinical data were extracted from medical records.

No formal a priori sample size calculation was performed because of the retrospective design. The sample size was determined by the number of consecutive eligible patients who underwent isolated CABG during the study period and met the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. A total of 1,702 patients were included in the final analysis, among whom 151 experienced the primary outcome of PMV.

Definitions of exposures, covariates, and outcomes

The main exposures were preoperative spirometric parameters, including predicted FVC, predicted FEV1, and the FEV1/FVC ratio, measured before surgery. Candidate covariates included patient-related factors, surgery-related factors, and perioperative complications that may be associated with PMV. Patient-related factors included age, sex, body mass index, smoking history, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), history of percutaneous coronary intervention, history of stroke, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and preoperative serum creatinine. Surgery-related factors included on-pump CABG and operation time. Perioperative complications included reintubation, re-sternotomy, acute renal failure, intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) and/or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support, and perioperative stroke. Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) support was recorded but was not included as a candidate covariate because of its collinearity with acute renal failure.

The primary outcome was PMV, defined as postoperative mechanical ventilation for more than 48 h. This included patients who required mechanical ventilation for > 48 consecutive hours after surgery and those who were extubated but subsequently reintubated, resulting in a cumulative mechanical ventilation duration of > 48 h. Perioperative mortality was defined as death from any cause during the index hospitalization after CABG. Acute renal failure was defined as serum creatinine > 353.6 µmol/L or a new requirement for dialysis. The use of IABP, ECMO, or CRRT was determined collaboratively by the cardiac care unit (CCU) physician and cardiac surgeon. Perioperative stroke was diagnosed by a neurologist based on clinical presentation and confirmed by CT or MRI.

Perioperative management and extubation protocol

Preoperative pulmonary function tests were performed before surgery by trained rehabilitation physicians according to standardized spirometry procedures. Spirometric parameters, including FVC and FEV1, were measured using a pulmonary function testing system (Spirolab; MIR Medical International Research, Rome, Italy). Predicted values and the FEV1/FVC ratio were calculated using the reference equations embedded in the system.

All patients received general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation according to the institutional protocol for cardiac surgery. Anesthetic induction and maintenance, intraoperative hemodynamic management, fluid administration, blood transfusion, and vasoactive medication use were determined by the attending anesthesiologist according to the patient’s clinical condition and surgical requirements. Intraoperative complications, such as severe hemodynamic instability, malignant arrhythmia, major bleeding requiring hemostatic intervention or massive transfusion, difficulty with oxygenation, and the need for mechanical circulatory support, were managed jointly by the cardiac surgery and anesthesia teams. Perioperative pain control was generally provided using a sufentanil-based analgesia pump for three days, with additional analgesic regimens individualized according to the patient’s clinical condition.

After surgery, patients were transferred to the CCU with mechanical ventilation. Primary extubation followed a nurse-led protocol, and extubation was generally considered on postoperative day 1 when patients met the following clinical criteria: clear consciousness or adequate recovery from anesthesia, recovery of spontaneous breathing and limb muscle strength, hemodynamic stability, adequate oxygenation and ventilation on reduced ventilatory support, stable acid-base and metabolic status, acceptable chest drainage, and adequate urine output. Patients who did not meet these criteria or who developed respiratory, hemodynamic, neurologic, or bleeding-related instability remained intubated and were reassessed by the CCU physician. Patients requiring PMV were evaluated daily by a senior CCU physician, who made final decisions regarding airway management, delayed extubation, reintubation, tracheostomy, and other individualized interventions.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using the Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate.

The associations of predicted FVC, predicted FEV1, and the FEV1/FVC ratio with PMV were first assessed using logistic regression models with restricted cubic splines to evaluate potential non-linear relationships. Non-linearity was examined using likelihood ratio tests. When no significant non-linear association was observed, spirometric parameters were analyzed as continuous variables in logistic regression models.

For the primary analysis, all candidate variables, including spirometric parameters, patient-related factors, surgery-related factors, and perioperative complication variables, were first assessed using univariable logistic regression analyses. Variables with P < 0.10 in the univariable analyses were subsequently included in the multivariable logistic regression model. Reintubation was not included as a candidate covariate because it was part of the definition of PMV in patients who were extubated and subsequently reintubated with a cumulative mechanical ventilation duration of > 48 h. The discriminative ability of the multivariable model was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and quantified by the area under the ROC curve (AUC).

Sensitivity analyses were conducted sequentially by excluding patients with reintubation, then excluding patients with COPD or those undergoing on-pump CABG, and finally excluding patients with perioperative major complications (Fig. 1). Predicted FVC was additionally categorized into quartiles. Quartiles were modeled as a four-level categorical variable, with the lowest quartile serving as the reference category, to estimate the odds ratios for PMV across quartiles. For the trend analysis, quartiles were assigned ordinal values of 1, 2, 3, and 4 according to increasing predicted FVC and entered into the logistic regression model as a continuous term. The corresponding P value was reported as the P for trend. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence interval (CI) were reported for all models. A two-sided P ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4.2 (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria).

Results

A total of 1,702 patients who underwent isolated CABG and had preoperative pulmonary function testing were included. The mean age was 63.0 ± 8.8 years, and 1,303 patients (76.6%) were male. COPD was present in 98 patients (5.8%), and 32 patients (1.9%) underwent on-pump CABG. The mean predicted FVC was 87.6 ± 14.9%, the mean predicted FEV1 was 92.3 ± 16.2%, and the mean FEV1/FVC ratio was 81.7 ± 9.5%. Perioperative complications included reintubation in 36 patients (2.1%), resternotomy in 35 (2.1%), acute renal failure in 23 (1.4%), CRRT support in 16 (0.9%), IABP and/or ECMO support in 46 (2.7%), perioperative stroke in 28 (1.6%), and perioperative death in 8 (0.5%). The mean postoperative hospital stay was 8.2 ± 2.9 days, and the median hospitalization cost was 18,425.6 USD (IQR, 16,802.3–20,629.2).

Overall, 151 patients (8.9%) experienced PMV. Compared with patients without PMV, those with PMV were older (65.1 ± 8.5 vs. 62.8 ± 8.8 years, P = 0.003), had a higher prevalence of COPD (9.9% vs. 5.4%, P = 0.034), and were more likely to undergo on-pump CABG (8.6% vs. 1.2%, P < 0.001). Patients with PMV also had lower LVEF (57.1 ± 10.3% vs. 59.1 ± 8.7%, P = 0.007), lower predicted FVC (82.9 ± 14.0% vs. 88.1 ± 14.9%, P < 0.001), and lower predicted FEV1 (88.7 ± 15.5% vs. 92.6 ± 16.2%, P = 0.007) (Table 1). In addition, perioperative complications were more frequent in the PMV group, including reintubation (13.2% vs. 1.0%, P < 0.001), resternotomy (15.2% vs. 0.8%, P < 0.001), acute renal failure (6.6% vs. 0.8%, P < 0.001), CRRT support (5.3% vs. 0.5%, P < 0.001), IABP and/or ECMO support (18.5% vs. 1.2%, P < 0.001), perioperative stroke (6.6% vs. 1.2%, P < 0.001), and perioperative death (4.0% vs. 0.1%, P < 0.001). They also had longer postoperative hospital stays (10.9 ± 4.2 vs. 8.0 ± 2.6 days, P < 0.001) and higher hospitalization costs (25,309.4 [IQR, 22,119.4–33,359.3] vs. 18,155.4 [IQR, 16,665.8–20,001.5] USD; P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Logistic regression analyses with restricted cubic splines showed no evidence of non-linear associations between predicted FVC, predicted FEV1, or the FEV1/FVC ratio and PMV (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Figs. 1–2). In univariable logistic regression analyses, several factors were significantly associated with PMV, including the spirometric parameters predicted FVC and predicted FEV1; patient-related factors, including age, a history of COPD, and LVEF; surgery-related factors, including on-pump CABG and operation duration; and perioperative complications, including resternotomy, acute renal failure, CRRT, IABP and/or ECMO support, and perioperative stroke. After multivariable adjustment for patient-related, surgery-related, and selected perioperative complication variables, each 1% increase in predicted FVC was independently associated with lower odds of PMV (OR, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.95–0.99; P = 0.005), whereas predicted FEV1 was not significantly associated with PMV (OR, 1.01; 95% CI, 0.99–1.03; P = 0.234) (Table 3). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for the multivariable model was 0.74 (95% CI, 0.69–0.79) (Supplementary Fig. 3).

In sensitivity analyses that sequentially excluded patients with reintubation, patients with COPD or on-pump CABG, and patients with perioperative complications, the association between lower predicted FVC and a higher risk of PMV remained consistent (Fig. 3 and Supplementary Tables 1–3). In the quartile analysis of predicted FVC, the mean predicted FVC values across quartiles were 69.8 ± 8.3%, 82.9 ± 2.5%, 92.3 ± 2.9%, and 107.1 ± 8.0%, respectively (Supplementary Table 4). The incidence of PMV decreased progressively across increasing quartiles of predicted FVC: 12.1% (54/448), 11.3% (47/417), 6.7% (29/434), and 5.2% (21/403), respectively. Compared with patients in the lowest quartile, those in the highest quartile had an approximately 50% lower risk of PMV (OR, 0.47; 95% CI, 0.26–0.85). A significant trend toward lower PMV risk with increasing predicted FVC was observed (P for trend = 0.004) (Table 4).

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of patients undergoing isolated CABG, 151 patients (8.9%) required PMV. Compared with patients without PMV, those with PMV were older, had lower LVEF, a higher prevalence of COPD, and poorer preoperative pulmonary function. Perioperative complications were also more frequent in the PMV group, and these patients had longer postoperative hospital stays and greater healthcare costs. Notably, predicted FVC, but not predicted FEV1, remained independently associated with PMV after multivariable adjustment.

The reported incidence of PMV after cardiac surgery varies depending on the definition used. A 48-hour threshold is commonly adopted to differentiate clinically meaningful prolonged ventilation from delays related to transient postoperative factors, such as delayed emergence from sedation, minor or temporary complications, and logistical factors related to staffing or workflow [17]. Previous studies have reported PMV rates ranging from 5% to 14% [9–11, 17–19], and the incidence in our cohort was 8.9%, which is consistent with this range. In line with previous reports [5–10], patients who developed PMV in our cohort had higher perioperative mortality, longer postoperative hospital stays, and greater hospitalization costs.

Early identification of patients at high risk for PMV is critical for optimizing ICU resource allocation, guiding extubation strategies, and improving perioperative planning. Consistent with previous studies, our findings showed that older age, COPD, the use of cardiopulmonary bypass, longer operation time, and perioperative complications, including re-sternotomy, acute renal failure, CRRT support, IABP and/or ECMO support, and perioperative stroke, were associated with PMV [7, 20–24]. Nevertheless, the relationship between preoperative pulmonary function and PMV remains less clearly defined. Several previous studies have examined the association between preoperative pulmonary function and postoperative ventilatory support after cardiac surgery, but their findings have varied. Durand et al. reported that the duration of mechanical ventilation increased in patients with FEV1 <1.5 L or vital capacity < 2.5 L [12]. Cohen et al., Reddy et al., and Saleh et al. reported that reduced FEV1 was independently associated with PMV [5, 10, 11]. Reddi et al. further reported that FVC < 80% predicted and diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) < 60% predicted were significantly associated with PMV [13]. In our cohort, both predicted FEV1 and predicted FVC were associated with PMV in univariate analyses; however, only predicted FVC remained independently associated with PMV after multivariable adjustment. This finding was consistent across sensitivity analyses that sequentially excluded patients with reintubation, patients with COPD or those who underwent on-pump CABG, and patients with perioperative complications. Furthermore, quartile analysis demonstrated a graded association between predicted FVC and PMV, with progressively lower PMV risk across increasing predicted FVC quartiles.

Previous studies have demonstrated that reduced pulmonary function is associated with higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in the general population [25–28]. McAllister et al. reported that both FEV1 and FVC were associated with in-hospital mortality after cardiac surgery [29]. Although FEV1 is central to COPD staging and prognostic assessment in patients with COPD undergoing cardiac surgery [30, 31], FVC may provide complementary prognostic information, particularly in patients without established obstructive lung disease. For example, a U.S. population-based cohort that excluded participants with chronic respiratory disease or persistent respiratory symptoms found that survival was more closely related to FVC than to FEV1 [32]. In the cardiac surgical setting, reduced predicted FVC may reflect not only impaired pulmonary mechanics but also reduced respiratory reserve and respiratory muscle weakness. Preoperative respiratory sarcopenia, characterized by reduced respiratory muscle strength and mass, has been shown to independently predict poor functional recovery after cardiovascular surgery [33]. Moreover, preoperative handgrip strength correlates strongly with FVC in cardiac surgical populations [34]. Collectively, these findings suggest that low FVC may identify a vulnerable phenotype characterized by limited respiratory reserve and generalized muscle weakness. Consistent with this concept, our study found that predicted FVC, rather than predicted FEV1, remained independently associated with PMV in patients undergoing isolated CABG.

The findings of this study may have several clinical implications. Preoperative predicted FVC is a simple and routinely available spirometric parameter that may help identify patients at increased risk for PMV before isolated CABG. Incorporating FVC into perioperative risk assessment may complement existing clinical risk stratification and support more proactive planning of postoperative monitoring and extubation strategies. Patients with reduced predicted FVC may represent a subgroup that could benefit from targeted preoperative optimization, including smoking cessation, respiratory training, pulmonary rehabilitation, nutritional support, and assessment of frailty or sarcopenia when feasible. In addition, recognition of low predicted FVC before surgery may help guide CCU resource planning, early postoperative respiratory support, and individualized recovery pathways. Given the association of PMV with longer hospitalization and higher costs, integrating pulmonary function into perioperative planning may contribute to more efficient resource allocation and improved postoperative management.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center cohort study, and the generalizability of the findings requires validation in other settings. Second, only CABG patients were included, most of whom underwent off-pump procedures. Although this improved patient homogeneity, it may limit the applicability of our findings to other cardiac surgical populations, such as patients undergoing valve or aortic surgery. Third, despite multivariable adjustment, residual confounding from unmeasured factors cannot be excluded. In particular, detailed anesthetic-related factors, such as intraoperative ventilatory settings, anesthetic agents, fluid administration, and vasoactive medication use, as well as patient-related factors such as frailty, were not available in the database. In addition, data on perioperative myocardial infarction and postoperative pulmonary infection were not reliably available. Therefore, we could not evaluate the potential influence of these factors on PMV or include them in the adjusted models. Future prospective studies incorporating detailed anesthetic, intraoperative management, frailty-related, and postoperative complication variables are warranted to further clarify their contribution to PMV after CABG.

Conclusion

Nearly one-tenth of patents undergoing CABG required PMV, and these patients consumed greater healthcare resources and experienced higher rates of perioperative complications. Predicted FVC, rather than predicted FEV1, was an independent predictor of PMV incidence.

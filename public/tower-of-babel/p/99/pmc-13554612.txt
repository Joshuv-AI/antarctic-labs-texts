Single-cell expression quantitative trait locus Mendelian randomization reveals immune cell-specific causal regulatory networks and actionable targets in polycystic ovary syndrome
Authors: Jiahuan Zhang, Yikai Liu, Danhong Lin, Dexia Li, Haoyi Liang, Wei Jin, Huibiao Quan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo systematically investigate whether the pathogenesis of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is causally related to dysregulated gene expression in specific immune cell subsets, and to evaluate the potential of these causal genes as actionable drug targets.MethodsThis study employed a two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) framework using publicly available genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics. The participant data included 797 PCOS cases and 140,558 controls (no direct patient recruitment was involved). Instrumental variables were derived from high-resolution immune cell-specific single-cell expression quantitative trait locus (sc-eQTL) data (OneK1K project) across 14 immune cell types. Primary analyses utilized the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method. Shared causal variants were validated using Bayesian colocalization. Phenome-wide association analysis (PheWAS), external transcriptomic dataset validation (GSE8157), and DrugBank database screening were conducted for pleiotropy assessment and drug repositioning.ResultsMR analysis revealed genome-wide significant causal associations for GLIPR1 in non-classical monocytes (Mono NC) and XBP1 in CD4+ effector memory T cells (CD4 ET) with PCOS risk. Higher GLIPR1 expression was associated with a decreased PCOS risk (OR = 0.669, P = 4.34×10-6), whereas higher XBP1 expression was associated with an increased risk (OR = 1.406, P = 9.53×10-8). Colocalization analysis confirmed that GLIPR1 shares a causal variant with PCOS (PP.H4 = 96.73%). PheWAS and external validation confirmed the safety profile and significant upregulation (P = 0.03) of GLIPR1. Drug repositioning identified SOT-107, a Phase III protein therapy drug, as a potential interacting agent for GLIPR1.ConclusionsThis sc-eQTL MR study reveals immune cell-specific causal regulatory networks in PCOS. GLIPR1 in non-classical monocytes represents a high-confidence protective target, while XBP1 provides suggestive evidence for immune-mediated pathogenesis. The candidate drug SOT-107 highlights theoretical repositioning opportunities, though rigorous preclinical validation remains required.Keywords: sc-eQTL, Mendelian randomization, PCOS, immune cells, GLIPR1, XBP1

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome is a heterogeneous endocrine disorder and one of the most common endocrine diseases affecting women of reproductive age. Due to the considerable clinical heterogeneity among PCOS patients, current management relies on combined assessment of multiple markers. 1 Among these, anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), androgens (T), and the homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) are used to reflect ovarian microenvironment changes, hyperandrogenemia, and systemic metabolic abnormalities, respectively. 2 Androgen excess is a hallmark of PCOS, as elevated androgens disrupt follicle maturation, leading to ovulatory dysfunction and polycystic ovarian morphology. 3 Concurrently, metabolic disturbances are regarded as important clinical indicators for assessing the physiological dysregulation in PCOS. Integrating multiple metabolic defects, including insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and impaired glucose tolerance, provides a crucial basis for evaluating long-term health risks and cardiovascular prognosis. 4 The hormone fluctuations and glucolipid metabolic features specific to PCOS underscore its clinical value as an indicator of overall female endocrine health. 5 Further exploration of relevant biomarkers can help elucidate the complex pathogenesis and provide key entry points for developing precise, individualized intervention strategies. 6 Recent findings by Vale-Fernandes et al. (2025) further highlight that women with PCOS exhibit increased follicular fluid AMH levels alongside glycolytic and mitochondrial dysfunction markers, which impair oocyte maturation and fertilization potential, emphasizing the intricate immune-metabolic interplay in PCOS pathogenesis. 7

Over the past decades, research into the pathological mechanisms of PCOS has consistently focused on the two core axes of endocrine dysregulation and insulin resistance (IR). A substantial body of molecular evidence indicates that the development of PCOS is not driven by a single target imbalance but is a complex process jointly driven by multiple signaling networks, including impaired PI3K/Akt signaling, abnormal MAPK cascades, and chronic low-grade inflammation. 8 The aberrant activation or inhibition of these pathways not only disrupts steroidogenesis related to follicular development but also mediates systemic metabolic alterations. Currently, traditional intervention systems centered on metformin, oral contraceptives, and ovulation-inducing agents have demonstrated definitive efficacy in improving clinical phenotypes. Moreover, clinical practice is accelerating its transition from universal protocols to individualized precision medicine based on genetic and phenotypic backgrounds, in line with the latest international guidelines. 9 Furthermore, modulators such as inositols and GLP-1 receptor agonists have garnered attention for their potential to restore endocrine homeostasis and metabolic balance.10,11 Despite these advances, discovering novel drug targets for PCOS remains a key challenge in contemporary reproductive medicine research.

Recently, the central role of the immune system in PCOS pathogenesis has become increasingly prominent, and targeting immune regulation is emerging as a cutting-edge strategy to ameliorate ovulatory dysfunction and metabolic abnormalities in patients. 12 As the disease progresses, PCOS patients often exhibit systemic and ovarian local immune homeostasis imbalances. This pathological process is typically characterized by the persistent drive of chronic low-grade inflammation, dysregulation of cytokine secretion networks, and significant alterations in immune cell lineages. 13 Studies have shown that aberrant polarization and infiltration of key immune cell subsets, including T cells, monocytes/macrophages, and dendritic cells (DCs), are deeply involved in the pathological progression of PCOS. 14 Based on these findings, immune interventions, such as blocking core inflammatory cytokines or remodeling the ovarian local immune microenvironment, show promising potential for clinical translation. 15 Faced with the complex immune-metabolic interaction network in PCOS, further elucidation of the underlying immune-mediated mechanisms will provide crucial theoretical support for the development of novel targeted drugs.

Genetic studies have made significant contributions to revealing PCOS-related pathways and potential therapeutic targets. To date, large-scale genome-wide association study (GWAS) meta-analyses have successfully identified numerous core genetic variants associated with PCOS susceptibility, particularly in regions involved in luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) signaling (e.g., LHCGR and FSHR loci) and metabolic regulation (e.g., INSR).16–18 However, despite the rich genetic clues provided by GWAS, their inherent limitation lies in the difficulty of establishing causality behind the association signals, hindering the direct translation of these statistical findings into targeted drug development strategies. To overcome this bottleneck, Mendelian randomization (MR) methods have been employed. By using genetic variants as instrumental variables, MR has become an effective and robust means of inferring causal effects between gene expression and PCOS phenotypes. 19 Previous studies have used bulk expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) data to identify potential drug targets for PCOS. 20 However, bulk eQTL approaches cannot capture cell type-specific gene regulatory mechanisms, which are crucial for understanding immune-mediated PCOS pathways.

The advent of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has propelled transcriptomic research into the era of single-cell resolution. This not only overcomes the “averaging” flaw of traditional tissue homogenate sequencing but also provides unprecedented opportunities to dissect the heterogeneous regulatory networks of genetic variants across different immune cell subsets. 21 Recent advances in single-cell expression quantitative trait locus (sc-eQTL) mapping enable researchers to associate genetic variants with gene expression at a cell type-specific level, identifying potent eQTLs unique to specific immune cells. 22 Cutting-edge studies have fully demonstrated the invaluable role of sc-eQTLs in dissecting the genetic mechanisms of complex diseases. However, in the field of PCOS, a systematic study integrating this high-resolution data with an MR framework is still lacking, making it difficult to ascertain true causal associations between immune-related genes and PCOS. To fill this knowledge gap, this study innovatively applies an sc-eQTL MR framework to systematically investigate the causal relationship between immune cell-specific gene expression and PCOS risk, and further pinpoints candidate intervention drugs with translational potential.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethics

This study adhered to the STROBE-MR (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology using Mendelian Randomization) guidelines to ensure rigorous reporting standards. 23 The MR method requires satisfying three key assumptions 1 : genetic variants used as instrumental variables (IVs) are strongly associated with the exposure 2 ; these IVs are independent of confounders 3 ; IVs affect the outcome (PCOS) only through the exposure and not via alternative pathways. All analyses were conducted using publicly available GWAS summary statistics. As these data do not involve individual-level information, additional ethical approval was not required. The original GWAS studies included in this analysis obtained informed consent from participants and were approved by their respective institutional ethics committees.

Exposure and instrumental variable selection

To construct robust IVs for MR analysis, we utilized cis-expression quantitative trait loci (cis-eQTLs) derived from the OneK1K sc-eQTL dataset. 24 A multi-step filtering strategy was employed to ensure the validity of selected IVs. First, we retained conditionally independent cis-eQTLs significantly associated with gene expression at the genome-wide significance threshold (P < 5.0×10-8) to minimize false positives. Second, we performed linkage disequilibrium (LD) clumping based on the 1000 Genomes European reference panel, applying a strict LD threshold (r2 < 0.001) and a 10 Mb window to ensure independence among single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Finally, we calculated the F-statistic for each SNP to assess instrument strength, excluding SNPs with F < 10 to mitigate weak instrument bias. Following these filtering steps, we identified high-power SNPs across 14 immune cell types as valid instruments for downstream MR analysis. 25

Outcome data

Genetic association data for PCOS were obtained from the IEU Open GWAS database (ID: ebi-a-GCST90044902), 26 comprising 797 PCOS cases and 140,558 control individuals. PCOS diagnosis followed the revised Rotterdam criteria, 27 requiring at least two of the following three: clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound examination. The choice of this specific GWAS dataset, along with considerations regarding its statistical power and potential false negative/positive rates, are thoroughly addressed in the discussion section.

Sc-eQTL MR analysis

MR analysis was performed using the R package TwoSampleMR (version 0.6.6) to assess the causal effects of immune cell-specific gene expression on PCOS. For eGenes with only one valid instrumental SNP, the Wald ratio method was used. When two or more independent SNPs were present, the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method was used to derive the overall causal estimate. Comprehensive sensitivity analyses—including heterogeneity tests (e.g., Cochran’s Q) and pleiotropy assessments—were systematically conducted to ensure the robustness of the causal inferences. To control for multiple hypothesis testing, a stringent Bonferroni correction was applied based on the total number of eGenes tested in the eQTL dataset, setting the statistical significance threshold at P < 6.78×10-6. This corrected threshold ensured robust identification of causal eGenes while minimizing the false discovery rate. 28

Colocalization analysis

To assess whether the same genetic variants influence both gene expression and PCOS, Bayesian colocalization analysis was performed using the coloc R package. This method evaluates the posterior probability of five different hypotheses, including the probability that both traits share a causal variant (PP.H4). A PP.H4 > 80% was set as the threshold for strong colocalization evidence for an eGene, while PP.H4 > 50% was interpreted as moderate evidence supporting a potential shared causal signal. 29 Only eGenes passing these thresholds were prioritized for downstream biological interpretation and drug target assessment.

Exploring systemic associations and drug repurposing of eGenes via PheWAS

To assess the potential pleiotropic effects and off-target risks of the prioritized candidate eGenes, we performed phenome-wide association analysis using the AstraZeneca PheWAS platform (https://azphewas.com/). 30 This resource provides association data for approximately 13,000 binary phenotypes and 5,000 continuous phenotypes (including NMR metabolomics measures) based on whole-exome sequencing data from nearly 500,000 UK Biobank participants.31,32 We queried the identified causal genes to capture significant associations with various clinical outcomes and biochemical markers, aiming to detect any unintended effects that might affect their suitability as therapeutic targets for PCOS. Following the significance cutoffs recommended by the platform, we employed P < 1×10-6 as the statistical significance threshold to strictly control for multiple testing and ensure result robustness.

External dataset validation

To validate the expression differences of key causal genes, we utilized an independent whole-transcriptome dataset of peripheral blood from PCOS patients (GSE8157). This dataset comprised samples from 22 PCOS patients and 22 normal controls. 33 The expression level of GLIPR1 was compared between case and control groups using a non-parametric Student's t-test, with P < 0.05 considered the statistical significance threshold.

Results

Immune cell type-specific effects of gene expression on PCOS

To systematically evaluate the causal effects of gene expression in different immune cell subsets on PCOS, a two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis was conducted using cis-eQTLs for 14 immune cell types from the OneK1K project as instrumental variables, with the inverse-variance weighted method as the primary approach. After controlling for multiple testing using FDR (significance threshold P < 6.78×10-6), two immune cell-specific eGenes were identified with significant causal relationships to PCOS risk.

Manhattan plots (Figure 1) illustrated the distribution of association signals for eGenes across immune cell types genome-wide, revealing that the signals predominantly concentrated in two immune cell subsets: non-classical monocytes (Mono NC) and CD4+ effector memory T cells (CD4 ET). In non-classical monocytes, expression level of the GLIPR1 (GLI pathogenesis related 1) gene was significantly negatively correlated with PCOS risk, with an odds ratio (OR) of 0.669 (95% confidence interval: 0.57–0.79) and a P-value of 4.34×10-6, indicating that each standard deviation increases in GLIPR1 expression was associated with approximately a 33.1% decrease in PCOS risk. In CD4+ effector memory T cells, expression level of the XBP1 (X-box binding protein 1) gene was significantly positively correlated with PCOS risk, with an OR of 1.406 (95% CI: 1.24–1.60) and a P-value of 9.53×10-8, indicating that each standard deviation increases in XBP1 expression was associated with approximately a 40.6% increase in PCOS risk. No eGenes reached the genome-wide significance threshold in the remaining 12 immune cell types (including B cells, NK cells, other T cell subsets, and monocyte subtypes). The F-statistics for all significant association instruments exceeded 10 (range: 18.6–42.3), excluding the possibility of weak instrument bias.

Colocalization analysis prioritizes key eGenes

While MR analysis established a causal relationship between gene expression and PCOS, it cannot exclude the possibility that the association arises from linkage disequilibrium or distinct causal variants. Therefore, Bayesian colocalization analysis was performed to assess whether the expression regulatory loci for each significant eGene and the PCOS risk loci share the same causal genetic variant. For GLIPR1 in Mono NC, colocalization analysis showed a posterior probability PP.H4 = 96.73% (Figures 2 and 3), exceeding the 80% threshold for strong evidence, indicating a very high probability that GLIPR1 expression and PCOS are driven by the same causal variant. Further analysis of the regional association plot for this locus (chromosome 12, 75–77 Mb region, Figure 3) revealed that the lead variant rs7971504 drives both the eQTL signal for GLIPR1 (top panel) and the GWAS signal for PCOS (middle panel). The LD degree (r2) of this variant with surrounding SNPs exhibited high consistency, with the association signal peaks for both traits completely overlapping. For XBP1 in CD4 ET, colocalization analysis showed PP.H4 = 65.4%, exceeding the moderate evidence threshold (50%), suggesting a potential shared causal signal, though the evidence was less strong than for GLIPR1. eGenes not reaching the MR significance threshold were not included in colocalization analysis.

Phenotype-WAS analysis to assess potential off-target effects and pleiotropy

To evaluate the safety and pleiotropic effects of the prioritized eGene (GLIPR1) as a potential drug target, we performed phenome-wide association analysis using the AstraZeneca PheWAS platform (based on whole-exome sequencing data from approximately 500,000 UK Biobank participants, covering approximately 13,000 binary phenotypes and 5,000 continuous phenotypes). The analysis results (Figure 4, Supplementary Table S1) showed that genetic variants associated with GLIPR1 were not significantly associated with any malignancies (including ovarian, breast, or endometrial cancer), severe autoimmune diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus), or major adverse cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke) at the preset significance threshold (P < 1×10-6). However, GLIPR1 showed significant associations with multiple metabolism-related phenotypes, most notably creatinine levels (P = 3.97×10-7, effect size = -4.34, standard error = 0.85). Furthermore, GLIPR1 was significantly associated with multiple NMR metabolomic features, including very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL)-related lipid components, branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) levels, and glycolytic metabolites (detailed data in Supplementary Table S1). The direction of these associations was negative, suggesting that higher GLIPR1 expression is associated with a more favorable metabolic profile. No significant associations were found between GLIPR1 and common diseases of the respiratory, digestive, or nervous systems.

External clinical dataset validation of key eGene expression differences

To verify whether the computational predictions from MR and colocalization analysis are reproducible in real biological samples, we performed differential expression validation using an independent peripheral blood whole-transcriptome dataset from PCOS patients (GSE8157, containing 22 PCOS patients and 22 normal controls). Violin plots (Figure 5) show the expression distribution of GLIPR1 in both groups. The results showed that GLIPR1 mRNA expression levels were significantly higher in the PCOS patient group compared to the normal control group (Student's t-test, P = 0.03). Specifically, the mean GLIPR1 expression (log2-transformed) was 6.82 ± 0.45 in the PCOS group and 6.48 ± 0.52 in the normal control group, a difference that was statistically significant. This result validates the upregulation of GLIPR1 expression in the context of PCOS in an independent population, reflecting a potential endogenous compensatory defense mechanism activated against persistent metabolic and inflammatory stress.

Validation of GLIPR1 mRNA expression levels using an independent microarray dataset (GSE8157). Violin plots with jittered scatter points show that GLIPR1 expression is significantly upregulated in the PCOS patient group compared to the normal control group (Student’s t-test, P = 0.03).

Drug repurposing for PCOS-associated eGenes

Given the strong protective causal association (PP.H4 = 96.73%) and favorable safety profile suggested by PheWAS for GLIPR1 in Mono NC, we further explored drug repositioning opportunities targeting GLIPR1. By querying the DrugBank database (version 6.0), we screened for drugs with known or predicted interactions with the GLIPR1 protein. The analysis results (Table 1) identified SOT-107 (a targeted protein therapy currently in Phase III clinical trials for brain cancer) as a high-confidence candidate compound interacting with GLIPR1. The precise molecular mechanism of action of this drug is not fully elucidated but is known to be involved in the regulation of cell surface signaling. SOT-107 is currently in Phase III clinical trials, primarily for brain cancer (including glioblastoma), with no reported trials for PCOS or other reproductive endocrine diseases. In the DrugBank database, no other approved or clinical-stage drugs with direct interactions with GLIPR1 or XBP1 were identified.

Discussion

PCOS is a complex disorder influenced by multiple factors including genetic susceptibility, environmental factors, and imbalances in the endocrine and immune microenvironments. 34 Identifying immune-mediated regulatory mechanisms during the pathological progression of PCOS is crucial for developing novel and precise individualized treatment strategies. This study employed an integrative analytical framework combining single-cell expression quantitative trait locus Mendelian randomization and colocalization analysis to reveal immune cell-specific gene regulatory mechanisms underlying PCOS risk. Through these analyses, we identified eGenes with significant cell type specificity and high-confidence colocalization evidence, indicating that the occurrence of PCOS is not only driven by systemic hormonal networks but is also profoundly shaped by regulatory networks specific to the immune cell microenvironment.

Through immune cell-specific analysis, we successfully uncovered unique mechanistic pathways linking genetic regulation to PCOS susceptibility. Among the various immune cell types analyzed, non-classical monocytes and CD4+ T cells with a memory phenotype emerged as major contributors to PCOS-associated immune features. These two core subsets exhibited highly representative PCOS-associated core eGenes (GLIPR1 and XBP1, respectively), suggesting their central role in orchestrating the immune-metabolic imbalance characteristic of PCOS. These identified key eGenes jointly act on two core pathological pathways in PCOS, systemic metabolic homeostasis and local immune-inflammatory regulation.35–37 Regarding metabolic regulation and energy homeostasis, GLIPR1 in monocytes showed strong colocalization evidence (PP.H4 = 96.73%) and acted as a significant protective factor. Through PheWAS, we further found that GLIPR1 expression was closely associated with creatinine levels and multiple NMR metabolomic features. This suggests that disruption or aberrant expression of this gene in monocytes may lead to impaired systemic energy homeostasis, 38 and impaired lipid and metabolite transport is a key driver of insulin resistance and chronic low-grade inflammation (“metaflammation”) in PCOS. From a functional perspective, GLIPR1 encodes a protein with similarity to both the pathogenesis-related protein superfamily and the cysteine-rich secretory protein family. Increased expression of this gene is associated with myelomonocytic differentiation in macrophages, and the protein exhibits proapoptotic activities in various cancer cell types. 39 Although previous studies have primarily focused on its tumor suppressor functions, inducing apoptosis through the ROS-JNK signaling pathway, 40 our study is the first to reveal its protective role in non-classical monocytes in the context of PCOS. The PheWAS finding that GLIPR1 associates with systemic creatinine levels and multiple NMR metabolomic features requires particular attention. Rather than merely an isolated renal marker, this link suggests that GLIPR1 may serve as a critical central hub integrating systemic metabolic homeostasis, renal excretory mechanisms, and immune regulation. Notably, although MR analysis suggested that high GLIPR1 expression reduces PCOS risk, 41 we observed significant upregulation of this gene in an independent PCOS patient transcriptomic cohort (GSE8157). This apparent contradiction may actually reflect a compensatory defense mechanism activated by the body in response to persistent metabolic stress. GLIPR1 in PCOS patients may be attempting to support the organism’s stress adaptation by suppressing aberrant inflammatory responses or maintaining local metabolic homeostasis.

In the realm of immune regulation and endoplasmic reticulum stress, XBP1 was identified as a significant risk factor in CD4+ T cells. XBP1 is a master transcription factor of the unfolded protein response (UPR) and is activated during B cell differentiation into plasma cells, where it also regulates the production of proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6. 42 Overexpression of XBP1 in CD4+ T cells may accelerate PCOS pathology through multiple mechanisms, disruption of the local immune tolerance microenvironment in the ovary by promoting Th17 polarization while suppressing Treg differentiation and, as revealed in cancer research, XBP1 can drive cholesterol synthesis and secretion, thereby activate myeloid-derived suppressor cells and induce immunosuppression.43,44 Although the immunosuppressive function of XBP1 in the PCOS context awaits experimental validation, its overexpression in CD4 ET cells may similarly remodel the immune microenvironment and perpetuate chronic low-grade inflammation. 45 It is worth noting that the colocalization evidence for XBP1 (PP.H4 = 65.4%) met the moderate support threshold but was weaker than that for GLIPR1, suggesting that the regulatory effects of XBP1 may be subject to more complex genetic background and environmental modifications, a point warranting further investigation.

Beyond classical endocrine tissue abnormalities, the compelling colocalization evidence for eGenes in this study highlights the independent yet synergistic roles of innate and adaptive immunity in PCOS pathogenesis. In the non-classical monocyte subset representing innate immunity, GLIPR1 showed strong colocalization evidence and a significant protective association with PCOS risk. In the CD4 ET cell subset representing adaptive immunity, XBP1 was identified as a significant risk factor. This indicates that even within the same complex disease network, the pathogenic logic of different immune subsets is highly context-dependent: monocytes tend to drive pathological progression through failure (or reaching the limit of compensation) of metabolic sensing, while memory phenotype T cells more often maintain chronic low-grade inflammation through aberrant cellular stress responses.

Our findings complement and dialogue with other recent multi-omics MR studies in the PCOS field. For instance, a recent multi-omics MR study of 2,888 druggable genes prioritized CBLN3 as a protective target and NRBP1 as a risk-associated target for PCOS, with immune infiltration analysis revealing that CBLN3 correlated positively with resting dendritic cells and inversely with eosinophils and neutrophils. 46 Another study integrating plasma proteomics with MR identified CDCP1, GLRX2, and KIRREL2 as PCOS risk-associated protein-coding genes, all closely related to immune function and metabolism. 47 Compared with these studies, the unique contributions of our work are threefold. First, by using single-cell resolution eQTL data rather than bulk tissue eQTLs, we captured cell type-specific regulatory signals that may be masked by heterogeneous cellular backgrounds in bulk data. Second, the identification of GLIPR1 and XBP1 points to two mechanistic dimensions, endoplasmic reticulum stress pathways and monocyte metabolic sensing pathways, that have received less attention in previous PCOS research. Third, our systematic assessment of off-target effects via PheWAS provides a safety foundation for subsequent drug translation. Together, these findings enrich the understanding of the complex genetic architecture of PCOS and highlight that integrating multi-layered omics data will be an important direction for future precision medicine research in PCOS.

To enhance the translational value of our findings, we conducted a drug repurposing study targeting PCOS-associated eGenes with clear clinical potential. Taking GLIPR1 as an example, this gene showed a significant protective association with PCOS risk in non-classical monocytes and exhibited compensatory upregulation in PCOS patients. Although compensatory upregulation occurs in patients, this endogenous defense may be insufficient to reverse the established metabolic-immune vicious cycle; therefore, external intervention to further enhance this signaling pathway using candidate drugs may be necessary to break the disease stalemate. As revealed by our drug repositioning analysis, the protein therapy drug SOT-107 is a potential interacting compound for GLIPR1, currently under Phase III clinical evaluation for high-grade glioma. SOT-107 is a conjugate of transferrin and diphtheria toxin that has received orphan drug status in Japan and fast-track designation from the FDA. Previous drug target studies have identified PDGFRB and KDR as known targets of SOT-107, with GLIPR1 also being identified as a potential target of this compound. 48 The mechanism of action of SOT-107 involves the selective delivery of diphtheria toxin to target cells via transferrin receptor-mediated endocytosis. In the PCOS context, the protective role of GLIPR1 in non-classical monocytes suggests that SOT-107 or its derivatives might ameliorate the immune-metabolic phenotype by enhancing the biological function of GLIPR1 rather than through cytotoxic effects. However, current evidence is limited to computational predictions and in silico target identification; no preclinical or clinical studies have yet investigated SOT-107 for PCOS or other reproductive endocrine disorders. Moreover, drug repurposing in PCOS faces broader challenges: most completed clinical trials on repurposed medications for PCOS have small sample sizes and lack conclusive efficacy results. Therefore, the therapeutic potential of SOT-107 for PCOS requires a full translational research pipeline from target validation to cellular models, animal experiments, and eventually clinical trials, necessitating interdisciplinary collaboration across reproductive endocrinology, immunology, and pharmacology. In summary, while SOT-107 stands out as an intriguing candidate targeting GLIPR1, rigorous preclinical validation and clinical translation remain essential to ascertain its therapeutic viability for PCOS.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically apply single-cell expression quantitative trait locus data to investigate PCOS susceptibility, establishing a new paradigm for exploring immune-mediated PCOS pathogenesis. Whereas previous studies on the genetic mechanisms of PCOS primarily relied on bulk eQTL datasets that integrate gene expression from heterogeneous cell populations,49–51 our approach overcomes this limitation by utilizing single-cell resolution data. The use of immune cell-specific sc-eQTL data allowed us to discover a novel set of PCOS-associated eGenes, such as GLIPR1 (monocytes) and XBP1 (CD4+ T cells), with remarkable cell type specificity. This difference likely reflects the higher resolution and sensitivity of single-cell technology in detecting subtle regulatory signals that may be masked by heterogeneous cellular backgrounds in bulk data. By integrating MR analysis, Bayesian colocalization, and sc-eQTL data, we constructed a robust framework to identify causal genes related to the immune microenvironment in PCOS with higher resolution and biological specificity. This innovative approach significantly improved the accuracy of causal inference, effectively reducing confounding biases from linkage disequilibrium and horizontal pleiotropy, while enhancing the biological and clinical translational relevance of the findings through an independent clinical transcriptomic cohort.

In conclusion, this study presents an innovative analytical framework integrating single-cell expression quantitative trait locus data, Mendelian randomization, and Bayesian colocalization analysis to reveal immune-mediated genetic regulatory mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of polycystic ovary syndrome. We identified high-confidence immune cell-specific eGenes, notably GLIPR1 and XBP1, with strong causal associations to PCOS risk, demonstrating that gene regulation in specific immune subsets (e.g., monocytes and CD4+ T cells) plays a central role in driving the immune-metabolic imbalance in PCOS. By integrating phenome-wide association analysis and the DrugBank drug-target database, we further identified promising precision therapy candidates, including the protein therapy drug SOT-107, with the potential to improve the metabolic phenotype. This study provides novel insights into the complex immune-endocrine-metabolic genetic architecture of PCOS and lays a solid foundation for novel precision-targeted interventions to break the vicious cycle of this disease.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the genetic data used in the primary analyses were predominantly derived from individuals of European ancestry, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic populations. Future studies incorporating multi-ethnic cohorts are warranted to validate the cross-ethnic applicability of the identified causal genes and therapeutic targets. Second, although the single-cell expression quantitative trait locus (sc-eQTL) data provide high-resolution cell type-specific insights, they represent only a static snapshot of gene regulation and cannot capture dynamic transcriptional changes during disease progression or in response to therapeutic interventions. Longitudinal single-cell transcriptomic studies are needed to better understand the temporal dynamics of these regulatory networks. Third, our Mendelian randomization analysis was restricted to cis-eQTLs and did not assess trans-regulatory effects, which may also contribute to the genetic architecture of PCOS. Fourth, the sample size of the external validation dataset (GSE8157) was relatively modest, and the differential expression of GLIPR1, while statistically significant, should be confirmed in larger, independent cohorts. Finally, we did not perform reverse Mendelian randomization to test whether PCOS or its metabolic features causally influence the expression of the identified immune cell-specific genes. Addressing these bidirectional relationships will be an important direction for future research.

Conclusion

This study reveals immune-mediated genetic regulatory mechanisms in PCOS using sc-eQTL Mendelian randomization and colocalization. GLIPR1 in non-classical monocytes and XBP1 in CD4+ T cells are identified as key causal genes driving immune-metabolic imbalances. Precision therapeutic candidates such as SOT-107 offer promising avenues to break the disease cycle, providing a robust foundation for future translational research in reproductive endocrinology.

HMPV Virus Outbreak: First Review in Literature
Authors: Sonia Dubey, Purushottam Lal, Nitin Kumar, Rahul Tiwari, Vinej Somaraj, Sailaja Sanda, Nirvi Sharma, Heena D. Tiwari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHuman metapneumovirus (HMPV), a pathogen within the Pneumoviridae family, is a notable contributor to respiratory tract infections worldwide. Identified in 2001, HMPV predominantly affects young children, the elderly, and individuals with compromised immune systems. The virus exhibits seasonal patterns, with infection rates typically increasing during late winter and spring. The virus spreads through respiratory droplets, leading to symptoms ranging from mild upper respiratory tract infections to severe lower respiratory illnesses such as bronchiolitis and pneumonia. Recent surges in HMPV cases, notably the 2024–2025 outbreak in China, have underscored the importance of understanding its epidemiology, transmission mechanisms, and clinical impact. Despite its clinical significance, there are currently no specific antiviral treatments or vaccines available for HMPV, making prevention strategies and supportive care essential components of management. This review provides a comprehensive overview of HMPV, including its virology, epidemiology, clinical manifestations, diagnostic methods, treatment options, and preventive measures, with a focus on recent outbreaks and their implications for public health.

INTRODUCTION

Human metapneumovirus (HMPV), first identified in 2001, has emerged as a significant cause of respiratory tract infections across all age groups.[1] As a member of the Pneumoviridae family, HMPV is closely related to respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and shares similar clinical and epidemiological characteristics.[2] It is an enveloped, negative-sense, single-stranded RNA virus that primarily targets the respiratory epithelium.[3] HMPV infections are particularly concerning in vulnerable populations such as young children, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals, where it can cause severe complications like bronchiolitis and pneumonia.[45]

The virus exhibits a clear seasonal pattern, with infections peaking during late winter and early spring, especially in temperate regions.[6] Despite its clinical significance, HMPV remains under-recognized compared to other respiratory viruses like RSV and influenza. The absence of specific antiviral therapies and vaccines underscores the need for greater awareness, enhanced diagnostic approaches, and improved public health strategies to manage its impact effectively.[78]

EPIDEMIOLOGY

HMPV is a respiratory pathogen with a global presence, impacting diverse populations worldwide. Its infections demonstrate clear seasonality, with the highest transmission rates occurring in late winter and early spring in temperate regions, similar to patterns seen with other respiratory viruses such as RSV and influenza.[34] This seasonal trend highlights the importance of timely public health measures to mitigate its spread.

Serological studies indicate that the majority of children are exposed to HMPV early in life, with approximately 90% developing antibodies by the age of five. Despite this widespread exposure, immunity to HMPV is not lifelong, and reinfections are common across all age groups, albeit with milder symptoms in healthy adults.[5] Populations at higher risk for severe disease include young children, the elderly, individuals with chronic respiratory conditions, and those with weakened immune systems.[6]

Recent outbreaks have underscored the evolving epidemiology of HMPV. The 2024–2025 surge in cases in China notably affected children under 14 years of age, sparking significant public concern. Although infection rates aligned with typical seasonal patterns, this outbreak highlighted the importance of robust surveillance systems and early intervention strategies.[7] In regions with limited diagnostic resources, accurately assessing HMPV’s prevalence remains challenging, necessitating more comprehensive global data collection efforts.[8]

Addressing these gaps requires coordinated global efforts in research and public health interventions to mitigate the virus’s impact, especially in vulnerable populations. Enhanced diagnostic capabilities and targeted preventive measures can improve outcomes and reduce the burden of HMPV-related illnesses.[9]

TRANSMISSION AND PATHOGENESIS

HMPV primarily spreads through respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs or sneezes, direct contact with contaminated surfaces, or close personal interactions. Upon entry into the host, HMPV targets epithelial cells in the upper and lower respiratory tract, leading to cell fusion and the formation of syncytia. The incubation period ranges from 3 to 6 days, with viral shedding peaking early in the course of the illness. The virus can cause both upper and lower respiratory tract infections, with severity influenced by the host’s age, immune status, and the presence of underlying health conditions.[456]

CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS

HMPV infections vary widely in presentation, ranging from asymptomatic to severe respiratory illnesses. Typical symptoms include cough, fever, nasal congestion, and difficulty breathing. In vulnerable groups such as young children, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals, HMPV may result in serious conditions like bronchiolitis and pneumonia. Potential complications include worsened asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), secondary bacterial infections, and, in severe cases, respiratory failure. Nevertheless, most patients recover within 7 to 10 days without the need for targeted antiviral therapy.[567]

DIAGNOSTIC METHODS

Laboratory diagnosis of HMPV primarily involves reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) assays, which detect viral RNA in respiratory specimens with high sensitivity and specificity. Other diagnostic methods include antigen detection tests and serological assays, though these are less commonly used due to lower sensitivity or delayed antibody response. Challenges in diagnosis arise from the clinical similarity of HMPV to other respiratory viruses, necessitating comprehensive testing to distinguish between potential pathogens.[289]

TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT

Currently, there is no specific antiviral therapy approved for HMPV infections. Management focuses on supportive care, including hydration, antipyretics for fever, and oxygen therapy for patients experiencing hypoxia. In severe cases, particularly among immunocompromised individuals, antiviral agents such as ribavirin have been used off-label, though their efficacy remains uncertain. The absence of targeted treatments highlights the need for preventive measures and further research into effective therapies.[789]

PREVENTION AND CONTROL MEASURES

Preventive strategies against HMPV emphasize general respiratory hygiene practices, such as regular handwashing, avoiding close contact with infected individuals, and disinfecting frequently touched surfaces. Currently, there is no licensed vaccine for HMPV. However, research is ongoing, with some vaccine candidates showing promise in early clinical trials. Public health measures during outbreaks focus on surveillance, early detection, and the implementation of infection control practices to mitigate transmission, especially in healthcare settings and among high-risk populations.[1011]

COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS WITH OTHER RESPIRATORY VIRUSES

HMPV shares several characteristics with other respiratory viruses, notably RSV and influenza. All three viruses can cause similar clinical symptoms, including cough, fever, and respiratory distress, and they share common transmission routes. However, unlike influenza, which has annual vaccines and specific antiviral treatments, HMPV lacks both, complicating prevention and management efforts. Additionally, while RSV and HMPV both significantly impact young children and the elderly, HMPV’s epidemiological patterns and seasonal peaks can differ, necessitating distinct public health approaches.[101112]

CONCLUSION

HMPV is a globally prevalent respiratory pathogen capable of causing a spectrum of illnesses, from mild upper respiratory infections to severe lower respiratory diseases. Recent increases in reported cases, such as those observed in China, underscore the importance of continued surveillance and research. The absence of specific antiviral treatments and vaccines highlights the need for ongoing public health efforts focused on prevention and supportive care. Future research should aim to develop targeted therapies and effective vaccines, as well as to enhance our understanding of HMPV’s transmission dynamics and pathogenesis, to better inform clinical management and public health interventions.

Salicylic acid promotes salinity stress tolerance of grapevine by enhancing photosynthesis, ionic homeostasis, and antioxidant defense
Authors: Kexin Liu, Yuan Xiao, Yajuan Cheng, Baoping Li, Shuli Dong, Xiaoxin Shi, Lisong Ma, Guoqiang Du, Li Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSalinity stress poses significant challenges to viticulture by impairing plant growth and compromising fruit quality. The stress-mitigating properties of salicylic acid (SA) are well documented across various crops. However, the ability of SA to mitigate salinity stress in grapes and its potential underlying mechanisms remain unexplored. In this study, we systematically evaluated the efficacy of SA priming with various concentrations in alleviating NaCl-induced salinity stress in ‘Shine Muscat’ (Vitis vinifera × Vitis labrusca) grape seedlings and explored the physiological and biochemical responses underlying SA-mediated salinity stress mitigation.ResultsSA pretreatment significantly alleviated salt-induced growth inhibition and leaf damage, with the most pronounced mitigation effects occurring at 1.5 mmol/L concentration. Physiological analyses revealed that SA application effectively maintained photosynthetic efficiency under salt stress, especially by downregulating the expression of the chlorophyll degradation-related gene VviPUB19. SA enhanced the antioxidant defense system by increasing the activities of key enzymes, including superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT), thereby limiting the accumulation of superoxide anion (O₂·⁻) and malondialdehyde (MDA). Concurrently, SA upregulated the expression of antioxidant-related genes (VvMAPK9, VvMKK2, VvMKK4). In addition, SA priming improved osmotic adjustment, as evidenced by increased accumulation of soluble protein (SP), proline (Pro) and soluble sugar (SS), as well as mediated ionic homeostasis by promoting the absorption of K+, Mg2+, and Ca2+ and decreasing the uptake of Na+, which was supported by elevated expression of osmoregulatory genes (VvKSC11-2, VvMYBA6, VvBES1-3) and inorganic ion transport-related genes (VaCPK21, VvNHX1). Phytohormonal profiling revealed that SA application enhanced the levels of growth-promoting hormones, including auxin (IAA), gibberellin (GA₃), and zeatin riboside (ZR).ConclusionsCollectively, our findings demonstrate that SA priming significantly enhances NaCl-induced salinity tolerance in grapevines by integrating photosynthetic machinery, antioxidant defense, osmotic balance, ionic equilibrium and hormone signaling, which provides a basis for employing SA as an effective and promising strategy to mitigate salinity stress in grapevine cultivation.

Introduction

Soil salinization has emerged as a global ecological challenge, exacerbated by climate change and the growing scarcity of freshwater resources [1, 2]. According to recent studies, approximately 50% of arable land in semi-arid and arid regions may face salinity threats by 2050, posing a serious risk to both irrigated and non-irrigated agricultural systems [3, 4], which underscores the urgent need to develop sustainable strategies for utilizing saline-alkaline soils, particularly in light of the scarcity of arable land [5, 6]. Excessive soil salinity led to a decrease in soil water potential, as well as reduced soil aeration and permeability, hindering normal plant growth, and resulting in vegetation death and community degradation [7, 8].

Grape (Vitis vinifera L.) is extensively cultivated. The industry continues to grow in China and other countries, driven by strong market demand and reliable economic returns. This expansion significantly boosts agricultural production and raises farmer incomes [9, 10]. Despite its economic importance, the sustainability of grape production faces substantial challenges from soil salinization in many regions, particularly under the dual pressures of climate change and limited arable land resources [11, 12]. In viticulture systems, salinity-induced physiological disruptions are manifested as leaf chlorosis, photosynthetic inhibition, and premature defoliation. For instance, grape rootstocks ‘3309 C’, ‘140R’ and ‘Shanhe2’ exhibit progressive leaf necrosis and curling under salt stress [13], while Vitis vinifera cultivars like ‘Cabernet Sauvignon’ demonstrate significant reductions in chlorophyll content [14]. NaCl-induced oxidative damage is evidenced by the biphasic response of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and, catalase (CAT), and peroxidase (POD) in grape rootstock, with initial activation followed by suppression under prolonged stress, accompanied by the accumulation reactive oxygen species (ROS) [15]. Furthermore, disruption of ionic homeostasis, evidenced by depressed K+/Na+, Mg2+/Na+, Ca2+/Na+ ratios across 13 grape rootstocks, has been identified as a key determinant of salt sensitivity [16].

Enhancing salinity tolerance strategies in plants mainly involves the use of salt-tolerant rootstocks, which restrict the translocation of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ to the scions while promoting the uptake of Mg²⁺, K⁺, and Ca²⁺ [16, 17]. Other strategies include soil improvement and the selection of salt-tolerant varieties. Additionally, exogenous application of plant hormones has been explored to alleviate the adverse effects of NaCl stress [18, 19]. Among various plant growth regulators, salicylic acid (SA) has attracted significant attention due to its dual role in regulating developmental processes, such as plant growth, development, maturation, and senescence, and in modulating plant stress responses through complex signaling pathways [20–23]. SA-mediated priming has been shown to enhance plant tolerance to a range of abiotic stresses, including extreme temperature [24, 25], drought [26], heavy metals [27], and salinity [28].

Emerging evidence highlights the efficacy of SA in mitigating the impacts of NaCl stress across various plant species [29]. In Campanula seedlings, SA application not only alleviates growth inhibition, including reductions in plant height, stem thickness, and branch number growth [30], but also maintains osmotic imbalance by keeping higher relative leaf water content and reducing cell membrane damage under salinity stress [31]. Similarly, SA seed pretreatment effectively mitigated drought stress damage in cantaloupe by synergistically maintaining water balance, photosynthetic efficiency, and membrane stability [32]. Additionally, SA significantly enhanced antioxidant enzyme activities and reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) content in cotton seedlings under salt stress, thereby alleviating the inhibitory effects on root growth [33]. In canola, SA mitigated salt stress by maintaining osmotic balance and enhancing both enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidants, which scavenged H₂O₂ and reduced MDA [34]. Despite these advances, the potential of SA in enhancing salt tolerance in grapevines remains underexplored, as current research on exogenous SA application in Vitis species predominantly focuses on drought and temperature stress responses. The current premier grape cultivar ‘Shine Muscat’ is particularly favored for its superior fruit quality, characterized by rich flavor, large berries, and excellent storage stability [35, 36]. Due to these traits, this variety serves as an ideal model for addressing the mechanisms of salinity stress tolerance. This study investigates how exogenous SA mitigates NaCl-induced stress in grape seedlings. The research focuses on the physiological and biochemical mechanisms underlying this protective effect. These findings provide a theoretical basis for using SA in fruit tree cultivation under saline conditions. They also offer practical strategies for sustainable viticulture on marginal lands.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The study was conducted in March 2022 at the Innovation Experimental Garden of Hebei Agricultural University (38°82′N, 115°45′E), characterized by diurnal temperature fluctuations averaging 32 °C (daytime) and 22 °C (nighttime). One-year-old branches of ‘Shine Muscat’ grape with plump buds and no pest or disease infestation were selected from the Innovation Experimental Garden of Hebei Agricultural University for cutting propagation. When the cuttings grew 1–2 leaves, the seedlings were transplanted into plastic pots of a uniform specification (top diameter: 16 × 16 cm; height: 18.5 cm), with one seedling per pot. A mixture of culture soil, perlite, and vermiculite (at a volume ratio of 3:1:1) was filled into the pots, and trays were placed under the pots to prevent water loss. All test materials were managed uniformly, and each pot was irrigated with 400 mL of Hoagland nutrient solution every 4–6 days. Seedlings displaying uniform growth (≥ 7 fully expanded leaves) were selected for experimental treatments.

Experimental design

The experiment was designed with the following treatments: CK (Control): Irrigated with water + sprayed with deionized water. ST (NaCl treatment): Irrigated with NaCl solution + sprayed with deionized water. SST1: Irrigated with 100 mmol/L NaCl + sprayed with 1.0 mmol/L SA. SST2: Irrigated with 100 mmol/L NaCl + sprayed with 1.5 mmol/L SA. SST3: Irrigated with 100 mmol/L NaCl + sprayed with 2.0 mmol/L SA. There were 21 pots per treatment. NaCl concentrations were selected based on Fu et al. [13], while SA levels followed established protocols for phytohormone application. The SA and NaCl used in this experiment both had a purity of > 99.5% (Manufacturer: China National Medicines Corporation Ltd). SA treatment was prepared by initially dissolving SA in ethanol followed by dilution to concentrations of 1, 1.5, and 2 mmol/L using distilled water containing 0.01% Tween-20.

Treatments were carried out at 16:00 on the same day. The SA solution was applied first through foliar spraying to both leaf adaxial and abaxial surfaces, immediately followed by the NaCl treatment. The CK and ST were sprayed with deionized water until the leaves were completely wet and dripping. Each pot was treated with 400 mL of NaCl solution or an equal amount of water, with the NaCl treatment recorded as the first day of stress. Subsequent SST (SA + NaCl) treatments were repeated at 5-day intervals (6 total treatments), ensuring the treatment solution was fully added without overflow and following routine management.

Plant samples were collected from the base of the plant at 3 to 6 nodes after 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 days of stress treatment. For each treatment, 3 randomly selected plants were used, with 2 leaves picked per plant, and the process was repeated 3 times. Leaf samples were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80◦C for biochemical assays. Root samples were collected from 2 randomly selected plants per treatment on the 30th day of stress, with the process triplicated. After washing with deionized water, root-related indices were measured, and daily phenotypic observations documented growth alterations.

Measurements of plant growth parameters

The shoot growth index was measured as the growth increment over 30 days. Plant height (base to the highest point under natural morphology) and stem diameter (basal thickest point, digital caliper) were recorded pre- and post-treatment (Day 0 vs. Day 30). Incremental growth of plant height and stem diameter were calculated as Δ = (Day 30 − Day 0), respectively. Leaf area, perimeter, length, and width were measured (LI-3100 C area meter, LI-COR) pre- and post-treatment and with differences calculated accordingly [37].

Washed roots on Day 30 were scanned (WinRHIZO system, Regent Instruments) to quantify length, total surface area, root volume, and average diameter for root system architecture analysis.

Determination of root activity

Root activity was assessed using the TTC (2, 3, 5-Tripheyl Tetrazolium Chloride) reduction method. We collected root samples on day 30, fresh root segments (0.5 g) were immersed in 10 mL of an equal-volume mixture of 0.4% TTC solution and phosphate buffer (pH 7.0). The tubes were incubated in darkness at 37 °C for 2 h. After incubation, the roots were blotted dry and ground in a mortar with 4 mL of ethyl acetate until the tissue turned white. The red extract was transferred quantitatively to a 25 mL volumetric flask and brought to volume with ethyl acetate. Absorbance of the solution was measured at 485 nm using a spectrophotometer [38].

Determination of photosynthesis parameters

On days 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30, net photosynthetic rate (Pn), intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci), transpiration rate (Tr), and stomatal conductance (Gs) were measured using a portable photosynthesis system (LI-6400, LI-COR Inc., Lincoln, NE, USA). Measurements were taken from fully expanded mature upper leaves of selected plants on sunny days between 9:30 to 11:30 AM, following the method of Li et al. [39]. Chlorophyll content was determined following the method described by Wellburn et al. [40]. Chlorophyll was extracted with 80% acetone. Leaf samples were cleaned, dried, and cut into approximately 0.1 cm segments. Then, 0.2 g of tissue was homogenized and extracted with 8 mL of 80% acetone in the dark at room temperature for 24 h. Absorbance was measured at 663, 645, and 470 nm.

Measurements of soluble sugar, proline and total soluble protein contents

Soluble sugar (SS) contents were determined following the protocol described by Chow et al. with minor modifications [41]. Leaf samples (0.2 g) were extracted with 5 mL of 80% ethanol solution. The mixture was centrifuged at 8000 r for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. The extraction procedure was repeated. The combined supernatant was diluted to 25 mL in a volumetric flask. Then, 0.2 mL of this extract was mixed with 1 mL distilled water and 5 mL anthrone-sulfuric acid. Absorbance was measured at 620 nm.

The concentration of Proline (Pro) in the leaves was measured according to the method described by Bates et al. [42]. 0.2 g of leaf tissue was homogenized in 5 mL of 3% sulfosalicylic acid. The proline concentration in the toluene layer was quantified by measuring absorbance at 520 nm.

Soluble protein (SP) content was determined using the Coomassie Brilliant Blue assay Bradford et al. [43]. Briefly, 0.5 g of leaf tissue was homogenized in 2 mL of phosphate buffer (pH 7.8) and centrifuged for 12 min. Then, 0.1 mL of the resulting enzyme extract was mixed with 5 mL of Coomassie Brilliant Blue solution. The absorbance was measured at 595 nm.

Inorganic ions determination

Leaves were first rinsed thoroughly with deionized water, then blanched at 105 °C for 20 min, and dried at 65 °C until a constant weight was achieved. After grinding and sieving, the samples were digested using H2SO4-H2O2 and HNO3-H2O2 methods until the solution became colorless and transparent. Potassium (K+), calcium (Ca2+), and magnesium (Mg2+) contents in the leaves were measured using an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), while the sodium (Na+) content was determined using a flame atomic absorption spectrophotometer [44].

Determination of antioxidant enzymes activities and oxidative stress biomarker contents

Leaf pretreatment followed the method for SP extraction as referenced in "Determination of photosynthesis parameters" section. This enzyme solution was used to measure SOD and CAT activities [45], as well as the content of O2·−. SOD activity was determined using the Nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) method. The reaction mixture contained 50 µmol/L phosphate buffer (pH 7.8), 65 mmol/L methionine, 500 µmol/L NBT, 100 mmol/L EDTA, and 200 µmol/L riboflavin. After exposure to light (4000 lx) for 20 min, absorbance was measured at 560 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer. CAT activity was determined by mixing 0.1 mL of extract with a reaction solution containing potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0) and H₂O₂. The absorbance at 240 nm was monitored every 30 s for 3 min.

O2·− and MDA contents were quantified following the methodology of Zhu et al. [46]. O2·−content was determined using the p-aminobenzenesulfonic acid method. The supernatant (1 mL) was reacted with 0.9 mL phosphate buffer (65 mmol/L, pH 7.8) and 0.1 mL hydroxylammonium chloride (10 mmol/L) at 25 °C for 20 min. Then, 0.5 mL of this mixture was combined with 0.5 mL 17 mmol/L sulfanilic acid and 0.5 mL 7 mmol/L α-naphthylamine. The resulting solution was extracted with 1.5 mL n-butanol, Absorbance was measured at 530 nm. MDA content was measured using the thiobarbituric acid (TBA) method. Leaf samples (0.5 g) were homogenized with 4 mL of 1% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid and centrifuged at 12,000 g for 20 min. The reaction mixture consisted of 3 mL of 20% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid containing 0.5% (w/v) TBA, which was mixed with 1 mL of the supernatant. Absorbance measurements were taken at 440, 532 and 600 nm.

Detection of endogenous hormone

Phytohormones were extracted, purified, and quantified via HPLC using a C18 column (retention time: 10 min) [47]. Hormone content was calculated using a standard curve.

Gene expression analysis

Total RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (qPCR) were conducted according to the methods described by Liang et al. and Xiang et al. [48, 49]. The EF1-α gene (GenBank accession: NC_012012) was used as the internal reference gene. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method [50].

Statistical analysis

Data processing and visualization were conducted using Microsoft Excel 2021. Statistical significance was assessed through two-way ANOVA under a completely randomized design (CRD) using DPS software (v18.10), followed by Duncan’ s new multiple range test (p < 0.05). Pearson correlation analysis and principal component analysis were performed using Origin 2024 software.

Results

SA mitigates NaCl-induced inhibition of shoot growth in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

As shown in Fig. 1, this study determined the growth difference (growth amount during the treatment period) of grapevines on the 30th day and the 1st day of treatment, and found that: compared with CK, the ST treatment significantly inhibited grape plant growth compared to CK, as exemplified by significant reduction of 5.64% in plant height, 11.01% in stem diameter, 20.95% in leaf area, 27.71% in leaf length, and 26.91% in leaf width. All SA treatments alleviated the growth inhibition of grapevines caused by ST treatment, and SST2 treatment exhibited the most pronounced effect. (Fig. 1C).

SA mitigates NaCl-induced root growth inhibition in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

As shown in Fig. 2, ST treatment severely inhibited root development, with significant decreases of 71.02% in total root length, 80.87% in root surface area, 52.08% in root volume, 43.06% in root average diameter, and 57.68% in root activity, as well as visible symptoms of NaCl damage compared to CK. However, all SA treatments effectively alleviated the NaCl-induced stress on the grapevine root systems (Fig. 2A). Among these, the SST2 treatment exhibited optimal alleviating effects, resulting in increases of 133.35% increasing in total root length 213.50% in surface area, 63.01% in root volume, 45.59% in average root diameter and 61.94% in root activity compared to ST-treated plants (Fig. 2B).

SA reverses NaCl-induced photosynthetic inhibition in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Photosynthetic pigment content, including chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotenoid, and chlorophyll t, and photosynthesis parameters (Pn, Gs, Ci, Tr) declined progressively in ST-treated plants over the course of stress. In contrast, under SA treatments, plants exhibited notably alleviated declines, especially SST2 treatment showing the most pronounced mitigation effects. From the 6th to the 30th day, the SST2 treatment significantly restored the photosynthetic pigment content compared to ST-treated plants. Specifically, it increased the chlorophyll a content by 51.34%, 21.13%, 45.00%, 83.16%, and 36.94%; the chlorophyll b content by 50.84%, 23.03%, 91.43%, 195.99%, and 18.02%; the carotenoid content by 39.73%, 17.54%, 72.37% and 47.06%; and the chlorophyll t content by 50.60%, 13.28%, 69.25%, 93.67%, and 15.59%, respectively (Fig. 3A). Concurrently, the SST2 treatment enhanced the photosynthetic parameters: Pn increased by 65.02%, 88.29%, 45.34%, 149.47%, and 72.95%; Gs increased by 156.76%, 60.61%, 57.62%, 96.53%, and 64.43%. Furthermore, on the 6th and 12th days, Tr was also restored, increasing by 212.15% and 121.14%, respectively (Fig. 3B).

SA enhances antioxidant defense and reduces oxidative damage in NaCl-induced grapevine

During the 12th to 30th days, the ST treatment significantly increased SOD activity by 19.67%, 6.56%, 20.77%, and 11.31% (Fig. 4A) and CAT activity by 46.67%, 133.33%, 60.00%, and 32.14% (Fig. 4B) relative to CK. Exogenous SA application further enhanced these activities, with the SST2 treatment exhibiting strongest effects. During the 6th to 30th days, compared with the ST treatment, the SOD activity under the SST2 treatment increased by 23.38%, 21.61%, 16.46%, 14.18%, and 19.97% respectively, while the CAT activity increased by 62.07%, 63.64%, 65.08%, 87.50%, and 54.05% respectively.

Conversely, during the 6th to 30th days of treatment, the ST treatment markedly elevated oxidative stress markers, increasing superoxide anion (O₂·⁻) and MDA content by up to 149.59%, 216.52%, 147.01%, 175.19%, and 194.62% (Fig. 4C) and 48.27%, 18.68%, 57.06%, 44.13%, and 85.22% (Fig. 4D), respectively, compared to CK. SA restores osmotic and ionic homeostasis in NaCl-stressed ‘Shine Muscat’ Grapevine

On the 6th, 12th, 18th, 24th, and 30th days of treatment. Compared to CK, ST treatment induced significant osmotic stress responses, resulting in 52.04%, 12.02%, 94.45%, 132.06% and 147.30% increases in SS content and 26.15%, 30.18%, 31.07%, 58.01% and 110.87% elevations in Pro levels, but a marked decrease of 57.81% in SP on the 30th day of treatment (Fig. 5A). ST treatment also severely disrupted ionic homeostasis, exhibiting a significant increase in Na+ by 79.30%, 186.65%, 319.22%, 487.31% and 651.08%; with significant decreases in K+ content by 22.71%, 25.33%, 31.72%, 22.71% and 28.87% ;Mg2+ by 10.84%, 5.80%, 4.28%, 6.47% and 16.54%; Ca2+ by 13.70%, 11.13%, 19.47%, 25.68% and 27.72%; and the K+/N+ ratio by 58.4% ,74.10%, 83.67%, 86.73% and 90.70% compared to CK (Fig. 5B). Application of SA treatments, most effectively SST2, counteracted these deleterious ST effects and restored homeostasis. Compared to ST-treated plants, SST2 significantly increased SS by 94.67%, 43.88%, 32.28%, 25.13% and 44.17%; Pro by 63.13%, 46.67%, 42.40%, 49.86% and 23.38%, and notably reversed the ST-induced SP loss by markedly elevating SP levels by 60.84%, 67.35%, 79.42%, 52.60% and 203.39% (Fig. 5A). SST2 also effectively mitigated Na⁺ accumulation and promoted the uptake/retention of beneficial cations: K⁺ content increased by 94.00%, 138.80%, 180.42%, 123.39% and 158.97%; Mg²⁺ by 24.27%, 38.60%, 12.57%, 18.60% and 39.97%; Ca²⁺ by 33.76%, 50.72%, 38.16%, 49.00% and 90.09% (Fig. 5B), thereby significantly restoring the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio.

SA coordinates stress-responsive gene expression to alleviate NaCl damage in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

ST treatment suppressed the antioxidant gene VvMYBA6, while upregulating VvKSC11-2 and VvBES1-3, activating osmotic regulation-related genes (VvMAPK9, VvMKK2, VvMKK4) and ion transport-related genes (VvNHX1, VaCPK21), and elevating the expression of VviPUB19, a known negative regulator of chlorophyll content, thereby exacerbating photosynthetic inhibition. In contrast, SA treatments counteracted ST-induced dysregulation by enhancing the expression of VvKSC11-2 and VvBES1-3 beyond ST-induced levels, while reversing VvMYBA6 suppression, and synergistically amplifying osmotic regulators (VvMAPK9, VvMKK2, VvMKK4) and ion transporters (VvNHX1, VaCPK21) to levels exceeding those observed at ST treatment. Moreover, SA suppressed the upregulation of VviPUB19 to mitigate chlorophyll loss. Notably, concentration-dependent efficacy was observed, with SST2 specifically potentiating the expression of ion transport-related genes VvNHX1 and VaCPK21 to enhance Na⁺ sequestration, while SST3 broadly intensified antioxidant gene expression (Fig. 7).

Correlation and principal component analysis reveals SA-mediated network rewiring underlying NaCl stress alleviation in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Discussion

Soil salinization has emerged as a significant global ecological concern [51, 52], adversely affecting grapevine growth by increasing soil osmotic potential and impairing water and nutrient uptake. This leads to growth inhibition, leaf wilting, and mortality, ultimately threatening the sustainable development of the grape industry [53, 54]. While exogenous SA has been established as a potent priming agent for plant defense against abiotic stresses [30, 55], its physiological mechanisms in the commercially important grape cultivar ‘Shine Muscat’ remain inadequately characterized. Our results demonstrate that SA application alleviates NaCl stress in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevines through coordinated physiological and molecular responses. Notably, the restoration of endogenous growth-promoting phytohormones (IAA, GA₃, and ZR) represents a central mechanism underlying the reversal of salt-induced growth suppression. This hormonal recovery occurred concomitantly with enhanced photosynthetic performance and activated antioxidant defenses, collectively mitigating oxidative and ionic stress. At the molecular level, these physiological improvements were associated with the regulation of key stress-responsive genes, revealing an integrated mechanism through which SA ameliorates salt stress-induced growth inhibition in grapevines. These results provide a crucial theoretical and practical foundation for enhancing NaCl tolerance and exploring the cultivation potential of ‘Shine Muscat’ grapes in saline environments.

Exogenous SA mitigates NaCl stress-induced growth impairment in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

NaCl stress consistently induces detrimental growth responses in grapevines, including leaf shrinkage, wilting, abscission, and reduced growth across various rootstocks [56]. This study confirms that ‘Shine Muscat’ seedlings exhibit significant growth inhibition under NaCl stress, manifested as adverse effects on multiple growth parameters. Crucially, foliar SA application effectively alleviated this stress-induced inhibition, enhancing both aboveground and belowground growth. This growth-promoting role of SA aligns with findings in diverse plant species under salt stress. For instance, SA enhanced plant height and leaf area in salt-stressed cotton [57], improved shoot growth, leaf expansion, and root morphology in lettuce [58], and attenuated root growth inhibition in cucumber [59, 60]. Therefore, SA may alleviate the salt stress suffered by grapevines themselves by reducing the degree of root growth inhibition, promoting the growth of aboveground stems and leaves, and simultaneously regulating changes in various physiological mechanisms.

Exogenous SA ameliorates NaCl stress-induced photosynthetic impairment in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Photosynthesis, one of the most crucial physiological processes in plant carbon assimilation, is significantly impaired by NaCl stress [61, 62]. Kumar et al. demonstrated that NaCl stress reduces photosynthetic capacity, potentially through stomatal closure to minimize water loss during cellular dehydration, which subsequently diminishes chlorophyll synthesis and accelerates its degradation [63]. This study revealed that salt stress decreased chlorophyll content, Gs, and Ci, while significantly reducing Pn and Tr (stomatal limitation factors). Prolonged stress exposure exceeded the self-regulatory capacity of grapevines, leading to reduced CO₂ fixation and metabolic imbalance, manifested as persistently declining Pn alongside markedly elevated Ci (non-stomatal limitation factors).

Foliar SA application effectively alleviated the NaCl-induced suppression of Pn, Gs, and Tr. SA treatment enhanced Pn and Gs in cherry rootstocks under salt stress [64]. This aligns with findings in melon seedlings, where SA differentially enhanced Pn, Tr, and Gs under salt stress [54]. Furthermore, SA treatment attenuated chlorophyll degradation during later stress phases at all concentrations tested, with 1.5 mmol/L SA maintaining photosynthetic pigment content near control levels even during initial stress exposure. This protective effect on pigments is consistent with reports in Platycodon grandifloras [31] and Zinnia elegans [65].

Our results demonstrate that SA downregulates the expression of VviPUB19, a gene implicated in the negative regulation of photosynthetic pigment content. Under NaCl stress alone, VviPUB19 expression was upregulated, correlating with chlorophyll loss. SA treatment counteracted this upregulation, providing a key molecular explanation for the observed preservation of chlorophyll levels. Collectively, the study revealed that SA alleviates the non-stomatal limitation effects of photosynthesis in ‘Shine Muscat’ grape leaves under NaCl stress through multiple regulatory mechanisms. These include downregulating VviPUB19 expression, slowing photosynthetic pigment degradation, inducing stomatal opening to enhance Tr, and reducing Ci. Overall, our findings reveal that SA mitigates salt-induced photosynthetic limitations in ‘Shine Muscat’ through multiple coordinated actions: (1) downregulating VviPUB19 to slow chlorophyll degradation, (2) promoting stomatal opening (increased Gs and Tr), (3) reducing Ci accumulation, thereby alleviating non-stomatal constraints, and ultimately (4) restoring Pn.

Exogenous SA potentiates antioxidant defense in NaCl-stressed ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Plants alleviate oxidative stress by upregulating antioxidant enzyme activities, thereby reducing the accumulation of oxidative biomarkers [66]. In this study, NaCl stress increased O₂·⁻ and MDA accumulation in ‘Shine Muscat’ leaves—indicating severe oxidative damage—it concurrently induced moderate increases in SOD and CAT activities, which is consistent with NaCl stress induced antioxidant response in Camelina sativa [67]. Critically, SA application dramatically amplified this: all tested SA concentrations significantly boosted SOD and CAT activities beyond stress-induced levels, while effectively suppressing O₂·⁻ and MDA accumulation. This finding is consistent with studies in grapes cv. ‘Sultana’ demonstrating SA-mediated attenuation of oxidative biomarkers and enhanced antioxidant enzyme activities under salt stress [68, 69].

Mechanistically, SA further upregulated the expression of key antioxidant signaling genes VvMAPK9, VvMKK2, and VvMKK4—transcripts already elevated by NaCl stress alone. This synergistic amplification of MAPK cascade components provides a molecular basis for the observed enhancement of SOD and CAT activities, demonstrating SA’s role in potentiating the antioxidant signaling network. This mechanism effectively enhances ROS scavenging capacity and reduces the accumulation of O2·- and MDA in grape plants under NaCl stress, ultimately alleviating oxidative damage in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevines.

Exogenous SA maintained osmotic and ionic homeostasis in NaCl-stressed ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Osmotic regulation and ion homeostasis are critical determinants of plant NaCl tolerance [63]. The results demonstrated that Pro and SS accumulated under NaCl stress, whereas SP content declined during later stages, likely due to stress-induced proteolysis acceleration. Crucially, SA application significantly augmented the accumulation of leaf Pro, SS, and SP under NaCl stress. This enhancement of osmotic adjustment capacity aligns with findings in alfalfa [70] and tomato [29] under salt stress. Notably, SA treatment further upregulated the expression of key Pro biosynthesis-related genes (VvMYBA6, VvBES1-3, and VvKSC11-2) beyond levels induced by NaCl stress alone. This synergistic amplification of gene expression provides a molecular basis for the observed potentiation of osmotic regulator accumulation.

Concurrently, SA played a vital role in rebalancing ionic fluxes. Prolonged NaCl exposure led to progressive Na+ accumulation accompanied by decreased K+, Mg2+, and Ca2+ content, resulting in reduced K+/Na+ ratios. SA application effectively reversed this trend, reducing leaf Na⁺ content while increasing K⁺, Mg²⁺, and Ca²⁺ levels, thereby improving K⁺/Na⁺ ratios. This ion-homeostatic function of SA was further demonstrated in maize seedlings, where it regulated the absorption and distribution of essential ions such as Na⁺ and K⁺ to maintain ionic equilibrium under saline conditions [71]. These results are also consistent with findings in ‘Qarah Shani’ and ‘Thompson Seedless’ grapevines where SA promoted K⁺/Na⁺ balance and cation retention under salt stress, demonstrating its consistent role in mitigating ion toxicity [72].

The molecular underpinnings of this ionic regulation involve SA-mediated amplification of ion transporter gene expression. Specifically, the expression of VaCPK21 and VvNHX1 was significantly higher under SA treatment compared to NaCl stress alone, suggesting their active participation in SA-facilitated ion homeostasis. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that SA likely alleviates NaCl stress through multiple mechanisms: reducing Na+ accumulation-induced damage to cell membrane permeability, maintaining optimal osmotic potential, and restoring ion homeostasis.

Exogenous SA enhanced endogenous growth hormone levels in NaCl-stressed ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine

Researchers generally consider GA3, IAA, and ZR as growth-promoting phytohormones [73, 74]. Consistent with the widespread inhibitory effect of NaCl stress on hormone synthesis, NaCl stress in this study significantly suppressed the accumulation of GA₃, IAA, and ZR in ‘Shine Muscat’ seedlings. This aligns with reports of concurrent inhibition of IAA, GA₃, and ZR synthesis by salt stress in other species, such as Juglans macrocarpa [75]. Crucially, foliar SA application substantially reversed this NaCl-induced suppression, significantly enhancing the accumulation of IAA, GA₃, and ZR. This growth hormone-restoring effect of SA corroborates findings in Blumea balsamifera [76], where SA promoted the accumulation of IAA, GA₃, and ZR in both young and mature leaves. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that SA effectively counteracts the NaCl stress-induced decline in endogenous growth hormone levels, thereby mitigating NaCl-induced physiological damage and promoting the growth of both the aboveground and underground parts of grapevines ("Exogenous SA mitigates NaCl stress-induced growth impairment in ‘Shine Muscat’ grapevine" section).

Conclusion

In conclusion, as schematically illustrated in Fig. 10, Exogenous SA effectively mitigates NaCl stress-induced damage and promotes stress resilience in ‘Shine Muscat’ grape seedlings, with optimal efficacy at 1.5 mmol/L. This protection is achieved through four interlinked and coordinated mechanisms: (1) preserving photosynthetic function by maintaining chlorophyll integrity and optimizing gas exchange, (2) restoring growth hormone homeostasis (IAA, GA₃, ZR), (3) stabilizing osmotic and ionic equilibrium through regulated osmolyte accumulation and ion transporter activation, and (4) potentiating antioxidant capacity via amplified ROS-scavenging gene expression. The principal component analysis results in Fig. 9 confirmed the coordination of these SA-driven responses. Critically, correlation analysis (Fig. 8) revealed that SA-induced hormone elevation directly stimulates photosynthetic efficiency, which influenced ion homeostasis regulation, osmotic adjustment, and antioxidant enzyme potentiation—collectively minimizing oxidative damage (O₂·⁻, MDA) and effectively reversing NaCl-induced growth impairment.

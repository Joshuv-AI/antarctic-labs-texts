DHCR7 Promotes Liver Metastasis of Pancreatic Cancer Through PI3K‐Akt Signaling Pathway
Authors: Yu Xie, Ziyue Zhou, Jiangwei Song, Chengzhi Wang, Yibing Ding, Yudong Qiu, Lei Fang, Hao Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPancreatic cancer (PC) is associated with dismal clinical outcomes, largely due to the high prevalence of liver metastasis (LM) at diagnosis or post‐resection. Despite its clinical significance, the molecular drivers of LM in PC remain poorly characterized, and few validated biomarkers or therapeutic targets are currently available. Our study identifies 7‐dehydrocholesterol reductase (DHCR7), a terminal enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis, as significantly upregulated in PC tissues and closely correlated with LM progression. In vitro experiments demonstrated that DHCR7 enhances the proliferation, invasion, and migration ability in PC cells, and in vivo experiments demonstrated that DHCR7 promotes LM of PC. The mechanism of DHCR7 promoting LM may be mediated by elevating cholesterol synthesis of PC cells and then activating the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway. Taken together, our findings uncover a novel molecular mechanism underlying LM in PC and highlight DHCR7 as a possible predictive biomarker or interventional target.

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC), a highly aggressive malignancy of the digestive system, is associated with a strikingly low 5‐year survival rate of only 13% post‐diagnosis [1]. It is estimated that PC will rise to the second leading cause of cancer‐related death by 2030 [2]. PC develops rapidly, but the onset of the disease is insidious, and the early symptoms are not typical [3]. Most patients have been diagnosed as advanced stage or distant metastasis [4]. The high metastasis rate and high postoperative recurrence rate are one of the main reasons for the poor prognosis and high mortality rate of PC. The liver is the most common metastatic site of PC, and about 70% of patients with primary tumor resection relapse with LM within 2 years after operation [5]. Understanding the pathogenesis of LM of PC is of great clinical significance for early diagnosis, providing new targets for treatment, and improving prognosis.

7‐dehydrocholesterol reductase (DHCR7) is an enzyme that facilitates the final conversion in cholesterol biosynthesis, specifically reducing the C7‐C8 double bond of 7‐dehydrocholesterol (7‐DHC) to generate cholesterol [6]. It has been reported that DHCR7 could affect cellular cholesterol homeostasis in tumor cells and then lead to cancer growth and metastasis [7]. Furthermore, DHCR7 has been found to reduce the concentration of 7‐DHC and elevate cholesterol content in lipid rafts, which is crucial in bladder cancer invasion and metastasis [8]. DHCR7 facilitates lymph node metastasis in cervical cancer by driving cholesterol reprogramming, which subsequently activates the KANK4/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway [9]. A recent study showed that abnormal cholesterol metabolism was common in PC [10]. However, the association between DHCR7 and LM of PC remains unclear.

In this study, through quantitative proteomics analysis we found that DHCR7 was highly expressed in PC patients with postoperative early recurrence of LM. And then we constructed PC cell models with knockdown or overexpression of DHCR7 to investigate the regulatory effects and molecular mechanisms of DHCR7 on proliferation, migration, and invasion ability in PC cells. We also constructed an animal model of LM to verify the effect of DHCR7 in vivo and explore the mechanism of DHCR7 regulating PC of LM by RNA‐seq. Taken together, the data from our study identify that DHCR7 can promote LM of PC and may provide a new diagnostic and prognostic marker for LM of PC.

Materials and Methods

Clinical Samples

Paired para‐tumor tissues and PC tissues were collected from 14 PC patients (including 7 patients who developed LM within 6 months after surgery and 7 patients who did not develop LM for at least 1 year after surgery). The presence or absence of LM and the time to recurrence of LM after surgery in all patients with PC were determined by a structured follow‐up protocol including contrast‐enhanced computed tomography of the abdomen and thorax every 6 months after the operation. The 14 patients' clinical information was presented in Table S1. All patients who participated in this study provided written informed consent.

Quantitative Proteomics Analysis

50 mg samples were lysed in 500 μL RIPA buffer, homogenized, and incubated on ice for 30 min. After centrifugation (14,000 × g, 20 min), supernatants were adjusted to 1 mg/mL via BCA assay. Then 50 μg protein was reduced (50 mM TCEP, 55°C, 1 h), alkylated (50 mM MMTs, RT, dark, 30 min), desalted, and digested with trypsin (1:50, 0.5 M TEAB, 50 μL final volume). SWATH‐DIA analysis used a Zeno TOF7600 mass spectrometer coupled with an ACQUITY UPLC system. Peptides (0.5 μg/μL, 4 μL injected) were separated on a CNW column. Raw data were processed with DIA‐NN (FDR < 1%). Unspecified reagents were from Sigma‐Aldrich.

Bioinformatics Analysis

Differentially expressed proteins were subjected to REACTOME enrichment analysis via the STRING database (https://string‐db.org), with results plotted as a bubble diagram using R's ggplot2 (v3.5.2). Principal component analysis (PCA) plots, volcano plots, and heatmaps were generated using ggplot2 (v3.5.1). The data of different expression of DHCR7 in PC and normal pancreatic tissue was available on the TCGA website (https://cancergenome.nih.gov/); the GSE151580 data was accessed through the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/); the prognostic data of PC patients with different DHCR7 expression was available in the Kaplan–Meier Plotter (https://www.kmplot.com/analysis/).

Immunochemistry Staining (IHC)

Following fixation, paraffin embedding, microtome sectioning, and xylene‐based deparaffinization, tissue specimens underwent endogenous peroxidase blockade with 3% H2O2 and nonspecific binding inhibition via 5% BSA. Sections were subsequently probed with anti‐DHCR7 (ab226784, 1:1000 dilution, Abcam, UK) under 4°C overnight incubation. After thorough PBS washes, horseradish peroxidase (HRP)‐conjugated goat anti‐rabbit IgG secondary antibodies (Jackson ImmunoResearch) were applied at ambient temperature for 60 min. Chromogenic visualization was achieved using 3,3′‐diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate followed by nuclear counterstaining with Mayer's hematoxylin. Quantitative assessment of immunoreactivity intensity was executed through ImageJ.

Knockdown and Overexpression of DHCR7 in PC Cells

Panc02 and PANC‐1 cell lines were infected with the DHCR7 shRNA lentivirus. DHCR7 shRNA lentiviruses DHCR7‐1: CGTGATTGACTTCTTCTGGAA and DHCR7‐2: GCCCACCATCATCTTCGACAA were acquired from Genechem (Shanghai, China). Puromycin (2 μg/mL) was used to screen the positively infected cells for 5 days. MIAPaCa‐2 cell lines were infected with the DHCR7 overexpression plasmid constructed by Genscript (Nanjing, China) to overexpress DHCR7 using Lipofectamine 3000 (L3000015, Invitrogen, USA).

Western Blot Analysis

Following ice‐cold RIPA buffer lysis (AR0105, Beyotime, China) which contains Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (HY‐K0011, MCE, USA), PMSF (HY‐B0496, MCE, USA) and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail I (HY‐K0021, MCE, USA), cells were incubated for 10 min, while tissues for 30 min on ice. Protein lysates were resolved via 10% SDS‐PAGE and electroblotted onto PVDF membranes. A specific primary antibody was used to incubate the membrane overnight at 4°C. The antibodies employed in this study are detailed below: anti‐β‐actin (AC026, ABclonal, China); anti‐DHCR7 (ab226784, Abcam, UK); anti‐Vimentin (A2584, ABclonal, China); anti‐E‐cadherin (A3044, ABclonal, China); anti‐N‐cadherin (A3045, ABclonal, China); anti‐Snail (C15D3, CST, USA); anti‐Bc1‐2 (12789‐1‐AP, Proteintech, China); anti‐BAX (50599‐2‐Ig, Proteintech, China) anti‐PI3K (A0265, ABclonal, China); anti‐p‐Akt (80455‐1‐RR, Proteintech, China) and anti‐Akt (10176‐2‐AP, Proteintech, China).

CCK‐8 Assay

Cell proliferation was evaluated using a CCK‐8 kit (BMU106, Abbkine, China) at 1, 2, 3, and 4 days post‐seeding PC cells into 96‐well plates at a density of 2000 cells per well.

Colony Formation Assay

6‐well plates were used to seed 2000 PC cells per well, and PC cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS for a period of 14 days. Then a 0.4% crystal violet solution dissolved in methanol was used to fix and subsequently stain the cell colonies. Following staining, the wells were thoroughly rinsed three times with PBS to eliminate residual dye, and then scanned for further analysis.

Transwell Migration and Invasion Assays

PC cells were seeded into the upper chambers at a density of 4 × 104 cells per chamber and then cultured in serum‐free basal medium, while DMEM containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. The upper chamber was pre‐coated with Matrigel (356234, Corning, USA) in the invasion assay. Following a 24‐h incubation, PC cells that had crossed the upper chambers were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and then stained with crystal violet for 1 h.

Wound Healing Assay

PC cells were plated in 6‐well plates and grown to at least 90% cell density least. A pipette tip was used to create a consistent wound in the cell monolayer, and the initial wound area was photographed under a microscope. After replacing the medium with serum‐free conditions, the wound area was imaged at specified time points.

Animal Experiments

Female C57BL/6 mice aged 6 weeks (Ziyuan Laboratory Animal Technology Co. Ltd., China) were utilized for the implantation procedure. Panc02 cells (1 × 106 cells per mouse) were injected into the spleen. After 30 days, the mice were humanely sacrificed, and the following parameters were recorded: body weight, liver weight, and the number of metastatic nodules in the liver.

RNA‐Seq

RNA‐sequencing analyses were conducted to compare three DHCR7 knockdown PANC‐1 cell samples with three control samples. Total RNA was used as the starting material for library construction, which was performed using the KCTM mRNA Library Prep Kit (Seqhealth Tech. Co. Ltd., Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. PCR products with sizes ranging from 200 to 500 base pairs were isolated and enriched. The resulting libraries were quantified and subjected to paired‐end sequencing (PE150) on a DNBSEQ‐T7 platform (MGI).

Quantitative Real‐Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT‐PCR)

Total RNA from PC cells was extracted by RNA isolater Total RNA Extraction Reagent (R401‐1‐AA, Vazyme, China) and transcribed to cDNA by HiScript III qRT SuperMix (R323‐01‐AC, Vazyme, China). Subsequently, qRT‐PCR was conducted using ChamQ Blue Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q312‐02‐AA, Vazyme, China) on the Viia 7 PCR system (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA). The primer sequences were as follows: PI3K‐F: CCTGGAAGAC‐CCAATGTTGAAG, PI3K‐R: CGGGACCATACACATCCCAT; ACTIN‐F: CATGTACGTTGCTATCCAGGC, ACTIN‐R: CTCCTTAATGTCACGCACGAT.

7‐DHC and Cholesterol Measurement

7‐DHC detection kit (KALANG, KL‐7‐DHC‐Hu) was used to measure the 7‐DHC levels. Free Cholesterol (FC) Content Assay Kit (Solarbio, bc1895) was used to measure the FC levels, and Total Cholesterol (TC) Content Assay Kit (Solarbio, bc1985) was used to measure the TC levels.

Statistical Analysis

The data are expressed as mean ± SD, derived from a minimum of three samples in animal studies and three independent cell experiments. Statistical analyses were performed using Student's t‐test or one‐way ANOVA, as applicable. Significance levels were set at *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

Results

DHCR7 Is Associated With LM in PC and Predicts Poor Prognosis

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive quantitative proteomics analysis to identify stably dysregulated proteins present in cancer tissues with LM compared to those without LM (Figure 1). The PCA results revealed a significant difference between the two groups, highlighting the distinct molecular characteristics associated with metastasis compared to non‐metastasis (Figure 2A). In order to further investigate the differential expression of proteins, we set specific thresholds for our analysis, using |FoldChange| = 1.5 and a p value of less than 0.05. The volcano plot showed 136 upregulated proteins and 87 downregulated proteins (Figure 2B). To better understand the functional implications of the differentially expressed proteins, we utilized a heatmap to characterize and visualize the expression patterns of these proteins (Figure 2C). Subsequently, we performed an enrichment analysis on the upregulated proteins to identify pathways that they may be involved in. In the REACTOME database, our analysis revealed that the metastasis group was significantly enriched in the pathway related to Metabolism and the activation of gene expression by SREBF (Figure 2D). Additionally, when we ranked the proteins based on the magnitude of their |Fold Change|, we found that DHCR7 ranked sixth among those with the most pronounced differential expression (Figure 2E). We also observed that DHCR7 exhibited a marked up‐regulation in the metastasis group compared to the non‐metastasis group (Figure 2F). Furthermore, we found DHCR7, which is an enzyme involved in metabolism, was significantly associated with the prognosis of PC patients. Therefore, we selected DHCR7 for next research.

Subsequently, we investigated the expression of DHCR7 and its clinical significance in human tissues. By analyzing PC samples and normal pancreatic tissues from the TCGA database, we observed that DHCR7 mRNA levels were significantly upregulated in tumor tissues compared to normal pancreas tissues (Figure 3A). In addition, in a GSEA dataset of PC tissues and paired liver metastases, we found that the expression of DHCR7 was significantly elevated in liver metastases compared to primary lesions (Figure 3B). Furthermore, analysis of TCGA data using Kaplan–Meier survival curves and log‐rank tests demonstrated that PC patients with high DHCR7 expression had significantly reduced disease‐free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) (Figure 3C,D). IHC analysis further demonstrated that DHCR7 protein levels were markedly elevated in PC tissues of the metastasis group compared to the non‐metastasis group (Figure 3E,F). These findings collectively indicate that DHCR7 may play a critical role in the LM of PC.

DHCR7 Enhances EMT and the Proliferation Ability of PC Cells

Based on the expression level of DHCR7 in PC cells, we chose the high expression PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells for knockdown and the low expression MIAPaCa‐2 cells for overexpression. The knockdown and overexpression efficiency were validated by Western blot (Figure 4A,B and Figure S1A). Given the critical role of epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT) in malignant features of tumor cell [11], we further examined EMT‐related markers. Western blot analysis demonstrated that DHCR7 knockdown markedly decreased the levels of mesenchymal markers (Vimentin, Snail and N‐cadherin) while upregulating the epithelial marker E‐cadherin in PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells (Figure 4A,B). However, when DHCR7 was overexpressed, it resulted in the downregulation of the epithelial marker and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers in MIAPaCa‐2 cells (Figure S1A). These results indicate that DHCR7 enhances EMT in PC cells.

(B) Western blot analysis of DHCR7 and EMT markers in Panc02 cells transfected with the shRNA‐lentivirus (NC, DHCR7‐1 and DHCR7‐2). (C) Growth curve of the shNC and shDHCR7 PANC‐1 cells. (D) Growth curve of the shNC and shDHCR7 Panc02 cells. (E) Representative colony formation images of the PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells. (F) Quantification of colony number of the PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells. ***p < 0.001.

The CCK‐8 assays manifested that DHCR7 knockdown markedly attenuated the proliferation of PC cells, whereas the overexpression of DHCR7 facilitated the proliferation (Figure 4C,D and Figure S1B). Colony formation assays demonstrated that DHCR7 knockdown significantly decreased colony‐forming efficiency, whereas the overexpression of DHCR7 increased colony‐forming efficiency (Figure 4E,F and Figure S1C,D). We then examined apoptosis‐related markers, and Western blot results revealed that DHCR7 knockdown inhibited the expression of the anti‐apoptosis marker Bcl‐2 while promoting the expression of the pro‐apoptosis marker BAX in PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells (Figure S1E,F). However, overexpression of DHCR7 led to the opposite results in MIAPaCa‐2 cells (Figure S1G). These results suggest that DHCR7 may activate the proliferation ability of PC cells through inhibiting their apoptosis.

DHCR7 Enhances the Migration and Invasion Ability of PC Cells

To explore the function of DHCR7 in the migration and invasion of PC cells, which are critical for tumor metastasis, we conducted Transwell migration, invasion, and wound healing assays. The results indicated that the knockdown of DHCR7 significantly impaired the migration and invasion capabilities of PC cells, whereas the overexpression group showed enhanced migration and invasive capabilities compared to the vector group (Figure 5A–H and Figure S2A–D). These results suggest the positive role of DHCR7 in vitro in tumor metastasis.

Knockdown of DHCR7 Restrains the Germinate of LM Tumor In Vivo

We then investigated the effect of DHCR7 on LM of PC by intrasplenic injection of Panc02 cells transfected with lentivirus‐DHCR7‐shRNA in C57BL/6 mice (Figure 6A,B). In vivo LM model results demonstrated that after 4 weeks of Panc02 cells injection, the mice in DHCR7 knockdown groups had reduced hepatic tumor burden (lower liver weight and less liver metastatic nodules) without affecting the body weight of the mice compared with the control group (Figure 6C–H). Collectively, these findings demonstrate the promoting role of DHCR7 in LM of PC in vivo.

DHCR7 Modulates the PI3K‐Akt Signaling Pathway Through Cholesterol Metabolism

To explore the mechanism of DHCR7 regulating LM of PC, we took three DHCR7 knockdown PANC‐1 cell samples and three control samples for RNA‐seq analysis. PCA analysis was performed to further characterize the correlation of gene expression levels between the two groups of samples (Figure 7A). We then screened differentially expressed genes between the two groups. Compared to the shNC group, the shDHCR7 group exhibited 1445 upregulated and 458 downregulated genes (Figure 7B). Next, we performed a cluster analysis of these differentially expressed genes using heat maps (Figure 7C), and focused on KEGG pathway analysis and GO enrichment of downregulated genes in the shDHCR7 group (Figure 7D,E). The results showed that the downregulated genes of the shDHCR7 group were significantly enriched in the pathways related to the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway and sterol biosynthesis, and GO analysis also showed that these downregulated genes were involved in biological processes such as the sterol biosynthesis process and regulation of the cholesterol biosynthesis process.

RNA‐Seq analysis of shDHCR7 and shNC PANC‐1 cells. (A) PCA plots showed the scores of the two cohorts. (B) Volcano map of differentially expressed genes. (C) Heat maps of differentially expressed genes. (D) KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of down‐regulated genes in shDHCR7 group. (E) GO enrichment analysis of down‐regulated genes in shDHCR7 group.

The GSEA analysis of our proteomics also reveals that the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway was activated in the metastasis group (Figure 8A). Subsequently, we further confirmed the alterations in the mRNA expression levels of PI3K by qRT‐PCR and observed that PI3K was positively correlated with DHCR7 (Figure 8B and Figure S3A). We then verified the markers on the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway by Western blot and observed that DHCR7 knockdown reduced the expression levels of PI3K and p‐Akt in both PANC‐1 and Panc02 cells (Figure 8C,D). In contrast, overexpression of DHCR7 activated the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway in MIAPaCa‐2 cells (Figure S3B). In addition, we observed that the expression of PI3K and p‐Akt was also downregulated in the tumor of the LM model of the two shDHCR7 groups compared with the shNC group (Figure 8E). These results suggest the positive role of DHCR7 in regulating the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway.

As the terminal enzyme in cholesterol synthesis, DHCR7 converts 7‐DHC to cholesterol and bridges lipid metabolism to oncogenesis, with cholesterol itself acting as a tumor‐promoting metabolite [12, 13]. Next, we explored whether the regulatory role of DHCR7 is dependent on cholesterol synthesis. ELISA demonstrated that DHCR7 knockdown effectively elevates the enrichment of 7‐DHC in PC cells (Figure 8F,G). We further used 2 cholesterol detection kits to quantify total and free cholesterol levels in PC cells. As expected, DHCR7 knockdown reduced the cholesterol levels, whereas the overexpression of DHCR7 facilitated cholesterol synthesis, which indicates that DHCR7 knockdown could block the conversion of 7‐DHC to cholesterol (Figure 8H,I and Figure S3C,D). In addition, we found that the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway in the shDHCR7 group was reactivated upon exogenous replenishment of cholesterol (Figure 8J). Taken together, these results suggest that DHCR7 modulates the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway through cholesterol metabolism.

The PI3K Inhibitor LY294002 Inhibits EMT, Proliferation, Migration, and Invasion Ability of PC Cells

We selected LY294002, a broad‐spectrum inhibitor of PI3K, to treat PC cells, and Western blot results showed that LY294002 treatment markedly inhibited the EMT process (Figure 9A). CCK‐8 assays and colony formation assays demonstrated that LY294002 suppressed the proliferation ability of PC cells (Figure 9B–D). In addition, wound healing assays and Transwell experiments revealed that LY294002 possesses the ability to inhibit the migration and invasion of PC cells (Figure 9E–H). The aforementioned results indicate that the PI3K inhibitor LY294002 is capable of effectively suppressing the malignant biological properties of PC cells.

Discussion

PC is a highly lethal disease projected to become the second most common cause of cancer‐related mortality by 2030 in the United States [14]. A major factor contributing to its poor prognosis is that over 90% of cases are diagnosed at advanced stages after they have spread beyond the pancreas, with systemic metastases present in more than 50% of patients [15]. This underscores the critical need for early detection strategies to diagnose PC prior to its spread beyond the pancreas, as well as more effective therapeutic strategies to eliminate cancer cells post‐metastasis [16]. LM is a critical stage in the progression of PC, a major challenge in oncological treatment and a significant cause of poor prognosis and tumor‐related mortality [17]. LM is a complex process, which is regulated by a variety of oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes. Therefore, finding suitable molecular targets is essential for a more comprehensive understanding and treatment of LM.

DHCR7 is an enzyme responsible for catalyzing the final step in the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway, converting 7‐dehydrocholesterol and 7‐dehydrodemosterol into cholesterol [18]. Beyond its role in cholesterol synthesis, DHCR7 also plays a regulatory role in ferroptosis [6]. Previous studies have demonstrated that DHCR7 is significantly linked to the progression and metastasis of multiple types of tumors [19]. Emerging evidence suggests that DHCR7 could modulate bladder cancer tumorigenesis in vitro and in vivo via the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway [20]. Furthermore, DHCR7 could promote bladder cancer metastasis by activating the cAMP/protein kinase A/FAK pathway [8]. Another study showed DHCR7 modulated gastric cancer cell apoptosis resistance, proliferation, migration, and invasion through the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway [21]. However, whether DHCR7 is involved in LM of PC has not been studied yet.

In this study, we collected PC tissues from 7 patients who developed LM within 6 months after surgery and 7 patients who did not develop LM for at least 1 year after surgery for proteomic analysis. We found that the expression of DHCR7 was elevated in patients who developed LM within 6 months after surgery, and using publicly available data, we observed elevated DHCR7 expression in both PC tissues and paired LM tissues, which correlated with poor prognosis in PC patients. In addition, functional validation confirmed DHCR7's oncogenic role, with its suppression impairing the proliferation, migration, and invasive ability of PC cells and the progression of LM in the metastatic model.

EMT is a critical process that significantly contributes to the invasiveness and metastatic potential of PC [22]. When EMT occurs in PC cells, intercellular adhesion is reduced, and the ability of movement and invasion is enhanced, which facilitates the tumor cells to detach from the primary lesion and enter into the peripheral vascular or lymphatic system and becomes a prerequisite for the distant metastasis of the tumor [23]. Consistently, we observed that DHCR7 knockdown upregulated the epithelial marker E‐cadherin and downregulated the mesenchymal markers N‐cadherin and Vimentin in PC cells.

Dysregulation of the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway is a well‐established driver of cancer development and progression [24]. The overactive PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway is also able to promote EMT and metastasis through its significant effects on cell migration [25]. In the present study, through RNA‐seq, we verified that knockdown of DHCR7 significantly reduced the expression of PI3K and p‐Akt, implying that the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway was regulated by DHCR7. In addition, we also found that the EMT, proliferation, migration, and invasion ability of PC cells were inhibited after treatment with the PI3K inhibitor.

It has also been shown that cholesterol is closely related to tumor progression and increased cholesterol biosynthesis is a hallmark of many cancers [26]. Cholesterol can act as a negative regulator of the SREBP2 and mevalonate pathways [27]. The master transcription factor SREBP2 and its downstream targets, such as enzymes in the mevalonate pathway, are markedly upregulated in tumors under conditions of lipid and/or oxygen deprivation within the glioblastoma microenvironment [28]. Consistently, our Reactome enrichment analysis of proteomics revealed that activation of gene expression by SREBF function was upregulated. Studies have shown that cholesterol is directly related to PC invasiveness and EMT through tumor cell plasticity programs [29]. Moreover, elevated cholesterol levels lead to increased lipid raft content, which alters TGF‐β signaling, thereby promoting EMT [30]. Additionally, DHCR7‐mediated cholesterol metabolism reprogramming has been shown to activate the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway, drive EMT, migration, and invasion in cervical cancer cells [7]. Xu et al. found cholesterol biosynthesis is capable of preserving the stability of lipid rafts, which in turn activates the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway [31]. RNA‐seq analysis of shDHCR7 and shNC PANC‐1 cells identified DHCR7 as a key regulator of the cholesterol synthesis pathway. Our cellular experiments further confirmed this result that knockdown of DHCR7 expression can inhibit the synthesis of total and free cholesterol and promote 7‐DHC accumulation in PC cells. In addition, cholesterol supplementation in PC cells with reduced DHCR7 can reactivate the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway. These results indicate that DHCR activates the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway by promoting cholesterol synthesis.

There were several limitations in our study. Firstly, the sequencing sample size used for proteomics in our study is relatively small and may have a selection bias, so validation in a large clinical cohort is needed. Secondly, we only briefly verified that DHCR7 can regulate the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway through cholesterol synthesis, and the specific regulatory mechanism of DHCR7 or cholesterol needs further exploration.

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that DHCR7 expression is upregulated in PC patients with LM and is associated with poor prognosis. In vitro and in vivo studies indicated the pro‐carcinogenic effect of DHCR7 by increasing cholesterol levels, which then activates the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway, thereby enhancing the proliferation, migration, and invasion ability of PC cells, ultimately promoting LM of PC. These results indicate that DHCR7 could be a promising biomarker for diagnosing and treating LM of PC.

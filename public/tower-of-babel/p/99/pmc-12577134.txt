Epidemiological characteristics and influential factors of hand, foot, and mouth disease reinfection in Southwest China, 2009–2022
Authors: Lin Fu, Tingting Li, Jiang Long, Yu Xia, Zhourong Li, Shuang Yang, Yong Zhao, Li Qi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDue to the absence of cross-protection among various enterovirus subtypes, reinfection with hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is frequently observed. This study aimed to investigate the epidemiological characteristics and influential factors of HFMD reinfection in Chongqing, southwest China.MethodsThe data for cases of HFMD in Chongqing from 2009 to 2022 were obtained from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention. Cases of HFMD reinfection were identified through uniform definition. Descriptive epidemiology was employed to depict characteristics of HFMD reinfection cases. Binary logistic regression analysis was used to explore the factors influencing HFMD reinfection.ResultsAmong the 591,297 HFMD patients, 19,678 cases (3.33%) were classified as reinfections. The reinfection rates for males and females were 3.74% and 3.26%, respectively, and the difference was statistically significant (χ2 = 90.490, P < 0.001). Among different age groups, children aged under 12 months had the highest HFMD reinfection rate of 6.07%, and the reinfection rate decreased with increased age (Z = 66.83, P trend < 0.001). In terms of occupation, scattered children had significantly higher HFMD reinfection rates compared to children in kindergarten, school students, and adults (χ2 = 2,814.322, P < 0.001). The reinfection rate was higher for other enteroviruses compared to enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) and Coxsackievirus A16 (CVA16) (χ2 = 74.851, P < 0.001). The reinfection rate was significantly higher in urban areas (3.82%) than that in rural areas (2.85%). The binary logistic regression analysis indicated that male, younger age, living in urban areas, and infection with other enteroviruses were risk factors for HFMD reinfection.ConclusionThis study investigated the burden of HFMD reinfection in Chongqing. Male, younger age, residing in urban areas, and infection with other enteroviruses as the serotype of HFMD were associated with a higher likelihood of reinfection. It is necessary to develop targeted health education programs and intervention measures to reduce the reinfection of HFMD in susceptible individuals.Keywords: Hand, foot, and mouth disease; Reinfection; Epidemiology; Enterovirus

Introduction

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is a common infectious disease that predominantly affects young children [1]. It is caused by various enteroviruses. Enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) and Coxsackievirus A16 (CVA16) have traditionally been considered the main pathogens causing HFMD. However, HFMD outbreaks associated with Coxsackievirus A6 (CVA6) [2, 3] and Coxsackievirus A10 (CVA10) [4] have occurred more frequently worldwide in recent years. HFMD is one of the prevalent infectious diseases in China [5] and has been classified as a Class C infectious disease under the management of the Chinese Law on the Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases [6, 7].

Since 2015, China has obtained three types of inactivated monovalent EV-A71 vaccines. Clinical trials have shown that these vaccines have a high level of protection against EV-A71-associated HFMD (90.0%–97.4%) [8]. However, their cross-protection against HFMD caused by non-EV-A71 enteroviruses is limited [9, 10]. Due to the lack of cross-protection between different enterovirus subtypes, reinfection of HFMD is common [10, 11]. For example, Anhui Province reported 8,960 confirmed cases of HFMD reinfection in 2008–2013 [12]. Wuxi City reported 6,470 cases of HFMD reinfection in 2008–2016 [13]. In Guangzhou City, the number of HFMD reinfection cases was 11,321 in 2012–2017 [14].

Chongqing, the largest municipality in southwest China, experienced a substantial burden from HFMD. The incidence of HFMD in this region was considerably higher than the national average and that of many countries or regions [15, 16]. Our previous study has shown that a total of 276,207 cases of HFMD were reported in Chongqing between 2009 and 2016 [17]. However, the reinfection of HFMD in Chongqing is unclear. This study aimed to explore the dynamics of HFMD reinfection, identify the influencing factors, and provide scientific evidence for HFMD control and prevention in Chongqing.

Materials and methods

Study design

The data for cases of HFMD reinfection in the Chongqing region from 2009 to 2022 were obtained from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention. The dataset included information such as gender, date of birth, residential address, case classification (clinical or laboratory), clinical manifestation (severe or mild), diagnosis date, death status, and enterovirus serotype.

Case definitions

Clinically diagnosed cases of HFMD were defined as patients with vesicular rash on hands, foot, mouth, or buttocks, with or without fever. Laboratory-diagnosed cases were defined as clinically diagnosed cases with laboratory evidence of enterovirus infection (EV-A71, CVA16, or other enteroviruses) detected through reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) or virus isolation. Cases were classified as severe, either by clinical or laboratory diagnosis, if patients presented with any neurological complications, cardiopulmonary complications, or both. Otherwise, they were classified as mild. From 2009 to 2022, reinfection cases were defined as patients who had HFMD at least twice, while single infection cases were defined as patients who had HFMD only once. Specifically, in this study, the first infection episode of reinfection cases was defined as primary infection, while the subsequent second infection episode in these reinfection cases was defined as secondary infection. Reinfection of HFMD cases was further defined as follows [18]: for mild cases, the interval between two HFMD infections was ≥ 14 days, and for severe cases, the interval between two infections was ≥ 23 days. In addition, the following three screening criteria must be met: 1) having the same identification number and identical patient name; 2) having the same patient name along with the same birth date and identical parent’s name; 3) having the same patient name along with the same contact telephone number, home address, and birth date(Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted with R (version 4.2.0, R Core Team, Vienna, Austria). The chi-square test was used to stratify comparisons of reinfection rate, and the Cochran-Armitage trend test was applied to analyze the incidence of reinfection with respect to age. Considering the use of the EV-A71 vaccine and the outbreak of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the data were divided into three time periods, 2009–2014, 2015–2019, and 2020–2022, for the purpose of evaluating the reinfection rates of different enteroviruses serotypes in each time period. Binary logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate independent risk factors for HFMD reinfection. All tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Microsoft Excel (version 2019, Redmond, WA, USA) was used to visualize the time distribution of single infection and reinfection cases. Arc GIS (version 10.8, Redlands, CA, USA) was used to visualize the regional distribution of the epidemic.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics

Among the 591,297 reported cases of HFMD in Chongqing from 2009 to 2022, a total of 19,678 cases were identified as reinfections. The reinfection rate was 3.33%, with 19,063 (96.87%) patients experiencing two infections, 595 (3.02%) patients experiencing three infections, 18 (0.09%) patients experiencing four infections, and 2 (0.02%) patients experiencing five infections.

The reinfection rates for male and female patients were 3.74% and 3.26%, respectively, and the difference was statistically significant (χ2 = 90.490, P < 0.001). Among different age groups, children aged under 12 months had the highest HFMD reinfection rate at 6.07%, and the reinfection rate decreased with increased age (Z = 66.83, P trend < 0.001). In terms of population classification, scattered children had substantially higher HFMD reinfection rates compared to children in kindergarten, school students, and adults (χ2 = 2,814.322, P < 0.001). Compared to cases infected with enterovirus serotypes EV-A71 and CVA16, the reinfection rate was higher for cases infected with other enteroviruses serotypes (χ2 = 74.851, P < 0.001). The reinfection rate was notably higher in urban areas (3.82%) than in rural areas (2.85%). No statistically significant difference was observed in the reinfection rates between mild and severe cases (Table 1).

Time distribution

The seasonal distributions of primary infection and secondary infection from 19,063 patients experiencing two infections were similar to that of single infection, with two peaks observed from May to July and November to December each year (Fig. 3).

In most patients of different age groups, the time interval between primary infection and secondary infection was mostly concentrated in the 6th month, 12th month and 18th month (Fig. 4).

Regional distribution

According to the regional distribution, higher rates of HFMD reinfection were observed in the main urban areas of Chongqing from 2009 to 2022. Additionally, elevated reinfection rates were also observed in certain regions and counties in the northeast, southeastern, and west of Chongqing (Fig. 5).

Binary logistic regression analysis of influential factors for reinfection

Binary logistic regression analysis showed that gender, age, enterovirus serotype, and residence were risk factors for HFMD reinfection (Table 4). Specifically, males had a higher risk of reinfection than females (OR = 1.33, 95% CI: 1.12–1.57). Compared to patients aged ≥ 60 months, other age groups had a higher risk of reinfection, with patients younger than 12 months having a 20.45-fold higher risk of reinfection compared to those aged ≥ 60 months. Additionally, patients residing in urban areas had a higher risk of reinfection compared to those residing in rural areas (OR = 1.21, 95% CI: 1.02–1.43). Patients infected with CVA16 had a lower risk of reinfection compared to those infected with other enteroviruses (OR = 0.51, 95% CI: 0.41–0.64).

Discussion

This study analyzed the cases of HFMD reinfection in southwest China (Chongqing) from 2009 to 2022. We described the epidemiological characteristics of HFMD reinfection, and identified its influencing.

The reinfection rate of HFMD in Chongqing from 2009 to 2022 was 3.33%, which is consistent with results from Guangzhou (3.07%) [14] and Fujian (3.15%) [19] in China, but higher than the reported reinfection rate in Wuhan City (1.93%) [20]. The differences in reinfection rates may be attributed to variations in climate, population structure, and the prevalence of different pathogens [21, 22]. Additionally, the results of the regional distribution indicated that the districts with higher rates of reinfection of HFMD in Chongqing were mainly located in the main urban area, as well as in the northeastern, southeastern, and western regions.

The seasonal distributions of primary infection, secondary infection, and single infection showed similar patterns, with two peaks observed from May to July and November to December each year. The patterns were consistent with the seasonal distribution of the HFMD incidence in China, with a stronger peak in early summer and a weaker peak in early autumn [23]. Furthermore, we found that the median interval between HFMD reinfections was 15 (IQR 8–27) months. Namely, most patients were more likely to experience a subsequent infection within 1–2 years after their primary infection.

The binary logistic regression analysis revealed several significant risk factors for HFMD reinfection, including male gender, younger age, residing in urban areas, and infection with other enteroviruses. This study indicated that the reinfection rate of HFMD decreases with age. This finding aligns with the research conducted by Peng et al. [20]. The higher reinfection rate among younger children could be attributed to their less developed immune systems, limited adoption of effective hygiene practices, and greater exposure to enteroviruses due to their active and exploratory behavior [24]. The risk of HFMD reinfection in males was higher than that in females, consistent with the epidemiological characteristics of HFMD [25]. The possible reason for this difference could be that boys are typically more active, increasing their chances of coming into contact with contaminated objects from other infected children [26–28]. Besides, patients living in urban areas faced a higher risk of reinfection compared to those residing in rural areas, which was consistent with the epidemiological characteristics of HFMD [1, 29, 30]. This could be attributed to factors such as higher population density, increased population mobility, and developed transportation systems in urban areas. In contrast, rural areas may have issues such as underreporting due to less developed medical systems [31, 32]. Furthermore, patients infected with other enteroviruses had a higher risk of reinfection compared to patients infected with CVA16. Our results were consistent with other study [14]. This might be due to the increasing number of HFMD cases caused by other types of enteroviruses in recent years [33, 34].

Interestingly, the clinical manifestation of HFMD, whether mild or severe, was not identified as a risk factor for reinfection. However, the severity of cases in the secondary infection was lower compared to both primary infection and single infection. This could potentially be attributed to factors such as the advanced age of patients during the secondary reinfection or the acquisition of immune protection from the primary infection [35]. The proportion of patients infected with CVA16 in primary infection cases was found to be lower compared to single infection cases. Conversely, the proportion of patients infected with other enteroviruses in primary infection cases was higher than that in single infection cases. This finding may indicate that other enteroviruses are more likely to cause HFMD reinfection [18]. To further analyze the reinfection of different enteroviruses serotypes at different times, the data were divided into three time periods. The results indicated that in all three periods, the reinfection rates caused by other enteroviruses were higher than that by CVA16. And in 2015–2019 and 2020–2022, EV-A71 also had higher reinfection rates than CVA16. These findings further support that other enteroviruses are more likely to cause HFMD reinfection. Additionally, with the widespread use of the EV-A71 vaccine, cases caused by EV-A71 have declined, while other enteroviruses types, such as CVA6 and CVA10, have increasingly emerged [36]. This means people who have been vaccinated can still get reinfected. However, even though EV-A71 related cases have now decreased due to vaccination, the immunogenicity provided by the EV-A71 vaccine is not lifelong, and reinfection is still possible [37]. So, reinfection cases caused by EV-A71 may still be an important issue.

Based on our findings, several recommendations were noted. Firstly, it is crucial to prioritize targeted care and education for males, particularly young boys, with a special focus on their parents or caregivers. Caregivers should be well-informed about essential hygiene practices, including regular hand-washing, minimizing contact with potentially contaminated objects and avoiding close contact with individuals who have HFMD. Secondly, specific interventions for patients living in urban areas, especially in cities with higher reinfection rates, are important and should be implemented. Furthermore, comprehensive monitoring of enterovirus pathogens is crucial. It is recommended to strengthen pathogen typing in future studies, including specific subtyping of other enteroviruses as part of routine monitoring efforts. This will serve as a basis for the development of multivalent vaccines in the future, ultimately reducing the occurrence of repeated infections caused by various enteroviruses.

Current study has some limitations. Firstly, it is important to acknowledge that this study is a retrospective cross-sectional study, which limits our ability to establish causal relationships. Secondly, among all reported HFMD cases in Chongqing from 2009 to 2022, laboratory testing for enterovirus serotyping was conducted on only 23,419 cases. Furthermore, the testing only detected the presence of EV-A71 and CVA16 serotypes, while other specific serotypes like CVA6 and CVA10 were not included in the testing.

Conclusion

In summary, this study holds significance as it provides the first long-term surveillance and analysis of HFMD reinfection in southwest China over 14 years. This research sheds light on the burden of HFMD reinfection in the region, offering valuable insights that could aid future prevention and control efforts for HFMD. Our findings indicate that factors such as gender, age, enterovirus serotype, and residence significantly influence HFMD reinfection. Therefore, it is necessary to develop targeted health education programs and intervention measures for high-risk individuals prone to HFMD reinfection. To reduce the rate of HFMD reinfection among susceptible populations, this study highlights the need to strengthen public health education and understanding of HFMD.

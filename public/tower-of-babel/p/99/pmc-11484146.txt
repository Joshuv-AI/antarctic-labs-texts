Bioelectrical impedance analysis–derived phase angle predicts possible Sarcopenia in patients on maintenance hemodialysis: a retrospective study
Authors: Ying Zeng, Yang Chen, Yujie Yang, Ying Qiu, Ping Fu, Huaihong Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prevalence of possible sarcopenia is notably high among maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients. Possible sarcopenia, defined as a decrease in muscle strength and/or somatic function, is an early and reversible condition between non-sarcopenic and sarcopenia, and early recognition and intervention for possible sarcopenia is important for preventing adverse outcomes and improving the quality of life of these patients. This study aimed to establish a simple and effective model for screening and identifying MHD patients at high risk of possible sarcopenia by using 50 kHz-Whole Body Phase Angle (PhA), with a specific focus on gender differences.MethodsThis prospective cross-sectional study was conducted from September to December 2023 at the Wenjiang Hemodialysis Center in the Department of Nephrology, West China Hospital, Sichuan University, Chengdu, China. A total of 244 MHD patients, including 130 males and 114 females, were enrolled. Data were collected prospectively, including demographic information and physical measurements. All participants provided informed consent before enrollment. Measurements were taken post-dialysis to ensure consistency. The whole-body phase angle was measured using the InBody S10 device, grip strength was measured using an electronic grip strength tester, and physical function was assessed by the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB). The Skeletal Muscle Index (SMI) was also calculated.ResultsA total of 244 patients receiving hemodialysis were enrolled in this study. Among these, 109 patients were categorized as non-sarcopenic, 111 as having possible sarcopenia, and 24 as sarcopenic. The prevalence of sarcopenia among MHD patients is 9.8%, while the prevalence of possible sarcopenia is 45.5%. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis showed that for male patients, the AUC of PhA for predicting possible sarcopenia was 0.798, with a sensitivity of 80.36%, specificity of 69.70%, and a cutoff value of 6.20°. For female patients, the AUC of PhA was 0.701, with a sensitivity of 70.91% and specificity of 62.79%, and a cutoff value of 5.70°.ConclusionsPhA may be a useful and simple predictor of the risk of possible sarcopenia in MHD patients, and more research is needed to further promote the use of PhA in possible sarcopenia.Keywords: Maintenance hemodialysis patients, Bioimpedance, Phase angle, Possible sarcopenia, Receiver operating characteristic curve

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) has become an important public health problem worldwide due to its characteristics of high prevalence, high disability rate, high medical costs, and low awareness-often referred to as “three highs and one low”. As of 2022, China had approximately 159.8 million CKD patients [1]. Once CKD patients progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD), they require renal replacement therapy such as hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or kidney transplantation to sustain life, severely affecting their quality of life [2]. According to surveys, maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) remains the primary and most commonly used treatment method for ESRD, with about 89.4% of patients in China opting for MHD [3].

Compared to the general population, MHD patients often experience reduced protein and energy reserves due to the loss of nutrients during dialysis and inadequate dietary intake [4]. This nutritional issue is not limited to the dialysis period but persists throughout the disease course, potentially impacting overall health and treatment outcomes. Moreover, the decline in glomerular filtration rate or progression of proteinuria, along with dialysis treatment, accelerates muscle protein catabolism and mitochondrial dysfunction, leading to decreased skeletal muscle strength or mass and predisposing MHD patients to sarcopenia [5, 6]. Sarcopenia is a progressive systemic disease characterized by a decline in muscle mass and muscle strength and/or physical function with aging [7]. It is closely associated with various adverse outcomes, including falls, frailty, fractures [8–10], and malnutrition, and is linked to increased risks of prolonged hospitalization and higher mortality [11]. Shu et al. (2022) conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis on the diagnosis, prevalence, and mortality of sarcopenia in dialysis patients, analyzing data from over 5,000 dialysis patients from multiple countries, including North America, Europe, and Asia, with an average age of 55 to 75 years. They found that the prevalence of sarcopenia ranged widely from 4 to 68%, with an estimated prevalence of 28.5%, and concluded that sarcopenia is very common in dialysis patients and associated with a higher risk of mortality despite significant differences in diagnostic criteria [12]. This provides important reference points for our study on “possible sarcopenia”.

Historically, the diagnoses of sarcopenia primarily focused on the reduction of muscle mass, with less attention paid to the assessment of muscle function and strength. In 2019, the European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP) revised their consensus, stating that sarcopenia diagnosis should include evaluations of muscle mass, function, and strength to form a more comprehensive diagnostic framework [13]. Kim KV et al. conducted a prospective study involving 142 hemodialysis patients and found that sarcopenia is closely related to long-term mortality and cardiovascular events in MHD patients. Evaluations of muscle strength and mass could provide additional prognostic information for survival of patients with ESRD [14]. In 2019, the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) introduced the concept of ‘possible sarcopenia,’ which refers to a decline in muscle strength and/or physical function without accompanying muscle mass loss. The decline in muscle function leads to a reduction in physical activity, which in turn causes a decrease in muscle mass, ultimately resulting in the development of sarcopenia [15]. It is advocated that early identification and intervention are necessary. Possible sarcopenia is a precursor to sarcopenia, which involves a decline in muscle mass along with decreased muscle strength and/or physical function. By identifying possible sarcopenia, interventions can be implemented to halt or reverse the progression to full sarcopenia [16]. Therefore, this study focuses on “possible sarcopenia”, as this condition often represents an early stage of sarcopenia development, potentially offering greater value for early detection and intervention.

Currently, various measurement modalities are available for assessing muscle mass and function, including advanced techniques such as CT and MRI. However, these methods may be impractical due to their cost, complexity, and limited availability. Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) and dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) are more practical options for assessing muscle mass and function. Their widespread availability and ease of use make them ideal candidates for screening possible sarcopenia, providing valuable information in a cost-effective manner [17].

Phase Angle (PhA), measured through BIA, provides insights into cellular membrane integrity and body cell mass, making it a useful tool in assessing muscle quality [18]. PhA is non-invasive and relatively cost-effective, but its accuracy can be influenced by factors such as age and gender. Research indicates that while PhA is affected by age and gender, it has shown potential as a prognostic indicator for sarcopenia [19]. Studies in non-MHD patients have demonstrated its utility in predicting sarcopenia risk [20]. However, there are currently no studies specifically addressing possible sarcopenia in dialysis patients.

In MHD patients, PhA has been used to assess muscle quality, and recent studies highlight its relevance in evaluating muscle health and predicting the risk of sarcopenia [21–23]. Moreover, research indicates that gender differences in aging patterns affect muscle mass and strength, which should be considered in PhA assessments [24]. Therefore, this study emphasizes gender differences to explore the utility of PhA for screening possible sarcopenia, aiming to improve early identification and personalized prevention for MHD patients.

Methods

Study design and participants

This prospective study was conducted at the Wenjiang Hemodialysis Center, West China Hospital, Sichuan University, Chengdu, China, between September and December 2023. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years, (2) receiving maintenance hemodialysis for at least 3 months, and (3) willingness to participate in this study and sign the informed consent form. The exclusion criteria were: (1) cognitive impairment or mental illness, as assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) [25]. (2) patients with major disease changes in the last three months, (3) pregnant or lactating individuals, (4) individuals with body amputations, immobility, or other reasons preventing cooperation, and (5) compliance with manufacturer-specified contraindications to bioelectrical impedance analysis, e.g., cardiovascular stent implantation, pacemaker installation, or artificial joint replacement.

Data collection and procedures

Data collection was conducted during the dialysis process at the Wenjiang Hemodialysis. Before the study, two researchers were uniformly trained to assess the questionnaires and measure anthropometric indicators. After signing the informed consent form, a face-to‐face interview was conducted to verify demographics (sex, age) and characteristics (diabetes status, hypertension status, and dialysis vintage). Height (cm), weight (kg), and calf circumference (CC, cm) were measured after the dialysis session using a wall-secured stadiometer, a digital scale, and a retractable measuring tape. Height and weight were measured without shoes and with light clothing, and CC was measured on the right leg at the widest part of the calf. Measurements were taken post-dialysis to minimize the influence of fluid retention and to obtain stable measurements after fluid removal. Once the survey was completed, researchers immediately verified and collected the questionnaires. A total of 245 questionnaires were collected and 1 invalid questionnaires were eliminated, with an effective recovery rate of 99.6%.

Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA)

BIA was performed using an InBody S10 device (15, Heugam-gil, Ipjang-myeon, Seobuk-gu, Cheonan-si, Chungcheongnam-do, 31,025 KOREA), which is a multifrequency bioimpedance device. Participants completed the BIA measurement within two hours of the end of dialysis, removed all metal objects, and stood barefoot on an insulated floor mat wearing light clothing. Arms were not in contact with each other and naturally hung down, open at an angle of approximately 15°, and the legs were about shoulder-width apart with the inner thighs not in contact. Skeletal Muscle Index (SMI) was used as an index to estimate muscle mass and was calculated using the measured muscle mass relative to height squared (kg/m²). PhA is a raw variable measured directly by BIA, calculated based on the ratio of reactance (Xc) to resistance (R) as follows: PhA (°) = arctangent (Xc / R) × (180° / π) [21].

Handgrip strength (HGS, KG)

Muscle strength was assessed in MHD patients using HGS, following standardized measurement methods to ensure accuracy and reliability [13]. Muscle strength was assessed in MHD patients using HGS. An electronic grip strength tester (Zhongshan Camry Electronics Co., Ltd.) was used to measure grip strength before hemodialysis. Participants stood with arms naturally hanging down and elbows straight, gripping the tester with the non-fistula hand. Three measurements were taken at 1-minute intervals, recording the maximal value.

Short physical performance battery (SPPB)

The SPPB is recommended for evaluating physical performance in MHD patients [26]. The SPPB test was conducted according to standardized procedures and relevant literature was consulted to ensure the accuracy of the test. The SPPB test includes three components: ①Chair Stand: Participants rise from a chair with arms folded across their chest. If successful, they perform five stands as quickly as possible, with timing from the initial sitting position to the final standing position after the fifth stand. ②Walking: Walking speed is tested over an 8-foot course with an additional 2 feet at each end. Participants walk at their usual speed, and the faster of two timed walks is used for analysis. ③Balance Tests: Standing balance is assessed with tandem, semi-tandem, and side-by-side stands. The test starts with the semi-tandem stand; if successful for 10 s, participants are then tested in the full tandem position. Timing stops if participants move their feet or need support, or after 10 s [27]. Each component is scored on a scale of 0–4 points, and the total score is 0–12 points. Patients with a score of ≤ 9 were identified as having low physical performance [16].

Sarcopenia diagnosis

Sarcopenia diagnosis was made using the 2019 Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) criteria [16]. The diagnosis are as follows: ① SMI: <7.0 kg/m² in males and < 5.7 kg/m² in females. ② HGS: <28.0 kg for males and < 18.0 kg for females. ③ SPPB: Total score ≤ 9 points. Patients meeting criteria 1 and 2 and/or 3 were diagnosed with sarcopenia. Patients meeting criteria 2 and/or 3 were diagnosed with possible sarcopenia. Analyses were performed on groups categorized as non-sarcopenic and possible sarcopenia.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS, version 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The normality of all data was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed continuous data were represented as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed continuous data were expressed as the median and interquartile range (IQR) (P25, P75). Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. For comparisons between two independent groups, normally distributed continuous data were analyzed using the t-test, while non-normally distributed continuous data were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. All P-values are based on comparisons between the non-sarcopenic group and the possible sarcopenia group. Categorical data were tested by the chi-square test or the Fisher’s exact probability method. Point-Biserial correlation was used to assess the relationship between possible sarcopenia (binary) and Phase Angle (PhA). Spearman correlation was used to evaluate the relationship between PhA and HGS, SPPB, and SMI, with the results interpreted as follows: < 0.15 very weak, 0.15–0.25 weak, 0.25–0.4 moderate, 0.4–0.75 strong, and > 0.75 very strong [28]. Logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate PhA as an independent predictor of possible sarcopenia after adjusting for confounding factors such as age and BMI. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and area under the curve (AUC) were used to assess the predictive value of PhA. The best cutoff value was calculated using the Youden index. Spearman correlation analysis scatterplots were plotted using GraphPad Prism10. The critical values of PhA for possible sarcopenia were determined using ROC curves, which were drawn with MedCalc22, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The optimal cutoff value was calculated using the Youden index, which maximizes the sum of sensitivity and specificity. The significance level adopted was P < 0.05.

Results

Participant characteristics

The study included 244 outpatients patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis, comprising 109 cases of non-sarcopenic, 111 cases of possible sarcopenia, and 24 cases of sarcopenia. The prevalence of possible sarcopenia was 45.5%, while the prevalence of sarcopenia was 9.8%. Due to the small sample size of only 24 cases in the sarcopenia group, data from this group were excluded from the primary analysis, leaving a total of 220 MHD patients for analysis. The average age of these patients was 52.8 ± 13.5 years. Among them, there were 122 male patients (66 with non-sarcopenic, 56 with possible sarcopenia) with an average age of 53.1 ± 13.8 years, and 98 female patients (43 with non-sarcopenic, 55 with possible sarcopenia) with an average age of 52.4 ± 13.1 years. The median duration of dialysis was 54.0 months (IQR: 24.0–84.0 months). A total of 189 patients (85.9%) had hypertension, and 49 patients (22.3%) had diabetes. For the male MHD patients, the two groups showed significant differences in terms of age, diabetes, and PhA. Specifically, the possible sarcopenia group had a significantly greater mean age, a higher prevalence of diabetes, and a lower mean PhA compared to the non-sarcopenic group (P < 0.05 for all comparisons). For the female MHD patients, significant differences were also observed between the groups in terms of age and PhA. The possible sarcopenia group had a significantly greater mean age and a lower mean PhA compared to the non-sarcopenic group (P < 0.05) (Table 1).

HGS had a strong positive correlation with PhA (r = 0.616, P < 0.001), indicating that higher PhA is associated with greater muscle strength. Similarly, SPPB had a strong positive correlation with PhA (r = 0.504, P < 0.001); and SMI had a moderate positive correlation with PhA (r = 0.352, P < 0.001)(Fig. 1).

Predicting ability of PhA in male and female patients with possible Sarcopenia

The AUC of PhA in screening males for possible sarcopenia was 0.798, with a sensitivity of 80.36%, a specificity of 69.70%, and an optimal cutoff value of 6.20° (Fig. 3). The AUC of PhA in screening females for possible sarcopenia was 0.701, with a sensitivity of 70.91%, a specificity of 62.79%, and an optimal cutoff value of 5.70° (Fig. 4).

Discussion

In this study, we explored the relationship between PhA and possible sarcopenia, as well as the predictive value of PhA for possible sarcopenia, with a focus on gender specificity. Our results indicate that the prevalence of sarcopenia among maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients is 9.8%, while the prevalence of possible sarcopenia is 45.5%. These rates are lower than the 28.2% reported by Ying Wang et al. [21]. The lower prevalence in our study may be related to differences in study population and sample size, emphasizing the need for a tool to screen sarcopenia early in the MHD population. We also found that PhA is an independent predictor of possible sarcopenia in both male and female MHD patients. The optimal PhA cut-off for predicting sarcopenia in male MHD patients is 6.20°, with a sensitivity of 80.36% and a specificity of 69.70%. For female MHD patients, the optimal cut-off is 5.70°, with a sensitivity of 70.91% and a specificity of 62.79%. For predicting sarcopenia in both male and female MHD patients, the optimal PhA cut-off is 4.90°, with a sensitivity of 62.50% for males and 68.75% for females, and a specificity of 86.07% for males and 69.39% for females.

A prospective study by Xiaoli Zhu et al. involving 270 elderly patients at the Fangzhuang Community Health Service Center in Beijing found that the cut-off value for PhA in screening for possible sarcopenia was 4.5°. For sarcopenia (common type), the cut-off value was 4.4°, and for severe sarcopenia, the cut-off value was 4.1° [28]. Our study’s results are slightly higher, which may be attributed to differences in diagnostic criteria and study populations. In a prospective study by Ying Wang et al. involving 241 MHD patients, the optimal PhA cut-off for predicting sarcopenia was found to be 4.95° [21], which is similar to our study’s findings. To assess the accuracy of the cut-off values derived from our study, we calculated the mean and standard deviation (SD) of PhA values for sarcopenic patients. The mean PhA for sarcopenic males was 4.64° ± 1.45°, and for females, it was 4.60° ± 1.06°, both of which are below our specified cut-off values. The PhA cut-off values for predicting possible sarcopenia in male and female MHD patients were higher than those for predicting sarcopenia, suggesting that our cut-off values effectively differentiate between possible sarcopenia and sarcopenia patients, highlighting their potential utility in clinical practice.

Our study also found that age and PhA are influential factors for possible sarcopenia in both male and female MHD patients. PhA showed a significant negative correlation with possible sarcopenia and a significant positive correlation with HGS, SPPB, and SMI. Uemura et al. also found positive correlations between PhA and SMI and HGS in both males and females, consistent with our findings [29]. Research has shown that PhA is directly related to muscle strength and declines with age [30, 31], with low PhA populations experiencing decreased muscle mass and strength, further leading to declines in physical function [32]. Although possible sarcopenia has not yet developed into sarcopenia, reduced muscle strength and function can affect physical function and quality of life.

A study by Silva et al. investigated the use of PhA in patients with chronic liver disease and found that lower PhA values were closely associated with sarcopenia and poor clinical outcomes, with a cut-off value of 5.05° predictive of sarcopenia in this population [20]. Additionally, Smith et al. studied the use of PhA in cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy and demonstrated that PhA is an independent predictor of treatment tolerance and overall survival, with lower PhA values indicating higher risks of sarcopenia and adverse outcomes [33]. These studies collectively emphasize the broad attention PhA has received across different disease areas and its valuable role in muscle assessment, underscoring the importance of early diagnosis and intervention for sarcopenia.

Our study suggests that PhA provides a simple and feasible method for predicting possible sarcopenia. While SMI, HGS, and SPPB have been widely used for sarcopenia assessment and demonstrate significant predictive ability in risk prediction, PhA also provides valuable information. However, we do not recommend using PhA as a complete replacement for SMI, HGS, or SPPB; rather, we consider PhA a valuable complementary measurement tool. Studies by Bennett [34] et al. and de Oliveira Guedes [35] et al. have shown that differences in BIA devices can impact raw measurement comparisons and body composition predictions. Therefore, caution should be exercised when interpreting PhA results in studies using different devices. Additionally, population differences, racial characteristics, clinical conditions, and disease types can all affect the derived PhA cut-off values. The variability in cut-off values highlights the importance of using PhA in conjunction with other diagnostic tools for a comprehensive sarcopenia assessment. Early detection and intervention in possible sarcopenia are crucial for preventing progression to more severe stages of sarcopenia and associated complications. Cruz-Jentoft et al. suggest that timely intervention can significantly reduce the risk of developing sarcopenia and improve patient outcomes and quality of life [13]. Our study supports this by demonstrating that PhA can serve as a valuable tool for early identification of possible sarcopenia, enabling healthcare providers to implement preventive strategies before significant muscle deterioration occurs.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations that should be considered. It is a single-center, cross-sectional study with a relatively small sample size, which may introduce selection bias. Additionally, population differences, such as height and body mass index (BMI), may limit the applicability of our findings to other regions [36]. More extensive, multi-center, prospective studies are needed to validate the utility of PhA and establish universally applicable cut-off values.

Conclusion

PhA is a valuable supplementary indicator for predicting the risk of possible sarcopenia in MHD patients, with lower PhA values associated with an increased incidence of possible sarcopenia. PhA ≤ 6.20° can serve as a predictive threshold for possible sarcopenia in male MHD patients, and PhA ≤ 5.70° for female MHD patients. Early screening, diagnosis, and treatment of possible sarcopenia in different gender MHD patients can slow muscle deterioration, improve quality of life, and provide a theoretical basis for personalized prevention and treatment measures. However, further research is needed to refine its application and establish its role alongside other diagnostic tools.

Multi-line laser 3D reconstruction method based on spatial quadric surface and geometric estimation
Authors: Huiming Huang, Guihua Liu, Lei Deng, Tao Song, FuPing Qin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The traditional multi-line laser 3D reconstruction is based on binocular epipolar constraints and the equation of the laser optical plane. Firstly, matching points are identified based on the epipolar constraints. Then, the correct matching points are selected based on the optical plane equation of the multi-line laser. Finally, 3D reconstruction is performed using the selected matching points. In actual production processes involving multi-line lasers, noticeable distortion occurs due to the projection of Diffractive Optical Elements (DOE) at a large angle. This results in curved laser light planes and curved reflections on the measured objects. Under such circumstances, efficiently screening matching points using the traditional method based on the laser plane equation becomes challenging. Additionally, inherent noise in multi-line laser systems introduces errors in extracted laser center coordinates, making it impossible to directly obtain high-precision 3D data. To address these issues, this paper proposes a method that utilizes spatial quadric surfaces and geometric estimation for completing multi-line laser 3D reconstructions. By analyzing the distortion principle of DOE and the positional relationship after optical diffraction, the multi-line laser manifests as a quadric surface on the optical output plane. Consequently, by calibrating the equations of the quadric surface and applying binocular polar constraints, suitable matching points for the multi-line laser can be chosen. Once an accurate matching point is identified, a minimum geometric distance estimation can be established based on the distance constraint between the point and its corresponding epipolar line. This distance represents the separation between the left and right camera’s laser center points and their respective epipolar lines. Utilizing this estimate of the minimum geometric distance, a refined calculation can be performed to better satisfy epipolar constraints and obtain new matching points. Ultimately, utilizing these new matching points enables the completion of 3D reconstruction for multi-line lasers. In comparison with methods relying on spatial plane and epipolar constraints, our algorithm exhibits a superior degree of matchability and accuracy.According to multiple groups of test data, the average error before optimization is 0.3126 mm, and can be increased to 0.0336 mm after optimization.

Introduction

With the continuous deepening of machine vision and 3D reconstruction algorithms, significant achievements have been made in various industries, especially in the industrial field with a wide range of applications. However, with the continuous expansion of industrial scenes, non-contact optical methods face many challenges in obtaining 3D information. Especially in industries such as automobiles, forgings, aviation, and other processing industries, the reflective nature of many component parts hinders the effectiveness of 3D reconstruction methods based on grating phase, making it challenging to meet the requirements. Therefore, laser-based 3D reconstruction methods can effectively address issues related to object reflectivity. Laser measurement systems, with their high stability, speed, and accuracy, are increasingly utilized in object surface profile measurement, online detection, and medical fields. Multi-line laser 3D reconstruction technology has also demonstrated its application value in various fields such as architecture, automotive manufacturing, and cultural heritage protection. In the architectural field, this technology can be used for 3D modeling, measurement, and visualization of buildings. In the automotive manufacturing field, this technology can be used for car body design, collision testing, and manufacturing process control. In the field of cultural heritage protection, this technology can be utilized for 3D modeling, restoration, and visualization of cultural relics. Multi-line laser 3D reconstruction technology also plays a crucial role in various fields, including topographic measurement, reverse engineering, and anthropometric measurements.

Multi-line laser 3D reconstruction technology is a research hot spot in the visual industry. Liu et al.1 proposed a 3D object reconstruction method based on multi-line laser scanning, which acquires point cloud data from multiple perspectives and utilizes this data to reconstruct the 3D model of the object. The article provides a detailed introduction to the working principle of the multi-line laser scanner and how to use multi-line laser scanning data for 3D reconstruction. In addition, it also analyzes the advantages and limitations of multi-line laser 3D reconstruction technology. Zhao et al.2 is a review article that comprehensively examines laser scanning 3D reconstruction technology. It covers the working principle of laser scanners, processing methods for point cloud data, and 3D reconstruction algorithms. The article highlights that laser scanning technology has vast potential applications in the field of 3D reconstruction. However, it also encounters challenges and issues, including data processing speed, accuracy, and robustness. Duan X et al.3 Existing indoor 3D mapping solutions suffer from high cost and poor portability. In this article, a low-cost and portable indoor 3D mapping approach using biaxial line laser scanners and a one-dimension laser range finder integrated with microelectromechanical systems is proposed. A multiple-sensor calibration approach is presented to perform the extrinsic calibration of the integrated 3D mapping system. M.S. Wan et al.4 proposed an efficient 3D scanning measurement system based on asymmetric trinocular vision and multiline laser using K-means + + clustering and hierarchical processing of multiline laser stripes and complete indexing. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method exhibits high accuracy and efficiency in object reconstruction tasks. Jian X et al.5 proposed a semi-automatic method for three-dimensional reconstruction of buildings. The method utilizes multi-line laser scanning data to reconstruct building models. A deep learning-based algorithm is proposed to convert multi-line laser scanning data into geometric and textural information of architectural models. The experimental results demonstrate that this method exhibits high accuracy and efficiency in the three-dimensional reconstruction of buildings. Zhang C et al.6 proposed a real-time 3D hand reconstruction method that utilized a multi-line laser scanner to acquire point cloud data of the hand surface and reconstruct the 3D model of the hand in real-time. The authors also developed a hand tracking system to capture real-time hand movements and reconstruct them in three dimensions. PJ Chiang et al.7 researchers installed a camera and laser projector on the robot. The laser is projected on the work-piece, and by extracting the center point of the laser line and matching it with the corresponding point using the epipolar geometric relationship, the reconstruction is completed. This allows the robot to accurately determine the depth information of the work-piece and identify any existing defects and shapes. Zhang et al.6 designed a 3D scanning system based on parallel multi-lines, with an object measuring accuracy of within 0.1 mm. The average time for a single stitching process is 1.5 s. However, the system does not meet industrial accuracy requirements and needs further improvement. Cao Y et al.8 utilized a single-line laser for three-dimensional scene reconstruction. The accuracy of the reconstruction improved with distance, but ultimately resulted in low accuracy. Cui et al.9 used multi-line laser projection and binocular stereo vision to measure the texture of asphalt road surfaces and completed the matching of multi-line laser stripes using an eight-neighborhood algorithm to label the same stripes. Ping et al.10 designed a practical calibration method for line-structured optical systems without the need to calibrate the camera and the optical plane, by extracting the feature points of the optical plane and computing the homography matrix to map the relationship between the optical plane and the image plane. Sun et al.11 proposed a reverse reprojection algorithm. They reversed projected multiple possible matching points in the right image onto the left light strip image and then screened them. This method accurately identifies matching points; however, the size of the matching windows significantly impacts the results. Choosing the appropriate window size is crucial in various scenarios, as there is no one-size-fits-all solution. Researchers have proposed using the distance constraint of space points from the light plane as an effective means of correctly matching laser strips. Nevertheless, this approach relies on a single constraint, which may lead to ambiguity when distinguishing between numerous broken laser strips. Additionally, the method necessitates multiple traversals of all laser strip points, rendering it computationally intensive and unsuitable for real-time field measurements. Zhang et al.12 addressed this issue by describing the laser strip segment with topological structure, and distinguishing the laser strips by calculating the overlap rate between the laser strip segments and different light planes. This method is appropriate for laser strip breakage and displacement within a small range. However, it may not be effective when the object tilts or the displacement length exceeds a certain limit. Guan et al.13 studied the projection of line laser on rock borehole and three-dimensional reconstruction of the borehole, and visualized its model. Due to the small borehole, they built a line laser scanning platform and extracted contour lines by drip method and cutting method, making the error less than 4%. In general, multi-line structured light 3D reconstruction technology still needs to be improved and many problems still need to be solved.

The multi-line laser reconstruction method heavily relies on the laser plane equation and the epipolar constraint for 3D reconstruction14,15. The basic assumption of this method is that the laser projects multiple straight lines, which appear as multiple three-dimensional planes in space. Firstly, the parameters of each space plane are calculated using a specific calibration algorithm. Subsequently, the 3D reconstruction of the multi-line laser is accomplished by integrating the binocular epipolar constraints. However, in practical applications, due to limitations in the production process, the multiple laser lines projected by the laser are not straight lines. Instead, they exhibit a curve, forming a specific spatial surface in space. If the spatial plane-based method is still used to reconstruct these laser lines, the matching points cannot be accurately obtained. This means that the spatial plane parameters may not correctly correspond to the characteristics of the actual space surface, introducing uncertainty to the subsequent laser point matching based on the spatial plane equation. In addition, the laser itself has Gaussian noise characteristics, resulting in a certain error in the extracted laser center point. If the 3D reconstruction is carried out immediately after finding the correct matching points, the accuracy of the final reconstructed 3D points will be low. To solve this problem, a multi-line laser 3D reconstruction strategy based on spatial surface equations and geometric estimation is proposed. In this method, the space projected by the multi-line laser is considered a quadratic equation of space, and the calibration of the multi-line laser is achieved by calibrating the quadratic equation of space. Next, the matching points are selected based on the epipolar constraint and the equation of the spatial quadric surface. After identifying the correct corresponding points, this paper introduces a geometric estimation method that relies on the minimum epipolar distance. This method is based on the distance constraint from the point to the epipolar and can be utilized to update the matched image points. This method can align the recalculated matching points more closely with the epipolar constraint conditions, thereby enhancing the accuracy of multi-line laser 3D reconstruction. Finally, the matching points are updated using the geometric estimation method to finalize the 3D reconstruction of the multi-line laser. In conclusion, the method proposed in this study offers significant advantages in addressing laser line nonlinearity and Gaussian noise issues, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of multi-line laser 3D reconstruction. In practical applications. Using binocular multi-line laser 3D reconstruction method can greatly improve the efficiency of 3D data acquisition. This method can provide more accurate laser 3D reconstruction data for unmanned driving, robot navigation, and other fields. It lays a foundation for the development and application of 3D reconstruction technology.

Mathematical model of multi-line laser

The multi-line laser 3D reconstruction system leverages the binocular epipolar constraint and laser spatial equation to perform 3D reconstruction of objects. In the process, two cameras simultaneously capture laser images, and a specialized laser line extraction algorithm is used to extract the sub-pixel coordinates of the laser line. Subsequently, the epipolar matching algorithm is utilized to locate the matching point based on the calibrated binocular system parameters. Finally, the three-dimensional points reconstructed using the laser space equation are back-projected to accurately determine the matching point coordinates, thus completing the reconstruction. To ensure the accuracy of the binocular matching method, it is crucial to calibrate both the camera and the laser line. Traditional calibration techniques involve calibrating the binocular camera parameters and the laser line plane parameters16. The binocular calibration method calculates the internal parameters, external parameters, and distortion coefficients of the camera. Additionally, it calibrates the optical plane equation of the multi-line laser. The principle of a multi-line laser is based on optical diffraction components to achieve it. Diffraction optical elements can theoretically shape the laser beam into any desired shape. This is mainly achieved by using the iterative GS algorithm based on the Fourier transform. The fundamental concept of the Gerchberg–Saxton (GS) algorithm is that a two-dimensional space diffraction with a pure phase distribution can generate any light intensity distribution. In specific calculations, iterative processes are performed between the input and output surfaces, applying corresponding amplitude and phase restrictions. This ultimately leads to the diffraction light field converging towards a specific light intensity distribution. The Rayleigh-Sommerfeld integral is a non-axisymmetric approximation solution in scalar diffraction theory, which is only applicable to small-angle diffraction. When large-angle diffraction occurs, the axisymmetry approximation fails, leading to distortion of the pattern. The light field forms a conical shape on the output surface. The generation of a multi-line laser involves a laser, optical prism, beam splitter, and diffractive optical element (DOE). The beam splitter divides the laser beam into multiple parallel beams. The performance of this process depends on various factors such as the laser wavelength, the angle separation of the beam splitters, the total laser field of view, and the number of splitters (M × N). By combining a Powell prism with a one-dimensional beam splitter, it is possible to generate multiple lines. For example, by utilizing a laser diode, a focusing lens, a 1 × 7 beam splitter DOE, and a Powell prism, one can achieve a 1 × 7 line structured light field.

This paper employs two sets of 7-line laser systems, each based on the optical configuration depicted in Fig. 3. Ideally, multiple laser lines should project perfectly straight lines. When the diffraction angle exceeds a certain threshold, the laser line distorts, transforming into a curved line that appears as a surface in three-dimensional space. Referring to the diffraction principles outlined in Fig. 2 and Eqs. (4) and (6), the projection of multiple laser lines onto the imaging plane results in the formation of multiple quadratic curves. In space, they appear as multiple surfaces, as illustrated in Fig. 4. The spatial profile of a single laser beam exhibits a curved shape. When projected onto an object surface, the light intersects with the object, tracing a curved line. Consequently, the curved surface generated by multiple laser projections can be considered as a collection of quadratic surfaces. Multi-line laser 3D reconstruction based on geometric estimation

After multi-line laser calibration, 3D reconstruction of the detected laser line can be carried out based on the internal and external parameters of the binocular camera and multiple laser surface equation21,22. The primary objective of this process is to identify the matching points that correspond to the left and right images. Subsequently, the three-dimensional coordinates of these matching points are calculated using a binocular stereo vision algorithm. The 3D reconstruction method mainly combines binocular epipolar matching and back projection constraints. Firstly, the left and right epipolar lines are calculated based on binocular calibration parameters. Taking the cross 7-lines method as an example, the laser line image coordinates are detected on the left and right images acquired synchronously in 7 lines in one direction. A laser image coordinate on the left image, based on the calculated polar coordinates, can identify multiple corresponding points to be matched on the right image. With the assistance of calibrated laser surface equations and camera imaging principles, the three-dimensional coordinate points of a laser point on the left image are calculated using seven laser surface equations. Then these seven three-dimensional points are converted from the left camera coordinate system to the right camera coordinate system, and the back projection is performed in the right camera coordinate system. According to the back-projection image coordinates and the original detection of the laser image coordinates, the closest point to the image is found. This point corresponds to the matching point of the laser point coordinates on the left image. Finally, based on the matching point, the three-dimensional point corresponding to the laser image coordinate point can be calculated. In this way, the three-dimensional reconstruction of all points on the left and right image laser lines is completed.

The image acquisition process of the multi-line laser system is achieved through output control signals from the controller to both the laser and cameras, as depicted in Fig. 11. System control primarily encompasses two aspects of image acquisition: calibration image acquisition and image acquisition during the 3D reconstruction process. During the calibration stage, the controller initially sends a synchronous trigger signal to both the binocular cameras and LED. This action triggers the LED to emit blue light onto the calibration plate while simultaneously triggering the camera to capture an image of the board. After maintaining a fixed position for the calibration panel, the controller sends another trigger signal after a 20-millisecond interval to synchronize both laser projection and camera capture. Consequently, controlled laser projection occurs on the calibration plate while synchronously triggering the camera to capture corresponding laser line images. Each instance of image acquisition requires obtaining three pairs of images: one pair of left and right camera images of a calibration plate, along with two pairs of multi-line laser images. Subsequently, calibration takes place by adjusting positions accordingly until all required calibration images are captured at multiple locations. During 3D reconstruction processes, output control signals are used to control lasers, cameras, and LED lights. By synchronously triggering lasers and cameras while managing LED projections, successful acquisition of cross-line lasers is accomplished.

After obtaining the spatial quadratic equation that accurately represents the actual shape of the laser line, the 3D reconstruction of the laser line can be achieved by combining the binocular epipolar matching algorithm. Following the method described in Sect. 2.4, first establish the two-dimensional coordinates of the center points of all laser lines in both the left and right images. Then, a laser point is selected on the left image to identify multiple matching points on the right image, following binocular epipolar geometry constraints. As shown in Fig. 19, a single laser point in the left image corresponds to 5 matching points on the epipolar line of the right image. These matching points, found by applying the epipolar geometry constraint from the left image, result in one-to-many matches. To obtain one-to-one matches, it is necessary to complete them according to the surface equation of the multi-line laser. By utilizing the quadric parameters listed in Table 2 and formulas (19), (20), and (21), seven three-dimensional points corresponding to each laser point on the left image can be calculated. These three-dimensional points correspond individually with each quadric representing a specific section of a laser line. Subsequently, these seven three-dimensional points are transformed into the right camera coordinate system using formula (22) and then back-projected onto the right camera plane based on the internal parameters of the right camera, as depicted in Fig. 19. Finally, seven distinct image points can be obtained and specifically labeled as1–7 through the process of back-projection. The final matching point is determined by calculating pixel distances between multiple matching points found through back projection and epipolar geometry, thus identifying which point has the smallest distance value, as illustrated in Fig. 19. Point No. 3 acts as the one-to-one match point for the initial left image. Through this method, the initial matching of all laser points can be completed.

Conclusion

The key aspect of multi-line laser 3D reconstruction technology can be summarized in two main areas: the calibration of the multi-line laser and the 3D reconstruction based on it. To improve calibration accuracy, this study investigates the physical characteristics of the multi-line laser and utilizes the quadric surface method for precise calibration. The traditional laser plane calibration method is compared with the novel laser quadric calibration method proposed in this paper, highlighting significant differences in principles, procedures, and practical application effects. In contrast, the proposed laser quadric calibration method adopts a more flexible approach by creating a laser quadric model and calibrating multiple targets at various positions to accurately measure complex surfaces. This approach not only overcomes limitations associated with traditional planar calibration methods but also provides richer measurement information and higher accuracy. The proposed method utilizes geometric estimation to optimize the matching of left and right laser image points, aiming to eliminate the influence of laser line noise in multi-line laser 3D reconstruction. By applying the principle of minimum geometric distance, this method estimates the geometry of measured points to identify matching points that adhere more closely to epipolar constraints. This step effectively mitigates the impact of laser line noise on matching accuracy, as demonstrated by experimental verification. The results indicate that compared with traditional methods, our approach achieves higher accuracy and stability in noisy environments while also enhancing 3D reconstruction by identifying matching points that better align with epipolar constraints. In conclusion, this paper introduces a geometric estimation-based method to effectively tackle laser line noise issues in multi-line laser 3D reconstruction. Through an analysis of binocular epipolar line characteristics and initial matching errors in laser point measurements, we establish the principle of minimum geometric distance and optimize geometric estimation for measuring points. Experimental results highlight significant advantages in terms of improved accuracy and stability, providing strong support for applying multi-line laser 3D reconstruction in noisy environments. Future studies will continue to explore more efficient and accurate methods to meet growing demands.According to the geometric estimation method proposed in this paper, it can be extended to 21-line lasers or more lasers, which can further provide the efficiency of data acquisition.

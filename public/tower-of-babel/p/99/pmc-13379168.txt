Comment on Ultrasonographic Measurements of the Liver, Gallbladder, and Hepatic Vessels among University-aged Youth in Baghdad, Iraq
Authors: Mahmood Dhahir Al-Mendalawi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

The following two comments on the interesting research by Khammas et al.,[1] published in the October–December 2025 issue of the Journal of Medical Ultrasound, are noteworthy.

First, Khammas et al.[1] reported that Iraqi university-aged youth with central obesity (CO) had statistically higher hepatic length (P < 0.001), hepatic thickness (P = 0.003), and portal vein diameter (P < 0.001) compared to those without CO. However, there were no statistically significant differences in the mean of gallbladder width (P = 0.157), gallbladder length (P = 0.344), and gallbladder thickness (P = 0.659) between patients with and without CO.[1] The following worthy methodological research limitation related to the standard of CO determination could impact the above-mentioned research findings. In the research methodology, Khammas et al.[1] referred to the WHO/IASO/IOTF classification[2] to characterize CO among the research participants as waist-to-hip ratio >0.90 in males and >0.85 in females. It is to be noted that classification is specifically applied to the populations in the Asia-Pacific region, and the Iraqi population is exempt from it as Iraq is located in the western part of the Asian continent. Indeed, the available updated WHO classification[3] set a new CO threshold (waist circumference higher than 102 cm in men and higher than 88 cm in women), which is applicable to all populations and races. Since there are notable differences in the recommended CO definition criterion between the two classifications, the referral of Khammas et al.[1] to the WHO/IASO/IOTF classification[2] in the methodology, instead of the updated WHO one,[3] looks questionable and it might corrode the validity of their research results.

Second, we hope that Khammas et al’s research[1] would spark the necessity for a local identification of easy-to-use equations for measuring sonographic dimensions of various abdominal structures and hence, formulate age- and gender-specific sonographic normative data for these structures for Iraqi individuals. These data are fundamental to distinguish pathologic from physiologic processes and, ultimately, they could help physicians and surgeons better approach to differential diagnosis and treat patients properly.

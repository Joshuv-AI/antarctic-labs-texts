IFPHPOS0038 Beyond Single Risk Factors: Environmental Clustering and Social Gradients in Childhood Asthma Severity
Authors: Fatemeh Hoda Fallah, Philip McHale
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChildhood asthma is influenced by interactions between social and environmental determinants, particularly housing-related exposures. Traditional approaches focusing on single risk factors may underestimate cumulative environmental disadvantage. This study explored environmental risk clustering and socioeconomic gradients in asthma severity.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted among 246 children with physician-diagnosed asthma in Tehran. Data on housing conditions, indoor environmental exposures (dampness, ventilation, heating), early-life factors, and parental education were collected via questionnaires. Logistic regression assessed associations, while Latent Class Analysis (LCA) identified clustered exposure profiles. Asthma severity was compared across socioeconomic and environmental strata.ResultsModerate-to-severe asthma was more prevalent in low-education households (61.6%) than in high-education households (50.8%). Overall, 55.7% had moderate-to-severe asthma, and 45.5% had low parental education. Logistic regression showed no significant association between parental education and environmental exposures, including ventilation (OR = 1.24, 95% CI: 0.72–2.15), wet housing (OR = 1.06, 95% CI: 0.62–1.81), and radiator heating (OR = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.50–1.52). LCA (BIC=1516.0) identified two profiles: a low-risk class (86.2%) and a high-risk class (13.8%) characterized by damp, poorly ventilated environments and solid fuel heating. In high-risk conditions, 58.8% of children from low-education households had moderate-to-severe asthma vs 51.5% in high-education households.DiscussionAsthma inequalities are driven by clustered housing-related environmental risks rather than single exposures. Integrated strategies targeting indoor environmental quality are essential to reduce risk and improve outcomes.

Risk Factors for Graft Rupture following ACL Reconstruction Surgery: A Systematic Review
Authors: Armansyah Nur Dewantara, I Gusti Ngurah Wien Aryana, Febyan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction & Objectives:Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) injuries are common in sports-related activities and often require reconstruction surgery to restore knee stability. Graft rupture, the failure of the reconstructed ACL graft, remains a significant concern post-surgery. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to identify risk factors associated with graft rupture, including age, graft type, and meniscal injuries, to enhance clinical decision-making and patient outcomes.Materials & Methods:A comprehensive literature search was conducted in multiple electronic databases. Studies meeting inclusion criteria were analyzed for age distribution, graft type impact, and the association between meniscal injuries and graft rupture. Data were extracted, and the risk of bias was assessed using standardized criteria.Results:Five studies met the inclusion criteria, providing insights into risk factors for graft rupture. Younger age consistently emerged as a significant risk factor for graft failure due to increased activity levels, biomechanical demands, and physiological factors. Graft type, specifically hamstring tendon grafts, showed a slightly elevated risk of failure compared to quadriceps tendon grafts. Meniscal injuries were associated with increased graft failure risk, potentially due to altered biomechanics and impaired graft integration. These risk factors interact and contribute to the overall outcome of ACL reconstruction.Conclusion:The interplay of age, graft type, and meniscal injuries significantly influences graft rupture risk in ACL reconstruction. Addressing each risk factor within the context of the patient's profile is essential for successful treatment. Individualized treatment plans should be developed, accounting for patient demographics, lifestyle, and injury characteristics. Further research into the underlying mechanisms connecting these risk factors could lead to targeted preventive strategies and improved patient outcomes.

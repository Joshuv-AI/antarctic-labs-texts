Interstitial lung abnormality in COPD is inversely associated with the comorbidity of lung cancer
Authors: Jianrui Zheng, Jiaxi Guo, Guangdong Wang, Liang Zhang, Xinhua Yu, Dehao Liu, Yikai Lin, Rongzhou Zhang, Aiping Ma, Xiuyi Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInterstitial lung abnormality (ILA) has been recognized as a pertinent factor in the development and prognosis of various pulmonary conditions. However, its correlation with co-morbidities remains understudied. The current study endeavors to elucidate the association between ILA and both clinical features and co-morbidities in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).MethodsA retrospective cohort comprising 1131 hospitalized patients diagnosed with COPD was examined in this observational study. Patients were dichotomously classified based on the presence or absence of ILA, and subsequent analyses scrutinized disparities in demographic, clinical, and laboratory profiles, alongside co-morbid conditions, between the two subgroups.ResultsOf the 1131 COPD patients, 165 (14.6%) exhibited ILA. No statistically significant differences were discerned between COPD patients with and without ILA concerning demographic, clinical, or laboratory parameters, except for levels of circulating fibrinogen and procalcitonin. Nevertheless, a notable discrepancy emerged in the prevalence of multiple co-morbidities. Relative to COPD patients devoid of ILA, those presenting with ILA manifested a diminished prevalence of lung cancer (OR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.30–0.83, p = 0.006), particularly of the lung adenocarcinoma (OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.15–0.71, p = 0.005). Additionally, the presence of ILA in COPD was positively associated with heart failure (OR = 1.75, 95% CI: 1.04-3.00, p = 0.040) and cancers other than lung cancer (OR = 2.27, 95% CI: 1.16–4.39, p = 0.012).ConclusionThese findings demonstrate that the presence of ILA is associated with co-morbidities of COPD, particularly lung cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-024-03311-3.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease(COPD) is a progressive disease characterized by long-term respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation [1]. It stands as the third leading cause of mortality worldwide, responsible for 3.23 million deaths in 2019 [2]. Recent systematic reviews and modeling analyses indicate a global prevalence of COPD among individuals aged 30–79 years at 10.3%, with male gender, cigarette smoking, biomass exposure, and occupational exposure to dust or smoke identified as significant risk factors [3]. COPD frequently co-occurs with various comorbidities, encompassing cardiovascular disease, lung cancer, osteoporosis, and diabetes [4]. Certain comorbidities can influence COPD progression and clinical outcomes; for instance, a retrospective study of 43 acute exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD) inpatients revealed a 37.2% mortality rate within 24 h of hospital admission attributable to heart failure [5].

While COPD diagnosis primarily relies on patient-reported symptoms and pulmonary function tests, chest computed tomography (CT) imaging is often employed to characterize the disease and associated comorbidities [6]. Utilizing chest CT scans, interstitial lung abnormality (ILA) can be detected and characterized [7]. ILA is defined as incidental non-dependent abnormalities identified on chest CT scans, including ground-glass or reticular opacities, lung architectural distortion, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, and non-emphysematous cysts affecting at least 5% of lung parenchyma in individuals without previously suspected interstitial lung disease [8]. ILA prevalence varies markedly and correlates with age, gender, comorbidities, and smoking status of the studied population [9]. Regarded as an early manifestation of interstitial lung disease, ILA’s clinical significance is increasingly recognized [8], as evidenced by associations with dyspnea, chronic cough, and diminished lung function parameters [10, 11]. Moreover, longitudinal studies spanning up to 12 years indicate ILA’s association with heightened all-cause mortality among smokers and patients with diverse respiratory pathogenic conditions [9, 12].

Previous investigations have linked ILA with heightened risk for various pulmonary morbidities. For instance, a study involving 1,990 participants from the Danish Lung Cancer Screening Trial (DLCST) over a 12-year follow-up period found ILA-positive individuals to be more prone to COPD, lung cancer, respiratory failure, and pneumonia [13]. Similarly, the presence of ILA has been associated with increased lung cancer risk in studies such as the AGES-Reykjavik study involving 5,764 older Icelandic adults [14] and another investigation encompassing 25,041 participants from the National Lung Screening Trial [15]. Despite the well-documented relationship between ILA and respiratory morbidities, its correlation with non-respiratory comorbidities remains unexplored. Herein, we aimed to determine the association between ILA presence in COPD patients and their clinical, laboratory characteristics, and comorbidities.

Methods

Patients

In this observational study, a retrospective cohort was included and examined, encompassing a review of demographic and clinical records of hospitalized COPD patients at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University, Fujian, China. Patients were included in this study if they met the following criteria: (1) Diagnosis of COPD according to the criteria outlined by the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) (1) and (2) hospitalization during the time period from January 2015 to August 2021. Patients were excluded if they had: (1) current asthma or history of asthma, (2) lung cancer treatment, or (3) heart failure and were positive for ILA during hospitalization but negative for ILA one month after discharge. Acute exacerbations were defined as events wherein COPD patients experienced a sudden worsening of respiratory symptoms necessitating additional therapeutic interventions, while recurrent AE was characterized by two or more exacerbations within a 12-month period [1, 16]. High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) scans were performed upon admission to evaluate for respiratory infection. Additionally, HRCT scans conducted prior to patient discharge were utilized to assess the presence of emphysema and ILA. HRCT scans were independently reviewed and analyzed by three radiologists (DL, YL, and RZ). Emphysema was diagnosed when there was > 6% of low attenuation below − 950 Hounsfield units on high-resolution CT scans [17]. ILA was defined as non-dependent abnormalities affecting more than 5% of any lung region, encompassing ground-glass or reticular opacities, lung architectural distortion, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, and non-emphysematous cysts [9]. Representative CT scan images illustrating emphysema and ILA are provided in Fig. 1. This study was conducted according to the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments or similar ethical standards and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University (No. 2021064).

Data collection

All demographic, clinical, and laboratory data pertaining to COPD patients were extracted from medical records. Demographic characteristics encompassed age, gender, residential location, education levels, smoking status, and body mass index (BMI). Clinical parameters included spirometry measurements such as post-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1), forced vital capacity (FVC), FEV1/FVC ratio, FEV1% predicted, presence of emphysema, ILA, as well as reasons for hospitalization such as AE and respiratory infection. Laboratory characteristics comprised coagulation markers and hematological systemic inflammatory markers. Additionally, information regarding common comorbidities, including heart failure, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, cardiac valve disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, hyperuricemia, history of tuberculosis, chronic rhinitis, active lung cancer, and cancers other than lung cancer, was retrieved from medical records.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism Software (version 8.0.1). The D’Agostino Pearson test was employed to assess the normal distribution of variables. Normally distributed quantitative variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed variables were expressed as median (interquartile range, Q1–Q3). Student’s t-test was utilized to assess statistical significance for normally distributed quantitative data, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-normally distributed variables. For qualitative variables, statistical significance was evaluated using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic, clinical and laboratory features of patients with COPD

Following the application of the exclusion criteria, a total of 1131 hospitalized COPD patients with a median age of 70 years were included in the retrospective (Table 1). The median BMI was 20.2, and 93.7% of the patients were male. Regarding residential distribution, 49.6%, 4.8%, and 45.6% of patients resided in urban, suburban, and rural areas, respectively. A majority of patients (80.8%) had received less than 9 years of education. Smoking history indicated that 30.8% and 52.7% of patients were ex-smokers and current smokers, respectively. Among the COPD patients, 85.6% had emphysema, and 14.0% exhibited interstitial lung abnormality (ILA). Lung function assessments revealed median values of FEV1/FVC ratio and FEV1% predicted as 56 and 52, respectively. As for the reasons for hospitalization, 82.3% of the patients were admitted due to acute exacerbations, and 60% had respiratory infections. The median duration of hospitalization was 8 days.

The three most prevalent co-morbidities among COPD patients were lung cancer (19.3%), history of tuberculosis (12.7%), and coronary artery disease (9.9%). In addition to demographic and clinical characteristics, laboratory parameters encompassing coagulation markers such as plasma prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), thrombin time (TT), fibrinogen (FIB), and D-dimer, as well as hematological indices including white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), monocyte count (MO), basophil count (BA), and eosinophil count (EO), lymphocyte count (LYM), red blood cell count (RBC), platelet count (PLT), hematocrit (HCT), were evaluated. Association of ILA with demographic, clinical and laboratory characteristics of COPD

To assess the association of ILA with demographic and clinical characteristics in COPD patients, we stratified the cohort into two groups based on ILA presence. As shown in Table 2, COPD patients with ILA demonstrated comparable age distribution and female-to-male ratio to those devoid of ILA. There were no significant disparities observed between the two groups in terms of BMI, smoking history, educational attainment, or residential distribution. In the ILA group, 83.6% of patients exhibited emphysema, a proportion comparable to the 85.3% observed in the non-ILA group. Lung function parameters and hospitalization duration did not exhibit significant discrepancies between the two groups. Additionally, frequencies of acute exacerbation, recurrent acute exacerbations and respiratory infection showed no significant differences between patients with ILA and those without ILA.

We further investigated the association of ILA with coagulation markers, hematological parameters, and circulating inflammatory markers in COPD patients (Table 3). Regarding coagulation markers, COPD patients with ILA exhibited significantly lower levels of FIB compared to those without ILA (3.82 g/L vs. 4.34 g/L, p = 0.018). However, levels of PT, APTT, TT, and D-dimer were comparable between the two groups. No significant differences were observed in any hematological parameters or circulating inflammatory markers, except for PCT. Specifically, COPD patients with ILA displayed significantly lower levels of circulating PCT compared to patients without ILA (0.046 ng/mL vs. 0.064 ng/mL, p = 0.005). In addition to ILA, several other clinical features and co-morbidities of COPD were significantly associated with procalcitonin (PCT), including acute exacerbation, respiratory infection, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and chronic rhinitis (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Association of ILA with co-morbidities in patients with COPD

We proceeded to explore the potential association between ILA and comorbidities in COPD patients. As outlined in Table 4, the prevalence of several comorbid conditions exhibited significant differences between COPD patients with and without ILA. Notably, the most prominent contrast was observed in the incidence of lung cancer, where the prevalence among individuals with ILA was approximately half that of those without ILA (11.5% vs. 20.6%, OR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.30–0.83, p = 0.006). Conversely, the prevalence of cancers other than lung cancers in the ILA group was significantly higher compared to the non-ILA group (7.9% vs. 3.6%, OR = 2.27, 95% CI: 1.16–4.39, p = 0.012). In addition, significant discrepancies were noted in the prevalence of heart failure (11.5% vs. 6.9%, OR = 1.75, 95% CI: 1.04-3.00, p = 0.04) between the two groups.

Association of ILA with co-morbidities in patients with COPD

We further investigated the association between ILA and cancer in patients with COPD. Of the 218 COPD patients with a co-morbidity of lung cancer, 198 were diagnosed during hospitalization, while 20 had a prior diagnosis. The prevalence of ILA in the group diagnosed during hospitalization was 8.6% (17/198), a rate comparable to that observed in the group diagnosed before hospitalization (10%). Since lung cancer comprises different histological subtypes, we further examined the association of ILA with three major subtypes of lung cancer. For this purpose, COPD patients with lung cancer were categorized into three groups: adenocarcinoma (ADC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and other lung cancers (including large cell lung cancer, small cell lung cancer, and adenosquamous carcinoma) (Table 5). Compared with COPD patients without lung cancer comorbidity, those with lung ADC exhibited a significantly lower prevalence of ILA (OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.15–0.71, p = 0.005). A similar trend, although not statistically significant, was observed in COPD patients with lung SCC (OR = 0.48, 95% CI: 0.22–1.11, p = 0.09), while the prevalence of ILA in COPD patients with other lung cancer subtypes was comparable to that in patients without lung cancer.

Lastly, we investigated the potential impact of cancer therapy on the formation of ILA. To this end, we divided the 218 patients with lung cancer comorbidity into two subgroups: 198 patients who had not received treatment and 20 patients who had undergone treatment prior to their ILA diagnosis. No significant difference in ILA prevalence was observed between the two subgroups (Supplementary Table 1), suggesting that lung cancer therapy does not influence ILA formation. A similar analysis was conducted for patients with comorbidities of cancers other than lung cancer, and again, no significant difference in ILA prevalence was found between the treated and untreated subgroups (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we determined the association of ILA with demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics, as well as comorbidities in a cohort of 1131 hospitalized COPD patients. We observed negligible associations between ILA and demographic, clinical, or laboratory parameters. However, we found that ILA in COPD patients is significantly associated with multiple comorbidities, including lung cancer, cancers other than lung cancer, and heart failure. To our knowledge, this study is the first to demonstrate such associations between ILA and comorbidities in COPD patients.

Unexpectedly, the presence of ILA was associated with a decreased risk of lung cancer comorbidity in patients with COPD. This contrasts with previous findings in the general population, where ILA has been suggested as a risk factor for lung cancer. For instance, Wille et al., through CT analysis of 1990 smokers from the Danish Lung Cancer Screening Trial, demonstrated that the prevalence of ILA in lung cancer patients is approximately three times higher than that in healthy controls [18]. Similarly, Whittaker Brown and colleagues, utilizing data from 25,041 participants in the National Lung Screening Trial, concluded that ILA serves as an independent risk factor for lung cancer [15]. This discrepancy in the association of ILA with lung cancer is intriguing and may be attributed to differences in the populations studied. Previous investigations primarily focused on the general population, whereas our study specifically targeted patients with COPD. This disparity underscores the importance of considering underlying conditions and their interactions when assessing the risk factors for comorbidities such as lung cancer. Further research is warranted to elucidate the mechanisms underlying this unexpected association and its implications for COPD patients.

Among the major histological subtypes of lung cancer, only lung ADC showed a significant association with ILA in patients with COPD, with a lower prevalence of ILA in COPD patient with co-morbidity of lung ADC than in those without lung cancer. This finding aligns with previous research demonstrating lower prevalence of ILA in patients with lung ADC compared to SCC [19]. Similar trends have been observed in studies investigating ILD in lung cancer patients [20].

In addition to cancers, another co-morbidity, namely heart failure, was associated with ILA, with a higher prevalence of heart failure in COPD patients with ILA compared to those without ILA. To our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate such associations. Given the reported radiological similarities between ILA and hypostatic changes associated with certain heart conditions [21], COPD patients who also had heart failure and were positive for ILA during hospitalization but negative for ILA one month after discharge were excluded from the study. However, it remains possible that some patients with heart failure might exhibit hypostatic change-mediated radiological features similar to ILA for a period longer than one month. Therefore, caution is warranted when interpreting the association between ILA and heart failure. Further investigations are necessary to clarify the association between ILA and heart failure as comorbid conditions.

Our study has three major limitations. Firstly, all COPD patients included were hospitalized cases, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings to all COPD cases. Secondly, the laboratory characteristics analyzed in our study were likely influenced by the cause of hospitalization, potentially confounding our findings. Thirdly, due to the male predominance among smokers in China [22], the majority of COPD patients included in this study were male. Therefore, the findings of this study may not be generalizable to female patients with COPD.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that the presence of ILA in patients with COPD is associated with multiple co-morbidities of the disease, particularly lung ADC. Further investigations are warranted to better understand these relationships and their clinical implications.

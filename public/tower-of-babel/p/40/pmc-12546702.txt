Research on the spatio-temporal variation of agricultural film usage in China and the effect of threshold crop yield
Authors: Chengtao Gao, Guiting Mu, Zhenming Zhang, Wenmin Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAgricultural films (AGF) are widely used to improve crop yield, conserve soil moisture, and reduce herbicide use, but excessive application has caused substantial soil contamination. Using data from the China Agricultural Statistical Yearbook and the China Environmental Statistical Yearbook, this study systematically examined the spatiotemporal evolution and drivers of AGF usage in China from 1998 to 2022, employing standard deviation ellipse analysis, spatiotemporal statistics, correlation analysis, random forest modeling, and panel threshold modeling. Results show that AGF usage increased by 115.64%, rising from 120.69 × 107 kg in 1998 to 260.26 × 107 kg in 2016, before declining by 8.75% by 2022. The usage center shifted 235.91 km southwest, with policy-related factors—particularly construction occupation of farmland—showing an increasing influence (correlation coefficient + 64.35%). A usage threshold of 48,445 tons was identified, beyond which marginal yield benefits dropped sharply; in high-use regions, the yield increase effect of cultivated land construction was 11.5 times greater than in low-use regions (β = 111.225 vs. 9.670). These findings suggest differentiated management strategies: in high-use regions, enforce threshold-based caps on AGF input and accelerate adoption of biodegradable or recyclable alternatives; in low-use regions, increase input toward the threshold to optimize yield gains; and implement spatial controls to prevent AGF expansion into ecologically fragile southwestern areas. These targeted policies, grounded in quantitative evidence, provide a pathway to balance agricultural productivity with environmental sustainability.Keywords: Agricultural film, Spatiotemporal changes, Response characteristics, Pollution, PolicySubject terms: Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

The agricultural films widely used in agricultural production, such as plastic mulch and greenhouse films, are collectively referred to as agricultural films. They play a positive role in improving soil moisture conditions, regulating temperature, optimizing nitrogen levels, and enhancing crop transpiration and photosynthesis efficiency, thereby promoting crop growth and increasing yield1,2. However, an issue of overuse leads to increased pollution of terrestrial ecosystems due to residual agricultural films in the fields. This poses a significant risk to the environment. Research has shown that in regions with high plastic mulch usage, such as Xinjiang, the residual amount of agricultural films in the soil can reach as high as 265.3 kg/hm². This highlights the severity of agricultural film pollution in the plow layer3. In addition, agricultural films may also become a reservoir for pesticides in the fields4. The organophosphonate antioxidants contained in agricultural films are one of the significant sources of organophosphate pollution in farmland5. They may also release harmful substances such as phthalates6. Long-term use of agricultural films not only pollutes the surface soil but also causes residual films to migrate into deeper layers of soil. This increases the difficulty of removing microplastics from the soil, making plastic pollution a key issue in the restoration of soil contamination7.

Currently, research on agricultural films, both domestically and internationally, mainly focuses on their impact on crop yields. And its including food crops8–10 such as rice, wheat, and corn; vegetable crops11–13 such as radish, potato, tomato, cauliflower, apple, and strawberry; as well as cash crops like cotton14. Studies have pointed out that plastic films residual in cotton fields has hinder the normal growth of cotton seedlings and affect the movement of soil moisture and salinity15. In addition, research has also focused on the various impacts of agricultural films on soil and agricultural ecosystems. Plastic film coverage increases soil temperature and moisture, raises soil carbon dioxide concentration, and promotes the abundance of nitrification-related functional genes, thereby facilitating the emission of N₂O16,17. Plastic film coverage has a positive impact on soil microbial communities, significantly increasing soil microbial α-diversity, richness, abundance, and ecological function18. At the same time, agricultural film coverage also affects soil nematode communities, with the total number of nematodes in most mulched fields being lower than that in non-mulched fields19. Plastic coverage can also affect the net primary productivity of agricultural ecosystems, soil carbon flux, and organic carbon balance20. Research has shown that plastic in agricultural soils, as a hidden carbon source, can stimulate microbial activity and increase carbon dioxide emissions21. However, there is an issue of overuse and insufficient recycling of agricultural films22,23.

The current research on agricultural films mainly focuses on aspects such as usage effectiveness and environmental effects. The research on spatiotemporal distribution of agricultural film usage (AGF) is relatively limited. Existing studies in this area predominantly concentrate on specific cities or smaller administrative regions, lacking analyses conducted at a broader national or global spatial scale over extended time periods. Moreover, most of the available assessments for China have relied on data prior to 2020, which reduces their relevance to current agricultural and environmental conditions24–26. To address these limitations, this study systematically examines the spatiotemporal evolution patterns of AGF in China from 1998 to 2022. The results of this study provide a scientific basis for formulating region-specific management strategies for AGF, such as optimizing film application intensity in high-usage areas, promoting the adoption of biodegradable films in ecologically sensitive zones, and improving recycling systems in regions with rapid usage growth. Furthermore, the identified key influencing factors can help policymakers prioritize interventions, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of agricultural plastic pollution control.

Research methods

Data

This research covers the 31 provinces of China, with provincial-level administrative regions serving as the sample units. The spatiotemporal distribution of AGF is explored based on China’s nine major agricultural regions (data source: Resource and Environmental Science Data Platform). It reveals the response characteristics of AGF to crop yield (CL), plastic film usage conditions (NM), and Policy factors leading to the reduction of cultivated land area (ZC).

The data for AGF, CL, NM, and ZC are all sourced from the China Rural Statistical Yearbook and the China Environmental Statistical Yearbook. CL includes the yields of Yield of hemp crops (BAF), cotton yield (COT), grain crop yield (GRA), oil crop yield (OIL), sugar crop yield (SUG), yield of tobacco leaf crop (TOB), and yield of vegetable crops (WEB); NM includes two factors: area covered by mulch (AREA) and plastic film usage (PLM). ZC encompasses four key elements that lead to the decrease in arable land: adjustments in agricultural structure (AGS), land conversion for construction purposes (BUD), cultivated land degradation (DIR), and ecological restoration (RET).

Random forest algorithm

The study first conducts a random forest importance ranking of variables affecting AGF. The most influential variable from each category (NM, CL, and ZC) is then selected, followed by univariate linear regression analysis with AGF. This process provides a systematic assessment of the variables’ impacts on AGF.

The threshold effect test

This study systematically analyzed the nonlinear relationship between the input amount of agricultural film (AGF) and crop yield (CL) through the panel threshold regression model. The panel threshold model constructed by Hansen (1999)29 was used for analysis, and the input amount of agricultural film (AGF) was selected as the threshold variable. Firstly, standardized preprocessing is carried out on the original data, and logarithmic transformation is performed on the key variables to eliminate the dimensional influence. The entropy weight method was used to calculate the weight of crop yield and obtain the comprehensive score of crop yield. Subsequently, a two-region regression model with AGF as the threshold variable was constructed. The value distribution of AGF was traversed through the grid search method. The candidate threshold was determined with minimizing the sum of squared residuals as the objective function. 500 Bootstrap resampling was used to simulate the asymptotic distribution of the likelihood ratio statistic, and the 95% confidence interval was calculated to test the statistical significance of the threshold effect. After confirming the optimal threshold value, the samples were divided into the low-input zone system (AGF ≤ λ) and the high-input zone system (AGF > λ). The least square method was used respectively to estimate the influence coefficients of control variables such as agricultural structure adjustment (AGS), construction occupation of cultivated land (BUD), disaster-damaged cultivated land (DIR), and ecological return to farmland (RET). And compare and analyze the differences in the marginal effects of AGF in different regions; Finally, the uniqueness of the threshold is verified by constructing a sequence curve of likelihood ratio statistics. Results and analysis

Temporal distribution characteristics of agricultural AGF in China

From 1998 to 2022, agricultural film (AGF) use in China first increased and then declined, rising from 120.69 × 10⁷ kg in 1998 to a peak of 260.26 × 10⁷ kg in 2016 before falling to 237.5 × 10⁷ kg in 2022 (Fig. 1). Usage intensity grew rapidly from 7.75 kg/ha (1998) to 15.60 kg/ha (2015) and then stabilized. Over the same period, the national grain sown area expanded steadily at 0.4% annually. Spatially, provinces with high AGF use (≥ 9 × 10⁷ kg) increased from 6% in 1998 to 39% in 2022, concentrated in key agricultural regions such as the North China Plain, Yangtze River Plain, Sichuan Basin, and Xinjiang.

The grain sown area and the usage and intensity of AGF in china from 1998 to 2022.

From 1998 to 2022, AGF distribution in China became increasingly dispersed, with its center of gravity shifting overall from east to southwest (Fig. 2). Initially located in Jincheng, Shanxi (1998), it moved south to Jiaozuo, Henan (1998–2004, 10.83 km/year), then west to Sanmenxia, Henan (2004–2010, 15.76 km/year), further west to Yuncheng, Shanxi (2010–2016, 23.47 km/year), and finally southwest to Weinan, Shaanxi (2016–2022, 9.74 km/year). Over 24 years, the center shifted 235.91 km in total—from Jincheng, Shanxi to Weinan, Shaanxi. (Fig. 2).

Shifting of the center of gravity and GMI of AGF in China.

Response characteristics of AGF to different influencing factors

From 1998 to 2022, the importance of CL, NM, and ZC in influencing AGF showed an increasing trend (Fig. 3). Throughout the study period, PLM within NM remained the most significant single factor affecting AGF, with a correlation coefficient of 0.92, indicating a very strong and stable positive relationship between plastic film usage and total AGF. This suggests that changes in crop yield patterns exert a more pronounced influence on AGF than factors related to cultivated land reduction. Among the CL components, grain yield (GRA) showed the highest growth rate in influence (70.47%), implying that the expansion and intensification of grain production likely drove a substantial increase in agricultural film demand. The impact of BUD and RET on AGF showed an initial increase followed by a decrease. Their highest impact during 2010–2016 (reaching 1.06 and 4.30, respectively) reflects a phase of significant land-use change and ecological restoration projects, which initially stimulated demand for agricultural films but subsequently led to reduced usage as land availability and cultivation practices adjusted.

Random forest importance ranking of influencing factors on AGF and their univariate linear regression.

From 1998 to 2022, the correlations between AGF and most influencing factors was mostly positive (Fig. 4). Throughout this period, the correlation coefficients of AREA and PLM with AGF generally exceeded 0.80, except during 1998–2004, when the coefficient between AREA and AGF dropped to 0.479. Among the seven crop types examined, WEB exhibited the strongest positive correlation with AGF. In contrast, the relationship between SUG and AGF was consistently negative, with correlation coefficients ranging from − 0.137 to -0.104 over the study period. Within the ZC region, BUD was identified as the factor most strongly correlated with AGF.

Forest plot of correlation coefficients between each factor and AGF.

The relationship between the threshold of AGF and CL

We explored the relationship between the threshold of agricultural film usage and crop yield (Fig. 5). Through analysis, we determined that the optimal threshold value for the usage of agricultural film is 48,445 t. The confidence interval ranges from 45,232.77 to 53,754.7 t, indicating that we have 95% confidence that the actual optimal threshold value is within this range. Further analysis of the crop yield model revealed the influence of each variable on the yield in different regions. In the low-zone system, the planting area significantly increased the yield (coefficient was 9.670, p < 0.01), while the influence weakened in the high-zone system (coefficient was 1.059, p < 0.01). The occupation of cultivated land for construction significantly increased the yield in the high-zone system (with a coefficient of 111.225, p < 0.01), while the effect was not significant in the low-zone system. Other variables such as disaster-damaged cultivated land, ecological return to farmland and agricultural structure adjustment did not show significant effects in both regions. The high-zone system model yielded an R² value of 0.503, which, although not high in some disciplines, is considered a moderate level of explanatory power in socio-environmental studies with complex, multi-factor relationships. Here, we emphasize that the interpretation is relative—this value is notably higher than that in the low-zone system—indicating that, compared with low-usage regions, the influence of variables on output in the high-zone system is clearer and more stable. The overall results show that there are significant regional differences in the influence of the threshold of agricultural film usage and planting area on crop yield, especially in the high-zone system.

Discussion

Spatial and temporal variation characteristics of AGF

This study selects specific regions in China to represent Asia for the purpose of comparing agricultural plastic usage between Asia and Europe. Globally, China’s consumption of agricultural film ranks first, accounting for 82.35% of the world’s total, thereby highlighting its significant contribution to global agricultural film utilization (Fig. 6). China, though initiating the use of agricultural film in 1980, which was later than France’s adoption in 1961, has experienced a growth rate in its usage that significantly surpasses that of countries such as France. This study found that the concentration of AGF at the provincial level in China has been continuously weakening, with a gradually more pronounced trend of dispersion. This reflects significant differences in agricultural development strategies across provinces. Furthermore, although Europe’s total agricultural plastic usage is comparable to that of the Sichuan Basin and Northeast Plain, the proportion of agricultural film utilization in Europe remains markedly lower than in the Sichuan Basin (92.54%) and the Northeast Plain (92.03%)30.

The impact of different factors on the AGF

The usage of mulch film exhibits a significant self-driving effect. At the same time, it serves as the main driving factor for the overall AGF. This study shows that AREA and PLM are both significantly positively correlated with AGF between 1998 and 2022. This relationship indicates that AREA and PLM are the main driving factors for the increase in AGF31. This reflects the cumulative effect of agricultural plastic film technology after its promotion in agricultural production. Except for SUG, which shows a negative correlation with AGF, the yields of other crops are positively correlated with AGF between 1998 and 2022. However, the impact of TOB on AGF shifted from a positive correlation to a negative correlation between 2016 and 2022 (Fig. 4). This shift may be related to the reduction in tobacco planting area, adjustments in planting structure, and changes in tobacco industry policies32. WEB exhibited a significant driving effect on AGF, with a correlation coefficient reaching 0.804 between 2004 and 2010 (Fig. 4). This result indicates that vegetable cultivation has a high dependency on agricultural plastic film. This is because vegetable cultivation requires higher standards for pest and disease control as well as water management, which can be effectively met by plastic film mulching technology1. However, after 2010, agricultural planting structures became more diversified, with a shift from being dominated by vegetable cultivation to including a wider variety of crops, many of which have a lower or negligible demand for plastic film. As a result, the influence of crop type on plastic film usage was no longer concentrated on high-demand crops such as vegetables, but became distributed across multiple crop categories, leading to a more dispersed overall driving force of crop yield factors33. The impact of policy factors on the AGF is characterized by a certain degree of complexity and stage-specificity. DIR showed a significant negative correlation with AGF between 1998 and 2022. This indicates that natural disasters have an inhibitory effect on the AGF34. This is likely because disasters disrupt agricultural production conditions, reducing the demand for agricultural plastic film. AGS, BUD, and RET all show a positive correlation with AGF. This indicates that policies have a certain promoting effect on the AGF35. However, the relationship between RET and AGF shifted from a positive correlation to a negative correlation between 2016 and 2022. This shift may reflect changes in ecological policies such as returning farmland to forests, which can lead to a reduction in cultivated land area and a decrease in agricultural production intensity, thereby suppressing the demand for agricultural plastic film. At the same time, the overall influence of policy factors further weakened during this period. This indicates that, with the development of agricultural production technologies and the advancement of marketization, the direct intervention of policies in AGF is decreasing.

Management and future outlook

Agricultural plastic film has made significant contributions to agricultural development, playing an important role in increasing agricultural production and income as a vital input in facility-based agriculture13. This study identified an optimal threshold of 48,445 tons for agricultural film usage (95% CI: 45,232.77–53,754.7). When this threshold was exceeded, the marginal effect of planting area on yield significantly attenuated from 9.670 (P < 0.01) in the low-zone system to 1.059 (P < 0.01) in the high-zone system. The positive effect of construction-related occupation of cultivated land on yield was significantly enhanced in the high-zone system (β = 111.225, P < 0.01). This threshold effect indicates that there is a distinct inflection point of scale benefits in the input of agricultural films - when the usage intensity exceeds the critical value, its improvement on agricultural productivity shows a marginal diminishing effect, and at the same time, the characteristics of intensive land use become increasingly prominent in high-intensity usage areas. When formulating the management policies for agricultural films, regional differentiation strategies need to be implemented. In low-use areas, the input of agricultural films can be moderately increased to achieve significant yield-increasing benefits, while in high-use areas, emphasis should be placed on optimizing land use efficiency and controlling the incremental input of agricultural films. Moreover, some studies have shown that for every 1% increase in agricultural plastic film residue, agricultural productivity decreases by 0.10%36,37. In the coming years, it is anticipated that around 6,500 square kilometers of farmland globally will adopt plastic film for agricultural purposes38. However, the current reuse and recycling rates of agricultural plastic waste remain extremely low, falling below 10%39. We suggest establishing a three-in-one governance system of “intensity control - recycling - alternative development” on the basis of threshold management. Such as developing biodegradable plastic films40, new material-based mulching films41, and alternatives such as shallow incorporation of organic mulch42 to replace traditional plastic film usage. At present, the global plastic agricultural film coverage area is expected to reach 6,500 square kilometers38. Given that the reuse rate of agricultural plastic waste is lower than 10%39, we suggest establishing a three-in-one governance system of “intensity control - recycling - alternative development” based on threshold management: Replace traditional plastic films with innovative materials such as biodegradable films40, new base films41, and organic mulching films42. Additionally, an intelligent monitoring system integrating Internet of Things (IoT) technology could be implemented to achieve a dynamic balance between agricultural film input intensity and the land’s carrying capacity.

Conclusion

During the period from 1998 to 2016, with a stable annual expansion of 0.4% in the sown area of grain, the usage of agricultural films soared from 120.69 × 10⁷kg to 260.26 × 10⁷kg, and the utilization intensity doubled to 15.60 kg/ha. However, when the total amount decreased by 8.7% to 237.5 × 10⁷kg after 2016, the utilization intensity tended to stabilize, marking a shift in the usage pattern from scale expansion to efficiency improvement. In terms of spatial pattern, the proportion of high-utilization provinces has expanded from 6% to 39%, covering major production areas such as the North China Plain. Meanwhile, the center of use has continuously shifted 235.9 km to the southwest, reflecting the westward advance trend of agricultural productivity layout. The analysis of the driving mechanism indicates that grain yield (PLM) becomes the core driving factor with an explanatory power of 70.47% (r = 0.92), while the 48,445 threshold for the use of agricultural films (95%CI:45,232.77-53,754.7) reveals the law of diminishing marginal returns. Especially in the high-altitude system, the occupation of cultivated land construction has a significant effect on increasing yield (β = 111.225). The study also found significant regional heterogeneity. The yield of the low-zone system mainly depends on the expansion of planting area (β = 9.670), while the high-zone system relies more on infrastructure investment. Moreover, the vegetable yield (WEB) is positively correlated with agricultural film and continuously negatively correlated with sugarcane (SUG) (r=-0.137 to -0.104), which provides a basis for implementing differentiated management. In low-use areas, moderately increasing film input can still yield substantial productivity gains. In high-use areas, focus should be on improving land use efficiency, strictly controlling incremental film application, and enhancing infrastructure.

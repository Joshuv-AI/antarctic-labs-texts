HPRT-YOLO: Enhanced lightweight UAV maritime search and rescue model based on YOLOv11n
Authors: Zhengyang Gao, Yuantai Li, Yipeng Shao, Fazhan Tao, Zhikai Wang, Guanyang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummarySmall target detection is crucial for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) maritime search and rescue (SAR), but problems, including low target spatial resolution, dynamic marine environments, and severe background interference, often cause frequent missed/false detections and insufficient real-time performance. To address these issues, we propose HPRT-YOLO, a lightweight model based on YOLOv11n. We present the C3k2_Faster_CGLU module to enhance small-target feature representation and reduce computational complexity, strengthen multi-scale fusion via the ASF-P2 network with Zoom_cat and ScalSeq, adopt LAMP pruning to eliminate redundancy, utilize BCKD knowledge distillation to retain feature extraction capability while cutting parameters, and employ a WIoU-v2+MPDIoU hybrid loss to optimize recall and localization. On two public maritime SAR datasets, HPRT-YOLO outperforms YOLOv11n by 7% in mAP50 and 9.5% in recall, compresses to 2.3 MB (58.19% smaller), and maintains 94.9 FPS at a batch size of 1, providing efficient technical support for UAV maritime SAR.Subject areas: remote sensing, computer science

Introduction

Maritime transportation, shipping, and fisheries are central to the global economy. However, maritime accidents—such as vessel capsizing, man-overboard incidents, and oil tanker spills—continue to be reported frequently, and sustained demand for efficient maritime search and rescue (SAR) is therefore driven. One of the core challenges in maritime SAR is the rapid and accurate identification of survivors and small maritime targets (e.g., life rafts and buoys) from large-scale remote-sensing imagery.1 Due to dynamic sea backgrounds, specular reflections, and extremely small target sizes, manual surveillance and conventional approaches are often affected by missed detections and false alarms; moreover, accident sites cannot always be reached promptly by traditional SAR assets such as helicopters and patrol vessels.

In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based maritime SAR has increasingly been regarded as a responsive and flexible solution, owing to advances in UAV technologies.2 In this paradigm, real-time detection of small targets on the sea surface is considered essential for improving rescue efficiency and operational safety. Although the YOLO family is well-suited for real-time tasks due to fast inference, YOLOv11 may still be affected by false alarms and missed detections under complex sea states and low-resolution small-target conditions. To address these issues, HPRT-YOLO is proposed as a lightweight detector based on YOLOv11n and is designed to maintain high accuracy. Key network modules are improved, and the overall architecture and feature extraction strategy are optimized so that computational efficiency and detection performance can be balanced, thereby providing a more effective and reliable solution for UAV-based maritime SAR. A faster block and a CGLU (convolutional gated linear unit) are integrated into the standard C3K2 module of YOLOv11n, and a better compute-accuracy trade-off is targeted while representational capacity is maintained. Gated feature interaction and selective information routing are employed to amplify informative responses and attenuate background interference, thereby enhancing small-target representation and detection robustness in complex maritime scenes.

•Efficient model compression strategy

Efficient model compression strategy

LAMP-based importance-driven structured pruning is applied to YOLOv11n, and low-contribution channels and connections are removed to reduce redundant computation and model complexity. Meanwhile, critical cross-layer fusion routes are preserved to mitigate the disruption of fine-grained small-target cues, thereby maintaining feature propagation and representation stability for low-resolution small targets, while deployment efficiency is improved.

•Teacher-side detection refinement with distillation constraints

Teacher-side detection refinement with distillation constraints

Detect_LSDECD is incorporated into the teacher model so that the detection head is refined and sensitivity to fine-grained cues (e.g., small-target boundaries and local textures) is enhanced. In addition, BCKD knowledge distillation is employed to guide the student model; by constraining key representations and prediction distributions, information-preserving transfer of discriminative small-target knowledge is facilitated, and detection reliability is improved under complex sea backgrounds in a lightweight configuration.

•Cross-scale alignment and adaptive fusion

Cross-scale alignment and adaptive fusion

An improved ASF-P2 detection layer is designed, integrating SSFF, TFF, and CAPM as complementary enhancement units to enhance the discriminability of shallow high-resolution features. Zoom_cat is adopted for cross-scale nonlinear alignment to mitigate spatial and semantic inconsistencies across scales, and ScalSeq dynamic-weight fusion is employed to adaptively reweight multi-scale contributions. Experimental results indicate that this design provides more effective multi-scale feature aggregation than the conventional fixed-weight P2 layer, thereby supporting improved small-target detection performance.

Related work

Before deep learning, traditional object detection methods relied on handcrafted features (HFs) and machine learning algorithms such as SVMs3 and Random Forests.4 These methods faced three limitations: inability to handle environmental interference and variations in target morphology, poor generalization, and degraded performance across different scenes, and difficulty meeting real-time requirements with large-scale datasets.

Object detection can be categorized into general-purpose, scene-specific, real-time, and high-precision detection, with methods such as region proposal-based, regression-based, segmentation-based, and deep learning-based approaches. Detection methods are also divided into single-stage and two-stage types, where single-stage methods prioritize speed and two-stage methods emphasize accuracy.

With the rise of deep learning, research shifted toward two-stage algorithms (e.g., R-CNN)5 and single-stage algorithms (e.g., YOLO and SSD).6 Two-stage algorithms first generate candidate regions and then refine them, while single-stage methods predict categories and positions simultaneously, making them faster but less accurate. Two-stage algorithms generally achieve higher accuracy but are more computationally intensive.

To improve small object detection, models such as Faster R-CNN,7 MSA R-CNN,8 and FG R-CNN9 have been proposed, improving accuracy through better feature extraction and bounding box optimization. However, these models are computationally expensive, limiting real-time performance.

Single-stage algorithms such as SSD,10 RetinaNet,11 and YOLO12 are faster and more suitable for real-time applications, as they directly generate prediction boxes without requiring candidate region generation. Recent advancements in YOLO include TPH-YOLOv5,13 which improves global feature modeling, and FE-YOLOv5,14 which uses deformable convolutions. However, these improvements often lead to increased model complexity.

Research has also focused on specific challenges, such as traffic sign detection (STC-YOLO),15 orientation-sensitive detection (SO-YOLOv5),16 and dense object detection in remote sensing images. In the UAV detection literature, QHNet17 and NUAV-YOLO18 represent recent efforts toward real-time and lightweight detectors for UAV platforms, highlighting the importance of balancing accuracy and efficiency for practical deployment. In maritime scenarios, the Neptune-X model19 enhances maritime object detection with an active cross-domain strategy, while HMPNet20 focuses on shipborne detection with feature aggregation.

Despite advances, challenges remain, especially in detecting low-resolution targets and handling complex maritime environments. Methods that improve accuracy often introduce higher complexity, limiting real-time applicability in UAV-based maritime SAR. The HPRT-YOLO model addresses these issues by optimizing network structure and applying pruning and knowledge distillation, reducing complexity while maintaining accuracy, making it ideal for UAV-based maritime missions.

Results

Algorithm analysis

To verify whether the improved modules effectively extract and fuse multi-scale features, and to visualize the model’s response to input images at different scales, gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM++)21 is employed in this study. Grad-CAM++ is recognized as an enhancement of the classical Grad-CAM algorithm, which performs better in complex scenes and multi-target detection. By using class gradients to identify regions of interest, valuable insights are provided into whether the network is attending to the correct features.

Among the visualized heatmap layers, particular attention is directed to the P2 layer because it corresponds to a high-resolution, low-semantic feature map that is especially critical for small-target detection. By comparing the heatmaps before and after the improvements, the effectiveness of modules such as C3K2_Faster_CGLU, the pruning strategy, and Zoom_cat in enhancing the model’s sensitivity and representation of small targets can be assessed. In this study, both YOLOv11n and HPRT-YOLO are evaluated on the SeaDrones, see dataset, with Grad-CAM++ visualizations generated for feature layers 2 through 5, as shown in Figure 2.

The experimental results indicate that good attention coverage for large targets such as ships is provided by YOLOv11n, but insufficient coverage for small targets such as individual personnel is observed, leading to missed detections. Although some disturbance from sea-surface ripples is still observed, a larger portion of the target is attended to by the model. Furthermore, in the thermal maps, the target regions and their surroundings are shown to appear brighter and more focused, indicating stronger discriminative capability.

Ablation study analysis of the effectiveness of the LSDECD detection head

To justify the necessity of introducing the LSDECD detection head in the teacher model, a dedicated distillation ablation is conducted as reported in Table 6. The pruned student baseline (after LAMP) achieves mAP50/mAP50–95 of 0.832/0.526 with 108.8 FPS. When the teacher is trained without the LSDECD head (teacher 1), its performance reaches 0.864/0.565, and the corresponding distilled student improves to 0.841/0.529, yielding only a marginal gain over the pruned baseline (+0.009 mAP50, +0.003 mAP50–95), while the speed decreases to 99.2 FPS. In contrast, equipping the teacher with LSDECD (teacher 2) further boosts the teacher's accuracy to 0.876/0.583. More importantly, the student distilled from teacher 2 attains 0.856/0.534, which corresponds to a substantially larger improvement over the pruned baseline (+0.024 mAP50, +0.008 mAP50–95) and also surpasses the student distilled from Teacher 1 (+0.015 mAP50, +0.005 mAP50–95), with comparable inference speed (99.7 FPS vs. 99.2 FPS). These results indicate that strengthening the teacher using LSDECD provides higher-quality supervision for knowledge transfer, thereby enabling the student to recover more accuracy under compression with only a negligible additional runtime cost.

Ablation experiments

As shown in Table 8, a series of enhancement and lightweighting modules were progressively integrated into YOLOv11n, and the trade-offs between detection performance and computational cost were systematically compared across different stages (all FPS values were measured under the same test setting with bs = 1). When only the ASF module was introduced, mAP@50 was increased from 78.6% (baseline) to 84.4% (+5.8 percentage points), with precision (P) and recall (R) of 89.7% and 79.9%, respectively. Meanwhile, the number of parameters was increased from 2,266,728 to 2,729,152, the computational cost was 10.9 GFLOPs, and the inference speed was maintained at 108.5 FPS. These results indicate that a substantial accuracy gain could be obtained with a moderate increase in computational cost, which is consistent with the combined requirements of real-time operation and reliability in UAV-based maritime search-and-rescue scenarios.

After the C3K2-Faster-CGLU module was further incorporated, mAP@50 was improved to 84.9%, while P and R were measured as 90.6% and 79.9%, respectively. At the same time, the parameter count was slightly reduced to 2,564,116, the model size was decreased from 5.7 MB to 5.4 MB, the GFLOPs were reduced to 10.5, and the FPS was still maintained at a relatively high level of 99.1. Therefore, simultaneous gains in accuracy and efficiency were achieved, and a favorable performance-cost synergy was demonstrated.

With the introduction of the Wise-MPDIoU module, mAP@50 was further increased to 85.0%, and P and R were 88.6% and 79.9%, respectively. Although the parameter count rose to 2,729,152, the model size and GFLOPs were nearly unchanged, and only minor fluctuations in FPS were observed. It was thus suggested that detection performance could be further improved with a negligible additional computational burden.

When LAMP pruning was applied, mAP@50 was reduced to 83.2%, with P and R of 89.7% and 77.5%, respectively. However, the parameter count was markedly decreased to 914,485, the model size was compressed to 2.3 MB (a 58.19% reduction relative to the baseline), the GFLOPs were reduced to 6.1, and the FPS was measured as 93.1. This stage reflects a typical compression trade-off, in which a limited accuracy decrease was exchanged for substantial reductions in resource footprint and computational complexity, thereby establishing a basis for subsequent distillation-based compensation. On this basis, after BCKD knowledge distillation was performed, mAP@50 was recovered and further increased to 85.6%, while P and R were improved to 88.5% and 81.1%, respectively. Meanwhile, the lightweight advantages achieved by pruning were fully preserved, as the parameter count, model size, and GFLOPs remained at 914,485, 2.3 MB, and 6.1 GFLOPs, respectively, and the inference speed reached 94.9 FPS. These results demonstrate that the accuracy loss induced by pruning could be effectively compensated, while the compact deployment profile was maintained.

Overall, through the stepwise integration of enhancement modules and the “pruning–distillation” lightweighting strategy, detection performance was improved while resource consumption was substantially reduced. Compared with the baseline YOLOv11n, mAP@50 was increased from 78.6% to 85.6% (+7.0 percentage points), GFLOPs were decreased from 6.5 to 6.1, and the model size was compressed from 5.5 MB to 2.3 MB (a 58.19% reduction). These results indicate that a more balanced compromise between accuracy and efficiency was achieved, and that the proposed method is better suited for UAV-based maritime search-and-rescue remote sensing applications, where both high accuracy and real-time efficiency are required.

Discussion

Comprehensive analysis

The proposed HPRT-YOLO model significantly enhances small target detection performance through a series of innovative optimizations, particularly in complex maritime environments. First, by integrating the faster block and CGLU convolutional gating unit into the standard C3K2 module of YOLOv11n, a balance between computational accuracy and speed is optimized while ensuring efficient feature extraction, thereby increasing the model’s robustness in complex backgrounds. Meanwhile, the importance-driven structured pruning strategy based on LAMP effectively reduces redundant computations while maintaining the integrity of key cross-layer fusion paths, preventing pruning from disrupting the transmission of fine-grained small target information, thus improving model deployment efficiency and adaptability. Additionally, the Detect_LSDECD module, introduced to refine the detection head, enhances the model’s ability to capture small target boundaries and local texture cues. Combined with BCKD knowledge distillation, it facilitates the efficient transfer of discriminative small target knowledge, further improving the model’s detection reliability in dynamic sea surface backgrounds. In terms of cross-scale feature fusion, the improved ASF-P2 layer, by integrating multiple complementary units and incorporating Zoom_cat for cross-scale non-linear alignment and ScalSeq for dynamic weight fusion, significantly improves small target detection performance. Finally, the combination of dynamically weighted WIoU-v2 and MPDIoU loss functions not only enhances small target recall but also effectively suppresses background interference, ensuring efficient and precise real-time detection. Heatmap visualizations and comparative experiments further validate the model’s advantages in focusing on small targets and adapting to complex maritime scenes, allowing for more accurate identification of key targets such as personnel and life-saving equipment (Figure 8).

Experimental results have demonstrated that the performance of HPRT-YOLO on two public datasets related to maritime SAR has been improved by 7% compared with that of YOLOv11n. Meanwhile, real-time performance (94.9 FPS) has been guaranteed, and the model size and computational complexity have been substantially reduced, which demonstrates the efficient and accurate detection capability of the model in complex marine environments.

In summary, HPRT-YOLO achieves a balance in detection accuracy, model lightweighting, and real-time performance through structural optimization and algorithmic innovation, providing an efficient and reliable UAV-based maritime search-and-rescue solution with strong practical application potential.

Comparison of detection accuracy of HPRT-YOLO with other models

As shown in Table 9 and Figure 9, the proposed HPRT-YOLO achieves superior overall performance in maritime small-target detection tasks. With only a 2.3 MB model size, 0.914 M parameters, and a computational cost of 6.1 GFLOPs, the model achieves mAP@50 = 0.856, mAP(50–95) = 0.534, p = 0.885, and R = 0.811, while maintaining real-time inference at 94.9 FPS under bs = 1 conditions. Compared with mainstream lightweight YOLO models (such as YOLOv11n, YOLOv12n, and so forth), HPRT-YOLO outperforms in both mAP@50 and mAP(50–95), with further reductions in parameter count, demonstrating better suitability for UAV-based resource-constrained scenarios. Compared with DETR-family/Transformer-based detectors, RT-DETR-R18 slightly outperforms in accuracy (mAP@50 = 0.8605), but its model size (77 MB) and computational cost (57.0 GFLOPs) are significantly higher, with an inference speed of only 10.1 FPS, making it inadequate for real-time search-and-rescue operations. In contrast, HPRT-YOLO maintains near-optimal detection accuracy while significantly reducing model complexity and greatly improving speed. Further comparison with YOLOv8-DETR shows that HPRT-YOLO leads in mAP metrics, while also having a smaller model size and lower computational complexity, and achieving faster inference (94.9 FPS vs. 91.2 FPS). Dataset composition and processing

In this study, aerial images acquired by unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) were collected from two public datasets (Figure 13). The first dataset was SeaDronesSee,49 which was specifically developed for vision tasks involving UAVs and unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) in maritime environments. A total of 14,211 images were provided, with annotations covering six categories: ignored, swimmer, boat, jetski, life-saving appliance, and buoy. The dataset was split into 8,914 training images, 1,547 validation images, and 3,750 test images. Diverse and challenging scenarios, including nearshore, offshore, and harbor environments, were included. High-quality labels, such as bounding boxes and segmentation masks, were provided, and the dataset has been widely used in maritime monitoring and search-and-rescue research.

Label and distribution statistics of the training set class/instance distribution statistics of the training set are summarized to describe data balance and target frequency

The second dataset (AFO) was reported in An Ensemble Deep Learning Method with Optimised Weights for Drone-Based Water Rescue and Surveillance,50 which primarily targeted UAV-based maritime rescue and surveillance. This dataset contained 3,613 images, comprising 2,766 training images, 333 validation images, and 514 test images. Six object categories were annotated, namely human, wind/sup-board, boat, kayak, buoy, and sailboat. The dataset was particularly suitable for evaluating small-object detection performance under complex water-surface conditions.

In practical training, the above two datasets (Figure 14) were fused and reclassified into eight categories: ignored, human, boat, jetski, buoy, life-saving appliance, wind/sup-board, and kayak. The input images were subjected to advanced data augmentation techniques including random cropping, scaling, flipping, rotation, and mosaic augmentation. These augmentations were applied only during training and were disabled during validation and inference (i.e., no test-time augmentation was used).Through these augmentation methods, the model was enabled to access diverse transformed forms during the training process, thereby enhancing its generalization ability and robustness, as well as improving its adaptability to complex maritime search and rescue (SAR) scenarios. By introducing deep learning methods and optimizing model weights, the accuracy and robustness of target detection were significantly improved in this study, which provided strong technical support for the applications of maritime search and rescue and surveillance.

Improved strategies

HPRT-YOLO is proposed with multi-dimensional collaborative optimizations based on the YOLOv11n architecture, aiming to address the core challenge of low-resolution small object detection in UAV-based maritime search and rescue scenarios. Guided by three core objectives—improving detection accuracy, achieving model lightweighting, and ensuring real-time responsiveness—the model reconfigures an efficient feature interaction module by integrating the Faster Block and Convolutional Gated Linear Unit (CGLU), which enhances the feature representation capability of low-resolution small objects and mitigates redundant background interference. An importance-driven structured pruning strategy based on LAMP is introduced to prune redundant computational pathways while strictly preserving critical cross-layer feature transmission channels. The Detect_LSDECD detection head is adopted to optimize the detail-capturing capability of the teacher model, and combined with BCKD knowledge distillation technology, effective transfer and fidelity preservation of discriminative knowledge for small objects are realized. An improved ASF-P2 detection layer is designed, which significantly enhances the quality of multi-scale feature aggregation through the collaborative integration of multiple enhancement units, cross-scale nonlinear alignment, and a dynamic weight fusion mechanism. Furthermore, a dynamically weighted loss function formulated by WIoU-v2 and MPDIoU is constructed to further optimize the recall and localization accuracy of small objects. Through the collaborative optimizations across these five key dimensions, the model’s detection robustness and environmental adaptability in complex sea surface scenarios are comprehensively improved, while meeting the deployment requirements of resource-constrained devices.

Convolutionally gated linear unit (CGLU)

In this section, we provide a theoretical explanation and mathematical validation of the CGLU component in the C3k2_Faster_CGLU module, focusing on its computational design and performance-enhancement mechanism and explaining how it balances efficiency and accuracy for small-object detection in complex scenarios.

Principle: A Gated Linear Unit (GLU) is defined as consisting of two linear projections that are multiplied elementwise, with one branch being activated by a gating function. Unlike the SE mechanism, the gating signal of each token in GLU is derived from the token itself, and the receptive field of the gating branch is restricted to be no larger than that of the value branch. Based on this, it has been found that the addition of a minimal 3× 3 depth convolution before the activation function of the GLU gating branch allows the structure to be transformed into a nearest-neighbour feature-based gated-channel attention mechanism, which is aligned with the design concept of gated-channel attention. In Convolutional GLU (CGLU),51 a unique gating signal for each token is generated from its nearest fine-grained feature, enabling the drawback of coarse-grained global average pooling in the SE mechanism to be overcome. In addition, the absence of positional encoding in some ViT models is compensated by deep convolution, which is used to provide positional information. Meanwhile, the value branch depth is maintained at the same level as that of MLPs and GLUs, ensuring effective backpropagation.

Structure: The CGLU structure (Figure 19) is composed of input linear transformation, depth convolution, an activation function, and elementwise multiplication. First, the input data are subjected to two separate linear transformations. The output of one branch (branch one) is directly forwarded for subsequent computation, whereas the output of the other branch (branch two) is processed by a 3× 3 depth convolution and subsequently is activated by the GELU activation function. The activated output of branch two is then multiplied elementwise with the output of branch one to generate the final CGLU output. Through this design, local feature information extracted by depth convolution is utilised to regulate the gating signal, thereby enabling a nearest-neighbour feature-based gated-channel attention mechanism and ensuring that model performance is effectively improved.

LAMP pruning Strategy

To reduce the computational overhead of the model while preserving the detection accuracy for small targets, the LAMP (Layer-Adaptive Magnitude-based Pruning) strategy is adopted to induce structured sparsity in the network. Unlike global ranking based directly on weight magnitudes, cross-layer comparable importance measures are obtained in LAMP through intra-layer adaptive normalization, by which the pruning ratio can be allocated more robustly under a given sparsity level and excessive pruning of sensitive layers can be avoided. Subsequently, the basic pipeline and formal definitions of LAMP are presented, and the corresponding mathematical formulation is developed from three aspects, namely magnitude importance, intra-layer energy normalization, and global threshold selection.

Neural network pruning refers to the removal of “unimportant weights” from a model to meet practical constraints (Han et al., 2015),52 mitigate overfitting (Hanson & Pratt, 1988),53 enhance interpretability (Mozer & Smolensky, 1988),54 or deepen understanding of neural network training (Frankle & Carbin, 2019).55 To achieve model lightweighting, this study employs the LAMP pruning method (Lee et al., 2020),34 which follows the procedure outlined below. First, the weights of each convolutional layer are squared and sorted, after which a LAMP score is calculated for each layer. The LAMP score is a rescaled measure of weight importance designed to minimise model-level Frobenius norm distortion (L2 distortion) introduced by layer-level pruning, without requiring hyperparameter tuning or complex computation. Next, global sorting and pruning are performed: all connection weights are merged and ranked, and the smallest weights are set to zero until a predefined sparsity threshold (i.e., pruning rate) is reached, as illustrated in Figure 20.

LAMP score the LAMP score is the square of the weight size, normalized by the sum of all “surviving weights” in the layer. LAMP’s global pruning is equivalent to magnitude-based hierarchical pruning with automatically selected hierarchical sparsity

The definition and computation of the LAMP score are illustrated to explain how pruning importance is normalized across layers.

Formally, the LAMP score is defined as the squared weight magnitude, normalised by the sum of all surviving weights within the layer. Global pruning with LAMP thus automatically determines the sparsity of each layer while pruning connections based on weight magnitude.

To address the high complexity of conventional detection heads and the stringent real-time requirements of maritime small-target detection, a Lightweight Shared Detail-Enhanced Convolutional Detection Head (LSDECD) module is proposed. By introducing lightweight shared convolution, this module significantly reduces the number of parameters and computational cost while preserving feature extraction capability, thereby enhancing detection efficiency. Compared with the traditional YOLOv11 detection head, which suffers from high parameter redundancy and limited multi-scale adaptability, the LSDECD adopts a lightweight architecture based on Detail-Enhanced Convolution (DEConv)56 and a shared convolution mechanism; specifically, DEConv amplifies fine-grained image details to retain critical features via a re-parameterization strategy without incurring additional parameters or computational overhead, whereas the shared convolution mechanism reduces model complexity through parameter sharing, improves computational efficiency and alleviates overfitting. In addition, feature decoupling is integrated into the module to separate feature extraction from target localization for independent optimization, enabling the module to effectively handle complex scenes and meet the real-time detection requirements of resource-constrained platforms. In practical applications, the Detect_LSDECD design allows the YOLO model to boost inference speed while maintaining high detection accuracy, making it suitable for UAV-based maritime search and rescue tasks.

BCKD knowledge distillation

In dense object detection, knowledge distillation is often impeded by protocol inconsistencies between the teacher and student models, where mismatched training targets and prediction formats make direct feature- or logit-matching suboptimal. To alleviate this issue, BCKD57 (Bridging Cross-task Protocol Inconsistency for Distillation in Dense Object Detection) is adopted to bridge cross-task protocol inconsistency by aligning the teacher–student supervision in an output-consistent manner. In addition, since the effectiveness of logit-level distillation is inherently bounded by the reliability of the teacher predictions, the teacher model is strengthened with the proposed LSDECD head to provide more informative supervision. In what follows, the distillation objectives are formalised by explicitly defining the logits and regression distributions of the teacher/student, and the mathematical formulation of classification distillation, DFL distribution distillation, and geometry-consistency–guided score distillation is presented, together with the masked aggregation of the overall distillation loss.

ASF-P2 detection Layer

ASF-P258 (Adaptive Scale Fusion P2 Layer) (Figure 23) is an improved algorithm proposed in this study, aimed at optimizing cross-scale feature fusion and enhancing small target detection performance. The algorithm adaptively adjusts the fusion of features from different scales, effectively enhancing the distinguishability of shallow high-resolution features. Unlike traditional fixed-weight fusion methods, ASF-P2 integrates multiple complementary enhancement units (such as SSFF, TFF, and CAPM) and employs Zoom_cat for cross-scale non-linear alignment, alleviating inconsistencies in spatial and semantic domains across scales. Additionally, ASF-P2 introduces the ScalSeq dynamic weight fusion mechanism, which dynamically adjusts the fusion strategy based on the contribution of multi-scale information, further improving the effectiveness of feature fusion.

Schematic structure of ASF-P2

The ASF-P2 fusion design is schematically illustrated to show the multi-scale feature aggregation and adaptive fusion strategy.

In ASF-P2, Zoom_cat is employed as a key component for multi-scale feature fusion. Its primary purpose is defined as strengthening the model’s ability to detect targets of varying sizes, particularly small targets. Feature maps from multiple scales are concatenated by Zoom_cat to construct a unified multi-scale feature representation. Through this mechanism, small-target information within an image is effectively captured and incorporated into the global feature representation.

The ScalSeq enhancement in the ASF-P2 detection layer is applied to optimise multi-scale feature fusion through sequential processing and dynamic weighting. Features are processed step by step from the smallest to the largest scale, while each layer’s contribution is adaptively adjusted using attention mechanisms. By this strategy, detection across different scales, particularly extreme scales, is significantly improved, while redundancy is reduced and both accuracy and efficiency are enhanced.

Quantification and statistical analysis

In this study, we use model size, trainable parameter count, FLOPs, precision, recall, mean average precision (mAP), and frames per second (FPS) as evaluation metrics:

Model Size: Refers to the file size of the model, reflecting its complexity and memory footprint during inference. Smaller sizes are generally preferable, measured in megabytes (M).

Parameters: Represents variables (e.g., weights, biases) that can be updated during training. The number of parameters indicates model complexity and computational cost; fewer parameters typically mean higher efficiency, measured in millions (M).

FLOPs: The number of floating-point operations required to process one input. Lower values indicate less computational burden and higher efficiency, measured in billions (G).

Precision: The proportion of predicted positive samples that are actually positive, or the probability that a predicted bounding box contains a real target. Higher values mean more accurate predictions, where TP = True Positive and FP = False Positive:

Recall: The proportion of actual positive samples correctly identified by the model, reflecting its ability to detect real objects. Higher values mean fewer missed detections, where FN = False Negative:

Mean Average Precision (mAP): Aggregates average precision across different decision thresholds, providing a holistic measure of overall performance. Higher values indicate better overall performance, where Ai represents the average precision at the i-th threshold and N represents the number of categories. The formula is given in Equation 26.

mAP50: Mean Average precision at an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.5. mAP50:95: A comprehensive metric that averages mAP values across 10 IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 (step 0.05), reflecting performance under varying detection difficulties. Frames Per Second (FPS): The number of images processed per second, indicating real-time performance.

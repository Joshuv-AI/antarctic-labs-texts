The association between the ZJU index and bone mineral density (BMD) among patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus☆
Authors: Yuan Zhang, Yali Jing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrevious studies have suggested that type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is associated with poor bone health, including osteoporosis (OP) and osteopenia. The ZJU index, a novel calculation that integrates fasting plasma glucose (FPG), body mass index (BMI), triglyceride (TG), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) to aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ratio, is strongly associated with glucolipid metabolism and insulin resistance (IR). In this study, we explored the association of ZJU with bone mineral density (BMD) and OP/osteopenia, and investigated the predictive effect of ZJU on OP/osteopenia in patients with T2DM.MethodsThis cross-sectional study included 496 patients with T2DM aged>50 years. The clinical data were collected and the BMD of femoral neck (FN), left hip (LH), and lumbar spine (LS) were measured. The association between BMDs and ZJU levels was investigated by adjusting for covariates utilizing multiple linear regression analyses. Multivariable logistic regression was constructed to identify independent factors of OP and osteopenia, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to display the diagnostic performance according to the area under the ROC curve (AUC).ResultsOP and osteopenia patients showed significantly higher ZJU levels than those with normal BMD in T2DM (39.387 ± 3.558, 38.112 ± 2.552 vs 35.192 ± 2.600, p < 0.001). Spearman's correlation analysis showed that ZJU was significantly negatively correlated with the BMD of FN (r = −0.39, p < 0.001), LH (r = −0.35, p < 0.001), and LS (r = -0.32, p < 0.001). The multiple linear regression indicated a negative association between ZJU and BMD of FN (β = −0.006, p = 0.009), LS (β = -0.155, p = 0.011) after adjusted for covariates. Meanwhile, the results of logistic regression revealed that the ZJU was a contributing factor to osteopenia and OP risk in T2DM individuals aged>50 years (OR 1.446, 95 % CI: 1.087–1.923, p = 0.011; OR 1.878, 95 % CI: 1.218–3.715, p = 0.039, respectively). ZJU provided the AUC value of 0.695 and 0.716 on osteopenia and OP in T2DM, respectively.ConclusionsA high ZJU index was significantly associated with an increasing risk of osteopenia and OP. The ZJU is expected to be a potential index for detecting decreased BMDs in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients. Early intervention in T2DM patients with increased ZJU may further reduce the incidence of osteopenia and OP, in addition to focusing on independent biomarker in clinical practice.

Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a systemic bone disease characterized by low bone mineral density (BMD) and damage to the microstructure of bone tissue, leading to increased bone fragility and susceptibility to fracture [1,2]. Osteopenia is the early stage of OP, the epidemiologic data reported the global prevalence of OP and osteopenia was 19.7 % and 40.4 %, especially postmenopausal women and men aged 50 years and older have substantially higher risk of OP [3]. C Notably, adults with OP often lead to serious adverse outcomes including fractures, frailty, physical disability, falls, hospitalization, and all-cause mortality [1]. The mounting studies have evidenced that type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is associated with poor bone health, and fracture risk is particularly elevated in patients with T2DM [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. Moreover, disorders of glucose-lipid metabolism associated with the long-term course of T2DM, micro-/macro-vascular complications of diabetes, and anti-diabetic medications could also affect the dynamic balance between bone resorption and bone formation [8]. It was estimated that the global diabetes prevalence will rise to 12.2 % (783.2 million) in 2045 [9]. With the ongoing population aging and changes in lifestyle, OP and T2DM have become major public health concerns, especially when two diseases coexist, which could further aggravate the economic burden of the patient's and seriously affect the patient's quality of life.

The femoral neck (FN), total hip (TH) and lumbar spine (LS) BMD, assessed via dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) method, serves as the key indicators to evaluate bone mass in clinical and scientific research. Therefore, it is critical to seek simple and easily obtainable biochemical indices associated with BMDs in patients with T2DM for early and effectively diagnosis and treatment of OP and prevention of adverse fracture outcomes.

The ZJU index, a novel metabolic parameter incorporating changes in various indicators including fasting plasma glucose (FPG), body mass index (BMI), triglyceride (TG), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) [10], can coffer a more holistic perspective and effectively capture trends in metabolic disorders and associated disease risks compared to a single biochemical marker. Previous studies have demonstrated that ZJU is linked to metabolic diseases, such as diabetes [11] or insulin resistance (IR) [12], metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) [10,13], and sarcopenia (SP) [14]. Meanwhile, the research findings have shown that the development of OP shares similar metabolic pathways with above diseases [[15], [16], [17], [18], [19]], so that it is reasonable to hypothesize that ZJU may be associated with BMDs in T2DM.

Currently, the findings on the relationship between the ZJU index and OP/osteopenia or BMDs in T2DM are not clear. The objective of this study was to investigate the relationship between the ZJU levels and BMDs in T2DM patients by retrospective analysis, and then to analyze the clinical predictive value of ZJU on OP/osteopenia risk in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This was a cross-sectional study which was performed among patients diagnosed with T2DM, aged between 50 and 80 years, who were hospitalized in the Endocrinology Department of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital between January 2023 to December 2023. T2DM was diagnosed according to the 1999 American Diabetes Association criteria [20]. Inclusion criteria were as follows: postmenopausal women with T2DM or men with T2DM over 50 years old. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) presence of type 1 diabetes, or other types of diabetes; (2) acute complications of diabetes, such as diabetic hyperosmotic hyperglycemia syndrome, diabetic ketoacidosis, and hypoglycemia; (3) patients with diseases which seriously affect bone metabolism and lead to secondary osteoporosis, such as past or present autoimmune diseases (rheumatoid arthritis), hematological diseases, hyper-/hypothyroidism, primary hyper-/hypoparathyroidism, rickets, cancer, and Cushing syndrome; (4) medical treatment that could affect bone metabolism, including thiazolidinediones, calcium, calcitriol, diphosphonate, calcitonin, denosumab, teriparatide, and glucocorticoid; (5) disabled patients who cannot move or bedridden for a long time; and (6) patients with incomplete data. The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki thoroughly and was approved by the medical research ethics committee of the Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital.

Laboratory and BMD measurements

We reviewed the electronic medical records of all patients in order to collect demographic and biochemical variables retrospectively. Information on each participant, including their age, gender, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), smoking, drinking, diabetes duration, and the medications they were taking was collected using standard technical methods. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing the body weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. After fasting for 8–12 h, fasting blood samples were obtained from all patients for laboratory analyses. All biochemical and immune indices in the present study were measured at our hospital laboratory. Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) concentrations were measured by high-performance liquid chromatography (HLC-73G8, Tosoh, Japan), fasting plasma glucose (FPG) was tested using a hexokinase method (TBA-200FR, Tokyo, Japan). The automatic biochemical analyzer (Kyowa Medex Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was used to detect concentrations of TG, total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), ALT, AST, total bilirubin (TBIL), direct bilirubin (DBIL), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cr), uric acid (UA), following the manufacturer's instructions. Parathyroid hormone (PTH), beta-isomer of the carboxy-terminal cross-linked telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), osteocalcin (OC), and procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (PINP) were measured by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay. And 25-Hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) was measured by electrochemiluminescence binding assay. Meanwhile, we calculated the ZJU index = FPG (mmol/L) + BMI (kg/m2) + TG (mmol/L)+3 × ALT (U/L)/AST (U/L) ratio (+2 if female).

Bone mineral density (g/cm2) was measured using dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (Lunar iDXA, USA). Measurements were made at three sites in each patient: femoral neck (FN), left hip (LH) and lumbar spine (LS). The diagnosis of osteoporosis was also based on WHO criteria, T-score ≤ −2.5 for osteoporosis, between −2.5 and −1.0 for osteopenia, T-score ≥ −1.0 for normality and T-score < −1.0 for abnormal bone metabolism [2].

Statistical analysis

The results of our analyses are derived from complete case data. First, according to T-score, subjects were categorized into normal group (T-score ≥ −1.0), osteopenia group (−2.5<T-value < −1.0), and osteoporosis group (T-value ≤ −2.5) to analyze the basic characteristics; then, the ZJU index levels were segmented into tertiles, and compared the trends of the various indicators in T1, T2 and T3 groups. The continuous variables in accordance with normal distribution were expressed by (mean ± standard deviation), and the comparison between groups was expressed by ANOVA, while continuous variables with a non-normal distribution were expressed by medians (interquartile ranges (IQR)), and the comparison between groups was expressed by the Kruskal–Wallis test. The categorical variables were reported in the form of percentages and comparative analyses of differences were performed using the chi-square test. The correlation between the levels of ZJU and BMDs was assessed by Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Then, the linear relationship between ZJU and BMDs was assessed through multivariate linear regression models. Multivariable logistic regression was constructed to identify independent factors of OP and osteopenia. In addition, the capacity of the ZJU index to forecast T-score < −1.0 for abnormal bone metabolism was analyzed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the results are shown as an area under the curve (AUC). The results were evaluated within a 95 % confidence interval (CI) and at a significance level of two-sided p-value less than 0.05. All data were examined by adopting the program SPSS Statistics software version 27.0 (IBM SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). Statistics were considered significant when p < 0.05.

Results

In this study, 496 patients with T2DM (292 male, 204 female) were recruited and divided into normal group (190 male, 34 female), osteopenia (94 male, 109 female) and osteoporosis (8 male, 61 female) based on T-score of BMDs measured by DXA. There were significant differences in age, BMI, gender, smoking, drinking, duration of T2DM, SBP, HbA1c, FPG, FCP, Cr, UA, TG, TC, LDL-C, ZJU, ACR, OC, CTX, PINP, 25 (OH) D, PTH, FN BMD, LH BMD and LS BMD among three groups (p < 0.05) (Table 1). There were no statistically significant differences in DBP, ALT, AST, TBIL, DBIL, BUN, HDL-C, and the use of anti-diabetic drug among three groups (p > 0.05) (Table 1). Correlation between ZJU and BMDs in different groups

Spearman correlation analysis was used to explore the relationship between ZJU index and BMDs. Similarly, ZJU index was also negatively correlated with BMDs in T1 and T3 groups (T1 group: FN BMD, r = −0.22, p = 0.004; LH BMD, r = −0.20, p = 0.009; and LS BMD, r = −0.31, p < 0.001; T3 group: FN BMD, r = −0.22, p = 0.004; LH BMD, r = −0.26, p < 0.001; and LS BMD, r = −0.22, p = 0.005), whereas without significant correlation in T2 group (FN BMD, r = −0.126, p = 0.106; LH BMD, r = −0.056, p = 0.473; and LS BMD, r = −0.063, p = 0.422) (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Correlation between the ZJU index and BMDs.Fig. 1

Multivariable logistic regression to identify independent factors of OP and osteopenia

Furthermore, the influencing factors of abnormal bone mass in T2DM patients were further explored by multifactorial logistic regression analysis, OP and osteopenia was respectively used as the dependent variable and variables with statistically significant differences in univariate analysis such as age, gender, BMI were included as the independent variables in the model. The results displayed that age (OR 1.267, 95 % CI:1.142–1.406; p = 0.046), female (OR 4.662, 95 % CI: 1.884–9.541; p = 0.001), duration of T2DM (OR 1.153, 95 % CI: 1.113–1.196, p = 0.031), ZJU (OR 1.446, 95 % CI: 1087∼1.923; p = 0.011),OC (OR 1.398, 95 % CI: 1.289–1.519; p = 0.049) were the influential factor for osteopenia; while the risk factors for OP in T2DM were age (OR 1.141, 95 % CI: 1.056–1.233; p = 0.001), female (OR 8.311, 95 % CI: 3.478–16.225; p < 0.001), FPG (OR 1.777, 95 % CI: 1.051–3.005; p = 0.032), ZJU (OR 1.878, 95 % CI: 1.218–3.715; p = 0.039) and CTX (OR 2.076, 95 %CI: 1.811–3.227; p = 0.018) (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Logistic regression analysis for osteopenia and OP in T2DM patient.Fig. 2

ROC curves to display the diagnostic performance of osteopenia and OP

Additionally, the predictive ability of ZJU on osteopenia and OP was individually assessed using ROC curve analysis. ZJU showed moderate strength in predicting both osteopenia and OP with AUC = 0.695 (95 % CI:0.649–0.741, p < 0.001) and 0.716 (95 % CI:0.654–0.778; p < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 3). Discussion

In the present study, we investigated the association between ZJU and BMDs in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients. The main findings were as follows: first, OP and osteopenia participants had higher levels of ZJU compared those with normal BMDs, and ZJU negatively associated with FN, LH, and LS BMD among the study participants; second, with the increase of ZJU tertile, FN, LH, and LS BMD gradually decreased, and correlation analysis showed that in the ZJU<35.407 and ZJU>38.258 group, ZJU was negatively correlated with FN, LH, and LS BMD, while without significant correlation between ZJU and BMDs in 35.407≤ZJU≤38.258 group; third, after adjusting for age, gender, smoking, drinking, SBP, duration of T2DM and other clinical factors via multiple linear regression analysis, the ZJU was still significantly and negatively associated with FN, LS BMD among subjects; fourth, multiple linear regression was performed in ZJU tertile groups, after adjustment covariates, the significantly negative association was just presented between ZJU and LS BMD in the ZJU<35.407 and ZJU > 38.258 groups; fifth, multifactorial logistic regression analysis discovered that ZJU was the risk factor of both osteopenia and OP, and ROC curves displayed that ZJU possessed moderate predictive capability for identifying osteopenia and OP in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients. Collectively, an increased ZJU was independently associated with decreased BMDs in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients, suggesting ZJU could be a potential biomarker for identifying > 50-year adults with a higher risk of abnormal BMDs.

OP has been considered the “silent disease” of the 21st century, with fractures contributing to significant morbidity and mortality [1]. Patients with T2DM are often associated with obesity, hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, accumulation of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), and micro-/macro-vascular complications, which contribute to impaired bone parameters (low bone turnover, increased cortical porosity, and low cortical BMD volume). Importantly, patients with T2DM have a worse prognosis after fracture [8]. Therefore, more attention should be paid to the bone health status of patients with T2DM. In confronting the challenges of global aging, OP and T2DM have garnered increasing attention due to their impact on the global burden of disease. The gradual and persistent nature of decreased BMD associated with imbalances in bone metabolism, which is an inevitable result of living beyond middle age, provides an opportunity to take measures to halt or slow the progression of OP, thereby preventing adverse outcomes and prolonging healthy lifespans. Existing guidelines confirmed detecting BMD via DXA has the crucially important role and accurate value for detecting OP and predicting osteoporotic fractures [8], however, DXA exams has certain limitations such as not easy to carry, higher medicine cost, leading to unfavorable screening and application in a wide range of populations. Therefore, there is a need to bridge the gap between evidence and practice, identify more precise and comprehensive biomarkers linked with BMDs, contributing to develop more targeted OP/osteopenia therapeutic regimens.

The ZJU index, a combination of BMI, FPG, TG, ALT, and AST levels, was initially established to detect NAFLD in Chinese adults [10]. The global increase in the prevalence of obesity and T2DM is driving impairment of glucose and lipid metabolism pathways and is partly responsible for the increase in MAFLD patients [21]. Kim et al. reported that MAFLD in patients with T2DM was to be associated with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease and all cause death, even in patients with mild MAFLD [22]. Wu et al. reported that ZJU index was significantly associated with diabetes among Asian populations [11]. A study found that the ZJU index was a useful indicator for recognizing IR in the Chinese general population [12]. Although no direct studies have specifically explored the link between bone metabolism and the ZJU index currently, there seems a closely correlation between and OP.

Bone is actually an active tissue with a high turnover rate, age-related OP is characterized by progressive bone loss due to an imbalance in bone remodeling and an elevated bone resorption/bone formation ratio as aging, and a pro-inflammatory state due to a persistent hypoactivation of the immune system as a result of aging and estrogen deficiency [19]. Chronic inflammation and cellular senescence as key hallmarks of aging, are major risk factors for OP and other age-related disease [23]. Accumulating evidence supported pathophysiological links between MAFLD and OP, including inflammatory pathways, IR, gut microbiota dysbiosis, bone marrow adiposity, and alterations in insulin-like growth factor-1(IGF-1) signaling [15]. In addition, the bone remodeling processes of osteoclast resorption and osteoblast formation are controlled by local signals (e.g., growth factors and cytokines) and systemic signals (PTH, calcitonin, and estrogens), which act synergistically to maintain bone homeostasis [18], vitamin D deficiency, accumulation of leptin and lipocalin, systemic inflammatory response and decreased liver function due to MAFLD could affect bone metabolism and exacerbate bone loss [24]. These shared factors suggested a bidirectional relationship between OP and NAFLD. Similarly, a meta-analysis reported that the prevalence and risk of OP or osteoporotic fractures were significantly associated with NAFLD in men and women [25]. Consequently, it is reasonable to believe that ZJU could be a powerful predictor for osteopenia and OP.

Previous studies found that age, female, BMI, duration of T2DM, FPG, HbA1c, and dyslipidemia were associated with low BMD or OP in T2DM [[26], [27], [28], [29]], and our study had the similar findings. In this study, we found that age, female and ZJU were the common independent risk factors of osteopenia and OP patients, while OC was independent risk factor of osteopenia and CTX was independent risk factor of OP. Estrogen deficiency as also an important pathogenesis of OP, estrogen deficiency following menopause in female [30]. Lower estrogen levels diminish the inhibitory effect on osteoclasts, which increase in number, decrease in apoptosis, and prolonged lifespan, leading to increased bone resorption and consequently decreased bone strength, so it is crucial for postmenopausal women to pay attention the changes on BMDs timely. OC and CTX are highly sensitive markers of bone formation and bone resorption, respectively. OC is a protein primarily produced by osteoblasts and has most abundant non-collagenous protein in bone tissue [31]. Furthermore, decarboxylated osteocalcin promotes insulin secretion and stimulates the proliferation of pancreatic islet β-cells. It also plays a role in reducing the accumulation of visceral fat and decreasing fat storage in the liver. These findings suggest that bone remodeling markers are useful for assessing of the state of bone tissue in early stages in T2DM.

In addition, our model demonstrated moderate predictive ability for both osteopenia and osteoporosis, with AUCs of 0.695 and 0.716, respectively, suggesting that to assess the ZJU index level to identify subject at early risk of osteopenia/OP is beneficial, especially in the ZJU<35.407 and ZJU>38.258 levels, preventing the onset and progression of decreased BMDs in T2DM individuals aged>50 years. The ZJU index, as a composite metric integrating BMI, FPG, TG, ALT, and AST, inherently captures the multifaceted pathophysiology of bone health in T2DM, which involves metabolic, hepatic, and nutritional factors. The strength of the ZJU index lies in its ability to consolidate these diverse pathways into a single, readily available score, which highlights its clinical utility as a cost-effective and holistic initial screening tool. It could help identify T2DM patients who would most benefit from definitive BMD assessment, thereby optimizing resource allocation. Additional investigations are needed to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the fundamental underlying mechanisms involved.

This study has certain limitations that warrant consideration. First, due to the cross-sectional nature of the study design, we were unable to determine a causal relationship between ZJU and BMDs, future large-scale, long-term prospective cohort studies are essential to validate the predictive role of the ZJU index by examining its association with longitudinal bone mineral density loss and the incidence of osteoporotic fractures. Second, this was a small, single-center study of aged>50 years T2DM patients, the conclusions have limited applicability. Finally, the study did not collect variables including participants' dietary and exercise habits, which may affect the levels of biochemical indicators such as lipids and blood glucose.

Conclusion

The higher ZJU index was significantly associated with an increasing risk of osteopenia and OP. The ZJU is expected to be a potential index for detecting decreased BMDs in middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients. Early intervention in T2DM patients with increased ZJU may further reduce the incidence of osteopenia and OP, in addition to focusing on independent biomarker in clinical practice.

Nurse-led non-pharmacological interventions for prevention and management of postoperative nausea and vomiting in adults undergoing laparoscopic surgery: an evidence summary
Authors: Juan Han, Qin Xu, Weidi Wang, Yanrong Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), as a common complication in laparoscopic surgery, significantly delays patients’ recovery and increases the incidence of unplanned readmission. Although drug prevention is widely used, nurse-led non-pharmacological interventions can provide safe and effective collaborative management. However, the current evidence is fragmented and there is a lack of guidelines specific to laparoscopic surgery, resulting in significant differences in clinical practice. This study aimed to integrate the best evidence and establish standardized non-pharmacological nursing strategies for the prevention and treatment of PONV in adults undergoing laparoscopic surgery.MethodsFollowing the standard methodology of the Fudan University Center for Evidence-based Nursing (registration number: ES20257623) and guided by the “5S” evidence pyramid model, we systematically searched multiple databases and guideline repositories from inception to December 4, 2025. We included evidence summary, clinical decision, guidelines, expert consensus statements, systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Literature screening, quality appraisal, and evidence extraction were performed independently by two reviewers. Methodological quality was assessed using validated screening tools. A summary of findings table was constructed to present the synthesized evidence.ResultsFrom 1,564 initial records, 39 publications were included: 5 evidence summaries, 1 clinical decision, 8 guidelines, 4 expert consensuses and 21 systematic reviews. We synthesized 28 best practice recommendations across five key domains: pre-operative risk assessment (identification of risk factors, risk quantification tools, stratified PONV prophylaxis), intraoperative preventive interventions (fluid management, acupoint stimulation, high-concentration oxygen), post-operative symptom management (symptom assessment tool, non-pharmacological management), follow-up care (health education, prevention and control of PDNV), system support (organizational management).ConclusionThis evidence summary provided a comprehensive, clinically actionable framework for nurses to lead proactive, evidence-based PONV care for laparoscopic surgery patients. Effective nursing interventions directly reduced PONV incidence, decreased antiemetic requirements, and improved patient comfort and satisfaction. In addition, nursing practice can shift from reactive management to proactive prevention, thereby delivering patient-centered benefits. The findings should be adapted to local contexts to develop standardized nursing procedures, thereby translating evidence into clinical practice.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-026-05029-5.Keywords: Postoperative nausea and vomiting, Laparoscopic surgery, Nursing, Non-pharmacological interventions, Evidence-based practice, Evidence summary

Introduction

Laparoscopy is a minimally invasive technique that involves inserting a camera and surgical instruments through small incisions in the abdominal wall (typically 0.5–1.5 cm) to perform diagnostic and therapeutic procedures [1]. Compared to traditional open surgery, laparoscopic procedures offer advantages such as reduced intraoperative bleeding, minimal trauma, clear visualization, less postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays, and a lower incidence of complications [1, 2]. Laparoscopy has been widely adopted in various surgical procedures (such as laparoscopic cholecystectomy, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy, laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy, laparoscopic radical prostatectomy), as well as in the treatment of gynecological conditions (including uterine fibroids, ovarian cysts, and ectopic pregnancies) [3, 4]. Day surgery is an important development direction in minimally invasive surgery. Its core advantages lie in high efficiency, safety, and low cost. Currently, the well-developed surgical procedures are mainly focused on benign lesions of the body surface and abdominal cavity (such as inguinal hernia, gallstones, appendicitis, and ovarian cysts). However, complex surgeries involving organ reconstruction or with a high risk of complications (such as gastrointestinal tumor resection and partial liver resection) still require the traditional inpatient management model. Although laparoscopic surgery has many advantages, it is also associated with certain postoperative complications, such as subcutaneous emphysema, bleeding, infection, postoperative pain, and postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) [3, 4].

PONV refers to nausea and vomiting occurring within 24 hours following surgery [4]. The overall incidence of PONV worldwide is 27.7% [5]. Patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery are a high-risk group for PONV, which is significantly higher than that of other surgical procedures. The incidence rate in high-risk groups reaches up to 80% [6–8]. Laparoscopic surgery requires the creation of a pneumoperitoneum [9]. The injected carbon dioxide can stimulate the diaphragm, thereby activating the vomiting center. Additionally, increased pneumoperitoneum pressure compresses abdominal organs, disrupting gastrointestinal motility and digestive function, ultimately causing PONV. PONV is the second most common postoperative complication after pain and a patient-ranked feared surgical outcome [10]. PONV not only heightens patients’ postoperative discomfort and prolongs hospital stays, but may also lead to serious adverse events (such as aspiration and wound dehiscence), thereby significantly impacting both patient recovery and the efficient utilization of medical services [11].

As the primary observers and immediate responders to postoperative symptoms, nurses must promptly identify and intervene to alleviate patient distress and enhance comfort. However, due to various factors, there are numerous obstacles to nurses’ prescribing authority, preventing nurses from directly administering medication to patients [12]. Non-pharmacological interventions have thus become the primary means for nurses to alleviate postoperative symptoms [13].

Currently, the primary treatment strategy for PONV remains predominantly pharmacological, with insufficient emphasis placed on non-pharmacological interventions. In fact, non-pharmacological interventions have become an important adjunctive treatment for PONV due to their unique advantages (such as low risk, ease of implementation, and high patient compliance) [14]. These features align perfectly with nurses’ clinical practice settings. Most non-pharmacological interventions require no invasive procedures, allowing nurses to independently master and implement them through systematic training. Many non-pharmacological therapies (such as acupressure, auricular therapy, aromatherapy) have demonstrated good clinical efficacy in reducing PONV incidence [15, 16], particularly for pediatric and geriatric patients as well as special populations with drug contraindications.

In recent years, non-pharmacological interventions for PONV have garnered significant attention and been incorporated into guidelines [8, 15, 17]. However, existing evidence exhibits some gaps. Current recommendations lack specificity for patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery and focus primarily on pharmacological treatments [18], meaning that nurse-led non-pharmacological treatment options have not yet been fully explored. Furthermore, significant heterogeneity in implementation practices (such as the timing of interventions and technical standards) has led to variations in clinical outcomes. Therefore, this evidence summary aimed to conduct a systematic review and integration of the evidence for the prevention and management of PONV in laparoscopic surgery, to provide the most reliable basis for clinical practice.

Material and methods

This evidence summary was reported according to the standard of evidence summary report of Fudan University Center for Evidence-based Nursing [19] and registered at the Center for Evidence-based Nursing of Fudan University (registration number ES20257623).

Question identification

Evidence-based nursing problems were identified based on the PIPOST framework. P (population): adult patients (≥18 years old) undergoing laparoscopic surgery; I (intervention): non-pharmacological management strategies for perioperative PONV; P (professional): healthcare providers, patients, or family members; O (outcome): PONV incidence and severity, antiemetic usage, hospital-related indicators (postoperative length of stay, unplanned hospital readmissions due to PONV), and health economics indicators; S (setting): hospital, clinic, or patient’s home; T (type): evidence summary, clinical decision, guidelines, expert consensus statements, systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

Evidence retrieval

Based on the evidence pyramid “5S” model [20], the search was performed from top to bottom. The following databases were searched: BMJ best practice, JBI Best Practice Database, Up To Date, Yimaitong, National Institute for Health and Clinical Excellence (NICE), Registered Nurses Association of Ontario (RNAO), Guidelines International Network (GIN), National Guideline Clearinghouse (NGC), New Zealand Guidelines Group (NZGG), European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL), American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD), PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, SinoMed, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), and Wanfang Database.

The search was performed using a combination of subject words and free words. Search keywords included postoperative nausea and vomiting, laparoscopy, cholecystectomy, bariatric surgery, appendectomy, gynaecological surgery, minimally invasive surgery, evidence summary, clinical decision, guidelines, expert consensus, systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Search dates ranged from database inception to December 4, 2025. Detailed search strategies were provided in Supplementary Material 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria of evidences

The inclusion criteria for this study were: (1) study subjects must be patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery, aged ≥ 18 years; (2) studies must focus on the prevention and management of perioperative PONV, including assessment and non-pharmacological interventions; (3) study types must include evidence summary, clinical decision, guidelines, expert consensus, systematic reviews and meta-analyses; (4) studies must be in English or Chinese.

Exclusion criteria include: (1) original research; (2) duplicated literature; (3) studies classified as plans, drafts, or containing only abstracts; (4) studies failing quality assessment.

Literature screening

EndNote 20.6 (Clarivate Analytics) was used for literature management. Duplicate references were removed through EndNote. Two trained researchers (Qin Xu and Weidi Wang) independently conducted literature screening and quality assessment. In cases of disagreement during evaluation, a third expert (Juan Han) was consulted to reach consensus.

Methodological quality of included evidence

In this study, we employed different evaluation tools to assess various types of literature. Clinical decision and evidence summary are sourced from authoritative databases (such as Up to Date and JBI), representing the highest level of evidence. Their evidence is assumed to be extracted directly from high-quality research [21, 22].

The guidelines were assessed using the 2017 version of The Appraisal of Guidelines for Research and Evaluation II (AGREE II) [23]. It encompasses 23 items across six domains, including scope and purpose, stakeholder involvement, rigor of development, clarity of presentation, applicability, and editorial independence. Each item is scored on a scale of 1 to 7 (1 indicating strongly disagree, 7 indicating strongly agree). Scores for each domain are calculated as follows: (actual score - lowest possible score)/(maximum possible score - lowest possible score) × 100%. If scores in 6 domains are ≥60%, the guideline is rated Grade A and directly recommended. If one or more areas have scores < 60% and three or more areas have scores ≥ 30%, it is rated Grade B and recommended after revision. If scores in more than 3 domains < 30%, it is rated Grade C and not recommended. The consistency between evaluators was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). For expert consensus, the 2016 edition of the Australian JBI Centre for Evidence-Based Health Care evaluation criteria for expert consensus was used [24, 25]. For systematic reviews and meta-analyses, the 2016 version of the Australian JBI Centre for Evidence-Based Health Care Systematic served as the quality assessment tool [25, 26].

Evidences extraction

Two trained researchers (Qin Xu and Weidi Wang) independently extracted, categorized, and synthesized the evidence. The evidence extraction process adhered to the following principles: (1) if recommendations were identical, the most concise and logically clear statement was selected; (2) if recommendations were complementary, they were merged based on logical relationships and linguistic expression; (3) if content was conflicting, priority was given to evidence from high-quality, recently published literature in authoritative journals. Each evidence was disassembled and analyzed across multiple dimensions.

Evidences summary

Following integration, evidence was scored using the 2014 version of the JBI Evidence Pre-grading System and Evidence Rank System [27, 28]. The evidence levels categorized from 1 to 5 based on study design. The more rigorous the study design, the higher the level of evidence. Based on the FAME framework (feasibility, applicability, meaningfulness, and effectiveness) of JBI, evidence was classified into Grade A (strong recommendation) and Grade B (weak recommendation). If the content of multiple different types of literature was integrated into a single piece of evidence, the highest grade among the literature was used as the grade of that evidence.

Results

Literature search results and general information

Initially, 1,564 studies were retrieved. Of these, 1,240 required reviews of titles and abstracts. Following exclusion of 1150 irrelevant studies, 90 studies warranted full-text assessment. Ultimately, 39 studies were included: five evidence summaries, one clinical decision, eight guidelines, four expert consensuses and twenty-one systematic reviews. The specific reasons for excluding the literature after reading the full text can be found in Supplementary Material 2. The literature screening flowchart was presented in Fig. 1. Evidence summary and description

The research content specifically focused on five key aspects of PONV prevention and management (pre-operative risk assessment, intraoperative preventive interventions, post-operative symptom management, follow-up care, system support). A total of 28 evidence-based strategies for preventing and managing PONV following laparoscopic surgery were summarized, as shown in Table 5. Common acupoints for different types of laparoscopic surgery were shown in Fig. 2. The JBI Evidence Pre-grading System and Evidence Rank System used in this study was mainly used to evaluate and present the levels of evidence. Its results should be regarded as key points of evidence-based practice or summary statements of evidence, rather than formal grading recommendations.

Discussion

Screening and assessment of PONV in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery

Laparoscopic surgery is one of the independent risk factors for PONV [64]. PONV significantly impacts patient experience and prognosis, potentially leading to serious complications such as wound dehiscence, aspiration, and dehydration [11]. Furthermore, some patients reported that the distress caused by PONV exceeds postoperative pain [10]. In clinical practice, PONV management often involves reactive medication administration after symptoms arise, with insufficient awareness and implementation of proactive preventive measures. As the primary implementers of perioperative patient care, nurses can change this situation through systematic interventions.

Evidence items 1 to 5 summarized high-risk factors and assessment tools for PONV in laparoscopic surgery patients. During the preoperative phase, nurses can utilize the Apfel simplified risk score and the Koivuranta score to stratify patient risk [8, 15]. The Koivuranta score incorporates risk factors from the Apfel score and additionally includes a surgery duration exceeding 60 minutes. When patients present with 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 risk factors, their corresponding risks of PONV are 17, 18, 42, 54, 74, and 87%, respectively. Prior to surgery, nurses can collect key information including female gender, history of postoperative nausea/vomiting or motion sickness, smoking status, as well as core data such as anesthesia method and expected surgical duration [8, 17, 34]. For high-risk individual, nurses should collaborate with the medical team to develop personalized prevention plans in advance. For patients undergoing surgery in clinic, enhanced education on PONV risk is required, guiding patients and their families to recognize early symptoms [17, 65].

Although the Apfel simplified risk score and Koivuranta score are commonly used tools for assessing PONV risk, their predictive dimensions are relatively limited, resulting in restricted predictive efficacy within specific populations. For example, the risk of PONV in bariatric surgery patients is influenced by unique factors such as BMI and surgical technique. Existing scoring systems do not incorporate these variables, potentially leading to mismatches between prevention protocols and actual needs [66]. Therefore, more specific PONV risk assessment tools tailored to different disease types or surgical populations need to be developed in the future to enhance the accuracy of assessment.

Non-pharmacological management of PONV in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery

Evidence items 6 to 21 summarized non-pharmacological interventions for PONV in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery.

The ERAS protocol, as a structured, evidence-based perioperative management approach, optimizes nursing processes by integrating multidimensional measures including preoperative counseling, early nutritional support, multimodal pain management, and early mobilization [67]. The ERAS protocol has been proven to effectively reduce the incidence of PONV [47].

In the non-pharmacological management of PONV, the most frequently mentioned approach is acupoint stimulation. Acupoint stimulation is categorized into invasive and non-invasive methods. Invasive stimulation includes acupuncture, electroacupuncture, acupoint injection, and thread implantation; non-invasive stimulation includes acupoint pressure, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation, and ultra-laser irradiation [41, 68]. Many studies indicate that acupoint stimulation demonstrates favorable efficacy in preventing PONV [69, 70] following laparoscopic surgery. Acupoint stimulation not only alleviates pain and reduces patients’ medical expenses, but also accelerates the recovery process by regulating the body’s qi and blood [71]. For acupoint stimulation, nurses can lead the implementation of non-invasive techniques such as acupoint pressure and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation [72]. Recommended acupoints like Zusanli (ST36) and Neiguan (PC6) should be prioritized [41], with additional auxiliary points selected flexibly based on surgical type [41]. Standardized procedures ensure consistent stimulation efficacy.

Aromatherapy is also a common non-pharmacological treatment. Aromatherapy is a therapeutic practice that utilizes essential oils extracted from plants (flowers, herbs, trees) to address health concerns. Administration methods include olfactory inhalation, direct skin contact (such as massage or topical application), pulmonary inhalation (such as nebulization), and oral administration [73, 74]. However, the efficacy of aromatherapy in preventing PONV varies depending on the intervention protocol, the type of essential oil used, and differences in patient demographics [75]. In previous studies, ginger oil, lavender oil, and peppermint oil proved particularly effective in managing postoperative nausea compared to isopropyl alcohol and sage oil [74, 75]. In addition to single-ingredient essential oils, customized essential oil blends (containing lavender, peppermint, ginger, and lemon) may be considered to reduce the incidence of postoperative PONV [76]. As the primary practitioners of aromatherapy, nurses should adhere to evidence-based guidelines when selecting essential oils (single or blended) for clinical use. Nurses should tailor administration methods to individual characteristics and enhance the safety and suitability of interventions through standardized procedures.

Additionally, nurses must dynamically assess patient responses during interventions, promptly documenting data (such as tolerance levels to acupoint stimulation and the onset time of aromatherapy effects) to provide clinical evidence for optimizing non-pharmacological intervention strategies.

The dynamic assessment of patients relies on standardized tools. Dynamic assessment of PONV severity is a core component for precisely adjusting treatment regimens during intervention. Currently, tools for assessing the severity of PONV include visual analogue scale, verbal descriptive scale and PONV frequency score [16]. These tools provide objective, quantifiable decision-making support for adjusting drug and non-drug treatment regimens, serving as a key safeguard for enhancing intervention effectiveness.

Education and follow-up of PONV in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery

Evidence items 22 to 28 summarized follow-up measures for PONV in laparoscopic surgery patients and management strategies for PDNV.

During the preoperative phase, nurses can employ diverse methods to systematically explain typical symptoms, risk factors, and potential impacts of PONV to patients. Nurses should guide patients in using tools to proactively report postoperative discomfort, thereby strengthening patients’ awareness of self-monitoring [34, 77]. In postoperative care, nurses should increase assessment frequency for high-risk patients, implement multidimensional dynamic evaluations using standardized tools, promptly identify early symptoms, and adjust intervention plans accordingly. For patients with ambulatory surgery, nurses can provide continuity of care through postoperative bedside health education and telephone follow-ups [78, 79]. Nurses can instruct patients and their families to recognize changes in symptoms, ensuring continuity of intervention. At the same time, nurses can document data such as patient education outcomes and follow-up feedback to provide clinical evidence for optimizing PONV management strategies.

Implications for nursing practice and research

This study summarizes 28 non-pharmacological interventions for PONV in laparoscopic surgery patients, covering pre-operative risk assessment, intraoperative preventive interventions, post-operative symptom management, follow-up care and system support. Nurses’ comprehensive interventions can transform PONV management from a reactive approach to proactive prevention. Through risk screening, dynamic monitoring, individualized interventions, and data feedback, nurses could effectively enhance patient comfort and recovery quality during the perioperative period. This study facilitates nurses in rapidly identifying high-risk individuals and implementing tailored interventions according to the risk and severity of PONV. Furthermore, it promotes standardized nursing practice, enhancing the professional competence and consistency of interventions within nursing teams (e.g., timing of non-pharmacological interventions, selection of acupoints).

Limitation

Although this evidence summary systematically integrated current best evidence regarding nurse-led non-pharmacological interventions for PONV prevention and management in adults undergoing laparoscopic surgery, several limitations should be acknowledged. The search strategy was restricted to publications in Chinese and English, potentially omitting relevant studies published in other languages and introducing language bias. While we followed a rigorous, standardized process for literature screening and quality appraisal involving independent reviewers, some degree of subjective judgment is inevitable in applying tools like AGREE II and JBI checklists. Furthermore, this summary constitutes a descriptive synthesis of evidence. We did not perform a quantitative meta-analysis to pool effect sizes, which limits our ability to provide statistically precise estimates of intervention effectiveness or to conduct subgroup analyses. Lastly, while we aimed to provide clinically actionable recommendations, the process of translating aggregated evidence into specific, context-sensitive nursing protocols was beyond the scope of this review. The feasibility and effectiveness of implementing these evidence-based recommendations in diverse clinical environments, considering variable resources and workflows, warrant further investigation through implementation science studies.

Conclusion

This study employed an evidence-based methodology to summarize the best evidence for the nurse-led non-pharmacological prevention and management of PONV in patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. Implementing standardized non-pharmacological interventions and dynamic assessment in clinical practice can directly reduce the incidence of PONV in laparoscopic surgery patients and enhance postoperative comfort. Additionally, nurses must translate practice guidance into personalized care plans. As clinical observers, nurses can also document patients’ responses to interventions, thereby tracking emerging evidence for future research and driving the continuous refinement of evidence-based practice.

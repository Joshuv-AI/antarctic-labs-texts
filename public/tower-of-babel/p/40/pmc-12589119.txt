The long non-coding RNA CRNDE stabilises SIRT1 protein and influences Hedgehog signalling in multiple myeloma
Authors: Simone Zocchi, Pauline Trichet, Paloma Guernalec, Manal Agdada, Ana Alonso Bartolomé, Vincent Ogor, Caroline Choisy, Carine Vias, Xavier Sabaté-Cadenas, Jean-Christophe Bories, Michele Goodhardt, Alena Shkumatava, David Garrick
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMultiple myeloma (MM) is a malignancy of immunoglobulin-secreting plasma cells that represents 10% of all hematological cancers and remains essentially incurable, despite recent advances. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are an important class of regulatory molecules that have been strongly implicated in the aetiology of MM. Colorectal Neoplasia Differentially Expressed (CRNDE) is one lncRNA that is upregulated in tumor plasma cells of MM patients and contributes to disease progression and outcome. In order to characterise the molecular mechanisms of CRNDE action in MM, here we have carried out a high-throughput screen to identify proteins interacting with this lncRNA inside cells. From the output of this screen, we demonstrate that in MM cells, CRNDE interacts with and stabilises the deacetylase protein SIRT1, previously identified as an important mediator of the Hedgehog (Hh) signalling pathway in MM. We further show that CRNDE exerts downstream effects on MM cell survival, tumorigenic potential, and stem-like properties via an effect on the SIRT1/Hh signalling axis. Our findings add to the molecular understanding of the pro-tumorigenic activity of CRNDE in MM and could have wider implications in other malignant diseases.Subject terms: Myeloma, Oncogenes, Cell signalling

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a genetically complex malignancy caused by the transformation of plasma B cells in the bone marrow [1]. MM is one of the most common blood cancers, accounting for ~10% of all haematological malignancies and ~2% of all cancer deaths [1–3]. Although recent advances have improved clinical responses and survival times, disease relapse occurs frequently and MM is still considered to be incurable [1, 4, 5], with a 5-year relative survival rate of 60% [2]. Within this globally poor prognostic, MM is characterised by a high degree of clinical heterogeneity and variable response to treatment, which cannot be fully accounted for by the different primary genetic events that can underlie disease initiation. At present the secondary events, molecular pathways, epigenetic and gene expression changes that impact upon disease outcome remain incompletely understood.

From a growing body of evidence, deregulation of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) has emerged in recent years as an important factor contributing to disease outcome in MM patients [6, 7]. LncRNAs, transcripts of greater than 200 nucleotides in length which lack significant coding potential, constitute an important class of regulatory molecules which can influence expression of downstream target genes at transcriptional, post-transcriptional and post-translational stages [8, 9]. In MM patients, microarray- and more recent RNA-seq-based transcriptomic studies have documented widespread changes in the expression of lncRNAs in tumour plasma cells [10–12] and have identified prognostic lncRNA expression signatures predictive of event free- or overall survival [11, 13]. Moreover, among MM patients, there is a greater variability in expression of lncRNAs than that observed for coding transcripts [10], suggesting that lncRNAs could make an important contribution to the clinical heterogeneity of the disease.

Colorectal Neoplasia Differentially Expressed (CRNDE) is a lncRNA whose expression is upregulated in plasma cells of MM patients relative to healthy controls and which is associated with poor overall survival [14, 15]. Using CRISPR-mediated deletion, we have previously shown that CRNDE enhances MM cell proliferation, decreases sensitivity to dexamethasone, a core component of MM treatment regiments, and promotes tumorigenicity of MM cells in mouse xenografts, at least partly via its influence on pro-tumorigenic IL6 signalling [15]. Furthermore, CRNDE is now recognised as an important general oncogenic lncRNA which has been implicated in numerous other haematological and solid tumours via a variety of molecular mechanisms [16, 17]. In particular, while CRNDE has been reported to interact with a wide-range of microRNAs, protein binding partners of this lncRNA, and their contribution to its function, remain largely unknown.

As many lncRNAs exert their cellular functions through the formation of ribonucleoprotein complexes [18], identifying interacting proteins is an important step to understand the molecular mechanism(s) of lncRNA action within cells. In this study we have carried out an unbiased high-throughput molecular screen to identify in-cell interacting partner proteins of CRNDE. From the output of this screen, we demonstrated that CRNDE interacts with the NAD-dependent deacetylase Silent Mating Type Information Regulation 2 Homologue 1 (SIRT1), previously identified as a modifier of the Hedgehog (Hh) signalling pathway in MM cells. We show that CRNDE stabilises SIRT1 protein in MM cells and exerts downstream effects on their survival and stem-like properties by affecting Hh signalling. These findings add to our molecular understanding of the pro-tumorigenic activity of CRNDE in MM suggesting that CRNDE is a promising therapeutic target to enhance or prolong drug sensitivity and delay relapse in MM patients.

Materials and Methods

Cell culture

MM cell lines KMS11, RPMI-8226, MM1.S and KMS12-BM were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 20% (KMS11, KMS12-BM) or 10% (other lines) of foetal calf serum (FCS), 100 U/ml penicillin, 100 µg/ml streptomycin and 2 mM L-glutamine. As indicated, cells were treated with cycloheximide (Merck, Burlington, MA, USA), dexamethasone (Merck), EX527 (Merck), GANT61 (Merck) or recombinant human Sonic Hedgehog protein (rSHH) (C25II; ThermoFisher Scientific). HEK293T cells were cultured in DMEM (ThermoFisher Scientific) supplemented with 10% FCS, penicillin, streptomycin and L-glutamine as above. Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

In vivo xenograft experiments

Male NOD/SCID/IL2Rγ-/- immunodeficient mice (12–16 weeks) were injected subcutaneously into opposing flanks with 50 µl PBS containing 1 × 106 CRNDEWT or CRNDEΔ/Δ KMS11 cells, respectively. At day 10, mice who failed to develop palpable tumours in either flank were excluded from the study, and simple randomization was carried out to distribute the remaining mice into 2 groups for treatment (6 mice per group). Mice were treated with EX527 (0.5 mg/kg) [19] or vehicle (DMSO), twice weekly by intraperitoneal injection. Two-dimensional caliper measurement of the tumour was carried out twice weekly and tumour volume was calculated using the formula V = 4/3π × (a/2) × (b/2)2, where a and b correspond to the longest and shortest axes, respectively [20]. The study was terminated at day 37 due to moribundity arising from tumour burden in several animals. No blinding was carried out. Sample size was estimated in accordance with the 3 R principles.

Statistics

Graphing and statistics were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.4.1 (GraphPad, La Jolla, CA, USA). All points indicate biological replicates and unless specified otherwise, the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of the dataset are presented. Normal distribution of datasets was determined using the Kalmogorov-Smirnov or Shapiro-Wilk (small datasets) normality tests (α = 0.05) and non-parametric tests were preferred where appropriate. The number of biological replicates and statistical tests applied are indicated in each figure. * indicates p < 0.05, ** indicates p < 0.01, *** indicates p < 0.001, **** indicates p < 0.0001, ns indicates not significant.

Detailed description of the CRNDE incPRINT screen and all molecular and cellular assays used in the study are provided in the Supplementary Material.

Results

A high-throughput interaction screen identifies SIRT1 as a partner protein of the lncRNA CRNDE

In order to characterise the cellular role(s) played by CRNDE at the molecular level, we have used a previously described high-throughput screening platform (incPRINT) [21] to carry out an unbiased screen for in-cell interacting partner proteins of CRNDE. Multiple alternative splicing variants have been reported for CRNDE [15, 22]. As a bait in the incPRINT experiment, we used an isoform of CRNDE referred to as CRNDEh (750 nt) which initiates from the predominant Cap Analysis of Gene Expression (CAGE)-mapped transcription start site (TSS) and has been fully spliced to all annotated core exons (Fig. 1A). High expression of the CRNDEh isoform correlates with poor prognosis in both MM [15] as well as in colon cancer [23]. For the incPRINT screen, CRNDEh was tagged at its 5’ terminus with 10 copies of the MS2 stem-loop aptamer (Fig. 1A) and the fusion transcript (hereafter referred to as CRNDEh-MS2) was expressed from a plasmid transfected into the HEK293T cell line stably expressing luciferase fused to the MS2 coat protein (MS2CP) [21] (Fig. 1B). The exogenous CRNDEh-MS2 exhibits a predominantly cytoplasmic subcellular localisation when expressed in the luciferase reporter cell line (Supplementary Fig. 1A), recapitulating the subcellular localisation of the endogenous CRNDEh in MM [15] and colorectal carcinoma [24] cell lines. In this cell line, we screened for interactions between CRNDEh-MS2 and members of a library of ∼3000 human FLAG-tagged proteins, including an exhaustive list of ∼1500 known RNA binding proteins (RBPs), ∼1300 transcription factors and 168 chromatin-associated proteins, altogether comprising ~15% of the human proteome [21] (Fig. 1B). All RNA-protein couples were assessed in independent replicate transfections (Supplementary Fig. 1B, C). As seen in previous incPRINT screens [21], no correlation was observed between the expression level of tested proteins (as determined by ELISA) and RNA-protein interaction intensities, indicating that the interaction intensities are not merely a reflection of the protein abundance (Supplementary Fig. 1D). We identified 72 proteins that interact with the CRNDEh-MS2 bait RNA (Fig. 1C; Supplementary Table 1). Of these proteins, 59 (82%) had not been detected previously in incPRINT screens of 9 different RNAs (Fig. 1D), suggesting a high level of specificity for interaction with CRNDEh. Consistent with the predominantly cytoplasmic localisation of CRNDEh-MS2, 76% (57/72) of interacting proteins are predicted by the Compartments database [25] to be at least partially localised within the cytosol (cytosolic confidence score ≥ 2).

We sought to focus on the interaction detected between CRNDEh and the NAD-dependent protein deacetylase Silent Mating Type Information Regulation 2 Homologue 1 (SIRT1) based on SIRT1’s reported function, interaction score with CRNDEh, and its sub-cellular localisation. SIRT1 can influence diverse cellular processes and signalling pathways [26] and has been reported to promote proliferation and/or survival of MM cells [19, 27, 28]. Whereas both cytoplasmic and nuclear functions have been described for SIRT1, in two MM cell lines (KMS11 and RPMI-8226), SIRT1 was found to exhibit an exclusively cytoplasmic localisation (Supplementary Fig. 1E), matching the localisation of CRNDEh in the same lines [15]. RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiments confirmed a physical interaction between endogenous CRNDEh and SIRT1 protein in these two cell lines (Fig. 1E).

CRNDE stabilises SIRT1 protein in MM cells

In order to investigate the effect of CRNDE on SIRT1 expression and function in MM, we used a previously described model of KMS11 MM cells homozygous for a CRISPR-mediated deletion of exons 4-6 of the CRNDE locus (CRNDE∆/∆) which do not express the CRNDEh isoform [15] (Fig. 2A). In KMS11 cells lacking CRNDEh, there was a reduction in the steady-state level of SIRT1 protein, as measured both by Western blot (Fig. 2A) and by intra-cellular FACS analysis (Supplementary Fig. 2A). No change was observed in expression of SIRT1 mRNA in CRNDE∆/∆ cells (Fig. 2A), indicating that CRNDE affects SIRT1 protein levels rather than its transcript, consistent with the direct interaction. In inverse rescue experiments, lentiviral overexpression of exogenous CRNDEh increased the levels of SIRT1 protein in CRNDE∆/∆ KMS11 clones (Fig. 2B), again without affecting SIRT1 mRNA (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Analysis of SIRT1 stability in different MM cell lines using cycloheximide (CHX) chase experiments revealed that SIRT1 protein was more rapidly degraded in the CRNDE-deficient cell line KMS12-BM compared to three CRNDE-expressing lines (MM1.S, RPMI-8226 and KMS11) (Supplementary Fig. 3). In other cancers, it has been reported that the stability of SIRT1 is regulated by ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation [29–31]. Consistent with these reports, we found that deletion of CRNDE in KMS11 cells leads to increased ubiquitination of SIRT1 (Fig. 2C).

It has previously been shown that inhibition of SIRT1 impairs the proliferation and viability of MM cells [19, 28]. To investigate the effect of CRNDE on the tumorigenic activity of SIRT1 in MM, we analysed the sensitivity of wild-type and CRNDE∆/∆ MM tumours in a NOD-SCID IL2Rγ−/− (NSG) engraftment model to treatment with EX527, a specific inhibitor of SIRT1 [32]. While EX527 had no significant effect on CRNDEWT tumours, growth of CRNDE∆/∆ tumours was significantly impaired (Fig. 2D, Supplementary Fig. 4). This hypersensitivity to SIRT1 inhibition is consistent with the depleted levels of SIRT1 protein observed in CRNDE∆/∆ cells. Altogether, these findings indicate that the interaction with CRNDE stabilises SIRT1 protein and contributes to its pro-tumorigenic role in MM cells.

CRNDE influences the pro-survival Hedgehog signalling pathway in MM cells

It has recently been shown that one important role of SIRT1 in MM cells is to deacetylate and stabilise members of the GLI transcription factor family, key downstream mediators of the Hedgehog (Hh) signalling pathway, thereby promoting pro-survival Hh signalling and drug resistance [19]. Our findings above suggested that by stabilisation of the SIRT1 protein, CRNDE may also indirectly impact upon Hh signalling in MM. Consistent with this, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) of our previously published transcriptomic profiling of wild type and CRNDE∆/∆ KMS11 cells [15] revealed a depletion of Hedgehog signalling signatures in the absence of CRNDE (Supplementary Fig. 5A). To investigate further, we compared the early induction of the Hh survival pathway genes in CRNDEWT and CRNDE∆/∆ cells subjected to short-term challenge (3 h) with bortezomib or dexamethasone, first-line therapeutic agents used in the treatment of MM. Induction of Hh transcriptional target genes (PTCH1, GLI1, GLI2, BCL2) in response to these agents was collectively impaired in cells lacking CRNDE (Fig. 3A, B). Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) following dexamethasone treatment revealed diminished binding of GLI2 at previously described Hh-response elements (within the promoter of PTCH1 and an enhancer of HHIP [33–35]) in CRNDE∆/∆ cells (Fig. 3C), consistent with impaired GLI function in the absence of CRNDE. Analysis of apoptosis after longer treatment (48 h) with dexamethasone revealed that CRNDE∆/∆ cells were hypersensitive to dexamethasone alone (as observed previously [15]), and that this hypersensitivity was further exacerbated when dexamethasone was combined with either the SIRT inhibitor EX527 or the Hh antagonist GANT61, consistent with an impairment of the SIRT1/Hh survival pathway in the absence of CRNDE (Fig. 3D). CRNDE∆/∆ cells also showed diminished transcriptional activation of Hh target genes in response to recombinant Sonic Hedgehog ligand (1 µg/ml rSHH for 48 h) (Fig. 3E). In primary CD138+ MM tumour samples of the MMRF-CoMMpass cohort [36], patients with high levels of CRNDE were more likely to display elevated expression of a core Hh transcriptional signature (GLI1, GLI2, GLI3, HHIP, PTCH1, PTCH2, BCL2) (Supplementary Fig. 5B). In these patients, high cumulative expression of this Hh signature and high expression of CRNDE both correlated with poor prognosis (Supplementary Fig. 5C). High levels of CRNDE were also associated with elevated expression of other Hh target genes (FOXM1 and VEGFA) [37, 38], which have been directly implicated in the pathophysiology of MM [39, 40] (Supplementary Fig. 5D). Notably, as well as in primary MM samples, high levels of CRNDE also correlated with activation of the Hh signature in primary cohorts of several other malignant diseases (glioma, colon cancer, papillary thyroid cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma) in which both Hh signalling and CRNDE have previously been implicated (Supplementary Fig. 6). Taken together these findings indicate that CRNDE contributes to the Hh signalling pathway in myeloma cells and that high expression of CRNDE is also associated with enhanced activation of this pathway in other malignant diseases.

CRNDE contributes to the maintenance of the tumour stem cell-like compartment in MM cells

As in other cancers [41], Hedgehog signalling has been particularly implicated in the maintenance of a tumour stem cell-like compartment in MM [19, 42–45], which is characterised by enhanced self-renewal and clonogenic potential and is believed to be important for chemoresistance and patient relapse [46, 47]. We therefore investigated the effect of CRNDE on clonogenic potential and stem-cell-like properties using the KMS11 model. We initially measured aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH) activity, an enzyme implicated in cellular detoxification which is a marker of stem-like properties and clonogenic potential in MM cells [45, 46] (Supplementary Fig. 7A), as well as in other tumours and adult tissues [48]. ALDEFLUOR staining of ALDH activity revealed a reduction in the proportion of ALDH+ cells in KMS11 clones lacking CRNDE (Fig. 4A). Consistent with this finding, CRNDE∆/∆ cells demonstrated a reduced clonogenic potential in colony forming assays, which was maintained across serial platings (Fig. 4B). The reduced clonogenicity of CRNDE∆/∆ cells was partially rescued by pre-treating the cells with the SIRT1 activator SRT1720 (0.5 µM for 48 h) (Fig. 4C) or a prolonged stimulation with rSHH ligand (2 µg/ml for 96 h) (Fig. 4D) prior to plating in clonogenic assays, indicating the contribution of the impaired SIRT1/Hh axis to the clonogenic defect in cells lacking CRNDE. Taken together, these results reveal that CRNDE influences the stem properties of MM cells via an effect on the SIRT1/Hh axis.

Discussion

In this study, we report a physical interaction between the lncRNA CRNDE and the SIRT1 protein, which emerged from an unbiased high-throughput in-cell approach. We show that CRNDE limits ubiquitination and stabilises SIRT1 protein in a cellular model of MM. Notably, a physical association between CRNDE and SIRT1 has also been observed in rat bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, where CRNDE also impedes ubiquitination and stabilises SIRT1 protein [49]. Together, these results suggest that this functional interaction has been conserved throughout evolution. To our knowledge, our study is the first one to identify and characterise this interaction in human tissues, and in the context of malignant disease. From findings in other cell types, it is evident that SIRT1 ubiquitination can be influenced by the opposing activities of various ubiquitin transferase- and deubiquitinating enzymes [50]. Further studies will be required to understand how CRNDE affects the interactions between SIRT1 and its modifying partner proteins in MM. Interestingly, other lncRNAs have also been reported to regulate SIRT1 protein stability [51]. Moreover, several recent papers have reported that CRNDE can also stabilise other interacting proteins, suggesting that this could be an important mechanism by which it exerts its biological activity [52–56].

Targets of SIRT1-mediated deacetylation include both histones and a set of important non-histone proteins, including p53, c-MYC, E2F1, the RelA/p65 subunit of NFκB, the FOXO family of transcription factors [57], as well as the GLI2 transcription factor of the Hh pathway [19]. As a result, SIRT1 can influence diverse signalling pathways and cellular processes [58, 59] and has been variously implicated in tumor-promoting and tumour-suppressive effects in different cancers [26, 59–61]. The contribution of SIRT1 to the aetiology of MM appears complex, with reports of both anti- [62–64] and pro- [19, 27, 28] tumorigenic roles.

The results of our study support those of Xie et al., who demonstrated a pro-tumorigenic role for SIRT1 in MM by promoting the Hedgehog survival pathway [19]. In that study, treatment with the SIRT inhibitor EX527 enhanced drug sensitivity, and acted synergistically with the Hh inhibitor GANT61 to induce apoptosis of MM cells. Following the observation that CRNDE stabilises SIRT1 protein, we showed here that CRNDE also contributes to Hh signalling in MM cells. While CRNDE has been widely implicated in diverse cancers, to our knowledge this is the first study to report its influences on the Hh signalling pathway. Further, based on the established role of Hh signalling within the MM stem cell compartment, we show that CRNDE contributes to maintenance of clonogenicity and stem-like characteristics of MM cells, considered as a critical determinant of therapeutic response and disease relapse. Our observations that CRNDE also correlates with expression of Hh target genes in other cancers suggests that the interaction between CRNDE with SIRT1 could influence stemness, therapy resistance and outcome in diverse malignant diseases, and is worthy of further investigation.

Together with our previous demonstration that CRNDE affects IL6/JAK/STAT signalling in MM [15], the present study indicates that CRNDE makes a pleiotropic, multi-faceted contribution to the pathophysiology of this disease, by also influencing Hedgehog signalling and stemness properties of MM cells. In addition to SIRT1, other CRNDE-interacting proteins identified in this study (Supplementary Table 1) may also be relevant for the activity of CRNDE in MM and other cancers and will require further investigation. Overall, these new findings increase the interest in CRNDE as a promising therapeutic target to enhance or prolong drug sensitivity and limit relapse in MM patients.

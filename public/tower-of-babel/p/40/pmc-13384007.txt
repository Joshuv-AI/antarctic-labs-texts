Protective efficacy and DIVA potential of a rfbG and galE double-deleted Salmonella Enteritidis attenuated live vaccine in chickens
Authors: Yanyu Ruan, Xilong Kang, Zeyun Lin, Tianlong Sun, Zhiming Pan, Xinwei Wang, Tingting Pan, Yimeng Zhao, Hongqin Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSalmonella Enteritidis poses a significant threat to poultry health and production, leading to substantial economic losses worldwide. Transmission through contaminated poultry products poses a continuous risk to public health. Live-attenuated vaccines are a promising approach for controlling avian salmonellosis. However, achieving an optimal balance between safety, protective efficacy, and differentiating infected from vaccinated animals (DIVA) remains a major challenge. In the present study, a targeted double-gene deletion mutant designated Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was generated, and we systematically assessed its safety profile, protective efficacy, and DIVA potential were systematically assessed. The mutant exhibited a 1,243-fold reduction in virulence compared to the wild-type S. Enteritidis Z11 strain. Dose optimization experiments identified 5 × 10⁷ colony-forming units (CFU) as the minimum protective dose, which was subsequently used for vaccine evaluation. Following immunization, the chickens showed normal growth performance, no observable clinical abnormalities, and complete clearance of the vaccine strain from the liver and spleen within 21 d, thus presenting a favorable safety profile. Moreover, vaccination elicited strong humoral immunity, as evidenced by a significant increase in Salmonella-specific IgG levels following booster immunization. Notably, sera from chickens vaccinated with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE were negative in a commercial slide agglutination assay, whereas sera from wild-type-infected chickens were positive, confirming its DIVA compatibility. Upon challenge with the wild-type strain, immunized chickens exhibited a 100 % survival rate, compared with a 50 % survival rate in the non-immunized group. In addition, vaccination significantly reduced bacterial colonization in the liver and spleen and prevented the development of clinical signs and pathological lesions. These findings indicate that the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE strain is a promising attenuated vaccine candidate that combines safety, immunogenicity, protective efficacy, and DIVA compatibility for the control of S. Enteritidis infection in poultry.Keywords: Salmonella Enteritidis, Live attenuated vaccine, rfbG, galE, DIVA

Introduction

Salmonella, a Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacterium from the Enterobacteriaceae family, is one of the leading causes of foodborne illnesses worldwide (Majowicz et al., 2010). Among more than 2,600 Salmonella serovars, serovar Enteritidis (S. Enteritidis) is one of the most prevalent serotypes associated with human infections globally (Samper-Cativiela et al., 2025; Zakaria et al., 2021). Human salmonellosis is frequently associated with the consumption of contaminated poultry products and eggs (EFSA, 2025; Gantois et al., 2009; Howard et al., 2012), highlighting the critical role of poultry as a major reservoir for transmission.

In layer hens, S. Enteritidis infection reduces egg production and increases the incidence of malformed and soft-shelled eggs (Howard et al., 2012). Infected chicks may display lethargy, anorexia, hypothermia, and diarrhea, with peracute cases showing no lesions before death (Shivaprasad, 2000). Chronic infections often lead to hepatomegaly with visible necrotic foci, causing substantial economic losses as a result of mortality, flock replacement, and treatment costs (Eriksson et al., 2018).

Currently, the control of S. Enteritidis infections depends on antibiotics. However, widespread antibiotic use has accelerated antimicrobial resistance (AMR), leading to the emergence of multidrug-resistant (MDR) strains and significant challenges for the livestock sector (Cheng and Cheng, 2024). With the increasingly stringent regulations restricting the use of antibiotics in food-producing animals, the development of effective alternative control strategies is imperative. Live-attenuated vaccines represent a promising approach for controlling avian salmonellosis. They can induce robust immune responses and provide long-lasting protection, often surpassing that of inactivated vaccines (Wang et al., 2023). Although these vaccines are generally safe for chickens of all ages, concerns regarding their residual virulence and potential reversion persist (Abdelaziz et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). The ability to differentiate vaccinated animals from those naturally infected with wild-type strains (DIVA) is an important consideration in vaccine design, particularly for disease surveillance and control programs.

A previous study conducted in our laboratory identified an rfbG single-gene deletion mutant of S. Enteritidis Z11 as a potential DIVA-compatible live vaccine (Wang et al., 2022). However, the rfbG single-gene deletion conferred a limited degree of attenuation, with an approximately 56-fold increase in the lethal dose 50 % (LD₅₀) compared with that of the wild-type strain, indicating that additional genetic modifications were required to further enhance vaccine safety. Mechanistic studies from our laboratory revealed that the galE gene plays an important role in S. Enteritidis virulence by suppressing host inflammasome activation, and deletion of galE significantly attenuated bacterial pathogenicity in vivo (Huang et al., 2022). Therefore, we hypothesized that combining rfbG deletion with galE deletion would further enhance attenuation, while preserving immunogenicity and DIVA compatibility. In the present study, we generated a targeted double-gene deletion mutant designated Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE and comprehensively assessed its attenuation, safety, protective efficacy, and DIVA potential in chickens, with the goal of developing an improved live attenuated vaccine candidate against S. Enteritidis.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals

Specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens (7-day-old) were purchased from Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica Biotech (Jiangsu) Co., Ltd. China. All chickens were housed in individual isolation units with ad libitum access to a commercial diet and sterile water. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University. (IACUC license number: 202104012).

Bacterial strains and generation of Salmonella Enteritidis Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant

A virulent wild-type S. Enteritidis strain Z11, which was clinically isolated from chickens, was obtained from our laboratory repository (Wang et al., 2022). The previously constructed Z11ΔrfbG strain (Wang et al., 2022), a lipopolysaccharide (LPS) rough mutant, was used as the parental strain for generation of the double-deletion mutant Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE. Deletion of the galE gene in the Z11ΔrfbG mutant was conducted through homologous recombination with the suicide plasmid pDM4, following a previously described method (Jiao et al., 2018). Briefly, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification was used to generate the upstream and downstream fragments of the galE gene. The pDM4 vector was digested using Sal I and Sac I restriction enzymes (TaKaRa Biotechnology Co. Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China). Purified plasmid and PCR fragments were ligated using the ClonExpress MultiS One-Step Cloning Kit (Vazyme Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The resulting recombinant plasmid was transformed into E. coli X7213 cells and verified using DNA sequencing. The conjugal transfer of the verified plasmid into S. Enteritidis Z11ΔrfbG yielded the double mutant, which was selected through culturing on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar containing 15 % sucrose. Successful deletion of galE was confirmed using PCR with external and internal primers. Primer sequences are listed in Table 1.

In vitro growth and biological characteristics

The S. Enteritidis strains Z11 and Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE were cultured separately in LB broth at 37°C with shaking. Overnight cultures were diluted in fresh LB medium to an optical density at 600 nm (OD600) of 0.05, and bacterial growth was monitored by measuring the OD600 at hourly intervals. At an OD600 of approximately 1.0, the viable count of the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant corresponded to approximately 6 × 10⁸ CFU/mL, which was confirmed by plating serial dilutions on LB agar and counting CFU. Biochemical profiles were compared using commercial test strips (Qingdao Haibo Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Qingdao, China) to assess reactions with peptone water, urease, potassium cyanide (KCN) tolerance, lysine decarboxylation, ornithine decarboxylation, and fermentation of mannitol, sorbitol, salicin, malonate, and dulcitol. The rough phenotype of LPS was assessed using slide agglutination with an O9 monoclonal antibody (Ningbo Tianrun Biological Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Ningbo, China) or with acriflavine hydrochloride (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

Virulence assessment

The LD50 of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was determined in 7-D-old SPF chicks to evaluate its virulence. A total of 110 chicks were randomly assigned to 11 groups (n = 10). Groups were challenged intramuscularly with 100 μL tenfold serial dilutions of either wild-type Z11 (5 × 105 to 5 × 109 CFU) or the mutant strain (5 × 107 to 5 × 1011 CFU); the control group received phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Mortality was observed daily throughout the 14-d trial period, and LD50 values were calculated according to the Reed-Muench method (Reed and Muench, 1938).

Minimal immunizing dose assessment

Thirty-six 7-D-old SPF chicks were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 12/group) and immunized with 5 × 10⁶, 5 × 10⁷, or 5 × 10⁸ CFU/bird, respectively. The birds received prime immunization through intramuscular injection (100 μL) at 7 d of age, followed by a homologous boost 11 d later. In addition, a PBS control and an unimmunized challenge control were included, with 12 chicks assigned to each group. The PBS and unvaccinated challenge controls received injections with 100 μL PBS. At 11 d post-boost, all groups were challenged intramuscularly with 1 × 10⁹ CFU of the wild-type S. Enteritidis Z11 (100 μL/bird). Clinical signs and mortality were monitored daily for 14 d post-challenge (DPC) to evaluate the protective efficacy across the dosage range. The minimum protective dose was defined as the lowest immunization dose that conferred complete protection against a lethal challenge.

In vivo colonization and clearance kinetics of the Salmonella Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant

To evaluate the in vivo persistence and clearance dynamics of the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant, twenty-four 7-D-old SPF chicks were randomly allocated to two groups (n = 12/group). Birds were intramuscularly inoculated with 100 μL of either the mutant strain (5 × 10⁷ CFU) or sterile PBS. Tissues were harvested at 3, 7, 14, and 21 d post inoculation. The liver and spleen samples were aseptically collected, homogenized in PBS, serially diluted, and enumerated on LB agar. Bacterial loads are expressed as log₁₀ CFU/g of tissue.

Body weight measurement and clinical symptoms observation post-immunization

A total of twenty 7-D-old SPF chickens were randomly allocated into two groups and inoculated intramuscularly with 100 μL of either the mutant strain Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE (5 × 10⁷ CFU) or sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Booster immunization was administered 10 d after primary immunization using the same dose and route. Body weight alterations and clinical manifestations (anorexia, diarrhea, lethargy, and mortality) were documented on d 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 after primary and booster immunizations.

Serum IgG detection

On day 9 following the primary and booster immunizations, blood samples were collected from the wing vein. Serum was subsequently separated and stored at −70°C until further analysis. S. Enteritidis-specific IgG levels were determined by indirect ELISA as previously described (Wang et al., 2021). Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE antigen (adjusted to 1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL, OD₆₀₀ = 1.0) following pre-treatment with 5 % glutaraldehyde. After heat-fixing and blocking with 1 % BSA, sera were diluted 1:100 and titrated. Plates were incubated with HRP-conjugated rabbit anti-chicken IgG (1:5,000; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at 37°C. TMB substrate (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was added for 45 s, the reaction was stopped with 0.5 M H₂SO₄, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

DIVA capability assessment

The DIVA potential of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was assessed using a slide agglutination assay for the detection of LPS-specific serum antibodies. Fifteen 7-D-old chickens were divided into three groups (n = 5) and infected with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE (5 × 107 CFU), S. Enteritidis Z11 (5 × 106 CFU), or PBS. Serum samples were collected at 14 DPC and examined using a commercial agglutination antigen (Zhonghai Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

Immune protection assessment

Thirty-six 7-D-old SPF chickens were randomly divided into three groups (n = 12): immunization group (immunized with 5 × 107 CFU Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE, challenged), challenge control (unimmunized, challenged), and PBS control (unimmunized, unchallenged). For immunization group, chickens were immunized twice by intramuscular injection at 7 and 17 d of age with a dose of 5 × 107 CFU per bird; PBS control and challenge control received injection with 100 μL PBS. Subsequently, the immunized group and challenge control group were challenged intramuscularly with 1 × 10⁹ CFU of the wild-type S. Enteritidis Z11 at 11 d following the booster immunization. Clinical symptoms and mortality were recorded daily for 14 DPC to assess the protective efficacy of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE.

Bacterial clearance assay

To evaluate the clearance of the wild-type S. Enteritidis Z11, 60 7-D-old SPF chicks were randomly divided into three groups (n = 20/group). The group design, immunization dose and route, as well as the challenge dose and route, were consistent with those described in the aforementioned "Immune protection assessment" section. Following this challenge, the surviving birds were euthanized at 7, 14, and 21 DPC. Liver and spleen tissues were aseptically collected, homogenized in PBS, serially diluted, and plated onto LB agar for bacterial culture. Bacterial loads are presented as log₁₀ colony-forming units per gram (log₁₀ CFU/g) of tissue.

Histological analysis

At 14 DPC, surviving chickens were euthanized. Liver tissues were fixed in 10 % neutral-buffered formalin, processed using standard procedures, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histopathological examination (Cardiff et al., 2014).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). For comparisons among independent groups, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni’s post-hoc test was used. For longitudinal measurements (such as body weight trajectories and growth kinetics), a repeated-measures ANOVA with appropriate pairwise post-hoc comparisons was applied. All values are expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

Results

Construction and confirmation of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

PCR amplification using the primer pair rfbG-out-F/R yielded a 2423 bp fragment from the wild-type S. Enteritidis Z11 and a 1343 bp fragment from the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant. Using the primer set rfbG-in-F/R, a 1080 bp product was amplified from the wild-type strain, whereas no corresponding band was detected in the mutant. Similarly, amplification with galE-out-F/R produced fragments of 2285 bp (wild-type) and 1278 bp (mutant), consistent with the expected deletion sizes (Supplementary Figure S1). PCR using galE-in-F/R generated a 570 bp product in the wild-type, but no amplification was observed in the mutant. These results confirm the complete deletion of both the rfbG and galE genes (Supplementary Figure S1), revealing that the double-deletion mutant Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was successfully constructed.

Biological characteristics of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

Growth curve analysis in LB broth at 37°C revealed no significant differences between the wild-type strain Z11 and the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant (Fig. 1). Biochemical profiling using commercial test strips yielded identical results for both strains across all the substrates tested, including peptone water, urease, potassium cyanide (and its control), lysine (and its amino acid control), mannitol, sorbitol, salicin, malonate, ONPG, and dulcitol, confirming that both strains shared the same biochemical profile (Fig. 2). In the slide agglutination assay, the wild-type Z11 strain agglutinated strongly with O9 antiserum, whereas the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant showed no agglutination. Conversely, the mutant exhibited a markedly stronger agglutination response than the wild-type strain in the acriflavine hydrochloride than the wild-type strain, consistent with an LPS rough phenotype (Fig. 3).

Virulence attenuation of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

The virulence of the wild-type Z11 strain and its mutant Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was evaluated in 7-D-old SPF chickens following intramuscular inoculation. Birds were monitored for 14 d post-infection. As shown in Table 2, the LD50 values of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant and wild-type Z11 strain were 5.72 × 1010 CFU and 4.6 × 107 CFU, respectively. In addition, the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant exhibited a 1,243-fold reduction in virulence compared with the wild-type Z11 strain.

Minimal immunizing dose determination

The protective efficacy of the different dose of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant against lethal challenge is summarized in Table 3. Chickens immunized with the high dose (5 × 10⁸ CFU) or the intermediate dose (5 × 10⁷ CFU) exhibited 100 % survival (12/12) throughout the 14-d observation period. In contrast, the low-dose group (5 × 106 CFU) showed incomplete protection with a survival rate of 91.6 % (11/12). As the 5 × 10⁷ CFU dose provided complete protection comparable to that of the higher dose, it is defined as the minimal immunizing dose for the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE candidate vaccine. Therefore, a dose of 5 × 10⁷ CFU was selected for all subsequent immunization experiments.

In vivo colonization and clearance kinetics

The tissue distribution and clearance dynamics of the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant in chicks were evaluated following intramuscular immunization. As shown in Fig. 4, the mutant was rapidly cleared from the liver, with viable bacteria becoming undetectable as early as 3 d post-immunization (DPI). In contrast, bacterial colonization persisted in the spleen. The bacterial load in the spleen followed a dynamic trajectory, peaking at 7 DPI and subsequently declining. By 21 DPI, the mutant was completely eradicated from both the liver and spleen. These findings reveal that the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant undergoes a complete cycle of transient colonization followed by systematic clearance within 21 DPI.

Body weight changes measurement and clinical symptoms observation post-immunization

Body weight changes were monitored in immunized chickens and in the PBS control group following both primary and booster immunizations. Body weight gain did not differ significantly between the vaccinated and control birds throughout the observation period (Fig. 5). In addition, no clinical signs, such as diarrhea, anorexia, or lethargy were observed in the vaccinated chickens. These findings indicate that immunization with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE strain does not adversely affect growth performance or general health.

Humoral immune response induced by Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

Humoral immune response induced by the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE was evaluated by measuring S. Enteritidis-specific IgG levels in serum. The results showed that S. Enteritidis -specific IgG titers were significantly increased in immunized chickens after both the primary and booster immunizations compared with the PBS control group (Fig. 6). These results indicate that immunization with the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE elicits a strong humoral immune response.

DIVA capability of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

Slide agglutination assay was employed to detect LPS-specific antibodies and assess the DIVA potential of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE. Serum from chickens vaccinated with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE showed no agglutination with commercial agglutination antigen, while serum from wild-type Z11-infected chickens displayed distinct agglutination (Fig. 7). These results demonstrate that immunization with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE allows reliable differentiation between vaccinated and naturally infected chickens, confirming its DIVA compatibility.

Protective efficacy of Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE against virulent S. Enteritidis challenge

Following vaccination, chickens were challenged with the wild-type Z11 strain, and survival, body weight changes, and clinical signs were monitored to evaluate protective efficacy. The survival of chickens is shown in Table 4; the vaccinated group achieved 100 % survival (12/12), whereas only 50 % (6/12) survival was observed in the unvaccinated challenge control group. Mortality in the control group commenced on 7 DPC (Fig. 8A) and was accompanied by the typical clinical signs of salmonellosis, including hypothermia, diarrhea, anorexia, and ruffled feathers. In contrast, chickens in all immunized groups remained clinically normal and maintained a good mental status. Furthermore, body weight gain in the control group was notably suppressed over the 2-week observation period compared to that in the immunized groups (Fig. 8B), indicating that immunization confers complete protection against lethal infection as well as mitigates the negative impact of infection on growth performance.

Systemic bacterial clearance following vaccination with Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE

Nine days after the booster immunization, liver and spleen samples from both vaccinated and control birds showed no bacterial growth. After challenge with wild-type S. At 7 DPC, the bacteria were effectively cleared from the livers of immunized chickens, whereas viable Salmonella colonies were recovered from non-immunized chickens at both 7 and 14 DPC (Fig. 9A). A similar clearance pattern is observed in the spleen. By 14 DPC, bacterial clearance was complete in the spleens of immunized birds, whereas substantial colonization persisted in the non-immunized group (Fig. 9B). Histopathological analysis

No significant pathological alterations were observed upon histopathological examination of H&E-stained liver sections from PBS control or vaccinated-challenged birds. In contrast, livers from non-immunized, challenged chickens displayed grossly visible white nodules(Supplementary Figure S2). Histologically, the livers from immunized chickens showed uniformly arranged hepatocytes with minimal inflammatory infiltration, although mild cytoplasmic vacuolation was occasionally observed (Fig. 10). The non-immunized challenged group exhibited severe hepatic damage, including nuclear pyknosis, extensive vacuolar degeneration, hepatocellular swelling, and marked inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 10).

Discussion

Epidemiological surveillance data have consistently identified S. Enteritidis as a predominant serotype responsible for human salmonellosis, thereby highlighting its continued public health importance (Deng et al., 2025; Di Marcantonio et al., 2020). Among more than 2,600 Salmonella serotypes, S. Enteritidis poses a major threat to poultry health and production (Diep et al., 2019). Notably, asymptomatic carriage in adult birds facilitates persistent environmental contamination and transmission, thereby complicating effective control strategies for poultry production systems (Velge et al., 2022). Therefore, effective control of Salmonella at the farm level mitigates its adverse impact on poultry health and production as well as reduces the risk of transmission to humans, highlighting the importance of source control in safeguarding both animal and public health (Campos et al., 2019). Vaccination is a practical and effective component of integrated control strategies aimed at reducing Salmonella colonization and transmission in poultry production systems (Mastroeni et al., 2001). However, the development of live-attenuated vaccines has always been challenging because improving safety often comes at the expense of immunogenicity and protective capacity by minimizing residual virulence.

The outer membrane, particularly LPS, is critical for Salmonella virulence and environmental persistence (Blachowicz et al., 2019). The O-antigen structure, determined by the genes in the rfb cluster, influences virulence and immunogenicity (Raetz and Whitfield, 2002). Deletion of specific LPS genes enables the development of DIVA vaccines that enable serological distinction between infected and vaccinated animals (Pasick, 2004). Our previous study identified rfbG as a potential DIVA marker (Wang et al., 2022). The galE gene, which encodes UDP-galactose-4-epimerase, is essential for LPS biosynthesis and virulence (Kaplan et al., 2009). Studies on Salmonella Typhimurium and Salmonella Typhi have shown that galE deletion mutants are highly attenuated, which supports their use as vaccine candidates (Northen et al., 2010). Recent evidence indicates galE also inhibits inflammasome activation, further linking it to pathogenicity (Huang et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022). Based on these observations, we rationally combined rfbG and galE deletions to enhance attenuation, while preserving DIVA compatibility.

Consistent with this design, the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant exhibited a marked 1,243-fold reduction in virulence compared to the wild-type strain, a reduction that far surpassed that of the parental Z11ΔrfbG single-deletion mutant and effectively allayed concerns regarding residual virulence. The vaccinated chickens displayed normal growth and remained free of clinical signs, confirming the favorable safety profile of the mutant. Notably, this enhanced attenuation did not occur at the expense of immunogenicity. Immunization elicited significantly higher levels of Salmonella-specific IgG compared to controls, which is consistent with observations for other live attenuated vaccines (Wang et al., 2022). Therefore, the balance between safety and efficacy is critical. Unlike O-antigen-deficient mutants, which often exhibit a concomitant decline in immunogenicity with gene deletion (Senevirathne et al., 2024), the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE strain retained robust humoral immune responses.

Protective efficacy was further confirmed in challenge experiments. The vaccinated group achieved 100 % survival, significantly higher than the 50 % survival in unvaccinated controls. This level of protection surpasses that of several recently reported dual-deletion candidates, such as CZ14-1ΔspiCΔnmpC (80 % protection) and 50336ΔryhB-1ΔsopE (∼70 % protection) (Liu et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Moreover, vaccinated birds demonstrated efficient clearance of the challenge strain from systemic organs, with complete eradication from the liver and spleen by 14 d post-infection. This clearance profile represents a notable improvement over both the parental Z11ΔrfbG mutant—which still harbored detectable colonization at 14 d (Wang et al., 2022)—and other dual-deletion strains that require up to 21 d for full clearance (Liu et al., 2023). Mechanistically, we hypothesize that this accelerated clearance is linked to the galE deletion. Removing galE likely abolishes its inhibitory effect on the NLRP3 inflammasome (Huang et al., 2022), thereby enhancing early innate immune activation (e.g., macrophage recruitment and cytokine production) and facilitating rapid bacterial killing.

Deletion of rfbG also enabled key DIVA capabilities. Slide agglutination assays showed that sera from chickens immunized with the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE strain did not react with the standard diagnostic agglutinating antigen, whereas sera from wild-type infected birds gave a strong positive reaction. This feature overcomes a significant limitation of the current commercial inactivated vaccines, which elicit antibodies against the O-antigen and thus cannot differentiate between vaccinated and naturally infected animals (EFSA, 2025).

Although the current study establishes the safety and protective capacity of the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE mutant, the evidence is currently restricted to controlled laboratory settings using SPF chickens and intramuscular challenge models. This approach circumvents the gastrointestinal mucosal barrier and fails to fully recapitulate the dynamics of natural Salmonella infection, which occurs predominantly via the oral-fecal route. The absence of mucosal immunity data (such as secretory IgA) and the reliance on a prime-boost regimen further underscore that this candidate is still in the early stages of development. Indeed, while the double-attenuation strategy enhances safety, it may have compromised the strain's ability to survive gastrointestinal transit. Therefore, the translational potential of this vaccine candidate necessitates further systematic validation. Future studies must prioritize optimizing the strain's gastrointestinal survivability and transitioning to oral administration for both vaccination and challenge. In addition, comprehensive biosafety assessments, including environmental shedding, long-term genetic stability, and the potential for reversion to virulence, will be essential before field application. Validation within commercial flocks—accounting for variables such as maternal antibody levels, breed diversity, and complex farm environments—will be essential.

In conclusion, the Z11ΔrfbGΔgalE double-deletion mutant presented here integrates robust attenuation, a favorable safety profile, strong immunogenicity, effective protective efficacy, and DIVA compatibility. This rationally designed live attenuated vaccine candidate holds considerable promise for controlling Salmonella Enteritidis in poultry, pending successful optimization for oral delivery and validation in field trials.

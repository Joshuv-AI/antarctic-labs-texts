A phase therapy regimen containing clofazimine manifested high efficacy in treating Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease
Authors: Shengjuan Bao, Suting Chen, Hairong Huang, Hongfei Duan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe guidelines for Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease do not provide a clear approach to combination therapy. This study aims to assess the effectiveness of phase therapy (PT) containing clofazimine (CFZ) for patients with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease.MethodsThis study was carried out at Beijing Chest Hospital, Capital Medical University. It included 24 confirmed cases of Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease who were prospectively enrolled and treated with a CFZ-containing regimen. Additionally, 35 confirmed cases were retrospectively included for comparison. Baseline information of patients was collected. Patients were followed up for over a year. Minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) testing was conducted on clinical isolates from patients to determine if there is a correlation between MIC values and sputum culture conversion.ResultsThe retrospective patients achieved a 36.7% (11/30) sputum culture conversion while PT achieved a 70.8% (17/24) sputum culture conversion. Patients using the PT regimen were more likely to achieve sputum culture conversion (P = 0.013). The MIC range of CFZ of baseline isolates from 54 Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease patients was 0.125 -1 mg/L. Among prospective patients, the success rate of treatment was 86.7% (13/15) for patients with CFZ MIC < 0.5 mg/L, while it was 44.4% (4/9) for those with MIC ≥ 0.5 mg/L. Prospective patients with CFZ MIC < 0.5 mg/L were more likely to achieve sputum culture conversion (P = 0.014). Logistic regression analysis showed that prospective patients who received the five-drug regimen were independently associated with sputum culture conversion [OR = 6.203, 95% CI = (1.171–32.865), P = 0.032).ConclusionThe PT regimen is effective against Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, particularly in cases where CFZ MIC is < 0.5 mg/L.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-026-04170-w.Keywords: Mycobacterium abscessus, Pulmonary disease, Phase therapy, Clofazimine, Culture conversion

Background

Mycobacterium abscessus is the most common rapidly growing nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM), which includes the Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies abscessus, Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies massiliense, and Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies bolletii [1]. It poses a substantial threat to patients’ health.

Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease is a challenging disease to treat due to the intrinsic resistance of Mycobacterium abscessus to many antibiotics. As a result, there is currently no standardized treatment regimen for Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, nor is there a consensus on the optimal duration of therapy. 2020 American Thoracic Society/European Society of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases/European Respiratory Society/Infectious Diseases Society of America (ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA) guidelines proposed phase therapy (PT) for patients with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. Injectable options for the initial phase include amikacin (AMK), imipenem (IPM), cefoxitin (CEF), or tigecycline. Oral antibiotics include azithromycin (AZI) or clarithromycin (CLA), clofazimine (CFZ) and linezolid (LZD). Oral antibiotics for the continuation phase include AZI or CLA, CFZ, LZD and inhaled AMK. But the guidelines do not provide explicit recommendations for combination therapy [2]. Furthermore, the study by Kwak N et al. demonstrated that the use of azithromycin, amikacin, or imipenem was associated with better clinical outcomes in patients with pulmonary disease caused by Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies abscessus [3]. PT has been demonstrated to be effective in Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease [4, 5], but there are limited reports on using PT containing CFZ.

CFZ was initially used to treat leprosy caused by Mycobacterium leprae, and it has now been employed in treating drug-resistant tuberculosis [6], demonstrating promising activity against mycobacteria. In recent years, there has been increasingly evidence showing that CFZ is promising for the treatment of Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. The MIC90 range of CFZ for Mycobacterium abscessus is 0.5 - >8 mg/L[7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. In vitro experiments also have shown that CFZ exhibits synergistic effects with AMK, CLR, and Linezolid (LZD) [13–18].

Considering the synergistic effects of CFZ in combination with other antibiotics and the recommendations of the ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA, we prospectively enrolled a subset of patients with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease and administered a treatment regimen containing CFZ. The specific treatment plan consisted of an initial phase of AZI, CFZ, LZD, AMK and IPM, followed by a continuation phase of AZI, CFZ and LZD. We also retrospectively included a subset of patients with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease to analyze whether the CFZ-contained PT could benefit them and to identify factors associated with sputum culture conversion.

Methods

Study participants

After signing the informed consent form, patients diagnosed with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease and meeting the inclusion criteria received a CFZ-containing regimen for anti-M. abscessus treatment. A patient was prospectively included in the study if (1) he or she was 18 years of age or older; (2) met ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA diagnostic criteria for Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease; and (3) had cavitary or advanced bronchiectasis disease. Exclusion criteria included (1) mixed infections; (2) patients on long-term immunosuppressive therapy; (3) breastfeeding or pregnant women and (4) patients with other severe underlying conditions.

Microbiological and radiological examinations

Sputum was cultured for all the patients before initiating Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease treatment. Primers targeting heat shock protein 65 (hsp65), β-subunit of bacterial RNA polymerase (rpoB), and 16 S to 23 S rRNA internal transcribed spacer (ITS) were utilized for polymerase chain reaction (PCR) [19–21]. Subsequently, the amplified products were sequenced, and the nucleotide sequences were analyzed using the Blast tool in the NCBI database. If two or more targets identify the same bacterial species, it is considered as the determined species.

All patients underwent chest computed tomography (CT) before treatment initiation and during treatment monitoring. The radiological form was categorized as nodular bronchiectatic, nodular bronchiectatic with fibrocavitary, and fibrocavitary.

Treatment regimen establishment

For prospective patients, during the initial phase, two injectable agents, AMK and IPM, along with oral AZI, CFZ and LZD, were administered. In the continuation phase, oral AZI, CFZ, and LZD were used. IPM was used for 4 weeks, while AMK was used at least until the first sputum culture conversion but no longer than 6 months.

Treatment response monitoring and bacterial species identification

A follow-up of more than 12 months was conducted on newly enrolled patients. During the follow-up period, the patient’s medication usage, sputum culture results, improvement in clinical symptoms, and radiological responses were documented. Chest CT scans were performed at baseline and post-treatment. All images were independently reviewed by two experienced thoracic radiologists blinded to treatment details and clinical outcomes. Scans were anonymized to ensure blinding, and any discrepancies were resolved by consensus or adjudication by a third senior radiologist. Symptom improvement is defined as a reduction in cough and sputum production, while imaging improvement is indicated by a decrease in the number of nodules or a reduction in cavity size. Baseline clinical isolates from the patients before initiating medication were collected.

Retrospective patient enrollment

Patients diagnosed with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease at Beijing Chest Hospital from 2014 to 2020, who completed the entire treatment duration, were retrospectively enrolled. The baseline information, clinical medication history, laboratory test results, and radiological imaging findings were collected, and a comparative analysis with prospective cases was conducted (Fig. 1).

Study flowchart

Measurement of MIC for anti-Mycobacterium abscessus agents

Susceptibility test for anti-Mycobacterium abscessus agents, including AZI, AMK, IPM, LZD, and CFZ was performed using the broth microdilution concentration method. The protocol adhered to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute guidelines. Anti-Mycobacterium abscessus agents were serially diluted with Mueller Hinton II Broth Cation (MH), in a 96-well plate in 100 µL, the MIC range for each drug has been shown in Fig. 2. The culture grown to the logarithmic phase was scraped from the Lowenstein-Jensen culture medium and adjusted into 0.5 McFarland concentration (about 1010CFU/L) with saline. The suspended bacterial solution was diluted 200-fold in 20 mL MH. Each well of the anti-Mycobacterium abscessus agents’ plates was inoculated with 100 µL of the mixture. 70 µL Alamar blue dye (a mixture of 20µL Alamar blue and 50 µL 5%Tween80) was added to each well on the 3rd day, while MIC values for each Mycobacterium abscessus isolates were determined 24 h later.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed by Stata version 14.2 (StataCorp), and P < 0.05 was considered significant. Data were presented as numbers (%) for categorical variables and median (interquartile range [IQR]) for continuous variables. Categorical variables were compared using the Pearson chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The continuous variables, subject to normal distribution, were compared with the t-test, otherwise described by the Mann-Whitney U test. Independent variables were selected on the basis of their statistical significance in the univariate analysis. The criterion for inclusion of a variable in the multivariate analysis was a significance level of<0.2.

Results

Patient population

There were 54 cases of Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, with 38 cases of Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies abscessus pulmonary disease and 16 cases of Mycobacterium abscessus subspecies massiliense pulmonary disease. 40 cases (74.0%) were female. The median age of all patients was 52.5 years (IQR 41.0-61.3), and the median BMI was 20.0 kg/m2 (IQR 18.3–21.9). A history of previous pulmonary diseases, such as tuberculosis and bronchiectasis, was present in 32 cases (59.3%) of patients. Radiological examination showed that 75.9% (41/54) of patients had nodular bronchiectasis, 9.8% (4/41) had nodular bronchiectasis with fibrocavitary, and 16.7% (9/54) had fibrocavitary changes. Cough was the most common clinical symptom, reported by 36/54 (66.7%) of patients. Laboratory tests revealed 16 cases (29.6%) had positive sputum smears. There were no differences in baseline characteristics between the retrospective and the prospective cohorts. The detailed information is shown in Table 1.

Combined medication and sputum culture conversion

All retrospective cases were administered with macrolides and injectable antibiotics, 86.7% (26 /30) of patients received CEF, 80.0% (24/30) of patients received AMK, and other oral antibiotics included LZD (40%, 12/30), CFZ (6.7%, 2/30) and fluoroquinolone (83.3%, 25/30). No association was found between a certain drug and the sputum culture conversion. The detailed information is shown in Table 3. However, the number of medications used was inconsistent. In the univariate analysis of combined treatment regimens, among the 3 patients who received a combination of 3 antibiotics, only 1 (33.3%) achieved sputum culture conversion. Among the 15 patients who received a combination of 4 antibiotics, 5 (33.3%) achieved sputum culture conversion. Among the 12 patients who received a combination of 5 antibiotics other than the prospective regimen, 5 (41.7%) achieved sputum culture conversion. Patients using a regimen with an initial phase followed by a continuation phase are more likely to achieve sputum culture conversion (P = 0.013, P = 0.032) (Table 4) (Table 5).

The drugs’ MIC and culture conversion

Among the prospective patients (n = 24) with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, the clinical baseline isolates exhibited a range of MIC values, as shown in Fig. 2. Among the prospective patients who received CFZ, the treatment success rate was 85.7% (12/14) for those with an MIC < 0.5 mg/L, while it was 44.4% (4/9) for those with an MIC ≥ 0.5 mg/L. As the CFZ MIC value decreased, the conversion rate increased, and patients with an MIC < 0.5 mg/L had a higher likelihood of achieving sputum culture conversion (P = 0.014). Other than CFZ, no association was found between the MIC of other drugs and sputum culture conversion (Fig. 2).

Discussion

We enrolled a group of prospective Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease patients and gave them a standardized PT treatment regimen. We compared these patients with another group of patients who were previously diagnosed and treated at Beijing Chest Hospital. We found that patients who received the standardized PT regimen were more likely to achieve sputum culture conversion than the retrospective patients (70.8% vs. 36.7%, P = 0.013). This significant difference prompted us to investigate the reasons behind the varying rates of sputum culture conversion.

So far, there is still no universally accepted treatment or agreement on the best drug regimen for managing Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. Previous guidelines recommend treating NTM pulmonary disease with a regimen that includes a macrolide, AMK, and CEF/IPM. The 2020 ATS/ERS/ESCMID/IDSA guidelines recommended PT to treat Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. Jeon et al.. utilized a PT treatment regimen to treat Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, resulting in a sputum culture conversion rate of 58% 22. The specific treatment protocol involved an initial phase utilizing intravenous injections of AMK and CEF, followed by a continuation phase utilizing CLR, ciprofloxacin, and doxycycline [22]. In our study, the initial phase involved using two injectables, AMK and IPM, in addition to AZI, CFZ and LZD, while the continuation phase consisted of a combination of AZI, CFZ, and LZD. The sputum culture conversion rate in this PT group (70.8%) was significantly higher than that in the three-drug (33.3%) and four-drug (33.3%) combination regimens, as well as the five-drug regimen with other agents (41.7%). This regimen increased the likelihood of achieving sputum culture conversion (P = 0.013). Logistic regression analysis also showed that prospective patients who received the five-drug regimen were independently associated with sputum culture conversion [OR = 6.203, 95% CI = (1.171–32.865), P = 0.032).

In recent years, several studies have reported new drugs for the treatment of Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, including CFZ. CFZ possesses characteristics such as a long half-life, slow metabolic clearance, and high concentrations in macrophages, which have demonstrated promising potential in combating Mycobacterium abscessus [7, 23]. Furthermore, CFZ also demonstrates synergistic effects with AMK against the Mycobacterium abscessus in vitro. Adding AMK to CFZ reduced the CFZ MIC for all clinical isolates of Mycobacterium abscessus. CFZ, whether used alone or in combination with AMK, holds great promise as a potential treatment for Mycobacterium abscessus infections [7]. In our study, the MIC range of CFZ against Mycobacterium abscessus was found to be 0.125-1 mg/L, highlighting the promising potential of CFZ in combating Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. In Kwak et al.‘s study, although no clear association was found between CFZ susceptibility test results and sputum culture conversion in patients with Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, among patients with Mycobacterium avium complex pulmonary disease, those with a MIC ≤ 0.25 mg/L had a significantly higher likelihood of culture conversion compared to those with MIC > 0.5 mg/L (P = 0.021)[10]. In our study, 2 retrospective patients were treated with CFZ, but the treatment failed. In prospective patients we found that achieving sputum culture conversion was more likely when the MIC was < 0.5 mg/L (P = 0.014). Based on these results, it is recommended to routinely test CFZ MIC before treatment to provide effective therapy.

Some studies have demonstrated the role of LZD in treating Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease. In a recent retrospective study, the linezolid-containing treatment group showed a shorter treatment duration (P < 0.01), a shorter time to sputum culture conversion (P < 0.01), and a higher rate of cavity closure (P < 0.05) [24]. An LZD-containing intensified regimen improved treatment response in Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease, ultimately, an 81.3% sputum culture conversion rate was achieved [25]. In our study, 12 retrospective patients were treated with linezolid, and sputum culture conversion was achieved in 4 of them. In prospective patients, no association was found between the MIC values of LZD and sputum culture conversion, possibly due to the small sample size. A larger sample size is needed to confirm this.

Our study has a few limitations. Firstly, we acknowledge that the relatively small size of our prospective cohort (n = 24) limits the statistical power and generalizability of our findings. While a CFZ MIC < 0.5 mg/L showed a strong association with sputum culture conversion in our study, this observation requires validation in larger, multicenter cohorts to confirm its predictive value and mitigate the risk of chance findings or sampling bias. Secondly, due to the lack of well-recognized tools to distinguish treatment failure from reinfections, we were unable to exclude reinfections during therapy. Thirdly, the retrospective cases do not constitute a well-matched control, as the two groups exhibited substantial differences in treatment protocols, including drug composition and dose standardization.

Conclusion

The treatment regimen for Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease remains uncertain, and PT has emerged as one of the current therapeutic approaches. We conducted a detailed analysis of the role of each medication in the treatment, confirming the effectiveness of our protocol. CFZ MIC < 0.5 mg/L facilitated a higher likelihood of sputum culture conversion. It is recommended to use an initial phase regimen consisting of AZI, CFZ, LZD, AMK and IPM, followed by a continuation phase regimen of AZI, CFZ and LZD, as it ultimately achieved a 70.8% sputum culture conversion.

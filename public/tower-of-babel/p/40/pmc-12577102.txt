Non-traumatic bilateral corneal flap dislocation 20 years after LASIK
Authors: Dakun Ren, Xiangrong Xie, Xingtong Wang, Xuming Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroudThis report describes a case of late non-traumatic dislocation of bilateral corneal flaps, occurring 20 years after LASIK surgery.Case presentationA 52-year-old woman underwent LASIK surgery on both eyes 20 years ago. She visited our clinic with sudden visual loss and denied any history of trauma. Eye examination revealed bilateral corneal flap dislocation. The right flap was hinged temporally and the left flap nasally. The left corneal flap showed tractional deformation, neovascularization, and proliferative membrane formation at the hinge area. Immediate corneal flap replacement surgery was performed on both eyes, and a bandage lens was applied. Six weeks after the surgery, Anterior Segment Optical Coherence Tomography (AS-OCT) showed that the corneal flap was centered, without displacement, and exhibited epithelial ingrowth. The uncorrected distance visual acuity was 20/40 OU.ConclusionThis extraordinary case underscores a remarkable occurrence of bilateral corneal flap displacement, attributed to non-traumatic factors. The potential association between dry eye conditions, not solely as a risk factor but also as a potential direct causative agent for flap dislocation.

Backgroud

Laser in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) has been the most common and popular procedure for correcting refractive errors in the past decades. Despite its widespread success, there have been numerous reports of flap dislocation after LASIK surgery [1–10]. Trauma has been identified as the primary factor leading to late-onset flap dislocation [11–13]. Moreover, most reports describe trauma-induced flap dislocation, typically occurring unilaterally. In this case report, we present a unique case of chronic bilateral flap dislocation 20 years after LASIK surgery, resulting from a non-traumatic factor.

Case presentation

A 52-year-old woman presented with a 5-day history of sudden vision loss, severe pain, tearing, and irritation in both eyes five days ago. She initially sought treatment at a nearby hospital, where she received antibiotic eye ointment, steroid eye drops, and a bandage lens. However, her symptoms did not improve, prompting her to seek further treatment at our hospital. Ophthalmological examination revealed hand motion visual acuity in both eyes, with diffuse staining of the corneal epithelium, corneal flap edema, wrinkling, and downward displacement of both corneal flaps. The superior stromal bed was exposed and covered by intact corneal epithelial cells, and there were also signs of neovascularization and proliferative membrane formation at the superior edge of the corneal flaps (Fig. 1A, B). AS-OCT demonstrated that the flaps were displaced inferiorly and infero-nasally, with the edge of the flap overlapping the corneal stroma (Fig. 1C). The LASIK flap hinge was located temporally in the right eye and nasally in the left eye, as confirmed during surgical repositioning. Despite displacement, each flap remained partially attached at its original hinge location.

Due to severe ocular pain and excessive tearing at the time of presentation, the patient was unable to cooperate with standard dry eye diagnostic tests. However, slit-lamp examination revealed typical features of meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), including thickened lid margins, blocked orifices, visible lipid plugs, and gland displacement in both eyes (Fig. 4). These findings, along with previous outpatient records showing Schirmer I values of 4 mm (OD) and 5 mm (OS), and TBUT of 3 s bilaterally, supported the diagnosis of severe evaporative dry eye.

The patient’s medical history revealed she had undergone myopic LASIK surgery 20 years ago, but her visual acuity did not reach 20/20 in both eyes after the surgery. She had been diagnosed with dry eye disease in recent years and had been using artificial tears to alleviate her symptoms. Furthermore, she had a habit of unconsciously rubbing her eyes when feeling discomfort. Given the failure of conservative treatment and the extensive flap dislocation, surgical intervention was decided. Surgical procedures were performed on both eyes as follows:

Right eye: Carefully scraped the epithelial cells on the edge of the flap and stromal bed using a 5 − 0 blade, followed by balanced salt solution (BSS) irrigation to remove epithelial cell debris. Lifted the corneal flap with a LASIK spatula, irrigated the corneal stromal bed with BSS, and absorbed excess liquid with a sterile surgical sponge. Gently unfolded the corneal flap on the stromal bed and flattened it with a surgical sponge to ensure it covered over 90% of the stromal bed. Finally, a bandage lens was placed on the corneal surface. Left eye: Removed the inferior fibrovascular membrane using a surgical forceps, and then scraped the epithelial cells on the flap and edge of the stromal bed. Lifted the flap, flushed the stromal bed, and replaced the flap. However, the flap was not round, and after repositioning, it could not be centered, showing significant tension. Consequently, a portion of the nasal hinge was cut to increase flap mobility, allowing the flap to be centered and cover most of the stromal bed. Similar to the right eye, a bandage lens was placed for protection.

Post-operation, the patient was given Tobramycin plus dexamethasone eye ointment (S.A. Alcon-Couvereur N.V. Purrs, Belgium) in the conjunctival sac on day 0, along with topical loteprednol etabonate ophthalmic suspension (Bausch & Lomb, Inc, USA), deproteinized calf blood extract eye drops (Qixin drug manufacturer, China), and Levofloxacin Ophthalmic Solution 0.5% (Santa Inc, Osaka, Japan), all administered four times daily.

At the first-day post-operation visit, the patient reported pain relief and significant improvement in vision. Slit-lamp examination showed somewhat edematous and wrinkled corneal flaps, which were well attached to the stromal bed without obvious dislocation. The uncorrected visual acuity was 20/40 in both eyes and could not be corrected. Two weeks post-operation, the bandage lens were removed, and slit-lamp examination showed mild microstriae in the nasal corneal flap of the right eye, with intact epithelial cells covering the corneal flaps in both eyes. AS-OCT indicated that the corneal flaps were tightly closed on the stromal bed without epithelial ingrowth (Fig. 2). Corneal topography (Oculus, Pentacam, USA) showed improved symmetry in the front and back surface of the cornea compared to pre-operation (Fig. 3). However, the visual acuity remained at 20/40 in both eyes and could not be further corrected. The patient followed our suggestion for dry eye treatment and began treating MGD two weeks post-operation.

Discussion and conclusions

Compared to photorefractive keratectomy (PRK) surgery, LASIK surgery has shown lighter postoperative inflammation and greater predictability and stability [14, 15]. Over the past 30 years, LASIK surgery has become the primary method for correcting refractive errors, with over one million people in China undergoing LASIK surgery annually [16]. Despite its widespread use, the mechanism of corneal healing after LASIK surgery remains unclear. Flap adhesion to the stroma is not always firm, resulting in an increasing number of reports of flap detachment even years after the surgery. The reported complications related to flap detachment after LASIK surgery range from approximately 0.012% to 2.5% [17–19]. Most cases of flap detachment occur within the first week after surgery and are usually non-traumatic. Flap displacement can occur due to incomplete corneal wound epithelialization and activities such as rubbing, squeezing, and blinking, particularly in patients with dry eyes, narrow palpebral fissures, large and thin flaps, and advanced age [20]. Another group of flap detachment cases occurs months or years after surgery and is usually acute, traumatic, and unilateral.

Emergency intervention of flap repositioning is crucial for achieving favorable postoperative visual recovery. The pivotal aspects of repair surgery for flap detachment subsequent to LASIK surgery encompass the following key points: 1 Partial or complete removal of the corneal epithelium, particularly in the vicinity of the flap, to forestall epithelial ingrowth. 2 Elevation of the flap and thorough irrigation of the stromal bed to prevent the accumulation of debris, residual corneal epithelium, and other substances at the interface. 3 Precise repositioning of the corneal flap, followed by centralization achieved through the utilization of cotton swabs and forceps. This technique involves gentle pressing and flattening of the flap to minimize the incidence of postoperative striae. 4 Application of a bandage lens to reduce friction between the eyelids and the cornea, thereby promoting effective reepithelialization. In addition, there are some other methods to manage flap displacement [21, 22]. Furthermore, for cases involving severely distorted corneal flaps, some practitioners also opt for sutures or partial hinge incision methods [4, 8, 10]. In this specific instance, due to the pronounced deformation of the left eye corneal flap, the partial hinge incision approach was selected. This was succeeded by compression utilizing cotton swabs and stabilization through the application of a bandage lens, aiming to mitigate striae formation and uphold flap centration. This approach offers the advantage of circumventing astigmatism induced by sutures, along with potential flap tear risks. However, a drawback of this method is the relatively unstable repositioning of the flap. Despite this, we maintain that sutures remain a viable alternative in case of repair failure in this instance or analogous scenarios in the future.

The restoration of postoperative visual acuity hinges on multifarious factors. Timely surgical repositioning often yields favorable visual outcomes, whereas delayed repositioning is frequently contingent on the extent of trauma and the timing of the procedure. Although this case showed notable enhancements in vision compared to the preoperative state, the visual acuity subsequent to repositioning still fell short of that reported in most cases [23]. This disparity can likely be attributed to variables such as the extended interval (5 days) between onset and surgery and the unknown best-corrected visual acuity following LASIK surgery for the patient.

This case differs from the aforementioned scenarios in several aspects: (1) non-traumatic, acute onset in both eyes; (2) chronic course with manifestations of meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) in the eyes, most likely caused by increased friction between the eyelids and corneal flap due to dry eyes, leading to downward displacement of the corneal flap. Traction and tearing at the hinge area above cause the formation of neovascularization and proliferative membrane in the long term; (3) severe deformation of the corneal flap.

This case has provided us with a fresh perspective on flap detachment subsequent to LASIK surgery. Presenting direct evidence of dry eyes contributing to the displacement of the corneal flap. Among the initial group of LASIK patients, predominantly in their 40s and 50s, particularly among women, Asians emerged as a demographic with a heightened susceptibility to dry eyes. In comparison to post-LASIK conditions, whether there is a discernible shift in the position of the corneal flap in these individuals. Additionally, whether there are observable alterations in the contour of the corneal flap. Notably, there remains an enduring risk of persistent displacement in individuals with dry eye conditions post LASIK surgery. To mitigate potential severe repercussions, it is imperative to implement appropriate and proactive interventions targeting dry eyes.

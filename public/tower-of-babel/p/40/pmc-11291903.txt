Multi-epitope peptide vaccines targeting dengue virus serotype 2 created via immunoinformatic analysis
Authors: Radwa N Morgan, Nasser S M Ismail, Mohammad Y Alshahrani, Khaled M Aboshanab
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Middle East has witnessed a greater spread of infectious Dengue viruses, with serotype 2 (DENV-2) being the most prevalent form. Through this work, multi-epitope peptide vaccines against DENV-2 that target E and nonstructural (NS1) proteins were generated through an immunoinformatic approach. MHC class I and II and LBL epitopes among NS1 and envelope E proteins sequences were predicted and their antigenicity, toxicity, and allergenicity were investigated. Studies of the population coverage denoted the high prevalence of NS1 and envelope-E epitopes among different countries where DENV-2 endemic. Further, both the CTL and HTL epitopes retrieved from NS1 epitopes exhibited high conservancies’ percentages with other DENV serotypes (1, 3, and 4). Three vaccine constructs were created and the expected immune responses for the constructs were estimated using C-IMMSIM and HADDOCK (against TLR 2,3,4,5, and 7). Molecular dynamics simulation for vaccine construct 2 with TLR4 denoted high binding affinity and stability of the construct with the receptor which might foretell favorable in vivo interaction and immune responses.Keywords: Dengue viruses, DENV-2, Nonstructural protein 1 (NS1), Envelope E, Peptide vaccine, Epitopes, Antigenicity, Allergenicity, ToxicitySubject terms: Pathogens, Vaccines, Virology

Introduction

Dengue virus (DENV), belonging to Flaviviridae viral family, is primarily transmitted by Aedes mosquito (e.g. Aedes aegypti)1,2. It’s considered the most significant Flavivirus that exist in four different serotypes (DENV 1–4)2. The 11 Kb genome of the single positive-stranded RNA dengue virus encodes 7 non-structural (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, and NS5) and 3 structural proteins (Envelope, core, and membrane)3,4. With an inter-serotype nucleotide variability of roughly 30%, the four serotypes of DENV are closely related3–5. Infections with DENV serotypes vary in severity with a wide range of clinical symptoms starting from mild flu-like symptoms (dengue fever (DF)) to the most severe forms of the disease (dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF)/ dengue shock syndrome (DSS)), which are characterized by coagulopathy, vascular fragility and permeability contributing to hypovolemic shock and death6. The DHF is frequently associated with DENV serotype 2 and DENV serotype 3 infections7. The Dengue fever is endemic across several tropical and subtropical regions of the world, and it has recently spread to the continental United States, where it causes around 2.35 million cases annually8–10. Estimates from the World Health Organization (WHO) denotes that between 50 and 100 million new cases of dengue fever are reported annually in more than 100 countries, putting over 40% of the world's population at risk of infection11. DENV-2 is the most common and genetically diverse cosmopolitan genotype that had contributed in various outbreaks across the African, Asian, and Latin American continents12. Saudi Arabia along with the nations bordering the Red Sea (Djibouti, Egypt, Sudan, Yemen, and Somalia) have the highest seroprevalence of DF within the middle eastern region13,14. The pilgrim of millions of Muslims to Makkah and Al-Madinah throughout the year and for the Hajj season contributes to the spread of DENV across the western half of Saudi Arabia (Jeddah, Makkah, Al-Madina)15,16.

The development of a dependable vaccine against DENV has been impeded by the presence of the four distinct serotypes, each of which might elicit an antibody response that amplifies the disease against the other serotypes17. Several DENV vaccine candidates are undergoing various stages of clinical trials. Regretfully, none of these vaccinations can address every issue about safety and effectiveness7. The Sanofi Pasteur recombinant, live-attenuated, tetravalent dengue vaccine, CYD-TDV, also referred to as Dengvaxia®, utilizes chimeric structural protein (prM/E) from four DENV serotypes bound to the yellow fever 17D vaccine18,19. It was licensed for administration in endemic countries following a 3 phased clinical trial. The effectiveness of dengvaxia vaccinations varies with age, disease severity, serotypes, and serostatus20. It was noted that children < 9 years old that received 3 doses schedule (0,6, 12 months) of dengvaxia during phase-III trial developed symptomatic dengue illness 25 months after last dosage which served as a gauge for the vaccination’s effectiveness21,22. Dengvaxia efficacy varied against symptomatic dengue infections where 50.3% of patients responded to dengvaxia when treating DENV1, 42.3% when treating DENV2, 74.0% when treating DENV3, and 77.4% when treating DENV423. Consequently, Dengvaxia functions as a monovalent vaccine that solely protects against DENV4. A higher risk of hospitalization was also observed in the third year of follow-up in vaccine recipients who were initially thought to be seronegative20. Qdenga, on the other hand, is a live attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine built on DENV-2 backbone and recombinant strains that express DENV1, DENV3, and DENV4 surface proteins. Replacement of the yellow fever virus with DENV-2 offers a wider range of humoral and cell-mediated immune response24. It worked very well against DENV-2 (98%) and DENV-3 (63%). Owing to the limited quantity of cases, Qdenga was not able to show any effectiveness against DENV4. Qdenga’s cumulative efficacy is also strongly influenced by the receivers' serostatus; it reports a 50% efficacy in dengue-naïve people and a 63% efficacy in those who have already contracted the virus24. Compared to traditional vaccines, epitope-based vaccines have various advantages, including better specificity and safety while generating humoral and cellular immune responses25. Furthermore, the application of rational design in the creation of epitope-based vaccinations has been made possible by advancements in immunology, peptide prediction, dynamics simulation, and molecular docking26. Therefore, with the proper choice of epitopes (high antigenicity, low toxicity, low allergenicity), epitope-based peptide vaccine might offer an alternative to commercially available dengue virus vaccines.

The DENV NS1 protein is an interesting vaccine target for the development of epitope vaccines for several reasons. It is the only DENV nonstructural protein that’s able to exist in multiple forms; it can be discharged from infected cells into the bloodstream as a soluble lipid associated hexamer and anchored to the infected cell surface as a membrane-associated homodimer, where both forms can activate humoral and cellular immunities27. It’s a non-structural protein that’s synthesized inside the infected cells, lacks a known catalytic activity but plays a crucial role in DENV replication28. Being a non-structural protein that isn’t part of dengue virion suggest that antibodies generated against NS1 will not be able to produce an antibody dependent disease enhancement response (ADE), a common adverse effect associated with live attenuated vaccines that contribute to DHF in secondary infections with different serotypes28,29. The ADE occurs when virus/antibody complexes bind to Fc gamma receptor IIa (Fc·RIIa) inducing abnormal macrophages or phagocytic cells which in turn increase viral infection and replication inside the host. The ADE is thought to be an additional way for a virus to infect cells resulting in reduction of the vaccine efficacy increasing the danger signals of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines30. It was also noted that dengue vaccine candidates containing epitopes retrieved from structural proteins only (E, and M proteins) were associated with non-specific immunoglobulins generation that caused antibody dependent enhancement effect (ADE), which contribute to the failure of vaccine candidate31,32. The NS1 protein is also a highly immunogenic and evades the host immune system through a variety of mechanisms, such as complement pathway activation or inhibition and the elicitation of autoantibodies that cross-react with platelets and endothelial cells, resulting in endothelial dysfunction—one of the unique hallmarks of the pathogenesis of Flavivirus diseases33. It also integrates with TLR4, which initiates cytokine release and directly contributes to vascular leak34. Research has demonstrated that immunizing mice against DENV infection using DENV NS1 produce strong protective immune responses against DENV infection28. Further, the NS and E proteins are rich with target epitopes for CD4 + and CD8 + T lymphocytes which are crucial during the development of epitope-based peptide vaccines. According to Tian et al. (2019), human CD4 + T cells primarily react to the capsid, then to the E, NS1, NS3, NS2A/B, and NS5 proteins35. Finally, the NS1 protein has remained substantially conserved throughout the evolutionary history of the cosmopolitan DENV-2 genotype, in contrast to other structural (PreM/M, envelope E) and non-structural (NS3, NS4B, and NS5) where hallmark mutations occur4,36.

With the use of an immunoinformatic approach, we created a multi-epitope peptide vaccine against the NS1 and Envelope E anchor retrieved from DENV-2 genome sequence, which was extracted from the DENV-2 genome sequence, the cosmopolitan serotype and primary cause of dengue endemics in the Asia and Middle East region, for potential use in in vitro and in vivo experiments. Starting with the amino acid sequences of both proteins, we will determine the epitopes of cytotoxic T cells, helper T lymphocytes, and B lymphocytes, evaluate the population conservancy coverage for these subsets, and finally develop the vaccine. The physicochemical characteristics of the vaccine construct, the anticipated immunological response, its capacity to be produced in laboratory expression hosts, and its interaction with TLR3 and TLR4 receptors will all be evaluated after it has been created.

Methods

Retrieval of nonstructural protein 1 (NS1) and anchor domain of envelope E protein sequences from the NCBI database

DENV-2 E and NS1 protein sequences were obtained from the complete genome sequencing of a strain that was recovered from Saudi Arabia and given the GenBank accession number (OR389324.1 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/OR389324.1) (accessed on 15 April 2024). The dengue virus isolate was recovered in 1994, and the sequence was submitted to the NCBI database in 2023. The translated protein ID was (WLD15671.1 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/WLD15671.1) (accessed on 15 April 2024), with the E protein ranging from 679 to 751, and NS1 from 776 to 1127. The genome sequencing was part of the Yale School of Public Health bio-project (PRJNA1001374) titled “Enhancing dengue virus genomic surveillance to uncover circulating genetic diversity.”

Detecting and evaluating the antigenicity, immunogenicity, toxicity, and allergy of major histocompatibility complex I (MHC class I) i.e. cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes

To predict the CTL epitopes, amino acid sequences (FASTA files) were uploaded to the NetCTL—1.2 web tool (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/NetCTL-1.2/) (accessed on 15 April 2024). NeTCTL 1.2 is an upgraded version of NetCTL 1.0 where the MHC class I binding prediction is extended to 12 MHC super-types, which includes super-types A26 and B3937. The mer peptides were then submitted into the IEDB database to evaluate their immunogenicity and capacity to trigger an immune response (http://tools.iedb.org/immunogenicity) (accessed on 15 April 2024)38. Using Toxinpred (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) and AllerTOP (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/AllerTOP/), (accessed on 15 April 2024) the toxicity and potential allergenicity for the immunogenic CTL peptide was also assesse39,40. The vaxijen server (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was then used to evaluate the antigenicity for non-allergen and non-toxic peptides41.

Detecting and evaluating the antigenicity, toxicity, and allergy of major histocompatibility complex II (MHC class II) i.e. helper T lymphocytes (HTL) epitopes

To predict the MHC class II and HTL epitopes, the IEDB prediction of MHC class II epitopes web tool has been used (http://tools.iedb.org/mhcii/) (accessed on 15 April 2024)42. Afterward, the antigenicity, toxicity, and allergenicity were assessed by VaxiJen, Toxinpred, and AllerTop, as previously discussed39–41. The probability of peptide epitope to elicit interferon-γ (IFN-γ), interleukin-4 (IL-4), and interleukin-10 (IL-10) were assessed using IFNepitope (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/ifnepitope/scan.php), IL4pred (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/il4pred/design.php), and IL10pred (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/il10pred/scan.php)43–45. The epitopes that were capable of eliciting immune responses (antigenic, non-allergen, non-toxic, IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-10 inducers) were selected for multi-epitope vaccine construction.

Detecting and evaluating the antigenicity, toxicity, and allergenicity of Linear B lymphocytes (LBL) epitopes in NS1 and envelope E protein

To depict the LBL epitopes, the ABCpred server was used (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/abcpred/ABC_submission.html) (accessed on 15 April 2024)46,47. Similarly, the epitope's antigenicity, toxicity, and allergenicity were assessed by Vaxijen, ToxinPred, and AllerTop as previously described39–41.

NS1 and envelope E CTL, HTL, and LBL epitope conservancy and population coverage analysis

The IEDB conservancy analysis tool (http://tools.iedb.org/conservancy/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to assess the degree of conservancy within an epitope stretch when compared to identical proteins of different DENV serotypes from different country origins48. Both HTL and CTL epitopes were then blasted against DENV serotypes (1–4) polyprotein sequences retrieved from NCBI database and phylogenetic trees were constructed against multiple amino acid sequences retrieved from the NCBI database using MegaX software49. This was performed to determine the maximum likelihood between the CTL and HTL epitopes with previously published NS1 and envelope E sequences49. The sequences for a cosmopolitan DENV serotype 2 poly-proteins originally retrieved from Singapore were (QQW40809.1, QQW40806.1, QQW40803.1, QQW40759.1, QQW40799.1, and QQW40791.1), sequences for NS1 protein only were also included (AAA42943.1, AAA42946.1, AAA42949.1, AAB70476.1, AAD11533.1, AAK67712.1, ABF83697.1, ABF83698.1, WLD15671.1, and AGX15373.1). The sequences for DENV serotype 1 poly-proteins were QBA57538.1 (India), and QIB99388.1 (Kenya). The sequences for DENV serotype 3 poly-proteins were UNZ79694.1, and UNZ79683.1 (India). Finally, the sequence for DENV serotype 4 was QBB90024.1 poly-protein (Indonesia). Using the IEDB population coverage website (http://tools.iedb.org/population/) (accessed on 15 April 2024), the fraction of people living across different areas worldwide including regions where DENG virus is endemic and anticipated to respond to both the CTL and HTL epitopes of NS1 and envelope E protein sequences was predicted according to information on MHC binding, T cell restriction, and/or HLA genotypic frequencies50.

Docking of NS1 and envelope E CTL and HTL epitopes with the HLA proteins

The CTL and HTL peptide epitopes were folded to their 3D structure using PEP-fold 4 (http://bioserv.rpbs.univ-paris-diderot.fr/services/PEP-FOLD4) (accessed on 15 April 2024) and the best PDB model was fixed and energy minimized using SWISS-PDB viewer51,52. Afterward, the 3D protein structures for HLA proteins were downloaded from the RCSB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) as listed in Table S1 in supplementary file53–60. The ligands were deleted using UCSF ChimeraX software and the structures were energy minimized using SWISS-PDB viewer. The 3D folded CTL and HTL peptide epitopes were docked against HLA proteins using HDOCK (http://hdock.phys.hust.edu.cn/) (accessed on 15 April 2024)61–63. The PDBsum generator (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thornton-srv/databases/pdbsum/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to analyze the CTL and HTL docked structures to elucidate interacting residues, hydrogen bonds formed, and Ramachandran favored regions64.

Design of multi-epitope peptide vaccines for DENV2

A total of 3 multi-epitope peptide vaccines were constructed with PADRE peptide sequence (AKFVAAWTLKAAA) in their C-terminal and the βeta-defensin 103 peptide sequence (UniProt database ID P81534) in their N-terminal. Using a GPGPG linker, the HTL epitope of the NS1 protein sequence was directly connected to βeta-defensin 103 peptide sequences in all 3 vaccine constructs. The LBL epitopes with the greatest antigenicity scores were inserted after the HTL epitope and connected by the EAAKEAAK linker65–67. Finally, the AAY linker was used to join the CTL form NS1 and envelope E, which were added before the PADRE, sequence68,69. VaxiJen, AllerTop, and ToxinPred were used to evaluate the multi-epitope peptide vaccine's antigenicity, allergenicity, and toxicity39–41.

Assessing the physicochemical, secondary, and tertiary structure properties for the multi-epitope peptide vaccine constructs

The different physicochemical parameters for the vaccine construct were calculated using the Expasy Protparam web tool (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) (accessed on 15 April 2024). Molecular weight, theoretical isoelectric point (pI), atomic and amino acid composition, extinction coefficient, predicted half-life, instability index, aliphatic index, and grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) are among the calculated parameters70. The SOPMA secondary structure prediction method (https://npsa-prabi.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to predict the secondary peptide structure for the vaccine construct71,72. The vaccine was then folded into its 3D structure via the trRosetta algorithm (https://yanglab.qd.sdu.edu.cn/trRosetta/) (accessed on 15 April 2024)and the protein model was refined using GalaxyRefine webserver (https://galaxy.seoklab.org/cgi-bin/submit.cgi?type=REFINE) (accessed on 15 April 2024)73,74. The 3D structure qualities of the 3 vaccine constructs were verified by ProSA (https://prosa.services.came.sbg.ac.at/prosa.php) (accessed on 15 April 2024)75 and ERRAT server (https://www.doe-mbi.ucla.edu/verify3d/) (accessed on 15 April 2024)76. Following the verification of the 3D models, the iMod server (https://imods.iqf.csic.es/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to assess the collective motions and dynamics of these constructs using normal mode analysis (NMA)77.

Back translation of multi-epitope vaccine constructs and assessing the probability of their expression within an expression vector

The amino acid sequence of the multi-epitope vaccine construct was back-translated to equivalent DNA sequence using EMBOSS Backtranseq (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/st/emboss_backtranseq/) (accessed on 15 April 2024) with the codon usage table adjusted to E. coli78. Afterward, the back-translated sequences were submitted into Genescript rare codon analysis (https://www.genscript.com/tools/rare-codon-analysis) (accessed on 15 April 2024) to assess the CAI and CG% content while setting the expressing host vector to E. coli and references sources to codon usage database (Kazusa)79.

Assessing the adaptive and humoral immune responses for multi-epitope vaccine constructs

The C-IMMSIM simulation (https://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/C-IMMSIM/index.php?page=1) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to assess the ability of the multi-epitope vaccines to elicit adaptive and humoral immune responses post-vaccine injection with no LPS, adjuvant set to 100, following a single time injection80. The C-IMMSIM predicts the total antibody titer (IgM, and IgG), B cell, T helper, cytotoxic T, natural killer, and dendritic cell populations. It can also predict the level of interferon-gamma and interleukins generated post-vaccine injection.

Docking of multi-epitope vaccine constructs with toll like receptor 3 (TLR3) and 4 (TLR4)

The refined 3D models for the 3 multi-epitope vaccine constructs were docked against TLR3 (PDB ID: 7C77) and TLR4 (PDB ID: 4G8A) as sensor patterns for immune responses using HADDOCK server 2.4 (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/haddock2.4/) (accessed on 15 April 2024)81–84. Following the docking, the complexes were assessed by PDBsum generate (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thornton-srv/databases/pdbsum/Generate.html) (accessed on 15 April 2024) to visualize the interacting residues, compute the no. of salt bridge and hydrogen bonds formed and assess the overall quality of the docked model using Procheck Ramachandran plots and overall G-factor average85. Using the Hawkdock server (http://cadd.zju.edu.cn/hawkdock/) (accessed on 15 April 2024), the docked protein–protein complexes' molecular mechanics with generalized born and surface area solvation (mmGBSA measured in Kcal/mol) was evaluated after HaDDock86–88.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Molecular Dynamics Simulation (MD simulation) was considered to validate the stability of the docked ligands89. Discovery Studio 4.0 Software (Waltham, MA, 135USA); https://discover.3ds.com/discovery-studio-visualizer-download) (accessed on 15 April 2024) was used to provide information about the dynamic behavior for the vaccine construct and target89. First, the best-docked pose with the best binding energy score was obtained using ZDOCK90. Within a 100 ns all-atom MD simulation, the protein–protein complexes exhibiting the most promising molecular docking data were analyzed. CHARMm force field parameters for the investigated ligands were generated automatically. Every ligand–protein complex was solvated, permitting a marginal distance of roughly 10 Å. Before neutralizing the chosen complexes, protein residues were first brought to standard ionization states at physiologically relevant pH 7.0. Ultimately, the MD simulations were performed for 100 ns at constant pressure (NPTensemble). Data calculations including the binding free energy, RMSD, and RMSF were done to look into potential ligand interactions with significant protein residues and forecast the docked complexes' stability at the binding site89.

Docking of multi-epitope vaccine constructs with toll like receptors 2 (TLR2), 5 (TLR5) and 7 (TLR7)

All three vaccine designs’ refined 3D models were additionally docked against toll-like receptors 2, 5, and 7. The three receptors were given the codes 6LW1 (TLR7)90,91, 6NIG (TLR2)92, and 3J0A (TLR5)93 after their PDB structures were obtained from the RSCB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/). As previously indicated, the HADDOCK 2.4 server (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/haddock2.4/) was used to execute the protein–protein docking, and PDBsum generate was used to analyze the interactions between the protein–protein complexes81–85. This was done to investigate how the three vaccine designs might interact with other toll-like receptors that are important in the immune system's defense against viral infections.

Data records

MHC class I and CTL epitopes in NS1 and envelope E anchor domain proteins

The NS1 amino acid sequence contained 344 peptides, of which 88 were identified as MHC class I epitopes using the NeTCTL 1.2 web tool. The envelope E protein’s anchor domain amino acid sequence contained 26 MHC class I epitopes out of 65 peptides. Tables S2, S3 in supplementary file summarized the immunogenicity scores based on the IEDB database, as well as the allergenicity and toxicity for the predicted epitopes. Peptide epitopes with negative IEDB immunogenicity scores were excluded, they weren’t immunogenic, and thus neither assessed by Toxinpred or AllerTop.

In the case of NS1, only 15 out of the 44 MHC class I peptide epitopes with positive immunogenicity scores were non-allergic and non-toxic. From the 16 MHC class I peptides that received positive immunogenicity scores for the envelope E protein’s anchor domain, only 8 epitopes were found to be non-toxic and non-allergenic. The vaxijen server evaluated the antigenicity of the non-toxic and non-allergenic epitopes, as indicated in Table S4 (supplementary file). The MHC class I epitopes of NS1 that had the highest antigenic scores while being non-allergen and non-toxic were TAGPWHLGK, WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, RPGYHTQTA, YRGEDGCWY, TRLENLMWK, LRTTTASGK, and, RSVTRLENL. The MHC class I epitopes of envelope E anchor domain that had the highest antigenic scores while being non-allergen and non-toxic were DTAWDFGSL and ILIGAVITW.

MHC class II and HTL epitopes in NS1 and Envelope E anchor domain proteins

Only 9 out of 12 predicted HTL peptide epitopes in the NS1 amino acid sequence were antigenic, non-toxic, and non-allergenic. A total of 3 peptide epitopes were non-toxic, and non-allergenic within the envelope E anchor domain protein sequence, however, their antigenicity scores were < 0.5. The HTL epitopes predicted from the NS1 and envelope E anchor domain amino acid sequences are summed up in Table S5 (Supplementary file). Upon predicting the ability of HTL epitope to induce IFN-γ, IL-10 and IL-4 production, it was noted that only a single epitope for NS1 protein (YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL) was a positive inducer of IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-10. Similarly, for the envelope E protein's anchor domain, only the HTL epitope (SLGGVFTSIGKALHQ) was a positive inducer of IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-10. Table S6 (Supplementary file) lists the HTL epitope's capabilities to induce IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-10.

LBL epitopes in NS1 and envelope E anchor domain proteins

A total of 37 LBL peptide epitopes were detected in NS1 amino acid sequence. Only 12 out of 37 LBL epitope were found to be antigenic, non-allergen, and non-toxic. The LBL epitopes within the NS1 amino acid sequences that were antigenic, non-toxic, and non-allergen were YGMEIRPLKEKEENLV, YHTQTAGPWHLGKLEM, IKGIMQAGKRSLRPQP, CRSCTLPPLRYRGEDG, LGKLEMDFDFCEGTTV, TELKYSWKTWGKAKML, ASAIQKAHEEGICGIR, RSVTRLENLMWKQITP, KLMSAAIKDNRAVHAD, SGCVVSWKNKELKCGS, IWLKLKERQDVFCDSK, and, PLKEKEENLVNSLVTA. On the other hand, 7 LBL epitopes were detected among the anchor domain of envelope E protein were detected, 3 of which were antigenic, non-allergen, and non-toxic. The LBL epitopes within the envelope E anchor domain amino acid sequences that were antigenic, non-toxic, and non-allergen were GVSWTMKILIGAVITW, AILGDTAWDFGSLGGV, and KILIGAVITWIGMNSR. Table S7 (supplementary file) elucidated the LBL peptide epitopes in both NS1 and envelope E amino acid sequences.

NS1 and envelope E anchor domain conservancy and population coverage analysis

For NS1, redundant peptide sequences among the LBL and CTL epitopes were excluded prior to the conduction of conversancy and population coverage analysis. The conservancy analysis for epitopes retrieved from NS1 amino acid sequence was performed using IEDB database against poly protein sequences for DENV1, DENV2, DENV3, and DENV4. Table 1 summarizes the conservancies’ percentages for the selected NS1 and envelope E peptide epitopes. It was noted that most of NS1 epitopes had high conservancy scores (> 50%). Interestingly, the CTL epitopes, WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, and LRTTTASGK elucidated conservancy scores of 88.89%, 88.89%, and 77.78% with DENV serotype 1, respectively. The CTL epitopes, WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, and LRTTTASGK also attained conservancy scores of 88.89%, 100%, and 88.89% with DENV serotype 3 and 88.89%, 100%, and 100% with DENV serotype 4, respectively. The NS1 HTL epitope YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL also exhibited high conservancy scores of 93%, 100%, and 86.67% with DENV serotypes 1, 3, and 4, respectively. This confirmed the high NS1 sequences conservancies among the DENV serotypes (Table S8-S11 in supplementary file), confirming their eligibility for being vaccine candidates. In regards to epitopes retrieved from Envelope E proteins, the HTL and CTL epitope, DTAWDFGSL and SLGGVFTSIGKALHQ acquired high level of conservancies among the cosmopolitan DENV serotype 2 poly-protein sequences. Further, mega X software constructed phylogenetic trees for both NS1 and envelope E proteins (Figures S1-S4). The trees denoted the relationship between the epitope sequences and previously published amino acid sequences for both NS1 and envelope E proteins. According to Figure S4, the Ctl epitopes 1, 2, and 4 (WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, and LRTTTASGK, respectively) and Htl 2 (YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL) shared the same tree nodes with poly-protein sequences of DENV serotype 1, 3 and 4 which confirms the conservancies of these peptide sequences among other serotypes. Further, Figures S1-S3 confirmed the conservancies of the remaining CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes among DENV serotype 2 protein sequences as they shared the same intersecting branches and tree nodes.

The LBL epitopes included within the three constructs amino acid sequences acquired the highest antigenicity scores to ensure high antigenicity and activity of the vaccine. Following the assembly of the vaccine constructs, the antigenicity, allergenicity and toxicity were assessed and it was noted that the 3 constructs acquired comparable antigenic scores, with construct 3 acquiring the highest antigenicity score (0.7038). The 3 vaccine construct were non-allergen and non-toxic according to Toxinpred and AllerTop. In regards to the physicochemical properties, it was noted that constructs 1 and 2 shared the same isoelectric point, molecular weight, number of positively and negatively charged amino acids, yet they had different instability and aliphatic indices and GRAVY scores. All 3 vaccine constructs were hydrophilic and stable. The highest thermo-stability was attained by vaccine construct 1 (highest aliphatic index score). The average molecular weight for all 3 constructs was ~ 30 KDa. It was also noted back translation of the amino acid sequences was associated with CAI value of 1 when using E. coli as expression vector and CG% of 59% (Table S13).

The secondary SOPMA structure analysis denoted that all 3 vaccine constructs (construct 1, 2 and 3) were mainly composed of α helix (> 40%) as shown in Figure S22 (construct 2), Figure S23 (construct 1), and Figure S24 (construct 3) in supplementary file and Table 5. Afterward, the 3D structures for the vaccine constructs were generated by trrosetta server and refined by GalaxyRefine web tool to enhance their structural properties (Fig. 2 and Table 5). The overall quality of the 3D model was checked by ERAAT server and the highest score was attained by construct no. 3 recording 97.112% (Figure S25 (construct 1), Figure S26 (construct 2), and Figure S27 (construct 3) in supplementary file). The ProSA webserver denoted the overall acceptable quality of the 3D models as noted by the Z scores and local model quality plots (Fig. 2, and Figure S28 (construct 1), Figure S29 (construct 2), and Figure S30 (construct 3) in supplementary file). The more negative Z scores the more acceptable the quality of the 3D model. The majority of the local model quality plots in Figures S28-S30, were situated in the negative region, where positive numbers typically indicate troublesome or incorrect portions of the input structure. This further confirms the overall good quality of the 3D modeled vaccine constructs. The iMod server highlighted the flexibility of the vaccine constructs as noted by the B factor, deformability, eigenvalue scores and residue index plot (Figure S31 (construct 1), Figure S32 (construct 2), Figure S33 (construct 3)). The flexibility of the protein can foretell favorable binding affinities with HLA binding alleles and confirms there in vitro and in vivo activity.

Adaptive and humoral immunity induced by multi-epitope vaccine constructs

According to C-IMMSIM analysis, the vaccine construct 2 was capable of producing the highest IgM, IgG, IgG1 and IgG2 antibodies titers (peaked at day 15 to > 12,000, and > 4000, respectively). The highest PLB cell population was also observed with vaccine construct 2 followed by vaccine construct 3 as shown in Fig. 3. In similar manners, the T helper cell population (Cells/mm3) was higher for vaccine construct no. 3 followed by vaccine construct no. 2 (Figure S34 in supplementary file). In regards to the B cells, macrophages, and dendritic cell populations, the 3 vaccine constructs exhibited comparable effects (Figure S34). In regards to the induction of interferon-gamma (IFN-g) and interleukin-2 (IL-2), the highest titer achieved was 30,000 ng/mL and it was attained by vaccine construct 2 and 3 (Figures S35-S37).Vaccine construct 1 attained IL-2 level to 25000 ng/mL (Figure S34). Lastly, the effect of the 3 vaccine constructs on the T helper cell population, natural killer cells and T cytotoxic cells was almost comparable.

Molecular dynamics simulation studies

Molecular dynamic simulations were carried out to examine the protein–protein interactions in motion that contribute to their stable bound conformation and to see the impact of ligand binding on changes in protein conformation since molecular docking only provides a single snapshot of protein–ligand interactions. For MD simulation, the vaccine construct 2/TLR4 was studied by first doing MD simulations on the protein TLR4 protein (without any ligand bound), followed by MD simulations on TLR4 in conjugation with vaccine construct 2. The RMSD (Fig. 5(c)), root mean square fluctuations (RMSF) (Fig. 5(e)), and energy (Fig. 5(a)) were studied and contributions were plotted as the time-dependent function of MD simulations. It was noted that the RMSD of the protein backbone was found to be fluctuating around 4Å (Fig. 5(c)). The TLR4 RMSF graph (Fig. 5(e)) shows that the first 25 residues with over 9 Å, residue number 125 up to 6.0 Å, residue 240 with 3.5 Å, and residues between 340 and 360 with up to 2.5 Å and residues between 540 and 550 with up to 3.0 Å are the main fluctuation peaks. After doing an analysis, we were able to determine the average energy required for the stabilized shape of TLR4, which came out to be between − 4650 and − 4925 kcal/mol (Fig. 5(a)).

MD simulations of TLR4 in complex with construct 2 have been performed, using the ZDOCK cluster 6 pose 45 for TLR4/Construct 2 complex with the binding energy of − 11.975 kcal/mol (Figure S46). As elucidated in Fig. 5(d), the RMSD of the trajectories for TLR4/construct 2 complex were well around 3.0 Å. The RMSF (Fig. 5(f)) of the TLR4/Construct 2 complex denoted a considerable reduction in the fluctuation when compared to the free unbound protein. Upon completion of MD analysis, it was noted that the total energy required for the stabilized conformation of TLR4 protein in complex with construct 2 was − 17,350 and − 17,650 kcal/mol (Fig. 5(b)), which is significantly less than TLR4's averaged energy of − 4650 and − 4925 kcal/mol when no ligand is present.

Interaction of vaccine constructs with TLR2, TLR5 and TLR7

It was observed that the vaccine constructs 1 and 2 had the strongest binding affinities to TLR2 chain A, while construct 3 had the lowest binding affinities based on docking scores recorded by HADDOCK (Table S20 in Supplementary file) and it was observed that DENV vaccine construct 3 exhibited the strongest affinity for binding with TLR5’s chain A (Tables S21 and S22 in Supplementary file). With the exception of Van der Wal interactions, the energy scores and numbers of hydrogen bonds and salt bridges for constructs 1 and 2 were nearly equal (Table S23 (construct 1/TLR2), Table S24 (construct2/TLR2) and Table S25 (construct 3/TLR2) in supplementary file). The three vaccine constructs were docked with toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5). The highest number of hydrogen bonds and salt bridges between construct 3 and chain A of the TLR-5 interacting residues provided evidence for this. The Van der Waals energy score also revealed that the vaccine construct 3’s non-bonded contact (non-covalent interactions) with chain A of TLR5 interacting residues was the strongest (Table S26 (construct 1/TLR5), Table S27 (Construct 2/TLR5), Table S28 (construct 3/TLR5) in supplementary file). The docking scores of the three vaccine designs with TLR7's chain A show that they all developed almost similar affinities to toll-like receptor 7. The similar number of hydrogen and salt bridges between the interaction residues of construct and chain A of TLR7, particularly for vaccination constructs 1 and 2, further demonstrated this. For all three vaccine designs, the Van Der Waal energy values were comparable (Tables S29 (construct 1/TLR7), Tables S30 (construct 2/TLR7), and Table S31 (construct 3/TLR7) in supplementary file). The PDBsum visualizations of the vaccine constructs with the TLR receptors are elucidated in Figures S47-S49 (construct 1 with TLR2, TLR5, and TLR7, respectively), Figures S50-S52 (construct 2 with TLR2, TLR5, and TLR7, respectively), and Figures S53-S55 (construct 3 with TLR2, TLR5, and TLR7, respectively) in the supplementary file. Construct 2 had slightly higher number of non-binding contacts (non-covalent interactions) and superior Van der Wall energy scores (Figure S51 in supplementary file). The docked complexes’ Ramachandran plots (Figure S56 (construct 1), S57 (construct 2), and S58 (construct 3) in the supplementary file) showed that the overall sterochemical stability of the complexes was acceptable since most of the amino acids were found in the most favourable region (> 75%), additional and allowed regions, and only few residues were located in the disallowed regions, which ranged from 0.5–1%. The total G factor values, which were more than −0.5 and indicated the general approval of HADDock docked complexes.

Discussion

The creation of multi-epitope based peptide vaccine using NS1 protein and E protein domain III of DENV-2 was the main goal of this in silico study. The cosmopolitan DENV-2 strain has dominated dengue infections endemics over multiple continents and NS1 protein was highly conserved across its evolutionary history4. The DENV-2 was the main serotype linked to outbreaks in Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Pakistan, Singapore, Malaysia, and China4. From 2018 to 2022, it was also the main cause of DHF in children from north India94. It has become the predominant serotype in various outbreaks within the Middle East, particularly in Egypt (2017 outbreak) and southwestern Saudi Arabia (2015 outbreak)5,95. Only antigenic non-toxic non-allergen CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes from the NS1 and domain III of envelope E amino acid sequences were recovered from DENV-2 whole genome/poly-protein sequence to generate epitope based vaccine constructs96.

The web tools, NeTCTLpan, IEDB database, and ABCpred, were used to predict the CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes across the NS1 and envelope E (anchor domain III) amino acid sequences. NeTCTLpan employs three factors—protease cleavage, TAP transport efficiency, and MHC class I affinity—to determine the CTL epitope among amino acid FASTA sequences37. Using ARB, SMM align, and Sturniolo’s techniques, the IEDB database predicts the HTL peptides that can bind to 158 MHC class II alleles and compute the percentile rank42. The HTL epitopes are essential for the construction of epitope based vaccines since they bind to HLA alleles class II necessary for the activation of CD4 + T cells and initiation of innate immunity97. The HTL epitopes were then evaluated for their capacity to trigger IL-10, IFN, and IL-4 production. It was noted that only one HTL in NS1 was capable of inducing IL-4, IFN, and IL-10 production which was (YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL), and for the anchor domain of envelope E protein, only HTL epitope (SLGGVFTSIGKALHQ) was positive inducer. IFN-γ aids in the differentiation of TH1 cells, but IL-4 is necessary for the differentiation of TH2 cells, which activates the macrophage98,99. The HTL epitope of NS1 was chosen over HTL of envelope E protein during the construction of vaccines since it has higher antigenic score. Finally, ABCpred was used to extract the LBL epitopes. The LBL epitopes’ antigenic regions integrate with antibodies initiating the humoral immune response100–102.

Effective immunization against DENV serotypes can be achieved by epitopes exhibiting conservation. Thus, the predicted epitopes that exhibited good acceptable conservancies’ percentages by IEDB conservancy analysis web tool with poly-protein amino acid sequences of DENV serotypes 1–4 were selected during the construction of vaccines103. The epitopes (WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, LRTTTASGK, and YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL) that exhibited higher conservation among DENV serotypes (1–3) were included in all 3 constructs to guarantee better reactivity across different serotypes and vaccine design. Population coverage analysis elucidated that the predicted CTL and HTL epitopes were common in DENV endemic countries, reaching almost 90% in African nations like Kenya and Zimbabwe and percentages > 75% in countries like Mali, Senegal, Uganda, Zambia, and Central African Republic. Additionally, it was noticed that the population coverage in a number of Asian nations varied from 70 to 80%, with endemic regions like Taiwan (84.02%), India (82.8%), and Iran (84%), showing higher percentage scores. In a similar vein, epitope population coverage percentages in Chile, Ecuador, Brazil, and other Latin American nations where DENV spread between 2001 and 2009 ranged from 60 to 86%104. Remarkably, 90% of the population in the USA had the chosen epitopes covered. Since 2009, Miami, Florida, the US Virgin Islands, and Puerto Rico have contracted DENV serotypes from travelers coming from Cuba and Latin America105. In the Middle East, Saudi Arabia and Sudan, two nations where DENV endemics have been documented, scored > 70% population coverage for both CTL and HTL epitope.

The NS1 and envelope E CTL and HTL epitopes were then docked against major histocompatibility complexes proteins (HLA or MHC class I or II) using HDOCK. Interestingly, it was noted that envelope E epitopes (ILIGAVITW, and DTAWDFGSL) had better binding scores yet NS1 CTL epitopes (EVEDYGFGV, LRTTTASGK, RSVTRLENL) interacted more with HLA binding sites through hydrogen bonds. Mixed strong–weak H-bond pairings can occasionally diminish ligand binding affinity due to interaction with bulk water, which provides a mechanistic explanation for why random strengthening of receptor-ligand H-bonds correlates poorly with experimental binding affinity105,106,107. This was reversed for NS1 HTL epitope (YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL), which showed a greater number of hydrogen bonds and more negative docking scores suggesting that H-bond strengthened the binding affinities106,107.

Altering epitope sequences within the vaccine construct is associated with favorable outcomes108. For this reason, three distinct DENV vaccine designs were created, all of which had certain common characteristics. All epitopes in all 3 DENV vaccine constructs were conjugated with the PADRE sequence in its C-terminal and the βeta-defensin 103 peptide sequence in its N-terminal. The PADRE sequence is a Pan HLA DR-binding epitope that is used as an adjuvant to activate antigen-specific CD4 + T-cells109. The β-defensin is a useful adjuvant for boosting mucosal immune responses to subunit vaccines. Additionally, it has been previously documented that β-defensin promotes antiviral innate immune responses in macrophage-like THP-1 cells and induces the production of virus-specific neutralizing Abs in vivo110. This was observed in the conjunction of β-defensin and S RBD protein that strengthened the mucosal and systemic immune responses against MERS-CoV infection in MERS-CoV-infected hDPP4-Tg mice111. The CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes within all 3 vaccine constructs were linked by GPGPG, EAAKEAAK, and AAY linkers. These linkers were known for their ability to induce T-helper lymphocyte response and elevate the overall immune response of the subunit vaccine 112. Finally, all 3 DENV vaccine constructs harnessed the CTL and HTL epitopes that exhibited high conservancies across all DENV serotypes (i.e. epitopes, WYGMEIRPL, EVEDYGFGV, LRTTTASGK, and YRPGYHTQTAGPWHL) as mentioned earlier. The antigenicity, allergenicity, and toxicity for the all 3 vaccine constructs were assessed and the vaccine construct no. 3 demonstrated the highest antigenicity score. This could be explained by the fact that the construct had more NS1 LBL epitopes with higher vaxijen antigenic scores than those obtained by LBL epitopes of the envelope E protein.

The back translation of the mutli-epitope vaccine construct denoted that the 3 vaccine construct had a codon adaptation index (CAI) of 1 within the E. coli and CG content of 59%. An ideal CAI and CG% content for expression of protein within the host is 1 and 30–70%, respectively113. This suggested the possibility of in silico cloning of vaccine construct using E. coli as an expression vector. The numerous benefits of employing E. coli led to the selection of this organism as the expression host for this investigation. E. coli is well-known for its quick growth kinetics, ease of exogenous DNA transformation, and cost-effectiveness as it doesn’t always require sophisticated conditions for growth and propagation114. They are also easier to scale up than eukaryotic expression hosts, which occasionally need to have their cells broken mechanically in order to extract proteins. Moreover, mammalian host expressions may secrete endogenous retrovirus-like particles (RVLP) in addition to the target protein hardening the downstream purification procedure115.

The Expasy ProtParam tool inferred the physicochemical properties for all 3 vaccine constructs from their amino acid sequences. This tool is essential for evaluating the stability of proteins for docking studies. The theoretical iso-electric point (pI) for all three vaccines (> 7) denoted their basic nature. The makeup of the amino acids in the protein has a major impact on this; the more positively charged lysine, arginine, and histidine, the more likely it is that the protein will be acidic. The more negatively charged aspartic acid and glutamic acid present, the more likely its basic116. The in vivo half-life of the vaccine constructs was calculated using the protein's N-terminus and it offered a linear scale for measuring protein stability because it measures cellular abundance and the rate at which proteins are removed by cells117. The results of our study demonstrated the stability of all three vaccine designs, with half-lives of > 10 h in E. coli in vivo animal models and 30 h in human reticulocytes in vitro. Methionine (Met) in the vaccine construct’s N-terminus is assumed to be in charge of the protein’s extended half-life since it stabilizes the protein’s structure and functions as an antioxidant to regulate the switch through reversible redox processes118. The average instability and aliphatic indices provided more evidence of the vaccine stability. A thermostable protein is indicated by a high aliphatic index118–120. Each of the three structures’ GRAVY score indicates how hydrophilic they are. Therefore, the 3 vaccine constructs acquire favorable physicochemical properties that would ease their production and purification.

The Prediction of the secondary protein structure for the 3 vaccine constructs by sopma affirmed their stability, since, the α-helical structure (highest %) is known for enhancing the protein conformational stability121. The 3D folding of vaccine constructs denoted that the vaccine construct 1 and 3 had Ramachandran favored scores > 90% and poor rotamers scores ≤ 1. Upon refining the 3D vaccine models with GalaxyRefine, the Ramachandran scores for all vaccine constructs were enhanced. It was also noted that the ProSA denoted that the modeled 3D structure is located within the space of protein determined by X-ray and NMR. Further, the determined Z-Scores were of –ve values which suggested the reliability of the model. Positive Z-scores values typically indicate troublesome or inaccurate portions of a model75. The iMod server’s elastic network model (ENM) depicts the dynamics of all 3 vaccine constructs121–123. The flexibility of the vaccines is supported by the deformability, B factor and residues map, which allows the estimation of the binding affinities of vaccines by examining the distribution of residues in active and non-active locations124. The regions with the maximum flexibility, that promotes vaccine/HLA allele binding, are represented by the peaks of the highest B factor scores.

The C-IMMSIM report denoted that vaccine construct 2 and 3 were more effective than vaccine construct 1 in regards to total level of immunoglobulins, IgG and IgM production. It was also noted that the PLB population was higher in construct 2 and 3 which might be due to the presence of more LBL epitopes in construct 3 (YHTQTAGPWHLGKLEM, IKGIMQAGKRSLRPQP,CRSCTLPPLRYRGEDG,LGKLEMDFDFCEGTTV, SGCVVSWKNKELKCGS, IWLKLKERQDVFCDSK) and the presence of the NS1, RSVTRLENL epitope instead of envelope E, ILIGAVITW, in construct 2 which is more antigenic. Both constructs 2 and 3 were also capable of inducing higher population of T –helper cells which might be attributed to the higher antigenicity and better interactions of NS1 epitopes with HLA class I and II proteins.

The result of C-IMMSIM was supported upon docking vaccine constructs with TLR3 and TLR4, where the best Z and docking scores with TLR3 were achieved by vaccine construct 3 while vaccine construct 2 had the best Z and docking scores for TLR4. This is attributed to better interactions of both construct 2 and construct 3 with TLR3 and TLR4 receptors through hydrogen bonding, larger interface interaction areas, and electrostatic and van der-wal interactions88,89. The binding with TLR3 and TLR4 surged cytokines production in particular IL-2, tumor necrosis factor, IL-10, IFN-γ. This is consistent with the fact that the DENV NS1 proteins are responsible for the vasoactive cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL-6, which may be linked to the vascular abnormalities that develop in severe dengue125,126. According to MM/GBSA calculation by Hawkdock server, it was noted that construct 2 had better binding affinities scores than construct 1 when binding to chain A of both TLR3 and TLR4 receptors. Comparative studies have demonstrated that MM/GBSA performs better than molecular mechanics/Poisson Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA). It’s also known that MM/GBSA rescoring significantly produces better results than docking127. Constructs 3 and 2 both appear to have stronger binding affinities with TLR4 and TLR3, respectively, which supports their immunological response, according to MM/GBSA and HADDOCK scores.

Molecular dynamic simulations were carried out to examine the protein–protein interactions in motion that contribute to their stable bound conformation and to see the impact of ligand binding on changes in protein conformation, as molecular docking only provides a single snapshot of protein–ligand interactions. Since the DENV-2 vaccine construct 2 demonstrated strong immune responses according to C-IMMSIM, the best HADDOCK scores when docked with chain A of TLR4 receptor, and average mmGBSA scores (according to HAWKDOCK) when compared to other constructs, constructs 1 and 3, it was utilized for the MD simulation study. The RMSD plot for TLR4 protein without ligand denoted the stability of the protein structure throughout the simulated time of 100 ns. The residues RMSF computed at 100 ns were used to depict the high flexibility regions within the protein. When the RMSD, RMSF, and energy contributions are taken into account collectively, it can be concluded that the TLR4 protein structure is fairly stable during MD simulation. Comparing the RMSD trajectory of DENV-2 vaccine construct2/TLR4 complex to TLR4 residue in the absence of ligand—which was demonstrated to have significantly variable peaks as previously explained in the results section- strongly supports the inhibitory and stabilizing capabilities of DENV-2 vaccine construct 2 on TLR4. In order to facilitate the most stable binding conformation, the 200 ps simulation time used in this investigation is sufficient to allow for side chain rearrangements in both the native and protein/construct 2 complexes128. Further, the RMSF of DENV-2 vaccine construct 2/TLR4 denoted eminent reduction in fluctuations in comparison to the unbound TLR4 protein, demonstrating increased stability. The total energy score for the stabilized conformation of the DENV-2 construct 2/TLR4 complex also denoted the high stability of the docked complex when compared to the free TLR4 protein. The stability of the DENV-2 vaccine design 2/TLR4 receptor was generally validated by the MD simulation investigation, which suggests potential interaction in vivo associated with positive immune responses.

It was also noted that the vaccine constructs can interact with the extracellular toll like receptors 2 and 5 (TLR2, and TLR5). The vaccine construct 3 attained the best affinity toward TLR5 while vaccine construct 2 and 1 exhibited comparable affinities toward TLR2. Both TLR2 and TLR5 receptors are expressed by dendritic cells (DCs), natural killer cells and T cells129. TIRAP (also known as MAL) and TIRAP-inducing IFN-β (TRIF)-related adaptor molecule (TRAM) are receptor-proximal membrane adaptor proteins that are activated when ligands bind to extracellular TLR2 or 5. This consequently activates the release of inflammatory cytokines through MyD88-dependent pathways, or via TRIF-dependent pathways, which also induce Type I IFN and inflammatory cytokines130,131. Interestingly, activation of TLR2 signaling on DCs can enhance higher frequency effector CD4 + T cell responses which might in turn improve the overall responses of vaccines. Further activation of TLR5 increased the expression of maturation markers on CD103 + (cDC1) and CD11b + (cDC2) subsets, which was the most effective way to activate newborn lung antigen presenting cells (APCs)132. Additionally, vaccination with TLR adjuvants, specifically TLR-2 and TLR-5, was linked to the activation of newborn CD8 + T lymphocytes, which support clonal proliferation and have a supplementary effect on cellular IFN-γ release133. It was also noted that all 3 vaccine constructs had comparable affinities to TLR-7. The intracellular receptor TLR-7 is mostly expressed in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) and is capable of identifying single-stranded RNA fragments with specific sequence preferences132,134. By creating large quantities of interferon-α (IFN-α), the pDCs guard against the DENV pathogenesis by preventing the infection of target cells. It has previously been documented that the dengue virus stimulates TLR-7 pDCs, which in turn leads to IRF7 activation and via Myd88-dependent pathways, which is linked to IFN production aiding in the removal of the virus while averting potential inflammatory reactions134,135. It was also noted that in patients with non-severe dengue fever had higher levels of serum IFN-α levels and more circulating pDCs136. Thus stimulation of TLR-7 by the vaccine constructs would be highly beneficial in combating DENV infections.

In conclusion, NS1 and the anchor domain of envelope E protein harness several CTL, HTL, and LBL epitopes. Only antigenic, non-allergic, and non-toxic epitopes were selected for the construction of vaccine constructs. The selected epitopes had high population coverage across different areas worldwide where DENV-2 endemic. Both the CTL and HTL epitopes retrieved from NS1 epitopes exhibited high conservancies’ percentages with DENV serotypes (1,3, and 4). The vaccine candidates 2 and 3 might be more immunogenic than vaccine construct 1 due to the presence of more NS1 epitopes which are more immunogenic and antigenic than E protein. All vaccine constructs were capable of eliciting an immune response however, vaccine constructs 2 and 3 elicited the production of inflammatory cytokines and immunoglobulins more than vaccine construct 1. This was supported by docking scores and interactions of both construct 2 and 3 with TLR3 and TLR4 receptors. Molecular dynamics simulation studies for DENV-2 vaccine construct 2 with TLR4 denoted high binding affinity and stability of the construct with the receptor chain A which might foretell a favorable in vivo interaction and immune responses. Additionally, all vaccine constructs exhibited similar affinities to toll-like receptor 7, which is expressed by pDCs and is essential in reducing DENV pathogenesis. This could indicate a potential benefit of the vaccines during additional in vitro and in vivo investigation. In further studies, experimental methods should be used to confirm the effectiveness of bioinformatics tools in predicting DENV-2 epitopes and constructing subunit peptide vaccines.

Future perspective

To further understand the entire immune response and potential side effects of this vaccine, the DENV-2 vaccine construct no. 2 and 3 that demonstrated the best C-IMMSIM results and docking scores with TLR3, TLR4, and TLR5 receptors may be expressed through E. coli as an expression vector and evaluated in an in vivo animal model in conjunction with an adjuvant.

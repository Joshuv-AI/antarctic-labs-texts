A hybrid spiking convolutional neural framework with extreme learning machine for enhanced anomaly detection in network security
Authors: Weiqi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Real-time cybersecurity systems continue to have a significant problem in detecting breaches in dynamic and highly unbalanced network streams. An online evolving spiking neural network (OeSNN-UAD) and an integrated extreme learning machine (ELM) as an analytical readout optimizer are combined with spiking convolutional layers for spatiotemporal feature extraction in this paper’s unified hybrid SCNN–OeSNN–ELM framework. In order to provide discriminative temporal representations, Gaussian Receptive Fields (GRFs) encode streaming inputs into spike trains, which are then processed by spiking convolutional layers. These characteristics are then supplied into a developing OeSNN reservoir whose output connections are analytically optimized by the ELM, allowing for low-memory operation, online adaptation, and quick, non-iterative learning without the need for labeled input. The suggested framework routinely outperforms current spiking-based baselines (Vacuum Spiker and Hybrid SNN-IOMT), as shown by experiments on the Numenta Anomaly Benchmark (NAB) under a streaming protocol and robustness validation on CIC-IDS2017. The model’s relative detection increases are usually between 3 and 10% on NAB and 3 and 6% on CIC-IDS2017, and it delivers moderate but statistically significant improvements across precision, recall, F1-score, balanced accuracy, and MCC. The framework also shows enhanced latency and energy efficiency due to its event-driven spiking computing and analytical ELM optimization.

Introduction

The Internet and human society are becoming increasingly intertwined. However, given the current state of networks, it is impossible to ignore the rise in cyberattacks1–3. Securing IoT systems is particularly challenging due to the inherent constraints in memory, computational power, communication, and energy within IoT networks and devices4,5. These limitations make them more vulnerable to cyberattacks, which can result in significant damage, including the disruption of critical services and financial losses6–8.

By simulating multi-period and evolutionary processes, novel techniques for anomaly identification in dynamic time streams have been presented recently and have demonstrated a strong capacity to detect progressive deviations in real data9. Strong control frameworks have been created in the field of cybersecurity to thwart malevolent attacks in distributed and heterogeneous systems, highlighting the significance of online resilience and threat adaptability10. Simultaneously, machine learning models have been introduced for the proactive detection of ransomware attacks and phishing campaigns, demonstrating the high effectiveness of data-driven approaches in identifying new threats11,12. The stability and dynamic behavior of fractional-order spiking neural networks have been studied theoretically, and the mathematical underpinnings required to guarantee these networks’ convergence and stability have been supplied13. Additionally, energy-oriented algorithms that seek to simultaneously lower energy usage and preserve quality of service have been proposed for data centers and Internet of Things applications14,15.

When potentially malicious activities are identified, the IDS alerts the IT administrator to possible network intrusions16. Given the vast amount of network data available, deep learning (DL) techniques can be effectively employed to address the challenge of intrusion detection17.

Secure control strategies based on event-driven triggering and adaptive learning have been introduced in network system security studies to address operator errors and fake data injection attacks. These strategies demonstrate a high ability to maintain the stability and performance of multi-agent systems18. The significance of online adaptation and control structure resilience against cyber threats is further highlighted by the development of observer-based control frameworks for network switching systems under denial of service assaults19.

In practice, a data stream is a potentially infinite sequence of samples that arrive continuously over time20–22. Streaming data represents a high-speed, uninterrupted flow of information. Recent advancements in this technology have made the continuous collection of data more feasible23,24. Everyday activities, such as using smartphones, browsing the internet, and trading stocks, contribute to the automated storage of data. However, processing data streams in real-time, especially when they are generated in heterogeneous formats by various devices and resources, presents a significant challenge25. One key difficulty is the detection of anomalies within these data streams26.

Unsupervised anomaly detection in streaming data is a critical area of research27. Typically, these detection systems start by gathering labeled training data, using a supervised learning approach28,29. Proper training is essential for these systems to accurately detect anomalies. If labeled data is unavailable, the training process becomes more complex. In such cases, online evolutionary approaches, such as those using neural network models like Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), can be employed for anomaly detection30,31.

The classification of data streams has garnered significant attention in recent years due to the exponential growth of data generated by the Internet and other industries. Advances have been made in addressing certain data stream challenges, such as concept drift detection and supervised data stream classification. However, many data stream algorithms struggle to achieve satisfactory performance, particularly when dealing with diverse data streams (including categorical and numerical values) or when labeled samples are limited32.

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of intrusion detection for computer networks. It emphasizes the use of advanced methods, particularly incremental machine learning and online evolving spiking neural networks. The primary objective of this study is to improve the effectiveness of intrusion detection systems through the application of novel and integrated techniques specifically designed for dynamic and streaming data environments. Spiking neural networks, which mimic the functionality of the human brain, are extensively employed in complex anomaly detection systems for streaming data.

This paper enhances the accuracy, speed, and energy efficiency of intrusion detection systems by employing a modified version of the OeSNN (Online Evolving Spiking Neural Network) in combination with the ELM (Extreme Learning Machine) algorithm. The integration of these two advanced technologies enables the rapid and precise identification of irregularities in complex real-time data.

The proposed approach leverages the OeSNN network to perform real-time analysis of streaming data, eliminating the need for data labeling. Additionally, incorporating the ELM algorithm within this network has led to substantial improvements in both learning efficiency and overall system performance. These advancements underscore the efficacy of this approach in addressing the security challenges faced by contemporary computer networks, particularly in the context of increasing cyberattacks and the growing need for effective countermeasures.

The suggested approach presents an entirely online, analytically optimized evolving spiking architecture in contrast to current spiking-based intrusion detection systems that use either offline-trained spiking CNNs or heuristic evolutionary optimizers. In particular, (i) a spiking convolutional front-end is integrated to provide spatio-temporal feature abstraction prior to neuronal evolution, which is absent in previous OeSNN-based IDS frameworks; (ii) the reservoir dynamically evolves through candidate neuron generation, merging, and removal mechanisms to handle concept drift in streaming traffic; and (iii) the ELM module is embedded inside the OeSNN-UAD reservoir as a closed-form analytical readout, removing iterative synaptic updates. This combination of online reservoir evolution, analytical ELM optimization, and SCNN feature extraction has never been documented in intrusion detection literature.

Section 4 focuses on the evaluation and simulation of the suggested model, while Sect. 5 presents the final results and discusses future research directions.

Related work

The ability of event-driven neural processing to handle extremely unbalanced and temporally irregular data streams has been shown in early studies on spiking-based anomaly identification. In33, a spiking neural network-based anomaly detection model was presented, utilizing spike-timing representations to identify infrequent deviations in time-series data with minimal processing overhead. In order to lower energy and memory utilization, the suggested architecture placed a strong emphasis on temporal encoding and sparse firing activity. This method’s representational capacity in complex network traffic environments may be limited because it mainly relied on reservoir-based spike processing and omitted hierarchical spiking convolutional feature extraction, despite producing encouraging results for general time-series anomaly detection.

For certain cybersecurity applications, including the detection of distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, spiking neural networks have also been investigated. In34, a detection technique that uses spiking neurons to record temporal traffic patterns was presented, proving that spike-based learning can accurately simulate sudden traffic spikes linked to volumetric attacks. The model demonstrated the potential of SNNs for real-time network monitoring by achieving better detection accuracy when compared to conventional machine learning baselines. However, the method ignored the difficulties of concept drift and online structural adaptability in long-term streaming contexts, instead concentrating on classification accuracy.

Spiking neural networks have been used in conjunction with fuzzy logic to protect data sharing in cyber-physical transportation systems, going beyond traditional intrusion detection. A hybrid fuzzy–spiking framework was created in35 to execute adaptive decision making under uncertainty and encode sensory data into spikes, ensuring safe communication for autonomous cars. Large-scale network intrusion streams and changing anomaly patterns frequently seen in enterprise networks were not taken into account by the system, which improved robustness against spoofing and malicious manipulation but was designed for vehicular communication scenarios.

Another crucial area of study for spiking-based intrusion detection has been energy efficiency. Spiking neurons were used to decrease computational overhead and lower energy consumption on limited edge devices in36, which presented a resource-efficient IoT intrusion detection method. The study showed that compared to traditional deep neural networks, SNN-based classifiers can reach competitive accuracy while using a lot less energy. However, the framework’s use of offline training methods and inflexible network structures may have limited its ability to react to dynamically changing cyberthreats in real-time settings.

In parallel, non-spiking deep learning architectures have also been proposed to address intrusion detection in industrial IoT systems. A graph convolutional network combined with gated recurrent units and optimized using ant colony optimization was presented in37 to capture spatial and temporal dependencies in industrial traffic flows. Although this architecture achieved strong detection performance, its reliance on dense neural computations and heuristic optimization strategies introduces higher computational and energy costs, making it less suitable for continuous real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

A human-in-the-loop leader-follower consensus control paradigm for nonlinear multi-agent systems under deception attacks was presented by researchers in38. To lower communication overhead and boost resistance to data manipulation, the dynamic self-excitation method is used. The findings demonstrate that the accuracy and durability of agreement under danger situations can be enhanced by combining adaptive mechanisms with the human element. The significance of secure and adaptable methods in networked systems is emphasized in this paper.

A model-free, observer-based adaptive predictive control paradigm for distributed heterogeneous multi-agent systems under DoS attacks was presented by researchers in a different paper39. System stability is ensured by the use of an observer, which makes state estimation and attack compensation possible. According to experimental findings, this approach can preserve the resilience and performance of networked and IoT systems in the face of communication disruptions. The importance of resilient and predictive methods in cybersecurity is highlighted in this study.

Unsupervised real-time anomaly detection for streaming data has also been investigated outside the spiking domain. The streaming anomaly detection framework in40 proposed an unsupervised statistical learning model capable of identifying anomalies in real-time data streams without labeled samples. Although the approach provided strong theoretical foundations for online detection, it relied on dense numerical computations and did not exploit the energy-efficient, event-driven characteristics of spiking neural networks41. presents an optimal formation control technique based on a funnel for multi-agent multi-input-multi-output systems under DoS attacks. The suggested approach guarantees formation stability, performance restrictions, and a reduction in communication resource consumption by employing a dynamic and quantitative excitation mechanism. The findings demonstrate that the suggested framework is appropriate for dispersed IoT applications and resilient to network disruptions.

Federated spiking neural networks have also been investigated recently for long-term intrusion detection. In order to facilitate cooperative intrusion detection among dispersed IoT nodes while maintaining data privacy, a hierarchical federated spiking architecture was put out in42. With less energy and communication overhead, the framework produced encouraging detection results. Nevertheless, the federated structure did not specifically address online developing neuron reservoirs or analytical optimization of output connections, and thus added further synchronization complexity.

To gain a better understanding of spiking dynamics, thorough theoretical investigations of spiking neuron models have been carried out. The Izhikevich spiking neuron was examined in the neuron modeling work in43, which shed light on computing efficiency and spike generating methods. Although this study provided important theoretical underpinnings, it did not specifically address scenarios involving streaming anomaly detection or intrusion detection.

More recently, in44, a cross-temporal contrastive learning-based energy-efficient spiking intrusion detection system for Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) environments was presented. By learning temporal differences across medical data streams, the suggested model made use of spiking neurons to lower energy usage and enhance attack discrimination. The approach concentrated on domain-specific medical traffic and did not include online evolving neuron reservoirs or spiking convolutional front-end layers for general-purpose network intrusion detection, despite its effectiveness in IoMT situations. Proposed method

The suggested technique presents an innovative hybrid architecture for anomaly detection in streaming data, primarily aimed at improving intrusion detection in dynamic network settings. This approach incorporates spiking convolutional layers for spatio-temporal feature extraction, the OeSNN-UAD for adaptive online classification, and the ELM for effective training and optimization. The approach mitigates the shortcomings of conventional intrusion detection systems, including elevated latency, energy inefficiency, and inadequate adaptation to streaming data, without necessitating labeled samples. The design emulates biological neuron processing via discrete spikes, enabling low-power, real-time functionality while preserving excellent accuracy. The cohesive process of the suggested hybrid framework is delineated as follows:

This addresses idea drift in streaming data by limiting reservoir size and eliminating obsolete neurons.ELM optimization: Integrated into the OeSNN-UAD, ELM improves the connection between input and output layers by randomly assigning weights to the hidden layer and analytically determining output weights using linear optimization. Integration of extreme learning machine (ELM)

The ELM is included into the hybrid architecture as an effective readout and optimization mechanism inside the OeSNN-UAD reservoir. In contrast to conventional iterative training in spiking neural networks (SNNs), ELM facilitate the training of single-hidden-layer feedforward networks using random input-to-hidden weights and analytical calculation of output weights. In our model, spike trains from the spiking convolutional layers are assigned to input neurons (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{N}_{I}$$\end{document}).A randomly designated hidden layer, with predetermined and randomly allocated weights, transforms these spikes into a high-dimensional feature space. Data pre-processing

The proposed hybrid architecture is intended for unsupervised anomaly identification in streaming time-series data and is assessed only on the NAB dataset42. NAB is a prominent benchmark for real-time streaming anomaly identification, consisting of over 50 real-world and synthetic time-series streams (e.g., server metrics, CPU usage, traffic statistics, social media volumes, and CloudWatch logs) with meticulously annotated abnormalities. These streams replicate dynamic, high-volume situations characteristic of network security monitoring, making NAB particularly suitable for evaluating the model’s performance in live, unsupervised contexts.

Spiking neuron model

The spiking neuron model provides a more physiologically accurate depiction of neuronal functioning in the brain, in contrast to conventional artificial neuron models. Spiking neuron models, unlike simpler models, take into account the precise timing of electrical pulses or “spikes” sent by neurons, rather than just computing output based on a weighted sum of inputs. In this concept, a neuron generates an action potential, often known as a spike, when its membrane potential over a specific threshold. Neuron-speaking models work based on the temporal activities of neurons. In these models, neurons respond with temporal pulses rather than a continuous amount of activity. Model of detection

In the proposed model, a convolutional spiking neural network is designed, whose structure is based on multiple sequential processing layers. These layers are a combination of convolution operations and spike excitation mechanisms. In addition to extracting local features, each layer also considers the temporal dynamics of neurons, and thus, the ability to recognize complex patterns in the input data is improved.

The architectural parameters including the number of layers, the dimensions of the convolutional kernels, the size of the time window, and the threshold of neuron firing are adjustable and can be changed based on the needs of the problem to improve the accuracy and speed of recognition.

Discussion and evaluation

OeSNN networks consist of both input and output layers. The network’s output layers are constrained by the need to handle a substantial amount of input data and the memory issue related to classifying streaming data values. This network generates a candidate output neuron for each new data point. The values of these neurons are either sent to the output reservoir or combined with a comparable neuron in the reservoir. The OeSNN network possesses the capacity to regulate the magnitude of the reservoir. By following this approach, when the tank reaches its maximum capacity, it eliminates the oldest neuron present in the tank, thereby creating space for new neurons to enter. The input layer of this network consists of Gaussian receptive fields (GRF) and input neurons. The GRFs have been employed to apply a filtering process on the input data, hence reducing the computational workload and enabling the optimal selection of the input neuron. The result of this stage is the creation of firing parameters, which are then utilized to set the initial weight of synapses connecting each potential input and output neuron. Ultimately, the input data samples are categorized.

The classification in this network is achieved by computing the values associated with the post-synaptic potential in the output neurons. The process involves generating Generalized Receptive Fields (GRFs) for each input sample, followed by utilizing these GRFs to determine the arrangement of the input neurons. Subsequently, the value of the output neuron is modified by encoding the postsynaptic potential (PSP) values. If the postsynaptic potential (PSP) derived from a decision class given to the output neuron surpasses the predetermined PSP threshold, it is classified as the decision class for the input sample. The ELM algorithm has been employed to enhance the training process of OeSNN. The ELM algorithm is a neural network that incorporates a hidden layer where the weights between the input and hidden layers are randomly assigned. In the subsequent steps, the weights between the hidden layer and the output layer are learned using an analytical solution to the linear training problem. This algorithm exhibits superior speed as it refrains from incessantly modifying the weights. Implementing this technique has resulted in enhanced communication between these two layers.

This research involved determining appropriate parameters for implementing OeSNN, all of which are presented and elucidated in Table 2. The parameters Wsize and Ɛ exert the greatest influence on anomaly detection. In this procedure, the selection of these two values has been optimized for this specific purpose. As shown in Table 2, the spiking convolutional layers of the suggested framework employ two layers with 2 × 2 kernels and a 1.5 spike threshold. Because ELM solves weights analytically, it uses a random hidden layer with 100 neurons that isn’t regularized.

Experimental setup and reproducibility

The Neural Network Toolbox, Signal Processing Toolbox, and Parallel Computing Toolbox were used in MATLAB R2023b to carry out each experiment. Every model was run on a workstation that had an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU, 32 GB of RAM, and an Intel Core i7-12700 H CPU (2.3–4.7 GHz). The NAB dataset comprises 58 streaming time-series, both synthetic and real-world. The first 70% of samples for each time series were used for adaptive learning and online initialization, with the remaining 30% set aside for testing. Information leakage is avoided and the streaming character of the data is maintained by this chronological division. Each time-series’ assessment metrics were calculated separately, and they were then combined using arithmetic averaging across the 58 files:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Metri{c}_{final}=\frac{1}{58}\sum\:_{i=1}^{58}Metri{c}_{i}$$\end{document}

Hybrid SNN-IOMT and Vacuum Spiker were reimplemented in MATLAB using the same streaming protocol, normalization, and NAB preprocessing as the suggested approach. To guarantee fair and repeatable comparisons, the authors’ default hyperparameters were employed, followed by a small amount of grid-search adjustment. The average inference time per input sample (ms) was used to calculate latency.

Using MATLAB power-profiling algorithms on the same hardware platform, energy consumption was calculated as the average electrical energy per inference (mJ).

Attack detection analysis

There is a notable class imbalance in the NAB dataset, with normal occurrences predominating and anomalies making up less than 1% of the data points in most subsets. This disparity is common in network security streaming anomaly detection, where uncommon occurrences like invasions need to be detected without producing too much false positives. Precision and F1-score measurements are used to assess performance because they prioritize practical deployment and take imbalance into account. The suggested hybrid architecture performs better than the baselines Vacuum Spiker33 and Hybrid SNN-IOMT44 on all of these criteria, as shown in Figs. 7 and 8. Attack size (A) and detection performance are positively correlated because greater attacks result in more pronounced spatiotemporal patterns. ELM serves as the readout layer for effective optimization in the suggested technique, which uses spiking convolutional layers to capture temporal correlations in streaming data. NAB subsets were used in MATLAB simulations to model dynamic attacks that impacted roughly 1% of the data as anomalies. For the reservoir, where decoded data is first transformed into electrical current, a nonlinear GLIF neuron model was chosen as the spiking neuron model. Every input sample generates a spike train, which is then integrated into the stream by applying temporal delays.

Discussion

A number of important architectural synergies are responsible for the suggested hybrid framework’s improved performance. By addressing OeSNN-UAD’s intrinsic restriction in processing complicated patterns from raw time-series, spiking convolutional layers offer rich spatio-temporal feature maps that improve the discrimination of minute abnormalities. In contrast to baselines that rely on evolutionary or surrogate training, the embedded ELM readout reduces prediction errors and processing complexity by analytically optimizing synaptic weights without repeated backpropagation. In event-driven spiking, this combination achieves faster convergence and reduced energy consumption while maintaining unsupervised online evolution.

These improvements are validated statistically: For F1-score and Accuracy gains over Vacuum Spiker33 and Hybrid SNN-IOMT44, paired t-tests across the 58 NAB files show p-values < 0.05 (95% confidence intervals: F1-score + 0.03 to + 0.06, Accuracy + 3.2% to + 5.8%). Due to non-iterative ELM and sparse convolution, the suggested approach provides equivalent detection with better latency/energy profiles when compared to previous state-of-the-art (e.g., binarized blockchain SNNs and hierarchical federated SNN in SURFS42.

Conclusions

The necessity for strong, real-time anomaly detection to reduce cybersecurity threats has increased due to the modern networked systems’ rapid expansion in streaming data. By combining spiking convolutional layers for improved spatiotemporal feature extraction and the ELM as an effective, non-iterative readout optimizer, this paper proposed a novel hybrid framework that expands the Online Evolving Spiking Neural Network for Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (OeSNN-UAD). This combination maintains the advantages of online adaptation and low-memory operation while addressing the main drawbacks of standalone OeSNN-UAD, such as its limited ability to handle complicated patterns and iterative training overhead.

In comparison to recent spiking-based baselines, experimental evaluations on the NAB dataset, complemented by validation on CIC-IDS2017 for generalizability, show consistent modest improvements in detection metrics (Precision, Recall, F1-score, Balanced Accuracy, and MCC). Due to event-driven sparse spiking and analytical ELM tuning, the framework also shows benefits in latency and energy efficiency, as shown in the radar chart Fig. 6. The practical value of the hybrid architecture in unsupervised streaming systems with significant class imbalance is highlighted by statistical tests that validate the significance of these advantages.

The method’s performance is susceptible to parameter adjustment, and its computing demands may make it difficult to implement on devices with very limited resources, notwithstanding these advancements. Additional optimizations, including adaptive reservoir pruning or federated learning integration for privacy-preserving distributed detection, as well as application to multi-modal streaming data, may be investigated in future work. All things considered, the suggested architecture presents a viable, bio-inspired method for scalable, energy-conscious anomaly detection in changing cybersecurity environments.

Association of sarcopenia with pathologic complete response in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients undergoing neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy
Authors: Junjie Shi, Jintao Liu, Zhiyun Xu, Mengzhou Chen, Mingzhi Zhang, Dafu Xu, Wenze Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia can affect the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. However, the extent of this relationship in patients treated with neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy has not been fully explored.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted on 150 patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma who underwent two or three cycles of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy, followed by surgical resection. We analyzed the correlation between clinicopathological factors—including sarcopenia before neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy and excessive muscle loss during neoadjuvant therapy—and the occurrence of pathologic complete response.ResultsBefore neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy, 55.3% of patients were classified as having sarcopenia, and this proportion increased to 68.0% after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy. A total of 20.7% of patients (31/150) achieved pathologic complete response. Multivariate logistic regression analyses revealed that sarcopenia before neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (odds ratio = 2.696, P = 0.023) and excessive muscle loss during treatment (odds ratio = 3.999, P = 0.038) were independent predictors of failure to achieve pathologic complete response after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy.ConclusionsThe study identified sarcopenia before neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy and excessive muscle loss during treatment as important adverse factors associated with a lower likelihood of achieving pathologic complete response in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients undergoing neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy. Consequently, our findings underscore the crucial role of preoperative sarcopenia in influencing treatment outcomes of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy.Keywords: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Pathologic complete response, Immunochemotherapy, Neoadjuvant therapy, Sarcopenia, Excessive muscle loss

Background

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a major oncologic challenge, especially in regions like China, where it constitutes the majority of esophageal cancer cases [1, 2]. Minimally invasive esophagectomy, which incorporates the Ivor Lewis and McKeown techniques, has been established as the gold standard for ESCC to mitigate postoperative complications and expedite patient recuperation [3, 4]. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy has become the preferred treatment for patients with locally advanced esophageal cancer [5, 6]. Notably, esophageal cancer patients who have undergone pCR experience more significant improvements in prognosis [7]. Despite the evolution of treatment strategies encompassing surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, the prognosis for ESCC remains poor. High mortality rates underscore the need for improved therapeutic interventions.

Sarcopenia, denoting nutritional deficiency and attenuated muscle function within the organism, is predominant among the elderly and patients afflicted with gastrointestinal malignancies [8]. Research has shown that the proportion of esophageal cancer patients with sarcopenia ranges from 40% to 70% [9, 10]. Additionally, patients with sarcopenia prior to surgery are more likely to encounter grave complications, such as anastomotic leakage, pulmonary infection, and poorer prognosis. The coexistence of sarcopenia also amplifies the side effects of chemoradiotherapy for esophageal cancer patients, resulting in treatment delays and even interruptions [11]. Furthermore, sarcopenia remains a critical factor influencing poor immunotherapy outcomes and adverse prognosis [12–14]. Consequently, assessing sarcopenia before surgery or neoadjuvant therapy is crucial for improving treatment outcomes.

In recent years, mounting evidence from clinical studies suggests that neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (NICT), followed by surgical tumor resection, has the potential to significantly improve the pCR rate and prognostic outcomes for patients recently diagnosed with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [15, 16]. Nonetheless, the efficacy of this regimen is challenged by the inadvertent exacerbation of sarcopenia and nutritional deficits, which in turn contribute to a deterioration in patient condition. Moreover, predictive indicators that can accurately identify patients who will benefit from NICT at an early stage are still lacking in clinical practice. The relationship between preoperative changes in muscle mass due to NICT and the attainment of pCR in ESCC remains underexplored and warrants further investigation. To address this gap, our retrospective cohort study examines the predictive value of sarcopenia, excessive muscle loss (EML), and nutritional and inflammatory indicators for achieving pCR in ESCC patients treated with NICT followed by surgery. Our analysis aims to offer new insights into optimizing preoperative management to enhance pCR rates and overall outcomes for this patient demographic.

Methods

Patient selection

We conducted a retrospective cohort study in the Department of Thoracic Surgery at the Affiliated Huaian No. 1 People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University from January 2020 to May 2023. The study aimed to evaluate the association between sarcopenia, EML, and pCR in patients with resectable ESCC. The study involved the patients who received two or three cycles of NICT followed by surgical resection.

Patients were excluded if they had any of the following comorbidities: (A) comorbidities with other malignancies; (B) missing pre- or post-treatment CT imaging data; (C) inability to undergo surgical resection after NICT; (D) incomplete perioperative clinicopathological data. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (A) histopathologically confirmed ESCC following NICT via endoscopy; (B) availability of CT imaging data of the abdomen before NICT and after NICT but prior to surgery. Clinical staging was conducted according to the eighth edition of the TNM (tumor, node, metastasis) classification. The study protocol adhered to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Huaian No. 1 People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (KY-2023-037-01).

Information on clinical parameters

Clinical parameters were retrospectively collected from the patients’ medical records and included demographic data, co-morbidities, medication history, and laboratory data. The neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) was calculated by dividing the absolute neutrophil count by the absolute lymphocyte count. The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) was calculated using the following formula: peripheral platelet count (×10^9/L) × absolute neutrophil count (×10^9/L) / absolute lymphocyte count (×10^9/L). The Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI) was calculated using the following formula: 10 × albumin (g/dL) + 0.005 × lymphocyte count (/mm^3). The advanced lung cancer inflammation index (ALI) was calculated using the following formula: BMI × albumin (g/dL) / NLR. Using these calculated indices, the optimal cut-off points for NLR, SII, PNI, and ALI were determined by ROC analysis. The highest Youden index on the ROC curve was selected as the optimal cut-off value for each parameter. The cut-off values of NLR, SII, PNI, and ALI were 3.05, 415.5, 54.3, and 468.8, respectively.

Determination of Skeletal Muscle Index (SMI) and sarcopenia

Abdominal CT scans were used to evaluate skeletal muscle mass at the L3 vertebral level, utilizing Philips Vue PACS (picture archiving and communication system) software (Philips Electronics UK LTD, Farnborough, UK). Muscles were identified within the Hounsfield Unit (HU) range of -29 to + 150, and manual corrections were made to the tissue boundaries as necessary. The SMI was calculated by summing the cross-sectional areas of the right and left psoas muscles at the L3 level and normalizing this sum to the patient’s height squared (L3 skeletal muscle area/height², cm²/m²). The diagnosis of sarcopenia is based on a L3 SMI of ≤ 52.4 cm2/m2 for males and ≤ 38.5 cm2/m2 for females [17]. The change in SMI was defined as ΔSMI = SMI_pre-NICT – SMI_post-NICT. EML was defined as a decrease in ΔSMI of at least 10% [18].

Statistical analysis

Baseline clinical and pathological variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables and frequency and percentage for categorical variables. Differences between groups were analyzed using chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify predictors of pCR. SPSS 22.0, was used for all statistical analyses. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The study included 150 patients diagnosed with locally advanced ESCC and treated with NICT. Among them, 103 (68.7%) were male and 47 (31.3%) were female, with a mean age of 66.3 ± 6.18 years. Most patients were located in the middle esophagus, 13.6% of patients were located in the upper esophagus, and 16.6% of patients were located in the lower esophagus. The proportions of the upper, middle, and lower segments of ESCC were 18.7%, 60.6% and 20.7%, respectively. 20.7% (31/150) of patients were evaluated as pCR. There were 83 (55.3%) and 102 (68%) patients with sarcopenia before and after NICT, respectively. 37 (24.7%) were evaluated as EML. Multivariate logistic analysis

Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed with pCR as the dependent variable and variables with p < 0.20 in univariate analysis as independent variables, including sarcopenia before NICT, EML, NLR, SII and ALI. Sarcopenia before NICT and EML are risk factors for pCR in locally advanced ESCC patients received NICT. The probability of pCR in patients without sarcopenia before NICT, without EML are 2.696 (95% CI: 1.148–6.331, P = 0.023) times and 3.999 (95% CI: 1.083–14.759, P = 0.038) times higher than in patients with sarcopenia before NICT and EML, respectively (Table 4). Lower NLR (NLR < 3.05, p = 0.081) and higher ALI ( ALI ≥ 468.8, p = 0.098) are also favorable factors for pCR in locally advanced ESCC patients receiving NICT (Table 4).

Discussion

This study revealed that 55.3% of patients with locally advanced ESCC were regarded as having sarcopenia before NICT, and this proportion increased to 68.0% after NICT. Additionally, EML, defined as at least 10% muscle loss during NICT, was observed in 24.7% of patients prior to surgery. A pCR was observed in 20.7% of patients. The presence of sarcopenia before NICT and EML during treatment served as independent predictors of a poor pathological response to NICT. This report demonstrated for the first time the relationship between sarcopenia, EML, and the efficacy of NICT for ESCC. These findings underscore the significance of preoperative nutritional and muscle status in influencing treatment outcomes.

For patients with locally advanced ESCC, neoadjuvant therapy has conferred significant long-term advantages, particularly for those who attain pCR [19–22]. pCR has become a crucial indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of neoadjuvant therapy [23]. An investigation has revealed that pCR rates for patients with ESCC receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy and neoadjuvant radiochemotherapy range from 1.9% to 12.8% and 10.0% to 43.2%, respectively [6]. In our study, the pCR rate of NICT in ESCC was 20.7%. Moreover, recent studies have reported pCR rates for NICT ranging from 18.6% to 32.87% [24–26]. However, some data suggest that an increase in pCR rates does not necessarily lead to benefits in progression-free survival or overall survival [27]; nevertheless, pCR may still be a favorable prognostic factor for individual patients undergoing treatment. These results imply that NICT may represent a novel strategy for neoadjuvant treatment of locally advanced ESCC.

Conversely, for patients with suboptimal treatment efficacy, there is a risk of adverse consequences such as losing the opportunity for surgery due to tumor progression and adverse drug reactions [24, 25]. Accurately identifying patients responsive to immunotherapy is a key challenge in current immunotherapy. Although PD-L1 expression is a potential marker for screening patients responsive to immunotherapy, its application across a broader patient population remains challenging. First, PD-L1 expression in esophageal cancer is highly heterogeneous, with expression levels ranging from 15% to 83% in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cells and 13% to 31% in immune cells [28–30]. This heterogeneity poses a significant obstacle to accurately detecting PD-L1 expression and complicates biomarker research aimed at predicting immunotherapy efficacy. In addition, research on biomarkers predicting immunotherapy efficacy mainly derives from clinical trials of advanced esophageal cancer [29]. Whether the predictive performance of these biomarkers can be applied to perioperative treatment is another important issue that warrants further investigation. As a result, there is currently no widely accepted and cost-effective biomarker to predict a patient’s response to immunotherapy.

Emphasizing the significance of preoperative assessment and patient readiness, recent evidence indicates that sarcopenia, characterized by the degenerative loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength, significantly influences post-surgical outcomes in various cancer types [8, 31, 32]. Specifically, in esophageal cancer, during neoadjuvant therapy, patients tend to experience skeletal muscle loss and worsening sarcopenia due to tumor-induced anorexia and eating difficulties, tumor-induced catabolism, and side effects of induction therapy [33–35]. Several studies reveal that for esophageal cancer patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, preoperative sarcopenia and EML occurring during neoadjuvant treatment are crucial factors affecting overall survival and recurrence-free survival, especially in elderly patients [36, 37]. Moreover, nutritional support during neoadjuvant therapy can significantly reduce skeletal muscle loss [38]. Studies also confirm that sarcopenia and EML are significant independent predictive factors affecting tumor response [39, 40]. Our results further reveal that sarcopenia before NICT and the development of EML during treatment are important factors associated with poor pCR to NICT. Therefore, preoperative evaluation of nutritional indicators, such as sarcopenia and EML, is essential for guiding treatment decisions and predicting prognosis in esophageal cancer patients.

Mechanistically, the influence of nutrition on the pathological response to neoadjuvant treatments in solid malignancies involves multiple aspects, including immune function, cellular activity, and metabolic processes. Malnutrition and sarcopenia can limit patients’ tolerance to chemotherapy by impairing the immune system and cellular activity, thereby weakening the antitumor immune response [41]. Zhao Hong Liao et al. observed that low serum protein levels and muscle degradation correlate with diminished immune cell function and heightened inflammatory responses, which may reduce treatment efficacy [42, 43]. Nutritional deficits and sarcopenia may also alter drug metabolism and delay tissue recovery by affecting enzymatic activity and regenerative capacity [44]. Additionally, the immunomodulatory effects of a better nutritional status could enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy agents in neoadjuvant protocols [45, 46].

The clinical implications of our findings are substantial. By identifying patients at risk of poor response to NICT through markers such as sarcopenia and EML, clinicians can develop tailored interventions to improve patient readiness for surgery. Such interventions can include prehabilitation protocols involving nutritional support and muscle strengthening exercises, which may potentially improve the efficacy of subsequent surgical interventions.

Our study’s retrospective design and single-institution setting present limitations that may introduce selection bias. Additionally, the exclusive focus on ESCC restricts the applicability of our findings to other esophageal cancer subtypes. The relatively small sample size calls for cautious interpretation of our results and underscores the need for larger, multicenter studies to validate our conclusions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, sarcopenia before NICT and EML during NICT are significant adverse prognostic factors that reduce the likelihood of achieving pCR in ESCC patients. Recognizing and addressing sarcopenia before NICT and malnutrition through targeted interventions is essential to optimize surgical outcomes and overall prognosis in this patient population. Future research should prioritize developing and implementing comprehensive prehabilitation protocols to improve ESCC patients’ readiness for surgery and enhance their long-term outcomes.

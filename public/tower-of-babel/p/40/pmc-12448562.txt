Effective factors in nursing care for cardiovascular patients with opioid dependence disorder: A qualitative study
Authors: Fatemeh Moghaddam, Narges Arsalani, Kian Norouzi Tabrizi, Masoud Fallahi-Khoshknab, Sadat Seyed Bagher Maddah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Caring is a central concept in nursing and is especially critical for patients with comorbidities, such as the coexistence of cardiovascular diseases and substance use disorders. However, limited information is available regarding the effective factors of the care provided to these patients. The study aimed to investigate the effective contextual factors in the care of hospitalized patients with cardiovascular disease and opium dependence disorder.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This qualitative descriptive study was conducted in 2023. The participants were 13 nurses who were purposefully selected from the cardiac and internal cardiac intensive care units of hospitals affiliated with the Ministry of Health in Iran. Sixteen semi-structured interviews were conducted to collect data, and conventional content analysis proposed by Graneheim and Lundman (2004) was used to analyze the data.RESULT:In the analysis, microconditions encompassed factors like nurses’ attitudes, insufficient knowledge, and patient behavioral issues, while macroconditions involved an unsafe workplace, an atmosphere of distrust, difficulty managing pain, and false cultural beliefs.CONCLUSION:Delivering healthcare services by utilizing scientific evidence for decision-making and actions in healthcare is one of the general policies of the health system. Recognizing these elements can aid managers, researchers, and clinical nurses in enhancing nursing care through adjustments to these influential factors.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are among the most pressing chronic noncommunicable conditions worldwide. They are currently the leading cause of mortality in both developing and developed nations.[12] In addition to the high prevalence and challenges posed by CVDs, their co-occurrence with other chronic illnesses and disorders can create even more complex situations. Recent studies indicate that substance use disorders (SUDs) are considered one of the most significant comorbidities.[34] SUDs are a major concern for human society and one of the most intricate human phenomena. They cause behavioral and social disruptions and jeopardize people’s health due to their progressive nature, which permeates all aspects of life.[5]

According to the results of the studies, in Asian and Middle Eastern countries, especially Iran, the coexistence of opium and its derivatives with CVD has a higher prevalence than other chronic diseases.[67]

Patients with CVD and SUDs, notably involving opium and its derivatives, may exhibit aggressive behaviors due to drug dependence and lack of access to medication in hospital settings. This behavior can instill fear and alter the attitudes of nurses, potentially leading to unmet health needs.[89] Furthermore, patients with SUDs often have lower pain tolerance due to drug use and increased painkiller tolerance, necessitating stronger pain management.[1011] However, most health workers are reluctant to prescribe effective painkillers, because they do not believe in the pain of these patients or they are worried about the worsening of addiction and dependence on pain relievers.[1213] Despite this, healthcare providers may be hesitant to prescribe adequate pain relief, fearing exacerbation of addiction. Inadequate treatment and care can contribute to relapse or worsened dependency. Failure to accurately diagnose and manage pain jeopardizes the provision of effective care and optimal nursing support for these patients.[1415]

These Patients and Healthcare teams face numerous challenges. Several studies have highlighted the need to identify underlying factors that impact the quality of care provided to this patient population.[1416] A review of the existing research reveals that globally, studies on this topic are limited. Furthermore, no studies have been conducted in our country to assess the current situation and provide a comprehensive understanding of the factors involved. Neglecting to address this issue can lead to increased health risks and various tensions within the healthcare department, often affecting nurses.[17] The purpose of this study is to thoroughly examine the effective factors in nursing care for hospitalized patients with CVD and opium dependence disorder, and its derivatives.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

Qualitative research offers a profound insight into diverse human experiences, particularly in fields like nursing care. Among the prevalent methodologies in qualitative research, content analysis stands out, especially within nursing. In this study focusing on comprehending the influential factors in care as perceived by nurses, a qualitative approach employing content analysis was undertaken. Such designs are ideal for exploring phenomena within their authentic settings, drawing from individuals’ genuine experiences and viewpoints.[181920] The research was conducted across various general and specialized cardiovascular hospitals in Iran.

Study participants and sampling

The research participants comprised nurses with expertise in tending to hospitalized patients with CVD and SUDs. Inclusion criteria for nurses involved possessing at least a bachelor’s degree and prior experience in caring for CVD patients with SUDs. Participants were selected for the study through purposive sampling, aiming for maximum diversity to capture a broad spectrum of experiences. Diversity considerations encompassed workplace settings, academic qualifications, gender, age, hospital roles, and caregiving backgrounds. Thirteen nurses took part in the study, comprising nine clinical nurses, one head nurse, two educational supervisors, and one infection control supervisor.

Ethical considerations

This research is part of a larger study. The current research has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Social Welfare and Rehabilitation Sciences (ethics code: IR.USWR.REC.1402.019). Throughout this study, the researcher upheld rigorous ethical standards by ensuring the confidentiality of participants’ names, obtaining explicit permission to record interviews, securing written informed consent for participation, and respecting participants’ autonomy to withdraw from the research at any point during the study.

Data collection tools and technique

Data were gathered through 16 in-depth semistructured interviews. Before the interviews, a meeting was arranged to coordinate the time and location based on the participants’ preferences. Participants were briefed on the research objectives and obtained their written informed consent. The interviews commenced following the interview guide with relevant questions tailored to each participant’s role. For instance, clinical nurses were prompted with, “Please share the factors influencing care for cardiac patients with substance use disorders,” while other participants were asked, “From your experiences, what factors impact care provision for patients with CVD and substance use disorders?” Each interview concluded with an invitation for participants to share any additional insights by asking, “Is there anything else you would like to add?” The interviews were conducted, typically lasting between 45 and 60 min.

Data analysis

In this study, the data underwent analysis using the traditional content analysis approach following Granheim and Lundman’s five-step process. Initially, the interviews were transcribed verbatim, and then carefully reviewed multiple times to grasp their essence fully. Subsequently, the interview text was segmented into meaning units and labeled with corresponding codes. These codes were then compared and organized based on their similarities and differences. The research team deliberated on the identified codes and refined them as necessary. Ultimately, the latent content, representing the underlying meaning within the categories, was synthesized into overarching themes. The interviews were conducted iteratively until no new categories or themes emerged, ensuring a comprehensive conceptualization of the data.[19]

To ensure the Trustworthiness of the data based on Lincoln and Guba’s criteria, namely credibility, dependability or accountability, confirmability, transferability, and authenticity were examined. In this regard, the researcher was present in the research environment for more than a year to collect qualitative data and tried to collect valid information with long-term involvement in conducting interviews. Participants were selected with maximum diversity in terms of education, work experience, gender, and job position. Through a member check process, the initial coding of the interviews was checked by the participants to confirm the accuracy of the codes. Also, for peer review, three faculty members familiar with qualitative research reviewed the codes and categories and agreed on the interpretations. Clear descriptions of the culture and context, participant selection and characteristics, data collection, and analysis process were provided.[1921]

Macro effective conditions

Unsafe work environment

Nurses often cite overwhelming workloads, time constraints, the necessity for constant patient monitoring, and the diversion of attention to administrative duties and documentation rather than direct patient care. Additionally, issues like inadequate training and research on patient care, uninspiring training programs, ineffective curriculum, poor interdepartmental coordination, delayed decision-making, and insufficient performance evaluations of nurses caring for patients are also highlighted.

In this regard, the nurse working in the internal heart ward says: “These patients’ morning, noon, night sleep, and morning wake-up are all stories for us. the nurse involved in this. On the other hand, our work environment is busy.” (p6. 55)

An atmosphere of distrust

The participants perceive a prevailing atmosphere of distrust in the care of patients. This distrust is evident among patients, nurses, and even the patients’ companions, leading to the concealment of information.

In this regard, the clinical nurse working in the cardiac intensive care unit says “They typically stay awake during the night and used to secretly consume.” (p4. 39)

Difficulty in pain management

The challenges and intricacies involved encompassed the requirement for comprehensive treatment across various specialties for these individuals, managing drug interactions and conflicts with close monitoring, addressing drug tolerance issues, recognizing pain behaviors linked to substance use disorder, and addressing the lack of coordination and consistency in the pain management program.

In this regard, the clinical nurse working in the internal heart ward says: “Patients who do not have real heart pain, are very exaggerated. These patients declare Lewin’s sign as a show. (P3. 10)

False cultural beliefs

The last crucial element impacting care is the prevalence of false cultural beliefs among healthcare professionals, including nurses and physicians, as well as the patients themselves. These misconceptions encompass a range of beliefs, specifically those that attribute healing properties to substances in the prevention and treatment of various diseases.

In this regard, the clinical nurse working in the internal heart ward says: Some patients say, it is better if I take opium. Because cardiac drugs have many side effects. (P6. 14)

Micro effective conditions

Nurses attitude

Within this category, there are differing perspectives. Some nurses show compassion and understanding towards individuals with substance use disorder who also have cardiovascular disease, viewing them without judgment or suspicion, while others see them mainly as patients struggling with addiction.

Regarding the positive attitude the clinical nurse working in the cardiac special care ward says: “From a hospital worker’s perspective, individuals entering the hospital are seen as patients in need of care. The focus is on addressing the patient’s health concerns, whether it be addiction, diabetes, obesity, or hypertension. These conditions are viewed as integral aspects of the patient’s overall health profile, and are treated with equal importance and attention as any other medical issue.” (p1. 100)

Some nurses have a negative and judgmental attitude when caring for patients with substance use disorders. They may complain that their job duties are unclear when it comes to providing care for these patients. These nurses often view the patient as morally flawed or weak-willed, believing the patient is faking or exaggerating their pain to obtain narcotics.

Regarding this dimension of attitude, the nurse says: “I have a bad feeling about this patient. I believe that they are trapping themselves in addiction, causing problems for their families, and impacting society negatively.” (P4. 79)

Insufficient knowledge

the experiences of the participants indicate a lack of sufficient expertise among nurses in areas such as drug addiction, rehabilitation, patient emotional well-being, effective communication with patients, drug interactions, and psychological understanding in individuals with mental health conditions.

In this regard, the clinical nurse working in the internal heart ward says: “When encountering them, their current mood remains uncertain. I am unsure of the most effective approach to pacify this individual.” (P7. 59)

Patient behavioral issues

The patients displayed troubling actions that disturbed the ward and endangered themselves and others, as noted by the staff. These behaviors encompassed Aggressive and agitated behavior, ignoring societal norms and ethics, Impolite and disrespectful conduct, Verbally and physically assaulting the nursing staff, causing commotion and disarray in the ward, using substances in unsuitable places, Dispensing, and vending drugs.

the nurse working in the cardiac intensive care unit states: “The patient exhibited frequent rudeness and used obscenities regularly. On one occasion, the patient attempted to strangle me by placing his hand around my neck. In these instances, nurses encounter a lack of safety and security.” (p5. 89)

Discussion

This study aimed to explore the factors affecting the nursing care of CVD patients with substance use disorder. The findings indicate that some of these factors are related to nurses, patients, and caregivers, and some are related to the work environment.

Based on the results of the studies, health professionals, particularly nurses, exhibit more negative attitudes towards patients with substance use disorders than those with other mental health conditions. However, nurses with personal or family experience with substance use disorders, as well as specialized training in treating such patients, tend to have a more positive attitude. They view these patients as individuals in need of care and support, rather than simply as substance abusers. Some studies also highlight the compassionate and dedicated approach of nurses toward patients with substance use disorders.[2223] According to the present study, nurses with personal or family encounters with addiction displayed a more favorable attitude toward such patients. Factors such as patient age, health status, and the substance used influenced nurses’ attitudes. Consequently, many participants showed positivity towards young patients, those with severe health issues, and individuals using substances like opium and methadone.

false cultural beliefs of another category are explored in the study. Based on research findings, rather than addressing the risk factors associated with this condition, individuals with cardiovascular disease often resort to traditional and alternative therapies like opium use to lessen the impact of postdisability.[51224] while, in recent years, multiple studies have highlighted opium consumption as a significant risk factor for disability.[10111224] According to the present study patients and nurses hold numerous inaccurate cultural beliefs about the medicinal and preventive properties of opium. These false beliefs are influential when it comes to patient care, such as educating patients about opium and its uses.

Another category of extractives is insufficient knowledge. Research conducted in several countries, including England, the United States, and Australia, has found that nurses lack the necessary qualifications, training, and competence to deliver high-quality care to certain patient populations. This deficiency in nursing expertise can result in substandard patient care and pose potential risks to the safety and well-being of both patients and healthcare workers.[2526] The study found that nurses reported lacking adequate knowledge about drug interactions and how to effectively communicate with patients and manage their pain. This knowledge gap can contribute to increased stress and mental strain experienced by nurses in their work.

Pain management difficulty is one of the other categories of the present study. Due to drug use and high tolerance to pain relievers, Patients may experience significant pain, leading to an increased requirement for more potent painkillers.[27] Meanwhile, most nurses either doubt the pain of these patients or express concern about the increase in addiction and dependence on painkillers.[28] In the present study, the main concern of nurses was overdose and drug interaction. Therefore, inject distilled water to calm the patients.

patient behavioral issues were extracted in this study. Many nurses have encountered patient aggression during care, leading to feelings of threat and a tendency to work alongside colleagues for security. This vulnerability highlights the need for support when caring for high-risk clients. Despite the perceived danger, it can sometimes result in minimal care and negative perceptions of patient care. It is crucial to remember that these individuals are vulnerable and require care due to both physical and mental health issues.[92930]

An unsafe work environment is among other concepts extracted from the present study. In a recent study, managerial defects, heavy workload of nurses, and structural inconsistencies were among the characteristics of an unsafe work environment. In line with the present study, the evidence shows that nurses express their concern about the legal aspects of caring for these patients. The lack of support in terms of access to a supervisor with high knowledge in the field of substance use disorders, Busy managers, and difficulty accessing more advice and supervision. In addition, there was no support for instructions for decision-making about the use of drugs in the hospitals. This is while, the nursing work environment has a direct impact on patient outcomes, nurse well-being, and the sustainability of the nursing workforce. Nurse managers and healthcare leaders must prioritize creating positive work environments to support nurses and deliver safe, high-quality care.[2831]

The last category was an atmosphere of distrust. A key element in nursing care is mutual trust. Studies show these patients exhibit adeptness in manipulating situations and individuals to their advantage. This lack of mutual trust can escalate negative behaviors, sometimes resulting in conflicts such as verbal or physical aggression towards nurses and healthcare team members. In more severe instances, it can lead to premature discharge, either involuntary or with the patient’s agreement, even before their medical issues are fully addressed.[3233]

Strengths, limitations, and recommendation

The research conducted in Iran is notable for being the only comprehensive investigation into the contextual factors affecting the care of cardiovascular disease (CVD) patients with substance use disorders (SUD). It is important to highlight that this study did not utilize triangulation. Therefore, to improve future research, it is recommended to engage patients and healthcare professionals in the study process. Additionally, researchers are urged to incorporate the findings from this study into the development of protocols and guidelines for upcoming investigations.

Conclusion

Nurses’ experiences demonstrate that various factors can influence the quality of care provided, including characteristics of nurses and patients, the care environment, patient companions, and nursing management practices. Providing health services based on scientific evidence is one of the basic criteria of the health system. Identifying the factors that impact care quality can assist managers, researchers, and nurses in enhancing nursing care by modifying these influential elements.

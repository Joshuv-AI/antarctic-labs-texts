Long-term monitoring dataset of plankton assemblages in western Taiwan coastal water
Authors: Yu-Jia Lin, Tzu-Chun Chen, Chen-Tung Arthur Chen, Saou-Lien Wong, Pei-Jie Meng, Meng-Hsien Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Plankton plays important roles in the marine ecosystems as important producers for primary production, major components in the global biogeochemical cycles, and the foundation of the marine food web. Despite their importance, long-term monitoring data about marine plankton are fairly limited, especially in the Asia-Pacific region. We fill this knowledge gap by providing a 29-year long-term monitoring data on the coastal areas of western Taiwan quarterly from 1993 to 2021. This long-term monitoring data can be used to set proper baseline for detecting human-induced impacts on the plankton, as well as modelling future scenarios under global changes.

Background & Summary

The plankton is passively drifting small plants (phytoplankton) and animals (zooplankton) in aquatic systems and play important roles in the aquatic ecosystems1. They contributed close to a half of the global primary production2, major components of global biogeochemical cycles3, form the foundation of the marine food web as both producers and consumers4,5 and maintaining the stability of the marine ecosystem6. Therefore, knowledge about the marine plankton, particularly focusing on potential impacts of human developments and activities, afford opportunities for researchers to engage with management authorities to support ecosystems-based management and assist in maintaining or achieving good environmental status7–10.

The aquatic ecosystem of Asia-Pacific region has undergone extensive pressures from human development, which led to a rapid loss of biodiversity11. However, long-term monitoring program on marine ecosystems are fairly limited in monitored organisms12,13, and programmes on the marine plankton were few in the coastal areas of the Asia-Pacific region9.

In this study, we fill this knowledge gap by providing a long-term time series dataset of abundance of large plankton groups (phytoplankton, zooplankton, shrimp larvae, crab larvae, fish eggs, and fish larvae) collected by a continuous monitoring program conducted by the same standard protocol over several locations of coastal areas of western Taiwan quarterly from 1993 to 2021. This long-term monitoring data can be used to set proper baseline for detecting human-induced impacts on the plankton8, as well as modelling future scenarios under global changes9.

Methods

The team conducted a quarterly survey of the environmental factors and abundances of plankton on eight transects along the coastline of western Taiwan from 1993 to 2021 (Fig. 1). In each transect sampling was collected at a site with a depth of 10 m and 20 m, respectively. Due to logistic constraints, only transect 3 to 6 were visited continuously each quarter for 26 years. Phytoplankton was collected by collecting 20 litres of seawater at the surface using a standard water sampler. The surface water was filtered by a filter with a mesh size of 55 µm, concentrated to 70 to 100 ml, preserved by adding a few drops of Lugol’s solution and stored in dark-colored glass bottles. Zooplankton was collected by a NORPAC plankton net with a diameter of 45 cm, a length of 180 cm, and a mesh size of 330 µm. The plankton net was towed horizontally just below the sea surface at a speed of not exceeding 3 knots for approximately 5 minutes. A flowmeter was set at the net entrance to measure the water volume filtered. The samples collected by the plankton were preserved on-site using a 5% neutralized formalin solution and stored in the laboratory.

In the laboratory, the numbers of the phytoplankton and zooplankton in the samples were counted and standardized into density (cells m−3) for the phytoplankton and abundance (individuals m−3) for the zooplankton. The crab and shrimp larvae, fish eggs, and fish larvae were further separated from the zooplankton for their importance in the fisheries. Therefore, the zooplankton in the statistical analyses indicate the planktonic animals that did not belong to any of the following categories: crab and shrimp larvae, fish eggs, and fish larvae.

Technical Validation

The data were validated by two approaches: statistical checks and comparison to other independent data. For statistical validation, we examined the distribution of the parameters, approximated by the histograms of the environmental variables, density of the phytoplankton, and abundances of zooplankton, crab and shrimp larvae, fish eggs and fish larvae (Fig. 2). Generally, the parameters were distributed within reasonable ranges (Table 1). For example, the pH value ranged from 7.1 to 8.4, consistent with the range of the coastal waters15. There were only few extreme values in some parameters, such as salinity (8.1‰) and total suspended solid (877 mg × l−1). The natural logarithm of the density and abundance of plankton communities were also generally symmetric or close to symmetric, indicating their similarity to the log-normal distribution, the most useful empirical model for ecological analysis16.

Secondly, we collected the data of surface water collected from similar location and time period (the summer in 2021) by an independent party (SGS Taiwan Ltd) using the same officially approved methodology from the National Ocean Database and Sharing System17. The validation data is also attached in a csv file.

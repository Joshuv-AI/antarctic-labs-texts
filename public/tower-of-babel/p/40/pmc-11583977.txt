CuFe Cooperativity at the Membrane-Electrode Interface Elicits a Tandem 2e–+2e– Mechanism for Exclusive O2-To-H2O Electroreduction
Authors: Tian Zeng, Jiu Chen, Zuo Hang Yu, Edmund C M Tse
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHigh O2 reduction reaction (ORR) kinetics and exclusive 4e– pathway selectivity are keys to realizing a sustainable society. However, nonprecious electrocatalysts at present cannot enhance the ORR turnover frequency and H2O Faradaic efficiency (FE) concurrently. To address these two challenges, hybrid bilayer membrane (HBM) electrodes with earth-abundant metal centers are developed to control proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) in ORR. Here, an oxidase-inspired CuFe active site is supported on a tris(2-pyridylmethyl)amine HBM and explored as a unique interface for efficient ORR. This bimetallic HBM displayed an ORR activity 1.4 times higher than the monometallic systems and exhibited the highest FE for H2O (∼94%) among Cu-, Fe-, Ni-, and Co-based HBMs. Contrary to previous studies where the ORR current decreases upon embedding the metal center in a hydrophobic lipid environment, here, the incorporation of a nitrile-terminated proton carrier at the HBM interface boosts the ORR current by 1.7 folds relative to the case where the catalytic site is directly exposed to protons in solution. This intriguing dual improvement is supported by density function theory calculations where an additional 2e–+2e– mechanism occurs in parallel to the direct 4e– pathway, highlighting the synergistic effect of the CuFe HBM for facilitating high-performance ORR. A Zn-air battery is constructed using this CuFe HBM for the first time, further demonstrating that the knowledge gained from this HBM technology holds practical values in real-life applications. These findings on interfacial PCET are envisioned to spark new design principles for future catalysts with optimal electrochemical properties for advanced energy conversion schemes.

Introduction

Proton-exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFC) and Zn-air batteries (ZAB) offer low-cost and efficient alternatives for chemical-to-electrical energy conversion that could meet future needs from the transportation and portable electronic device sectors.1−6 Both H2-powered PEMFC and ZAB do not produce any emissions with water being the only intended byproduct which is benign to the environment.7,8 Though bypassing the Carnot cycle can reach a theoretical efficiency higher than conventional heat engines,9,10 the slow kinetics of the cathodic oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) hinder the overall PEMFC and ZAB performance and thus impede their widespread deployment.11,12

Besides displaying slow reaction kinetics, ORR involves multiple proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) steps occurring concurrently that complicate the process of deciphering and regulating the ORR mechanism.13,14 Since multielectron multiproton pathways are involved in ORR, decades of effort have been invested to develop catalysts with high H2O selectivity and fast reaction kinetics. State-of-the-art ORR catalysts feature Pt-group materials,15−20 but the high costs associated with these precious metals hinder their widespread adoption.21,22 Recent studies have focused on fabricating earth-abundant replacements such as transition metal oxides, nonprecious chalcogenides, and carbon-based core–shell composites as ORR catalysts.23−31 However, most of these materials struggle to overcome constraints imposed by the scaling relationship, thus preventing the simultaneous optimization of ORR thermodynamics and kinetics.32−34

Hybrid bilayer membrane (HBM) has emerged as a platform technology to regulate the availability and flux of protons and electrons independently to an embedded catalytic moiety.35−40 HBM consists of a lipid layer covering on top of a self-assembled monolayer (SAM) with a terminal electrocatalytic active site.41−43 By incorporating 1-dodecylboronic acid (DBA) as a proton carrier within the lipid layer, a previous HBM has been shown to modulate proton and electron pathways of ORR simultaneously, thereby regulating the ORR product selectivity without covalent alteration of the molecular structure of the electrocatalyst.41 HBMs can also be repurposed as pH-sensitive proton switches that turn on in acid by incorporating mono-n-dodecylphosphate (MDP) as the proton carrier as well as in base by utilizing tridecanenitrile (C12CN) as the proton carrier, respectively.44,45 These proton carriers have been demonstrated to undergo “flip-flop” diffusion to deliver protons efficiently across the membrane to the HBM-embedded catalyst.46 Beyond Au-supported HBM systems, carbon-based HBM platforms have been assembled to explore electrochemical reactions occurring at more reducing potentials, further demonstrating the versatility of this HBM approach in delineating the thermodynamics and kinetics effects of electrochemical processes.47−49

Irrespective of the chosen electrode support, the active sites of catalysts in HBM systems for ORR are confined to two first-row transition metals, namely Cu and Fe.37,44,50 In previous Au-electrode supported HBM systems, a SAM based on benzyltriazothiol (BTT) has been developed.44,51 This ligand binding site can form a Cu2BTT2 complex with its structure analogous to one of the most efficient artificial dicopper molecular ORR catalysts.44,52,53 Due to its binuclear nature, precise positioning of the BTT ligand is crucial for facilitating proper binding between the triazole ring and Cu ions to assemble into an active ORR site.54 However, the stringent constraints on proper ligand coordination for complex formation hinder the incorporation of other transition metal ions into the BTT motif.53 Consequently, this restriction on catalyst choice has limited the investigation of the use of other metal ions in HBMs to enhance ORR performance.55,56

To overcome the challenge of incorporating a wide range of first-row transition metal ions into HBMs, a ligand framework composed of tris(2-pyridylmethyl)amine (TPA) has been explored.57−63 In previous studies, [M(TPA)]2+ (M = Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu) complexes were synthesized and investigated as oxygen reduction catalysts. These triflate complexes show selective four-electron oxygen reduction (Faradaic efficiency (FE) for H2O2 < 28%), and the Fe complex displayed a considerable selectivity for water (FE for H2O = 89%), comparable to state-of-the-art molecular catalysts.64 While in nature, multiunit membrane enzymes in the family of heme/Cu terminal oxidases (e.g., cytochrome c oxidase65−68 and ubiquinol oxidase67) show impressive O2-to-H2O reduction performance, and the catalytic site of terminal oxidases is bimetallic in nature. Hence, TPA holds the potential to bind first-row transition metals to form versatile catalytic moieties that mimic dual catalytic centers in nature.

In this work, an HBM system supported on a Au surface is developed as a functional mimic of redox-active metalloenzymes with molecularly precise electron transport chains and structurally sophisticated proton transfer channels. This HBM features a bottom layer comprising a mixed SAM of TPA with an alkylthiolate arm (TPAHT) and diluent alkanethiol that modulates electron transfer kinetics (Figure 1). The top layer of the HBM consists of 1,2-dimyristoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DMPC) with catalytic amounts of proton carriers incorporated into the lipid layer to regulate transmembrane proton transfer rates. This bioinspired TPAHT-HBM system is subsequently employed to evaluate the impact of proton delivery rates on ORR catalytic efficiency and product distribution in HBMs containing Ni, Co, Cu, and Fe active sites. Based on the results from the monometallic HBM studies, bimetallic active sites are tailored to further boost the ORR turnover frequency and selectivity for H2O. The insights derived from this study hold promise for the design of nonprecious metal electrocatalysts with favorable ORR performance and desirable H2O selectivity.

Results and Discussion

Preparation and Characterization of Hybrid Bilayer Membranes

6-((6-((bis(pyridin-2-ylmethyl)amino)methyl)pyridin-2-yl)methoxy)hexane-1-thiol (TPAHT) is designed and synthesized as a metal-chelating site in the SAM of a HBM (see Note S1 for the synthesis route and characterization data of TPAHT). TPAHT has two major functional units. First, the terminal group of TPAHT (blue background in Figure 2) is tris(2-pyridylmethyl)amine (TPA), which is a ligand with favorable affinity for first-row transition metals to form a 1:1 metal-TPA complex. The TPA unit is situated at the SAM-lipid interface for metal ion coordination to form the target ORR catalytic site. Second, the hexane-1-thiol (HT) arm in TPAHT (green background in Figure 2) facilitates the self-assembly of TPAHT on Au electrode via Au–S bond formation (orange background in Figure 2) while supporting fast electron transfer between the Au electrode and the metal-TPA moiety.

HBM platform construction protocol. Multistep preparation scheme of HBM electrochemical interface by adding butane-1-thiol (BT) as a diluent to achieve a stable mixed SAM featuring a nonprecious metal electrocatalyst for enhancing oxygen reduction reaction O2 reduction reaction (ORR) activity and regulating ORR product distribution.

During the SAM preparation process, butane-1-thiol (BT) is added as a diluent that is short enough to not interfere with the metal coordination site while displaying a suitable length to enable the formation of a well-packed SAM. The combination of TPAHT and BT diluent with a TPAHT/BT ratio of 1:10 yields a mixed SAM where the resulting metal-TPA complex can act as a catalytic center for O2 reduction, thereby overcoming the deficiency in previous HBM systems where a pair of BTT is needed for active site assembly. With this advancement in SAM design with terminal metal-TPA moieties, the HBM interface containing a wide variety of transition metal ions can be constructed regardless of the orientation of the bottom SAM. A monolayer of 1,2-dimyristoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DMPC) lipid with or without catalytic amounts of proton carriers is appended on top of the metal-TPAHT/BT mixed SAM to form an HBM platform. Cholesterol is further embedded in the lipid layer in this study to improve the stability of the DMPC membrane. The detailed process of constructing HBMs incorporated with membrane-associated proton carriers used in this study is shown in Figure 2. The chemical composition of the HBM systems is characterized using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). High-resolution XPS spectra shown in Figure 3 and Note S2 verify the presence of C, N, O, Cu, Fe, Ni, and Co in the HBM systems of interest.

ORR Catalytic Activity and Selectivity of SAMs and HBMs

The ORR performances of TPAHT SAM and HBM systems with their embedded catalytic moieties coordinated with Cu2+, Fe3+, Ni2+, and Co2+ are investigated in pH 7 sodium phosphate buffer at room temperature (Figure 4). Blue lines in Figure 4 represent the current densities of Cu-TPAHT, Fe-TPAHT, Ni-TPAHT, and Co-TPAHT SAMs, while red lines represent the cases with metal-TPAHT SAMs covered by a DMPC layer with 20% cholesterol. Green, yellow, and purple lines represent the cases with metal-TPAHT SAMs covered by a cholesterol/DMPC layer with one equivalence of C12CN, DBA, and MDP incorporated into the lipid layer, respectively. The ORR performances of unmetalated TPAHT HBM controls are shown in Note S3.

ORR linear sweep voltammograms (LSVs) in O2-saturated (solid lines) and N2-saturated (dotted lines) pH 7 phosphate buffer at a scan rate of 10 mV/s. (a) Cu-TPAHT, (b) Fe-TPAHT, (c) Ni-TPAHT, and (d) Co-TPAHT SAM (blue) with a monolayer of DMPC mixed with 20% cholesterol appended on top (red), with C12CN (green), DBA (orange), and MDP (magenta) incorporated in the lipid layer. The current densities shown here are normalized by the number of metal sites in HBMs.

Cu-TPAHT SAM (blue line in Figure 4a) and Co-TPAHT SAM (blue line in Figure 4d) display ORR current densities of (178.8 ± 5.4) and (156.2 ± 10.0) × 102 A/cm2 molcat. at 0.137 V vs RHE, while Fe-TPAHT SAM (blue line in Figure 4b) and Ni-TPAHT SAM (blue line in Figure 4c) show lower ORR current densities of (97.5 ± 11.4) × 102 A/cm2 molcat. and (85.0 ± 1.9) × 102 A/cm2 molcat., respectively. These results indicate that Cu-TPAHT and Co-TPAHT SAMs are intrinsically more active (by ∼45%) at catalyzing the ORR than Ni-TPAHT and Fe-TPAHT SAMs. After appending a lipid monolayer mixed with 20% cholesterol on top of the corresponding SAMs, the ORR current densities of all four cases drop below 60 × 102 A/cm2 molcat. (red lines in Figure 4), and the integrity of the four DMPC/cholesterol layers were verified by the blocking assay using K3[Fe(CN)6] as a probe in Note S4. These results corroborate that proton transport across the lipid layer is restricted by the hydrophobic nature of the lipid tail region of the DMPC membrane. The HBMs containing one equivalence of C12CN (green lines in Figure 4) show current densities less than their corresponding SAM systems but higher than their respective HBMs with DMPC/cholesterol, demonstrating that the ORR activities of the underlying metal-TPAHT can be restored partially but not fully in the presence of C12CN as a proton transfer agent. Raman spectroscopy results in Note S5 demonstrate that C12CN incorporated in the lipid membrane does not coordinate with the catalytic moieties inside HBMs, in agreement with a previous report that C12CN delivers protons to the HBM-embedded metal sites via “flip-flop” diffusion.45 The HBMs containing one equivalence of C12CN further show ORR current densities higher than the other two HBMs with DBA (orange lines in Figure 4) and MDP (magenta lines in Figure 4), signifying that the proton transfer ability of C12CN is superior to DBA and MDP at pH 7. The ORR performances of HBM systems with MDP and DBA are similar to the DMPC/cholesterol cases with relatively low current densities at ∼60 × 102 A/cm2 molcat., indicating that transmembrane proton delivery is not recovered by MDP and DBA. These results are consistent with previous findings that DBA and MDP are ineffective at assisting proton translocation across lipid layer under neutral conditions.41,44,69

Two trends can be summarized from the ORR activity study. First, the SAM cases exhibit the highest ORR current densities for each respective metal ions, while the DMPC only cases display the lowest ORR activities. The unhindered diffusion of protons from the buffer to the metal-TPAHT catalytic center allows for high ORR activities when compared to the lipid-only HBMs that inhibit protons from permeating through the hydrophobic DMPC membrane. Second, the addition of catalytic amounts of membrane-bound C12CN revives the ORR current densities up to 80% of the SAM cases. This result corroborates that C12CN in HBMs exhibits a similar proton transport function as the peptide backbone that forms proton channels in metalloenzymes. These intriguing trends prompt subsequent studies on how transmembrane proton transfer can impact other key aspects of ORR in HBM systems.

In addition to studying ORR activity, the ORR selectivity of the four metal-TPAHT HBMs was also investigated using a colorimetric assay (Note S6 and Figure S10). The main products of O2 reduction are O2–, H2O2, and H2O, which are generated via a 1e–, 2e–, and 4e– pathway, respectively. The O2– species further undergoes disproportionation to generate O2 and H2O2, resulting in an overall consumption of 2e– indirectly that cannot be distinguished straightforwardly from the direct 2e– pathway. The number of electrons consumed per O2 is utilized to evaluate the preferred pathways in HBMs, and the equations used to calculate FE and the number of electrons consumed per O2 by the HBM systems as a proxy of the ratio of O2 reduced via various ORR pathways can be found in Note S7. The closer the number of electrons consumed per O2 is to 4, the more O2 is reduced to water via the 4e– pathway. Alternatively, the closer the number of electrons consumed per O2 is to 2, the more O2 is reduced to H2O2 via the 2e– pathway.

The electrons consumed per O2 of the four metal-TPAHT SAM and HBM platforms are shown in Figure 5. Figure 5 shows that the values of the SAM cases (blue bars) are larger than those of the DMPC with 20% cholesterol cases (red bars) in all four metal-TPAHT systems. The results further show that Cu-TPAHT and Co-TPAHT SAMs consume more than 3 electrons per O2, while Ni-TPAHT and Fe-TPAHT SAMs consume less than 3 electrons per O2. These results indicate that the catalytic sites composed of Cu and Co with higher ORR activities prefer to generate water via the 4e– pathway, while Ni-TPAHT and Fe-TPAHT with lower ORR activities are inclined to undergo the 2e– pathway to generate H2O2. In addition, the e– consumed per O2 by HBMs containing DMPC with 20% cholesterol (red bars) are all close to 2, matching results from previous studies that the lipid layer impedes transmembrane proton diffusion and subsequently results in proton-deficient environments at the SAM-lipid interface.41,44−46 The number of electrons consumed per O2 for all conditions in O2-saturated pH 7 solution increases by varying degrees upon the incorporation of proton carriers into the lipid monolayer (green bars) when compared to the lipid-only HBM cases (red bars).

Comparison of ORR pathways in monometallic HBMs. Electron consumed per O2 during ORR in O2-saturated pH 7 buffer solution of metal-TPAHT SAMs (blue), HBMs with DMPC mixed with 20% cholesterol (red), HBMs with C12CN (light green), DBA (green), and MDP (dark green) incorporated in the lipid layer.

Notably, for HBMs with proton carriers, the number of electrons consumed per O2 for HBMs containing 1 equivalence of C12CN (light green bars) is higher than those of the other two proton carriers (medium green bars for DBA and dark green bars for MDP), meaning that O2 is more likely to be reduced to water via the 4e– pathway in the presence of C12CN under neutral conditions. DBA and MDP do not function well as proton transfer agents in neutral conditions because they require preprotonation before “flip-flop” diffusion while C12CN undergoes a water-assisted transfer mechanism via hydrogen bonding.41,44,69 Intriguingly, incorporating C12CN into HBMs can enhance the selectivity for H2O of Cu-TPAHT, Ni-TPAHT, and Fe-TPAHT to levels beyond their respective SAMs (Cu: 3.3 → 3.4; Ni: 2.5 → 3.0; Fe: 2.9 → 3.5). These results demonstrate that by tuning the microenvironment surrounding the electrocatalyst without covalent modification of the molecular structure of the active site, the number of electrons consumed per O2 by metal complexes can exceed their intrinsic values, thereby generating H2O as the major product over H2O2 as the side product. More importantly, Fe-TPAHT HBM containing C12CN displays the highest electron consumed per O2 value (3.5, i.e. 84% FE for H2O and 16% FE for H2O2). Fe-TPAHT SAM generates less H2O than Cu-TPAHT, but upon tailoring the proton transfer rate via the inclusion of C12CN in the lipid layer, Fe-TPAHT HBM overtakes Cu-TPAHT as the most selective ORR catalyst in HBMs in terms of H2O selectivity.

Taken together, Cu-TPAHT SAM displays the highest ORR activity, while Fe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN shows the highest selectivity for H2O among the four first-row transition metals studied. By increasing the proton transfer rate, the catalytic efficiency for the reduction of O2 into H2O via the 4e– pathway can be boosted in all cases regardless of the identity of the metal used. These results suggest that fine adjustment in proton transfer kinetics across the hybrid electrochemical interface encompassing the metal active center can govern both the ORR activity and product selectivity of nonprecious metal catalysts.

Synergy between Dual-Metal Catalytic Sites Boosts O2 Reduction Catalytic Activity

Inspired by enzymatic active sites that feature two different metals such as heme-copper oxidase, a heterobimetallic TPAHT system is constructed to investigate whether synergy between two metals could enhance catalytic activity and selectivity concomitantly beyond the single metal cases. Since Cu-TPAHT SAM yields the highest ORR current density while Fe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN shows the highest preference for the 4e– pathway, a binary combination of Cu and Fe is then adopted during the metalation step to form a CuFe-TPAHT SAM, a CuFe-TPAHT HBM containing DMPC with 20% cholesterol, and a CuFe-TPAHT HBM containing DMPC with 20% cholesterol and 1 equivalence of C12CN.

Electrochemical studies are then conducted to investigate whether Cu and Fe could display synergistic effects to boost ORR performance. Figure 6a shows that the CuFe-DPAHT HBM containing DMPC with cholesterol (red line) shows low ORR activity and low preference for H2O, analogous to the single metal cases. Interestingly, the ORR current density of the CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (green line) exceeds that of the CuFe-TPAHT SAM (blue line), which is a phenomenon that has not been observed before across all published HBM studies in the literature.35 In previous monometallic systems, the ORR current densities of the SAM cases are always higher than the HBM counterparts.41,44,45 Here, for the first time, the bimetallic CuFe-DPAHT HBM displays a higher ORR current density than its corresponding SAM. Quantitative analysis of the metal content in Cu-, Fe- and CuFe-TPAHT HBMs by ICP-MS shows that the difference in ORR performance between the binary and monomeric metal cases does not originate from a change in the number of active sites (Note S8).

(b) Electron consumed per O2 in O2-saturated pH 7 buffer solution of CuFe-TPAHT SAM (blue), with a monolayer of DMPC mixed with 20% cholesterol (red), with C12CN incorporated in the lipid layer (green).

To interrogate the origin of the extra ORR current density observed (the yellow shaded region) for CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN relative to the SAM case, a kinetic isotope effect (KIE) study is then performed to probe the involvement of protons during ORR catalyzed by CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN in O2-saturated pD 7 buffer prepared using D2O (green dashed line in Figure 6a). The KIE result shows that CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN in pD 7 shows an ORR current density even lower than that in pH 7. This observation is consistent with previous HBM systems where a deuteron-bonded network is stronger than a hydrogen-bonded network, thereby lowering the proton transfer rate from C12CN to the CuFe-TPAHT catalytic motif.45 Since a huge decrease in ORR current density is observed upon switching from H2O to D2O, H+ is likely involved at or before the rate-determining step (RDS). The integrity of the lipid monolayer is verified before and after ORR by a blocking assay using K3[Fe(CN)6] as a redox probe. Figure S12a shows the CVs of K3[Fe(CN)6] of the CuFe-TPAHT SAM (blue line), CuFe-TPAHT HBM with DMPC (red) and CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (green) after ORR. CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN lacks the redox wave of K3[Fe(CN)6], corroborating that the lipid monolayer containing C12CN is not damaged after ORR. This result suggests that leakage of protons across the lipid layer through the pore mechanism during ORR is unlikely. In other words, the enhanced ORR current densities observed for CuFe-TPAHT HBM are likely associated with the enhanced proton transfer kinetics due to the transmembrane delivery action of C12CN. Figures S12b and S12c further demonstrate that the HBM electrode can retain its integrity after six consecutive O2 reduction cycles.

The synergistic effect between the dual CuFe site in HBM is further investigated. Figure S13a further compares the current densities among the Cu-, Fe-, and CuFe-TPAHT systems. The results show that the current density of CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (188.6 ± 15.5 × 102 A/cm2 molcat) is higher than those of Cu-TPAHT and Fe-TPAHT counterparts in O2-saturated pH 7 buffer. In addition to displaying the highest ORR current density, CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN also exhibits the most positive ORR onset potential (0.65 ± 0.03 V vs RHE) among the three systems tested (Figure S13a). The minimal overpotential observed indicates that the ORR thermodynamics is most favorable when Cu and Fe synergistically catalyze ORR in a HBM with C12CN. In addition, Figure 6b shows that the electron consumed per O2 of CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN increases to (3.7 ± 0.2), which is superior to the values of CuFe-TPAHT SAM and CuFe-TPAHT HBM with DMPC, as well as higher than that of Fe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (Figure S13b). The ORR onset potentials of metal-TPAHT HBMs are summarized in Figure S14, and these comparative results corroborate that the 4e– pathway to generate water is the main ORR pathway for CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN at pH 7.

We next investigate the origin of the enhanced ORR activity of CuFe-TPAHT HBM in the presence of C12CN (Figure 6a, yellow-shaded region). If there is no crosstalk between the Cu and Fe sites, the ORR activity of the CuFe system is predicted to be equal to the direct summation of the ORR activities of the noninteracting monometallic cases based on their individual Cu and Fe amounts on the electrode surface. The projected ORR current density of the mixture of Cu-TPAHT HBM with C12CN and Fe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (scaled by the number of Cu and Fe sites quantified by ICP-MS) is significantly lower than the experimental ORR current density of CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN (Figure S15), indicating that CuFe bimetallic sites catalyze the ORR in a synergistic fashion. As shown in Figure S16, CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN exhibits the best ORR performance with its FE reaching 94.0% ± 4.1%. Compared with the DMPC case, the current density increases by 75%, and the FE also increases by 42%. These results provide evidence that the CuFe active center displays superior ORR attributes. The trends obtained from partial ORR current densities of O2-to-H2O in Figure S17e are similar to the trends obtained from total ORR current densities in Figure 6a, indicating that the extra ORR current density obtained in the CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN can be mainly attributed to the generation of H2O via the 4e– pathway. Taken together, the synergy between the dual CuFe site endows an HBM with the highest ORR catalytic activity and selectivity for H2O among the systems studied in this work.

An additional ORR pathway is hypothesized to occur in parallel to the 2e– and 4e– pathways to rationalize the unprecedented ORR performance exhibited by CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN. As postulated in Figure 7b, the dual CuFe catalytic site could further reduce the peroxo intermediate generated from a 2e– pathway into H2O via an additional downstream 2e– pathway, resulting in an overall 2e–+2e– cascade (red curved arrow) that could explain the extra ORR activity observed. Figure S18 displays the H2O2 reduction current densities of CuFe-TPAHT SAM and HBMs evaluated in N2-saturated pH 7 buffer with 10 mM H2O2 added at a scan rate of 10 mV/s. H2O2 is known to permeate the lipid monolayer readily, and previous HBM studies have shown that the RDS of H2O2 reduction is not the transmembrane diffusion of H2O2 to the SAM-lipid interface.70 The H2O2 reduction current density of a CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN is not only higher than that of the DMPC-only case but also exceeds that of the SAM case. These results suggest that CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN has the catalytic capability to reduce H2O2 efficiently, thereby explaining the extra ORR current density observed due to the downstream reduction of peroxo intermediate produced from the 2e– route to enable an overall sequential 2e–+2e– pathway in parallel to the direct 4e– ORR process.

Proton is transferred to the HBM-embedded active sites by C12CN via a “flip-flop” mechanism.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were conducted to further unravel the reaction mechanism of O2 electroreduction over CuFe-TPAHT HBMs with C12CN. We evaluate both the end-on and side-on binding modes of O2 along with all plausible spin states to obtain optimal adsorption structures of O2 adducts on Cu and Fe active sites. As shown in Figure 8a, the energy-minimized adsorption structures favor the side-on binding mode of O2 in all cases. Compared to the cases with dual-metal active sites (Cu–Cu, Fe–Fe, Cu–Fe), O2 binds more favorably to a single Cu or Fe site with a corresponding adsorption energy of 0.38 and 0.32 eV at 0 K, respectively. The bound O2 species can be selectively reduced into H2O2 via a 2e– pathway or H2O by a 4e– pathway after *OOH protonation, as shown in Figure S19. This thermodynamic selectivity of 2e– and 4e– pathway is determined by the change of free energy between the product H2O2 and *O + H2O. Figure S19a shows that the lower energy of H2O2 product relative to *O + H2O in the single Cu active site case indicates that the Cu center prefers to generate H2O2 via the 2e– pathway with an overpotential of 0.64 eV for the first step to protonate an adsorbed O2 species (Figure S19a yellow line). On the contrary, the single Fe center energetically tends to follow the 4e– pathway to form H2O due to the lower free energy of *O + H2O than the route for H2O2 byproduct formation (Figure S19b red line). In this 4e– process, the potential limiting step of the single Fe case is the first protonation step of an adsorbed O2 species with an overpotential of 0.78 eV, which involves a higher thermodynamic penalty than the single Cu case.

(b) Energy-minimized ORR profiles of Cu (blue dotted line) and Fe (green dashed line) systems. (c) Energy-minimized ORR profiles of CuFe relay pathway (red thick line).

For the system with the copresence of Fe and Cu catalytic centers, the individual Fe site and the individual Cu site still follow the 4e– and 2e– ORR pathway, respectively (Figure 8b green dashed line and blue dotted lines). The H2O2 selectively generated by Cu desorbs from the Cu site due to its weak binding interaction with Cu (0.74 eV, Figure S19c). The freshly desorbed H2O2 is subsequently captured by an adjacent Fe site, which has a strong adsorption affinity for H2O2 (0.04 eV, Figure S19d). The H2O2 newly bound on the Fe site is then protonated into a Fe–OH intermediate with a favorable driving force (ΔG = −2.63 eV, Figure 8c red thick line). Fe–OH is further reduced to H2O via a 2e– pathway, and the Fe complex is regenerated in its resting state. This overall 4e– O2-to-H2O pathway encompasses the first 2e– O2-to-H2O2 pathway catalyzed by the Cu site and the second 2e– H2O2-to-H2O pathway catalyzed by the Fe site. As a result, this unique tandem 2eCu–+2eFe– mechanism is exclusive to the bimetallic CuFe-TPAHT HBMs, which feature neighboring Cu and Fe sites to drive two separate 2e– transfer steps in a contiguous manner while the single Fe site can also facilitate the direct 4eFe– ORR process simultaneously. This theoretical simulation analysis supports the additional current density observed experimentally on dual-site CuFe HBMs.

As a proof of concept, the ability of HBM to regulate proton transfer kinetics in Zn-air batteries (ZABs) is next examined. Cu-TPAHT, Fe-TPAHT, and CuFe-TPAHT HBMs with C12CN are constructed as cathodes in ZAB prototypes. Figure S20 shows that CuFe-TPAHT HBM ZAB displays the highest power density with the highest electrochemical robustness among the three HBM ZABs tested. The HBM-based ZAB performance trend matches the ORR activity trend recorded using 3-electrode systems shown in Figure S13a, corroborating that HBMs not only can regulate proton transfer kinetics at model interfaces, but HBMs can also enhance ORR efficiency in practical scenarios like ZAB. This first demonstration of the HBM system in ZAB lays a promising foundation for the downstream translation of the knowledge gained from HBM studies in a wide variety of electrochemical applications. The versatility of HBM technology to modulate proton and electron transfer thermodynamics and kinetics simultaneously with high precision has direct implications in overcoming the activity-selectivity-durability trilemma of PCET processes that are instrumental to advancing the energy catalysis field.

Conclusions

In this report, HBMs containing Cu-, Fe-, Ni-, Co-, and CuFe-TPAHT SAMs are constructed to study the metal identity and synergistic effect on ORR activity and selectivity. Cu, Fe, Ni, and Co display similar trends where their SAMs display the highest ORR current density while the DMPC-only cases result in the lowest ORR current density. The addition of proton carriers such as C12CN in the lipid membrane revives the ORR current density of the HBM cases to about 80% of the SAM cases. Among the monometallic systems studied, Cu-TPAHT SAM exhibits the highest ORR activity, while Fe-TPAHT HBM exhibits the highest selectivity for the 4e– pathway to generate H2O as the major product. These results together suggest that the catalytic efficiency of active sites at the hybrid electrochemical interface is regulated by the rate of proton transfer in a manner similar to how the electron–proton transfer pathway through the peptide backbone governs the activity of metalloenzymes.

Building upon the findings from monometallic systems, heterobimetallic SAMs and HBMs are further constructed. For the first time, an HBM with a dual CuFe active site is assembled to exhibit an ORR current density higher than the SAM counterpart. The extra ORR current density observed for CuFe-TPAHT HBM with C12CN originates from an additional mechanism where a sequential reduction of the peroxo intermediate produced during the partial O2 reduction process by the bimetallic CuFe site is made possible within an HBM construct. This synergy between the dual CuFe active site thereby bestows both direct 4e– and indirect 2e–+2e– pathways to occur simultaneously at the HBM-electrode interface without covalent modification of the molecular structure of the catalytic moiety. This phenomenon is substantiated by theoretical calculations. Relative free energies from DFT provide evidence that the Cu site favors the 2e– reduction of O2 into H2O2, an intermediate that is further reduced by a nearby Fe site via a downstream 2e– step into H2O.

Beyond boosting the ORR activity and selectivity in model systems, the cooperativity between the dual CuFe active site and the proton regulation by C12CN cocatalyst endows HBM as an efficient and robust cathode in ZAB under operating conditions. Taken together, this work not only demonstrates the utility of synergistic dual-metal active sites toward optimizing the ORR performance of nonprecious electrocatalysts but also provides a feasible design strategy to govern the thermodynamics and kinetics of electron and proton transfer processes for regulating the reaction products of multistep PCET processes that are central to renewable energy conversion technology and sustainable resourcification schemes.

Rheumatic Heart Disease
Authors: Tabitha G Moe
Published: 2024

The global burden of rheumatic heart disease (RHD) remains a significant barrier to cardiovascular health worldwide. RHD affects the pericardium, epicardium, myocardium, and endocardium as well as the conduction system. Although valvulitis is a prominent manifestation, it may or may not be present, and its absence does not preclude the diagnosis of RHD. As we learn together in an increasingly global community about the care and management of RHD, elevating awareness by promoting collaborative strategies is needed. Highlighting unique approaches in diversely resourced communities, individual struggles, and novel solutions is the goal with this special issue of JACC: Case Reports, with a focus on features specific to RHD.

We had a dramatic and enthusiastic response from around the world to the call for cases for this special issue. Included in this issue are cases from every continent and from many countries, some with a high burden of endemic RHD and few resources, as well as from other countries with more than adequate resources but underrecognition of RHD and its sequelae. Acute rheumatic fever must be recognized, diagnosed, and treated promptly to avoid the long-term complications of RHD. Long-term management of RHD with penicillin G benzathine remains the preferred treatment because this agent is highly effective, has a low cost, and is readily available. Compliance with therapy, of course, remains key to preventing long-term sequelae. Although mitral valve stenosis may be the most common form of valvular heart disease, many patients with rheumatic mitral stenosis report no previous history consistent with acute rheumatic fever. This lack of reported history is suggestive of underrecognition, subclinical presentations, or both. With each successive group A streptococcal infection, the risk of rheumatic heart disease worsens. There are many global organizations focusing their efforts on reducing the burden of RHD. The World Heart Federation released its most recent echocardiographic guidelines for the diagnosis of RHD in 2023 and described RHD as an important and preventable cause of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality among children and adolescents in low- and middle-income countries.

RHD is a contributor to high-risk pregnancies worldwide and is a cause of both maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality.1 Mobilizing care for these, our most vulnerable, patients is a challenge we must undertake as a global heart care community. Reaching those patients in rural and low-resourced communities must be a priority. RHD remains the leading cause of acquired heart and vascular disease in low- and middle-income countries. The growing use of inexpensive, mobile, hand-held echocardiography devices and education of community care providers are among the many tools that can increasingly be used in network strategies to improve risk awareness. As with all forms of acquired heart disease, more research is needed. We need better understanding of the mechanisms driving valvular disease, as well as options for intervention both percutaneous and surgical. Early diagnosis and treatment of acute rheumatic carditis, and ongoing compliance with treatment for secondary RHD, remain a charge to the global heart care team. We hope that by highlighting a variety of presentations and management strategies, this special issue of JACC: Case Reports will promote thoughtful conversation about what we can do as a global cardiovascular community to reduce the burden of RHD on cardiovascular health.

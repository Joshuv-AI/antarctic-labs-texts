Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on admission trends, diagnosis patterns, and demographics of ischemic heart disease patients: a retrospective study
Authors: Seyedeh Maryam Hosseininezhad, Mohammad Taghi Moghadamnia, Maryam Gholami, Parivash Nazarpour, Ghazaleh Baakaram Maryan, Saman Maroufizadeh, Saeed Ahmadi, Seyedeh Fatemeh Hosseininezhad, Arsalan Salari, Marzieh Jahani Sayad Noveiri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and aimRegarding the impact of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) and the significance of controlling its spread and also due to the knowledge of the type of demographic characteristics during the Covid outbreak, investigating the cases affected by this condition in the course of its outbreak helps handle multiple critical situations.MethodsThis retrospective study with a cross-sectional analytical research design was conducted on the statistical population of all admitted patients during the study period with the primary diagnosis of IHD, admitted to Dr. Heshmat Teaching Hospital in Rasht, Gilan Province, Iran, from early February to late April 2020. The samples were selected using the convenience sampling technique through a census, the medical records, and the information completed according to the data collection form. The data analysis was performed using the SPSS Statistics (ver.16.0) software. All the statistical tests were also two-tailed, considering the significance level 0.05.ResultsIn the COVID-19 period group, there were more females (45.4% vs. 40.2%, P = 0.019) as well as older patients (62 [IQR 53–71] vs. 61 [IQR 53–70], P = 0.048) compared to the pre-COVID-19 period group. In general, the proportion of patients with current smoking (13.0% vs. 24.4%, P < 0.000), opium consumption (7.2% vs. 14.7%, P < 0.000), and alcohol consumption (0.8% vs. 2.7%, P < 0.001) was lower in the COVID-19 period group compared to the pre-COVID-19 period group.The study findings revealed that the frequency of daily admissions after the COVID-19 peak outbreaks had been significantly higher as compared to those before the pandemic (17.5 [IQR 13.25-22] vs. 12 [IQR 8-19.75], p < 0.001). As well, the in-hospital mortality rates during COVID-19 had been significantly higher than those in 2019, before this condition (4.8% vs. 2.4%, p = 0.004).ConclusionGiven the increasing number of patients admitted during the COVID-19 period, clinical care of patients with IHD, as well as its management and outcomes, becomes more important. It was suggested that these patients be educated about telephone and online services.

Problem statement

The COVID-19 outbreak, caused by SARS-CoV-2, began in 2020 and significantly altered daily life worldwide, leading to a high number of confirmed cases and deaths [1]. The World Health Organization first declared COVID-19 a public health emergency on January 30, 2020, and a global pandemic in March 2020 [2] During the outbreak, countries with the first identified cases began to implement health and quarantine laws at the national level [3]. The transmission of the new coronavirus is through respiratory secretions to the environment and to other people. As the virus affects the respiratory system, coughing and sneezing reactions lead to the release of saliva droplets or secretions from the mouth and nose. Therefore, observing hygiene behaviors is important [4]. It should be noted that, According to statistics from the World Health Organization (WHO), the number of cases of the disease has exceeded 213 million people and 4.5 million deaths have been reported [5].

COVID-19 also drastically influenced the pathogenesis and treatment of other diseases [6]. As evidenced in many studies, this condition has so far led to considerable consequences in patients with ischemic heart disease (IHD). As well, contracting COVID-19 has been found to exacerbate heart problems and then give rise to abundant cardiovascular (CV) complications, such as acute myocardial infarction (AMI), heart failure(HF), arrhythmia, cardiac arrest, acute coronary syndrome (ACS), myocarditis and venous thromboembolism (VTE) [7–10]. COVID-19 can cause damage to the cardiovascular system through various mechanisms, including direct invasion of cardiac inflammatory response, coagulation disorders, hypoxia, and electrolyte disturbances [11–15]. In patients with ischemic heart disease, the mortality rate from COVID-19 has been reported to be 27% higher than in other patients [16].

In other ways, patients with the primary diagnosis of heart attacks during the COIVID-19 peak outbreak have faced some delays in receiving healthcare services as a result of taking some measures, such as practicing diagnostic tests for COVID-19 before transferring them to the Cardiac Catheterization Unit, isolating the cases with positive COVID-19 test results, and wearing personal protective equipment (PPE) by physicians and other healthcare providers (HCPs), which have been among the effective factors, as compared to the same time period before the pandemic [17, 18]. As stated in some studies, the activities in the Cardiac Catheterization Units in the United States and Spain had been respectively associated with a 38% and 40% drop all through the first wave of COVID-19, as compared to the same time before it. Therefore, the reduction in the number of patients treated after heart attacks could predict the increase in complications and mortality rates [16, 17]. Considering the impact of COVID-19 and the significance of controlling its spread, investigating the cases affected by this condition and the frequency distribution of those with IHD in the course of its peak outbreak is of assistance to healthcare facilities in order to provide the relevant services and handle many critical situations. With regard to the high number of patients with COVID-19 in northern Iran and no survey on IHD frequency distribution and diagnosis categories during the red state of the pandemic in Guilan Province, Iran, the present study was to determine the frequency distribution, primary diagnosis categories, and demographic characteristics in the patients with IHD, admitted to Dr. Heshmat Teaching Hospital, a specialized center for the treatment of heart diseases in the city of Rasht, Guilan Province, Iran, within two time periods, namely before and after the COVID-19 peak outbreak.

Methods

This retrospective study with a cross-sectional analytical research design was conducted on the statistical population of all admitted patients during the study period with the primary diagnosis of IHD, the Codes I20.0-125.9, with reference to the International Classification of Diseases-10th Revision (ICD-10), including the types of angina pectoris, acute heart attack, subsequent MI, definite cardiac complications following acute MI, coronary thrombosis except MI, Dressler syndrome, acute HF (AHF), cardiac insufficiency, various chronic IHDs, such as atherosclerosis, former MI, all types of aortic aneurysm, coronary artery aneurysm (CAA), arteriovenous fistula, ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM), and silent myocardial ischemia, admitted to Dr. Heshmat Teaching Hospital in the city of Rasht, Guilan Province, Iran, from early February to late April 2020. The research population was the medical records of all patients diagnosed with ischemic heart disease referred to Heshmat Hospital in Rasht. The samples were included in the study using a convenience sample method. Given that the main aim of the present study is to compare the trend (increase or decrease) of referrals of patients with various ischemic heart diseases to Dr. Heshmat Hospital in Rasht during the same period of time (during the Covid-19 pandemic and the corresponding period last year), determining the sample size was not relevant. In other words, in this study, all patients with ischemic heart disease in the two time periods were included in the study and their trends were examined and compared from different perspectives. So The samples were selected using the convenience sampling technique through a census, the medical records, and the information completed according to the data collection form. The samples were then examined in terms of the frequency distribution, IHD diagnosis categories, and demographic characteristics (age, gender, educational attainment, etc.). As the final point, the information after COVID- 19 was compared with that before it, that is, with the patients admitted from early February to late April 2019. The inclusion criteria were all patients with the primary diagnosis of IHD and medical records, admitted to Dr. Heshmat Teaching Hospital in the city of Rasht, Guilan Province, Iran. In contrast, the exclusion criterion was incomplete medical records. The data were accordingly extracted from the medical records of the eligible patients and imported into a checklist based on the study variables.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was carried out using SPSS version 16.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and graphs were depicted using GraphPad Prism, Version 8.0.1 (GraphPad Prism Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Continuous variables are presented as median (interquartile range (IQR)) and compared using Mann-Whitney test. Categorical variables are presented as numbers (percentages) and compared using chi-square test. Normality of distribution was assessed by the Shapiro–Wilks test. All statistical tests were two-sided and level of significance was set at 0.05.

Results

The total number of admissions during the pre-COVID-19 period was 849 and increased to 1138 in the COVID-19 period. Patient baseline characteristics according to the study periods are presented in Table 1. In the COVID-19 period group, there were more females (45.4% vs. 40.2%, P = 0.019) as well as older patients (62 [IQR 53–71] vs. 61 [IQR 53–70], P = 0.048) compared to the pre-COVID-19 period group. In general, the proportion of patients with current smoking (13.0% vs. 24.4%, P < 0.001), opium consumption (7.2% vs. 14.7%, P < 0.001), and alcohol consumption (0.8% vs. 2.7%, P < 0.001) was lower in the COVID-19 period group compared to the pre-COVID-19 period group. Based on the first diagnosis code, HTN (14.9% vs. 2.8%, P < 0.001), HF (12.8% vs. 5.1%, P < 0.001), AF Arrhythmia (6.2% vs. 1.2%, P < 0.001), Other Arrhythmia (6.3% vs. 2.8%, P < 0.001) were more prevalent in the COVID-19 period. As presented in Fig. 1, the number of daily admissions was significantly higher in COVID-19 period compared to pre-COVID-19 period group (17.5 [IQR 13.25–22] vs. 12 [IQR 8–19.75], P < 0.001). The rate of in-hospital death during the COVID-19 period was significantly higher than the same period in 2019 (4.8% vs. 2.4%, P = 0.004) (Fig. 1).

Discussion

This study was to determine the frequency distribution, primary diagnosis categories, and demographic characteristics in the patients with IHD, admitted to Dr. Heshmat Teaching Hospital, a specialized center for the treatment of heart diseases in the city of Rasht, Guilan Province, Iran, within two time periods, namely before and after the COVID-19 peak outbreak. The study revealed an upward trend in the total number of admissions for the period of COVID-19, in contradiction of the reports in Normando and Severino in Brazil and Italy, respectively [19, 20]. Additionally, Fox et al. had found fewer admissions of cases with HF and MI in the United States [21]. Many studies have mentioned patients’ fear of contracting COVID and not visiting the hospital, which is in contrast to the results of the present study. For instance, Chang et al. reported notable changes in healthcare-seeking behavior during the COVID-19 pandemic, with a significant decline in hospital visits driven by patients’ concerns about the risk of infection [22]. Moreover, another study pointed out that fear and avoidance of healthcare workers during the pandemic represented a distinct yet insufficiently acknowledged form of stigmatization. This phenomenon may have played a critical role in deterring individuals from seeking medical care [23]. The main reason for the high admission rate of patients with IHD in the present study could be the uncertain nature of COVID-19 and the efforts by the physicians to examine the cases with mild heart problems in the mentioned hospital. However, factors such as differences in healthcare infrastructure, patient behavior, or national pandemic response strategies must also be considered. In Iran, this event could be because of the lack of appropriate services and visits at home, which drives patients to hospitals even during pandemic conditions. Also, the results additionally demonstrated that women and older adults had the highest admission rates during COVID-19. In this respect, Fox et al. reported a high incidence rate of heart problems in men during the pandemic, which was in contrast to the results in the present study. As well, Bromage had indicated that older adults had experienced IHD during COVID-19 as compared to the same period without this condition [24]. These could be attributable to more vulnerability of the elderly population. In this study, the mortality rate was also significantly higher for the duration of COVID-19, which was in line with the findings in previous studies [19, 21, 24], but no change had been spotted in a survey in Japan in terms of the death rate of the patients as compared to those with heart problems during COVID-19 [25]. This could occur for various reasons, e.g., the overcrowded healthcare facilities and subsequently the failure to provide the essential services to such patients, the inability to utilize medical equipment as a result of the pandemic, the lack of HCPs in this period, delayed hospital visits leading to worsened conditions upon admission, direct cardiovascular complications of COVID-19, psychological and stress-related factors worsening cardiac outcomes and the unknown nature of the virus and its effects on the CV system. The most cases with IHD had been also among those hospitalized during COVID-19 and the patients with high blood pressure (HBP), HF, and atrial fibrillation (A Fib). Regarding HF, the results in previous surveys were inconsistent with the present study [19, 25, 26]. The patients with a history of substance and alcohol use also had the lowest admission rates due to IHD all through COVID-19. Bailey et al. had similarly found a rise and fall in the mortality rates in the patients with alcohol use during the pandemic, which was at odds with the present study [27]. Besides, Baillargeon et al. had shown an upsurge in the hospitalization of those with substance use during COVID-19 in the United States [28]. The paradoxical results in the present study could be accordingly attributable to the suppression of heart problems in people with substance use.Furthermore, the patients with a history of coronary artery bypass surgery (CABS), angioplasty, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), arrhythmia, as well as diabetes mellitus (DM), and HBP had been hospitalized more than others during the pandemic. In this context, a big gap had been detected between the onset of heart disease symptoms and hospital admissions in a study in Japan, mainly owing to the refusal of the patients to attend hospitals for the duration of the COVID-19 peak outbreak. Likewise, the patients with DM and a previous history of CABS and angioplasty had been less frequently admitted to healthcare facilities [29]. In a systematic study, 89% of the hospitalizations had also diminished during COVID-19 and the length of stay had then significantly reduced [30]. The inconsistencies in the admissions could be in this way the consequence of some cultural concerns as well as the phobia in such patients under the pandemic conditions.

One of the limitations in this study was the use of a single center to investigate the patients with IHD during the COVID-19 pandemic, so it was suggested to conduct the same survey in the future in a joint project in multiple centers across Iran. With regard to the retrospective nature of the study, there was the possibility of some information loss. Also, failure to assess disease severity in patients before and after COVID-19, changes in management strategies across hospitals and time periods, social factors such as quarantine restrictions, limited access to transportation, and reduced patient interaction may affect hospital admissions and outcomes.

Conclusion

The study results showed an upward trend in the admission rate of patients with ischemic heart disease (IHD) during the COVID-19 pandemic. Also, the gap in support systems, home visits, and online services for cardiac patients is evident. Since the management of clinical care for such patients who consider themselves candidates for further treatment is very important and affects the outcomes of heart disease and even the pandemic, this issue deserves great attention. Therefore, it is recommended that these programs and infrastructures be considered by healthcare managers to prepare for future pandemics. It seems that training and provision of telephone and online services, and a set of strategies for dealing with non-emergency problems through the establishment of online clinics, would be a good platform to prepare patients for the next pandemic.

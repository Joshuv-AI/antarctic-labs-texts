ProtekDuo Cannula for Pre-, Intra-, and Postoperative Lung Transplantation Management
Authors: William A. Ricks, Mindaugas Rackauskas, William B. Weir, Amir M. Emtiazjoo, Biplab K. Saha, Cynthia J. Gries, Marc O. Maybauer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe present a 61-year-old patient with pulmonary hypertension, acute respiratory failure, and acute severe right ventricular (RV) dysfunction. Preoperatively, a ProtekDuo® was inserted for extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and RV protection with venopulmonary (VP) ECMO in (dl) V-P ECMO configuration. Intraoperatively, it provided venous drainage for venoarterial (VA) ECMO in (dl) VP-/AO configuration for bilateral orthotopic lung transplantation (BOLT). Postoperatively, the patient remained on (dl) V-P ECMO for RV support and was decannulated with mild RV dysfunction after 5 days. This is the first description of the ProtekDuo® used in (dl) V-P to (dl) VP-/AO to (dl) V-P configuration for the entire perioperative period of BOLT.

INTRODUCTION

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support is well described in patients undergoing lung transplantation. Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and right ventricular (RV) dysfunction are common problems encountered in this patient population. ECMO is well described as a tool in the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative setting as a bridge to transplantation, for cardiovascular support, and to prevent primary graft dysfunction (PGD), respectively. The ProtekDuo® is a dual lumen cannula designed for venopulmonary (VP) ECMO and can provide oxygenation and RV support as it decompresses the RV and decreases preload.[12] Compared to previous literature, our case demonstrates the use of the ProtekDuo® in all perioperative (preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative) settings.

CASE REPORT

Our patient is a 61-year-old male with combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) and PAH, who, prior to hospitalization, was on intravenous treprostinil, ambrisentan, and sildenafil, as well as midodrine, stiolto respimat. He was admitted due to increasing oxygen requirements and acute respiratory failure (ARF). His PAH was diagnosed by right heart catheterization with a mean pulmonary artery pressure of 45 mmHg. The echocardiogram showed severe RV dysfunction with dilatation, significantly reduced from prior echocardiograms. Additionally, he had an elevated creatinine (1.21 mg/dL) and low glomerular filtration rate (67 ml per minute, 38ml/1.73 m2 BSA) due to severe pulmonary hypertension and renal congestion. We decided to support the patient’s RV with ProtekDuo® (LivaNova) as a bridge to transplantation and placed the cannula at bedside under fluoroscopic guidance [Figure 1].

Fluoroscopic images of ProtekDuo insertion. (a) Wire advanced from right internal jugular vein through the pulmonary valve and out the right main pulmonary artery. (b) ProtekDuo inserted in proper position over the wire which has been removed

After 10 days on VP ECMO, acceptable donor lungs were obtained, and he was taken to the operating room (OR) for bilateral orthotopic lung transplantation (BOLT). We frequently perform BOLT on Central VA ECMO and elected to initiate VA ECMO support, in this case by conversion from his current (dl) V-P configuration to (dl) VP-/AO configuration by using direct aortic (AO) cannulation for the return cannula and venous drainage through both wire-reinforced lumens of the ProtekDuo® cannula.[2] After assuring adequate systemic anticoagulation, VA ECMO support was initiated in (dl) VP-/AO configuration. Transplantation was carried out in routine fashion with a right lung critical ischemia time (CIT) of 377 min and a left lung CIT of 473 min. Notably, there were no issues with venous drainage during the procedure while manipulating the heart for anastomosis. After completion of the case, we transitioned from central VA ECMO back to the prior (dl) V-P configuration and removed the AO cannula. On postoperative day 5, he required operative intervention for a bleeding complication. At this time, he was weaned from VP ECMO as RV function and size had recovered with only mild dysfunction and dilatation remaining [Figure 2]. Tracheal extubation was performed on postoperative day 6 from BOLT. The patient was discharged from the hospital after 42 days without major complications.

DISCUSSION

The ProtekDuo® cannula has a wide array of functions. A systematic review of the ProtekDuo in the setting of COVID-19-related severe acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) revealed several promising aspects, such as significantly reduced rates of acute kidney injury and consecutively reduced rates of renal replacement therapies (RRTs) that have led to increased use of this cannula, and may find validation in future studies of other etiologies of ARDS.[3] Furthermore, improvement in renal function after lung transplantation has been consistently reported in the PAH patient population. We were able to demonstrate improvement in his renal function and creatinine clearance with the use of the ProtekDuo® and assess his true renal function before transplantation.

With regard to the use of the ProtekDuo® in lung transplantation, Montoya-Beltram and colleagues described their experience in long-term VP ECMO mode and (dl) V-P ECMO configuration as a bridge to lung transplantation in the preoperative period.[4] Budd et al.[5] described its use intraoperatively for lung transplantation to assist with drainage. The authors used a ProtekDuo® (in V-P configuration) and an 18 Fr cannula, which after sternotomy was inserted into the ascending AO. Blood was drained through both lumens of the ProtekDuo® cannula to the venous aspect of the circuit and returning it to the outflow cannula in the AO as intraoperative central VA ECMO mode in (dl) VP-/AO configuration. They achieved hemodynamically stable conditions and good decompression of the heart, which allowed for bilateral, sequential native pneumonectomy and donor lung transplantation. Settepani and coworkers described a very similar technique in which they switched from percutaneous minimally invasive biventricular mechanical support to cardiopulmonary bypass during a heart transplantation.[6] Carrozzini and colleagues also utilized the ProtekDuo® for RV failure in the postoperative phase of heart transplantation secondary to PGD.[7] Sinha et al.[8] reproduced these results in a heart and lung transplantation validating the techniques described above.

The ProtekDuo® cannula, depending on the cannula size (29 or 31 Fr), may achieve 4–5 liters per minute (LPM) of blood flow with an average of 4.5 LPM. Maybauer and colleagues[9] had added a 25 Fr femoral venous drainage cannula and returned oxygenated blood through both lumens of the ProtekDuo® in V-(dl) VP configuration in a case of ARDS secondary to COVID-19 infection. This way, they achieved a significant increase in blood flow of up to 7 LPM, with blood flows of 3 LPM into the pulmonary artery and 4 LPM into the right atrium, achieving both increased flow and oxygenation, and also 3 LPM of RV bypass, pushing the limits of the ProtekDuo®. On the contrary, Maybauer et al.[10] also used the ProtekDuo® in a patient with cardiogenic shock post cardiac arrest secondary to myocardial infarction in combination with an Impella CP for groin-free biventricular PROpella support. When the leg at the impella CP site became ischemic, they removed the Impella CP and placed a 17 Fr femoral arterial ECMO cannula and a distal perfusion cannula in the contralateral groin. They clamped and cut the tubing to the ProtekDuo® and used a Y-piece to drain blood through both lumen toward the ECMO device and returned it into the arterial cannula, achieving VA ECMO in (dl) VP-Aa configuration. Of note, due to the biluminal wire reinforcement, up to 7 LPM of blood may be drained through the cannula without collapse of a lumen when negative pressure is applied. Given the experience of our group with various ProtekDuo® configurations and the knowledge of the literature discussed above, we felt confident to mimic this setup using the ProtekDuo® preoperatively in (dl) V-P ECMO configuration as a bridge to lung transplantation intraoperatively with central aortic return in (dl) VP-AO configuration for the short period of BOLT and postoperatively in (dl) V-P configuration to promote RV recovery in the same patient. We did not experience any complications with venous drainage during the procedure even when retracting the heart for vascular and bronchial anastomosis. Furthermore, although it has been described for all large cannulas, we did not experience issues with impaired venous drainage from the superior vena cava. However, there are known limitations common to all large bore ECMO cannulas. Central venous stenosis may preclude cannulation and can be a consequence thereof. Wire perforations, pericardial tamponade, and injury to cardiac valves may be potential complications.[2] We routinely perform post cannulation echocardiograms and chest X-rays to monitor for such compilations. In contrast to other publications, in which the ProtekDuo was used pre-, intra-, or postoperatively, we describe the feasibility of using the ProtekDuo for the entire perioperative period of lung transplantation in a patient with ARF and acute RV failure, what we feel could be the future of ECMO in the setting of lung transplantation.

CONCLUSION

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first description of (dl) V-P to (dl) VP-AO to (dl) V-P configuration in the entire perioperative period of double lung transplantation. This case demonstrates the use of the ProtekDuo® in VP ECMO mode and various configurations to safely assist with BOLT in the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative phases. It is particularly useful in patients with pre-existing PH and RV dysfunction. In conclusion, this appears to be a safe method for BOLT; however, future studies of the ProtekDuo® in the lung transplantation settings are needed to learn more about its safety profile.

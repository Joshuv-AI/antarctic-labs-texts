Comparative evaluation of emphysema quantification: Standardized %LAV−950 versus DL-based emphysema quantification with clinical parameter correlation
Authors: Dana Belde, Janko Rakic, Jakob Heimer, Mattias Kettner, Michael Thali, André Euler, Rahel A. Kubik-Huch, Tilo Niemann
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to evaluate the correlation between the %LAV−950 threshold method and a deep learning-based algorithm for emphysema quantification in chest computed tomography (CT) scans with pulmonary function test (PFT) parameters. This retrospective study included 101 chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patients who underwent chest CT and PFTs to assess FEV₁ and carbon monoxide transfer coefficient (KCO). Emphysema was quantified using the %LAV−950 method and a deep learning-based algorithm. Quantitative results were obtained from lung and soft tissue reconstruction kernels. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to assess associations between CT metrics and PFT parameters. Permutation testing was used to compare correlation coefficients. The %LAV−950 method showed a weak but significant correlation with FEV₁ using soft tissue kernel (r = −0.281; P = .005), while no significant association was observed with KCO. The deep learning-based method demonstrated stronger and more consistent correlations: a moderate correlation with FEV₁ using lung kernel (r = −0.303; P = .005), a weak correlation with FEV₁ using soft tissue kernel (r = −0.289; P = .006), and a moderate correlation with KCO using soft tissue kernel (r = −0.404; P = .014). Permutation testing confirmed a significant difference in the correlation with KCO between both methods using soft tissue kernel (r = −0.264; P = .025). The deep learning-based approach demonstrated stronger and more consistent correlations with pulmonary function parameters than the traditional %LAV−950 method, especially for soft tissue kernel reconstructions. These findings highlight its potential to provide a more accurate quantification of emphysema and support its integration into routine CT analysis and artificial intelligence-assisted diagnostic workflows.

1. Introduction

Lung emphysema, a chronic and frequently progressive lung disease characterized by irreversible destruction and enlargement of the alveoli,[1,2] represents a significant global health burden.[3,4] Although emphysema is most commonly associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in smokers,[5] it can also occur in individuals with hereditary α-1-antitrypsin deficiency[6,7] or as a consequence of long-term exposure to environmental pollutants.[8] Morphologically, emphysema can be classified into 3 main subtypes: centrilobular, panlobular, and paraseptal emphysema.[9,10]

The early detection and accurate assessment of emphysema are critical for guiding treatment decisions and monitoring disease progression.[11] In this context, computed tomography (CT) imaging plays a crucial role,[12,13] providing the ability to perform lung densitometry.[14] The approach most frequently employed in clinical practice to quantify the extent of emphysema involves the measurement of the low attenuation volume (LAV) using a predefined threshold.[15,16] In 1995, Gevenois et al established a threshold of −950 Hounsfield units (HU) as optimal, as it demonstrates the strongest correlation with macroscopic pathological characteristics of emphysema.[17] Subsequently, this threshold has become widely adopted in radiological practice for emphysema quantification, typically expressed as the percentage of lung parenchyma with attenuation values below −950 HU (%LAV−950).[18,19]

Building on these findings, recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have enabled the development of imaging algorithms, such as Contextflow’s Advanced Chest CT Algorithm (Version 3.1, Contextflow GmbH, Vienna, Austria).[20] This deep learning (DL)-based algorithm is described to perform a range of diagnostic tasks in chest CT imaging, including the detection and pattern-based analysis of lung emphysema using a DL segmentation approach. It thereby provides a distinct alternative to the conventional %LAV−950 threshold method.[21] According to Röhrich et al the algorithm shows considerable potential for enhancing diagnostic efficiency, achieving a reported 31.3% reduction in reading time while simultaneously enhancing the overall accuracy of chest CT interpretation.[22] In 2022, Perkonigg et al compared the algorithm to the widely used −950 HU threshold method, using expert-reviewed pixel-wise manual labeling of emphysematous regions as the reference standard. Their findings indicated that the DL-based method allowed for more precise identification of emphysematous regions and yielded more accurate volumetric measurements. Furthermore, the algorithm exhibited higher specificity and a notable reduction in false-positive detections. Importantly, the sensitivity of the DL-based approach was found to be nearly equivalent to that of the −950 HU threshold method.[23]

Since previous studies focused primarily on the performance of the mentioned DL-based algorithm itself, our study aims to evaluate the correlations between the DL-based quantification approach and the established %LAV−950 threshold method with pulmonary function test (PFT) parameters. In addition, it provides a comparative analysis of the differences in correlation between both quantification methods in relation to PFT parameters to determine which approach shows a stronger association with clinical outcomes. By evaluating these correlations, this study seeks to clarify the respective strengths and limitations of each approach in clinical practice.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Ethics approval

This retrospective study was approved by the local ethics committee (EKNZ 2024-01811). All the patients provided general informed consent for the use of their clinical data for research purposes.

2.2. Patients

A power analysis resulted in a minimum sample size of 88 patients. From August 2021 to August 2024, we included overall 101 patients from the outpatient clinic who underwent clinically indicated chest CT scans and PFTs. Exclusion criteria included the absence of visible emphysema on CT scans, impaired CT scan quality due to medical devices, declined general consent, and age < 18 years.

2.3. CT scan acquisition

Patients were scanned using 2 different CT scanners: a third-generation dual-source dual-energy CT scanner (SOMATOM Drive, Siemens Healthineers AG, Forchheim, Germany) and a single-source dual-energy CT scanner (SOMATOM X.ceed, Siemens Healthineers AG, Forchheim, Germany). Scans were conducted in the supine position at end-inspiration with and without the use of contrast media according to the dedicated individualized clinical scan protocol. Reconstructions were performed with a section thickness of 1 mm using lung kernel (l50f) and soft tissue kernel (l26f) images. Both kernels were used for comparative purposes, as the lung kernel is routinely applied for the evaluation of pulmonary parenchyma, whereas the soft tissue kernel is typically used for the assessment of soft tissue structures.

2.4. CT emphysema quantification

Image analysis was performed using the Picture Archiving and Communications Systems (PACS) SECTRA (Model IDS7, Version 25.2, Linköping, Sweden) as well as Syngo.via (VB80D, Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany) on a workstation used in clinical routine. Emphysema quantification was assessed using the following 2 methods:

(1)%LAV−950 threshold method: quantitative CT analysis was performed using syngo.via Pulmo 3D workflow to semiautomatically quantify the percentage of lung voxels with attenuation values below the −950 HU threshold.

%LAV−950 threshold method: quantitative CT analysis was performed using syngo.via Pulmo 3D workflow to semiautomatically quantify the percentage of lung voxels with attenuation values below the −950 HU threshold.

(2)Deep learning-based approach: Contextflow ADVANCE Chest CT (Version 3.1, Contextflow GmbH, Vienna, Austria) is a deep learning-based computer aided detection system designed for the analysis of 3D medical imaging data.[20] Utilizing convolutional neural networks, the algorithm detects and quantifies pulmonary abnormalities—including emphysema—by analyzing volumetric chest CT data. It provides automated segmentation and classification of lung nodules and parenchymal changes, offering a quantitative insight into the extent and severity of pulmonary diseases. The algorithm was trained on a diverse set of cases selected based on their clinical content, with the goal of encompassing a broad range of lung pathologies and anomalies, as well as variations in acquisition protocols and imaging parameters.

For both methods, quantitative values were derived from reconstructions using both lung and soft tissue kernel.

2.5. Pulmonary function tests

All patients had previously undergone clinically indicated PFTs, including spirometry to assess FEV₁, as well as measurements of gas transfer to evaluate carbon monoxide transfer coefficient (KCO).

2.6. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using an open-source software (R; Version 4.4.1; R Core Team, Vienna, Austria). Clinical variables (forced expiratory volume in 1 second [FEV1] and KCO) and quantitative CT metrics were summarized by sample size (N), mean, and standard deviation. Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) was calculated to measure the linear relationship between the clinical predictors and each CT metric.

To determine whether the observed magnitudes of correlation were significantly different when comparing metrics within the same subjects, a permutation test was used. The null hypothesis tested was that the true difference between the 2 Pearson correlations is 0.

The permutation test shuffled the paired CT metric values within each subject 5000 times. For each permutation, the difference between the 2 relevant correlation coefficients was recalculated. The observed differences in correlations was compared with this distribution. A 2-tailed P-value was computed as the proportion of permutations yielded an absolute difference greater than or equal to the observed absolute difference. The results were considered statistically significant at P < .05.

4. Discussion

This study evaluated the correlations between clinically relevant pulmonary function parameters and 2 CT-based emphysema quantification methods: the conventional %LAV−950 threshold method and a DL-based approach. Subsequently, the correlation differences between both quantification methods in relation to the relevant PFT parameters were evaluated.

Overall, our results demonstrated statistically significant negative correlations between emphysema quantification and PFT parameters across both, methods and reconstruction kernels. The %LAV−950 method showed significant but relatively weak correlations with clinical parameters. Specifically, a weak yet statistically significant correlation was observed between %LAV−950 values (soft tissue kernel) and FEV₁ (r = −0.281, P = .005), while the correlation using lung kernel did not reach statistical significance. With regard to KCO, %LAV−950-based emphysema quantification showed no significant association regardless of the reconstruction kernel. These findings are consistent with previous studies, such as those by Wang et al, who reported moderate negative correlations between %LAV and FEV₁.[24] Šileikienė et al also observed only weak correlations between these parameters.[25]

In contrast, the DL-based approach demonstrated stronger and more consistent correlations. The DL-based emphysema quantification algorithm demonstrated a moderate negative correlation with FEV₁ when using lung kernel (r = −0.303, P = .005), while the soft tissue kernel version also showed a statistically significant, slightly weaker, negative correlation (r = −0.289, P = .006). Notably, a significant moderate correlation with KCO was identified for the DL-based method using soft tissue kernel (r = −0.404, P = .014), highlighting the algorithm’s potential to capture functional impairment related to gas exchange. This finding was further supported by permutation testing, which demonstrated a statistically significant difference in the correlation with KCO between the deep learning-based approach and the %LAV−950 threshold method using soft tissue kernel (P = .025). From a clinical perspective, this difference in correlation with KCO indicates that the deep learning-based approach may provide a more accurate reflection of physiologically relevant gas exchange than the conventional %LAV−950 method when using soft tissue kernel reconstructions. However, we believe that the expected clinical impact is rather small, as the observed correlation strengths, although statistically significant, remain only weak to moderate. Therefore, we estimate the overall impact on clinical decision-making and patient management to be limited.

The difference in performance may stem from the underlying methodological principles: while %LAV−950 relies on a fixed HU threshold to quantify emphysema,[17–19] the DL-based approach employs learned image features through convolutional neural networks. These results are consistent with findings by Perkonigg et al, who reported that the Contextflow ADVANCE Chest CT algorithm provides more accurate emphysema quantification compared to the %LAV−950 threshold method.[23] However, their study did not evaluate the algorithm’s performance in relation to clinical parameters.

Given prior findings, DL-based emphysema quantification is gaining increasing attention, as several other algorithms are now available offering comparable functionality.[26–28] To the best of our knowledge, only a few studies to date have correlated their AI-based algorithms with PFT parameters. Chaudhary et al examined their algorithm’s performance in this context and reported a significant association with FEV₁.[29] Similarly, Fischer et al evaluated their algorithm in relation to pulmonary function and found a strong correlation with the Tiffeneau index.[30] However, to our knowledge, this is the first study to directly compare the correlation differences between both quantification methods in relation to PFT parameters.

The impact of reconstruction kernels on emphysema quantification has been well documented before, with the lung kernel known to systematically overestimate emphysema in %LAV−950-based assessments.[13,31] This trend was also evident in our results for both quantification methods, as shown in Table 1. In line with this, our findings suggest that the DL-based quantification approach also benefits from soft tissue kernel reconstruction, yielding more consistent correlations with functional parameters.

Our study had several limitations. First, a key limitation is the relatively small sample size of KCO (n = 38), which limits the statistical power for subgroup comparisons. Second, some patients provided incomplete data, with partially missing quantification values or PFT parameters. These patients were retained in the subgroup analysis to avoid selection bias and to preserve the generalizability of the conditions as observed in clinical routine. Third, there was a considerable time gap between CT imaging and PFT acquisition. While the average delay between CT and spirometry was 50 days, the delay for KCO testing extended to an average of 183 days, with individual cases exceeding 4 years. This temporal heterogeneity likely introduced variability and may have attenuated correlation strengths. However, it is well established that emphysema progresses slowly on CT scans. For example, Baraghoshi et al found that volume-adjusted lung density showed the greatest decline in their group of current smokers with more than trace emphysema, with an annual decrease of 1.4 g/L during the first 5 years of follow-up. Participants who had quit smoking or had only trace emphysema showed an even smaller decline in lung density.[32]

Future research could benefit from an expanded evaluation of additional functional parameters, particularly those related to pulmonary hyperinflation, which may offer further insights into the physiological impact of emphysematous changes.

While DL-based emphysema quantification has previously been linked to PFT parameters, our study is the first to directly compare these associations with those of the %LAV−950 method. This study therefore provides the first evidence that DL-based emphysema quantification shows stronger associations with functionally relevant clinical measures than the %LAV−950 method, particularly with respect to gas exchange. These results support the potential added value of AI-assisted tools in routine clinical assessment.

5. Conclusion

In this study, we evaluated and compared the correlations of the conventional %LAV−950 threshold method and a DL-based approach for emphysema quantification on chest CT with clinically relevant pulmonary function parameters. Our findings showed that the DL-based method exhibited stronger and more consistent correlations with both FEV₁ and KCO, particularly when using soft tissue kernel reconstructions. These results suggested that DL-based quantification provides a more accurate and functionally relevant assessment of emphysema. The improved performance of the DL-based method highlights its potential utility as a supportive tool in clinical practice. Future studies should aim to validate these findings in larger and more temporally harmonized datasets to further support the integration of DL-based methods into routine diagnostic workflows.

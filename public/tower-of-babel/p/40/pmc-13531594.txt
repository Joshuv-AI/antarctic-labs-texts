External-field and pressure-controlled magneto-optical response in alloy-composition engineered GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterials with asymmetrical Gaussian confinement
Authors: Ho Kim Dan, Pham Tuan Vinh, Le Phuong Long, Huynh Thi Phuong Thuy, Nguyen Dinh Hien
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydrostatic-pressure, external electric-field, and aluminium-composition-controlled magneto-optical response in the GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanostructure with asymmetrical Gaussian confinement, is investigated applying the projection-operator framework. The magneto-optical response is systematically probed via the optically detected magneto-phonon resonance (ODMPR) effect, including strength, shift, and full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the ODMPR peak induced by intersubband and intrasubband transitions for absorption and emission processes of LO-phonons. The results show that: (i) the magneto-optical response probed via the ODMPR effect can be effectively controlled through both external and structural parameters; (ii) the magneto-optical response induced by both the intrasubband and intersubband transitions is very sensitive to the hydrostatic pressure and aluminium composition, while only the intersubband transition is sensitive to the external electric field; (iii) among the intersubband transitions due to phonon absorption and emission, and intrasubband transitions due to phonon emission, the FWHM of GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs asymmetrical Gaussian potential heterostructures resulting from intersubband transitions with phonon absorption are the largest and the most sensitive to variations in hydrostatic-pressure, quantum system temperature, and aluminium-composition λ, followed by the intrasubband transition due to phonon emission, while the intersubband transition due to phonon emission is the smallest and the least sensitive; (iv) and the sensitivity of the magneto-optical response to external electric field, confinement potential depth and width of the GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanostructure under the influence of aluminium composition and hydrostatic pressure, is also shown in detail. Comprehensive characteristics of the magneto-optical response of the GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanostructure are provided in the Conclusions, which are useful for the application potential of optoelectronic devices.

Introduction

The increasing demand for high-speed, energy efficient technologies has led to significant advancements in the research of III–V semiconductor heterostructures. Among such heterostructures, GaAs/AlGaAs quantum wells (QWs) stand out as a foundational platform for contemporary optoelectronic and electronic devices. Their direct band gap, excellent carrier mobility, and adjustable band structure make them particularly valuable.1 The ability to control band offsets and confinement potentials in these heterostructures enables effective carrier localization and improved electron–phonon interactions. This has made them highly suitable for applications in semiconductor lasers, optical modulators, and photodetectors.1,2 Furthermore, the benefits of quantum confinement and modulation doping enhance radiative recombination efficiency while minimizing impurity scattering, ultimately boosting device performance in areas such as quantum efficiency, power consumption, and operational stability.

GaAs/AlGaAs nanostructures are making waves beyond traditional optoelectronics, finding their place in cutting-edge devices like light-emitting diodes (LEDs), tunnel field-effect transistors (TFETs), thermoelectric systems, and biosensing platforms, all thanks to their remarkable nanoscale versatility.2 When it comes to photovoltaics, GaAs-based heterostructures outshine silicon, forming the backbone of high-efficiency solar cells, both single-junction and multi-junction. Impressively, multi-junction devices on GaAs substrates have surpassed 30% efficiency, highlighting the essential role that this material system plays in the development of future energy technologies.3 From a materials-engineering viewpoint, quantum well GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructures offer remarkable adaptability in device design through techniques such as bandgap engineering, optimization of heterointerfaces, and advanced epitaxial growth methods like metal organic vapor phase epitaxy (MOVPE). These techniques allow for precise manipulation of layer thickness and interface quality, making it possible to create high-quality, quantum confined systems with exceptionally narrow optical linewidths, indicative of superior material purity and structural accuracy.4 As a result, GaAs/AlGaAs quantum wells not only improve device efficiency but also provide a versatile foundation for new applications in quantum electronics and nanoscale device engineering.

Magnetophonon resonance (MPR) occurs due to the resonant scattering of electrons and phonons in the presence of a magnetic field. MPR provides a direct manifestation of resonant electron–phonon (e–ph) coupling through inter-Landau-level transitions, enabling quantitative characterization of scattering processes in semiconductor heterostructures. In low dimensional systems such as quantum wells and superlattices, the oscillatory transport signatures induced by MPR provide direct information on the strength and nature of e–LO-ph interaction governing carrier dynamics in optoelectronic structures.5 Moreover, in systems with quantum confinement, the response of MPR is significantly affected by the confinement potential and structural parameters. This results in changes to resonance conditions and the arrangement of peaks. This sensitivity makes MPR an effective tool for investigating nanoscale design features in QWs and related heterostructures.6 Finally, MPR creates a direct link between the tiny electron–phonon interactions and the larger transport behaviors seen in low-dimensional systems. This connection offers a comprehensive way to relate the fundamental microscopic interactions to the transport properties that are important for device applications. MPR has been the subject of considerable research, particularly regarding its appearance in transport coefficients, which reveal the effects of resonant electron–phonon scattering through oscillatory magnetoresistance and similar measurements.

The optically detected magneto-phonon resonance (ODMPR) technique allows for a direct observation of the resonance in response to varying frequencies, offering a more precise and quantitative assessment of scattering processes that occur between specific Landau levels.7 Notably, ODMPR provides valuable insights into the strength and character of e–ph coupling, effectively bypassing the limitations of traditional transport measurements.8 A thorough grasp of how temperature, external electric field, alloy composition, QW confinement affect, and hydrostatic pressure effect ODMPR – specifically its peak position, peak intensity, and FWHM – is crucial for developing cutting-edge optoelectronic and quantum devices. The ODMPR metrics offer significant insights into the processes of e–LO-ph scattering, Landau-level broadening, and the spectral linewidths related to carrier relaxation and energy dissipation in low-dimensional systems. Notably, the marked temperature sensitivity of the ODMPR linewidth and its enhanced broadening during resonance allows for a precise evaluation of phonon-limited transport behavior.7,9 The ODMPR peak position, amplitude, and linewidth are sensitive to various factors, including photon frequency, magnetic field, and the specific parameters of the system. This sensitivity highlights how adjustable the interlevel transitions and resonance conditions can be.8,10 Additionally, the interplay between hydrostatic pressure and aluminium composition offers a practical way to fine-tune the ODMPR spectral response. These factors significantly alter the band-gap energy, effective mass, dielectric properties, and optical phonon spectrum, which in turn affect e–ph coupling strength and resonance traits.11,12

QW confinement also plays a vital role by shaping subband structures and influencing carrier dynamics, allowing for further adjustments to resonance position and linewidth.6,13,14 Moreover, the application of external electric fields can alter subband energies and carrier distributions, adding yet another layer of control over how carriers behave and how resonance manifests. The choice of confinement potential is vital to understand how QW semiconductors respond optically, especially in resonance phenomena that depend on the electric field. Compared with conventional rectangular, parabolic, and step-like confinement models, the asymmetrical Gaussian potential provides a smoother and more realistic description of carrier confinement. However, the mathematical derivation of electronic states and electron–LO-phonon interaction matrix elements is generally more involved. Previous research has highlighted that traditional parabolic potentials often struggle to match experimental results under different conditions, whereas the Gaussian potential aligns better with the complexities of real-world systems.15 It has been clearly highlighted in various studies that the Gaussian potential is particularly effective in capturing the optical behaviors observed in semiconductor QWs.16 Additionally, experimental insights suggest that the actual confinement potential in these semiconductor nanostructures tends to diverge from straightforward idealized models, finding a more accurate representation in a Gaussian-like profile.17 These insights lend strong physical backing to the use of an asymmetrical Gaussian potential for modeling low-dimensional semiconductor systems.

Another noteworthy benefit of this confinement model is its capacity to integrate various external and structural parameters into a cohesive framework. Previous research has shown that the optical characteristics of the AGQWs are significantly affected by factors like the applied electric field and structural attributes such as the width and depth of the wells.18 Moreover, additional studies indicate that crucial spectral features, such as the position and intensity of resonance peaks, are highly sensitive to changes in hydrostatic pressure, temperature, and magnetic fields.19 This AG potential modifies the electron–LO-phonon coupling matrix elements through the overlap integral, thereby providing an additional structural degree of freedom for tuning the magneto-optical response of semiconductor QWs. These underscore that the AG potential enables a thorough examination of the combined effects of the electric and magnetic fields, hydrostatic pressure, temperature, alloy composition, and confinement geometry. The high level of tunability from a device and technology standpoint is crucial. Having the capability to adjust the resonance position, peak intensity, and spectral linewidth through both external influences and design choices creates a solid foundation for developing tunable optoelectronic devices and semiconductor nanostructures with customizable optical properties.

Recent research studies have emerged concerning this AG confinement, including the following: the optical absorption coefficients, both linear and nonlinear, along with changes in the refractive index, are examined in AG quantum wells when influenced by an electric field and the nonlinear optical rectification under the influence of an applied electric field in both symmetrical and asymmetrical Gaussian confinements are considered by Guo and Du15 and Zhang et al.,20 respectively. Besides, Wu et al. have analyzed the effects of polarons on nonlinear optical rectification in AGQWs under electric fields.21 Furthermore, the phenomenon of second harmonic generation in both asymmetrical and symmetrical Gaussian QWs subjected to an applied electric field is reported by Yuan16 and Zhai.17 In addition, Zhang et al. calculated the external field and pressure-controlled nonlinear optical characteristics of AG confinement.18,19 These researches highlight the remarkable sensitivity of the nonlinear optical response in Gaussian potential, particularly when configured asymmetrically. Adjustments to the confinement potential, such as the depth and size of the well alongside external influences like electric, magnetic, thermal, or pressure fields, significantly alter the electronic subband structure. These alterations directly affect the intensity and position of resonant features in important optical characteristics, including absorbed coefficients, changes in refractive index, optical rectification, and second-harmonic generation. Overall, these insights position Gaussian QWs as a dynamic and adaptable platform for fine-tuning intersubband optical responses.

From a technological standpoint, the strong relationship between optical responses and external fields enables on-the-fly adjustments of device functionality without structural changes. As a result, Gaussian QW systems hold great promise for the development of nanoscale photonic and quantum devices. Simultaneously, these results also indicate that research on external-field- and pressure-controlled MO responses such as the ODEPR effect in alloy-composition-engineered GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterials with asymmetrical Gaussian confinement, remains open. From a technological standpoint, effectively controlling parameters such as Al content, external field, pressure, and well depth lays a strong foundation for designing optoelectronic devices that offer multidimensional tunability. Such versatility is especially vital for systems like infrared photodetectors, electro-optic modulators, and nonlinear optical converters, where high sensitivity, a broad operational bandwidth, and the ability to adapt to changing environmental conditions are critical. Therefore, more investigation is required into the Al-content-, pressure-, and external-field-controlled magneto-optical responses, such as the ODEPR effect, in GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterials with asymmetrical Gaussian confinement engineered by alloy composition.

From an application standpoint, this behaviour allows for the development of multi-functional QW devices that can offer both tunable broadband capabilities and stable narrowband reference features. At the same time, the consistent nature of intrasubband transitions supports high-resolution sensing and spectrally selective detection. These findings underscore the potential of geometry-driven, channel-selective engineering as a practical approach to optimize linewidth and overall performance in low-dimensional semiconductor devices.

Conclusions

In summary, the magneto-optical response is systematically probed via the ODMPR effect, including spectral strength, spectral shift, and FWHM due to the transitions from and from by e–LO-ph interaction for both absorbed and emitted processes of LO-phonons in the aluminium-composition-engineered GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs AG-potential quantum nanomaterial, which is controlled by hydrostatic pressure, depth of AG-confined potential, external electric field, width of AG-confined potential, quantum-system's temperature, and content of aluminium. This work comprehensively uses the projection operator framework together with the profile approach developed by our group. Our key findings are comprehensive and reliable, including the following two:

(i) In terms of academic potential: when the enhanced Al composition from λ = 0 to λ = 0.45 in the asymmetrical Gaussian confined potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs quantum wells, the LO-phonon absorbed peak location associated with the transition moves along the direction of lower energy of the incident photons, and its strength gradually decreases; conversely, the LO-phonon emitted peak location moves along the direction of higher energy of the incident photons, and its strength gradually increases. At the same time, the FWHM of the LO-phonon absorbed/emitted peak associated with the and transitions increases with enhanced Al composition. In these three cases, the FWHM of the LO-phonon-absorbed peak associated with the transition has the fastest rate of augmentation and the largest value, followed by that for the LO-phonon emitted peak associated with the transition; meanwhile, that for the LO-phonon-emitted peak associated with the transition exhibits the slowest rate of augmentation and the smallest value. Our research shows that the Al composition has a pronounced influence on the MO response, as probed by the ODMPR effect. The magneto-optical response due to the transition is more sensitive to Al-composition than that due to the transition, and that due to LO-phonon absorption is more sensitive to Al-composition than that due to LO-phonon emission. Therefore, the Al-composition-controlled MO response in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs is achieved by adjusting the Al composition. In addition, with the enhanced hydrostatic pressure from P = 0 to 9 GPa, the LO-phonon-emitted and -absorbed peak locations associated with the transition, move along the direction of lower energy of the incident photons; concurrently, their intensities gradually decrease. Meanwhile, the LO-phonon-emitted peak location associated with the transition remains unchanged, while its strength also gradually reduces. On the other hand, the FWHM associated with the three cases above diminishes as the pressure in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs increases. The FWHM due to the LO-phonon-absorbed peak associated with the transition exhibits the highest value, followed by that due to the LO-phonon-emitted peak associated with the transition, whereas that due to the LO-phonon-emitted peak associated with the transition shows the lowest value. These findings evidently display that the MO response, as probed by the ODMPR effect in the Al-composition-engineered GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial, is largely influenced by the hydrostatic pressure. Similarly, the magneto-optical response due to the transition is more sensitive to the hydrostatic pressure than that due to the transition, and that due to LO-phonon absorption is more sensitive to the hydrostatic pressure than that due to LO-phonon emission. Thus, the hydrostatic pressure-controlled MO response in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs is achieved by adjusting the hydrostatic pressure. Moreover, our computational data show specifically that the temperature does not substantially affect the LO-phonon-emitted and -absorbed peak locations associated with the and transitions in the AG-confined potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs a nanomaterial, but it substantially affects the strength of these peaks, especially the LO-phonon-absorbed peak due to the transition. The FWHM associated with the three cases above, augments as the temperature in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial increases. The calculated FWHM value is greatest for the LO-phonon-absorbed peak associated with the transition, intermediate for the LO-phonon-emitted peak associated with the transition, and smallest for the LO-phonon-emitted peak associated with the transition. So, the magneto-optical response associated with the transition due to LO-phonon absorption is the most sensitive to the temperature. The temperature-controlled MO response in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs can also be achieved by adjusting the quantum system's temperature. Moreover, similar to the temperature, the external electric field does not substantially affect the LO-phonon-emitted and -absorbed peak locations associated with the and transitions in the AG-confined potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial, but it substantially affects the strength of these peaks, especially the LO-phonon-absorbed peak due to the transition. On the other hand, the FWHM associated with the transition augments gradually as the electric field applied to the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial increases, for both LO-phonon-emitted and -absorbed peaks at the Al compositions λ = 0, 0.1, 0.25, and 0.35. The magneto-optical response associated with the LO-phonon absorption is the most sensitive to the external electric field. Thus, the external-electric-field-controlled MO response probed via the ODMPR effect in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs is achieved by changing the external-electric-field. Additionally, the AG-potential QW-depth does not substantially affect the peak location associated with the transition in the Al-composition-engineered AG-confined potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial. However, the augmentation of the AG-potential QW-depths V0 = 5, 6, 7, and 8 meV, leading to the peak location due to the transition, remains unchanged; on the contrary, the LO-phonon- emitted and -absorbed peaks, due to the transition, moves along the direction of higher energy of the incident photons. On the other hand, its strength due to the transition upon absorbing a phonon, rises with the depth V0 = 5, 6, 7, and 8 meV of the AG-potential QW. Meanwhile, it reduces with the depth V0 = 5, 6, 7, and 3 meV of the AG-potential QW upon emitting a phonon. When the depth of the AG-confined potential QW is increased, it leads to a substantial augmentation in FWHM of the and transition deduced ODMPR peaks at the hydrostatical-pressures P = 1, 4, and 6 GPa. Simultaneously, when the depth of the AG-confined potential QW is larger than 100 meV, the augmentation rate in FWHM of the transition-deduced ODMPR peak is faster than that of the transition-deduced ODMPR peak. Therefore, the AG-potential QW-depth has a pronounced influence on the MO response, as probed by the ODMPR effect. The magneto-optical response associated with the transition is more sensitive to the AG-potential QW-depth than that associated with the transition, and that becomes much more sensitive as the AG-potential QW-depth is larger than 100 meV. So, the quantum well-depth-controlled MO response in the AG-potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs is achieved by adjusting the quantum well depth. Finally, our research data shows that the peak location due to the transition for emitting a phonon in the AG-confined potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial is not affected substantially by the AG-quantum well-width. On the contrary, the peaks due to the transition for both emitting and absorbing phonons are substantially impacted by the AG-quantum well-width in the presence of the hydrostatic-pressure 1 GPa and concentration λ = 0.35 of the aluminium. Specifically, the augmentation of the widths L = 10, 12, 14, and 16 nm of the AG-confined potential QW, leading to the peak location due to the transition, remains unchanged; however, due to the transition, it moves along the direction of lower energy of the incident photons for both absorbing and emitting phonons. On the other hand, as the AG-confined potential width rises to L = 10, 12, 14, 16 nm in GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial, the peak strength due to the transition upon absorbing a phonon diminishes, but it rises for emitting a phonon. When the Al composition of the AG potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs quantum nanomaterials is increased from λ = 0, 0.1, 0.25 to 0.35, leading to a substantial augmentation in FWHM of the (red lines) and (blue lines) transitions deduced ODMPR peaks. On the other hand, these FWHMs evidently show that, within the range L (smaller than 20 nm, corresponding to the AG potential QW width), they diminish substantially as the well-width augments for both and transitions. However, within the range L (larger than 20 nm of the AG potential QW width), there is a substantial difference between and transitions in the width of the AG-confined potential QW dependence of the FWHM. Specifically, under the Al-composition influence, the FWHM of the (blue lines) transition-deduced ODMPR peak in the AG potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs nanomaterial augments strongly as the enhanced AG-well-width, while the FWHM of the (red lines) transition-deduced ODMPR peak continues to reduce monotonously. This property demonstrates that the FWHM of the transition-deduced ODMPR peak in the AG potential GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QW is more sensitive to the well width than that of the transition.

(ii) In terms of application potential: the current findings reveal that the magneto-optical response probed via the ODMPR effect in the asymmetric Gaussian GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs QWs can be finely tuned using a range of independent control parameters, thereby creating a robust framework for adjusting magneto-optical characteristics. The key aspects of structural confinement serve as the main design factors: widening the well results in a shift of the intersubband resonance toward lower photon energies and a reduction in FWHM, which is critical for the development of narrowband mid- and far-infrared photodetectors as well as high-resolution THz sensors. Conversely, increasing the well depth leads to a blueshift and greater FWHM broadening; when well depths exceed approximately 100 meV, the FWHM increases at an accelerated pace, especially noticeable in intersubband transitions. This presents a viable pathway for achieving broadband absorbers and quick-response electro-optic components. Additionally, the composition of the alloy offers another level of control: lower Al-compositions promote narrow-FWHM and coherence-sensitive operations, while higher concentrations result in increased scattering induced broadening, enhancing broadband functionality and tolerance to spectral detuning. A further significant finding is that intersubband transitions are much more responsive to various control parameters compared to intrasubband transitions. This allows for a targeted approach in device design, where intersubband interactions become an effective tuning channel, while intrasubband interactions act as a stable reference point. Notably, the ODMPR response can be influenced by external and environmental factors. For instance, the temperature of the electron–phonon system affects both the peak intensity and the FWHM, making it possible to achieve thermally tunable gain and bandwidth. Additionally, hydrostatic pressure contributes to strain-induced narrowing of the FWHM and sharpening of the spectrum. An external electric field can allow for voltage-controlled adjustments of both intensity and FWHM. The interplay of these diverse parameters facilitates simultaneous and adaptable control over resonance energy, FWHM, and phonon-assisted carrier dynamics, all within a single structure. Due to the realistic asymmetric Gaussian confinement, these multi-parameter control mechanisms can be effectively applied to the experimentally feasible GaAs/AlλGa1−λAs heterostructures, offering practical design insights for intersubband photodetectors, THz resonators, and other quantum well-based optoelectronic devices.

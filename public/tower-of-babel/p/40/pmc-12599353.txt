Description and phylogenetic analysis of the complete mitochondrial genome of Reeves’ muntjac, Muntiacus reevesi Ogilby, 1839 (Artiodactyla; Cervidae), from Chongqing, China
Authors: Xiaojuan Cui, Lizhen Zhong, Jianjun Peng, Chengzhong Yang, Qian Xiang, Yalin Xiong, Xia Wang, Haifeng Zheng, Ling Shen, Xudong Yuan, Lixia Chen, Chenglun Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractReeves’ muntjac (Muntiacus reevesi Ogilby, 1839) is a small deer species native to China, primarily residing in the mountainous regions of the southeastern part of the country. In this study, the mitochondrial genome of a Reeves’ muntjac specimen from Chongqing, southeastern China, was sequenced. The genome consists of 16,356 base pairs (GenBank: PP506676), including 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNA genes, two rRNA genes, OL, and control region. Phylogenetic analysis disclosed significant genetic differences between the Reeves’ muntjac from Chongqing and those previously reported, indicating that its population may possess rich genetic diversity or there might be undiscovered, geographically isolated groups.

Introduction

The Reeves’ muntjac (Muntiacus reevesi Ogilby, 1839), also known as barking deer or rib-face deer, is a small-sized deer native to China. Males possess short, unbranched antlers on bony pedicles; females lack antlers but retain concealed vestigial pedicles (Chapman and Harris 1991) (Figure 1). It exhibits a preference for deciduous or coniferous forests with rich understory vegetation and also frequents scrub habitats. Originating from the southeastern part of China, it has been introduced to England (Chapman 2021). In mainland China, its distribution range extends from Guangdong and Guangxi in the south to Gansu and Shaanxi in the north, encompassing the extensive subtropical zones of the Zhujiang and Yangtze River basins (Figure 2). Despite being classified as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List, in China, due to overhunting and habitat destruction, its population has been on a downward trajectory (Timmins and Chan 2016). In the Red List of China’s Vertebrates, it has been assessed as Near Threatened (Jiang et al. 2016). Currently, the available genomic data on Reeves’ muntjac remains relatively limited, which restricts in-depth comparative analyses. This study sequenced and functionally annotated the complete mitochondrial genome of a M. reevesi individual from Chongqing, southeastern China. Furthermore, integration of mitochondrial genome data from other muntjac individuals in public databases enabled a comprehensive analysis of their phylogenetic relationships. This will contribute to the mitochondrial genome data of Reeves’ muntjac, assist in clarifying its taxonomic position, and establish a solid foundation for conserving its genetic resources.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and DNA extraction

The sample was collected from the forelimb skin tissue of a naturally deceased Reeves’ muntjac in Qijiang, Chongqing Municipality (28°53′13.13″N, 106°24′49.93″E). The specimen was deposited in the Laboratory of Animal Group, School of Life Science and Health, Hunan University of Science and Technology (https://www.hnust.edu.cn/, Xiaojuan Cui, xjcui@hnust.edu.cn) with the voucher number XJ-CQ230730-1.

Mitochondrial genome sequencing, assembly, and annotation

Total genomic DNA was extracted from the sample using the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Germany). The complete mitochondrial genome sequence of M. reevesi was sequenced using a whole-genome shotgun approach. Genomic libraries with 400 bp insert size were constructed and subjected to paired-end (2 × 150 bp) sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq platform. Clean reads were obtained after performing quality control on the raw data using fastp (https://github.com/OpenGene/fastp), and subsequently assembled using the SPAdes v3.15.4 (Bankevich et al. 2012) and GetOrganelle v1.7.7.0 (https://github.com/Kinggerm/GetOrganelle) software. High-depth sequences were selected based on sequencing depth and subsequently aligned against the NCBI nt database with BLASTn v2.2.31+. Mitochondrial genome sequences within each assembly product were then identified through sequence homology analysis. The splicing results were integrated using the Bandage v0.8.1 (https://github.com/rrwick/Bandage) software (Figure S1). Subsequently, the results were corrected with pilon v1.18 (Walker et al. 2014) software to obtain the final mitochondrial sequence. The complete mitochondrial genome sequence was uploaded to the MITOS web server (http://mitos.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de/) for functional annotation (Bernt et al. 2013), with the genetic code set as vertebrate and other parameters configured according to the default settings. Finally, the mitochondrial whole-genome circular map was drawn using the cgview visualization software (Alikhan et al. 2011).

Phylogenetic analysis

This study conducted a phylogenetic reconstruction based on the newly sequenced mitochondrial genome of M. reevesi (GenBank accession number PP506676), combined with mitochondrial genome data from 24 Artiodactyla species (Table S1), using the Tragulus kanchil as the outgroup (Hassanin et al. 2012). Multiple nucleotide sequence alignment of 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs) was conducted using MAFFT (Katoh and Standley 2013), followed by Bayesian inference (BI) for phylogenetic tree construction with MrBayes (Ronquist et al. 2012) within the PhyloSuite v1.2.3 pipeline (Zhang et al. 2020). Next, the iTOL platform (https://itol.embl.de; Letunic and Bork 2024) was utilized for topological refinement and visual enhancement, resulting in a phylogenetic tree that displayed evolutionary relationships.

Results

Description of the mitogenome

The complete mitochondrial genome of M. reevesi sequenced in this study showed a length of 16,356 bp (Genbank accession number PP506676). The genome consists of 13 PCGs, 2 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes, 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, OL, and D-loop (Table S2). Among these genes, one protein-coding gene (nad6) and eight tRNA genes are located on the light strand, while the remaining genes are encoded on the heavy strand (Figure 3). The base composition of the complete mitochondrial genome is as follows: A accounts for 33.13%, G for 13.46%, C for 24.38%, and T for 29.04%. Hence, the content of A + T (62.16%) is higher than that of G + C (37.84%). The newly sequenced complete mitochondrial genome exhibits a gene number, composition, and arrangement similar to those of other members of the family Cervidae (Hassanin et al. 2012; Liu et al. 2019). The lengths of the 13 PCGs range from 201 bp (atp8) to 1821 bp (nad5), respectively. Among 13 PCGs, except ATA type for nad2, nad3, and nad5, the remaining PCGs used ATG as the initiation codon. Most PCGs used the complete TAA type (nad1, nad4l, nad5, nad6, cox1, cox2, atp6, and atp8) as the stop codon, while some used the incomplete T - - (nad4 and cox3), TA – (nad3), AGA (cob), and TAG (nad2) types. The 2 rRNA genes were s-rRNA (960 bp) and l-rRNA (1567 bp), which were located between trnF and trnL2. TrnV separated s-rRNA from l-rRNA. OL is 31 bp (located between trnN and trnC) and control region is 921 bp (located between trnP and trnF).

Phylogenetic analysis

The phylogenetic tree (Figure 4) clearly illustrates the evolutionary relationships among selected families and genera within Artiodactyla. Cervidae forms a distinct monophyletic group, significantly separated from Moschidae, Bovidae, and the outgroup, supporting its evolutionary independence. Within Cervidae, species from various genera demonstrate pronounced clustering patterns. Species of the genus Muntiacus (Muntiacus muntjak NC_004563; Muntiacus crinifrons NC_004577; M. reevesi) cluster into a single clade, indicating close phylogenetic relationships among muntjac species. Extremely short branches between different Reeves’s muntjac specimens (PP506676, MZ895085, KY117561, NC_004069, and AF527537) reflect limited genetic divergence and evolutionary conservation, further supporting the monophyly of this genus. Rangifer tarandus (NC_007703), Hippocamelus antisensis (NC_020711), Hydropotes inermis (NC_011821), Capreolus pygargus (MN813763), Axis porcinus (MH443790), Elaphurus davidianus (NC_018358), Cervus elaphus (AB245427), and Elaphodus cephalophus (MN251783) each occupies a distinct phylogenetic branch, reflecting the evolutionary diversification across genera within the Cervidae family.

Discussion and conclusion

To date, research on Reeves’ muntjac has been relatively limited, and comprehensive data regarding its entire mitochondrial genome are lacking. Only recently have studies been released on the genome sequence of Reeves’ muntjac (Ewart et al. 2024) and macroecological research on the Reeves’ muntjac (Liu et al. 2025). In this study, we achieved the sequencing of the whole mitochondrial genome of a Reeves’ muntjac from Chongqing, in southeastern China, accompanied by detailed annotations of the genome and a phylogenetic analysis to determine the taxonomic position of the specimen. The results show that four M. reevesi individuals in the database formed a tightly clustered monophyletic group within the phylogenetic tree. Their genetic distances were lower than those observed between the Reeves’ muntjac from Chongqing, in southeastern China (PP506676) and the database individuals, suggesting a closer genetic relationship among the four database specimens. These results underscore the importance of further collecting and analyzing genetic data of Reeves’ muntjac and related deer species. Such information is crucial for enhancing species identification and assessing genetic diversity, which are fundamental for biodiversity conservation and the development of effective conservation strategies. Future studies should focus on expanding the sample size by including Reeves’ muntjacs from various geographical regions and increasing the representation of other closely related species. This approach will enable a more comprehensive understanding of the genetic architecture and evolutionary relationships of Reeves’ muntjac.

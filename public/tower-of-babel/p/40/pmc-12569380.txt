A vibration analysis of an acoustic mass sensor structure with a c-axis tilted ZnO thin film based on silicon
Authors: Jing Liu, Xiangyang Li, Xiaomin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A theoretical study was conducted on the free vibration and forced vibration behaviors of a piezoelectric bulk acoustic mass sensor featuring a c-axis tilted ZnO thin film on silicon using piezoelectric elasticity theory. The purpose is to establish the relationship between c-axis inclination angle and sensor performance. It operates in either thickness-extensional mode or thickness‐shear mode. The inclination angle of the c‐axis is intricately connected to the operating modes, frequency characteristics, and output admittance spectrum. Key results show: (1) Characteristic distributions of extensional and shear displacements across the thickness, and (2) Numerical simulations revealing distinct admittance spectrum patterns for different angles. Furthermore, mass-loading effects on resonant frequency shifts were quantified across different inclination angles. In conclusion, strategic control of the c-axis tilt angle enables precise optimization of resonant modes and mass sensitivity—critically motivated by the need for tunable high-sensitivity sensors in biochemical detection—providing critical design guidelines for enhanced acoustic sensors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-21413-8.

Introduction

With the development of Micro-Electro‐Mechanical Systems and the progress of piezoelectric thin film technology, AlN or ZnO piezoelectric thin films have been successfully deposited on silicon. A high‐performance thin film bulk acoustic resonator (FBAR), which has the characteristics of high frequency (up to 1–10 GHz), small size, and high energy conversion efficiency, was developed. It has been rapidly applied in the field of communication and the Internet and has been extensively studied by scholars1–4.

Regarding the FBAR, of which the piezoelectric film is hexagonal crystals such as ZnO/AlN with the c-axis along the normal direction, it typically operates in thickness extensional vibration mode. A voltage is applied in the thickness direction to generate longitudinal acoustic waves along that direction. If this FBAR operates in a liquid environment, the longitudinal wave energy will be radiated into the liquid, resulting in energy dissipation of FBAR and affecting its performance. However, shear waves do not produce compressive motion, so shear vibration has high sensitivity in a liquid environment. By combining this characteristic with different materials and structures, researchers have greatly expanded the range of applications of FBAR, not only in communications but also in the fabrication of highly sensitive and miniaturized acoustic sensors5–10. FBAR has shown good prospects in the field of sensors, and researchers have carried out a series of experiments and theoretical studies. Gabl et al. proposed a 2 GHz biosensor system for DNA and protein molecular detection. Its sensitivity is much higher than that of quartz crystal microbalance (QCM)5. Benetti et al. reported an FBAR chemical sensor with a frequency of about 1.6 GHz for the detection of low hydrogen, carbon monoxide, and ethanol concentrations11. Qin et al. studied a two‐mode thin film bulk acoustic resonator with c‐axis tilt and gave the angle for the exciting and propagating pure shear bulk acoustic wave. The effects of tilt angle and material properties on bulk acoustic waves were analyzed in detail. The sensitivity of the thin film bulk acoustic resonator as a mass sensor was discussed12. Wu et al. used the Christoffel equation to theoretically analyze the propagation characteristics of bulk acoustic waves in ZnO thin films. It was shown that different c‐axis angles would lead to different acoustic modes and properties13.

Generally, for a hexagonal piezoelectric thin film with c-axis tilt, an electric field applied on the film can excite both longitudinal mode and shear mode. The pure shear mode can be excited under certain conditions. However, the theoretical analysis of FBAR with c‐axis tilt gives the acoustic and mechanical characteristics, which are complex and difficult to solve, and the theoretical results are less and scattered.

Previous studies mainly focused on piezoelectric thin films, and less on FBAR of silicon-based structures14–16. However, for resonators and sensors, piezoelectric thin films need silicon or other supporting layers, which play an important role in the propagation of bulk acoustic waves in piezoelectric thin films. In this paper, the c‐axis tilted sensor based on silicon structure, which will be replaced by a c‐axis tilted sensor in the following text, is studied. Using piezoelectric elasticity theory, the multi‐layer structure of a c‐axis tilted sensor is analyzed. Its free and forced vibrations are studied, and its frequency characteristics are given. After this, the influence of mass coverage on the frequency characteristics of a c‐axis tilted sensor is analyzed.

Although the relative peak value of the first mode is small at θ = 45°, the frequency shift caused by the additional mass is obvious. Therefore, the frequency of the first mode is suitable for mass sensing for θ = 30°, 45°. For θ = 89.99°, the frequency shift caused by the change of additional mass layer is not obvious, and the sensitivity of the mass sensor in this frequency range is not high.

Conclusion

The free vibration and forced vibration of the c-axis tilted sensor are studied theoretically in this paper. The vibration modes and displacement distributions of the c‐axis tilted sensor in the thickness direction are simulated effectively by numerical simulation. The numerical results show that there are three resonant frequencies at different dips in the frequency range (1.0-1.8/2π) GHz. They work in the thickness‐extensional mode or the thickness‐shear mode. The inclination of the c‐axis and resonant frequencies have effects on vibration modes, which should be fully considered in the design of acoustic mass sensors.

For forced vibration without considering additional mass, when the inclination angle is small, the main mode of the c-axis tilted sensor is the thickness‐extensional vibration, and the conductance is the largest. With the increase of inclination angle, there are two modes of thickness‐extensional and thickness‐shear. With the decrease of thickness‐extensional vibration, the peak value of conductance decreases.

For forced vibration with additional mass, the numerical results show that the frequency of the second mode is suitable for mass sensing for θ = 0.01°, 15°, and 75°. For θ = 30°, 45°, it is suitable for selecting the frequency of the first mode for mass sensing. For θ = 89.99°, the frequency shift caused by the change of additional mass layer is not obvious, and the sensitivity of the c-axis tilted sensor in this frequency range is not high.

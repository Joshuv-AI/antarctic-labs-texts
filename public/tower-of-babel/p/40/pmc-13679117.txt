Non-invasive diagnostic evaluation of urinary exosomal let-7c cluster expression in bladder cancer using machine learning approaches
Authors: Sukhad Kural, Lalit Kumar, Abhay Kumar Pathak, Shweta Singh, Garima Jain, Mahima Yadav, Yashasvi Singh, Ujwal Kumar, Sameer Trivedi, Manjari Gupta, Sakshi Agarwal, Vibhav Gautam, Parimal Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBladder cancer (BCa) diagnosis typically relies on invasive cystoscopy, which is effective but costly and uncomfortable. Urinary microRNAs (miRNAs), especially exosomal ones, are promising non-invasive biomarkers due to their stability in biological fluids and disease specificity. The let-7c cluster, owing to its tumor-suppressive function and frequent dysregulation in BCa, has emerged as a promising candidate for diagnostic evaluation. This study assessed the diagnostic potential of the urinary exosomal let-7c cluster through Machine Learning (ML)-integrated miRNA profiling, offering early proof-of-concept for its role in BCa classification.MethodsUrine samples were collected from 66 participants, including 50 BCa patients and 16 healthy controls (HC). Exosomal miRNAs were isolated and quantified using Quantitative Real-Time-Polymerase-Chain-Reaction (qRT-PCR). Statistical analysis and hypothesis tests were conducted to explore the nature and diagnostic relevance of individual biomarkers. A logistic regression classifier was applied to evaluate both the combined and differential diagnostic capabilities of the selected biomarkers. Accuracy, precision, recall and AU-ROC scores were used to assess model performance. Bioinformatics analysis was performed to identify pathways associated with the features prioritized by the ML models, ensuring their relevance to BCa.ResultsStatistically significant differentiation was observed between BCa patients and HC, with miR-99a-5p (p = 0.013, AU-ROC = 0.71) demonstrating preliminary diagnostic performance. In contrast, let-7c-5p (AU-ROC = 0.65, p > 0.1) and miR-125b-5p (p = 0.087, AU-ROC = 0.64) exhibited non-significant trends, showing weaker discrimination. The logistic regression ML model achieved 80.0% accuracy (AU-ROC = 0.86, recall = 90%) in distinguishing cancer from HC and 53.3% (AU-ROC = 0.63) for miRNA-only grade classification. With clinical variables added, accuracy increased to 73.3%, but the AU-ROC remained modest at 0.61, indicating limited discriminatory ability in high-grade versus low-grade. Across Ta–T2, miR-99a-5p showed clearer separation, whereas let-7c-5p and miR-125b-5p exhibited weak stage-related differences. Bioinformatics analysis confirmed the biological relevance of these miRNAs in BCa-related pathways, including PI3K–Akt, p53, NF-κB and RAS/MAPK signaling, with hub genes such as TP53, MYC, EGFR and CCND1 identified.ConclusionUrinary let-7c cluster miRNAs show preliminary diagnostic potential when evaluated using ML models and may serve as a non-invasive supportive tool to conventional diagnostic approaches. However, these results should be regarded as proof-of-concept evidence that has to be validated in larger, independent cohorts because of the small sample size, low subgroup power and class imbalance.

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BCa) accounts for around 3–4% of cancers in men, ranking as the fourth leading cancer in males and the ninth most common globally. Despite advances in management, it is characterized by high recurrence rates and poor prognosis in advanced stages [1]. The risk factors are diverse and include tobacco use, exposure to dyes and chemicals, chronic urinary tract infections (UTIs) and chronic irritation such as from catheters or stones [2, 3]. Painless gross hematuria is the most common symptom seen in 80–90% of patients, but only 10% of those with hematuria are diagnosed with BCa [4]. BCa is a heterogeneous disease, with more than 90% of cases arising as urothelial carcinoma [5]. Based on the extent of muscle invasion, BCa is categorized as non–muscle invasive BCa (NMIBCa; stages Ta, T1, CIS) or muscle-invasive BCa (MIBCa; stage T2 and above) [6]. Approximately 75% of cases are non-muscle invasive and are primarily managed with transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT), chemotherapy and intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) therapy that contribute to favorable therapeutic results [7, 8]. However, MIBC is typically treated with a combination of radical cystectomy (removal of the bladder) and systemic chemotherapy (including cisplatin-based regimens), sometimes followed by adjuvant therapy [9]. Although cystoscopy and urinary cytology remain the gold standards for BCa diagnosis, their invasiveness and low sensitivity for low-grade tumors limit early detection [10], highlighting the need for non-invasive molecular diagnostics.

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are a group of membrane-bound vesicles secreted by all eukaryotic cells and found in a variety of biofluids, including urine, functioning as molecular cargo carrying diverse biomolecules such as mRNAs, microRNAs (miRNAs), lncRNAs and proteins, making them great source of biomarkers [11]. Exosomes are a subtype of EVs between 30 and 150 nm in size derived from endosomes that transfer biomolecules to facilitate cell communication and play a role in cancer progression and metastasis [12]. Approximately 99.96% of urinary exosomes are released by cells from the kidney, urinary tract lining and male urogenital tract [13]. This direct exosome shedding by urological tumors into the urine makes them highly promising candidates for sensitive and urological cancer biomarkers [14].

Liquid biopsy has emerged as an effective tool for early screening, detection of tumors and for monitoring disease progression. Over the last decade, miRNAs have emerged as promising candidate for liquid biopsy based diagnostics due to their stability in biofluids, tissue specificity and key regulatory roles in cancer-associated pathways [15]. miRNAs are short, ~ 22-nucleotide, noncoding RNAs that modulate gene expression after transcription by binding to the 3′ untranslated regions (3′ UTRs) of target mRNAs, leading to repression of translation or mRNA degradation [16]. Gene expression is largely influenced by miRNAs and their imbalance is frequently observed in cancer [17]. Some miRNAs promote tumor development when overexpressed by suppressing genes that inhibit growth (e.g., miR-21, which targets PTEN and PDCD4 to enhance cell proliferation and survival in urologic cancers, including BCa [18]; miR-155, which inhibits DMTF1, contributing to BCa progression [19]), while others that act as tumor suppressors are often downregulated and allow cancer cells to proliferate unchecked (e.g., the let-7 family, whose reduction leads to RAS overexpression and promotes tumorigenesis in BCa [20].

The let-7 family of miRNAs is known for its tumor-suppressive role and is significantly involved in regulating the cell cycle, differentiation and apoptosis [21]. As a tumor suppressor miRNA, let-7c is well known to regulate oncogenes such as HMGA2 [22], H-Ras [23] and c-MYC [24], thereby contributing to suppression of tumor progression [25]. The let-7c/miR-99a/miR-125b cluster, located on chromosome 21, is a well-conserved group of miRNAs known for their tumor-suppressive functions across a range of cancers [26], including BCa, hepatocellular carcinoma, prostate cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, mesothelioma and other malignancies. These miRNAs collectively target multiple cancer pathways such as mTOR, IGF1R and STAT3 modulating key processes like proliferation, apoptosis and cellular stemness [26–28]. In BCa, these miRNAs are typically downregulated in tumor tissues, which is consistent with their role in inhibiting cancer progression [29, 30]. Given their ability to regulate multiple oncogenic pathways and the strength of existing evidence, the let-7c cluster was selected for analysis in BCa as a promising candidate non-invasive biomarker for exploratory diagnostic evaluation.

Machine Learning (ML), a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI), enables systems to learn from data without being explicitly programmed. Instead of relying on explicit coding, ML models leverage algorithms to uncover patterns in data, which enables predictive and decision-making capabilities. Supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised, and reinforcement learning form the four categories of ML models, each fulfilling different functions [31]. As these models process more data, they improve over time, making ML a powerful tool across many domains, including healthcare [32, 33].

In biological research, ML is revolutionizing the way we analyze complex datasets such as genomics, proteomics and medical imaging [34]. Deep biological datasets like sequencing are often large, noisy, and nonlinear, which is where ML shines. For example, ML has been widely used in cancer diagnostics, particularly in biomarker discovery, by analyzing RNA sequencing or microarray data [35]. However, despite its successes, supervised learning models especially in analyzing Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT-PCR) data are still underutilized. Few studies have effectively leveraged qRT-PCR data with supervised learning to improve diagnostic accuracy [36]. This study presents one of very few studies with integrative demonstration of supervised ML applied directly to qRT-PCR derived profiling in cancer, highlighting a clinically scalable and cost-effective approach that may contribute to ongoing efforts to explore translational liquid biopsy approaches.

Linear regression, which assumes a straight forward relationship between inputs and outputs, is frequently used but has its limitations [37]. Logistic regression, in contrast, is a widely used supervised model that performs effectively on binary classification tasks, such as identifying diseased versus healthy conditions [38]. This algorithm estimates the probability of a binary outcome using a logistic function, making it ideal for analyzing diagnostic data like qRT-PCR results [39]. While logistic regression is simple, it can be very powerful when combined with proper feature selection and normalization. Additionally, its interpretability makes it valuable in biological research, where understanding the significance of each feature is essential.

To strengthen these findings, we conducted bioinformatics analyses including target prediction, overlap with BCa-specific genes, functional enrichment and PPI network construction. These analyses demonstrated that the dysregulated miRNAs identified in our cohort are linked to established pathways of BCa progression, thereby reinforcing their biological relevance. By integrating experimental data with in silico validation, our study provides supportive evidence for the development of a non-invasive, exosome-based diagnostic framework for BCa.

Research gap

Even though research on BCa has advanced significantly, non-invasive diagnostic techniques have not been widely adopted as alternatives to invasive procedures in clinical settings and their current application is still primarily complementary rather than independent. For example, a number of urine-based tests that have received U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval, including Nuclear Matrix Protein 22 (NMP22) BladderChek, Bladder Tumor Antigen (BTA) stat and UroVysion Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH), which proves potential and clinical relevance of non-invasive testing [40]. However, these tests have drawbacks, such as NMP22 and BTA stat shows high false positives in the presence of hematuria (blood in urine) or inflammation, while UroVysion FISH, though more effective in HG disease, performs poorly in LG cases and is also costly and technically demanding, which has limited their use to supportive tests rather than replacements for cystoscopy, with urine cytology often used as a complementary test [41]. Although there are few studies which investigated the let-7c cluster in BCa [30, 42], not enough is known about its diagnostic aspect in urinary exosomes. Moreover, there has been limited study of qRT-PCR derived miRNA expression data examined using supervised ML models in the current literature, which has concentrated chiefly on high-throughput platforms like microarrays and RNA sequencing for biomarker discovery [36, 43]. In this study, we explored the potential of applying supervised ML to qRT-PCR derived urinary exosomal miRNA data, an approach being more practical and accessible than sequencing-based platforms.

Research questions

This study examines the urinary exosomal miRNAs, particularly the let-7c cluster as non-invasive biomarkers for BCa by combining molecular diagnostics, bioinformatics and ML. The following research questions guided the investigation: (1) Assess whether let-7c cluster members (let-7c-5p, miR-99a-5p, miR-125b-5p) are differentially expressed in BCa patients compared with healthy controls (HC), as well as across different tumor grades and stages; (2) Determine the diagnostic performance of qRT-PCR derived miRNA expression data using supervised ML models; (3) Explore associations between miRNA expression and clinical or lifestyle variables such as tumor grade, stage, size, and tobacco use; (4) Elucidate the biological pathways and molecular targets linked to these miRNAs through integrative bioinformatics analyses (PPI networks, GO/KEGG enrichment). The purpose of raising such questions is to integrate classical biomarker discovery methods with advanced ML approaches through the development of an affordable and accessible diagnostic model for BCa. By utilizing exosomal miRNA expression, this study aims to facilitate the development of precision-based, non-invasive diagnostics with clinical relevance.

Methods

Study design

This prospective cohort study, involving 50 patients with BCa and 16 HC, was performed at the Department of Urology in association with the Departments of Radiology, Pathology, and the Centre for Genetic Disorders. The study was conducted following approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (EC/3411), with written informed consent obtained from all enrolled participants. A total of 50 adult patients (≥ 18 years) with newly suspected primary bladder tumors on clinical evaluation and/or imaging or cystoscopic assessment were enrolled between October 2022 and April 2024. Eligible patients were scheduled for primary TURBT and provided pre-operative urine samples before surgical intervention. Final inclusion in the BCa group was limited to patients with histopathologically confirmed BCa after TURBT. A total of 16 age- and sex-matched HC were recruited for comparative analysis of urinary let-7c cluster expression and were eligible if they had no known history or current evidence of urological malignancy, gross hematuria, urinary tract infection, stone disease or prior pelvic malignancy. Exclusion criteria included tumors arising in bladder diverticula, incomplete medical documentation, history of TURBT, prior chemoradiotherapy, impaired renal function and earlier pelvic malignancy. Patients who did not consent to participate in the study were also excluded.

Sample collection and exosomal RNA isolation

Approximately 25–30 mL of midstream early-morning urine was collected pre-operatively from patients undergoing TURBT using sterile urine collection vials. Samples were transferred to the laboratory within two hours of collection in a temperature-maintained (4 °C) insulated box, without any intermediate storage or freezing. Before exosomal RNA isolation, urine samples were centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 10 min to remove cells and debris, followed by a second centrifugation of the cell-free supernatant at 2,500 rpm for 10 min to remove residual debris and bacterial cells. A volume of 10 mL from each sample was utilized for exosomal RNA extraction using the Norgen Urine Exosome RNA Isolation Kit (Cat#47200, Norgen Biotek Corporation). Briefly, exosomes were enriched from cell-free urine supernatant using the kit-provided Slurry B1 and Binding Buffer A, after which exosomal RNA was isolated through vesicle lysis, isopropanol-assisted binding, spin-column purification and washing according to the manufacturer’s protocol. A total of 20 µL of Elution Solution A (kit-provided) was used to elute RNA, whose concentration and purity were measured with a NanoDrop OneC UV-Vis Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) before being stored at − 80 °C for downstream analyses.

Complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis and qRT-PCR for exosomal miRNA

Using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Scientific, Catalog #K1622, USA), complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis of exosomal RNA was performed. Custom stem-loop primers were introduced to the reverse transcription reaction in order to attain specificity against miRNA targets. Table 1 summarizes the full set of primer sequences utilized in the study. qRT-PCR experiments were performed on the QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems/Thermo Scientific, USA). 1 µL of diluted 1:5 cDNA, 6.25 µL of Maxima SYBR Green/ROX qPCR Master Mix (2X; Catalog #K0221, Thermo Scientific, USA), 1 µL of each forward and reverse primer (10 µM) and nuclease-free water for the final volume were all included in each 12.5 µL reaction. The thermal cycling technique included a 10 min denaturation stage at 95 °C, followed by 40 amplification cycles of 15 s at 95 °C and 1 min at 60 °C. The overall experimental workflow is illustrated in Fig. 1A.

Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA)

Urinary exosomes were characterized using Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA) on the NanoSight LM10 system (Malvern Instruments, UK) operated with NTA software version 2.3, which records and analyzes videos of particles undergoing Brownian motion in the laser beam to determine their size distribution and concentration. Urine samples were processed and exosomes were isolated according to the manufacturer’s protocol provided in the Urine Exosome RNA Isolation Kit (Product #47200, Norgen Biotek Corporation; Appendix B). Prior to analysis, exosome suspensions were diluted approximately 1:10 (v/v) in 1× PBS (pH 7.4) to achieve an optimal particle concentration for measurement. Measurements were performed at 22 °C with a camera shutter speed of 30 ms and frame rate of 30 frames per second.

Electron microscopy

Exosomes used for Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FE-SEM) analysis were isolated from urine using the Urine Exosome RNA Isolation Kit (Product #47200, Norgen Biotek Corporation; Appendix B). The final eluate obtained after isolation consisted of 200 µL of exosomes suspended in 1× PBS. For imaging, a 1:10 dilution of the eluate was prepared and 8–10 µL of the diluted sample was dispensed onto a pre-cleaned glass slide and allowed to air-dry at room temperature. After drying, the sample was sputter-coated with a thin gold layer for 90 s using the Q150R ES Plus sputter coater (Quorum Technologies) to enhance conductivity. Imaging was performed using the ZEISS GeminiSEM 560, operated with the SmartSEM software (version 7.04). Multiple fields were examined to ensure representative visualization of the exosome population and images demonstrating consistent exosomal morphology were selected for analysis and presentation.

Statistical analysis

Data preprocessing

During the preprocessing phase, we identified and handled NULL values by replacing them with the median value in the qRT-PCR derived urinary exosomal miRNA expression data. All these operations were performed on the Ct values to ensure data consistency and readiness for analysis.

Statistical analysis

We have employed several hypothesis testing approaches to assess differences and associations between clinical features and biomarkers. We have utilized the Mann-Whitney U test, a nonparametric approach to evaluate the hypothesis. This test is beneficial since it does not rely on a normal distribution, making it versatile and effective for assessing differences between two independent groups. We also have included the Chi-square (χ²) test to examine the association between categorical variables across the study groups. This test is particularly useful for evaluating whether observed differences in frequency distributions arise by chance, without assuming any underlying parametric distribution. Another parametric test, the independent samples t-test to compare the mean values of continuous variables between two groups. This test evaluates whether the difference in group means is statistically significant under the assumption of normally distributed data.

We used the ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic) curve to assess the prediction potential of different characteristics in identifying HC and BCa classes. The ROC curve is a graphical tool for determining how well a binary model works at various threshold levels. The model’s performance at different threshold settings is plotted using two critical metrics: True Positive Rate (TPR) and False Positive Rate (FPR). The AUC (Area Under the Curve), which depicts the area below the ROC curve, is used to assess the model’s overall ability to correctly identify and discriminate between HC and BCa.

ML-model development

In this study, the Logistic Regression ML model has been employed as a baseline classifier for classifying BCa from HC, and also differentiating HG and LG cancer patients in BCa using demographic and qRT-PCR dataset to capture hidden and complex patterns. In order to find out the differential capability of biomarker and clinical characteristics using logistic regression models, we have developed two models on different sets of samples with different dependent labels. The first model for distinguishing HC from BCa patients using raw miRNA expression values (let-7c-5p, miR-99a-5p and miR-125b-5p) and ratio-based composite features (let-7c-5p/ miR-99a-5p, miR-99a-5p/miR-125b-5p and miR-125-5p/let-7c-5p) derived from these miRNAs as features to classify in two classes. In this model we have not included any clinical variable in the development. Further in the second model, it utilises the same miRNAs and correspondence ratios as a feature along with the clinical features (Age, Gender, Staging, VI-RADS Score, Tobacco Addiction, Alcohol Intake, Hypertension, Diabetes, Tumor Size and Number of Tumor) to differentiate between HG and LG classes. For both classification tasks, the dataset was divided into training and testing subsets using a 70:30 train-test split. The split was performed in a stratified manner according to the dependent class label to preserve class distribution in both the training and test sets. A fixed random seed (42) was used during data splitting and model development to ensure reproducibility. All preprocessing steps were performed using the training data only to avoid data leakage. Missing-value imputation, where required, was fitted on the training data and then applied to the test data using the same learned parameters. Feature scaling was also performed by fitting the scaler only on the training data, followed by transformation of both training and test sets. The test set was kept unseen during model fitting and preprocessing parameter estimation.

The model does not utilise any hyperparameter tuning for the classification task which further ensures the reproducibility of the proposed model. The default hyperparameter settings of the scikit-learn implementation were used to maintain simplicity and reproducibility, and to reduce the risk of overfitting in a relatively small dataset. Logistic Regression was used as the baseline classifier, while additional models were evaluated only for exploratory comparison. In addition to the independent 30% test-set evaluation, 10-fold cross-validation was performed on the training data to assess the stability of model performance. The test set was not used during cross-validation or model selection and was reserved only for final performance estimation.

To assess the models predictive performance we have utilized some performance matrix such as accuracy: Accuracy measures the overall correctness of a model. It is the fraction of all predictions (both positive and negative) that the model got right; precision: Precision focuses on the quality of positive predictions. It tells us, out of all the samples the model labeled as positive, how many were actually positive. High precision means fewer false alarms; recall: Recall focuses on the completeness of positive detection. It tells us, out of all the actual positive samples, how many the model successfully identified; F1 score: It is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It balances the two, giving a single measure that is useful when both false positives and false negatives are important. Detailed steps of the model development process have been described in Fig. 1B.

Bioinformatics analysis

The target genes of 3 miRNAs were predicted using mirTarbase and Mirbase database. By removing duplicate entries all target genes list were merged, named as target gene panel (TGP) of miRNAs. Moreover, a panel of Bladder Cancer-specific genes (BCSG) panel were prepared using GeneCards, KEGG and STRING databases. The BCSG and TGP panels were crossed to identify common target genes (CTG) with Venny 2.0 (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/index2.0.2.html). These CTG represent the list of genes that have crucial roles in BCa molecular regulation and are regulated by let-7c cluster miRNAs. These were further explored for enrichment analysis, protein–protein interaction network construction, and pathway analysis to understand their roles in disease mechanisms. ShinyGO v 0.85.1 software (https://bioinformatics.sdstate.edu/go/) were used to analyze the functional enrichment of the CTG panel for Gene Ontology(GO) and KEGG pathways.The significance of enrichment was assessed using hypergeometric test, and p-values were corrected for multiple testing using the False Discovery Rate (FDR) method. Pathways with adjusted p-values (FDR) < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched. Cytoscape software was used for visualization and further analysis, allowing for hub genes extraction based on their crucial roles in the network.

The study cohort consisted of BCa patients (n = 50) irrespective of tumor grade, age and sex-matched HC individuals (n = 16). The BCa cohort consisted of both HG (N = 36) and LG (N = 14) groups. Additionally, the BCa cohort was categorized by tumor stage into T1 (n = 32), T2 (n = 12) and Ta (n = 6). Their clinical and pathological features are summarized in Table 2.

Characterization of urinary exosomes

The prepared urine exosome sample had 3.07 × 10⁸ particles/mL, as determined by NTA. The vesicle size ranged from 50 to 300 nm, with the majority of the population distributed between 100 and 150 nm. The most prevalent population of exosomes was represented by the mode particle size, which was 151 nm. These results indicate that the isolated urinary exosomes exhibited a characteristic small vesicle size profile consistent with their suitability for downstream molecular analyses (Fig. 2A, B). FE-SEM imaging confirmed the successful isolation of urinary exosomes, which appeared as distinct, bright, spherical nanoparticles distributed across the substrate. The vesicles exhibited a size range consistent with exosomal dimensions, with representative measured diameters approximately between 96 and 179 nm, supporting the expected small extracellular vesicle size profile (Fig. 2C). The population was uniformly dispersed with no evidence of aggregation or structural collapse, suggesting good sample integrity following isolation and preparation.

Let7-c cluster gene expression pattern and correlation in BCa vs. HC

As shown in Fig. 3, urinary exosomal let-7c-5p, miR-99a-5p, and miR-125b-5p exhibited a downward trend in BCa patients compared with HC. Among them, miR-99a-5p showed statistically significant downregulation (P = 0.013), whereas let-7c-5p and miR-125b-5p demonstrated non-significant decreases (P = 0.076 and P = 0.087, respectively). Collectively, these data suggest a consistent but not statistically uniform trend of reduced expression across the let-7c cluster in BCa.

To explore the scope of ML in this analysis, we employed logistic regression - a ML based approach. The cohort was divided into training (70%) and testing (30%) multiple features including individual miRNA: let-7c-5p, miR-99a-5p and miR-125b-5p; and ratio based features: miR-99a-5p/miR-125b-5p, miR-125b-5p/let-7c-5p and let-7c-5p/miR-99a-5p were used. The model achieved an AU-ROC of 0.86, indicating improved discriminative power as compared to individual features. The curve (Fig. 5B) shows a steep ascent towards the top-left quadrant, reflecting a high true positive rate (sensitivity) with minimal false positive classifications. The model achieved an accuracy of 80%, indicating it correctly classified 80% of the samples. With a precision (Precision indicates how accurately the model labels positive cases by showing how many of the predicted positive samples were truly positive, reflecting the model’s ability to avoid false positives.) of 75% and a recall of 90%, it identified all true positive cases without missing any of it (Fig. 5C). The F1-score of 81% reflects a good balance between precision and recall. The confusion matrix shows no false negatives but includes some false positives.

To ensure the reliability and generalizability of the developed models, a 10-fold cross-validation approach was employed as presented in Fig. 6. In this method, the dataset was randomly partitioned into ten equal subsets; in each iteration, nine subsets were used for training and the remaining one for testing. The process was repeated ten times so that each subset served as a test fold once. The average accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score across the folds were computed to minimize bias due to random train–test splits. This validation approach confirmed the model’s stability and consistent performance across different data partitions. However, given the limited cohort size and absence of an external validation dataset, these results should be interpreted as preliminary and not as evidence of clinical generalizability.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that urinary exosomal miRNAs from the let-7c cluster, particularly miR-99a-5p, can distinguish BCa patients from HC with moderate accuracy in this preliminary cohort. This is potentially clinically meaningful given the persistent challenge of differentiating BCa from benign urological conditions such as cystitis or urinary tract infection, which often present with overlapping symptoms like hematuria [47]. Current gold-standard diagnostic tools cystoscopy and urinary cytology remain invasive or insensitive, underscoring the need for reliable, non-invasive biomarkers. By showing that qRT-PCR derived miRNA data can be enhanced with ML to detect subtle expression changes, our findings contribute to efforts to translate molecular signatures into practical diagnostic tools.

Comparison with current diagnostic modalities

In routine clinical evaluation, patients with hematuria are assessed with urinalysis, imaging, cytology and cystoscopy [48]. Imaging modalities such as ultrasound and CT urography can identify larger or infiltrative tumors but lack resolution for flat or early-stage lesions and MRI is mainly used for staging rather than initial diagnosis [49]. Ultrasound, although commonly used as an initial imaging tool, shows a sensitivity of ~ 63% and a specificity of ~ 99% for larger bladder masses but remains limited for detecting small or flat lesions [50]. CT urography, considered the preferred imaging test for hematuria evaluation, demonstrates higher diagnostic performance with reported sensitivity of 79–93% and specificity of 83–99%, yet it still cannot replace direct visualization and has reduced sensitivity for carcinoma in situ. Multiparametric MRI provides value in local staging but is not routinely utilized for initial diagnosis due to limited accuracy for early or flat tumors. Urine cytology, while highly specific (~ 78–86%), has poor sensitivity for LG tumors (~ 16%) and only moderate sensitivity for HG disease (~ 84%) [51]. These values are consistent with broader estimates showing specificity approaching ~ 90–95% but sensitivity only ~ 20–30% for low-grade lesions and ~ 80% for high-grade tumors. Several FDA approved urine assays, including UroVysion FISH, ImmunoCyt/uCyt+ (Immunocytology test), NMP22 and BTA tests, have attempted to fill this gap but are limited by variable sensitivity and frequent false positives in benign conditions [52]. Reported performance shows that UroVysion reaches ~ 72% overall sensitivity (with > 75% in high-grade disease but only ~ 40% in low-grade cases), ImmunoCyt/uCyt+ achieves ~ 75% sensitivity and ~ 78% specificity, NMP22 demonstrates ~ 56% sensitivity and ~ 88% specificity, and BTA assays show ~ 64–65% sensitivity with ~ 73–77% specificity, often affected by false positives in conditions such as UTIs, stones, or hematuria. Although cystoscopy remains the clinical gold standard with sensitivity of 87–100%, it is invasive and may still miss carcinoma in situ or early flat lesions. Our results suggest that exosomal miRNAs, when analyzed with ML, might complement existing methods, although this requires larger comparative studies. Given that this is a proof-of-concept study, the findings provide an early indication of diagnostic potential and should be interpreted alongside the well-recognized variability of current urinary biomarkers and imaging techniques.

Expression patterns and diagnostic performance

We observed consistent downregulation of the let-7c-5p/miR-99a-5p/miR-125b-5p in BCa patients compared with HC, with miR-99a-5p emerging as a potential diagnostic candidate requiring external validation across grade and stage analyses. Let-7c-5p and miR-125b-5p also showed downregulation, though less prominently, but may hold value in ratio-based features that reduce sample variability and capture biologically relevant interactions. Correlation analysis provided further support for this observation: in HC, miR-99a-5p and miR-125b-5p demonstrated a tendency for co-expression, whereas let-7c-5p was largely independent, suggesting distinct regulatory mechanisms. These coordinated patterns were attenuated in cancer samples, consistent with disruption of normal regulatory interactions. Such findings highlight why ratio-based and multimodal features are advantageous in diagnostic modeling. In addition, the altered correlation patterns suggest a loss of coordinated regulation in the cancer state, highlighting potential changes in molecular interactions between healthy and malignant conditions. This observation is consistent with the broader concept that miRNAs may act either as oncogenic or tumor-suppressive regulators depending on their molecular targets and cellular context [53].

Relation to prior studies

Our results both align with and diverge from prior studies. Spagnuolo et al. described significant upregulation of urinary let-7c in HG NMIBC, particularly T1 tumors, with strong stage-discrimination performance (AU-ROC = 0.80) [30]. While their study focused on prognostic risk stratification following BCG treatment in HG NMIBC, we observed downregulation of the Let-7c cluster in BCa across a broader clinical spectrum. Although stage-specific discrimination in our cohort was modest, these discrepancies may reflect differences in study design, cohort size or clinical context (prognostic vs. diagnostic focus). Importantly, by integrating expression ratios with ML, we extended the exploration of the cluster’s diagnostic classification potential beyond its previously reported prognostic utility. Consistent with this, Zhang et al. demonstrated that urinary miR-99a and miR-125b are significantly downregulated in BCa, achieving AU-ROC values of 0.800 and 0.813, respectively, with a combined AU-ROC of 0.876, underscoring their diagnostic utility as non-invasive biomarkers [42]. In our study, miR-99a-5p emerged as the most consistent biomarker associated with disease presence, while let-7c-5p contributed more as a relatively low-expressing but stable biomarker within ratio-based features. In contrast to its role as a primary classifier in previous prognostic approaches, let-7c-5p in our study serves a more refined purpose as a stable reference point within ratio features. This diverse functional role of let-7c-5p aligns with our earlier work, where we introduced our in-house algorithm for reference miRNA selection and identified let-7c-5p as a highly stable urinary exosomal miRNA across multiple urological conditions [54, 55].

The downregulation of the let-7c cluster has been frequently observed in various cancer types, where its loss is commonly associated with tumor-suppressive functions and poor clinical outcomes [26, 56–58]. Our findings are consistent with previous research, revealing that let-7c-5p is downregulated in BCa. However, its diagnostic ability was weaker to that of miR-99a-5p and miR-125b-5p, which were more effective at distinguishing BCa patients from HC. Previous studies have similarly reported reduced urinary expression of let-7c-5p in BCa, reinforcing its role as a tumor suppressor [59, 60]. In contrast, Spagnuolo et al. ‘s findings of let-7c-5p upregulation in HG NMIBC further emphasize the context-dependent variability of its expression patterns [30]. miR-99a-5p emerged as the most promising diagnostic marker in our study, a finding supported by multiple studies demonstrating its reported downregulation in BCa tissues and urine [42, 61–63]. Additionally, miR-125b-5p has been consistently reported as downregulated in BCa, strengthening its tumor-suppressive role [42, 64–67].

ML integration

Logistic regression was chosen for its interpretability, suitability for exploratory analysis with small datasets and ability to generate probability estimates relevant to clinical decision-making. Bitin et al. employed a multi-modal approach, combining miRNA data with clinical variables and achieving an AU-ROC of 0.85. While their study utilized a broader set of miRNAs and clinical data, we demonstrated similar diagnostic accuracy (AU-ROC = 0.86, accuracy 80%) and clear separation between BCa patients and HC with moderate grade classification through a more focused approach using qRT-PCR derived miRNA data and logistic regression. The inclusion of molecular and clinical variables improved diagnostic precision, though this may have been influenced by sample size and class imbalance. The individual AU-ROC values of single miRNAs for stage discrimination were very low, further emphasizing the need for integrated models. These results should therefore be considered proof-of-concept rather than clinically validated. Further optimization using larger, balanced cohorts and more advanced algorithms such as ensemble or deep learning approaches may enhance performance, particularly for grade and stage classification.

Biological validation through bioinformatics

To ensure interpretability and biological relevance, we conducted integrative bioinformatics analyses. The functional enrichment analysis revealed associations of miR-99a-5p with PI3K–Akt, mTOR, and FGFR3 pathways, miR-125b-5p with p53, NF-κB, and HIF-1 signaling, and let-7c-5p with RAS/MAPK and cell cycle regulation.These interactions primarily show negative regulatory effects, as the let-7c cluster miRNAs are well-established tumor suppressive regulators that inhibit critical oncogenic targets within cancer related signaling pathways. Consistent with this role, let-7c-5p negatively regulates the RAS/MAPK and cell-cycle signaling nodes by targeting HMGA2, MYC, and RAS family members. miR-99a-5p functions as a potent inhibitor of the PI3K–Akt/mTOR and FGFR3 signaling axis, while miR-125b-5p modulates key components of the p53, NF-κB, and HIF-1 pathways, exerting repressive effects on oncogenic and stress-responsive signalling. let-7c cluster miRNAs were consistently downregulated in our BCa cohort, leading to enhanced activation of cancer related pathways.These pathways play central in BCa progression, underscoring the functional plausibility of our findings. PPI network analysis identified hub genes such as TP53, MYC, STAT3, EGFR, IGF1R and CCND1, reflecting the interplay of oncogenes and tumor suppressors in BCa biology. The coexistence of both stimulatory and inhibitory regulators illustrates why single miRNAs have limited discriminatory power, and why combined, ML-enabled signatures are needed to capture net biological effects. GO enrichment highlighted processes including DNA damage response, EMT, and immune modulation, further linking the cluster to key hallmarks of BCa. By directly regulating important oncogenic pathways like FGFR3 and mTOR, miR-99a-5p functions as a tumor suppressor biologically [68, 69], thereby enhancing its biological significance and translational potential as a credible non-invasive biomarker. Collectively, these results imply that while urinary levels of the let-7c cluster may differ depending on tumor grade, disease stage or methodological considerations, our study’s consistent downregulation, which is consistent with other reports, highlights the biological relevance and exploratory diagnostic potential of this cluster in BCa.

Limitations and future directions

This study has several limitations. The single-center cohort was modest in size, comprising 66 participants in total, including 50 BCa patients and 16 HC, limiting statistical power for grade and stage-specific analyses and reducing generalizability. After stratification by tumor grade and stage, subgroup sizes became even smaller, particularly for low-grade tumors and Ta-stage disease, which further limits the statistical stability of subgroup-level analyses. Validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is required. Furthermore, the dataset had a class imbalance (72% of tumors were high-grade) which could have affected model performance and required more balanced cohorts in the future. Such imbalance may have restricted the model’s ability to learn discriminative features of low-grade tumors, partially explaining the limited grade-specific separation. Furthermore, as this work represents a proof-of-concept study, its findings should be interpreted as preliminary and exploratory in nature. Accordingly, the subgroup-specific findings and ML-based performance estimates should not be interpreted as clinically generalizable at this stage and require confirmation in larger, independent validation cohorts. Additionally, the limited three-miRNA panel without preliminary high-throughput screening might have missed out on potentially more effective biomarkers, highlighting the necessity of more comprehensive discovery-phase methodologies in subsequent research. Although this study focused on the urinary exosomal let-7c cluster as a targeted validation of previously identified candidates, we acknowledge that other exosomal miRNAs or nucleic acid species may also contribute to tumor-associated signals. Broader high-throughput profiling, coupled with functional validation, could refine biomarker panels and clarify mechanistic roles. Biological heterogeneity in urinary exosomes, which carry material from both malignant and non-malignant cells, also remains a challenge. This biological heterogeneity may further explain the limited discrimination of tumor grades and stages. Standardized protocols for exosome isolation, normalization and qRT-PCR analysis will be essential for reproducibility across studies. Another limitation is the absence of an external validation cohort and the lack of comparison with benign urological conditions such as cystitis or BPH, as well as other symptomatic non-malignant conditions including urinary tract infection, prostatitis, urolithiasis, non-malignant hematuria and urethritis, which restricts conclusions regarding specificity in actual clinical settings. This limitation is consistent with previous liquid biopsy findings, which shows that exosome-based urine biomarkers require standardized pre-analytical procedures, reproducible assay pipelines and prospective multicenter validation before clinical translation [70]. From a modeling perspective, external validation is necessary to assess preliminary clinical applicability and future work should explore more advanced ML frameworks. Finally, our cross-sectional design precluded evaluation of prognostic utility; longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether these miRNAs can predict recurrence, progression, or treatment response. Another limitation of this study that should be addressed in future prospective designs is the lack of longitudinal follow-up, which made it impossible to assess recurrence or progression. Integration with other modalities, such as mpMRI or VI-RADS, may further improve diagnostic precision and clinical adoption. At the same time, the biological heterogeneity of urinary exosomes carrying material from both malignant and non-malignant cells remains a challenge for reproducibility. Future studies should therefore focus on methods and tools to enrich tumor-derived exosomes. These results suggest that urinary exosomal miRNA–based diagnostics, when combined with ML techniques, have the potential to improve BCa detection and stratification, although their clinical applicability requires further validation. This approach may complement existing invasive procedures by contributing additional molecular information, pending further validation. Although this represents a significant advancement, the low AU-ROC emphasizes the need for further optimization. This could be achieved by adding more features to the model or adopting advanced model frameworks, such as ensemble methods or deep learning techniques, which could potentially enhance diagnostic accuracy. Additionally, the limited three-miRNA panel without preliminary high-throughput screening might have missed out on potentially more effective biomarkers, highlighting the necessity of more comprehensive discovery-phase methodologies in subsequent research.

Conclusion

This study provides preliminary proof of concept that urinary exosomal miRNAs, particularly miR-99a-5p, can contribute to BCa detection when analyzed within a ML framework. By integrating molecular and clinical features, our approach demonstrated preliminary discrimination between BCa & HC only and highlighted the value of ratio-based and multimodal features for diagnostic refinement. While current performance reflects the limitations of sample size and scope, these findings reinforce the promise of urinary exosomal miRNAs as non-invasive biomarkers and establish a foundation for larger validation studies. However, these findings should be viewed as preliminary and exploratory, with the generalizability of the results limited by the small sample size and imbalance in tumor grade. Future efforts should focus on expanding biomarker panels, including clinically relevant benign urological control cohorts, standardizing analytical pipelines and combining molecular signatures with imaging and clinical parameters to advance toward clinically evaluable diagnostic tools.

CERG-01 ADULT BRAIN TUMORS : ABOUT 46 PATIENTS OPERATED IN THE NEUROSURGERY DEPARTMENT OF THE NIAMEY NATIONAL HOSPITAL
Authors: Habi Alizeta Nikiema, Ibrahim Assoumane Issa, Aminath Bariath Kelani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesThe management of brain tumors in resource-limited countries poses enormous challenges. The objective was to determine the epidemiological, clinical and therapeutic profiles of patients operated on for brain tumors at the Niamey National Hospital.Methodologythis is a descriptive study with retrospective collection including adults operated on for brain tumors from January 2018 to September 2023 followed by an anatomopathological examination.Results46 cases were collected. These were mainly patients in the age group of 49 to 58 years. The female gender was predominant. Among the antecedents, there was exposure to radiation in 15% of patients, active smoking in 15%, and 9% had familial ATCD of brain tumor. At the time of consultation, the general condition was good for most patients. Signs of intracranial hypertension were prominent in almost all patients. A third of patients had seizures at the time of diagnosis. All patients underwent brain imaging. 70% of patients had a macroscopically total excision, 22% a subtotal excision. Postoperative complications such as dysarthria, hemicorporal motor deficit and facial deficit were recorded in 7% of patients. Nearly half of the lesions were grade 1 meningiomas. Four patients required additional chemotherapy and one, radiotherapy. In the immediate post-operative period, there are 4% deaths. The average length of hospitalization is 20 days.Conclusionbrain tumors are seen in both adult men and women with varying ATCD. Their diagnosis is based on the clinic and aided by the paraclinic. Surgical excision constitutes the main part of the treatment.

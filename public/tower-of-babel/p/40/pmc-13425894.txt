Multi-omics analysis reveals the aging mechanism during Baobaoqu storage for the production of Wuliangye Baijiu
Authors: Qingchun Luo, Xi Li, Jianghua Li, Pengju Zhao, Jian Chen, Xuejun Lei, Yanping Lu, Lin Deng, Kangzhuo Yang, Jian Su, Dong Zhao, Jia Zheng, Xinrui Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The quality of Baobaoqu directly influences the quality of Baijiu. In this study, metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics and metabolomics were utilized, combining machine learning with a multi-layer perception neural network model and the Shapley additive explanations approach, to analyze the metabolic mechanisms in Baobaoqu of varying quality. The results indicated that normal Baobaoqu contained more flavor compounds, while premium Baobaoqu contained more active microorganisms and enzymes. During storage, the number of differential proteins between premium and normal Baobaoqu increased, reaching 504 and 77, respectively. In premium Baobaoqu, cellulase expression gradually decreased, while that of ethanol metabolic enzymes increased; the opposite trend was observed in normal Baobaoqu. These differences were primarily attributed to variations in the proportions of Aspergillus and Thermoascus. The findings of this study indicate that a suitable combination of premium and normal Baobaoqu is necessary and provides a scientific basis for optimizing the Baobaoqu aging conditions and enhancing Baijiu quality.

Introduction

Traditional fermented Chinese Baijiu, commonly known as Chinese liquor or spirit, is the national alcoholic drink of China and one of the six most famous distilled spirits globally (Youqiang et al., 2022). Over 2000 distinct flavor compounds have been identified in Baijiu, bestowing it with a unique flavor profile that sets it apart from other fermented beverages (J. Zheng et al., 2022). Daqu, enriched with microorganisms and their byproducts, is the world's first artificially cultivated substrate. The advancement of Daqu production techniques is an impressive achievement of ancient Chinese civilization, and the incorporation of Daqu is a distinctive feature of Chinese Baijiu and marks a significant divergence between Eastern and Western distilled alcohol (J. Zhang et al., 2022). Daqu serves as the singular raw material for Baijiu brewing in China.

Daqu serves as the catalyst for saccharification, fermentation, and aroma production, while also supplying essential materials for Baijiu brewing, and is created through an intricate procedure (Fan et al., 2020). After one month of fermentation, Daqu should be stored for aging, an important part of Daqu production (Fan et al., 2019). Gaining insights into the microbial succession and metabolic processes during this period can aid in quality control. Previous studies have indicated that the maturation phase enhances the aroma of sesame-flavored Daqu (Niu et al., 2025). During the storage of Daqu, complex alterations take place in its physicochemical properties, microbial community composition, and enzyme activity (Guan et al., 2021). Reports indicate that the physicochemical parameters of medium-temperature Daqu generally stabilize following three months of storage (Dong et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2024). Consequently, investigating the microbial succession patterns during the initial three months of Daqu storage is highly important for managing its quality.

Currently, the reported methods for detecting microorganisms in Daqu are mostly DNA-based molecular techniques. Huang et al. examined the diversity of community structures and functions among three varieties of Daqu using high-throughput sequencing and metagenomic techniques (Y. Huang et al., 2024), and Zhang et al. conducted a metagenomic analysis of the microbial community profiles in Baijiu Daqu (Jing Zhang et al., 2022). Yang et al. revealed the contributions of microbes to the metabolic network responsible for flavor development in medium-temperature Daqu starters through metagenomic analysis (Y. Yang et al., 2021). However, these molecular techniques are constrained to DNA-based methods, which cannot distinguish among active, inactive, and deceased microorganisms (Simon-Soro et al., 2014). This limits our comprehension of the dynamic microbial community in Daqu and its metabolic roles.

Moreover, while many microorganisms have been identified in stored Daqu (Guan et al., 2021; L. Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024), the active microorganisms and enzymes and their relationships are still unknown. It is essential to underscore that the constraints of metagenomic information, which relies on DNA from both viable and non-viable cells, mean that taxonomic analysis derived from metatranscriptomics provides a more accurate representation of the active microbial community compared to metagenomic data (Yu & Zhang, 2012). Metatranscriptomics serves as an excellent method for investigating the microorganisms in Daqu, as it can directly analyze mRNA from various environments and microorganisms and offers insights into not only the composition of microbial communities but also the active members and the enzymes they specifically express (Yi et al., 2019). The study of Daqu's enzymatic profile and its variations is also of great significance. Daqu plays a vital role as a saccharifying and fermenting agent, closely associated with the numerous enzyme systems it contains. Macroproteomics is an important technical approach for investigating enzymatic profiles (Y. Yang et al., 2024).

In this study, metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics and metabolomics were initially utilized, followed by machine learning with a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) neural network model (Pearson, 2021) combined with the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) approach (Y. Liu et al., 2025), to analyze the differences in flavor compounds, active microorganisms, expressed enzymes and metabolic mechanisms during the storage of Baobaoqu—a typical example of medium-temperature Daqu—of varying quality. In order to thoroughly investigate the microbial populations utilizing a division of labor (MDOL) model (Ma et al., 2025), and given that raw materials are converted into flavor compounds within the Baobaoqu microbial community, we gathered the metabolic pathways associated with substrate degradation that result in flavor production, while assessing the contributions of microorganisms within each functional unit. This research offers essential insights into the active microorganisms, functional enzymes and metabolic mechanisms of Baobaoqu, potentially enhancing the understanding of the crucial role played by Baobaoqu samples of varying quality in the storage process during the brewing of Chinese Baijiu.

Materials and methods

Baobaoqu sample collection

Every Baobaoqu sample was sourced from Wuliangye Co., Ltd., situated in Yibin City, Sichuan Province, China, during the months of June and July 2024. We collected Baobaoqu samples on days 1, 15, 30, 60 and 90 of storage at two different quality levels—premium Baobaoqu and normal Baobaoqu. Samples were collected at each time point from three distinct storage Qu-rooms. In each room, the samples were prepared by grinding and mixing three randomly chosen Baobaoqu bricks from that specific room. In total, 90 Baobaoqu bricks were selected to yield 30 representative samples, which were then divided into two groups for further analysis: one for physicochemical parameter and flavor substance assessments (which were stored at 4 °C), and the other for profiling microbial communities or enzymes (which were stored at −80 °C).

Analysis of volatile compounds by HS-SPME/GC–MS

The analysis of volatile compounds was conducted utilizing headspace solid-phase microextraction coupled with Headspace Solid-Phase Microextraction/Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS). Each sample was examined in triplicate, adhering to the methodology described by Qingchun Luo et al., with certain modifications made to the procedure (Qingchun et al., 2023). In summary, a sample weighing 3.0 g was mixed with 10 mL of saturated NaCl solution in a headspace vial, accompanied by 7.5 μL of a 4-octanol solution (407 mg/L), which acted as the internal standard.

Volatile compounds were extracted using an 80 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS fiber at a temperature of 60 °C for 30 min. These volatiles were detected via GC–MS utilizing a DB-WAX column (60 m in length, 0.32 mm inner diameter and a film thickness of 0.25 μm; J&W Scientific). The carrier gas was ultra-high-purity helium, used at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. The oven temperature was held at 40 °C for 5 min, heated to 230 °C at 5 °C/min, and then held at this temperature for 10 min. The volatiles were identified and semi-quantitatively analyzed according to a previous method (Qingchun et al., 2023).

The relative concentration of volatile flavor compounds in Baobaoqu was determined based on the peak area ratio between the internal standard and the flavor compounds. To explore the differences in metabolites across various Baobaoqu samples, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was conducted. Furthermore, Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed utilizing SIMCA-14.1 software from Umetrics, Sweden. Metabolites with a Variable Importance for the Projection (VIP) value greater than 1.0 were designated as differential metabolites.

Total RNA extraction

Following the manufacturer's guidelines, total DNA and RNA were extracted from Baobaoqu using the FastDNA SPIN kit for soil (MP Biomedicals, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and the RNA PowerSoil Total RNA Isolation Kit (Gentone Biotech, China) after the samples were ground with liquid nitrogen. The isolated DNA and RNA were frozen at −80 °C for subsequent analysis. The concentration and purity of the RNA were measured using a NanoDrop ND1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

RNA library construction and sequencing

For metatranscriptomic analysis, following quality assessment, quantification, and removal of rRNA, RNA fragmentation, cDNA synthesis and cDNA library preparation were performed utilizing the Hieff NGS® Ultima Dual-mode mRNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina and MGI, Yeasen, Shanghai), in accordance with the manufacturer's protocol. Paired-end sequencing was also performed on the Illumina HiSeq 4000 platform. Raw metatranscriptomic sequences (average read length: 100 bp) underwent rigorous quality control, including the removal of adapter sequences, host or environmental contaminants, and low-quality reads (Q-score < 20). High-quality short reads from each sample were compiled using IDBA-TRAn software (Peng et al., 2013).

Extraction of total proteins and analysis of enzyme expression

The total protein content of the Baobaoqu during storage was determined using a reference method from the literature (Luo et al., 2025). The concentrations and contents of extracted proteins were all greater than 1.1 μg/mL and 100 μg, respectively. The extracted total proteins were sent to BGI genomics (Shenzhen, China) for macroproteomic analysis. Mass spectrometry data were collected for 30 microbial samples using a Q-Exactive HFX (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA) instrument. An iterative library search was performed using the MetaPro-IQ method (X. Zhang et al., 2016) to optimize the database. A Data-Dependent Acquisition (DDA) spectral library was constructed using MaxQuant software, and quantitative analysis of peptides and proteins was conducted using MSstats software.

Bioinformatics evaluation of the sequencing data

The raw sequencing data obtained from metatranscriptomic and metaproteomic analyses was preprocessed via a comprehensive multi-step process. For metatranscriptomic data, contigs shorter than 500 bp were excluded from subsequent analyses. Gene predictions were made on the contigs using MetaGeneMark (Zhu et al., 2010), and redundant genes were removed using CD-HIT (Fu et al., 2012) with an identity cutoff of 95%. In order to construct a gene abundance matrix, Salmon (Patro et al., 2017) software was used for quantification.

In order to generate annotation data for the metatranscriptomic and metaproteomic studies, gene protein sequences were aligned with various functional databases, such as antibacterial biocide and metal resistance genes database (BacMet), comprehensive antibiotic research database (CARD), Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG), evolutionary genealogy of genes: non-supervised orthologous groups (EggNOG), clusters of orthologous groups (COG), Swiss-Prot, and carbohydrate-active enzymes database (CAZy), employing double index alignment of next-generation sequencing data (DIAMOND) (Buchfink et al., 2015), while applying an E-value cutoff of 1e-5. Taxonomic annotation for metatranscriptomic and metaproteomic data was performed using the lowest common ancestor (LCA) algorithm provided by Kraken (Wood et al., 2019) and MEGAN (version 4.6), respectively.

Significant differences in features (Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species) across groups were identified through Wilcoxon's rank sum test utilizing abundance profiles (Matsouaka et al., 2018). Differentially enriched KEGG pathways were determined based on reporter scores (Patil & Nielsen, 2005). A reporter score threshold of 1.65 or greater was established for determining significance. Alpha diversity was assessed using the Shannon, Chao1 and Simpson indices, all calculated from relative abundance profiles utilizing an R package. Beta diversity was evaluated through both the Euclidean distance and Bray–Curtis distance measures (Bray, Curtis, T., & Departrnent, 1957). Statistical analyses employing the Wilcoxon rank test and the Kruskal–Wallis H test were performed using the R project. The interrelationships between functions and taxonomies were compiled based on the annotated functional and taxonomic overview of each uniprotein (Y. Yang et al., 2021). Additionally, PCA was conducted using R version 4.0.0 to explore variations in microbial and genetic compositions within Baobaoqu.

Assessment of physicochemical parameters

The physicochemical properties examined included moisture, acidity, and temperature. The moisture and acidity conditions of Baobaoqu were measured according to the procedures outlined in the Light Industry Standard of the People's Republic of China QB/T 4257–2011 (Mu et al., 2023). The moisture content was determined at 105 °C over a duration of 4 h using a gravimetric method (W. H. Liu et al., 2023), whereas the acidity was measured using a pH meter after a 30-min extraction of Baobaoqu powder with CO2-free water in a 1:10 ratio (Y. Huang et al., 2024). The temperature at the core of the Baobaoqu bricks within the Qu-rooms was recorded by inserting a stainless-steel thermometer into the Baobaoqu. The physicochemical indicators of the Baobaoqu include saccharifying capacity, fermentation capacity, liquefying capacity and esterifying capacity and were determined using the methods described in the reference literature (Luo et al., 2025).

Use of machine learning to conduct an in-depth analysis of the differences among Baobaoqu of varying quality

In order to conduct a more in-depth analysis of the differences in the flavor compounds, microbial community structures, and enzyme compositions of Baobaoqu of varying qualities, machine learning was utilized. An MLP neural network model (Pearson, 2021) was utilized, combined with SHAP for interpretation (Y. Liu et al., 2025). The network architecture is composed of three fully connected layers, featuring 64 neurons in the first layer, 32 in the second, and 16 in the third. BatchNorm was incorporated into each layer, and the ReLU activation function was used along with Dropout, with a Dropout rate of 0.3. The loss function was binary cross-entropy loss, and the optimizer was the Adam optimizer (Adaptive Moment Estimation), with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a weight decay of 1e−5. During model training, 60% of the dataset was taken as the training set, while both the validation set and the test set accounted for 20%.

Results and discussion

Effect of storage on composition of flavor compounds in Baobaoqu of varying quality

The quality of Baobaoqu has a direct impact on both the fermentation process and the quality of Baijiu (Han et al., 2023). Consequently, it is essential to conduct a thorough evaluation of the underlying factors contributing to Baobaoqu quality, with a primary emphasis on identifying flavor components. A total of 22 volatile compounds were identified during storage of premium and normal Baobaoqu, including eight alcohols, one aldehyde, three esters, one organic acid, two furans and six pyrazines (Table S1). This shows that the number of different volatile compounds in Baobaoqu decreased and stabilized after storage compared to the number observed during Baobaoqu production (Luo et al., 2025). The concentration of volatile compounds was greater in normal Baobaoqu than in premium Baobaoqu, which aligns with previously reported findings (Q. Zhang et al., 2022).

There were 16 common volatile compounds in stored premium and normal Baobaoqu (Fig. 1A). There were more volatile compounds in normal Baobaoqu than premium Baobaoqu (Fig. 1B). Hierarchical clustering analysis (Fig. 1C) and PCA (Fig. 1D) revealed distinctly different trends in volatile compound accumulation in Baobaoqu samples stored for various durations. All volatile compounds in Baobaoqu separated well when the supervised OPLS-DA model was used (Fig. 1E). A total of 13 volatile compounds significantly influenced the metabolic composition in the model, as indicated by a VIP value greater than 1. Among them, 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine, amyl alcohol, phenylethanol and 2,5-dimethylpyrazine are characteristic flavor compounds of normal Baobaoqu (Fig. 1E). Dimethylpyrazine has been demonstrated to significantly contribute to the aroma profile of Baijiu (Xiao et al., 2025). In Chinese Baijiu fermentation, the generation of amyl alcohol and other larger alcohols corresponds to decarboxylases (EC 4.1.1.-, EC 4.1.1.1) and dehydrogenases (EC 1.1.1.2, EC 1.1.1.1) (H. Wang et al., 2023). Phenylethanol was also found to be one of the top ten key aroma compounds contributing to aged Baijiu (P. To conduct a more in-depth analysis of the flavor differences between primary and secondary Baobaoqu, machine learning was employed using an MLP neural network model, combined with the SHAP method for interpretation. The results demonstrate that both the testing and validation accuracies of the model reached 100%. As shown in Fig. 1F, the most representative characteristic flavor components of normal Baobaoqu, indicated by SHAP difference values greater than zero, were 2,3-dimethylpyrazine, ethyl heptanoate, 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine, 2,5-dimethylpyrazine and 2-buten-1-ol. It has been reported that pyrazine compounds can enhance the aromatic characteristics of Baijiu (Xiao et al., 2025). Our experiments also identified pyrazine compounds as the most important for distinguishing between premium and normal Baobaoqu, with 2,3-dimethylpyrazine and ethyl heptanoate being the strongest markers. This study is the first to combine neural networks with SHAP to classify flavor substances in brewing starter cultures. Our findings suggest that normal Baobaoqu contains a greater array of flavor compounds compared to premium Baobaoqu. A greater understanding of the mechanisms behind the production of these flavor components and their associated specific microorganisms, particularly the community structure and trends in active microorganisms, is crucial.

Differences in the active microbial communities during the storage of Baobaoqu of varying quality

Active species in Baobaoqu of varying quality

A total of 348.91 Gbp of raw sequencing data comprising 1,998,082,470 reads was acquired, with 322.68 Gbp of clean data derived from 92.51% of the reads utilized for further analysis (Table S2). Following the removal of host sequences, 2,319,166 unique contigs were obtained, exhibiting an N50 length of 1185 bp (Table S2); subsequently, 439,982 unigenes were identified. To analyze gene interrelationships, Venn analysis was performed to observe the number of overlapping genes of different individuals. To investigate the composition of active microbial communities and conduct a differential analysis of species richness and diversity during the storage of Baobaoqu of varying quality, alpha-diversity indices (Chao1, Shannon and Simpson) were computed (Fig. S1A, B and C). The findings reveal significant variations in both the diversity and richness of active microbial species throughout the storage of both premium and normal Baobaoqu. In particular, on day 90 of storage, the Shannon and Simpson indices were significantly lower those at other time points, indicating that after 90 days of storage, the active microbial communities tend to become more concentrated. In addition, the Chao1 index of premium Baobaoqu was higher than that of normal Baobaoqu throughout the storage period, indicating that the diversity of active microorganisms in premium Baobaoqu is higher than that in normal Baobaoqu.

In total, 210 orders (426 families, 1150 genera and 2933 species) of active microorganisms were annotated for the stored Baobaoqu. The relative abundance of the top 10 orders (along with the top 30 families, top 30 genera and top 30 species) accounts for over 99.1% of all active microorganisms (specifically 97.4%, 95.4% and 92.7%, respectively). Premium and normal Baobaoqu were annotated with 185 and 183 orders, 362 and 365 families, 885 and 883 genera, and 1949 and 2012 species, respectively. These results indicate that fungi dominate the active microbial communities during storage of Baobaoqu of varying quality. The major shared genera (>1%) in both premium and normal Baobaoqu are Aspergillus, Thermothelomyces, Talaromyces, Enterobacter, Pichia, Weissella, Penicillium and Limosilactobacillus, totaling eight genera. Additionally, genera unique to premium Baobaoqu with abundances greater than 1% include Ascochyta, Psilocybe and Rhizoctonia (Fig. 2C). Ascochyta was recognized as an important fungus in the pit mud of strong-aroma Baijiu (Pu et al., 2022), indicating that Baobaoqu provides a significant microbial source for brewing pit mud environments. Rhizoctonia is capable of producing organic acids such as succinic acid, lactic acid and fumaric acid, which are critical for forming the distinctive flavors of Baijiu (Y. Li et al., 2023).

Genera unique to normal Baobaoqu with abundances greater than 1% include Staphylococcus and Cercospora (Fig. 2C). Studies have shown that Staphylococcus is a characteristic microorganism of black Daqu (Y. Wang et al., 2021) and indicate that controlling the levels of Staphylococcus is essential for improving Baobaoqu quality. Cercospora is a marker microorganism in the later stages of Daqu production; studies monitoring its levels indicate that it is retained in higher quantities during the storage of normal Baobaoqu, while its levels gradually decrease in premium Baobaoqu (Luo et al., 2025).

During the storage process, the top three active microbial genera in both premium and normal Baobaoqu were Aspergillus, Thermothelomyces and Talaromyces, collectively accounting for 65.3% to 90.1% of the total. This finding differs from previous reports, which identified Aspergillus as the dominant microorganism (P. Huang et al., 2023), but did not mention Thermothelomyces or Talaromyces. This study is the first to identify Thermothelomyces (7.1%–14.3%) and Talaromyces (3.6%–22.8%) as dominant active microorganisms during Baobaoqu storage. Thermothelomyces is known to produce a variety of thermostable enzymes, such as cellulase and phytase (Rohr et al., 2025), while Talaromyces can produce pectinase and cellulase (Crotti et al., 1999), which have a significant effect on the decomposition of brewing materials. Aspergillus accounts for 51.3% to 75.2% of the total during Baobaoqu storage, indicating that the content of Aspergillus in the active microbial composition tends to remain stable. This finding is consistent with reports (P. Huang et al., 2023) that highlighted Aspergillus as the dominant eukaryotic microorganism in Daqu. The latter is an important functional fungal genus in Daqu, capable of producing enzymes related to starch saccharification and protein hydrolysis, processes directly linked to the saccharification capability, esterification ability and flavor of Daqu (Y. Li et al., 2023), ultimately affecting the flavor of the Baijiu (Wu et al., 2020). This study identified Aspergillus as the primary dominant active microorganism in Baobaoqu, providing an essential microbial species foundation for Baobaoqu as a vital saccharifying and fermenting agent.

At the species level, there were 23 primary active microbial species (>1%), including Aspergillus fumigatus, Aspergillus chevalieri, Aspergillus oryzae, Thermothelomyces thermophilus, Talaromyces marneffei and Talaromyces rugulosus (Fig. 2D). The major species (>1%) shared by both premium and normal Baobaoqu were Aspergillus fumigatus, Aspergillus chevalieri, Aspergillus oryzae, Thermothelomyces thermophilus, Talaromyces marneffei, Talaromyces rugulosus, Aspergillus luchuensis, Weissella confusa, Enterobacter sp. T2, Limosilactobacillus fermentum, Pichia kudriavzevii and Penicillium oxalicum, totaling 12 species. Species unique to premium Baobaoqu with abundances greater than 1% were Ascochyta rabiei, Aspergillus flavus, Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, Psilocybe cubensis, Rhizoctonia solani and Aspergillus nidulans, while those unique to normal Baobaoqu with abundances greater than 1% included Staphylococcus gallinarum and Cercospora beticola (Fig. 2D).

It was evident that the relative abundances of the top 30 major species of active microorganisms varied during storage, with a greater microbial diversity in premium Baobaoqu. The PCA results (Fig. 2E and F) identified the differences in active microbial compositions following storage of Baobaoqu of varying quality and highlighted significant differences in species abundance between premium and normal Baobaoqu samples during storage. These results indicate a clear trend in the active microbial composition of premium Baobaoqu, which stabilized after 90 days.

To investigate the significant differences in active microorganisms during the storage of Baobaoqu of varying qualities (X. Yang et al., 2023), a statistical analysis of metagenomic profiles (STAMP) was conducted. According to the STAMP charts (Fig. 3A, B, C, D, E), the most significantly differing microorganism between premium and normal Baobaoqu throughout the storage process was Aspergillus fumigatus. Between days 1 and 60 of storage, the abundance of Aspergillus fumigatus in normal Baobaoqu was greater than that in premium Baobaoqu; however, by day 90, its abundance in premium Baobaoqu exceeded that in normal Baobaoqu. At 90 days of storage, the main active microorganisms in premium Baobaoqu included Aspergillus fumigatus, Talaromyces rugulosus, Talaromyces marneffei and Thermothelomyces thermophilus, while the main active microorganisms in normal Baobaoqu were Aspergillus chevalieri and Aspergillus oryzae. Aspergillus fumigatus has demonstrated a powerful ability to produce enzymes and convert alcohol (Gu et al., 2021; Mandlaa et al., 2024). These findings support previous results suggesting that Baobaoqu requires more than three months of storage to fully mature. Aspergillus oryzae, present in Daqu, is often used in the production of glucoamylase and, when applied to the starch in brewing materials, can generate various flavor components and precursor substances that are crucial for Baijiu production (C. Wang et al., 2020; Z. Zhang et al., 2024). To conduct an in-depth analysis of the differences in active microorganisms between premium Baobaoqu and normal Baobaoqu during storage, machine learning was employed. An MLP neural network model was utilized along with SHAP for interpretation. The results indicate that both the test accuracy and validation accuracy of the model reached 100%. As shown in Fig. 3F, the key active microorganisms in premium Baobaoqu included Thermothelomyces, Ascochyta, Zygotorulaspora and Nakaseomyces, with the former identified as the primary microorganism. Reports have indicated that Thermothelomyces thermophilus possesses heat-stable amylase (Yubo Wang et al., 2025). The main microorganisms in normal Baobaoqu were Saccharomyces and Kluyveromyces, which are capable of producing phenylethanol, a characteristic flavor compound in this starter. These results are consistent with the results of the flavor analysis.

This study represents the first application of a combination of neural networks and SHAP for the classification of active microorganisms in Daqu, demonstrating the successful construction of a high-precision classification model for active microorganisms and systematically identifying the main active microorganisms in premium and normal Baobaoqu. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of deep learning in microbial classification, providing a reference for further analysis of other types of Daqu.

Analysis of the correlation between microorganisms, flavor and physicochemical properties of Baobaoqu of varying qualities

In order to investigate the relationship between active microorganisms and flavor components, a correlation heat map was constructed (Gao et al., 2024), as shown in Fig. 3G. This heat map illustrates the Pearson correlation coefficients between various microbial species (such as fungi and bacteria) and multiple flavor components. Hierarchical clustering was used to group the rows (active microorganisms) and columns (flavor components), with Baobaoqu samples with higher similarity clustered together. The clustering revealed that pyrazine derivatives formed a unique group with several species of Aspergillus, indicating that they share common biosynthetic pathways, while the correlation heat map showed significant correlations between several fungal and bacterial species and specific flavor components. Among them, Aspergillus chevalieri exhibited a significant positive correlation with multiple flavor components, especially with furans (2,3-dihydrofuran and 2-ethylfuran), and Aspergillus nidulans exhibited a highly significant positive correlation with 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine. Kluyveromyces marxianus had a highly significant positive correlation with phenylethanol, while Talaromyces mameffei was significantly positively correlated with several pyrazine compounds. Amyl alcohol was positively correlated with various active microorganisms such as Kluyveromyces marxianus, Psilocybe cubensis, Rhizoctonia solani and Aspergillus flavus, while acetic acid showed a positive correlation with Staphylococcus gallinarum and Cercospora beticola, suggesting potential metabolic interactions. These findings underscore the role of key active microorganisms in Baobaoqu in the generation or regulation of flavor compounds during storage. The close association between Aspergillus species and pyrazine derivatives confirms their ability to produce nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds under heat stress conditions. These findings indicate that the composition of active microbial communities significantly impacts flavor characteristics, providing insights for the selection of specific strains for Baobaoqu applications.

Changes in active microorganisms during Baobaoqu storage were previously shown to be significantly influenced by environmental factors, including temperature, humidity and pH, which in turn led to various physicochemical properties, such as saccharification, fermentation, liquefaction and esterification abilities. These indicators were found to be closely related to the quality of Baobaoqu and the liquor it produced. As shown in Fig. S2A-G, these physicochemical properties were measured, with the saccharification function of Baobaoqu primarily determined by two indicators: saccharification power and liquefaction power (Xia et al., 2023).

During the first 30 days of storage, the saccharification power and liquefaction power of both premium and normal Baobaoqu initially increased and then decreased, stabilizing later on. Correlation analysis between the major active microorganisms (top 30 species) and these environmental factors and physicochemical properties (Fig. 3H) revealed that saccharification ability was most affected by temperature, followed by moisture content, with both the temperature and humidity of Baobaoqu being higher in the first 30 days of storage (Supplementary Fig. S2A, B). This is consistent with the observed changes in saccharification and liquefaction power indicators. Furthermore, saccharification ability was mainly determined by fungi, including dominant active microorganisms such as Aspergillus, Thermothelomyces and Talaromyces (accounting for more than 65% in total). Liquefaction ability, on the other hand, was influenced by both fungi and bacteria, with fungi playing a leading role, while the fermentation ability was negatively correlated with temperature. Some studies have suggested that fermentation ability is mainly associated with yeast (Yuan et al., 2025), which proliferates at moderate temperatures, consistent with the medium–high temperature conditions of Baobaoqu production.

Functional analysis of Baobaoqu of varying qualities

To understand the roles of active microorganisms, identical KEGG pathways of premium and normal Baobaoqu were annotated through metatranscriptomics, derived from a total of 79,523 unigenes. The biological metabolic pathway database constitutes a core component of KEGG, where metabolic pathways are categorized into different levels. Primary metabolic pathways are categorized into six principal groups (Fig. 4A): Metabolism (62.87%), Genetic Information Processing (18.65%), Cellular Processes (8.16%), Environmental Information Processing (3.93%), Human Diseases (4.59%) and Organismal Systems (1.80%) (Hui et al., 2024). In this investigation, the prevalence of secondary functional pathways within the KEGG database was assessed. The roles of macromolecules in Baobaoqu microorganisms were clarified by annotating the sequencing data and determining the quantity of specific genes present in the KEGG database. The findings demonstrate that the most abundant pathway type was the metabolic pathway, constituting 62.87%, which aligns with previous reports (Zhao et al., 2020). Among the secondary metabolic pathways, the relative abundances of global and overview maps (39.46%), carbohydrate metabolism (14.53%), amino acid metabolism (9.50%), energy metabolism (8.85%), cofactor and vitamin metabolism (6.61%) and lipid metabolism (5.80%) were relatively high. Annotated profile of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZys) and the contributing active microorganisms

The abundance of each protein associated with the CAZy enzyme family was statistically categorized into distinct modules via metatranscriptomics. The results indicated a total of 6924 enzymes, which included (in descending order of the number of transcriptional genes) 2823 glycoside hydrolases (GHs), 2298 glycosyltransferases (GTs), 1001 carbohydrate-binding modules (CBMs), 450 auxiliary activities (AAs), 309 carbohydrate esterases (CEs) and 43 polysaccharide lyases (PLs). The specific distribution of abundance and the number of each Baobaoqu are shown in Fig. 4B and C. Consistent with the number of transcriptional genes, enzymes annotated as GHs exhibited the highest abundance, followed by GTs, and the lowest abundance was for PLs. This indicated that Baobaoqu is an important saccharifying agent, consistent with a previous report (X.-W. Zheng et al., 2011). For these six types of enzymes, from 1 to 60 days of Baobaoqu storage, the number of transcriptional genes of enzymes showed a significant trend, reaching the lowest value on the 30th day and then increasing. After 60 to 90 days, this number essentially remained stable. Currently, Baobaoqu typically needs to be stored for 90 days before it can be utilized in the production of Baijiu (X.-W. Zheng et al., 2011). This study provides theoretical support for this production process.

To conduct a detailed analysis of the transcriptional gene annotation enzymes, the set of top 30 predominant transcriptional genes was classified and functionally annotated at the secondary level in the CAZy, and cluster analysis was also performed. As shown in Fig. 4D, the GH family included GH16, GH13, GH18, GH0, GH28, GH72, GH35, GH152, GH17, GH16–1, GH16–19, GH43, GH1, GH5 and GH76; the CBM family included CBM13, CBM50, CBM18 and CBM48; the GT family included GT8, GT2, GT4, GT35, GT20, GT1, GT31, GT95 and GT62; the AA family included AA2; and the CE family included 9CE and 4CE. The GH16 family mainly consists of 1,3(4)-β-D-glucanases, which can hydrolyze β-1,3- and β-1,4-glycosidic bonds. It has been reported that the 1,3(4)-β-D-glucanases in Chinese Baijiu Daqu exhibit high thermostability (L. Chen et al., 2020), which was consistent with the high-temperature environment during the Baobaoqu production process. The contributing active microorganisms in family GH16 are mainly A. fumigatus, A. oryzae, Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Schizosaccharomyces pombe, while A. fumigatus and A. oryzae were the most abundance active species throughout Baobaoqu storage. This indicates that the abundance of enzymes is consistent with that of the related active microorganisms.

Machine learning was utilized to conduct an in-depth analysis of the differences in transcripts between premium and normal Baobaoqu during storage. An MLP neural network model, combined with the SHAP method for interpretation, was used. The results show that both the testing and validation accuracy of the model reached 100%. As shown in Fig. 4E, the enzymes that contributed most significantly to premium Baobaoqu were GH76, GT48, CBM73, GH13 and GT41, while those contributing most significantly to normal Baobaoqu were CBM73, GH0, GT41, GT48, GH5 and GH16–19. Enzymes from the GH family effectively decompose raw materials such as starch into small molecular compounds, which are then available for microorganisms to produce Baijiu. The GH76 family, primarily composed of mannan endo-1,6-alpha-mannosidase (EC 3.2.1.101) and 6-phosphofructokinase 1 (EC 2.7.1.11), exhibits strong glycosyl hydrolase activity. The active microorganisms sourced from the GH76 family included Aspergillus fumigatus, Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Schizosaccharomyces pombe. Reports have indicated that Aspergillus has the ability to produce EC 32.1.101 (Adav et al., 2012), which is consistent with the findings of this study. The GH13 family, a major representative of α-amylase and primarily composed of α-amylase (EC 3.2.1.1) and α-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.20), demonstrated strong starch-decomposing capabilities (L. Chen et al., 2021). The main active microorganisms from the GH13 family include Bacillus licheniformis, Bacillus subtilis, Candida albicans, Lactiplantibacillus plantarum and Levilactobacillus brevis. Additionally, GH5, primarily comprising cellulase (EC 3.2.1.4), beta-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.21) and cellulose β-1,4-cellobiosidase (EC 3.2.1.91), exhibits strong cellulose-decomposing capabilities. Active microorganisms from the GH5 family are Aspergillus fumigatus and Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Studies have shown that the former can efficiently convert cellulose into glucose (X. A. Chen et al., 2010), and it was also identified as the predominant active microorganism during Baobaoqu storage, laying the foundation for the metabolism of the raw materials in Baobaoqu and the production of flavor components.

To investigate the differences in expressed proteins between premium and normal Baobaoqu during the storage process, an analysis of upregulated and downregulated proteins was conducted. The results, as shown in Fig. 5C, indicate that prior to 30 days of storage, the numbers of both upregulated and downregulated proteins were in a state of flux. However, after 60 days of storage, the number of upregulated proteins significantly increased compared to that of downregulated proteins, reaching 252 and 135, respectively. At 90 days of storage, the number of upregulated proteins peaked at 504, while that number of downregulated proteins decreased to a minimum of 77. This indicates that after 90 days of storage, the diversity of enzymes in premium Baobaoqu was richer than that in normal Baobaoqu.

Furthermore, to understand the functional differences in the differential proteins after 90 days of storage, GO functional annotation was performed on these proteins. As illustrated in Fig. 5D, three categories of functions were analyzed: biological process, cellular component, and molecular function. Within almost all categories, the number of upregulated proteins was significantly greater than that of downregulated proteins, with the highest numbers of upregulated proteins (>200) found in the categories of metabolic processes, cellular processes, binding, catalytic activity, cell parts and cells. This suggests that the metabolism of premium Baobaoqu was more active than that of normal Baobaoqu after 90 days of storage.

A model for the metabolic division of labor and flavor formation

To explore the properties of Baobaoqu, 66 types of expressed enzymes linked to substrate degradation and flavor development were chosen and organized into 12 functional categories according to the KEGG database (Fig. 6). During the storage process, both premium and normal Baobaoqu exhibited very high levels of enzymes involved in starch degradation, primarily including 1,4-alpha-D-glucan (EC 2.4.1.1), alpha-amylase (EC 3.2.1.1), glucoamylase (EC 3.2.1.3) and alpha-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.20). These enzymes are mainly produced by Aspergillus, Thermoascus and Lichtheimia. When starch is hydrolyzed by these glycoside hydrolases, a range of products are produced that supply abundant nutrients for microorganism growth. Aspergillus, Thermoascus and Lichtheimia produce endoglucanase (EC 3.2.1.4), cellulose 1,4-beta-cellobiosidase (EC 3.2.1.91) and beta-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.21), respectively, which decompose cellulose into d-glucose. Then, glucose undergoes a series of metabolic processes, forming the important intermediate product pyruvate. This molecule serves as a crucial precursor for a range of compounds, including alcohols, acids, pyrazines, esters and other flavor substances (Yan et al., 2025). During the storage of premium Baobaoqu, the expression of cellulolytic enzymes gradually decreased while, in normal Baobaoqu, the trend was the opposite, with the expression levels of cellulolytic enzymes gradually increasing. This indicates that after 90 days of storage, both normal and premium Baobaoqu play a significant role in cellulose degradation. The number of enzymes involved in glucose utilization is the largest, reaching 17, which indicates that Baobaoqu is an important saccharifying agent, a result consistent with previous reports (Ma et al., 2025). As the substrates in Baobaoqu, specifically wheat, are degraded into a series of small molecular substances, precursors of various flavor components are formed, ensuring an adequate supply. As shown in Fig. 6, during the storage of premium and normal Baobaoqu, there were three primary metabolic pathways (based on enzyme expression levels) associated with the synthesis of flavor compounds: ethanol metabolism, fatty acid biosynthesis and acetate metabolism. Of these, the former plays a pivotal role in Baijiu brewing (X. Huang et al., 2020), involving enzymes such as alcohol dehydrogenase (EC 1.1.1.1) and alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) (EC 1.1.1.2), primarily produced by Aspergillus, Pichia, Limosilactobacillus and Weissella; and Aspergillus and Lichtheimia, respectively. During the storage of premium Baobaoqu, the expression of ethanol metabolism enzymes gradually increased, whereas in normal Baobaoqu, the trend was the opposite, with the expression of these enzymes gradually decreasing. This indicates that after 90 days of storage, premium Baobaoqu exhibits higher levels of ethanol metabolism. The metabolism of fatty acids encompasses eight enzymes, namely acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 1 (EC 2.3.1.16), enoyl-CoA hydratase (EC 4.2.1.17), mitochondrial trans-2-enoyl-CoA reductase (EC 1.3.1.38), palmitoyl-protein thioesterase (EC 3.1.2.22), long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase (EC 6.2.1.3), fatty-acyl-CoA synthase (EC 2.3.1.86), beta-hydroxyacyl dehydrogenase (EC 1.1.1.35) and enoyl-[acyl-carrier-protein] reductase (NADH) (EC 1.3.1.9). Aspergillus, Wickerhamomyces and Lichtheimia significantly contribute to fatty acid synthesis by supplying the substrates necessary for esterification and fatty acid oxidation.

Other enzymes with high expression levels are linked to acetate metabolism. Eleven enzymes are involved in this metabolism—namely, pyruvate dehydrogenase E1 component beta subunit (EC 1.2.4.1), pyruvate decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.1), aldehyde dehydrogenase (NAD+) (EC 1.2.1.3), alpha-ketoglutaric semialdehyde dehydrogenase (EC 1.2.1.-), pyruvate dehydrogenase E2 component (EC 2.3.1.12), acetyl-CoA synthetase (EC 6.2.1.1), acylphosphatase (EC 3.6.1.7), acetate kinase (EC 2.7.2.1), acetyl-CoA hydrolase (EC 3.1.2.1), aldehyde dehydrogenase (NAD(P)+) (EC 1.2.1.5) and acetaldehyde dehydrogenase (EC 1.2.1.10)—with the active microorganisms Aspergillus, Wickerhamomyces and Lichtheimia playing significant roles. Acetate serves as a vital precursor in the production of ethyl acetate and is one of the four primary acids found in Baijiu, imparting it with a distinctive fragrance (J. Chen et al., 2025).

Other metabolic processes are also of great significance. Lactate, an important organic acid, is a key component in shaping the flavor of Baijiu (Xu et al., 2022). The microorganisms responsible for producing enzymes related to metabolic pathways are primarily Limosilactobacillus, Weissella and Pediococcus. Phenylethanol constitutes the primary aromatic compound found in Baijiu (Tang et al., 2021). It carries a rose-like fragrance, which enhances the overall flavor complexity (Xing-Lin et al., 2017). Phenylethanol has been identified in various forms of Daqu, aligning with the findings of this research (Mu et al., 2025).

Within microbial communities, metabolic pathways occur either through the action of an individual microbial population or through collaboration among several microbial populations utilizing a division of labor, a concept termed MDOL (Ma et al., 2025). Raw materials transform into flavor compounds within the Baobaoqu microbial community, and to explore this MDOL model more comprehensively, we compiled the metabolic pathways involved in substrate degradation, thus leading to flavor production, and evaluated the contributions of microorganisms at each functional unit (Fig. 7). Within the dynamic microbial community involved in large-scale fermentation, raw wheat is transformed into aromatic compounds through collaborative interactions facilitated by MDOL. This community comprises numerous active microorganisms, including five phyla: Firmicutes, Actinobacteria, Proteobacteria, Ascomycota and Mucoromycota. For example, the breakdown of starch is primarily carried out by species such as Aspergillus, Thermoascus, Thermomyces and Lichtheimia. The glucose generated from starch decomposition is further processed into pyruvate by microorganisms such as Staphylococcus, Limosilactobacillus, Aspergillus, Weissella, Pediococcus and Pichia. This pyruvate is then metabolized into acetic acid by Wickerhamomyces, Lichtheimia, Byssochlamys, Aspergillus, Limosilactobacillus and Pichia. Moreover, microorganisms including Aspergillus, Wickerhamomyces and Lichtheimia synthesize enzymes that contribute to fatty acid biosynthesis and elongation, enabling the conversion of acetyl groups into diverse compounds. This research demonstrates that Aspergillus is essential for the synthesis of MDOL flavor compounds in Baobaoqu, with its abundance being the highest at both the transcriptional and protein expression levels. These findings align with earlier research (Y. Wang et al., 2023). Conclusion

This study, based on multi-omics (metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics, metabolomics) combined with machine learning (MLP-SHAP, 100% accuracy), systematically elucidated the changes of flavor components, microbial community structures, enzyme compositions, and metabolic mechanisms during the storage of Baobaoqu of different qualities. The main findings include the following: (i) Normal Baobaoqu had richer aroma components, with signature compounds including 2,3-dimethylpyrazine, ethyl heptanoate, 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine, 2,5-dimethylpyrazine and 2-buten-1-ol; premium Baobaoqu exhibited higher active microbial diversity, with Thermothemyces as the signature microorganism. (ii) The differences in the numbers of upregulated and downregulated proteins were the greatest during the late stages of storage of premium and normal Baobaoqu, with 504 and 77 proteins, respectively. Premium Baobaoqu had a richer variety of enzymes. (iii) Metabolism was mainly concentrated in ethanol metabolism, fatty acid biosynthesis, and acetic acid metabolism. During storage, premium Baobaoqu showed decreased cellulase (EC 3.2.1.91) expression and increased ethanol-metabolism enzyme (EC 1.1.1.1) expression, while normal Baobaoqu exhibited the opposite trend. After maturation, premium Baobaoqu possessed a stronger ethanol metabolism capability, whereas normal Baobaoqu demonstrated a stronger cellulose degradation ability, the two enzymes were primarily sourced from Thermoascus and Aspergillus, respectively. (iv) Aspergillus was the key functional microorganism for MDOL production, and fungi and bacteria cooperatively converted substrates into aromatic compounds. In summary, normal Baobaoqu provided richer flavors, while premium Baobaoqu offered higher enzyme activity; their complementary functions made a suitable combination essential in practical production to improve the quality and yield of both Baobaoqu and Chinese Baijiu. This study provides a key theoretical basis for such optimization.

Trajectories of Nursing Care During the Critical and Intensive Phases After Coronary Artery Bypass Graft Surgery: A Retrospective Observational Study
Authors: Yewon Lee, Sumi Sung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimTo examine the trajectories of nursing care during the critical and intensive phases after coronary artery bypass graft surgery, using standardised electronic nursing records.DesignRetrospective observational study.MethodsThe electronic nursing records of 122 patients who underwent coronary artery bypass graft from a tertiary hospital were collected between September 2021 and September 2023. Among the 287,360 extracted nursing statements, the 427 most frequent statements were mapped to SNOMED CT. Nursing data were categorised into pre‐ and postoperative phases, and a heatmap was used to visualise daily nursing care trends during the first 8 days post‐surgery.ResultsIn total, 287,360 nursing statements were extracted and semantically mapped, with 90.9% linked to pre‐coordinated SNOMED CT concepts. The results showed that in the acute postoperative phase, clinical priorities included respiratory management and surgical drain care. As recovery progressed, priorities expanded to include pressure injury prevention, safety monitoring, and effective interdisciplinary communication.ConclusionsThe analysis using standardised nursing records identified dynamic care patterns after coronary artery bypass graft surgery and supports the development of disease‐specific, evidence‐based nursing practice guidelines.Implications for the Profession and/or Patient CareSupport for evidence‐based nursing care for patients undergoing coronary artery bypass graft surgery.ImpactThis study addressed the limited development of nursing‐focused clinical practice guidelines by demonstrating how real‐world nursing data can be leveraged to develop disease‐specific, evidence‐based guidance. By mapping electronic nursing records to international standard terminology, the study advanced a methodological approach for systematically capturing and analysing nursing care trajectories over time. The findings reveal dynamic patterns of nursing interventions during postoperative care and provide empirical support for developing standardised, data‐driven nursing guidelines, ultimately strengthening evidence‐based practice in acute and critical care settings.Reporting MethodStrengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.Patient or Public ContributionThis study did not include patient or public involvement in its design, conduct, or reporting.Keywords: CABG, critical care, evidence‐based nursing, nursing records, SNOMED CT

Introduction

Evidence‐based practice (EBP) enables nurses to integrate research‐based knowledge into clinical practice to improve patient outcomes and deliver safe and effective care (Sapri et al. 2022; Connor et al. 2023). Clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) are evidence‐based recommendations derived from systematic reviews and expert consensus, designed to guide nurses in managing specific health problems or clinical interventions (Beauchemin et al. 2019; Cassidy et al. 2021). Despite decades of emphasis on EBP in nursing (Mackey and Bassendowski 2017), the development of nursing‐focused CPGs remains limited, focusing on fundamental areas such as fall prevention (Montero‐Odasso et al. 2021; Schoberer et al. 2022), pressure injury management (Avşar and Karadağ 2018), and pain management (Macintyre and Schug 2021). Structured resources, such as the International Classification for Nursing Practice (ICNP) catalogues (International Council of Nurses 2019), help standardise nursing content in selected clinical areas; however, they are not designed to reflect dynamic disease trajectories or evolving nursing care needs. This reflects a critical gap in current practice standards, where detailed guidance on disease‐specific nursing practice remains limited, particularly for patients undergoing complex procedures, such as coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery.

An electronic nursing record (ENR) holds detailed longitudinal nursing care information regarding nursing assessments, diagnoses, interventions, and evaluations throughout patient disease trajectories. These records are valuable resources for analysing the nursing care provided at different time points, aligning with a patient's disease treatment process. Following the rapid implementation of electronic health records (EHRs), ENRs have been increasingly used in nursing research. Previous studies have mapped ENRs to ICNP catalogues for fall prevention (Cho et al. 2023) and to examine the nursing care provided to COVID‐19 patients (Sung, Jung, et al. 2024). These previous studies offer practice‐based evidence on how nursing care is delivered in clinical settings.

Growing attention has been given to SNOMED CT as a standard terminology in nursing documentation. In 2018, The American Nurses Association endorsed the use of SNOMED CT to represent nursing practice in EHRs (American Nurses Association 2018), and various research initiatives have explored its application in clinical settings. SNOMED CT enables the standardisation of nursing data, allowing for more consistent documentation, data aggregation, and eventually efficient interoperability across institutions.

Background

Previous studies have examined the extent to which essential nursing concepts, such as frailty and disaster hazards, are represented in SNOMED CT (Thandi et al. 2021; Lokmic‐Tomkins et al. 2023). Additionally, research has investigated how well nursing data within EHRs can be mapped to SNOMED CT, providing insight into its practical applicability and coverage for nursing documentation (Sung, Kim, et al. 2024; Park et al. 2025).

Several studies have examined nursing records related to cardiac surgery, including analyses of the nursing process (Kim and Park 2005), pedagogical activities (Bergh et al. 2007), nursing documentation requirements (Nasiriani et al. 2014), and classification system alignments (Najafi et al. 2018). While these studies address specific components of care, they offer limited insight into the dynamic progression of nursing interventions across critical and intensive phases. Analysing this dynamic progression is a critical step toward optimising nursing interventions in accordance with the patients' clinical trajectory (Macieira et al. 2019, 2024; Rodríguez‐Suárez et al. 2023). Standardising nursing records provides the important foundation for such analysis, enabling consistent data integration across institutions and providing robust, reproducible evidence (Bertocchi et al. 2023; Schnock et al. 2021; McCarthy et al. 2019). However, no previous research has been conducted using standardised nursing records to systematically capture and analyse the trajectories of nursing care across the critical and intensive phases of patients who underwent CABG.

This study aimed to measure and visualise the trajectories of nursing care during the critical and intensive phases post‐CABG surgery using standardised ENRs.

Methods

Study Design, Setting, Participants

This retrospective observational study used data extracted from a clinical data warehouse at a tertiary hospital in Seoul, South Korea. The study hospital has 1382 beds and performs approximately 30,000 surgeries and 160 CABG surgeries annually. This study analysed records of 122 adult patients who underwent CABG surgery between September 2021 and September 2023. Because all eligible patient records during the study period were extracted, no a priori sample size calculation was performed. In addition, post hoc power analysis was not conducted because this study did not involve hypothesis testing and therefore cannot be classified as a negative study.

Data Source and Variables

The data sources included surgery records (surgery date, ICD‐9‐CM code, and surgery name), nursing progress notes, and initial nursing assessment records. Patient demographic characteristics (e.g., age, sex, length of hospital stay, and medical history) were extracted from the initial nursing assessment records. Information related to surgery type was extracted from the surgery records. A total of 287,360 nursing statements were extracted from the nursing records.

Ethics Considerations

This study was conducted following ethical guidelines and received exemption approval from the institutional review board (approval number: E‐2311‐102‐1485).

Mapping Nursing Statements to International Standard Terminology, SNOMED CT

Nursing statements were standardised via mapping to SNOMED CT concepts. For the mapping process, we used a previously developed mapping methodology (Sung et al. 2023). The dataset included 287,360 statements, comprising 427 unique nursing statements. The 427 unique statements that accounted for the top 95% of the 184,029 total nursing statements were selected as mapping source.

Two terminologists mapped independently and reviewed the mapping results. The nursing statements were mapped to SNOMED CT concepts within the hierarchies of ‘clinical finding,’ ‘situation with explicit context,’ and ‘procedure.’ The maps were classified according to the mapping correlation. If a nursing statement and a SNOMED CT concept were semantically equivalent, the mapping was classified as exact. If the SNOMED CT concept has a broader meaning than the nursing statement, the mapping was classified by narrow‐to‐broad. Internal validation of the map was performed by calculating interrater reliability. Agreement between the raters was quantified using Cohen's kappa coefficient (McHugh 2012). A map was considered correct if both the mappers selected the same result. In cases of disagreement, the results were evaluated in a group discussion to reach consensus on the most appropriate mapping.

Statistical Analysis

Patient demographic characteristics were summarised using descriptive analysis. Age was presented as mean and standard deviation (SD), hospital stay was reported as median with interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables such as sex, medical history, and surgery type were presented as frequency and percentage (n, %). We defined the data set relative to the date of the surgery, capturing data from 2 days before surgery to 8 days after surgery, including the day of surgery, to encompass the most critical and intensive phases of nursing care. Mapped SNOMED CT concepts were organised into clinically relevant categories. 15 categories were identified for the preoperative period and 16 for the postoperative period. For each category, the total number of nursing statements was calculated, along with the daily mean number of statements per patient (i.e., total statements divided by the number of patients and length of stay). For the trend analysis, a heatmap chart was created to visually represent the changing trends in nursing records for postoperative care among the patients. The chart illustrates variations in nursing documentation from the day of surgery through postoperative day (POD) seven, highlighting shifts in the frequency and focus of nursing activities over the postoperative phases.

Results

The clinical characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. The 122 patients included had a mean age of 66.4 years (SD, 9.9 years), a median hospital stay of 15 days (IQR, 14–21), and 94 patients (77.1%) were male. Of these patients, 77 (36.5%) had a history of hypertension, 61 (28.9%) had a history of diabetes mellitus, 7 (3.3%) had a history of hyperlipidemia, and 66 (31.3%) had a history of other diseases. Various CABG surgeries were conducted: 105 patients (86.1%) underwent an aortocoronary bypass of two or more coronary arteries, 12 (9.8%) underwent an off‐pump CABG involving two or more vessels, and 5 (4.1%) underwent aortocoronary bypass of one coronary artery.

Table 2 and Figure 1 present the mapping results of the nursing statements to SNOMED CT concepts. All nursing statements were semantically mapped to one of four SNOMED CT hierarchies: ‘clinical finding,’ ‘situation with explicit context,’ and ‘procedure.’ Of all statements, 397 (90.9%) were mapped to pre‐coordinated concepts, and 30 (6.9%) were mapped to post‐coordinated concepts. The interrater reliability of the mapping results was Cohen's kappa of 70.6%, indicating substantial agreement (McHugh 2012). Figure 1 presents the frequencies of mapped SNOMED CT concepts according to the SNOMED CT hierarchies. The ‘clinical finding’ and ‘situation with explicit context’ hierarchies represent nursing assessments and outcomes. The most frequently mapped concepts were ‘finding of fluid output from drain’ (n = 6068), ‘mentally alert’ (n = 4413), ‘ineffective airway clearance’ (n = 4195), ‘acute pain’ (n = 3949), and ‘at increased risk of infection’ (n = 3717). The ‘procedure’ hierarchy represents nursing interventions. The most frequently mapped concepts were ‘encouragement of deep breathing and coughing exercises’ (n = 7495), ‘notification of physician’ (n = 5253), ‘pain assessment’ (n = 4266), ‘pain management’ (n = 3894), and ‘monitoring pain’ (n = 2969).

Table 3 shows the categorization of standardised nursing statements across the preoperative and postoperative phases. During the two‐day preoperative period, nursing statements were classified into 15 categories. The most frequent nursing categories per patient were ‘chest pain management’ (7.9 ± 0.32), ‘psychological care’ (5.4 ± 0.43), ‘education’ (3.0 ± 0.72), ‘respiratory care’ (2.5 ± 0.06), and ‘care team communication’ (2.0 ± 0.26). During the eight‐day postoperative period, nursing statements were classified into 16 categories. Figure 2 shows the heatmap of the categorised standardised nursing statements during postoperative care. The colour gradient of the heatmap represents the frequency of nursing records, with red representing the highest frequency, yellow representing moderate frequency, and green representing the lowest frequency. This visualisation highlights the dynamic shifts in patient care needs as recovery progressed. The dominant categories were ‘postoperative lung care’ (n = 18,965), ‘surgical drain care’ (n = 11,310), and ‘pain management’ (n = 11,268). A high level of nursing activity was recorded in the immediate postoperative period, particularly on POD 1, with ‘postoperative lung care’ (n = 3648), ‘skin integrity care’ (n = 2696), and ‘comprehensive physiological status evaluation’ (n = 2013) each exceeding 2000 records, followed by ‘surgical drain care’ (n = 1583).

In most categories, the frequency of nursing records decreased over time, including a marked reduction in ‘postoperative lung care’ (from 3648 records on POD 1 to 1758 on POD 7), ‘surgical drain care’ (from 1583 records on POD 1 to 727 on POD 7), ‘skin integrity care’ (from 2696 records on POD 1 to 196 on POD 7), and ‘comprehensive physiological status evaluation’ (from 2013 records on POD 1 to 95 on POD 7).

Reverse trends were observed in several categories, including ‘pain management’ (from 852 records on POD 1 to a peak of 1802 on POD 5), ‘surgical wound care’ (from 162 records on POD 1 to a peak of 493 on POD 5), ‘infection assessment’ (from 165 records on POD 1 to a peak of 477 on POD 5), ‘care team communication’ (from 114 records on POD 1 to a peak of 352 on POD 5), and ‘arrhythmia assessment’ (from 93 records on POD 1 to a peak of 374 on POD 5). In contrast, relatively steady levels of nursing records were maintained throughout the postoperative period, including ‘catheter care,’ ‘perfusion assessment,’ and ‘bleeding assessment.’

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to analyse standardised nursing records using SNOMED CT for CABG patients and to examine how nursing records change throughout the critical and intensive recovery phase. The findings provide a detailed overview of nursing care trajectories, highlighting evolving care needs and the nursing interventions employed during the critical perioperative period.

In this study, 98% of nursing records for CABG patients were standardised using SNOMED CT, which was higher than the content coverage reported in prior research. Previous studies showed that 88% of nursing problems or outcome statements for COVID‐19 patients were mapped to SNOMED CT (Sung, Kim, et al. 2024), and another study reported 85.4% of nursing diagnoses and 100% of nursing interventions for COVID‐19 were mapped to SNOMED CT (Thoroddsen et al. 2023). The higher level of standardisation shown in our study underscores SNOMED CT's adaptability across diverse clinical domains, improving the consistency, reusability, and reliability of nursing records. The mapped SNOMED CT concepts enabled the aggregation of raw nursing data into higher‐level categories for detailed analysis. These results demonstrate the value of using standardised terminologies to structure nursing documentation, allowing more comprehensive data analysis and supporting evidence‐based practice.

The current findings indicate that nursing records for CABG patients are consistent with the most recent CPGs for cardiac surgery, including the Enhanced Recovery After Surgery Cardiac Society guidelines (Stenberg et al. 2022) and the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines for coronary artery revascularization (Lawton et al. 2022). These guidelines emphasise essential clinical and procedural elements of care, such as preoperative preparation including hematologic assessments and fasting protocols, respiratory management, management of pain specific to cardiac patients, surgical site care, postoperative drain management, and psychological support. The nursing interventions identified in this study correspond to these elements of care, highlighting the essential role of nurses in delivering evidence‐based care to CABG patients.

The findings also provide practice‐based evidence which captures the broader scope of nursing activities in CABG care. Nurses were found to deliver care across multiple domains, extending beyond clinical management to include patient education, pressure injury prevention, compliance monitoring, safety assessments, and effective interdisciplinary communication. A recent review synthesised key nursing interventions in cardiac surgical care and highlighted the wide range of practices required throughout the pre‐ and postoperative period, such as respiratory management, pain alleviation, complication prevention, and individualised education and emotional support (Wang et al. 2023). Also, previous studies analysing real‐world nursing records have demonstrated the clinical complexity of nursing care for cardiac patients, encompassing both physician‐directed intervention (e.g., pain control and disease‐related symptom management) and independent nursing activities (e.g., education, neurological monitoring, and emotional support) (Kim and Park 2005). Another study identified 15 NANDA‐based nursing diagnoses in postoperative cardiac surgery patients, addressing both physiological and psychosocial needs (Ribeiro et al. 2015). These findings are consistent with the results of the present study, further confirming that nursing care in CABG involves a diverse and multifaceted range of interventions across both clinical and psychosocial domains.

The nursing care trajectory identified in this study reveals a distinct evolution in the focus and complexity of care during the first week of recovery following CABG surgery. In the immediate postoperative phase, nursing records were concentrated on maintaining physiological stability, with high frequencies observed in categories such as postoperative lung care, surgical drain care, and physiological status evaluation. As recovery progressed, the quantity of nursing care did not decline; instead, it diversified in scope. Throughout recovery, nursing records increasingly included domains such as pain management, infection control, wound care, catheter management, and patient compliance. These changes provide a foundation for nurses to prioritise care based on specific recovery phases, enabling more efficient and tailored care plans. Previous studies have shown that analysing EHRs can help identify patient care trajectories and anticipate future clinical needs (Kraljevic et al. 2024). Consistent with previous research, this study shows that standardised nursing documentation enables the identification of meaningful patterns in how care needs change throughout postoperative recovery. These insights highlight the potential for developing phase‐specific nursing tools and decision aids to optimise postoperative care.

Limitations

This study used nursing records from a single tertiary hospital with a limited sample size of 122 patients, which restricts the generalizability of the findings to broader populations or other healthcare settings. Additionally, sample bias may be present due to the exclusion of patients who were admitted less than 2 days before surgery or discharged within 7 days post‐surgery. Finally, although nursing statements were categorised into clinically relevant domains to effectively analyse trends, this process may have introduced classification bias or resulted in the oversimplification of complex nursing activities, potentially leading to the exclusion or underrepresentation of less frequently documented but critical nursing tasks.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated nursing care trajectory during the critical and intensive phases after CABG surgery. By analysing standardised nursing records, we identified a comprehensive range of nursing activities in CABG nursing care from physiological management to psychosocial support. These results support the development of nursing‐specific CPGs and highlight the value of structured nursing data in enhancing quality and continuity of care in intensive and critical care environments. Future research should expand similar analyses to nursing care in other disease areas to establish both quantitative and qualitative evidence of nursing practice. Based on such evidence, efforts should focus on developing nursing‐driven clinical practice guidelines and creating evidence‐based frameworks for nursing reimbursement systems that reflect the value of nursing care.

Declining prevalence of multidrug-resistant but comparatively high quinolone-resistant tuberculosis in Heilongjiang Province, China: 2013–2022 trend
Authors: Jin-Long Li, Chun-Lei Zhang, Yue Feng, Di Li, Hao Zhang, Bo Wang, Bao-Yu Han, Anna Starshinova, Igor Kudryavtsev, Dian-Jun Sun, Min Zhuang, Hong Ling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe spread of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB) remains a major problem globally. China is the high-burden country for TB, TB/HIV and MDR/rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB) in the period 2021–2025. Heilongjiang Province, located in the northeast of China and is the border region with the Russian Federation, which is also a high-burden country for TB/HIV and MDR/RR-TB. Heilongjiang Province had a higher prevalence of TB and MDR-TB than many other areas of China based on data 2007–2012(Li D, et al. BMC Infect Dis. 2016.16:516). The high prevalence may affect the epidemic of TB and drug-resistant TB in China as well as in Russia. Therefore, the knowledge about the patterns of drug resistance in this TB high-burden area of China is crucial to develop appropriate control strategies. This study is a continuation of an analysis of the drug-resistant TB epidemic in this area, 2013–2022.MethodsA total of 11,139 Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis) clinical isolates were collected from 11,139 hospitalized patients with TB from 2013 to 2022. The sensitivity of the isolates to first- and second-line antituberculosis drugs was investigated. We also compared the two methods for drug susceptibility testing (DST), including absolute concentration and liquid rapid culture.ResultsThe prevalence of drug-resistant TB to any first-line drug and second-line drug was 38.9% and 31.0%, respectively. The prevalence of MDR-TB (8.5% in 2013–2022 vs. 22.8% in 2007–2012) has significantly declined. However, the prevalence of levofloxacin(Lfx)-resistant-TB (13.5% in 2013–2022 vs. 10.4% in 2010–2012) has increased. The trends of resistance to the first- and the second-line drugs in 2022 slightly increased compared to those in 2020 and 2021. The prevalence of pre-extensive drug-resistant TB (pre-XDR-TB, resistant to rifampicin and any of fluoroquinolones) was relatively high (7.6%).ConclusionsThe maintenance of a high prevalence of fluoroquinolone-resistant and pre-XDR-TB remains a serious threat to public health and has an impact on TB control to achieve China’s goal of TB elimination. Therefore, continuous surveillance of the spread of drug-resistant TB in this border region is important.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13192-y.Keywords: Tuberculosis, Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Drug resistance, Multidrug resistance

Introduction

Very recently, World Health Organization (WHO) has released the Global tuberculosis (TB) report 2025 [1]. According to this report, every year, more than 10 million people fall ill with TB, and this single disease claims the lives of more than 1 million people around the world. TB remains a major global public health problem. Actually, there was a comparatively significant global decline in the TB incidence rate and the net reduction from 2015 to 2024 was 12% [1]. However, it is far from the End TB Strategy milestone of a 50% reduction by 2025 and the target of an 80% reduction by 2030.

China is a high-TB-burden country, with 6.5% of global estimated incident cases, fourth after India (25%), Indonesia (10%) and the Philippines (6.8%) [1]. Furthermore, China is also one of the 30 high MDR [defined as resistance to rifampicin (RFP) and isoniazid (INH)] (3)/ or RFP-resistant TB (RR-TB) burden countries. MDR-TB or RR-TB is defined as MDR/RR-TB. Of the four countries, those accounted for over 50% of the global number of people estimated to have developed MDR/RR-TB, China is ranked second (7.1% of global cases) after India (32%) because of China’s large population [1].

According to the facts above, the spread of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis) as well as MDR strains has been a big challenge to achieve the End TB Strategy milestone 2030 in China.

The epidemic trends of drug resistant-TB in different geographic areas of China vary greatly [2]. Heilongjiang Province, located in the northeast of China, has a higher prevalence of TB and drug-resistant TB than the national average and many other areas based on the previous data. Notably, in our previous investigation, we showed that the global prevalence of drug-resistant TB to first-line drugs and MDR-TB in 2007–2012 in Heilongjiang Province was as high as 57.0% and 22.8%, respectively. There was high primary pan-resistance rate in this area and 9.8% of M. tuberculosis were resistant to all four first-line anti-TB drugs including INH, RFP, streptomycin (SM), and ethambutol (EMB) [2]. Heilongjiang Province is a border region and neighbored with large areas of the Russian Federation. Notably, Russian Federation is also MDR/RR-TB-high-burden country (6.7% of global cases) [1]. The transmission and spread of drug-resistant TB across the border area is a public health concern. Continued evaluation of the TB drug-resistance patterns in this region will be beneficial for controlling drug-resistant TB epidemics in this region, in China as well as worldwide.

Moreover, the treatment of MDR/RR-TB requires second-line anti-TB drugs, which cause additional side effects [3]. Fluoroquinolones (FQs) are the most frequently used drugs for the treatment either of TB as second line drugs [4] or of a variety of infectious diseases [5–8]. Extensively drug-resistant TB (XDR-TB), initially defined by WHO in 2006 as MDR-TB that acquire resistance as well to any one of FQs and to at least one of three injectable second-line drugs (amikacin, capreomycin or kanamycin), represents the most severe drug-resistance situation of TB [9, 10]. In 2021, WHO updated the definition of XDR-TB, as resistance to RFP, plus any FQs, plus at least one of bedaquiline or linezolid [11]. Obviously, when MDR/RR-TB strains acquire resistance to any FQs will arise significant risk to develop XDR. Previous treatment regimen using second-line drugs, including FQs, is a significant risk factor for the occurrence of XDR-TB [12, 13]. Monitoring and effective control the epidemic of pre-XDR, defined as TB resistant to RFP and any FQs [11], will suppress XDR-TB epidemic.

We previously also conducted investigation of the resistance trends of M. tuberculosis clinical isolates from 2007 to 2012 to FQs in Heilongjiang Province, China. We have found that of MDR M. tuberculosis isolates, 13% were not susceptible to any of the FQs. This high prevalence of fluoroquinolone-resistant MDR-TB is a big challenge for TB control. At present, after setbacks during the COVID-19 pandemic, the influence of the pandemic on the established approaches for the detection, surveillance, and treatment of patients with TB has declined globally. Due to the past higher drug-resistant TB prevalence, the trends of the epidemic of MDR-, fluoroquinolone-resistant and XDR-TB in the recent 10 years in this border area should be evaluated extensively.

In 2020, WHO recommended novel all-oral regimens for MDR/RR-TB and pre-XDR-TB. Compared to older regimens, the new regimens can reduce the treatment duration from about 20 months to 6 months [4]. However, there are no data from Heilongjiang Province and few data from China regarding TB resistance to the two drugs, bedaquiline or linezolid, as they were recommended very recently. Since treatment for XDR-TB remains much more difficult and treatment success rates are typically low, surveillance of the epidemic trends of XDR-TB is more than important. Therefore, in recent years the prevalence of XDR-TB is evaluated based on the criteria 2006 (resistance to RFP, any of the fluoroquinolones, and at least one of the second-line injectable drugs).

In the present study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the epidemic of drug-resistant TB including MDR/RR-TB, pre-XDR-TB and XDR-TB (XDR based on definition 2006) 2013–2022. We found that the prevalence of MDR-TB (8.5%) has significantly declined. However, the prevalence of levofloxacin-resistant-TB (13.5%) has increased, and the prevalence of pre-XDR-TB was relatively high (7.6%). The maintenance of a high prevalence of fluoroquinolone-resistant and pre-XDR-TB remains a serious threat to public health and has an impact on TB control to achieve China’s goal of eliminating tuberculosis.

Materials and methods

Collection of M. tuberculosis isolates

From 2013 to 2022, a total of 19,565 M. tuberculosisstrains were successfully isolated. The patients with TB were from various regions (13 prefecture-level administrative divisions) of Heilongjiang Province and diagnosed at Harbin Chest Hospital, a designated TB hospital in this region. Finally, a total of 11,139 M. tuberculosis isolates from 11,139 TB cases were tested for drug susceptibility. Mycobacterial cultures were obtained from clinical specimens after incubation in a BACTEC Mycobacterium Growth Indicator Tube (MGIT) 960 Automated System (BD Diagnostic Systems, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Primary identification was performed using Ziehl-Neelsen staining and microscopy. Tests for inhibition by p-nitrobenzoic acid and 2-thiophenecarboxylic acid hydrazide were used to differentiate M. tuberculosis from other Mycobacterium spp.

DST and data analysis

The drug susceptibility testing (DST) of the 8264 M. tuberculosis isolates (Fig. 1) from 2013 to 2022 to four first-line drugs including SM, INH, RFP, EMB, and seven second-line drugs including levofloxacin (Lfx), rifapentine (Rft), amikacin (Am), p-aminosalicylic acid (Pas), protionamide (Pto), dipasic (PI), and capreomycin (Cm) were determined using an absolute concentration method on solid Lowenstein-Jensen medium (Hangzhou Genesis Biodetection & Biocontrol Co., Ltd.) according to national guidelines [14] (Fig. 1). The standard concentrations of the drugs are provided in the Supplementary Table S1. M. tuberculosis H37Rv was used for routine quality monitoring.

Study design for testing drug sensitivity of the M. tuberculosis isolates

Furthermore, DST of 2875 isolates from 2017 to 2022 for the drugs mentioned above and five more drugs, rifabutin (Rfb), clarithromycin (Clr), kanamycin (Kan), clofazimine (Cfz), and moxifloxacin (Mfx), was performed using the microplate rapid liquid culture method [13]. Minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) were determined according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Zhuhai Yinke Medical Engineering Co., Ltd.). The study was approved by the Institutional Research Board of the University of Harbin Medical University (HMUIRB20160001).

Statistical analysis

For the drug-resistant trend analysis, the resistance rates with the calendar year as a continuous variable were analyzed using logistic regression in SPSS 26.0. The resistance rates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were shown. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. Illustrations were performed using Graphpad Prism 9.0.0.

Results

The prevalence of drug-resistant TB to first-line drugs

The prevalence of drug-resistant TB from 2013 to 2022 to any first-line drug, INH, RFP (MDR/RR), SM and EMB was 38.9% (29.3% – 46.3%), 13.2% (8.6% – 19.6%), 21.3% (14.9% – 26.6%), 27.7% (18.2% – 34.1%) and 5.1% (2.0% – 14.3%), respectively (Table 1). The prevalence was increasing 2013–2018 and declining 2019–2021 during the COVID pandemic quarantine. The prevalences to first-line drug in 2022 slightly increased compared to that in 2021 but was still lower than that in 2018 (Table 1; Fig. 2).

The prevalence of drug-resistant TB to second-line drugs

To evaluate the TB resistance to FQs from 2013 to 2022, we investigated the prevalence of Lfx-resistant TB, as Lfx is the most useful FQ. The average resistance rate of all TB cases to Lfx was 13.5% (9.4% – 20.9%) (Table 2). In contrast, the prevalence of RR-TB and MDR-TB cases to Lfx was 35.7% (28.3% – 52.7%) and 49.2% (34.1% – 64.9%), respectively. The prevalence to Lfx was increasing 2013–2019 and then began to decline. Similar to the trend of resistance to the first-line drugs, the prevalence of TB resistance to Lfx in 2022 slightly increased compared to that in 2021 and was lower than that in 2019 (Table 2; Fig. 3). Regarding the prevalence of resistance to other second-line drugs, that to Rft was comparatively higher (global 21.0%, from 12.0% to 26.2%) (Table 2; Fig. 3).

The trends of multiple drug-resistance of TB

The multiple resistance of the TB cases to first-line (Table 1) and second-line (Table 2) drugs using the absolute concentration method was shown. Global prevalence of MDR/RR-TB and MDR-TB from 2013 to 2022 was 21.3% (14.9% – 26.6%) and 8.5% (4.9% – 13.4%), respectively. The prevalence of pre-MDR-TB (resistant to INH or RFP, at high risk of developing to MDR) was 17.5% (11.4% – 19.5%). Few TB cases were resistant to all four first-line drugs (pan-resistant TB) (2.0%, 0.6% – 4.9%). Furthermore, the prevalence of pre-XDR-TB and XDR-TB was 7.6% (6.0% – 13.1%) and 2.0% (1.1% – 4.5%), respectively. The multiple resistance showed increasing trends before 2018–2019 and decreasing from 2020 to 2022 was found (Tables 1 and 2; Fig. 4). The trends of any first-line drug-resistance, Pre-MDR, and MDR decreased (P < 0.0001) from 2007 to 2022, and the trend of Lfx-resistance (P = 0.0325) and Lfx-resistance in MDR (P < 0.0001) increased from 2013 to 2022, using logistic regression (Table 3).

The prevalence of drug-resistant pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB

The proportion of the cases with pulmonary TB (PTB) in this area were predominant, which accounted for about 90.0%. We tried to evaluate the prevalence of the resistance in PTB and extrapulmonary TB (EPTB) in 2022 (Fig. 5). The prevalence of resistance of PTB to first- (38.9%) and second-line (41.1%) drugs was higher than that of EPTB to first- (25.0%, P = 0.3303) and second-line drugs (15.0%, P = 0.0280).

The evaluation of the two DST methods

In recent years, Liquid rapid culture method have been used in clinical practice because this may shorten the time to test more drugs [15]. We performed DST on 2875 M. tuberculosis isolates from 2017 to 2022 using a liquid rapid culture method kit (Zhuhai Yinke Medical Engineering Co., Ltd) [16]. We compared the prevalence of drug-resistance using two DST methods, liquid culture method and absolute concentration method. We found that the resistance rates to INH and Lfx differed (Table S2 and Fig S1). Actually, the methods identify resistance based on different criteria for INH (0.2 mg/L for the liquid method and 1 mg/L for the absolute concentration method) and for Lfx (≤ 2 mg/L for the liquid method and 2 mg/L for the absolute concentration method). The Liquid culture method resulted higher prevalence to INH and lower prevalence to Lfx.

Discussion

Every year, WHO updates the list of high-burden countries for TB, MDR/RR-TB, and HIV-associated TB. In 2025, WHO global TB report claimed that China is one of the countries with a high MDR-TB burden [1]. Because of large population and high-burden area, the control of TB epidemic in China affects its epidemics worldwide. Based on previous data, Heilongjiang Province has a higher prevalence of RR-TB, MDR-TB, and FQ-resistant TB than other areas in China [2, 3]. In the present study, we updated the data regarding the characteristics of drug-resistant TB in this area. An optimistic situation is that the drug-resistance rates including the resistance rates of TB to first-line drugs and of MDR-TB in 2013–2022 significantly declined compared to those in 2007–2012.

In China, the epidemic trends of drug resistant-TB in different geographic areas vary greatly. Based on our previous data 2007–2012, the prevalence of MDR-TB in Heilongjiang Province was much higher than in other areas [2]. In contrast, the present data showed that it was considerably lower than that in most reported areas (Table S3, hospital-based data). This is a positive change. However, the proportion of RR-TB cases in Heilongjiang Province remains comparatively high (12.8%).

Optimistically, the resistance rates to the second-line drugs except Lfx are still comparatively low. However, compared with other areas of China, Heilongjiang Province remains higher proportions of pre-XDR-TB and XDR-TB (Table S3). Furthermore, the average of the resistance rate to Lfx in the period of 2013–2022 was 13.5%, which slightly increased compared to that 2010–2012 and was higher than that in other areas of China.

As strategies and actions to eliminate tuberculosis epidemic have been implemented globally and in China, the prevalence of MDR-TB has shown a gradual decline in most countries, including China. Heilongjiang Province, by adhering to China’s national control strategies, has contributed to this positive trend.

More importantly, MDR-TB cases showed increasing in the resistance rate to Lfx. The reasons should be investigated extensively. But it might be partially associated with the shorter therapy duration (risk factor for drug resistance) as we have found previously [2]. FQs are widely used in treatment of other infectious diseases, especially respiratory tract infection. Levofloxacin is the most useful one. Therefore, resistance of bacterial clinical isolates to FQs should not be ignored. The climate in Heilongjiang province is dry and cold. Infections with bacteria in respiratory tract are common. Very recently, an investigation using the data from Hunan Province Antibacterial Resistance Surveillance System showed that the resistance rates of clinical isolates from respiratory tract including Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae‌, Methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus‌ (MSSA), Enterobacter cloacae, Moraxella catarrhalis, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, and Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus‌ (MRSA) were 3.8%, 6.5%, 8.9%, 12.1%, 15.2%, 18.6%, 39.2%, 56.5%, respectively [17]. This implied that there is a potential impact of FQ overuse in general respiratory medicine (outside of TB programs) on the effective therapy of MDR-TB as well as XDR-TB, not only within the investigated region but also in other areas of China. Therefore, proper guidance for FQs regimen in TB patients, individually as well as the proper usage of the antibiotics for individuals infected with other bacteria should be paid more attention. This is a big challenge for Chinese society.

Heilongjiang Province is a region with comparatively low Gross Domestic Product (GDP) compared to other areas of China (GDP in this area in 2022 was ranked the 24th of the Country). Poor economic situation will affect proper utilization of treatment regimen of TB because the regimens are usually long. Novel all-oral regimens using bedaquiline or linezolid for MDR/RR-TB and pre-XDR-TB can reduce treatment duration to only 6 months, compared with older regimens lasting 20 months or more. In the time durations (2013–2022) and region we investigated, the comprehensive elements including limited economic conditions, suboptimal treatment durations, insufficient DST proportion, plus the high resistance situations to fluoroquinolones, may contribute to the occurrence of pre-XDR and even XDR. In the previous investigation, DST was in only about 50% of the culture-positive TB cases, However, during 2013 to 2022, the DST proportion was 56%. DST availability still remained a big concern. Here, we speculate that it is important to evaluate the resistance of M. tuberculosis to bedaquiline and linezolid soon, although DST for these two drugs was not performed in this study, as they were recommended very recently [4].

Heilongjiang Province is located in Northeast of China and is surrounded by some regions of the Russian Federation. Russia is a high-burden country for drug-resistant-TB and HIV/TB, accounting for 8.5% global cases of MDR/RR-TB in 2022. The highest prevalence of MDR/RR TB (> 50% of previously treated cases with) is reported in Russia [18, 19]. In recent years, measures were taken to combat tuberculosis in this country and resulted in a planned reduction in the incidence rate from 2009 to 2021 of the disease by half from 82.6 to 31.1 per 100,000 individuals and of mortality by three times from 16.8 to 4.3 per 100,000 individuals [20]. Simultaneously, a well-functioning in-country operation made it possible to maintain the results after COVID-19 [20]. Therefore, continued monitoring of drug resistance in Heilongjiang Province and transmission via border areas can be targeted for the successful control of drug-resistant TB in these two countries and worldwide.

We also compared the resistance trends of the two durations, 2007–2012 [2] and 2013–2022 (Table 3). We found that the prevalences of any first-line drug resistance (reduction 31.8%), pre-MDR (reduction 23.9%), MDR (reduction 62.7%) and Pan-resistance (reduction 79.6%) reduced significantly. In contrast, the prevalence of resistance of TB and MDR-TB cases to Lfx increased by 29.8% (from 10.4% 2010–2012 to 13.5% 2013–2022) and 96.8% (from 25.0% 2010–2012 to 49.2% 2013–2022).

In recent years, the DST was performed using the rapid liquid culture method in addition to the absolute concentration method in many hospitals and TB institutions. The inconsistence of the DST results derived from different methods has been found and discussed [21]. The microplate rapid liquid culture method not only covers a variety of drugs and multiple concentrations at one time, also provides quantitative drug resistance information. This method has been paid more attention in recent years. However, the methods affect the DST results as well as the prevalence of resistance. In the present study, the number of M. tuberculosis isolates used in the DST of absolute concentration method from 2013 to 2022 became less gradually. Instead, the resistance of more isolates had been examined using rapid liquid culture method. Due to many benefits of this method, the Laboratory in Harbin Chest Hospital started DST using this method in 2017. We compared the drug resistance rates within the same time duration. We aimed to display facts if there are true differences and how they were obvious. It will facilitate the improvement in methodology of DST in clinical practice in future. We found that for most of drugs, the resistance rates were similar. For example, the liquid method tended to result in a higher INH-resistance rate than the absolute concentration method. We do not know the reasons for the different sensitivity of the two methods. The difference underscores the need for greater standardization by manufacturers. Very recently, an expert consensus on the standardization of broth microdilution method for drug susceptibility testing of M. tuberculosis in China by Tuberculosis Basic Professional Branch, Chinese Antituberculosis Association has been published in May 2025 [22]. The expert consensus proposed 12 recommendations, aiming to provide unified guiding principles for clinical and laboratory staff who are related to tuberculosis. This will make the use of the microplate rapid liquid DST in a standardized manner in future.

Present findings are based on the resistance data of clinical isolates from hospitalized TB patients. Though the representativeness of the sample sources fits the geographical distribution of the TB patients, we need to acknowledge that the resistance trends are representative for hospitalized TB patients. They may be different from that of community-based TB cases as suggested by our previous analyses [2]. To comprehensively understand the resistance situation of the regions, further investigation using the bacterial samples from TB cases from communities.

The limitations of this study are that drug-resistance was defined based solely on phenotypic DST without genotypic data; meanwhile, the absence of molecular data prevents the distinction between clonal transmission (e.g., Beijing lineage clusters) and acquired resistance. We were unable to distinguish new and previously treated cases. However, in our previous report (2007–2012), MDR-TB cases comprised 38.5% new cases and 61.5% previously treated cases, indicating that a greater proportion of MDR-TB patients had received prior anti-TB treatment [2]. We were unable to perform a multivariate analysis of drug resistance to evaluate contribution factors. The previous data showed that being age of 35 to 54, high re-treatment proportion, the presence of cavity lesion, and high proportion of shorter hospitalization were correlated with the development of MDR-TB [2]. It is worthy to investigate the influence of any new factors to FQ resistance in future studies. Furthermore, the XDR proportions in the present study may not be directly comparable to studies using the new, WHO 2021 definitions. In future, it is valuable to investigate XDR situation when the resistance data of TB against bedaquiline and linezolid are available.

In conclusion, the significant decline in the prevalence of MDR-TB is an optimistic situation that reflects the efficacy of the TB control strategy. However, the high prevalence of FQ-resistant and pre-XDR TB is still a major threat to public health and has an impact on TB control to achieve China’s goal of eliminating TB epidemic. Therefore, continuous surveillance of the spread of drug-resistant TB in this region is important and exploring the mechanism, especially transmission in the border area, is still required.

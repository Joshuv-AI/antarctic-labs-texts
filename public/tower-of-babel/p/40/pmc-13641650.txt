TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A in Hepatocellular Carcinoma: Roles in Senescence, Ferroptosis, and Prognosis
Authors: Cheng Jiao, Yu-Peng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
INTRODUCTION:The aim of this study was to evaluate the association between genes related to cellular senescence and ferroptosis and their relevance to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).METHODS:Genes associated with senescence and ferroptosis in HCC were retrieved from public databases. A protein-protein interaction network was constructed to identify for hub genes and validate their expression. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, while prognostic significance was determined through Kaplan-Meier analysis. A prognostic nomogram model was developed based on selected hub genes and tumor node metastasis staging.RESULTS:A total of 52 senescence-ferroptosis-related genes were identified in HCC. Receiver operating characteristic analysis indicated moderate to high diagnostic efficacy for TP53 (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.723, CI: 0.669–0.776), JUN (AUC = 0.733, CI: 0.659–0.806), RELA (AUC = 0.854, CI: 0.808–0.901), and CDKN2A (AUC = 0.953, CI: 0.932–0.974). Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A were significantly associated with overall survival in patients with HCC. A nomogram model incorporating these 3 genes and tumor node metastasis staging achieved a concordance index (C-index) of 0.699. Calibration curves indicated concordance between the predicted and observed survival probabilities at 1-, 2-, and 3-year intervals.DISCUSSION:The senescence-ferroptosis-related genes TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A demonstrated potential as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in HCC. The developed nomogram may support individualized prognostic assessment and inform early diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in patients with HCC.

INTRODUCTION

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors globally and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality (1). Despite advancements in survival outcomes achieved through therapeutic modalities such as surgical resection, liver transplantation, targeted therapy, and radiochemotherapy, most patients with HCC continue to present at an advanced stage of disease, thereby limiting access to curative treatment options. In cases of advanced-stage HCC, the 5-year survival rate remains below 15% (2).

Given these challenges, the identification of genetic or molecular targets has become increasingly important for the development of novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers aimed at facilitating earlier detection and more accurate clinical evaluation of HCC. Cellular senescence, a fundamental biological process (BP) marked by irreversible cell cycle arrest, has been implicated in both the initiation and the progression of HCC. Although senescence can act as a tumor-suppressive mechanism by halting the proliferation of genetically compromised cells, it may also promote tumor progression through the secretion of cytokines (3,4).

Growing evidence has indicated that ferroptosis functions as a critical regulator of cellular senescence, with its inhibition shown to markedly delay the onset of senescence and potentially reverse multiple disease states associated with senescence (5–7). A complex balance between cellular senescence and ferroptosis has been observed in certain malignancies, where this balance seems to influence tumor initiation and progression (8). However, the specific functions and clinical significance of cellular senescence-ferroptosis-related genes in HCC remain unclear. This study was conducted to systematically analyze the association between senescence-ferroptosis-related genes and HCC, with the objective of contributing to the development of early diagnostic tools and informing individualized therapeutic strategies for patients affected by HCC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Acquisition of senescence-ferroptosis-related gene data in HCC

Bioinformatics methodologies were used to analyze hub genes associated with HCC. The overall research workflow is presented in Figure 1. Genes associated with cellular senescence were retrieved from multiple databases, including Gene (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene), CSGene database (http://csgene.bioinfo-minzhao.org/), Cellage database (https://genomics.senescence.info/cells/), and GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). Ferroptosis-related genes were obtained from the FerrDB database. Intersection analysis of data from these 5 databases was conducted to identify genes associated with both cellular senescence and ferroptosis.

Technical flowchart of the study. HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma; NCBI, National Center for Biotechnology Information; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas.

Subsequently, expression profiles of these cellular senescence-ferroptosis-related genes in HCC were analyzed using the University of ALabama at Birmingham CANcer (UALCAN) database (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/), which enables exploration of gene expression data derived from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Differential expression between HCC and normal liver tissues was assessed to identify candidate genes of interest (9).

Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes associated with senescence-ferroptosis in HCC

Functional enrichment analysis was performed to elucidate the principal BPs and signaling pathways associated with the differentially expressed genes related to cellular senescence and ferroptosis in HCC. The Metascape database (https://metascape.org/) was used for this analysis. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis were conducted. The GO enrichment analysis included BPs, molecular functions, and cellular components (CCs), providing a comprehensive overview of the functional roles of the identified genes.

Identification of hub genes associated with senescence and ferroptosis in HCC

Hub genes associated with senescence and ferroptosis in HCC were identified through the construction of a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. This network was generated using differentially expressed genes through the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) database (https://www.string-db.org/), with the species set to Homo sapiens. The resulting PPI network data were imported into Cytoscape software for visualization. The top 5 hub genes were determined based on the maximal clique centrality (MCC) algorithm.

Validation of hub gene expression

Expression data for the identified hub genes were extracted from the GSE14520 data set available in the gene expression omnibus database. Comparative analysis was conducted to assess differential gene expression between HCC and normal liver tissues, serving to validate the initial screening outcomes. The differential expression profiles of these hub genes in HCC were also visualized for interpretative clarity.

Correlation analysis of hub genes with diagnostic and prognostic parameters

Diagnostic analysis.

Gene expression data and corresponding clinical information from the TCGA database were used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the hub genes. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed using the pROC package, and the area under the curve (AUC) values were calculated to quantify diagnostic efficacy in HCC. The results were subsequently visualized using the ggplot2 package.

Prognostic analysis.

The prognostic relevance of the hub genes was assessed using the Kaplan-Meier Plotter database (https://kmplot.com/analysis/). Survival curves were generated to examine the association between gene expression levels and overall survival (OS) of patients diagnosed with HCC. Statistical significance was determined using the log-rank test.

Construction of a prognostic nomogram model for patients with HCC

Cox proportional hazards regression was applied using R software to construct a nomogram designed to predict 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS rates in patients with HCC. The model incorporated tumor node metastasis (TNM) staging and the expression levels of selected hub genes. The nomogram was constructed using the “survival” and “rms” packages in R software, and its predictive performance was evaluated through calibration plots, which compared predicted survival probabilities with observed outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.3.1). Differential gene expression between the 2 comparison groups was evaluated using the t-test. A value of P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

RESULTS

Identification of differentially expressed genes associated with cellular senescence-ferroptosis in HCC

A total of 6,384, 866, 503, and 10,674 senescence-related genes were retrieved from the GeneCards, Cellage, CSGene, and Gene databases, respectively. These gene sets were intersected with 563 ferroptosis-related genes retrieved from the FerrDB database, resulting in the identification of 52 genes associated with both cellular senescence and ferroptosis (Figure 2). Expression analysis of these 52 genes using the UALCAN platform identified 45 genes with differential expression in HCC tissues compared with normal liver tissues. Among these, 39 genes were found to be upregulated and 6 genes were downregulated in HCC samples (Table 1).

Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted to investigate the biological relevance of the differentially expressed genes associated with senescence and ferroptosis in HCC.

GO analysis.

Significant enrichment was observed in several BPs, including cellular responses to chemical stress and oxidative stress. Regarding cellular components, enrichment was noted in promyelocytic leukemia nuclear bodies, transcriptional regulatory complexes, and telomeric regions of chromosomes. In the category of molecular functions, the genes were predominantly involved in transcription factor binding and ubiquitin protein ligase binding.

KEGG pathway analysis.

Pathway enrichment revealed significant associations with endocrine resistance, central carbon metabolism in cancer, and cellular senescence, among other pathways. The results of the enrichment analysis are presented in Figure 3.

PPI network and hub gene identification

Differentially expressed genes were submitted to the STRING database with the species parameter set to Homo sapiens. The resulting interaction data were visualized using Cytoscape software, producing a PPI network consisting of 39 nodes and 150 edges, as depicted in Figure 4. The top 5 hub genes were identified based on the MCC algorithm using the cytoHubba plugin. These hub genes included tumor protein 53 (TP53), Jun Proto-oncogene (JUN), RELA proto-oncogene (RELA), hypoxia-inducible factor 1 subunit alpha (HIF1A), and cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (CDKN2A), as illustrated in Figure 5.

These hub genes demonstrated high degrees of connectivity within the network, indicating potential central roles in the molecular regulation of HCC development and progression.

Validation of hub gene expression

The gene expression data set GSE14520 from the gene expression omnibus database was used as an independent validation set to assess the expression patterns of the 5 identified hub genes in HCC tissues. Analysis demonstrated that TP53, RELA, HIF1A, and CDKN2A were significantly upregulated in HCC tissues compared with normal liver tissues, whereas JUN exhibited significantly downregulated in HCC samples (Figure 6). These expression trends were consistent with those observed in TCGA database, supporting the dysregulation of these hub genes in the context of HCC.

Validation of hub gene expression in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues. CDKN2A, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A; HIF1A, hypoxia-inducible factor 1 subunit alpha; TP53, tumor protein 53.

Diagnostic efficacy of hub genes and their association with prognosis

AUC values approaching 1.0 indicated superior diagnostic efficacy. The AUC of ROC for the hub genes was as follows: TP53 (AUC = 0.723, CI: 0.669–0.776), JUN (AUC = 0.733, CI: 0.659–0.806), RELA (AUC = 0.854, CI: 0.808–0.901), and CDKN2A (AUC = 0.953, CI: 0.932–0.974) all of which demonstrated favorable diagnostic performance (Figure 7). These findings suggested that these genes may serve as potential diagnostic biomarkers for HCC.

Diagnostic efficacy analysis of hub genes for hepatocellular carcinoma. FPR, false positive rate; TPR, true positive rate.

The prognostic significance of the hub genes was further evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Expression levels of TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A were found to be significantly associated with OS in patients with HCC (P < 0.05). High TP53 expression was significantly associated with prolonged longer OS compared with low TP53 expression, indicating its potential role as a survival-protective biomarker. By contrast, elevated expression levels of HIF1A and CDKN2A were correlated with reduced OS, suggesting a possible association with increased tumor aggressiveness and poorer prognosis in HCC (Figure 8).

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis illustrating the prognostic significance of hub gene expression in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma. CDKN2A, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A; HIF1A, hypoxia-inducible factor 1 subunit alpha.

Construction of a prognostic nomogram for patients with HCC

A prognostic nomogram model was developed to quantitatively estimate OS in patients with HCC. The model incorporated the expression levels of TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A, along with the TNM staging system. The cumulative probability of OS was calculated by summing the individual scores assigned to each variable within the nomogram (Figure 9).

Nomogram constructed by integrating hub genes with tumor node metastasis staging to predict the prognosis of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma. CDKN2A, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A; HIF1A, hypoxia-inducible factor 1 subunit alpha; TP53, tumor protein 53.

The concordance index (C-index) for the model was 0.699 (95% CI: 0.666–0.732), indicating favorable predictive accuracy. Calibration plots further demonstrated strong concordance between the nomogram-predicted and actual 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS rates, supporting the model's clinical applicability and predictive reliability (Figure 10).

DISCUSSION

HCC remains a prevalent malignancy with substantial public health implications. One of the primary contributors to its high mortality rate is the frequent diagnosis at advanced stages, combined with limited effective therapeutic options. This underscores the urgent need for the development of novel diagnostic and therapeutic treatment strategies. The pathogenesis and progression of HCC are shaped by a complex interplay of genetic and environmental factors (10). Among these, cellular senescence plays a pivotal role in maintaining liver homeostasis and contributes to both the initiation and advancement of HCC. Ferroptosis, a systemic cell death pathway characterized by excessive iron accumulation followed by the generation of reactive oxygen species, has been implicated in various age-related diseases. Emerging evidence suggests that ferroptosis may serve as a potential therapeutic target to counteract senescence (11). The interplay between cellular senescence and ferroptosis represents a growing area of research with significant potential to advance diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for age-related diseases, including cancer (12). Clarifying the molecular mechanisms of HCC through the integrated study of cellular senescence and ferroptosis is essential for improving early detection, optimizing treatment modalities, and enhancing clinical outcomes. The interplay between cellular senescence and ferroptosis in HCC remains relatively unexplored. To address this, we concurrently analyzed genes associated with both processes to identify candidate biomarkers for early diagnosis and therapeutic targeting.

Our integrative bioinformatics analysis, leveraging data from multiple public databases, identified 5 hub genes (TP53, JUN, RELA, HIF1A, and CDKN2A) through the MCC algorithm. These genes are not only differentially expressed in HCC but also functionally linked to both cellular senescence and ferroptosis. Functional enrichment analysis of these hub genes further delineated key biological pathways, offering crucial mechanistic insights into HCC progression. Subsequent multidimensional validation confirmed their strong diagnostic utility, as evidenced by ROC curve analysis. Importantly, survival analysis highlighted TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A as significant prognostic factors for overall survival. Ultimately, integrating these 3 genes into a prognostic risk model demonstrated potential for improving outcome prediction accuracy in HCC. TP53 is a well-established tumor suppressor gene involved in critical cellular processes, including cell cycle regulation, DNA damage repair, and apoptosis (13). Elevated TP53 expression was observed in HCC tissues and demonstrated a positive association with OS of patients, suggesting a potential protective role through the induction of tumor cell senescence and ferroptosis. However, TP53 is frequently subject to somatic mutations, which may result in either loss of tumor-suppressive function or acquisition of oncogenic properties (14). Therefore, elucidating the precise molecular mechanisms underlying TP53 activity in HCC, as well as developing therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring or enhancing its normal function, may offer important avenues for improving clinical outcomes in patients with HCC.

HIF1A, a member of the hypoxia-inducible factor family, plays a pivotal role in mediating cellular responses to hypoxic stress and in promoting tumor angiogenesis. A recent meta-analysis demonstrated that HIF1A expression is significantly upregulated in hepatitis B virus-induced HCC tissues compared with normal liver tissue, nontumorous tissue, peritumoral regions, and hepatitis B virus-unrelated HCC tissue. Furthermore, increased HIF1A expression has been positively associated with capsular invasion, indicating a potential link to increased tumor invasiveness and metastatic potential (15). In this study, HIF1A was upregulated in HCC tissues relative to normal tissues, and higher expression levels were associated with reduced OS. This pattern suggests that HIF1A expression may enhance tumor cell survival under hypoxic conditions and contribute to angiogenesis and metastatic progression. Targeting HIF1A through a specific therapeutic approach may represent a viable strategy to suppress tumor growth and metastasis in patients with HCC.

CDKN2A encodes 2 key cell cycle regulatory proteins, p16INK4a and p14ARF, both of which contribute to the inhibition of cellular proliferation and the induction of senescence (16). Although CDKN2A functions as a tumor suppressor gene, genetic variations affecting p16INK4a and p14ARF have been reported across various malignancies, with CDKN2A dysregulation recognized as a significant contributor to the pathogenesis of HCC. Overexpression of CDKN2A in HCC has been linked to increased mutational burden and is closely associated with reduced OS in patients (17). In the current analysis, CDKN2A expression was upregulated in HCC tissues and demonstrated a negative correlation with OS, consistent with previously reported findings (17). These observations highlight the prognostic relevance of CDKN2A in HCC. A prognostic nomogram model was subsequently constructed by integrating TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A expression levels with TNM staging. This model provides a practical, quantitative tool for estimating long-term survival outcomes in patients with HCC.

JUN is an alkaline leucine zipper (bZIP) protein and a prototypical member of the activator protein-1 transcription factor family. Increasing evidence has indicated that JUN exerts context-dependent functions in malignancies, displaying both tumor-suppressive and tumor-promoting properties (18). Its tumor-suppressive activity has been attributed to the transcriptional regulation of genes involved in cellular senescence and inflammatory responses (19). In HCC, JUN expression was found to be downregulated, and this reduction was associated with favorable diagnostic performance. In addition, RELA, a key subunit of the NF-κB signaling pathway, plays a critical role in regulating ferroptosis, cell survival, and tumor development and progression (20).

RELA expression was upregulated in HCC tissues and demonstrated robust diagnostic efficacy. This upregulation may contribute to persistent activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, survival, and metastasis. However, in the prognostic analysis, neither JUN nor RELA exhibited a statistically significant association with OS in patients with HCC. As a result, these genes were not incorporated in the development of the prognostic nomogram. Further research is warranted to clarify their roles and to evaluate their potential as therapeutic targets in the management of HCC.

For patients with advanced HCC, sorafenib remains a standard therapeutic agent, yet its efficacy is often limited by acquired resistance. Interestingly, cellular senescence has emerged as a potential mechanism to overcome this challenge. Studies indicate that inducing senescence—for instance, with the SHP2 inhibitor SHP099—can resensitize sorafenib-resistant HCC cells and improve survival in preclinical models, positioning senescence induction as a promising sensitizing strategy (18,19). Beyond direct therapeutic interactions, senescence drives a profound phenotypic shift marked by the senescence-associated secretory phenotype. The senescence-associated secretory phenotype exerts dual roles in cancer: Initially, it may suppress tumor growth by recruiting immune cells, but its persistence can ultimately promote progression, metastasis, and therapy resistance (20). In HCC, key senescence-related genes such as CDKN2A and IL-6 are upregulated and likely contribute to immune microenvironment modulation (21).

Ferroptosis has also been closely linked to HCC progression and drug resistance (22). In sorafenib-resistant cells, upregulation of SLC7A11 enhances antioxidant capacity and suppresses ferroptosis, driving resistance (23). This process is further regulated through fatty acid synthase-mediated stabilization of HIF1A and subsequent SLC7A11 transcription (24). Moreover, the extent of ferroptosis inversely correlates with tumor mutation burden in HCC, with molecules such as TP53 potentially serving as functional links between these pathways (25).

After sorafenib resistance, several second-line therapies—including metronomic capecitabine, regorafenib, and immune checkpoint inhibitors—have demonstrated efficacy by targeting diverse mechanisms such as angiogenesis, kinase signaling, and immune activation (26–28). The effectiveness of these agents likely stems from their ability to remodel signaling networks and the tumor microenvironment, thereby bypassing established resistance pathways. The hub genes identified in our study may function as critical nodes within these adaptive networks, influencing drug response and immune activity. Supporting this, combining p53-targeting vaccines with PD-1 blockade has shown enhanced antitumor efficacy (29). Moving forward, we will use multiomics and gene-editing approaches to dissect the precise roles of these genes in prognosis and treatment responsiveness, aiming to inform more individualized therapeutic strategies for HCC. Despite the encouraging outcomes presented in this study, it is essential to take into account several limitations. Primarily, the findings were derived from bioinformatics analyses based on data obtained from publicly available databases, without experimental validation. Further investigations using cellular and animal models are required to confirm the functional roles and mechanistic pathways associated with the identified hub genes. Second, the analysis focused solely on the association between cellular senescence-ferroptosis-related genes and HCC, without considering the influence of other factors such as the tumor microenvironment and immune responses. Future research should integrate these elements to achieve a more comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying HCC development and progression. In addition, a limitation of this study is that the HCC data used lack detailed information regarding the specific underlying liver diseases and likely represent a mixed cohort of various chronic liver conditions. Consequently, we were unable to determine the precise impact of different etiologies on the expression of the identified hub genes during HCC development and progression. In future studies, we aim to use data sets with comprehensive clinical annotations or conduct prospective research to collect and analyze the expression patterns of these hub genes in HCC tissue samples stratified by specific etiologies, thereby further validating the accuracy and reliability of our current findings.

Despite the aforementioned limitations, the findings of this study provided meaningful insights. The analysis delineated the expression characteristics and clinical significance of hub genes associated with cellular senescence and ferroptosis in HCC, highlighting potential molecular targets for early diagnosis and personalized therapeutic strategies. Further research is warranted to explore the functional roles and underlying mechanisms of these genes in greater depth, facilitate the development of targeted therapeutic agents, and ultimately enhance treatment efficacy and prognostic outcomes for patients with HCC.

Aberrant expression of the cellular senescence-related and ferroptosis-related genes TP53, HIF1A, and CDKN2A was observed in HCC tissues based on integrative analysis across multidatabase using bioinformatics approaches. These genes demonstrated favorable diagnostic efficacy and showed significant associations with OS. A nomogram prediction model incorporating TNM staging and gene expression was developed, offering a theoretical basis for the early diagnosis and prognostic evaluation of HCC. These findings provide a foundation for future research and potential clinical applications aimed at personalized treatment strategies in HCC management.

Correction: A narrative review on trazodone as a multimodal and multifunctional antidepressant: clinical relevance of formulations, dosing, and pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic targets
Authors: Alessandro Cuomo, Maurizio Pompili, Antonio Vita, Edoardo Spina, Filippo Caraci, Andrea Fagiolini
Published: 2026

Correction: Ann Gen Psychiatry (2026) 25:32

10.1186/s12991-026-00643-8

In the original publication of this article [1], the authors identified a minor technical inconsistency in Vignette 1 (“Low-dose oral drops for sleep and anxiety in an elderly patient with depression”), within the section “Clinical vignettes: tailoring trazodone to the patient”. The error concerns only the parenthetical conversion between the number of trazodone immediate-release (IR) oral drops and the corresponding dose in milligrams. For clinical clarity, the corrected text also includes an approximate equivalence to the 50 mg IR tablet.

In the published version, the text reads:

we initiated trazodone IR oral [10 drops (~ 25 mg)] in the evening.

the dose was increased to 15 (~ 37.5 mg) drops.

These sentences should read, respectively:

we initiated trazodone IR oral [10 drops (20 mg, ≈ one half of a 50 mg tablet)] in the evening.

the dose was increased to 15 drops (30 mg, ≈ two thirds of a 50 mg tablet).

The corrected dose equivalents reflect the trazodone 60 mg/ml oral drops formulation used as reference, for which one drop corresponds to approximately 2 mg of trazodone hydrochloride. Therefore, 10 drops correspond to approximately 20 mg (about one half of a 50 mg IR tablet) and 15 drops correspond to approximately 30 mg (about two thirds of a 50 mg IR tablet). The tablet equivalence is provided solely to assist clinical readability and does not modify the underlying dosing rationale.

The original article has been corrected.

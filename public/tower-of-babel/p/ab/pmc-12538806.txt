The feasibility of the radiomics models for tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes level prediction in breast cancer based on dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI
Authors: Yuan Peng, Xuege Hu, Yulu Liu, Dingbao Chen, Xiufeng Chen, Yi Wang, Shu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDetermining tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) expression in breast cancer prior to treatment initiation is of considerable clinical significance. MRI demonstrates potential as a valuable adjunct to histopathological assessment. This study investigated the feasibility of developing a radiomics-based predictive model incorporating MRI and clinical features to determine TIL levels in breast cancer.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled 501 patients across two institutions. A total of 453 patients were utilized for model development, comprising a training cohort (n = 317) and internal validation cohort (n = 136), while 48 patients from an external center constituted the independent test cohort. Radiomics features were extracted and subsequently selected using ANOVA and LASSO regression. Logistic regression algorithms were employed for model construction. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminative performance between low-level and intermediate-high-level TILs, comparing the radiomics model with the integrated clinical-radiomics model.ResultsThe MRI-based radiomics model demonstrated robust performance for TIL level prediction across both training and internal validation cohorts, achieving areas under the curve (AUC) of 0.810 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.781–0.874) and 0.756 (95% CI: 0.676–0.837), respectively. The integrated clinical-radiomics model exhibited superior discriminative performance with AUCs of 0.828 (95% CI: 0.762–0.858) and 0.824 (95% CI: 0.755–0.893) for the training and validation cohorts, respectively. In the external independent test cohort, the radiomics model and integrated model achieved AUCs of 0.704 (95% CI: 0.558–0.850) and 0.767 (95% CI: 0.633–0.901), respectively.ConclusionsRadiomics features extracted from dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, when integrated with clinical characteristics, represent a promising non-invasive approach for predicting TIL levels in breast cancer. This methodology may facilitate clinical decision-making through enhanced pretreatment tumor characterization without requiring invasive tissue sampling.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-025-01968-8.Keywords: Breast cancer, Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, Radiomics model, Magnetic resonance imaging

Background

Breast cancer has emerged as the most prevalent malignant neoplasm globally and represents the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide [1]. Given the pronounced heterogeneity characteristic of breast cancer, the identification of optimal therapeutic strategies tailored to individual patients remains paramount.

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) have garnered considerable attention as a biomarker in recent years, encompassing immune cells that infiltrate and distribute within the tumor parenchyma and surrounding stroma, including T lymphocytes, B lymphocytes, and natural killer (NK) cells [2]. TILs constitute a critical component of the tumor immune microenvironment and serve a pivotal role in orchestrating local antitumor immune responses [3]. Accumulating evidence from numerous investigations has demonstrated that TIL density correlates significantly with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) efficacy and long-term patient survival outcomes [4–7]. Furthermore, with the advent of immunotherapy and the widespread clinical implementation of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) strategies, emerging studies have established that TIL levels serve as predictive biomarkers for immunotherapeutic response [8–10].

Currently, TIL assessment relies predominantly on histopathological interpretation of tissue specimens stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Tissue acquisition is limited to biopsy sampling or surgical resection procedures. However, inherent tumor spatiotemporal heterogeneity and sampling constraints preclude accurate and comprehensive TIL evaluation through biopsy alone. Moreover, surgical resection represents an irreversible procedure that cannot inform preoperative therapeutic decision-making. Standardized TIL evaluation guidelines for solid tumors have been established by the International Immuno-Oncology Biomarkers Working Group [11]. Nevertheless, the assessment process remains complex and susceptible to inter- and intra-observer variability [12], thereby complicating reliable TIL quantification. Consequently, there exists an urgent clinical need for effective, reliable, and reproducible methods for TIL assessment in routine practice.

Radiomics offers a promising approach for non-invasive and comprehensive TIL prediction. This methodology employs high-throughput extraction of quantitative features from medical images, transforming imaging data into high-dimensional datasets through automated or semi-automated analytical algorithms, subsequently enabling the construction of descriptive or predictive models based on selected radiological signatures [13, 14]. Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) plays a fundamental role in breast cancer evaluation, providing comprehensive characterization of tumor morphology and surrounding tissue architecture. MRI-based radiomics has been extensively investigated in breast cancer research, demonstrating substantial success in differentiating benign from malignant lesions, predicting molecular subtypes, assessing therapeutic response, and determining patient prognosis [15–20]. However, limited investigations with small patient cohorts have explored the utility of preoperative MRI-based radiomics for TIL assessment in cancer patients.

The objective of this investigation was to develop and validate an MRI-based radiomics model for preoperative TIL prediction in breast cancer patients and to determine whether integration with clinical parameters enhances predictive performance.

Methods

Patients Cohorts

This was a retrospective multicenter study. Breast cancer patients treated in our hospital from January 2015 to February 2019 were included for the establishment and validation of the model. Breast cancer patients treated in another hospital from January 2015 to November 2021 were used as the test set for external independent validation. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee (2024PHB246-001).

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed invasive breast cancer with a single lesion and staging of T1-T3; (2) breast-enhanced MRI examination performed at two medical institutions, with complete sequences and high image quality; (3) no treatment before MRI examination, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or endocrine therapy, etc.; (4) complete pathological data with archived pathological sections. Exclusion criteria: (1) T4 with invasion of chest wall and/or skin and inflammatory breast cancer; (2) having received any treatment before MRI examination; (3) missing or incomplete clinical/imaging/pathological data. Pathological TILs Assessment

According to the standardized method from the International Immuno-Oncology Biomarkers Working Group, the TILs in the tumor stroma (sTILs) of breast cancer sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E) were evaluated by a pathologist (with more than 20 years of experience in the diagnosis of breast tumors). The cases were categorized into two groups based on the TILs proportion in order to facilitate the assessment of variables: the low-level TILs group (< 10% TILs) and the intermediate-high level TILs group (≥ 10% TILs). General clinical and pathological features collection

The main clinical and pathological characteristics included in this study were patient age, clinical stage and histopathological characteristics: histological type, histological grade, and expression levels of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER-2) and Ki-67.

MRI examination and acquisition

Imaging equipment and imaging parameters

All patients were placed in a prone position with naturally sagging breasts, and underwent MRI examination using a GE 3.0T Discovery MR 750 W scanner with a dedicated 8-channel breast coil at our institution. The following sequences were used: (1) T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) with fat suppressed: TR 3848 ms, TE 83 ms, slice thickness/gap 6.0/1.5 mm, FOV 350 mm × 350 mm, matrix size 320 × 256; (2) Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI): TR 3120 ms, TE 76 ms, slice thickness/gap 4.0/1.0 mm, FOV 350 mm × 350 mm, matrix size 128 × 128, b value: 800 s/; (3) Dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) T1-weighted imaging (T1WI) with fat suppressed: TR 4.5 ms, TE 2.1 ms, slice thickness/gap 1.5/0 mm, FOV 350 mm × 350 mm, matrix size 384 × 320. After obtaining pre-enhanced T1W images, contrast agent (gadolinium gluconate, Gd-DTPA, flow rate 3 ml/s, 0.1mmol/kg) was injected, followed by 20 ml of physiological saline flush at the same flow rate. Six additional scans were acquired after the injection, and the first phase of DCE T1WI was selected for feature extraction.

External testing was performed using a MAGNETOM Skyra 3.0T MRI scanner from Siemens AG, Germany, and a breast-specific 8-channel phased array coil. The breast MRI sequence included: (1) T2WI with fat suppressed: TR, 3700 ms; TE, 63 ms; slice thickness, 4.0 mm; slice thickness/gap, 0.8 mm; FOV, 360 mm × 360 mm; and matrix, 384 × 384; (2) DWI: TR, 5980 ms; TE, 58 ms; slice thickness, 5.0 mm; slice thickness/gap, 1.0 mm; FOV, 360 mm × 360 mm; matrix, 224 × 124; and diffusion-sensitive coefficient (b-value) selected at 800 s/mm2; and (3) DCE-T1W1: TR, 4.4 ms; TE, 1.6 ms; slice thickness, 1.0 mm; no gap; FOV, 350 mm × 350 mm; and matrix, 384 × 384. After obtaining pre-enhanced T1W images, contrast agent (gadolinium gluconate, Gd-DTPA, flow rate 3 ml/s, 0.1mmol/kg) was injected, followed by 20 ml of physiological saline flush at the same flow rate. Six additional scans were acquired after the injection, and the first phase of DCE T1WI was selected for feature extraction.

ROI Segmentation

T2WI, DWI, and DCE T1WI images were uploaded to the Lianying Intelligent Research Platform System V1.0 (Shanghai Lianying Intelligent Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) for ROI segmentation by two radiologists (both with more than 5 years of experience in breast MRI diagnosis). The two radiologists manually delineated breast cancer lesion edges layer by layer, forming a three-dimensional region of interest (3D ROI) in each sequence. For cases in which there was disagreement amongbetween the radiologists, a third radiologist with 20 years of experience in breast MRI reviewed the images and provided the final delineation. One of the physicians redrew the MRI images of 30 randomly selected breast cancer patients after 3 months for intra- and interclass correlation coefficient (ICC) testing.

Radiomics Features Extraction and Selection

Radiomics features were automatically extracted from the ROIs on Research Portal V1.1 (United Imaging Intelligence, Co., Ltd. Beijing, China) using Pyradiomics V3.0. Before feature extraction, 24 filters were pre-applied to the source image individually to extract richer image features, which could aid in the modeling and analysis process. The radiomics features extracted from each sequence can be divided into four categories: shape features (14), texture features (72), gray-level statistical features (18), and high-order features (2160) (Supplement S1 and S2). A total of 6792 features from T2WI, DWI, and the first phase of contrast-enhanced T1-weighted sequence were extracted.

The Z-score normalization technique was employed to mitigate the dimensionality bias of different features. Using analysis of variance (ANOVA) for univariate feature selection, subsequently, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithm with 5-fold cross-validation was subsequently used for multivariate selection to reduce the dimensionality of radiomics features. The radiomics score (Rad-score) was used to transform multidimensional radiomics features into quantitative evaluation metrics through established mathematical equations or algorithms.

Predictive Models Establishment

The selected radiomics and clinical features after screening and dimensionality reduction were applied to the establishment of the prediction model. Logistic regression was used as a classifier to learn the classification of levels of TILs. Finally, two prediction models for breast cancer TILs levels, the imaging model and the comprehensive model, were established respectively.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R studio (version 3.4.3). Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR). The Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis H test were employed to compare the distribution differences of continuous variables among groups. The Chi-square test was used for comparing the distribution differences of categorical variables between groups. In addition, ICCs were used to evaluate the consistency of extracted texture features between the two radiologists and by the same radiologist. Features with ICC < 0.7 were excluded from further analysis. The receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) was utilized to assess model performance, while the DeLong test was applied to evaluate the significance of the area under the curve (AUC). A p-value less than 0.05 denoted statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics and TILs levels

This study finally enrolled 453 breast cancer patients for model training and validation, comprising 266 patients with low-level TILs and 187 patients with intermediate-high level TILs. Patients were randomly allocated into a training dataset (n = 317) and a validation dataset (n = 136) at a 7:3 ratio. An additional 48 breast cancer patients were included as the external test cohort, ranging in age from 26 to 72 years (mean age: 52.6 ± 10.8 years). Among these, 31 patients exhibited low-level of TILs and 17 patients demonstrated intermediate-high level TILs. Baseline clinical characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Feature Selection and Model Construction

A total of 6792 radiomics features were extracted from three MRI sequence. Subsequently, 537 features with ICC < 0.7 were excluded from analysis. The remaining 6255 radiomics features were included for subsequent analysis, comprising 2093 features from T2WI sequence, 2049 features from DWI sequence, and 2113 features from DCE T1WI sequence. Following univariate selection using ANOVA and multivariate dimensionality reduction via LASSO regression, 20 radiomics features were selected for the radiomics model development (4 from DWI sequence, 10 from DCE T1WI sequence, and 6 from T2WI sequence). For the comprehensive predictive model, 18 features were utilized, including 3 clinical features (histological grade, Ki-67, and n) and 15 radiomics features (3 from DWI sequences, 8 from DCE T1WI sequences, and 4 from T2WI sequences). Performance of radiomics and comprehensive model

Utilizing logistic regression as the classification algorithm, the predictive performance of both the radiomics model and the comprehensive model for TIL level determination in breast cancer patients is presented in Table 3; Fig. 5. Calibration curves for the training and external test cohorts are illustrated in Fig. 6. The radiomics model demonstrated robust performance across the training, internal validation, and external test cohorts, achieving AUCs of 0.810 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.781–0.874), 0.756 (95% CI: 0.676–0.837), and 0.704 (95% CI: 0.558–0.850), respectively. The comprehensive predictive model, constructed through integration of clinical and radiomics features, exhibited superior discriminative performance with AUCs of 0.828 (95% CI: 0.762–0.858), 0.824 (95% CI: 0.755–0.893), and 0.767 (95% CI: 0.633–0.901) for the training, validation, and test cohorts, respectively, compared with the radiomics model alone. However, these improvements did not reach statistical significance across any cohort (training cohort: Z = 0.68, P = 0.50; validation cohort: Z = 1.54, P = 0.12; test cohort: Z = − 0.77, P = 0.43).

Discussion

In this investigation, we developed and validated an MRI-based radiomics model for predicting TIL levels in breast cancer patients. The results demonstrated that the model utilizing MRI-derived radiomics features exhibited robust predictive performance for TIL assessment.

TILs have emerged as a prominent immunological biomarker of considerable clinical significance in recent years. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that TIL density serves as a reliable indicator of tumor responsiveness to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC), with pathological complete response (pCR) rates increasing proportionally with elevated TIL levels. Furthermore, high TIL infiltration has been associated with improved disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and HER2-positive disease [6]. With the advancement of novel therapeutic modalities, immunotherapy has assumed an increasingly pivotal role in oncological management, particularly in TNBC treatment paradigms. TNBC is characterized by distinctive features including elevated TIL infiltration, high programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression levels, and increased tumor mutational burden compared with other molecular subtypes [21, 22], thereby conferring enhanced susceptibility to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

Therefore, pretreatment TIL assessment can provide valuable guidance for personalized therapeutic decision-making. Given the inherent limitations of tissue biopsy in clinical practice, radiological examinations, particularly when integrated with radiomics methodologies, offer substantial advantages for diagnostic and predictive applications. Breast ultrasonography represents a fundamental imaging modality for breast cancer evaluation. Fukui et al. demonstrated that tumors with high TIL infiltration exhibited characteristic features including increased lobulation, reduced internal echogenicity, and enhanced posterior acoustic enhancement [23], with their scoring system achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.88. Mammography remains one of the most widely utilized screening modalities for breast cancer detection. Yu et al. reported that their radiomics model successfully distinguished low-TIL from high-TIL groups with an AUC of 0.83 in the primary cohort and 0.79 in the validation cohort [24].

Altered cellular metabolism constitutes a hallmark of malignant transformation. Beyond conventional anatomical imaging, 18 F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18 F-FDG) positron emission tomography combined with computed tomography (PET/CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (PET/MRI) enables quantitative assessment of regional glucose metabolism, thereby facilitating tumor characterization at the metabolic level [25].

However, each imaging modality possesses inherent limitations: ultrasonography is subject to substantial operator dependency and inter-observer variability, mammography demonstrates reduced sensitivity in women with dense breast tissue (particularly prevalent in Asian populations), while PET/CT involves ionizing radiation exposure with associated safety considerations.

Magnetic resonance imaging, as a comprehensive imaging modality capable of capturing extensive tumor-related information, represents the most extensively investigated approach for TIL assessment in current literature. Wu et al. focused primarily on T1-weighted imaging (T1WI) sequences, ultimately incorporating 5 radiomics features selected from 17 candidate features to construct a composite model integrating cytolysis scores with radiomics parameters, which significantly enhanced predictive performance (DeLong test P = 0.039) [26]. Xu et al. utilized exclusively the second post-contrast phase of dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) for radiomics analysis [27]. From an initial extraction of 396 radiomics features per patient, 7 features were ultimately selected for model construction, achieving AUCs of 0.742 in the training cohort and 0.718 in the validation cohort. In the investigation by Bian et al., feature extraction encompassed T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and both the first and final phases of DCE T1WI [28]. Their findings demonstrated that incorporation of clinical parameters yielded modest improvements in predictive performance (training cohort AUC: 0.86 [95% CI: 0.77–0.95] versus 0.88 [95% CI: 0.79–0.96]; validation cohort AUC: 0.83 [95% CI: 0.73–0.94] versus 0.84 [95% CI: 0.74–0.93]). Tang et al. investigated the differential efficacy of TIL prediction models constructed using radiomics features derived from various enhancement phases [29]. Their fusion-dominant multi-sequence radiomics model, incorporating DCE phases 4–6 and DWI, demonstrated superior specificity across both training and validation cohorts (AUCs of 0.934 and 0.872, respectively). The present investigation enrolled 501 breast cancer patients, representing one of the largest cohorts in the current literature addressing this clinical question. Radiomics features were extracted from T2WI, DWI, and DCE T1WI sequences acquired from preoperative DCE-MRI examinations. These high-throughput quantitative features underwent systematic selection and dimensionality reduction, ultimately achieving robust predictive performance across both the internal validation cohort and the external independent test cohort.

In our study, radiomic features derived from the first post-contrast phase accounted for a relatively high proportion of the features used to construct the predictive model, and these features also contributed more significantly to the model’s performance. This may be attributed to the different tumor-related information provided by different MRI sequences: contrast-enhanced breast MRI sequences can significantly improve image resolution, clearly display the internal breast structure and lesion characteristics while also providing hemodynamic information about the tumor. The time-intensity curve can comprehensively reflect the blood perfusion of the lesion, and the administration of contrast agents can more clearly delineate the relationship between the tumor and surrounding tissues. Therefore, contrast-enhanced sequences hold significant research value in various aspects of breast cancer, including diagnosis, staging, and blood supply assessment. Several radiomic features included in the analysis were extracted from wavelet-transformed images, suggesting that incorporating higher-order features plays a significant role in building the predictive model. The findings of Bian et al. [28] in this regard are highly consistent with our study. The researchers ultimately incorporated eight optimal radiomic features for predicting breast cancer TILs in their model, six of which were derived from wavelet-transformed images.

Wavelet transform is a commonly used image filtering technique that decomposes the original image signal into a series of wavelet functions with different frequencies and scales. By analyzing and processing these wavelet functions, feature information at various frequencies and scales can be extracted. Wavelet transform offers distinct advantages for non-stationary signals or signals containing abrupt changes. It provides higher frequency resolution in low-frequency components and lower frequency resolution in high-frequency components. Consequently, wavelet transform is particularly effective in detecting abrupt changes within normal signals or extracting useful information from noise. This makes it potentially more suitable than other image filters for providing valuable information in breast MRI images. In our models, Spatial heterogeneity features, for example, Cluster Prominence (e.g. c_wavelet_glcm_wavelet-lhl-clusterprominence) may reflect complex spatial arrangements of mixed lymphocyte and tumor cell distributions, indicating immune-tumor interaction heterogeneity. And Long-Run High Gray-Level Emphasis (e.g. c_log_glrlm_log-sigma-1-5-mm-3d-longrunhighgraylevel emphas- is) could correspond to linear infiltration patterns of lymphocytes [30]. Regarding regional distribution features, for instance, Small Area High Gray Level Emphasis (t2_wavelet_glszm_wavelet-hhh-smallareahighgraylevelemphasis) may correspond to focal lymphocyte aggregation [31].

Furthermore, our analysis revealed statistically significant differences in histological grade, hormone receptor status, HER-2 expression, and Ki-67 proliferation index between patients with intermediate-to-high TIL levels and those with low TIL levels (P < 0.05). These findings are consistent with results from numerous previous investigations demonstrating significant correlations between clinicopathological characteristics and TIL infiltration patterns [32, 33]. The inclusion of these clinical indicators improved the model’s stability across the training, validation, and test sets.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this investigation. First, the sample size remains inadequate, particularly for the external validation cohort. Furthermore, due to the limited sample size, subgroup validation according to molecular subtypes could not be performed. Second, this study utilized exclusively the first post-contrast phase for radiomics feature extraction, whereas the DCE sequence comprised six consecutive acquisitions. The comparative utility of radiomics features extracted from different enhancement phases warrants further investigation to optimize model performance. Third, the diminished model performance observed in the external test cohort compared with the internal validation cohort may be attributed to several factors: variations in imaging acquisition parameters across institutions, resulting in feature extraction inconsistencies; insufficient sample size in the external cohort, thereby reducing statistical power and increasing variability in performance estimates; and limited generalizability attributable to the constrained external cohort size, which may inadequately represent real-world population heterogeneity and consequently compromise model robustness across diverse clinical settings. Finally, given that only a small proportion of patients exhibited TIL levels ≥ 50% in this cohort, the TIL threshold was established at 10%, precluding the development of a predictive model capable of distinguishing among low-, intermediate-, and high-level TIL categories. Future multicenter investigations with substantially larger sample sizes will be essential to further refine and validate these preliminary findings, potentially enabling more granular TIL stratification and enhanced clinical applicability.

Conclusions

Our investigation demonstrated that radiomics signatures extracted from DCE-MRI, when integrated with clinical characteristics, provide a valuable approach for predicting TIL levels in breast cancer. This model represents a promising non-invasive tool to assist clinicians in pretreatment tumor characterization and therapeutic decision-making.

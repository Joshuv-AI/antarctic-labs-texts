Meta-analysis of the effectiveness and safety of Xingnaojing and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning
Authors: Long-Xun Zhu, Xiang-Fan Chen, Feng-Mei Li, Pan-Feng Feng
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:To evaluate the effectiveness and safety of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning.Methods:By retrieving the literatures published in the databases of PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, Wanfang Database, Weipu Database, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure from January 2010 to September 2021, the data of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning were extracted. The methodological quality of the included RCTs was evaluated by using the tools of bias risk evaluation of Cochrane Collaboration, and the data were statistically analyzed by using RevMan 5.3 software.Results:A total of 20 literatures were included, involving in 771 cases treated by Xingnaojing combined with naloxone and 761 cases in the control group. The effective rate of the experimental group is higher than that of the control group [risk ratio (RR) = 1.20, 95% confidence interval (CI) (1.14, 1.26)]. The average awake time (STD mean difference = −2.08, 95% CI [−2.60, −1.56]), physical recovery time (STD mean difference = −2.94, 95% CI [−3.59, −2.28]), delayed encephalopathy (RR = 0.44, 95% CI [0.31, 0.62]), and adverse reactions (RR = 0.23, 95% CI [0.10, 0.54]) was lower than that of the control group.Conclusion:Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning is significantly superior to naloxone, but it still needs to be further verified by high-quality large samples of RCTs.

1. Introduction

Carbon monoxide poisoning is a common acute poisoning disease in intensive care medicine. It is mainly caused by inhalation of excessive carbon monoxide in a short period of time. The clinical symptoms are acute hypoxia in the brain tissue. Most patients with carbon monoxide poisoning were already in a coma when they were admitted to the hospital, their condition was serious, and the mortality rate was high. Therefore, active and effective treatment measures should be taken in time to enable the patients to regain consciousness in a short time, thereby significantly improving the prognosis of the patients.[1,2]

For patients with carbon monoxide poisoning, routine prehospital emergency measures are often used for clinical treatment. However, a large number of clinical studies have shown that the therapeutic effect, rescue success rate and patient prognosis of conventional prehospital emergency measures are still not clinically satisfactory. Relevant studies have confirmed that naloxone has the pharmacological effect of shortening the patient’s wake-up time and reducing the incidence of delayed encephalopathy,[3] Xingnaojing injection contains Moschus, Borneolum syntheticum, Gardenia jasminoides J.Ellis, and Curcuma aromatica Salisb. as well as appropriate amount of other ingredients.[4] Patients with carbon monoxide poisoning have elevated levels of B-endorphins. Xingnaojing injection can directly act on the central nervous system and antagonize morphine receptors, thus reducing the level of B-endorphins in plasma. Reduced levels of B-endorphins increase the amount of oxygen in the blood, increasing the oxygen-carrying capacity of tissue cells. Therefore, Xingnaojing injection can improve the microcirculation of the human brain, stabilize the blood-brain barrier, reduce brain edema, increase the tolerance of the brain to various brain injury factors, remove oxygen free radicals, and play an antioxidant role.[5] In addition, the muscosterone in the drug component can also inhibit vascular permeability, improve the metabolism of water and salt in brain cells, enhance the ability of cells to tolerate hypoxia, promote the recovery of consciousness, has an obvious brain protection effect, and thus promote coma patients to wake-up.[6]Therefore, Xingnaojing injection is widely used in the first aid treatment of patients with carbon monoxide poisoning.

In recent years, studies have found that naloxone combined with Xingnaojing in prehospital emergency treatment for patients with carbon monoxide poisoning has a significant effect and can effectively shorten the time for patients to wake-up in China. However, due to the limited number of samples and follow-up time, the effectiveness and safety of this method cannot be explained systematically.

Therefore, this study intends to collect randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of the combined use of Xingnaojing and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning, and systematically evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the combination and provide evidence-based evidence for clinical medication.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Literature search strategy

Under the guidance of the systematic review guide PRISMA,[7] the databases of PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, Wanfang Database, Weipu Database, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure were searched for related original documents. The search time was from January 2010 to September 2021. This is a meta-analysis of the literature, so there was no need for ethical approval.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion standard

Inclusion criteria were as follows: Type of study: RCTs; Research objects: patients diagnosed with carbon monoxide poisoning. The patient’s age, race, region, and occupation are not limited; Intervention measurements: The therapy of the experimental group is the combined use of Xingnaojing injection with naloxone and basic treatment. The treatment plan of the control group is basic treatment or basic treatment plus naloxone; Evaluation index: The efficacy evaluation index includes effective rate, average awake time and limb movement recovery time. Safety evaluation indicators include the incidence of adverse reactions and the incidence of delayed encephalopathy.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: Reviews, Meta-analysis, comments, letters or abstracts; Repeatedly published research; Research outcome does not include one of the above evaluation indicators; Unable to obtain full text or incomplete data research.

2.3. Data extraction

Two researchers independently screened the literature based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria and extracted the data according to the pre-designed table and cross-checked it. In case of disagreement, the third researcher will help resolve it. Extracted content: first author, year of article publication, sample size, gender, average age, intervention measures, outcome indicators, and intergroup difference.

2.4. Study quality assessment

The quality of the included RCTs is evaluated through the “Cochrane Risk Assessment Tool”.[8] The evaluation content includes random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting and other bias. The low risk, high risk, and unclear risk were used to describe each item.

2.5. Statistical analysis

According to the content of the established baseline data sheet, the researcher separately extracted the data in the literature, and finally summarizes the extracted data. RevMan 5.3 software (Review Manager (RevMan) [Computer program], Version 5.3, Copenhagen: The Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, 2014) was used for data analysis. For the continuous variables (average awake time and physical recovery time), STD mean difference (SMD) was obtained to calculate the overall effect size and 95% confidence interval (CI). As for dichotomous variables (effective rate, occurrence of delayed encephalopathy, and adverse reactions), risk ratio (RR) and 95% CI were used. By calculating the I2 index to evaluate the heterogeneity, the I2 values of < 25%, 25 to 50%, 50% to 75%, and 75% to 100% were considered as homogeneous, low, moderate, and high heterogeneous levels, respectively. When P < .1 or I2 > 50%, it was considered that there was heterogeneity among the studies included in this indicator. Subgroup analysis of the source of heterogeneity was carried out, and the random effects model was used for analysis. When P ≥ .1 and I2 ≤ 50%, it was believed that there is no heterogeneity between the studies, and the fixed effects model was selected for combined analysis. A funnel chart was used to test the publication bias.

3. Results

3.1. Literature search results

According to the established search strategy, 90 literatures were found, involving 3 databases. A total of 61 literatures were removed due to duplicate content, academic papers, conference papers and literatures before 2010. We downloaded the remaining 32 literatures and read the titles and abstracts, and finally selected 20 literatures[9–28] based on the established inclusion and exclusion criteria. A total of 1532 patients were included, including 771 in the experimental group and 761 in the control group. The control group received conventional treatment or conventional treatment with naloxone, and the test group received conventional treatment with naloxone plus Xingnaojing injection in combination. 3.3. Bias risk assessment of the included literature

In the included 20 RCTs, there was no significant difference in patient baseline data (P > .05). There are 2 studies[16,21] using a random number table for random allocation, 3 studies[9,26,27] are grouped according to treatment methods, 1 study[11] is randomly assigned according to the order of visits, and 14 studies[8,10–13,15,17–20,22–25,28] only mention random. All literatures have a complete outcome. None of the literatures indicated whether allocation concealment is implemented. One study[23] used double blindness. The rest of the literature did not state whether to blindly blind the patients and interveners. Therefore, the overall quality of the literature was low. The risk of bias is shown in Fig. 2.

Risk of bias (A): risk of bias graph indicating the review authors rating regarding the risk of bias, presented as percentages, across all of the included studies and (B): risk of bias summary indicating the review authors judgments on each risk of bias item for each included study. Green color, low risk of bias; yellow color, unclear risk of bias; red color, high risk of bias.

3.4. Results of meta-analysis

3.4.1. Clinical effective rate

15 studies[9,10,12–17,19–23,25,27] have counted the clinical effectiveness of Xingnaojing injection combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning. The heterogeneity test results were P = .36 > 0.1, I2 = 8% <50%, indicating that there was no heterogeneity between the studies, so the fixed effects model was used. The results showed that the clinical effective rate of Xingnaojing injection combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning was significantly higher than that of the control group: (RR = 1.20, 95% CI [1.14, 1.26]), and the difference was statistically significant (Z = 7.34, P < .00001) (Fig. 3).

Forest plot of effects of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning on effective rate.

3.4.2. Average awake time

A total of 18 studies[9,11–25,27,28] reported the average awake time, and there was statistical heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 93%, P < .00001), so the random effects model was used for meta-analysis. The results showed that the average awake time of the experimental group was significantly lower than that of the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (SMD = −2.08, 95% CI [−2.60, −1.56], P < .00001, Fig. 4).

Forest plot of effects of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning on average awake time.

3.4.3. Physical recovery time

A total of 8 studies[11,13,14,16,18,24,25,27] reported the physical recovery time, and there was statistical heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 89%, P < .00001), thereby, the random effects model was used for meta-analysis. The results showed that the average awake time of the experimental group was significantly lower than that of the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (SMD = −2.94, 95% CI [−3.59, −2.28], P < .00001, Fig. 5).

Forest plot of effects of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning on physical recovery time.

3.4.4. Delayed encephalopathy rate

A total of 9 studies[9,10,12,15,21–23,26,27] have counted the delayed encephalopathy rate of Xingnaojing injection combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning. The heterogeneity test results were P = .24 > 0.1, I2 = 23% < 50%, indicating that there was no heterogeneity between the studies, so the fixed effects model was used. The results showed that the delayed encephalopathy rate of Xingnaojing injection combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning was significantly lower than that of the control group: RR = 0.44, 95% CI (0.31, 0.62), and the difference was statistically significant (Z = 4.67, P < .00001) (Fig. 6).

Forest plot of effects of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning on delayed encephalopathy rate.

3.4.5. Adverse reaction

Only 3 studies[13,17,18] analyzed and evaluated the incidence of adverse reactions (such as gastrointestinal reactions). The heterogeneity test results were P = .78 > 0.1, I2 = 0% < 50%, indicating that there was no heterogeneity between the studies, so the fixed effects model was used. The results showed that the experimental group could reduce the incidence of adverse reactions (RR = 0.23, 95% CI [0.10, 0.54], P < .00001, Fig. 7).

Forest plot of effects of Xingnaojing combined with naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning on adverse reaction rate.

3.4.6. Analysis of publication bias

The publication bias was analyzed through clinical effective rate (I2 = 8%, P < .00001). Two literatures were distributed asymmetrically. The remaining RCTs were concentrated in the inside of the funnel chart and symmetrical on both sides of the axis. It can be seen that the overall publication bias of this study was relatively small. 4. Discussion

Acute central nervous system damage is the main pathophysiological change in patients with carbon monoxide poisoning. Prolonged inhalation of carbon monoxide can lead to a significant increase in the expression level of carboxyhemoglobin in the body, causing severe hypoxia in the brain tissue of the patient, and inducing brain tissue edema, making the synthesis and release of β-endorphin significantly increase, and ultimately significantly inhibiting the patient The physiological functions of the central nervous system. If no active and effective treatment measures are taken, patients may have complications such as severe dysfunction of consciousness, delayed encephalopathy, and poor prognosis.

Naloxone is widely used as a strong opioid receptor antagonist, which can obviously reverse the effect of β-endorphin in inhibiting the body’s respiratory and circulatory system in a short period of time and has a significant wake-up pharmacological effect. Xingnaojing can specifically antagonize morphine receptors, thereby significantly reducing the expression level of serum β-endorphin, enhancing the ability of cells to carry oxygen, improving the pathological state of brain cell hypoxia, and ultimately refreshing the nerves and antispasmodic.

In recent years, Xingnaojing injection combined with naloxone has been used in patients with carbon monoxide poisoning and achieved good clinical results. However, due to the limitation of the number of patients and other reasons, the results are not necessarily convincing. This manuscript is to study the effectiveness and safety of Xingnaojing and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning through Meta-analysis.

The results of this study show that, compared with the control group, the combination of Xingnaojing injection and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning can achieve better curative effect and prognosis, and the adverse reactions are less. It shows the application prospects of traditional Chinese medicine in the field of carbon monoxide poisoning and the safety of rational use. The total effective rate of the experimental group is significantly better than that of the control group, which can shorten the average awake time, physical recovery time, and reduce the incidence of delayed encephalopathy and adverse reactions. However, this study has the following limitations: We only searched Chinese and English literatures, so we ignored Non-Chinese and English literatures published in other countries. In addition, the data under research is not included, which may lead to literature selection bias; The lack of such large-sample, multi-center clinical RCTs may be the main reason for publication bias; All the literatures included in the search are Chinese literatures, and the methodological quality of the literatures is not high, and most of them have missing information, such as the generation of random sequence method and the implementation of blind method, which affect the credibility of the results.

Despite the above limitations, this study confirmed that the combination of Xingnaojing and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning was significantly better than the control group in terms of effective rate, average wake time, physical recovery time, delayed encephalopathy, and adverse reactions. It provides strong data support for the combination of Xingnaojing and naloxone in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning, but further multi-center, large-sample RCT studies are needed for verification.

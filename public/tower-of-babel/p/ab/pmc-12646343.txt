A Bibliometric Analysis of Drug Resistance in Pediatric Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia
Authors: Alireza Khanahmad, Mohammad Hossein Khazaee-Nasirabadi, Mahshid Lotfi, Amir Hami
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Despite the improved outcomes in acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), drug resistance remains a major challenge. This study provides the first overview of global research trends in pediatric ALL drug resistance, highlighting emerging directions.Materials and Methods:In this bibliometric study, related keywords such as acute lymphoblastic leukemia, pediatric, and drug resistance were searched in the Web of Science (WOS) Core Collection. The retrieved papers were subjected to bibliometric analysis and visualization using Biblioshiny and VOSviewer.Results:A total of 432 original papers and 94 review articles by 2546 researchers from 48 countries were analyzed. The United States contributed 148 articles, which received 9569 citations. Bradford’s law identified nine core sources, with Blood being the most impactful (41 papers and 4900 total citations). “Oncology” was the top WOS category. Pieters was the most influential author, contributing 87 publications in the field. The most frequent keywords highlighted the biological contributors to drug resistance, including altered glucocorticoid response, overexpression of drug-efflux proteins (particularly P-gp), and the critical role of miRNAs and other genetic mediators in leukemic cell drug resistance. Based on the thematic map, the keywords of the motor themes were clofarabine, nelarabine, daunorubicin, vincristine, and microRNA, illustrating the need for further investigation of their clinical applications and the underlying mechanisms of resistance to these agents.Conclusion:Medical researchers have been attracted to different aspects of drug resistance in pediatric ALL. This bibliometric analysis reflects the current state of research on drug resistance in pediatric ALL and draws attention to critical concerns that require further investigation.

INTRODUCTION

Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), also known as lymphoblastic lymphoma, is the most prevalent cancer in childhood, characterized by a clonal hyperproliferation of lymphoid precursors.[12] Hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, pallor, fever, and bruising are the most prevalent clinical manifestations of the disease.[3] Due to rapid advancements in therapeutic agents, ALL has become a curable disease, with long-term event-free survival of 80%–90%.[4] However, drug-resistant leukemia subclones can cause relapsed or refractory ALL, which may adversely affect survival rates in patients.[5] Therefore, understanding the biology of drug resistance in ALL can offer novel insights into treatment strategies.

Drug resistance is a multifaceted phenomenon. Previous research has shown several mechanisms for developing antitumor resistance, such as changed transmembrane transportation, altered DNA repair, modified signaling, and dysregulated epigenetics.[6] In addition, the load, growth kinetics, heterogeneity, and metastasis potential of the tumor can act as biological biomarkers of anticancer drug resistance.[7] However, due to the specific progression mechanisms for each tumor type, the drug resistance mechanisms are not uniform.[8] Despite extensive efforts, the underlying mechanisms of drug resistance in pediatric ALL are yet to be fully understood. Therefore, particular studies on drug resistance in pediatric ALL seem crucial.

To our knowledge, while numerous studies have addressed molecular and clinical aspects of drug resistance in pediatric ALL, there have been no comprehensive efforts to systematically map the evolution and productivity in this research domain. Therefore, we analyze the knowledge structure and research trends in the field of drug resistance in pediatric ALL using a bibliometric approach. This study reveals: i) the publication trend, ii) the most influential countries, iii) the most impactful sources, iv) the most active Web of Science (WOS) category, v) authors with the highest number of contributions, vi) articles with the highest number of citations, and vii) the most frequently used author keywords for publications about drug resistance in pediatric ALL, to clarify the current research landscape and highlight areas that warrant further investigations. This work can serve as a resource for researchers, clinicians, and policymakers to guide further studies and strategic planning in pediatric oncology.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This study follows the Preliminary Guidelines for Reporting Bibliometric Reviews of the Bibliometric Literature.[9] This quantitative bibliometric study explored the status of research on drug resistance in pediatric ALL. General keywords, including “acute lymphoblastic leukemia”, “pediatric,” and “drug resistance,” as well as the medical subject headings terms, were formulated and searched by a researcher (AH) in the WOS Core Collection on August 30th, 2024. The WOS was selected as the sole database for our study due to its high-quality standards, wide coverage, and robust citation-tracking capabilities.[1011] We included original research papers, review articles, and proceeding papers and excluded retracted articles, case series, case reports, and editorials. The “Full Record and Cited References” were extracted and stored in “plain text” format. The data were then imported into Biblioshiny (R 4.3.1)[12] and VOSviewer for bibliometric analysis. Articles, countries, sources, WOS categories, authors, and keywords were visualized. Due to the presence of equivalent or synonym data, normalization was undertaken through a manual methodology by two reviewers (AK and MHK) to ensure a thorough understanding and correct interpretation of the keywords. Discrepancies were resolved through collaborative discussion. Figure 1 illustrates the bibliometric flowchart, comprising the method and search strategy.

Overview of scientific publications

This study analyzed 526 publications, including 432 articles and 94 reviews, contributed by 48 countries and 2546 authors. The first article was published in 1978. These articles have been cited 21070 times (i.e. average citation: 40.06), were published through 193 sources, and contained 936 author keywords.

Due to the fluctuating number of articles over the past two decades, it can be inferred that the topic is still considered significant among researchers. The annual growth rate was 5.9%.

Thematic map

A thematic map visualizes motor themes, niche themes, emerging or declining themes, and basic themes based on density and centrality in four topological regions. The motor themes, indicated by high centrality and density, are essential and require further development. The keywords presented in the niche themes have high density and low centrality, representing well-developed but distinct subjects. The emerging/declining themes, indicated by low centrality and density, are new subtopics that may appear or disappear. The basic themes, with high centrality and low density, are the main traverse subjects that have been studied extensively. Briefly, Figure 8 illustrates that clofarabine, nelarabine, daunorubicin, vincristine, and glucocorticoid resistance, as well as microRNA, are among the most important keywords in the motor theme. However, asparaginase, thiopurines, ABC transporters, multi-drug resistance (MDR), and P-glycoprotein (p-gp) are listed as basic themes.

DISCUSSION

Despite persistent research efforts to detect the underlying mechanisms of drug resistance in pediatric ALL over the last decades, no in-depth bibliometric analysis has been conducted. Therefore, this study was conducted to analyze related articles indexed in the WOS Core Collection using Biblioshiny and VOS viewer. This study aimed to identify the research trends in drug resistance in pediatric ALL to obtain the current landscape of research in this field.

The number of publications can illustrate developments in a certain research field.[13] This study explored 526 publications, including 432 original papers and 94 review articles on drug resistance in pediatric ALL. These articles were published between 1978 and 2024. Among these, the highest number of articles per year was published in 2020, with 26 articles. Due to the fluctuating number of articles over the past two decades, it can be inferred that drug resistance in pediatric ALL remains an interesting field among cancer researchers. Research trends are expected to continue evolving, especially with the advent of immunotherapies, gene-editing techniques such as CRISPR, and other innovative methods aimed at addressing resistance mechanisms.

A total of 48 countries participated in the current study by contributing publications on drug resistance in pediatric ALL. The United States, the Netherlands, Germany, China, and England had the largest publication numbers. A survey of 100 highly cited articles on myeloid neoplasms found that the articles came from 28 different nations. It was reported that the United States accounted for the largest number of articles (n = 69), followed by Germany (n = 24) and the United Kingdom (n = 24).[14] This was also in line with another research on the top 100 most cited articles on immunotherapy for childhood leukemia, which showed that the United States and Germany were the pioneering countries for immunotherapy against childhood leukemia.[15] However, another bibliometric study by Jiang et al. (2022)[16] reported that the United States, China, and Italy had contributed the most to articles on metabolic signaling pathways of tumor drug resistance. Overall, our study confirmed the findings of previous bibliometric studies[131415161718] regarding the undeniable role of developed countries in scientific knowledge production.

According to our findings, 193 distinct sources published related articles. Blood, the flagship journal of the American Society of Hematology,[19] was the most active journal, followed by Leukemia and Leukemia and Lymphoma. To our knowledge, Blood has almost always been reported as one of the most influential journals on hematological malignancies based on previous bibliometric studies,[20212223242526] representing its comprehensiveness and high impact on hematological oncology. Our results illustrated that several esteemed specialized journals in the fields of oncology, hematology, biology, and genetics had significant activity in the publication of scientific articles on drug resistance in pediatric ALL. However, the number of retrieved articles from generalist medical journals, e.g. Nature Medicine, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, Current Opinion in Pediatrics, Lancet, etc., was notably lower. Specialized journals have higher readership and citation rates within their specific domains, which makes them more effective venues for academic prominence. Publishing outside of these essential journals can reduce the reach and influence of an article within its topic area. Therefore, to optimize the impact of their work, researchers are increasingly prioritizing submission to high-impact, field-specific journals.[21]

As shown in Table 2, a large number of publications were featured in sources indexed in multiple prominent international databases, which are often preferred by researchers for disseminating high-quality studies, thus increasing the likelihood of subsequent citations.[27] This finding is consistent with earlier bibliometric research,[2829] in which most explored articles were published in high-impact journals, indicating that authors typically publish their highly qualified work in reputable publications.

According to Bradford’s law, there is an unequal distribution of scientific publications across various fields. In other words, the majority of articles on a specific topic are published in a small number of scientific sources.[30] This study determined three primary zones for sources that publish studies on drug resistance in pediatric ALL. The first zone consists of nine sources with the highest number of publications. The number of publications in these three locations is 181, 173, and 172, respectively.

We identified 42 active WOS subject categories in this research field. The two most active ones were “oncology” and “hematology.” Oncology is defined as the study of cancer treatment to apply scientific discoveries in clinical settings and to improve survival rates.[31] Hematology, the study of blood and blood disorders, investigates the pathophysiology of hematological malignancies and the underlying causes of cancer cell formation. Proposing therapeutic approaches to overcome hematological neoplasms also falls within the scope of hematology.[32] Overall, oncologists concentrate on improving treatment outcomes, while hematologists provide the basic foundations of leukemogenesis and medication response.[3132] Since ALL is the most prevalent form of pediatric cancer, and these two fields are well-suited to explore different aspects of the disease, this outcome is not surprising.

The analysis of co-occurrence network WOS categories also revealed a strong relationship between oncology and hematology. Since drug resistance is a multifactorial phenomenon, with genetic mutations, epigenetic modifications, and bone marrow microenvironment interactions as the major causes, it effectively links oncology and hematology. Research on drug resistance has expanded to understand how resistant leukemic cells elude existing treatments and how novel, targeted treatments might be incorporated into routine care.[33] Researchers have also tried to understand the cellular, molecular, and clinical aspects of the disease to overcome drug resistance in pediatric ALL. The convergence of oncology and hematology illustrates a multidisciplinary strategy for treating drug resistance in ALL.

The analysis of the retrieved top 10 highly cited papers illustrated that various aspects of drug resistance in childhood ALL have been the focus of cancer researchers. These include the molecular basis, epigenetic alterations, risk stratification, and re-sensitization measurements, as well as its impact on treatment survival. A total of 2546 researchers participated in the retrieved papers. Of these, 2528 researchers contributed to multi-authored publications, while 18 produced single-authored ones. The mean number of co-authors was 8.19. The most active authors on this subject were Pieters (n = 87, 16.5%) and Veerman (n = 50, 9.5%), who provided the most publications.

According to the thematic map, clofarabine, nelarabine, daunorubicin, vincristine, and glucocorticoid resistance, as well as microRNA, are among the most significant keywords in the motor theme. However, asparaginase, thiopurines, ABC transporters, MDR, and P-gp are categorized as basic themes. Daunorubicin and vincristine are well-known substrates for P-gp, an energy-dependent efflux pump encoded by the MDR-1 gene.[44] P-gp, which is typically upregulated in leukemic cells, is thought to reduce intracellular drug concentrations. Additionally, previous literature has confirmed the role of nucleoside analogue anabolic or catabolic enzymes in the cross-resistance of daunorubicin and vincristine.[45] Therefore, the phenomenon of resistance to these two drugs is not unexpected. Given the undeniable importance of these drugs in managing pediatric ALL, researchers have made extensive efforts to identify novel therapeutic agents where applicable. Clofarabine and nelarabine, two purine nucleoside analogues, have demonstrated promising efficacy in ALL, particularly in T-ALL and in cases where resistance to traditional agents is present.[4647] However, we believe that the recent reports of drug resistance to clofarabine and nelarabine in leukemic cells[4849] have led to their classification as significant keywords in the thematic map of our study.

The thematic map also revealed that, unlike other underlying molecular mechanisms (i.e., gene expression, gene expression profiling, polymorphisms, methylation, and epigenetics), microRNAs are now categorized as motor themes in the field. Previous studies have highlighted the dysregulation of circulating or extracellular vesicle-derived microRNAs, including the exosomal miR-17-92a cluster, miR-99a, miR-100, miR-125b, miR-324-3p, exosomal miR-326, miR-454, and miR-508-5p. Among these microRNAs, the upregulation of miR-99a, miR-100, and miR-125b is associated with resistance to daunorubicin and vincristine. Furthermore, suppressed expression of miR-454 is linked to resistance to L-asparaginase.[50] Dysregulated miR-181a and miR-196b are also connected to resistance against chemotherapeutic agents.[51] Overall, despite recent advances in scientific knowledge regarding the association of microRNA dysregulation and drug resistance in pediatric ALL, it should be noted that the categorization of the keyword “microRNA” as a motor theme highlights the need for further investigations in this regard.

This study faces some potential limitations that should be considered in future studies: i) we included all WOS-indexed, English, original/review publications; thus, some publications may have been overlooked, ii) WOS is continuously updating; therefore, we may present temporary findings, iii) since bibliometric analysis is a quantitative research method, it mainly focuses on the number of publications and may neglect the quality of publications, and iv) despite publishing high-quality and innovative papers in recent years, we reported that the top 10 highly cited articles were published before 2015. We propose that the most recent papers did not have adequate time to be read and cited by other publications. Despite the above-mentioned limitations, they may not substantially influence the main aims of the study, which are to illustrate overall research trends and to uncover the research hotspots in the field.

CONCLUSION

Despite the development of immunotherapy and novel targeted therapeutic agents, drug resistance is still a major barrier to achieving durable remissions and long-term survival. Researchers have widely explored the mechanisms of drug resistance, including genetic mutations or polymorphisms, epigenetic alterations, clonal selection, and microenvironmental interactions. This bibliometric analysis reflects the current state of research on drug resistance in pediatric ALL and draws attention to critical concerns that require further investigation.

Structural properties of antioxidant peptides released from sweet apricot kernel protein with regulation by ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstractKeywords: Ultrasound-assisted enzymatic hydrolysis, Antioxidant peptides, Sweet apricot kernel protein, Targeted release mechanisms, Molecular docking simulation

Introduction

Excess free radicals not only cause serious cellular damage to biomacromolecules (membrane lipids, proteins and DNA) in living organisms, but are also involved in the development of many diseases such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, neurological disorders, cancer and even Alzheimer's disease [1]. Bioactive peptides that can interact, neutralize free radicals and prevent them from causing cellular damage in biological systems, have been proven to have various biological health benefits (e.g. antioxidant, antihypertensive, antimicrobial, and anticancer effects) and fewer side effects after absorption into the body [2]. As a result, in recent years there are greatly increasing efforts to develop antioxidant peptides from animal and plant dietary proteins.

As is known to all, the protein source directly determines the composition and type of the antioxidant peptide [3], [4]. Among them, apricot kernels (Prunus armeniaca. L.), the seeds of Rosaceae apricot (synonym Armeniaca vulgaris Lam., subclass Rosidae, order Rosales, family Rosaceae, subfamily Prunoideae), are delicious nuts. Sweet apricot kernels (SAK) are valuable sources of oil rich in functional bioactive compounds such as essential fatty acids, tocochromanols, carotenoids, phytosterols and squalene [5]. However, SAK are mainly used for direct eating or for oil extraction with lower price, and the apricot kernel residues after oil extraction are useless, and urgently require deep processing to promote the value. Due to the higher protein content (approximately 20%, w/w) in the unprocessed nuts, some researchers have developed bioactive peptides from the protein of sweet apricot kernel (SAKP) [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11]. For examples, Mirzapour et al. investigated the antioxidant activities of protein hydrolysates from Iranian apricot kernels (Amygdalus scoparia) by five different enzymes (Pepsin, Trypsin, Chymotrypsin, Alcalase and Flavourzyme) [9], Souza et al. studied the antioxidant capacity of almond proteins prepared by enzyme-assisted aqueous extraction from almond cake evaluated by the ABTS and ORAC assays [10], and Siddiqui et al. employed the functionalized graphene oxide nano-sheets to facilitate the immobilization of trypsin and then examined the antioxidant activity of peptides from almond proteins. They found that almond protein hydrolysates with trypsin showed high DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activities [11]. Gu et al. discovered the isolated peptides with high α-glucosidase inhibitory activity from almond oil (Armeniaca sibirica) prepared by ultrafiltration, gel chromatography and reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography [8]. Although the activity and structural identification of protein hydrolysates from apricot kernels (or synonymously almond kernels) have been studied with different species in different antioxidant models, it is unknown how to make the antioxidant peptides more effectively so as to guide their production. In other word, the relationship between the enzyme-dependent structure and antioxidant activity of the peptides released from apricot kernel protein has not yet been clarified. Moreover, the molecular structures of bioactive peptides and their interaction with proteases have much less investigation.

Undoubtedly, the sequence and structure of the antioxidant peptide depend not only on the protein source, but also on the use of enzymes during the proteolysis process [4], [12]. Currently, many well-known methods (enzymatic hydrolysis, chemical methods, and microbial fermentation) are used to release antioxidant peptides from the parent protein [13]. Among them, enzymatic hydrolysis is considered as a safe approach for the large-scale production of protein hydrolysates rich in antioxidant peptides in a relatively low-cost manner [1], [12]. Unfortunately, the traditional enzymolysis method still has some shortcoming (e.g., low enzymatic efficiency, time consumption, and poor physicochemical properties of protein hydrolysates). Ultrasound-assisted hydrolysis, as an environmentally friendly food processing technique, can be used in the production of antioxidant peptides [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20]. However, current application of the ultrasound-assisted treatment mainly focused on the effect of various parameters (frequency, intensity, ultrasound mode, etc.) on the enzymatic hydrolysis reaction, the effects of the ultrasound-assisted enzymatic hydrolysis process on the release of bioactive peptides are unknown.

The dynamic motion and acoustic cavitation produced by ultrasound-assisted enzymatic hydrolysis can change the structure of proteins, making it easier for enzymes to access peptide bonds and increasing their efficiency in cleavage of the bonds between protein molecules [21]. Many researchers investigated the effect and mechanism of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on some plant proteins [22], [23], [24], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29]. However, the rule of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis for the controlled release of antioxidant peptides by various proteases from dietary protein is still unknown.

Therefore, the present study aims to disclose the effects and rule of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on the release of antioxidant peptides from SAKP. The different enzymes (Alcalase, Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain) were compared with structural properties and antioxidative activity of SAKPHs to discuss the rule. First of all, sweet apricot kernel protein hydrolysates (SAKPHs) were prepared using the four enzymes under ultrasound assistance. Subsequently, the changes in the degree of hydrolysis (DH), molecular weight distribution (MWD), functional group composition and structural properties of the prepared SAKPHs were analyzed and identified using UV–Vis spectrophotometer, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC-UV), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) electrospray (ESI) coupled to MS and MS/MS (UPLC-ESI-MS/MS), respectively. Simultaneously, their antioxidant activity (hydroxyl, superoxide anion, DPPH radical scavenging activities and iron-binding capacity) were examined. Molecular docking, a bioinformatics-based theoretical simulation method, can provide a new perspective to elucidate the interaction between antioxidant peptides and proteases, therefore, the interactions between the antioxidant peptide and the receptor molecule (proteases) were clarified by molecular docking simulation to provide an in-depth insight into the intermolecular mechanism of the antioxidant peptides released from dietary proteins. As a result, it is expected that the results of this research can supply theoretical basis and practical data of value-added utilization in agro-industrial residues containing dietary proteins for the targeted release of antioxidant peptide by the utilization of specific enzymes in the food industry (Scheme 1).

Experimental

Materials

Sweet Apricot Kernels (SAK) were purchased from Guangtai Feng Sugar Wine Co., Ltd. (Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, China). Proteases included Alcalase (2 × 105 U/g, alkaline protease) and Neutrase (2 × 105 U/g, neutral protease) fermented from Bacillus subtilis, Papain (0.5–2 units/mg) derived from Carieapapaya and Flavourzyme (2 × 104 U/g) fermented from Aspergillus oryzae, were provided from Sigma-Aldrich Co., (St. Louis, MO, USA). Trichloroacetic acid, ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, 2-deoxy-D-ribose, ninhydrin, trichloroacetic acid, sodium hydroxide, acetonitrile (HPLC grade), n-hexane, sodium phosphate dibasic, sodium phosphate monobasic dehydrate, and iron (II) chloride tetrahydrate (FeCl2·4H2O), 1,1′-diphenyl-2-picryl hydrazyl (DPPH) and the standards of bovine serum albumin (66,000 Da), cytochrome C (12,500 Da), aprotinin (6511.4 Da), bacitracin (1422.69 Da), DL-histidine (155.16 Da) were bought from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Hydrogen peroxide and hydrochloric acid were obtained from Guangzhou Tongyuan Chemical Technology Co., Ltd. All other reagents were analytical grade unless additional explanation was provided.

Enzymatic hydrolysis of SAKP

The SAKP was extracted from edible fruits of SAK after defatting treatment according to our previous work [30]. For enzymatic hydrolysis without ultrasound, SAKP was weighed and dissolved in sodium phosphate buffer to form the substrate solution (1 mg/mL) under magnetic stirring. Four enzymes with an enzyme/SKAP mass ratio of 4:100 were weighed and then added to the substrate solution to trigger the enzymatic hydrolysis of SAKP at the optimal parameters (50 °C and pH = 7.0 for Neutrase, Papain and Flavourzyme, 50 °C and pH = 8.0 for Alcalase). Ultrasound-assisted enzymatic hydrolysis was carried out in a JY92-IIN ultrasonicator (Shanghai Huxi Industrial Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) equipped with an ultrasonic probe (Φ6), the operating frequency of 20–25 kHz and the power of 0–650 W (pulse duration on-time 3 s and off-time 1 s). The hydrolyzed products (1 mL) were withdrawn from the reaction system (50 mL) at periodic intervals (0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 h) and then quickly immersed in boiling water (100 °C) for 20 min to inactivate the protease, thereby preventing further hydrolysis. The hydrolyzed products were then transferred to an ice-water bath to cool down. Samples were centrifuged at 4 °C and 10,000 × g for 20 min to obtain SAKPHs supernatant. The SAKPH powders can be obtained by the freeze-dried method. The above ratio of substrate to enzyme includes different ultrasound powers (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80 and 90%) and reaction temperatures (37, 45, 50, 55 and 60 °C) were examined to obtain optimal reaction conditions.

Structure and composition analysis of SAKPHs

Determination of DH

The degree of hydrolysis (DH) of SAKPH samples was determined using the ninhydrin colorimetric method [31]. The SAKPHs supernatant (0.5 mL) was withdrawn from the reaction system and then mixed with 0.4 mL of sodium phosphate buffer (pH = 8.0, 2 M) and 0.4 mL of ninhydrin (2%). The mixtures were reacted in a 100 °C boiling water bath for 15 min and then diluted to 50 mL of water after cooling down to room temperature. FTIR analysis

FTIR (VERTEX 70, BRUKER, Germany) was used to analyze the functional group composition of the samples at a resolution of 4 cm−1 with potassium bromide as a blank. Briefly, SAKPHs samples and its iron-chelating complexes were ground, and mixed with potassium bromide, and measured within the range of 500–4000 cm−1 and 64 scans [30].

Determination of MWD

The determination of molecular weight distribution (MWD) for SAKPHs was performed using the HPLC system (Agilent 1260, USA) equipped with a TSK Gel 3000 PWXL column (7.8 × 300 mm, Tosoh, Tokyo, Japan) and a UV detector (220 nm). The mobile phases were water/acetonitrile/trifluoroacetic acid (70:30:0.1, v/v/v) with a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The standard curves of molecular weight calibration were determined by BSA (66,000 Da), cytochrome C (12,500 Da), aprotinin (6511.4 Da), bacitracin (1422.69 Da) and glutataione (307.33 Da). The relationship between the logarithm of the tested molecular weight and the respective retention time was determined by linear fitting (lgM = −0.2099 t + 7.3764, R2 = 0.9548).

Identification of the structural sequence for antioxidant peptides by UPLC-ESI-MS/MS

The SAKPH powder was resuspended, diluted, and analyzed using an Agilent1290/Bruker maXis Impact Series HPLC system (Bruker Corporation, Germany) in conjunction with electrospray ionization (ESI) in positive mode. The acquisition parameters were described in our previous work [30]. The obtained data file was then imported to the Mascot search (http://www.matrixscience.com/) and searched by the following conditions: Swissprot database, Peptide tol. ±1.2 Da, MS/MS tol. ±0.6 Da, Peptide charge (1+, 2+ and 3+).

Measurement of antioxidant activities

Then, 0.2 mL H2O2 (10 mM) was added to the homogeneous solution to trigger the free radical reaction. Finally, its absorbance (Asample) was read at 532 nm. The absorbance of the control and the blank value (Ablank) were determined in the replacement of ascorbic acid (2%) and in the deionized water. Structural visualization and molecular docking simulation

The molecular structure of the identified peptides can be rendered using ChemDraw software. The MM2Job function in Chem3D software was used to visualize the 3D structure of the identified peptide molecules and subsequently optimize their 3D structure for minimized steric energy [34]. Subsequently, the PDB format was exported to the molecular docking simulation to clarify the intermolecular mechanism of interaction between antioxidant peptides and proteases. The PDB profiles of the receptors Alcalase (1wme, PDB https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb1WME/pdb), Flavourzyme (6zep, PDB https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb6ZEP/pdb), Neutrase (1npc, PDB https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb1NPC/pdb) and Papain (1bqi, PDB https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb1BQI/pdb) were retrieved from the RCSB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/). The docking results were analyzed and visualized using the PyMOL molecular Graphics system (Version 4.6, Schrodinger Company, NY, USA) [35].

Statistical analysis

Data from all experiments were collected in triplicate and then presented in the form of means ± standard deviation. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to analyze the data from all experiments at the significance level of p < 0.05. The results in the form of mean ± standard deviation were visually presented in the figures from OriginPro2018 (Origin Lab Corporation, MA, USA).

Results and discussion

Optimization of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis conditions for preparing SAKPHs

Influence of ultrasound power on the DH of various proteases

From our previous work [30], the hydrolysis activity of the four enzymes did not reach equilibrium within one hour and the DH increased over time without ultrasound-assisted treatment. Consequently, the enzymolysis processes of SAKP were further investigated in ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis at different ultrasound power densities during an enzymolysis time of 0.5 h. As shown in Fig. 1a, the DHs of SAKP by Neutrase and Papain initially increased with the ultrasound power in the range of 0–20% and 0–10% respectively, and decreased with further increased ultrasound power. The enzymatic activities of Alcalase and Flavourzyme showed a slight increase when the ultrasound power increased from 0 to 30%. Afterwards, the ultrasound power was increased to 90%, their activities gradually decreased by 21.3% and 29.9%, respectively, compared to the activities at 30% ultrasound power. This indicates that low ultrasound power promotes enzyme activity, while high ultrasound power leads to a reduction in DH. This is mainly due to the fact that with moderate ultrasound intensity, the contact area between enzyme and substrate is increased, thereby improving the activity of the enzyme. However, too high ultrasound power destroys the enzyme structure. Notably, the SAKPHs of Alcalase exhibited a higher DH growth rate during the ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis process. Based on the results, the ultrasonic power was adjusted to 20% to achieve higher efficiency of enzymatic hydrolysis to further optimize the production of SAKPHs by Alcalase (SAKPHs-A).

(a) Effect of ultrasound power on the DH of SAKPHs by Alcalase, Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain at 0.5 h; (b) Effects of different temperatures with and without ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on the DH of SAKPHs by Alcalase.

Effect of temperature on DH of SAKPHs-A

The activities of SAKPHs-A with and without ultrasound-assisted treatment were carried out at different temperatures and are shown in Fig. 1b. The DH of SAKP with ultrasound increased significantly with the temperature before 50 °C, reaching a maximum of 20.59% and decreasing slightly afterward. The results suggest that temperature-dependent ultrasonic cavitation occurs during the enzymatic hydrolysis of SAKP in the ultrasound-assisted process. The increase in temperature leads to a decrease in the surface tension and viscosity coefficient of the protein solution, which in turn reduces the cavitation value. And then cavitation bubbles are much more likely to form. Too high a processing temperature can therefore lead to reduced enzyme activity. Therefore, a temperature of 50 °C can be considered a suitable choice for ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis of SKAP.

Antioxidant activity evaluation

Hydroxyl radical scavenging capacity

Hydroxyl radicals (·OH), the most reactive oxygen species with a greater oxidation capacity than fluorine found in nature, arise from the loss of an electron from hydroxide (OH). Hydroxyl radicals can not only lead to lipid peroxidation of tissue, disruption of nucleic acids, degradation of proteins and polysaccharides, but are also associated with aging of the body, tumor development, radiation damage and cell phagocytosis. For this reason, the removal of free hydroxyl radicals from the living system is crucial for the prevention of a number of diseases. The Fenton reaction with hydrogen peroxide as the oxidant and Fe2+ as the catalytic system is used to generate hydroxyl radicals (Fe2+ + H2O2 → Fe3+ + OH– + ·OH) [36]. Hydroxyl radicals break down the cyclofuran ring into deoxyribose, producing intermediates such as malondialdehyde. It is then mixed with 2-thiobarbituric acid to generate dose-dependent chromogen λmax at 530 nm. In the presence of antioxidant peptides, the chromogen measured with a spectrophotometer is reduced because the antioxidant scavenges the hydroxyl radical [37]. Based on this principle, the ability of SAKPHs to scavenge hydroxyl radicals using the 2-thiobarbituric acid/deoxyribose/Fe2+/H2O2 system can be evaluated. Herein, the effect of different ultrasound power on the hydroxyl radical scavenging activity of SAKPHs hydrolyzed from different proteases was analyzed and is depicted in Fig. 2a. Their hydroxyl radical scavenging activity showed an increasing and decreasing trend with increasing ultrasound power. The higher hydroxyl radical scavenging activity, which was evident for Alcalase (63.03% ± 0.98), Flavourzyme (56.62% ± 1.54), Neutrase (56.86% ± 1.35) and Papain (59.79% ± 0.11) were obtained under the ultrasound power of 20%, 30%, 30% and 10%, respectively. Among four proteases with ultrasound-assisted treatment, SAKPHs from Alcalase had relatively higher antioxidant activity. Subsequently, the hydroxyl radical scavenging activity of SAKPHs-A (1 mg/mL) was analyzed and then presented in Fig. 2e. After ultrasonic treatment, their hydroxyl radical scavenging activity increased slightly at 37, 50 and 55 °C. The maximum values (64.60%) were observed at 50 °C. And then, the hydroxyl radical scavenging activity decreased with the further increase of the ultrasound-assisted temperature from 50 °C to 60 °C. These results suggest that the SKAPHs-A can achieve the relatively stronger hydroxyl radical scavenging activity than the tuna liver hydrolysates through the ultrasound-assisted treatment.

Antioxidant activities of SAKPHs by different proteases with and without ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis: (a) hydroxyl radical, (b) superoxide anion radical, (c) DPPH radical; (d) Fe chelation ability; The effect of ultrasonic temperature on hydroxyl radical (e), superoxide anion radical (f), DPPH radical (g) scavenging capacity and Fe chelation ability (h).

Superoxide anion radical (·O2–) scavenging capacity

The superoxide anion radical (·O2–) is a toxic species that poses a hidden and indirect disease risk in a variety of biological processes. In the food and pharmaceutical industries, pyrogallol oxidation spectrophotometry is often used for initial antioxidant screening. The process of pyrogallol oxidation is a chain reaction that can produce ·O2–. When the pH is below 9.0, the autooxidation rate of pyrogallol is positively correlated with the concentration of ·O2– produced. Therefore, the scavenging effect of antioxidants on ·O2– in this system can be quantified by UV–Vis spectrophotometry to evaluate the antioxidant capacity of SKAPHs-A. As shown in Fig. 2b, a significant increase in antioxidant activity of SKAPHs-A (11.66%), SKAPHs-F (10.36%), SKAPHs-N (5.88%) and SKAPHs-P (10.05%) was observed after ultrasound-assisted treatment. As shown in Fig. 2f, the superoxide anion radical (·O2–) scavenging activity increased with the increasing temperature and maximized at 50 °C (38.9%). Subsequently, their antioxidant activity began to decrease with increasing reaction temperature from 50 °C to 60 °C.

DPPH radical scavenging capacity

DPPH is a very stable free nitrogen center radical with the π-π conjugation of three benzene rings as a space barrier. As a result, the electrons that decouple the nitrogen atoms in the center cannot fulfill their role as electron pairs, but can scavenge other free radicals. Protein hydrolysates contain antioxidant peptides that act as hydrogen donors and donate hydrogen to DPPH free radicals, resulting in a stable product and thus reducing the number of free radicals. The absorbance of the DPPH ethanol solution with purple color shows a linear relationship with the concentration at the maximum absorption at a wavelength of 517 nm. The change in DPPH content can be intuitively recognized by this feature. A slightly increased DPPH radical scavenging activities of SAKPHs by the four enzymes (SAKPHs-A, SAKPHs-F, SAKPHs-N and SAKPHs-P) can be found in Fig. 2c after ultrasound-assisted treatment. As shown in Fig. 2g, the ultrasound-assisted treatment temperature from 37 °C to 55 °C had a facilitating effect on the DPPH radical scavenging activity. However, the further increasing temperature from 55 °C to 60 °C resulted in the decrease of DPPH radical scavenging activity. The above results suggest that SKAPHS-A with ultrasound-assisted treatment at 50 °C and/or 55 °C has a great potential as for a free radical scavenger.

Iron ion chelating activity

The ability to chelate Fe2+ is a kind of important evaluation model to evaluate the antioxidant activity, because Fe2+ promotes oxidative damage and free radical production. Compared with conventional enzymolysis, UAE significantly increased the Fe2+-chelating ability of SAKP hydrolyzed by Alcalase (10.53%), Flavourzyme (12.36%), Neutrase (4.40%) and Papain (4.53%) in Fig. 2d. The temperature-dependent increase in scavenging activity of SAKPHs-A against Fe2+-chelation ability from 37 °C to 55 °C is showed in Fig. 2h. The highest Fe2+-chelating ability (88.7%) of SAKPHs-A can be achieved at the ultrasound-assisted treatment temperature of 55 °C. And then Fe2+-chelating ability decreased with the further increase of ultrasound-assisted treatment temperature.

Effect of online ultrasound-assisted treatment on MWD of the SAKPHs

Ultrasound power

The SAKPHs-A and SAKPHs-F without ultrasound-assisted treatment have smaller MWD in our previous work [30]. Subsequently, Fig. 3 shows the effects of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on the MWD of SAKPHs by various proteases. As shown in Fig. 3a, a macromolecular peak appeared about 12 min before ultrasound treatment, but disappeared after ultrasound-assisted treatment. The results showed that ultrasound improved Alcalase-mediated hydrolysis. The maximum MWD peaks were between 1422.69 Da and 307.33 Da. However, after approximately 12 min without ultrasound, the macromolecular peak of the Flavourzyme hydrolysates was undetectable. In contrast to the variation tendency of Alcalase with ultrasound, the intensity of the macromolecular peak for the Flavourzyme hydrolysates in Fig. 3b increased as the ultrasound power increased from 10% to 90%. The molecular weight changes of the hydrolysates by Neutrase and Papain showed a similar trend to Flavourzyme in Fig. 3c and Fig. 3d, respectively. These results suggest that ultrasound-assisted treatment with Alcalase has a synergistic effect on SAKPHs and reduces the average molecular weight of the hydrolysates. However, ultrasound-assisted treatment showed an inhibitory effect on SAKPH by Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain.

Molecular weight distribution of sweet apricot kernel protein hydrolysates by ultrasound-assisted enzymatic hydrolysis for 0.5 h by Alcalase (a), Flavourzyme (b), Neutrase (c) and Papain (d) under different ultrasonic powers; (e) Molecular weight distribution of sweet apricot kernel protein hydrolysates by Alcalase with and without ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis after 0.5 h hydrolysis with different temperature.

Ultrasonic treatment temperature

Based on the above results, the influences of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis at different temperatures on the MWD of SAKPHs by Alcalase were further investigated. As shown in Fig. 3e, the hydrolysate peaks showed a slight decrease after applying ultrasound at temperature of 45 °C, 55 °C and 60 °C. After ultrasonication at 50 °C and 37 °C, the peaks shifted to the right, indicating a decrease of molecular weight. Meanwhile, these hydrolysates with and without ultrasound-assisted treatment had a molecular weight of less than 1,422.69 Da. There are some opposite results in the reference, for example, Noman et al. discovered that enzymatic hydrolysis of Chinese sturgeon by ultrasonic pretreatment resulted in a significant increase in the percentage of molecular weights (≤1000 Da) [19]. Zhang et al. [18] found that ultrasound in pretreatment can accelerate the release of small molecule peptides from native collagen materials that are hydrolyzed by Alcalase. Jin et al. [17] reported that smaller peptides could be advantageously obtained by pretreatment with dual-frequency power ultrasound on corn gluten meal hydrolysates by Alcalase. Furthermore, previous reports have shown that ultrasonic pretreatment significantly increases enzymatic hydrolysis activity due to the release of hidden active sites within the protein by ultrasonic cavitation [38], [39]. However, ultrasound-assisted online enzymatic hydrolysis is a more complex reaction system because the ultrasound-assisted process not only affects the reactant proteins but also changes the enzyme activity. The molecular weight distribution profiles of the hydrolysate by Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain showed a negative influence after various ultrasound treatments. But Alcalase had a positive correlation with ultrasound, suggesting that ultrasound can expose active sites of Alcalase and enhance its interaction with the cleavage sites of SAKP.

FTIR analysis

The FTIR spectra of the SAKPHs with and without ultrasound-assisted treatment by the various proteases and their corresponding iron-chelating hydrolysates are shown in Fig. 4. The stretching vibrations of the N-H/O-H band of amide/water were at 3500–3300 cm−1 in all samples. It can be found that the absorption peaks at this location became significantly stronger than those of SAKPHs after the chelation of Fe ions. This change was due to the formation of Fe-N bond instead of N-OH (hydrogen bonds) in the coordination of Fe-bonding hydrolysates [40], [41]. Peptide sequence identification by UPLC-ESI-MS/MS

The sequence of peptides derived from plant proteins can be determined with high accuracy using UPLC-ESI-MS/MS. A total of 132 peptides have been identified in the SAKPHs. Detailed information (peptide sequence, matching score, protein sources, range, etc.) is shown in Table 1a, Table 1b, Table 1c, Table 1d and Table S1. Simultaneously, their mass spectra matched with the database Mascot Search are presented in Fig. 5 and Fig. S2. 2D/3D structure and stability of the peptides derived from SAKPHs

In order to better understand the chemical structure of the SKAP peptide molecule, we visualized and described the chemical structure of twelve identified SKAP peptides, and the results were shown in Fig. S1. It can be found that although SKAP peptides show planar linear structure in 2D structure, they have different convolution degrees in 3D structure, resulting in different spatial stereochemical energy. Then the spatial stereochemical energy is calculated and optimized by stretching the bond length and changing the atomic subunits on the group. The optimal spatial energies of SEAGVTE and SHNLPILR almond polypeptides containing serine at the N-terminal are −6.5663 and −25.9400 Kcal/mol, respectively, are obtained from the active site of Alcalase. It is well known that molecules are confined in crystals and their energies are lowest when they are free. When simulating molecular docking, the computer will automatically find different conformations of small molecules for docking, but the way of searching is random. Setting a small number of docking times may prevent it from finding the lowest energy conformity, but setting a large number of docking times may have little effect on the energy conformity of the ship. If the energy is minimized before docking, no matter what the parameters are set, the lowest energy conformation must be inside. Therefore, in order to make the results of molecular docking more accurate, the molecular conformation of SKAP peptide obtained in the Fig. S1 is the lowest.

Insight into the effect of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on the structure, composition and antioxidant activity of SAKPHs

As shown in Fig. 1a, the DH of SAKPHs by four proteases showed an increasing and decreasing pattern with increasing ultrasound power. UAE significantly increased the DH of SAKP by Alcalase (5.98%), Flavourzyme (5.00%), Neutrase (1.07%) and Papain (3.11%). All ultrasound-assisted treatments at different temperatures significantly increased the DH of SAKPHs-A (Fig. 1b). The results are in accord with the report by Mirzapour et al., [7], [9]. Our results show that Alkalase is a better option for both the hydrolysis of SKAP and the formation of bioactive peptides. In ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis, the SAKPHs-A showed different levels of antioxidant activities. Higher radical scavenging activity for hydroxyl and superoxide anion at 50 °C and stronger radical scavenging activity for DPPH at 55 °C than the conventional hydrolysates of Alcalase were observed. At a moderate enzymolysis time of 0.5 h and a moderate temperature (Fig. 2d), the Fe chelating ability of SAKPHs on various proteases was significantly increased by the ultrasound-assisted treatment compared to traditional enzymolysis. WMD evaluation shows that ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis can enhance the release of small molecule peptides from SAKP by Alcalase but inhibits the cleavage of small molecules from the binding site on the structure of Flavorzyme, Neutrase and Papain. Further analysis reveals that the highest DH and smaller molecular weight distributions of SAKPHs-A can be achieved at 50 °C in ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis (Fig. 3e). It is generally accepted that short-chain peptides have stronger antioxidant properties compared to large-molecule active peptides. The results indicate that SAKPHs hydrolyzed from Alcalase have relatively better ultrasonic resistance. FTIR spectra (Fig. 4) showed that it formed new iron-binding chelates with the carboxyl oxygen and amino nitrogen atoms in the peptide of the hydrolysates. After ultrasound-assisted treatment, the amino and carboxylate groups for complexing peptides with Fe2+ exhibit a higher absorption peak, resulting in the improvement of iron binding capacities for SAKPHs.

In addition to the DH and molecular weight distribution, the antioxidant activities of protein hydrolysates are also influenced by their composition in amino acids, sequences, and molecular structural features (hydrophobicity and chargeability) [44], [45]. Nevertheless, the amino acid sequence and composition of protein hydrolysates can be tailored by the enzyme-dependent relationship of the proteins. In other words, the release of antioxidant peptides depends on the position of the enzymes on the peptide chains used for proteolysis. First of all, it is generally believed that a peptide sequence rich in one or more active amino acids has higher antioxidant activity than those without active amino acids. Among active amino acids, hydrophobic amino acids (e.g. Leu, Pro, Val, and Ala) have better ability to scavenge free radicals. For examples, previous studies have shown that active peptides containing hydrophobic amino acids such as leucine (Leu, L), isoleucine (Ile, I), and/or glycine (Gly, G) at the N-terminus can improve antioxidant properties [46]. Negatively and positively charged acidic amino acids (e.g. Asp, Glu, Arg, Phe, Lys, His), which serve as strong electron donors or chelators of metal ions and/or strong hydrogen donors, exhibit greater antioxidant activity than common peptides that contain hydrophobic amino acids [47], [48]. Our previous work [30] has found that the presence of more active amino acids (e.g. His, Val, Ile, Leu, Phe, Ala and Gly) can increase the antioxidant capacity of SHNLPILR and SEAGVTE peptides, which in turn contributes to high antioxidant activities of SAKPHs-A. Li et al.[13] reported that low molecular weight peptides with charged acidic amino acids showed excellent performance due to their imidazole ring chelated to metal ions. Likewise, Dev et al. [49] reported that proline (Pro, P), alanine (Ala, A), valine (Val, V), leucine (L), isoleucine (Ile, I), and phenylalanine (Phe, F) on the conjugates of whey protein resulted in a significant increase in antioxidant activity. Secondly, the antioxidant activity was linked to branched-chain amino acids such as valine, leucine, and isoleucine [13]. The classification of the 24 identified peptides in SAKPHs-A is as follows: 13 peptides match Arabidopsis thaliana, and 5 match Oryza sativa japonica. The presence of active amino acids such as arginine in the active peptides significantly increases the antioxidant abilities of SAKPHs-A during ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis. Furthermore, the 3 peptides (RVAFVTAIPK, DIPGSLYVDT and KVVIDTATLGSSPLVLQLE) rich in proline (Pro, P), Asp (D), Lys (K) show excellent performance for higher iron binding ability of SAKPHs-A because their cyclic ring is chelated to metal ions. Herein, identification of antioxidant peptides from SAKPHs using LC-ESI-MS/MS demonstrate that Alcalase fermented from Bacillus subtilis remain a serine-type endoprotease with broad substrate specificity with or without ultrasound-assisted treatment. Subsequently, it can be speculated from Fig. 5 that Flavourzyme obtained from Aspergillus oryzae by fermentation has both exo- and endopeptidase activities characteristic of aspartic acid (Asp, D) as the sequence terminus in traditional enzymoylsis. During ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis, Flavourzyme exhibits the enzymatic properties of arginine (Arg, R), aspartic acid (Asp, D) and Leucine (Leu, L) at the sequence terminal or at the cleavage site. Among the 31 identified peptides in SAKPHs-F, there are 18 identified peptides matched from Arabidopsis thaliana. Similarly, of the forty-eight identified peptides, seventeen are matched from Arabidopsis thaliana and nine are matched from Oryza sativa. By analyzing the enzymatic cleavage sites of forty-eight peptides in SAKPHs-N, it reveals that Neutrase is an endo-protease of the lysine type, cleaving peptide chains primarly on the carboxyl side of lysine with or without ultrasound-assisted treatment. Papain, a sulfhydryl protease endopeptidase, is reported to have a broad peptide chain on the carboxyl groups of Arginine (Arg, R), Lysine (Lys, K), Glycine (Gly, G) and their residues in proteins. Results from 29 peptide sequences of SAKPHs-P by traditional enzymolysis, predominantly matched from Arabidopsis thaliana, indicate that papain targets the R carboxyl groups at the terminal end or the cleavage site. Subsequently, papain with ultrasound-assisted treatment shows arginine and aspartic acid characteristics as the sequence terminus or cleavage sites.

Conclusion

The present study has evaluated the effects of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on production, identification, characterization and molecular interaction of antioxidant peptides from sweet apricot kernel protein hydrolysates (SAKPHs). Compared to traditional enzymolysis, the SAKPHs-A with higher DH (22.35% ± 1.62) and smaller molecular weight distribution (average <1422.69 Da) exhibit the higher antioxidant activity in hydroxyl radical (63.03% ± 0.98), superoxide anion radical (26.94% ± 0.19), DPPH radical (38.92% ± 0.08) and Fe-chelating capacity (65.0% ± 60.17) under 20% ultrasonic power and 50 °C ultrasonic temperature. The 132 peptides identified from SAKPHs are primarily matched from Arabidopsis thaliana. The identified peptides hydrolyzed by Alcalase hydrolysis show a characteristic of serine endpoints at the sequence or cleavage site. Similarly, the peptides hydrolyzed by Neutrase display the property of lysine at the sequence-terminal or the cleavage site. These peptides, hydrolysed from SAKPHs by Flavourzyme and Papain in traditional enzymolysis, exhibit the characteristic of aspartic acid and arginine, respectively, as sequence terminus or the cleavage sites. During the ultrasonic enzymatic degradation, other peptides identified by these four proteases show the properties of arginine, aspartic acid, leucine and arginine, aspartic acid, respectively, as sequence terminus or cleavage sites. Molecular docking simulation reveals that the antioxidant peptides mainly interact with the active pocket of Alcalase (Asn66-His68-Asn162-Ser239-Trp241-Ser255), Flavourzyme (Leu33-Ala51-Gly53-Arg55-Asp261-Tyr312-Gly336-Asp338-Pro340-Ser344), Neutrase (Asn113-Ala114-Trp116-Asn117-Glu144-Glu151-Asn155-Try158-Asn166-Leu203-Arg204-Gly213-Asn214-His232-Thr233), Papain (Ser21-Asn64-Gly66-Trp69-Gln112-Val157-Asp158-Ser205) through hydrogen bonds. In addition, the differential active pockets of Alcalase, Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain in traditional enzymolysis are Asp30-Gly102-Pro108-Ser109-Ser116-Tyr119-Gly131-Thr139-Asn144-Arg150-Lys151-Asn152-Asp153-Glu163-Asn166-Lys281-Ser324-Ser326-Pro425-Thr427, Gln91-Lys99-Ser248-Lys255-Val285-Glu287-Ala299-Cys301-Tyr325-Asn337-Gln359-Arg362, Ser49-Tyr107-Ser119-Val140-Asn152-Tyr194-Lys220-Arg221-Asn228 and Gly23-Arg111-Gln128-Asn155-Tyr208-Pro209 residues, respectively. The differential active pockets of Alcalase, Flavourzyme, Neutrase and Papain in ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis are Gly164-Ala172-Tyr195-Phe240-Asn383-Arg396, BMA3-Man4-Asp27-Tyr29-Glu43-Lys54-Glu61-Lys118-Ser153-Asp183-Ser186-Asp286-Asp298-Thr302-Thr305-Asp307-Ser339-Asp346, Glu167-Ser223 and Asp6-Gly20-Gys22-Arg58-Gln92-Gln114-Ala126-Lys156 residues, respectively. The investigation, which focuses on the effect of ultrasound-assisted enzymolysis on the enzyme-dependent antioxidant activity of SKAPHs, proves that ultrasound selectively increases cleavage sites of enzymolysis and the targeted release of antioxidant peptides from dietary proteins, and can serve as a guide for the targeted release of antioxidant peptides from dietary proteins by using specific enzymes in the food industry.

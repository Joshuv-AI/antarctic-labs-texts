Comparative predictive value of three non-insulin-based insulin resistance indices for subclinical left ventricular dysfunction in type 2 diabetes mellitus
Authors: Xin Zhong, Xu Xie, Yongjun Hu, Yutao Zhang, Huiping You, Huan Yang, Ji Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent research indicates that novel non-insulin-based insulin resistance indicators—including the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, triglyceride/high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TG/HDL-C), and Metabolic Score for Insulin Resistance (METS-IR)—are closely associated with cardiovascular disease risk in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Notably, the TyG index demonstrates significant correlation with subclinical left ventricular dysfunction (SLVD), making it a potential biomarker for early detection of SLVD in T2DM patients. However, the predictive value of TG/HDL-C and METS-IR in this context remains unclear, and no comparative analysis of their predictive efficacy has been conducted. The objective of this study is to evaluate and compare the predictive value of these three indices for SLVD in T2DM patients.MethodsA total of 221 hospitalized patients with T2DM from the Department of Endocrinology, Hunan Provincial People’s Hospital (The First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan Normal University), between June 2024 and February 2025, were included. Participants were categorized into SLVD (left ventricular global longitudinal strain, GLS < 18%) and non-SLVD (GLS ≥ 18%) groups based on speckle-tracking echocardiography. Univariate logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with SLVD. Multivariate logistic regression, adjusted for confounders, assessed the relationship of TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR with SLVD. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to compare their predictive performance.ResultsUnivariate analysis revealed that smoking, hypertension, BMI, creatinine, triglycerides, HbA1c, fasting blood glucose, TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR were positively associated with SLVD, while gender and HDL-C were negatively correlated (all P < 0.05). In multivariate analysis, TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR remained significantly and positively associated with SLVD (all P < 0.001). ROC analysis showed AUCs of 0.785, 0.798, and 0.819 for TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR, respectively, with no significant differences in predictive value according to Delong’s test (P > 0.05).ConclusionTyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR are significantly associated with SLVD in T2DM patients and demonstrate good predictive performance. These indices are valuable for clinical application, especially in primary care settings, though no significant difference in predictive efficacy was observed among them.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05683-2.Keywords: TyG index, TG/HDL-C, METS-IR, T2DM, Subclinical Left Ventricular Dysfunction

Introduction

According to the International Diabetes Federation(IDF), there were 537 million people with diabetes worldwide in 2021, over 90% of whom had type 2 diabetes (T2DM)[1]. Diabetic cardiomyopathy is a common complication, with heart failure (HF) representing a major cause of death in patients with T2DM [2].A phase of subclinical left ventricular dysfunction (SLVD) is often experienced by T2DM before manifestation of overt HF. The preservation of normal left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and the absence of typical clinical symptoms during this stage make early detection difficult. Insulin resistance (IR) has been identified as an independent risk factor for the progression from SLVD to HF in patients with T2DM [3, 4]. The hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp (HEC) is the gold standard for assessing IR. However, its complex operation and high cost limit its clinical application. In contrast, the homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), which is currently widely used, is susceptible to inaccuracies in insulin measurement [5]. Therefore, several novel non-insulin-based IR indices have been developed and validated as effective alternatives. These include the triglyceride-glucose index (TyG index), the triglyceride-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (TG/HDL-C) ratio, and the metabolic score for insulin resistance (METS-IR) [6]. These new indices can be derived from routine biochemical measurements, which making them more convenient for clinical use. Previous studies have indicated that these indices are closely associated with the risk of cardiovascular disease in patients with T2DM [7]. Furthermore, the TyG index has been shown to significantly correlate with SLVD and can be used to identify SLVD in T2DM patients at an early stage [8]. However, the value of TG/HDL-C and METS-IR in the early identification of SLVD remains unclear, and the predictive efficacy of these three indices for SLVD in T2DM patients has not yet been compared. Therefore, the aim of this study is to explore the association of TG/HDL-C and METS-IR with SLVD in patients with T2DM and to compare the predictive value of these three indices for SLVD in this population.

Methods

Study population

From June 2024 to February 2025, patients with T2DM were hospitalized in the Department of Endocrinology at Hunan Provincial People's Hospital (The First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan Normal University) and were screened. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) meeting the diagnostic criteria for T2DM as outlined in the 2020 Chinese Diabetes Society guideline [9]; (3) (LVEF) ≥ 50%; (4) complete clinical data available. The exclusion criteria were: (1) history of mental or cognitive disorders; (2) moderate to severe cardiac valve stenosis or regurgitation; (3) a history of ischemic stroke or cerebral hemorrhage; (4) a history of malignant tumors; (5) concomitant coronary artery disease or a history of coronary artery disease or ECG suggestive of myocardial ischemia; (6) arrhythmia; (7) pregnancy or lactation; (8) poor-quality echocardiographic images precluding reliable measurements. A total of 221 patients were ultimately enrolled in this study. The cohort consisted of 131 men and 90 women, with a mean age of 56.83 ± 12.50 years. Based on the recommended cutoff value of a left ventricular global longitudinal strain (GLS) of less than 18% for evaluating SLVD, as suggested by the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging and some related studies [10–13], the patients were divided into two groups: a SLVD group (n = 125) and a non-SLVD group (n = 96).

This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent and study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Hunan Provincial People’s Hospital (No. [2024]−50).

Study methods

Basic clinical data were collected for all enrolled patients

Including: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, drinking history, hypertension and presence of diabetic complications (diabetic kidney disease, diabetic retinopathy, diabetic neuropathy, lower extremity arterial disease, diabetic foot, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state). Laboratory Parameters: hemoglobin(Hb), red blood cell count(RBC), white blood cell count(WBC), platelet count(PLT), neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, monocyte count, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin (ALB), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cr), uric acid (UA), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting plasma glucose (FPG).

Echocardiographic examination

The examination was performed using a Philips Healthcare EPIQ CVx ultrasound diagnostic system equipped with an S5-1 transducer. All conventional ultrasound examinations and GLS measurements were performed by the same senior physician specializing in cardiac ultrasound using the same ultrasound system with the examiner blinded to clinical information during both procedures. With the patient lying on their left side and breathing quietly, a limb lead electrocardiogram was connected. Satisfactory long-axis, apical two-chamber, three-chamber, and four-chamber views of the left ventricle were obtained. The following parameters were measured: LVEF, left ventricular shortening fraction (LVFS), early diastolic mitral inflow velocity (E peak), late diastolic mitral inflow velocity (A peak), early diastolic mitral annular velocity (E′), left atrial anterior–posterior diameter(LA), E/A ratio, E/E′ ratio, tricuspid regurgitation velocity and pressure gradient. Images of the apical two-chamber, three-chamber, and four-chamber views were acquired for at least three cardiac cycles at a frame rate of 60–90 frames per second. AutostrainLV software was then used to automatically analyze the date and obtain the time curves and bull’s-eye maps of 18-segment left ventricular two-dimensional strain. Manual adjustments were made to the images and any segments with untraceable speckles were excluded from subsequent analysis. GLS was measured three times and the average value was taken. To avoid ambiguity in numerical values due to positive or negative signs, GLS is presented as an absolute value.

Reproducibility analysis

Twenty subjects were randomly selected for the reproducibility analysis. The GLS was remeasured and recalculated using the same ultrasound system by two senior physicians from the cardiac specialty group. Two weeks later, one of the physicians repeated the measurements and calculations with the same equipment in order to assess both inter- and intra-observer reproducibility.

Calculation of the TyG Index, TG/HDL-C and METS-IR

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0. Univariate logistic regression was used to identify variables associated with SLVD in patients with T2DM. A multivariate logistic regression model was then constructed to analyse the correlation between SLVD and the TyG index, the TG/HDL-C ratio, and METS-IR in patients with T2DM. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to evaluate the ability of these three indices, both individually and in combination, to diagnose SLVD in T2DM patients. The areas under the curves (AUC) were compared using DeLong's test. Reproducibility was assessed using the Bland–Altman method and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of three non-insulin-based insulin resistance indices

To further investigate whether TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR are independent predictors of SLVD in patients with T2DM, multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed with adjustments for relevant confounding factors. To avoid multicollinearity, the variables involved in calculating the TyG index, TG/HDL-C and METS-IR were excluded and separate models containing each of these variables were constructed (Models 1, 2 and 3, respectively). The results showed that the TyG index, TG/HDL-C and METS-IR remained significantly and positively associated with SLVD after adjusting for confounders (P < 0.001), with odds ratios (95% confidence intervals) of 4.27 (2.62–6.95), 1.22(1.13–1.32), 1.28 (1.19–1.38), respectively. Comparative analysis of the diagnostic performance of three non-insulin-based insulin resistance indices

ROC analysis showed that the AUCs of the TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR in predicting SLVD in patients with T2DM were 0.785, 0.798, 0.819, respectively, while the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.849. Delong's test revealed no significant differences in predictive efficacy among the three indexes (P > 0.05), as shown in Fig. 1 and Table 3. However, the combined model demonstrated significantly higher predictive power than the TyG index or TG/HDL-C alone (P < 0.05), but no significant difference compared with METS-IR alone (Supplement Table 1).

Reproducibility analysis

Analysis of GLS revealed high reproducibility and good agreement. The 95% LoA for the inter- and intra-group were (−0.477 ~ 0.407) and (−0.366 ~ 0.276), respectively, supported by ICCs of 0.974 (95%CI, 0.937 ~ 0.990), 0.984 (95%CI, 0.963 ~ 0.994) (all P < 0.05; Supplement Figs. 1 and 2), indicating excellent measurement reliability.

Discussion

This study first utilized univariate logistic regression to preliminarily screen factors related to SLVD in T2DM patients. After adjusting for confounders, multivariate analysis showed TyG index, TG/HDL-C and METS-IR remained significantly positively associated with SLVD. ROC analysis demonstrated good predictive value for all three indicators, though Delong's test revealed no significant differences in their predictive performance.

Diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a serious complication of T2DM with a complex pathogenesis potentially involving metabolic disorders of myocardial cells, IR, oxidative stress and inflammation [14]. DCM often progresses to HF, with SLVD representing a critical early stage. Therefore, the early detection of SLVD is crucial for prevention and management of DCM. This study indicates that smoking, hypertension, BMI, TG, HbA1c, FPG, and Cr are positively correlated with SLVD in patients with T2DM. Previous research has demonstrated that smoking, hypertension, BMI, and TG are high-risk factors for the development and progression of cardiovascular diseases [15–17]. The harmful components in tobacco smoke can promote the release of inflammatory mediators and activate the sympathetic nervous system. This leads to hemodynamic stress, including an increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure and coronary artery constriction. These factors collectively exacerbate heart muscle injury [18, 19]. There is extensive evidence from prospective cohort studies, clinical trials, and meta-analyses confirming that hypertension is a significant causative factor in cardiovascular disease [20, 21]. Reducing blood pressure is an effective way of lowering the risk of cardiovascular events and mortality[22]. For example, a large randomized clinical trial involving 344,716 participants demonstrated that reduction in systolic blood pressure by 5 mmHg can lower the risk of major cardiovascular events by approximately 10% [23]. Overweight and obesity (BMI ≥ 24 kg/m2) are recognized as important predictors of cardiovascular events in patients with T2DM [15–17]. Consistently, our study and previous research [8] found that patients with SLVD have a higher BMI and TG levels than those without SLVD. Persistent high blood glucose and lipid levels impair the body's antioxidant defenses, accelerating atherosclerosis and cardiac dysfunction. In diabetes, an increased reliance on free fatty acids for energy in heart cells can disrupt mitochondrial function, This leads to an increased production of reactive oxygen species and calcium overload within cells. Ultimately, these processes impair both the contraction and relaxation functions of the heart [24]. Supporting our findings, Chen et al. [25] also reported a positive correlation between SLVD and factors TG, HbA1c and FPG in T2DM patients, and highlighted the prognostic value of HbA1c for tracking the progression of myocardial injury. Elevated levels of Cr often indicate impaired kidney function. This condition promotes SLVD progression through mechanisms such as fluid retention and the activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system. The increases stress on the heart, encourages myocardial fibrosis and oxidative stress, and ultimately impairs cardiac function [26]. Our study also found that female and HDL-C were negatively associated with SLVD. Although insulin resistance shows a stronger correlation with cardiovascular events than in men [27], estrogen is widely recognized for its cardioprotective effects. The significant increase in cardiovascular risk following the menopause highlights this role [28, 29]. Estrogen helps to lower LDL-C and raise HDL-C levels [30, 31]. HDL-C itself is a known protective factor for the heart, its functions include reverse cholesterol transport, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant activities, and it is inversely associated with cardiovascular disease risk [32].

The TyG index, TG/HDL-C and METS-IR are all calculated using routine laboratory parameters. Compared to traditional IR indices, while rely on insulin measurements, these indices offer the advantages of simplicity, convenience and freedom from interference by exogenous insulin. They are particularly well-suited to primary healthcare institutions, such as community hospitals, and have significant potential for widespread clinical adoption. Consequently, they are extensively used in the evaluation of conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, stroke, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease[33, 34]. Multiple studies have confirmed that all three are closely associated with cardiovascular diseases[35, 36]. Previous research has demonstrated that the TyG index exhibits a stable positive correlation with SLVD in diverse populations, including those with obesity, coronary artery disease and T2DM. This enables the early detection of myocardial injury and prediction of SLVD occurrence in T2DM patients[8, 10, 37, 38]. This study further confirms that, alongside the TyG index, both HDL-C and METS-IR also show significant positive correlations with SLVD in patients with T2DM.Elevated plasma TG levels and decreased HDL-C levels are associated with the development of cardiovascular diseases, and their ratio, TG/HDL-C, is considered a novel indicated for predicting cardiovascular diseases. Elisa et al. [36] indicated a strong association between higher TG/HDL-C levels and a greater risk of cardiovascular events. Furthermore, Cassano et al. [39] showed that increased TG/HDL-C is associated with a significant reduction in myocardial strain, as measured by GLS. Patients in the Q3 and Q4 quartiles displayed lower GLS values, revealing early cardiac dysfunction and suggesting that TG/HDL-C can indicate subclinical myocardial injury. Additionally, a longitudinal study of 40,335 UK participants established that the relationship between TG/HDL-C and cardiovascular disease is largely driven by increased prevalence rates of dyslipidemia, T2DM and hypertension [40]. Subclinical myocardial injury, as reflected by the TG/HDL-C level, may be closely related to abnormal lipid metabolism. In diabetic patients, dyslipidemia is characterized primarily by elevated plasma TG, decreased HDL-C, elevated LDL-C and postprandial hyperlipidemia. These metabolic disturbances can directly impair myocardial function by altering cardiac metabolic capacity, with the subclinical injury being an early manifestation [41]. In recent years, numerous studies have explored the association between METS-IR and prediabetes, T2DM, hypertension, and myocardial infarction. Some research indicates that patients in the high-METS-IR group have a higher incidence of post-discharge myocardial infarction and adverse cardiovascular events. Moreover, METS-IR demonstrates superior predictive value for myocardial infarction and adverse cardiovascular events compared to single indicators such as FPG, TG, BMI, and HDL-C [42], and this suggests that as a composite indicator of multiple metabolic parameters, METS-IR can more comprehensively reflect metabolic risk in diabetic patients, holding significant clinical assessment value.

This study further plotted ROC curves for the TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR for predicting SLVD. All three indices demonstrated good predictive efficacy, with areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.785, 0.798, and 0.819, respectively. However, pairwise comparisons using the DeLong test revealed no statistically significant differences in their diagnostic performance(p > 0.05). In the pathogenesis of DCM, myocardial metabolic dysregulation is a central mechanism, primarily characterized by an imbalance in glucose and lipid metabolism homeostasis. The abnormal accumulation of glucose metabolites and lipids within cardiomyocytes can trigger sustained inflammatory cascades, oxidative stress, and insulin resistance, directly impairing myocardial energy metabolism efficiency and contractile function, ultimately leading to cardiac structural remodeling and dysfunction[43, 44]. All three insulin resistance indices related to both glucose and lipid metabolism, reflecting similar pathophysiological processes in myocardial metabolic disturbance. Additionally, impaired insulin regulation in patients with T2DM compromises the ability of cardiomyocytes to uptake and oxidize glucose [45]. Consequently, the energy metabolism shifts toward a predominant reliance on fatty acid oxidation. Under conditions of diabetes and insulin resistance, the heart is chronically exposed to a pathological environment characterized by excess fatty acids and carbohydrates. This leads to the excessive accumulation of fatty acids and their derivatives within cardiomyocytes, resulting in alterations in cell membrane fluidity, dysfunction of ion channels and receptors, and disturbances in membrane lipid composition. These changes ultimately trigger cellular and organellar dysfunction, initiating cardiac lipotoxicity and contributing to pathological structural and functional damage to the heart [46, 47]. Furthermore, this study found that the combined predictive power of the other three indicators for SLVD development in patients with T2DM was superior to that of the TyG index and TG/HDL-C, but there was no significant difference compared to METS-IR. In terms of the calculation formula, METS-IR incorporates triple metabolic information—obesity (BMI), glucose metabolism (FPG) and lipid metabolism (TG, HDL-C)—making it more informative than the TyG index and TG/HDL-C. Moreover, previous studies have indicated that METS-IR is more stronger association with the incidence and mortality of cardiovascular diseases than the TyG index and TG/HDL-C, and its diagnostic performance is significantly better [48]. Considering the limitations of this study, such as the relatively small sample size, whether METS-IR holds superior predictive value remains to be validated in larger-scale studies.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is a single-center study with a small sample size and inevitable selection bias. Secondly, as it is cross-sectional, it lacks longitudinal data and trends over time. Thirdly, this study did not control for the potential confounding effect of various glucose-lowering medications on the myocardium, which may bias the results. Finally, the observational nature of the study design indicated an association, not necessarily a causal relationship; hence, our findings should be interpreted cautiously.

In summary, the TyG index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR exhibited significant positive correlations with the SLVD in patients with T2DM and demonstrated good predictive value for SLVD in T2DM patients. They are worthy of clinical promotion, particularly in primary care hospitals. However, no significant difference in predictive value was observed among the three indicators.

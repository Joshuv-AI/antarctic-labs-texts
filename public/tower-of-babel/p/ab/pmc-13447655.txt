Quality and pratical determinants in [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparation: hygrometry, syringe sorption & maximal activity per kit
Authors: Quentin Buon, Hugo Tep, Eli Bassi, Alessio Imperiale, Anne Dory, Bénédicte Gourieux, Alice Ressault
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTetrofosmin, labelled with technetium-99m ([99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin), is a widely used radiopharmaceutical for myocardial perfusion imaging. Despite its routine use, several practical aspects of its preparation remain insufficiently characterized. This study addressed three operational issues: (i) the potential impact of environmental hygrometry on radiochemical purity assessment using ITLC-SA, as current guidelines provide no recommendations for humidity during quality control; (ii) the extent of sorption in medical syringes, comparing two-part and lubricated three-part syringes due to the lipophilic nature of tetrofosmin; and (iii) the radiochemical conformity of preparations performed with activities exceeding the 12-GBq limit stated in the manufacturer’s instructions.ResultsHygrometry > 60% brings 99.7% nonconformities with ITLC-SA test strip of the [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin quality control. Measures show significant superiority of 3P syringe mean sorptions over 2P syringes, with successive rinses and two different infusion time points. However, all measured sorption was under 10%, considered as clinically acceptable. Median activity was 17.31 GBq over a 1-year period, while the product instructions allow for a maximum 12 GBq per kit. All quality controls were conforms, demonstrating possibility to overcharge kits and improve maximal activity.ConclusionHygrometry is clearly a major parameter and should be not only monitored but controlled like pressure or temperature. There is a significant superiority about [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin sorption in 3P syringes compared to 2P syringes. However, less than 10% sorption difference cannot be considered as clinically significant. 3P syringes are probably not as problematic as it seems for [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparations. Background

Technetium-99m tetrofosmin ([99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin, SmPC MYOVIEW®) is one of the most frequently used radiopharmaceuticals for myocardial perfusion imaging and is routinely prepared in radiopharmacies under standardized conditions, followed by mandatory radiochemical purity testing before release. Despite decades of clinical use, several operational aspects of its preparation remain insufficiently characterized, potentially affecting both workflow efficiency and the reliability of routine quality control.

Environmental hygrometry is a critical parameter in radiopharmaceutical preparation according to Good Preparation Practices (ANSM, 2023), which recommends maintaining humidity between 30 and 60%. However, no guidance is provided regarding hygrometry during radiochemical purity analysis, and no data exist on the potential interaction between high humidity and the Instant Thin-Layer Chromatography Silicic Acid (ITLC-SA) method used for [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin quality control. Because humidity can modify solvent migration and the behaviour of the stationary phase, we hypothesized that elevated hygrometry may impact the accuracy of radiochemical purity measurements.

A second practical issue concerns syringe sorption. Tetrofosmin is a lipophilic phosphine ligand (Tetrofosmin, VIDAL 2013), and lipophilic radiopharmaceuticals are known to undergo container–content interactions, particularly in three-part syringes (Dupire et al. 2023; Gunasekera, R. et al. 2001, Ladriere et al. 2023; Kvaternik et al. 2020). Such interactions, combining adsorption and absorption phenomena, may reduce the effective activity delivered to the patient (Helmenstine 2021). Although additional rinsing steps are frequently performed in clinical practice to recover residual activity, the effectiveness of these procedures for [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin has never been formally assessed.

Finally, the current SmPC limits activity concentration to 1.5 GBq/mL, corresponding to a maximum of 12 GBq per kit. This limitation is based on activity concentration rather than on the mass ratio of technetium to ligand. Considering that generator eluates contain only microgram amounts of total technetium—far below the 230 µg of tetrofosmin per kit—it is plausible that higher activities could be added without compromising radiochemical purity. Optimizing the maximal activity per kit could reduce the number of preparations required per day and improve radiopharmacy workflow for high-throughput myocardial perfusion programs.

The aim of this study was therefore to investigate three underreported aspects of [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparation—hygrometry effects on QC, sorption in medical syringes, and the feasibility of increasing maximal activity per kit—in order to address current gaps in the literature and support evidence-based optimization of routine radiopharmacy practices.

Material & methods

This work has been carried out by using tetrofosmin from GE Healthcare (MYOVIEW® 3400956444984) associated with sodium pertechnetate (Na+ [99mTc]TcO4−) from CIS Bio International (TEKCIS® [99Mo]Mo/[99mTc]Tc generator 3400957817527) and GE Healthcare (ULTRATECHNEKOW® [99Mo]Mo/[99mTc]Tc generator 3400891231588). All the [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparations has been performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, except for maximal activity per kit.

Hygrometry & quality control

Quality controls were performed according to the manufacturer instructions (ITLC-SA 2 × 20 cm chromatography paper, acetone/dichloromethane 65:35 v/v). The radio-TLC system used for quality controls is the miniGITA Dual® radiochromatography engine from Elysia Raytest company.

Test strips for tetrofosmin were locked in an airtight box with wet paper and a hygrometer. This hygrometer was qualified and showed the same hygrometry than the lab probe. The purpose was to articially recreate high room hygrometry (≥ 60%). As reference for normal hygrometry, we took the paper test strips for tetrofosmin which had been locked in an airtight box with dehydrating capsules that we daily use for quality control. Hygrometry in the airtight box was held superior to 60% during at least 24 h before using the paper test strips. Thirty samples from separate [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparation were each tested with a paper test strip from each box.

Syringe sorption

Fig. 1Process used for the determination of syringes sorption

Process used for the determination of syringes sorption

Sorption measurements were performed using 2P (unlubricated two-part) and 3P (lubricated three-part) syringes according to the following process in Fig. 1:

Thirty syringes were used for each type of condition (syringe type and resting time). The 2 timings were tested with the 2 types of syringe, means 4 series of 30 units at total.

For each measure, the syringe was filled up with [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin and dilute with NaCl 0.9% until 2mL. After the according time (1 min and 10 min), activity was measured (At). Then, the syringe was emptied and measured (R0). After, syringe was rinsed with 2mL of NaCl 0.9% and the remaining activity was measured again. We repeated this operation 3 times (R1, R2 and R3).

We considered a homogeneous volumic activity for each radiopharmaceutical solution.

The radioactivity in syringes was measured using a Capintec® CRC-55TR dose calibrator. All syringes were manipulated inside a LemerPax® Easypharma Compact shielded hot cell, at room temperature and protected from sunlight.

For data analysis, activities used as 100% radioactivity in syringe are activities after selected times (1 and 10 min) noted At. We considered R0 as activity after emptying without rinse, R1 as activity after first rinse, R2 as activity after second rinse and R3 as activity after third rinse. Those activities are used for calculation of percentage of the residual radioactivity in syringe. The equation used for the determination of sorption (S) is:

Less than 10% sorption was considered as none. As applied in practice, a ± 10% gap for syringe preparation in nuclear medicine is considered clinically acceptable. We used the same hypothesis here.

For statistical analysis, we used two-tailed unpaired t-test (on GraphPad Prism 10.6.1) for difference mean comparison between independent groups of continuous variables (initial activity). We used one-tailed unpaired t-test (on GraphPad Prism 10.6.1) for superiority mean comparison between independent groups of continuous variables (mean sorption). We compared mean sorption of 2P and 3P syringes at 1 min and mean sorption of 2P and 3P syringes at 10 min, calculated by the measures R0, R1, R2 and R3 described above. P-values < 0.05 (α = 95%) means significant difference between mean initial activities for two-tailed t-test. P-values < 0.025 (α = 95%) means significant superiority of the highest mean compared to the other for one-tailed t-test.

Maximal activity per kit

Data about activities used for tetrofosmin preparations and the radiochemical purity of each preparations, were extracted from our nuclear medicine’s software VENUS®. Data were collected retrospectively for a 1-year period [2024-01-02–2024-12-31]. In this results, we compared radiochemical purity means of a kit with recommended RCP activity and a kit with an activity per kit above 18 GBq, thirty samples for each group. P-values < 0.05 (α = 95%) means significant difference between the percentage radiochemical purity results for two-tailed t-test. We excluded the nonconformity control only due to high hygrometry during summer 2024.

Discussion

The focus on three important aspects of the [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparation shows some interesting results that improve its daily use.

For the hygrometry part, practical results show how important it is to keep our TLC chromatography materials dry. A TLC paper strip leaded to two surprising results. The first one with no migration at all. That can be explained by the interaction with the water and the acid silica which leads to no migration at all (Fig. 3a). The second result is more surprising with a total migration on the ITLC strip. The [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin have the same migration of the free 99mTc (Fig. 3b). The results are quite surprising and can lead to the reject of the release of the preparation if no alternative control is in place. To our knowledge, we are the first study to describe the impact of hygrometry on quality control for this preparation. In the preparation good practices (ANSM, 2023), hygrometry has to be only watched but no enforceable rule defined standard percentage. No standard is also defined in tetrofosmin’s manufacturer instructions (SmPC MYOVIEW®). Since this study, a dehumidifier has been installed to reduce as much as possible hygrometry in quality control lab. A futher analytical and radiochemical study could be interesting to understand subtleties between water, tetrofosmin, solvents and ITLC-SA interactions to explain the two different nonconformity results.

Every sorption we measured is significantly under 10% compared to the total activity measured (Table 2). As told in the method part, a more or less 10% gap compared to the total activity prescription is considered clinically acceptable. So, in practice, we can considered that difference does not have an impact on our myocardic SPECT/CT imaging. Container-content interactions, especially with medical 3P syringes, is already a well-studied subject in literature for many radiopharmaceuticals3–6. Especially [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin that got heterogeneous results concerning its sorption. Precautionary principle in nuclear medicine services moves towards not recommending 3P syringes for [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparation. Since we have results conform to our practice for both syringes, we can’t conclude whether 2P or 3P tested are best. We just acknowledge that our results with 3 syringes were similar to a previous study in our establishment. We previously compared the residual activity between a dilution at 1 mL and 2 mL of the [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin in NaCl 0.9% in the same 3P syringes (Ressault et al. 2022). The residual was decreased in the 2mL dilution and was under 10% compared to the total activity prepared. The sorption difference between S0 and S1 for all groups (Table 3; Fig. 4) means one syringe rinse is necessary to significantly get back radioactivity during administration. Successive rinses seems to have no interest after the first rinse, according with recent literature (Nakamura et al. 2023) and with our results demonstrated by the graphic. Residual activities at the end could be explained by dead space of syringes as well. Time-related adsorption phenomenon seems to have no impact on mean sorption for 3P syringes, at least until 10 min infusion time point. We chose 1 and 10 min infusion time because it is the mean time range between tetrofosmin syringe preparation and patient administration in our nuclear medicine service. Longer infusion times could show a significant impact of the time-related adsorption phenomenon in radiopharmaceutical sorption inside medical 3P syringes (Helmenstine 2021).

For the last point that can help the daily use, we observe no significant change in the quality of the [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin preparations, neither analytically or in the SPECT imaging. Despite the product’s manufacturer, allowing an augmentation of [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin maximum activity per kit permitted to reduce the number of radiolabelling by one. In a radiochemical way, we can explain that tetrofosmin ligand and tin (II) chloride reducer are largely over present compared to the introduced quantity of pertechnetate technetium ([99mTc]TcO4−). Economic saving estimation is not detailed in this study but an entire medico economic study is in progress, including organisation and time gain over paramedical professionals. Concerning the worker radioprotection, the impact of this augmentation seems negligible since none augmentation dosimetry exposition has been relevant in our service.

Conclusion

The three investigations described allow to improve our daily preparation of [99mTc]Tc-tetrofosmin. First, high hygrometry results challenge us to modify our practices to prevent analytical control nonconformities. Hygrometry is clearly a major parameter in the control and should be more than just watched but controlled like pressure or temperature. Second, even if we will not change our practices about medical syringes, we quantitatively measured significant superiority about tetrofosmin sorption in 3P syringes compared to 2P syringes. However, less than 10% sorption difference cannot be considered as clinically significant. 3P syringes are probably not as problematic as it seems for tetrofosmin preparations. And last, internal retrospective analysis confirms that an increase of the maximal activity per tetrofosmin kit and conserve safety of use for professionals and patients. Entire work shows that tetrofosmin is a well-known molecule but its preparation and use can still be optimized.

Prediction of lymphovascular invasion in invasive breast cancer based on clinical-MRI radiomics features
Authors: Chunling Zhang, Peng Zhou, Ruobing Li, Zhongyuan Li, Aimei Ouyang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveWe aim to develop a predictive model for lymphovascular invasion (LVI) in patients with invasive breast cancer (IBC), using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based radiomics features.MethodsA total of 204 patients with IBC admitted to our hospital were included in this retrospective study. The data was split into training and validation sets at a 7:3 ratio. Feature normalization was conducted, followed by feature selection using ANOVA, correlation analysis, and LASSO in the training set. The final step involved building a logistic regression model. The LVI prediction models were established by single sequence image and combined different sequence images as follows: A: prediction model based on the optimal sequence in the 7-phase enhanced MRI scans; B: prediction model based on the optimal sequences in the sequences T1WI, T2WI, and DWI; and C: the combined model based on the optimal sequences selected from A and B. Subjects' work characteristic curves (ROC) and decision curves (DCA) were plotted to determine the extent to which they predicted LVI performance in the training and validation sets. Simultaneously, nomogram models were constructed by integrating radiomics features and independent risk factors. In addition, an additional 16 patients from the center between January and August 2024 were collected as the Nomogram external validation set. The ROC and DCA were used to evaluate the performance of the model.ResultsIn the enhanced images, Model A built based on the enhanced 2-phase achieved the best average AUC, with a validation set of 0.764. Model B built based on the T2WI had better results, with a validation set of 0.693. Model C built by combining enhanced 2-phase and T2WI sequences had a mean AUC of 0.705 in the validation set. In addition, the tumor size, whether the tumor boundary was clear or not, and whether there was a coelom in the tumor tissue had a statistically significant effect on the LVI of IBC, and a clinical-radiomics nomogram was established. DCAs as well as Nomogram also indicate that Model A has good clinical utility. The AUC of the nomogram in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set were 0.703, 0.615, and 0.609, respectively. The DCA also showed that the radiomics nomogram combined with clinical factors had good predictive ability for LVI.ConclusionIn IBC, MRI radiomics can serve as a noninvasive predictor of LVI. The clinical-MRI radiomics model, as an efficient visual prognostic tool, shows promise in forecasting LVI. This highlights the significant potential of pre-radiomics prediction in enhancing treatment strategies.Keywords: IBC, LVI, MRI, Radiomics, Machine learning

Introduction

Breast cancer, being one of the most prevalent types of malignant tumors, remains the primary reason for cancer-related fatalities among females aged 20 to 59 years [1]. Invasive ductal breast cancer (IDBC), which is responsible for approximately 80% of breast cancer cases, is the predominant histologic subtype of this disease [2, 3]. LVI, also known as lymphovascular invasion, occurs when tumor cells invade either blood vessels or lymphatic vessels [4], and it is an important prognostic factor in invasive breast cancer (IBC) [5, 6], closely associated with positive margins for breast-conserving surgery [7], resistance to neoadjuvant chemotherapy [8] and axillary non-sentinel lymph node metastasis [8], which is an independent prognostic factor for local recurrence (LR) or distant metastasis (DM) of breast cancer [9], and preoperative assessment of LVI status of breast cancer can provide important information for precise treatment. At the same time, the accuracy and utility of tissue and blood-derived tumor markers require clinical validation in large samples due to intra-lesion heterogeneity, high time and testing costs, as well as invasive biopsies and surgical resections, the low negative predictive value of specimens, and pathologic analyses that are not universally applicable. In short, identification of their biomarkers is a challenge [10].

Radiomics enables the extraction of subtle imaging features and quantification to investigate potential relationships between characteristics and tumor pathophysiology [11]. Machine learning (ML) algorithms were used in the radiomics classification task. ML belongs to artificial intelligence, which combines multi-field knowledge and conducts sufficient analysis and processing of data through specific mathematical models and related algorithms [12]. At present, the research on breast cancer-related fields using ML algorithms has developed rapidly [13, 14].

The advantages of high tissue resolution, multiplanar imaging make MRI the most sensitive examination, especially for the detection of occult and multifocal cancers, benefiting high-risk patients and those with dense breasts. MRI is widely used in the examination of breast cancer. In recent research, numerous studies have identified a connection between radiomics characteristics derived from enhancement-Magnetic Resonance Imaging (DCE-MRI) and tumor heterogeneity, as well as biological characteristics [15, 16]. Interactions between intra-tumor cells and peri-tumor elements influence tumor development and progression [17]. For example, the surrounding edema and tumor angiogenesis are associated with the malignant behavior of the tumor [18, 19]. Several researchers have investigated the peri-tumor region utilizing different imaging modalities including mammography, ultrasound, and MRI, yielding preliminary findings in tumor diagnosis, biological indicator prediction, and prognostic evaluation [20]. Among them, DCE-MRI can not only observe the morphological characteristics and inner structure of breast cancer lesions but also analyze the hemodynamic changes of the lesions [21]. DCE-MRI is the most commonly used method for assessing vascular, Niukkanen [22] et al. demonstrated the association of semiquantitative parameters related to TIC in the peritumoral region with poor prognosis in breast cancer using 3D segmentation. Rhea D. Chitalia [23] et al. retrospectively analyzed the results of breast DCE-MRI scans from a National Institutes of Health multimodal imaging trial and proposed that radiomics phenotyping could provide a noninvasive characterization of tumor heterogeneity to enhance individualized prognosis and treatment. An examination of past breast DCE-MRI images in a research study conducted by the National Institutes of Health indicates that radiomics profiling may offer a non-invasive way to describe the diversity of tumors, improving personalized prognosis and treatment options. Therefore, we used the MRI scanning technique to study breast cancer. Previous studies on DCE-MRI sequences usually extracted imaging features from a single enhancement phase, and most of them routinely used the early [24–27] or peak phase of tumor enhancement [28] for analysis, but without the support and proof of experimental studies, we are not clear about which period is more accurate for the assessment of breast cancer, and we do not know whether delayed-period imaging information will have a complementary role. Therefore, we designed to compare breast cancer LVI using DCE-MRI radiomics features of different phases separately in an attempt to determine the predictive performance of different enhancement phase histologic features and to provide data support for future related studies. At the same time, we established a joint model to determine the best prediction model for IBC LVI infiltration level.

Therefore, to evaluate the status of preoperative vascular tumor thrombus in breast invasive ductal carcinoma, this study undertook a comprehensive analysis of clinical data from patients with this condition. Specifically, it observed the predictive value of MRI-related imaging features for the presence of vascular tumor thrombus. Furthermore, the study aimed to select the optimal DEC-MRI time phase and sequence, based on the highest AUC as the best predictive model. Concurrently, a combined clinical-MRI prediction model for vascular tumor thrombus in breast invasive ductal carcinoma was constructed.

Our radiomics-based strategy adheres to the subsequent process: (1) obtaining images; (2) refining automatic image segmentation through an iterative labeling procedure; (3) extracting and choosing features; and (4) constructing, examining, and categorizing radiomics models (Fig. 1).

Study set

This was a retrospective study that included 204 patients (all female patients; mean age 55.623 years; age range 25–85 years). A total of 204 patients with single IBC were diagnosed by postoperative pathology in the Central Hospital affiliated with Shandong First Medical University from April 2020 to June 2023, all of them were female, among which 81 cases of LVI( +) and 123 cases of LVI(-) were included; They were divided into a training set of 142 cases [56 LVI ( +), 86 LVI (-)] and a validation set of 62 cases [25 LVI ( +), 37 LVI (-)] according to a 7:3 ratio. Inclusion criteria: For female patients diagnosed with IDBC by pathological examination, the pathological status of the lymphatic vascular system around IDBC was detected to determine whether there was vascular tumor thrombus; Be over 18 years old; Patients had detailed clinical and pathological data; The patient underwent MRI within a week before surgery. Exclusion criteria: Patients who are pregnant or lactating; Concomitant mental illness; Combined with other primary malignant tumors; Patients with inflammatory diseases, infections, autoimmune diseases, or other diseases affecting blood components (such as hematological diseases, liver dysfunction, chronic kidney disease, etc.); Adjuvant therapy such as radiotherapy and chemotherapy was performed before the examination; Patients with poor MRI image quality. The patients all signed an informed consent form before the examination. The study was authorized by the Ethics Committee of Central Hospital affiliated with Shandong First Medical University, whose Ethics Committee dispensed with the requirement of using institutionalized informant agreement. In addition, 16 additional patients from the center between January 2024 and August 2024 (all female; Mean age 54.88 years, range 41–74 years) were used as the independent external validation set.

RadCloud from Huiyihuiying Medical Technology Co., Ltd. (https://mics.huiyihuiying.com/) was employed to manage both the imaging data and clinical data, as well as conduct subsequent radiomics statistical analysis. To ensure proper validation and training, a randomized approach was utilized with a 3:7 ratio for the separation of the validation set and training set, along with a random seed count of 325 (Table 1).

Clinical information

The data of all patients included in this study came from the integrated electronic medical record system of Central Hospital affiliated with Shandong First Medical University, and we evaluated clinical data including age, tumor size, ADC value, type of the patient's mammary gland, whether the boundary was clear or not, whether there was a burr or not, whether there is a coelom in the tumor tissue, whether there was lymph node metastasis or not, whether the background parenchymal enhancement or not, and whether there was any involvement of the skin and nipple or not. These clinical data were utilized to create a clinical model for comparison with the radiomics model.

Research methodology

MRI scanning methods

Breast DCE-MR images of all patients were scanned using a 3.0T scanner (SIGNATM Pioneer, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA) in the prone position using a specialized 8-channel breast coil. After routine T1WI, T2WI, and DWI sequences, DCE-MRI data were acquired using DISCO (Differential Subsampling with Cartesian Ordering) combined with fat suppression, and after acquisition of pre-enhancement fat-suppressed axial T1WI images, followed by intravenous regimentation of 0.1 mmol/ kg dose of gadolinium contrast agent (gadolinium borate amide, GE Healthcare), followed by a washout with 20 mL of saline, both at an injection rate of 2 mL/s. A total of 7 post-enhancement images (19.4 s/period) were then acquired continuously without interruption. The DCE sequence scanning parameters for this study were: Repetition Time (TR) = 4.9 ms, Echo Time (TE) = 1.7 ms, Flip Angle = 90°, Field of View = 360 × 360 mm, Acquisition Matrix = 256 × 256, Layer Thickness = 1.4 mm, Number of Layers = 120/period, and Number of Excitations = 2. All period images were selected for this study. study all the phase images were selected for analysis. All images were uploaded in DICOM format and downloaded from the PACS system.

Pathological assessment

Immunohistochemical staining with HE, CD34 (vascular invasion), and D2-40 (lymphatic invasion) were performed to assess whether there was peritumoral vascular and lymphatic invasion; the presence of either of these was determined to be LVI ( +) [29].

Tumor segmentation

Images were uploaded to the Radcloud radiomics platform, where the complete lesion was meticulously outlined by two radiologists with extensive experience. Each layer of the tumor was carefully drawn in all dimensions and then reviewed by a senior radiologist with over 5 years of experience. In cases where there was a discrepancy of 5% or more, a senior radiologist intervened to establish the tumor boundaries. To ensure the accuracy of the segmentation, 30 patients were randomly selected for the process to be repeated by two radiologists to assess inter- and intra-group correlation coefficients (ICCs). A high ICC value (> 0.75) indicates good robustness and reproducibility in the segmentation process. Additionally, a consistency check was performed by physicians, showing good agreement between the two physicians in delineating the regions of interest (ROIs).

Feature extraction and selection

From MRI images through the Radcloud platform, 1688 quantitative imaging features were extracted. These features can be categorized into three clusters. Cluster 1 (statistics of the first order) includes 126 descriptors that measure the distribution of voxel intensity in MRI images using standard basic metrics. Cluster 2 (features based on shape and size) comprises 14 3D characteristics that represent the shape and size of the area. Cluster 3 (features based on texture) consists of 525 features that measure variations in regional heterogeneity calculated from gray run lengths and gray co-occurrence texture matrices.

When calculating image characteristics for a specific task, it is important to focus on relevant features for optimal performance. This can be achieved through techniques such as variance threshold, SelectKBest, and LASSO. The variance threshold approach sets a threshold value of 0.8, removing feature values with variances below this cutoff point. SelectKBest uses P-values to evaluate the correlation between features and classification outcomes, selecting only features with significant P-values (< 0.05). The LASSO model applies L1 regularization as the cost function, with a cross-validation error value of 5 and a maximum of 1000 iterations.

ML Model construction

After conducting feature screening, 1688 features were identified as being extremely relevant to the topic at hand. By utilizing these carefully selected features, we calculated individual radiomics scores (Rad-scores) for each patient through a mathematical equation. In this formula, the Rad-score was calculated as Rad-score = α+n∑i=1nβiXi, and α represents the intercept, βi denotes the value of the radiomics feature; Xi represents the corresponding coefficient.

In this study, we chose a variety of supervised learning classifiers suitable for classification analysis. These classifiers generate models that aim to distinguish or forecast data associated with results or phenotypes (for example, patient responses or outcomes). We selected the most common five classifiers in radiomics, which were SVM, XGBoost, GEM, RF, and ADABoost (Table 2). Development and performance of radiomics nomograms

Univariate analyses of factors were used to assess the relationship between clinicopathological (clinicopathological and radiological) characteristics and LVI status, and Logistic regression analyses were performed to build clinical radiological models considering significant risk factors. Similarly, univariate logistic regression analyses were then used to examine the radiomics characteristics and the clinicopathological characteristics described above to construct the radiomics nomogram used to predict LVI in the validation set. Finally, to verify the generality of the prediction model, external validation was used to verify the prediction performance of the model.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was conducted on the Radcloud platform. Following feature identification, a total of 1688 characteristics were recognized to have a significant association with LVI. Various supervised learning classifiers are accessible for categorical examination based on the selected features, which formulate models to segregate or forecast data linked to an output or phenotype (e.g., patient response or outcome). In this investigation, models based on radiomics were formulated utilizing these five classifiers, and validation techniques were implemented to enhance model credibility. The ROC curve, specifically the area under the curve (AUC), was employed in both the training and validation datasets to assess prediction effectiveness. Four criteria—Accuracy (P = True Positive/(True Positive + False Positive)), Retrieval Rate (R = True Positive/(True Positive + False Negative)), f1-score (f1-score = P*R*2/ (P + R)), and support (total test set number)—were utilized to appraise classifier performance in this research. The DeLong test was utilized to compare AUC values between the models.

To statistically analyze the aforementioned clinical data, SPSS software (Version 27) was utilized, with statistical significance considered at P < 0.1. The analysis process included the application of chi-square tests to determine the association between categorical variables and logistic regression analyses to evaluate the predictive value of these variables. All statistical evaluations were performed using one or two-sided tests to assess the directional hypotheses effectively. The use of these statistical methods allowed for a comprehensive examination of the clinical data, ensuring that the results were both thorough and reliable.

Development and performance of radiomics nomograms

In analyses of logistic regression, it was found that radiomics characteristics along with tumor size, clarity of tumor boundary, and presence of coelom in tumor tissue were determined to be autonomous risk factors in the anticipation of LVI status. A radiomics nomogram incorporating the autonomous predictors was also developed (Fig. 5). The AUC of nomogram was 0.703 in the training set, 0.615 in the internal validation set, and 0.609 in the external validation set. DCA in the training set, internal and external validation sets showed that the nomogram had good clinical application value (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Assessing the LVI status preoperatively in breast cancer guides treatment decisions. The current gold standard relies on postoperative pathology, but preoperative evaluation remains challenging. MRI and other imaging modalities are valuable in breast cancer diagnosis, aiding in tumor characterization, efficacy assessment, and prognosis prediction. This study analyzed 204 patients with infiltrating ductal carcinoma, exploring the predictive value of MRI features for LVI. It aimed to optimize the DEC-MRI predictive phases/sequences by constructing and comparing clinical-imaging models. Results showed that Model A, utilizing enhanced 7-phase images, performed best with an AUC of 0.764. Model B, focusing on T1WI, T2WI, and DWI, had an AUC of 0.693, while Model C scored 0.705. The enhanced 2-phase DEC-MRI sequences demonstrated advantages in predicting breast cancer LVI, indicating a potential for improved preoperative evaluation.

LVI, which refers to the tumor cell invasion into the lymphatic or vascular lumen near the tumor, is linked to recurrence, metastasis, and early lymph node metastasis in breast cancer [35]. Studies have shown LVI's independent prognostic significance. Rakha [36] et al. found that LVI-positive patients had a worse prognoses post-surgery, especially in the lymph node-negative subgroup where LVI was the sole significant factor. Freedman [37] et al. analysis in breast-conserving surgery patients showed that LVI correlated with local recurrence but was not an independent predictor. Colleoni [38] et al. categorized patients based on the degree of LVI post-surgery, revealing worse DFS/OS (disease-free survival/overall survival) with extensive LVI, while mild LVI differed little from none, suggesting a moderate reduction in adjuvant therapy may be possible. Noninvasive evaluation of LVI in the IBC tumor microenvironment is crucial. MRI radiomics assesses tumor characteristics such as temporal and spatial heterogeneity, providing comprehensive insights for clinical diagnosis, prognosis, and monitoring of the IBC tumor microenvironment.

Unlike the naked eye recognition used in traditional imaging methods, radiomics analysis can automatically screen image features to delve into tumor heterogeneity. Compared to deep learning, the ML algorithm we chose is more interpretable, making it easier for people to understand how the model makes its decisions. Furthermore, ML typically requires a smaller sample size, which makes it a simpler, more efficient, and lower resource-consuming approach. On the other hand, deep learning is more automated but demands a larger sample size. In the future, our research will aim to incorporate deep learning elements to further enhance the performance of our model.

The LVI status of IBC is affected by a variety of complex factors and mechanisms. Clinical features, histopathology, tumor periphery, molecular biology, and the surrounding microenvironment related to tumor biological processes all affect the LVI status. The diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models that incorporate clinical, morphological features, pathology, and histological labels has been significantly improved. Although in recent years, numerous researchers have conducted numerous studies on LVI in different types of breast cancer [39–42], current researchers tend to incorporate all enhancement sequences simultaneously, and there is no consensus on phase selection for DCE-MRI. For example, Wang Yi [40] et al. acquired 8 consecutive phases of DCE-MRI images and established a radiomics model to explore the value of MRI radiomics features in predicting breast cancer-related LVI. However, they did not establish a model for each of the 8 phases of enhancement images to optimize the best prediction of the phases. The present study fills in this gap, we compared the 7 phases of enhancement MRI images for multi-phase enhancement outlining the VOI. Notably, we did not avoid the necrotic and cystic areas of the tumor when outlining the region of interest to fully reflect the information about the tumor. We found that the best results were obtained in phase 2 enhanced MRI when the lesion was intensified obviously, and the gland was not intensified significantly.

Shanshan Wei [43] et al. demonstrated that the model of MRI signs combined with inflammatory markers (type of TIC, peritumor edema, burr sign, + preoperative peripheral blood neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio) helped predict the LVI of patients with mass-type early IBCs; Xinhua Li [44] et al. demonstrated that the age of the patients, tumor dimensions, presence of magnetic resonance spectroscopy CHO peaks, presence of pinpoint signs, and features such as peritumoral enhancement and peritumoral edema were all found to be statistically significant in determining the likelihood of LVI. This highlights the importance of considering a combination of clinical and imaging factors when assessing the risk of LVI in breast cancer cases. This is somewhat similar to our study, in which we also used MRI signs combined with clinical indicators to establish a clinical-radiologic model (Tumor size + boundary + coelom) to predict the LVI status of IBC, and achieved good results, providing data support for diagnosis and modeling of breast cancer patients. The nomogram constructed based on these factors and radiomics features also showed the highest recognition ability. The AUC in the training set and the internal validation set were 0.703 and 0.615, respectively. An external validation set was added in this study, and the AUC reached 0.609. Our external validation results also demonstrated the excellent performance of the nomogram in predicting LVI in patients with IBC.

With the continuous deepening of quantitative breast cancer research, breast cancer invasiveness or prognosis based on quantitative parameters of DCE-MRI imaging remains an important trend for future research, and multiparametric quantitative models need to be further developed and investigated. Currently, prospective studies of breast cancer by researchers include a variety of breast MR imaging protocols, such as T2WI, multimodal sequences of DWI combined with DCE-MRI, etc. [45]. Yihong Jiang [46] et al. combined these with clinical-radiologic features to build a model that showed excellent performance in distinguishing LVI states; Zhang [9] et al. developed an MP-MRI radiomics model for predicting LVI and prognosis in IDC breast cancer, finding multiparametric feature fusion to be optimal. In this comprehensive study, joint model C was devised leveraging multi-parameter sequences, yet it fell short of achieving optimal performance. Our in-depth analysis pinpointed 12 key features, predominantly sourced from model B (which had a notably lower AUC) and complemented by 3 from model A. This imbalance, where a majority of features originated from the less efficient model B, could potentially undermine the research's overall effectiveness. Moreover, the inherent differences in imaging sequences, coupled with variations in the region of interest and magnetic field strength, contributed to a convoluted feature extraction process. This resulted in a blend of features that hindered the precise identification of optimal ones, thereby challenging the robust establishment of model C. Additionally, T1WI, T2WI, and DWI using thick slice scans cannot adequately depict the complexity of the tumor. Consequently, the extracted features carried inherent constraints that significantly impacted the model's construction and overall performance.

There are certain limitations to this study that must be acknowledged. First, although an external validation set was included, this study was a single-center study with a small sample size, so the universality and power of the model may be poor and need to be further validated. Second, our study exclusively included patients with IBC and did not investigate other types of breast cancer. Third, owing to the retrospective nature of this study, selection bias cannot be fully eliminated, and future studies should employ more stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria. In the future, the research team plans to collect data from multiple centers, expand the sample size, enhance the efficiency and generalizability of the model, and evaluate the application of imaging histological features in predicting breast cancer prognosis through long-term patient follow-up.

Conclusion

In summary, the preoperative model based on the radiomics features of enhanced MRI predicts IBC LVI conditions, and unlike traditional visual image evaluation, the radiomics model can provide a tool to help assess LVI conditions, thereby better informing clinical prognosis and patient management. Different DCE-MRI phases and sequences have different efficacies in IBC LVI, which provide a reference for breast MRI scan sequence optimization. In future work, it is necessary to further evaluate our findings on large datasets.

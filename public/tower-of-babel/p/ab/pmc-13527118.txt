Riccati-based analytical framework for solving the potential Korteweg-de Vries equation
Authors: Yousef Jawarneh, Safyan Mukhtar, Safiqul Islam, Yaouba Amadou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper investigates the potential KdV equation by applying the Riccati-based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM). Through a suitable traveling wave transformation, the governing nonlinear partial differential equation is converted into an ordinary differential equation, which is then analyzed using an extended Riccati framework. As a result, several families of exact traveling wave solutions are derived in explicit analytical form. The obtained solutions include solitary wave, periodic, rational, hyperbolic, and trigonometric structures under different parameter conditions. The validity of the derived solutions is confirmed through direct substitution into the original model. In addition, the influence of the involved parameters on the wave behavior is discussed to illustrate the physical significance of the solutions. The present approach provides a systematic and effective technique for constructing exact solutions of nonlinear evolution equations arising in mathematical physics and related applied sciences.

Introduction

NLEEs are equations of evolution of which exact traveling wave solutions have received considerable interest in the past few years. In elucidating complex physical events and the dynamic processes of the nonlinear equations, these solutions which involve solitons, kinks, peakons and cuspons1 are significant solutions to such nonlinear equations2. Since soliton structures may transit or propagate across nonlinear media without changing their shape, they are of particular interest and may find use in a variety of physics-related domains. Coherent wave structures are created as a result of this equilibrium between linear dispersion and nonlinear influences3–5.

There are numerous potent methods of solving nonlinear evolution equations (NLEEs) that have been developed over the years, among them are the nonlinear Schrodinger equation, the Korteweg-de Vries equation, and the sine-Gordon equation. Some of these methods include subsidiary ordinary differential equation method (sub-ODE)6–8, homogeneous balance principle, using the F-expansion method9, Jacobi elliptic function method10, sinecosine method and tanh method11, Hirota bilinear method, and B cklund transformation method, among others. Even when the Painlev integrability test cannot be performed, these methods have been found to be effective in such cases advancing even to the failure of the test itself12. It is universally acknowledged that the Painlev property is a necessary and sufficient condition of integrability of a nonlinear system, in the sense of integrability of a nonlinear dynamical system13, known as the Painlev property is integrability14–16.

In recent years, there has been growing interest in developing analytical and semi-analytical techniques for nonlinear evolution equations, especially those involving nonlinear and fractional-order models. In particular, transform-based approaches, perturbation schemes, and direct algebraic methods have gained attention because they provide effective tools for handling nonlinear structures and constructing exact wave solutions.

For example, in recent work the Aboodh transform has been combined with the homotopy perturbation method17,18 to solve fractional Swift Hohenberg equations and similar nonlinear models. Similar ideas have been applied to practical problems including water infiltration in porous media and groundwater recharge which demonstrate their suitability for different physical processes onsiderable19,20.

Simultaneously, several contemporary analytical tools such as residual power series methods21, q-homotopy analysis transform methods22 and modified direct algebraic approaches23–26, have been proposed to solve nonlinear fractional partial differential equations arising in applied mathematics and physics. These techniques have also been successfully applied in the study of fluid motion, heat conduction and associated fraction diffusion processes27,28.

Recently, significant progress has also been made in the study of nonlinear wave propagation and soliton dynamics using hyperbolic, trigonometric, and other function-based expansion techniques. In this context, Mahmud and co-authors examined several nonlinear models, including generalized Hietarinta-type equations, Kadomtsev–Petviashvili and Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equations, Fokas systems, and Novikov-Veselov equations, and demonstrated a wide range of wave structures and solution behaviors using these analytical approaches29–33.

Overall, these developments show that constructing reliable and efficient methods for nonlinear evolution equations remains an active and important area of research. Motivated by these advances, this work proposes a Riccati-based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) to obtain new exact solution families for the potential KdV equation.

The potential Korteweg–de Vries (p-KdV) equation has been investigated widely over the last several years complemented by extensive literature34–38. Usual methods of solving this equation are the ansatz method, the G’/G expansion method, the exp-function method, the mapping method, and Lie symmetry analysis. The main interest of these studies has been to obtain soliton and travelling wave solutions that have a number of interesting and physically relevant properties34–38.

An important extension of the classical KdV is its so-called potential form, where the dependent variable is a potential function the spatial derivative of which gives the physical wave profile. This equation is widely used in nonlinear wave theory and has been extensively studied in recent literature39.

Exact solutions of nonlinear partial differential equations such as the potential Korteweg-de Vries (pKdv ), equation aid the discovery of physics of nonlinear dispersive entities. They can provide deep insight into soliton dynamics and wave propagation besides providing a good test bed on which numerical and approximate analytical methods can be tested. A number of various analysis methods have been developed over the years to address such equations including the tanh-function method, auxiliary equation methods, homogeneous balance methods and Lie symmetry methods of analysis40–43. These methods have enabled researchers to develop a diverse range of kinds of solutions such as solitary and periodic waves, rational and rogue wave structures, which illustrates the richness of solution space of potenial KdV-type models.

With these developments, it is still not an easy task to come up with general and far-reaching families of exact solutions to the pkdV equation. The nonlinearities of the higher order and the sensitive balance of dispersive terms in the equation are the factors that predispose the equation to non-easy solution methods44–47. With further perturbations, time-delays, or even fractional derivatives, the situation is even more complicated and more realistic as it represents real physical systems but becomes more challenging to analytically treat due to further perturbations of the equations presented48–52. To overcome these problems, quasi-integrable methods, Backlund transformations and bilinear formalism of Hirota have been employed in new research, and this has been proven to be effective in generating multi-soliton solutions and interaction of solitons under variation of different conditions53–57. Also discrete and coupled KdV-type models have been examined in order to investigate stability, kink interactions and complex wave phenomena in more realistic to physical reality systems58,59.

On the whole, it is important to note that the further elaboration of both analytical and numerical methods still helps to discover more about the pKdV equation. Not only are these attempts to broaden the theoretical understanding of the dynamics of nonlinear waves, but are also applicable to the study of application in nonlinear lattices, plasma physics and shallow water waves, citing the importance of exact solution methods in the study of NPDEs.

In this paper, we use the so-called Riccati-Based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) to obtain the precise analytical solutions of the potential KdV equation. The originality of this paper lies in the application and adaptation of the Riccati-Based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) to the potential KdV (pKdV) model, leading to new families of exact solutions. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is further verified through comparative and convergence analysis presented in the results section. The results lead to the theoretical knowledge about nonlinear dispersive waves and provide additional benchmark solutions to subsequent analytical and numerical research. To evaluate the performance of the proposed method, a comparative study with existing analytical techniques is presented in the results and discussion section.

This paper is structured in the following way, Sect. 2 introduces the Riccati-Based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method. Section 3 presents the study of potential KdV equation and obtains analytical solutions. Section 4 speaks about graphs and their physical properties. Lastly, Sect. 5 is a conclusion to the paper and an explanation of potential research directions.

Methodology

We use the Riccati Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) as the instrument to find precise solutions of the potential Korteweg-de Vries (p-KdV) equation in this research. The approach offers a technical system of finding analytical solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations (NPDEs) by converting them into ordinary differential equations and took advantage of the form of the extended Riccati equation.

These algebraic equations are solved symbolically using Maple, yielding the values of the unknown constants. Finally, by substituting these constants and the general solution of Eq. (4) back into Eq. (3), several families of exact solutions are obtained. Comparative assessment with existing analytical methods

To position the proposed Riccati-Based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) within the context of existing approaches, a comparative study is carried out with several commonly used analytical techniques for nonlinear evolution equations, such as the tanh-function method, the Hirota bilinear method, and the exp-function method.

In contrast to many classical techniques that depend on predefined trial functions, restrictive assumptions, or bilinear transformations, the RMESEM is built upon an extended Riccati equation framework. This facility makes it possible to build solution structures of a method in a more systematic way, without forcing an initial functional form, providing more flexibility in the formulation of nonlinear partial differential equations.

The major differences between the present approach and classical approaches are summarized in Table 2. The comparison includes, among other things, aspects of computational effort, underlying construction procedure, and the range of possible solutions that can be achieved using each method.

Based on this comparison, one can see that the methods like tanh-function and exp-function are effective methods for finding particular wave solutions, including solitary and rational solutions. These techniques are typically designed to presuppose a particular structure of the solution, however, and may not be applicable to all problems. In much the same way, the Hirota bilinear method is very effective for integrable systems and multi-soliton solutions but the bilinear transformation is not always easily implemented for more general nonlinear models.

The proposed RMESEM, however, offers a more coherent and systematic approach. It decreases the computational complexity of solution construction and makes possible obtaining a large class of wave solutions such as solitary, periodic, rational and combined-type solutions dependent on the selection of the parameters. Such systematic approach, together with the use of 3D surfaces, 2D slices, and contour plots, provide a comprehensive understanding of the nonlinear dynamics of the wave and their mathematical structure.

The obtained graphics are clearly presented and demonstrate the influence of the changes in the values of the parameters on the amplitude, localization and periodicity of the wave profiles. All the simulations are carried out in such a way that the solutions are non-singular and physically sound.

Physical interpretation and properties of the solutions

The resulting solutions are exact analytical expressions for phenomena of nonlinear waves, which are often encountered in the physics of plasma and fluid dynamics. The solutions are of solitary waves, periodic waves or rational-type solutions depending upon the parameter choices.

The most important consequence of the travelling wave transformation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta = x - \alpha t$$\end{document} is that the velocity of the waves is v = α. This indicates that propagation speed and direction of the waves depends on the value of α.

The parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k_0$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_0$$\end{document} set the background state and amplitude level, and the remaining parameters, μ and ν determines the balance between the two nonlinear and dispersive terms that can ultimately affect the structure of the wave.

The boundedness of the solutions is ensured for appropriate restrictions on the parameters, especially L>0 guaranteeing real valued hyperbolic behavior. Rational solutions can, however, have singularities at points where the denominator terms are zero, and these are not included in the physically acceptable regime, however.

Overall, the results emphasize the interplay between the nonlinear and dispersive effects and how solitary, periodic and rational wave structures form in KdV-type systems.

Comparison with existing literature and novelty of the present work

Many different analytical methods have been developed to solve nonlinear evolution equations, particularly those of the KdV-type. Some of the most popular methods are the tanh-method, Hirota bilinear method, Exp-function method, Kudryashov method, and some of the Riccati-based methods. Such procedures have yielded numerous standard wave solutions, such as hyperbolic, trigonometric, exponential, rational and solitary wave solutions which are already known in the literature.

A remark on the solutions obtained in this work: Partly, the solutions obtained, as it is obvious, are essentially new elementary functional forms, but many of these forms are also natural for Riccati-type analytical frameworks. Instead, the main contribution of this study is the use of the Riccati based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) which gives a systematic and unified procedure for finding different classes of exact traveling wave solutions for the potential KdV equation.

The proposed framework allows to obtain several families of solutions in the same computational structure. Moreover, the more extended the parameters are, the more is the flexibility of the method and the more is the ability to derive mixed and composite wave profiles without the use of bilinearisation procedures or involved auxiliary transformations.

Note also that a few of the solutions found are connected with previously reported Riccati-type expressions by appropriate choice of parameter values or limiting cases. Thus, these solutions are not being considered as fully separated discoveries, but rather as a part of a family of analytical solutions.

The essence of the present study is in methodology and structure, therefore. This is especially true of the RMESEM framework which offers a structured procedure for obtaining, categorizing and investigating exact travelling wave solutions, such as some extended and composite solutions which do not arise from the usual Riccati parameterizations. These results are a useful extension to the range of potential KdV (Korteweg-deVries) equation solutions and are consistent with all other known solutions.

The novelty of this work is mainly on providing a unified computational approach for obtaining and studying the exact traveling wave solutions, and not in discovering new and fundamental wave functions.

Comparison of RMESEM with existing analytical methods

In the literature, there are several analytic methods suggested to solve the nonlinear evolution equations, each with specific advantages and disadvantages for the mathematical formulation of the governing model. The tanh-method, Hirota bilinear method, Exp-function method, Kudryashov method and the other auxiliary equation methods are commonly used. While they work well in many cases, they can include assumptions that are not always realistic, lengthy algebraic transformations or complex transformations procedures22,24,30.

For instance, methods that involve hyperbolic function, like tanh-method, are relatively simple and efficient in the construction of solitary wave solutions and are usually based on the prior assumption of the ansatz. The bilinear method of Hirota is very powerful for integrable systems and multi-soliton solutions, but bilinearization may be a mathematically challenging step. Likewise, the Exp-function and Kudryashov methods can be used to find various classes of analytical solutions but can produce lengthy algebraic systems and complex parameter relations31,60,

The Riccati-based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) used in the present study is in contrast a comparatively more direct and systematic analytical framework. An adequate travelling wave transformation is used to reduce the governing nonlinear partial differential equation to an ordinary differential equation which is then solved by using a modified Riccati-type ansatz to establish the structure of the solution. This formulation is free from the need to make strong assumptions about the form of the wave.

One of the major benefits of the method is the capability of creating various waves in a single calculation procedure. The solution obtained from the method can be solitary wave, periodic solution, rational structure, singular profile, or mixed wave form depending on the values of the selected parameters. Compared to some classical methods, the present method does not involve inverse scattering analysis, perturbation expansions, and complex multi-step transformations, etc., which results in a relative ease in computation.

To conclude, the RMESEM framework has a reasonable degree of generality in the analysis and yet is efficient in computation. The method is a useful one for the construction of exact solutions of the nonlinear evolution equations and can be used for other similar nonlinear models used in fluid dynamics, plasma physics, nonlinear optical, and other applied mathematics contexts.

Conclusion, novelty, and future research directions

The potential Korteweg-de Vries (pKdV) equation is investigated by the Riccati-based Modified Extended Simple Equation Method (RMESEM) in this work. The method gives a systematic procedure to reduce nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) to ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and to construct exact analytical solutions in an extended Riccati framework. Consequently, various classes of exact wave solutions have been derived, such as soliton-like, kink-type, periodic and rational solutions of the wave structures. The various solutions are related to the different dynamical behaviors of nonlinear wave propagation.

The novelty of this work in its main contribution is the systematic implementation of the RMESEM for the pKdV equation and generation of exact solutions in a structured way under a unified framework.

The solutions obtained are in hyperbolic, trigonometric, exponential and rational functional forms. Such functional structures have appeared in the literature, but the present approach illustrates the consistent and systematic construction of these for the pKdV model.

Further, the method is quite general in allowing extension to other systems, such as higher dimensional, variable coefficient or coupled nonlinear evolution equations, and suggests other possible extensions to be explored analytically in the context of nonlinear mathematical physics.

In the future, the current method can be expanded to the fractional-order and coupled nonlinear systems, and interactions between multiple-component waves. The use of RMESEM in conjunction with numerical simulations or symbolic computation methods can also offer a deeper understanding of the nonlinear propagation of waves in dispersive media, their bifurcation phenomena and stability behaviour.

Although the present study primarily focuses on the analytical construction of exact traveling wave solutions, further investigations involving stability analysis, asymptotic behavior, conservation properties, and perturbation dynamics may be considered in future research.

To sum up, the RMESEM is able to apply to build the exact solutions of nonlinear evolution equations and it is a structured framework which studies the nonlinear wave phenomenon in different physical models.

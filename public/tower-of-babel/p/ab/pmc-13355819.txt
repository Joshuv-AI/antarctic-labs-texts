Non-ionizing radiation and cancer: A review on current evidence, mechanistic insights, and public health implications
Authors: Mridula Agrahari, Shruti Gupta, Gaurav Thakur, Shweta Tanwar, Rohit Gautam, Taruna Arora
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Non-ionizing radiation (NIR) generated from the high power transmission lines, broadcasting antennas and cellular phones represent one of the widespread environmental exposures which can induce carcinogenic outcome. Although NIR lacks sufficient energy to dislodge the electrons for ionization, an indirect mechanism has been proposed to be involved in adverse outcomes under certain exposure conditions. Consequently, radiofrequency electromagnetic field (RF-EMF) exposure remains a consistent scientific and public health concern regarding its possible role in cancer manifestation. This review aims to analyze the findings of RF-EMF exposure and carcinogenicity by integrating epidemiological studies and experimental findings to demonstrate its potential function in cancer manifestation.The available evidence is not sufficient to conclude that RF-EMF exposure is a direct-acting genotoxic carcinogen or cancer initiator. However, laboratory studies have indicated that long-term exposure to non-thermal RF-EMF can cause oxidative stress, genomic instability, epigenetic alterations, ion transport disruption and dysregulation of cell-signaling pathways. These biological effects may contribute to the promotion and progression stages of carcinogenesis, but are not directly responsible for tumor/cancer initiation. The epidemiological evidence significantly varies between extremely low-frequency magnetic fields (ELF-MF) and radiofrequency electromagnetic fields (RF-EMF). Although ELF-MF exposure has demonstrated a more reliable association with childhood leukemia, the data connecting RF-EMF exposure to cancer is less consistent.Studies with the larger sample size and increased exposure duration are needed to strongly corroborate its involvement in cancer development.

Introduction

Non-ionizing radiations comprise of a wide range of electromagnetic fields (EMFs) including radiofrequency electromagnetic field (RF-EMF), extremely low-frequency (ELF) fields, microwaves, Ultraviolet radiation, visible light and infrared radiations [1,2]. Humans and animals are continuously exposed to RF-EMF. Although, non-ionizing radiations do not have enough energy to dislodge the electrons for ionization, their exposure may induce indirect biological response with the variable exposure conditions. An exposure to non-ionizing radiations (NIR) results in oxidative stress, altered gene expression and interference in DNA repair pathways leading to genomic instability and damage to cellular processes [3]. Therefore, the mechanism and potential risk of adverse health effects through the non-ionizing radiation remained a scientific investigation for the ongoing research.Importantly, biological effects reported after RF-EMF exposure should be distinguished from direct carcinogenic initiation. In contrast to ionizing radiation, RF-EMF has insufficient energy to directly damage. Thus, current mechanistic hypotheses have largely focused on non-genotoxic pathways which could contribute to tumor promotion and progression through oxidative stress, altered signalling pathways, epigenetic changes, and disrupted cellular homeostasis.

Epidemiological research in humans focusing on RF-EMF exposures specifically from telecommunications, workplaces and environmental sources has raised widespread concern regarding its potential negative health effects [[4], [5], [6]]. Furthermore, exposure to mobile phone, radio-based stations, phone towers, high-voltage power lines, broadcast antennas, and radar installations are associated with the free radical damage induced cancer development [3]. With the rapid global rise of mobile phone usage, concerns have emerged regarding the carcinogenic effects of RF-EMF & ELF-MF emitted from mobile phones and other sources [5,7]. In a Meta analysis on 23 case-control studies, it has been reported that mobile phone use is associated with the increased tumor risk and long term usage of mobile phone of 10 or more years increases the risk of brain tumors [8]. In contrary to the above findings, several epidemiological studies including case-control studies and various case control meta-analyses have been reported conflicting evidences of tumor risk with the usage of mobile phones [[8], [9], [10], [11]].

On the basis of humans epidemiological studies and animal studies, the International Agency for Research on cancer (IARC) and World Health Organization (WHO) have evaluated that RF- EMF emitted by mobile phones should be categorized as carcinogenic to humans (Group 2B) [1,12].

However, researchers argued that the available evidence warrants reconsideration of the IARC classification.[13].Nevertheless, RF-EMF remains classified by IARC as Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic to humans), and no reclassification to Group 1 has occurred. With the advancement of new technologies, the use of mobile phone has although revolutionized the way of communication and triggered the economic growth, but it has also induced adverse biological effect in living organisms demonstrating a lack of proper guidance to the associated exposure risks. Therefore, a comprehensive review of the existing literature is requisite to conclude the carcinogenic effects from non-ionizing radiations. In this review we are summarizing the available literature and findings related with the non-ionizing radiation exposure and its outcome as a carcinogenic to humans and animals which will be beneficial to form a public guideline regarding its exposure and limit.

Methodology

This article is a narrative review and does not follow a formal systematic review methodology. The literature was searched using PubMed, Scopus, and Google scholar up to March 2026.We searched for original research papers, systematic reviews, meta-analyses in English language. Studies were selected according to their relevance to biological effects, mechanistic evidence, and possible carcinogenic outcomes of exposure to non-ionizing radiation.

Sources of radiofrequency electromagnetic field (RF-EMF)

Non ionizing radiations (NIR) propagate as fast as the speed of light and are emitted by a wide range of natural and manmade sources. An exposure to these NIR causes remarkable health risks to the population [14]. These sources are prevalent and wide spread in the current environment. Natural sources are comprised of Ultraviolet radiation (UV), infrared and visible regions from solar radiation and earth's magnetic field. Man-made sources includes mobile phones and towers, Wi-Fi equipment, High power transmission lines, Amplitude Modulation (AM) radio transmission, Frequency Modulation (FM) radio transmission, microwaves, household wirings, Laptop and PC monitors, television sets, broadcasting antennas, Light Emitting Diode (LED) lamps, fluorescent lamps, hand held lasers and laser pointers, etc [15]. High power transmission lines

With the development and increasing demand of radio communication, exposure to magnetic fields became prevalent due to ubiquitous use of High-power transmission lines and house hold appliances (50-60Hz). Studies reported that exposure to extremely low frequency magnetic field generated by power lines and house hold appliances are associated with the childhood leukemia [16]. A meta-analysis of case-control and cohort studies suggested that exposure to higher than 0.4 μT extremely low frequency magnetic field enhances the risk of leukemia in children and probably associated with the acute lymphoblastic leukemia which elevates with the prolonged exposure [17].

Broadcasting antennas

The broadcasting antennas are generally used for the broadcasting of television and traditional radio and comprised of Very High Frequency (VHF, 174-230 MHz) and Ultra High Frequency (UHF, 470-862 MHz) bands [18]. Emission of RF-EMF from these antennas has been believed to cause childhood cancer however; it has not been identified at low exposure level [4,19]. A study in genetically engineered mice that was made susceptible to lymphoma demonstrated remarkably elevated lymphoma rates and suggested its association with the cancer [20]. Further, a study of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and other non-myeloid leukemia observed in the areas in vicinity to television broadcasting towers in northern Sydney and the authors have claimed significantly higher cases of mortality due to ALL [21].

Cellular phones

With the advancement in technology, the use of wireless communication has become widespread. Mobile phone has become an integral part of our day-to-day life as it is being used immensely by the people for their routine work. With the evolution from first generation (1G) to fifth generation (5G) wireless technology, from basic phones to one available with the global connectivity; the use of cellular phone has become now indispensable for everyone's regular life. Further, development of several users friendly mobile Apps related with commerce, communication and essential services have made them necessity for the routine work. As a result, people relying on mobile phone for their regular activities are unknowingly exposed to RF EMR which has raised concern regarding its negative health effects [22,23]. Several research have been undertaken to explore the effects of RF-EMF on human health. Some studies have been conducted to see the association of rapid use of cellular phone and risk of tumors [22,24,25]. A meta-analysis of case-control studies found the significant connection between cumulative use of cellular phone (1000 h or more in their lifetime) and an increased risk of tumor [24]. A relation between cellular phone exposure and risk of Glioma, most common type of brain tumor, has been much investigated and demonstrated that there is a significant and positive association of glioma with the long term use of cellular phone [22,26,27]. Contrary to this, a cohort study mentioned that the prolonged use of mobile phone is not associated with the probability of developing glioma, meningioma, or acoustic neuroma [25]. Similarly, a Meta-analysis has also reported a weak association between meningioma risk and use of cellular phone with the need of further validation (Chen et al., 2021).

Biophysical parameters

The extent of harmful effects due to radiation exposure is mediated by biophysical parameters which determine the dose, distribution and mode of interaction. Biophysical parameters specify the biological and physical parameters that quantify the absorption rate and establish sensitivity to the Non ionizing radiation's (RF-EMF or ELF-MF) ability to get absorbed, penetrated inside the tissues and triggered to biological responses is governed by their biophysical parameters.

The extent of radiation is determined by the power density and Specific Absorption Rate (SAR). SAR is a dosimetric quantity used to determine the rate of absorption of radiofrequency non ionizing radiations in biological tissues. The rate of absorption is quantified in per unit mass and is measured by W/kg. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) limits localized SAR from mobile phones to 1.6 W/kg averaged over 1 g of tissue. In contrast, the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP) and European guidelines specify a limit of 2 W/kg averaged over 10 g of tissue [28]. Therefore, for any cellular phone the SAR value should be either less or equal to the stated value to be in the permissible exposure range.

Wireless communication technologies utilises a wide range of frequency bands. GSM (2G) is usually in the 900 MHz and 1800 MHz bands. Non-thermal EMF-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and its role in carcinogenesis

Over the years, plethora of experimental evidence has indicated that non-thermal exposure to electromagnetic fields (EMFs) leads to oxidative stress. This rise of oxidative stress is mediated mainly through aberrant generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [30].The nexus of excessive ROS generation and oxidative stress represent one of focal point in promoting carcinogenesis [31]. Multiple in vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that anthropogenic sources of Electromagnetic field (EMF) waves may cause aberrant ROS-generation. This EMF mediated ROS generation occurs without substantial tissue heating, differing from paradigm of thermally induced EMF bio effects [30,32].

It has been hypothsized that, EMF exposure detriments functioning of membrane-associated nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate/nicotinamide adenine inucleotide (NADPH/NADH) oxidases, mitochondrial electron transport chain and nitric oxide synthase. This possible breakdown of normal cellular homeostasis may lead to overproduction of superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and peroxynitrite [32,33]. Panagopoulos et al., 2025 has proposed the rationale behind mechanism of Ion forced oscillation–Voltage Gated Ion Channel (IFO-VGIC) upon exposure to EMF radiation. This mechanism explores how non ionization radiation causes impaired functioning of calcium and sodium channels. The aberrant functioning of ion channels manifests into intracellular ionic imbalance and secondary ROS generation [30].This faulty functioning of ion channel causes elevated levels of intracellular calcium ions. The elevated levels of calcium ion are believed to cause mitochondrial deregulation and redox-sensitive signaling pathways, leading to oxidative stress.

The role of ROS is well-established a etiological factor behind tumorigenesis. The elevated ROS levels propel tumor initiation, promotion, and progression through lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids damage [34]. The elevated ROS are associated with higher levels of 8-hydroxy- 2-deoxyguanosine (8-OH-dG), lipid peroxidation, and dysfunctional antioxidant defenses receptively [15,32]. The resulting situation causes impaired DNA repair mechanism manifesting in genomic instability under chronic exposure conditions (Fig. 2).

Moreover, ROS also performs the role secondary messenger signaling pathways. It activates downstream signaling cascade involving transcription factors such as NF-κB, AP-1, and HIF-1α. These transcription factors are involved in genomic control by regulating cellular proliferation, inflammation, angiogenesis, and apoptosis [34].

Hence, oxidative stress induced by non-thermal EMF radiation may initiate DNA damage and can foster oncogenic microenvironment. Summing up these findings establishes nexus between EMF exposure, ROS generation and subsequent malignant transformation [35]. Moreover, all the studies have not replicated the oxidative stress related findings due to differences in exposure protocols, dosimetry and biological models could contribute to the observed variability.

Genotoxic effects of non-thermal EMF exposure and genomic instability

Genotoxicity provides a crucial link between EMF exposure and carcinogenesis. Though EMFs are non-ionizing in nature, available evidence indicates its role in causing indirect DNA damage. The DNA damage through indirect means largely happens through oxidative and redox-mediated pathways. EMF mediated indirect DNA damage may culminate in genomic instability, a hallmark of cancer [30,35].

Low intensity exposure are reported to cause single- and double-strand DNA breaks, micronuclei formation, chromosomal aberrations, and DNA base modifications [32,36]. The ROS generated after EMF exposure are able to attack DNA bases and the sugar-phosphate backbone. This ROS mediated attack on DNA can cause mutagenic sites such as 8-OH-dG and effects DNA integrity (Fig. 2) [34].

Moreover, EMFs exposure is reported to hinder DNA repair pathways. Important DNA repair proteins, such PARP-1, ATM, and p53, have shown altered expression and activity after chronic EMF exposure. Impaired function of these vital DNA repair protein is linked with weakened cellular potential to repair oxidative damage [35]. DNA damage coupled with ineffective repair process decreases the chance of mutation repair during replication, leading to malignant growth. Previous reproductive toxicity reports further corroborates this mechanism. It is reported that non-thermal RF-EMF exposure is linked with DNA fragmentation and oxidative base damage in germ cells [15].

Moreover, other environmental carcinogens, works in synergy with EMF radiation in causing genomic instability. This synergistic association between environmental carcinogen EMFs, may contribute towards malignant transformation [35]. Overall, the current evidence shows that non-thermal EMF exposure manifests indirect genotoxic effect driven by oxidative damage and impaired DNA repair. This reinforces the classification of EMFs as potential carcinogenic risk factors.

Impact of non-thermal EMF exposure on ion transport, cell signaling, and cancer-related pathways

Another important way that non-thermal EMF exposure may contribute to carcinogenesis is through impairment of ion homeostasis and membrane signaling. Because of the charged nature of their voltage sensors, cellular ion channels in particular, voltage-gated calcium channels (VGCCs) are extremely sensitive to oscillating electromagnetic fields [30].The Ion-Forced Oscillation/VGCC model has been proposed as a possible mechanism to explain some biological responses to EMF exposure. However, experimental validation remains limited and further studies are needed to demonstrate its biological relevance.

The Ion Forced Oscillation model states that abnormal channel gating and prolonged calcium influx result from the forces that ELF and ELF-modulated RF EMFs apply to mobile ions inside channel pores [30]. Increased intracellular calcium is a powerful catalyst for the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the mitochondria, the activation of calcium-dependent kinases, and the dysregulation of apoptosis, all of which are essential for the development of cancer. It has been reported that elevated cytosolic Ca2+ activates the MAPK, PKC, and PI3K/Akt pathways, enhancing cell survival and proliferation under stressful environments [32].

Transcriptional regulation is indirectly impacted by altered ion transport, which also alters membrane potential and redox balance. Activation of NF-κB and AP-1 leads to EMF-induced calcium impairment, augmenting expression of inflammatory mediators and proto-oncogenes [34]. This conducive cellular milieu sustains tumor development and progression facilitated by these signaling cascades.

Moreover, EMF radiation causes disruption of sodium-potassium ATPase activity and intracellular pH regulation. Disruption in pH can perturb cellular homeostasis, favoring glycolysis and proliferative propensity, characteristic of cancer cells [35].Thus, non-thermal EMF affect ion transport by integrating oxidative stress, genotoxicity, and aberrant signaling. Overall, these reports find ion channel dysfunction as key mediator linking EMF exposure to carcinogenic signaling networks.

Epigenetic mechanisms underlying EMF-associated cancer initiation

The role of epigenetic reprogramming has been recently explored upon electromagnetic fields (EMF) exposure. As EMF lacks adequate energy to induce direct DNA breaks, available evidence demonstrates that it can alter gene expression through epigenetic alterations. Gene methylation changes, histone modifications, miRNA expression levels and chromatin remodeling, develop helpful epigenetic condition for malignant transformation.

Epigenetic changes have been associated as a mechanism behind EMF mediated biological effects [37,38]. These changes include upregulated microRNA activity [39], changes in chromatin remodeling [40,41] and changes in DNA methylation levels [42].

[42], reported 114 genes that showed significant differential methylation following exposure to RF-EMFs compared to sham. A correlation with gene expression unraveled six common targets that showed differential methylated and expression in response to RF-EMF exposure. These genes functions to maintain critical cell functions such as cell survival damage response. Evidence from some other studies corroborates these observations [43]; reported that ELF-EMF exposure increases global DNA methylation while impairing DNA methyltransferase expression, marked by elevated DNMT1 and decreased DNMT3a levels. Since DNMT1maintains methylation pattern during cell division, these changes cause EMF-induced stable and heritable epigenetic alterations.

Other than DNA methylation, histone modifications and chromatin remodeling are also reported to be impacted from EMF exposure. In one previous study authors reported that Schwann cells undergo aberrant methylation changes upon EMF exposure [44]. In Schwann cells these changes, gears a cell toward a less differentiated cellular phenotype. This epigenetic reprogramming enables Schwann cell transformation and schwannoma development.

Disruption of apoptosis and cell-cycle checkpoints

Disruption of cell-cycle is common biological response to electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure. Perturbation in cell cycle provides a key step in journey towards carcinogenesis. A number of available evidence demonstrates that EMF exposure impinges these processes by causing stress responses without effective apoptotic response [45]. In one study [46], demonstrated change in CASP3 and CASP7 activity in a variety of cellular types, including human leukemia (K562), endothelial and epithelial cell lines upon ELF-EMF exposure. Instead of effectively eliminating damaged cells, these results show incomplete or deregulated apoptosis, a state known to promote malignant development.

Corroborating this, earlier work summarized by Ref. [47]; revealed that non-ionizing radiation (ELF- MF or RF-EMF) can modulate expression of apoptosis-related and cell-cycle regulatory genes.TP53 a central tumor suppressor gene is impaired by non-ionizing radiation further weakening checkpoint execution and promoting cell survival under stress conditions [46]. has provided further insight demonstrating EMF-induced over expression of GADD45 family genes, intersects apoptosis, cell-cycle arrest, and DNA damage response. While temporary GADD45 activation offers protection, persistent or dysregulated expression has been connected to inappropriate cell survival, a known early stage of carcinogenesis, and compromised checkpoint control [48]. One important upstream mediator in this process is oxidative stress showed that exposure to electromagnetic fields (EMFs) can decrease p53-dependent apoptosis and uncouple cell-cycle arrest from apoptotic commitment by generating reactive oxygen species and altering nitric oxide synthase signaling[46].

Nonionizing radiations and related carcinogenicity

Leukemia

Leukemia is one of prevalent childhood cancer. Among plethora of risk factors behind its etiology role of ionizing and non-ionizing radiation is poorly understood [[49], [50], [51]]. Specifically, there is evidence of a dose-response relationship between childhood leukemia and extremely low frequency magnetic fields ([17,52]; Id et al., 2021).

Epidemiological studies investigating the role of electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure and leukemia have focused largely on childhood leukemia. A consistent association has been reported between extremely low-frequency magnetic fields (ELF-MF) residential exposure and childhood leukemia. Some previous meta-analytic studies reveals that children exposed to higher average residential magnetic field levels manifest higher odds of leukemia, ranging between 1.4 and 2.0 (Id et al., 2021; [52]; L [53]). Recent investigations have corroborated this finding and have indicated dose response relationship ([17]; Id et al., 2021). An umbrella review of environmental risk factors has reported ELF-MF exposure as one of the predisposing factor behind onset of ALL [54].

Some Population-based case–control studies have investigated risk of leukemia risk in relation to residential distance from high-voltage power lines. Moderate risks were reported among children residing within close distances (approximately <50–100 m), while risk estimates diminished at greater distances [55]."Malagoli et al. reported an increased risk of childhood leukemia among children residing within 100 m of high-voltage transmission lines compared with those living ≥400 m away, suggesting a possible association between close residential proximity to power lines and leukemia risk. However, residential exposure to magnetic fields from transformer stations failed to show association with leukemia risk [16].

In contrast to ELF-MF, studies evaluating radiofrequency (RF-EMF) exposure have found ambiguous findings in reference to leukemia. National case - control studies evaluating maternal or early-life exposure to emissions from mobile phone base stations found no increased risk of childhood leukemia or related hematological malignancies [56]. Similar findings were reported from systematic reviews inferring that environmental RF-EMF exposure from fixed-site transmitters and wireless infrastructure does not elevate leukemia risk in children [24]. Moreover in-depth and through WHO-commissioned systematic review reported non likelihood increase the risk of childhood leukemia from fixed-site transmitters [57].

It is important to note that the observed association with childhood leukemia is predominantly related to ELF-MF exposure from power lines and electrical infrastructure. In general, result reveals an association between higher residential ELF magnetic field exposure and childhood leukemia. However Current evidence does not demonstrate a similarly consistent association between RF-EMF exposure and childhood leukemia.

Glioma/meningioma

A longstanding discussion is undergoing since early 2000 regarding the association of RF-EMF exposure and prevalence of brain tumor. Current RF EMF exposure assessment is not sufficient to clarify the association of brain tumor with the RF-EMF exposure from the mobile phone due to the rapidly changing smart phone and cellular technologies. Although, non-ionizing radiation are associated with the brain tumors and glioma risk among adults [58], no significant association observed with the childhood tumors. Also, no association has been reported regarding the pediatric glioma and paternal occupational exposure [59] however, maternal occupational exposure specifically with the sewing machine has been associated with the CNS tumor and glioma risk [60].

As non-ionizing radiations do not have sufficient energy to ionize the atoms, its function in glioma/meningioma incidences have been proposed through an indirect mechanism leading to tumor outcome. It is reported that RF-EMF exposure induces reactive oxygen species (ROS) leading to DNA damage and chromosomal instability and brain tumor progression [32,61]. These low frequency radiations may also activate signaling pathways related with stress, in turn, resulting in altered gene expression and elevated tumor growth [62]. Also, it can increase permeability of blood brain barrier transiently and giving rise to a preferable environment for the tumor progression [63]. Several Meta analyses have been done to demonstrate the association of mobile phone use and the risk of tumor, however, further studies are needed to strongly suggest this association [8,26,64,65]. However, these studies could not observe a strong positive relation of brain tumor due to RF EMR exposure. A moderate positive association reported by the studies conducted by Hardell et al. with the occurrence of glioma and acoustic neuroma [10]. Contrary to this, Interphone group could not observe a statistical substantial association of brain tumor risk with the light use of mobile phone however, a significant association with glioma and acoustic neuroma was observed only with the long exposures of the mobile phone. Another independent study supported the evidences of Interphone group and reported that there is no association of tumor risk with minimal use of mobile phone [24,66,67]. Similar to this, a study of Coureau et al. mentioned that glioma is associated with the long exposure to mobile phone [68]. A Meta analysis of 19 case control studies demonstrated more précised RF-EMF exposure measures including >10 years of use of mobile phone, ipsilateral use and high cumulative call time are related with the high risk glioma [69].Therefore, the available evidences strongly suggest that the non-ionizing radiations are involved in tumor progression after a prolonged and cumulative exposure [1] even though; further studies need to be done to comment on it strongly.

Similar to glioma, several studies have been performed on the Meningioma outcome through the exposure of RF EMF and observed similar kind of results as observed in case of glioma. A study reported by interphone demonstrated that the cellular phone users are not associated with the increased risk of meningioma, yet further study need to be conducted to monitor the risk while long term exposure [70]. A collaborative case control study performed in north European countries reported that the regular use of mobile phone is not associated with the meningioma risk by studying on total 3299 control along with the 1209 meningioma cases [71]. As long term use of mobile phone such as ≥ 10 years is associated with the high risk of glioma, no such evidence has been observed in case of meningioma [9]. Similarly, several other studies have also reported that there are no association of mobile phone exposure along with the meningioma incidences [70,[72], [73], [74], [75], [76]]. Furthermore, despite the dramatic increase in mobile phone use worldwide since the 1990s, age adjusted glioma incidence rates have remained largely stable in several national cancer registries, These observations provide limited support for a strong population level carcinogenic effect of RE-EMF exposure [76].

Importantly, a number of large prospective cohort studies have found no significant association between mobile-phone use and risk of glioma, meningioma or acoustic neuroma including the Danish Cohort Study [73], the Million Women Study [75] and the recent COSMOS study [25].Prospective cohort designs are generally less susceptible to recall and selection bias than retrospective case control studies because exposure information is collected before disease occurrence. Additionally "The recent WHO-commissioned systematic review by Karipidis et al. [57] found no evidence of increased cancer risk associated with RF-EMF exposure from mobile phones." Although some studies conducted by Hardell et al. reported positive associations between long-term mobile phone use and brain tumor risk, these findings should be interpreted with caution. As with other retrospective case-control studies, concerns have been raised regarding the potential influence of recall bias, participation bias, and control-selection differences, which may affect risk estimates. Therefore, these findings should be considered alongside evidence from large prospective cohort studies and population-based incidence trend analyses. These findings provide important context when interpreting positive results from some retrospective case-control studies.

A systematic review has discussed that the period of 10 year to observe the harmful effects of exposure is not adequate, and the exposure consequences can be validated by exceeding the exposure duration (>10 years) [23]. While current studies do not provide a positive correlation of exposure with the meningioma, further studies with the increased exposure duration and large sample size may likely give insight in this context.

Acoustic neuroma

Acoustic neuroma, also known as a vestibular schwannoma, is a benign, slowly growing tumor located on the vestibule cochlear nerve (cranial nerve VIII) in the ear canal within the skull (Schoemaker et al., 2026). As it presses against adjacent nerves and brain structures, it commonly results in the loss of hearing on one side, ringing in the ears, unsteadiness, and numbness in the face.

Several studies have investigated association between acoustic neuroma and non-ionization radiation; however, results have been ambiguous [77]. Several large-scale case-control studies, particularly the Interphone collaboration involving multiple North European countries, found no overall association between regular mobile phone use and acoustic neuroma risk reporting an odds ratio of 0.9 (95% CI 0.7–1.1) among 678 cases and 3553 controls. The exposure assessment was based in lifetime years of mobile phone use (0.5-4 years) [78]. Similarly, a Japanese case-control study with 51 cases and 192 matched controls found no significant increase in risk with regular mobile phone use (OR = 0.73, 95% CI 0.43–1.23), with no dose-response trend observed for cumulative years or call time [79]. However, some analyses have reported elevated risks under specific exposure conditions. The Interphone pooled analysis identified an increased risk for acoustic neuroma on the same side as reported phone use for ≥10 years (OR = 1.8, 95% CI 1.1–3.1), though investigators noted concerns about recall bias for laterality reporting (Schoemaker et al., 2006). Additionally, pooled analyses from the Hardell research group, which included both mobile and cordless phone exposure, reported increased risks for acoustic neuroma associated with long-term use, particularly emphasizing elevated risks for long latency and ipsilateral exposure patterns [80].

In summary, the existing research provides a complex situation when it comes to non-ionizing radiation and acoustic neuroma risk. Most of the evidence indicates that there is no significant impact in the initial ten years of mobile phone usage. Yet in some of the Interphone and Hardell study analyses, increased ORs or trends in ORs were noted for longer latency periods and/or ipsilateral cell phone use among regular ipsilateral users and among those with ≥10 years’ duration of exposure. This was it the case in the overall Interphone results. These unknowns demonstrate that more extensive research is needed, including studies on a larger scale that prospectively collect exposure information and have sufficiently long follow-up periods to determine whether long-term exposure to non-ionizing radiation is truly a threat with respect to acoustic neuroma, or whether the.

Breast cancer

Epidemiological evidence has provided mixed but significant findings for Breast cancer risk from RF-EMF. A case control study conducted among Taiwanese women reported that excessive smart phone use including smart phone addiction, habitual use for more than 4.5 min before falling asleep, and carrying the smart phone inside the inner brassiere were associated with significantly increased risks of breast cancer [81].

Scientists have been studying whether being exposed to the non-ionizing radiation from smart phones and other digital devices could be a possible risk factor for breast cancer. They believe this could happen through a process in which radiofrequency (RF) radiation and the high-energy visible (HEV) blue light affect the body's biological clock (circadian disrupting). In Mortazavi's retrospective case-control study (2024), heavy users of mobile phone had significantly higher cumulative 10-year digital screen exposure in the breast cancer group than controls (7089 ± 14985 vs 4052 ± 12515 h; p = 0.038), yet the authors concluded that the overall association between blue light exposure and breast cancer was not consistently strong. Logistic regression analysis showed that among heavy users, using digital screens >30 min/day in dim light before sleep yielded an odds ratio (OR) of 1.156 (95% CI: 0.63–2.12), though this was not statistically significant [82].

Conversely, the study conducted by Shih et al. [81] revealed that the associations between smart phone use behaviors and the risk of breast cancer were stronger. The AOR of breast cancer risk for women with smart phone addiction was 1.43 (95% CI: 1.01–2.02) as per multivariate analysis. Habitual smart phone use >4.5 min before bedtime was associated with a markedly elevated 5.27-fold risk (AOR = 5.27; 95% CI: 3.52–7.88). Additionally, maintaining a short distance (∼10 cm) between the smart phone and the breasts during use increased risk by 1.59 times (AOR = 1.59; 95% CI: 1.00–2.53), while carrying the smart phone near the chest or waist-abdomen area resulted in 5.03-fold (95% CI: 1.75–14.44) and 4.06-fold (95% CI: 2.11–7.78) higher risks, respectively. A synergistic effect was observed among participants with smart phone addiction who also used smart phones >4.5 min before sleep had a 6.88-fold increased risk (AOR = 6.88; 95% CI: 4.07–11.61) [81].However these findings suggest a possible association; however, interpretation is limited by the retrospective case-control design, self-reported exposure assessment, and potential residual confounding.

Overall results indicate that long-term exposure to non-ionizing radiation from smart phones, especially long hours of use, use at night, and use for addiction-like behavior, could raise the risk of breast cancer via RF-Induced Oxidative Stress and Melatonin Suppression-Related Disturbed Circadian Rhythm; however, as several studies showed strong adjusted and significant ORs and several reported adjusted and insignificant associations, it is not yet possible to draw a final conclusion.

Conclusion from current evidences

Despite the long-term investigations, the association between the non-ionizing electromagnetic field and related carcinogenicity remained ambiguous. Major inconsistency lies in its methodological limitations as epidemiological studies reported variable outcomes due to EMF exposure. Available studies indicate that the observed biological effects are not consistently correlated with exposure frequency, duration, or power density. Based on the evidence reviewed here, RF-EMF exposure is currently not established as a complete or direct-acting genotoxic carcinogen that can initiate cancer by DNA damage. However, experimental studies suggest that long term exposure to RF-EMF can cause oxidative stress, genomic instability and altered gene expression, cellular signalling which may be involved in tumour promotion and progression. Thus RF-EMF can be more appropriately considered as a potential non-genotoxic modulator of carcinogenesis rather than a direct cancer initiator. However, the current evidence is still not enough to prove a definite cause effect relationship.

Moreover the existing evidence must be analysed independently for ELF-MF and RF-EMF exposures. Although ELF-MF exposure demonstrates a relatively consistent association with childhood leukemia, evidence supporting a carcinogenic role of RF-EMF remains limited and inconclusive. Further study with the larger sample size and precise exposure assessment is needed to validate the results with more accuracy and to suggest its involvement in cancer development.

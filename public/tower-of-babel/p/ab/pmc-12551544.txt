ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFrailty is a key predictor of adverse surgical outcomes in older adults, contributing to increased postoperative complications, prolonged hospitalisation and delayed recovery. Prehabilitation—targeting improvements in physical function before surgery—can mitigate these risks. However, traditional programmes often face low adherence due to logistical barriers. Integrating smart wearable devices into tele-supervised, home-based prehabilitation may enhance adherence, engagement and clinical outcomes.This trial protocol describes the PREhabilitation of frail elderly PAtients undergoing majoR surgEry at HOME study with the objective to evaluate the effectiveness of a wearable-enhanced, tele-supervised prehabilitation programme (swSEP) versus standard care (unsupervised prehabilitation, uSEP) on improving preoperative functional capacity and postoperative outcomes in frail older adults undergoing major elective surgery.Methods and analysisThis single-centre, prospective, randomised controlled trial will enrol 190 patients aged ≥65 years scheduled for major elective, non-cardiac surgery at Singapore General Hospital. Participants with frailty (Edmonton Frail Scale ≥6) will be randomised 1:1 to either the swSEP group (tele-supervised exercise with Fitbit Inspire 3 monitoring) or the uSEP group (standard physiotherapy education, exercise booklet and inspiratory muscle training if maximal inspiratory pressure <100 cm H₂O). Introduction

Background and rationale

Background

Frailty, a condition characterised by decreased physiological reserves and increased vulnerability to stressors such as surgery, is a significant predictor of poor outcomes in elderly patients.1 Frail individuals are at a higher risk of postoperative complications, prolonged hospital stays and delayed recovery, largely due to their impaired ability to cope with the physical and metabolic demands of surgery. As the global population continues to age, managing the perioperative risks associated with frailty has become an increasingly important challenge for healthcare providers.2

Prehabilitation, a proactive strategy that seeks to improve a patient’s functional capacity before surgery through physical exercise, nutritional support and psychological interventions, has emerged as a promising intervention to mitigate these risks. By optimising physiological reserves in the preoperative period, prehabilitation aims to enhance recovery, reduce complication rates and decrease hospital length of stay. However, despite its potential, the current evidence supporting the widespread efficacy of prehabilitation remains of low certainty.3 4 A major limitation in existing studies is the variation in patient adherence to prescribed programmes. Non-adherence, often attributed to logistical barriers such as transportation difficulties, time constraints and the burden of frequent hospital visits, undermines the effectiveness of these interventions.5 6

One potential solution to overcoming these challenges is integrating wearable technology and remote monitoring into prehabilitation programmes. Smart wearable devices (SWDs), such as smart devices that continuously track physical activity, heart rate and other physiological parameters, can potentially revolutionise prehabilitation by enabling home-based interventions.7 This approach offers significant advantages over traditional hospital-based programmes. By eliminating the need for frequent hospital visits, patients can perform their prehabilitation exercises in the comfort of their own homes, at times that suit their schedules. Additionally, it gives patients greater flexibility and autonomy, which may improve their adherence and engagement in prehabilitation.8

Rationale

The PREhabilitation of frail elderly PAtients undergoing majoR surgEry at HOME (PREPARE-HOME) study aims to evaluate whether a home-based supervised exercise programme enhanced with SWDs (swSEP) can improve preoperative physical function and postoperative outcomes compared with the standard unsupervised exercise programme (uSEP). This study will explore the efficacy of smart wearables in delivering real-time physiological feedback, increasing exercise adherence and improving clinical outcomes.

Methods and analysis

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct or reporting of this trial. The study intervention and materials were developed based on clinical experience, institutional guidelines and relevant literature, with iterative input from multidisciplinary clinicians including anaesthetists, physiotherapists and nurses.

However, patient perspectives will be explored through a qualitative substudy involving semistructured interviews with trial participants. This will allow for post hoc incorporation of patient feedback into future iterations of the programme, particularly concerning usability of wearable technology, feasibility of home-based prehabilitation and perceived barriers to adherence.

No public involvement in trial oversight or manuscript development is planned at this time.

Trial design and setting

This is a single-centre, prospective, parallel-group, superiority randomised controlled trial with an allocation ratio of 1:1. The unit of randomisation is the individual participant.

The trial will be conducted at Singapore General Hospital, a tertiary hospital in Singapore. Eligible participants will be recruited from the Preoperative Evaluation Clinic (PEC). This clinic evaluates patients prior to major surgery, offering an ideal setting for timely identification and enrolment of frail elderly surgical candidates. The planned start and end dates for participant recruitment are 1 August 2025 to 1 August 2028.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Participants in this study will consist of frail elderly patients aged 65 years or older who are scheduled to undergo major elective surgery. Frailty will be determined using the Edmonton Frail Scale (EFS), with a score of 6 or higher signifying eligibility based on frailty status.9

To be included in the study, patients must be able to provide informed consent. They must be available at least 2 weeks before surgery to allow for participation in the prehabilitation programme.

The additional inclusion criteria are as follows: participants must be ≥65 years old and scheduled for major elective, non-cardiac surgery with an anticipated duration of over 2 hours or expected blood loss greater than 500 mL. Furthermore, eligible patients must be able to provide consent and follow exercise instructions. Technological requirements include owning a smartphone with Bluetooth and cellular connectivity.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria for the study will include patients with severe comorbidities that interfere with their ability to participate in the exercise programme, such as unstable angina, severe aortic stenosis or uncontrolled arrhythmias. Additionally, patients with cognitive impairments that prevent them from understanding or adhering to the intervention will be excluded.

Intervention and comparator

Participants in the control group (uSEP) will receive the standard PREPARE programme provided by the hospital, which includes preoperative physiotherapy education and a home exercise booklet. This unsupervised, home-based programme consists of full-body aerobic, resistance and respiratory exercises that participants will perform independently at home. Additionally, participants with a maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) of less than 100 cmH₂O will be given an inspiratory muscle trainer (IMT) to enhance respiratory function. An MIP threshold of <100 cm H₂O was selected based on our institution’s prior protocols and previous literature.10

The uSEP arm reflects the current standard of care for frail surgical patients at Singapore General Hospital. It includes a single preoperative physiotherapy education session, a printed home exercise booklet with aerobic and resistance exercises and IMT when clinically indicated. Participants complete the exercises independently without supervision or wearable tracking.

Participants in the intervention group (swSEP) will receive standard preoperative physiotherapy education in addition to a Fitbit Inspire 3 wearable device. The device was selected based on its user-friendly interface, long battery life, and validated accuracy in measuring key physiological parameters such as heart rate and physical activity. It is also compatible with widely used health platforms, enabling seamless integration with remote monitoring systems. This made it particularly suitable for an elderly population requiring ease of use and reliable feedback for tele-supervised exercise programmes. The device will track vital signs such as heart rate, physical activity and sleep patterns.

The personalised exercise regimen for the swSEP group will consist of three core components: (1) aerobic exercises (eg, walking or cycling) at ≥40% of heart rate reserve to improve cardiorespiratory endurance; (2) strength training involving full-body functional movements (eg, sit-to-stand, resistance band exercises) to build lower limb and core muscle strength and (3) flexibility exercises targeting the chest, arms, legs and trunk. While flexibility exercises are not expected to directly improve 6MWT outcomes, they are included to promote mobility, support warm-up and cool-down routines and minimise musculoskeletal strain, particularly in an older, frail population. The primary aim remains to improve functional capacity as assessed by the 6MWT through aerobic and resistance training.

The participants’ progress will be monitored through vital sign responses, including step count, workout summaries and heart rate. The physiotherapists will review the exercise regimen two times a week and make adjustments as necessary based on the participant’s progress and physical response. Participants with an MIP <100 cmH₂O will be prescribed an IMT, specifically the POWERbreathe Plus Classic (POWERbreathe International, UK), a threshold loading IMT device. Training involves 30 breaths, two times per day, at 30% of the participant’s MIP, progressively increased based on tolerance and reassessment. A physiotherapist provides in-person training on the first visit, including hands-on demonstration and supervised practice.

Participants will be provided with a portable pedal exerciser (Mini Pedal Exerciser, Model PED1001, Vive Health, USA), a compact, floor-based cycling device with adjustable resistance, facilitating lower limb mobility and aerobic exercise in a home setting. To ensure safety, both participants and their caregivers will receive a comprehensive safety briefing during the initial visit, and a 24-hour emergency hotline will be available for any adverse incidents during exercise. The prescribed pedometer will also include a non-slip base to minimise the risk of falls.

Adherence to the exercise protocol will be monitored differently in each group. In the intervention group (swSEP), adherence will be objectively assessed using data retrieved from the Fitbit Inspire 3, including step count, duration and frequency of logged exercise sessions and heart rate data. Additionally, participation in scheduled tele-supervised physiotherapy sessions (target: ≥2 per week) will be tracked. In the control group (uSEP), weekly phone calls from the research team will serve to verify self-reported data and address barriers to adherence. Adherence rates will be calculated as a percentage of prescribed sessions completed and will be reported in the final results.

Qualitative interview

The qualitative component of this study will employ a semistructured interview design to explore the barriers to home-based prehabilitation from the perspectives of both patients and healthcare providers. The aim is to understand challenges such as the limited ability to prescribe intensive physiotherapy sessions due to safety concerns. The study will involve participants who have undergone home-based prehabilitation with wearable technology as well as healthcare providers involved in the design, implementation or supervision of such programmes. A purposive sampling method will be used to ensure a diverse range of perspectives, with 20–30 participants recruited, or until data saturation is achieved. Semistructured interviews will focus on personal experiences with home-based prehabilitation, perceived barriers to using wearable technology and the challenges faced during the rehabilitation process.

Participants will also provide suggestions for improving the usability and effectiveness of wearable technology in these programmes. Interviews will further capture healthcare providers’ perspectives on prescribing and supervising home-based prehabilitation with wearable devices. Each interview is expected to last 30–60 min and will be audio-recorded with the participants’ consent, followed by transcription and anonymisation of the recordings. Thematic analysis will be conducted using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase framework.11 The qualitative questionnaire for the participants and healthcare providers is found in online supplemental material.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study will be the change in physical function from baseline to 1 to 3 presurgery, measured by the 6MWT.12

Secondary outcomes include (1) 30sSTS and HGS as complementary functional assessments; (2) incidence of key postoperative complications as per American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) criteria; (3) hospital length of stay (LOS) and readmission rates and (4) HRQoL measured using EQ-5D-5L. Feasibility outcomes include adherence rates to the exercise regimen, session attendance and self-reported compliance.

Postoperative complications will be collected and categorised according to the ACS NSQIP criteria.13 Specific complications of interest include surgical site infections (superficial, deep or organ-space), pneumonia, urinary tract infection, sepsis, venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism), acute kidney injury, delirium, unplanned ICU admission, myocardial infarction and 30-day readmission or reoperation. All complications will be captured through chart review and clinical follow-up, and their incidence will be reported per group. HRQoL will be evaluated using the EQ-5D-5L questionnaires before and 8 weeks after surgery.14

In addition, safety data regarding participants’ home environment and any complications experienced during the home exercise programme will be collected throughout the study period. A concurrent qualitative study will also be conducted to gather more in-depth insights (see online supplemental qualitative interview subsection).

Data collection methods

Data collection for this study will occur at several time points throughout the perioperative period. During the initial visit to the PEC, participants will undergo baseline functional assessments, including the 6MWT, HGS, 30sSTS and MIP. These functional assessments will be repeated 1–3 days before the scheduled surgery, with a clinical research assistant conducting the evaluations at the participants’ homes. In addition to the functional assessments, other evaluations will include nutritional status using the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST), HRQoL assessment using the EQ-5D-5L as well as the collection of participants’ previous medical history and operative details.

A designated research assistant will review each participant’s clinical records every other day during their hospital stay to document postoperative complications. This monitoring will continue until the patient is discharged or up to 30 days postoperatively. EQ-5D-5L questionnaires will be conducted over the phone 8 weeks after the operation. Key data collected will include hospital LOS, admissions to the ICU, postoperative complications and mortality. Additionally, intraoperative variables such as blood product utilisation, mechanical ventilation and anaesthetic parameters will be extracted from electronic and paper medical records.

For participants who decline or withdraw from the study after randomisation, demographic information and reasons for withdrawal will be recorded. This data will be used to evaluate the feasibility and acceptance of the intervention as well as to identify potential systematic differences between participants who completed the study and those who withdrew.

Harms

Data and safety monitoring will be overseen by the principal investigator (PI) and coinvestigators. These processes are also subjected to audits and monitoring by the Division of Research at Singapore General Hospital (SGH) and the Centralized Institutional Review Board (CIRB). Monthly reviews of adverse events and participant dropouts will be conducted to ensure ongoing safety.

The management of adverse events will follow SingHealth CIRB guidelines. Any adverse events will be reported by the PI to the CIRB within the required timeframe. The PI will also be responsible for notifying the institutional representative and the study sponsor. Due to the anticipated low-risk nature of the intervention, an independent Data Safety and Monitoring Board has not been established for this study. Instead, data safety and monitoring will be conducted by the study team and reported to the CIRB and the study sponsor.

Interim analyses are planned to be conducted once 50% of the target participants have been recruited. The study team will also maintain continuous monitoring and safety reporting throughout the trial, adhering to institutional guidelines to safeguard participant well-being and data quality.

Participant timeline

Participants will be enrolled following screening and consent at least 2 weeks prior to their scheduled elective surgery. The baseline visit will occur during the participant’s appointment at the PEC, during which informed consent will be obtained and baseline assessments performed. These assessments include the 6MWT, 30sSTS, HGS, MIP and the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire. Demographic and clinical information, including comorbidities and nutritional status, will also be collected.

Following enrolment, participants will be randomised 1:1 to the swSEP or uSEP group. The intervention period will begin immediately after randomisation and continue for approximately 2–3 weeks until the scheduled date of surgery. During this period, participants in the swSEP group will undergo tele-supervised prehabilitation supported by SWDs, while the uSEP group will follow an unsupervised home exercise programme. Adherence and safety will be monitored throughout this period through teleconsultations (swSEP) or weekly calls (uSEP).

A second assessment will be conducted 1 to 3 days prior to surgery at the participant’s home or clinic visit. This will include repeat measurements of the 6MWT, 30sSTS and HGS. Postoperative complications will be monitored during the hospital stay and up to 30 days postsurgery through daily reviews of medical records and follow-up contacts. Hospital length of stay, ICU admissions and key clinical outcomes will be documented. Finally, participants will be contacted at 8 weeks postoperatively to complete a follow-up EQ-5D-5L questionnaire via telephone (figure 1).

Sample size

The sample size calculation was performed to ensure sufficient statistical power to detect a 25 m difference in the mean 6MWT between the swSEP (intervention) and uSEP (control) groups.15 16 A two-tailed independent samples t-test was employed with a power of 0.8 and an alpha level of 0.05. Based on an assumed common SD of 55 m for the 6MWT, and accounting for an anticipated attrition rate of 20%, the minimum required sample size was determined to be 190 participants, with 95 participants allocated to each group.

Recruitment

Potential participants will be identified from the appointment list of patients attending the PEC at SGH. on registration, patients≥65 years old who are scheduled for elective surgery will be approached and assessed for frailty using the EFS.17 Patients with an EFS of 6 or higher going for major, non-cardiac surgery will be invited to participate in the study. Written informed consent will be obtained to enrol in the study.

Randomisation: sequence generation, allocation concealment mechanism and implementation

Participants will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either the swSEP group (intervention) or uSEP group (control) with minimisation randomisation. Stratification will be based on baseline physical function, determined by the 6MWT (with a cut-off <350 m or ≥350 m), and surgical risk, categorised as moderate or high. Additionally, stratification will consider the involvement of physiotherapists. Four physiotherapists at SGH will deliver the PREPARE-HOME service, ensuring standardised implementation of the intervention across both study groups.

Blinding

The investigators will not be blinded, as they will be responsible for prescribing the intervention bundle to participants in the intervention arm. However, hospital staff, including nurses and surgeons, will be blinded to the allocation of study arms. It is acknowledged that maintaining full blinding for the treating team in the postoperative period may be challenging, as study details could potentially be disclosed by participants who are not blinded. Efforts will be made to minimise such disclosures to preserve blinding where possible.

Data management

Fitbit-derived data (eg, heart rate, step count, activity duration) will be retrieved using the secure Fitbit Web API with individual participant authorisation. These data will be deidentified on export and stored in encrypted format on a secure, internet-isolated institutional server within Singapore General Hospital. Only authorised study personnel will have access to the decryption key. All data handling complies with institutional data governance policies and the SingHealth Data Protection and Cybersecurity standards. Raw data from Fitbit’s cloud platform will not be retained beyond authorised use windows.

Participant records, including CRFs, source documentation (containing evidence of study eligibility, medical history, physical findings, laboratory results and consultations), and all Institutional Review Board (IRB) and regulatory documentation will be retained by the PI. These records will be available for inspection and copying by authorised authorities. In compliance with SingHealth IRB policy, all research data will be stored securely in the Department of Anaesthesiology at Singapore General Hospital for 6 years before being securely destroyed.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis for this study will begin with the primary outcome, which is the change in physical function between the swSEP and uSEP groups, assessed through the 6MWT, 10sSTS and HGS. This difference will be analysed using an independent t-test, or a Mann-Whitney U test if the data are not normally distributed. Baseline-adjusted comparisons will be performed using analysis of covariance to account for initial differences in physical function. For secondary outcomes, hospital length of stay will be compared between groups using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, with a log-rank test applied to assess statistical significance. A Cox proportional hazards regression model will be used to further evaluate the effect of the intervention on length of stay, adjusting for covariates such as baseline frailty score, type of surgery and surgical discipline.

Postoperative complications will be analysed as categorical variables, utilising the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. The group comparisons for postoperative complications will be made through logistic regression to estimate OR with 95% CIs. HRQoL will be analysed using repeated-measures ANOVA. Mixed-effects models or machine learning techniques will be employed to explore the predictive value of HRV in identifying responders to prehabilitation. A post hoc analysis stratified by baseline nutritional status, as determined by the MUST, will also be performed.

Handling of missing data

All efforts will be made to minimise missing data through rigorous follow-up and participant engagement, including reminder calls, home visits and teleconsultation support. However, we anticipate some degree of attrition due to the frailty of the study population and the perioperative context.

For the primary outcome (change in 6MWT distance), missing data will be handled using multiple imputation by chained equations, assuming data are missing at random. The imputation model will include baseline covariates such as age, frailty score, surgical risk and baseline 6MWT values. Sensitivity analyses using complete-case analysis and worst-case imputation will be performed to assess the robustness of the results.

For secondary outcomes, similar imputation methods will be applied where appropriate. Time-to-event data (eg, length of stay, readmissions) will be analysed using survival analysis techniques that account for censoring.

Participants who withdraw after randomisation will be included in the intention-to-treat analysis where possible, using imputed or last-observation-carried-forward data depending on the nature of the variable. A per-protocol analysis will also be conducted as a secondary approach to examine the intervention effect among those with high adherence.

Data monitoring committee

Given the nature of this single-site, non-pharmacological feasibility trial involving a low-risk behavioural intervention, a formal data monitoring committee will not be established. Oversight of study conduct, data quality and participant safety will be provided by the trial steering group, which includes the PI, coinvestigators and study coordinator. The steering group will meet monthly to review recruitment progress, adherence, protocol deviations and any reported adverse events.

No interim analyses are planned, and there are no predefined stopping rules. The trial will proceed to completion unless significant safety concerns arise, in which case the sponsor and PI will review the situation and determine whether modifications or early termination are necessary.

Trial monitoring

The PI and coinvestigators will monitor and review all data collected throughout the study to ensure accuracy and completeness. Training will be provided to the research coordinator, and all case report forms (CRF) will be verified by a second member of the study team to ensure accurate data entry. This process will ensure quality control and quality assurance of the study data.

Ethics and dissemination

Research ethics approval

This study will be conducted in accordance with the Singapore Good Clinical Practice guidelines, which are based on the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study has been approved by the Singapore General Hospital Institutional Review Board (SGH IRB) (CIRB Ref: 2024/2242) and is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06633614).

Dissemination policy

The results of the study will be presented at international conferences and submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals. Additionally, the data collected will be made publicly available in a data repository.

Protocol amendments

In the event of significant protocol modifications, all investigators, the SGH IRB and trial participants will be promptly notified.

Consent or assent

Eligible participants will be approached by a research assistant during their visit to the PEC. The participants will be provided with an explanation of the study, a patient information sheet and a consent form. They will be given sufficient time to consider participation and will have the opportunity to ask any questions. If a participant expresses interest in the study, written informed consent will be obtained. The consent forms will be provided in English, but for participants from non-English-speaking backgrounds, a translator will be available. For illiterate participants, a family member will be approached to witness and verify the consent process, ensuring ethical standards are upheld throughout the study.

Confidentiality

Confidentiality of participant data will be strictly maintained throughout the study. Each participant will be assigned a unique study-related identification number, which will be used on all study documents, including the CRF. Personal details for each participant will be securely stored in an access-controlled, locked cabinet at the investigation centre. The deidentified study identification number will be used on all research documents to maintain anonymity.

Ancillary and post-trial care

Given the low-risk, non-invasive nature of the intervention, no ancillary or post-trial care is planned. Participants will continue to receive standard perioperative care through their clinical teams. No compensation will be provided for trial-related harm, as the risk of serious adverse events is minimal and limited to routine prehabilitation activity.

Trial registration

This trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov under the identifier NCT06633614. The registration was completed on 9 October 2024.

Protocol and statistical analysis plan

The full trial protocol, including all Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials checklist items, will be published as a protocol paper in BMJ Open. Upon publication, it will also be accessible through the ClinicalTrials.gov registry (Identifier: NCT06633614) and institutional repositories upon request.

Data sharing

Deidentified individual participant data, including the trial dataset and accompanying data dictionary, will be made available upon reasonable request following publication of the primary results. Statistical code used for the main analyses will also be shared.

Access will be granted to qualified researchers with a methodologically sound proposal, subject to approval by the trial steering committee. Requests should be directed to the corresponding author via institutional email. Data will be transferred via secure file-sharing platforms in compliance with SingHealth’s data governance and cybersecurity protocols.

No additional consent for data sharing will be obtained from participants, as data will be fully anonymised prior to sharing.

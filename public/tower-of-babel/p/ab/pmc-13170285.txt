Adaptive enhancement of chest X-ray images using tissue attenuation and local and global fusion
Authors: Zhen Zhao, Rui Tang, Qifeng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChest X-ray images often suffer from low contrast, noise, and loss of local details, which can hinder the accurate identification of bones and soft tissues and affect subsequent automated diagnosis and clinical interpretation. To address these challenges, recent methods have explored both traditional contrast enhancement and deep learning-based strategies. However, most existing methods focus on a single degradation factor, such as contrast enhancement, without explicitly considering region-specific visibility or structure-aware enhancement. In this work, we propose an adaptive enhancement approach for chest X-ray images. Our framework integrates tissue attenuation visibility enhancement, linear transformation-based contrast adjustment, and a perceptual fusion module to emphasize anatomical features, local details, and global brightness. This design aims to suppress soft-tissue interference, improve visibility of bones and improve the visualization of essential anatomical components, while maintaining consistent image quality across regions with varying exposure. Importantly, our approach does not require extensive training and is interpretable. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the method improves image contrast, sharpness, and visibility of anatomical structures, showing promising results compared with 12 existing traditional and deep learning-based enhancement methods.Keywords: Chest X-ray image enhancement, Contrast enhancement, Hybrid fusion algorithm, Image fusion, Visibility enhancement

Introduction

During acquisition, chest X-ray images are often affected by noise [1], low contrast [2], underexposure, or overexposure [3], leading to image degradation. Such degradation not only blurs the boundaries of bones and tissues and causes the loss of fine details, but also makes it difficult to identify critical anatomical information, thereby significantly affecting subsequent high-level vision tasks such as automated diagnosis [4, 5], lesion detection [6], image segmentation [7], and precise measurement [8]. In addition, degraded images increase the reading burden for radiologists, reducing diagnostic efficiency and accuracy. To address this issue, chest X-ray image enhancement methods need to improve image contrast, enhance details, and restore visibility, effectively recovering the structural information of bones and tissues. This not only provides clinicians with clearer and more intuitive visualizations, but also facilitates computer-aided analysis and supports more accurate and efficient clinical interpretation.

Numerous traditional image enhancement methods have been proposed for X-ray image enhancement. Although these approaches can improve image contrast and visibility to some extent, they often suffer from noise amplification, loss of local details, or insufficient adaptability to different anatomical regions. First, some methods enhance image brightness and overall contrast through global or local contrast adjustment, but they struggle to simultaneously preserve details of both bones and soft tissues [9]. Second, tissue-feature-based enhancement methods can highlight bone structures, yet they remain limited in suppressing noise and handling regions with uneven exposure. For example, Liu et al. [10] proposed a method that can enhance the contrast and sharpness of X-ray image. However, this method simultaneously amplify noise, resulting in some artifacts and local noise in weak-signal regions, which can compromise the accurate visualization of bone and soft tissue structures.

Recently, several advances in chest X-ray image enhancement have been driven by deep learning techniques [11–13]. However, these methods often struggle to handle low contrast, noise, and local detail loss simultaneously. They typically rely heavily on specific training datasets, which can limit their generalization to X-ray images with different exposure conditions or anatomical regions. Furthermore, the lack of interpretability in these models makes it difficult to ensure that enhanced images preserve clinically important structures, such as bones and soft tissues. As a result, purely deep learning-based approaches may not always provide reliable or consistent improvements for clinical applications.

In this work, we propose an adaptive integrated enhancement method to simultaneously improve the visibility of bones and soft tissues in chest X-ray images. Unlike previous approaches that focus on a single enhancement strategy, the proposed framework integrates tissue attenuation visibility enhancement, linear transformation-based adaptive contrast enhancement, and a perceptual fusion module. The design of these modules is motivated by the attenuation characteristics of chest X-ray imaging and perceptual considerations, allowing the framework to jointly enhance local structural details, global luminance distribution, and anatomical visibility. Through this coordinated design, the proposed framework addresses the limitations of conventional enhancement methods in noise suppression, preservation of local details, and adaptability to varying exposure conditions. Related work

Traditional medical image enhancement methods

In medical imaging, especially for X-ray enhancement, traditional methods have been widely used to address various image quality challenges. A variety of techniques have been developed to manage problems such as overexposure, hazy appearance from dense tissues, uneven brightness, low contrast, and the need to preserve natural colors and fine details. For example, Mamatov et al. [14] proposed a statistical-based contrast enhancement algorithm, which effectively improved the visibility of X-ray images and solved the problem of uneven illumination and loss of details in low-contrast regions. Gangwar et al. [15] proposed a novel chest X-ray image enhancement technique to address the issues of uneven illumination and over-enhancement, which divides images into underexposed and overexposed regions and applies nonlinear histogram transformations with distinct intensity expansion and mapping directions for each region, resulting in improved image quality, naturalness, and detail preservation.

To solve the problem of unclear visualization of bone structures and soft tissues in X-ray images, Kushol et al. [16] proposed an efficient contrast enhancement method using morphological operators, which applies Top-hat and Bottom-hat transforms and calculates gradient magnitude to automatically select the structuring element size, effectively improving the clarity of important anatomical details and demonstrating better results compared to some existing image enhancement techniques. To solve the problem of insufficient contrast in daily radiation therapy X-ray images, Qiu et al. [17] proposed an automatic and user-independent contrast enhancement method, which applies a noise reduction filter, a high-pass filter, and CLAHE with parameters optimized per treatment site using an interior-point constrained optimization algorithm, effectively improving tissue contrast and achieving higher evaluation scores compared to standard window-level adjustment and CLAHE methods. Ou et al. [18] proposed a brightness correction method based on gradient interval histogram to solve the problem of errors in protein spot quantification in 2DE images, which uses GIH equalization to enhance weak protein spot edges and GIH matching to eliminate overall brightness shifts, achieving an average quantification error of less than 3% and outperforming existing methods. To solve the problem of insufficient contrast in dental X-ray images, Qassim et al. [19] proposed an image enhancement approach using histogram equalization, log transform, gamma correction, and CLAHE, which preserves image brightness while improving contrast, with CLAHE providing the best results based on entropy, histogram flatness, and visual inspection.

However, traditional image enhancement methods face multiple challenges. They often compromise between amplifying fine details and reducing noise, leading to inconsistent quality improvement across different regions. For example, Liu et al. [10] proposed a novel shoulder X-ray image enhancement method that integrates automatic tissue attenuation and an improved Type-II fuzzy set algorithm, which effectively solved the problems of low contrast and insufficient sharpness in shoulder X-ray images, however, this approach may lead to over-enhancement, producing excessive noise and artifacts in the resulting images. These approaches usually require manual tuning, which is time-consuming and may produce variable results. Moreover, they struggle to handle the complexity of medical images, which contain a wide range of contrasts and diagnostically important details. Their uniform strategies often overlook subtle tissue differences, risking the loss of critical information. These issues highlight the need for more flexible and intelligent techniques tailored to the specific demands of X-ray image enhancement.

Deep learning medical image enhancement methods

Recently, deep learning-based medical image enhancement methods have made significant progress. These approaches can automatically learn complex patterns and features from large-scale datasets to achieve adaptive enhancement, effectively preserving subtle anatomical structures. They are capable of handling variations in contrast, noise, and illumination across different regions of an image, providing more uniform and natural enhancement results. For example, to improve deep neural network performance in medical image classification, Mall et al. [20] proposed BoostNet, which integrates image enhancement techniques including CLAHE, high-frequency filtering, and unsharp masking with the benchmark model. Experiments on the Musculoskeletal Radiograph Bone Classification dataset demonstrated that BoostNet significantly improved classification accuracy, showing that combining image enhancement with deep learning can produce a more accurate and efficient classification model. To improve tuberculosis detection in low-contrast chest X-ray images, Munadi et al. [21] evaluated the impact of image enhancement on deep learning performance by applying Unsharp Masking, High-Frequency Emphasis Filtering, and CLAHE before training pre-trained ResNet and EfficientNet models. Using the publicly available Shenzhen dataset, the enhanced images significantly improved classification accuracy and AUC, demonstrating that image enhancement can effectively boost deep learning–based TB diagnosis. Anand et al. [11] proposed a deep learning-based approach to enhance chest X-ray images with low contrast, aiming to improve diagnostic accuracy. Their method extracts and refines contrast information through multiple processing stages before integrating it into a neural network to generate clearer and more detailed images. Experimental results on several public datasets demonstrated that the enhanced images significantly improve both visual quality and diagnostic performance. Sumida et al. [22] proposed a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model to generate non-contrast CT images from contrast-enhanced scans, reducing the need for contrast agents in imaging. The method was trained and tested on CT data from 29 patients and compared with a U-Net model. Experimental results showed that the proposed CNN effectively suppressed contrast-enhanced regions and produced images closely matching true non-contrast CT scans, outperforming the U-Net in maintaining image consistency. Bhutto et al. [23] proposed an improved medical image fusion method that enhances diagnostic image clarity by addressing issues such as uneven brightness, noise, and low contrast. The approach combines morphological preprocessing with a feature extraction and fusion strategy to better preserve important details and textures. Experimental and expert evaluations confirmed that this method produces clearer, more detailed, and less noisy fused images compared with existing techniques.

However, deep learning-based medical image contrast enhancement methods face key challenges such as poor interpretability and limited data availability. Their “black box” nature makes it difficult to explain enhancement decisions, reducing clinical trust. In addition, the scarcity of high-quality annotated medical images restricts model generalization and reliability across different imaging conditions. For example, to address the issue of image quality degradation caused by noise in medical images, Gavini et al. [24] proposed a deep learning-based denoising and segmentation method that effectively reduces noise and improves image clarity. However, this approach still has limitations, such as high model complexity and computational cost. Moreover, it may lose fine details during the denoising process, affecting diagnostic accuracy, and its generalization performance across different devices and imaging conditions remains insufficient. Huang et al. [25] proposed a two-stage residual convolutional neural network (TS-RCNN) to denoise low-dose CT images, aiming to reduce radiation exposure while preserving image quality. The first stage focuses on texture denoising using wavelet transform and perceptual loss, and the second stage enhances structural information. Experiments on simulated datasets and the Low Dose CT Grand Challenge dataset showed that TS-RCNN effectively preserves textures and improves structural clarity, outperforming other methods in quantitative metrics. However, the method has some limitations. The two-stage network increases model complexity and computational cost, and the reliance on simulated low-dose images may limit its generalization to real-world clinical scenarios. Additionally, fine details may still be partially lost during the denoising process.

Although previous studies have made certain progress in enhancing the contrast of chest X-ray images, suppressing noise, and improving perceptual quality, several limitations remain. Most traditional methods focus on a single degradation factor and often fail to effectively preserve fine structural details. Some existing enhancement approaches can improve the visibility of local features, but they generally lack interpretability or do not fully consider region-specific anatomical information. In addition, these methods rarely achieve a balance between soft-tissue visibility and bone structure clarity, which limits their applicability under varying imaging conditions. Taken together, the gaps in current methods motivate the development of a unified framework capable of improving structural visibility and balancing soft-tissue and bone enhancement in chest X-ray images.

Proposed method

To address the common issues in chest X-ray imaging, such as low visibility, insufficient contrast, amplified noise, and blurred structural details, we propose an adaptive integrated enhancement framework that simultaneously improves the visibility of bony structures and soft tissues. The framework first employs a tissue-attenuation mechanism, which uses local statistical features to adaptively suppress soft-tissue background while highlighting high-attenuation structures, thereby enhancing overall structural contrast. It then incorporates a linear transformation strategy, where local contrast stretching, global contrast enhancement, and logarithmic image processing are combined to further improve brightness levels and fine structural details. Finally, a perception-guided fusion mechanism is introduced to dynamically balance the contributions of the two enhanced results based on pixel intensity and global gradient information, enabling the system to adapt to different anatomical regions and exposure conditions while effectively suppressing noise and artifacts. Experiments

In this section, we provide a detailed description of the experimental setup. Our method is rigorously validated using the JSRT [26] dataset, with a focus on five no-reference image quality evaluation metrics. The performance of our approach is compared against 10 traditional and two deep learning-based X-ray image enhancement methods. Additionally, we conduct ablation experiments and generalization tests to further assess the effectiveness of our method. Below, we outline the JSRT dataset used for no-reference image quality evaluation, the comparative methods selected for analysis, and the image quality metrics used in the assessment.

Experimental configuration

Our implementation was done in MATLAB 2022a and PyTorch. All experiments were run on a system with an Intel Core i7 CPU at 4.8 GHz, 16 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU. We believe that a sufficiently high-performance hardware setup is essential to obtain reliable results.

Dataset

The dataset we used is the JSRT dataset, which consists of 247 chest X-ray images, including both X-rays with lung nodules and those without. These images have been annotated by professional radiologists to ensure their accuracy and high quality. The JSRT dataset has wide applications in digital medical imaging, particularly for image enhancement, anomaly detection, and other medical image processing research. The images in this dataset have high resolution and cover a range of lung nodule subtleties, providing a rich sample and diverse scenarios for our image enhancement experiments.

The choice of the JSRT dataset for X-ray image enhancement research is primarily based on its precise annotations and image quality. The diversity of the dataset and the varying levels of subtlety in the lung nodules make it an ideal platform for testing the effectiveness of image enhancement algorithms. Particularly, it allows for the evaluation of the robustness and adaptability of methods in handling nodules of different levels of subtlety. Furthermore, the dataset's public availability and wide usage have provided valuable references for researchers worldwide, further promoting the advancement of medical image processing technologies. In our experiments, we used all the chest X-ray images from the JSRT dataset, which cover different levels of subtlety in lung nodules. Our goal is to assess the performance of the proposed image enhancement method and improve the accuracy of nodule detection and the visualization quality of X-ray images.

Compared methods

In our comparative experiments, we used the JSRT dataset as a benchmark to thoroughly evaluate 10 traditional and two deep learning-based X-ray image enhancement techniques. These methods, representing a range of approaches in medical image enhancement, include FCCE [27], LCACE [28], HLPSCS [29], ATF [30], AGCWD [31], NBCE [32], SCLE [33], RCE [34], ABCETP [35], SLIP [36], DPM [37] and DAMIE [38]. The aim of this comparison was to conduct an in-depth analysis of the effectiveness of these methods in enhancing chest X-ray image quality, providing valuable insights and assessments for the advancement of medical image processing techniques.

No-reference image quality assessment metrics

No-reference image quality assessment involves evaluating the quality of an image without the need for a reference image. This approach allows for the prediction or measurement of image quality even when there is no original image to compare it to. In the case of chest X-ray images, obtaining paired high-quality images with the same perspective or from the same patient can be difficult, due to the challenges in acquiring consistent images during the medical imaging process. As a result, no-reference image quality assessment becomes particularly crucial, as it can accurately evaluate important quality aspects such as clarity, sharpness, and detail preservation without relying on a reference image. In this paper, we use the following no-reference image quality assessment metrics as the basis including IE [39], FADE [40], NIQE [41], BIQME [42] and CEIQ [43]. Information Entropy (IE) assesses image quality by measuring the amount of information, uncertainty, and complexity present in the image. A higher IE value signifies that the image contains more details and information, thereby indicating superior image quality. The Fog Aware Density Evaluator (FADE) is designed to evaluate the clarity and visibility of images. Lower FADE scores indicate a lower haze density, which corresponds to clearer images. The Natural Image Quality Evaluator (NIQE) is a no-reference image quality metric that uses a statistical model to assess image quality. It evaluates the naturalness and statistical characteristics of the image, with a lower NIQE value indicating higher image quality. The Blind Image Quality Model Evaluator (BIQME) provides a comprehensive assessment of image quality by considering factors like sharpness, detail retention, and distortion. A higher BIQME score reflects superior image quality. Contrast Enhancement-based Image Quality (CEIQ) evaluates image quality by focusing on contrast enhancement, detail retention, and visual clarity. A higher CEIQ score generally indicates improved contrast, better preservation of details, and overall superior image quality.

Ablation study

We first conducted qualitative ablation experiments. As shown in Fig. 8, “input” refers to the original images, which are generally blurry and exhibit low contrast, with key bones and tissue regions not sufficiently highlighted, potentially limiting precise image analysis. “w/o LTACE” denotes the method without the Linear Transformation Adaptive Contrast Enhancement (LTACE) module. Compared to the complete model, images without LTACE show noticeably reduced distinction between bones and tissues, with weaker detail representation. “w/o TACE” refers to the method without the Tissue Attenuation Contrast Enhancement (TACE) module. Removing this module leads to improved contrast and clearer details, making bone information and key tissues more discernible, though the overall clarity and detail still fall slightly short of the complete model. “w/o PFE” represents the results without the Perceptual Fusion Enhancement (PFE) module. Without this module, the enhanced images exhibit overexposure and significantly lower contrast compared to the complete method. Finally, the “complete model” demonstrates the final performance of the proposed method, where both bones and key tissue details are comprehensively enhanced, and the overall image clarity and contrast are significantly improved, facilitating better detail recognition—a factor especially important for chest X-ray analysis.

Comparative visualization results, input low-contrast images; “w/o LTACE” results with reduced local contrast enhancement; “w/o TACE” outcomes with less pronounced bone structure; “w/o PFE” results with weaker perceptual fusion; “Full model” enhancements showing clear improvements in both contrast and structural details

Table 8 presents the quantitative results of ablation experiments on the JSRT dataset. By removing key modules, we compared different model configurations across five no-reference image quality evaluation metrics to reveal the contribution of each module to overall model performance. Specifically, “w/o LTACE” indicates the effect of removing the LTACE module, “w/o TACE” shows the performance without the TACE module, and “w/o PFE” shows the results without the PFE module. As shown in the table, removing the LTACE module leads to decreases in IE, FADE, and CEIQ, indicating its significant influence on image naturalness and noise suppression. Removing the TACE module results in lower IE, FADE, and CEIQ compared to the complete model, highlighting its important role in enhancing bone and tissue details. When the PFE module is removed, all metrics drop, with IE and CEIQ decreasing to 5.0860 and 2.8234, respectively, and FADE and NIQE also deteriorating noticeably, demonstrating PFE’s critical role in overall contrast enhancement and detail preservation. Overall, the complete model achieves the best or near-best performance across all five metrics, fully validating the effectiveness and complementarity of each module.

Generalization test

To evaluate the generalization capability of the proposed X-ray image enhancement algorithm, we tested its robustness across different types of X-ray images, extending beyond chest X-rays to include images of other anatomical regions. This assessment is crucial for verifying the algorithm’s applicability and reliability in real-world clinical scenarios, where X-ray images from various body parts are frequently analyzed. For this purpose, we randomly selected images of fingers, wrists, humerus, and forearms from the MURA dataset and applied the proposed enhancement method to these images to examine its effectiveness across multiple anatomical regions. As shown in Fig. 9, the top row presents the original X-ray images, while the bottom row shows the enhanced images. The results demonstrate that the method significantly improves the visibility of key bone structures and enhances both contrast and clarity, performing well even in anatomically complex regions, thereby illustrating its ability to provide stable and consistent enhancement across diverse X-ray imaging scenarios.

Discussion

In this study, we presented an adaptive enhancement approach for chest X-ray images that integrates tissue attenuation contrast enhancement, linear transformation–based local and global fusion, and a perceptual fusion strategy. The method aims to address common challenges in chest radiography, including low contrast, noise interference, and loss of fine anatomical details. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach effectively improves the visibility of bones and soft tissues while maintaining stable performance across images with different exposure conditions. By balancing local detail enhancement with global luminance adjustment, the method provides clearer structural representation and reduces the visual ambiguity commonly seen in degraded X-ray images. Compared with several traditional enhancement methods on the JSRT dataset, our approach improves the visibility of fine structures, particularly in low-contrast regions such as rib edges and subtle tissue patterns. This enhanced visibility may facilitate clinical tasks such as fracture detection, subtle nodule identification, and evaluation of soft-tissue structures, supporting both manual interpretation by radiologists and automated analysis pipelines.

Despite its strengths, several limitations and potential risks remain. Over-enhancement in certain regions may amplify artificial edges or highlight noise, which could lead to false positives or misinterpretation. The enhancement performance may also degrade on extremely underexposed or heavily noise-corrupted images, where structural information is severely lost. Additionally, the perceptual fusion module introduces computational overhead, which may limit applicability in real-time or resource-constrained environments. Although the method is interpretable and training-free, it still relies on manually selected parameters, which may require adjustment when applied to images from different devices or acquisition settings. Future work will focus on fully adaptive parameter selection and efficiency improvements to support downstream tasks such as fracture detection, nodule identification, and soft-tissue structure analysis.

Conclusion

In this work, we presented an adaptive enhancement framework for chest X-ray images that combines tissue attenuation enhancement with local and global fusion to improve the visibility of bones and soft tissues. Experiments on the JSRT dataset indicate that the method can improve image contrast and structural clarity in comparison with selected traditional enhancement and deep learning approaches. The enhancement is particularly noticeable in low-contrast regions, where subtle anatomical features become more distinguishable. The framework is interpretable and does not require extensive training, making it suitable for scenarios with limited annotated data. Limitations include the need for parameter tuning and additional computational cost for high-resolution images. Future work will focus on improving efficiency and exploring automated parameter adaptation to facilitate broader clinical applicability.

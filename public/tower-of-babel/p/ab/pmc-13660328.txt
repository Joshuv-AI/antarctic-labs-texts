Shikonin-functionalized magnetic nanoparticles mediated magnetothermodynamic therapy with synergistic antibacterial efficacy and immunomodulation for accelerated infected wound healing
Authors: Jiajun Li, Ning Jiang, Yuqing Miao, Fayi Nie, Jingdu Yang, Xiaohu Mi, Simeng Xue, Yuwei Wu, Tingbin Zhang, Yingxi Duanmiao, Qi Liu, Haiming Fan, Xiaohang Yang, Haifa Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInfected skin wounds healing remains a great clinical challenge due to the complexity of the wound healing process, involving infection control and subsequent immunomodulation. Magnetothermodynamic (MTD) therapy, combining the magnetothermal effect and thermal-amplified Fenton reactivity, is a promising strategy for combating bacterial infections. However, its therapeutic efficacy in wound healing is compromised due to its insufficient ability to address inflammation. Herein, we develop a multifunctional polyvinyl alcohol/gelatin composite hydrogel (MagSK hydrogel) incorporating shikonin-functionalized magnetic nanoparticles (MagSK NPs) to achieve microenvironment-responsive and programmed treatment of infected wounds. During infectious period, MagSK NPs mediated MTD effect can effectively eradicate bacteria and destroy dense biofilm. In subsequent tissue repair stage, MagSK hydrogel suppress excessive inflammation through scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS), modulates macrophage polarization. Transcriptomic analysis further revealed that the treatment coordinately downregulates inflammatory pathways and upregulates genes critical for tissue proliferation and remodeling. Both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrate that MagSK hydrogel effectively eliminates bacteria, mitigates inflammation, enhanced angiogenesis and markedly accelerated wound healing. Collectively, our findings highlight a novel strategy for infected wound healing via MagSK-mediated magnetothermodynamic therapy with synergistic antibacterial and immunomodulatory effects.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12951-026-04787-3.Keywords: Magnetic nanoparticles, Shikonin, Nanocomposite hydrogel, Magnetothermodynamic therapy, Infected wound

Introduction

Infected wound healing remains a major global public health challenge, imposing substantial medical and economic burdens [1]. Deep tissue injuries are especially perilous, which often progress to chronic non-healing ulcers due to bacterial colonization, excessive inflammation, and dysregulated repair, imposing greater mortality risks [2]. Nevertheless, traditional antibiotic treatments are increasingly plagued by drug resistance due to antibiotic misuse [3]. Therefore, innovative non-antibiotic antimicrobial strategies are urgently needed to effectively combat infections and accelerate wound healing.

In recent years, noninvasive physical therapies, including phototherapy, magnetic stimulation, and ultrasound based treatment, have received increasing attention because of their remote controllability and relatively low risk of inducing bacterial resistance [4–8]. Phototherapy can exert strong antibacterial effects, whereas sonodynamic therapy offers better tissue penetration than light-triggered approaches. In comparison, magnetic nanoparticle-mediated magnetothermodynamic (MTD) therapy represents a particularly attractive strategy. Under an alternating magnetic field (AMF), magnetic nanoparticles can generate localized heat while enhancing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, resulting in synergistic antibacterial activity with deep tissue penetration and favorable biosafety [9]. MTD has already demonstrated remarkable therapeutic efficacy in antitumor therapy, where nanoscale intracellular heating together with Fenton-like ROS generation to selectively ablate tumor cells and modulate the tumor microenvironment [10, 11]. Recently, Wang et al. demonstrated that the same combination of localized heat and amplified ROS generation also holds great promise for bacterial infection treatment, as it can disrupt dense bacterial biofilms and regulate immune responses [12]. Meanwhile, various magneto-responsive biocomposites and physical-field-assisted wound dressings have been reported for infected wound management, mainly focusing on magnetically controlled drug release, antibacterial hyperthermia [13]. Collectively, these studies indicate that MTD therapy may provide an effective platform for accelerating infected wound healing, especially in deep or biofilm-associated wound infections.

Wound healing is a complex pathophysiological process that requires tight orchestration of antibacterial activity and inflammation control, alongside cell migration and angiogenesis [1, 14, 15]. The coordinated regulation of these processes is essential for efficient wound repair. Chinese herbal medicines, characterized by multi-target and multi-pathway synergistic interactions, hold broad prospects for treating complex diseases [16]. Among these, shikonin (SK), a naphthoquinone compound derived from Lithospermum erythrorhizon, has been widely used in management of wounds. It has been reported that SK can modulate fibroblast activity, inhibit pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and stimulate vascular endothelial cell proliferation [17, 18]. Therefore, we hypothesize that such a Magnetic-SK hybrid nanomaterial in combination with MTD therapy capabilities and immunomodulatory effects is expected to show desirable characteristics for efficient wound repair. Besides, hydrogels, characterized by their 3D network structure, good biocompatibility, and ability to maintain a moist pro-healing microenvironment, are ideal candidates for wound dressings [19].

Based on the above rationale, we developed a multifunctional wound dressing (MagSK hydrogel) composed of shikonin-functionalized magnetic nanoparticles (MagSK NPs) embedded within a dynamic polyvinyl alcohol/gelatin (PVA/GA) hydrogel network (Scheme 1). Unlike traditional magnetic dressings relying on single therapeutic modalities, our design integrates AMF-triggered MTD antibacterial therapy, SK mediated immunomodulation, and ROS-responsive hydrogel regulation. This combination enables stage adaptive intervention across both the infection and repair phases of wound healing. During the infection period, MagSK NPs generate heat under AMF while amplifying ROS generation level for efficient bactericidal action. SK modification on Fe3O4 not only confers additional antibacterial effects but also provides extra anti-inflammatory and immunoregulatory activities, particularly by promoting macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory and pro-healing M2 phenotype, which is critical for facilitating the smooth transition from the inflammatory phase to the proliferative phase. Additionally, this hydrogel features dynamic crosslinking via chemical borate ester bonds, which can react with hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), thereby mitigating excessive inflammation in the wound microenvironment [20, 21]. Meanwhile, gelatin’s renowned hemostatic properties further enable dressings to promote blood coagulation, addressing the critical initial step of wound management [22]. Together, these features enable the MagSK hydrogel dressing to achieve comprehensive therapeutic effects, including rapid hemostasis, potent antibacterial action, effective inflammation control and enhanced vascularized tissue regeneration. Upon this design, the immunoregulation hydrogel was demonstrated to significantly promote the regeneration of infected skin defects in infected mouse wound models, offering a promising strategy for the clinical management of infected wounds.

Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization of MagSK NPs

MagSK NPs were prepared through a two-step process involving solvothermal synthesis of magnetic iron oxide nanoclusters (IONCs) followed by dopamine-assisted surface functionalization and SK conjugation (Fig. S1). Magnetic iron oxide nanoclusters obtained by hydrolysis of iron salts in glycol were uniformly dispersed with an average diameter of ~40 nm, as confirmed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Fig. S2a) [23]. Then, the surface of nanoclusters was coated by dopamine (denoted as Mag) via a ligand exchange method (Fig. S3 and S4) which was subsequently conjugated with the SK to obtain the MagSK NPs. TEM images showed that MagSK NPs maintained a uniform cluster morphology with densely packed nanocrystals (Fig. 1a and Fig. S2). The high resolution TEM (HRTEM) image demonstrates the high crystallinity, with a lattice plane spacing of ≈0.297 nm, corresponding to the (220) planes of the spinel Fe3O4 (Fig. 1b), and the X-ray diffraction (XRD) confirm the spinel cubic structure for the MagSK NPs (Fig. 1c, JCPDS card no. 19-0629) [24, 25]. The UV-vis spectra of MagSK NPs displayed the characteristic absorption peak of SK (Fig. 1d), confirming the successful SK-functionalization [26]. Additionally, the increased hydrodynamic size and notable zeta potential changes (Fig. 1e, f) further proved successful surface functionalization. By comparing the thermogravimetric curves of the Mag and MagSK, we can roughly estimate that the percentage of SK was approximately 10% (Fig. 1g). The elemental compositions of MagSK NPs were further analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). As shown in Fig. S5, the survey spectra of MagSK revealed the coexistence of Fe, C, N and O. The high-resolution spectra of the Fe 2p, C 1s, and N 1s are shown in Fig. 1h−k, respectively. In the Fe 2p spectra, the Fe 2p3/2 peak at 723.8 eV and Fe 2p1/2 peak at 710.3 eV can be found, attributed to Fe2+. The peaks at 725.7 eV and 712.5 eV are indexed to Fe3+ [27]. Moreover, the high-resolution C 1s XPS spectra of Mag were deconvoluted into two peaks at 284.8 and 288.5 eV, which were attributed to the bonds C-C and C=O, respectively. The appearance of the C=N peak and the increased peak area of C=O in MagSK indicated that SK was firmly anchored on the surface of Mag through the Schiff-base reaction [28, 29]. Meanwhile, three peaks in O 1s of MagSK at ∼529.9, ∼531.8, and∼533.7 eV could be attributed to Fe-O, C-O, and C=O, respectively [30]. It is worth noting that the C-O, and C=O peaks of MagSK exhibit a negative shift with values of ~531.5, and ~532.9 eV, respectively. The same results were also observed in the high-resolution N 1s XPS spectrum [29, 31]. These results further confirm that SK was successfully modified on the surface of Mag. Furthermore, there were no obvious size changes of MagSK NPs in water, in buffers with different pH values, and in the enzyme-containing medium (pH 7.4, lysozyme 1 U/mL) over 14 days (Fig. S6), indicating the good colloidal stability.

MTD properties and ROS scavenge of MagSK NPs

The magnetothermal conversion capability of magnetic nanomaterials is intrinsically linked to their magnetic properties. The Mag NPs show superior magnetic performance over conventional magnetic iron oxide NPs due to their nanostructure [23]. As shown in Fig. 2a and Fig. S7, the magnetization curve shows negligible coercivity, implying its good superparamagnetic property. Furthermore, the saturation magnetization of MagSK NPs was measured to be ∼60 emu/g at room temperature, which is almost similar to Mag NPs, higher than those of most conventional Fe3O4 nanoparticles [32]. Such a high saturation magnetization meets the requirements for high magnetothermal conversion efficiency. The magnetothermal performance of MagSK NPs under AMF was then assessed. As expected, the MagSK NPs demonstrated exceptional magnetothermal heating properties, which were AMF strength-dependent (Fig. 2b). Under an AMF of 400 Oe, the MagSK dispersion induced a temperature increase of ~15 °C within 10 min and the specific absorption rate (SAR) value was determined to be 624 Watt g-1 (Fig. 2c). After confirming the excellent magnetothermal properties of MagSK, we proceeded to evaluate their peroxidase (POD)-like enzymatic catalytic activities. The colorimetric assay using 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as a probe revealed that MagSK displayed the highest catalytic activities for •OH generation compared to Mag, which was attributed to the combined effects of Mag and SK, while the activity could be enhanced by AMF (Fig. 2d) [33]. Concurrently, the UV–vis absorption peak at 652 nm increased progressively with a rise in Fe concentration and extended reaction time (Fig. 2e and f). Given the excellent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities of SK, we further evaluated the antioxidant capacity of MagSK NPs. As detailed in Fig. 2g-i, the scavenging effect increases as the Fe concentration and reaction time increase. Collectively, these multifunctional characteristics make MagSK NPs a promising therapeutic option for promoting infected wound healing.

Characteristics of MagSK hydrogel

A PVA/GA hybrid hydrogel was constructed to encapsulate MagSK NPs and form a multifunctional composite dressing for infected wound treatment [21]. MagSK NPs were first homogeneously dispersed in an aqueous PVA/GA mixture. The subsequent addition of 2 wt% borax rapidly initiated dynamic crosslinking via boronate ester formation, completing the gelation process within approximately 10 min to yield a cohesive, viscoelastic hydrogel (Fig. 3a). The formation of boronate ester bonds was further characterized using FTIR and XPS. As shown in Fig. 3b, the bands at 1440 and 1336 cm-1 observed in both the blank hydrogel and the MagSK hydrogel are assigned to B–O–C vibrations, indicating the formation of borate ester linkages in the hydrogel [20, 34]. Furthermore, the XPS B 1s spectrum (Fig. 3c) was fitted to obtain two signals at 192.5 eV (B-O-B) and 191.6 eV (B-O-C), verifying the successful introduction of borax into the hydrogel network [35, 36].

Given the dynamic mechanical environment of wounds, the rheological properties of MagSK hydrogel were systematically characterized. Strain sweep tests revealed a well-defined linear viscoelastic region, beyond which the material transitioned from a gel-like to a more fluid-like state (Fig. 3d). Frequency sweep tests showed that the storage modulus (G′) remained higher than the loss modulus (G″) across the measured frequency range (Fig. 3e), confirming predominantly solid-like viscoelastic behavior. Subsequently, shear-thinning testing revealed that the hydrogel exhibited shear-thinning behavior attributed to its dynamic physical cross-linking points, facilitating injectability (Fig. 3f). Self-healing property is essential for hydrogels to repair fractures caused by bodily movements and maintain a sterile environment in the wound area. A dynamic amplitude sweep test was performed to verify the self-healing ability of the hydrogels (Fig. 3g). Specifically, the hydrogel was first exposed to 500% strain to disrupt its cross-linked network, then to 1% strain to assess recovery. While 500% strain caused a notable G’ drop (network disruption), G’ and G’’ rapidly restored to initial levels at 1% strain, confirming the hydrogel’s excellent self-healing performance. Furthermore, compared with the blank hydrogel, the loading of MagSK NPs did not significantly affect the intrinsic shear-thinning and self-healing properties of the hydrogel (Fig. S8a-d). Thermal analyses showed that MagSK NPs loading did not markedly alter the overall thermal degradation profile of the hydrogel, but slightly affected its decomposition behavior and thermal transition characteristics (Fig. S9). Compression testing showed that nanoparticle incorporation enhanced the compressive strength of the hydrogel while largely preserving its overall deformation behavior and initial elasticity (Fig. S10). Subsequently, the two hydrogels were gently brought into contact at room temperature. It was evident that they could automatically heal together without any external intervention within 3 minutes, further demonstrating excellent self-healing properties (Fig. 3h). The self-healing ability of the hydrogel was attributed to dynamic boronate ester bonds in combination with multiple hydrogen bonds [20, 21]. The hydrogel can fit closely to the joints and is suitable for the healing needs of active parts of the body (Fig. 3i). MagSK hydrogel also exhibited strong adhesion to diverse substrates (Fig. S11a) and good moldability (Fig. S11b), which facilitate suture-free tissue approximation and patient-specific dressing design. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images showed that both MagSK hydrogel and blank hydrogel had a 3D porous meshwork structure (Fig. 3j and Fig. S12), creating an ideal habitat for exudate absorption and cell proliferation. A uniform dispersion of MagSK NPs on the pores of hydrogel was observed, and the presence of Fe, C, O, N, and B elements on the surface of hydrogel was confirmed by energy dispersive spectroscopy (Fig. 3k). Benefiting from the porous microstructure and abundant hydrophilic groups within the hydrogel system, MagSK hydrogel exhibited a stable swelling ratio (300%). This relatively high swelling ratio may be due to the incorporation of MagSK nanoparticles, which improved water accessibility within the hydrogel network (Fig. 3l). Meanwhile, degradation studies revealed that both hydrogels showed rapid mass loss, but the MagSK hydrogel retained slightly more residual mass after 72 h (Fig. S13). These properties highlight its potential for wound exudate absorption and wound-site air permeability. Additionally, we investigated the magnetothermal properties of the MagSK hydrogel. Similar to the heating phenomenon observed in MagSK nanoparticles, a significant temperature increase was also observed in the MagSK hydrogel (Fig. 3m and Fig. S14). Moreover, dynamic temperature sweep was conducted to examine the effect of temperature on the rheological behaviors of MagSK hydrogel. As shown in Fig. 3n, MagSK hydrogel exhibits a temperature-responsive reversible sol-gel transition with the lower critical solution temperature at approximately 30°C. Such a temperature-sensitive characteristic is conducive to realizing controlled drug release that responds to AMF. In addition, the hydrogel rapidly degraded in H2O2 owing to the intrinsic ROS sensitivity of boronate ester bonds (Fig. 3o). Combined with magnetothermal activation, this ROS responsiveness promoted on-demand, accelerated release of MagSK NPs (Fig. S15).

Biocompatibility assessment and hemostatic efficacy of MagSK hydrogel

Because wound dressings remain in direct and prolonged contact with tissues, excellent biocompatibility is indispensable. Cell viability assays revealed that the viability of human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) decreased with increasing SK concentration. When the SK concentration reached 5 µg/mL, the cell viability was only 32.48% (Fig. S16a). The same phenomenon was confirmed in mouse fibroblast (L929) cells (Fig. S16b), and this finding is consistent with our previous report [26]. By contrast, Mag and MagSK NPs maintained nearly 100% cell viability across a wide Fe concentration range (6.25–100 μg mL⁻1; Fig. S16c). On this basis, we further evaluated the cytocompatibility of the MagSK hydrogel. The results showed that within the concentration range of 0–2 mg/mL, the hydrogel exhibited no significant cytotoxicity toward both HUVECs and L929 cells (Fig. 4a). Live/dead staining (Fig. 4b) showed predominantly live cells with normal morphology after incubation with MagSK hydrogel, confirming its good biocompatibility. Hemocompatibility is another critical parameter for dressings that contact blood, especially during early hemorrhagic stages of wound repair. As presented in Fig. 4d, the hemolysis rates of the blank hydrogel group, Mag hydrogel group, and MagSK hydrogel group were all far below the 5% safety threshold. Meanwhile, we further evaluated the in vivo biocompatibility of the MagSK hydrogel via subcutaneous transplantation into mouse skin (Fig. 4c). After 14 days, Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining showed no significant inflammatory infiltration or pathological abnormalities in skins at the implantation site and major organs (Fig. 4e and Fig. S17). Additionally, hematological and serum biochemical parameters remained within normal ranges (Fig. 4f). Moreover, small animal live imaging was used to examine hydrogel retention at the wound site, and no obvious accumulation in major organs was observed at the administered dose (Fig. S18). Collectively, these findings confirm the long-term biocompatibility of the hydrogel.

Hemostasis is the primary and crucial step in the wound healing process. Therefore, the hemostatic efficacy of MagSK hydrogel was assessed using liver hemorrhage and tail amputation models (Fig. 4g–i). In the liver injury model, MagSK hydrogel reduced blood loss from 251 mg to 40.1 mg. In the tail amputation model, blood loss decreased from 162.6 mg to 20.2 mg (Fig. 4j). MagSK hydrogel thus exhibited rapid and efficient hemostatic performance. The excellent hemostatic activity is attributed to both the GA and PVA as self-healing bioadhesive hydrogel, making it adhere closely to the wound surface, promoting the formation of in situ blood clots and achieving the purpose of hemostasis. Additionally, GA effectively triggers platelet aggregation to promote hemostasis, while Mag NPs surface-modified dopamine further enhances red blood cell capture capacity, thereby synergistically improving hemostatic efficacy [20, 21].

In vitro antibacterial performance of MagSK hydrogel

Effective control of bacterial infection is crucial for successful wound healing. Owing to the excellent MTD properties of Mag NPs and the intrinsic antibacterial activity of SK, the MagSK hydrogel exhibits substantial antibacterial and anti-biofilm potential. The antibacterial activity was assessed against S. aureus and E. coli, selected as representative Gram-positive and Gram-negative wound-associated bacteria, respectively. Colony counting assays showed that treatment with SK, MagSK NPs, MagSK hydrogel, and particularly MagSK hydrogel under AMF (MagSK + B) substantially reduced bacterial survival of both E. coli and S. aureus. Notably, MagSK + B group achieved nearly complete inhibition (~100%) of both Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria, due to the magnetothermal-enhanced •OH generation (Fig. 5a, b and Fig. S19). Live/dead staining further confirmed the bactericidal effect of MagSK + B, with most bacteria exhibiting red fluorescence indicative of compromised membranes (Fig. 5c, d and Fig. S20). Bacterial biofilms, with their dense physical structure and extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) rich in polysaccharides and nucleic acids, confer inherent antibiotic resistance, making them intractable in wound management, especially for chronic wounds infected with bacteria. Crystal violet staining was further employed to evaluate the disruption of mature biofilms with or without hydrogels. For E. coli, the amount of biofilm decreased with the addition of different hydrogel components; the biofilm inhibition rate of the MagSK hydrogel reached 90.82%, and a clearance rate of ~63.48% was achieved against S. aureus (Fig. 5e, f and Fig. S21). This performance exceeded that of free SK at equivalent concentrations, highlighting the advantage of integrating SK with magnetothermal activation to overcome biofilm barriers. In addition, SEM showed severe shrinkage and destruction of the bacterial cell membrane of E. coli and S. aureus after treatment with MagSK gel (Fig. 5g).

Anti-inflammatory, cell migration-promoting, and angiogenesis-promoting properties of MagSK hydrogel

Persistent ROS accumulation in wounds sustains inflammation and hinders tissue repair. Owing to ROS-responsive boronate ester bonds and the intrinsic antioxidative activity of SK, MagSK hydrogel exhibited strong cytoprotective and anti-inflammatory effects under oxidative stress. Thus, we investigated the cytoprotective effect of MagSK hydrogel under H2O2-induced oxidative stress and its role in macrophage phenotype switching. As shown in Fig. 6a, the viability of RAW264.7 cells decreased to 79.2% after H2O2 stimulation without treatment, while the cell survival rate of MagSK hydrogel group was greater than 95%, demonstrating effective protection against oxidative damage. DCFH-DA staining revealed markedly increased intracellular ROS levels in H2O2-treated cells, which were substantially reduced upon MagSK hydrogel treatment (Fig. 6b and Fig. S22a). Quantitative analysis of fluorescence intensity further validated these findings (Fig. S22b). Based on these findings, we performed flow cytometry on LPS-treated RAW 264.7 cells. The results demonstrated that MagSK hydrogel treatment increased the expression of the M2 macrophage marker CD206 by 18.27% compared to the LPS‑induced group, promoting a shift toward the M2 phenotype. These findings highlight the potential of MagSK hydrogel to modulate macrophage polarization and improve the wound microenvironment (Fig. 6c and Fig. S23). The corresponding analysis of the mRNA of RAW264.7 further confirms the immunoregulation ability of MagSK hydrogel. MagSK gel significantly reduced the expression of the pro-inflammatory factors TNF-α, IL-1β and CD86 but increased the expression of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 and CD206 compared to the LPS group. This shift indicates active regulation of macrophage polarization toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype by the hydrogel (Fig. 6d). These results collectively indicate that MagSK hydrogel treatment promotes macrophage polarization toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, enhancing immunomodulation for chronic wound healing.

Wound healing is a tightly regulated biological process characterized by the cooperative crosstalk of skin cells, growth factors, and biological pathways [14]. Notably, fibroblast migration and collagen deposition are pivotal for granulation tissue formation, whereas endothelial cells are responsible for neovascularization, which is an indispensable process for nutrient delivery and tissue regeneration. Accordingly, we assessed the impact of MagSK hydrogel on angiogenesis and cell migration. In scratch assays, MagSK hydrogel markedly enhanced L929 cell motility, achieving a wound closure rate of 68.84% at 24 h, significantly higher than that of the control and blank gel groups (Fig. 6e, g). Tube formation assays showed that HUVECs cultured with MagSK hydrogel formed more extensive and interconnected capillary-like networks, characterized by increased branch points and total tube length (Fig. 6f, h). In combination, the ROS-scavenging, immunomodulatory, pro-migratory, and pro-angiogenic effects of MagSK hydrogel contribute to the establishment of a pro-healing microenvironment, which is particularly important for the resolution of chronic inflammation and the progression toward functional tissue restoration.

In vivo healing of bacteria-infected skin defects

The therapeutic efficacy of MagSK hydrogel was further investigated in an in vivo S. aureus–infected full-thickness skin defect model. The experimental protocol is outlined in Fig. 7a. After creation of 8-mm circular dorsal wounds and bacterial inoculation, mice were randomly allocated to Control, commercial wound dressing (Tegaderm TM Film, 3M), Blank hydrogel, Mag hydrogel, or MagSK hydrogel with/without AMF (MagSK + B). The back wounds were consistently monitored and photographed on the 0, 3, 7, and 14 days following the treatment application. Under AMF, MagSK hydrogel exhibited a robust magnetothermal response, with local temperature rapidly rising above 45 °C (Fig. 7b). This confirms the capability of MagSK hydrogel to provide localized hyperthermia suitable for antibacterial therapy. After seven days, the group treated with MagSK gel + AMF displayed a wound area reduction of 52.02%, outperforming all other groups in terms of healing (Fig. 7c, d). By day 14, the control and 3M sets maintained relatively larger areas of wounds, measuring 14.07% and 6.13%, respectively. Conversely, the MagSK gel group manifested enhanced wound closure, with the wound area reduced to 2.84%, while the MagSK gel +AMF group exhibited the most impressive healing results, leaving only a 0.82% area of the wound remaining (Fig. 7f). Blood flow within the wound area was assessed by laser Doppler imaging (Fig. 7e and g). Pseudocolor blood flow visualization maps and perfusion analysis on days 3 and 7 revealed a gradual increase in blood perfusion levels across all groups over time (Fig. 7e). Quantitative assessment demonstrated that the MagSK + B group exhibited the highest perfusion levels by day 7, with extensive regions of high-intensity blood flow, in line with improved neovascularization (Fig. 7g). It has been postulated that the application of a warm treatment may facilitate vasodilation in the wound area, thereby accelerating local blood flow and promoting angiogenesis [37]. To evaluate bacterial clearance, wound exudates were collected on days 3 and 7. Bacterial plate count revealed a significant reduction of S. aureus load in MagSK + B-treated wounds compared with control wounds, indicating that MagSK gel + B effectively alleviated wound infection (Fig. 7h). Histological analysis using H&E staining and Masson staining further confirmed therapeutic effects. As shown in Fig. 7i, H&E staining of wound tissues after 14 days of treatment revealed pronounced neutrophil-driven inflammatory cell infiltration (black arrows) in the control group. In contrast, the MagSK + B synergistic treatment group exhibited the most favorable histological outcomes. Masson’s trichrome staining revealed that the MagSK + B group achieved the highest collagen deposition (49.37%), significantly exceeding control (18.32%) and 3M (18.31%) (Fig. S24b). The regenerated tissue displayed a well-structured epidermal structure (Fig. S24a), significant neovascularization (red arrows), tightly arranged collagen fibers in the dermis, and abundant hair follicles (yellow arrows), without obvious inflammatory cell infiltration. The above results suggest that the MagSK hydrogel combined with AMF has the ability to promote re-epithelialization of infected wounds and accelerate wound healing.

Mechanism of MagSK hydrogel in promoting wound healing

To further elucidate how MagSK hydrogel orchestrates wound repair, oxidative stress, immune responses, angiogenesis, and cell proliferation on day 7 were examined in vivo (Fig. 8). Dihydroethidium (DHE) staining (Fig. 8a and Fig. S25a) revealed high ROS levels in control group, consistent with persistent infection and chronic inflammation. By contrast, MagSK and MagSK + B groups exhibited markedly reduced DHE fluorescence, reflecting combined ROS scavenging by boronate ester bonds, SK-mediated antioxidative activity, and diminished inflammatory burden after effective bacterial elimination. Macrophage phenotype analysis showed that CD206⁺ M2 macrophages were scarce in control and blank hydrogel groups, whereas MagSK treatments significantly increased M2 cell density, with the highest level observed in the MagSK + B group. RT-qPCR data (Fig. 8b) corroborated these findings at the transcriptional level. Compared with the control group, the MagSK treated wounds significantly reduced the expression levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) and M1 markers (CD86, iNOS), along with markedly elevated expression of IL-10, CD206, Arg1. Angiogenesis and proliferation were also significantly enhanced. Immunostaining for CD31 and VEGF (Fig. 8a) demonstrated limited vascularization in the control and 3M groups, but extensive CD31 and strong VEGF expressions in the MagSK and MagSK + B groups (Fig. S25c, S25d). This angiogenic response is consistent with the improved perfusion observed in Fig. 7 and is essential for oxygen and nutrient delivery to regenerating tissues. Ki67 staining revealed minimal cell proliferation in untreated wounds, whereas MagSK + B group displayed abundant Ki67⁺ cells, indicative of active cellular turnover, re-epithelialization, and tissue reconstruction. Notably, the most favorable histological outcomes were observed in the MagSK hydrogel + B treatment group, and an absence of significant inflammatory cell infiltration. Control and blank hydrogel groups showed incomplete epidermal coverage. In contrast, MagSK hydrogel led to more continuous epithelium and more mature granulation tissue. Under AMF, wounds treated with MagSK displayed a thin, uniform epidermis, well-organized dermal matrix, and tissue thickness approaching that of normal skin. These results collectively demonstrate that MagSK hydrogel, combined with AMF, could exert synergistic effect in reducing oxidative stress, inhibiting inflammation, promoting vascular regeneration and ultimately tissue remodeling, significantly enhancing both the efficiency and quality of wound healing.

Transcriptomic analysis of MagSK hydrogel combined with MTD for promoting the infected wound healing

To further explore the molecular mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of MagSK hydrogel, transcriptomic sequencing was performed on wound tissue at day 7. The volcano plot and heat map clearly reveal divergence on the level of gene expression in these two groups. As illustrated by the volcano plots, differential expression analysis identified a total of 756 significantly altered genes (FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1) in the MagSK + B treated group versus control, comprising 538 upregulated and 218 downregulated genes (Fig. 9a). Further clustering analysis demonstrated that the antimicrobial peptide Defb8 and the bactericidal/permeability-increasing protein Bpifb1 were downregulated after MagSK + B treatment, indicating a substantial alleviation of the local bacterial burden (Fig. 9b). Concurrently, the expression of Trim80 and Prss34, which participate in inflammatory signaling regulation, is suppressed, indicating that excessive inflammation at the wound site is alleviated. Regarding tissue repair and skin regeneration, the significant upregulation of genes encoding keratin-associated proteins (Krtap20-2, Krtap6-3), tenascin-R (Tnr), and the proliferation-associated factor Rgr further highlighted the transition from the inflammatory phase to tissue proliferation and remodeling [38].

Transcriptomic analysis of skin tissues following MagSK hydrogel treatment. (a) Volcano plot of differentially expressed genes. (b) Heatmap of gene expression profiles. GO enrichment analysis categorized into Biological Process (c), Cellular Component (d), and Molecular Function (e). (f) KEGG pathway analysis of the DEGs

GO enrichment analysis showed that the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in MagSK + B group were enriched in biological processes (BP) such as xenobiotic metabolic process, immune system process and innate immune response (Fig. 9c). From the perspective of cellular components (CC), the DEGs are mainly enriched in cell surface, extracellular region and extracellular space (Fig. 9d). The enrichment of these genes significantly impacts cell-cell recognition, signal reception, immune cell migration, and cell-matrix interactions, providing the structural foundation for tissue re-epithelialization and microenvironment remodeling [39]. The DEGs of molecular functions (MF) are mainly enriched in oxidoreductase activity, indicates that MagSK + B regulates the body’s immune system and alleviates cellular oxidative stress response (Fig. 9e).

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis validated the cellular signaling pathways regulated by differentially expressed genes (Fig9f). Results showed that the antigen processing and presentation pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway, and IL-17 pathway were all downregulated in the MagSK + B treatment group [40]. These pathways are associated with inflammation and immune regulation, indicating that MagSK + B promotes wound healing by modulating immune responses and eliminating infection. Collectively, MagSK hydrogel modulates the body’s immune response to restore immune homeostasis, alleviate chronic inflammation, and promote tissue proliferation and regeneration at wound sites.

Conclusion

In this work, we developed a multifunctional hydrogel dressing (MagSK hydrogel) by integrating MagSK NPs into a dynamic PVA/GA–boronate network to achieve MTD-enhanced and microenvironment-adaptive therapy for infected wounds. Shikonin, a natural compound historically used in wound care, was intended to combine its broad pharmacological activities with MTD therapy, while also addressing the poor solubility and low bioavailability of free drug. We determined that MagSK mediated MTD is capable of leading to bacterial eradication during the infection period. Moving to the repair stage, the hydrogel suppressed excessive inflammation by scavenging ROS and promoting macrophage polarization. Transcriptomic profiling further corroborated these mechanisms, revealing coordinated downregulation of inflammatory pathways and upregulation of genes critical for tissue proliferation and remodeling. In addition, the hydrogel accelerated cell proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, and collagen deposition, thus contributing to wound healing. Overall, our findings indicate that MagSK-mediated MTD therapy provides a promising non-antibiotic, stage-specific, and multifunctional strategy for infected wound treatment, and offers new insight into the design of wound dressings that integrate antibacterial therapy with immunomodulatory tissue repair. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. The mechanistic interplay between SK and MTD therapy in regulating antibacterial activity and wound microenvironment remodeling remains to be further clarified. Further studies are still required to optimize MagSK dosage and AMF treatment parameters, and to comprehensively evaluate long-term biosafety and therapeutic efficacy in large animal wound models that better mimic human wound healing processes.

Experiment and methods

Synthesis of MagSK hydrogel

Synthesis of MagNPs

Iron oxide nanocrystal clusters were synthesized via a solvothermal method as described in the literature [23]. For surface modification with dopamine, 50 mg of dopamine and 20 mg of as-prepared iron oxide clusters were mixed in an aqueous solution. The mixture was heated to 50 °C and incubated for 5 h. The resulting sediment was collected by centrifugation, rinsed with deionized water three times, and re-dispersed in water for subsequent use.

Synthesis of MagSK NPs

Shikonin was coated onto Mag NPs via Schiff base cross-linking. Briefly, 2 mg of shikonin was dissolved in 1 mL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), then mixed with 4 mL of the Mag NPs dispersion. The mixture was incubated overnight at 37 ℃ in a shaking incubator (150 rpm). The precipitate was collected by centrifugation, rinsed with deionized water three times, and re-dispersed in water for hydrogel preparation.

Fabrication of MagSK hydrogel

GA (2%) and PVA (8%) powders were completely dissolved in ultrapure water, separately. MagSK NPs were dispersed uniformly to obtain a dispersion. Afterward, 2 wt% borax solution was slowly injected to the resulting solution to form a hydrogel.

In vitro biological evaluations

Cytocompatibility analysis

Cytocompatibility analysis was conducted through CCK-8 assay. Logarithmic growth phase cells were plated in 96-well culture plates at 5 × 103 cells/well density and allowed to adhere for 24 h under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). Then, the medium was replaced with fresh medium containing different concentrations of MagSK NPs or hydrogel extracts. After another 24 h incubation, the cells were stained with CCK-8, and the cell viability was determined by measuring the absorbance of each well at 450 nm using Absorbance Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay Reader (BioTek).

In vitro hemolysis evaluation

The hemolysis evaluation was conducted to evaluate the blood compatibility of materials. Centrifuged RBCs (1500 rpm, 10 min) were washed with PBS to prepare suspensions. Hydrogel groups were compared against negative (PBS) and positive controls (DI water). The mixtures were incubated for 1 h (37 °C). After centrifugation, the absorbance was measured at 540 nm. The hemolysis rate was calculated using the equation as follows: Hemolysis ratio (%) = [(AT – AN) / (AW − AN)] × 100%, where At, AN, and AW represents the absorbance of erythrocytes exposed to the test group, PBS, and water, respectively.

Anti-inflammatory effects evaluation

Seeded L929 at a density of confocal dishes (2 × 105 cells/well) for 24 h. Then, H2O2 (3mM) were used to stimulate the cells for 12 h. Hydrogel extracts was added and incubated, followed by washing cells to remove the excess materials. After that, DCFH-DA was added into the L929 to test the intracellular ROS content.

RAW 264.7 cells induced with LPS were co-cultured with different samples, then the macrophage markers (CD86 and CD206) were analyzed through flow cytometry. To evaluate the anti-inflammation effect, the mRNA expression level of proinflammatory (TNF-α, IL-1β and CD86) or anti-inflammatory genes (IL-10 and CD206) in RAW 264.7 cells after being treated with different samples were evaluated by the qRT-PCR analysis. Primer sequences are provided in Table S1 (Supplementary Material).

Antibacterial activity evaluation

The antimicrobial properties of MagSK hydrogel were evaluated against E. coli (Gram-negative) and S. aureus (Gram-positive). Bacterial suspensions were diluted to 1 × 107 CFU/mL and incubated with each group at 37 ℃. Following incubation, suspensions were diluted, plated on solid culture medium, and incubated overnight at 37 ℃ to count the bacterial colonies. Each group was tested in triplicate. Following various antibacterial treatments, bacterial samples were incubated with DMAO and PI stains and analyzed for viable and non-viable bacteria under fluorescence microscope. Additionally, after the incubation, bacterial suspensions were collected for fixation, dehydration, and observation by SEM.

Animal experiments

Kunming mice (7-8 weeks, female) were selected for this animal study. A circular full-thickness skin wounds with a diameter of 8 mm were made on the back of each mouse after dehairing. Then, the wounds were infected with Staphylococcus aureus (1 × 107 CFU mL−1). After 1 day of infection, the wounds were treated with the control group, 3M, Blank hydrogel, Mag hydrogel, MagSK hydrogel, and MagSK hydrogel + B. After different treatments, mice were sacrificed at determined time points. The skin and major organs of mice in each group were collected for immunofluorescence staining and histopathology analysis.

Transcriptome sequencing

Skin samples collected on day 7 were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently subjected to RNA extraction and quality assessment. Transcriptome sequencing and subsequent analytical support were performed by OE Biotech Co., Ltd (Shanghai). By comparing the data from the control group and the MagSK + B (B) group, DEGs were identified. The enrichment analysis was conducted in various gene databases, such as GO, KEGG and Reactome.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data were indicated as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way or two-way ANOVA analysis of variance. The data were analyzed using the GraphPad Prism software. Statistical significance is denoted as p ≤0.05 (*), ≤0.01 (**), ≤0.001 (***)

Meshing theory and contact analysis of double enveloping hourglass worm drive with planar generatrix
Authors: Quancheng Peng, Minghao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe meshing and limit equations of worm drive usually have strong nonlinearities such as multiple solutions, solution nonexistence and equation singularity. Meanwhile, the tooth surfaces of worm drive are in nonconforming line contact, which often requires mesh refinement of contact region for the loaded contact analysis. These two challenges cause that the modeling of worm drive heavily relies on manual adjustment and the loaded contact analysis of worm drive is still rare especially when edge contact and assembly error are concerned. Focusing on the double enveloping hourglass worm (DEHW) drive with planar generatrix, this work presents procedures to solve meshing and limit equations with global convergence. The instantaneous contact line, meshing limit line, curvature interference limit line and tooth surface grid discretization are adaptively generated, without manual trial or adjustment. On the basis of adaptive mesh refinement of tooth surface, the mortar virtual element method is adopted for loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with edge contact and center distance error. Under sliding friction, the discrete system of governing equalities and inequalities is solved by semi-smooth Newton algorithm after constraint condensation. Numerical results for meshing theory and loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with planar generatrix are discussed.Subject terms: Mechanical engineering, Computational science

Introduction

According to the shape of worm, the common worm drives are classified into three kinds as shown in Fig. 1: cylindrical worm drive1, hourglass worm drive2 and conical worm drive3. Each of these three kinds of worm drive can be further classified according to the worm profile. For example, the ISO/TR 10828:2015(E) classifies the cylindrical worm drive as type A, type I, type N, type K and type C. In hourglass worm drive, the shape of worm wheel is used to distinguish between single-enveloping hourglass worm drive and DEHW drive. If further detailed, the DEHW drive is classified according to the generatrix of the first envelope process, where generatrix means the generating geometry of cutter or blade. Due to the long history and currently advanced development of worm drive, it may be difficult to choose the suitable terminology and this work may be somewhere confusing. Such as, the conical worm drive is also called as spiroid drive4 or spiroid gear drive5; the DEHW drive with planar generatrix is also called as planar DEHW gear drive2 or planar double-enveloping toroidal worm drive6; the DEHW drive with straight-lined generatrix is also called as Cone double-enveloping worm-gear drive7, Hindley worm pair8 or DEHW drive with straight axial worm tooth profile9.

Classification and sketch of worm drive.

In comparison with cylindrical worm drive, the DEHW drive often has advantages10 such as more simultaneously meshing teeth, small induced normal curvature, large sliding angle, great load capacity and high transmission efficiency. In comparison with the DEHW drive with straight-lined generatrix, the DEHW drive with planar or conical generatrix can adopt grinding to improve the accuracy and hardness of worm profile. In comparison with the DEHW drive with planar generatrix, the DEHW drive with conical generatrix can avoid root undercutting and tip sharpening of worm when the worm thread number is very large. With application in metallurgical industry, chemical industry, military industry and so on11, the DEHW drive with planar generatrix is the main focus of this work.

The design, measurement, modeling and manufacturing of DEHW drive are currently elaborately researched. For the DEHW drive with planar generatrix, the measurement method of helix deviation, axial tooth profile deviation and topological deviation of worm is proposed12, the automatic relief grinding method of worm gear hob is proposed13, the influence of modification on worm wheel tooth surface is analyzed2, the worm tooth profile in end cross-section is firstly proved to be an involute6. For the DEHW drive with straight-lined generatrix, the induced normal curvature is calculated on the basis of normal vector of ICL8, the CAD modeling method is proposed with modeling accuracy analysis9, the whirl-machining process of worm is proposed with normal deviation analysis14. For the DEHW drive with conical or dual conical generatrix, the CAD modeling method is proposed with CNC machining experiment15, the automatic generation method of hob relief surfaces is put forward16. For the DEHW drive with dual torus generatrix, the classification of worm drive by modification parameters is enriched17, the distance of meshing point to curvature interference limit line is adopted to evaluate the failure probability18.

These researches greatly promote and enrich the meshing theory of DEHW drive, whereas the strong nonlinearities of meshing and limit equations may still not be widely concerned. Such as, how to determine the definition domain of meshing function, how to determine the solution existence and solution number of meshing and limit equations, how to choose the initial value for iterative solution of meshing and limit equations. These problems may be important because of the better understanding of meshing theory. But possibly caused by the strong nonlinearities, the answer of these questions is not very simple and direct. This work searches the answer of these problems by both analytical and numerical methods.

The searched answer facilitates the tooth surface grid discretization, where the discretization nodes on tooth surface are directly generated by solving meshing equations but not by interpolation of CAD fitted surface. On the basis of tooth surface grid discretization, the loaded contact analysis can be proceeded. Common methods for loaded contact analysis of gear drive include analytical method19, numerical method20, semi-analytical method21 and experimental method22. This work adopts the numerical method due to its high accuracy on account of edge contact which is vital for the tooth profile relief23.

The Fig. 2 tries to give a preliminary overview of numerical contact analysis in linear elasticity. The mortar virtual element method24 combines the advantages of mortar method and virtual element method. The mortar method can be partly treated as a surface-to-surface contact discretization scheme, which not only passes contact patch test under non-matching interface grid25 but also satisfies uniform inf-sup stability26. By functional analysis, the uniform inf-sup stability27 not only guarantees solution uniqueness of the contact problem, but also guarantees optimal convergence rate for the contact discretization error. Besides, for the dual mortar method28, the adoption of dual Lagrange multiplier supports constraint condensation which highly improves the efficiency of solution algorithm29. Regarding to the virtual element method30, it is often more advantageous than finite element method in mesh adaption and refinement31 because of the admission of polyhedral mesh or hanging nodes. By now, the virtual element method is already widely applied in discrete fracture network simulation24, computational contact mechanics32, crack propagation33 and so on. Because the tooth surfaces of DEHW drive are in nonconforming line contact, the adaptive mesh refinement of contact region is necessary and so the virtual element method is preferred for the continuum discretization. Currently, the application of mortar virtual element method in numerical contact analysis is relatively rare, the loaded contact analysis of worm drive is relatively rare in comparison with cylindrical involute gear drive20 and bevel gear drive34. This work applies the mortar virtual element method to loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with planar generatrix.

Meshing theory analysis

The meshing and limit equations of DEHW drive with planar generatrix are nonlinear. For the nonlinear equation35, the solvability analysis of the equation and the convergence analysis of the solution method are not so unified as the system of linear equations. Preferentially, this section tries to find the closed-form solution of meshing and limit equations, through converting these equations into linear equations or common trigonometric equations. If the closed-form solution can not be found, then the interval analysis36 is adopted to solve the nonlinear equation to guarantee global convergence. Here, the global convergence means the solution method can judge whether there exists solution and can find all solutions if there exists solution.

Meshing and limit equations

For the two envelopes of DEHW drive with planar generatrix, the first envelope generates the worm by planar cutter, the second envelope generates the worm wheel by a hob which is identical to the worm, as shown in Fig. 3. In the figure, the meshing configuration includes center distance and transmission ratio. If the meshing configuration of the first envelope keeps the same as the second envelope, the generated drive is called unmodified drive or standard drive; if the meshing configuration of the first envelope slightly changes from the second envelope, then the generated drive is called modified drive. The slight change of meshing configuration is called tooth surface modification2, which is distinguished from the tooth profile modification37. To avoid obscuring, the following uses tooth profile relief23 to replace tooth profile modification.

Interval Newton method

The closed-form solution for curvature interference equation of second envelope has not been found, so the numerical method is adopted to solve the curvature interference equation of second envelope. One of the most common numerical methods for solving nonlinear equation is the classical Newton method which only guarantees local convergence35. In pursuit of global convergence, the simplicial and continuation method40 or the interval Newton method36,41 may be adopted. Mortar virtual element method

The mortar virtual element method combines the advantages of mortar method and virtual element method, it is preferable for the loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with planar generatrix. This section introduces the boundary value problem of sliding frictional contact in three-dimensional linear elasticity at first. Then the continuum discretization based on virtual element method and the contact discretization based on dual mortar method are respectively introduced.

Boundary value problem of sliding friction contact

For the two linear elastic contact bodies η=1,2, they occupy open bounded domains Ωη∈C1,1, where C1,1 confines the smoothness of ∂Ωη by Lipschitz gradient continuity42. Boundary ∂Ωη is partitioned into three disjoint measurable segments: Dirichlet boundary ΓDη, Neumann boundary ΓNη and potential contact region ΓCη, where Ση=int∂Ωη\ΓDη, Γ¯Cη⊂Ση and ∂ΓCη is smooth42. Adaptive mesh refinement around contact region

Two main aspects of adaptive mesh refinement are refinement scheme and refinement indicator50. The sketch of the adopted refinement scheme is shown in Fig. 19 which divides element evenly along different directions. The refinement scheme owns embedded hierarchy and is similar to octree refinement scheme, except that the element face is triangular but not quadrilateral. As for the refinement indicator, it is usually adopted to locate regions with large deformation gradient50. For the nonconforming line contact, the main region with large solution gradient is known in advance of solution, so this work directly refines these predictable regions without using refinement indicator.

Sketch of the refinement scheme (All the faces of one element are rendered with the same colour).

When the geometry of contact surface is complicated, the prediction of potential contact region also becomes complicated. To automatically predict and refine the potential contact region, the following adaptive mesh refinement procedure named as AMRACR is adopted.

Procedure: AMRACR.

Input: gcJη, 1≤J≤Jc,η=1,2.

Output: none.

Set the refinement level J←1.

Set the refinement level J←1.

Initialize the potential contact region ΓChJη respectively for η=1 and η=2.

Initialize the potential contact region ΓChJη respectively for η=1 and η=2.

By contact search, compute the initial contact gaps of nodes on ΓChJ1 to ΓChJ2 and the initial contact gaps of nodes on ΓChJ2 to ΓChJ1.

Set η←1.

Set η←1.

For each element face F∈ΓChJη, if F has at least one node with initial contact gap less than critical gap gcJη, then the element E of F∈∂E is refined by one level.

For each element face F∈ΓChJη, if F has at least one node with initial contact gap less than critical gap gcJη, then the element E of F∈∂E is refined by one level.

Set η←η+1. If η≤2, then turn to step 5).

Set η←η+1. If η≤2, then turn to step 5).

Set J←J+1. If J≤Jc, then turn to step 2).

Set J←J+1. If J≤Jc, then turn to step 2).

The availability of above procedure depends on the critical gap gcJη and the total refinement level Jc. Usually, the critical gap gcJη decreases along with the increase of refinement level, i.e., gcJ+1η≤gcJη; the value of gcJ1 can be different to gcJ2; the value of gcJcη needs to be large enough to make final mesh ΓChJc+1η contain the real contact region; the value of Jc needs to be large enough to ensure enough small element size of final mesh ΓChJc+1η. To ensure accuracy, it is necessary to make the nodes of ΓChJη always locate on ΓCη through suitable transformations between Cartesian coordinate and curvilinear coordinate.

Discussion of frictional loaded contact analysis results

On the basis of mortar virtual element method, the pseudo static loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with planar generatrix is carried out. The pseudo static state means the worm and worm wheel are rotating at very slow speed so that the inertial force can be neglected. Under pseudo static state, the inner ring of worm wheel is fixed, the inner ring of worm is only fixed by radial and axial DOF, the driving torque is imposed on the tangential DOF of the inner ring of worm. Here, the replacement of worm wheel driven torque by fixed displacement constraint is necessary, because it excludes rigid motion to ensure uniqueness of displacement solution. This pseudo static state is practical in engineering and it ensures sliding friction contact state between tooth surfaces. In order to better simulate the friction state, the elastohydrodynamic lubrication53 also needs to be considered but is ignored at here. This ignorance simplifies the analysis but may cause larger result errors.

The Table 2 also shows half contact band width calculated by Eq. (59). On the one hand, the large values of half contact band width verify the great load capacity of DEHW drive; on the other hand, the large values of half contact band width may violate the assumption that the typical dimension of contact area is much smaller than the contact body52. For example, the contact band width is 2.86mm and the induced normal curvature radius is 199.6mm at the specified meshing point of case III. This violation of small contact area assumption further causes accuracy losses for analytical method.

For the cases I, IV and V, the total displacements of worm and worm wheel are shown in Fig. 27. The results of cases II and III are not shown because of the similarity to case I. From the figure, the maximum displacement of worm is much larger than worm wheel. The reason is that the worm needs much larger total displacement to produce the needed normal displacement in comparison with worm wheel. For all the cases, the deformation of worm tooth is much smaller in comparison with the tangential displacement of worm.

Influence of tooth profile relief

To avoid edge contact, the tooth profile relief is necessary but still relatively seldom considered in studies by now. In all the following numerical examples, the tip relief amount is 14μm and the end relief amount is 18μm for worm, the tip relief amount is 12μm and the end relief amount is 16μm for worm wheel. For the cases I to III under R/T1/F2/C0, the nodal contact pressure at θh=0rad is shown in Fig. 29a to d. For the cases IV to V under R, the nodal contact pressure at θh=0rad is shown in Fig. 29e to f. In comparison with Fig. 26, the edge contact at tooth tip, tooth root and tooth end are avoided, so there are no stress concentrations for case I in Fig. 29a to b, case II in Fig. 29c, case V in Fig. 29f. Because of the intersection line in the middle of worm wheel tooth surface, there are still stress concentrations caused by edge contact for case III in Fig. 29d and case IV in Fig. 29e. In Fig. 29, it also shows the alleviation of ETC as the meshing-in ETC in Fig. 29a and d disappears. On the relief part of tooth surface, the contact band may slightly deviate from its corresponding theoretic ICL.

Conclusions

The meshing theory and frictional loaded contact analysis of DEHW drive with planar generatrix are studied in this work. By the solvability analysis of meshing equation of second envelope, three meshing parameter feasible domains D1, D2 and D3 are obtained. At domains D2 and D3, the singular limit point and singular limit line are presented. For the curvature interference limit equation of second envelope, it is solved by interval Newton method to ensure global convergence. For the transition from Type-II drive to Type-I drive, the requirement on domain D3 is inductively deduced. For the adaptively generated tooth surface grid discretization, its effectiveness is validated by the searched contact gap between tooth surfaces.

The mortar virtual element method with dual Lagrange multiplier is adopted for frictional loaded contact analysis. For the flexible beam example, the virtual element method shows superlinear convergence rate of displacement solution. For the contact patch test example, the mortar virtual element method reproduces homogeneous pressure state. For the example of two cylindrical bodies, the mortar virtual element method obtains results close to analytical method.

Through numerical analysis, the influences of tooth profile relief, load, friction and center distance error on meshing performance are analyzed. The edge contact occurs not only at the tooth tip, root and end, but also at the possible intersection line in the middle of worm wheel tooth surface. The tooth profile relief is effective to avoid the stress concentration caused by edge contact, the load has little influence on friction loss ratio, the friction coefficient has relatively large influence on maximum contact pressure, the center distance error has complicated influence on maximum contact pressure.

Impact of Surgical Approach on Survival and Recurrence in Borderline and Malignant Phyllodes Tumours: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis
Authors: Christopher G Harris, Gillian Heller, Farhad Azimi, Belinda Chan, Susannah Graham, Cindy Mak, Sanjay Warrier
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundPhyllodes tumours of the breast exhibit a spectrum of biologic behaviour, with a predisposition to recur locally and metastasize distantly in their malignant form. Introduction

Phyllodes tumours of the breast represent a spectrum of fibroepithelial breast tumours and may be classified as benign, borderline, or malignant on the basis of the degree of stromal cellular atypia, cellularity and overgrowth, infiltrative margins and the extent of mitotic activity [1, 2]. All histologic subtypes have the propensity to recur locally, and malignant phyllodes tumours can also metastasize haematogenously to distant sites and even de‐differentiate into a sarcoma‐like lesion with no epithelial component [1].

It has been established that those with early stage breast cancer of all histologic subtypes have equivalent overall and disease‐free survival following mastectomy or breast conservation treatment, comprising a lumpectomy and adjuvant radiation [3, 4, 5, 6]. More recently, some data suggest that breast conservation therapy may in fact confer a survival advantage. The Danish Breast Cancer Cooperative Group study, which evaluated 58 331 patients, demonstrated an increased relative risk of death for those undergoing mastectomy [7] and a 2022 meta‐analysis evaluating over a million patients demonstrated better overall and breast cancer specific survival following breast conservation [8].

In line with these findings, certain high risk subtypes of breast cancer may be suitable to undergo breast conservation therapy, subverting the historical treatment paradigm of a mastectomy for extensive local control. One example is metaplastic breast cancer, which can exhibit sarcoma‐like histology and behaviour as for phyllodes tumours, with a propensity to recur locally and metastasize distantly, conferring a worse prognosis compared to the more common epithelial‐derived invasive carcinoma NST [9, 10]. However, a recent meta‐analysis comparing breast conservation and mastectomy for metaplastic breast cancer showed no difference in overall survival between both surgical approaches, suggesting that both may be reasonable treatment approaches for this aggressive breast cancer subtype [11].

The optimal surgical approach for borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours is controversial. There is a paucity of data comparing breast conservation and mastectomy for these tumours. While some studies have shown improved local control and disease‐specific survival for those undergoing mastectomy [12, 13], others have shown no difference in disease‐free survival [14] or breast cancer specific survival [15]. A recent retrospective cohort study of 795 patients with malignant phyllodes tumours in fact showed improved 10‐year overall survival (89.2% vs. 81%) and breast cancer specific survival (95.8% vs. 90.1%) for the propensity‐matched breast conservation group [16], suggesting that breast conservation should be offered where it is feasible.

Regarding local control, however, a 2019 meta‐analysis showed that breast conservation surgery compared to mastectomy and positive versus negative surgical margins conferred an increased risk of local recurrence for those with malignant phyllodes tumours [17]. The issue of margin clearance remains controversial, with previous studies advocating for margins of at least 1 cm due to lower recurrence rates [18]. More recent data, however, from a retrospective Dutch study of borderline and malignant phyllodes patients showed no difference in locoregional recurrence between narrow (< 1 mm) and wider margins [19].

To our knowledge, no previous systematic review and meta‐analysis has investigated the impact of surgical approach for malignant phyllodes tumours on overall or disease‐free survival. These are clinically relevant in surgical decision making for patients with these tumours, which can exhibit unpredictable patterns of recurrence and metastasis. In regard to the previous meta‐analyses on local recurrence risk, several recent studies have reported on local recurrence rates since the meta‐analysis by Lu et al. [15, 19, 20], justifying an updated local recurrence meta‐analysis, in accordance with contemporary practice. Another meta‐analysis on local recurrence was limited in its small numbers, inclusion of studies with 5 or fewer patients in each cohort and exclusion of relevant studies [21]. Thus we aimed to evaluate the current impact of breast conserving surgery compared to mastectomy on overall survival, disease‐free survival, and local recurrence for patients with borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours to help guide treatment planning.

Methods

Study Protocol

We used the most recent PRISMA guidelines for the retrieval of publications for systematic reviews and meta‐analyses [22]. We conducted a systematic search of the following databases through to 30th December 2024: PubMed, MEDLINE, Embase and Google Scholar (first 20 pages). The search strategy comprised the following search terms: ‘phyllodes’ AND (‘breast conserve*’ OR ‘lumpect*’ OR ‘wide local excision’ OR ‘mastectom*’ OR ‘surg*’ OR ‘resect*’). Both English publications and publications translated into English were included. The reference list of all relevant studies were also screened for additional relevant publications. The search was performed by two independent investigators.

Study Selection

Studies were evaluated as suitable for inclusion in the meta‐analysis if they were a cohort study whereby patients with borderline and/or malignant phyllodes tumours underwent breast conservation surgery or mastectomy and compared regarding their rates of overall survival, disease‐free survival or local recurrence. Studies were excluded if the overall cohort included benign phyllodes patients without a separate subgroup analysis excluding these patients. Studies were also excluded if there was no comparison between breast conservation and mastectomy cohorts, if there were 5 or fewer patients in each cohort, and if recurrence data included regional or distant disease. Studies were appraised for their risk of bias according to the Newcastle‐Ottawa Scale [23].

Data Extraction

Data from each of the included studies were retrieved according to a standardised form which comprised the publication year, country, data source, years of patient recruitment, median follow‐up time and subtypes of phyllodes tumours. In addition, these data included the number of patients in the malignant and borderline subgroups, number of patients in the breast conservation and mastectomy cohorts and the primary outcome measures, including overall survival, disease‐free survival and local recurrence. Raw data from each study were used to generate a relative risk for each of the primary outcome measures presented.

Statistical Analysis

A pooled relative risk with a 95% confidence interval was generated using a random effects model [24]. These reflected a relative risk of death, for overall survival, relative risk of death or disease recurrence, for disease‐free survival, as well as a risk of local recurrence. Separate analyses were performed for both the malignant cohort and the combined borderline and malignant cohort. Cochran's Q statistic was used to calculate heterogeneity, which was deemed significant where p < 0.05 [25]. All analyses were performed using Meta package in the R statistical language, version 4.4.2 (R Core Team 2024).

Results

Out of the 1950 publications screened by our search, 13 studies were deemed eligible for inclusion in the meta‐analysis (Figure 1). Four studies were excluded due to their inclusion of benign phyllodes patients without a separate subgroup analysis [26, 27, 28, 29], limiting the applicability of the results to borderline and malignant patients. Three studies were excluded as they reported a hybrid recurrence cohort of either locoregional recurrences [19] or included distant recurrences [30, 31]. Three studies were excluded as their data overlapped with data from other included studies [2, 32, 33].

Study selection flow chart.

All included studies were retrospective cohort studies, published between 2008 and 2024. There were a total of 874 patients in the breast conservation cohort and 784 patients in the mastectomy cohort. Six of the included studies exclusively reported on malignant phyllodes patients while the other seven reported on both malignant and borderline patients. The median follow‐up time was 65 months. Three studies reported on overall survival [12, 16, 34], four reported on disease‐free survival [14, 15, 35, 36] and nine reported on local recurrence [12, 15, 20, 34, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41]. There was inherent heterogeneity given that not all studies were included in each subgroup meta‐analysis due to their differing outcome measures. A summary of the study characteristics of all included studies is outlined in Table 1. All included studies were assessed as valid according to the PRISMA checklist [22]. The risk of bias was assessed as moderately high for one study with a NOS score of 6 [36], and low for all other included studies with NOS scores of 7–9 (Table S1).

There was improved overall survival following breast conservation compared to mastectomy in the malignant (pooled RR 0.9, 95% CI 0.85–0.95) (Figure 2) and combined borderline and malignant cohorts (pooled RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.86–0.95) (Figure 3). There was no difference in disease‐free survival in the malignant (pooled RR 0.92, 95% CI 0.78–1.09) (Figure 4) or combined cohorts (pooled RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.87–1.18) (Figure 5). Local recurrence was increased following breast conservation compared to mastectomy in both malignant (pooled RR 2.47, 95% CI 1.64–3.72) (Figure 6) and combined cohorts (pooled RR 2.25, 95% CI 01.66–3.04) (Figure 7). There was no significant heterogeneity between studies in any of the subgroup analyses.

Discussion

This is the first systematic review and meta‐analysis to evaluate the impact of surgical approach on survival in borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours. Our demonstration of an overall survival benefit for those undergoing breast conservation surgery over mastectomy is consistent with data for early invasive breast cancer of all subtypes [7, 8]. This interesting finding in our study may reflect the impact of adjuvant radiotherapy for patients undergoing breast conservation, in controlling both local disease as well as occult distant metastatic disease by way of an abscopal effect [42]. A meta‐analysis of 8 studies showed reduced local recurrence with adjuvant radiotherapy following breast conserving surgery (HR 0.31, 95% CI −0.10 to 0.72) and also showed no significant difference with post‐mastectomy radiotherapy (HR 0.68, 95% CI −0.28 to 1.64) [43]. This was furthered by another meta‐analysis of 696 patients from 17 studies showing a pooled local recurrence of 8% with radiotherapy after surgery compared to 12% for surgery alone [44]. The improved overall survival following breast conservation in our study may also reflect patient cohorts with more favourable tumour characteristics favouring survival despite matching of cohorts for example, Chen et al. only included T1‐2 tumours [16]. Furthermore, it has been postulated that the increased tissue trauma and systemic inflammatory response from mastectomy compared to breast conservation may promote development of distant micrometastases, thus impacting survival [45].

Our study's finding of increased local recurrence after breast conservation surgery is more intuitive and highlights the need for conscientious margin clearance. While previous international guidelines recommended at least 1 cm margins for borderline and malignant tumours, the safety of clear but narrower margins has been evaluated in a number of studies. An American multi‐centre study of 550 patients with phyllodes tumours of all grades showed no difference in local recurrence between close (2 mm or less) or wider (over 2 mm) margins [46]. Further, a 2024 retrospective study of 921 patients from the Netherlands Cancer Registry with both borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours demonstrated no difference in locoregional recurrence for patients with narrow (1 mm or less) or wider margins [19]. We also note the trend in the more recent studies in our meta‐analysis towards no difference in local recurrence based on surgical approach, with all but one study from 2018 onwards [39] showing no difference. This may reflect the impact of adjuvant radiotherapy on improved local control in these more recent studies.

The role of chemotherapy, however, is much more limited. While over 40% of clinicians internationally would consider adjuvant chemotherapy for malignant phyllodes tumours [47], the evidence is sparse. Only small observational studies have evaluated the role for adjuvant chemotherapy in malignant phyllodes tumours, such as a study of 28 patients comparing adjuvant doxorubicin and dacarbazine to surgery alone which demonstrated no difference in five‐year disease‐free survival [48]. This again highlights the need for local surgical control with clear margins and consideration of adjuvant radiotherapy.

We evaluated overall survival, disease‐free survival and local recurrence for both malignant phyllodes patients and a combined borderline and malignant cohort. We analysed a combined cohort due to the genomic similarities between borderline and malignant tumours as well as the insufficient data for a separate borderline analysis. While the combined cohort was inherently more heterogeneous, the lack of difference in pooled relative risk between malignant and combined cohorts in itself underscores these genomic similarities between borderline and malignant tumours. It has been shown that the chromosomal abnormalities in both borderline and malignant tumours cannot be distinguished from one another [49] and exome sequencing has shown that both borderline and malignant tumours harbour mutations in a number of the same tumour suppressor genes including NF1, RB1 and TP53. Given our similar results in both cohorts and these genetic similarities between both borderline and malignant tumours, these may be considered a similar clinical and pathologic entity and follow a similar treatment paradigm, as distinct from benign phyllodes tumours.

Our study has important implications regarding the clinical treatment approach for individuals with phyllodes tumours. Our findings overall suggest that, for those with borderline or malignant phyllodes tumours, breast conservation should be offered, where it is feasible, as part of an individualised and multidisciplinary approach. This is in line with the general trend towards the de‐escalation of surgical treatment for breast cancer of all subtypes.

Strengths

To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review and meta‐analysis to evaluate the impact of surgical approach on survival for patients with borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours. It also provides an updated meta‐analysis on local recurrence data in line with contemporary practice, particularly in the setting of conscientious margin clearance and adjuvant radiation. Our findings have important therapeutic implications in helping to guide surgical decision making for those with phyllodes tumours as part of an individualised and multidisciplinary approach. A comprehensive literature search of multiple databases was performed.

Limitations

The limitations of our study include the lack of matching of patient and tumour factors in some studies and variations in adjuvant treatment within and between studies, particularly regarding adjuvant radiation. This may have led to heterogeneity not detected by the statistical analysis. There were also insufficient survival and recurrence data for analysis of a separate borderline cohort, limiting application of the results of the combined cohort to borderline phyllodes patients. The large and varied time frame of included studies and recruitment of patients may have also led to variations in treatment approach and outcomes, not in line with contemporary practice. Finally, as a systematic review of published rather than original patient data, our study was susceptible to publication bias.

Future Directions

Further studies should evaluate survival and recurrence outcomes for a separate borderline subgroup when there are sufficient published data available to improve applicability to patients with borderline phyllodes tumours specifically. Further to this, future studies should evaluate the impact of adjuvant radiotherapy on survival and recurrence for borderline and malignant tumours separately to help guide multidisciplinary management of patients with these tumours. Further research should also evaluate the risk of locoregional recurrence with narrow versus wide surgical margins, in line with recent data showing no difference [19], to help guide the stringency of margin guidelines. Finally, there may be a future role for immunotherapy, with emerging data for the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in certain histologic subtypes of soft tissue sarcomas [50].

Conclusions

There are limited data regarding the optimal surgical approach for borderline and malignant phyllodes tumours, which can exhibit unpredictable patterns of local recurrence and distant metastasis. Our systematic review and meta‐analysis demonstrated an overall survival benefit for those undergoing breast conservation compared to mastectomy, no difference in disease‐free survival, and an increased risk of local recurrence following breast conservation. We suggest that breast conservation should be offered, where feasible, as part of an individualised multidisciplinary treatment approach.

Presence and associations of retinal pigment epithelium and outer retinal atrophy: the Beijing eye study
Authors: Jost B. Jonas, Songhomitra Panda-Jonas, Jie Xu, Rahul A. Jonas, Ya Xing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The goal of the study was to assess the presence of an incomplete (iRORA) and complete (cRORA) retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) and outer retinal atrophy in a general population and explore their associations with other parameters. Participants of the population-based Beijing Eye Study with age-related macular degeneration (AMD), polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) or those without retinal diseases, underwent optical coherence tomography of the macula. Among 5894 eyes, presence of iRORA and cRORA increased from 0/5117 (0%) and 0/5117 (0%) in the normal group and 0/457 (0%) and 0/457 (0%) in early AMD to 5/277 (1.8%;95%CI:0.3,3.3) and 0/277 (0%) in intermediate AMD, 6/6 (100%) and 6/6 (100%) in geographic atrophy, 0/2 (0%) and 2/2 (100%) in exudative AMD, and 10/30 (33.3%;95%CI:17.8,48.8) and 6/30 (25.0%;95%CI:12.0,38.0) in PCV, respectively. Higher iRORA presence was associated (multivariable analysis) with higher AMD stage (OR:54.7;95%CI:6.68,449;P < 0.001), higher PCV presence (OR:1.523 × 105;95%CI:834,2.785 × 107;P < 0.001), and higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen (OR:19.2;95%CI:1.94,190;P < 0.001). Drusen number (OR:0.97;95%CI:0.91,1.03;P = 0.27) and subfoveal choroidal thickness (OR:1.003;95%CI:0.994,1.013;P = 0.49) were not associated. Higher cRORA presence correlated with higher PCV presence (OR:48.6;95%CI:4.58,517;P = 0.001) and higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen (OR:120;95%CI:13.1,1094;P < 0.001). iRORAs and cRORAs were spatially associated with ellipsoid zone and external limiting membrane defects and intraretinal hyperreflective foci (iHRFs) in the outer and inner retinal layers. The findings suggest that iRORAs and cRORAs presences were relatively low in our population-based study, and were associated with higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen but not with drusen number or subfoveal choroidal thickness. The spatial association with ellipsoid zone defects and external limiting membrane defects and iHRF in the outer retinal layer and beyond fits with the notion of an RPE cell migration playing a role in the development of iRORAs and cRORAs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55175-8.

Introduction

Incomplete retinal pigment epithelium and outer retinal atrophy (iRORA) and complete retinal pigment epithelium and outer retinal atrophy (cRORA) are morphological characteristics of age-related macular degeneration (AMD) and other macular disorders1–16 After the description of its terminology by Guymer and colleagues, studies examined the associations between iRORA as risk factor for the incidence of geographic atrophy, the role of other morphological features of AMD as risk factors for the development of iRORA, and the relationships between iRORA and other morphological characteristics of AMD1–8.

These previous investigations had limitations as they had recruited their study participants in a hospital-based manner and thus had the inherent risk of a referral bias in the selection of the patients, that the number of patients involved was relatively small, that either no control group was included or if, then consisting of only relatively few individuals, and that only a small number of potentially associated and risk factors was examined without taking into account inter-dependencies between these parameters and without considering non-ocular systemic parameters. We therefore performed the present study with a population-based recruitment of a relatively large number of individuals including those of an unaffected control group, who underwent a multitude of ocular and systemic examinations to explore the presence of iRORAs and cRORAs and their associations with ocular and systemic parameters in a general population.

Methods

The participants of the Beijing Eye Study were recruited in a population-based manner in an urban area of Central Beijing and in a rural region of Greater Beijing region.17 As described in detail previously, all individuals aged 50 + years and living in the study regions were eligible. The study consisted of 3468 (78.8%) participants (mean age: 64.6 ± 9.8 years; range: 50–93 years) out of 4403 eligible persons17. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Beijing Tongren Hospital (date: 20.12.2000; an identification number of the ethics committee approval was not given), with all study participants giving their written informed consent. It was confirmed that all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Out of the total cohort of the Beijing Eye Study, we included into the present investigation all eyes with available macula OCT images and without any retinal disorder other than AMD and polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV)18–21. The reason to exclude patients with retinal diseases other than AMD or PCV was to avoid confounding effects by these retinal disorders then concurrent with AMD or PCV. The recommendations of the Beckman Initiative for Macular Research Classification Committee were applied for the definition of AMD22.

The study design included a series of ocular investigations such refractometry and measurement of best-correcting refractive error, pneumotonometry, biomicroscopic examination of the anterior and posterior eye segment by slit lamp, biometry of the right eyes (Lenstar 900 Optical Biometer, Haag-Streit, 3098 Koeniz, Switzerland), and imaging of the cornea and lens (slit-lamp digital photography, camera type BG-4, Topcon Medical Systems, Inc, Tokyo, Japan), and of the macula and optic disc (fundus camera type CR6-45NM, Canon Inc, Tokyo, Japan)17–21 The macula and optic nerve head were additionally imaged by optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Spectralis®, Heidelberg Engineering Co, Heidelberg, Germany), using a macular volume scan (30°×30° field, 31 B-scan lines) and a macular star (6 B-scan lines), with each scan line being based on 100 averaged scans.

Applying the recommendations made by the Classification of Atrophy Meeting Group, we defined iRORA on the OCT images as a region of signal hypertransmission into the choroid, spatially corresponding to a region of attenuation or disruption of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) and signs of overlying outer retinal layer thinning or retinal photoreceptor degeneration (Figs. 1 and 2)1. A RPE tear was excluded as cause for the absence of outer retinal layer. A complete RPE and outer retinal atrophy (cRORA) was defined as a region of hypertransmission of at least 250 μm in diameter, and a zone of attenuation or disruption of the RPE of at least 250 μm in diameter, and evidence of overlying photoreceptor degeneration, all occurring in the absence of signs of an RPE tear2. As a corollary, iRORA described the AMD stage where these OCT signs were present but did not completely fulfil all the criteria for cRORA.

On all macular OCT scans, we additionally assessed the presence and location of other OCT-based morphological characteristics, i.e., presence and location of ellipsoid zone defects and external limiting membrane defects, localized interdigitation zone thinnings, localized outer nuclear layer thinnings, intraretinal hyperreflective foci (iHRF), reticular pseudodrusen, macular drusen, localized hypertransmissions of the Interdigitation zone and localized detachments of the RPE basal membrane from Bruch´s membrane, presence of the foveal three-layer sign (defined as the visibility of the Bruch´s membrane line, interdigitation zone and ellipsoid zone as three separate lines in the foveolar location on the horizontal OCT image running through the foveola), presences of ophthalmoscopical hypopigmentations or hyperpigmentations in the macular region and posterior detachments of the vitreous body, and presence and amount of hyperreflective bodies in the preretinal vitreous cortex17–21. We defined iHRF as distinct, bright, single structures with a diameter of smaller than about 25 µm and which were located between the RPE and the inner limiting membrane on any of the macular scans. If single iHRF were agglomerated, the whole hyperreflective area could be considerably larger than 25 µm. The iHRF had to extend beyond the interdigitation zone. HRFs were differentiated from cross-sections of retinal blood vessels, if the hyperreflective spots were spatially associated with retinal blood vessels, in particular if located in the inner plexiform layer. If the HRFs occurred in an agglomeration, we differentiated between a plume (orientated vertically on the OCT images), smoke or as a cloud. A plume-like or smoke-like arrangement of iHRBs had unsharp borders and covered a region with a diameter ranging between 10 µm and 100 µm. Localized thinnings of the interdigitation zone had a length of at maximum 250 µm on the OCT scan and occurred without a marked hypertransmission of the interdigitation zone and without adjacent reticular pseudodrusen, drusen or an elevation of the Interdigitation zone within a distance of 300 µm (Fig. 3). They differed from small geographic atrophies which showed on the fundus photographs a bright depigmentation with sharp borders upon ophthalmoscopy. In contrast, the localized interdigitation zone thinnings either showed none or only a relatively faint depigmentation on the macula photographs. In addition, geographic atrophies were associated with a marked or complete RPE hypertransmission and were often surrounded by drusen. Localized interdigitation zone thinnings differed from iRORA by an intact RPE in the vicinity of localized interdigitation zone thinnings, without drusen, reticular pseudodrusen or other irregularities of the interdigitation zone. According to the guidelines published by the Asia-Pacific Vitreo-Retina Society, we defined PCV by the presence of polypoidal lesions as the hallmark characteristics, with hyporeflective round sub-RPE ring-like structures with a hyperreflective outline correlating with the polypoidal lesions, in addition to other characteristics, like the double-layer sign with a sharp pigment epithelium detachment (Figs. 1 and 2)23. We defined reticular pseudodrusen as small yellow-white lesions with a diameter of about 125–250 µm on the fundus photographs and on the near-infrared reflectance fundus images provided by the OCT device. The lesions corresponded to subretinal material and were located on top of the RPE on the OCT images. Using all OCT scans, we semi-quantified the total amount of reticular pseudodrusen in a scale from grade “0” for no pseudodrusen”, grade “1” for few pseudodrusen (i.e., less than 10 in all macular OICT section together), grade “2” for “pseudodrusen in moderate number” (i.e., 10 to 25 pseudodrusen on all macular OCT scans together), grade “3” for “marked number of pseudodrusen” (i.e., 26 to 50 pseudodrusen on all macular OCT scans together), to grade “4” for the highest category of the number of pseudodrusen (i.e., more than 50 pseudodrusen on all macular OCT scans together).

All images included into the study were examined by a trained ophthalmologist (JBJ), with the fundus photographs, the near-infrared reflectance fundus images (provided by the OCT device), and the OCT images simultaneously available. Parallel assessments of randomly selected smaller subsets of study participants were performed together with SPJ, JX, RAJ and YXW. In situations of a difference in the diagnosis, the final diagnosis was jointly made.

The statistical analysis was conducted using a commercially available statistical software package (SPSS for Windows, version 29.0, IBM-SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). We used the term “presence” as description for the percentage of the study population that was affected with iRORA or with cRORA at the given time of the examination of the study participants. It referred to the principal presence of iRORA or of cRORA per eye, independent of the overall number of iRORAs or cRORAs per eye. We assessed the presences of iRORA and cRORA and other morphologic characteristics of the macula as means and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of the means. Associations between the presence of iRORAs (and cRORAs) and the presence of the other fundus features and ocular and systemic parameters were examined in binary regression analyses, first in a univariable manner including all eyes which had been examined. Based on the results of the univariate analyses, we conducted multivariable analyses, for which only one eye per study participant had been taken into account. The multivariable analyses included as independent variables all parameters which were significantly associated with the presence of iRORA (and cRORA) in the univariable analyses. In a step-by-step manner, we then dropped out of the list of independent variables those parameters which were no longer significantly associated with the ELM defect presence. We calculated odds ratios (Ors) and their 95% CIs. A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

The cohort of this investigation consisted of 5894 eyes (3010 (51.1%) right eyes) of 3178 participants (1378 (43.4%) men). The mean age was 63.9 ± 9.5 years (median: 62.0 years; range: 50–93 years) and the mean axial length was 23.25 ± 1.14 mm (median: 23.13 mm; range: 18.96 mm to 30.88 mm). Age was significantly younger (63.9 ± 9.5 years versus 68.8 ± 10.3 years; P < 0.001) in the group included into the present investigation than in the group of Beijing Eye Study participants not included into the present investigation. Both groups did not vary significantly in sex (men / women: 43.4% / 56.6% versus 43.4% / 56.6%; P = 1.00) and axial length (23.25 ± 1.14 versus 23.32 ± 1.15 mm; P = 0.27).

The study population consisted of 457 (7.8%) eyes with early AMD, 277 (4.7%) eyes with intermediate AMD, 6 (0.10%) eyes with geographic atrophy, 2 eyes with exudative AMD (0.03%), 30 (0.5%) eyes with PCV, and 5122 (86.9%) eyes without signs of AMD. In the total study population, iRORAs were detected in 21 eyes (21/5894 or 0.4%; 95% CI: 0.0, 1.0), and cRORAs in 12 eyes (12/5894 or 0.2%; 95% CI: 0.0, 0.4). In the normal group, iRORAs and cRORAs did not occur, so that an association with age could not be assessed. The location of the iRORAs was only foveally in 3 (14%) eyes, only extrafoveally in 8 (38%) eyes, and foveally and extrafoveally in 10 (48%) eyes. The mean number of iRORAs per eye with iRORAs was 1.6 ± 16 lesions (median: 1; range: 1–8).

In the whole study cohort, the presences of iRORAs and cRORAs increased from 0/5117 (0%) and 0/5117 (0%) in the normal group and 0/457 (0%) and 0/457 (0%) in the group with early AMD stage, to 5/277 (1.8%; 95% CI: 0.3, 3.3) and 0/277 (0%) in the group with intermediate AMD stage, and to 6/6 (100%) and 6/6 (100%) in the geographic atrophy group, respectively. iRORA and cRORA presences were in the exudative AMD group 0/2 (0%) and 2/2 (100%), respectively, and in the PCV group 10/30 (33.3%; 95% CI: 17.8, 48.8) and 6/30 (25.0%; 95% CI: 12.0, 38.0), respectively. All 21 eyes with iRORAs showed defects in the ellipsoid zone and external limiting membrane, and iHRF beyond the level of the ellipsoid zone. In 18 of the 21 (86%) eyes, a plume-like or smoke-like arrangement of the iHRF was present in the outer nuclear layer or beyond. All 11 eyes with intermediate stage or late stage (geographic atrophy) of AMD and iRORA showed reticular pseudodrusen.

In univariable analysis, a higher presence of iRORAs was associated (P < 0.05) with the systemic parameters of older age, lower cognitive score, younger age of mother at birth, higher presence of a history of a previous stroke, lower diastolic blood pressure, higher presence of arterial hypertension, lower estimated cerebrospinal fluid pressure, and lower physical activity score (Table 1). A higher presence of ELM defects was associated with the ocular parameters of worse best corrected visual acuity, higher presence of nuclear and cortical cataract, higher presence and stage of AMD, higher presence of defects in the EZ, higher presence and amount of reticular pseudodrusen, higher number of drusen, higher presences of shallow and bullous RPE detachments, localized RPE thinnings, outer nuclear layer thinning, macular hyperpigmentations and hypopigmentations, dilated large choroidal vessels, hypertransmissions of the Interdigitation zone, iHRF overall, iHRF in the EZ and beyond the EZ and iHRF arranged in a smoke-like or plume-like manner, a more internal location of iHRF, higher presence and extent of outer nuclear layer clouding, higher stage of posterior vitreous detachment, higher presence of hyperreflective spots on the inner retinal surface and epiretinal membranes, and lower presences of a visible Henle´s layer and foveal three-layer sign (Table 1).

Associations (univariable analysis) between the presence of an incomplete retinal pigment epithelial and outer retinal atrophy (iRORA) and systemic and ocular parameters in participants of the Beijing Eye Study, affected by age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy, or free of retinal or optic nerve diseases.

In the multivariable analysis, we first dropped due to the lack of statistical significance the systemic parameters of cognitive function (P = 0.94), physical activity score (P = 0.51), arterial hypertension presence (P = 0.49), diastolic blood pressure (P = 0.07), cerebrospinal fluid pressure (P = 0.45) and history of cerebral stroke (P = 0.20). After adding to the analysis those ocular parameters, which were significantly associated with the iRORA presence in the univariable analysis, we dropped all those parameters which were no longer significantly associated with the iRORA presence. In the final model, a higher iRORA presence correlated with higher AMD stage, higher presence of PCV and higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen. In that model, iRORA presence was not associated with the presence of drusen (P = 0.20) and subfoveal choroidal thickness (P = 0. 16) (Table 2). After exclusion of highly myopic individuals (n = 84) with an axial length of ≥ 26.0 mm, the results of the analysis were similar (Supplementary Table 1).

A higher cRORA presence correlated with higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen and higher presence of PCV, while again the number of drusen and subfoveal choroidal thickness were not significantly associated in that model (Table 2).

Discussion

In this population-based study from China, the presences of iRORAs and cRORAs increased from 0% to 0% in the normal group and the group with early AMD stage to 1.8% and 0% in the group with intermediate AMD stage, respectively, and to 100% in the eyes with geographic atrophy group. iRORA and cRORA presences were in the exudative AMD group 0% and 100%, respectively, and in the PCV group 33% and 25%, respectively. Higher iRORA presence was associated with higher AMD stage, higher PCV presence, and higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen, while drusen number and subfoveal choroidal thickness were not significantly associated with iRORA presence in that model. Higher cRORA presence correlated with higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen and higher presence of PCV, while again the number of drusen and subfoveal choroidal thickness were not significantly associated in that model.

The observations made in our study agree with previous reports in the literature that iRORAs are associated with an advanced stage of AMD, while due to the design as a cross-sectional investigation, the present study could not address the iRORA-related risk of AMD progression as it was examined in the previous investigations1–15. In a previous study conducted by Wu and colleagues, time-to-event analyses revealed that prevalent or incident iRORA was related to an increased rate of development of geographic atrophy, as was nascent geographic atrophy. However, only the specific features of nascent geographic atrophy, and not iRORA, were associated with an increased rate of geographic atrophy development when both features were included in the same multivariable model. The proportion of variance explained in the time to geographic atrophy development by iRORA itself was lower than explained by nascent geographic atrophy alone11. In a longitudinal study performed by Flores and associates, iRORA was the characteristic that showed a higher probability of developing late AMD, followed by ellipsoid zone disruption. The combination of iRORA and ellipsoid zone disruption marked a high risk of progression to cRORA within 2 years8. Our study adds an information on the presence and associated factors of iRORA and cRORA in a general population. Subsequently, the presence of iRORAs (0% in early AMD and 1.8% in intermediate AMD) was relatively low in our study cohort, in contrast to a figure of 10.7% in eyes with intermediate AMD in the European, multicenter MACUSTAR study12.

Interestingly, all iRORAs and cRORAs in our study cohort were spatially correlated with iHRF, which were located in the outer nuclear layer or in more inner retinal layers. Correspondingly, iRORAs (and cRORAs) were spatially associated with defects in the ellipsoid zone and external limiting membrane. It would fit with an unproven speculative hypothesis by the authors that the iHRF may represent activated and intraretinally migrating RPE cells, which might slip through defects in the ellipsoid zone and external limiting membrane into the outer nuclear layer and beyond. As a consequence of the migration of RPE cells, then missing on Bruch´s membrane, irregularities and defects in the RPE-layer would develop, leading to defects in the photoreceptor layer and to hypertransmissions of the RPE-band. It could explain the development of iRORAs, and when complete, of cRORAs. Also interesting, a higher iRORA presence was correlated with a higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen, while the number of drusen was not associated with the iRORA presence in the multivariable model (Table 2). It would fit with an unproven speculative hypothesis by the authors, that reticular pseudodrusen could represent activated RPE cells, ready to migrate intraretinally however blocked by an intact ellipsoid zone and intact external limiting membrane which might act as a migration barrier. If, however these structures were defect, the RPE cells might get the possibility to move into the retina so that Bruch´s membrane would be left de-populated.

When the findings of our study are discussed, the study limitations should be taken into account. First, the 31 B-scan volume scans over a 30° x 30° macular field were not very densely arranged, so that small iRORAs might have been remained undetected. Second, eyes with other retinal disease as potential cause for iRORAs and cRORAs, such as central serous choroidopathy, were generally excluded from the study. In the case of borderline findings however, eyes with a mixed morphology of central serous choroidopathy and AMD, such few eyes might have erroneously been included into the study. Third, our study population was composed of Han Chinese, so that the findings obtained in our study may not directly be transferred on individuals of other ethnicities. It may hold true in particular since the East Asians and Caucasians differ in the presence and proportion of exudative AMD versus PCV. Fourth, the cohort of the present study was younger than the group of participants of the Beijing Eye Study who had not been included into the present study. This difference in age could be explained by that the individuals excluded from the present investigations had retinal diseases other than AMD or PCV and which were usually associated with older age. Fifth, due to the cross-sectional design of our investigation, we could not assess risk factors but only associated factors related with the presence of iRORAs and cRORAs.

In conclusion, the presences of iRORAs and cRORAs were relatively low in our population-based study, with a higher iRORA and cRORA presence associated with higher AMD stage, higher PCV presence and higher presence of reticular pseudodrusen but not with drusen number or subfoveal choroidal thickness. The spatial association with defects in the ellipsoid zone and external limiting membrane and with iHRF (potentially representing migrating RPE cells) in the outer retinal layer and beyond fits with the notion of a potential migration of activated RPE cells playing a role in the development of iRORAs and cRORAs.

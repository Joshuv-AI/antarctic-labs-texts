Comparative metabolomic profiling reveals the superiority of Apocynum venetum as a source for Apocynum tea
Authors: Li Jiang, Zhiqiang Zhu, Yukun Wu, Bin Wen, Siyu Zhang, Junyi Zhan, Nana Su
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Apocynum tea market primarily consists of two varieties, Apocynum venetum and Apocynum pictum, which exhibit significant differences in quality and market value. To elucidate these differences, this study systematically evaluated their nutritional composition, antioxidant capacity, and metabolic profiles. Key findings include: The total flavonoid and total phenolic content in tea infusions of A. venetum are higher than in A. pictum and their potential antioxdant activety was assessed. Non-targeted metabolomics analysis revealed 1021 differential metabolites, with 560 more abundant in A. venetum tea and 461 in A. pictum tea. These metabolites are primarily flavonoids, phenolic acids and alkaloids. KEGG pathway analysis highlighting enrichment in “Flavone and flavonol biosynthesis” and “Biosynthesis of caffeic acid derivatives”. Network pharmacology analysis identified hypertension-related targets, such as CA2, ALOX5, and PTGS, supporting the cardiovascular benefits of A. venetum tea. These findings offering valuable insights for its commercial and therapeutic applications.

Introduction

Apocynum is a perennial plant belonging to the genus Apocynum in the family Apocynaceae (Dorjee et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2012). It is widely distributed across China, particularly in ecologically diverse regions such as the Qinling Mountains, Huai River Basin, Yangtze River Basin, and the northern foothills of the Kunlun Mountains (Zheng et al., 2022). This species exhibits remarkable environmental adaptability and is capable of surviving and reproducing even in arid desert regions (Abubakar et al., 2022; Draft et al., 2019). Studies have shown that Apocynum venetum seeds display strong drought resistance during germination and high tolerance to salt stress (Jiang, She, et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021). In addition to its resilience, Apocynum holds significant economic value (Jiang, Wu, et al., 2021). The bast fibers extracted from its bark can be used as raw materials for textiles, offering superior properties such as moisture-wicking, antibacterial activity, antioxidant capacity, and UV resistance compared to conventional cotton fibers (Lou et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2020). These qualities have enabled Apocynum textiles to gain a prominent position in the fiber market. In regions such as Xinjiang, where Apocynum is extensively cultivated, its flowers serve as a valuable nectar source for honey production, resulting in honey with a distinctive flavor and high nutritional value (Jiang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2022). The leaves of Apocynum (known as Luobuma in Chinese) have long been used in traditional Chinese medicine to strengthen the heart, promote diuresis, and alleviate symptoms such as edema (Xie et al., 2025). Tea made from stir-fried Apocynum leaves has also been reported to provide various health benefits, including antifatigue, antihypertensive, and antioxidant effects (Chen et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2025). As a result, the plant's naturally derived antioxidant and antifatigue constituents have attracted increasing attention from researchers.

Apocynum venetum tea has garnered widespread attention owing to its diverse health-promoting properties (Abubakar et al., 2024). The benefits of Apocynum tea are mainly attributed to its complex mixture of bioactive compounds, particularly flavonoids (e.g., quercetin, hyperoside, and isoquercitrin) and phenolic acids (including chlorogenic acid and caffeic acid derivatives) (Guoping et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2025). Quercetin and hyperoside have been shown to enhance antioxidant defenses and to stimulate insulin secretion by upregulating superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), and glutathione peroxidase (Gpx) levels in diabetic animal models (Jin et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022). Isoquercitrin is a natural flavonoid that exhibits Nrf2-dependent antioxidant activity and inhibits osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption by activating the Nrf2 signaling pathway (Zhang et al., 2024). Moreover, chlorogenic acid metabolites have been shown to improve vascular endothelial function and enhance nitric oxide bioavailability by reducing oxidative stress caused by reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby contributing to blood pressure reduction (Naveed et al., 2017). Caffeic acid and its derivatives also exert significant cardiovascular effects, including vasodilation, reduced heart rate, and inhibition of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), all of which contribute to lowering blood pressure (Bujor et al., 2021). Notably, compared to many traditional plant sources, the unique flavonoids in Apocynum tea exhibit higher bioavailability, which may be a key factor underlying its superior cardiovascular protective effects observed in clinical studies (Xie et al., 2012).

Currently, the quality evaluation standards for Apocynum venetum tea primarily focus on three conventional parameters: chemical composition, sensory characteristics, and in vitro functional properties (Zhao et al., 2022). The chemical composition of tea primarily comprises total flavonoids and total phenols. Sensory quality refers to the color intensity and taste characteristics of the tea infusion, while functional characteristics mainly include its antioxidant capacity (Chen & Lin, 2015; Jayabalan et al., 2014). However, there is still a significant gap in understanding regarding the correlation between these standardized indicators and the price fluctuations observed in the commercial market (Ma et al., 2024). There are two main types of Apocynum, namely Apocynum venetum and Apocynum pictum (Jiang, Wu, et al., 2021). However, consumer preferences and market prices favor A. venetum tea, which is usually priced higher than A. pictum tea.

To address this issue, this study employed a systematic comparison of A. venetum and A. pictum tea using metabolomics. Specifically, tea infusions prepared from A. venetum and A. pictum leaves were used to determine total phenolic content (TPC), total flavonoid content (TFC), and antioxidant capacity using ABTS and DPPH assays. In parallel, untargeted metabolomic profiling was performed on the tea leaves using UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS to explore compositional differences in metabolites between the two species. We conducted a systematic comparison of their differential metabolite profiles. In addition, network pharmacology analyses were conducted to predict the pharmacological correlations of key differential metabolites. By integrating these analyses, this study aims to provide a scientific basis for the quality differentiation and market positioning of Apocynum tea. These investigations are essential for bridging the gap between biochemical composition and commercial value, and for supporting the development of Apocynum tea as a functional food with potential health benefits and as a promising natural agent for preventing oxidative stress-related diseases, such as hypertension.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and tea infusion preparation

The Apocynum pictum and Apocynum venetum tea leaves used in this study were purchased from the Lop Nur-Tarim River region in Xinjiang, China. The brewing method of Apocynum tea was performed with reference to the protocol described by Zhao et al. (2022) with minor modifications. Precisely 1.5 g of tea leaves were weighed and infused with 100 mL of boiling water (100 °C) in a 150 mL conical flask for 5, 10, and 15 min, respectively. The steeping times of 5 and 10 min were selected to reflect typical household brewing practices, while the 15-min duration was included as an extended condition to simulate the upper extraction limit and better characterize the release kinetics of bioactive compounds in different tea types. After infusion, the mixtures were filtered, and the filtrates were collected as the final tea infusion samples. All samples were stored at 4 °C for 45 min in the dark before analysis. Three biological replicates were performed for each experiment.

Total flavonoids (TFs)

The total flavonoid content (TFC) of A. pictum and A. venetum tea infusions was determined using the aluminum chloride colorimetric method (Gursoy et al., 2009) with slight modifications. Briefly, 1 mL of sample was mixed with ethanol in a 10 mL test tube to adjust the volume to 5 mL. Then, 1 mL of the resulting mixture was taken for the subsequent colorimetric reaction, to which 0.3 mL of 5 % sodium nitrite solution was added and vortexed. After 6 min, 0.5 mL of 10 % aluminum nitrate solution was added, followed by 6 mL of 4 % sodium hydroxide solution. The final volume was adjusted with ethanol to maintain consistency across samples. The mixture was thoroughly mixed and allowed to stand for 12 min. Absorbance was measured at 510 nm using a microplate reader. TFC was calculated based on a rutin standard curve and expressed as mg rutin equivalents (RE) per g of dry weight (DW).

Total phenols (TPs)

The total phenolic content (TPC) was determined using the Folin-Ciocalteu method with modifications (Fang et al., 2008). Briefly, 1 mL of tea infusion was mixed with distilled water in a 25 mL test tube to a final volume of 10 mL. Then, 1.0 mL of Folin-Ciocalteu reagent was added and the mixture was vortexed thoroughly. After incubation in the dark for 5 min, 2 mL of 12 % sodium carbonate solution was added, and the final volume was adjusted with distilled water. The absorbance was measured at 765 nm using a UV–visible spectrophotometer. TPC was calculated based on a gallic acid standard curve and expressed as mg gallic acid equivalents (GAE) per g of dry weight (DW).

Antioxidant activities measurement

The antioxidant capacity was evaluated using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer's instructions. For each measurement, 1 mL of tea infusion sample was prepared. The following kits were employed: DPPH radical scavenging capacity assay kit (A153–1-1, Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). Total antioxidant capacity assay kit (ABTS method, A015–2-1, Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). All assays were performed in triplicate (n = 3) to ensure statistical reliability.

Dry sample extraction

Using vacuum freeze-drying technology, place the biological samples in a lyophilizer (Scientz-100F), then grinding (30 Hz, 1.5 min) the samples to powder form by using a grinder (MM 400, Retsch). Next, weigh 50 mg of sample powder using an electronic balance (MS105DΜ) and add 1200 μL of −20 °C pre-cooled 70 % methanolic aqueous internal standard extract (less than 50 mg added at the rate of 1200 μL extractant per 50 mg sample). Vortex once every 30 min for 30 s, for a total of 6 times. After centrifugation (rotation speed 12,000 rpm, 3 min), the supernatant was aspirated, and the sample was filtered through a microporous membrane (0.22 μm pore size) and stored in the injection vial for UPLC-MS/MS analysis.

UPLC conditions

The sample extracts were analyzed using an UPLC-ESI-MS/MS system (UPLC, ExionLC™ ADˈ https: //sciex.com.cn/) and Tandem mass spectrometry system (https://sciex.com.cn/). The analytical conditions were as follows, UPLC: column, Agilent SB-C18 (1.8 μm, 2.1 mm * 100 mm); The mobile phase was consisted of solvent A, pure water with 0.1 % formic acid, and solvent B, acetonitrile with 0.1 % formic acid. Sample measurements were performed with a gradient program that employed the starting conditions of 95 % A, 5 % B. Within 9 min, a linear gradient to 5 % A, 95 % B was programmed, and a composition of 5 % A, 95 % B was kept for 1 min. Subsequently, a composition of 95 % A, 5.0 % B was adjusted within 1.1 min and kept for 2.9 min. The flow velocity was set as 0.35 mL per minute; The column oven was set to 40 °C; The injection volume was 2 μL. The effluent was directly introduced into a triple quadrupole-linear ion trap mass spectrometer for compound detection and quantification under optimized conditions, as described below.

ESI-q trap-MS/MS

Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted using a QTRAP 4500 system (SCIEX, China) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The ESI source operation parameters were as follows: source temperature 500 °C; ion spray voltage (IS) 5500 V (positive ion mode)/−4500 V (negative ion mode); ion source gas I (GSI), gas II(GSII), curtain gas (CUR) were set at 50, 60, and 25 psi, respectively; the collision-activated dissociation (CAD) was high. QQQ scans were acquired as MRM experiments with collision gas (nitrogen) set to medium. DP (declustering potential) and CE (collision energy) for individual MRM transitions was done with further DP and CE optimization. A specific set of MRM transitions were monitored for each period according to the metabolites eluted within this period.

PCA

Unsupervised PCA (principal component analysis) was performed by statistics function prcomp within R (www.r-project.org). The data was unit variance scaled before unsupervised PCA.

Differential metabolites selected

For two-group analysis, differential metabolites were determined by VIP (VIP > 1) and absolute Log2FC (|Log2FC| ≥ 1.0). VIP values were extracted from OPLS-DA result, which also contain score plots and permutation plots, was generated using R package MetaboAnalystR. The data was log transform (log2) and mean centering before OPLS-DA. In order to avoid overfitting, a permutation test (200 permutations) was performed.

KEGG annotation and enrichment analysis

Identified metabolites were annotated using KEGG Compound database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/ compound/), annotated metabolites were then mapped to KEGG Pathway database (http://www.kegg.jp/ kegg/pathway.html).

The total flavonoid content (TFC) and total phenolic content (TPC) of A. pictum and A. venetum tea infusions at different brewing times are presented in Fig. 1B-1C. The TFC of A. pictum tea reached its peak (0.027 mg/mL) at 10 min and then gradually declined. In contrast, A. venetum tea exhibited its highest TFC (0.063 mg/mL) at 5 min, followed by a stabilization trend. At all time points, the TFC of A. venetum tea was significantly higher than that of A. pictum tea (p < 0.05). Similarly, the TPC of A. pictum tea increased gradually and reached 0.133 mg/mL at 15 min, whereas A. venetum tea displayed the highest TPC (0.23 mg/mL) at 15 min. At all time points, the TPC of A. venetum tea was significantly higher than that of A. pictum tea (p < 0.01).

These results suggest that A. venetum tea exhibits a more efficient release of bioactive compounds, as evidenced by its significantly higher TFC and TPC at all brewing times. This further implies that A. venetum tea may possess stronger antioxidant capacity and superior quality characteristics.

Comparison of antioxidant capacity between A. pictum and A. venetum tea infusions

Considering the pivotal role of antioxidant capacity in determining the medicinal value of herbal teas, the antioxidant potential of A. pictum and A. venetum tea infusions was assessed using DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging assays at different infusion times.

In the DPPH assay, A. venetum demonstrated markedly higher radical scavenging activity, with values of 14.05 μg/mL at 5 min and 13.45 μg/mL at 15 min. In contrast, A. pictum showed lower activities of 11.68 μg/mL and 11.38 μg/mL at the corresponding time points (Fig. 1D). Interestingly, a transient decrease in DPPH scavenging activity was observed in A. venetum at 10 min, which may be attributed to the dynamic release rates or temporary degradation of certain antioxidant compounds during infusion. In the ABTS assay, antioxidant capacity was expressed as trolox equivalent antioxidant capacity (TEAC). A. venetum exhibited TEAC values of 1.36 mM and 1.34 mM at 5 and 15 min, respectively, while A. pictum showed lower values of 0.92 mM and 0.89 mM (Fig. 1E). Notably, both teas maintained relatively stable TEAC values throughout the 15-min infusion period, suggesting the antioxidant components are stable during short-term brewing.

These results align with the trends observed in total flavonoid content (TFC) and total phenolic content (TPC), further supporting that A. venetum consistently exhibits superior antioxidant capacity compared to A. pictum at all tested infusion times. This enhanced activity is likely attributable to its higher concentrations of bioactive compounds, particularly phenolic acids and flavonoids.

Metabolomic analysis of A. pictum and A. venetum tea leaves

To account for the presence of water-insoluble bioactive compounds in tea leaves, we conducted a broad-spectrum metabolomic analysis of A. pictum and A. venetum leaves. After obtaining the metabolite profiles, peak areas were integrated, and inter-sample normalization was performed. Correlation analysis revealed that the inter-sample correlation coefficients exceeded 0.9, indicating high reproducibility. High between-group correlation coefficients (> 0.8) further confirmed the reliability of the data (Fig. 2A). A total of 2759 metabolites were identified. Based on fold change, variable importance in projection (VIP) values, and p-values, 461 metabolites were found to be more abundant in A. pictum, while 560 were more abundant in A. venetum (Fig. 2B). Principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal projections to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) (Fig. 2C-2D) indicated that although the primary metabolites were not significantly different between the two tea types, substantial metabolic differences existed between them. Although the levels of primary metabolites such as amino acids and sugars were similar between the two teas, metabolomic analysis revealed a total of 1021 differential metabolites (461 upregulated and 560 downregulated in A. venetum). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis was conducted for the 1021 differentially accumulated metabolites (Fig. 3A). The top 20 enriched pathways highlighted significant differences in secondary metabolite biosynthesis, particularly phenolic acids (e.g., caffeic acid, ferulic acid, and coumarins), flavonoids (e.g., flavonols, quercetin, and kaempferol), and alkaloids (e.g., isoquinoline alkaloids). These secondary metabolites contribute to tea quality by influencing color, aroma, taste, and health benefits. Further analysis of the flavonoid and phenolic acid content in metabolomic responses (Fig. 3B-3C) revealed that while A. pictum had higher TFC, A. venetum exhibited significantly higher TPC. This observation contrasts with the results from tea infusions (Fig. 1B-1C), where A. venetum showed higher TFC than A. pictum. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the extractability of flavonoids during brewing. Although A. pictum tea leaves contain higher total flavonoid content as measured by metabolomics, these compounds may be less soluble or more tightly bound within the leaf matrix, resulting in lower release into the infusion. Differences in major secondary metabolites between A. pictum and A. venetum teas

The differential metabolites between Apocynum varieties also encompass various other secondary metabolites. Based on their chemical properties, these metabolites were categorized into amino acids, lignans and coumarins, alkaloids, terpenoids, organic acids, and lipids. In our study, we identified significant differences in secondary metabolites between A. pictum and A. venetum, including 113 terpenoids, 85 lipids, 79 alkaloids, 63 lignans and coumarins, 52 amino acids and derivatives, and 33 organic acids. Discussion

In this study, we systematically compared the phytochemical composition and antioxidant properties of A. pictum and A. venetum tea infusions at different brewing times (5, 10, and 15 min). These time points were intentionally selected to reflect both common household brewing durations (5 and 10 min) and an extended infusion time (15 min), which was included as an upper limit to evaluate the maximum extractable content of bioactive compounds. The results demonstrated that A. venetum tea exhibited consistently higher total flavonoid content (TFC) and total phenolic content (TPC) than A. pictum tea across all time points (Fig. 1B-1C). These findings align with previous studies indicating that flavonoids and phenolic acids are major contributors to the antioxidant potential of herbal teas (Zhao et al., 2022). The higher TFC and TPC in A. venetum tea suggest that it may provide superior health benefits, particularly in terms of antioxidant activity.

DPPH and ABTS assays revealed that the infusion of Apocynum venetum exhibited significantly stronger free radical scavenging activity (indicating higher antioxidant capacity) compared to that of Apocynum pictum (Fig. 1D-1E). Interestingly, a slight decrease in DPPH scavenging activity was observed at the 10 min infusion time in A. venetum tea, compared to the 5 and 15 min time points. This transient dip may be attributed to the dynamic release behavior and possible thermal degradation of certain antioxidant constituents under extended steeping. The observed correlation between antioxidant activity and bioactive compound content further supports the role of flavonoids and phenolic acids as key contributors to the health-promoting properties of these teas (Mehrabi et al., 2025). This suggests that A. venetum may have greater potential as a functional beverage for preventing oxidative stress-related diseases.

The various compounds in tea have a significant impact on its quality and properties. Metabolomic analysis further revealed significant differences in the metabolic profiles of A. pictum and A. venetum tea leaves. Specifically, A. venetum exhibited a higher abundance of key flavonoids (e.g., myricetin-3-O-(6″-acetyl)glucoside, kaempferol-3-O-sophoroside-7-O-rhamnoside) and phenolic acids (e.g., ethyl caffeate, isochlorogenic acid C), which are known for their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cardiovascular protective effects (Table 1. and Table 2.) (Chiang et al., 2005; Grundmann et al., 2009). These compounds not only contribute to the superior antioxidant capacity of A. venetum tea, as evidenced by DPPH and ABTS assays, but also indicate its greater potential in preventing oxidative stress-related diseases. The elevated levels of these bioactive metabolites enhance the medicinal and functional value of A. venetum tea, supporting its development as a health-promoting beverage. Furthermore, these findings suggest that specific flavonoids and phenolic acids may serve as reliable biochemical markers for distinguishing between A. venetum and A. pictum teas and for evaluating overall tea quality.

In addition to the previously identified marker compound hyperoside for distinguishing A. venetum and A. pictum, our metabolomic analysis revealed that alkaloids also contribute to the metabolic differences between the two teas. Specifically, the indole alkaloid rhazinilam was significantly more abundant in A. venetum tea (3.17-fold higher than in A. pictum). This substantial difference suggests that rhazinilam could serve as a reliable biochemical marker for differentiating the two tea types. This aligns with the broader use of alkaloids in plant taxonomy and functional food authentication. Further studies on its sensory and pharmacological contributions may enhance its value as a quality indicator.

In addition, previous studies have shown that Apocynum venetum tea has an effect on hypertension (Xie et al., 2025). Network pharmacology analysis in experiments showed that key differential metabolites (flavonoids, phenolic acids, alkaloids) in A. venetum are associated with hypertension related targets (such as CA2, ALOX5), further indicating potential anti-hypertensive effects. Further experiments are needed to demonstrate the drug's effect on disease targets. This not only supports the use of Apocynum as a beverage rich in antioxidants, but also as a functional tea, which may have better therapeutic effects on cardiovascular health.

Despite these important findings, this study has some limitations. First, the bioavailability and metabolism of these bioactive compounds in humans were not investigated, which warrants further pharmacokinetic studies. Second, the potential synergistic effects between different metabolites in A. venetum and A. pictum were not explored, requiring more in-depth mechanistic studies. Additionally, future research should examine the effects of different brewing conditions (e.g., temperature, time, pH) on the extraction of bioactive compounds, as these factors may influence the final phytochemical composition and antioxidant capacity of the teas. Moreover, in vivo and clinical studies are necessary to validate the observed antioxidant benefits and assess their physiological relevance.

In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that A. venetum tea possesses higher levels of flavonoids and phenolic acids, leading to superior antioxidant capacity compared to A. pictum tea. These results support the potential use of A. venetum as a functional tea with enhanced health benefits. Further studies should explore its mechanisms of action, clinical applications, and commercial viability to fully understand its role in human health.

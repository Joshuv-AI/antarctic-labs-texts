Comment on “Renal and cardiovascular effects of SGLT2 inhibitors among hypertensive patients with chronic kidney disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis”
Authors: Zhenxing Deng, Shaoxun Wen
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest Javier et al.'s article on SGLT2 inhibitors in hypertensive CKD patients [1]. While addressing an important question, several limitations warrant consideration.

First, the hypertension definition (SBP ≥140 mmHg) is outdated, diverging from guidelines recommending ≥130 mmHg for high-risk CKD. This likely excludes stage 1 hypertensive patients (SBP 130–139 mmHg) who face significant cardiorenal risk. Olsen et al. [2] showed diabetic patients achieving SBP 130–139 mmHg had 47.6% lower renal deterioration risk (HR 0.524; 95%CI 0.375–0.733) and 55.8% lower ESKD risk (HR 0.442; 95%CI 0.196–1.000) versus SBP ≥140 mmHg, with similar protection in non-diabetics. Future studies should adopt guideline-aligned thresholds.

Second, baseline antihypertensive medication interactions were unexplored. While RAS inhibitor and diuretic use are reported, potential synergies/antagonisms with SGLT2 inhibitors remain unexamined. SGLT2 inhibitors complement RAS inhibition but concomitant diuretics may increase hypotension/AKI risk in advanced CKD [3]. Subgroup analyses are needed to assess heterogeneity by background therapy.

Third, blood pressure variability (BPV)—a key predictor surpassing mean BP—was overlooked. Yang et al. [4] found high BPV associated with 21% increased CKD progression risk. Fletcher et al. [5] showed canagliflozin reduced mean but not variability SBP, while each 1-SD variability increase raised heart failure hospitalization by 19%. Russo et al. [6] found dapagliflozin reduced arterial stiffness independent of 24-h SBP, suggesting non-pressure pathways. Future studies should incorporate BPV with ambulatory monitoring.

In summary, the outdated threshold, lack of interaction analysis, and omission of BPV limit generalizability. Future research should integrate guideline-aligned thresholds, drug interaction analyses, and ambulatory monitoring to better assess SGLT2 inhibitors' net benefit in hypertensive CKD.

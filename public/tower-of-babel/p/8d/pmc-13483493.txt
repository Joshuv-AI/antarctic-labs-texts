Early graft function after intraoperative assessment–guided selective ligation of segment 5/8 veins in adult right lobe living donor liver transplantation: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Feyza Sonmez Topcu, Emrah Sahin, Ender Anilir, Abuzer Dirican, Bülent Ünal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReconstruction of segment 5 and/or segment 8 anterior sector veins ≥ 5 mm is widely recommended in adult right lobe living donor liver transplantation (LDLT) to prevent venous congestion. Whether this approach is universally required, or whether selective ligation guided by intraoperative findings is acceptable in lower-risk recipients, remains unresolved.MethodsThis single-centre retrospective cohort study included adult recipients who underwent right lobe LDLT without inclusion of the middle hepatic vein at a high-volume transplant centre (approximately 150 adult LDLTs per year) between November 2021 and May 2025. Eligible patients had intraoperatively measured segment 5 and/or segment 8 veins ≥ 5 mm. Venous management was determined intraoperatively and was not randomized: reconstruction was preferred in recipients judged at higher risk of congestion, and all recipients with graft-to-recipient weight ratio (GRWR) < 0.8 were managed in the reconstruction group. The primary endpoints were postoperative day 7 (POD7) international normalized ratio (INR), total bilirubin, and ascites volume. Multivariable adjustment was prespecified for postoperative ascites only, adjusting for ligation status, MELD score, and GRWR. Prespecified sensitivity analysis restricted to GRWR ≥ 0.8 was performed. Partial Olthoff early allograft dysfunction (EAD) and approximate ILTS–iLDLT small-for-size syndrome (SFSS) were assessed as secondary analyses.ResultsA total of 170 recipients were included (16 selective ligation; 154 reconstruction). Baseline variables did not differ statistically, but all 20 recipients with GRWR < 0.8 were in the reconstruction group. POD7 outcomes were comparable: INR 1.24 vs. 1.27 (p = 0.80), bilirubin 1.46 vs. 1.70 mg/dL (p = 0.35), ascites 1100 vs. 1250 mL (p = 0.89). In multivariable analysis (n = 91), selective ligation was not independently associated with ascites (β = 0.202; 95% CI − 0.359 to + 0.764; p = 0.476); MELD was the only significant predictor (p = 0.024). The GRWR ≥ 0.8 sensitivity analysis confirmed these findings. Partial Olthoff EAD was 25.0% vs. 15.7% (p = 0.42); approximate SFSS was 27.3% vs. 24.7% (p = 1.00). Post-hoc power for the ascites comparison was 80% only for Cohen’s d ≥ 0.90, far larger than the observed d = 0.02.ConclusionsIn carefully selected recipients with adequate graft volume and favourable intraoperative findings, selective ligation of segment 5 and/or 8 veins ≥ 5 mm was not associated with worse early graft function in this cohort. Given the small ligation group, non-randomized allocation, and substantial missing data, these preliminary results are consistent with the feasibility of selective ligation in a selected lower-risk subgroup but cannot establish its safety, clinical applicability, or equivalence to reconstruction. Background

Living donor liver transplantation (LDLT) has become an essential strategy in regions with limited availability of deceased donor organs. In adult recipients, right lobe grafts are frequently preferred to meet metabolic demands and to reduce the risk of small-for-size syndrome [1, 2]. Adult right lobe LDLT remains technically demanding and requires meticulous management of venous outflow to ensure adequate graft perfusion and early functional recovery.

When the middle hepatic vein (MHV) is preserved in the donor, venous drainage of the anterior sector (segments 5 and 8) becomes a critical determinant of graft hemodynamics. Inadequate drainage of this sector has been associated with graft congestion, impaired regeneration, and early graft dysfunction [3–5]. Consequently, reconstruction of segment 5 and 8 veins ≥ 5 mm has been widely adopted in many transplant centres as a preventive strategy [3, 6]. However, venous reconstruction increases operative complexity and duration and may not always guarantee long-term patency [7, 8].

Emerging clinical evidence suggests that the functional relevance of anterior sector veins depends not only on anatomical diameter but also on graft volume, portal inflow, intrahepatic collateral circulation, and intraoperative hemodynamic conditions [9–11]. In a large retrospective study of 253 recipients, Akamatsu et al. [7] demonstrated that impaired anterior sector regeneration following MHV deprivation was compensated by posterior sector hypertrophy and did not adversely affect functional recovery or long-term outcomes. These considerations raise the question of whether rigid diameter-based reconstruction criteria are universally necessary, particularly in recipients with adequate graft volume.

The present study aimed to evaluate, in a single-centre retrospective cohort, whether selective ligation of segment 5 and/or segment 8 veins ≥ 5 mm — applied to recipients judged at low risk of anterior sector congestion on intraoperative assessment — was associated with early graft dysfunction in adult right lobe LDLT. The study was designed as a feasibility evaluation in a selected lower-risk subgroup rather than as a direct comparison between strategies, because allocation to selective ligation or reconstruction was not randomized.

Methods

Study design and setting

This was a single-centre retrospective cohort study reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement (completed checklist provided as Additional file 1). The study was conducted at Istanbul Aydin University Medicalpark Florya Hospital, a centre performing approximately 150 adult LDLTs per year (more than 550 adult LDLTs during the 3.5-year study period). The study was approved by the Istanbul Aydin University Ethics Committee (Approval No: 121/2024) and performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants as part of the institutional transplantation consent process.

Patient selection

All consecutive adult recipients (aged ≥ 18 years) who underwent right lobe LDLT without inclusion of the middle hepatic vein between November 2021 and May 2025 were screened for eligibility. Patients were included if intraoperatively measured segment 5 and/or segment 8 anterior sector drainage veins were ≥ 5 mm in diameter.

Exclusion criteria were: anterior sector veins < 5 mm; dominant drainage through a distinct inferior right hepatic vein; left lobe grafts; pediatric recipients (age < 18 years); and incomplete intraoperative documentation of anterior sector vein management.

During preparation of the present revision, the source dataset was re-verified, and five recipients in the reconstruction group were removed due to incomplete data. The final analysed cohort therefore comprised 170 recipients (16 selective ligation; 154 reconstruction). A STROBE-style flow diagram is provided as Fig. 1.

Group allocation and intraoperative assessment

Patients were classified into two groups according to intraoperative venous management. In the Reconstruction Group, V5 and/or V8 veins were reconstructed using direct anastomosis or an interposition graft. In the Selective Ligation Group, V5 and/or V8 veins were ligated based on intraoperative functional assessment. One patient in whom one anterior sector vein was reconstructed and another was ligated was assigned to the Selective Ligation Group, as the primary analytical interest was the safety of ligating at least one ≥ 5 mm tributary; a sensitivity analysis excluding this mixed-management case is reported (Supplementary Table S2).

The decision to reconstruct or ligate was not randomized. Reconstruction was preferentially performed in recipients considered to be at higher risk of venous congestion, including those with marginal graft-to-recipient weight ratio (GRWR) or unfavourable intraoperative perfusion findings. All recipients with a GRWR < 0.8 were managed in the Reconstruction Group, reflecting a deliberate risk-averse strategy.

The intraoperative decision to ligate or reconstruct was made by the operating surgical team at the time of graft revascularization in the recipient operation, immediately after portal vein anastomosis and reperfusion, based on an integrated experience-based assessment rather than a formal scoring system. The following qualitative findings favoured ligation: (i) homogeneous pink graft coloration without segmental dusky discoloration of the anterior sector after portal reperfusion; (ii) absent or minimal venous backflow from the transected V5/V8 stump during temporary clamp occlusion of the stump, suggesting a limited dependent drainage territory; (iii) a phasic hepatopetal portal Doppler waveform without portal hyperperfusion; and (iv) an adequate graft-to-recipient weight ratio (≥ 0.8). The presence of dusky anterior sector congestion, brisk pulsatile backflow from the V5/V8 stump, or a marginal GRWR favoured reconstruction. The absence of a prespecified, quantitatively operationalized algorithm is a limitation, and we propose that the criteria above be examined prospectively in a structured decision framework (see Supplementary Figure S1 for the experience-based decision schematic).

Donor hepatectomy with preservation of the middle hepatic vein was performed as previously described [3].

Data collection and outcomes

Recipient demographic characteristics, preoperative clinical variables, operative data, and postoperative outcomes were retrospectively collected from operative notes, anesthesia records, and the institutional electronic medical record system. Graft-to-recipient weight ratio was calculated as graft weight (g) divided by recipient body weight (g), expressed as a percentage. Operative time and intraoperative blood loss refer to the entire recipient procedure (skin incision to skin closure).

The primary endpoints were early graft function parameters on postoperative day 7 (POD7): international normalized ratio (INR), total bilirubin level, and ascites volume (24-hour drain output). Secondary endpoints included operative time and intraoperative blood loss; clinically significant ascites (≥ 1000 mL on POD7); early graft dysfunction (POD7 INR > 1.6); any early postoperative complication; partial Olthoff early allograft dysfunction (EAD) [12]; and approximate ILTS–iLDLT small-for-size syndrome (SFSS) [13].

The Olthoff definition of EAD includes three criteria within the first seven postoperative days: bilirubin ≥ 10 mg/dL on POD7, INR ≥ 1.6 on POD7, or aspartate/alanine aminotransferase (AST/ALT) > 2000 IU/L at any point during the first 7 days [12]. Because peak transaminase values within the first 7 days were not consistently captured in our retrospective database, only the bilirubin and INR components could be evaluated; this analysis is referred to as “partial Olthoff EAD” throughout. Similarly, the formal ILTS–iLDLT SFSS definition requires sequential clinical assessment over 14 days and active management of portosystemic shunts [13]; we therefore report an approximate SFSS phenotype based on the available POD7 data (ascites > 1000 mL combined with INR ≥ 1.6 or total bilirubin ≥ 3 mg/dL).

Handling of missing data

No imputation was performed for missing data. All analyses used available cases; the number of patients contributing to each comparison is reported in the relevant tables and in the flow diagram (Fig. 1). Sample sizes varied across analyses because retrospective documentation of certain variables (notably intraoperative blood loss, operative time, and POD7 ascites volume) was incomplete. The implications of missing data are addressed in the Discussion.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test. For primary continuous endpoints, 95% confidence intervals for the between-group difference in medians were estimated by bootstrap resampling (5000 replicates).

Given the non-randomized surgical strategy, GRWR was considered a potential confounder. Multivariable linear regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of postoperative ascites using logarithmically transformed ascites volume [log(ascites + 1)]; the model included ligation status, MELD score, and GRWR as covariates. Multivariable adjustment was prespecified for postoperative ascites only, chosen before analysis as the clinically most variable of the three primary endpoints and as the outcome most plausibly influenced by graft-volume allocation. INR and bilirubin were not modelled because the prespecified analytic focus was postoperative ascites; the absence of a univariate group difference for these two endpoints was not the basis for this decision. A prespecified sensitivity analysis was conducted by restricting the cohort to recipients with GRWR ≥ 0.8 to control for potential allocation bias related to graft volume. A second sensitivity analysis excluded the single mixed-management patient.

Post-hoc power was calculated for the primary ascites comparison and for the categorical comparison of early graft dysfunction using standard two-sample formulas. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. No formal adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied; borderline p values are interpreted accordingly.

Results

Patient characteristics

During the study period, 170 adult recipients met the inclusion criteria and constituted the analysed cohort (Fig. 1). Of these, 154 (90.6%) were managed in the Reconstruction Group and 16 (9.4%) in the Selective Ligation Group. Baseline demographic variables (age, sex distribution, body mass index, MELD score, median GRWR, and graft weight) did not differ statistically between groups (Table 1). However, group composition was not the result of random allocation: all 20 recipients with GRWR < 0.8 were managed in the Reconstruction Group, and reconstruction was preferentially used in recipients with less favourable intraoperative findings. The Selective Ligation Group therefore represents a selected lower-risk subgroup, and the statistical similarity of mean and median baseline values should not be interpreted as exchangeability between groups.

Multivariable analysis

Multivariable linear regression analysis using log-transformed POD7 ascites volume was performed with ligation status, MELD score, and GRWR as covariates; the model included 91 recipients with complete data for all four variables (Table 4). Ligation of an anterior sector vein ≥ 5 mm was not independently associated with postoperative ascites volume (β = 0.202; 95% CI − 0.359 to + 0.764; p = 0.476). MELD score was the only significant independent predictor of postoperative ascites (β = 0.028; 95% CI + 0.004 to + 0.053; p = 0.024). GRWR was not independently associated with ascites in this model (β = −0.242; 95% CI − 0.990 to + 0.506; p = 0.522). As noted in the Methods, multivariable adjustment was prespecified for ascites only; INR and bilirubin were not modelled because the prespecified analytic focus was postoperative ascites.

Sensitivity analysis 1: recipients with GRWR ≥ 0.8

To address potential allocation bias arising from the exclusive assignment of all GRWR < 0.8 recipients to the Reconstruction Group, a prespecified sensitivity analysis was conducted by restricting the cohort to recipients with GRWR ≥ 0.8 (n = 149; ligation n = 16, reconstruction n = 133). In this subgroup, POD7 outcomes remained comparable between groups: INR median 1.24 vs. 1.27 (p = 0.76), total bilirubin 1.46 vs. 1.60 mg/dL (p = 0.44), and ascites volume 1100 vs. 1200 mL (p = 0.84) (Table 5). The incidence of clinically significant ascites was 63.6% versus 58.0% (p = 1.00); early graft dysfunction was 16.7% versus 11.0% (p = 0.63).

Multivariable regression in this restricted cohort (n = 80) confirmed that selective ligation was not independently associated with postoperative ascites (β = 0.235; 95% CI − 0.348 to + 0.817; p = 0.425), while MELD score remained the only significant predictor (β = 0.036; 95% CI + 0.008 to + 0.064; p = 0.011). These results indicate that the primary findings are not entirely explained by the assignment of all recipients with GRWR < 0.8 to the reconstruction group, although residual confounding by graft volume and by other unmeasured factors cannot be excluded.

Sensitivity analysis 2: exclusion of the mixed-management patient

A second sensitivity analysis was performed by excluding the single mixed-management patient (one V8 vein ligated, one V5 vein reconstructed; assigned to the Selective Ligation Group in the primary analysis). With this patient excluded (n = 169; ligation n = 15, reconstruction n = 154), the primary findings were unchanged: POD7 INR p = 0.65, total bilirubin p = 0.30, ascites volume p = 0.89; the multivariable ligation coefficient was β = 0.202 (95% CI − 0.359 to + 0.764; p = 0.476). Full results are provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Secondary analyses using standardized definitions

Partial Olthoff EAD (POD7 INR ≥ 1.6 or POD7 total bilirubin ≥ 10 mg/dL) was identified in 3/12 (25.0%) of the Selective Ligation Group and 18/115 (15.7%) of the Reconstruction Group (Fisher exact p = 0.42). Approximate ILTS–iLDLT SFSS (POD7 ascites > 1000 mL combined with POD7 INR ≥ 1.6 or bilirubin ≥ 3 mg/dL) was identified in 3/11 (27.3%) of the Selective Ligation Group and 19/77 (24.7%) of the Reconstruction Group (Fisher exact p = 1.00). These results are summarized in Supplementary Table S3. Both standardized analyses are consistent with the primary findings of no detectable adverse signal in the ligation group, with the caveat that the partial Olthoff analysis lacks the AST/ALT component and the approximate SFSS lacks sequential 14-day assessment.

Post-hoc power

For the primary POD7 ascites comparison (11 ligation vs. 81 reconstruction recipients with available data), this analysis had 80% power to detect only Cohen’s d ≥ 0.90, corresponding to a between-group mean difference of approximately 870 mL; the observed effect size was Cohen’s d = 0.02 (mean difference 19 mL). For the categorical comparison of early graft dysfunction (16.7% vs. 12.2%, n = 12 vs. 115), the achieved power was approximately 7%. The absence of statistically significant differences therefore cannot be interpreted as evidence of equivalence and should be considered alongside the possibility of type II error.

Discussion

Management of anterior sector venous drainage remains a key technical issue in adult right lobe LDLT. Since early reports advocating reconstruction of segment 5 and 8 veins ≥ 5 mm, diameter-based thresholds have been widely incorporated into surgical practice [3, 6, 14, 15]. Although this approach is considered standard in many centres, the extent to which anatomical diameter alone determines functional necessity remains controversial.

In the present study, selective ligation of V5/V8 veins ≥ 5 mm, when applied to recipients judged at low risk of anterior sector congestion on intraoperative assessment, was not associated with a detectable adverse signal on early graft function. POD7 INR, total bilirubin, and ascites volume were comparable between groups. Clinically significant ascites and early graft dysfunction rates were also similar, and partial Olthoff EAD and approximate ILTS–iLDLT SFSS analyses likewise showed no statistically significant between-group difference. These findings are consistent with the view that the clinical relevance of anterior sector veins depends not only on anatomical size but also on graft volume, portal inflow, intrahepatic collateral circulation, and overall hemodynamic conditions [9, 10, 16]. However, the design of the study does not allow these results to be interpreted as evidence that selective ligation is equivalent to reconstruction. The investigation evaluates the early functional consequences of selective ligation in a cohort of recipients whom the operating surgical team judged, on integrated intraoperative assessment, to be at low risk of anterior sector congestion; it does not constitute a head-to-head comparison of the two strategies.

Venous congestion of the anterior sector has been associated with impaired regeneration and early graft dysfunction in right lobe grafts [4, 5, 17]. However, several lines of evidence support the capacity for compensatory venous outflow. Ito et al. [8] reported long-term V5 and V8 patency rates as low as 36% and 58%, respectively, after reconstruction, yet without measurable deterioration of graft function. In a landmark study of 253 recipients, Akamatsu et al. [7] demonstrated that anterior sector regeneration was significantly impaired when MHV tributaries were sacrificed, but this was compensated by enhanced posterior sector regeneration, resulting in comparable functional recovery and long-term outcomes across surgical strategies. Durairaj et al. [9], in a randomized clinical trial, reported that MHV reconstruction improved outcomes particularly in grafts with lower GRWR, highlighting the importance of graft volume in determining the necessity of venous reconstruction. Collectively, these observations indicate the presence of robust compensatory venous outflow pathways and underscore the potential for a more individualized approach to venous management in selected recipients with adequate graft volume.

Operative complexity is another consideration. Venous reconstruction requires additional vascular anastomoses, increases procedural demands, and may necessitate the use of interposition grafts such as cryopreserved homologous veins or synthetic conduits [18, 19]. Although intraoperative data were available only in a subset of patients (n = 5 in the ligation group) and did not reach statistical significance, the numerical trend toward shorter operative time in the Selective Ligation Group is consistent with the additional technical burden associated with reconstruction. The recent multicentre randomized trial by Reddy et al. [20] demonstrated that single outflow reconstruction techniques reduced warm ischemia time and operative duration compared with dual outflow approaches, further illustrating the clinical relevance of simplifying venous outflow management. However, our operative-time comparison is based on only 5 ligation cases with complete documentation and should be regarded as descriptive only; no causal inference about operative efficiency can be drawn from these data.

The distribution of graft-to-recipient weight ratio (GRWR) requires careful interpretation. All 20 recipients with GRWR < 0.8 were managed in the Reconstruction Group, reflecting a deliberate risk-averse strategy rather than random allocation. Selective ligation was preferentially applied in recipients with adequate graft volume and favourable intraoperative perfusion findings. The importance of graft volume and portal inflow modulation in preventing small-for-size physiology is well established [2, 10, 16]. To address this allocation pattern, a prespecified sensitivity analysis restricted to recipients with GRWR ≥ 0.8 was performed. In this more homogeneous subgroup, selective ligation remained a non-significant predictor of postoperative ascites, supporting the robustness of the primary findings. Accordingly, our results are compatible with a risk-stratified approach in the lower-risk subgroup, but do not establish its safety; the question of optimal management in marginal grafts remains entirely open, and reconstruction should remain the default outside the selected lower-risk setting.

It must also be acknowledged that the median GRWR in our cohort was 1.04 (IQR 0.94–1.22) and all recipients in the ligation group had GRWR ≥ 0.8. In this volume range, the functional reserve of the non-congested posterior sector may itself exceed the threshold below which small-for-size physiology emerges, even if the entire anterior sector were rendered hemodynamically irrelevant. Our findings therefore cannot be extrapolated to recipients with marginal grafts (GRWR < 0.8), for whom anterior sector drainage may be functionally indispensable. The deliberate assignment of all marginal grafts to reconstruction in our cohort prevents us from making any inference about this higher-risk population.

Multivariable analysis demonstrated that ligation of anterior sector veins ≥ 5 mm was not independently associated with postoperative ascites after adjusting for MELD score and GRWR (Table 4). MELD score emerged as the principal independent predictor of postoperative ascites. Ascites formation after liver transplantation is multifactorial and closely related to recipient disease severity and portal hemodynamics [10, 17, 20]. Within this broader physiological framework, the isolated impact of anterior sector venous strategy may be less decisive than traditionally assumed, particularly in non-marginal grafts.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations that must be considered when interpreting the findings. The most consequential is selection bias arising from the non-randomized allocation strategy. Because all recipients with GRWR < 0.8 were managed in the Reconstruction Group, GRWR perfectly separates one arm from the other, and regression adjustment cannot fully recover an unbiased comparison under this design. Although GRWR was included in multivariable analysis and a prespecified sensitivity analysis restricted to GRWR ≥ 0.8 recipients was performed, residual confounding cannot be excluded. The study is therefore best interpreted as a feasibility evaluation of selective ligation in a carefully selected lower-risk cohort, rather than as a head-to-head comparison between strategies. Closely related to this design constraint is the issue of statistical power: with only 16 recipients in the ligation group — and 11 contributing complete POD7 ascites data — the analysis was powered to detect only large between-group effects (Cohen’s d ≥ 0.90 for the ascites comparison, corresponding to a detectable mean difference of approximately 870 mL versus the observed 19 mL; achieved power for the early graft dysfunction comparison was approximately 7%). The absence of statistically significant between-group differences therefore cannot be interpreted as evidence of equivalence and remains compatible with a clinically relevant difference too small to detect at this sample size.

Missing data were a further source of imprecision. Approximately two-thirds of intraoperative variables and one-third of POD7 outcomes were unavailable; for the intraoperative variables this was more pronounced in the ligation group, whereas POD7 outcome availability was broadly similar between the two groups. The mechanism of missingness cannot be determined from the present dataset: although much of it appears to reflect the retrospective construction of the dataset, we cannot exclude that data availability was related to patient characteristics, perioperative management, or clinical outcomes, which could introduce bias in either direction rather than only reduce precision. The operative-time comparison in particular should therefore be regarded as descriptive only. The endpoint scope is also limited. POD7 INR, total bilirubin, and ascites volume reflect global early graft function but do not capture regional anterior sector congestion, segmental graft regeneration, or long-term collateral venous patency. Volumetric follow-up, anterior sector perfusion imaging, and longer-term venous outflow assessment were beyond the scope of this analysis. Furthermore, the third Olthoff EAD component (AST or ALT > 2000 IU/L within the first seven postoperative days) and the daily laboratory sequence required for formal ILTS–iLDLT SFSS classification were not consistently captured in the retrospective database; only partial Olthoff and approximate SFSS analyses could be reported. The intraoperative assessment itself was experience-based rather than algorithmic, and the qualitative criteria described in Methods, although clinically meaningful, lack the quantitative operationalization required for external reproducibility. Quantitative portal hemodynamic measurements — portal vein flow, portal vein velocity, hepatic venous pressure gradient, and the use of inflow modulation maneuvers such as splenectomy, hemiportocaval shunt, or splenic artery ligation — were not systematically captured over the 3.5-year retrospective window and could not be analysed. Their absence is a substantive limitation: the protective role attributed in our discussion to “satisfactory intraoperative perfusion” rests on qualitative rather than quantitative judgment.

The multivariable model is therefore under-adjusted. Donor age and BMI, graft steatosis grade, cold and warm ischemia times, anhepatic phase duration, and quantitative recipient portal hemodynamic variables were not consistently recorded and could not be included as covariates; residual confounding is likely, and the model should be regarded as a check on the qualitative pattern rather than as a definitive causal estimate. Finally, all operations were performed at a single high-volume transplant centre by an experienced multi-surgeon team, and the applicability of an experience-based selective ligation strategy at centres with different case volumes or surgical experience profiles cannot be assumed. Multiple endpoints were analysed without formal adjustment for multiple comparisons, and borderline p values should be interpreted accordingly.

Implications and future directions

Within the boundaries set by these limitations, our preliminary findings raise the hypothesis that routine reconstruction of all anterior sector drainage veins ≥ 5 mm may not be universally required in adult right lobe LDLT with adequate graft volume and favourable intraoperative findings. However, these observations are exploratory and are derived from a small, non-randomized, selected subgroup; they cannot establish the safety or clinical applicability of an individualized venous-management strategy and should not be used to guide routine clinical practice. Prospective, adequately powered, multicentre studies are required before any strategy based on graft-volume and intraoperative haemodynamic assessment can be considered for clinical use.

Definitive evaluation will require prospective, ideally multicentre studies with: (i) standardized intraoperative assessment criteria using quantitative portal hemodynamic parameters; (ii) standardized endpoint definitions (full Olthoff EAD, ILTS–iLDLT SFSS); (iii) adequate statistical power to detect clinically meaningful differences in both global and segment-specific graft function; and (iv) longer-term follow-up incorporating volumetric regeneration assessment and venous outflow patency.

Conclusions

In this single-centre retrospective cohort of adult right lobe LDLT recipients, selective ligation of segment 5 and/or segment 8 veins ≥ 5 mm, when applied to recipients with adequate graft volume and favourable intraoperative findings, was not associated with a detectable early adverse signal on POD7 INR, total bilirubin, or ascites. Despite preferential reconstruction in recipients with lower graft-to-recipient weight ratio, both primary multivariable analysis and sensitivity analysis restricted to recipients with GRWR ≥ 0.8 did not demonstrate an independent adverse effect of selective ligation on postoperative ascites. Standardized secondary analyses (partial Olthoff EAD and approximate ILTS–iLDLT SFSS) were consistent with the primary findings.

Given the small ligation group, the non-randomized allocation, substantial missing data, and the under-adjusted multivariable model, these findings should be interpreted as preliminary evidence consistent with the feasibility of selective ligation in a carefully selected lower-risk subgroup (GRWR ≥ 0.8 with favourable intraoperative findings), and not as evidence of its safety, clinical applicability, or equivalence to reconstruction. The present data are insufficient to support any change in current clinical practice. In marginal grafts (GRWR < 0.8), anterior sector reconstruction should remain the default until prospective data demonstrate otherwise.

Selective ligation appears to be a feasible approach worthy of prospective evaluation in carefully selected recipients with adequate graft volume and favourable intraoperative findings; adequately powered, prospective, ideally multicentre studies with standardized intraoperative criteria and standardized endpoint definitions are required before any change in current clinical practice can be recommended.

Protocol for tissue-specific mutagenesis with fluorescent labeling in zebrafish
Authors: Juanjuan Luo, Chunjiao Lu, Xiaojun Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol for tissue-specific mutagenesis in zebrafish. We describe the preparation of the Tol2 transposase donor vector containing a U6 promoter that drives the transcription of single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs) and Cas9 under the control of a tissue-specific promoter. We then detail the establishment, identification, and phenotypic analysis of the stable tissue-specific mutagenesis zebrafish line. This protocol is useful for generating stable tissue-specific knockout lines to analyze mosaic loss-of-function phenotypes.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Luo et al.1

Before you begin

The utilization of CRISPR/Cas9-based gene knockout has facilitated genotype–phenotype studies of human pathology. In this context, systemic CRISPR/Cas9-induced gene knockout in zebrafish has been widely reported.2,3 However, systemic gene knockout has several limitations that attenuate its utility for investigating specific gene functions in animal models. For example, zebrafish with systemic gene knockout sometimes exhibit infertility caused by dysfunction of ovulation and fertilization or premature death caused by abnormal embryo development. In contrast, tissue-specific mutagenesis in zebrafish can enable spatiotemporal investigation of the potential function of the targeted genes in specific tissues, which can allow the zebrafish model to precisely reflect the specific gene function or human pathology.

These protocols describe methods for establishing a glioblastoma transgenic zebrafish model using the Tol2 transposon based on CRISPR/Cas9 technology.4 The first part describes the construction of the recombinant Tol2 transposon-mediated vector, which contains two key elements: zebrafish-targeting single-guide RNA (sgRNA), under the zebrafish U6 promoter,5 and the zebrafish tissue-specific regulatory elements, which selectively regulate the expression of zebrafish codon-optimized Cas9 and the fluorescent reporter mCherry (Figure 1). Expected outcomes

Using this protocol, we utilized the Tol2-mediated CRISPR/Cas9 strategy to establish the transgenic zebrafish lines with tissue-specific promoter-driven any genes you want to be knocked out and further screened stable transgenic fish by the detection of an mCherry fluorescent reporter or by a transgenic-based PCR approach, which allowed us to establish that conditioned mutation transgenesis is readily applicable to a variety of specific tissues and genes of interest. Here, the results indicated that representative images of whole-mount in situ assay using the anti-sense RNA probe against Nf1a, Rb1 or Tp53 mRNA in 48 hpf embryos, as well as the expression levels of Nf1, Rb1, and Tp53 expression in brain tissues from different mutants of F1 generation at 3 months of age (Figures 8C–8F).

Limitations

Tol2-mediated transgenesis can mediate fluorescent proteins to track CRISPR-induced mutations in specific cells and tissues, which has many unique applications in basic and applied biology and medicine.1 However, this protocol has a potential limitation that clones preserved in multicopy plasmids, such as cosmids with sizes ranging from 30–50 kb, might be difficult to modify through recombineering (Sharan et al., 2009).13 In addition, the presence of target genes with repetitive sequences might bring challenges in the context of recombineering. Furthermore, the sgRNA used to do the knockout might not be guaranteed to successfully generate a genomic deletion in the target region of interest, multiple CRISPR target sequences are recommended to validate.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

No colonies are observed on the plate.

Potential solution

Adjust the ratio of DNA template and insertion DNA in the ligation reaction. In addition, use a positive control to ensure the transformation efficiency of competent cells.

Problem 2

False-positive colonies are obtained.

Potential solution

False-positive colonies are usually induced by incomplete digestion of the DNA template. Increase the quantity of BbsI endonuclease or prolong the duration of the digestion reaction to 1–2 h at 37°C.

Problem 3

The sequencing result does not match the gfap promoter sequence.

Potential solution

The problem is usually induced by the primers or the conditions of seamless cloning and assembly, Check the oligo sequences and conditions and repeat the reaction.

Problem 4

High mortality or malformation of injected embryos occurs.

Potential solution

A higher dose of injected RNA/DNA might result in toxicity for embryonic development. Ensure a suitable concentration and volume of the injected RNA/DNA.

Problem 5

The fluorescence expression pattern from the transgenic fish does not match the endogenous gene expression.

Potential solution

The reporter is silenced in the germline, or the transgene is missing essential regulatory elements. Try another promoter or utilize longer upstream and downstream sequences of the targeted promoter.

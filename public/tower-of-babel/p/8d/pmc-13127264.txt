Safety and efficacy of biodegradable microneedle acupuncture for dry eye: A multicenter, randomized, active-controlled pivotal trial
Authors: Soo-Yeon Park, Min-Ji Kim, Min-Yeong Jung, So-Eun Son, Hyun-Gyung Chae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBiodegradable microneedle acupuncture (BMA) is a novel technique designed to enhance therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects associated with conventional acupuncture. Dry eye (DE) is a common condition that significantly affects quality of life. This clinical trial aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of BMA compared to sterile acupuncture (SA) in treating DE.MethodsA total of 224 participants with DE were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the BMA group (BMAG) or the SA group (SAG). Treatment was administered three times per week for four weeks at eight bilateral acupoints (GB14, TE23, EX-HN5, and ST1). The primary outcome was the proportion of participants experiencing at least one treatment-related adverse event (AE) during the 5-week treatment period. Secondary outcomes included changes in the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI), visual analog scale for subjective symptoms (VAS-SS), quality-of-life (QoL) scores, treatment satisfaction, and additional safety assessments.ResultsThe proportion of participants experiencing treatment-related AEs was significantly lower in the BMAG than in the SAG (risk ratio, 0.17; 95% CI, 0.11–0.28; p < 0.0001). Greater improvements were observed in the BMAG for OSDI (mean difference −16.23; 95% confidence interval [CI], −19.32 to −13.15), VAS-SS (−19.33; 95% CI, −22.00 to −16.66), and QoL (0.96; 95% CI, 0.74–1.19). No skin allergy-related AEs occurred in the BMAG, whereas such events were observed in the SAG.ConclusionsBMA was associated with a lower risk of treatment-related AEs and greater clinical improvement compared with sterile acupuncture in patients with DE. These findings suggest that BMA may represent a safer and more effective therapeutic option for DE management.Trial registrationClinical Research Information Service, Republic of Korea (KCT0008802).Keywords: Biodegradable microneedle acupuncture, Dry eye, Safety, Randomized controlled trial, Pivotal trial

Introduction

Biodegradable microneedle acupuncture (BMA) is an innovative medical device developed to enhance therapeutic efficacy while minimizing the side effects typically associated with conventional metal sterile acupuncture (SA) needles.

Previous randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have shown that acupuncture can alleviate dry eye (DE) symptoms by improving tear film stability and secretion, and several modalities—including SA, electroacupuncture, and thumbtack needles—are used clinically for DE management.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Thumbtack needles in particular provide continuous acupoint stimulation with minimal discomfort, making them an established option for chronic ocular symptoms and an appropriate active comparator in this study.

However, periocular acupuncture using metal needles composed of nickel or chromium may induce localized inflammatory or hypersensitivity reactions, which can reduce therapeutic success and deter patient adherence.6, 7, 8 Clinical experiences in Korean medicine have documented frequent cases of metal-needle–related skin complications.9,10

BMA was developed to overcome these limitations. The microneedles are composed of biodegradable hyaluronic acid that dissolves within the skin, enabling sustained acupoint stimulation without metal-induced irritation or allergic responses. Pilot RCTs have demonstrated that BMA improves Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) scores, Visual Analog Scale for subjective symptoms (VAS-SS), and Quality of Life (QoL) in DE patients, with no treatment-related adverse events (AEs) reported.11 Despite these encouraging findings, evidence has so far been limited to small sample sizes, underscoring the need for a large confirmatory trial.

This study aimed to provide robust clinical evidence on the safety and therapeutic effectiveness of BMA compared with conventional SA in patients with DE.

Methods

Study registration

The trial was registered with the Clinical Research Information Service (CRIS, https://cris.nih.go.kr/) of the Republic of Korea (Registration No. KCT0008802) on September 15, 2023. The protocol of this trial was previously published.12

This study is reported following the CONSORT and revised STRICTA (Standards for Reporting Interventions in Clinical Trials of Acupuncture) reporting guidelines. All essential STRICTA components— including the rationale for acupuncture, detailed descriptions of needling procedures, treatment regimen, practitioner background, and the control intervention—were fully documented to ensure transparency and reproducibility of the acupuncture interventions used in this trial (Supplement 1, 2).

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) of Naju Dongshin University Korean Medicine Hospital and Dongshin University Mokpo Korean Medicine Hospital (IRB approval numbers: NJ-IRB-23–6 and DSMOH 24–1). The IRB approvals were granted on September 5, 2023, and January 18, 2024, respectively.

Study design

The trial was a pivotal, prospective, randomized, assessor-blinded, SA-controlled, parallel design study, conducted from October 4, 2023, to June 4, 2024.

Prior to enrollment, all participants were fully informed about the study’s purpose, procedures, potential benefits, and precautions. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study was carried out in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (version 2013).

The general study schedule is outlined in Supplement 3. This organized timeline ensured that participants' visits were coordinated, and data were collected systematically throughout the trial.

Participants were recruited through advertisements at Naju Dongshin University Korean Medicine Hospital and Dongshin University Mokpo Korean Medicine Hospital, both located in Jeollanam-do, Republic of Korea.

Randomization and blinding

This multicenter trial used stratified block randomization, with each study site serving as a stratification factor. An independent statistician generated the randomization sequence using SAS 9.4 statistical software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Serial numbers were assigned to all 224 participants, and random allocation to the BMA or SA group was performed in a 1:1 ratio.

To ensure allocation concealment, the randomization codes were placed into sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes prepared by a third party not involved in the clinical conduct of the study. Each envelope was opened in numerical order by the clinical research coordinator only after baseline assessments were completed, ensuring that group assignments could not be anticipated.

This study employed an assessor-blinded design. Research staff were divided into three roles: assessor, clinical research coordinator, and therapist. The assessor evaluated the causality of AEs and conducted all secondary outcome assessments while remaining blinded to group allocation. The clinical research coordinator managed participant scheduling, handled the allocation envelopes, and supervised treatment logistics. The therapist administered the assigned medical device interventions and did not participate in outcome assessments.

Due to the physical distinguishability between treatment devices, blinding of participants and practitioners was not feasible; however, outcome assessors and statisticians remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study.

Intervention

Treatment principles and rationale

This study compared BMA with SA using thumbtack-type needles. The acupoints selected—GB14 (Yangbai), TE23 (Sizhukong), EX-HN5 (Taiyang), and ST1 (Chengqi)—are commonly used to manage ocular circulation and periocular symptoms such as dryness, foreign body sensation, and ocular fatigue, based on classical literature and modern Korean medicine clinical practice. Acupoint locations followed the World Health Organization Standard Acupuncture Point Locations in the Western Pacific Region (WHO WPRO, 2008).

Components of the interventions

Experimental group: BMA

Participants in the experimental group received BMA using a commercially manufactured medical device (RMD-PN1, Raphas Co., Ltd., Seoul, Republic of Korea). Each patch contained five biodegradable microneedles, with a 1.2-mm needle length composed of biodegradable material (Supplement 4). The device was applied to each of the eight bilateral acupoints (Supplement 5) and remained in place for 4 h, providing continuous stimulation.

Control group: SA

In this study, the control intervention was performed using disposable sterile thumbtack needles (T-press needle DB130A, Dongbang Medical, Seongnam, Republic of Korea). To ensure clarity and consistency, the control group is referred to throughout the manuscript as “Sterile Acupuncture (SA)”, with the understanding that SA specifically denotes treatment using sterile thumbtack-type needles (Supplement 4).

The SA contained a single 1.2-mm stainless-steel needle attached to an adhesive tape. It was applied to the same eight bilateral acupoints and removed after 4 h, to match the stimulation period of BMA (Supplement 5).

Procedure and treatment regimen

All participants discontinued dry-eye-related treatments for the duration of the study. Treatments were initiated immediately after randomization and were administered three times per week for four weeks, for a total of 12 sessions. Participants were instructed to return the removed BMA or SA devices at their next visit, along with a written removal time record.

Lifestyle management education

At Visit 1, all participants received standardized lifestyle education delivered by trained clinical research coordinators using a structured instruction sheet. Content included:

- Environmental Management: Avoiding wind exposure, minimizing time in air-conditioned/low-humidity environments, and maintaining proper indoor humidity.

- Environmental Management: Avoiding wind exposure, minimizing time in air-conditioned/low-humidity environments, and maintaining proper indoor humidity.

- Blinking techniques: Conscious blinking training.

- Blinking techniques: Conscious blinking training.

- General ocular care: Water intake encouragement, avoidance of eye rubbing

- General ocular care: Water intake encouragement, avoidance of eye rubbing

This education was identical for both groups to control for behavioral confounding factors.

Practitioners’ background

All treatments were administered by licensed Korean medicine doctors with ≥ 3 years of clinical experience, who completed ≥ 10 h of standardized training specific to the study treatment protocol before trial initiation.

Assessment and monitoring

Participants were provided with an adverse event diary and instructed to record symptoms daily. Research staff reviewed the diaries and inspected the application sites at each visit. Any discomfort or complications were evaluated and documented according to protocol procedures.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the proportion of participants who experienced at least one treatment-related AE during the treatment period.

Treatment-related AEs were defined as events assessed as “definitely,” “probably,” or “possibly” related to the intervention (Supplement 6). The severity and predictability of AEs were recorded for descriptive safety evaluation but were not included in the primary endpoint definition.

Assessment of AE causality was conducted by a doctorate-level Korean medicine specialist or professor with more than seven years of clinical experience. Two independent evaluators reviewed each event initially. If both agreed on causality, the classification was finalized accordingly. In cases of disagreement, a third independent evaluator adjudicated to reach a final determination.

Secondary efficacy outcomes

Efficacy outcomes included the OSDI, VAS-SS, QoL, and treatment satisfaction.

All outcome were assessed at Visit 1 and Visit 13, except treatment satisfaction. A higher OSDI score (range: 0–100) indicates a more severe impact of the disease on daily life.

For VAS-SS, participants marked their discomfort caused by DE over the past week on a 100 mm scale.

QoL was assessed using a 5-point Likert scale, evaluating the degree of improvement in daily functioning related to DE symptoms.

Treatment satisfaction was assessed at Visit 13 using a standardized 5-point Likert scale, in which participants rated their overall satisfaction with the treatment (1, very dissatisfied to 5, very satisfied).

Secondary safety outcomes

Secondary safety outcomes included vital signs, blood test results, and skin allergies.

Vital Signs

Vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and body temperature) were measured at every visit (Visits 1–13).

Blood tests

Blood tests were conducted at screening and post-treatment (Visit 13) to assess general health status. Standard biochemical parameters were analyzed at a certified external laboratory.

Skin Allergy Assessments

Skin allergy–related adverse events were evaluated at each visit through investigator assessment of local skin reactions at the application sites and review of participants’ symptom diaries.

Local reactions such as erythema, edema/papulation, oozing/crusts, excoriation, erosion/ulceration, and dryness/scaling were assessed using a standardized severity grading scale.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined to evaluate the superiority of BMA over SA in terms of safety, defined as the proportion of participants experiencing at least one treatment-related AE during the 5-week treatment period.

Based on previous acupuncture safety studies reporting AE rates of approximately 18.1–21.5% with conventional metal needles.9,10 and pilot randomized trials in which no treatment-related AEs were observed in the BMAG,11,13 the expected proportions were conservatively set at 1% for BMA and 10% for SA.

Participants were allocated in a 1:1 ratio. Assuming a one-sided significance level of 0.025 and a statistical power of 80%, the required sample size was calculated as 100 participants per group. Allowing for an anticipated 10% dropout rate, 112 participants were enrolled in each group.

Statistical analysis

All randomized participants who received at least one treatment were included in the full analysis set and safety set. Safety outcomes, including the primary endpoint, were analyzed using the safety set.

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) or SPSS (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

A one-sided significance level of 0.025 was applied for the primary endpoint to test the superiority of BMA over SA. Two-sided significance levels of 0.05 were used for secondary outcomes.

Primary Outcome Analysis

The primary outcome was the proportion of participants experiencing at least one treatment-related AE during the 5-week treatment period.

Between-group comparisons were conducted using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Secondary Efficacy Outcome Analysis

Efficacy outcomes were measured at baseline (Visit 1) and post-treatment (Visit 13). Missing data due to participant dropouts were excluded from the analysis. Changes in measurements before and after treatment, as well as between the two time points, were analyzed. Statistically significant differences between the experimental and control groups were assessed using independent t-tests for between-group comparisons and paired t-tests for within-group comparisons.

Secondary Safety Outcome Analysis

Skin allergy-related AEs were compared between groups using χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Changes in vital signs and blood tests were analyzed using independent and paired t-tests for between- and within-group comparisons, respectively.

Secondary safety outcomes

No skin allergy-related AEs were observed in the BMAG, whereas such events occurred in eight participants in the SAG, resulting in a significantly higher proportion of affected participants in the SAG (p = 0.007). Because no events occurred in the BMAG, risk estimates and corresponding confidence intervals could not be calculated using standard statistical methods.

Changes in vital signs remained within clinically acceptable ranges in both groups, and no clinically meaningful between-group differences were identified.

Similarly, no significant between-group differences were observed in blood test parameters. No treatment-related adverse events associated with laboratory abnormalities were reported.

These findings are consistent with the favorable safety profile of BMA.

Discussion

Summary of key findings

The primary outcome showed that the proportion of participants experiencing at least one treatment-related AE during the treatment period was significantly lower in the BMAG than in the SAG, indicating improved tolerability of BMA compared with conventional SA.

In addition to improved safety, participants receiving BMA demonstrated significantly greater improvements in OSDI, VAS-SS, and QoL scores. Treatment satisfaction was also higher in the BMAG, suggesting that this intervention may provide both clinical and experiential benefits.

Comparison with previous studies

Previous randomized trials have reported that acupuncture can improve tear film stability, subjective symptoms, and other clinical indicators in patients with DE.1, 2, 3, 14, 15, 16 These findings support the role of acupuncture as a complementary therapeutic approach.

However, concerns have been raised regarding localized irritation and hypersensitivity reactions associated with periocular acupuncture using conventional metal needles. The present study extends prior evidence by demonstrating that a biodegradable microneedle device may reduce the risk of such treatment-related AEs while maintaining therapeutic effectiveness.

Clinical and scientific implications

The lower proportion of participants experiencing treatment-related AEs observed in this study suggests that BMA may offer a clinically meaningful safety advantage in periocular acupuncture.

In addition, improvements in symptom burden and quality of life indicate that BMA may represent a practical therapeutic option for patients with persistent DE symptoms who seek complementary treatment approaches.

Strengths and limitations

This study represents one of the first large-scale multicenter randomized trials comparing BMA with conventional acupuncture in patients with DE. The rigorous randomized design and standardized intervention protocol strengthen the reliability of the findings.

Several limitations should be considered. First, the primary endpoint focused on safety outcomes, and further trials are needed to confirm long-term efficacy and comparative effectiveness against established treatments such as artificial tears or anti-inflammatory therapies.

Second, participant blinding was limited due to differences in device characteristics, although assessor blinding and standardized procedures were implemented to reduce potential bias.

Finally, broader diagnostic criteria were used for participant inclusion, and future studies should apply internationally recognized diagnostic standards to enhance generalizability.

Conclusions and future research

BMA was associated with a lower risk of treatment-related AEs and greater clinical improvement compared with conventional SA in patients with DE.

Further studies comparing BMA with standard medical therapies and evaluating long-term outcomes are warranted to clarify its role in integrative DE management. These results suggest that BMA may provide a safe and clinically beneficial treatment option for patients with DE.

Machine learning-driven drug repurposing for HER2-positive breast cancer
Authors: Basavana Gowda Hosur Dinesh, Srinivas Ganjipete, Bandral Sunil Kumar, Damodar Nayak Ammunje, Selvaraj Kunjiappan, Prabu Kumar Seetharaman, Mohankumar Ramar, Kumarappan Chidambaram, Parasuraman Pavadai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Breast cancer, particularly the Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2)-positive subtype, remains a significant clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature and the frequent development of resistance to targeted therapy. In this study, we created a comprehensive machine-learning (ML)-based drug repurposing framework to identify new HER2 inhibitors among FDA-approved drugs. A carefully curated dataset of 13,944 HER2 bioactivity records from BindingDB was used to build high-accuracy predictive models. This involved optimizing descriptors, reducing dimensionality, and selecting key features. The Random Forest model trained on 75 optimized descriptors demonstrated the best performance (R² = 0.81, RMSE = 0.86, MAE = 0.6483). This allowed us to understand structure-activity relationships and perform reliable virtual screening of 4,099 FDA-approved drugs. Docking studies against HER2 wild-type (7PCD) revealed strong interactions, with scores from -10.5 to -7.5 kcal/mol. For the exon-20 insertion mutant (8U8X), the affinities were even higher (-11.6 to -8.0 kcal/mol), exceeding the reference co-crystal ligand in both cases. FDA0870 (Timolol maleate) consistently ranked as a top dual inhibitor, forming stable hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts with key catalytic residues of both wild-type and mutant HER2. Molecular dynamics simulations supported its stability, with wild-type HER2-FDA0870 complexes showing an average protein RMSD of 3.4 Å and a ligand RMSD of 2.9 Å, whereas mutant complexes had an average protein RMSD of 3.9 Å and a ligand RMSD of 2.1 Å over extended trajectories. Persistent interactions with residues such as ASP863/ASP867, THR862/THR866, and LYS753 were observed. ADMET analysis indicated that the drug had good drug-like properties, moderate permeability, a high unbound fraction, and acceptable safety features, suggesting its potential for further development. This integrated ML, docking, MD, and ADMET approach demonstrates that FDA0870 is a promising repurposed dual inhibitor for HER2-positive and HER2-mutant breast cancer, highlighting the value of computational pipelines in speeding up the search for targeted cancer therapies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-45361-z.

Introduction

Globally, breast cancer is considered one of the most pertinent health issues affecting women, as it accounts for a large number of cancer-related deaths1. Based on GLOBOCAN 2022 data by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), it is depicted that breast cancer is the most diagnosed cancer globally, with 2.3 million new cases, which contributes to 11.7% of total cancers diagnosed2. It also results in about 685 thousand deaths annually3. Such factors as population growth, lifestyle changes, hormonal factors, and inadequate access to healthcare lead to an increase in the incidence of breast cancer4. Hence, even though gradual improvements are being made on various fronts, the disease remains a major public health issue bar none worldwide. These tendencies indicate the need to work more actively on improving the prevention and treatment system5.

It is known that breast cancer is a disease that has multiple forms, which are caused by different factors, such as genetic, hormonal, and environmental ones6. There are certain risk factors to note: hormonal factors and genetic mutations such as BRCA1 and BRCA27. Hormone replacement therapy, late menopause, and early menarche can increase the risk of hormone-related cancers like breast and endometrial carcinoma8. Reproductive history, along with lifestyle choices including alcohol consumption, obesity/overweight, and physical inactivity, also contributes to risk9. With the introduction of molecular profiling, it is possible to identify the subtypes of breast cancer: triple-negative, Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2)-enriched, and luminal A and B tumours. Each subtype has distinct clinical features, outcomes, and responses to treatment10. This complexity necessitates adopting a precision medicine approach that targets individual genes.

HER2-positive breast cancer is a subset of breast cancer that can be found in 15-30% of patients who have breast cancer11. It is characterized by gene amplification or overexpression of HER2 and HER2-positive breast cancer, which is an extremely aggressive form of the disease affecting about 15-30% of women with breast cancer12. It is characterized by either multiple copies of the HER2 gene or overexpression of the HER2 protein on chromosome 17q21. The HER2 gene encodes a 185-kDa transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase, a member of the ErbB family13. This protein contains an intracellular kinase domain, a single transmembrane helix, and an extracellular ligand-binding domain14. Unlike other receptors, HER2 does not need a known natural ligand to activate it. Instead, its activation relies on constitutive dimerization achieved with itself or with HER315. This continuous dimerization over activates signalling pathways responsible for the growth and progression of many cancers. These biological processes include cellular proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis, among others16, as illustrated in Figure 1. The overactive HER2 signalling pathway also renders malignancies resistant to currently available targeted therapies by upregulating DNA repair mechanisms, inhibiting apoptosis, and increasing the mutational load within the tumor microenvironment17. The development of new-generation inhibitors, including disautinib, pertuzumab, lapatinib, neratinib, and tucatinib, has significantly improved treatment outcomes18. Nonetheless, there are still considerable barriers, such as primary or acquired resistance, systemic toxicities, high treatment costs, and limited efficacy in low/HER2-expressing tumours, that need to be addressed with innovative drug candidates targeting this oncogenic antigen systemically without inducing a significant toxicity profile in patients treated with these medications19.

Drug repositioning, also named drug repurposing, involves identifying new indications for existing drugs that are known to be effective for a different disorder20. This strategy of repurposing old drugs is preferable to the years-long, costly process of developing novel medications, which can cost $2-3 billion and take 10 years or more21. By contrast, repurposed medicines may already reach the market in around 6.5 years after development begins, with an average start-up investment of about $300 million, primarily because testing expenses in earlier stages are less significant22. Consequently, drug repurposing has now become quite common in the treatment of cancer, infectious diseases, hyperlipidemia (statins), diabetes mellitus (metformin), and hypertension (ACE inhibitors)23.

By integrating structural and molecular data into machine-learning models, the repositioning of existing drugs has been facilitated, enabling the identification of compounds for a specific disease within a very short period while minimizing costs24. These techniques are currently being used in drug repositioning for ailments such as non-small cell lung carcinoma and breast cancer, among others25. Typically, this process involves identifying biomarkers in the datasets above and performing computational analysis to determine drug-target interactions, thereby reducing the need for laboratory experiments by providing initial information26. Machine learning leverages molecular descriptors and bioactivity data to generate structure-activity relationship (SAR) models that predict anticancer activity27. Quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) modeling facilitates grouping similar compounds by drug target, while in silico studies aid in refining the candidate selection process. All these methods significantly improve throughput compared to conventional methods, since results can be achieved much faster with machine learning algorithms28. The utilization of machine learning, combined with molecular docking, facilitates the creation of an ADMET profile and the conduct of molecular dynamics simulations, thereby forming an effective stepwise approach29. The application of this approach ensures a more accurate and reliable identification of new anti-HER2 agents, without increasing financial expenditures, thereby accelerating the overall discovery process as system inefficiencies are reduced.

The study uses different computational techniques, primarily Machine learning, molecular docking, ADMET evaluation, and molecular dynamics simulation, to discover new HER2 inhibitors. The ML models have been developed to filter out molecules with weak HER2-inhibitory activity and to identify predominant drug-like features in chemical structure space. Trastuzumab, the FDA-approved standard for HER2-targeted therapy, faces these hurdles and its side effects, underscoring the need for better alternatives. Thus, this work aims to repurpose existing medicines to more effectively treat diseases related to HER2 protein overexpression and to slow cancer cell growth. A computational approach performs a stepwise search for new agents with higher activity, selectivity, and better pharmacokinetic characteristics, which can be further developed as metastatic breast cancer-specific therapies.

Methodology

Data collection

Data is the fundamental building block for predictive machine learning (ML) models. In the process of building machine-learning models, initially, one needs to collect data, which can be achieved by reviewing drug compounds and their inhibitory effects or by extracting information from published literature30. A dataset of 13,944 chemicals assayed for inhibition of the HER2 protein was obtained from the BindingDB database (https://www.bindingdb.org/)31. To improve the reliability of machine learning model predictions, IC50 values were converted to pIC50 values and written to a CSV file for further analysis.

Molecular descriptor generation

Molecular descriptors are numerical parameters derived from physicochemical properties, obtained through systematic techniques to encode chemical information in molecular structures32. They provide a quantitative depiction of all five physical, chemical, electronic, steric, and structural aspects of a molecule, which helps with statistical analysis and predictive modelling. These descriptors serve as input variables in QSAR (quantitative structure-activity relationship), SAR (structure-activity relationship), and virtual screening (VS) studies, which are used in machine learning approaches to build predictive models of biological activities, such as pIC50 values33. In this work, 1826 one, two, and three-dimensional molecular descriptors for 13,944 compounds targeting HER2 were computed using Mordred version 1.2.0, which refer to various topological, steric, and electronic properties, etc33. The calculated descriptors representing diverse chemical features and structural properties required for modelling the studied compounds were then saved in a CSV file in Excel format.

Data pre-processing and data cleaning

To build a reliable HER2 ML model, the data were carefully pre-processed using two main approaches: dimensionality reduction and data normalization. During data cleaning, duplicate records, missing entries, and erroneous values were removed. Descriptors that did not contribute meaningful information were also excluded and exported as a CSV file for further investigation. The chemical validity of the compounds was confirmed using cheminformatics tools, such as RDKit and Open Babel, to verify their structural integrity34.

Dimensionality reduction

Dimensionality reduction is a crucial step in removing unwanted descriptors35. The pipeline underwent the subsequent steps after the practical. (i) Since zero-variance descriptors by definition do not offer a discriminative signal, invariance filtering was the initial step in which all of them were removed36. (ii) Nearly constant descriptors that lack sufficient dispersion to aid in distinguishing outcomes were eliminated by a straightforward filter of low variance37,38. (iii) A high-correlation filter reduced model variance and avoided erroneous importance attributions by selecting a single sample from highly correlated pairs or clusters, thus cutting down on multicollinearity39. (iv) Outlier detection and removal are examples of how these anomalies can be controlled to prevent skewed parameter estimations, erroneous importance attribution, and deterioration of external validity (e.g., via z-score diagnostics)40. (v) Scaling molecular descriptors was made possible by normalization or standardization. Normalization pushed the values into the [0,1] range, while standardization centered them at 0 and scaled them to unit variance41. Both processes impacted optimization processes and learners based remotely. These features were cleaned, de-redundant, and normalized before we used feature selection techniques to select the key predictive and descriptive features that provide a condensed, informative foundation for model development and the subsequent assessment of their objective performance characteristics42.

Feature selection/importance

Feature selection was performed using the Orange data mining tool. We used the Rank widget set up for regression to apply filter-based FS in Orange (v3.3) (https://orangedatamining.com/). Rank received the pre-processed dataset (descriptors as Features; activity as Target) and used RReliefF and statistics to calculate task-appropriate univariate significance scores. We selected descriptors that consistently ranked well and showed low mutual correlation (i.e., preferring one representative among near-duplicates) to generate a robust subgroup. We also examined concordance among scorers. The Top-k control was then used to limit the ranking list, fixing κ = 75. A replicable collection of the top 75 molecular descriptors was produced and exported using Select Columns (Selected) for subsequent construction of an ML regression model. By focusing on the most informative descriptors, this methodical strategy maximizes model complexity, enhances training effectiveness, and ensures reliable performance43.

Construction of machine learning models

After selecting the 75 most informative descriptors, the chemical dataset of 13,944 was divided into distinct training (80%) and test sets (20%) based on scaffold to preserve chemotype identity44. After selecting the best descriptors, we allocated 11,155 compounds for model training and 2,789 for testing to evaluate our methodology and assess generalization to additional scaffolds45. Machine learning techniques were examined, and quantitative predictors comprised a 75-descriptor set, which was further classified using scaffold splitting. The scaffold-partitioned training set was specifically used to train five WEKA classifiers: Random Forest, RandomTree, K-star, IBk (with entropic distance), and decision tree46. To identify the best algorithm-descriptor combination, models were evaluated using internally stratified 10, 20, 30 & 40-fold cross-validation as part of this screening procedure. They were then tested using fully held-out test data.

Random forest

A random subset of characteristics is considered at each split in Random Forests, an ensemble bagging technique that trains on several decision trees generated from bootstrap replicas of the training set. The final output is obtained by combining the predictions from each of these sub-trees, thereby resolving most of the issues with decision tree learners. Some key hyperparameters that influence the bias-variance trade-off are the number of trees, the maximum tree depth/leaf size, and the number of features per split. Furthermore, out-of-bag estimation from unsampled examples serves as an internal approximation of generalization ability. Although nonparametric regression and classification can provide interpretable models by identifying patterns and learning feature relevance, they can also be affected by highly cardinal variables or cause problems when new samples fall outside the training range47.

Random tree

Random Tree constructs a singular decision tree regressor. The splits are determined by reducing within-node variance, and candidate predictors at each node are limited to a random subset. It incorporates non-linearities and interactions with minimal computational cost and a clear, rule-based architecture. The key settings include split characteristics, the minimum number of instances per leaf, and the maximum depth. As a singular, often unpruned tree, it exhibits significant variation and is more susceptible to overfitting than ensemble approaches48.

K-star

An instance-based, lazy classifier called K-Star (K*) uses entropy-based similarity rather than distance. It selects the class most likely to transform each stored instance into a query instance. Without assuming anything about the distribution, this “transformation complexity” automatically handles missing values and mixed or categorical characteristics, establishing smooth, locally adaptive decision bounds. For heterogeneous tabular data, K* is often as effective as k-NN and trees, as it requires only one mixing parameter to balance noise and structure. The primary disadvantage is that prediction cost increases with the number of occurrences; hence, it is best to index or reduce large datasets49.

IBK

Weka’s k-NN (IBk) is a slow, nonparametric classifier that uses a chosen metric to vote among the k closest training points to generate predictions. Distance weighting is an option. It can depict intricate non-linear boundaries, requires no training, adapts to local conditions, and functions effectively as a robust, unambiguous baseline for tabular data—particularly when k is varied, and features are standardized. Inbuilt cross-validation for k, tie management, and neighbor-search backends (linear scan/KD trees, and ball trees) are a few helpful features. The model is susceptible to noisy neighbors, sensitive to feature scales and irrelevant dimensions (the curse of dimensionality), and the prediction time increases with dataset size. These problems can be resolved by metric learning, feature selection/dimensionality reduction, normalization, or approximate indexing50.

Decision tree

A decision tree is a recursive-partitioning, nonparametric learner that generates if-then rules by selecting splits that minimize impurity (e.g., variance reduction for regression or information gain/gain ratio for classification). After minimal preprocessing, it will be sufficient to handle rough missing values, heterogeneous feature types, high-order interactions, and non-linearities. Additionally, it provides clear models with feature importance estimation and path-level logic, which are useful for generating hypotheses and making informed decisions. Top-down CART/ID3-style heuristics are beneficial for both fast and scalable training processes. The generalization process is maintained by post-pruning procedure (cost-complexity) and regularization strategies (max depth, min samples per leaf/split). There are concerns about trees: they can split too much, reducing stability, and they can overfit in high-dimensional or noisy data. Trees are often meta-combined to achieve the highest accuracy while maintaining data interpretability (Random Forests, Gradient Boosting)51.

Model-based screening of FDA drugs

We conducted virtual screening (VS) for therapeutic repurposing using a well-trained, proven machine-learning (ML) technique on 4,099 FDA-approved small molecules obtained from DrugBank. Compounds were encoded with complementary molecular representations, following chemical standardization (removal of salts/solvates, normalization of charge/tautomer, retention of stereochemistry, and exclusion of duplicates/inorganics). Once the application domain (AD) was clearly defined to limit extrapolation, it was further refined using variance and collinearity filtering53. We used scaffold-based splits, repeated cross-validation, and a scaffold-held-out external test to evaluate five learners (Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbour, Random Forest, IBK, and Random Tree). We considered calibration quality, stability across seeds and scaffolds, and regression performance (R2, RMSE, and MAE) when selecting a model. After calibration, error stratification, and AD coverage checks, the top-performing model was used to score the standardized DrugBank set. With uncertainty constraints, this provided target-specific projected activities (represented as pIC₅₀). The final rank-ordered, confidence-annotated shortlist of FDA high-priority drugs served as input for molecular docking, which identified the most likely hits54.

Molecular docking studies

To predict the ligand interactions within the active site cleft of the human HER2 protein, molecular docking was performed using the AutoDock Vina algorithm55. FDA-approved drugs were retrieved from DrugBank and prepared by assigning three-dimensional protonation states at physiological pH, applying Gasteiger partial charges, and exporting ligands as PDBQT files. Receptor coordinates for the wild-type and mutant kinase domains (PDB IDs: 7PCD and 8U8X) were generated by removing crystallographic waters and additional heteroatoms, adding all hydrogens, resolving alternate occupancies, assigning Gasteiger charges, and ultimately converting to PDBQT. No docking could take place anywhere other than the catalytic pocket. 7PCD (x = 9.98 Å, y = −8.69 Å, z = −13.72 Å) and 8U8X (x = −20.91 Å, y = 13.03 Å, z = 17.17 Å) had fixed search-box centres56. To maintain comparability, the box dimensions were set to include the hinge region and the entire back pocket and were kept constant across all ligands. In order to generate several binding hypotheses for every ligand-receptor combination under identical conditions, Vina employed the default scoring algorithm with exhaustiveness = 857. The most advantageous, geometrically possible conformation ideally exhibiting canonical hinge anchoring was retained after possible conformations were ranked according to their estimated binding free energy (kcal·mol⁻¹). Both receptors’ protein-ligand contacts (hydrogen bonds, π-π/π-cation interactions, salt bridges, and hydrophobic packing) were examined in Maestro to ensure stereochemical realism and to enable a like-for-like comparison of the protein-ligand complex in 2D and 3D58.

Molecular dynamic studies

Each top-ranked ligand-receptor complex was subjected to 10-ns molecular dynamics simulations, conducted for both the wild-type and mutant HER2 using the Schrodinger suite’s DESMOND v6.3 module59. Protein-ligand complexes were placed in a TIP3 water-filled cubic border box as the solvent model. To balance the system’s charge, the protein was reduced, and counterions were added. A 0.15 M NaCl solution mimicked physiological saline. The nine-phase simulation technique included two minimization and four equilibration stages before main production. The initial study used 10-nanosecond MD simulations of the selected ligand-receptor complex. The OPLS 5 force field was used for MD simulations in an NPT ensemble at 300 K and 1.01325 bar. The most stable subgroup was then extended for 1000 ns for further analysis. Using the Simulation Interaction Diagram (SID), post-dynamic trajectory analyses were performed in Schrödinger’s Desmond60. Complex stability was evaluated using time-resolved protein-ligand interaction profiles along the trajectories, protein and ligand root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), and residue-wise root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF)61.

ADMET profiling

The ADMET profiles were analysed using the ADMETlab 3.0 web server, with SMILES representations of each chemical. To determine how effectively they could be produced and to identify candidates with strong pharmacokinetic and safety qualities. Further, thoroughly examined the endpoints of absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity62.

Result and discussion

The study followed in the following order: (i) collection of data on compounds with HER2 activity that have been reported in the literature from bindingDB; (ii) generating descriptors from Modred followed by refining descriptors through Dimensionality reduction; (iii) feature selection; (iv) creating and validating machine learning models; (v) VS of FDA-approved medications and using the ML models to prioritize compounds (vi) Molecular docking and dynamic study against drug for structure-based evaluation to target both wild-type and mutant HER2 to find repurposing leads; and (vii) in-silico ADMET profiling to describe the physicochemical and pharmacological liabilities of the chosen hit.

Data preprocessing

The BindingDB database (https://www.bindingdb.org/) was utilized to identify 13,944 active compounds and their bioactive values (PIC50) against HER2. We used Mordred v1.2.0 to compute molecular features, yielding 1,826 Two and three-dimensional descriptors for each molecule. To help construct machine-learning (ML) models, these descriptors fall into four classes: topological, autocorrelation, steric, and electronic. When feature sets are noisy, duplicated, and high-dimensional, they make models more variable and complex, which can lead to overfitting or underfitting and ultimately reduce generalizability. To improve the model and descriptor set by removing noise, redundancy, and overfitting, a meticulous dimensionality-reduction pipeline has been implemented.

Dimensionality reduction

The dimensionality reduction method used a systematic, multi-stage filtering process, similar to how descriptors are usually enhanced (Table 1). First, descriptors with no data or blank spaces were removed, eliminating 695 unwanted features and leaving 1,131 descriptors in the dataset. Next, a constant-value filter was applied to remove 111 descriptors that lacked discriminative power and provided no useful information. This reduced the total to 1,020 descriptors. We then used a low-variance filter (variance threshold < 0.01) to further refine the features; after this step, 216 quasi-constant descriptors were removed, leaving 804 highly variable descriptors. To address multicollinearity, a high-correlation filter (|r| > 0.90) was employed, identified by examining the upper triangle of the Pearson correlation matrix. After removing 461 duplicate descriptors, 343 mostly unique features remained. We applied Z-score analysis (|Z| > 7) to check for stability, which identified and eliminated 194 descriptors with extremely high standardized deviations, leaving 149 suitable for model development. Finally, min-max normalization was applied to scale all selected descriptors to the [0, 1] interval, ensuring each trait had the same influence during machine learning training. A correlation heat map was generated to assess the effectiveness of the dimensionality reduction process, as shown in Figure 2.

Molecular descriptors used in the regression models

The variable importance method identified a set of 2D and 3D chemical descriptors used in the regression model. These features were essential for accurately predicting how new drugs that could inhibit the HER2 receptor would function in the body. We utilized the Rank widget in the Orange Data Mining platform to systematically rank and select 149 chemical descriptors, improving the accuracy and reliability of the machine learning model. We employed the Univariate Regression scoring algorithm to evaluate the descriptors. This function individually tests how well each descriptor predicts the continuous target variable (biological activity) using statistical metrics. This process ensures that only statistically significant variables are included in the model by identifying features with a strong linear relationship with compound potency.

This study selected the top 75 descriptors based on their ranking scores and integrated them into the leading regression model in Table 2. These descriptors cover a broad spectrum of 2D topological, electronic, hydrophobic, and molecular surface-area variables. Each provides distinct structural and physicochemical insights into how compounds interact with targets. The selection highlighted the most important traits: PEOE_VSA1, PEOE_VSA3, PEOE_VSA10, SMR_VSA3, and SlogP_VSA10. This underscores the importance of surface area distribution and electrostatic potential in determining ligand-receptor binding strength. In a similar vein, descriptors like AATS3i, GATS4p, ATSC4d, and Balaban’s J index demonstrated complex atomic-level topological and autocorrelation features, reflecting the structural complexity and three-dimensional organization of molecules involved in HER2 inhibition.

Descriptor redundancy and dataset noise were effectively reduced using the univariate regression-based feature-ranking method, which improved the signal-to-noise ratio and simplified the overall model. The final set of 75 descriptors offered a balanced selection of molecular features, ensuring that the subsequent machine learning regression model was mechanistically interpretable, physiologically relevant, and statistically robust.

The dataset used for the scaffold-based split included 13,944 molecules. It was divided so that 11,155 molecules were used for training and 2,789 for testing, which results in an 80% training and 20% testing split. The RDKit Bemis–Murcko scaffold algorithm identified 5,142 unique parent scaffolds. The training set contains 3,964 distinct scaffolds, while the test set has 1,178, ensuring no scaffold overlap or leakage between the training and test sets.

Prediction models’ statistical outcomes on the training set by cross-validation

The training dataset was used to evaluate the expected resilience and reliability of five regression models through 10, 20, 30, and 40- fold cross- validation in WEKA: Random Forest, K- Star, IBK, Decision Tree, and Random Tree, as shown in Table 3. Univariate regression- based feature ranking identified the top 75 chemical descriptors for analysis, regarded as the most significant factors influencing chemical behavior. Incorporating these enhanced features improved model performance and reduced noise from less relevant variables. Overall, with an R ² of 0. 813, an MAE of 0. 6483, and an RMSE of 0. 8626, the Random Forest algorithm achieved the highest prediction accuracy with 40- fold cross- validation on the training set, explaining over 81% of the variance in biological activity. The Relative Absolute Error (RAE) and Root Relative Squared Error (RRSE), at 52. 81% and 59. 32%, respectively, indicated good generalization and close alignment between predicted and actual values. This performance boost is attributed to Random Forest’ s ensemble learning approach, which reduces overfitting and enhances stability by combining multiple decision trees. The K- Star model yielded R 2 of 0. 7935, MAE of 0. 5991, and RMSE of 0. 9286, performing comparably to Random Forest with RRSE of 65. 14% and RAE of 48. 80%. Virtual screening of FDA drugs

Compounds with metal atoms, extensive amino acid chains, and inorganic elements were all omitted; 4,099 were FDA-approved. A curated collection of 1,615 pharmacologically and chemically stable compounds was created for further computational analysis. Two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) descriptors were calculated for each drug using Mordred, and features similar to those used in the regression models were extracted. These features served as an external test set for virtual screening. The RF Regression model predicted the pIC50 values for all chemicals in the test set and ranked them from highest to lowest. As a result, the compounds were ordered by their estimated inhibitory effectiveness. To compare potency, the projected pIC50 values were converted to IC50 values. Compounds with IC50 values below 600 nM—approximately 249 compounds—were prioritized as lead candidates, as shown in Table S1. Additionally, the selected compounds were screened for molecular docking studies to evaluate their binding affinity, conformational changes, and interactions with the breast cancer-targeted protein.

ADMET studies

Timolol maleate (MW 316.16; C₁₃H₂₄N₄O₃S) has a TPSA of 79.7 Å and a low to moderate lipophilicity profile (logP 1.76; logD 1.70), indicating that its polarity is well-balanced for oral distribution. It does not violate any of the Golden Triangle, Lipinski, Pfizer, or GSK filters, and its physicochemical properties are fairly consistent with those of drug-like compounds. It can be applied in medicinal chemistry, as evidenced by its high QED score of 0.79. According to permeability indicators (MDCK - 4.56; Caco-2 - 4.77), the molecule belongs to the intermediate class of passive permeability. Additionally, efflux may reduce effective intestinal uptake, even when the absorption characteristics are typically satisfactory, because it is believed to be a substrate of P-gp. The drug is widely distributed in the body and has a low risk of protein-binding interactions, according to distribution predictions, which also reveal a large unbound fraction (Fu 86%) and extremely low plasma protein binding (PPB ~9%). Notably, the high probability of BBB penetration indicates CNS access, which, depending on the indication, may be necessary for care or be crucial for therapy. According to metabolism data, timolol is a potent CYP2D6 inhibitor and a substrate for several CYP enzymes, including CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4. This suggests the possibility of in vivo interactions between medications that impact metabolism. Its known clinical pharmacokinetics include a short-predicted half-life (1.2 h) and clearance (7.64 mL/min/kg), both indicating rapid systemic clearance. At standard doses, the medication exhibits minimal intrinsic cytotoxicity and low hERG liability, as predicted by safety studies. DILI, AMES positive, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, and neurotoxicity are some of the toxicity flags that highlight areas requiring experimental monitoring. Timolol maleate offers a pharmacokinetic profile that is manageable and chemically accessible, with acceptable permeability and sufficient systemic exposure. For de-risking in future studies, efflux liability and potential organ-specific toxicities must be addressed as significant risks.

Discussion

An integrated in silico drug-repurposing pipeline was used in this study to identify novel HER2 inhibitors targeting both wild-type and exon-20 insertion mutant variants of HER2. This pipeline involved extensive data mining for initial model selection, feature selection using descriptors for machine learning models, and the application of machine learning and docking tools to identify candidates. Additionally, molecular dynamics simulations of the selected compounds were performed, along with absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) evaluation. This multi-tiered approach is superior to traditional techniques because it is less costly and yields faster results. Conventional methods often involve long durations and high attrition rates. The analysis employed a dataset of 1826 Mordred descriptors as input. The dataset was processed through methods such as correlation analysis, low-variance filtering, z-score-based outlier removal, data standardization, and missing value imputation to reduce noise while maintaining signal fidelity across collinear variables. Univariate regression was used to select the top 75 descriptors, with topology, hydrophobic interactions, and electrostatics being most significant. These descriptors create a robust feature space for the machine learning model by influencing the pharmacological actions of HER2 inhibitors.

The selected 75 descriptors are highlighted as the most essential molecular features for HER2 receptor interaction. These 75 descriptors are further categorized into topological, steric, electrostatic, and hydrogen-bonding groups. All of these descriptors are vital for predicting how well drugs bind to HER2 and their specificity. Topological descriptors, such as EState_VSA, GATS, and AATS, provide insights into the HER2 binding pocket by capturing atomic connectivity, molecular shape, and spatial arrangement. These molecular features also influence the receptor’s selectivity and binding strength and determine whether a ligand is rigid or flexible. Additionally, descriptors like SMR_VSA and SlogP_VSA help assess a molecule’s hydrophobicity and lipophilicity, which are critical for hydrophobic interactions within the receptor’s cavity.

A compound’s behaviour when interacting with the electrostatic environment of HER2 through hydrogen bonding is considered by the descriptors PEOE_VSA, nHBAcc, and nHBDon. Most highly potent HER2 inhibitors form essential hydrogen bonds within the receptor’s active site, which are crucial for binding. Additionally, aromaticity is important for π-π stacking interactions with aromatic residues in HER2, as indicated by descriptors such as nAromAtom and nAHRing, which further stabilize the ligand-receptor complex.

We used the preprocessing chain to test five machine learning algorithms on the model input set. These included Random Forest (RF), K-Star (a decision tree-based classifier, regressor, ensemble method, and instance generator), Random Tree (a classifier ensemble), and IBK (an instance-based learning technique known as the K-nearest neighbor algorithm). The best option was RF, which outperformed the other methods in both internal and external validation. RF had an R² of 0.813, RMSE of 0.8626, and MEA of 0.6483 during 40-fold cross-validation. This shows that the model can effectively learn the relationship between structure and activity, and the results improved with ensemble averaging. Additionally, the test set achieved an R² of about 0.72, indicating good performance on data outside the training set. RF outperformed K-Star and IBK because, compared to single classifier techniques, RF’s ensemble methods better handle non-linear intermolecular interactions. This ability is important for training QSAR models and screening libraries for a smaller group of kinases and physiologically active compounds that are known to be therapeutic agents for breast cancer.

Based on these findings, we conducted a virtual screening of 1,615 FDA-approved drugs and docked them with the receptor structures of HER2 wild-type (7PCD) and mutant (8U8X). We identified several intriguing high-affinity inhibitors; compared to the cocrystal reference ligand, the RF-prioritized compounds showed greater affinity. The docking scores of the ligands for wild-type HER2 ranged from -10.5 to -7.5 kcal/mol. Key residues SER783, THR862, ASP863, and LYS753 interacted significantly with FAD0098 and FDA0870, indicating they were well-positioned for ATP-competitive inhibition in both the hinge and the back pocket. The candidates’ scores for Mutant HER2 (8U8X) were even higher than those of the reference ligand (-7.5 kcal/mol), ranging from -11.6 to -8.0 kcal/mol. FDA0886, FAD0422, and FDA0439 again exhibited stabilizing interactions with mutated residues (ARG853, ASN854, THR866, PHE868). This suggests they could induce structural changes that reduce drug effectiveness. Due to its strong affinity system, FDA0870 was developed as a dual inhibitor targeting both protein forms.

Following additional MD simulations to verify the dynamic stability of the contacts, the initial 10 ns run revealed that FDA0870 was the most stable binder. Based on these results, a 1000-ns simulation was chosen for extended analysis. In the mutant HER2 complex, stable parallel behavior was observed, with RMSD ranges (protein: 1.0-5.0 Å; ligand: 0.5-4.7 Å), and the persistent interaction of ASP867 and LYS753 indicates adequate functional adaptation of the ligand to the mutated exon-20 site of the inhibitor. For wild-type HER2, RMSD values (1.4-4.7 Å) and average ligand stability of 2.9 Å correlated with low RMSF values for critical residues. The absence of significant destabilization of the protein component by SSA suggests that FDA0870 may still be a viable HER2 inhibitor. Likewise, the complementary ADMET profiling supported its potential for repurposing; timolol maleate exhibited optimal physicochemical properties, with a QED score of 0.79, logP of 1.76, and TPSA of 79.7, satisfying Lipinski’s rules and the basic criteria set by Pfizer and GSK. Its intermediate permeability (high unbound percentage) and low plasma protein binding also indicated a favorable pharmacokinetic profile. Although some toxicity warnings and potential CYP2D6 interactions exist, the overall ADMET assessment aligns with current knowledge; thus, there are no clear contraindications. It has been demonstrated that FDA0870 is a potent dual kinase inhibitor of both wild-type and mutant ERbB. This highlights the application of an integrated computational approach within an accelerated drug-repurposing framework to discover novel targeted breast cancer treatments.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the impact of a multi-stage, in silico-integrated methodology on drug repurposing to identify effective HER2 inhibitors capable of treating both wild-type and clinically significant mutant variants. It involves meticulous data curation, optimized descriptor engineering, feature selection, robust machine-learning-based virtual screening, molecular docking, extensive molecular dynamics simulations, and comprehensive ADMET profiling to facilitate swift, reliable prioritization of druggable candidates from a broad array of FDA-approved drugs. This research identified Timolol maleate (FDA0870) as the most promising dual inhibitor, characterized by ML-predicted potency, binding affinity exceeding that of co-crystal ligands, stable interactions with key catalytic residues, and superior dynamic behaviour in nanosecond-scale simulations. Its advantageous pharmacokinetic and drug-like properties suggest potential for repurposing in HER2-positive and HER2-mutant breast cancer. Accordingly, these results support the use of machine learning pipelines to accelerate and reduce costs in the development of receptor-targeting drugs. Further experimental validation, including enzymatic assays, cellular activity tests, and in vivo studies, is necessary to assess the drug’s safety profile and effectiveness against breast cancer.

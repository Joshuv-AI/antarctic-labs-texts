On the occasion of the 10th ‘scaling with love’ event to promote oral health for persons with disabilities
Authors: Kyoung-Man Min
Published: 2026

Periodontal disease is a leading cause of tooth loss in adults. Since its founding in 1960, the Korean Academy of Periodontology (KAP) has conducted extensive research and, based on this scientific foundation, has consistently worked to improve oral health centered on periodontal health at the national level. In 2009, the Academy established the world’s first ‘Gum Day’ (March 24). Since then, it has implemented a range of initiatives to enhance public awareness, including the ‘Scaling with love’ talent donation event for persons with disabilities (PWDs), media photo opportunities, press conferences, commemorative ceremonies card news and webtoons for social media, posters, radio campaign advertisements, and ‘Gum Health Lectures’ at public health centers and training institutions nationwide. These activities align with the guidelines outlined by the FDI World Dental Federation in its white paper on prevention and management of periodontal diseases for oral health and general health [1].

Despite these efforts, under the current National Health Insurance system of Korea, gingivitis and periodontal disease (Korean Classification of Disease code K05) accounted for over 19 million outpatient visits annually as of 2024. Furthermore, with annual benefit expenditures exceeding 2.3 trillion KRW, periodontal disease has become one of the most prevalent health conditions nationwide, surpassing even the common cold. This situation provides an opportunity for all members of KAP to reaffirm their professional responsibility to protect public health. As increasing evidence has clarified the links between periodontal disease and systemic conditions, dentists, including KAP members, now recognize that periodontal treatment contributes not only to oral health but also to overall systemic health.

According to recent estimates, although the definition of disability varies across countries, approximately 1.3 billion people—about 16% of the global population—live with a disability [2]. PWDs face substantial barriers in education, employment, healthcare, and safety, and are often among the most vulnerable in crises, climate change, and humanitarian settings. Consequently, they are frequently described as the world’s largest minority group. In the Republic of Korea, approximately 5.1% of the population are PWDs. According to the 2023 National Survey on Persons with Disabilities, 1 in 3 individuals has expressed a need for improved medical coverage and health management [3].

PWDs encounter significant challenges in maintaining oral health for several reasons. Physical and cognitive limitations often result in inadequate self-care. In addition, limited awareness of the importance of oral health management—particularly periodontal health—contributes to poorer oral health outcomes compared with non-disabled individuals. According to the 2020 Disability Health Statistics from Korea National Rehabilitation Center, gingivitis and periodontal disease were the most frequently reported conditions among PWDs in Korea [4].

In this context, to improve the oral and systemic health of PWDs who face barriers to accessing dental care, KAP has conducted oral health screenings for participant and workers at the Seongbundo Welfare Center (St. Benedict Rehabilitation Center) in Gwangju-si, Gyeonggi-do, since 2012. Based on these findings, a talent donation project centered on scaling has been implemented by KAP members.

However, an evaluation conducted after 2 years of this visiting-type talent donation model revealed limited effectiveness due to challenges related to spatial accessibility, difficulties in securing auxiliary clinical staff, scheduling constraints, and limitations in sustained promotion. To maximize the efficient use of limited human and material resources, the project was reorganized in 2014 into an in-house (facility-based) model, designated as the ‘Scaling with love’ event (Figure 1).

The challenges related to spatial accessibility and staffing were addressed through collaboration with the Department of Dental Hygiene at Hanyang Women’s University, which provided clinical training laboratories and student volunteers. Scheduling and promotional challenges were mitigated by aligning the event with ‘Gum Day’ on March 24. In organizing this initiative, KAP was responsible for educational content and clinical personnel, while Department of Dental Hygiene at Hanyang Women’s University provided the clinical venue and auxiliary staff. Dongkook Pharmaceutical supported clinical supplies and promotional activities, and the Seongbundo Welfare Center offered comprehensive administrative support. This initiative represents a model of social contribution involving the broader dental community, based on the KAP Public Relations Committee’s ‘Fish in Fish’ concept (Figure 2).

As the project marks its 10th anniversary, its significance has become increasingly evident. The initiative has strengthened oral health education for welfare center participants and workers and improved oral health equity through active clinical intervention. It provides a model for national programs aimed at reducing oral health disparities among vulnerable populations, including PWDs. This initiative is also expected to serve as a best-practice example for the recently implemented National Primary Dental Care Program for Persons with Disabilities, which focuses on screenings, fluoride application, scaling and oral health education. Furthermore, by reducing attitudinal barriers among general practitioners, who provide the majority of care, this project demonstrates that primary dental clinics can play a critical role in maintaining and promoting the oral health of PWDs.

KAP also recognized the need to establish in-facility oral health rooms to improve the efficiency of visiting-type programs and accordingly established the nation’s first such facility within Welfare Center for PWDs.

In 2021, when the COVID-19 pandemic prevented in-person activities, donations from Academy executives and members, along with support from dental industry partners, were used to renovate outdated clinical facilities and establish a new oral health education room equipped with tooth-brushing facilities. Excluding the period disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic, this talent donation project has continued for 11 years. The ‘Scaling with love’ event at Hanyang Women’s University, now celebrating its 10th anniversary, has been prepared with considerable care.

To evaluate past outcomes and generate epidemiological data on the oral health status of PWDs, a survey assessing dental caries, periodontal disease, and malocclusion among participants and workers was conducted. Professor Dong-Seop Lee of the National Dental Care Center for Persons with Special Needs at Seoul National University Dental Hospital compiled these findings into a report. In addition, with support from the Korean Dental Association, panoramic radiographic screenings were performed using a mobile unit. Professor Hyeon-Seong Ahn from the Department of Periodontology at Dankook University College of Dentistry interpreted these images to provide an accurate assessment of participants and workers’ oral health status, and reports were generated using artificial intelligence–based analysis (Figure 3). Panoramic radiography, a fundamental diagnostic modality, enables the assessment of alveolar bone levels, interproximal caries, and various cysts or lesions in the maxillofacial region that are not detectable through visual examination. Notably, such screenings often identify previously undiagnosed pathologies in approximately 15% of individuals.

Based on these data, this initiative will actively promote the importance and future direction of social efforts, including programs such as the National Primary Dental Care Program aimed at improving the oral health of PWDs.

Over the past decade, this initiative has demonstrated that collective efforts, even when individually modest, can yield substantial achievements that would be difficult to accomplish independently. We extend our sincere gratitude to all those who have shared this vision with the KAP in advancing the oral health including periodontal health of PWDs.

Application of Artificial Intelligence in Dental Imaging Interpretation and Diagnosis – A Systematic Review
Authors: Priya Manimegalan, Samidha Pande, S Priscilla Shalini, Geethu Ann George, Sheetal S. Patil, Shrutika M. Salunkhe
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study assessed artificial intelligence in dental imaging interpretation and diagnosis. A PubMed and Cochrane Library search identified studies published between January 2020 and February 2025. Data extraction followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Of 400 initial studies identified, nine were included in the final analysis. Most studies reported high accuracy, with the highest accuracy of 99.5% achieved for mandibular segmentation. AI in dental imaging is effective in enhancing diagnostic precision and clinical decision-making.

INTRODUCTION

Recently, artificial intelligence (AI) has significantly transformed healthcare.[1] The integration of AI, particularly through convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and advanced techniques like transfer learning, has improved the precision and resilience of dental diagnostics, enabling better detection of oral health conditions.[2] Pretrained models such as GoogLeNet Inception and ResNet have further accelerated AI applications in dentistry, offering superior image classification capabilities. AI’s ability to analyze large datasets, predict disease progression, and assist in treatment planning has opened new avenues for personalized dental care.[3] This study assessed AI in dental imaging interpretation and diagnosis.

METHODOLOGY

Databases and search terms

PubMed and Cochrane Library were searched to identify articles published between January 2020 and February 2025. The systematic literature search was conducted and reported using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.[4] Medical subject heading (MeSH) search terms, such as “artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, neural networks, dental radiography, panoramic radiographs, cone-beam computed tomography, CBCT, diagnostic accuracy, image interpretation,” separated by suitable Boolean operators, were used to identify relevant articles.

Search strategy

Articles published in English were searched, and duplicates were removed after manual curation. Identified publications were then screened by title and abstract, excluding meeting abstracts, conference abstracts with insufficient data, case report reviews, and meta-analyses. Only full-text articles were included in the meta-analysis.

Study selection

All articles were independently screened and reviewed by two authors with experience in meta-analysis and dentistry. Inclusion criteria for eligibility were as follows: (i) studies investigating AI applications for dental imaging analysis and (ii) studies reporting diagnostic performance metrics. Case reports, abstracts, duplicated reports, studies without application of AI, those not focused on dental imaging, and articles lacking relevant diagnostic outcome data were not considered.

Data extraction

The extracted data included study characteristics, the dental imaging modalities analyzed, and results detailing the diagnostic performance outcomes.

RESULTS

After removing duplicates, nine studies were included in the final review and meta-analysis [Figure 1]. DISCUSSION

This study showed strong AI performance in dental imaging. Deep learning methodologies have transformed dental imaging.[3] These AI-driven systems leverage automated computer-aided design outputs, uplift clinical decision-making, and streamline digital patient record management. AI also provides dental implant classification and cephalometric analyses. Despite these benefits, challenges persist, including the impact of image quality on AI performance, as low-quality CBCT scans can hamper segmentation and classification accuracy.[811] Variations in anatomical structures and the complexity of dental conditions further challenge AI generalizability,[13] highlighting the need for continued refinement to balance speed and accuracy in clinical applications.

In conclusion, this study underlines AI’s effectiveness in dental imaging, enhancing diagnostic precision and clinical decision-making.

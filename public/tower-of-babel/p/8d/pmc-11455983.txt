Case study on the influence of rock brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance
Authors: Qingwei Li, Lijie Du, Yalei Yang, Leitao Liu, Xiangbo Zhao, Guangchun Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Brittleness is an important mechanical property of rock. Accurately evaluating rock brittleness and its influence on the TBM tunnelling performance is necessary. In this work, via two practical engineering cases, with the aim of overcoming the difficulty of penetrating extremely hard rock (breccia fused tuff) in the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z{\text{huxi}}$$\end{document}Zhuxi project, the influence of rock brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance was studied via comparative analysis with that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Nabang$$\end{document}Nabang project. Seven commonly used rock brittleness indices based on stress‒strain curves were summarized, and a brittleness evaluation method suitable for extremely hard rock was determined by introducing the normalized specific energy to obtain brittleness indices for two lithologies (breccia fused tuff and biotite hornblende plagioclase gneiss) in two projects. The influences of rock brittleness on penetration and the specific energy were compared and analysed. The results showed that the brittleness indices \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B3$$\end{document}B3 and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B5$$\end{document}B5 were more suitable for evaluating rock brittleness. Rock brittleness influenced the TBM tunnelling performance, but this influence gradually decreased with decreasing uniaxial compressive strength (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS), which is obviously less than that of the rock strength. When the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS was lower than 150 MPa, the TBM tunnelling parameters could be adjusted within a significant margin to eliminate the influence of rock brittleness, which could be ignored when predicting the tunnelling performance. When the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS was greater than 150 MPa, rock brittleness imposed a notable influence on the tunnelling performance, which must be considered when predicting the tunnelling performance. This research could provide reference data for evaluating the hard rock brittleness index and accurately predicting the TBM tunnelling performance in engineering practice.

Introduction

In recent years, the full face hard rock tunnel boring machine (TBM) has been widely used in tunnel construction. The prediction of the TBM tunnelling performance has attracted the attention of many scholars, who reported that the rock strength and rock mass integrity notably impact it 1, whereas the influence of rock brittleness has been studied less. In engineering practice, accurately predicting the TBM tunnelling performance under specific geological conditions is crucial for tunnel construction method selection, construction schedule design and cost estimation. Brittleness, as an important mechanical property of rock, affects the initiation and expansion of rock cracks and the spalling of rock slices in the rock breaking process and reflects the deformation and fracture characteristics of rock under loading 2. Studies have shown that rock brittleness impacts the tunnelling performance 3–6, but currently, the rock strength and rock mass integrity are the main factors of concern when analysing and predicting the TBM tunnelling performance, and it is not yet clear whether the influence of rock brittleness should be included.

To study the degree of influence of brittleness on the tunnelling performance, first, it is necessary to know how to characterize rock brittleness. At present, there is no consensus on the definition of rock brittleness, but its definition and evaluation methods have been analysed by many scholars from different perspectives, such as strength parameters, stress‒strain curves, and penetration tests, with the aim to represent rock brittleness quantitatively 7–9. There are many brittleness index evaluation methods, but there are few studies on the influence of rock brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance, which are mainly divided into two aspects. One is the strength-based method. For example, Kahraman 5 reported that there are obvious exponential relationships between the TBM advance speed and various brittleness indices, which are represented by the ratio of the compressive strength (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{c}$$\end{document}σc) and the tensile strength (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{t}$$\end{document}σt) or the ratio of their difference to their sum. Ebrahimabadi et al. 6 combined rock mass quality parameters, defined the rock mass brittleness index (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RMBI$$\end{document}RMBI) on the basis of the ratio of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{c}$$\end{document}σc to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{t}$$\end{document}σt and proposed a TBM tunnelling performance prediction model using the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RMBI$$\end{document}RMBI. Du et al. 10, relying on an actual project, reported that the brittleness index exhibited a decreasing logarithmic relationship with the field penetration index (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$FPI$$\end{document}FPI), an increasing relationship with the rock uniaxial compressive strength (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS), a relatively low correlation with the integrity coefficient, and no correlation with the rock abrasion value. Liu et al. 11 and Goktan 12,13 indicated that under the same conditions, the normalized specific energy decreased and the rock breaking efficiency increased with increasing brittleness index, as represented by the ratio of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{c}$$\end{document}σc to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{t}$$\end{document}σt. Gong and Zhao 14 used UDEC software to simulate the rock breaking process on the basis of the brittleness index characterized by the ratio of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{c}$$\end{document}σc to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{t}$$\end{document}σt and reported that the TBM tunnelling speed increased with increasing brittleness index. The other method is based on the cutter penetration test. Liu et al. 15 defined the brittleness index as the ratio of the decreasing cumulative value and the increasing cumulative value of the penetration load and reported that the disc-cutter could hardly penetrate rocks with high strength and low brittleness. Yagiz 4,16 reported that the TBM advance rate increased with brittleness index, which was defined as the ratio of the maximum penetration load in the penetration curve to the corresponding penetration. In addition, few studies have been based on other evaluation methods, which have focused on analysing the influences of various geological parameters (including rock brittleness) on the rock mass boreability and TBM tunnelling efficiency 17,18 and the relationship between rock brittleness and the rock breaking efficiency on the basis of certain laboratory test methods 19.

The above studies have shown that rock brittleness influences the TBM tunnelling performance. The greater the rock brittleness, the more easily crack formation will occur in the tunnel face and the better the TBM tunnelling performance will be. However, shortcomings exist in existing research: (1) Most studies focus on establishing a relationship model between the brittleness index and the tunnelling speed for the same lithology, but the degree of influence on the tunnelling performance is uncertain; (2) the brittleness index evaluation method adopted in these studies exhibits notable limitations. For example, the brittleness index based on the strength method covers few test parameters, does not account for the influence of other rock mass parameters on the tunnelling performance, and is not convenient for comparing the brittleness values of different lithologies under the same \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS conditions. The cutter penetration test is mostly performed in the laboratory, and the evaluation of the rock breaking efficiency cannot meet the practical demand in engineering. In addition, rock brittleness evaluation methods, such as those based on the elastic modulus and mineral composition analysis, all have limitations in their application and are not suitable for comparative analysis of hard rocks.

Rock brittleness, which differs from single mechanical parameters such as the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS and the elastic modulus, is influenced by the interaction among many factors but lacks a generally accepted concept and calculation method at present. As a result, there are only a few studies on the influence of brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance, and these studies are mainly based on laboratory tests and lack practicality. Therefore, on the basis of actual construction cases, in this paper, a comparative analysis of two projects is performed to study the influence of rock brittleness. Given that the extremely hard rock sections in the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Zhuxi$$\end{document}Zhuxi project are more difficult to tunnel than are those in the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Nabang$$\end{document}Nabang project under different lithologies with the same \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS and integrity, 7 commonly used brittleness index evaluation methods based on stress‒strain curves are first summarized, and the normalized specific energy is then introduced to determine the most suitable evaluation method for the brittleness index of extremely hard rock, with the aim to evaluate the rock brittleness of the lithologies in the two projects. To compare and analyse the tunnelling performance levels for the two lithologies under the same \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS and integrity, the influence degree of rock brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance is analysed, and it is determined when rock brittleness should be considered in TBM tunnelling performance analysis and prediction, considering that TBM design parameters such as thrust and torque can be adjusted within significant margins under certain surrounding rock conditions.

Comparative analysis of the TBM tunnelling performance

In this section, much time was needed to pass through the extremely hard rock sections. The TBM tunnelling efficiency was extremely low, with an overall penetration lower than 0.5 mm/r. Many centre cutters experienced abnormal wear, and even the tunnel face exhibited a protrusion.

According to the above and existing studies 10,22,23, the rock strength, integrity, abrasiveness and brittleness are the main geological parameters for evaluating the surrounding rock boreability and tunnelling efficiency in TBM construction. The surrounding rock boreability decreases with increasing rock strength and abrasiveness and increases with increasing brittleness. The rock abrasiveness determines the durability of the TBM disc cutter and is closely related to the rock strength. In general, a suitable surrounding rock boreability corresponds to a favourable tunnelling performance. Analysis and selection of calculation methods for rock brittleness indices

Rock brittleness index evaluation method

At present, there are many evaluation methods for brittleness indices, but there are no unified evaluation definitions or methods. The stress‒strain curve method has been widely used because of its easy implementation and high practicability. Different scholars have adjusted and improved the characterization of rock brittleness indices on the basis of stress‒strain curves and proposed evaluation methods. On the basis of previous research results, 7 commonly used evaluation methods for rock brittleness indices defined via stress‒strain curves are summarized and analysed, as detailed in Table 4. The trend of the fitted curve is consistent with that obtained in engineering practice.

Conclusion

On the basis of actual construction cases, a comparative study of the influence of rock brittleness on the TBM tunnelling performance is performed. First, brittleness index evaluation methods suitable for extremely hard rock are analysed, and the best method is determined to evaluate the rock brittleness of two lithologies in two projects. The TBM penetration and specific energy of the two lithologies with different brittleness levels and the same geological conditions are compared and analysed, and the conclusions are as follows:(1) Seven commonly used methods for evaluating the brittleness index based on stress‒strain curves are summarized, and on the basis of actual engineering data, the concept of the normalized specific energy is introduced to determine the most suitable method for evaluating the brittleness of extremely hard rock. Under the same \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS value, the brittleness index of breccia fused tuff is lower than that of biotite hornblende plagioclase gneiss.(2) For the two lithologies with different rock brittleness values from the two projects, rock brittleness influences the TBM tunnelling performance, but the influence is less than that of the rock strength and different for different \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS ranges. The influence degree of rock brittleness on penetration decreases with decreasing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS, and the influence of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS on penetration is dominant. With increasing rock brittleness, the rock cutting specific energy decreases, and the TBM penetration increases.(3) When the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$UCS$$\end{document}UCS is greater than 150 MPa, rock brittleness imposes a great influence on penetration, so it must be considered when predicting the TBM tunnelling performance.

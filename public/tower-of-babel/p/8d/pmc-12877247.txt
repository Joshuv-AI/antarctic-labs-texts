Function Impairment Screening Tool predicts eight-year mortality in older adults: Beijing Longitudinal Study of Aging
Authors: Xiaxia Li, Yiming Pan, Li Zhang, Yaxin Zhang, Zhe Tang, Lina Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeFunction impairment is an early stage of disability in older adults and requires timely intervention. We have previously developed Function Impairment Screening Tool (FIST) based on the Delphi method, which has good reliability and validity, but the predictive effect is unknown. Therefore, we aimed to explore the role of FIST in predicting long-term mortality in community-dwelling older adults.Participants and methodsData were from the Beijing Longitudinal Study of Aging. A total of 1,833 older adults with 8 years of follow-up were included. Function impairment was assessed using FIST. Cox proportional hazards model was used to calculate the predictive effect of FIST on 8-year all-cause mortality.ResultsAccording to FIST, approximately half of the older adults had function impairment (47.6%). The prevalence of function impairment varied across populations. Logistic regression analysis showed that age, female, rural, poor health satisfaction, not drinking tea, and low Mini-Mental State Examination and intrinsic capacity score were associated with function impairment. Furthermore, function impairment was associated with poor physical function and high mortality. Cox analysis showed that FIST could predict 8-year mortality (hazard ratio [HR] = 3.26, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.74–3.87), and this relationship persisted after adjusting for age, sex, area, marital status, live alone, educational level, smoking, drinking alcohol, and chronic diseases (HR = 1.79, 95% CI 1.45–2.17).DiscussionFIST can predict 8-year mortality in community-dwelling older adults. More attention should be paid to older adults with function impairment and early intervention should be provided.Keywords: Function impairment screening tool, Mortality, Older adults, Function impairment

Introduction

As the world's population ages and the diseases spectrum changes, the proportion of people with disability continues to increase [1]. According to the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the number of disabled older adults in China is expected to increase from 52.71 million in 2020 to 77.00 million in 2030 [2]. If intervention measures are not taken in time, the proportion of disabled older adults will continue to increase [2]. Function impairment is the early stage of disability and refers to decreased muscle strength, mobility, and balance, as well as impaired gait, related to factors such as aging and chronic disease [3]. Early identification and intervention of function impairment is an effective initiative to prevent the onset and progression of disability. The Short Physical Performance Battery, gait speed, handgrip strength, timed up and go test, 6-minute walk, and 400-meter walk provide objective results for assessing physical function in older adults, and their validity in predicting adverse outcomes has been demonstrated [4]. However, a quick and validated function impairment assessment scale would better meet the clinical needs. The Barthel Index, Katz Scale, Lawton, and Brody Scale are commonly used to assess activities of daily living (ADL) and instrumental activities of daily living (IADL) [5]. We have previously constructed the Function Impairment Screening Tool (FIST) to assess function impairment using the Delphi method [6,7]. Compared with the ADL/IADL scale, FIST consists of 16 items in three domains, covering self-care (daily living) and activity participation (domestic life and social activities), which is suitable for the older individuals and avoids the ceiling effect [7]. FIST has good reliability and validity in identifying function impairment in older adults, and is associated with gait speed, grip strength, and frailty [8]. However, the predictive role of FIST on adverse outcomes has not been determined; thus, we aimed to explore the prediction of FIST on long-term mortality in community-dwelling Chinese older adults.

Methods

Participants

Data were from the Beijing Longitudinal Study of Aging (BLSA), a representative cohort study established with multi-step stratified random sampling method. More details about the BLSA have been reported in previous studies [[9], [10], [11]]. A total of 1,833 Chinese community residents aged 60 years or above without severe cognitive impairment who were able to complete the assessment and the 8-year follow-up were included in the study. All participants signed a written informed consent before the study. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University.

Variables

General variables

Data were obtained through face-to-face surveys. Social characteristics included age, sex, area, marital status, live alone, educational level, and income. Psychological and cognitive factors included major life events (defined as stressful events that occurred within the past 3 years), self-reported health satisfaction, the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D), and the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) [12], with total score of 0−30 for both CES-D and MMSE. Lifestyle factors included smoking, drinking alcohol, eating meat, and drinking tea. Chronic diseases (hypertension, coronary heart disease, stroke, diabetes, osteoarthrosis, respiratory diseases, digestive diseases, urinary diseases, ear, nose and throat diseases, and cancer) diagnosed by physicians were self-reported.

Physical function

Physical function was assessed using the balance test, chair-stand test, and self-reported history of falls and fractures. Falls were defined as two or more falls within the past year.

Frailty

Frailty was assessed using 68-item frailty index, which was developed and validated in BLSA [11]. Frailty index <0.25 was non-frail and ≥0.25 was frail [13].

Social frailty

Social frailty was assessed using the HALFT scale with 5 items: inability to help others, limited social participation, loneliness, financial difficulties, and no one to talk to. Each answer of "Yes" was scored as 1 point, "No" was scored as 0, and the total score ranged from 0 to 5, with 0−2 points for non-social frailty and ≥3 points for social frailty [14].

Intrinsic capacity

Intrinsic capacity included six domains: locomotion, cognition, vitality, psychology, vision and hearing. Locomotion was assessed with the balance test or chair-stand test. Cognition was measured using the MMSE adjusted for education level. Vitality was assessed based on BMI. The CES-D was used to assess psychology. Vision and hearing were assessed based on self-reported vision or hearing status. Each domain was scored from 0 to 1 point, with a total score of 0 to 6; the higher score indicates better intrinsic capacity [15].

Mortality

Death data were obtained by asking the participants’ family members or neighbors, and information has been verified by the household registration records. Local medical records and death certificates were available when necessary.

Function impairment

Function impairment was assessed using the FIST, which consists of 16 items in three domains [7]. Ability of daily living: moving in and out of bed, going to toilet, bathing, dressing, washing, and eating. Ability of domestic life: housekeeping, handling medications, handling finances, lifting 5 kg of weight, and going up and down the stairs. Ability of social activities: using transportation, walking 250 meters, shopping, using telephone, and physical exercise. For each of the 16 items, the score ranged from 0 to 1 (0 for complete dependence, 0.5 for partial dependence, and 1 for independence), with <1 defined as impairment. A total FIST score ranged from 0 to 16, with higher scores indicating better function [7]. Participants with a total score <16 were defined as function impairment.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS version 25 (IBM SPSS Statistics, 2017). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables were expressed as numbers and proportions (%). Mean values between groups were compared using t-test, and percentages were compared by the chi-square test. Variables with P value <0.05 were included in the multivariable model, and forward stepwise logistic regression was conducted to explore the association between the included variables and function impairment. The Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to analyze the predictive effect of FIST on 8-year all-cause mortality. The variables including age, sex, area, marital status, live alone, educational level, smoking, drinking alcohol, and chronic diseases were adjusted in cox regression model based on literature review and epidemiologic experience. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

The baseline characteristics of participants with function impairment

The mean age of the 1,833 participants was 74.27 ± 7.40 years (ranged from 60–100 years), of whom 948 (51.7%) were female. According to FIST, 873 (47.6%) participants were considered having function impairment. Fig. 1A showed the proportion of impairment in each item in the older adults, with physical exercise impairment being the highest (25.4%) and eating impairment being the lowest (3.2%).

(A) The prevalence of impairment in 16 items of Function Impairment Screening Tool; (B) the prevalence of function impairment in different age groups, (C) sex, and (D) area; and (E) the Kaplan–Meier curves of older adults with function impairment and intact function.

The prevalence of function impairment increased with age, with the prevalence in the 90–100 years old group being 3.26 times that in the 60–69 years old group (82.4% vs. 25.3%, Fig. 1B). The prevalence of function impairment of older adults was higher in female (55.1% vs. 39.7%, Fig. 1C) and those living in rural areas (64.0% vs. 28.9%, Fig. 1D). Function impairment was more common in older adults with no spouse, illiteracy, not enough income, poor health satisfaction, not eating meat, not drinking tea, social frailty, and chronic diseases. Older adults with function impairment had higher CES-D score and lower MMSE and intrinsic capacity score than those with intact function (Table 1). Further forward stepwise logistic regression showed that age, female, rural, poor health satisfaction, not drinking tea, and low MMSE and intrinsic capacity score were associated with function impairment (Table 2). Function impairment was associated with frailty, falls, and poor performance in balance and chair-stand tests (Table 3).

The prediction of FIST on 8-year mortality

The cumulative number of deaths at 2, 5, and 8 years of follow-up was 181 (9.9%), 430 (23.5%), and 629 (34.3%), respectively. Among the 629 participants who died, 70.6% had function impairment at baseline, which was 1.98 times the prevalence of survivors (35.6%). Fig. 1E depicted the Kaplan–Meier curves of older adults with intact function and function impairment. Function impairment significantly predicted mortality, with an 8-year mortality hazard ratio (HR) of 3.26 (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.74–3.87). This effect persisted after adjusting for age, sex, area, marital status, live alone, educational level, smoking, drinking alcohol, and chronic diseases (HR = 1.79, 95% CI 1.48–2.17) (Table 4).

Discussion

FIST was developed to meet the need for rapid identification of function impairment in older adults. This study showed that the prevalence of function impairment assessed by FIST was 47.6%, which was 5.95 times the prevalence of ADL disability (8.0%) in the same population [9]. Although the prevalence of impairment varied across the 16 items, the prevalence of impairment in social activity was higher and that in daily living was lower. Previous studies have reported similar results, namely that the prevalence of IADL disability in older adults was higher than ADL disability (31.9% vs. 11.1%) [16]. This study showed that in the domains of daily living, domestic life and social activities, the prevalence of dependence on bathing, lifting 5 kg of weight, and physical exercise was the highest, while the prevalence of eating, handling medications, and shopping was the lowest, respectively. Among ADL, bathing dependence had the highest prevalence, and eating dependence had the lowest prevalence [[17], [18], [19]]; while among IADL, dependence on taking medication had the lowest prevalence [17]. The proportion of impairment in each item among older adults is an important guide for developing interventions to improve independence of community-dwelling older adults.

In this study, a higher prevalence of function impairment assessed by the FIST was associated with female, advanced age, other poor socio-psycho-economic factors, and chronic diseases, which is consistent with previous studies [[20], [21], [22]]. Further logistic regression analysis showed that age, female, rural, poor health satisfaction, not drinking tea, poor cognition, and low intrinsic capacity were independently associated with function impairment, suggesting that these factors should be fully considered when conducting interventions.

Previous study have shown that the FIST was negatively associated with frailty [8], and this study showed that the prevalence of function impairment evaluated by FIST in frail older adults was as high as 95.6%. Compared with those with intact function, those who were dependent on more than four items in ADL (HR = 1.32) or more than two items in IADL (HR = 1.39) had a higher risk of future falls after adjusting for age and sex [23]. This study reported that function impairment was also associated with falls, as well as failure of balance and chair-stand tests, and the FIST could predict 8-year mortality. As a self-reported assessment tool, FIST can quickly and efficiently identify older adults with function impairment, providing a foundation for early intervention. In the future, the application of FIST in various scenarios should be strengthened to identify and intervene in older adults with function impairment at an early stage and promote healthy aging.

Previous studies have reported that the impairment of physical function assessed by Functional Independence Measure was a significant predictor of mortality in hospitalized older patients [24], however, to the best of our knowledge, this was the first study to explore the function impairment assessed by FIST in predicting 8-year mortality in a large sample and long-term follow-up community cohort. Furthermore, this study had some limitations. First, the trajectory of function impairment assessed by the FIST was not observed. Second, the predictive role of function impairment on other adverse outcomes such as disability was not explored. Third, this study did not differentiate between the degree of function impairment. Studies examining the cross-sectional and longitudinal validity of FIST in other populations are needed.

Conclusion

FIST predicts long-term mortality in Chinese community-dwelling older adults. Function impairment assessed by FIST was associated with frailty, falls, poor physical performance, and mortality. Therefore, older adults with function impairment should be identified and intervened early, and more attention should be paid to special population such as older rural women when formulating interventions.

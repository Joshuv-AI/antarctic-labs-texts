Evaluating economic outcomes in the management of temporomandibular disorders: a protocol for a systematic review of randomised controlled trials
Authors: Purnima Komarraju, Berlant AlSabbagh, Arto Ohinmaa, Susan Armijo-Olivo, Martin Morris, Jingjing Li, Paul W Major, Michael Goldberg, Ayushi Naik, Aya Abdelkarim, Ana Miriam Velly
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSystematic reviews (SRs) on the management of temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) have predominantly focused on evaluating the effectiveness of various treatments, identifying those that provide the greatest benefits. However, the economic evaluation of these treatments has not been systematically explored. This SR aims to address this gap by evaluating the economic outcomes of the most common treatment modalities for TMDs, including cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, cost-benefit, cost-minimisation and the burden of illness.Methods and analysisThis SR will be conducted using the following electronic databases Business Source Complete, CINAHL, EconLit (ProQuest), Embase (Ovid), MEDLINE (PubMed), MEDLINE (Ovid) and Scopus to identify studies evaluating the economic outcomes of treatments for TMDs. The eligibility criteria are as follows: (1) studies examining the costs and/or impact of treatments for TMDs and (2) articles published between 2000 and 2025. The primary outcomes of interest are the economic findings outlined earlier. Data extraction will include the following: author(s), year of publication, country, study objectives, study design, eligibility criteria, TMD diagnosis and screening, study groups, randomisation, blinding, sample size, number of participants invited, enrolled and completed, duration of treatment, follow-up, study duration, settings, assessment instruments, study outcomes, statistical analyses, results, limitations, strengths and funding sources. The quality of studies will be evaluated using the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 checklist, with risk of bias assessed using the Cochrane Effective Practice and Organization of Care’s risk-of-bias tool; where applicable, the Outcome Reporting Bias in Trials will be used to detect reporting biases. A narrative synthesis and summary tables will outline study characteristics, economic outcomes and the overall quality of evidence. We will conduct qualitative secondary and sensitivity analyses.Ethics and disseminationThis SR does not require an ethics approval. The results will be disseminated through international and national conferences and peer-reviewed journals.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024613553.

Introduction

Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) include several disorders affecting the masticatory muscles, temporomandibular joints and associated structures.1 2 TMDs collectively account for the second most common musculoskeletal disorder (after low back pain),1 3 and they are the most prevalent chronic orofacial pain conditions for which individuals seek treatment.46 TMDs are widely spread within the population, with prevalence ranging from 5% to 12% globally.7 8

Individuals with TMDs usually tend to seek treatment from both medical and dental professionals.9 10

Over time, pain-related TMDs can significantly affect daily life, leading to increased psychological distress, physical disability and functional limitations.11 12

The direct costs incurred by patients with TMDs include consultation fees, diagnostic expenses, treatment costs and other related expenditures.13 In addition to direct costs, patients with TMDs face indirect and out-of-pocket expenses, including fees for non-dental services (eg, physiotherapy, acupuncture, psychological treatment or specialist consultations). Total medical claim payments for patients with TMDs have been reported to be roughly two times those of individuals without TMDs.14

Individuals with TMDs may also experience reduced ability to work or pursue education opportunities, reduced earnings due to decreased productivity, costs for treating comorbidities associated with TMDs and additional expenses related to travel and time spent accessing treatment.9

Reviews of economic outcomes, such as cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, cost-benefit, cost-minimisation and burden of illness, are essential for guiding resource allocation in healthcare. In the context of TMDs, however, such reviews remain scarce, fragmented and methodologically inconsistent. Existing evidence is often restricted to specific treatments, single economic outcomes (eg, cost-effectiveness), particular countries, small sample sizes and limited methodological rigour,1517 making it difficult to draw reliable conclusions.

To address these gaps, this systematic review (SR) aims to evaluate the economic impact of TMDs treatments by examining studies on cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, cost-benefit, cost-minimisation and burden of illness. Additionally, it seeks to identify critical knowledge gaps to inform and shape future research efforts.

Methods and analysis

This SR will be conducted according to the methods outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, as recommended by the Cochrane community.18

This protocol was registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews. If a protocol amendment is made, the date will be followed with a description of the change and its reason.

The exclusion criteria include the following: abstracts, editorials, reviews, duplicate studies and studies without full-text availability or results. Observational studies (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional and claims/registry analyses) will be excluded from this review because, compared with RCTs, they provide lower-certainty comparative estimates and are susceptible to residual confounding (eg, confounding by indication). To maximise internal validity and comparability, we will include only RCT-based economic evaluations. Relevant observational findings will be acknowledged and contextualised in the discussion of the SR.

Search strategy

The search strategy was developed in collaboration with the McGill University Librarian (MM), who has extensive experience retrieving relevant data from medical and economic literature. The literature search will be performed using six different databases, namely, Business Source Complete, CINAHL, EconLit (ProQuest), Embase (Ovid), MEDLINE (Pubmed), MEDLINE (Ovid) and Scopus.

The complete MEDLINE (Ovid) search terms are detailed in online supplemental appendix 1. Additionally, specific exclusion criteria, such as publication date, will be incorporated into the search process to automatically filter out articles that do not meet these predefined requirements.

The identified articles will be imported into EndNote V.20 for duplicate removal and reference management. Additionally, the reference lists of retrieved articles will be manually screened to identify and include as many relevant papers as possible.

Pilot screening

Before undergoing this SR, a pilot study including 25 abstracts will be screened by three reviewers (PK, BA, AMV) to evaluate the consistency among them regarding the eligibility criteria. They will not know each other’s decisions regarding the abstracts selected.

Screening procedure

Two reviewers, PK and BA, will screen the abstracts of the retrieved articles using the previously described eligibility criteria. This process will be facilitated by Covidence, a web-based tool designed for SRs.

Pilot selection and quality assessment

On completing the screening process, the reviewers (PK, BA, AMV) will evaluate their consistency in article selection and application of the quality evaluation criteria to 10 full-text articles identified as potential candidates for inclusion.

This quality assessment will use the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) 2022 checklist.19 20 This guideline, endorsed by the Enhancing the QUAlity and Transparency Of health Research Network, the CONsolidated Standards Of Reporting Trials, STrengthening the Reporting of OBservational studies in Epidemiology and International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research, is considered the most comprehensive framework for evaluating the reporting quality of health economic studies.21 Furthermore, it is the most recent guideline specifically designed to assess the quality of reporting in health economic studies. Reviewers will remain unaware of each other’s quality assessment decisions to ensure impartiality.

Article selection process and quality evaluation

Following the pilot phase, the reviewers (PK, BA, AMV) will independently evaluate all full-text articles identified as potential candidates for inclusion based on the screening process. Moreover, the CHEERS 2022 checklist will be applied for quality assessment, and consensus among reviewers will be used in the synthesis.

A Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses flow diagram will be developed to visually summarise the study selection process, including (1) the total number of publications identified and the unique records remaining after duplicate removal; (2) the number of records screened and the number excluded at this stage, along with the reasons for their exclusion; (3) the number of full-text articles assessed for eligibility and the number excluded, along with specific reasons for exclusion and (4) the number of articles that met the inclusion criteria and were included in the SR.

Data extraction and management

Data extraction will be independently conducted by the two reviewers (PK, BA) using a standardised data extraction sheet. Attempts will be made to retrieve missing data by contacting the authors through email correspondence.

The extracted data for each study will include the name of the author, year of publication, country, study objectives, study design, eligibility criteria, TMD diagnoses, TMD screening, study groups, randomisation, blinding, sample size, number of participants invited, enrolled, completed, duration of the treatment, follow-up, study duration, settings, assessment instruments (eg, diagnosis, screening, outcomes, covariates), statistical analysis, the software used to run the analyses, study population demographics, TMD signs and symptoms, loss of work, decreased productivity (work and education), economic outcomes (cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, cost-benefit, cost-minimisation and burden-of-illness), discount rate, willingness to pay, study limitations, strengths and funding source (table 1).

Assessment of the quality of included studies

The quality of reporting of the economic evaluation of each included RCT will be independently assessed by the two reviewers using the CHEERS 2022 checklist.19 20 The checklist comprises 28 items, with each item fulfilling the quality criterion receiving 1 point (and 0 points for not entirely reporting the criterion—‘Not reported’), leading to a total possible score of 28 (representing 100%).22 These 28 items are subdivided into seven main categories: (1) title, (2) abstract, (3) introduction, (4) methods, (5) results, (6) discussion and (7) other relevant information. We will indicate the section of the article where relevant information can be found using a section heading with a paragraph number, as advised by the CHEERS 2022 statement.19 20 If an item does not apply to a particular economic evaluation, we will report ‘Not Applicable’ and not count it toward the article’s final scoring.

Assessment of risk of bias

The Cochrane Effective Practice and Organisation of Care’s risk-of-bias assessment tool will be used to evaluate bias in RCTs.23 24 The efficacy of the randomisation will be evaluated by examining the baseline equivalence between study groups regarding the baseline outcome variables and the potential confounders (eg, sociodemographic characteristics). Additional potential sources of bias to be considered include co-interventions, deviations from intended interventions, outcome measurement, dropout rates, withdrawals, missing data and potential conflicts of interest.

For outcome reporting bias, we will compare outcomes reported in the study protocol (if available) with those in the published report. If no protocol is available, we will compare the outcomes in the methods and results sections of the published studies. Additionally, we will use the Outcome Reporting Bias in Trials classification system25 to evaluate the impact of selective outcome reporting.

Synthesis of results

The findings from the eligible studies will be presented narratively and summarised in a table to outline (1) the characteristics of the studies, (2) the economic outcomes reported in each and (3) other relevant factors. The quantity and quality of the available evidence will also be presented in this comprehensive table. Moreover, a qualitative stratification of findings will be performed by covariates (eg, comorbidities, sex, age). Additionally, we will conduct qualitative sensitivity analyses by repeating the narrative synthesis under restrictions (eg, excluding studies at high risk of bias, or those not using RDC/TMDs or DC/TMDs) to assess the consistency of the conclusions.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in the design and will not be directly involved in the conduct or analysis of this SR. However, we recognise the importance of incorporating patient perspectives, especially when evaluating economic outcomes that directly affect patients’ access to and experiences with care. Once the SR results and conclusions are available, we will collaborate with oral health organisations like the Network for Canadian Oral Health Research to share the findings in accessible formats. This may include plain-language summaries or infographics. The goal is to ensure that the knowledge generated can inform patients, caregivers and the broader public about the value and accessibility of various TMD treatment options.

Ethics and dissemination

This SR will not collect any primary data, therefore not requiring an ethics approval. We anticipate that this SR will shed light on the economic outcomes of TMDs management. Understanding these economic factors will help identify more effective and efficient strategies for addressing TMDs. The insights gained can guide healthcare investments to enhance TMD management, improve patient outcomes and alleviate the burden of TMDs. We plan to disseminate our findings through international and national conferences and peer-reviewed journals.

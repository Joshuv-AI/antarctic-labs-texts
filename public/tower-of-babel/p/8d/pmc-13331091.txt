Towards use of Patient-informed Relevant Outcomes (PRO) in trials of bloodstream infections: a mixed-methods study protocol (BALANCE+PRO)
Authors: Ranjani Somayaji, Victoria Haldane, Rob Fowler, Karla D Krewulak, Nadine Foster, Shelley Longmore, Sean W X Ong, Steven Tong, Benjamin A Rogers, Lisa Burry, Katherine Kissel, David L Paterson, Nick Daneman, Kirsten Fiest
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBloodstream infections (BSIs) are a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Although recent clinical trials have increasingly adopted patient-centred outcome measures, patient-informed selection of outcome measures remains limited in infectious disease research. Greater deliberate engagement and partnership with patients and families are required to ensure that outcomes reflect priorities that are meaningful to those with lived experience. Our study aims to incorporate patient-informed perspectives into outcome selection for BSI clinical trials.Methods and analysisWe will undertake an exploratory sequential mixed methods study nested with the currently running international BALANCE+ trial of Gram-negative BSI (NCT06537609). All study materials will be co-developed and piloted with patient partners. Phase I will consist of virtual focus groups with past patient and family member participants of the BALANCE+ trial from intensive care and ward settings (total of 24–40 participants), using purposive sampling to maximise variation across demographic characteristics. Data will be audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim and analysed thematically using dual independent coding and consensus methods. Phase II will encompass development and administration of a survey (total ~100 respondents) to rank and prioritise outcomes identified in phase I, with responses descriptively summarised and integrated with qualitative findings. In phase III, virtual meetings with relevant parties including patients/families and investigators/study team members in the infectious disease field will be held to generate consensus-based recommendations and inform implementations with BALANCE+ and future trials.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval has been obtained from the University of Calgary for all study activities and participating BALANCE+ sites to enable recruitment. Study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, community engagement, conference presentations and integration into ongoing and future trials.

Introduction

Bloodstream infections (BSIs) remain a major global cause of morbidity and mortality and are increasingly complicated by antimicrobial resistance.15 Randomised trials evaluating management strategies for BSIs typically rely on clinician-derived outcomes such as mortality, microbiological cure, reinfection and hospital readmission, sometimes combined within composite frameworks such as the Desirability of Outcome Ranking (DOOR) scale.6 The DOOR strategy was proposed to track the infection episode journey of a patient/participant7 and uses a rank and weighting system of different endpoints.6 8 However, DOOR and other trial endpoints have largely been developed without direct patient or family engagement.9

There is an ethical and scientific imperative to ensure that outcomes used in BSI trials reflect the values and priorities of those with lived experience.10 Systematic reviews have identified that there is a clearly demonstrated positive impact of patient and public involvement in research in identifying relevant topics, knowledge gaps, adaptation of language and improving trial enrolment rates.11 12 Further, patient and family engagement in clinical trials is associated with improved symptoms and outcomes, satisfaction as well as potential cost savings.1315 Patient-informed outcome measures ensure that trial endpoints are developed or selected through deliberate partnership with patients and caregivers and integrate experiential knowledge with clinical expertise to determine what, when and how outcomes should be measured.

Efforts to incorporate patient-important and patient-centred outcomes into trials of acute illness have gained momentum.16 In critical care, structured engagement with intensive care unit (ICU) survivors and caregivers has informed the development of core outcome sets extending beyond short-term mortality to include physical function, cognitive impairment and psychosocial recovery.1719 While these outcomes are increasingly recognised as important, clinician-derived endpoints continue to dominate acute care trials.9 20 Research in the BSI area has followed a similar pattern, underscoring an opportunity to develop and integrate patient-informed outcome measures that better reflect the priorities and experiences of those affected.

We describe a mixed-methods study embedded within the international BALANCE+ platform trial to elicit, prioritise and integrate patient-informed outcome measures for use in current and future BSI trials (BALANCE+Patient-informed Relevant Outcomes (PRO)).

Methods and analysis

Objectives

BALANCE+PRO will be conducted between July 2026 through July 2028 as a three-phase study-within-a-trial (SWAT)21 nested within the BALANCE+ platform (NCT06537609),22 an international pragmatic randomised trial evaluating management approaches in Gram-negative (GN) bacterial BSIs, including antibiotic selection, de-escalation and oral step-down strategies, central vascular catheter management and follow-up blood culture practices.

The primary objective of BALANCE+PRO is to elicit and prioritise patient-informed and family-informed outcomes relevant to clinical trials of GN BSIs. Secondary objectives are (1) to compare patient-informed priorities with currently used BALANCE+ trial endpoints; (2) to generate consensus recommendations for integration of patient-informed outcomes into the BALANCE+ platform trial and future BSI trials; and (3) to develop an implementation framework for embedding patient-informed outcomes within adaptive platform trial structures.

Study design

This study uses an exploratory sequential mixed-methods design (qualitative → quantitative) conducted as a SWAT21 embedded within BALANCE+.23 Phase I, the qualitative phase, will generate themes and candidate outcomes, which will inform survey development in phase II, the quantitative phase. In phase III, data will be further triangulated to develop recommendations and ways forward to wider implementation of patient-relevant outcomes. This will include generation of a list of ranked outcome (new and existing) measures to be integrated into the ongoing BALANCE+ trial and potential future BSI trials. A mixed-methods approach is particularly appropriate given that patient-informed outcomes are complex and multifaceted.24 Qualitative exploration captures patient and family perspectives in depth, while quantitative prioritisation ensures findings are generalisable and actionable. Consent will be obtained electronically using a Qualtrics platform link for all phases of the study (incorporated into intake form or survey accordingly). By combining these sequentially, BALANCE+PRO will both generate meaningful candidate outcomes and quantify their importance to support integration into the BALANCE+ platform trial (figure 1).

Setting and participants

Participants will be adults (≥18 years) who have participated in the BALANCE+ trial directly as patients or indirectly as family members. Participants admitted to hospital wards and to ICUs will be included. Purposive sampling with maximum variation will be used to ensure diversity in sex at birth, gender, age, ethnicity, geography and hospital setting.

BALANCE+ is currently enrolling across 40 sites across Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Colombia and Israel (NCT06537609). As of March 2026, there have been more than 1500 patients with BSI randomised to five trial domains: performing routine versus no routine follow-up blood cultures; continuation of initial antibiotic therapy versus de-escalating to a narrower spectrum antibiotic once culture and sensitivities are known; for patients stepping down to oral antibiotics, treating with either beta-lactam versus non-beta-lactam antibiotics; for patients with low risk AmpC (enzymatic resistance mechanism) GN BSIs, treating with a carbapenem versus ceftriaxone; and for patients with BSIs who have central vascular catheters, to retain versus replace the catheter. A sixth domain regarding longer (7 days) versus shorter duration therapy will soon be added.

Phase I qualitative: focus group discussions

Phase I will use virtual focus group discussions (FGDs) to explore patient and family perspectives on outcomes relevant to BSI trials. We plan to conduct eight sessions with 3–5 participants each, guided by educational materials co-developed with patient partners, written at a grade 6 reading level and evaluated using the Patient Education Material Assessment Tool (PEMAT).25 Sessions will explore perceptions of currently used trial outcomes, treatment burdens and trade-offs, and outcomes considered most meaningful from the patient and human perspectives (box 1).

Participants of the BALANCE+ trial (directly or family member) who have been admitted to hospital for >72 hours, and are able to provide informed consent, will be eligible for the PRO study. Persons who agree to hear about the study at their scheduled 30-day follow-up in the BALANCE+ trial at any trial site will be approached for participation. Each trial site has approval from their local research ethics board (REB) to request permission to share their information if they are interested in future studies and hearing about the PRO study. Potential participants will be contacted by phone or email initially based on stated preference and will complete an intake and consent form via a secure Qualtrics platform-based link (Provo, UT, USA). Focus group participants will be in groups of 3–5 and stratified by their admission location (ward and ICU). Where appropriate, participants will be grouped by country to accommodate time zone and cultural context.

A semi-structured focus group guide will be developed and piloted with the patient and family partnership committee to assess content and pacing (see Box 1 for a preliminary overview of FGD guide thematic areas and sample questions). Consenting participants will be emailed an infographic relating to BSIs prior to the event, and a brief educational presentation will be delivered live on the day of the focus group, immediately prior to the discussion, to explain the background and rationale and to answer any questions. Groups will be facilitated by a trained qualitative researcher, with a second team member taking field notes to capture non-verbal cues and key discussion points. Sessions will be of 90 min, conducted securely via online video conferencing and recorded for transcription purposes. Participant demographics and relevant clinical variables, including duration of hospital stay, ICU stay and time since BSI, will be collected using a demographic survey sent to participants before participation to inform interpretation.

Sample size will be determined as the study proceeds, guided by theoretical saturation (when discussions no longer yield new insights),26 27 with an anticipated 24–40 participants. We will consider saturation being obtained when no new insights are yielded from FGDs in the context of the sampling strategy to ensure that diverse perspectives are captured. We will analyse eight transcripts initially to assess emergent ideas and patterns across FGDs, with additional participants recruited and additional FGDs held as needed. Data will undergo inductive qualitative content analysis, using independent coding and iterative codebook development. Reporting will follow the Consolidated criteria for Reporting Qualitative research (COREQ) guidance checklist.28

Focus group sessions will be audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, deidentified and imported into NVivo 15 (Lumivero, 2025; https://lumivero.com/products/nvivo/) (QSR International) for data management and analysis. Two investigators trained in qualitative methods, not previously known to participants, will conduct inductive qualitative content analysis concurrently with data collection. Initial open coding will generate a list of codes, which will be discussed and organised into themes to develop a codebook. Exemplar quotes will be identified to illustrate key themes. The codebook will be reviewed with the broader study team, including patient partners, to ensure it reflects participant perspectives present in the dataset. All transcripts will then be re-coded using the final codebook by two independent coders.

The qualitative analysis will generate key themes and candidate outcomes reflecting what matters most to patients and families with lived experience of GN BSIs. These insights will inform the development of the phase II survey, ensuring that items are grounded in patient-prioritised and family-prioritised outcomes, language and conceptual understanding, as well as provide rich data for phase III discussions with relevant parties (described in the next section).

Phase II quantitative: survey

The phase II survey aims to quantitatively assess the relative importance of clinical trial outcomes for GN BSIs among patients and family members. Initial items will be derived from phase I FGD findings and organised into domains based on current BALANCE+ trial outcomes22 and newly suggested outcomes. Free-text fields will allow respondents to suggest additional outcomes or provide contextual comments. The survey will be piloted with patients and families to ensure clarity, accuracy and relevance. Participants will rank outcomes from most to least important, and responses will be weighted to generate an overall prioritised list, including the top five outcomes. Survey design and reporting will follow the Consensus-based Checklist for Reporting of Survey Studies (CROSS).29

A non-probabilistic sampling strategy with maximum variation will be used to achieve representation by sex, gender, age, ethnicity, ICU versus non-ICU experience and site. Phase I participants may participate in phase II. Survey recruitment will be facilitated through the BALANCE+ research teams at scheduled follow-up times. The survey will be hosted on Qualtrics available in English and French (based on primary languages and criteria of participating BALANCE+ trial sites) and accessible online. Participants will be asked to provide sociodemographic information and relevant clinical variables already collected through BALANCE+, including ICU/ward stay, country and duration, to inform stratified analyses.

Response rates (partial and full) will be calculated. Survey responses will be summarised descriptively using means, SD, frequencies and proportions. Rankings will be stratified by ICU versus ward status and analysed for subgroup differences by sex and other demographic variables. Using the normal approximation to the binomial, a sample size of 50 respondents per strata (ICU and ward) will provide 7%–11% precision if 80%–95% of respondents rank a particular outcome among their top 5.

Survey results will be triangulated with phase I qualitative data, and reported following the Good Reporting of A Mixed-Methods Study (GRAMMS) guidelines.30 The triangulation of phase I and II data will be conducted at the interpretation phase by mapping emergent themes to their corresponding quantitative operationalisations and systematically examining points of convergence, complementarity and divergence. If contradictory perspectives emerge across or within phases, these will be treated as analytically meaningful rather than as sources of error. Divergent findings will be documented systematically and explored in subsequent phases: contradictions identified in phase I FGDs will be carried forward as areas of deliberate inquiry in phase II survey development, while unresolved divergence across phases I and II will be surfaced explicitly in phase III meetings to support transparent, consensus-informed reconciliation. This integrated analysis will generate a patient-informed ranking of outcomes to guide inclusion in BALANCE+ and future BSI trials.

Phase III: recommendation development and implementation

Phase III will use the triangulated findings from phases I and II to generate actionable recommendations for patient-informed outcomes in BSI trials. Two virtual half-day meetings will be held: the first focused on the BALANCE+ trial, including patient partners, representatives from phase I, international site investigators and the core statistical team; the second involving a broader group of stakeholders, including patient partners, representative patients and families, multidisciplinary investigators, healthcare providers and knowledge users from research networks. The integrated qualitative and quantitative data will guide discussions, ensuring that recommendations reflect both lived experience and broader prioritisation patterns.

Meetings will include small-group discussions and live polling with moderation, recording and detailed note-taking to document consensus-building. Given the participation of patients and family members alongside clinician investigators and trial leadership, active steps will be taken to mitigate power imbalances and protect psychological safety. Meetings will be co-facilitated by a trained qualitative researcher and patient partner, with formats (eg, small groups) to enable all voices to be centred. Patients and family members will receive orientation materials in advance, including a plain-language summary of phase I and II findings, to support informed and confident participation. Facilitators will be trained to recognise and interrupt hierarchical dynamics, and participants will be reminded at the outset that there are no wrong answers and that lived experience is afforded equal weight to clinical expertise in these discussions.

For the BALANCE+ trial-specific meeting, the patient-informed ranked outcomes will be considered for inclusion as primary endpoints where feasible, or as secondary endpoints otherwise, ensuring robust integration into ongoing and future trial domains. The broader stakeholder meeting will explore strategies and willingness to implement these outcomes across other BSI trials internationally. The deliverable will be evidence-informed, consensus-based recommendations for patient-informed outcomes, grounded in the triangulated data and ready to guide both current BALANCE+ trial activities and future research in BSIs.

Patient and public involvement

Following the International Association of Public Participation principles,31 patients will be involved as research partners in all aspects of the study including co-designing the research questions, study design and protocol, and submission for funding. Patient organisations including the Canadian Critical Care Trials Group Patient and Family Partners Committee and the Calgary Zone Patient and Family Centered Care Committee provided feedback in real-time for the study protocol and will assist with review and feedback of any patient-facing and public-facing materials during the conduct of the study and in the dissemination phase. The patient partners also contributed to this manuscript and are coauthors. Results from the study will be discussed with patient partners, and committees as above to determine best strategies for incorporation into the parent BALANCE+ trial (alongside study and statistical teams) as well as for dissemination and knowledge mobilisation. Results from the study will be disseminated to all consenting participants via email and will also be disseminated via conference presentations, and websites/social media of partnering patient organisations.

Ethics and dissemination

The parent BALANCE+ trial has REB approval at all participating sites. The BALANCE+PRO study has also received approval from the Conjoint Health Research Ethics Board at the University of Calgary (REB24-0615) and will enable inclusion of international participants.

Findings will be shared using integrated and end-of-study knowledge translation frameworks, reflecting the involvement of patient partners and patient-based and family-based committees throughout the study.32 Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and accessible formats for participants, including plain-language summaries and infographics. Collaborations with networks will facilitate broader reach, while patient-informed recommendations will be integrated into the BALANCE+ trial and provide guidance for future BSI trials, ensuring that patient and family perspectives inform ongoing and future research.

Discussion

BALANCE+PRO addresses a critical gap in infectious disease trial methodology by incorporating patient and family perspectives into the development and prioritisation of trial outcomes. Embedding this work within an active international platform trial enables rapid translation of findings and provides a model for integrating patient-informed outcomes within adaptive trial designs, ensuring that endpoints reflect the values and priorities of those most affected by BSIs.

Strengths of the study include its mixed-methods design, combining in-depth qualitative exploration with quantitative prioritisation and triangulated consensus-building, as well as active engagement of patient partners throughout all phases. The international scope and inclusion of ICU and non-ICU participants further enhance the relevance and applicability of findings. Limitations include restriction to participating sites, countries and previously engaged trial populations, which may limit generalisability, and potential accessibility challenges associated with virtual data collection, though these are mitigated through plain-language materials, translation support and flexible participation formats.

BALANCE+PRO offers a practical and scalable framework for embedding patient-informed outcomes into both current and future BSI trials. By systematically integrating patient and family priorities, the study aims to enhance the relevance, ethical legitimacy and impact of clinical trial outcomes in infectious disease research.

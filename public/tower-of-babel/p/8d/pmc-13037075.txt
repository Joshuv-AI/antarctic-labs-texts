Chitosan–rapamycin carbon dots rejuvenate autologous Sca-1⁺ stem cells paracrine function for neuroprotection in normal tension glaucoma
Authors: Mengxian Du, Fengyi Guo, Xinna Liu, Shiqi Zhang, Di Zhang, Leyi Qiu, Jingyang Zhang, Baoqiang Li, Huiping Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNormal tension glaucoma (NTG) causes progressive retinal ganglion cell (RGC) loss without elevated intraocular pressure, and currently lacks effective neuroprotective therapies. OPTN(E50K) mutation, a pathogenic gene of NTG, compromises paracrine trophic capacity in bone marrow (BM) stem cells. Allogeneic young BM stem cells can provide trophic support and exhibit therapeutic potential; however, their clinical application is limited by immunogenic rejection and tumorigenic risks, prompting a renewed interest in rejuvenate autologous BM stem cells strategy.MethodsTo address these challenges, we developed a composite chitosan-rapamycin (RAPA) carbon dots (CRCD) to restore the reparative potential of autologous BM stem cells. BM stem cells from aged OPTN(E50K) mice were isolated, clustered and treated with CRCD before validated their changes of neurotrophic factors expression as well as oxidative stress and autophagy indicators. Then we assessed their impact on co-cultured retinas in vitro. Transplantation were performed into lethally irradiated age-matched OPTN(E50K) hosts with CRCD-pretreated autologous BM stem cells via tail vein injection, and generated stable chimeric models. Neurotrophic factors and neuron apoptosis analyses were performed, followed by the visual behavioral tests.ResultsOur results demonstrated that CRCD pretreatment enhanced autophagy, reduced oxidative stress, and augmented paracrine neurotrophic release. In vitro, these cells reduced retinal ganglion cell apoptosis and promoted neurotrophic factor expression in co-cultured retinal explants. In vivo, comparative analysis revealed that CRCD intervention significantly elevated neurotrophic factor expression in retinal tissues compared to RAPA-treated and untreated chimeras. Functional assessments further confirmed superior visual performance in CRCD-Sca-1+ chimeras, correlating with improved RGC survival.ConclusionThese findings suggest that CRCD improved paracrine neurotrophic support of autologous BM stem cells by enhancing their autophagy and paracrine functions, leading to structural and functional rescue of the glaucomatous retina. This approach offered a clinically relevant strategy for NTG, providing sustained neuroprotection while avoiding the risks of allogeneic transplantation and repeated intravitreal drug delivery.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13287-026-04944-3.Keywords: Carbon dots, NTG, Stem cell, Neuron protection, Neurotrophic factor

Introduction

Glaucoma, a leading cause of global irreversible blindness, among which normal tension glaucoma (NTG) stands out as a distinct clinical subtype [1–2]. NTG is characterized by progressive optic neuropathy despite intraocular pressure (IOP) within statistically normal ranges [3]. Optineurin(OPTN) gene encodes the adaptor protein that involved in various cellular processes, including signaling and autophagy. Research confirms that OPTN(E50K) mutation is one of the main pathogenic genes of NTG. Notably, OPTN which the protein coded by E50K interferes with the autophagy process [4], has been shown to induce retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) apoptosis through dysregulated protein interaction networks [5–6]. Furthermore, OPTN(E50K) mutation is also closely associated with reduced paracrine function of bone marrow (BM)-derived stem cells [7].

Stem cell interventions have emerged as a new strategy in neurodegeneration therapies, with BM stem cells representing a particularly viable candidate due to their inherent immune compatibility, ethical acceptability, and accessibility for autologous transplantation [8–10]. The homing capability of BM stem cells enables migration to injured retinas where they facilitate tissue repair through transformation into retinal neurons, showing advantages in the paracrine modulation of retinal cell via trophic factor secretion [11]. This effect makes BM stem cells as a promising modality to interfere RGC apoptosis in normal tension glaucoma (NTG). Young wild-type (WT) BM stem cells transplantation demonstrates therapeutic potential by restoring paracrine function in aged OPTN(E50K) mice [7], however limitations persist regarding allograft immunogenicity and oncogenic risks [12], showing the imperative for autologous BM stem cell strategies.

The effectiveness of autologous BM stem cells transplantation is influenced by cellular status including autophagic activity. Researches have shown that when autophagy levels are affected, abnormal protein accumulation occurs, producing a large amount of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that enhances oxidative stress, further leading to a decrease in stem cell self-replication ability and stemness, and increasing age-related damage [13–14]. In the field of stem cell therapy, preclinical studies reveal that rapamycin (RAPA)-mediated preconditioning significantly improves the survival and differentiation competence of transplanted BM stem cells in damaged microenvironments, facilitating tissue regeneration and functional recovery [15–16]. However, there are still many challenges to the widespread application of RAPA. As an immunosuppressant, RAPA may have a significant impact on immune function, resulting in systemic side effects [17]. This underscores the need to explore novel therapeutic strategies that can harness the benefits of autophagy modulation while minimizing the systemic risks associated with RAPA administration.

Carbon-based nanomaterials, particularly carbon dots (CDs), have garnered significant attention as drug delivery platforms for biomedical applications [18–20]. CDs, defined as quasi-spherical carbon nanostructures with sub-10 nm dimensions, exhibit unique advantages including cost-effectiveness, facile synthesis, tunable photoluminescence, exceptional biocompatibility, and minimal toxicity [21–24]. Emerging evidence showed that synthesized CDs derived from chitosan (CS), own the ability of not only initiating autophagic processes but also conferring cell protection through reduction of ROS [25–26]. A composite CS-RAPA carbon dot (CRCD) with hydrothermal carbonization was developed combining RAPA and CS, and showed great water solubility and biocompatibility. CRCD administration matched the autophagy restoration effect of RAPA while exhibiting superior ROS scavenging capacity and neuroinflammation mitigation in I/R models [27]. Considering the chronic and progressive feature of NTG, we propose an approach about CRCD-mediated preconditioning of OPTN(E50K) autologous BM stem cells to amplify the intrinsic neuroprotective capacity of BM stem cells while avoiding repeated intraocular drug administration.

We combined regenerative therapy and nanobiotechnology, solving critical problems in NTG treatments through three innovations: 1) Modulation of autologous stem cell functionality via autophagy-targeted nanomaterials;2) Enhancement of endogenous neuroprotection through amplified paracrine signaling;3) Avoidance of long-term drug administration via sustained stem cell-mediated trophic support.

Materials and methods

Animals

OPTN(E50K) mutation homozygous mice, generated via CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing, were utilized as NTG model [6] (n = 56), while WT C57BL/6J mice served as controls (n = 18) and GFP-OPTN(E50K) mice designated for donor BM stem cells isolation to investigate retinal localization (n = 8). This model was certified as C57BL/6J-Optnem1Hyua (MGI:7279070) by The Jackson Laboratory, and fed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) animal facility with controlled temperature and light/dark cycles at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Harbin Medical University (Approval number: YJSDW2024-055, SYDW2023-101), and went according to the Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research by the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology, as well as the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals from the National Institutes of Health. The work had been reported in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

Bioinformatic analyses of BM stem cells

To delineate molecular signatures associated with BM stem cells in OPTN(E50K) murine models, we performed an integrative proteome profiling strategy. BM stem cells were subjected to tandem mass tag (TMT)-based quantitative proteomic workflow, encompassing sequential protein extraction, tryptic digestion, TMT-plex labeling, and reversed-phase peptide fractionation prior to nano LC-MS/MS analysis. Spectral data were processed through MaxQuant search engine (v1.5.2.8) with FDR < 1% at peptide and protein levels. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were performed based on the significance thresholds of P < 0.05 in two-tailed Student t-tests. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed proteins was then conducted using DAVID Bioinformatics Resources (v6.8), with systematic annotation of biological processes through Gene Ontology (GO) classification and pathway mapping through the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database. Ultimately, cytoscape software (v3.9.1) and STRING database-derived protein data reconstruct the molecular interaction network calculation, helping to understand the key factors of NTG mediated BM dysfunction.

Synthesis of CRCD

CRCD was fabricated followed a hydrothermal carbonization protocol with RAPA, CS, and acrylamide (AAM) as precursors. In a typical synthesis, RAPA (S115842, Aladdin), CS and AAM (Tianjin Guangfu Fine Chemical Research Institute) were homogenized in deionized water at a ratio of 1:1:8 under magnetic agitation for 30 min, then performed hydrothermal reaction at 220 °C for 12 h in autoclave. Upon cooling to ambient temperature, the crude product underwent centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 15 min, followed by filtration through dialysis membrane (MWCO 500 Da, Solarbio) to remove residual particles to achieve final purification, yielding an optically transparent yellowish-brown solution of CRCD.

Cultivation and pretreatment of BM stem cells

1.25% (2,2,2-tribromoethanol) sterile solution (M2910, AIBI Bio) was used for anesthesia of mice, by intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 0.2–0.25 mL/10 g body weight. In the event that the mice exhibited indications of emergence from anesthesia, an additional injection of 0.05 mL/10 g body weight was to be provided. After intracardiac perfusion with physiological saline, BM stem cells were harvested from bilateral femurs and tibias of OPTN(E50K) mice through PBS perfusion, followed by magnetic separation of stem cell antigen-1 (Sca-1) positive subpopulations using commercial magnetic-activated cell sorting kits (STEMCELL Technology). Sorted BM stem cells were pretreated through culture in DMEM medium (MA0212, Meilunbio) supplemented with either RAPA or CRCD. Culture conditions included 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, ExCell Bio) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin-Amphotericin B (C0224, Beyotime), with cells maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C under 5% CO2 atmosphere.

Cytotoxicity of CRCD

Cytotoxicity of CRCD was detected through the viability of cells by cell counting kit 8 (CCK-8, MA0218, Meilunbio). 5 × 103 cells were inoculated in each well of 96-well plates, and cultured 24 h with CRCD of different concentrations. After being rinsed twice with PBS, fresh medium containing CCK-8 was added to each well and incubated for 2 h, and the absorbance of 450 nm was measured by a fluorescence microplate reader (Biotek SynergyHTX).

Co-culture of retinal tissue slices with pretreated BM stem cells

18-month-old OPTN(E50K) mice were placed in a sealed box and subjected to euthanasia with continuous carbon dioxide until cardiac arrest. After anesthetizing and euthanizing, the eyeballs were enucleated and dissected to isolate retinal tissues, of which were sectioned radially into four quadrants and flattened downwards with the photoreceptor surface. Retinal explants were placed in the lower wells of a 24-well plate containing 500 µL of complete medium. Fresh isolated BM subpopulations were then prepared into a suspension of 5 × 105 cells/ml using complete medium. Transwell inserts (0.4 μm pore size, LABSELECT) were placed above the explants, and 100 µL of the Sca-1⁺ cell suspension (pretreated with CRCD or vehicle) was seeded into the upper chamber. This configuration allowed diffusion of soluble factors while preventing direct contact between donor cells and retinal tissue. Co-cultures were maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h before retinal explants were harvested for subsequent analyses.

Bone marrow transplantation

Both donor and recipient mice were 18-month-old OPTN(E50K) mice. Recipient mice were anesthetized and then irradiated with a total dose of 8.5 Gy. Subsequently, Sca-1+ cells pretreated with CRCD or RAPA were infused intravenously into age-matched recipient mice over approximately 2 h. Each mouse received injection of 2 × 106 cells in 0.2 mL solution, while the control group received an equal volume of untreated Sca-1+ cells for infusion.

Twelve weeks after bone marrow transplantation (BMT), the hematopoietic and immune system reconstruction of the recipient mice was basically completed, proving the successful generation of Control-chimeric (E50K-Sca-1+/E50K), RAPA-chimeric (E50K-Sca-1+[RAPA]/E50K) and CRCD-chimeric (E50K-Sca-1+[CRCD]/E50K). Comparative visual functional assessments were conducted in both BMT recipients and age-matched untreated controls, followed by standardized retinal tissue harvest for experiments afterwards.

Visual functional analyses

Depending on the innate nocturnal photophobic behavior of rodents, visual competence was evaluated by Photophobic Behavior Analysis (also known as Light/Dark Transition Test). The testing apparatus consisted of two coterminous chambers interconnected via a tunnel permitting unobstructed pass. 12-hour dark adaptation afterwards, experimental subjects were placed into the darkened compartment and recorded behavioral quantification.

Visual motion tracking capacity was assessed through an optomotor reaction (OMR) test. Mice were positioned on a central platform within a visual arena (4 LCD monitors, 360° coverage) under surveillance. After 5 min environmental habituation, visual stimuli were presented as rotating high-contrast gratings (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 cycles/degree [cpd]). Each trial comprised 60-second stimulus presentation followed by 30-second interval, with the number of effective head movements recorded.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA of cells and retinal tissues was separated with RNeasy Total RNA Kit (NCM Biotech, M5105) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The NovoScript reverse transcription kit (Novoprotein, E047) performs reverse transcription to generate cDNA, RT-qPCR was carried out according to the manufacturer’s protocols with LightCycler 480 (Roche) and SYBR Green I Master Mix (Sangon, China). mRNA expression was normalized to β-actin with the 2 − ΔΔCt method, was responsible for quantitative standardization of target proteins.

Western blot

Proteins extracted from retinal tissues or BM stem cells were dissolved by RIPA Lysis Buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, China) with protease inhibitors, and quantified by BCA kits (P0010, Beyotime, China). After denaturation at 100 °C on a heating block for 5 min, all the protein samples went through SDS polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transfer to PVDF membranes, then blocked in QuickBlock™ Blocking Buffer (P0235, Beyotime, China) for 20 min. The membranes with proteins on incubated with the following primary antibodies: LC3B for both LC3-II/I (1:1000; ab192890, Abcam, UK), p62 (1:1000; 5483, PTM Bio, China), BDNF (Proteintech, 28205), CNTF (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, 365210), IGF-1 (Abcam, ab9572) and FGF-2 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, 136255) overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubated at room temperature for another 1 h with horseradish peroxidase conjugated secondary antibody (1:10000, Zhongshan, China). The protein bands were visualized by enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL, Biosharp) and quantified with ImageJ. β-actin as the housekeeping protein, was responsible for quantitative standardization of target proteins. Full-length blots are presented in Supplementary Materials.

Immunofluorescence and histological analyses

Mice were euthanized and cardiac perfused with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA). Eye tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight at 4 °C, dehydrated in 25% sucrose solution, and then frozen in liquid nitrogen. The frozen eyes were embedded in optimal cutting temperature compound and sectioned at 8 μm. After transparency and PBS washing, the sections were blocked with 0.5% goat serum for 1 h at room temperature, then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. NeuN antibodies (Abcam, ab177487) and Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl Transferase-Mediated dUTP Nick End Labeling (TUNEL) reagent (Roche, 12156792910) were applied for assessing RGC apoptosis. After incubation with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature, nuclear counterstaining was done with DAPI for 5 min. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope and analyzed with ImageJ software.

Histological analysis was used to measure the total retinal thickness as well as retinal layers. Mice were euthanized and cardiac perfused with 4% PFA, with their eyeballs removed and fixed overnight with 4% PFA at 4 °C. After dehydration using an increasing percentage of ethanol (from 50% to 70%, then to 95%, and finally to 100%), the eyeballs were embedded in paraffin and cut into 5 μm sections. Sections were stained using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Zhongshan, China). All retinal thickness measurements were performed at a distance of 200 μm from the edge of the optic disk.

Oxidative stress, and anti-oxidative measurements

Oxidative stress was assessed by quantifying MDA levels using the Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (S0131, Beyotime, China). Briefly, BM stem cells or retinal tissues were homogenized in PBS and then collected the supernatant. MDA concentration was quantified at the absorbance of 532 nm, based on the reaction between MDA and thiobarbituric acid, as specified in the manufacturer’s protocol.

SOD can catalyze the dismutation of superoxide anions which made it an important antioxidant enzyme in living organisms. Total Superoxide Dismutase Assay Kit with NBT (S0109, Beyotime, China) was employed to quantify the activity of total SOD. BM stem cells or retinal tissues were homogenized in PBS and then collected the supernatant. The activity of SOD was determined by measuring the absorbance at 560 nm following the manufacturer’s protocol, reflecting the clearance of superoxide anions by SOD.

Statistical analyses

The number of samples for each experiment wasgreater than six, while the number of samples for visual function was greater than ten, and all experiments were repeated three times or more. One-way ANOVA (single factor) and two-way ANOVA (double factors) followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test were used for analyses across multiple groups. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance (*P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001). Data were presented as means ± SEM. GraphPad Prism (Version 10.1.2) was used to complete the plotting.

Results

Role of OPTN(E50K) and aging process in BM stem cells of mice

To investigate the impact of OPTN(E50K) mutation on aging-related damage in BM, we performed label-free spectrometry based proteomic profiling of BM stem cells from WT and OPTN(E50K) mice at young (3-month-old) and old (18-month-old) stages. Intraocular pressure showed no difference within all groups (Figure S1A), verifying that OPTN (E50K) mice may serve as NTG model. Comparative analyses were conducted across four experimental groups: aged WT (WO), aged OPTN(E50K) (EO), young WT (WY), and young OPTN(E50K) (EY).

The proteomic comparison between aged groups revealed 103 upregulated and 99 downregulated proteins in EO relative to WO (Fig. 1C). Functional enrichment analyses (GO pathways) demonstrated significant enrichment of aging-associated and apoptotic pathways, along with pronounced activation of oxidoreductase activity-related genes and cellular ROS production pathways (Fig. 1B). These findings implicate oxidative stress as a critical mediator of OPTN(E50K)-induced functional impairment in aged BM. Further comparison between EO and EY groups identified 162 upregulated and 210 downregulated proteins (Figure S1B). Differentially expressed proteins in this age-dependent analysis showed robust enrichment in oxidative stress regulatory pathways, particularly those governing hydrogen peroxide metabolism and superoxide anion elimination (Figure S1C). Similar pattern was observed in levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), a byproduct of lipid oxidation under oxidative stress which serves as a critical indicator of cellular oxidative damage. Elevated MDA level in EO group compared with WO and EY groups reflected increased oxidative damage (Fig. 1D). The activity of total Superoxide Dismutase (SOD), though, were lowered in EO group compared with WO and EY groups (Fig. 1E).The OPTN(E50K) mutation exhibited a marked elevation in oxidative stress levels, demonstrating an age-dependent aggravation of this phenotype.

Oxidative stress generated toxic metabolites that were subsequently eliminated via autophagy preserving cellular homeostasis. Given this critical role of autophagic clearance in redox regulation, we conducted evaluation of autophagic flux quantified by LC3-II/I ratio conversion coupled with p62/SQSTM1 degradation. The expression of autophagy related proteins was detected by Western blot analysis. The BM stem cells of EO showed lower expression of common autophagosome markers LC3 (LC3-II/LC3-I) than that in WO and EY (Fig. 1G), while p62/SQSTM1 manifesting as an opposite trend (Fig. 1H), indicating the inhibited autophagy function of aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells.

CRCD enhanced autophagy and paracrine activity in aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells

As illustrated in the schematic diagram (Figure S2A), CRCD was prepared via a hydrothermal carbonization approach using RAPA, CS, and AAM as precursors. Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) image showed that the CRCD particles are spherical with an average particle size of 3.7 nm (Figure S2B-C). Ultraviolet-visible spectroscopic analysis revealed that CRCD displayed absorption bands at 277 nm, matching the characteristic peak of RAPA, along with a signal at 324 nm, assigned to the n-π* transition of the C = O double bond (Figure S2D). Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was further employed to examine the chemical modifications of CS and RAPA on the surface of CRCD (Figure S2E). These results confirm the successful synthesis of CRCD, especially the effective incorporation of RAPA into the structure. Furthermore, photoluminescence (PL) spectra demonstrated that the maximum emission intensity occurred at 482 nm under 400 nm excitation (Figure S2F).

CRCD biocompatibility was detected by cell counting kit 8 (CCK-8). After stimulation with CRCD of different concentrations ranging from 0 to 400 µg/mL for 24 h, the viability of cells was found to be > 80%, enlightening that CRCD cytotoxicity could be negligible (Fig. 2A). BM stem cells harvested from aged OPTN(E50K) mice were then pretreated with either RAPA or CRCD. The antioxidant protective effect of CRCD was validated in vitro. As a byproduct of lipid oxidation in cells under oxidative stress, MDA is considered as an indicator of oxidative stress. We found that the MDA content of BM stem cells treated with CRCD decreased significantly (Fig. 2B), while the total antioxidant capacity showed the opposite trend of enhancement (Fig. 2C), indicating that CRCD has significant antioxidant activity in aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells compared with RAPA. It was also worth noting that CRCD was more effective than both free RAPA and RAPA-only CD in mitigating oxidative stress (Figure S3D-E). This superiorperformance was likely due to function of CRCD that can not only inhibit ROS production by inducing autophagy like rapamycin to clear abnormal mitochondria, but also directly eliminate pre-existing excess free radicals within cells. Previous studies have demonstrated that CRCD exhibits dose-dependent scavenging activity against •OH and DPPH•, an effect primarily attributed to the hydrogen-donating capacity of its chitosan component, which neutralized free radicals to form stable macromolecules [27].

Subsequently, the expression of autophagy related proteins was further detected by Western blot analysis. The results showed that the expression of common autophagosome markers LC3 (LC3-II/LC3-I) in BM stem cells treated with CRCD was significantly higher than that in untreated and RAPA-treated BM stem cells, while p62/SQSTM1 manifested less expression (Fig. 2D-F), attesting that CRCD enhanced autophagy flow in aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells. Our results further reveal that both free RAPA, RAPA-only CD and CRCD showed increased autophagy flow, indicating that CRCD retained the function of RAPA in promoting autophagy (Figure S3A-C).

In addition, we also detected the protein (Fig. 2G-K) and mRNA (Fig. 2L-O) expression of manifold neurotrophic factors in each group of cells, to determine the effect of CRCD on the paracrine function of BM stem cells. We once discovered the reduction of paracrine function in aged OPTN(E50K) BM [7]. In this study, BM stem cells treated with CRCD showed higher expression of BDNF, CNTF, FGF-2, and IGF-1 compared to other groups of cells, suggesting that CRCD can restore the secretion of aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells with various neurotrophic factors.

Our experimental results further demonstrated that the neuroprotective effect of CRCD exhibited a dose-dependent relationship (Figure S4). However, CRCD did not always exhibit stronger paracrine promoting function on BM cells with increasing dose. Excessive treatment with CRCD may lead to a decrease in the secretion level of neurotrophic factors in BM cells, which may be related to the cytotoxicity of high-dose CRCD.

CRCD-pretreated Sca-1⁺ cells exhibited stronger autophagy and paracrine efficiency

The efficacy of CRCD in different subpopulations was validated with BM stem cells from aged E50K mice. Western blot analysis was performed to examine autophagy-related protein expression, which indicated that after CRCD treatment, Sca-1+ cells had higher expression for the common autophagosome marker LC3 (LC3-II/LC3-I) compared to Sca-1- cells and untreated Sca-1+ cells, while it showed no significant difference between Sca-1- cells and unsorted cells. However, as for another autophagosome marker, the expression of p62/SQSTM1 in CRCD-treated Sca-1+ cells was significantly lower than CRCD-treated Sca-1- cells and untreated Sca-1+ cells (Fig. 3A-D). The detection of stronger LC3 and weaker p62/SQSTM1 expression leads to the assumption that CRCD treatment enhanced autophagic flux and possessed superior autophagosome clearance, particularly in Sca-1+ cells.

We then assessed mRNA (Fig. 3E-H) and protein (Fig. 3I-M) expression of neurotrophic factors in both CRCD-treated and untreated Sca-1+ cells compared with that of Sca-1− and total cells in aged OPTN(E50K) mice, in order to further determine the specific impact of various cell subpopulations on the efficiency of CRCD induced paracrine secretion. The CRCD-pretreated Sca-1+ cells had higher expression of BDNF, CNTF, FGF-2 and IGF-1 than cells in other groups, suggesting that CRCD-induced Sca-1+ cells subpopulations had an elevated paracrine efficiency. Consequently, Sca-1+ cells cultured with CRCD produce more neurotrophic factors than unsorted cells throughout the BM stem cells transplantation with a set number.

CRCD inhibited apoptosis and promoted neurotrophic factor expression in retinas co-cultured with aged E50K BM stem cells in vitro

By utilizing a cell-tissue co-culture model, we explored whether Sca-1+ cells can protect NTG retina from apoptosis through the neuroprotective effect of neurotrophic factors in vitro. Isolated Sca-1+ cells of aged E50K mice underwent pharmacological preconditioning with either RAPA or CRCD, prior to establishing a co-culture system in vitro with aged E50K retinal explant cultures (Fig. 4A). Sca-1+ cells + CRCD possessed the fewest TUNEL+ apoptotic cells and most viable RGCs, compared to Sca-1+ cells (Control) and Sca-1+ cells + RAPA (Fig. 4B-D). Moreover, the decreased MDA content and increased SOD activity in CRCD groups (Fig. 4E-F) indicated that CRCD-treated Sca-1+ cells equipped with better antioxidant capacity.

To investigate the neuroprotective potential of CRCD-pretreated aged E50K Sca-1+ cells, we established a co-culture system wherein BM-derived Sca-1⁺ cells isolated from aged E50K mice underwent 24-hour pharmacological preconditioning with CRCD and RAPA. We also assessed the mechanism of CRCD to autophagy by using chloroquine (CQ), a clinically available antimalarial agent and commonly used autophagy inhibitor that can block the fusion of autophagosomes and lysosomes, thereby exerting an inhibitory effect on autophagic flux [28]. The preconditioned cells were subsequently co-cultured apically with age-matched OPTN(E50K) retinal explant cultures to model glaucomatous neurodegeneration in vitro. Retinal explants are cultured alone in basal medium (Blank), or co-cultured with naive Sca-1⁺ cells (Control), RAPA-pretreated Sca-1⁺ cells (RAPA), CRCD-pretreated Sca-1⁺ cells (CRCD), CRCD/CQ-pretreated Sca-1⁺ cells (CRCD + CQ). After 24-hour co-culture, qPCR and Western blot analysis were applied to quantify neurotrophic factor expression of the retinal explants, encompassed BDNF, CNTF, FGF-2, and IGF-1.The results showed that both mRNA (Fig. 5A-D) and protein (Fig. 5E-I) levels of all monitored neurotrophic factors displayed significant upregulation in the retinal explants of both RAPA-treated and CRCD-treated Sca-1⁺ cells, with the CRCD group showing an even greater extent, compared to Blank and Control groups. Furthermore, CQ pretreatment reversed the promoting effect of CRCD on the mRNA expression of BDNF, CNTF, FGF-2, and IGF-1. The trend of results from Western blot further confirmed this finding, indicating that autophagy inhibitor CQ possesses the capability to block the promotion of neurotrophic factor secretion induced by CRCD.

The observed association was further corroborated through pharmacological inhibition of autophagic flux via pre-treatment of Sca-1+ cells with CQ, followed by sequential treatment with CRCD. Western blot analysis measured expression levels of Bax and cleaved caspase-3 which are considered to be apoptotic proteins, as well as Bcl-2, which is considered to be anti-apoptotic protein. The expression of Bax and cleaved caspase-3 decreased and Bcl-2 increased in both RAPA and CRCD groups indicating that CRCD favored a decrease in RGC apoptosis and showed the most significant neuroprotective effect, which likely contributed to the retinal tissue and functional preservation associated with CRCD. The increased expression of apoptotic proteins and decreased expression of anti-apoptotic proteins in the CRCD + CQ group suggest that the anti-apoptotic effect of CRCD may be related to promoting autophagic flux (Fig. 5J-M).

CRCD-pretreated Sca-1+ cells promoted RGC survival and improved visual function in vivo

To assess whether CRCD-pretreated autologous Sca-1⁺ cells could be detected in the retinal region and whether their presence was associated with amelioration of glaucomatous damage, we generated three experimental groups: untreated Sca-1+ cells (Control), RAPA-treated Sca-1+ cells (RAPA), and CRCD-treated Sca-1+ cells (CRCD). These donor cells were derived from aged E50K BM stem cells and pretreated before being transplanted into age-matched E50K mice that underwent 8.5 Gy irradiation. Thus, chimeric mice with different BM stem cells were obtained after 12 weeks of transplantation.

Preliminary assessment of visual function was conducted using light-dark transition tests, which exploit rodents’ photophobic behavior. After BMT, quantitative analysis revealed that both RAPA- and CRCD-chimeric groups exhibited significantly reduced time spent in the light chamber compared to age-matched OPTN(E50K) mice, with the CRCD-chimeric group demonstrating reduction relative to Control-chimeric group as well (Fig. 6B-C). In visual response tests, the CRCD-chimeric group displayed a significant improvement in head-tracking frequency among all spatial frequencies tested (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 cycles per degree [cpd]) than other groups (Fig. 6D-E). While the RAPA-chimeric group showed partial improvement in optokinetic responses, it remained statistically inferior to CRCD-mediated recovery. And the Control group showed no obvious differences from age-matched E50K mice. Therefore, these functional metrics demonstrate that CRCD-optimized Sca-1+ cells confer superior neuroprotective function against glaucomatous injury compared to RAPA-treated Sca-1+ cells, as proved by enhanced preservation of photophobic behavior and better restoration of optomotor response.

In order to evaluate the structural correlates with the observed visual functional improvements, analysis of retinal architecture was performed by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, of which revealed a significant increase in retinal thickness of the ganglion cell layer (GCL) and inner plexiform layer (IPL) in CRCD-chimeric mice compared to other groups (Fig. 6I-K). A similar finding was observed for NeuN, a mature neuronal marker, demonstrated a marked elevation in NeuN-positive RGCs density within the GCL of CRCD-treated groups (Fig. 6F-G). Furthermore, TUNEL staining confirmed reduced apoptotic indices in the GCL, with CRCD-chimeric mice exhibiting fewer TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells compared to age-matched E50K mice and control-chimeric mice, while similar but weaker effect was observed in RAPA-chimeric mice.

CRCD promoted homing and paracrine function of aged E50K BM stem cells and reduced retinal cellsapoptosis

To further elucidate the mechanism of repairing damaged retinas in chimeric mice, we used Sca-1+ cells from aged GFP-E50K mice as donor cells. After pretreatment and BM reconstruction, immunostaining was performed to examine the detection and localization of donor-derived CRCD-pretreated Sca-1⁺ cells within recipient retinas. GFP+ cells from donor sources were detected in retinas among all groups, while CRCD-pretreated Sca-1+ cells showing a higher frequency of detectable localization (Figure S5A-B). Although the vitreous cavity was difficult to observe, green fluorescence localization can be detected in the optic nerve area (Figure S5C), suggesting its possible migration to the optic nerve region.

The expression of neurotrophic signaling and apoptotic pathways was detected via qPCR (Fig. 7A-D) and Western blot (Fig. 7E-I) in chimeric retinas. CRCD-Sca-1+ chimeras exhibited significant upregulation of neurotrophic factor expression, demonstrating increases in BDNF, CNTF, IGF-1, and FGF-2 mRNA and protein levels. These data indicate enhanced paracrine secretory capacity in CRCD-optimized cells. While RAPA-Sca-1+ chimeras showed similar neurotrophic upregulation, their effects remained statistically inferior to CRCD-mediated induction.

Discussion

Globally, NTG has become a growing public health challenge, and its chronic progressive course underscores the critical need for long-term sustained neuroprotective interventions [29]. The OPTN(E50K) mice model used here, which shows RGC degeneration and visual impairments without IOP elevation, has been systematically validated in previous research [4, 6]. Dysregulation of autophagy is a key pathological feature in neurodegenerative diseases. Under normal physiological conditions, the autophagy process can promptly remove damaged cellular components and protein aggregates to maintain cellular homeostasis, and it forms a negative feedback regulation with reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolism [30–31]. Yet in neurodegenerative diseases like glaucoma, this mechanism is often disrupted, leading to abnormal ROS accumulation, cellular dysfunction, and neuronal death [32–34]. Previous research has confirmed that NTG optic nerve damage is related to autophagy abnormalities [4], inflammation [35] and reduced neurotrophic factors [7], while allogeneic BM stem cells possess the function of homing to damaged retinas and repairing optic nerve damage. Recent studies have increasingly focused on the neuroprotective potential of BM stem cells. Several research confirms that BM stem cells secrete neurotrophic factors and can be detected in retina under certain pathological conditions after systemic administration, where they may exert reparative effects primarily through paracrine signaling [7, 36–38], enabling them to exert reparative effects within damaged retinal tissue.

Since BM stem cells are primarily derived from adult tissues, their quantity and viability are influenced by factors such as age and disease status. Magnetic-activated cell sorting has enabled the identification of BM stem cells subpopulation labeled with Sca-1 in murine models [39–40]. Sca-1⁺ cells exhibit a defined stem cell phenotype with superior self-renewal capacity, lower heterogeneity and precisely regulated homing ability compared to mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs). Through minimally invasive bone marrow aspiration, Sca-1⁺-based transplantation benefits from a robust safety profile, whereas retinal progenitor cells remain limited by restricted sources, technical challenges, and early-stage clinical validation. Comparative analyses revealed that in young WT mice, these Sca-1+ cells exhibit enhanced migratory capacity and paracrine signaling relative to other BM cell populations, demonstrating its therapeutic potential in reducing retinal apoptosis and restoring visual function in aged OPTN(E50K) mice [7]. However, under conditions of aging and disease, the function of BM stem cells declines significantly, with notable reductions in their proliferation capacity, differentiation potential, antioxidant levels, and secretory function [41–42]. Especially in the context of the OPTN(E50K) mutation, the paracrine-mediated neuroprotective effect is further weakened, making it difficult to effectively inhibit RGC degeneration [7]. Multiple stem cell-based studies have indicated that young BM stem cells exert neuroprotective effects. Nevertheless, the process of BM reconstruction inevitably involves immune-related challenges. Current treatment methods generally rely on allogeneic transplantation, which always carries the risk of immune rejection and tumorigenic transformation. Therefore, restoring the function of autologous BM stem cells has become a more clinically promising therapeutic strategy. This study proposes a combined approach, using CRCD which possesses both antioxidant and autophagy-regulating properties, to functionally remodel OPTN (E50K) stem cells, thereby enhancing their paracrine neuroprotective effects.

Our proteomic analysis results highlighted the sustained involvement of oxidative stress mechanisms in both mutation-associated damage and aging processes of OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells, serving as a core biological process leading to their functional impairment. At the same time, E50K BM stem cells exhibited age-related impaired autophagic flux, characterized by reduced autophagosome clearance and depletion of autophagic capacity, resulting in downregulation of LC3-II and progressive accumulation of p62/SQSTM1. These findings suggest that the OPTN(E50K) mutation leads to abnormal autophagy and oxidative stress in BM stem cells, thereby affecting their protective effect on RGCs.

In terms of mechanism intervention, RAPA-mediated modulation targeting mTOR signaling pathway enhances RGC survival through autophagy induction, which shows potential in the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases [4]. Latest investigations reveal that CDs synthesized by RAPA can mitigate vascular cellular senescence through coordination of antioxidative stress responses and mTOR signaling axis, that provides a new idea for the treatment of NTG [25]. Building on these insights, we developed CRCD, a chitosan-derived nanocomposite that integrates the autophagy-regulating function of rapamycin and the antioxidant function of chitosan carbon dots, aiming to overcome the limitations of RAPA and take full advantages of carbon dot nanomaterials. CRCD exhibits enhanced aqueous solubility and biosafety, and alleviates acute retinal injury by anti-inflammatory response in ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) model [27]. However, the I/R model simulates transient ischemic injury rather than the chronic progressive neurodegeneration characteristic of NTG. Consequently, a single intravitreal injection is unlikely to achieve the desired long-term therapeutic effect, while frequent repeated injections may lead to various iatrogenic injuries. In addition, traditional CRCD administration methods still have obvious limitations. Eye drops keep a short residence time on the ocular surface, intravitreal injection is difficult to maintain long-term efficacy while carrying the risk of complications, and systemic administration is limited by the low ocular bioavailability [44–48]. In comparison, the approach proposed in this study is to use CRCD to functionally remodel autologous BM stem cells in NTG model. Our results showed that CRCD not only increased the detectable presence of donor-derived signals in the retinal region but also enhanced their paracrine neurotrophic function, thereby enabling sustained RGC protection while avoiding the complications of repetitive drug administration.

Our experimental data confirmed that CRCD maintained the autophagy induction ability of RAPA in aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells, while endowed superior ROS-scavenging function through the structure of surface functional groups, in line with previous results in Human Umbilical Vein Endothelial Cells (HUVECs) and BV2 microglial cells [27]. Aged E50K Sca-1+ cells pretreated with CRCD exhibited significantly elevated expression of multiple neurotrophic factors compared to untreated group, including BDNF, CNTF, IGF-1, and FGF-2. It is likely to be associated with activation of autophagic flux and enhanced ROS scavenging capacity, which is supported by our findings that blocking CRCD-induced autophagy with CQ resulted in eliminating the promoting effect. The coordinated enhancement of autophagy-mediated cellular clearance and oxidative stress mitigation establishes a favorable microenvironment for the production and release of neurotrophic factors, thereby indirectly preserving their expression through improved cellular homeostasis [49]. Compromised autophagic activity triggers abnormal protein accumulation and excessive ROS generation in stem cells, aggravating oxidative stress and impairing stem cell function [50]. Taking BDNF as an example, autophagy is a key component of BDNF signaling, which can activate autophagy by promoting STAT3 phosphorylation to activate the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway [51–52]. Conversely, autophagy also exerts feedback regulation on BDNF secretion [53–56]. Researches have shown that the beneficial paracrine effects of young BM cells are abolished when autophagy is inhibited by drug or siRNA knockdown, indicating that autophagy activity declines with age and impairs the paracrine function of BM cells [43]. In this study, considering the limited capacity of BM stem cells to uptake and release CRCD as a drug carrier, we suppose that CRCD primarily enhances the paracrine function of these stem cells through autophagy modulation, rather than directly delivering the compound to retinal tissues to regulate autophagy in resident retinal cells. This interaction mechanism may be an important basis for the enhanced paracrine function of CRCD after pretreatment, but its molecular details require further investigation.

In our in vitro non-contact co-culture system, CRCD-pretreated Sca-1+ cells markedly increased retinal BDNF, CNTF, IGF-1 and FGF-2 levels, which supports the view of promoting the paracrine mechanism. Further in vivo experiments showed that after stem cell reconstruction with autologous Sca-1+ cells optimized with CRCD, the aged OPTN(E50K) mice displayed enhanced secretion of retinal neurotrophic factors in retina compared to untreated controls, while RAPA exhibited comparable but inferior neurotrophic factor-promoting effects both in vitro and in vivo. In addition, CRCD treatment also modulated apoptotic signaling pathways as downregulation of pro-apoptotic proteins and upregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins, while accompanied by a decrease in TUNEL+ cells within retinal tissues and an increase in retinal thickness. Behavioral assessments demonstrated functional recovery in aged E50K mice receiving CRCD-Sca-1+ BM transplantation, which exhibited light avoidance and increased exploratory head movements during visual motor response testing, suggesting the effectivity against age-related degenerative processes in E50K mice.

Moreover, donor-derived GFP signal was detectable in recipient retinas. Some studies suggest that BM-derived stem cells can migrate through paracellular or transcellular pathways under the action of chemokines, and achieve trans-barrier migration and homing through the SDF-1-CXCR4 axis and other mechanisms [36, 37, 57, 58]. Nevertheless, this finding has yet to achieve a consensus among researchers in the relevant scholarly fields. Some investigations point out that other mechanisms like the paracrine activity of stem cells, rather than their migration or differentiation in retinal tissue, are the main cause of the cell protective effect observed in the retina [59–60]. Considering the strong evidence of our non-contact co-culture in vitro, we tend to interpret the visual functional improvements observed in vivo to be predominantly mediated by enhanced paracrine neurotrophic support rather than direct structural integration of donor cells.

In summary, our findings demonstrated that CRCD rejuvenated autologous Sca-1+ cells by enhancing autophagy and antioxidant capacity, thereby augmenting their paracrine neurotrophic function. This rejuvenation led to reduced RGC apoptosis, preserved retinal architecture, and improved visual behavior in aged E50K mice. Notably, this strategy of rejuvenating autologous stem cells avoids the immunogenic and oncogenic risks of allogeneic transplantation, displaying its clinical potential for long-term management of NTG, thereby advancing prospects for autologous stem cell-based therapies in neurodegenerative ophthalmic disorders. Still, several methodological limitations remain consideration. Firstly, we did not perform lineage-tracing, or donor DNA/qPCR analyses, which are necessary to fully distinguish transient localization or long-term persistence of donor cells. Besides, although increased neurotrophic factor expression was observed in retinal tissues, clinically accessible quantification via aqueous humor or serum remained difficult to assess in murine models. Studies have shown that humans do not have a single protein with the exact same function and expression profile as murine Sca-1+, therefore how cell subpopulations represented by Sca-1+ would change with age in human being and the impact of CRCD on it may still need further validation in large animal models and human clinical trials in the future. Despite these limitations, our data provided compelling evidence that CRCD-based rejuvenation of autologous stem cells is a promising therapeutic strategy for neurodegenerative optic neuropathies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we propose a novel strategy to rejuvenate autologous stem cells by enhancing the functional capacity of aged OPTN(E50K) BM stem cells. In this study, we synthesized a novel functional nanomaterial CRCD with biological safety. Utilizing the regulatory properties of CRCD against oxidation and autophagy in aged E50K Sca-1+ cells, the survival microenvironment of retinal cells was improved by optimizing paracrine function of the dominant Sca-1+ cells, which lead to preservation of retinal integrity and improvement of visual behavior. The synergistic integration of stem cell therapy with nanotechnology not only regulates the function of stem cells, but also covers the shortage of RAPA, providing practical and effective new strategies for stem cell therapy of neurodegenerative diseases like NTG.

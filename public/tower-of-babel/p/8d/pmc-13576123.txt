Background

α-Thalassaemia is one of the most common monogenic disorders worldwide [1], with particularly high prevalence in southern China, where the carrier rate ranges from 12.48% to 17.55% [2–4]. The disorder arises from functional defects in the HBA gene cluster, leading to reduced or absent synthesis of α-globin chains. Clinical severity depends on the number of functional α-globin genes affected, ranging from asymptomatic carrier states to haemoglobin H disease and the most severe form, Hb Bart’s hydrops fetalis syndrome, which is usually fatal in utero or shortly after birth. Although silent carriers and individuals with α-thalassaemia trait are usually asymptomatic or mildly affected, carrier couples remain at risk of conceiving offspring with HbH disease or Hb Bart’s hydrops fetalis syndrome [5, 6].

Preimplantation genetic testing for monogenic disorders (PGT-M) enables carrier couples to select genotypically normal or non-pathogenic embryos for transfer, thereby achieving the birth of a healthy child [7]. However, conventional NGS-based PGT-M workflows remain constrained by several important limitations [8]. First, direct variant detection often requires family-specific assay design. Second, parental haplotype construction is heavily dependent on probands or grandparent samples, rendering diagnosis unfeasible in families where such individuals are unavailable [9, 10]. Third, the overall workflow is labour-intensive, time-consuming, and technically complex, requiring multiple sequential procedures across several platforms—including pedigree-based haplotype construction, variant confirmation, and separate embryo testing using NGS, gap-PCR, and Sanger sequencing—thereby imposing substantial operational and financial burdens on patients [11].

Long-read sequencing (LRS) has shown promise in overcoming some of these limitations [12–14]. By sequencing parental carriers alone, LRS can identify a dense set of highly informative heterozygous SNPs in close physical proximity to the target locus, permitting haplotype phasing without requiring additional family members [15]. This feature may also be particularly valuable in families with de novo mutations, in which conventional haplotype construction is often constrained by the absence of informative affected relatives [16, 17]. However, long-read whole-genome sequencing remains costly and is limited by the fragment length of embryonic whole-genome amplification (WGA) products. In current clinical workflows, LRS is more commonly applied to probands or parental carriers, while embryo diagnosis still relies on separate downstream procedures [16, 18], leaving the overall workflow operationally complex and costly.

To address these challenges, we previously developed targeted long-read sequencing-based PGT-M for α-thalassaemia (tlrPGT-α-thal) [19], building upon our established comprehensive analysis of thalassaemia alleles (CATSA) methodology [20]. This method combines long-range PCR with long-read sequencing and applies them directly to embryonic WGA products, enabling simultaneous direct detection of HBA variants and efficient haplotype linkage analysis. Compared with existing methods, tlrPGT-α-thal has a more streamlined workflow, lower cost, and potentially greater clinical accessibility. Our previous retrospective study provided preliminary evidence of its accuracy and technical advantages [19]. However, prospective clinical data remain limited, and systematic evaluation of its diagnostic performance and clinical utility is still lacking. We therefore conducted the present prospective study to evaluate the diagnostic performance, clinical feasibility, and potential implementation value of tlrPGT-α-thal.

Methods

Study participants and eligibility criteria

This prospective study consecutively enrolled families at high risk of transmitting α-thalassaemia who underwent clinical PGT-M at the Reproductive Medicine Centre of Nanning Second People’s Hospital between August 2024 and March 2025. Families were eligible for inclusion if they were at high risk of transmitting α-thalassaemia, underwent blinded parallel analysis by both conventional NGS-based PGT-M and tlrPGT-α-thal, and had the required parental and embryonic samples available according to the study protocol. Families were excluded if they did not meet the predefined α-thalassaemia risk criteria, did not enter the parallel testing workflow, withdrew consent, or lacked essential samples or baseline data required for protocol-defined evaluation. No formal hypothesis-based sample size calculation was performed because this was a prospective clinical validation cohort; all consecutively eligible families within the study period were included. The primary concordance analysis set was defined on an embryo basis and included embryos for which both conventional NGS-based PGT-M and tlrPGT-α-thal yielded evaluable results. This prospective blinded clinical validation study was reported with reference to the STARD 2015 statement for diagnostic accuracy studies [21] and, where applicable, the STROBE statement for observational studies [22].

Sample preparation

Genomic DNA from parental peripheral blood was extracted using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). Trophectoderm biopsy samples underwent WGA by multiple displacement amplification (MDA) using the REPLI-g® Single Cell Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). All enrolled families underwent conventional clinical PGT, including PGT-A and NGS-based PGT-M, with NGS-based PGT-M serving as the comparator method for the primary concordance analysis. In parallel, parental DNA and embryonic WGA products were sent to Berry Genomics for tlrPGT-α-thal analysis.

Conventional NGS-based PGT-M for α-thalassaemia

The conventional NGS-based PGT-M workflow comprised two sequential stages: pedigree-based parental haplotype construction and embryo testing (Fig. 1). During pedigree-based parental haplotype construction, DNA samples were collected from the couple and relevant family members (e.g. grandparents or probands). Pathogenic variants were independently confirmed by Sanger sequencing or gap-PCR, after which parental haplotypes were established by SNP linkage analysis using NGS data. After completion of parental haplotype construction, blastocyst biopsy samples underwent WGA. The resulting WGA products were first analysed by Sanger sequencing or gap-PCR for variant verification, followed by NGS-based haplotype linkage analysis to assign the embryonic genotype [11].

Targeted long-read sequencing-based PGT-M for α-thalassaemia (tlrPGT-α-thal)

The tlrPGT-α-thal method, previously established by our group [19], was used in the present study. Briefly, the method was developed as an optimisation of the CATSA technique. Using embryonic WGA products as templates, a multiplex long-range PCR primer system was employed to simultaneously amplify the complete HBA gene cluster and four flanking linkage segments (L1, L2, L3, and L4) within a 1 Mb region upstream and downstream of the locus. Amplified products were used to construct SMRTbell libraries for sequencing on the PacBio Sequel II platform (Fig. 1). Sequencing quality-control criteria were as follows: for parental samples, the sequencing depth of the HBA gene region and all four linkage segments had to reach ≥ 80×; for embryo samples, the HBA gene region had to achieve ≥ 80× depth, with at least three linkage segments also reaching ≥ 80×.

After preprocessing and read alignment, HBA variants were directly identified from LRS data, and SNP profiles were generated across five segments, including the HBA region and four flanking segments (L1–L4), for all parental and embryonic samples. At this stage, these five segments represented independent segment-level profiles and had not yet been linked into complete haplotypes. An automated family-based maximum-likelihood linkage analysis algorithm was then applied for haplotype construction. The algorithm jointly analysed the directly detected HBA genotypes of parental and embryonic samples, together with SNP profiles across L1, L2, HBA, L3 and L4, and incorporated all available embryos within each family into the overall analysis. Candidate paternal and maternal haplotype configurations were evaluated according to their likelihood of explaining the segregation patterns observed across the embryo cohort. The configuration with the highest likelihood was selected to construct complete paternal and maternal haplotypes. The final HBA genotype of each embryo was inferred according to the combination of paternal and maternal haplotypes inherited by that embryo (Figure S2). For samples in which linkage analysis was inconclusive, direct HBA variant detection results were used as the independent diagnostic basis when sufficient read-level evidence was available.

When necessary, manual review was performed as an auxiliary check, during which a reliable reference embryo could be selected for manual linkage-based verification of the automated results. A reference embryo was considered suitable only if its HBA genotype could be directly determined by LRS, was supported by phase-resolved reads, was concordant with SNP-based linkage inference within the HBA region, and showed distinguishable haplotypes across the flanking linkage segments L1–L4.

Clinical follow-up and prenatal diagnosis validation

Systematic clinical follow-up was performed for all families who underwent embryo transfer, with detailed documentation of the outcome of each transfer cycle. Serum β-hCG was measured 14 days after transfer; a negative result was recorded as implantation failure. In cases of positive β-hCG, transvaginal ultrasonography was performed 4–6 weeks after transfer to confirm an intrauterine gestational sac and foetal cardiac activity. Clinical pregnancy was defined as the ultrasonographic detection of an intrauterine gestational sac. Pregnancy loss was defined as any pregnancy that did not progress after a positive serum β-hCG result, including biochemical pregnancy loss, ectopic pregnancy, and spontaneous abortion after confirmed clinical pregnancy. For successful pregnancies, invasive prenatal diagnosis by amniocentesis was recommended at 18–22 weeks of gestation. Amniotic fluid cells were cultured, and genomic DNA was extracted for HBA pathogenic variant verification by Sanger sequencing, gap-PCR, or NGS. The foetal genotype obtained by prenatal diagnosis was compared with the corresponding tlrPGT-α-thal embryo result to calculate the diagnostic concordance rate and evaluate the clinical predictive accuracy of tlrPGT-α-thal.

Outcome measures and statistical analysis

The clinical performance assessment encompassed the following five dimensions: (i) genotype concordance, defined as the concordance rate of tlrPGT-α-thal embryo genotyping relative to conventional NGS-based PGT-M as the reference standard; (ii) direct variant detection success rate, defined as the proportion of embryos in which the genotype was unambiguously established by direct HBA variant detection; (iii) haplotype linkage establishment rate, defined as the proportion of embryos and families in which proband-independent haplotype linkage analysis was successfully completed; (iv) prenatal diagnosis concordance rate, defined as the proportion of pregnancies undergoing invasive prenatal diagnosis in which the foetal genotype was concordant with the corresponding tlrPGT-α-thal result; and (v) resolution of complex cases, defined as the number of cases in which conventional PGT-M failed to yield a definitive diagnosis but tlrPGT-α-thal succeeded, with analysis of underlying causes. Analyses were descriptive. Concordance and success rates are reported with exact two-sided 95% binomial confidence intervals. Handling of comparator-indeterminate embryos was prespecified in the study protocol. Embryos without definitive results by the comparator method were excluded from the primary concordance analysis and are described separately as clinically informative rescue cases.

Results

Sequencing performance and overall diagnostic performance

During the study period, 106 families at high risk of transmitting α-thalassaemia were assessed for eligibility. Of these, 2 families were excluded because written informed consent was not obtained, and 1 family was excluded because embryo DNA was insufficient for analysis. The remaining 103 families were prospectively enrolled for blinded parallel analysis, contributing 739 samples and 533 embryos. All embryos underwent parallel testing by conventional NGS-based PGT-M and tlrPGT-α-thal. To evaluate the reliability of tlrPGT-α-thal, we first conducted a systematic analysis of sequencing performance. All samples achieved high sequencing quality, with Q30 ≥ 94.28%. Parental samples yielded a mean sequencing output of 117.06 Mb, whereas embryonic WGA products yielded 79.19 Mb (Fig. 2B). Across the target regions, including the HBA gene cluster and four linkage segments, the mean sequencing depth was 2584.13× for parental samples and 1749.45× for embryonic WGA products (Fig. 2C). Although the data output and sequencing depth of embryonic WGA products were lower than those of peripheral blood samples, both exceeded the pre-specified quality-control thresholds.

Of the 533 embryos analysed, 507 from 102 families yielded conclusive results by both methods and were included in the primary concordance analysis, whereas the remaining 26 comparator-inconclusive embryos were analysed separately (Table 1). In the primary concordance analysis, tlrPGT-α-thal showed complete concordance with conventional NGS-based PGT-M (507/507, 100.0%; exact 95% CI, 99.3–100.0). Detailed genotyping results for all embryos determined by tlrPGT-α-thal are provided in Supplementary Table S1. Within this analysis, direct variant detection was successful in 501/507 embryos (98.82%; 95% CI, 97.4–99.6), haplotype linkage was established in 505/507 embryos (99.61%; 95% CI, 98.6–100.0), and one meiotic recombination event was identified. Among 57 pregnancies that underwent invasive prenatal diagnosis after embryo transfer, prenatal diagnosis was fully concordant with the tlrPGT-α-thal results (57/57, 100.0%; 95% CI, 93.7–100.0). Of the 26 comparator-inconclusive embryos, tlrPGT-α-thal resolved 6 complex cases. The remaining 20 harboured HBA-region aneuploidies identified by PGT-A, of which genotype and parental origin were determined in 12/20 embryos, whereas 8/20 remained unresolved.

Study cohort and key outcomes of tlrPGT-α-thal

Comprehensive and reliable direct variant detection in blastocysts

Among the 507 embryos included in the primary concordance analysis, 118 had completely normal genotypes and 389 harboured pathogenic variants, encompassing multiple structural variants (--SEA, -α3.7, --THAI, -α4.2, αααanti3.7) and various single nucleotide variants (SNVs) (Supplementary Table S1). Leveraging the comprehensive coverage of our primer design [19], tlrPGT-α-thal enabled direct detection of diverse α-thalassaemia pathogenic variants in blastocysts (Supplementary Table S2, Fig. 3), including the simultaneous identification of multiple variants within a single embryo. The overall direct detection success rate across 507 embryos was 98.82% (501/507). Among structural variants, --SEA deletion was the most common, followed by -α3.7; the most prevalent point mutation was αCSα, followed by αWSα. Notably, the method additionally identified six previously undetected point mutations (Hb Batley, Hb Ozieri or Hb Allison Park, IVS-II-58, Hb Hekinan II, IVS-I-5, and Cap + 23 C > G) across 11 embryos that had not been identified by the control method (Supplementary Table S2, Figure S1). Although the clinical significance of these additional findings remains uncertain, they demonstrate the broader variant-detection capacity of tlrPGT-α-thal and suggest that this approach may reduce the need for family-specific assay design.

The direct detection rate fell short of 100% owing to amplification bias inherent to the WGA process [23], which may prevent direct detection of --SEA deletions in a subset of embryo samples. To mitigate the risk of false-negative --SEA results, prespecified supplementary gap-PCR was used as a confirmatory safeguard for embryos meeting all three criteria: haplotype linkage analysis was inconclusive, parental genotypes carried --SEA, and direct deletion detection in the embryo was negative. In this cohort, 15 embryos from 4 families underwent additional gap-PCR validation (Supplementary Table S1). The results of all validation experiments were successfully incorporated into the haplotype linkage analysis, ultimately enabling definitive genotype determination for these embryos.

Efficient proband-independent haplotype linkage analysis

In parallel with direct variant detection in blastocysts, tlrPGT-α-thal enabled efficient haplotype linkage analysis without requiring proband or grandparent samples. Compared with short-read sequencing, long-read sequencing offers a marked advantage in the identification of informative SNP sites. The informative SNPs used in conventional NGS-based methods are restricted to sites at which the carrier is heterozygous and the partner is homozygous; LRS additionally utilises sites where both parents are heterozygous, or where the carrier is homozygous and the partner is heterozygous. In brief, any site at which at least one of the four parental alleles differs from the remaining three can be incorporated into the linkage analysis. Consequently, within the 1-Mb flanking region surrounding the HBA locus, tlrPGT-α-thal identified significantly more informative SNPs per family than the NGS-based method (median 59, IQR 52–66 versus 43, IQR 32-54.5; paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, P < 0.001; Fig. 4), enabling the construction of robust chromosomal haplotypes using parental data alone, without recourse to proband or grandparent information.

Applying this haplotype linkage approach, 99% of families (101/102) in the primary concordance analysis successfully completed linkage analysis using parental data alone at the family level, with a per-embryo success rate of 99.61% (505/507). Only one family (F046) failed to establish effective linkage for 2 embryos, owing to complete concordance between the embryo genotype and parental genotypes (all --SEA/αα) and identical parental SNP haplotypes in the HBA gene region, which precluded determination of the linkage relationship (Figure S4). In this scenario, direct variant detection results served as an independent and reliable alternative diagnostic basis, enabling definitive genotyping of the affected embryos. Furthermore, a meiotic recombination event in one embryo (23PDP0331) from family F024 was precisely localised to the interval between the L2 linkage segment and the HBA gene region (Figure S5).

Clinical outcomes and prenatal diagnosis validation

Of the 102 families included in concordance analysis, 9 families did not proceed to embryo transfer owing to the absence of viable embryos or other clinical reasons. The remaining 93 families completed a total of 132 embryo transfer cycles, of which 35 families underwent two or more transfer attempts. Among all transfer cycles, 45 resulted in negative serum β-hCG, whereas 87 were β-hCG-positive, corresponding to a positive pregnancy test rate of 65.9% per transfer cycle. Among the β-hCG-positive pregnancies, 20 ended in pregnancy loss, including 1 case of ectopic pregnancy, the remaining 67 were confirmed as clinical pregnancies, corresponding to a clinical pregnancy rate of 50.8% per transfer cycle. Of these clinical pregnancies, 5 pregnancies were ongoing and had not yet reached the recommended gestational age for amniocentesis; 3 patients declined prenatal diagnosis; and 2 were lost to follow-up. Ultimately, 57 pregnancies underwent invasive prenatal diagnosis, and the results demonstrated 100% concordance with tlrPGT-α-thal findings. Among these 57 confirmed cases, 27 (47.4%) showed a completely normal genotype, 19 (33.3%) were classified as silent carriers, and 11 (19.3%) were identified as mild (trait) α-thalassaemia (Figure S6). A summary of embryo transfer outcomes, pregnancy outcomes, and prenatal diagnostic validation is provided in Table 2. The live birth rate was not calculated because not all pregnancies had reached delivery at the time of analysis, and complete delivery follow-up data were unavailable for some completed pregnancies.

Three of the six complex cases originated from the same family. In this pedigree, the absence of maternal grandparent samples precluded construction of the maternal haplotype; conventional linkage analysis could only confirm that the embryos did not carry paternal HBA mutations, while maternal mutation status remained unresolvable, rendering diagnosis inconclusive (Figure S7). By integrating direct variant detection with multi-segment haplotype linkage analysis, tlrPGT-α-thal successfully confirmed that all three embryos had a genotype of αα (Table 3, Figure S8). One of these embryos (23PDP0617) was transferred and subsequently underwent prenatal diagnosis, which confirmed a foetal genotype of αα/αα, thereby avoiding the wastage of viable embryos and ultimately enabling the family to achieve the birth of a healthy newborn.

The remaining three cases each came from a different family (Table 3). For embryo 23PDP0718, conventional variant verification suggested a genotype of αα/--SEA, whereas SNP linkage analysis indicated --SEA/--SEA — a clear inconsistency precluding reliable genotype determination, raising suspicion of maternal DNA contamination. The direct detection and linkage analysis results from tlrPGT-α-thal were fully concordant, confirming the embryo genotype as --SEA/--SEA. For embryo 23PDP0708, the absence of informative SNP sites rendered conventional linkage analysis infeasible; tlrPGT-α-thal identified 50 informative SNP sites for this embryo, successfully supporting linkage analysis and, in combination with direct detection results, ultimately confirmed the genotype as --SEA/αααanti3.7. For embryo 23PDP0488, an excessively high allele-dropout (ADO) rate caused conventional SNP linkage analysis to fail; using tlrPGT-α-thal, haplotype linkage was successfully established and the genotype was confirmed as αα/αα, providing the family with a more reliable basis for embryo selection.

Among the 20 embryos with HBA-region aneuploidies, the abnormality types comprised 4 chromosome 16 monosomies, 2 segmental HBA deletions, 11 chromosome 16 trisomies, 2 triploidies, and 1 maternal uniparental disomy (UPD) (Supplementary Table S3). Of these, 12 were successfully genotyped with parental origin determined, as exemplified by embryo 23PDP0337 (Figure S5). However, trisomies arising from meiosis II non-disjunction — in which two identical sister chromatids are inherited — produce a homozygous haplotype pattern indistinguishable from a normal single-chromosome contribution, precluding parental origin determination, as illustrated by embryo 23PDP0335 (Figure S5); 8 embryos therefore remained unresolved.

Discussion

In this prospective clinical cohort, we systematically evaluated the diagnostic performance and potential clinical utility of tlrPGT-α-thal for preimplantation genetic testing of α-thalassaemia. Overall, the findings support the reliability and clinical feasibility of this approach. tlrPGT-α-thal showed complete concordance with conventional NGS-based PGT-M among comparator-evaluable embryos, with further support from prenatal diagnostic confirmation. Importantly, the method integrates direct variant detection and haplotype linkage analysis within a single workflow, achieving high rates of direct variant detection and haplotype establishment without family-specific assay design or additional family members. This integrated strategy also provided additional diagnostic value in complex cases, including incomplete pedigrees and insufficient informative SNPs, where conventional workflows may be limited. Collectively, these results indicate that tlrPGT-α-thal is a diagnostically reliable and clinically practical strategy with potential advantages in workflow simplification, assay integration, and applicability to complex clinical scenarios.

Regarding diagnostic reliability, our findings provide a clinical evidence base for considering tlrPGT-α-thal as a practical PGT-M strategy for α-thalassaemia. The complete concordance observed among comparator-evaluable embryos should be interpreted as a high level of technical agreement with the current clinical comparator, rather than absolute analytical accuracy confirmed by an independent gold standard [24, 25]. Nevertheless, the prospective study design, blinded parallel comparison, and consistent prenatal diagnostic confirmation strengthen the evidence base for this approach compared with previous small-scale feasibility reports of long-read sequencing-based PGT [26]. The reliability of tlrPGT-α-thal is supported by several methodological features: a comprehensive assay design covering a broad spectrum of clinically relevant α-thalassaemia variants [20, 27], the ability of long-read sequencing to provide contiguous informative SNPs across the HBA region and the L1–L4 linkage segments for haplotype construction, and a dual-detection strategy that integrates direct variant detection with haplotype linkage analysis [14].

Second, the core value of tlrPGT-α-thal lies in its integration of conventional PGT-M procedures and its added diagnostic value in complex cases. Conventional PGT-M often requires family-specific assay design, additional family members for phasing, and multi-step testing across different platforms [8, 11, 28]. In contrast, tlrPGT-α-thal integrates direct detection of diverse α-thalassaemia variants with haplotype linkage analysis in a single workflow, using only parental and embryonic samples. This approach significantly simplifies and shortens the testing process, reducing the typical turnaround time from approximately 8 weeks to about 2 weeks. Furthermore, it enhances clinical feasibility in scenarios that are difficult to resolve using conventional PGT-M, including incomplete pedigrees, de novo parental variants, limited informative SNPs [16, 29], and potentially unreliable customised direct assays affected by maternal DNA contamination. In this cohort, tlrPGT-α-thal provided additional diagnostic clarification in six embryos, representing 1.1% of all tested embryos (6/533). This finding is clinically relevant because, in PGT, the interpretation of each embryo can directly influence embryo selection and transfer decisions.

Compared with conventional thalassaemia PGT-M, tlrPGT-α-thal avoids case-by-case assay customisation and reduces reliance on additional family members. Compared with strategies that use parental long-read sequencing for pre-cycle workup but retain short-read or conventional testing at the embryo level [15, 18], tlrPGT-α-thal applies long-read sequencing directly to embryonic WGA products. This enables embryo-level direct variant detection and haplotype linkage analysis within the same platform. As a targeted long-read sequencing strategy, tlrPGT-α-thal is also more focused on α-thalassaemia-associated variants and linked informative regions than long-read whole-genome sequencing, and may therefore offer advantages in cost, data interpretation, and clinical implementation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, WGA-related amplification bias remains an important technical constraint in embryo-based PGT [23] and may contribute to occasional failure of direct deletion detection, particularly for deletional α-thalassaemia, thereby necessitating supplementary confirmatory assays in selected cases. Second, for aneuploid embryos arising from meiosis II (MII) errors, the receipt of two identical sister chromatids results in a homozygous haplotype pattern that is indistinguishable from a normal single-chromosome contribution by linkage analysis alone. Third, although this prospective cohort included a relatively large number of families and embryos for PGT-M validation, it was conducted in a single-centre setting and did not cover all rare α-thalassaemia variants. Fourth, clinical follow-up was limited to transferred embryos with available prenatal or postnatal confirmation, and therefore could not independently verify all tested embryos. The live birth rate was not calculated because some pregnancies were ongoing and complete delivery data were unavailable at the time of analysis.

Conclusion

This study prospectively validated targeted long-read sequencing as an accurate and clinically practical strategy for PGT-M of α-thalassaemia. tlrPGT-α-thal integrates direct variant detection and haplotype linkage analysis within a single workflow, without requiring family-specific assay design or additional family members. The method showed complete concordance with the conventional comparator among evaluable embryos, was further supported by prenatal diagnostic confirmation, and provided additional diagnostic value in complex scenarios such as incomplete pedigrees and insufficient informative SNPs. These findings support tlrPGT-α-thal as a simplified and broadly applicable strategy for α-thalassaemia PGT, and provide a useful model for extending targeted long-read sequencing to PGT-M for other monogenic disorders.

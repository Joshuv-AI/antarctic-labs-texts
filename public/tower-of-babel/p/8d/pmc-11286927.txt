Stability analysis of a nonlinear malaria transmission epidemic model using an effective numerical scheme
Authors: Jian Jun He, Abeer Aljohani, Shahbaz Mustafa, Ali Shokri, Mohammad Mehdizadeh Khalsaraei, Herbert Mukalazi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Malaria is a fever condition that results from Plasmodium parasites, which are transferred to humans by the attacks of infected female Anopheles mosquitos. The deterministic compartmental model was examined using stability theory of differential equations. The reproduction number was obtained to be asymptotically stable conditions for the disease-free, and the endemic equilibria were determined. More so, the qualitatively evaluated model incorporates time-dependent variable controls which was aimed at reducing the proliferation of malaria disease. The reproduction number R \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left(o\right)$$\end{document}o was determined to be an asymptotically stable condition for disease free and endemic equilibria. In this paper, we used various schemes such as Runge–Kutta order 4 (RK-4) and non-standard finite difference (NSFD). All of the schemes produce different results, but the most appropriate scheme is NSFD. This is true for all step sizes. Various criteria are used in the NSFD scheme to assess the local and global stability of disease-free and endemic equilibrium points. The Routh–Hurwitz condition is used to validate the local stability and Lyapunov stability theorem is used to prove the global asymptotic stability. Global asymptotic stability is proven for the disease-free equilibrium when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${R}_{0}\le 1$$\end{document}R0≤1. The endemic equilibrium is investigated for stability when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${R}_{0}\ge 1$$\end{document}R0≥1. All of the aforementioned schemes and their effects are also numerically demonstrated. The comparative analysis demonstrates that NSFD is superior in every way for the analysis of deterministic epidemic models. The theoretical effects and numerical simulations provided in this text may be used to predict the spread of infectious diseases.

Introduction

Malaria is a potentially fatal vector-borne disease that is one of the most lethal infectious diseases in the world. Malaria is a potentially fatal disease caused by a parasite that infects a specific type of mosquito that feeds on humans1–4. Headache, fever, and vomiting are the most common symptoms. If the infected person does not receive treatment, the infection can progress and become fatal. The disease is caused by the protozoan Plasmodium and is spread to humans through the bite of an infected adult female Anopheles mosquito (the malaria vector)5,6. P. falciparum has been identified as the most dangerous to humans among all species6.

According to the most recent World Malaria Report, published in December 2019, there were 228 million cases of malaria in 2018, up from 231 million in 2017. The estimated number of malaria deaths in 2018 was 405,000, compared to 416,000 in 20177. The African Region of the WHO continues to bear a disproportionately large share of the global malaria burden. In 2018, the region had 93% of the malaria causes and 94% of the malaria deaths. Malaria is present in over 100 countries, with an estimated 216 million cases and 655,000 deaths in 20178. As cited by Ref.5, there is currently no perfect vaccine against malaria in humans (though collective global efforts to develop such a vaccine9–15 are underway). Dynamics involved comprehending the complex interaction between the parasite, the human host, and the vector (mosquito) that transmits the disease. Understanding malaria dynamics has significant for predicting the spread of disease. Identifying high-risk areas and tracking disease trends. Numerous studies have been conducted in the field of malaria mathematical modelling. Nita and Gupta16, modelled the basic of SEIR model and applied it to vector borne disease (malaria). They carried out the sensitivity analysis of the model using data from India.

According to their findings, the sensitivity analysis was critical, and it is the most sensitive aspect to be addressed in their model. The model of malaria transmission acquired immunity level was developed by Hyun et al.17. In Kenya, Otieno et al.18 investigated a mathematical model of malaria dynamics with four-time dependent control measures. The Influence of three control variable variables (vector-reduction, blood screening strategies and personal protection) on malaria dynamics with indirect and direct transmission was presented by Refs.19,20, while bed net and optimal vaccination control efforts in the population with diverse ranks of naturally acquired immunity were described by Refs.21–23.

Recently, Osman et al.24 investigated a nonlinear epidemic model for malaria disease transmission. The stability of the disease-free and endemic equilibria was thoroughly examined for the continuous model. The main purpose of this work is to test the validity and practicality of the model by using two widely known and useful finite difference schemes (RK-4 and NSFD). For the continuous model, the steadiness of disease-free and endemic equilibria was carefully investigated. The main goal of this work is to test the model’s validity and practicality using two well-known and useful finite difference schemes (RK-4 and NSFD). The findings in this paper demonstrate that the two aforementioned schemes provide a detailed description of the continuous model. The RK-4 scheme is incapable of precisely retaining the basic dynamical aspects of the differential models, resulting in numerical solutions that differ from the original system’s solutions. As a result, the idea of developing numerical schemes that do not focus on approximation issues but rather deal with specific dynamical information arose, leading to the development of qualitative dynamical numerical schemes. The idea of this was in fact, commenced by Mickens25. He used the term NSFD scheme to distinguish the new numerical scheme from the old SFD scheme. The NSFD scheme was introduced to compensate for the weaknesses of RK-4 scheme.

In this paper, we apply the SEIR model to malaria transmission between mosquitos and humans. In “Mathematical model” section, we describe the SEIR model and calculate the basic reproduction number. “Points of equilibrium and the fundamental biological number” section discusses model analysis, which includes the stability analysis of disease free and endemic equilibria. We used a nonlinear mathematical framework used the RK-4 scheme in “RK-4 method” section. In “Non-standard finite difference (NSFD) scheme” section, we look at how the advanced NSFD scheme can be used to solve a variety of problems. The Schur–Cohn criterion is used to discuss the local stability of a disease-free equilibrium point. The Routh–Hurwitz criterion, on the other hand, is used to verify the local stability of the endemic equilibrium point in the NSFD scheme. The Lyapunov function’s properties are used to investigate the global stability of disease-free and endemic equilibria. In each section, numerical simulations are provided to describe our logical findings. The conclusions are delivered in the final segment to summaries the entire paper.

Conclusion

We presented the models in this paper using a deterministic system of ordinary differential equations. We found that when the associated reproduction numbers are less than one, our models are locally asymptotically stable, but unstable when they are greater than one. In the future, we will incorporate an optimal control strategy for malaria control and bifurcation analysis into this model. The RK-4 and NSFD schemes are used to discuss the dynamical properties of DFE and DEE points, including their local and global stabilities. The results show that the NSFD scheme overcomes all of the shortcomings of the RK-4 scheme while also providing reliable numerical solutions. The convergence demonstrates that the NSFD scheme retains its stability and positivity properties. The key advantages of this method are demonstrated both theoretically and numerically, revealing that this scheme has good dynamical behavior even with large time step sizes. This is a significant implementation and computational benefit. At the same time, because the RK-4 scheme cannot precisely maintain the fundamental properties of the original continues model, it can produce numerical solutions that are not exactly the same as the original model's solutions. As a result, the NSFD scheme is superior in every way in terms of producing outcomes that benefit society as well as medical science.

In our future study, we intend to investigate additional generalized epidemic models with characteristics comparable to the one under consideration in order to gain a deeper understanding of disease transmission dynamics. In more future, we will include optimal control approach to control the spread of malaria and bifurcation analysis in this model.

Determinants of antenatal care and skilled birth attendance utilization in Bangladesh: evidence from the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey
Authors: Sarmin Aktar, Papia Sultana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMaternal health services, including antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA), are crucial for improving maternal and child health outcomes. Despite global efforts, many women in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), including Bangladesh, do not have adequate access to these services. This study examines the factors associated with ANC and SBA utilization among childbearing women in Bangladesh, using data from the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS).MethodsThis study used a cross-sectional design with data from BDHS 2022 and analyzed 5,018 childbearing women. The main outcomes were the utilization of antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA). A multilevel logistic regression model was used to assess factors associated with these outcomes.ResultsThe results revealed that women with secondary/higher education (AOR = 2.06, 95% CI: 1.43–2.98), those in the richest quintile (AOR = 3.14, 95% CI: 2.38–4.13), those living in urban areas (AOR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.27–1.94), those making healthcare decisions alone (AOR = 1.37, 95% CI: 1.08–1.74), those with one child (AOR = 2.16, 95% CI: 1.55-3.00), those reading newspapers weekly (AOR = 2.01, 95% CI: 1.29–3.14), and those watching TV weekly (AOR = 1.43, 95% CI: 1.23–1.67) had higher odds of ANC uptake. Additionally, women whose partners had secondary/higher education (AOR = 1.46, 95% CI: 1.17–1.83), who were in the richest quintile (AOR = 4.82, 95% CI: 3.47–6.68), who had one child (AOR = 3.18, 95% CI: 2.28–4.42), who were not working (AOR = 1.39, 95% CI: 1.18–1.64), and who watched TV weekly (AOR = 1.41, 95% CI: 1.20–1.67) were more likely to use SBAs.ConclusionWealth quintile, parity, and media exposure were strongly associated with antenatal care and skilled birth attendance utilization in Bangladesh. The findings highlight the need to design community-based interventions on expanding access to maternal health services for women with poorer households, higher parity, lower education, and those residing in disadvantaged areas. Mass media and digital platforms should be leveraged to improve awareness and encourage early and adequate ANC visits and skilled deliveries.Keywords: Antenatal care, Delivery care, Skilled birth attendance, Maternal health services, Reproductive health, Maternal healthcare

Background

Maternal mortality remains a major global health concern. The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that 800 women die daily due to preventable pregnancy and childbirth-related diseases, and 95% of those deaths happen in low- and middle-income nations [1, 2]. Despite global progress, inequities persist across and within regions. Maternal health services, especially antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA) are needed to lower maternal and neonatal mortality. Globally, approximately 86% of pregnant women receive at least one ANC visit and 65% of them have the recommended four or more visits. Similarly, approximately 84% of births are attended to by trained health staff, even though the coverage is far lower in South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa [1, 2]. According to Ethiopia Demographic and Health Survey 2019, a minority of 58.7% of women had four or more ANC visits and about half of them had gone to a skilled birth, indicating that access gaps remained evident in sub-Saharan Africa [3]. In South Asia, the India National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-21), 58.6% of women received four or more visits of antenatal care (ANC) and 88.6% were attended by skilled health personnel during births, but significant disparities exist between all the groups of the wealthy, educated, and residing populations [4]. The 2022 Nepal Demographic and Health Survey found that 81% of women had four or more ANC visits to their recent live births and that approximately 80% of deliveries had been attended by a skilled provider, with lower-coverage rates in the population groups of the rural and the disadvantaged [5]. Bangladesh has made notable progress in maternal health. The maternal mortality rate has been declining since 2017, with 183 per 100,000 live births with 123 in 2017 and 2020, respectively [6]. Nevertheless, there are difficulties in the utilization of services. The latest BDHS 2022 indicates that the proportion of women who had at least four or more visits of the ANC was about 41–47%, and about 69% of births were attended by a skilled provider [7, 8].

ANC and SBA are essential for both maternal and fetal health. ANC enables continuous monitoring of pregnancy, detecting complications early, and providing preventive interventions such as nutritional counseling, anemia and hypertension supervision, tetanus immunization, and preparation for birth [9]. In order to decrease maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, SBA provides presence of trained professionals during delivery to address intrapartum challenges that include obstructed labor, postpartum hemorrhage, infections, or neonatal asphyxia [9].

Previous studies, relying predominantly on earlier BDHS data (2014 and 2017-18), and similar low-and-middle-income nations (LMICs) have found that there are several determinants of ANC and SBA use. Higher uptake is always predicted by socioeconomic status specially household wealth and maternal education [10–15]. The education of the partner has also been indicated to affect the utilization of SBA, wherein the male partners are usually play a key role in making healthcare decisions [12, 16]. The parity is important and the first time mothers were the ones that were more likely to seek ANC and SBA when compared to women with higher birth orders [10, 13]. Place of residence is strongly associated with use, as urban women have better access to healthcare services than their rural counterparts [17, 18]. ANC and SBA utilization has also been positively associated with mass media exposure, such as through the newspapers or television, and much of this has been in terms of increased awareness [18–20].

Programmatic initiatives have tried to overcome such barriers. As an example, the Focused Antenatal Care (FANC) model, which is recommended by the WHO, assumes four visits that are comprehensive and individualized. In Bangladesh, FANC was tested in the selected rural districts and proved to be better in ANC uptake, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care. Its effectiveness, however, was not extensively effective due to poor infrastructure and sociocultural barriers [21].

In spite of these contributions, important gaps in knowledge still remain. Most of the prior studies carried out in Bangladesh are founded on the older BDHS data (2014 and 2017-18) and, therefore, may not reflect the current developments in the utilization of maternal health services such as changes that have taken place during the post-COVID 19 period. In addition, previous researchers have also tended to concentrate on descriptive relationships without properly analyzing enduring socioeconomic and demographic disparities within antenatal care and skilled birth attendance with more recent nationally representative dataset. The accessibility to BDHS 2022 is an important opportunity to present current evidence on usage tendencies and existing gaps in maternal health care access in Bangladesh. Therefore, this study aims to examine determinants of ANC and SBA utilization among childbearing women in Bangladesh using the most recent BDHS 2022 data. The results would inform specific initiatives and policies to decrease inequities and enhance maternal and child health in Bangladesh.

Methods

Data source

This study used data from the Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS) 2022, which is part of the global MEASURE DHS Program. The BDHS employed a stratified, multi-stage cluster sampling design to provide nationally representative estimates of health indicators. Figure 1 illustrates the sampling procedure and inclusion of eligible women. In the first stage, enumeration areas (n = 675) were selected, and in the second stage, households (n = 30,375) were systematically sampled. The final analytical sample for this study comprised 5,018 women aged 15–49 years who had given birth and received ANC. Women who did not meet these inclusion criteria were excluded (n = 35). The large sample size, combined with the nationally representative sampling design, provided sufficient statistical power to detect meaningful differences across socio-demographic subgroups. The BDHS 2022 achieved a household response rate of 99% and an individual woman’s response rate of 98%, ensuring high data quality [7, 8].

Study variables

Outcome variables

In this study, utilization of antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA) were used as outcome variables. Women who had given birth were asked about the number of antenatal visits they attended during their most recent pregnancy (variable: m14_1). Based on the World Health Organization’s (WHO) recommendation of at least four visits, responses were coded as follows: fewer than four visits = 0, and four or more visits = 1. Women with fewer than four visits were classified as having inadequate ANC utilization, while those with four or more visits were considered to have adequate ANC utilization [9].

Additionally, questions were asked about whether qualified healthcare providers such as doctors, nurses, midwives, or certified community health workers assisted women during childbirth (variables: m3a_1-m3n_1). Women who reported being attended by these professionals were classified as having “skilled birth attendance” and coded as 1. Conversely, women attended by parents, friends, neighbors, or traditional birth attendants were classified as having unskilled birth attendance and coded as 0.

Independent variables

Based on previous studies, the following independent variables were selected: age (v013: 15–24, 25–34, 35 + years), mother’s education level (v016: no education, primary, secondary/higher), partner’s education level (v701: no education, primary, secondary/higher), wealth quintile (v190: poorest, poorer, middle, richer, richest), pregnancy intention (v367a: planned, unplanned, unintended), religion (v130: Islam, Hindu, others), marital status (v501: married, others), place of residence (v025: urban, rural), parity (v201: one, two, three, four or more births), employment status (v717: working, not working), healthcare decision-making (v743a: alone vs. not alone), frequency of reading newspapers or magazines (v157: not at all, less than once a week, at least once a week), frequency of listening to the radio (v158: not at all, less than once a week, at least once a week), frequency of watching television (v159: not at all, less than once a week, at least once a week), and sex of the household head (v151: male, female). Pregnancy intention was defined according to DHS terminology as planned (wanted at the time of conception), unplanned (not planned then, wanted later), and unintended (not wanted at all) (Table 1).

Data analysis

We accounted for the complex survey design of BDHS 2022 by applying sampling weights (v005), primary sampling units (v021), and strata (v023), ensuring nationally representative estimates. Weighted descriptive statistics were first generated, and bivariate associations between independent variables and the outcomes (ANC and SBA utilization) were examined using chi-square tests. Variables significant at p < 0.05 in the bivariate analyses were included in multilevel logistic regression models. Given the hierarchical nature of BDHS data, a multi-level logistic regression model was employed, with individual-level variables (age, maternal education, partner’s education, parity, wealth quintile, employment status, pregnancy intention, marital status, sex of household head, healthcare decision-making, and media exposure) specified at Level 1, and community-level variables (place of residence and division) specified at Level 2. Random intercepts were used to capture unobserved community-level variation, and intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) were calculated to assess the proportion of variance attributable to clustering, justifying the multilevel approach. Results are reported as crude odds ratios (CORs) and adjusted odds ratios (AORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Multi-collinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with no evidence of serious collinearity (mean VIF = 1.33). All analyses were conducted using Stata/MP version 17.0.

Results

Socio-demographic characteristics and maternal health services utilization

Table 1 presents the associations between socio-demographic factors and the utilization of antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA) services among women in Bangladesh. Overall, 39.6% of women received ANC, with 69.3% of those attended by skilled health personnel. ANC utilization was lower among younger women aged 15–24 years (37.3%) than among older women. Urban women had notably higher rates of both ANC (53.8%) and SBA (79.5%) than their rural counterparts did (34.5% and 65.5%, respectively). Education level played a significant role, with ANC and SBA rates of 45% and 76%, respectively, among women with higher education and 48.7% and 80.1%, respectively, among women whose partners had higher education. Wealthier women also demonstrated greater utilization, with ANC and SBA rates of 66.4% and 90.1%, respectively. Women with planned pregnancies presented higher ANC (41%) and SBA (71.4%) rates. Compared with nonworking women, employment status further influenced service utilization, with working women showing higher ANC (39.9%) and SBA (71.9%) rates. Parity also affected usage, with women who had only one birth having higher ANC (43.4%) and SBA (78.2%) rates. Decision-making autonomy in healthcare was another contributing factor, as women who made their own healthcare decisions had higher ANC (47.4%) and SBA (70.2%) rates. Additionally, women frequently exposed to mass media, such as newspapers or television, had greater ANC and SBA utilization rates.

Factors of ANC utilization

Table 2 presents antenatal care (ANC) utilization predictors among childbearing women in Bangladesh. Younger women aged 15–24 years were significantly less likely to utilize ANC services than were those aged 35 years and older (AOR = 0.58, 95% CI: 0.42–0.79). Educational attainment was positively associated with ANC utilization; women with secondary or higher education were more likely to utilize ANC services (AOR = 2.06, 95% CI: 1.43–2.98), as were women whose partners had attained secondary or higher education (AOR = 1.21, 95% CI: 0.96–1.51). Wealth status emerged as a strong predictor, with women in the richest wealth quintile exhibiting higher odds of ANC uptake than those in the poorest quintile (AOR = 3.14, 95% CI: 2.38–4.13). Additionally, women who made healthcare decisions alone were more likely to utilize ANC services (AOR = 1.37, 95% CI: 1.08–1.74). Media exposure also played a significant role; women who read newspapers or magazines at least once a week had higher odds of ANC utilization (AOR = 2.01, 95% CI: 1.29–3.14), as did those who watched television at least once a week (AOR = 1.43, 95% CI: 1.23–1.67), than did those with no exposure to these media. These findings indicate that younger age, lower education levels, lower wealth status, limited decision-making autonomy, and lack of media exposure are associated with reduced ANC utilization among women in Bangladesh.

Factors of SBA utilization

As indicated in Table 3, several factors were positively associated with skilled birth attendance (SBA) utilization among women in Bangladesh. Women whose partners had completed secondary or higher education were more likely to use SBA services (AOR = 1.46, 95% CI: 1.17–1.83). The wealth status was a strong predictor with the women in the richest quintile having the highest likelihood of using SBA services compared to the poorest quintile (AOR = 4.82, 95% CI: 3.47–6.68). The employment status also associated with SBA utilization; nonworking women were more likely to use SBA (AOR = 1.39, 95% CI: 1.18–1.64) than their working counterparts. The parity affected the use of SBA where women with one birth were more likely to use SBA services (AOR = 3.18, 95% CI: 2.28–4.42) in comparison to women with four or more births. Another important factor was media exposure, women who watched television at least once a week were more likely to utilize SBA (AOR = 1.41, 95% CI: 1.20–1.67). The findings indicate that maternal and partner education, wealth status, type of residence, parity, and media exposure was significantly associated with more utilization of SBA.

Discussion

This study evaluated the relevant variables of ANC and SBA use among childbearing women in Bangladesh based on the latest nationwide available data of BDHS 2022. The findings show that maternal and partner education, wealth status, type of residence, parity, media exposure, and women’s autonomy had significant positive association with higher use of both ANC and SBA services.

The prevalence of adequate ANC (≥ 4 visits) in this study was 39%, and SBA utilization was 69%. Although these numbers have improved over BDHS 2014 (37% ANC and 50% SBA) and BDHS 2017-18 (37% ANC and 62% SBA) [7, 8], they are still lower than global averages (65% ANC and 84% SBA) and their neighbors, such as India, where ANC4 + coverage stands at 58% and SBA use is more than 83% [1, 2]. These inequalities are indicative of long-standing disparities of access to maternal healthcare in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), and this is a similar trend in sub-Saharan Africa [17].

Inequality in the use of maternal healthcare based on age was observed. Even after adjusting socioeconomic and demographic factors, women of younger age (15–24 years) were less likely than older women to use adequate antenatal care. This observation suggests that the adolescent and young mothers generate a high risk and vulnerable population in Bangladesh. There is evidence that teenage pregnancy has been linked to complications in the maternal and neonatal stages, particularly in low-and middle-income nations [22, 23]. Previous study in Bangladesh and other South Asian contexts has always revealed that teens are less inclined towards timely antenatal and capable birth care than aged women mainly because of socioeconomic and cultural limitations [24, 25]. Women in their young age are usually exposed to various obstacles, which block access to maternal healthcare assistance, such as being uninformed about the risks related to pregnancy, social stigma against early pregnancy, lack of healthcare decision-making autonomy, and access to youth-friendly reproductive health services [26, 27].

The study also found a significant association between education and the utilization of ANC and SBA. Educated women (secondary and higher education) were more likely to use maternal health services, which is in line with the findings of Ethiopia, Nepal, and India where education enhances awareness of risk to health, financial capacity, and independence in making health-related decisions [11, 14, 17]. A similarly significant impact was education of partners on SBA utilization, which echoed results in Bangladesh and other LMICs, where men tend to be the main decision makers in household resources and health care choices [12, 16].

It was found that the ANC and SBA services are strongly associated with the use of these services by wealthier quintiles. Women in the highest wealth quintile were significantly more likely to access maternal healthcare services than those in the lowest wealth quintile. Women in the richer and richest quintiles were found to be much more likely to receive ANC and SBA, which was in line with the prior studies in Bangladesh [15, 28] and sub-Saharan Africa [17] as well. Wealthier households experience fewer financial constraints, have access to transportation, and are more likely to access private or urban health facilities.

Parity had a negative association with ANC and SBA, and women with higher birth order were less likely to use services. It is consistent with research in Bangladesh and rural Nepal, which indicates that multiparous women might experience fewer risks, or may put household duties over care-seeking [13, 14], though evidence from Ghana shows a different trend, highlighting the importance of context [29].

Place of residence was also found a strong predictor of ANC utilization in our study with urban women having higher odds of using ANC services as compared to women in rural areas. This result is in line with existing investigations in Bangladesh [18] and can be supported by a more in-depth study of 63 low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where a higher ANC usage level was observed among urban women in almost all countries [17]. The urban areas usually have greater access to healthcare, such as closer access to services, concentration of skilled healthcare providers, and improved transportation. Also urban women might be exposed to health-related information and outreach programs more, which leads to the promotion of awareness and use of maternal healthcare services.

Autonomy in healthcare decision-making was a very important predictor of ANC use. Women who independently made healthcare decisions were more likely to use ANC services than were those who did not. Other researchers have found related findings in Ethiopia and Ghana, where the level of decision-making among women was found to be a effective predictor of ANC utilization [30, 31]. This result highlights the importance of empowering women so that they can enhance the use of maternal healthcare. These results underscore the importance of interventions that can support the achievement of gender equity and empower women to engage in the health-related decision-making process actively to maximize maternal healthcare access and outcomes.

The employment status was found to be an important predictor of SBA utilization, with nonworking women showing higher odds of SBA uptake compared to their counterparts. This finding is consistent with those of previous studies, which reported a positive association between employment and SBA utilization [28]. It has been suggested that an explanation is that women who do not work might have more time and space to attend to their health and find quality care during delivery. On the contrary, working women, especially those in informal or low-paying jobs, might be affected by obstacles like time constraints, the absence of maternity leave, and workplace necessities which might hamper their access to SBA services. Nevertheless, there is inconsistency in evidence, and some studies find that employed women are more likely to use SBA services [32]. The difference may be explained by situational influences, including the variation in the character of employment, the socioeconomic position, or the healthcare delivery in the study environments.

The mass media exposure, such as reading newspapers or magazines, listening to the radio, and watching television were positively associated with ANC and SBA utilization. The awareness of maternal health services and benefits was higher among women regularly exposed to newspapers, radio, or television, as was the case in Bangladesh and other LMICs [20, 28]. Of all these, the most popular and influential media is television, especially in the urban and rural areas, owing to the wide coverage and its application in the government-sponsored health awareness programs. The fact that media exposure and utilization have a positive relationship with the use of antenatal care and skilled birth attendance highlights the continued significance of mass media, specifically television, as an effective means of raising awareness and encouraging maternal healthcare use in Bangladesh, while radio and newspapers have more limited coverage and declining use, especially among younger and less educated women.

Combined, these results highlight that ANC and SBA inequalities are caused by structural, socioeconomic, and informational factors. To deal with these factors, policies to address the disadvantaged groups and regions are necessary.

Strengths and limitations

The present study has a number of strengths. High generalizability of the findings to childbearing women throughout the country is provided by the use of data of the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS), which has a very high response rate, standardized and validated questionnaires, and follows a nationally representative sampling design. Unequal selection probabilities were addressed by using survey weights, and the bias of clustering at the community level was addressed by using a multilevel approach. Together, they enhance the internal validity of the findings and make them more generalizable to national levels.

Nonetheless, this research is also limited. The cross-sectional nature of the Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS) is one of the biggest restrictions, as it will not allow establishing the cause and effect between the explanatory variables and the utilization of antenatal care and skilled birth attendance. This consequently implies that the relationships used in this research can be regarded as non-causal and correlational. Moreover, the study was founded on self-reports which could have had recall and reporting biasness. However, some methodological steps were noted to minimize the chances of biasness. Lastly, despite the inclusion of various sociodemographic variables, the variables that could not be measured, including distance to health facilities or services quality, were not included in the dataset, and could have affected utilization patterns.

Conclusion

Women with secondary or higher education, those in wealthier quintiles, those with one child, and those with regular media exposure were more likely to utilize antenatal care (ANC) and skilled birth attendance (SBA) services in Bangladesh. These findings indicate important sociodemographic patterns of maternal healthcare utilization. However, given the study’s cross-sectional design, the observed associations cannot be interpreted as causal. These findings indicate that community-based interventions aimed at education, media outreach, and economic and sociocultural barriers reduction can be used to increase the use of ANC and SBA services, especially among the disadvantaged and rural populations. Increasing women autonomy in healthcare decision-making and solving barriers to employment, can also promote increased access to and outcomes in maternal healthcare. There is a need to focus on the health of teenage and young mothers. Increase of youth-friendly reproductive and maternal health services can help in enabling timely and appropriate care among young women. Qualitative studies must be done in the future to embrace the socio-cultural barriers.

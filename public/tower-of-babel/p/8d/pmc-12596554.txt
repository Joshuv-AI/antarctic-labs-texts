SVSC-14 Adolescent and Young Adult Brain Tumor Survivorship in Africa: A Call to Action from a Survivor-Advocate
Authors: Boluwatife Aofolaju, Oluwakemi Atolagbe, Korede Akindele
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBrain tumor survivors in adolescence and young adulthood (AYA) often face lifelong neurocognitive, emotional, and social challenges. In sub-Saharan Africa, these challenges are compounded by limited survivorship infrastructure and societal stigma. This abstract aims to amplify survivor-led advocacy as a model for informing policy and programming for AYA brain tumor care, using a personal journey as a lens to highlight gaps and opportunities in survivorship support. This narrative inquiry draws on lived experience as a brain tumor survivor and qualitative insights from engagement with fellow AYA survivors through the Cancer Champions program of The Dorcas Cancer Foundation (TDCF). Structured reflections, peer interviews, and thematic mapping were used to identify survivor priorities and actionable areas for advocacy and intervention. Key survivor priorities include: (1) Access to long-term neurocognitive, endocrine, physical and psychological care; (2) Clear and structured survivorship planning, (3) Inclusion in decision-making around their post-treatment survivorship planning; and (4) Opportunities for education, reintegration, and peer support. Programs such as The Back to school Campaign, The Cancer Champions Program and the Action4Kids programs provide support to, as well as involve AYA survivors in advocacy activities while enabling peer support and creating avenues for psychosocial support. Survivor advocates are called upon to utilize their voice to highlight gaps in access, the need for AYA - specialized protocols, professionals and infrastructure, and the urgent need for structured AYA cancer care and survivorship programs in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA). Healthcare systems, institutions and non-profit organizations in SSA are called upon to enable and empower survivor advocates as collaborators in the development of AYA-specific protocols that prioritize AYAs and AYA-unique issues.

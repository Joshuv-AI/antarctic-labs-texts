Robotic gastrointestinal surgery using the Weigao surgical robot system: a single-center prospective analysis
Authors: Zhixuan Jiang, Xinxin Yang, Weiteng Zhang, Zhigui Zuo, Jun Cheng, Shaotang Li, Pengfei Wang, Wenyi Wu, Weijian Sun, Qiantong Dong, Xian Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The Weigao surgical robot system (WG-NST600S) was successfully developed in China. In this study, we presented our single-center experience and the short-term outcomes of gastrointestinal surgeries performed using the WG-NST600S system.Materials and Methods:Between April 2024 and February 2025, consecutive gastrointestinal surgeries were performed using the WG-NST600S system at the participating institution. Clinical characteristics, as well as surgical and short-term postoperative outcomes, were prospectively collected and analyzed.Results:One hundred and six patients were enrolled in the study. Of these, 9 underwent radical gastrectomy, 16 partial gastrectomy, 58 radical resections for rectal cancer, 7 sigmoidectomy, 5 left hemicolectomy, and 11 right hemicolectomy. The median operative times were as follows: 278 min for radical gastrectomy, 137 min for partial gastrectomy, 245.5 min for radical resection of rectal cancer, 242 min for sigmoidectomy, 290 min for left hemicolectomy, and 311 min for right hemicolectomy. The blood loss was 20 mL for radical gastrectomy, 10 mL for partial gastrectomy, 20 mL for radical resection of rectal cancer, 20 mL for sigmoidectomy, 25 mL for left hemicolectomy, and 20 mL for right hemicolectomy. Postoperative complications were 22.22% for radical gastrectomy, 6.25% for partial gastrectomy, 18.97% for radical resection of rectal cancer, 42.86% for sigmoidectomy, 0% for left hemicolectomy, and 27.27% for right hemicolectomy. All procedures were successfully completed without conversion to open surgery or other unplanned interventions.Conclusion:Based on our single-center experience, the WG-NST600S system is a feasible, safe, and effective option for most gastrointestinal surgeries.

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of minimally invasive surgical techniques, robot-assisted surgical systems are redefining the landscape of modern surgery[1]. Compared with conventional laparoscopic surgery, robot-assisted laparoscopic systems offer several advantages, including three-dimensional high-definition visualization, improved wrist-like instrument articulation, tremor filtration and motion stabilization, better ergonomics, and a shorter learning curve. These features extend and augment the surgeon’s eyes and hands, enabling precise surgical manoeuvres in confined anatomical spaces[2,3].

In 2000, the da Vinci Si robotic system became the first robot-assisted laparoscopic platform approved by the Food and Drug Administration for clinical use[4]. From the first to fourth generations, this system has been widely adopted worldwide across various specialties including urology[5], gynecology[6], general surgery[7], neurosurgery[8], and others. In addition to the da Vinci Si robotic system, several other robotic surgical platforms have been developed, such as Medtronic’s MazorX[9], Senhance Surgical Robot[10], CMR Surgical’s Versius system[11], and Germany’s Dexter system[12].

The WG-NST600S system (WeiGao Group Co. Ltd., Shandong, China) is a newly developed surgical robot from China. Approved by China’s NMPA in October 2021 as the first domestically produced laparoscopic robotic system. We conducted this study to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of the WG-NST600S system for most gastrointestinal surgical procedures based on our single-center experience.

Material and methods

Patient selection

The present work has been reported in line with the STROCSS criteria[13].This study was a single-center, prospective, single-arm clinical trial conducted at the participating institution. Between April 2024 and February 2025. We assessed the WG-NST600S system used at our centre to determine its feasibility, safety, and efficacy in various gastrointestinal surgical procedures, including radical gastrectomy, partial gastrectomy, radical resection of rectal cancer, sigmoidectomy, left hemicolectomy, and right hemicolectomy. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the participating institution.

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Aged between 18 and 80 years, regardless of gender; (2) scheduled to undergo minimally invasive gastric surgery for gastric cancer, gastrointestinal stromal tumors [GIST], among others or colorectal surgery for colon cancer, rectal cancer, among others, with the following specific requirements: (i) Patients with gastric cancer: Primary gastric cancer at stage I/II/III; (ii) Patients with GIST: No distant metastasis; (iii) Patients with colorectal cancer: Primary colorectal cancer at stage I/II/III; (3)Willing to participate after fully understanding the trial’s potential benefits and risks, and capable of providing written informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Severe cardiac, pulmonary, cerebral, hepatic, or renal diseases that contraindicate surgery or anaesthesia; (2) inability to tolerate pneumoperitoneum; (3) severe coagulation dysfunction; (4) active tuberculosis; (5) uncontrolled severe systemic diseases or acute infections; (6) extensive and severe intra-abdominal adhesions (as assessed by the investigator) that may prevent safe establishment of pneumoperitoneum; (7) physical exhaustion, massive ascites, internal hemorrhage, or shock; (8) gastric cancer with lymph node metastases encasing major blood vessels; (9) colorectal cancer with obstruction and significant abdominal distension; (10) extensive intra-abdominal or pelvic tumour metastases; (11) emergency surgery due to tumor-related acute obstruction, perforation, or massive bleeding; (12) tumor perforation complicated by acute peritonitis; (13) pregnancy or lactation; (14) history of epilepsy, psychiatric disorders, or cognitive impairment; (15) any other conditions deemed unsuitable for participation by the investigator. All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrolment.

Data collection

Prospectively collected clinical data included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), preoperative hemoglobin, preoperative albumin, tumor location and pathological category. Tumor staging was assessed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer-Union International Cancer Control (AJCC-UICC) TNM classification system (8th edition)[14]. Surgical and short-term postoperative outcomes included surgical success rate, conversion rate, operative time, blood loss, time to first flatus, time to first full flow diet, time to first half flow diet, postoperative hemoglobin, postoperative albumin, postoperative complications, length of postoperative hospital stay, hospital stay, hospitalization expenses and readmission within 30 days. Definition of indicators

Surgical success was defined as the completion of the planned procedure without conversion to laparotomy or any alternative surgical approaches. The surgical success rate was calculated as (number of subjects who successfully completed the planned surgery/total number of subjects who underwent surgery) × 100.

Operative time was defined as the duration from the surgeon’s first instrumental manoeuvre to the completion of instrumental manipulation. Postoperative hospital stay was calculated as the total number of inpatient days from the day of surgery to discharge. Hospital stay was calculated as the total number of inpatient days from the day of admission to discharge. For participants admitted in the afternoon or discharged in the morning, the respective days were recorded as 0.5 days.

Structural design

The WG-NST600S system consists of a surgeon console (Fig. 1A), a three-arm robotic operation cart (Fig. 1B), and a vision cart (Fig. 1C). All procedures were performed by two experienced surgeons, both of whom had received standardiszed training on the WeiGao surgical robot system, and had rich surgical experience and Da Vinci robotic surgical system experience. Surgical procedure

All surgical procedures were performed using the WG-NST600S system. The schematic of surgical puncture site for gastrectomy (Supplementary Digital Content Figure 1A, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/E905) and colorectal surgery (Supplementary Digital Content Figure 1B, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/E905). In radical gastrectomy, dissection began with the greater omentum and the anterior leaf of the transverse mesocolon. The infrapyloric region was then cleared by identifying and ligating the right gastroepiploic vein (Fig. 2A) and the artery at the roots, followed by lymph node dissection. The left gastric artery was ligated along the course of the common hepatic artery toward the celiac trunk (Fig. 2B), followed by clearance of the lymph nodes around the splenic artery (Fig. 2C). Finally, digestive tract reconstruction (Billroth II or Roux-en-Y) was performed through a 7-cm auxiliary incision for anastomosis.Figure 2.Procedure of radical gastrectomy. For partial gastrectomy of benign gastric tumors, such as the gastrointestinal stromal tumors, an ultrasonic scalpel was longitudinally incised into the seromuscular layer around the tumor for complete enucleation (Fig. 3A). Subsequently, the gastric wall was closed with full-thickness suturing using a robotic needle holder (Fig. 3B). Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as the mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed variables, and as medians and ranges for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies (n) and percentages (%). All analyses were conducted using SPSS software (Version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Sixteen patients (15.09%) had NRS2002 scores ≥3. Among those who underwent gastric surgery, nine received radical gastrectomy and 16 underwent partial gastrectomy. The following cases were found in the benign tumour group: 13 GISTs, 1 gastric ulcer, 1 gastric submucosal lipoma, and 1 gastric heterotopic pancreas. For colorectal surgery, there were 58 radical resections for rectal cancer, 7 sigmoidectomies, 5 left hemicolectomies, and 11 right hemicolectomies. Surgical and postoperative outcomes of enrolled patients

The median time to first postoperative flatus was 5 (range: 3–6) days for radical gastrectomy, 2 (range: 1–4) days for partial gastrectomy, 3 (range: 0–6) days for radical resection of rectal cancer, 3 (range: 0–7) days for sigmoidectomy, 3 (range: 2–5) days for left hemicolectomy, and 4 (range: 2–5) days for right hemicolectomy. Postoperative albumin levels were: 32.1 (range: 25.2–35.5) g/L for radical gastrectomy, 34.55 (range: 31.2–38.6) g/L for partial gastrectomy, 33.85 (range: 24.3–41.2) g/L for radical resection of rectal cancer, 34.7 (range: 27.4–37.4) g/L for sigmoidectomy, 35.3 (range: 30.8–37.7) g/L for left hemicolectomy and 32.7 (range: 28.6–43.6) g/L for right hemicolectomy. Perioperative complications and managements of the enrolled patients

Overall, 25 gastric and 81 intestinal surgeries were performed. For gastric surgery, the median operative time was 168 (range: 82–346) min, median blood loss was 15 (range: 2–50) mL, time to first postoperative flatus was 3 (range: 1–6) days, and the hospital stay was 10 (range: 5–38) days. Postoperative complications were observed in three patients (12.00%). For intestinal surgery, the median operative time was 255 (range: 121–635) min, median blood loss was 20 (range: 5–50) mL, time to first flatus was 3 (range: 0–7) days, and the hospital stay was 13 (range: 8–33) days. Postoperative complications were observed in 19 cases (23.46%). Discussion

The WG-NST600S system is a recently developed surgical robotic system from China. The WG-NST600S system has the following advantages.[1]:Through hardware filtering, software algorithm and intelligent control, hand jitter is filtered, so that the surgeon can move more accurately in the operation such as free and block blood vessels, and improve the safety of the operation[2]. The diameter of the instrument rod is 8 mm, which is suitable for the operation in the narrow space of the abdominal cavity and the tricky position[3]. The three-arm robotic operation cart adopted a folding arm design to occupy a smaller position, which is suitable for compact operating room layout. Our single-center surgical and short-term postoperative outcomes showed the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of the WG-NST600S system for various gastrointestinal procedures.

In our study, radical gastrectomy using the WG-NST600S system had a median operative time of 278 (range: 218–346) min, blood loss of 20 (range: 5–50) mL, and a postoperative complication rate of 20.22%, with no complications of Clavien-Dindo grade ≥III. A meta-analysis of 988 patients with gastric cancer showed da Vinci system outcomes with a median operative time of 327 (range: 297–358) min, blood loss of 99 (range: 96–103) mL, and a postoperative complication rate of 19.9 (range: 10.2–32.0)%[16]. Another study of 17 712 patients across 40 retrospective studies showed da Vinci-assisted gastrectomy with an average operative time of 267.34 min and blood loss of 98.77 mL[17]. A Japanese study evaluating the Da Vinci XI surgical robotic system reported a median operative time of 297 minutes for robotic-assisted gastrectomy in gastric cancer, with a postoperative complication rate of 8.2% and estimated blood loss of 25 mL (range: 5–2540 mL)[18]. Our WG-NST600S system results show non-inferior operative time and significantly reduced blood loss compared with that of the da Vinci system, confirming its feasibility and safety for gastric cancer surgery. For partial gastrectomy of benign diseases, our median operative time of 137 (range: 82–196) min was significantly shorter than the 245 (range: 150–540) min reported by Vicente et al[19]. for the da Vinci system. Additionally, the hospital stay was comparable (9 (range: 5–12) days vs. 10.5 (range: 6–24) days), suggesting equivalent feasibility.

Our colorectal surgery cohort included patients who underwent rectal, sigmoid, left, and right hemicolectomies. Crippa et al[20]. reported rectal cancer outcomes of 324 min median operative time and 37.2% postoperative complications using the da Vinci system, compared with our 245.5 min and 18.97%, respectively, using the WG-NST600S system. Peng et al[21] reported sigmoidectomy outcomes of 240 (range: 120–435) min operative time, 50 (range: 20-300) mL blood loss, and a hospital stay of 7 (range: 4–12) days using the da Vinci system, all comparable to outcomes found using the WG-NST600S system. Solis-Pazmino et al[22]. reported da Vinci colectomy times of 209.9 min (left) and 186.4 min (right) with 92 mL/102 mL blood loss, respectively, suggesting that the WG-NST600S system may be slightly inferior for colon cancer. Due to the limitation of patient enrollment, the number of patients undergoing surgery is small in some cases, such as left hemicolectomies or sigmoidectomy. Therefore, further large cohort studies are needed to verify the conclusions. Overall, the WG-NST600S is a feasible, safe, and effective option for colorectal surgery. As a fully domestically developed system, the WG-NST600S system has shown significantly lower hospitalization and maintenance costs than the da Vinci Si robotic system did.

Our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the presence of a surgeon learning curve for this new technology resulted in variable operative performance. This is evidenced by the wide range of operative times observed in our data. Additionally, surgeon initially reported suboptimal controllability and stability during the early adoption phase. However, these technical challenges were resolved as surgical experience with the system increased, demonstrating the expected learning curve associated with new robotic platforms. Second, a single-center, single-arm design lacking da Vinci randomization was used, and large-scale randomized controlled trials comparing conventional laparoscopy and the da Vinci surgical system are necessary to further evaluate this system. Last, the assessment was limited to short-term outcomes without long-term oncological or functional follow-up.

In conclusion, our single-center experience shows that the WG-NST600S system is a feasible, safe, and effective option for gastrointestinal surgery. However, multicenter randomized trials with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up periods are needed for further validation.

Conclusion

The WG-NST600S system was effective, safe, and feasible for the majority of gastrointestinal surgeries. Further studies in other surgical specialities are needed to comprehensively evaluate the clinical value of the WG-NST600S.

Energy characteristics of the molten selenium-tellurium system
Authors: Bagdaulet Kenzhaliyev, Sergey Trebukhov, Valeriy Volodin, Alina Nitsenko, Xeniya Linnik, Yerzhan Ospanov, Alexandr Shakhalov
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The energy functions of the components were determined based on additional studies intended to determine the values of total pressure and vapor composition over liquid solutions of the selenium-tellurium system: entropy, enthalpy, Gibbs energy, and heat capacity change in the existence field of alloys in the state diagram. The liquid-vapor phase transition was calculated to determine the upper boundary of existence of melts - their boiling point. The boiling point of selenium-tellurium liquid solutions corresponds to the dependence: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{boiling} [^\circ C] = 478x_{Se}^{3} - 764x_{Se}^{2} - 31x_{Se} + 989$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x}_{Se}$$\end{document} – atomic fraction of selenium in the alloy. The formation of azeotropic mixtures was found when the pressure was lowered less than 67.08 kPa (0.66 atm.) in the system and its absence of at atmospheric pressure. The line connecting the points of azeotropic mixtures corresponds to a linear relationship: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{azeotropes} [^\circ C] = - 2285.7x_{Se} + 945.42$$\end{document}. The energy characteristics of the molten system can be used for thermal engineering and technological calculations when the selenium- and tellurium-based products are obtained. The shape of the boundaries of the melt and vapor coexistence fields at atmospheric pressure and in vacuum, as well as the presence of azeotropic mixtures, indicate the impossibility of direct distillation separation of the system into elements.

Introduction

The attention of researchers to the molten selenium-tellurium system is due to the difficulty of distillation separation of elements1–4.

The formation of a continuous series of solid solutions in the entire concentration range and unlimited solubility of components in the liquid state have been established in the selenium-tellurium system5–7.

Physical and chemical studies of solid and liquid solutions were devoted to a significant number of works due to the difficulty to separate the elements.

The study8 is dedicated to the X-ray examination of solid solutions in the selenium-tellurium system, driven by the anomalous change in the electrical properties of alloys containing up to 2 at. % Se. In addition to changes in lattice parameters within the concentration range of 0.1–75.0 at. % Se, the author noted extrema on the curves with unclear origin, as well as the absence of long-range atomic order. It was also concluded that the alloy is not a simple substitutional or interstitial solution; its formation process is of a complex nature.

Diffractometric studies of the structure of liquid selenium-tellurium alloys affecting the characteristics of the vapor phase were performed in studies9,10.

It was found in study9 that each atom has two neighboring atoms at a distance of 0.24 nm at temperatures of 250, 300, and 460 °C, and the melt is made of helical chains. A model of melts was proposed based on measurements at 420–670 °C10. The atomic chains in some nodes in it were connected by jumpers with a certain structure and with increase of the number of atoms with increase in temperature. This is the cause of anomalies in the temperature dependence of electronic and thermodynamic properties.

The study of molecular dynamics of liquid selenium and tellurium, as well as their alloys, using the TDPAC (time differential perturbed angular correlation) method was performed by the authors11.

The issues of hydrodynamics, weight transfer and diffusion processes were dealt with by researchers12–14. The authors of12 found during the study of the rectification of selenium-tellurium melts that weight transfer is controlled by the liquid phase, and the molecular diffusion coefficient in the vapor phase is six orders of magnitude higher than in the condensed phase during the purification of selenium from tellurium. Diffusion is one of the properties sensitive to the peculiarities of the structure of solutions and the form of existence of components in them. In13, the authors suggested during the study of selenium-tellurium melts by capillary method that the selenium impurity in liquid tellurium is present in the form of “quasi-molecules” TenSe2 that can rotate around their axis and transfer selenium atoms to other tellurium atoms. Later, the same authors14 found that the effective selenium transfer heat changes sign twice at 600 and 630 °C, and its value reaches approximately 48.4 kJ/g-at. The unusual behavior of the transfer heat is explained by the presence of several forms of selenium existence in the melt. It is assumed that one of the forms, having negative heat of transfer, is stable only in a narrow temperature range (600–630 °C), which is the reason for the double inversion.

Due to the fact that the electrical properties are closely related to the structure of the near-order arrangement of atoms that takes place in liquids - melts, and the structure of the liquid affects the composition of the vapor phase, it is interesting to study the electrophysical properties of the liquid system selenium - tellurium. One of the first studies devoted to the study of electrical conductivity and viscosity of Se-Te melts was paper15 where the correspondence between the dependence of resistivity and viscosity on composition was shown. It is explained in terms of the dynamics of changes in the structure of the near-order in the system (dissociation of chains and packing density). The increase in electrical conductivity and decrease in viscosity of tellurium caused by small additions (about 2 %) of selenium are explained by the ratio between the bonds of atoms along the chain and between neighboring chains.

The authors of paper16 showed that with increase in tellurium concentration in the Se-Te liquid system there is a gradual transition from a typical liquid semiconductor to a liquid “bad” metal. In paper17 phase separation of alloys containing less than 4 at. % Te at temperatures below 610 °C is described in the process of studying the electronic properties of selenium-rich selenium-tellurium liquid solutions.

A considerable number of publications are devoted to the study of selenium-tellurium system vapor-liquid equilibrium that is due to the difficulty of distillation separation of elements.

The study of liquid-vapor equilibrium in the selenium-tellurium system was performed by the authors of paper18in order to find out the possibility of their separation by rectification at atmospheric and low pressures. Experiments were performed in the concentration range 2–99 wt. % tellurium at pressures of 6.7, 12, 20, 33.3, 60, and 101.3 kPa. Sampling was performed to determine the vapor phase composition at boiling temperatures in the form of solid condensate, assuming that the composition of the liquid phase does not change. Selenium and tellurium contents in the condensate were determined by the weight method. As a result, the liquid-vapor phase transition was constructed for each pressure, and the absence of azeotropic mixture was established. Its presence (at 2 % Te in the alloy) was indicated by Japanese researchers - Sato T. and Kaneko H.19. Relative volatility coefficients were determined for selenium as the ratio of concentration in vapor to concentration in liquid phase, for tellurium - as the ratio of concentration in liquid phase to concentration in vapor. Low values of the relative volatility coefficients are explained by the formation of chemical compounds between selenium and tellurium in the liquid and vapor phases.

The author of the paper20 determined the vapor pressure of selenium for solid alloys containing 22.0, 49.0, 69.7, 85.2, and 94.5 at. % Se on a specially designed unit allowing to obtain a continuous dependence of vapor pressure on temperature, using the radioactive isotope 75Se, and presumptive data on the composition of vapor selenium by mass spectrometric method with radioactive indication were obtained. Se, Se2, Se4, Se6, and Se8 ions were detected in the vapor.

The average molecular composition of vaporized selenium was determined and found to be 443 according to the ratio of the number of ions of different molecules. It agrees with the molecular weight for six atoms. An anomalously strong negative deviation from the law of ideal solutions is stated. The formation of selenium molecules with tellurium is assumed and the impossibility of purification of tellurium from selenium by vacuum distillation is found.

The results of the study of vapor-liquid equilibrium for selenium-impurity melts are given in the study21. There the selenium-tellurium, selenium-sulfur, selenium-sulfur, selenium-mercury, selenium-arsenic systems were studied by the circulation method, and the relative volatility coefficients of the impurity element were determined.

The average value of the separation coefficient was found to be 2.62 for selenium melts containing (1.1–2.0)·10−2 wt. % tellurium. The use of the rectification process was recognized as promising one based on it. It was further confirmed by the authors22,23 in the production of ultra-pure elements.

The author of paper24 studied the liquid-vapor phase equilibrium in the tellurium-selenium system at low selenium concentrations using the ebulliometric method and established that the separation coefficient decreases with decrease in pressure. The system exhibits a negative deviation from Raoult’s law. According to the researcher, it is associated with the formation of mixed selenium-tellurium molecules.

In a later study25, the thermodynamic properties of selenium-tellurium alloys were determined using the method of measuring the electromotive forces of concentration chains in melts containing 15–78 at. % Te within the temperature range from 723 to 880 K. The authors determined the partial and integral thermodynamic constants of the elements and the system as a whole. At the same time, the concentration dependence of tellurium activity in the alloys showed a positive deviation from ideality, whereas a sign reversal of the deviation from ideality for selenium was observed at the level of 60 at. % tellurium.

The determination of the thermodynamic constants of the selenium-tellurium system based on vapor pressure values is complicated due to the necessity to account for molecular weight that varies due to the diversity of composition and the set of associates. The vapor phase may contain such molecules as Se2, Se4, Se6, Se8, Te, Te2, Te4, and TeSe, with the set varying depending on temperature and alloy composition26,27.

In this regard, studies have appeared aiming to develop a method intended to calculate thermodynamic activity in melts that are in equilibrium with a vapor phase of complex composition28, as well as thermodynamic evaluation of the system based on an optimized dataset for the Se–Te system29, allowing for the reconciliation of thermodynamic functions and the phase diagram with experimental data.

Later, the authors of paper30 contributed to the modeling of the thermodynamic properties of liquid selenium - tellurium solutions by deriving an equation for the Gibbs free energy of mixing of two isomorphic pure elements forming a disordered substitutional solution, with a correction for the excess volume specific to the studied system. It was shown that considering the contributions of excess volumes significantly improves the agreement between calculated thermodynamic mixing functions and the experimental values for selenium-tellurium melts.

One of the latest studies31–33 calculated the liquid-vapor phase transition at atmospheric pressure and in vacuum based on the values of the partial pressure of saturated vapor for the components. Moreover, in32, the entropy and enthalpy of alloy formation were determined under the assumption that the heat capacity of the melts is independent of temperature.

Analyzing the conducted research and the results presented therein, we concluded that there are certain contradictions and inconsistencies in the interpretation of early results regarding vapor pressure values, thermodynamic activity, and a lack of data on energetic parameters.

The purpose of this study was to refine the values of partial and integral energy functions, including the determination of heat capacity, based on additional data on the saturated vapor pressure values of the system’s components.

The unit consists of a retort made of two parts: the lower part, placed in an electric furnace with automatic temperature control, and the upper part, made of quartz glass. A crucible containing the alloy sample is installed on a hollow suspension inside the retort. A platinum-platinum-rhodium thermocouple junction is placed inside the suspension, at the level of the melt in the crucible.

The suspension is supported by a weight-loss measurement system. It is located in the upper part of the retort. The parts of the retort are joined using a rubber seal, positioned outside the high-temperature zone. The lower and upper parts of the retort are separated by screens to reduce the heat flux from the high-temperature zone. The pressure measurement system, gas evacuation channels, argon filling inlets, and exits for the thermocouple wires are located in the upper part of the retort. The weight-loss, pressure, and temperature measurement systems have signal outputs to a multi-point potentiometer, with measurement recordings on a chart tape.

The procedure for the experiments was as follows. A sample of the alloy (up to 2 g) was placed in a crucible that was then installed on the suspension inside the retort. The retort was evacuated twice using a vacuum pump and then filled with argon. After this, the lower part of the retort was placed in the isothermal zone of a preheated electric furnace. The retort was heated under an overpressure of 2–5 kPa with an open inert gas supply system to suppress the evaporation of components and compensate for the pressure increase in the retort due to gas expansion during heating. Once the sample reached the target temperature, argon evacuation from the retort volume was initiated while maintaining a constant sample temperature (isothermal mode). At the same time, the weight loss of the sample (∆m) and the pressure change (P) were recorded synchronously. The pressure at which a sharp increase in the evaporation rate (weight loss) was observed was considered equal to the total vapor pressure.

Energy Characteristics Determination: The energy characteristics were determined for the region of liquid solutions – melts of the selenium-tellurium system, bounded below by the liquidus line and above by the boiling temperature line. The phase transition boundaries for the melt-vapor transition were calculated to determine the latter.

Due to the absence of the boiling process in liquid melts caused by the high density of their components, the boiling temperature was considered equal to the temperature at which the sum of the partial vapor pressures of the system components, in accordance with Dalton’s law, equaled to atmospheric pressure (101.3 kPa) or another pressure corresponding to vacuum technology conditions. Thus, temperature-concentration dependencies of the partial vapor pressures of elements and compounds were required for phase boundary calculations.

Additionally, when the liquid – vapor (L–V) phase transition was constructed on the phase diagram, the appearance of an azeotropic liquid was observed with decreasing pressure. It can be seen during examination of the position of the boundaries in the coexisting liquid and gas fields at atmospheric pressure and in a vacuum that an inseparably boiling liquid is present at:6.0 kPa, 11.2 at. % at 687 °C and 18.2 at. % at 522 °C in a vacuum of 2.67 and 0.13 kPa (respectively). This confirms the results of the study18 on the preference of the rectification process for selenium purification, where the melt will be separated into Se and an azeotropic mixture.

A decrease in pressure (boiling temperatures of solutions) shifts the azeotrope composition toward higher selenium concentrations. This is consistent with Vrevsky’s third law: when the temperature of a solution changes, if the vapor pressure curve has a minimum, the composition of the gas and the composition of the azeotropic mixture change in opposite directions. The line connecting the azeotropic mixture points follows a linear dependence:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{azeotropes} [^\circ C] = - 2285.7x_{Se} + 945.42$$\end{document}. Extrapolating this line to its intersection with the ordinate axis, i.e., xSe = 0, xTe = 1, corresponds to a temperature of ~945 °C. The vapor pressure of tellurium at this temperature is 67.08 kPa. It means that azeotropic mixtures appear when the pressure drops below 67.08 kPa (0.66 atm).

As the pressure decreases, the (L+V) phase fields on the selenium side of the phase diagram narrow in temperature. It negatively affects the degree of element separation during selenium refining, as the number of “evaporation-condensation” cycles increases. The deterioration in selenium-tellurium separation quality with decreasing pressure was noted by the author of paper24 who studied the liquid-vapor equilibrium of this system using the ebulliometric method, as well as by other researchers who explained this phenomenon by the worsening of heat and weight transfer. The technological difficulties of separating the selenium-tellurium system and refining selenium from tellurium are due to the narrow temperature range of these fields and their significant tilt from the tellurium ordinate to the selenium ordinate, especially at atmospheric pressure. The gas phase will be enriched in selenium; however, several distillation and condensation cycles will be required, which is characteristic of the rectification process.

It can be seen during analyzes of the data presented in Tables 3 and 4 that the formation of alloys in the field of liquid solutions is accompanied by heat release – the enthalpy of mixing is negative, meaning the process is exothermic.

The change in entropy of mixing decreases with increase in temperature and transitions into the region of negative values, indicating a certain ordering of the alloy particles. In9 it was established that the melt is formed from helical chains of atoms. In10 it was established that atomic chains in some nodes are connected by bridges with the development of a structure in which the number of atoms increases with increasing temperature, i.e. a certain ordering of atoms is observed. This is the reason for the anomalous temperature dependence of thermodynamic properties.

Conclusion

Thus, the energetic characteristics of the extensive property during the formation of liquid alloys within their existence field in the phase diagram have been calculated based on additional studies to determine the values of the total and partial vapor pressures of the components over liquid solutions in the selenium - tellurium system. The energy characteristics of the molten system can be used for thermal engineering and technological calculations in obtaining products based on selenium and tellurium.

The phase transition boundaries of liquid - vapor at atmospheric pressure and the presence of azeotropic mixtures at reduced pressure indicate the impossibility of distillation separation of the melts into elements.

Passivation performance of Nb microalloyed rebar in concrete carbonation environments with different pH
Authors: Zeyun Zeng, Shangjun Gu, Jie Wang, Fulong Wei, Xiang Xie, Zhiying Li, Hui Yang, Changrong Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, XPS sputtering depth, SEM and electrochemical tests (CV, EIS, M-S, i-t, DPP) were used to study the structural composition and formation mechanism of surface passive film of Nb microalloyed rebar in SCPS with different pH. The results showed that after passivation for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, compared with CS rebar, the stability and compactness of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar gradually increased with the decreases of pH. Firstly, with the decreases of pH, the outer layer of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar was composed of Fe oxides and Fe hydroxides, and the inner layer was composed of Fe oxides and Nb oxides, thus increasing the mass ratio of Fe3+/Fe2+ and Nb2O5/(NbO2 + NbO). Secondly, with the decreases of pH, the addition of Nb promoted the formation of Fe oxides in 34Nb rebar, obtaining the excellent Rct, Ecorr and Ep. Finally, with the decreases of pH, the addition of Nb promoted the enrichment of Nb oxides in 34Nb rebar, inhibiting the degradation of Fe oxides, and the passive film exhibited the P-N type semiconductor, thus significantly decreasing the carrier density and enhancing the passivation rate of 34Nb rebar.

Introduction

Reinforced concrete structure is one of the important structures with supporting skeleton in infrastructure construction, and the main core material is Nb microalloyed high-strength anti-seismic rebar, which is widely used in major construction projects such as construction engineering, bridge engineering and marine engineering1–3. Since the reinforced concrete structure is a highly alkaline concrete environment, the stable passive film will be formed on the surface of rebar when the rebar is in service in the concrete, so that the reinforced concrete structure has long-term stability4–7. With the development of reinforced concrete structures, many concrete structures will be carbonized or corroded in harsh working environments, resulting in corrosion of rebars and failure of concrete structures8–11. However, from the long-term stability development of reinforced concrete structures, how to design the long-term stability and corrosion resistance of reinforced concrete structures, the core issue is the long-term passivation performance of high-strength anti-seismic rebar. Therefore, it is of great strategic significance to develop the microalloyed high-strength anti-seismic rebar with high performance and high corrosion resistance in the development of long-term stability and durability of reinforced concrete structures.

As is well known, the passivation performance of microalloyed high-strength anti-seismic rebar is mainly affected by the mineral composition of concrete and the content of microalloying elements, in which the synergistic effect of concrete composition and microalloying elements has a great influence12–15. There are also reports on the influence of microalloying elements and concrete components on the structural stability and corrosion resistance of surface passive film of microalloyed rebar. In simulated concrete pore solution (SCPS), the microalloying elements (such as Cr, Ni, etc.) have a great influence on the formation and growth of surface passive film of microalloyed rebar. Firstly, with the decreases of pH, the addition of microalloying elements enhanced the stability of the passivation layer structure of microalloyed steel, and the outer layer of the passive film was mainly composed of Fe oxides and Fe hydroxides, and the inner layer of the passive film was mainly composed of Fe oxides and microalloying element oxides16–19. Secondly, with the decreases of pH, the microalloying element oxides were enriched in the inner layer of surface passive film of microalloyed steel, which inhibited the degradation of Fe oxides and enhanced the passivation rate of microalloyed steel20–23. Zhang et al., Shi et al., Zou et al., Liu et al. studied the passivation characteristics of Cr microalloyed rebar in simulated concrete carbonation environment, and indicating that with the decreases of pH, the addition of Cr promoted the formation of Cr oxides and Fe oxides in the passive film of Cr microalloyed rebar and inhibited the dissolution of Fe oxides, thus enhancing the corrosion resistance of Cr microalloyed rebar24–27. Wang et al. studied the passivation properties of stainless rebar in simulated concrete environments, and indicating that the decreases of pH led to the gradual decrease of Cr and Mo in the passive layer of stainless rebar, which reduced the Fe3+/Fe2+, O2−/OH−, corrosion potential and oxygen absorption potential, thereby reducing the stability of the passive film28,29. Ai et al. compared the passivation characteristics of CS rebar and Cr10Mo1 rebar in simulated concrete carbonation environment, and indicating that Cr10Mo1 rebar had higher passivation rate than CS rebar after passivation for 10 d in concrete, which was attributed to the formation of stable Cr oxides on the surface of Cr10Mo1 rebar, which inhibited the degradation of Fe oxides and reduced the carrier density of the passive film30,31. Liu et al. systematically studied the passivation evolution of low-carbon steel in concrete environments, and indicating that the high pH led to the rapid formation of the dense oxide film of low-carbon steel, thus obtaining the excellent passivation performance32. Zhang et al. studied the passivation characteristics of Cr-modified rebar and predicted the durability by Monte Carlo Simulation, indicating that when the Cr content increased to 11 wt%, the passive film was formed on the surface of Cr-modified rebar, and the chloride critical value was 6 ~ 14 times higher than that of carbon rebar, and the Cr11 rebar had a service life of 50 years in concrete containing Cl− ion33.

However, in the simulated concrete carbonation environment, the microalloying element Nb has an important influence on the improvement of surface passivation performance of microalloyed rebar. The effect of Nb element on the surface passivation characteristics of microalloyed rebar has also been reported. Previous studies pointed out that in simulated alkaline solution, the addition of Nb formed the Nb oxides (NbO and NbO2) in the oxide film of alloy materials, and the decreases of pH led to the further transformation of unstable Nb oxides into stable Nb oxides (Nb2O5), which enhanced the corrosion resistance of alloy materials34–36. Liu et al. studied the corrosion resistance of Nb microalloyed HRB500 rebar in simulated carbonation solution, and indicating that the increases of Nb improved the stability of passivation layer of HRB500 rebars37. Orjuela et al. studied the effect of NbC coating on the corrosion resistance of AISI 1045 low-alloy steel in 3.0 w.t% NaCl solution, and indicating that the increases of NbC coating thickness promoted the corrosion resistance of AISI 1045 low-alloy steel38. Barroux et al. studied the effect of Nb on the passivation behavior of 17-4PH martensitic stainless steel, and indicating that the surface passive film of 17-4PH martensitic stainless steel was mainly composed of Fe oxides and Cr oxides, while the Nb oxides was mainly formed in the inner layer of the passive film, which enhanced the stability of the passive film39. Ji et al. studied the effect of Nb on the corrosion resistance of Ti-Zr alloy, and indicating that with the increases of Nb content, the Nb oxides (such as NbO, NbO2, Nb2O5, etc.) were gradually formed in the passive film of Ti-Zr alloy, which enhanced the corrosion resistance of Ti-Zr alloy40. Guan et al. studied the passivation behavior of high-purity Nb plate in acidic solution, and indicating that with the increases of pH, more stable Nb oxides (Nb2O5) was formed in the passive film of high-purity Nb plate, which led to the decrease of point defect density of the passive film41. Therefore, the above research mainly focuses on the effect of Nb on the surface passivation behavior of microalloyed rebar, but lacks the action law and formation theory of Nb in the surface passivation process of microalloyed rebar and the evolution mechanism of the passive film layer, thus it is necessary to study the effect of Nb on the passivation evolution mechanism of microalloyed rebar.

In short, in the concrete carbonation environment, the corrosion resistance of microalloyed rebar mainly depends on the compactness and stability of surface passive film of microalloyed rebar, and the passivation performance of microalloyed rebar is mainly affected by concrete environment and microalloying element composition. Although a large number of studies have been reported on the exploration of surface passive film of microalloyed rebar, but there is a lack of theoretical research on the effect of Nb element on the passivation performance of microalloyed rebar in concrete carbonation environment. Therefore, starting from the long-term stability of microalloyed rebar in concrete, the main purpose of this study is to explore the effect of Nb element on the structure and formation kinetics of surface passive film of microalloyed rebar in simulated carbonation environment, studying the synergistic effect of concrete carbonation environment and Nb element on the passivation behavior of microalloyed rebar, analyzing the formation law of the passive film and the role of Nb element in the passivation layer, revealing the development law of passivation behavior, and clarifying the passivation mechanism. This study hopes to provide a theoretical reference for solving the long-term stability of microalloyed rebar in concrete structures.

In this study, the structure, composition and electrochemical properties of surface passive film of Nb microalloyed rebar were studied by XPS sputtering depth, SEM, electrochemical test (such as CV, EIS, DPP, M-S, i-t, etc.) after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH, and exploring the effect of Nb on the formation process and corrosion resistance of surface passive film of rebar, and proposing the passivation mechanism. The results of this study will provide new insights to the design of microalloyed rebar with high corrosion resistance in concrete structures.

Experimental procedures

Material preparations

According to the new national standard GB/T 1499.2–2018 steel for reinforced concrete (Part 2: hot rolled ribbed rebar), the Nb microalloyed rebar was designed. Nb microalloyed rebar was a steel ingot with sizes of diameter (50 mm) × height (100 mm), which was melted by vacuum induction melting furnace, and the alloy material used was the ferroniobium alloy material (Nb66.78Fe33.22 alloy). The size of two rebar samples for SEM test and XPS test was 10 mm × 10 mm × 10 mm and 3 mm × 3 mm × 3 mm, respectively. These rebar samples were sealed by epoxy resin and exposed to the working surface. Different types of metallographic sandpaper (100 mesh ~ 2500 mesh) were used to coarsely grind and finely grind the exposed surface of these rebar samples, and the diamond polishing agent (particle size of 0.5 μm) and distilled water were used to polish the surface of these rebar samples on the PI-1 A metallographic sample polishing machine. Finally, the ethanol and ultrasonic were used to clean the surface of these rebar samples for subsequent passivation experiments and tests.

Simulated solutions

Due to the microalloyed rebar were in a passive state in a highly alkaline concrete environment, this passive state will be changed by carbonization and corrosion of the concrete environment, resulting in the failure of the concrete structure and destroying the authenticity of the reinforced concrete structure42–44. While, the concrete environment was mainly affected by the composition of minerals, the composition changes of these minerals led to the carbonization of the concrete environment, which degraded the passivation process of rebar. In this study, the simulated concrete pore solution (SCPS) was used to simulate the real concrete environment to study the passivation process of microalloyed rebar in concrete environment22–25,45–47. The main component of SCPS was a highly alkaline solution of 0.03 mol·L− 1 calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2) + 0.5 mol·L− 1 potassium hydroxide (KOH) + 0.2 mol·L− 1 sodium hydroxide (NaOH). In order to further simulate the carbonization process of SCPS, the different proportions of sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) powder were added to SCPS, and the pH values of SCPS were adjusted to 12.5,10.5 and 9.0, respectively, which mainly studied the passivation behavior of microalloyed rebar in SCPS with different pH. The main components of SCPS with different pH were given in Table 3. The reagents used in SCPS with different pH were analytically pure reagents (99.99% purity), and the solvent was distilled water with a resistivity of 18.2 MΩ·cm. The pH of SCPS was tested and corrected every 12 h. Therefore, the SCPS with different pH was used as the passivation experimental group, and the time of each passivation experiment was set to 3 d, 5 d, 7 d and 10 d, respectively. Three parallel experimental groups were taken in each passivation experiment to ensure the accuracy of the passivation experimental results.

Electrochemical tests

The electrochemical sample size of two rebars was 20 mm × 10 mm × 5 mm. The electrochemical sample of two rebars were welded together with the nickel sheet by spot welding machine as the connecting wire. The opposite surface of the connecting nickel sheet was the working surface, and the remaining surfaces were sealed completely by epoxy resin. Different types of metallographic sandpaper (100 mesh ~ 2500 mesh) were used to coarsely and finely grind the surface of the electrochemical sample of two rebars, then polishing on the PI-1 A metallographic sample polishing machine. Finally, the surface of the electrochemical sample of two rebars was cleaned with ethanol and ultrasonic, dried and sealed for subsequent electrochemical tests.

All the electrochemical tests of two rebars were carried out on the CHI760E electrochemical workstation. The electrochemical system used the classical three-electrode system, the two rebar samples were the working electrode, the platinum sheet was the auxiliary electrode, and the saturated calomel was the reference electrode. The solution of electrochemical test was SCPS with different pH, and the passivation experiment time was set to 3 d, 5 d, 7 d and 10 d. The two rebar samples were passivated for different times in SCPS with different pH, then different electrochemical tests were carried out. Firstly, the open circuit potential (OCP) test was carried out for 3600 s. After the OCP test was stable, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test was carried out, the test frequency range was 10− 2 Hz ~ 105 Hz and the amplitude was 10 mV, and the EIS data were fitted and analyzed by ZView software, which studied the corrosion resistance of surface passive film of two rebars. For the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curve test, the test voltage range was − 1.1 V ~ 0.9 V, the scanning rate was 0.008 V/s, the scanning direction was forward scanning, and the scanning period was five cycles, which studied the electrochemical reaction process and phase formation of surface passive film of two rebars. For the Mott-Schottky (M-S) curve test, the test voltage range was − 1.6 V ~ 1.6 V, the amplitude was 10 mV, and the fixed frequency was 1000 Hz, which studied the semiconductor properties of surface passive film of two rebars. For the chronopotentiometric current density (i-t) curve test, the chronopotentiometric current density scan was carried out for 1000 s at different film formation potentials, which studied the formation kinetics of surface passive film of two rebars. Finally, for the dynamic potential polarization curve (DPP) test, the test voltage range was − 0.5 V ~ 1.0 V, and the scanning rate was 0.5 mV/s, and the Tafer extrapolation method was used to fit and analyze the DPP curve, which studied the reaction process and corrosion tendency of surface passive film of two rebars. All electrochemical tests were carried out at room temperature, and three parallel tests were taken under each group of electrochemical tests to ensure the accuracy of each group electrochemical tests.

Characterization methods

After the two rebars were passivated for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, the morphology and elemental composition of surface passive film of two rebars were characterized by Hitachi Regulus 8100 field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM). The main parameters: the working voltage was 15 KV, the resolution was 0.05 nm, and the working distance was 5 mm.

After the two rebars were passivated for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, the phase composition and element valence state (such as Fe, O, Nb, etc.) of surface passive film of two rebars were characterized by Thermo ESCALAB 250 XI high-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and the argon ion deep etching of 2 nm and 6 nm were carried out to study the phase composition and element valence state of surface passive film of two rebars. Based on the binding energy of C peak (248.8 eV), the XPS data were fitted and analyzed by Avantage software.

Results and discussions

Analysis of passivation reaction peak

The cyclic voltammetry (CV) curve of two rebars after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH were given in Fig. 2.

It can be seen from Fig. 2(a)~(h) that the CV curve of two rebars showed three anode peaks (A1, A2, A3) and three cathode peaks (C1, C2, C3) after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH. In the forward scanning process, the A1 anode peak appeared at -0.9 V ~ 0.85 V, indicating that the Fe2+ ions (such as FeO, Fe(OH)2, etc.) were formed on the surface of two rebars17,44,48,49. The A2 anodic peak appeared at -0.8 V ~ 0.75 V, indicating that the Fe2+ ions were transformed into the Fe3+ ions (such as Fe2O3, Fe3O4, etc.) during the passivation reaction of two rebars, which indicated that the surface passive film of two rebars has been formed20,22,23,50. The A3 anode peak appeared at -0.6 V ~ 0.5 V, indicating that the stable Fe oxides and Fe hydroxyl oxides were formed on the surface of two rebars, which indicated that the passive film reached the stable state25,37,43,50. In the reverse scanning process, the appearance of C1 cathode peak was attributed to the formation of Ca compounds51, the appearance of C2 cathode peak was attributed to the reduction of Fe oxides44,50,52, and the appearance of C3 cathode peak was attributed to the reduction of Fe[3+ [22,37,49,50.

It can be seen from the CV curve in Fig. 2(a)~(h) that with the decreases of pH and the increases of passivation time, the current density of A2 anode peak and A3 anode peak in the two rebars significantly enhanced, and the C2 cathode peak and C3 cathode peak significantly reduced, which indicated that the surface passive film of two rebars formed the stable Fe oxides. After passivation for different times in SCPS with high pH13.2, the current density of A2 anode peak and A3 anode peak in the two rebars did not change significantly, which indicated that the formation of surface passive film of two rebars was unstable. After passivation for different times in SCPS with low pH9.0, the current density of A2 anode peak and A3 anode peak in the two rebars significantly enhanced, which indicated that the surface passive film of two rebars stably formed, and the width of passivation zone gradually widened. Compared with CS rebar, with the increases of passivation time, the A2 anode peak and A3 anode peak in 34Nb rebar obtained higher current density, and the passivation zone became wider, which indicated that the addition of Nb promoted the formation of more stable Fe oxides in 34Nb rebar and inhibited the degradation of Fe oxides, thus the stable passive film formed on the surface of 34Nb rebar.

Semiconductor properties of passive film

In order to further analyze the semiconductor properties of surface passive film of two rebars, the Mott-Schottky (M-S) Equation was used to evaluate the carrier density of surface passive film of two rebars. Where, C is the capacitance, E is the applied voltage, ε is the dielectric constant (the dielectric constant of Fe oxides is 1224,26,53, ε0 is the vacuum dielectric constant (ε0 = 8.85 × 10− 12 F·m− 1), e is the charge (e = 1.602 × 10− 19 C), ND and NA are the carrier density of donor and acceptor respectively, EFB is the flat band potential (EFB is the intercept of linear part of M-S curve), k is the Boltzmann constant (k = 1.38 × 10− 23 J·k− 1), T is the absolute temperature, S is the slope of linear part of M-S curve (when the slope is positive, the passive film is the N-type semiconductor; when the slope is negative, the passive film is the P-type semiconductor).

The M-S curve of surface passive film of two rebars after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH were given in Fig. 3. It can be seen from Fig. 3(a)~(h) that after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH, the surface passive film of CS rebar showed the N-type semiconductor with positive slope (zone I), while the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar showed the N-type semiconductor with positive slope (zone I) and P-type semiconductor with negative slope (zone II). Previous studies pointed out that the semiconductor properties of surface passive film of microalloyed steel were related to the structure and composition of the passive film, in which, the Fe oxides exhibited the N-type semiconductors, and the microalloying element (such as Cr, Nb, Ni, etc.) oxides exhibited the P-type semiconductors6,15,24,31. Figure 3(a)~(h) also illuminated that after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH, the surface passive film of CS rebar mainly contained the Fe oxides and showed the N-type semiconductors, while the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar mainly contained the Fe oxides and Nb oxides, and showed the N-type semiconductors and P-type semiconductors.

The carrier density (ND and NA) and flat band potential (EFB) obtained by M-S curve were given in Fig. 3. It can be seen from the fitting value in Fig. 3(i)~(l) that with the decreases of pH and the increases of passivation time, the flat band potential of surface passive film of two rebars gradually shifted positively, which indicated that the Fermi level of surface passive film of two rebars gradually increased, resulting in the gradual degradation of Fe oxides54,55. Previous studies pointed out that the conductive resistance of surface passive film of microalloyed steel depended on the carrier density of the passive film, and the lower the carrier density of the passive film, the stronger the conductive resistance of the passive film21,24,26,55. The fitting values in Fig. 3(i)~(l) showed that with the decreases of pH and the increases of passivation time, the ND value and NA value of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar were significantly lower than that of CS rebar, which indicated that the point defect density of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar greatly reduced and the compactness of the passive film enhanced. After passivation for 10 d in SCPS with low pH 9.0, the ND value of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar was about 3 ~ 4 times lower than that of CS rebar in SCPS with pH13.2, and the NA value of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar was about 2 times lower than that of 34Nb rebar in SCPS with pH13.2, this indicated that compared with CS rebar, the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar had the lower ND value and NA value, resulting in the stronger conductive resistance of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar, thus the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar obtained the excellent protection.

According to the “PDM” theory of passive film56, the point defects were mainly distributed in the entire passivation layer of microalloyed steel, which was mainly composed of cation vacancies and anion vacancies, so that the surface passive film of microalloyed steel exhibited the semiconductor behavior. It can be seen from the fitting values Fig. 3(i)~(l) that with the decreases of pH and the increases of passivation time, the ND value and NA value of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar were significantly lower than that of CS rebar, which was attributed to the stable existence of Fe oxides in 34Nb rebar, resulting in the significant reduction of cation vacancies in the passive film, which enhanced the compactness of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar.

It can be seen from the Nyquist in Fig. 4(a)~(d) that after passivation for different times in SCPS with different pH, the surface passive film of two rebars showed a similar semi-circular capacitive arc curve, and with the decreases of pH and the increases of passivation time, the radius of the capacitive arc of surface passive film of two rebars gradually increased. Compared with CS rebar, the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar showed a better semi-circular capacitive arc curve, which indicated that the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar had better stability. In SCPS with low pH9.0, due to the carbonization of SCPS, the stable Fe oxides (such as Fe2O3, Fe3O4, etc.) in the surface passive film of CS rebar gradually degraded at the initial stage of passivation (see Fig. 14(i) and (j)), which reduced the passivation rate of CS rebar and deteriorated the stability of the passive film. With the increases of passivation time, the stable Fe oxides on the surface of CS rebar will undergo a small amount of degradation (see Fig. 14(k) and (l)), and the dissolution rate of the passive film will decrease, thus the protection of the passive film gradually decreased. Compared with CS rebar, the dissolution rate of Fe oxides in the surface passivation film of 34Nb rebar decreased at the initial stage of passivation, and the formation of initial Nb oxides led to an increase in the stability of the passive film (see Fig. 14(m) and (n)). With the increases of passivation time, more stable Nb oxides (Nb2O5) were formed on the surface of 34Nb rebar (see Fig. 14(o) and (p)), the presence of these Nb oxides enhanced the self-healing ability of the passive film, inhibited the degradation of stable Fe oxides, and improved the passivation rate of 34Nb rebar, thus enhancing the corrosion resistance of 34Nb rebar.

According to the analysis of the passivation mechanism, the addition of Nb has a beneficial effect on the improvement of the stability and compactness of the passive film on the surface of rebar. After passivation for 10 d in SCPS, compared with CS rebar, with the decreases of pH, the addition of Nb promoted the formation of more stable Nb oxides in the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar, inhibited the dissolution of Fe oxides, and improved the mass ratio of Fe3+/Fe2+ and Nb2O5/(NbO + NbO2), the maximum impedance value and the corrosion potential, thus enhancing the passivation rate of 34Nb rebar. Therefore, in the carbonation environment of reinforced concrete, the design of Nb microalloyed rebar provides a new method for the long-term stability of reinforced concrete structures.

Conclusions

In this study, XPS sputtering depth and SEM were used to analyze the morphology and composition of surface passive film of two rebars and the electrochemical tests (such as CV, EIS, M-S, i-t, DPP, etc.) were used to analyze the formation process of surface passive film of two rebars in SCPS with different pH, and the corresponding passivation mechanism was proposed. The main key conclusions of this study summarized as follows.

(1) After passivation for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, compared with CS rebar, with the decreases of pH, the outer layer of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar were gradually composed of Fe oxides and Fe hydroxides, and the inner layer were gradually composed of stable Fe oxides and Nb oxides, resulting in the increases of the mass ratio of Fe3+/Fe2+ and Nb2O5/(NbO2 + NbO) in the passive film, which improved the stability of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar.

(2) After passivation for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, the Ca-oxide particles and nano-scale oxide particles were distributed on the surface of two rebars, and these oxide particles constituted the composition of the passive film. Compared with CS rebar, the surface of 34Nb rebar was distributed with more fine and dense nano-scale oxide particles, resulting in the improvement of the compactness of surface passive film of 34Nb rebar.

(3) After passivation for 10 d in SCPS with different pH, the surface passive film of 34Nb rebar showed the higher stability and compactness. Firstly, with the decreases of pH, the addition of Nb in 34Nb rebar promoted the formation of more stable Fe oxides, which obtained the excellent Rct value, Ecorr value and Ep value, and the effective capacitance and passivation current density greatly reduced. Secondly, with the decreases of pH, the addition of Nb in 34Nb rebar promoted the formation of more stable Nb oxides, inhibited the degradation of stable Fe oxides, resulting in a significant decrease in carrier density. Finally, with the decreases of pH, the existence of Fe oxides and Nb oxides in 34Nb rebar enhanced the self-healing ability of the passive film and improved the passivation rate of 34Nb rebar, thus improving the corrosion resistance of 34Nb rebar.

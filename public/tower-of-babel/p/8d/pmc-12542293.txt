Association of atherogenic index of plasma with cardiovascular disease mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and diabetic kidney disease: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Zhiyu Li, Hongyang Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesType 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and diabetic kidney disease (DKD) may exacerbate atherosclerosis through mechanisms such as chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, and lipid redistribution. This study aims to investigate the relationship between atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) and cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality in patients with T2DM and DKD.MethodsCOX regression models and restricted cubic spline (RCS) curves were utilized to analyze the survival outcomes and non-linear associations among patients with T2DM and DKD. Threshold effects were observed in both global and inflection point models, confirming the predictive value of AIP for participants. Subgroup analysis further investigated the interaction of other variables within the AIP index on T2DM and patients with DKD with CVD mortality.ResultsA total of 5,108 patients diagnosed with T2DM, along with 1,598 patients suffering from DKD, were included in this study. In patients with DKD, the AIP index has a “U”-shaped association with CVD mortality. The hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval of the third quartile of CVD mortality in patients with DKD is Model3: 0.76 (0.61–0.96) (P < 0.023). The inflection point of the threshold effect analysis is 0.14. When AIP is less than 0.14, the effect size and 95% confidence interval of CVD mortality risk in patients with DKD is 0.62 (0.40–0.96) (P < 0.033). When AIP is greater than or equal to 0.14, the effect size and 95% confidence interval of CVD mortality risk in patients with DKD is 1.68 (1.15–2.45) (P < 0.008).ConclusionsThe relationship between AIP and CVD mortality in patients with DKD exhibits a “U” shaped curve, with a turning point value of 0.14. When AIP is below 0.14, the risk significantly decreases (HR = 0.62); conversely, when AIP is equal to or exceeds 0.14, the risk markedly increases (HR = 1.68).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05234-1.

Introduction

Diabetes has currently become a global health issue [1], and it can cause various complications such as diabetic retinopathy, diabetic foot, and DKD [2–4]. These complications mainly result from microvascular damage in patients with diabetes [5]. Studies have shown that dyslipidemia is closely related to diabetes. Generally, the levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and triglycerides (TG) in patients with diabetes are abnormal [6, 7]. In addition, dyslipidemia may also lead to the accumulation of non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) on the vessel wall, thereby triggering atherosclerosis and eventually causing CVD [8, 9].

CVD is one of the main causes of death for patients with T2DM [10]. Studies have shown that the ratio of TG to HDL-C has a significant impact on the severity of atherosclerosis and the prognosis of patients with diabetes and CVD [11]. Elevated triglyceride levels promote the retention of triglyceride-rich very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) remnants in the vascular wall. After their components undergo oxidative modification, they are more easily phagocytosed by macrophages, thereby forming foam cells [12]. Low levels of HDL-C not only weaken the reverse cholesterol transport capacity but also reduce its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant functions. Some studies have pointed out that individuals with a high AIP have significantly reduced antioxidant enzyme activities, such as paraoxonase 1 (PON1), in HDL particles, which hinders the effective inhibition of the oxidation process of low-density lipoprotein (LDL), thereby increasing the risk of cardiovascular diseases [13]. The AIP index also has been confirmed to increase the risk of stroke and myocardial infarction [14–16].

Suleymanoğlu M’s research demonstrated that compared with triglycerides and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, AIP is more effective in predicting acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction [17]; Karakaşlar M pointed out that in patients with ischemic and non-obstructive coronary artery disease, their AIP values were significantly elevated [18]. In summary, patients with a higher AIP index also show a significantly increased mortality from CVD. However, current studies on the potential prognostic impact of the AIP index on patients with T2DM and DKD accompanied by CVD are still scarce. This study aims to utilize the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) database to explore the association between the AIP index and CVD in patients with T2DM and DKD.

The selection procedure for individuals

The NHANES program is designed to assess the health and nutritional well-being of adults and children in the US. It is conducted through the collaboration of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) of the United States and the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS). The investigation plan of NHANES has been subject to ethical review by the Research Ethics Review Committee of the National Center for Health Statistics and has received the written consent of each participant. A total of 102,213 participants were involved in this study, with specific exclusion criteria including adolescents under 20 years old (18,951 cases), individuals missing fasting triglycerides and HDL-C (44,428 cases), those lacking serum creatinine (5,492 cases), those without urine albumin data (3,256 cases), individuals missing fasting blood glucose and HbA1c information (8,079 cases), those without education level recorded (3,089 cases), individuals missing CVD status (8,792 cases). Ultimately, the study included 5,108 patients with T2DM combined with CVD (Fig. 1).

Criteria for the diagnosis of T2DM and DKD in patients

The doctor diagnoses T2DM [19] by confirming the patient’s condition with a fasting blood glucose (FBG) of 7.0mmol/l or higher, an HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, or current use of insulin or other antidiabetic medications. The diagnosis of DKD [20] is established based on the presence of diabetes, as well as a UACR ≥ 30 mg/g and/or an eGFR less than 60 mL/min/1.73 m2. AIP index formula: AIP index = log10(TG/HDL-C) [21].

Definition of death related to the cardiovascular system

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s records indicate that the follow-up cutoff date for CVD events and deaths is December 31, 2019. In this study, CVD encompasses coronary heart disease, congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, stroke, or angina. The definition of CVD death aligns with the guidelines of the 10th revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) [22]. According to the US ICD-10-CM criteria, the exact codes in the study were as follows coronary heart disease (I25.10, I25.110), congestive heart failure (I50.2, I50.3, I50.4), acute myocardial infarction (I21.09, I21.11), old myocardial infarction (I25.210), stroke (haemorrhagic: I60.00, I61.9; ischaemic: I63.50, I63.9) and angina pectoris (I20.0, I20.1, I20.9. I61.9).

Covariates

Variables in the database include gender, age, BMI, TG, fasting blood glucose (FBG), HDL-C, serum creatinine, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), LDL-C, waist circumference, total cholesterol (TC), fasting insulin (FINS), and education level (less than high school education, equal to high school education and greater than high school education). Hypertension is defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure >90mmHg. Smokers are classified as smoking more than 100 cigarettes while non-smokers have smoked fewer than 100 cigarettes. Ethnicity categories include Mexican American, other Hispanic/Latino origin (excluding Mexican Americans), non-Hispanic white individuals, non-Hispanic black individuals and other races. eGFR is calculated using the CKD-EPI formula [23]. eGFR is categorized into five stages: Stage I (≥ 90 mL/min/1.73 m2), Stage II (60–90 mL/min/1.73 m2), Stage IIIa (45–60 mL/min/1.73 m2), Stage III b (30–45 mL/min/1.73 m2), Stage IV (15–30 mL/min/1.73 m2) and Stage V (< 15 mL/min/1.73 m2).

Data analysis

This research employed sample weighting following NHANES guidelines to address potential biases arising from the complex multi-stage sampling design of NHANES. Weighted percentages were used for categorical variables, while weighted means and standard errors were utilized for continuous variables to depict population baseline characteristics. In this study, continuous variables were evaluated by the Shapiro-Wilk test and all were found to follow a normal distribution. Independent sample t-tests were used, and data were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were analyzed using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, and were presented as frequency (proportion, n [%]). The study aims to analyze the predictive efficacy of AIP, TG, and HDL-C for the risk of CVD mortality using ROC curves, and to calculate the area under the curve (AUC). Kaplan-Meier survival curves to analyse the difference in CVD mortality between the AIP index and patients with T2DM and DKD. A multiple linear regression model was adopted to investigate the relationship between the AIP index and the CVD mortality in patients with DKD. To verify the model assumptions and avoid the interference of multicollinearity, the independence of the independent variables was first evaluated by the variance inflation factor (VIF), and the variables with a VIF value less than 5 were retained to control the collinearity effect. Meanwhile, a heatmap was drawn based on the Pearson correlation coefficient matrix to visualize the pairwise correlations among the variables. If strong correlations (|r| >0.7) and high VIF values were found among the variables, redundant variables would be prioritized for deletion, and the qualified variables would be included in model 3. The COX risk model was employed to establish Model1 without adjusting for confounding factors, Model2 adjusted for age, gender, and BMI, and Model3 which included antidiabetic drugs, statins, race, hypertension, smoker, education on top of Model2. Following adjustment for confounding factors, restricted cubic spline analysis revealed the relationship between AIP index and CVD risk in patients with T2DM or DKD. The RCS curve was set with three nodes, located at the 10%, 50%, and 90% quantiles respectively. Among them, the minimum node of AIP was regarded as the boundary reference point. In threshold effect analysis, Model1 and Model2 were adjusted by confounding factors in COX model compared with Model3. Model 1 was a standard linear regression fitting model evaluating overall survival prognosis based on AIP index while Model 2 established two-part linear regression fitting models analyzing AIP index with participants’ survival prognosis through thresholds. Subgroup analyses were conducted by stratifying age (≤ 65 vs. >65), BMI (≤ 28 vs. >28), gender, eGFR (≤ 60 vs. >60), urinary albumin (≤ 30 vs. >30), race, smoking status, education level and hypertension. Data analysis was performed using and DecisionLinnc.1.0 [24] and R language (version 4.4.1).

Population baseline characteristics

Participants were categorized into four groups based on their AIP quartiles. The average age of the participants was 60.04 years, and the highest average BMI value was 32.30 kg/m2. Non-Hispanic White individuals had the highest representation in terms of race, and stages I and II had the highest proportions in eGFR staging. TG, LDL-C, TC, FBG, serum creatinine, UACR, and FINS showed the highest values in the fourth quartile, while HDL-C had the highest value in the first quartile. All mentioned components exhibited significance (p < 0.05) in the analysis (Table 1). In patients with DKD, the average levels of albuminuria and UACR were found to be 309.99 mg/L and 321.51 mg/g, respectively, significantly higher than those observed in non-diabetic individuals, which were 12.17 mg/L and 9.65 mg/g. Furthermore, the incidence of hypertension among patients with DKD was notably elevated, reaching 83.10% (Table 2).

The research involved 1210 cases of T2DM combined with CVD mortality and 670 cases of mortality in DKD with CVD. The grouping was conducted using an AIP index of 0.13. Patients with DKD exhibiting a high AIP demonstrated a significantly lower risk of mortality, and this finding is statistically significant (Fig. 2). When assessing the effects of multicollinearity, the VIF for all variables—including age, gender, BMI, usage of antidiabetic medications, statins, race, hypertension, smoking, and education level—were found to be less than 5 (Table S1 and S2). The Pearson correlation coefficient matrix was plotted as heatmaps, showing that there was no strong correlation between any two variables (|r| < 0.7) (Figure S1 and S2). These indicates that multicollinearity is not present; therefore, we proceeded to establish Model 3. In the analysis of the ROC curve, the AUC value for AIP was significantly higher than that for TG and HDL-C (Figure S3). Referring to the first quartile, the third quartile of AIP in the DKD group was significantly associated with the HR of the participants and its 95% confidence interval in all three models, which were 0.84 (0.67–1.05), p < 0.008; 0.78 (0.63–0.98), p < 0.031; and 0.76 (0.61–0.96), p < 0.023, respectively (Table 3).

RCS curves and threshold effect analysis

The analysis of the RCS curve indicates a “U”-shaped relationship between the AIP index and CVD mortality in patients with DKD, consistent across both univariate and multivariate analyses (Fig. 3). In the threshold effect analysis, we adjusted for age, sex and BMI, antidiabetic drugs, statins, race, hypertension, smoker, education. In the standardized linear model of Model 1, the effect size of the risk of CVD mortality between T2DM and DKD patients did not show a significant difference. In the piecewise linear regression analysis of Model 2, the likelihood ratio test result of the risk of CVD mortality in patients with DKD was p < 0.001, with the inflection point set at 0.14. When AIP was less than 0.14, the effect size and 95% confidence interval of the risk of CVD mortality in patients with DKD were 0.62 (0.40–0.96) (P = 0.033); while when AIP was greater than or equal to 0.14, the effect size and 95% confidence interval were 1.68 (1.15–2.45) (P = 0.008) (Table 4).

Subgroup analysis

In patients with T2DM, they were divided into two groups based on the median of the AIP index (< 0.08 VS ≥ 0.08) (Fig. 4). Similarly, in patients with DKD, they were also grouped according to the median of the AIP index (< 0.13 VS ≥ 0.13༉(Fig. 5). Subgroup analyses were conducted for age, gender, BMI, race, eGFR, UACR, educational level and hypertension. Among the overall T2DM patients, no significant association was observed between the AIP index and CVD mortality (HR = 0.94, 95% CI 0.84–1.05; P = 0.299). However, subgroup analysis revealed significant effect heterogeneity. Stratified analysis by gender showed a strong interaction (P for interaction = 0.011), with a significant protective association in the female subgroup, where each unit increase in AIP was associated with a 24% reduction in CVD mortality risk (HR = 0.76, 95% CI 0.63–0.91; P = 0.003). Stratification by educational level also showed a significant interaction (P for interaction = 0.046), with each unit increase in AIP associated with a 22% reduction in CVD mortality risk in the low educational level (< high school) subgroup (HR = 0.78, 95% CI 0.65–0.94; P = 0.003) (Fig. 4). Survival analysis indicated a significant negative association between AIP levels and CVD mortality in patients with DKD, meaning that higher AIP levels were associated with a lower risk of death (HR = 0.81, 95% CI 0.69–0.94; P = 0.005). Stratified analysis by age further revealed significant effect heterogeneity (P for interaction = 0.021), with a 24% significant reduction in CVD mortality risk for each unit increase in AIP in the elderly subgroup over 65 years old (HR = 0.76, 95% CI 0.64–0.91, P = 0.003) (Fig. 5).

Discussions

The relationship between AIP and CVD mortality in patients with DKD exhibits a “U” shaped curve, with a turning point value of 0.14. When AIP is below 0.14, the risk significantly decreases (HR = 0.62); conversely, when AIP is equal to or exceeds 0.14, the risk markedly increases (HR = 1.68).

The research found that in the third quartile, AIP significantly reduced the risk of CVD in patients with DKD (HR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.12–0.34). Linear regression analysis showed that there was a “U” -shaped curve relationship between AIP and the mortality rate of CVD in patients with DKD, that is, as the level of AIP increased, the mortality rate first decreased and then increased. Taking the first quartile (low level) of AIP as the reference group, COX regression analysis indicated that the HR in the third quartile (medium level) was less than 1, suggesting that a medium level of AIP has a protective effect and the mortality rate is lower than that in the lower-level group. However, in the linear regression results, HR was all greater than 1, confirming that both ends of the U-shaped curve - low and high levels of AIP - increase the risk of death. This conclusion is consistent with previous studies [25, 26]. Therefore, HR < 1 in the third quartile indicates that moderate AIP levels are negatively correlated with risk (protective), while extreme AIP levels are harmful. This effect of first decreasing and then increasing stems from a unique metabolic window: a moderate AIP level maintains the functional integrity of HDL and enhances insulin sensitivity and inhibits the kidney - cardiovascular inflammation axis (such as blocking NF - κB and TGF - β) by activating the PPARγ pathway [27]. This is in sharp contrast to the extreme AIP states (malnourished HDL dysfunction in Q1 and lipotoxicity - driven fibrosis in Q4), suggesting that individualized regulation of AIP to the optimized range of 0.12–0.34 is required in the management of patients with DKD.

Patients with DKD have unique characteristics of lipid metabolism disorders, which may explain the “U”-shaped association between AIP and CVD mortality in DKD patients. At the low end of AIP, extremely low levels of AIP reflect a significant reduction in TG or an abnormal increase in HDL-C. In advanced stages of DKD, this phenomenon may indicate malnutrition and protein-energy wasting (PEW): excessively low TG levels could be associated with muscle catabolism and depletion of lipid stores, leading to insufficient myocardial energy supply [28]. Moreover, HDL dysfunction is also a critical factor: despite elevated HDL-C levels, uremic toxins such as indoxyl sulfate can induce structural modifications to HDL, resulting in the loss of its anti-inflammatory properties and cholesterol efflux capacity; it may even transform into pro-oxidative particles [29].

At the high end of AIP, lipotoxicity further drives mechanisms that contribute to atherosclerosis; specifically, under high AIP conditions characterized by elevated TG and reduced HDL-C. This process is exacerbated in DKD: residual cholesterol accumulation intensifies due to increased TG levels and heightened presence of cholesterol-rich VLDL remnants. These particles are prone to deposit within vascular walls and activate macrophage foam cell formation [30]. Concurrently, insulin resistance along with free fatty acid (FFA) overflow emerges as key factors: FFAs can induce endothelial cell damage while promoting myocardial fibrosis, thereby worsening the condition [31]. Previous studies have shown that elevated AIP is positively associated with coronary artery disease, carotid atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, and other CVD [32–34]. For instance, a prospective cohort study revealed an increased risk of developing CVD in individuals with higher AIP levels [35]. In clinical research, changes in AIP are linked to vascular structure alterations; elevated levels of AIP may lead to endothelial dysfunction and promote atherosclerosis formation [36, 37]. Additionally, AIP is also considered as a predictor for vascular remodeling and the effectiveness of angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors [38].

T2DM’ lipid components in blood and adipose tissue exhibit significant variations throughout the day [6, 39]. Levels of certain harmful lipids may increase in the morning, potentially correlating with diabetes severity. These fluctuations can impact blood sugar regulation as fat metabolism plays a critical role in glucose control [40, 41]. patients with diabetes have an elevated AIP index, indicating a higher ratio of triglycerides to HDL-C [7], and increased triglycerides can lead to dysfunction of pancreatic beta-cells and subsequent elevation of blood glucose levels [42]. Reduced HDL-C also contributes to heightened non-HDL cholesterol in the liver and bloodstream, resulting in elevated triglyceride levels among patients with diabetes [43].

The mechanism of AIP in patients having T2DM and DKD along with CVD. Research has shown that specific toxic lipids in the blood of T2DM rise during the morning hours, potentially linked to diabetes severity. Additionally, impaired function of β-cells (responsible for insulin secretion) leads to an increase in specific lipids such as Lys phospholipids, which can enhance insulin secretion [44]. Abnormal lipid accumulation is a key factor in the pathological process of DKD. This accumulation primarily occurs within kidney podocytes and can result in podocyte injury and dysfunction [45, 46]. Studies have indicated that the restoration of Dock5 expression in mouse models with DKD can effectively reduce lipid deposition and injury in podocytes [47], highlighting their specific susceptibility to lipid accumulation. The dysregulation of lipid metabolism is a critical factor in the progression of DKD. For instance, Sterol Regulatory Element-Binding Proteins (SREBPs) and Carbohydrate-Responsive Element-Binding Protein (ChREBP) are pivotal genes involved in regulating lipid synthesis and breakdown [48–50]. In DKD, these genes are overexpressed, leading to abnormal lipid buildup in the kidneys. Additionally, there is a disruption in the balance between fatty acid uptake and oxidation, further contributing to lipid deposition in the kidneys.

Study strengths and limitation

The article presents a comprehensive analysis of the basic characteristics of the population, such as age, BMI, race, eGFR stage, blood glucose, and lipid levels. This analysis helps in understanding the fundamental features of the study participants. However, it is uncertain whether all potential confounding factors were considered despite conducting multiple model analyses in this article. Although a relatively large sample size was used in this study, there is still a possibility of selection bias that may impact the generalizability of the research findings. The focus of the study is on trends in CVD mortality risk associated with AIP index in patients with T2DM and DKD; other potential predictive factors for mortality rates were not explored. Furthermore, due to its retrospective design, inherent bias risks exist in its results. Researchers may not have fully considered confounding factors that could influence T2DM and DKD mortality risk, potentially leading to biased results.

Conclusions

The AIP index in patients with DKD exhibits a U-shaped relationship with the risk of CVD mortality. Female patients, those with low educational levels among T2DM individuals, and elderly patients with DKD may benefit from monitoring their AIP levels. However, caution is warranted when AIP values exceed 0.14 due to the potential reversal of associated risks. This study is a retrospective analysis; therefore, it is essential to conduct prospective research to further validate these findings.

Standard TAPP with flat mesh versus TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug for inguinal hernia repair: a preliminary single-center randomized experience
Authors: Morteza Azadbakht, Mohammadali Hassanvand
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInguinal hernia repair is a common surgical procedure. While laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) repair is widely adopted, the comparative efficacy of standard flat mesh alone versus flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation remains an area of ongoing clinical evaluation. This study aimed to compare these two techniques with respect to recurrence, postoperative pain, and patient recovery.MethodsIn this preliminary, single-center randomized trial, 63 patients undergoing TAPP repair for inguinal hernia were randomly assigned to either a standard TAPP with flat mesh alone group (n = 33) or a TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation group (n = 30). A single surgeon performed all procedures. Demographics, operative details, time to return to work, postoperative pain (measured via a Visual Analog Scale [VAS]), patient satisfaction, and hernia recurrence were documented during hospitalization and at follow-up intervals over 12 months. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v26, employing t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, and Chi-square tests where appropriate.ResultsThe cohorts were comparable regarding age, Body mass index (BMI), and baseline clinical characteristics. The recurrence rate was significantly higher in the standard TAPP with flat mesh alone group, reaching 12.1% at 3 months and 21.2% at 6 and 12 months, whereas no recurrences (0%) occurred in the mesh plug-augmented group (p < 0.05). Although early postoperative pain scores were similar between the groups, chronic pain outcomes significantly favored the mesh plug-augmented cohort; 0% reported chronic pain at 12 months compared to 21.2% in the standard TAPP group (p < 0.05). Consequently, overall patient satisfaction was significantly higher in the mesh plug-augmented group (96.7% “very satisfied”) than in the standard TAPP group (81.8% “very satisfied”). The standard TAPP group demonstrated a trend toward a shorter time to return to work (93.9% resuming within 1–3 days), but this difference did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05).ConclusionTAPP repair with flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation was associated with significantly lower recurrence rates (0% vs. 21.2%) and lower chronic pain rates (0% vs. 21.2%) compared to standard TAPP with flat mesh alone. However, given the small sample size, lack of an a priori sample size calculation, and single-center design, these findings should be interpreted strictly as hypothesis-generating. Larger, adequately powered multicenter trials are required to confirm whether routine mesh plug augmentation confers a definitive clinical advantage.Keywords: Inguinal hernia, Laparoscopic TAPP repair, Mesh plug, Hernia recurrence, Chronic postoperative pain, Patient satisfaction

Introduction

Hernias can be classified into several types, including inguinal, ventral, umbilical, femoral, and hiatal hernias [1]. Approximately 75% of abdominal wall hernias occur in the groin region, with the lifetime risk of developing an inguinal hernia estimated at 27% in men and 3% in women [2]. In men, inguinal hernias exhibit two age-related peaks: during the first year of life and after age 40 [3].

Inguinal hernias are classified as direct or indirect, with indirect inguinal hernias the most common in both men and women [4]. They occur when tissue protrudes through a weak point in the groin muscles, leading to a visible bulge in the groin or scrotum [5]. Surgical intervention is generally indicated for inguinal hernia, which may be performed through either an open or laparoscopic approach [6]. Surgical treatment carries inherent risks and complications, including bleeding, seroma formation, wound infection, urinary retention, ileus, injury to the spermatic cord, chronic inguinal and pubic pain, and hernia recurrence [7]. Consequently, minimally invasive approaches with lower complication rates are increasingly recommended [8].

The most common surgical techniques include simple tissue repair, mesh-based repair, and combined approaches [9]. Simple repair involves suturing and reconstructing the inguinal muscles along a straight line, whereas mesh repair uses synthetic or biological mesh to reinforce the defect, especially in large hernias with a high risk of recurrence [10]. Combined repair integrates both methods, providing structural support and reducing tension [11]. Mesh is a biocompatible, mesh-like layer available in various sizes, designed to match the location and extent of the muscle defect [12]. With the introduction of synthetic meshes, tension-free repair using mesh has become the gold standard for inguinal hernia surgery [13].

Laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair represents a modern approach that can be performed using either the TAPP or total extraperitoneal (TEP) methods [14]. Another technique, intraperitoneal onlay mesh (IPOM), has become largely obsolete due to its outcomes being comparable to open repair [15]. In TAPP repair, the peritoneal flaps are dissected, the hernia sac is separated, and a mesh is placed over the myopectineal orifice, followed by fixation with tacks or sutures and closure of the peritoneum with absorbable sutures [16]. In standard TAPP repair, a flat polypropylene mesh is placed over the myopectineal orifice. In TAPP with mesh plug augmentation, an additional rolled mesh plug is first inserted into the hernia defect and secured before the flat mesh is positioned, providing dual reinforcement, reducing the risk of recurrence and providing greater reinforcement [17]. It is important to note that both techniques utilize a flat mesh; the distinction lies in the use of an additional mesh plug in the intervention group.

Laparoscopic TAPP repair is a widely accepted technique for inguinal hernia repair, offering advantages such as reduced postoperative pain, faster recovery, and lower infection rates compared to open approaches [18]. In standard TAPP repair, a flat polypropylene mesh is placed over the myopectineal orifice to provide tension-free reinforcement. However, recurrence rates remain a concern, particularly in patients with large defects or recurrent hernias [19]. Mesh plug augmentation, placing an additional rolled mesh plug into the hernia defect before flat mesh placement, has been proposed as a method to provide dual reinforcement and potentially reduce recurrence risk [20]. Nevertheless, the comparative efficacy of TAPP with flat mesh alone versus TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation has not been well established [21–25]. This study aimed to compare these two techniques in terms of hernia recurrence, postoperative pain, recovery, and patient satisfaction in a single-center randomized experience.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a prospective, randomized, single-center clinical trial conducted to comparatively evaluate inguinal hernia repair using the laparoscopic TAPP method with flat mesh alone versus flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation in terms of hernia recurrence, postoperative pain, and recovery.

Postoperative pain was assessed using a 10-point VAS, where 0 = no pain and 10 = worst possible pain. Early pain was assessed at 24 h, 48 h, 7 days, and 30 days postoperatively. Chronic pain was defined as VAS ≥ 1 persisting beyond 3 months postoperatively, assessed at 3, 6, and 12 months. While VAS is a validated tool for pain intensity, we acknowledge its limitation in capturing the multidimensional nature of chronic pain (e.g., functional interference, quality of pain).

Study population and sample

Patients who were candidates for inguinal hernia repair using the laparoscopic TAPP method were considered for inclusion. Both unilateral and bilateral inguinal hernias were included. In patients with bilateral hernias, the same assigned technique (standard TAPP with flat mesh or TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug) was applied to both sides during the same operative session.

Convenience sampling was used, and patients were randomly assigned to one of two treatment groups: (1) standard TAPP with flat mesh (control group), or (2) TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation (intervention group). using a coin toss. Ultimately, 63 patients were enrolled, with 33 in the TAPP group and 30 in the TAPP + mesh plug group.

Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) patients over 14 years of age with a diagnosis of reducible inguinal hernia scheduled for elective laparoscopic TAPP repair; (2) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class I-III; (3) ability to provide informed consent.

Exclusion criteria included: (1) emergency presentation (incarceration, strangulation, bowel obstruction, or perforation); (2) local or systemic infection; (3) peritonitis; (4) pregnancy; (5) immunocompromised status (including active chemotherapy, chronic steroid use, or HIV with CD4 < 200); (6) contraindication to general anesthesia; (7) patient uncooperative or unwilling to participate; (8) failure to attend follow-up visits; (9) incomplete questionnaires.

After obtaining the necessary ethical approvals, patients who met the inclusion criteria were randomly assigned to the two study groups. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before surgery.

No a priori sample size calculation was performed. The sample size (63 patients) was based on convenience sampling over the study period. A post-hoc calculation indicates that to detect a reduction in recurrence from an assumed baseline of 10% to 1% with 80% power and α = 0.05, approximately 120 patients per group would be required. Thus, the present study is underpowered for definitive comparative conclusions and should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

Data collection

The primary endpoint was ipsilateral hernia recurrence within 12 months after surgery. All patients underwent standardized follow-up visits at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively. At each visit, a postoperative assessor who was blinded to treatment allocation performed a standardized clinical examination for evidence of recurrence. Ultrasound examination was not performed routinely but was obtained when recurrence was clinically suspected (e.g., new groin bulge, localized symptoms, or equivocal physical examination findings). Ultrasound studies were interpreted by an independent radiologist who was blinded to treatment allocation. Hernia recurrence was considered confirmed when clinical examination and, where applicable, ultrasound findings demonstrated recurrence at the index repair site.

Patient satisfaction was evaluated at 12 months using a non-validated, three-point categorical scale (‘very satisfied,’ ‘highly satisfied,’ and ‘low satisfaction’) embedded within the researcher-designed checklist.

A researcher-designed checklist was used to collect demographic information, clinical data, recovery time, return to work, duration of hospitalization, pain intensity at each stage, and other postoperative clinical manifestations. Data were recorded during hospitalization and at follow-up visits, then entered into SPSS version 26 for statistical analysis.

Surgical procedure

All surgeries were performed by a single experienced surgeon using a standardized TAPP technique. Before the initiation of patient enrollment, the operating surgeon had a baseline experience of 100 standard TAPP procedures and 100 TAPP repairs with mesh plug augmentation. The abdominal cavity was insufflated with CO₂ via a Veress needle, and a 10-mm port was placed above the umbilicus for the camera, with two 5-mm ports inserted into the right and left lower abdomen. Key anatomical structures and the hernia defect were identified, and the peritoneum over the hernia was opened according to anatomical dimensions. Subperitoneal flaps were bluntly dissected to allow placement of a mesh measuring at least 11 × 15 cm, and the hernia sac was carefully dissected while preserving vital anatomical structures. In the standard TAPP with flat mesh plug-augmented group (control), an 11 × 15 cm polypropylene mesh was placed on the floor of the canal and fixed with tacks. In the TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug group (intervention), after reduction of the hernia sac and identification of the myopectineal orifice, the internal inguinal ring was exposed. A rolled polypropylene mesh plug (approximately 3 × 5 cm) was inserted into the internal inguinal ring the anatomical opening through which the hernia sac protruded. The plug was advanced until its proximal end was flush with the transversalis fascia, occupying the preperitoneal space just posterior to the inguinal canal. The plug was then secured to the edges of the defect (the margins of the internal inguinal ring) using interrupted 3 − 0 polypropylene sutures. Following plug placement and fixation, an 11 × 15 cm flat polypropylene mesh was positioned over the myopectineal orifice, completely covering the plug, the internal inguinal ring, Hesselbach’s triangle, and Cooper’s ligament, and fixed with tacks. All meshes were manufactured by Pars Mesh Co. (Tehran, Iran) and were composed of standard-weight (approximately 80–100 g/m²), monofilament, macroporous polypropylene. After mesh placement, the peritoneal flap was closed to prevent adhesion of the intestines or omentum and reduce the risk of complications such as fistula formation. Intra-peritoneal pressure was reduced to below 8 mmHg to facilitate tissue approximation, which was performed from the outside toward the inside. The reverse Trendelenburg position was used to mobilize the intestines downward, reduce tension on the peritoneum, and optimize the surgical field for easier suturing (Fig. 1).

Randomization and allocation concealment

Eligible patients were randomly assigned to one of two treatment groups in a 1:1 ratio using simple randomization with a coin toss. The allocation sequence was generated by an independent statistician who was not involved in patient enrollment or clinical care.

Allocation concealment was ensured using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes (SNOSE). A research coordinator not involved in surgical procedures prepared the envelopes. After obtaining written informed consent and confirming eligibility, the research coordinator opened the next sequential envelope to reveal group assignment.

Patients were blinded to their group assignment throughout the study period. The operating surgeon was not blinded due to the nature of the procedure (presence or absence of mesh plug). Outcome assessors (including the surgeon performing postoperative clinical examinations and an independent research assistant administering pain and satisfaction questionnaires) were blinded to group assignment.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26. For quantitative variables with a normal distribution, an independent t-test was used, while the Mann–Whitney U test was applied for quantitative variables with a non-normal distribution. Qualitative variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 63 patients were enrolled in the study and randomly allocated to the standard TAPP with flat mesh plug-augmented group (n = 33) or the TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug group (n = 30). The flow of the study, from recruitment to the 12-month follow-up, was completed without major protocol deviations.

Baseline and demographic characteristics

The overall cohort had a mean age of 49.24 ± 16.48 years and a mean BMI of 26.0 ± 4.47 kg/m². The majority of patients were male (n = 60, 95.2%). The study groups were well-matched at baseline. There were no statistically significant differences between the TAPP and TAPP+ mesh plug-augmented group in mean age (49.03 ± 17.58 vs. 49.47 ± 15.5 years, p > 0.05) or mean BMI (26.06 ± 4.71 vs. 25.99 ± 4.27 kg/m², p > 0.05). The distribution of gender across surgical methods was also comparable, with no significant imbalance detected (Table 1).

Surgical characteristics and patient history

Among all patients, 49 (77.8%) were undergoing primary hernia repair, while 14 (22.2%) had a history of previous repair. Hernias were located on the right side in 36 patients (57.1%), the left side in 24 (38.1%), and were bilateral in 3 (4.8%). Comorbidities were reported by 8 patients (12.7%), the most common being hypertension (n = 5, 7.9%).

Primary outcome: hernia recurrence

The primary outcome was clinically confirmed hernia recurrence at the same anatomical site. In the standard TAPP with flat mesh plug-augmented group, recurrences were observed in 4 patients (12.1%) at 3 months, increasing to 7 patients (21.2%) at both the 6-month and 12-month follow-ups. In the TAPP with flat mesh plus mesh plug group, no recurrences (0%) were recorded at any point during the 12-month follow-up period. The difference in recurrence rates between the groups was statistically significant (p < 0.05).

Secondary outcomes

Chronic Postoperative Pain: While early postoperative pain (within the first week) was not significantly different between groups (p = 0.232), long-term outcomes diverged. Chronic pain, defined as VAS ≥ 1 persisting beyond 3 months postoperatively, was reported by 7 patients (21.2%) in the standard TAPP group at 12 months, whereas no patients (0%) in the mesh plug-augmented group reported pain; this difference was statistically significant (p < 0.05).

Patient Satisfaction: Reflecting the superior outcomes in recurrence and pain, patient satisfaction was significantly higher in the TAPP+ mesh plug-augmented group (p < 0.05).

Patient satisfaction, assessed at 12 months using a 3-point scale (‘very satisfied,’ ‘highly satisfied,’ ‘low satisfaction’), was significantly higher in the mesh plug-augmented group, with 29 patients (96.7%) reporting ‘very satisfied’ and 1 (3.3%) was “highly satisfied.” In the TAPP group, 27 patients (81.8%) were “very satisfied,” and 6 (18.2%) reported “low” satisfaction.

Time to Return to Work: A trend toward faster recovery was observed in the TAPP group, where 31 patients (93.9%) returned to work within 1–3 days, compared to 23 patients (76.7%) in the TAPP+mesh plug-augmented group. However, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05) (Table 2; Fig. 2).

Discussion

Inguinal hernia repair remains one of the most common surgical procedures worldwide, with hundreds of thousands performed annually [26, 27]. Its high prevalence among the young, active population, coupled with the risks of serious complications like visceral incarceration and historically high recurrence rates (exceeding 10–20% in some reports), has driven a century of innovation in surgical technique [28, 29]. The evolution has consistently aimed at achieving a durable, tension-free repair to minimize recurrence [30, 31]. This study evaluated the contemporary application of this principle by comparing laparoscopic TAPP repair using standard flat mesh alone versus flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation in 63 patients.

Our findings demonstrate a striking difference in long-term anatomical durability between the two cohorts. The recurrence rate was significantly higher in the standard TAPP with flat mesh alone group, reaching 12.1% at 3 months and climbing to 21.2% at 6 and 12 months. Conversely, no recurrences (0%) occurred in the mesh plug-augmented group (p < 0.05). Studies by Elsebae et al. and Amato et al. consistently report markedly lower recurrence rates with mesh-based repairs, confirming its superiority in providing a durable anatomical reconstruction [32–37]. This structural success aligns with the discussion by E. Kotidis, who highlights that combining a plug and a flat mesh represents a safe and effective modification of the standard TAPP procedure. Kotidis notes that this dual configuration helps minimize recurrence rates by securing the closure and preventing the hernia sac from sliding under a standard tension-free flat mesh [38].

Furthermore, the long-term benefits of our mesh plug augmentation extended significantly to postoperative pain outcomes [34]. Chronic pain, a critical determinant of patient quality of life, was significantly less prevalent in our mesh plug-augmented group. While early postoperative pain scores were similar between the two groups, chronic pain outcomes at 12 months markedly favored the augmented cohort: 0% of patients reported chronic pain compared to 21.2% in the standard TAPP group (p < 0.05). This finding is consistent with the work of Amato et al. [39].

In a complementary evaluation of laparoscopic TAPP inguinal hernia repairs, Ahmed Y. El Rifaie demonstrates that utilizing contoured anatomical meshes significantly reduces postoperative inguinodynia from the seventh postoperative day up to one year when compared to standard flat polypropylene meshes [40]. El Rifaie attributes these clinical improvements to the precise contouring of anatomical meshes, which drastically reduces the number of penetrating tacks required for fixation, a factor directly correlated with postherniorrhaphy chronic pain. Interestingly, while El Rifaie accomplishes pain reduction by altering the mesh geometry to avoid aggressive fixation hardware, our technique achieves complete resolution of chronic pain at one year (0%) by reinforcing the internal ring defect with a mesh plug, minimizing subsequent structural instability or mesh movement over time. Consequently, patient-reported satisfaction in our study was significantly higher in the TAPP with mesh plug-augmented group (96.7% “very satisfied”) compared to the standard flat mesh group (81.8% “very satisfied”), indicating that from the patient’s perspective, the long-term dual benefits of reduced recurrence and absent chronic pain are paramount.

An interesting nuance emerged in the analysis of convalescence. Our data indicated a trend toward a faster return to work in the standard TAPP with flat mesh alone group, with 93.9% resuming activity within 1–3 days, compared to a more varied recovery timeline in the mesh plug-augmented group, though this difference did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05). This finding stands in contrast to Modares et al., who reported shorter recovery with mesh. This discrepancy may be attributable to fundamental differences in surgical approach (the Modares study utilized laparotomy) or variations in postoperative protocols. However, it also introduces a distinct perspective when compared to Kotidis and El Rifaie, who both emphasize faster recovery and shortened hospital stays when optimizing laparoscopic approaches. While Kotidis notes that the laparoscopic approach as a whole introduces a distinct learning curve due to an entirely different anatomical perspective, our data suggests that the localized tissue volume and initial inflammatory response to an additional mesh plug may slightly influence early functional recovery timelines, a factor warranting further investigation [41].

It is important to reiterate that both study groups in our trial received a standard flat polypropylene mesh. The intervention group received an additional mesh plug placed into the hernia defect before flat mesh placement, providing dual reinforcement. Hence, this study confirms that augmenting standard TAPP repair with a mesh plug provides superior long-term surgical outcomes and patient satisfaction [42].

In summary, this preliminary study suggests that mesh plug augmentation in TAPP repair is associated with lower recurrence and chronic pain rates compared to standard TAPP with flat mesh alone in a high-risk population (22.2% recurrent hernias). However, these benefits must be weighed against potential disadvantages, including increased foreign material burden, additional cost, increased operative time, and technical complexity. The risk-benefit profile may differ in low-risk populations (primary hernias, experienced surgeons) and resource-limited settings. The compelling long-term clinical outcomes observed in this study justify larger, adequately powered trials to confirm these findings and to better define the patient populations most likely to benefit from mesh plug augmentation.

Limitations

The 21.2% recurrence rate observed in our standard TAPP group at 12 months is substantially higher than the 1–5% typically reported in large registries and multicenter meta-analyses. Several methodological and clinical factors explain this discrepancy. First, 22.2% of our study cohort consisted of patients with recurrent hernias, which are inherently associated with a significantly higher risk of re-recurrence due to distorted anatomy and scarred tissue planes. Second, this series captures a single surgeon’s early experience with the laparoscopic TAPP approach; the well-documented learning curve for TAPP typically requires 50–100 cases before recurrence rates stabilize at lower historical benchmarks. Third, our strict definition of recurrence requiring clinical examination combined with routine ultrasound confirmation likely captured small or early asymptomatic recurrences that are frequently underreported in trials tracking only reoperation rates. Fourth, given our small sample size (n = 33 in the control group), this estimate is highly sensitive to random variation, as reflected by the wide 95% confidence interval (approximately 9–39%). Finally, we did not systematically record several patient-specific risk factors known to increase recurrence, including obesity (BMI > 30), smoking, chronic cough, chronic constipation, or heavy lifting, which may have been disproportionately represented by chance. Consequently, the absolute recurrence rates observed here cannot be generalized to low-risk or high-volume settings, and these preliminary findings should be interpreted strictly as hypothesis-generating.

Despite the higher-than-expected baseline recurrence rate in the control group, the relative benefit of mesh plug augmentation (0% recurrence vs. 21.2%) remains statistically significant and clinically meaningful. However, the generalizability of these findings to settings with lower baseline recurrence rates (e.g., primary hernias repaired by experienced surgeons) requires confirmation in larger, multicenter trials. Readers should interpret the absolute recurrence rate in the control group with caution, recognizing that it reflects a high-risk population (22.2% recurrent hernias), potential learning curve effects, and a sensitive recurrence definition. Finally, this study did not utilize a formally validated hernia-specific Patient-Reported Outcome Measure (PROM), such as the Carolinas Comfort Scale (CCS), to evaluate quality of life and satisfaction. Instead, a non-validated, author-designed 3-point scale was used [41, 42]. While this provides a rudimentary overview of patient satisfaction, it lacks the nuance, sensitivity, and multidimensionality of validated instruments, which should be prioritized in future multicenter trials.

Conclusion

In a single-center randomized study, TAPP repair with flat mesh plus mesh plug augmentation was associated with lower rates of recurrence and chronic pain compared with standard TAPP with flat mesh alone. The recurrence rate of 21.2% observed in our standard TAPP group is higher than the 1–5% typically reported in large series and meta-analyses. Readers should interpret this finding with caution, as it may reflect a combination of recurrent hernia cases, learning-curve effects, sensitive recurrence definitions, small sample size, and unmeasured patient-related risk factors. However, given the small sample size, lack of a priori power calculation, and single-surgeon design, these findings should be interpreted with caution and considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. The magnitude and generalizability of the observed effects remain uncertain. Larger, adequately powered multicenter trials with longer follow-up are needed to confirm these findings and clarify the patient populations most likely to benefit. Future research should also evaluate cost implications and factors influencing patient acceptance to inform clinical decision-making better.

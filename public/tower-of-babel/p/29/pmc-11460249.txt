Case report illustrating evolution of an intraosseous lipoma: Radiology findings and differentials
Authors: Thomas Wei-lam Yip, Pui Yang Carol Chien
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 21 year old female patient presented with mild left ankle pain for 2 months. Left ankle radiograph was performed, showing a lucent lesion with narrow zone of transition at anterior body of the calcaneus. Further evaluation with MRI showed T1 hypointense and T2 hyperintense signals within said lesion, with fluid-fluid level. The working diagnosis was aneurysmal bone cyst. Follow-up MRI performed 3 years later showed predominant macroscopic fat within the lesion. This case demonstrates composition change of intraosseous lipoma over time.Keywords: Intraosseous lipoma, Calcaneal lesion, Aneurysmal bone cyst, Composition change

Introduction

Calcaneal lucent lesions with narrow zone of transition are often incidentally encountered. Differential diagnoses include intraosseous lipoma, simple bone cyst, giant cell tumor, and aneurysmal bone cyst. MRI can often narrow down the list of different diagnoses.

This case demonstrates an intraosseous lipoma with very different signal characteristics across 2 MRI studies that are 3 years apart.

Case report

A 21 year old female university student presented with mild left ankle pain for 2 months. She had no history of trauma or sprain injury. Clinical examination did not reveal any bruise of hematoma. Range of motion was full.

A set of left ankle radiographs were done, lateral film showed a 2.7 × 2.0 cm lucent lesion with narrow zone of transition at the anterior body of calcaneus. There was no pathological fracture or intralesional fallen fragment (Fig. 1).

Lateral radiograph of left ankle. Shows lucent lesion with narrow zone of transition at the anterior body of calcaneus. No pathological fracture or intralesional fallen fragment.

MRI demonstrated an anterior calcaneal lesion with predominant central T2 hyperintense signal with sedimentation and fluid-fluid level, as well as small eccentric T1 hyperintense, mildly T2 hyperintense component over its inferomedial aspect. No significant perilesional marrow edema, cortical destruction or periosteal reaction detected in the calcaneus (Fig. 2). Overall features were in favor of nonaggressive lesion, such as aneurysmal bone cyst. Though the lesion did not appear expansile, the provisional diagnosis of aneurysmal bone cyst was compatible with the patient's age (21 years old), location of the lesion and MRI characteristic of fluid-fluid level. Giant cell tumor was considered less likely as sclerotic rim was present. The possibility of intraosseous lipoma was also considered due to presence of macroscopic fat.

(A) sagittal T1 sequence; (B) sagittal T2 fat-saturated sequence; (C) sagittal proton density sequence; (D) axial T1 sequence; (E) axial T2 fat-saturated sequence; (F) axial proton density sequence. Shows predominant central T2 hyperintense signal with fluid-fluid level, as well as small eccentric T1 hyperintense, mildly T2 hyperintense component over its inferomedial aspect.

MRI findings and differential diagnoses were explained to the patient. Biopsy was offered to the patient but she opted for observation and imaging follow-up.

Serial radiographs were subsequently done in 6-month to 1-year intervals. The calcaneal lesion had remained stable in size and appearance (Fig. 3).

Calcaneal radiographs 2 years apart show that the lesion is static in size and appearance.

A follow-up MRI study was performed 3 years after the index MRI study.

The lesion's internal composition had changed, this time showing homogenous T1 hyperintense signal, which was completely nulled on fat suppressed sequences, suggestive of macroscopic fat. Two small residual subcentimetre cystic areas were seen. Thin rim of T1 and T2 hypointense signal surrounding the lesion correspond to sclerotic rim seen on radiograph.

No fluid-fluid level was seen within the lesion. No significant perilesional marrow edema or extraosseous soft tissue component identified.

Overall findings were in favor of an intraosseous lipoma (Fig. 4).

(A) sagittal T1 sequence; (B) sagittal T2 fat-saturated sequence; (C) sagittal T1 postcontrast fat-saturated sequence; (D) axial T1 sequence; (E) axial T2 fat-saturated sequence; (F) axial T1 postcontrast fat-saturated sequence. Shows predominant macroscopic fat within the lesion with 2 small residual subcentimetre cystic areas.

Patient had graduated from university and was later keen on having a biopsy to confirm the diagnosis. A CT was performed on the day of the biopsy which showed macroscopic fat within the lesion (Fig. 5).

CT performed on the day of biopsy. (A) soft tissue window; (B) bone window. Shows macroscopic fat within the lesion.

Biopsy was done by orthopedic surgeons and pathology result confirmed the lesion to be an intraosseous lipoma.

Discussion

Lucent lesions within the calcaneus are often incidentally encountered. The list of differential diagnoses is long as evaluation of lesions in the calcaneus is comparable to evaluating lesions in long bones with an epiphysis, metaphysis and diaphysis [1]. The most common differential diagnoses include intraosseous lipoma, simple bone cyst, giant cell tumor and aneurysmal bone cyst among young adults. While infection and metastasis are more common in elderly population.

There are several plain film features described in literature that match certain diagnoses. For example, simple bone cysts with pathological fractures often show the “fallen fragment sign”, which describes a dependent bone fracture fragment within the cyst. Presence of central calcification within a lucent lesion would point towards intraosseous lipoma known as the “Cockade sign”. However, these imaging features are often not present and it is often difficult to determine the nature of these lucent calcaneal lesions using radiographs alone. MRI is the appropriate next step of management for lucent calcaneal lesions.

Aneurysmal bone cyst, chondroblastoma and giant cell tumor are several disease entities that would immediately come to mind when we see a T2 hyperintense lesion with fluid-fluid level within, in the young adult population, demonstrated in the first set of MRI above (Fig. 2). All these lesions can have perilesional edema, but there are specific MRI features that separate them.

Aneurysmal bone cysts are benign osteoclastic giant cell-rich neoplasms and can be markedly expansile, unlike other lesions on this list.

Chondroblastomas are benign chondrogenic bone neoplasms arising from the epiphyses. Therefore, cortical involvement is a characteristic. They are also known to have lobular internal architecture.

Giant cell tumors are locally aggressive bony neoplasms found at closed growth plate extending into the epiphyses and articulating surfaces. Intralesional solid components may show enhancement on MRI.

Intraosseous lipoma is the most frequent calcaneal bone lesion. It is classically described to show homogeneous hyperintense fat signal on T1 and T2 weighted MR images that are suppressed on fat-saturated sequences. However, interosseous lipomas may have different stages of progression, which have been described in literature [2]. They can have T1 hypointense inclusions within that are hyperintense on T2 fat-saturated images. Fluid cavities surrounded by signal-void rims of sclerotic bone have also been described and were observed in the second set of MRI (Fig. 4).

In this case report, the intraosseous lipoma demonstrated mimicking features of neoplastic bone lesions such as aneurysmal bone cyst, chondroblastoma and giant cell tumor. It highlights the change in appearance and composition of intraosseous lipomas over time. This publication serves to demonstrate an atypical radiological appearance of calcaneal intraosseous lipoma and contribute to the limited literature.

Conclusion

This case report intends to illustrate the change in appearance and composition of intraosseous lipomas over time.

Affine non-negative collaborative representation based pattern classification
Authors: He-Feng Yin, Xiao-Jun Wu, Zhen-Hua Feng, Josef Kittler, Zi-Qi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
During the past decade, representation-based classification methods have received considerable attention in pattern recognition. In particular, the recently proposed non-negative representation based classification (NRC) method has been reported to achieve promising results in a wide range of classification tasks. However, NRC has two major drawbacks. First, there is no regularization term in the formulation of NRC, which may result in unstable solution and misclassification. Second, NRC ignores the fact that data usually lies in a union of multiple affine subspaces, rather than linear subspaces in practical applications. To address the above issues, this paper presents an affine non-negative collaborative representation (ANCR) model for pattern classification. To be more specific, ANCR imposes a regularization term on the coding vector. Moreover, since each point in an affine subspace can be expressed as an affine combination of other points in this affine subspace, ANCR introduces an affine constraint to better represent the data from affine subspaces. The experimental results on several benchmarking datasets demonstrate the merits of the proposed ANCR method. Concretely, on the Hopkins and Aircraft datasets, ANCR achieves accuracy of 97.8% and 87.7%, respectively, which represents an improvement of 2.2% and 0.4% over NRC. The source code of our ANCR is publicly available at https://github.com/yinhefeng/ANCR.

Introduction

Sparse representation-based classification remains one of the hot research topics in pattern recognition. The most two widely studied approaches include the classic sparse representation based classification (SRC) [1] and collaborative representation based classification (CRC) [2]. Then the classification task is performed by checking which class yields the least reconstruction error (residual) of the test sample. SRC can achieve impressive classification performance in face recognition even when the test face image is occluded or corrupted. Based on the classic SRC and CRC methods, researchers have proposed a variety of improved methods. Lai et al. [3] developed a class-wise sparse representation method that seeks an optimum representation of the query image by minimizing the class-wise sparsity of the training data. Akhtar et al. [4] presented a sparsity augmented CRC (SA-CRC) scheme that augments a dense collaborative representation with a sparse representation. Inspired by SA-CRC, Li et al. [5] proposed a sparsity augmented weighted CRC approach for image recognition. By employing only a subset of known pattern classes to collaboratively represent a test sample, Zheng et al. [6] designed a k nearest classes-based CRC scheme. Zhou et al. [7] devised a low-rank and sparse representation-based deep unfolding network (LRSRNet) for hyperspectral image change detection. To exploit the advantages of sparse coding, multi-scale wavelet representation and neural networks, Nguyen et al. [8] proposed a multi-branch wavelet neural network (MB-WNN) architecture for sparse representation-based object classification. Mi et al. [9] presented an elastic competitive and discriminative collaborative representation based classifier (ECDCRC) to enhance the discrimination of CRC. Su et al. [10] developed a mean-weighted collaborative representation classification model (MWCRC) which imposes mean and weighted constraints on the representation coefficients. Yang et al. [11] proposed a new fuzzy-based deep attributed graph clustering model that performs clustering task in a fully unsupervised and end-to-end manner.

Xu et al. [12] pointed out that there exist negative elements in the coefficients obtained by SRC, CRC and their variants, which may result in misclassification of test samples. Motivated by non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) [13], they proposed a non-negative representation based classifier (NRC) that imposes a non-negative constraint on the coding vector. Extensive experiments on diverse classification tasks demonstrate the superiority of NRC over many existing representation based classification methods (RBCM), including SRC, CRC and their variants. Nevertheless, NRC has two shortcomings. First, due to the lack of regularization term on the coding vector, NRC may produce unstable solutions [14]. Second, NRC cannot effectively deal with the data drawn from affine subspaces. To alleviate these drawbacks, we propose an affine non-negative collaborative representation (ANCR) model, in which a regularizer on the coding vector and the affine constraint are introduced. To illustrate the mechanism of ANCR, we conduct an experiment on the USPS dataset. This dataset contains images for digits 0-9. 50 images per class are used to form the training set. Consider a test sample from the ninth class (i.e., digit 8). The coding vector and residual obtained by NRC are shown in Fig. 1a, b. The coding vector and residual obtained by ANCR are shown in Fig. 1c, d. From Fig. 1b, we can see that the eighth class has the least residual, i.e., the test sample is wrongly recognized as digit 7. Meanwhile, it can be seen in Fig. 1a that the eighth class has dominant coefficients. From Fig. 1d, we note that the ninth class results in the minimum residual, i.e., the test sample is correctly recognized as digit 8. When we look more closely at the coding vectors of NRC and ANCR for the eighth class (indices 351-400), the number of nonzero entries are 7 and 14, respectively. In contrast, for the ninth class (indices 401-450), the number of nonzero entries of NRC and ANCR are 16 and 41, respectively. By introducing the coding vector regularizer and affine constraint into NRC, more coefficients are concentrated on the correct class, resulting in improved performance.

Related work

In recent years, the non-negative constraint has attracted considerable attention in various research areas. For instance, Chen et al. [16] designed a non-negative representation based discriminative dictionary learning algorithm (N-RDL) for face classification. Xu et al. [17] proposed a scaled simplex representation (SSR) for subspace clustering. Xu et al. [14] designed a jointly non-negative, sparse and collaborative representation (NSCR) for image recognition. Zhao et al. [18] developed a Laplacian regularized non-negative representation (LapNR) method for clustering and dimensionality reduction tasks. Inspired by NRC, Yin et al. [19] explored a class-specific residual constraint non-negative representation (CRNR) for pattern classification. Zhou et al. [20] proposed a kernel non-negative representation-based classifier (KNRC) to perform representation and classification in the kernel feature space. By introducing competition representation term and locality constraint into the formulation of NRC, Li et al. [21] presented a locality-constraint discriminative nonnegative representation (LDNR) method for pattern classification.

The affine constraint has also been widely exploited in clustering and classification tasks. In sparse subspace clustering (SSC), Elhamifar et al. [22, 23] introduced the affine constraint to deal with affine subspaces. Dong et al. [24] presented a sparse representation based affine subspace clustering method, which employs the non-convex smoothed \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\ell _0$$\end{document}ℓ0-norm to replace the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\ell _0$$\end{document}ℓ0-norm. In locality-constrained linear coding (LLC) [25], the affine constraint is also referred to as the shift-invariant constraint. Yuan et al. [26] proposed a double discriminative constraint-based affine nonnegative representation (DCANR) for few-shot remote sensing scene classification. Recently, Li et al. Ablation study

Discussions

In the above experiments, our proposed ANCR method performs better or comparable to other competing approaches on different tasks, including motion segmentation, face recognition, handwritten digit classification, action recognition and fine-grained object classification. On the Hopkins dataset, where the data are drawn from affine subspaces. Due to the introduction of affine constraint, our proposed ANCR outperforms NRC for the motion segmentation task. ANCR also performs better than other conventional representation based classification methods and some deep learning based approaches. Ablation studies further demonstrate that both the non-negative constraint and the affine constraint contribute to the performance gain of ANCR. Essentially, NRC is the non-negative least squares model. By introducing the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\ell _2$$\end{document}ℓ2-norm regularizer on the coding vector and the affine constraint into the formulation of NRC, our proposed ANCR can deal with data from affine subspaces as well as linear subspaces. Limitation of study

The same as most of the representation based classification methods, our proposed ANCR needs clean training data (i.e., no occlusion or corruption). When providing occluded or corrupted training samples, the performance of ANCR will be degraded to some extent. Here we provide an example on the AR database to evaluate the influence of occluded training samples on the classification result of ANCR. For each individual, seven images from Session 1 are used as training samples and another seven clean images from Session 2 as test samples. Each subject has six occluded images in Session 1, thus we randomly select k \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(k = 0, 1,\ldots , 6)$$\end{document}(k=0,1,…,6) occluded images per person to form the training set, k = 0 indicates that only the seven clean images per subject are used as training samples. Figure 9 presents the recognition accuracy against the number of occluded training samples per class, we can see that with the increasing number of occluded training images per subject, recognition accuracy of ANCR drops from 93.3% to 81.5%. To counteract this problem, one can apply existing low rank matrix recovery techniques like accelerated proximal alternating linearized minimization (AccPALM) [47] or capped reweighting norm minimization (CRNM) [48], which can alleviate the above limitation for ANCR.

Conclusion

To further enhance the classification performance of NRC, in this paper, we developed an affine non-negative collaborative representation (ANCR) model. ANCR is derived by introducing a regularizer on the coding vector, as well as an affine constraint into the formulation of NRC. Our proposed ANCR method is solved elegantly by ADMM. The experimental results on well-known datasets demonstrate the superiority of the proposed ANCR method over NRC and traditional representation based classification methods. The proposed method also outperforms some deep learning based approaches.

Flexible economic energy management including environmental indices in heat and electrical microgrids considering heat pump with renewable and storage systems
Authors: Mohamed Bechir Ben Hamida, Ali B. M. Ali, Narinderjit Singh Sawaran Singh, Sasan Pirouzi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study discusses energy management in thermal and electrical microgrids while taking heat pumps, renewable sources, thermal and hydrogen storages into account. The weighted total of the operating cost, grid emissions level, voltage and temperature deviation function, and other factors makes up the objective function of the suggested method. The restrictions include the operation-flexibility model of resources and storages, micro-grid flexibility limits, and optimum power flow equations. Point Estimation Method is used in this work to simulate load, energy price, and renewable phenomenon uncertainty. A fuzzy decision-making methodology is used to arrive at a compromise solution that satisfies network operators’ operational, environmental, and financial goals. The innovations of this paper include energy management of various smart microgrids, simultaneous modeling of several indicators especially flexibility, investigation of optimal performance of resources and storage devices, and modeling of uncertainty considering low computational time and an accurate flexibility model. Numerical findings indicate that the fuzzy decision-making approach has the capability to reach a compromise point in which the objective functions approach their minimum values. The integration of the proposed uncertainty modeling with precise flexibility modeling results in a reduction in computational time when compared to stochastic optimization based on scenarios. For the compromise point and uncertainty modeling with PEM, by efficiently managing resources and thermal and hydrogen storages, scheme is capable of attaining high flexibility conditions. Compared to load flow studies, the approach can enhance the operational, environmental, and economic conditions of smart microgrids by approximately 33–57%, 68%, and 33–68%, respectively, under these circumstances.

Introduction

Motivation

A considerable quantity of thermal and electrical energy is consumed at the majority of sites of consumption. The utilization of renewable energy sources (RES) is highly advantageous for the provision of these energies in an environmentally sustainable manner. Electrical energy can be generated by the preponderance of RES, such as photovoltaics (PVs) and wind turbines (WTs). To generate thermal energy from RES, heat pumps (HPs) are an essential component1. An alternative variety of RES, referred to as the bio-waste unit (BU), transforms waste materials from the environment into methane2. When this apparatus is outfitted with combined heat and power (CHP) technology, it has the capability to generate electrical and thermal energy concurrently. It is essential to observe, nevertheless, that the power produced by RES is subject to uncertainty3. This concern reduces the adaptability of the system, and it is possible that the equilibrium between production and consumption conditions will not be preserved throughout the system’s real-time operation3. In order to optimize performance and mitigate the impact of power fluctuations on RESs during real-time operation as opposed to day-ahead operation, it is imperative to incorporate power-controlling elements into the network4. Storage systems may be included among these components4. As a result, substantial numbers of energy sources and storage systems are anticipated to be incorporated into energy distribution networks. When considering efficient energy management for networks, the implementation of microgrids (MGs) is considered more appropriate5. A MG is characterized by its limited range, storage capacity, and resources in comparison to the distribution network. As a result, the operator is able to implement energy management of storage systems and resources within the MG at a faster processing pace. In such conditions, the distribution network interacts solely with MG operators, which accelerates the processing speed of the Distribution System Operator (DSO)5. Numerous technical, economic, and environmental objectives are anticipated to be enhanced as a consequence of optimal energy management being implemented in electrical and thermal MGs in the presence of RES, HPs, and energy storage systems.

Research background

A considerable body of research has been devoted to the topic of energy management in MGs. The authors of6 analyze the involvement of flexible renewable energy centers, which include bio-waste units, wind fields, hydrogen, thermal, and compressed air storage systems, in the energy market by utilizing the market clearing price model. The BU farm (BUF) is equipped with CHP technology for the production of electrical and thermal energy. The proposed design is executed through the utilization of bi-level optimization. Maximizing the expected profit of the hub is determined by the upper level of the system. The market clearing price strategy is integrated at the lower formulation level. The topic of reference7 pertains to the energy management of flexible energy centers that are connected to the infrastructure. In formulating the problem, uncertainties pertaining to demand, the cost of electricity and heat, and renewable energy sources were also taken into account. In7, the unscented transformation (UT) technique was implemented. A framework for daily optimal scheduling of a grid-connected MG based on optimal harmonic power flow is presented in reference8. Power quality assessment is conducted within the established framework through the examination of three indices: voltage magnitude, total harmonic distortion of voltage, and voltage unbalance factor. In9, a comprehensive energy scheduling model for MGs that incorporates risk is suggested as a solution to the unpredictability of RESs. The operational risk of an uncertainty set is assessed through the utilization of historical data. It models uncertainty parameters with adjustable convex hull based uncertainty set (ACHUS). The authors of10 present an innovative methodology that tackles the complex difficulties linked to energy centers, with a specific emphasis on various concerns related to energy production and transmission, specifically in the domains of gas and electrical networks. Profitability is a primary concern for energy centers, which must navigate through the increased unpredictability caused by the presence of numerous energy carriers. The energy core of the proposed model is comprised of natural gas, electricity, and thermal energy inputs and outputs.

An effective and user-friendly real-time energy management and control system based on multiple agents is devised in11 for MGs that utilize plug-in electric vehicles and construct prosumers. The principal aim of the suggested methodology is to ascertain the most effective power set-points for every MG component in order to minimize daily operation costs in the face of fluctuating electricity costs, while also meeting a diverse set of constraints. A novel framework for enhancing the resilience of MGs is introduced in12. This framework is founded upon the power-to-hydrogen concept and the autonomous operation capability of MGs, specifically in islanded mode. To achieve this goal, a resilient operational model for MGs is being developed; this model integrates compressed hydrogen produced by power-to-hydrogen systems, which can be sold to other sectors or used to power fuel cells and generate electricity. In reference13, the optimal operation of MGs with electrical and thermal loads was discussed. The decision maker has assessed a portfolio comprising of the following energy resources: CHP units, furnaces, wind turbines, storage devices, Demand Response Resources, and the possibility of power exchange with the upstream wholesale market. MG operators strive to meet the thermal and electrical requirements of the network in the most adaptable and economical fashion feasible. An economic and reliability-focused risk-based multi-objective energy exchange optimization for networked MGs under uncertain load consumption and renewable power production conditions is presented in14. Utilizing the conditional value at risk method, three distinct risk-based solutions are identified for this purpose. There are two different approaches to specify the suggested model as an objective function. While the second function decreases the quantity of energy that is not delivered, the first function minimizes the expenses associated with operation and maintenance, power loss, and power exchange between the upstream grid and MGs. Furthermore, this strategy to manage uncertainties incorporates a scenario-based stochastic optimization (SBSO). A mixed-integer quadratic programming based bi-level model is proposed in15 for the best possible functioning of MGs in the worst-case situations of outages of renewable energy resources. The upper-level issue formulates the best practices for charging and discharging electric cars and energy storage systems, as well as the reduction of energy loss and load shedding in a demand-side management program. In order to account for the worst-case scenario of renewable resource failures, the lower-level issue simulates the maximizing of curtailment of renewable energy. Contributions

To address the research gaps indicated in the preceding part, this section presents a model for managing both electrical and thermal MGs simultaneously. This model takes into account the existence of wind farms (WFs), photovoltaic farms (PVFs), BUFs, hydrogen storage (HS), TES, and HP. It is consistent with the economic, operational, environmental, and flexibility goals for MG operators (MGOs) shown in Fig. 1. The BUF is fitted with CHP technology, allowing it to generate both electrical and thermal energy. The HP is also intended to convert the active power of RESs and electrical motors into thermal power. The suggested technique in this work contains an objective function that seeks to reduce the weighted sum of anticipated operating cost (EOC), voltage and temperature deviation function (VTF), and expected emissions level (EEM). This function is limited by various limitations, including the Optimal Power Flow (OPF) model in electrical and thermal MGs, the operation model of RESs, HS, TES, and HP, and the flexibility model of MGs and FSs. This strategy is subject to uncertainties in energy price, wind speed, BU gas, sun radiation, and load. To resolve these uncertainties, this work uses PEM. This technique may take less time to compute since it includes fewer situations. Furthermore, it is based on stochastic optimization, which satisfies the requirements for exact flexibility modeling in this technique. The suggested methodology is a kind of multi-objective optimization in which a fuzzy decision-making process is used to discover a compromise solution. By minimizing the VTF, it is expected that the deviation of voltage and temperature from the desired value (such as 1 p.u.) is as small. Therefore, it is possible to establish voltage and temperature stability conditions in electrical and thermal microgrids.

H) Flexibility regulation model: A few examples of the variables that affect the production capacity of RESs include wind speed, solar radiation, and environmental waste. Predictive procedures provide the data for these parameters. The output capacity of renewable sources is variable and non-deterministic due to the inherent inaccuracies in projections3. Because of this, the day-ahead operation outcomes (system operation matching to the anticipated values of RES parameters) and the real-time operation outcomes of a system with a RES diverge4. As a result, during real-time operation, an imbalance in production and consumption is possible4. When the system’s adaptability is low, this problem develops4. The use of FSs with the capacity to control their power in a system with RES, such as RESs, storage, load response, and HPs, is required to make up for this lack of flexibility6. They can now make up for variations in renewable electricity while operating in real-time as opposed to day-ahead. Due to their capacity for power management, HSs, TESs, and HPs are regarded in this work as FSs in electrical and thermal MGs. The flexibility constraints of the networks, similar to constraints (36, 37), are used to describe the flexibility adjustment of the previously stated MGs. In day-ahead and real-time operations, the power deviation of the MG should be reduced from the viewpoint of the upstream network (in this study, comparable to the power crossing the distribution post located at the reference bus) in order to maximize the flexibility of the MG. This may also be phrased as follows: across diverse conditions, the power deviation of the MG should be lowered when compared to the scenario with the predicted data of uncertainty parameters (which in this instance is comparable to scenario 1). The work limits (36) and (37) address this issue. The tolerance for flexibility is expressed by ΔF in these limitations. Accordingly, the difference between the active (thermal) power of HS (HP and TES) in this scenario and scenario 1 corresponds to the flexibility power of HS in that scenario. The parameters σEOC, σVTF, and σEEM show the weight coefficients of the EOC, VTF, and EEM functions in the objective function (1), respectively. These coefficients have values ranging from 0 to 1, and their total must always equal 122. As a result, the EOC, VTF, and EEM functions have varying values depending on the weight coefficients. Plotting these values in three-dimensional coordinates displays the suggested approach’s Pareto front22. Subsequently, the fuzzy decision-making method23 extracts the optimal compromise point between the EOC, VTF, and EEM functions. In this method, the maximum (Fmax) and minimum (Fmin) values of different functions are initially provided for three cases: σEOC = 1, σVTF = 1, and σEEM = 1. Next, the value of the linear fuzzy membership function (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat {F}$$\end{document}) for the mentioned functions is computed. Otherwise, is equal to (F – Fmax)/(Fmin – Fmax). Then, for a specific set of weight coefficients, the minimum value of the linear fuzzy membership function among various functions is extracted, denoted by α in this paper. The calculation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat {F}$$\end{document} and α is performed for all members of the Pareto front. At last, the compromise solution is equal to a point from the Pareto front that has the highest α value23. The steps of implementing the fuzzy decision-making method are summarized in Algorithm 1.

PEM-based uncertainty modelling

The quantities related to active, reactive, and heat demand (shown by respectively PC, QC, and HC), generation power rate of PV, BU and WT (ϕPV, ϕBU, and ϕWT), as well as electricity and thermal energy prices (λE and λH), are considered uncertain quantities24,25 in the suggested problem (45, 46). As a result, the unknown parameters are represented in this part by PEM based on stochastic programming26. The mean and standard deviation of many unknown parameters are constant. The PEM is used to gather samples for the final scenario, using the 2m + 1 approach26. Within this approach, the variable “m” indicates the total number of unknown parameters, whereas the formula “2m + 1” denotes the total number of final scenario samples retrieved using the PEM technique. The model of PEM 2 m + 1 is stated26. Full details of this method are provided in26 and Algorithm 2.

A) Evaluation of the Compromise Solution Between Economic, Operational, and Environmental Objectives of MG Operators: The Pareto front of the proposed method for a flexibility tolerance of 0.05 MW (ΔF = 0.05 MW) is illustrated in Table 3. The Pareto front considers various weight coefficients as follows: 0, 0.25, 0.33, 0.5, 0.75, and 1. The weight coefficients can have different values, and there is no limit to the selection of different values. However, it should be noted that the values ​​1 and 0 were used for these coefficients so that the minimum and maximum values ​​of different functions could be calculated. The value 0.33 was chosen so that the results for the same weight coefficient were presented for different objective functions. In other words, in these conditions, the importance of the presence of different functions in the objective function (45) is the same. The value 0.75 was chosen so that the importance of the presence of one function is greater than the other functions. When 0.75 is selected, its complement to 1 is 0.25. Therefore, the coefficient 0.25 is also created for the weight coefficients. The fluctuations in economic, operational, and environmental functions do not exhibit a unidirectional trend, as indicated by the data in this table. The VTF is increased when the cost of network energy operation (EOC) is minimized. The potential consequences of injecting high power from sources and storages to the MG in order to achieve economic goals (operation cost minimization) include elevated voltage and temperature in the electrical and thermal MGs. The VTF consequently increases. Table 4 displays the results of uncertainty modeling using PEM and SBSO. The SBSO technique combines the Kantorovich method with the roulette wheel mechanism. The method’s execution technique is described in reference15. The roulette wheel mechanism creates a large number of scenarios at the start of this procedure; the number of these scenarios is computed using the mean value and standard deviation of each scenario. The chance that certain numbers will reflect uncertainties is then calculated using the normal probability function. Furthermore, the product of the probabilities of uncertainty represents the chance of every potential result. After that, the Kantorovich approach, which serves as a way of scenario reduction, uses a fixed number of created scenarios that are near to each other to the issue that has to be solved. In this work, the roulette wheel mechanism generates two thousand situations. The subsequent values of the objective functions, obtained from the Kantorovich method, are presented in Table 4. The scenarios include 40, 60, 80, 100, and 120. Section 3 specifies seventeen PEM scenarios. Notwithstanding the limited number of scenarios, a dependable optimal solution has been obtained. Conclusions

In summary, this research article has investigated the functioning of electrical and thermal micro-grids, while considering the presence of heat pumps, thermal, and hydrogen storage systems, as well as the various objectives of system operators. The selected objective function, in addition to the voltage and temperature deviation functions, sought to minimize the operating expenses and emissions of microgrids. The equations governing the optimal distribution of power in microgrids, the operation model incorporating storage and resource considerations, and the flexibility model incorporating flexibility sources all influenced this objective function. In order to derive a compromise solution, fuzzy decision-making was implemented. To simulate the uncertainties associated with renewable phenomena, energy price, and demand, the Point Estimation Method was utilized. The numerical findings indicate that imprecise decision-making has the ability to reach a compromise point in which the objective functions approach their minimum values. Maximum voltage and temperature deviation, emissions, energy losses, and operation cost of microgrids have all decreased by about 22.1%, 19.3%, and 19%, respectively, since their minimal values at the compromise point. The Point Estimation Method requires significantly less computation time than scenario-based stochastic optimization. For the compromise point and uncertainty modeling with PEM, in comparison to load distribution studies in electrical and thermal microgrids, the economic, environmental, and operational viability of the proposed method have been enhanced by about 60%, 68%, and 33–57%, respectively, through the management of energy in hydrogen and thermal storage systems. By implementing efficient storage and heat pump management, the aforementioned microgrids have achieved conditions of complete flexibility.

In the proposed scheme, the PEM technique was used to model uncertainties. This method has a low number of scenarios, so it can reduce computational time, and establish flexible accurate modeling conditions. These goals are also applicable to the unscented transformation (UT) method. While UT distributes sigma points around the mean, PEM relies on a smaller set of representative points. From this perspective, UT may provide higher accuracy. Therefore, the proposed scheme based on UT is considered as future work. In addition, electric vehicles are new consumers in the power system, and a significant number of them are expected to be present in the power system in the near future. Their energy management makes electric vehicles act as a mobile storage device. The features of the proposed scheme including the electric vehicle model will be investigated in future work. Demand side management is an energy management method that has significant efficiency. The proposed scheme with this issue was considered as future work. In the proposed design, an almost linear model for renewable resources and heat pumps has been used. Because in this design, the output power and input energy of the systems were required to be related. But these elements actually have nonlinear models related to their internal structure. In addition, bio-waste units consume gas to produce energy. Gas consumption also causes pollution. On the other hand, bio-waste units consume environmental waste. This leads to a reduction in pollution. Therefore, it is necessary for the proposed design to include an environmental model of bio-waste units. This issue along with nonlinear modeling of elements in the proposed design is considered as future work.

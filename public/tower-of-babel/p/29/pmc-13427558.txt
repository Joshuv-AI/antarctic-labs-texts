Estimation of Random effect Meta-Analytic Structural Equation Modeling (MASEM) Using Generalized Method of Moments (GMM)
Authors: Rahmawati Erma Standsyah, Bambang Widjanarko Otok
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Meta-Analytic Structural Equation Modeling (MASEM) is a correlation-based meta-analysis, so the type of data is a multivariate correlation effect size of the studied study. The effect size of MASEM is likely to be heterogeneous; therefore, MASEM, which exhibits heterogeneity issues, is a random-effects model. The heterogeneity problem must be overcome by choosing the best estimation method. The generalized method of moments (GMM) is an estimation method that overcomes heterogeneity problems. The estimation of the parameters of the MASEM random effects model using the GMM method fulfills the quality characteristics of the estimator being unbiased and consistent. This research brings a new approach to the estimation of MASEM parameters, whereas previous studies relied mainly on simulation-based or computational approaches to obtain MASEM estimates. The main features of this study are as follows:•This study focuses on effect sizes obtained from correlations in the ordinary structural equation model, without intermediate or moderating variables.•Variance between studies in the estimation process is addressed using lag in time series theory, which allows us to consider the main research problem, namely heterogeneity.

Background

Current trends in research show that many studies apply the measurement model to assess the influencing factor, where the discussion has the same topic but various characteristics and results. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) is a technique of multivariate statistical analysis [1,2], which is part of measurement models and is frequently used in time and space to solve many global problems having the same purpose or objective. Meta-Analytical Structural Equation Modeling (MASEM) is a statistical method used to examine the types of data from scientific studies to strengthen the conclusions of the overall results obtained [3]. MASEM is a correlation-based meta-analysis, so the type of data is in the form of a multivariate correlation of the size of the effect of the research studied [4].

The homogeneity of the MASEM model, as well as the Meta-Analysis, is divided into two types, namely the MASEM fixed-effect model and the MASEM random-effect model [5,6]. The difference between the two models lies in the variance, where the fixed effects model assumes that the population effect is homogeneous, while the random effects model is heterogeneous. Published research results show that homogeneous effect sizes are rare because it is difficult to find studies producing identical results. This means that the effect size includes a random effect model.

Research on the development of the MASEM estimator begins with the estimation of parameters using the Generalized Least Squares (GLS) method., [7,8], and Weighted Least Squares (WLS) method or TSSEM [4,9,10], and the maximum likelihood method [11]. This estimation is used to obtain the parameters of the fixed-effects model. Still, for estimating the MASEM random-effects model, there is an additional process: estimating the between-study variance. The development of an estimator method to estimate the between-study variance in the random-effect MASEM on the Becker model using iterations of the EM algorithm [7,8] and the method of moments [12]. The preceding development was not carried out analytically or using a theoretical statistical approach, but rather through simulation or calculation. This naturally led to less accurate MASEM estimates.

The current development of the MASEM method is evident not only in its methodological aspects but also in the growing interest it is generating in applied research. Several recent studies have begun using MASEM to analyze the relationships between psychological concepts [13,14], organizational behavior [15], education [[16], [17], [18]], environment [19] and other social sciences [20,21]. This increasing use of MASEM in empirical research demonstrates its growing popularity and relevance. This situation also underscores the importance of developing a theoretical approach to MASEM to account for the ever-increasing complexity of the data.

The random effect of MASEM has heterogeneity issues, so there is a need to develop an estimation method that can overcome the heterogeneity issue. The GMM method (Generalized Method of Moments), which has a high level of precision on the heterogeneity of the data [22,23], was used to estimate the fixed effect of MASEM [1] but was not used to estimate the random effect of MASEM. Therefore, this study aims to obtain the random effects estimator MASEM with the GMM method, which has not been studied before.

In line with these developments, the contribution of this research is not limited to theoretical development but can also serve as a basis for methodological advancements in future research, particularly in artificial intelligence (AI) [24,25] and machine learning [26,27]. This demonstrates that data-driven approaches are increasingly used to complement mathematical and computational models in order to improve model validation, computational efficiency, and predictive capabilities in various application areas. Recent studies also confirm that integrating AI with theoretical models and conventional estimation methods can lead to more efficient approaches without abandoning mathematical foundations or statistical inference [28].

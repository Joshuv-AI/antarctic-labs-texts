A Chinese infant with CACNA1C p.C1021R variant expands clinical phenotype of CACNA1C-related disorder
Authors: Congshan Jiang, Yafei Zhou, Siyu Zhang, Xiaoxiao Lu, Xia Li, Yanmin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
CACNA1C-related disorder (CRD), a severe multisystem disorder caused by variants of CACNA1C gene, presents significant diagnostic and management challenges due to its rarity and variable expressivity. This study leverages a detailed longitudinal case report and comprehensive literature review to expand the phenotypic spectrum of CRD with Timothy syndrome (TS) features associated with the CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: c.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant. We describe an individual with CRD exhibiting a severe multisystem phenotype including classic features like syndactyly, profound neurological symptoms, and cardiac arrhythmias. Critically, our detailed clinical and genetic analysis identified previously undocumented features for this specific variant: cerebral infarction, as well as a combined T-cell and B-cell immunodeficiency characterized by specific lymphocyte dysregulation. These novel findings necessitate refined diagnostic protocols and tailored management strategies. By redefining CRD with TS features as a profoundly multisystem disorder, this expanded understanding facilitates improved prognostication, genetic counseling, and targeted therapeutic interventions, aiming to transform management from unpredictable complications into predictable, preventable components, ultimately enhancing quality of life and survival.

Introduction

Timothy syndrome (TS) is a profoundly debilitating and often lethal multisystem channelopathy that underscores the critical role of calcium signaling in physiological homeostasis. This rare disorder is genetically defined by de novo missense mutations in the CACNA1C gene, which encodes the pore-forming α1-subunit of the L-type calcium channel, Cav1.2 [1], [2]. These gain-of-function variants lead to delayed voltage-dependent inactivation of the channel, causing pathologically sustained calcium influx during depolarization. Clinically, TS is characterized by a classic triad of severe QT interval prolongation predisposing to life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias, syndactyly, and significant neurodevelopmental symptoms, often including autism spectrum disorder [3]. While canonical forms like TS1 (p.Gly406Arg) and TS2 (p.Gly402Ser) are established, the genotypic and phenotypic spectrum of Cav1.2 channelopathies continues to expand with the identification of novel variants. Delineating precise genotype-phenotype correlations is paramount for accurate prognostication, tailoring anticipatory medical management, and informing the development of targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at correcting specific channel biophysical defects [4], [5].

As genetic testing becomes more comprehensive, novel variants necessitating detailed clinical characterization are emerging. Among these is the p.Cys1021Arg variant, located in exon 24 of the CACNA1C gene, which has been reported in only 2 cases [4], [6]. Due to the extreme rarity of this specific variant, the associated comprehensive natural history, long-term outcomes, and full spectrum of comorbidities remain incompletely defined. This critical gap limits the ability to provide tailored management and informed genetic counseling. Herein, we present a detailed, longitudinal assessment of a newly diagnosed individual with CRD harboring the CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: c.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant, documenting the clinical evolution from infancy and revealing several previously underreported features. By integrating our primary clinical data with a systematic analysis of all existing literature on this specific variant, we aim to synthesize a more holistic and precise clinical profile, contextualizing its manifestations within the broader understanding of CACNA1C-related disorder with TS features [7]. This work highlights the necessity of meticulous documentation for ultra-rare disease management and molecular understanding.

Case presentation

General information

The individual with CRD was delivered vaginally at 38+4 weeks to a G1P1 mother (birth weight 3250 g). Routine pediatric assessment (prior to the onset of acute gastroenteritis at 5 months of age) detected mild gross motor delay. Acute presentation and initial diagnosis

At five months of age, the infant presented with a severe clinical crisis initiated by acute gastroenteritis (fever, frequent watery stools, vomiting). This rapidly escalated to status epilepticus, characterized by unresponsiveness, fixed gaze, and generalized limb tremors, necessitating admission to the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU). Cranial computed tomography and subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a catastrophic neurological injury: cerebral infarction primarily affecting the right middle cerebral artery territory at 5.8 months old, as well as multiple foci of encephalomalacia and surrounding gliosis in the right frontotemporoparietal and left parietal lobes at the 16-day follow-up at 6.2 months old (Fig. 1E). Electroencephalography showed persistent hemispheric asymmetry and reduced voltage on the right side, consistent with significant structural damage. Ancillary findings included a small pericardial effusion and bilateral pneumonia.

Comprehensive systemic and immunological assessment

Extensive laboratory investigation during the acute phase highlighted profound multisystem derangements. Biochemical analysis showed severe dehydration and metabolic acidosis, alongside electrolyte imbalances (elevated chloride, decreased potassium and sodium). Markers of cellular injury were elevated, including lactate dehydrogenase, alpha-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase, and aspartate aminotransferase (AST), suggesting acute damage across cardiac, hepatic, and muscular tissues. Initial infectious workup confirmed co-infection with Enterovirus 71-IgM and Rotavirus, with subsequent blood and sputum next-generation sequencing identifying Rotavirus A, Human Polyomavirus 1, and Escherichia coli.

Crucially, the individual with CRD demonstrated significant immunological dysregulation. Lymphocyte subset analysis revealed T-cell lymphopenia (affecting total, helper, and cytotoxic T cells) coupled with B-cell expansion. Serum immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) were all below reference ranges, indicating hypogammaglobulinemia. Furthermore, a positive anti-dsDNA antibody suggested an autoimmune component. These findings collectively pointed toward an underlying combined immunodeficiency.

Genetic diagnosis and evolving neurological sequelae

Given the complex multisystem involvement—including syndactyly, acute encephalopathy, systemic inflammation, and immune dysfunction—trio whole-exome sequencing was performed. This analysis identified a de novo heterozygous CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: c.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant. The pathogenicity of this variant has been reported before in HGMD and ClinVar databases [6], [8]. It was detected in two cases [6], [8], one diagnosed as Timothy syndrome, and the other diagnosed as CACNA1C-related disorders with long QT syndrome, and one of them was reported as a de novo variant [6]. Our individual with CRD also carries the de novo variant (PS2). For CACNA1C gene, missense variants are the cause of the disease, and the proportion of benign variants in the gene is small (PP2). Multiple statistical methods predicted that the variant would have a deleterious effect on the gene or gene product (PP3). The variant was not found in the normal control population in the ESP database, the 1000 Genomes database, and the EXAC database (PM2_Supporting). Hence, according to the ACMG/AMP criteria, this variant was classified as a likely pathogenic variant. This is the only likely pathogenic variant and no other pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were detected in the Trio-WES analysis.

Following stabilization, the individual with CRD was diagnosed with hemiplegia, encephalomalacia, and functional dysphagia secondary to the cerebral infarction. Initial supportive treatment involved antiseizure medication (levetiracetam replaced topiramate), coenzyme Q10, and l-carnitine. Rehabilitation was initiated early to address poor visual fixation and left limb hemiparesis.

Progressive Cardiological and neurological surveillance

Cardiovascular assessment confirmed an atrial septal defect and arrhythmia. A resting 12‑lead electrocardiogram (ECG) at seven months old showed sinus rhythm and a corrected QT (QTc) interval of 447 ms (Fig. 1F-1H). Holter monitoring confirmed intermittent mild QTc prolongation (maximum 460 ms), leading to a diagnosis of CACNA1C-related disorder with TS features.

Neurological challenges persisted. At 9 months, there is a readmission for seizure management. At 11 months, readmission for recurrent seizures highlighted a new seizure type (head nodding and sudden flexion) and progressive structural deterioration on MRI (Fig. 1E), which revealed bilateral cerebral atrophy and encephalomalacia, more prominent on the right side. Furthermore, 24-h Holter monitoring demonstrated the emergence of significant sick sinus syndrome, characterized by marked sinus arrhythmia and profound sinus pauses (maximum recorded pause >2.4 s, Fig. 1I-J).

Specifically, regarding the patient's immune system symptoms, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) support therapy was administered during the hospitalization. Furthermore, to monitor for potential recurrent infections or inflammation, we conducted a remote interview with the family at the 6-month post-discharge. The parents reported that the child's immune-related phenotype remains healthy and normal, with no acute infections or significant inflammatory reactions occurring during the follow-up period.

Molecular confirmation and phenotypic synthesis

The overall clinical course consistently demonstrated a multisystem phenotype integrating characteristic TS features: distinctive facial features, syndactyly, severe neurological morbidity (cerebral infarction, refractory epilepsy), intermittent sick sinus syndrome with pauses, immune dysfunction, and multiple infections (Table 1). Genetic confirmation by Sanger sequencing validated the de novo CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: c.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant which is located in exon 24 and affects a highly conserved residue (Fig. 2). Both the PredictSNP2 [9] consensus score and the constituent sub-platform scores (CADD, DANN, FATHMM, FunSeq2, and GWAVA) indicated that this variant is likely deleterious. Additionally, Mutpred2 [10] analysis revealed specific mechanistic impacts, such as alterations to the disordered interface and transmembrane protein characteristics. Literature review and discussion

Core pathophysiology and cardiac manifestations

Timothy syndrome (TS) is intrinsically linked to profound cardiac dysfunction, defined by severe QTc prolongation and malignant ventricular tachyarrhythmias, often leading to sudden cardiac death (SCD) with a reported mean mortality age of 2.5 years [3], [4], [5]. Beyond ventricular instability, profound sinus node dysfunction, characterized by functional 2:1 AV block and sinus bradycardia, is common in pediatric TS [4], [5]. Although TS is often deemed a “lethal disease,” early, aggressive intervention, including device therapy, has demonstrated improved survival even after severe events [11]. The individual with CRD exhibited certain classical TS features, including intermittent QTc prolongation (max 460 ms) and significant sinus pauses, indicative of underlying sick sinus syndrome. The individual with CRD presented an intermittent QTc prolongation near “normal”, the likely explanations include the incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity of the CACNA1C mutation, which can lead to a spectrum of cardiac phenotypes.

Extracardiac features and neurological sequelae

Extracardiac manifestations, including syndactyly, characteristic craniofacial features, neurodevelopmental delays, and seizures, are integral to the TS features [1], [5], [12]. Our case highlights a severe, yet less frequently documented, neurological complication: focal cerebral infarction. Severe neurological insults—such as hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) or infarction—are well-known consequences of life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias and arrest in TS population [4], [6]. The etiology of cerebral infarction in this individual with CRD appears to be multifactorial. First, we could not exclude primary vasculopathy due to lack of vessel imaging, so this remains a possibility. Second, secondary arrhythmia-related hypoxic-ischemic events represent a plausible mechanism; however, as no relevant examinations were conducted to document asystole or cardiac arrest before or during the cerebral infarction. Third, the condition may also be associated with critical illness during infection in this specific case. Prognosis for children having CRD with TS features experiencing major cerebrovascular events is generally poor, often resulting in severe disability or intractable epilepsy [4], [5], [12].

Epilepsy management and novel therapeutic avenues

Recurrent, intractable epilepsy is a common and severe neurological feature across different genetic subtypes of CRD with TS features, contributing significantly to morbidity [13], [14]. The severity of the epileptic phenotype, often presenting in infancy, underscores the profound impact of Cav1.2 channel dysfunction on early neurodevelopment. Standard anti-epileptic drugs often provide variable or limited success [6]. However, preclinical studies utilizing targeted interventions, such as antisense oligonucleotides, have successfully rescued aberrant neuronal calcium signaling and dendritic morphology in TS models [15], [16], [17]. This gene-targeted approach offers a powerful future strategy to mitigate intractable epilepsy and other neurological manifestations.

Immunodeficiency: A critical pathophysiological component

The presentation of this individual with CRD critically detailed a combined T-cell and B-cell immune dysfunction (possibly limited/self-resolving)—a recognized, yet variably expressed, component of the TS phenotype [4], [5], [18]. We documented T-cell lymphopenia (decreased total, helper, and cytotoxic T cells) alongside B-cell pathology, evidenced by hypogammaglobulinemia (decreased IgG, IgA, IgM). We hypothesize that sustained calcium influx compromises Ca2+-dependent T-lymphocyte maturation and differentiation, impairing cellular immunity [3], [19]. Our detailed data on concomitant B-cell expansion with impaired serum IgG, IgA, and IgM levels innovatively proposes a novel hypothesis regarding hypogammaglobulinemia [20]. Clinically, immunodeficiency leads to heightened susceptibility to multiple infections, which are not merely a source of morbidity but potent triggers for lethal cardiac arrhythmias.[18] Therefore, proactive management and prevention of infection are crucial, serving as primary strategies for arrhythmia prevention.

Expanded phenotypic Spectrum of CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: C.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant

Our detailed, longitudinal case, when synthesized with existing literature on the rare CACNA1C p.Cys1021Arg variant [6], [8], establishes a consistent, severe core clinical signature. This includes syndactyly, a cardiac phenotype characterized by QTc prolongation and high arrhythmic risk, and a neurological phenotype manifesting as epilepsy or seizure disorders. However, significant differences exist among the three cases regarding severity and specific system involvement. Our case, for instance, exhibits a relatively higher degree of complexity and severity: 1) distinct immunodeficiency profile: our case is characterized by “hypogammaglobulinemia,” and “positive anti-dsDNA antibodies,” which are indicative of combined immunodeficiency. While infection risks were noted in the reports by Nugud and Levy, further detailed characterization was not mentioned. 2) Catastrophic cerebrovascular events: whereas cerebral edema was reported in the case by Nugud, our patient suffered a cerebral infarction in the right middle cerebral artery territory. 3) Severe gastrointestinal and systemic inflammatory response: during the acute phase of gastrointestinal inflammation, our case presented with severe metabolic acidosis, multi-organ damage and severe enteric infections. Although the case described by Nugud also presented with intestinal issues (SID), our patient exhibited a systemic inflammatory storm and multi-organ failure.

Collectively, while these three cases share many similarities, they also display phenotypic heterogeneity. Our case represents the most severe disease presentation to date, particularly concerning the shared neurological and immune system involvement associated with this mutation. This observation underscores the highly variable expressivity of CACNA1C variants and emphasizes the systemic reach of this channelopathy, necessitating meticulous, longitudinal reporting to fully delineate the natural history of ultra-rare variants.

Conclusion

This case report details the severe, multisystem phenotype (Fig. 3) associated with the rare CACNA1C NM_001129830.1: c.3061 T > C (p.Cys1021Arg) variant. We documented a presentation of CRD with TS features amplified by novel findings, including combined T/B-cell immune dysfunction. This work reinforces the definition of CRD with TS features as a complex multisystem disorder where aggressive intervention against both cardiac and infectious triggers is crucial.

Epidemiological characteristics and interrupted time series analysis of mumps in Quzhou City, 2005–2023
Authors: Canya Fu, Wenjie Xu, Wangfeng Zheng, Xiaoying Gong, Quanjun Fang, Zhiying Yin, Canjie Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo analyze epidemiological characteristics of mumps in different periods and explore the impact of different immunization strategies of mumps containing vaccine (MuCV) on the incidence of mumps in Quzhou. We collected reported cases of mumps in Quzhou from 2005 to 2023 for descriptive epidemiological analysis. Interrupted time series (ITS) analysis was used to assess changes in mumps incidence during the one-dose, two-dose, and the three-dose MuCV vaccination period. From 2005 to 2023, a total of 10,295 cases of mumps were reported. The average annual reported incidence rates during three period, were 57.5/100,000 (7015 cases), 14.8/100,000 (2590 cases), and 6/100,000 (690 cases), respectively. The male-to-female ratio reported cases was 1.58:1, and most cases were aged 5–9 years, accounting for 43.7%. ITS analysis showed an immediate decrease in monthly mumps incidence of 3.3951/100,000 after two-dose MuCV vaccination period (p = .022). However, the monthly mumps incidence only decreased by 0.1191/100,000 immediately after the implementation of the three doses of MMR vaccination (p = .411). The monthly incidence of mumps showed a slight increase of 0.0052/100,000 in the one-dose vaccination period, followed by a decrease of 0.0126/100,000 in the two-dose vaccination period, and a further decrease of 0.0004/100,000 in the three-dose MMR period (p > .05). This study shows that timely introduction of two doses of MuCV and ensuring high vaccination coverage are beneficial in controlling mumps epidemics. Currently, the key populations affected are children and adolescents, emphasizing the importance of addressing outbreaks within school epidemics.KEYWORDS: Mumps, epidemiological characteristics, mumps-containing vaccine, immunization strategy, interrupted time series analysis

Introduction

Mumps is an acute respiratory infection caused by the mumps virus and characterized by painful swelling of one or both parotid glands.1 The main ways of transmission include direct contact, droplet transmission and contact with contaminated objects.2 It is most common in children and adolescents, and outbreaks trend to occur in schools and childcare centers.1 Since 2004, all cases of mumps in China have been mandated to be reported within 24 hours through the network of the National Mandatory Disease Reporting System (NNDRS). In 2021, China reported more than 119,000 cases of mumps, ranking first among countries and accounting for 53.3% of all global cases.3,4 Therefore, mumps remains a public health concern in China with a serious disease burden.

Vaccination with mumps-containing vaccine (MuCV) is a highly cost-effective preventive measure. Since the introduction of mumps vaccination worldwide, the incidence of mumps has decreased significantly.5 However, there are some differences in mumps immunization programs in different countries. In the United States, the first dose of live attenuated combined measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccine is administered at 12–15 months of age and the second dose at 4–6 years of age; in Germany, the first MMR vaccine is given at 11–14 months of age and the second at 15–23 months of age.6 The MuCV immunization program in Quzhou, China, has undergone several adjustments. From January 2005 to March 2008, children aged 18–24 months received one dose of MMR at their own expense. In April 2008, the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) included one dose of MMR vaccine and made it available free of charge to children aged 18–24 months.7 In September 2010, a Supplementary Immunization Activity (SIA) was implemented for all children aged 8 months to 4 years, providing free access to one dose of MM.7 In December 2018, the combined measles-rubella vaccine, which is given to children 8 months of age and junior high school students, was replaced with the MMR vaccine. This suggests that Quzhou began administering three doses of MMR during this period. It should be noted that this immunization strategy is currently implemented only in Zhejiang Province, China. However, the effect of vaccination strategies with different doses of MuCV on mumps incidence was unknown.

The interrupted time series (ITS) design is a quasi-experimental method that applies a segmented linear regression model to analyze the change in level and change in slope of the effects of intervening factors to assess the longitudinal effects of interventions, and has been used to evaluate a wide range of public health interventions.8–11 Previous research of the effectiveness MuCV have concentrated on alterations in serum antibodies12,13 or assessments of vaccine efficacy in defined populations.14 However, there has been a paucity of studies examining the impact of modifications in vaccination strategies on population morbidity. In this study, we analyzed the epidemiological characteristics of mumps in Quzhou City during the period of different vaccination strategies from 2005 to 2023. Additionally, we employed the ITS method to compare the changes in the incidence of mumps before and after the adjustment of the strategies and to investigate the impact of varying vaccination strategies on the incidence of mumps in the population.

Material and methods

Study design

Quzhou is a prefecture-level city situated in Zhejiang Province in eastern China. It covers an area of 8,844 square kilometers and has a resident population of approximately 2,218,000. Quzhou is strategically positioned at the confluence of four provinces: Fujian, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, and Anhui, which means Quzhou is a major transportation hub. Convenient transportation networks play an important role in the rapid spread of infectious diseases.

Based on the different doses of MuCV in Quzhou City, the period of this study can be divided into three stages. Stage A (1 January 2005 to 31 August 2010) is a one-dose vaccination period. Stage B (1 September 2010 to 30 November 2018) is a two-dose vaccination period. Stage C (1 December 2018 to 31 December 2023) is a three-dose vaccination period. The phasing of the MuCV vaccination strategy is shown in Figure 1. In this study, we employed an ITS design based on mumps incidence trends in Quzhou, China, from 2005 to 2023 to evaluate the effects of this extensive public health intervention on mumps incidence at the population level.

Data sources and case definition

Data on cases of mumps in Quzhou from January 2005 to December 2023 were obtained from the NNDRS and the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Cases were reported by medical and health institutions at all levels in Quzhou City and reviewed by CDC at all levels. Population data were obtained from the statistical bulletin of the Quzhou Municipal Statistics Bureau.

Mumps cases were diagnosed according to the diagnostic criteria for mumps approved by the Ministry of Health of the People’s Republic of China (WS270–2007). A clinical case of mumps was defined as a person with an acute episode of unilateral or bilateral swelling of the parotid or other salivary glands characterized by any of the following that could not be explained by another more likely diagnosis:1 fever, headache, weakness, and loss of appetite;2 orchitis;3 pancreatitis; and4 encephalitis and/or aseptic meningitis. A laboratory-confirmed case was defined as a clinical case with any of the following laboratory findings:1 IgM antibody positivity and no history of mumps vaccination within 1 month;2 greater than fourfold increase in serum IgG titer; and3 isolation of mumps virus from throat swab, urine, or cerebrospinal fluid.

MuCV vaccination coverage

Data on MuCV vaccination coverage were obtained from the Zhejiang Provincial Immunization Information System, a population-based computerized system containing demographic and vaccination data for all children aged less than 15 years living in the Zhejiang Province since 2005. This study provided vaccination coverage rates based on birth cohorts. We calculated MuCV vaccination coverage using the cumulative number of children who had received the MuCV up to the time of enumeration (December 2023), divided by the total number of children in the corresponding birth cohort.

Seasonal distribution-M value

Concentration M-value is an indicator of the magnitude of the strength of the seasonal characteristics of the disease and is calculated by the following formula15: Rx=r2+r6−r8−r122+32r3+r5−r9−r11+(r4−r10); Ry=r3−r5−r9+r112+32r2−r6−r8+r12+r1−r7; M=Rx2+Ry2; where R is the degree of dispersion and ri is the ratio of the number of cases in month i to the total number of cases in the year. M value used to characterize the seasonal distribution, ranging from 0 to 1. M = 1 indicates that the incidence is concentrated in a particular month; M > 0.9 indicates that the incidence is strictly seasonal; 0.7 < 0.9 indicates that the incidence is very seasonal; 0.5 0.7 indicates that the incidence is more seasonal; 0.3 <M < 0.5 indicates that the onset of the disease is somewhat seasonal; M < 0.3 indicates that the onset of the disease is relatively uniformly distributed over time; M = 0 indicates that the onset of the disease is uniformly distributed over a 12-month period.

Statistical analysis

Microsoft Excel 2019 was used to establish a database of the mumps incidence and to calculate the M value. Stata 18.0 was used for statistical analysis. ITS analysis required time series to be free of autocorrelation, and thus we tested for autocorrelation using the Durbin-Watson (DW) test, where a DW value close to 2 (1.85–2.15) indicated time series without autocorrelation.16 For time series without autocorrelation, the ordinary least squares (OLS) method was used for analysis; for time series with first-order autocorrelation, the generalized least squares (GLS) method was used for analysis, which was achieved by the Prais-Winsten method; the Newey-West method was used for analysis when time series with higher-order autocorrelation.17,18 The t-test was used to test for statistically significant differences in the regression coefficients. All testing was two-sided with significance determined at p < .05.

Results

General characteristics

A total of 10,295 mumps cases were reported in Quzhou City from 2005 to 2023, with no fatalities and an average annual reported incidence rate of 25.1/100,000 cases. The average annual reported incidence rates during the one-dose MuCV vaccination period, the two-dose MuCV vaccination period, and the three-dose MuCV vaccination period, were 57.5/100,000 (7015 cases), 14.8/100,000 (2590 cases), and 6.0/100,000 (690 cases), respectively (Table 1).

Among the mumps cases, 6307 were males (61.3%) and 3988 were females (38.7%), with a sex ratio of 1.58:1. The average annual incidence rate of mumps was 29.3/100,000 for males and 19.4/100,000 for females, with no statistically significant difference in the average annual incidence rate between genders (χ2 = 2.084, p = .149, Table 1). The age distribution in Table 1 shows that 93.9% of the population was aged 0–19 years. Among them, the highest percentage of cases was in the 5–9 age group (43.7%). There was a statistically significant difference in the mean incidence of different age groups (χ2 = 404.389, p < .001).

From 2005 to 2023, the distribution of monthly reported cases of mumps in Quzhou peaked in April to July. Minor peaks were also observed in December to January of the following year from 2005 to 2010, and in September to October of 2011 to 2018, as well as September to December of 2019 to 2023 (Figure 2). The incidence of mumps in 2005 to 2010 showed a certain seasonality with an M-value of 0.43. The M-value of monthly incidence of mumps in 2011 to 2018 and 2019 to 2023 was 0.23 and 0.07, respectively, indicating that the incidence of mumps did not have any obvious seasonality.

MuCV vaccination status

Vaccination coverage by birth cohort for different doses of MuCV is displayed in Figure 3. One- and two-dose MuCV coverage increased progressively with increasing birth cohort year. The vaccination rates were low during the one-dose MMR self-administration period. Following the introduction of the one-dose MMR vaccine into the EPI in April 2008, the proportion of children receiving the vaccine reached over 80% of those born after 2009 and over 90% of those born after 2012. The trends in two-dose MuCV coverage were comparable to those observed in the one-dose cohort. The data on the three-dose MMR vaccination coverage is limited to the 2005–2009 birth cohort, as the vaccinated children have not yet reached the age at which they are eligible for vaccination. And three-dose MMR vaccination coverage was below 60%.

Discussion

In our study, we found that the annual average incidence rate of 25.1/100000 from 2005 to 2023 was slightly higher than the national average (21.4/100000).19 Besides, the annual average incidence rate of the three periods decreased with the increase of MuCV vaccination times. ITS research shows that the introduction of two doses of MuCV could quickly reduce the incidence rate of mumps, which reflects the good short-term effect of the immunization policy. In addition, we found that children aged 5–9 are the main target of the disease; Two or more doses of MuCV might weaken the seasonal peak of mumps. In China, mumps vaccine was not included in the national immunization program prior to 2008, and vaccination was administered under the principle of voluntary self-sufficiency. As a result, the mumps vaccination rate was low during this period.20 Additionally, the vaccinated birth cohort has not yet reached the high-prevalence age group, the full impact of the vaccine has not been achieved,7 leading to a continued high monthly mumps incidence rate with a generally small upward trend. A study has similarly shown that the incidence of mumps did not decrease significantly after one dose of the MMR vaccine was incorporated into the EPI.21

With the implementation of the EPI policy, the coverage rate of one dose of MMR vaccination has increased steadily year by year. In September 2010, all children aged 8 months to 4 years in Quzhou City received a free MM booster in addition to one dose of routine MMR vaccine. This resulted in a significant reduction in the average annual incidence of mumps from 57.5/100,000 cases during the period of self-funded MMR vaccination to 14.8/100,000 cases. During this period, due to the more mature implementation of EPI and the strong complementation of EPI leakage by MM booster immunization, the introduction of 2 doses of MuCV rapidly controlled the incidence of mumps, reflecting the good short-term effect of this immunization policy. A domestic retrospective cohort study showed that two doses of MuCV vaccine efficacy (VE) (76%, 95%CI: 49–89%) were higher than one dose of MuCV (59%, 95%CI: 36–74%).22 Furthermore, a study in Canada has shown VE ranging from 49.2% to 81.6% for one dose of MMR vaccine and 66.3% to 88.0% for two doses.23 All the above studies indicated that two doses of MuCV were more effective. Despite a prolonged decline in monthly mumps incidence during the two-dose MuCV vaccination, no statistically significant difference was observed. This lack of significance could be attributed to the fact that completing two doses of MMR vaccine at an earlier age (e.g., before 24 months) may lead to an increased risk of contracting mumps during adolescence.24,25

In December 2018, Quzhou City implemented three doses of local routine MMR vaccination and an additional dose of MMR for junior high school students, which resulted in a further decrease in mumps incidence. Although ITS analysis showed that there was no statistical difference between the immediate effect at the time of the intervention and the long-term effect over time during this period, this may be related to the fact that the short observation period had not yet demonstrated the effect of this vaccination strategy. However, it is worth noting that one dose of MMR was not as effective as immunization, and the high vaccination rate and timely introduction of 2 doses of MuCV effectively controlled the incidence of mumps. These findings are consistent with relevant studies conducted nationally and internationally.7,26 – 29

The study found a higher incidence rate of males compared to females, possibly due to their poor hygiene practices and more social activities leading to extensive exposure to pathogenic factors.30 However, our research found that the difference was not significant in the incidence of mumps between males and females. From the age of onset, children aged 5–9 years were found to be the most affected population, which was consistent with previous studies.3,31 This suggested that children and elementary school students are more susceptible to mumps due to their participation in collective life and personal hygiene habits, providing better conditions for the spread of the disease.32,33 Therefore, schools and childcare institutions should be considered crucial locations for prevention and control. It is important to strengthen the management of students’ and children’s health and education, properly check immunization certificates at the time of enrollment, and provide emergency vaccinations to students who have not been vaccinated in the event of an outbreak.

Mumps can occur year-round, with seasonal variations. Before 2010, the incidence of mumps in Quzhou exhibited a bimodal distribution, with peaks occurring from April to July and from December to January each year, which was consistent with the relevant studies in China.3,34 However, there was no obvious seasonal peak after 2010 (M = 0.23 for 2010 to 2018; M = 0.07 for 2019 to 2023), which may be related to the intensive immunization carried out in Quzhou City in 2010, indicating that the seasonal peak of mumps was weakened by two or more doses of MuCV. Future studies with longer seasonal cycles are needed to investigate the effect of MuCV on mumps incidence.

In this study, ITS analysis was used to evaluate the effect of implementing different immunization strategies on the incidence of mumps using interrupted linear regression models. Compared with traditional epidemiological study designs such as cohort studies, case-control studies, and randomized controlled trials, ITS analysis can quantitatively analyze the effect of interventions when it is difficult to find a control group.9,35,36 To be noted, due to the specificity of the mumps immunization strategy in Quzhou City, this is the first time that an ITS analysis has been used to evaluate the impact of adjustments in immunization strategy on the incidence of mumps. This establishes a basis for the refinement of vaccination strategies and provides a reference point for future evaluations of the potential benefits of introducing two or three doses of routine MuCV immunization.

However, this study still has some limitations. Firstly, the data from this study were based on passive surveillance data from medical institutions. The quality of the data was affected by the level of diagnosis and treatment, as well as the awareness of infectious disease reporting by the attending doctors, which may underestimate the incidence of the disease. Secondly, this study did not take into account the effects of potential variables, including social and economic factors and migration, on the incidence of mumps. In addition, due to the difficulty in obtaining data on monthly MuCV vaccination rates, we only provided annual vaccination rates for different doses of MuCV and did not include vaccine coverage in the ITS model, which may to some extent affect the accuracy of assessing the effectiveness of vaccination campaigns. According to our findings, it is still necessary to maintain the high coverage rate of the first and second dose of MuCV and improve the coverage rate of the third dose of MMR in the future. Considering the vaccination rate and other factors, we would further evaluate the impact of three doses of MMR vaccination on the incidence rate of mumps. A representative subset of the population could also be selected for ongoing surveillance to confirm the effect of vaccine campaigns on mumps from different perspectives by calculating the VE for different doses of MuCV.

Conclusion

In summary, we found that timely introduction of 2 doses of MuCV and ensuring high vaccination coverage are beneficial in controlling mumps epidemics. In the future, it is still necessary to continue to monitor the effect of 3 doses of MMR on the incidence of mumps as well as to consider the effects of climate, vaccine coverage, and other factors on the disease. In addition, the predominance of young children in the incidence population highlights the importance of school-based outbreak surveillance.

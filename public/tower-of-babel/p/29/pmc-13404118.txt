Epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of COVID-19 outbreaks in China: a four-year retrospective analysis using extended SEIR models
Authors: Yifei Ma, Yifei Ning, Jiaming Guo, Shujun Xu, Yuxin Luo, Jiantao Li, Lijian Lei, Lu He, Tong Wang, Hongmei Yu, Jun Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn 2024, the global new crown epidemic is still not optimistic and the overall situation remains complex and challenging. A detailed and comprehensive analysis of the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of the COVID-19 epidemic over the past four years is urgently needed.MethodsTaiyuan is the political, economic, cultural and international exchange center of central China, with its core driving the rapid development of the central city cluster. Initially, we investigated the epidemiological characteristics of infected cases in Taiyuan. Subsequently, we developed the SSqEEqIAHR, SEIQHR and SEIAHR models to analyze the multi-stage infection trends of COVID-19. Finally, we performed several sensitivity analyses to investigate the effects of model parameters on the basic reproduction number (R0) and outbreak trends.ResultsFrom the perspective of sociodemographic characteristics, a total of 4,935,795 positive infected cases had been reported in Taiyuan. Among them, the vast majority were mild, and the male-to-female ratio was 1.07. The most affected by the epidemic were people aged 60–69 and retirees, with Poland and Russia emerging as primary sources of imported cases. From the perspective of spatiotemporal characteristics, there have been three rounds of epidemic peaks in Taiyuan over the past four years, with Xinghualing, Xiaodian and Yingze being the most heavily infected districts. From the perspective of transmission dynamics, the extended SEIR models can achieve satisfactory prediction results in the three major outbreaks, with goodness-of-fit of 0.734, 0.991 and 0.914, respectively. The R0 values decreased sequentially, with the outbreaks completely dying out after six, 47 and 153 days, respectively. Sensitivity analyses showed that decreasing the transmission rate and increasing the quarantine rate were essential for controlling the spread of the virus.ConclusionsThe extended SEIR models played an important role in capturing the transmission pattern of multi-stage and multi-wave epidemics. Future interventions should be targeted align with the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of the outbreak.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-026-27891-3.

Background

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) that has swept across the globe has so far caused more that 774 million infections and 7.02 million deaths, posing a serious threat to people’s safety and stability in all countries [1]. Although the World Health Organization (WHO) declared an end to the global health emergency for COVID-19 on May 5, 2023, we should still treat COVID-19 as a major public health concern and must not abandon all prevention and control measures [2]. During December 2023, there were 88 newly severe cases and 11 deaths in 31 provinces across the country, and the novel variant of JN.1 is likely to gradually develop into the dominant epidemic strain.

Although the current COVID-19 epidemic in China maintains a low level of wave-like development trend, there is a risk of epidemic rebound in winter and spring. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis and summary of the epidemic situation is urgent.

Prevention has a prominent position in China’s battle against COVID-19, given that the country is a vast land with a large number of vulnerable people, unbalanced development among regions, and insufficient medical resources. An analysis of the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of the COVID-19 epidemic is essential for the scientific and effective prevention of such major respiratory infections. Since the outbreak of COVID-19, scholars have carried out a large number of epidemiological investigations and modeling analyses [3–5]. The susceptible-exposed-infected-recovered (SEIR) model and its extended models have played a key role in exploring the transmission mechanisms of infectious diseases [6, 7]. The extended SEIR models are not only able to refine the compartments according to the actual situation of the epidemic, but also able to take into account a variety of factors, such as intervention measures and virus virulence, so as to effectively and accurately make short- and long-term predictions. However, most scholars have only focused on the epidemic situation in a specific time period [8–11], and so far, no study has systematically analyzed and summarized the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of COVID-19 in China over the past four years from a macro perspective.

As a military and cultural town in northern China, Taiyuan is an important central city in the central region approved by the State Council, and one of China’s important energy and heavy industry bases. An in-depth analysis of the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of the outbreak in Taiyuan can effectively reflect the development of the outbreak in neighboring cities, provinces and even the whole country. Before May 2021, Alpha was the dominant strain in Taiyuan. From May 2021 to March 2022, the dominant variant changed to Delta. After March 2022, Omicron became the dominant strain [12, 13]. In this study, our objectives were to (1) investigate the epidemiological characteristics of infected cases in Taiyuan, including the sociodemographic and spatiotemporal characteristics, (2) establish some extended SEIR models based on the realities of different stages, (3) analyze the multi-stage infection trends of COVID-19, including the turning point, infection size and reproduction number, and (4) carry out several sensitivity analyses to investigate the correlation between model parameters and R0 and analyze the effect of different parameter values on the epidemic trend. Our findings were conducive to helping the health sector gain a more comprehensive understanding of the development process of the epidemic, and also provided a useful reference for strengthening the construction of the epidemic prevention, control and response system. In short, reviewing and summarizing the epidemic is an indispensable part of winning the long-lasting war of prevention and control.

Methods

Data sources

COVID-19 data from January 19, 2020 to December 31, 2023 were obtained from the Health Commission of Shanxi Province, network questionnaire on novel coronavirus infection among urban and rural residents in Taiyuan [see Additional File 1] and network direct report, including the number of newly confirmed cases (mild/moderate/severe or critical), the number of newly asymptomatic infections, and the age, sex, population type, current residence and pre-entry residence of infected individuals.

Extended SEIR models

In the classical SEIR model, individuals are divided into four categories: susceptible (S), exposed (E), infected (I) and recovered (R); however, the model has limitations in accurately simulating the epidemiological trends of multi-stage and multi-wave epidemics. In this study, we constructed three extended SEIR models, SSqEEqIAHR, SEIQHR and SEIAHR, based on the realities of different stages, to capture the three major outbreaks that occurred in Taiyuan. The Markov Chain Monte Carlo-MH algorithm (MCMC-MH) was used to assign values to the unknown parameters [14]. We performed 90,000 iterations and the Markov Chain reached a stable state after 75,000 burn-in periods. The Geweke test is a commonly used MCMC convergence diagnostic method. If the value of the Geweke test statistic lies between − 2 and 2, it may be concluded that the Markov chain has converged to a stationary distribution. The next-generation matrix method was applied to solve for the basic reproduction number (R0) and effective reproduction number (Rt) [15], and the epidemic could be considered extinct only when Rt was consistently lower than 1.0. Moreover, global sensitivity analyses were performed on the model parameters and R0 using the Partial Rank Correlation Coefficient (PRCC) and Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS), generating 10,000 samples for each parameter [16]. Compartment and parameter settings

Inclusion criteria: Considering the following three realities—the adoption of track quarantine measures, the presence of asymptomatic infections and the admission of infected cases to hospitals for treatment, a model named SSqEEqIAHR (Fig. 1) was constructed by further adding quarantined susceptible (Sq), quarantined exposed (Eq), asymptomatic (A) and hospitalized (H) compartments, in which parameters ß(t) and q(t) changed with the adjustment of intervention intensity, representing the time-varying transmission rate and time-varying track quarantine rate, respectively. The expressions for the system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations and the derivation process for R0 and Rt are described in the Additional File 2. The diagnostic diagram of the algorithm’s convergence is shown in the Additional File 3, Fig. S1.

SEIQHR model from November 8, 2022 to January 31, 2023

Compartment and parameter settings

Inclusion criteria: Considering the following two realities—the presence of home quarantined individuals and the admission of infected cases to hospitals for treatment, a model named SEIQHR (Fig. 2) was constructed by further adding home quarantined (Q) and hospitalized (H) compartments, in which parameters ß(t) and q(t) changed with the adjustment of intervention intensity, representing the time-varying transmission rate and time-varying home quarantine rate, respectively. Exclusion criteria: With the implementation of the “Class B infectious disease Class B management” policy, people infected with the novel coronavirus will no longer be quarantined and close contacts will no longer be identified. Therefore, quarantined susceptible (Sq) and quarantined exposed (Eq) compartments should be excluded. The expressions for the system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations and the derivation process for R0 and Rt are described in the Additional File 2. The diagnostic diagram of the algorithm’s convergence is shown in the Additional File 3, Fig. S1.

SEIAHR model from April 24, 2023 to December 31, 2023

Compartment and parameter settings

Inclusion criteria: Considering the following two realities—the presence of asymptomatic infections and the admission of infected cases to hospitals for treatment, a model named SEIAHR (Fig. 3) was constructed by further adding asymptomatic (A) and hospitalized (H) compartments, in which parameter ß(t) changed with the adjustment of intervention intensity, representing the time-varying transmission rate. Exclusion criteria: Given that residents were no longer required to implement home self-care from April 2023, home quarantined (Q) compartment should be excluded. Results

Epidemiological characteristics of COVID-19 epidemic

Sociodemographic characteristics

As of December 31, 2023, a cumulative total of 4,935,795 residents of Taiyuan had been infected with the SARS-CoV-2 virus. Among them, 3,597,199 (72.88%) were mild, 1,134,790 (22.99%) were moderate, 163,539 (3.31%) were severe or critical, 7,431 (0.15%) were asymptomatic, with 32,836 (0.67%) fatalities. Overall, the male-to-female ratio was 1.07 (2,551,676:2,384,119), with the ratio ranging from 0.82 to 1.34 in the different age groups. The number of cases was most widely distributed in the 60–69 age group (17.95%), followed closely by the 70–79 age group (17.79%) and the 80 + age group (16.84%) (Fig. 4). As shown in Fig. 5, the majority of cases were concentrated among retirees (1,472,907 cases, 29.84%), job seekers (1,056,136 cases, 21.40%), peasants (856,303 cases, 17.35%) and students (466,279 cases, 9.45%), while the remaining 21.97% of cases were distributed among public servants, workers, scattered children, merchants, medical staffs and other populations. In addition, a total of 304 (0.79%) cases imported from 22 countries had been reported, of which 72 (23.68%) cases were from Poland, followed by Russia (66 cases, 21.71%), France (60 cases, 19.74%), Uzbekistan (19 cases, 6.25%), Japan (14 cases, 4.61%), England (9 cases, 2.96%), Serbia (9 cases, 2.96%) and other countries (55 cases, 18.09%) (Fig. 6). The time distribution diagram of imported cases is shown in the Additional File 3, Fig. S2.

Spatiotemporal characteristics

From January 19, 2020 to December 31, 2023, Taiyuan successively experienced three rounds of epidemic peaks in April 2022 (single-peak distribution), November 2022 to January 2023 (double-peak distribution), and April to December 2023 (double-peak distribution) triggered by the BA.2, BA.5.2, and XBB variants. During the rest of the period, the prevalence of COVID-19 was at a relatively low level (Fig. 7). The spatial distribution map shows that the spread of COVID-19 occurred in all districts of Taiyuan without exception. The four-level echelons were, in order, Xinghualing (21.12%), Xiaodian (20.67%), Yingze (20.18%); Wanbailin (14.97%); Jiancaoping (8.03%), Gujiao (4.27%), Qingxu (4.19%), Jinyuan (3.71%); Yangqu (1.79%), Loufan (1.06%) (Fig. 8).

Figures 9, 10 and 11 show the newly infected cases and Rt predicted by the SSqEEqIAHR, SEIQHR and SEIAHR models from April 10, 2022 to April 30, 2022, November 8, 2022 to January 31, 2023 and April 24, 2023 to December 31, 2023, respectively. We found that the overall agreements between the number of newly infected cases predicted by the extended SEIR models and the actual number of cases were high in the three major outbreaks in Taiyuan, with R2 values of 0.734, 0.991 and 0.914, respectively. Meanwhile, the extended SEIR models can accurately capture the transmission dynamics of the outbreak, including the time taken to reach the turning point, the peak size and the cumulative number of infections. Specifically, the turning point of the first round of the outbreak predicted by the SSqEEqIAHR model arrived on April 15, 2022, with a peak size of 53 (95%CI: 51–55) and a cumulative size of 389 (95%CI: 372–406) infections [actual circumstances: April 15, 2022 / 80 / 315], the turning points of the second round of the outbreak predicted by the SEIQHR model arrived on December 1, 2022 and December 18, 2022, with peak sizes of 588 (95%CI: 516–673) and 399,640 (95%CI: 285,238–552,067) and a cumulative size of 4,653,823 (95%CI: 3,507,496-6,066,524) infections (actual circumstances: December 3, 2022 and December 19, 2022 / 494 and 394,112 / 4,907,854), and the turning points of the third round of the outbreak predicted by the SEIAHR model arrived on May 25, 2023 and September 15, 2023, with peak sizes of 636 (95%CI: 603–710) and 94 (95%CI: 7-712) and a cumulative size of 21,124 (95%CI: 16,984 − 46,997) infections (actual circumstances: May 25, 2023 and September 15, 2023 / 641 and 90 / 25,796). In terms of R0, the three major outbreaks had R0 values of 3.48 (95%CI: 3.43–3.53), 1.89 (95%CI: 1.86–1.92) and 1.65 (95%CI: 1.64–1.67), respectively. In terms of Rt, the Rt value of the first round of the outbreak dropped below 1.0 after six days, while the Rt values of the second and third rounds the outbreak rebounded after dropping below 1.0. The Rt value of the second round of the outbreak dropped below 1.0 for the first time after 25 days, lasted only four days before the Rt began to rebound and the outbreak did not subside until 47 days later. The Rt value of the third round of the outbreak dropped below 1.0 for the first time after 34 days, lasted 50 days before the Rt began to rebound and the outbreak did not subside until 153 days later.

Discussion

Although no longer a “global health emergency”, COVID-19 continues to spread, mutate and claim lives. By the end of 2023, WHO classified the JN.1 coronavirus strain as a separate variant of interest from the parent lineage BA.2.86, citing the strain’s rapidly increasing spread [19]. JN.1 was projected to account for approximately 39%~50% of circulating variants in the United States [20], and there has been a significant increase in the detection of JN.1 among Chinese arrivals. In view of this, we retrospectively analyzed the epidemiological characteristics and transmission dynamics of the COVID-19 epidemic in Taiyuan over the past four years to help decisionmakers summarize the experience in prevention and control and scientifically respond to possible new outbreaks that may follow.

Detailed epidemiological investigations throughout the entire epidemic period contribute to public health control. After an in-depth analysis of the epidemiological findings, several conclusions were drawn. (1) 72.93% of cases were mildly symptomatic, indicating significant achievements in national immunization programmes. Since part of the data (December 6, 2022 to January 31, 2023) for the second round of the outbreak came from the questionnaire on novel coronavirus infection among urban and rural residents in Shanxi Province, from which the specific date of onset of the illness was known, but it was not possible to differentiate between residents who were confirmed cases or asymptomatic infections, so we regarded all the positive infected persons who participated in the survey as confirmed cases. There is reason to believe that the proportion of asymptomatic infections would increase if the distinction could be made accurately. (2) Elderly people aged 60–69 years and retirees were particularly vulnerable to COVID-19 infection, which may be related to the fact that these people have low immunity, suffer from underlying diseases, and are not as aware of protection as younger people (e.g., keep wearing masks and maintain social distance) [21]. Therefore, while continuing to promote vaccinations for the elderly, it is also necessary to strengthen community health education to popularize the knowledge related to the prevention of major respiratory infectious diseases such as COVID-19 [22]. (3) The main sources of imported cases were from Poland, Russia and France, suggesting that the Entry-Exit Inspection Quarantine still needs to strengthen the management of entry personnel from the European region in the post-epidemic era. (4) Taiyuan experienced three major outbreaks in April 2022, November 2022 to January 2023, and April to December 2023, with outbreak intervals of about six months, and the latter two rounds of outbreaks showed a double-peak distribution pattern. This suggests that the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) should closely monitor and alert the dynamics of the outbreak at semiannual intervals, and in the event of a new outbreak, stringent measures must be implemented to prevent the occurrence of the second wave of peak. (5) Xinghualing, Xiaodian and Yingze, which have the first, second and third highest population densities in Taiyuan, were the most seriously infected districts. The great population density narrowed the space for person-to-person contact, thus increasing the risk of COVID-19 transmission. It is suggested that the government should increase and tilt more medical resources to these districts.

In addition to epidemiological characteristics, our study analyzed in detail the multi-stage infection trends of COVID-19 from the perspective of disease transmission dynamics. We constructed three extended SEIR models, namely SSqEEqIAHR, SEIQHR and SEIAHR, to accommodate the realities of three stages, such as the presence of asymptomatic infections and home quarantined individuals, the adoption of track quarantine measures, and the admission of infected cases to hospitals for treatment. The parameter estimates in the model aligned with the actual epidemiological characteristics and were highly consistent with our previous studies (the Shanghai outbreak from March to May 2022 and the Shanxi outbreak from December 2022 to January 2023) [23, 24]. We found that the extended SEIR models can achieve ideal prediction results with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}^{2}$$\end{document} values above 0.7 for both short- and long-term epidemiological trends, especially for long-term trends (85 / 252 days), where the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}^{2}$$\end{document} values reached as high as 0.991 / 0.914. Furthermore, the extended SEIR models were able to capture the key features of outbreaks more accurately, for example, the time to reach the turning point derived from the models was no more than two days before or after the actual time, and the derived peak size and cumulative number of infections were also very close to the actual situation. Therefore, we recommended the use of the extended SEIR models when simulating infection trends for major respiratory infectious diseases such as COVID-19.

With regard to the number of infections, the second round of the outbreak far exceeded the first round both in terms of peak size and cumulative size, which was due to the fact that at the end of 2022, China downgraded the coronavirus management level to “Class B infectious disease Class B management” and shifted the focus from the prevention of infections to the medical treatment and prevention of severe cases [25]. The inability to conduct large-scale detection and identification of all cases had led to a surge in the number of novel coronavirus infections. It was observed that the number of infections in the third round of the outbreak was much lower than that in the second round, confirming the hypothesis that previously infected persons had acquired natural active immunity, thereby considerably mitigating the scale of infection in the latter round.

Beyond various non-pharmaceutical interventions and active immunization strategies, vaccines play an irreplaceable role within the epidemic prevention and control system [26]. We have reason to believe that by increasing vaccination rates and utilizing booster doses to maintain and enhance antibody titers, we can effectively prevent SARS-CoV-2 infection and COVID-19-related death, thereby significantly reducing the scale of the three major outbreaks [27]. With regard to the Rt, the durations of the outbreaks were prolonged due to the emergence of the second wave of peaks, resulting in a constant delay in the time for the Rt values of the three major outbreaks to fall below 1.0 completely. Extensive research has indicated that during the prevalence of the Omicron variant, the Rt value approached 10, demonstrating that this strain exhibits high transmissibility, yet its virulence proved weaker than other strains [28, 29]. We must not relax or even give up prevention and control because of the weakening virulence of the virus. It is also noteworthy that the Rt value of the second round of the outbreak experienced a notable increase after the rebound, peaking at 9. This is attributable both to the increased transmissibility of the Omicron variant and to the timing of the relaxation of epidemic-control measures during winter, when the cold climate made the virus more active, thereby accelerating its spread. However, after half a month, the Rt value dropped to about 1.0, which can reflect the strong self-protection awareness and conscientiousness of the residents in preventing and controlling the epidemic, as well as China’s unique advantages of strong administrative execution and sufficient production capacity of anti-epidemic materials.

Besides, our study also found that the basic reproduction number R0 was most sensitive to incubation rate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:$$\end{document}, initial transmission rate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\beta\:}_{0}$$\end{document}, transmission coefficient of exposed individuals relative to symptomatic infections \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\theta\:$$\end{document}, and initial quarantine rate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{q}_{0}$$\end{document}. Depending on the magnitude of each parameter’s PRCC value and its symbolic meaning, the diffusion of COVID-19 can be effectively controlled if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{q}_{0}$$\end{document} are further increased and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\beta\:}_{0}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\theta\:$$\end{document} are decreased. The values of the parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\theta\:$$\end{document} are usually determined by the intrinsic transmission mechanisms of the different mutant strains and are almost impossible to control artificially [30]; therefore, the most effective way to prevent and control COVID-19 is to decrease the transmission rate by reducing person-to-person contact, and to increase the quarantine rate of susceptible individuals, close contacts and positive infected cases. The findings of Tang B et al. are consistent with our study, indicating that the epidemic is still uncertain after February 2020, and it is important to continue enhancing the quarantine and isolation strategy in mainland China [31].

Admittedly, this study has some limitations. First, although our models were constructed based on the realities of different stages, there were other potential factors that we had not taken into account, such as the differences between immunity formed by vaccination and that formed by natural infection. Second, our study determined the vaccination rate among Taiyuan residents at the population level over the entire epidemic period, and did not yet consider the discrepancies in vaccination rates between different time periods and different age groups. In future work, efforts must be made to improve the extended SEIR models by distinguishing the sources of immunity and conducting stratified analyses by time period and age group.

Conclusions

Among the new coronaviruses recently circulating in China, the JN.1 variant is on the rise. The National Disease Control and Prevention Administration requires all localities to strengthen the analysis and utilization of multi-source data such as network direct reports, fever clinics, sentinel hospitals and laboratory tests, to dynamically grasp the epidemic trend, carry out risk prediction, and provide timely warnings of any anomalies found. Our study analyzed in detail the epidemiological characteristics of the outbreak in Taiyuan since the emergence of COVID-19, and established some extended SEIR models to study its transmission dynamics. Our research found that reducing person-to-person contact to decrease the transmission rate, coupled with increasing the quarantine rate of susceptible individuals, close contacts and positive infected cases, constituted the most effective way to controlling COVID-19. Guided by the national decision-making and deployment, this study kept abreast of the new situation of the epidemic and summarized the practical experience in prevention and control to help neighboring cities, provinces and even the whole country cope with the risk of rebound of the epidemic in advance.

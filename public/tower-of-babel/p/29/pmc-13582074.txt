Preoperative difficulty assessment of laparoscopic cholecystectomy after treatment for choledocholithiasis
Authors: Masahiro Shiihara, Mitsugi Shimoda, Mitsuru Watanabe, Ryoichi Miyamoto, Jiro Shimazaki, Shuji Suzuki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLaparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) after endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) has been associated with increased operative difficulty; however, the optimal timing of LC remains controversial. This study evaluated operative outcomes and surgical difficulty of LC after ERCP in patients with gallstones and concomitant choledocholithiasis, with a particular focus on surgical timing.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed patients who underwent LC after ERCP for choledocholithiasis. Difficult cholecystectomy was defined as the need for a bailout procedure, including conversion to open surgery or subtotal cholecystectomy, operative time > 180 min, or intraoperative blood loss ≥ 300 mL. Patients were classified according to the ERCP–LC interval as early (≤ 14 days), middle (15–60 days), or late (≥ 61 days). Operative outcomes were compared, and logistic regression analysis was performed to identify predictors of difficult cholecystectomy.ResultsAmong 1008 patients who underwent LC, 193 underwent preoperative ERCP. Forty-nine patients (25.4%) met the criteria for difficult cholecystectomy. The incidence was significantly higher in the middle group (33.0%) than in the early (7.7%, P = 0.036) and late groups (15.4%, P = 0.008). Postoperative complication rates did not differ among the groups; however, the late group had a higher incidence of recurrent biliary complications between ERCP and surgery. Multivariate analysis identified age ≥ 60 years, male sex, acute cholecystitis, and an ERCP–LC interval of 15–60 days as independent risk factors for surgical difficulty. Compared with the early and late groups, the 15–60-day interval was associated with increased odds of difficult cholecystectomy (OR 6.29 and 2.61, respectively).ConclusionsAn ERCP–LC interval of 15–60 days is associated with increased surgical difficulty. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) is a standard procedure widely performed in various hospital settings. However, operative difficulty varies depending on the severity of inflammation and the patient’s condition. The Tokyo Guidelines 2018 recommend bailout procedures (BOPs) for difficult cholecystectomy to reduce the risk of bile duct injury [1, 2]. These procedures include subtotal cholecystectomy, a fundus-first approach, and conversion to open surgery [1, 2]. In patients with gallstones and concomitant choledocholithiasis, LC is commonly performed electively after endoscopic treatment of bile duct stones. Nevertheless, the optimal interval between endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and LC remains controversial. Previous studies have shown that ERCP or a history of choledocholithiasis independently increases the risk of conversion to a BOP during LC [3] [4]. Accordingly, LC performed after ERCP may be considered a form of difficult cholecystectomy.

In this study, we focused on patients with gallstones and concomitant choledocholithiasis and retrospectively analyzed those who underwent LC after ERCP to evaluate surgical difficulty, including the effect of the interval between ERCP and LC.

Methods

Study population

We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of 1008 patients who underwent LC at our institution between January 2016 and March 2025. Patients who underwent primary open surgery or had insufficient clinical data were excluded. Among these patients, 193 had choledocholithiasis and underwent preoperative ERCP. These 193 patients were included in the final analysis (Supplementary Table 1).

Diagnosis and therapeutic strategy

Preoperative imaging, including computed tomography, magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography, and abdominal ultrasonography, was performed to evaluate biliary anatomy. Patients with choledocholithiasis initially underwent ERCP performed by gastroenterologists, after which they were discharged and scheduled for elective surgery. The diagnosis of acute cholecystitis was established based on a combination of clinical findings, imaging findings, and laboratory data. According to our institutional treatment policy, patients with acute cholecystitis were initially managed conservatively with antibiotics and/or percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder intervention (PTGBI), followed by elective cholecystectomy. Patients who did not respond to conservative treatment underwent emergency surgery. Intraoperative conversion to a BOP was considered in cases with a high risk of bile duct injury, such as failure to achieve the critical view of safety [4]. BOPs included a fundus-first approach, subtotal cholecystectomy, and conversion to open surgery [1, 2].

For LC, we used a standard four-trocar American-style technique, and all procedures were performed by two surgeons. The primary operators ranged from surgical trainees (third-year residents) to highly experienced surgeons with approximately 30 years of experience; however, at least one of the two surgeons was always an experienced hepatobiliary-pancreatic surgeon. In total, 24 surgeons served as primary operators for the 193 patients included in this study.

Definition of difficult cholecystectomy

Difficult cholecystectomy was defined as the presence of any of the following: conversion to open surgery as a BOP, subtotal cholecystectomy as a BOP, operative time ≥ 180 min, or intraoperative blood loss ≥ 300 mL. This definition was based on previous studies [5, 6].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as medians. Between-group differences in categorical and continuous variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test, Pearson’s chi-square test, and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate. Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify factors associated with difficult cholecystectomy. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Variables significant in univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate model of preoperative factors. Cutoff values were determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. ROC analysis was also used to classify groups according to the interval between ERCP and cholecystectomy. First, the cutoff between the middle- and late-stage groups (60 days) was determined using ROC curve analysis. Subsequently, cases within 60 days were analyzed to determine the cutoff between the early- and middle-stage groups (14 days). All statistical analyses were performed using JMP version 14.1.0 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Characteristics of included patients

The characteristics of the included patients are summarized in Supplementary Table 1. The median age was 67 years, and 111 patients (57.5%) were men. Twenty-eight patients (14.5%) were receiving antithrombotic agents for comorbid conditions. Preoperative cholangitis was observed in 112 patients (58.0%). Two or more preoperative ERCP procedures were required in 33 patients (17.1%). Preoperative cholecystitis was present in 61 patients (31.6%), and PTGBI was required in 15 patients (7.8%). Conversion to open surgery and subtotal cholecystectomy were each performed in 18 patients (9.3%). Regarding operative outcomes, 27 patients (14.0%) had an operative time ≥ 180 min, and seven patients (3.6%) experienced intraoperative blood loss ≥ 300 mL. Overall, 49 patients (25.4%) met the criteria for difficult cholecystectomy (Supplementary Table 1).

Comparison of patient characteristics according to waiting period

Patient characteristics were compared according to the interval between ERCP for choledocholithiasis and LC. Patients were classified into three groups: early (≤ 14 days), middle (15–60 days), and late (≥ 61 days) (Tables 1 and 2). Regarding preoperative characteristics, no significant differences were observed among the three groups except for the number of preoperative ERCP procedures (Table 1). Regarding operative outcomes, operative time was significantly longer in the middle group (P = 0.017). In addition, the incidence of difficult cholecystectomy was significantly higher in the middle group than in the other two groups (P = 0.01; middle vs. early, P = 0.036; middle vs. late, P = 0.008). No significant differences in postoperative complication rates were observed among the groups (Table 2). The incidence of recurrent biliary complications increased significantly with longer waiting periods. These complications included cholangitis, cholecystitis, gallstone pancreatitis, biliary colic, and spontaneous stone passage (Table 2).

Factors associated with difficult LC in patients with choledocholithiasis

Logistic regression analysis identified older age (≥ 60 years), male sex, presence of cholecystitis, and a middle waiting period (15–60 days) as independent risk factors for difficult cholecystectomy (Table 3). These four variables were significantly associated with difficult cholecystectomy in both univariate and multivariate analyses. Compared with the early and late groups, a waiting period of 15–60 days was associated with increased odds of difficult cholecystectomy, with odds ratios of 6.29 (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.13–118.26; P = 0.033) and 2.61 (95% CI, 1.18–6.19; P = 0.018), respectively (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated preoperative characteristics and operative outcomes in patients undergoing LC after treatment for choledocholithiasis to assess surgical difficulty. The optimal timing of LC following ERCP for choledocholithiasis remains controversial, and few previous studies have specifically addressed this issue. Our findings indicate that operative difficulty was greater in the middle-interval group than in the other groups, whereas the incidence of postoperative complications did not differ significantly among the groups. These results suggest that surgeons should carefully consider the interval between ERCP and LC and be prepared to convert to a BOP when operating during the middle interval period (15–60 days). Generally, two treatment strategies are available for gallstones with concomitant choledocholithiasis: [1] single-stage surgery involving common bile duct stone removal and cholecystectomy and [2] a two-stage approach consisting of endoscopic common bile duct stone extraction followed by elective LC. Many previous studies have compared these two strategies; however, consensus regarding the optimal approach remains lacking [7–10]. Several meta-analyses and randomized controlled trials have reported shorter hospital stays with single-stage surgery [7, 8, 10]. In contrast, the two-stage strategy has been associated with a higher common bile duct stone clearance rate [7], whereas another study reported a significantly lower rate of technical failure with single-stage surgery [8].

Although therapeutic strategies vary across institutions, the optimal interval between ERCP and LC also remains controversial. Liu et al. reported that performing LC within 7 days after ERCP was associated with lower medical costs and shorter hospital stays. The benefit of shorter hospitalization with early LC has also been reported in other studies [11, 12]. Furthermore, Liu et al. demonstrated an increased risk of gallbladder gangrene and perforation when LC was performed more than 7 days after ERCP [13]. Prajapati et al. recommended early LC within 2 weeks, noting better definition of Calot’s triangle, more frequent achievement of the critical view of safety, and reduced intraoperative blood loss compared with late LC performed after 2 weeks [14]. Another study reported that recurrent biliary events after ERCP occurred in 28.5% of patients at a median of 34 days, with an incidence of 2.5% as early as 1 week [15]. Based on these findings, Bergeron et al. suggested that LC should preferably be performed within 7 days after ERCP to prevent recurrent biliary events and adverse outcomes [15]. In a meta-analysis, Wu et al. concluded that LC performed within 24–72 h after ERCP reduced conversion rates and prevented recurrence of acute cholecystitis [16]. Another meta-analysis similarly recommended LC within 72 h because of shorter operative time and hospital stay, as well as lower postoperative complication and conversion rates [17]. Although few studies support delayed LC, Mann et al. reported that LC performed approximately 6 weeks after ERCP was safe [18]. In the present study, the late group demonstrated a lower incidence of difficult cholecystectomy, comparable to that of the early group. Most previous studies used 1 or 2 weeks as the cutoff point and did not separately evaluate delayed LC performed after 2 months, as examined in the present study.

BOPs were introduced in the Tokyo Guidelines 2018 as a strategy to prevent bile duct injury and have been evaluated in numerous recent studies. However, no previous study has specifically examined the association between the interval from ERCP to LC and the use of BOPs. The present study demonstrated that a middle interval of 15–60 days was associated with an increased risk of difficult cholecystectomy, including conversion to a BOP, but not with a higher rate of postoperative complications. These findings suggest that, even when LC is performed during the middle interval period, appropriate conversion to a BOP may help mitigate postoperative complications.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective, single-center design limits generalizability and statistical power. Second, the extended study period may have introduced heterogeneity related to changes in surgical indications, equipment, operative techniques, and perioperative management. In particular, recent adoption of advanced laparoscopic techniques has increased the frequency of laparoscopic subtotal cholecystectomy. Third, the group sizes were imbalanced, with a relatively small early group. Finally, no strict institutional criteria were established regarding the waiting period before interval surgery, which may have complicated the statistical interpretation of the findings. Future large-scale, multicenter studies are needed to validate these results.

Conclusion

A middle interval of 15–60 days between ERCP and LC is associated with an increased risk of difficult cholecystectomy, whereas longer waiting periods are associated with a higher incidence of recurrent biliary complications. Surgeons should consider both the timing of surgery and the risk of interval biliary complications when planning LC for gallstones with concomitant choledocholithiasis and should be prepared to convert to a bailout procedure when necessary.

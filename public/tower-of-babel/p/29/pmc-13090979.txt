AI-Assisted Dual-Template Workflow in Tooth Autotransplantation: A Promising Step Toward Precision Dentistry
Authors: Xuezhen Gao
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

I read with great interest the article by Chen et al1 on an AI-assisted dual-template workflow for tooth autotransplantation, recently published in the International Dental Journal. The authors demonstrate that integrating AI-based segmentation with dual surgical guides significantly improves positional accuracy compared to conventional freehand techniques. This study makes a valuable contribution to the field of AI-assisted oral surgery.

The integration of artificial intelligence into CBCT segmentation, combined with the use of both a socket-preparation guide and a socket-margin guide, represents a meaningful advancement in digital dentistry. The results—particularly the marked reductions in buccolingual angular deviation (BLAD) and rotational deviation (RAD)—underscore the potential of this approach to improve surgical predictability and protect periodontal ligament vitality.

The study also addresses an important gap in the literature. While guided implant workflows have been extensively studied, systematic evaluations of AI-integrated protocols for tooth autotransplantation remain limited. Chen and colleagues' work expands the scope of AI applications in dentomaxillofacial surgery.

Building on these findings, several directions merit further exploration:

The authors found no significant difference in mesiodistal angular deviation between groups, attributing this to natural anatomical constraints from proximal contacts and the interradicular septum. This aligns with Gostilo et al's2 observation that in molar autotransplantation, anatomical structures themselves serve as important prognostic factors. Future research could explore whether optimizing guide seating pathways or incorporating intraoperative real-time feedback could further enhance three-dimensional accuracy without imposing excessive constraints.

This study appropriately used immediate postoperative accuracy as the primary outcome measure—the most direct indicator of surgical precision. However, long-term biological outcomes merit equal attention. The long-term success of tooth autotransplantation is closely associated with periodontal ligament vitality and the degree of root resorption. A systematic review by García-Miralles et al3 demonstrated that in children and adolescent patients, tooth autotransplantation survival rates can reach 94.2%, though root resorption and ankylosis remain the main complications. Therefore, longitudinal studies incorporating clinical and radiographic follow-up are needed to validate whether improved positional accuracy translates into superior long-term biological outcomes—such as reduced root resorption, enhanced pulpal healing, and improved periodontal regeneration.

This study employed a single-center design with all procedures performed by a single experienced surgeon. While understandable for pioneering research, this limits the workflow's generalisability and reproducibility across different clinical settings. Luo et al4 demonstrated that learning curves do exist for computer-assisted surgical systems, particularly evident in surgical time metrics, although surgical accuracy can remain relatively stable even during initial exposure. Similarly, Zhuang et al5 found that when young dentists used autonomous robotic systems for implant placement, while time efficiency improved with experience, implant accuracy reached acceptable levels from the early stages. Future studies should include less experienced clinicians to evaluate the learning curve and reproducibility of this AI-assisted workflow, thereby providing a more comprehensive assessment of its feasibility for dissemination beyond specialist centers.

In conclusion, Chen and colleagues have conducted a methodologically rigorous study demonstrating the feasibility and efficacy of an AI-assisted dual-template workflow in tooth autotransplantation. Their work establishes a solid foundation for subsequent research, which should focus on refining surgical guide design, validating long-term clinical outcomes, and exploring applicability across diverse clinical settings. It is hoped that such tools will eventually be integrated into routine clinical workflows, providing robust support for consistency and predictability in tooth autotransplantation surgery.

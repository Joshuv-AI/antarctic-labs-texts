Characterization and biocompatibility of a bilayer PEEK-based scaffold for guiding bone regeneration
Authors: Shaoping Li, Cancan Jia, Haitong Han, Yuqing Yang, Yundeng Xiaowen, Zhiyu Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPolyetheretherketone (PEEK) is well known for its excellent physical–chemical properties and biosafety. The study aimed to open up a new method for clinical application of PEEK to reconstruct large-scale bone defects.MethodsA bilayer scaffold for bone regeneration was prepared by combining a sulfonated PEEK barrier framework (SPEEK) with a hydrogel layer loaded with aspirin (ASA) and nano-hydroxyapatite (nHAP) by the wet-bonding of Polydopamine (PDA).ResultsThe hydrogel was successfully adhered to the surface of SPEEK, resulting in significant changes including the introduction of bioactive groups, improved hydrophilicity, and altered surface morphology. Subsequent tests confirmed that the bilayer scaffold exhibited enhanced compression resistance and mechanical compatibility with bone compared to a single hydrogel scaffold. Additionally, the bilayer scaffold showed stable and reliable bonding properties, as well as excellent biosafety verified by cell proliferation and viability experiments using mouse embryo osteoblast precursor (MC3T3-E1) cells.ConclusionThe bilayer bone regeneration scaffold prepared in this study showed promising potential in clinical application for bone regeneration.

Introduction

Dental implant gets an obvious priority in restoring missing teeth. However, serious bone defects caused by infection, trauma, and tumors often make dental implants difficult to implement in clinical practice. Therefore, reconstructing an ideal contour of the alveolar bone is prerequisite for dental implant installment [1, 2]. Guided bone regeneration (GBR) is the conventional solution, which was carried out by covering the bone substitute material with a barrier membrane to prevent the invasion of fibroblasts, providing the space and a stable environment for bone regeneration. The deproteinized bovine minerals (DBBM) usually be used accompany with either the flexible or the rigid barrier membranes to fill the bone defect area [3]. Absorbable collagen membrane got good biocompatibility and was easy to operate for its flexibility. However, it was short in spatial maintenance and persistence which makes it only suitable for small-scale bone augmentation [4]. Rigid titanium-reinforced polytetrafluoroethylene (e-PTFE) and titanium mesh (Ti) have been applied to large bone defects due to their excellent mechanical strength, but their high elastic modulus may produce a stress shielding effect on bone, resulting in bone resorption. Besides, the rigid and sharp edge of Ti mesh was aggressive to soft tissue, which increases the exposure risk of scaffold and leads to failure results [5]. It is urgent to develop a new form scaffold to meet the biocompatibility requirement and stabilize the bone regeneration space. For long, bone autografts have been considered the gold standard. However, a second surgical area is necessary and high technical sensitivity has discouraged many patients and dentists. Inorganic bone powder particles, just like DBBM, are unable to interconnect with each other to form a real functional bone system. They have bionic limitations and even may be displaced and leakaged when loaded with stress [6]. To overcome these shortcomings, a novel barrier framework with improved physical, chemical, and biological properties that can meet the PASS bone grafting principles in GBR, which requires primary wound closure, angiogenesis, space maintenance and stability, should be developed and would have significant application prospects [7].

Among the polymers, the PEEK appears to be more promising as a bone scaffold for its excellent biosafety, physicochemical properties, x-ray transmission, ideal processing convenience and modification potential. When loaded with bioactive materials, such as ASA and nHAP, the bioactivity of PEEK surface can be improved dramatically. Low-concentration aspirin can promote the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts, reduce the activity of osteoclasts, and regulate the polarization of macrophage inflammatory phenotype. The Ca2+ and PO43− released by nano-hydroxyapatite can regulate the proliferation, migration, and differentiation of osteoblasts, and has been widely used in the field of bone tissue engineering. It has been reported that osteogenesis would be promoted when the SPEEK implant surface coated with boron-hydroxyapatite nanoparticles (B-nHAP) [8]. However, because of the thin coating layer, this modification is not suitable for reconstructing large alveolar bone defects. ASA and bone forming peptide (BFP) once have been loaded on the surface of SPEEK implant by immersion method. Although surface modification can improve cell viability and osteogenic differentiation, the burst-release of functional components imposes difficulty to maintain a suitable environment [9]. Therefore, loading ASA and nHAP on PEEK and achieving a proper sustained-release rate of these bioactive components may be a promising and significant attempt.

Recently, hydrogels have been widely used due to their excellent capacities in releasing drugs, filling bone defects, and making close contact with the surrounding active tissue [10]. In addition, biological function advantages such as promoting cell adhesion and growth, regulating the inflammatory microenvironment have also stimulated interest [11]. Hydrogel scaffolds loaded with nHAP and ASA have greater structural stability and osteogenic capacity than artificial bone substitutes. However, some inherent disadvantages such as preventing fibroblast ingrowth and mismatch of the elastic modulus to cortical bone, remain a limitation for the application of single hydrogel scaffold [12, 13].

Combining the PEEK and the functional hydrogel may be a feasible solution, but the interfacial bonding of these two materials is a key point. Polydopamine (PDA) can form a thin film to adhere to the material surface and promote cell attachment and proliferation. In addition, the abundant functional groups of PDA such as carboxyl, amino, and imino groups can functionalize the substrate, and facilitate the bonding of various bioactive molecules [14, 15]. Untreated PEEK is biologically inert [16], while creating porosity on the PEEK surface is an effective strategy to increase surface area, roughness, and bioactivity. In this study, the sulfonated porous PEEK was used as a bone scaffold on the premise of keeping its mechanical and physical properties. And then, the PDA was used to firmly connect SPEEK with the multifunctional hydrogel scaffold to form a “physical–chemical-biological” bilayer bone regeneration scaffold. Subsequently, Fourier infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray spectroscopy (XPS), contact angle, mechanical strength, and peel experiments were used to evaluate the characterizations. The biosafety was evaluated by the proliferation and survival of MC3T3-E1. Materials and methods

Material

The PEEK was from Jilin China Research PEEK Company, the Tris–HCl and Dopamine were from Solarbio (Beijing, China). Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA-124) was from Guanghua Technology Company (Guangdong; China). Aspirin (ASA), gelatin (Gel, type B), sodium alginate (SA), and anhydrous calcium chloride (CaCl2) were from Aladdin (Shanghai; China). The nano-hydroxyapatite particle (size 20 nm) was from Eprinome (Nanjing; China). Phosphate buffer solution (PBS) and penicillin mixture (100 ×) were obtained from Solepipe (Beijing, China). Fetal bovine serum and ɑ-MEM medium were from Biological Industries (Israel). Cell Proliferation Kit (CCK-8) and Live/dead staining kit were also from Solebro (Beijing, China).

Sample preparation

Preparation of bilayer PEEK layer

PEEK Surface Modification

The PEEK disk (10 mm in diameter and 1 mm thick) was burnished sequentially with different sandpaper (400#, 800#, and 1500#) to get a smooth surface. Then, they were ultrasonically cleaned in acetone, anhydrous ethanol, and deionized water for 5 min in turn. Finally, the samples were air-dried at room temperature.

PEEK Sulfonation(SPEEK)

At room temperature, the dried pure PEEK disks were immersed in concentrated sulfuric acid (98%). The reaction was carried out on a magnetic stirrer with a speed of 500 rpm/min for 6 min, then the PEEK disks were removed and placed in ice-cold ultrapure water immediately to get SPEEK. The SPEEK was ultrasonically cleaned with acetone, anhydrous ethanol, and ultrapure water for 10 min under YM-008S ultrasonic cleaner (Shenzhen Clean Union Technology Company). Finally, the SPEEK was heated at 120℃ for 4 h to remove the residual sulfuric acid on the surface and dried at room temperature for 24 h.

Dopamine-treated sulfonated PEEK (PDA-SPEEK)

Tris–HCl buffer solution with a concentration of 10 mmol/L was prepared. Firstly, 121 mg of Tris powder was weighed and dissolved in 100 ml of deionized water, and the pH was adjusted to 8.5 with dilute hydrochloric acid. Then, dopamine solution with a concentration of 2 mg/ml was prepared: 50 mg of dopamine hydrochloride was weighed and completely dissolved into 24.6 ml of Tris buffer, the prepared dopamine solution was colorless and transparent. SPEEK was then placed in Tris dopamine solution and placed in a constant temperature incubator at 37℃ for 24 h. All the dopamine reaction was processed in the dark, and the dopamine solution became black when the reaction finished. Finally, the PDA-SPEEK was washed with deionized water for 5 min by ultrasonication and dried at room temperature.

Preparation of a bilayer scaffold (Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK)

The preparation of the hydrogel solution was carried out according to our previous study [17]. Briefly, the prepared solution containing ASA/n-HAP/PVA/Gel/SA was poured into a mold containing PDA-SPEEK. Then they were placed into a refrigerator and frozen at -20 °C for 18 h, finally thawed at room temperature for 4 h. After three cycles, the physically solidified hydrogel scaffold was immersed in a solution containing 2% anhydrous calcium chloride for chemical crosslinking for 24 h. The samples were gently washed with ultrapure water and then freeze-dried to obtain Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK.

Microstructure

The surface of the samples was gold-sprayed and observed with a S-4800 scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi, Japan) at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV. The morphology changes in surface microstructure were photographed.

Chemical structure and elements

FTIR: Dried samples were ground at room temperature, and completely mixed with potassium bromide, then the mixture was pressed into tablets. The IS50 Fourier infrared spectrometer (FTIR, Thermo, USA) was used to examine the chemical structure and functional groups by scanning 32 times at a resolution of 4 cm−1. The scan range was 400–4000 cm−1.

XPS: The elemental composition of carbon (C), oxygen (O), nitrogen (N), sulfur (S), and calcium (Ca) on the samples was analyzed by Scientific Nexsa X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo USA). At the same time, the vertical distribution and the changes of these elements were measured to verify whether the hydrogel scaffold was successfully bonded to the PEEK.

Mechanical properties and peeling strength

Compression experiments were carried out to verify the mechanical properties of the scaffolds. The compression test was performed with RGM-2100 universal mechanical testing machine (Guangzhou Rigel Company, China). The loading speed was 5 mm/min along the long axis of samples. The test would be finished until the interface was broken. The maximum compression strength and compression modulus were obtained.

The bonding strength and bonding stability between the hydrogel scaffold and the PEEK were tested through a 180° peel experiment. The sample was first fixed on a slide. Then cyanoacrylate adhesive (Harbin Tonglin Adhesive Factory) was applied to the surface of the hydrogel scaffold with a bonding area of 10 mm × 10 mm. The adhesion strength was measured by a universal mechanical testing machine at a loading rate of 50 mm / min−1. All measurements were repeated three times.

Hydrophilicity

The wettability and hydrophilicity of sample surface was characterized by an OCA contact angle measuring instrument (Dataphysics, Germany). First, l µl deionized water was squeezed from a micropipette and added to the test surface. The droplet was held on the sample surface for 5 s before photographing, and then the contact angle was measured. Three points at different locations on the surface were selected for measurement and the average value was calculated.

In vitro biocompatibility

An immersion solution for each experimental group was prepared before cell culture. The sample of each group was immersed in complete medium (CM, containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin and streptomycin) at a ratio of 0.1 g/ml. Then, the 0.22 μm filter was used to filter the obtained extraction and eliminate bacteria.

Cell viability

The live/dead cell staining kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was used to evaluate cell viability. First, MC3T3-E1 cells were inoculated in 96-well plates (n = 3) at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well. After 24 h, the original CM, SPEEK, PDA-SPEEK, and hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK group extractions were replaced with 100 μl of fresh CM. After 1 d, 3 d, and 5 d, the CM was replaced with 100 μl of live/dead cell staining reagent, and the cells were stained at 37℃ for 30 min in darkness. The cells were observed with an inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan) and photographed in darkness. The cell viability effect was assessed by observing the cell morphology and the ratio of live to dead cells.

Cell proliferation

The cell proliferation kit (CCK-8, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. MC3T3-E1 cells were cultured as described above. At the end of the experiment, 10 μl CCK-8 and 100 μl fresh CM were added to each cell-containing well and incubated for 1 h. The optical density (OD) values were detected at 450 nm using a SpectraMax M2 enzyme labeler (Molecular Devices, USA). The effect was evaluated by comparing the OD values relative to the control group.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 22.0 software was used to process the data. Comparisons between groups were performed according to one-way ANOVA and LSD tests. Data were expressed as x ± s. Data were considered statistically significant when the data *P < 0.05.

Chemical structure and elements

As shown in Fig. 2A, as for SPEEK, the characteristic peaks at 1487 cm−1 and 926 cm−1 were attributed to the benzophenone structure belonging to PEEK. The characteristic peak appeared at 1600 cm−1 and corresponded to the C = C group of the benzene ring of PEEK. The characteristic peaks at 1252 cm−1, 1100 cm−1, 1010 cm−1, and 679 cm−1 bands showed the presence of O = S = O. Asymmetric contraction and symmetric contraction of S = O also confirmed the successful sulfonation reaction on the PEEK. On the PDA-SPEEK scaffold, the broad peak at 3090 cm−1 was attributed to -OH as well as -NH2 groups from PDA coating. The characteristic peaks at about 1610 cm−1 and 1650 cm−1 were associated with bending vibrations of the N–H groups and stretching vibrations of the benzene ring structure, respectively. In the Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK group, the characteristic peak of the hydrogel scaffold could be observed at 3277 cm−1. Simultaneously, characteristic peaks of n-HAP also appeared at 555 cm−1, 603 cm−1, and 1024 cm−1, as well as characteristic peaks at about 1000 cm−1 attributed to the -PO43−. The mechanical properties were evaluated by the compression tests. Figure 3A and B showed the mechanical properties of each group with compressive strengths of 14.84 MPa, 14.01 MPa, and 14.38 MPa, respectively, and compressive modulus of 83.17 MPa, 94.55 MPa, and 107.20 MPa, respectively. As shown in Fig. 3C, the peeling force of the interface between the hydrogel scaffold and the PDA-SPEEK was approximately 1.5 N, and the morphology of the hydrogel was stable without fracture during the peeling process. The adhesion energy between these two layers was about 8 kJ/m2. The interfacial bonding force between the hydrogel scaffold and the PEEK bone scaffold was further verified to be stable and suitable, and it exceeded the hydrogel’s destructive force.Fig. 3Mechanical properties of the PEEK bone regeneration scaffold (A) Compressive strength (B) Compressive modulus (C) Peeling strength

Discussion

This study aimed to fabricate a novel multifunctional bilayer bionic scaffold. The original smooth morphology of the PEEK was modified into a three-dimensional (3D) interpenetrating porous structure by sulfonation treatment. The porous structure expanded the surface area and facilitated the bonding between SPEEK and PDA [18]. Active groups such as -NH2 and -COOH were introduced to the surface of PDA-SPEEK by immersing the peek in dopamine solution. Compared to conventional coating methods, the close bonding between the hydrogel and PDA-SPEEK was achieved through hydrogen bonds and electrostatic attraction [19].

The theoretical basis to use PEEK as a bone scaffold was that a high-level mechanical embedding of SPEEK implants in animal experiment [20]. The SPEEK surface with abundant and uniform porous structure provides enough attachment sites for PDA and hydrogel scaffold [21].In this study, the microporous structure was uniformly formed on the PEEK surface by sulfonation and the corresponding post-treatment, which was associated with acid etching, thermal expansion, and changes in the molecular chain [22]. Additionally, FTIR test showed that -SO4 on the SPEEK surface appeared at 1252 cm−1, 1100 cm−1, 1010 m−1, and 679 cm−1. The XPS results also verified that the existence of the S element appeared in the SPEEK, confirming the occurrence of the sulfonation reaction [23, 24].

Studies have proved that PDA has exceptional adhesion, bioactivity and other properties [25]. In a mildly alkaline environment, dopamine solutions can form PDA films on solid materials through self-polymerization, without compromising their properties, size, or shape [26]. Zhu et al. applied a PDA coating on SPEEK surface to integrate Arginine-Glycine-Aspartic Acid (RGD) and indicated that the treated PEEK had an excellent ability to promote cell proliferation, osteogenic differentiation, and the formation of bone-like apatite [27]. Like previous discovery, the SEM results in this study also illustrated the PDA particles on the PDA-SPEEK did not plug the micropores. FTIR results showed that on the PDA-SPEEK, the characteristic peaks of PDA appeared at 1610 cm−1 and 1650 cm−1, 3090 cm−1. XPS results demonstrated an increase in N element content to 8.17% on the PDA-SPEEK. The PDA coating has good biocompatibility, furthermore, its abundant functional groups such as -NH2, -COOH can serve as a “bridge” to react with other components [28]. Hence, PDA formed a complete and dense coating layer on the porous PEEK and modified the surface properties (hydrophobicity, etc.), which facilitated further activation [29]. As for Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK, the hydrogel scaffold’s hierarchical-level porous structures encompassed PDA particles and microporous structures, as demonstrated in the SEM observation. Characteristic peaks of hydrogel scaffold appeared at 555 cm−1, 603 cm−1, 1024 cm−1, and 3277 cm−1 due to interactions such as hydrogen bonding and electrostatic gravitational force [30]. XPS results confirmed the change in the ratio of C, N, and O elements. The effective bonding of PDA between the two layers was further confirmed by the emergence of the distinctive element Ca from nano-hydroxyapatite [31]. In short, the SEM, FTIR and XPS results confirmed the strong connection between the SPEEK barrier layer and hydrogel layer and the feasibility of PDA bio-adhesion.

The stability and duration of the barrier layer was crucial to ensure wound closure, angiogenesis, and tissue regeneration. Only using hydrogel to fill bone deficiencies in the alveolar region excessively can stimulate new bone growth, potentially leading to scaffold displacement [32]. A common method to bone augmentation involves a resorbable collagen membrane and DBBM/Bio-Oss, bone blocks, or pellets, which was proved effective for small horizontal and vertical bone defects less than 4 mm [33, 34], but showed limitation when dealing with larger bone defects [35]. The artificial bone particles cannot integrate as one functional system for their inorganic compositions, then they would shift and collapse under stress. Besides, artificial bone substitutes can exist for rather long time since it was hard to be replaced by new bone [36]. So, a resorbable hydrogel may be an ideal substitute close to newly formed bone. The hydrogel could degrade slowly in pace with the new bone forming, which overcame the poor affinity of DBBM powder. Concerning the mechanical properties, the results showed that the compressive strength and compressive modulus of Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK were 14.38 MPa and 107.20 MPa respectively, which were significantly higher than those of single hydrogel scaffold [17]. Additionally, the hydrogel’s swelling qualities enhanced the SPEEK layer’s lubricity and adequately absorbed water to create a “soft-hard” contact pattern between the material and the bone [37]. Furthermore, the PEEK can buffer stress and overcome shortcomings of the absorbable barrier membranes in premature absorption and insufficient space maintenance ability. In this work, a PEEK layer and a hydrogel layer loaded with aspirin and nano-hydroxyapatite by the PDA were more durable than the cold spraying technique [38]. Compared with medical adhesives such as methacrylate (MMA) which showed cytotoxicity and bioinertness, the PDA was more reasonable for its excellent biocompatibility [39], which also overcame the weak binding force of the pure coating method [40].

The excellent bonding between the hydrogel and the SPEEK presents a promising opportunity for future application. Cell behavior relies on the complex surface properties of materials, such as wettability, roughness, and material properties [41]. The contact angle of Hydrogel scaffold-PDA-SPEEK indicated that the hydrogel endowed SPEEK with hydrophilicity. It is reported that hydrogels loaded with ASA and n-HAP could promote cell proliferation [42, 43], and the strategy was adopted in this study. As expected, Hydrogel-PDA-SPEEK scaffold showed superior biocompatibility, providing a safe, dependable, and repeatable way to build a scaffold for bone regeneration. However, comprehensive in vivo studies and exploration are still necessary to validate the bone regenerative effects.

Conclusion

The Michael addition reaction of PDA was utilized in this study to covalently link hydrogel and the sulfonated PEEK into a “physical–chemical-biological” bilayer scaffold. The bilayer scaffold demonstrated excellent biocompatibility for supporting cell viability and promoting cell proliferation. The SPEEK can serve as a rigid base to maintain the space, while the hydrogel mimicked bone composition, filled bone defects, and enabled slow release of aspirin. When they were combined, the hydrogel layer significantly enhanced the hydrophilicity of SPEEK by introducing elements such as N and Ca to its surface. Mechanical property test confirmed that the bilayer scaffold exhibited superior compressive strength and modulus compared to hydrogel alone. Therefore, these bilayer scaffolds showed promise as a feasible solution to large bone regeneration procedure.

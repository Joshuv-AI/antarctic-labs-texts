Study on the optimization of natural gas station inspection paths considering station priority
Authors: Xinyan Peng, Jingyi Li, Yuan Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs the demand for natural gas supply chain security increases, the optimization of inspection efficiency in natural gas stations is increasingly critical to ensure the safe operation of equipment. This study identifies key issues, including the disregard for equipment priority differences and inadequate coordination of multiple objectives in traditional inspection path planning. By integrating digitalization and artificial intelligence technologies, and considering both inspection costs and priority satisfaction, a priority satisfaction function is constructed, and a multi-objective optimization model is proposed to minimize inspection costs and maximize priority satisfaction. A hybrid genetic-simulated annealing algorithm (GA-SA) is designed to solve this model. A case study was conducted to validate the model and algorithm’s effectiveness in reducing inspection costs and enhancing priority satisfaction. The study demonstrated that, compared to experience-based manual planning methods, the proposed model reduced inspection costs by approximately 20.70% and increased priority satisfaction by about 5.33%. These improvements are practically significant for the natural gas industry: the 20.70% cost reduction directly alleviates operational burdens, while the 5.33% enhancement in priority satisfaction strengthens coverage of high-risk equipment, aligning with the industry’s dual goals of economic efficiency and safety. A sensitivity analysis of key parameters (including β, ω1, Dmax, and α) is conducted to provide practical parameter setting guidelines, enhancing the model’s applicability. Based on the research findings, a dynamic inspection path management strategy based on artificial intelligence is proposed, which provides intelligent decision support that balances both economic and safety considerations for the natural gas industry. This strategy also offers theoretical insights for multi-objective path optimization problems in complex scenarios.Keywords: Natural gas station inspection, Path optimization, Station priority, Artificial intelligence, Digital transformationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As global energy demand grew steadily, countries actively developed clean energy sources, such as natural gas, to address climate change1. The security of natural gas supply and the stability of its industrial chain were key areas of focus in energy security planning during China’s 14th Five-Year Plan period (2021–2025). Natural gas consumption in China increased rapidly alongside urbanization, placing greater demands on supply chain security. In 2022, the National Energy Administration issued the “2022 Energy Work Guidance Opinions,” which explicitly emphasized the need to enhance technological innovation in energy, prioritize breakthroughs in green and low-carbon technologies, and accelerate the digital and intelligent transformation of the energy sector. Natural gas stations, as critical nodes in the collection and transportation process, rely on efficient inspection operations to ensure the safe functioning of equipment. Although new technologies such as drones and robots have been gradually applied, manual inspection remains indispensable due to complex terrain and high-precision sensing requirements. However, the dispersed distribution of station equipment and significant differences in equipment priorities often result in neglected resource allocation and poor priority management. These issues lead to low inspection efficiency, elevated costs, and limited coverage of high-priority equipment, ultimately jeopardizing operational safety.

Existing inspection path optimization approaches often rely on heuristics or basic rules of thumb, which hinders the coordination of multiple factors such as equipment priority, resource availability, and cost. Although intelligent algorithms like genetic algorithms and ant colony optimization have been widely adopted, they have exhibited limitations in the context of natural gas stations, such as imbalanced global-local search, susceptibility to local optima, and inadequate handling of multiple objectives and constraints. To address these challenges, a multi-objective optimization model was constructed to minimize inspection costs and maximize priority satisfaction. A hybrid genetic-simulated annealing algorithm (GA-SA) was proposed, combining the global search capacity of genetic algorithms with the local optimization capability of simulated annealing. This approach aimed to improve the scientific rigor and decision-making efficiency of inspection path planning, offering an optimization strategy that considers both economic efficiency and operational safety for the natural gas industry.

The contributions of this study can be summarized in four main aspects. First, this study integrates digital transformation with intelligent inspection by proposing a multi-objective optimization model based on station priorities and a hybrid algorithm (GA-SA), providing theoretical support for the intelligent upgrade of natural gas stations, in alignment with national policy directives. Second, it presents an innovative application of artificial intelligence in optimizing inspection paths, offering a solution that balances global exploration and local refinement while improving precision and efficiency in solving complex optimization problems. Third, it demonstrates interdisciplinary integration by applying theories and methods from operations research, artificial intelligence, and computer science, thereby enhancing both academic significance and practical application. Fourth, a dynamic strategy for inspection path management is introduced, which combines real-time optimization and data-driven techniques to adapt inspection plans to complex and evolving station environments. This enables efficient and flexible responses to the uncertainties encountered in real-world operations.

Literature review

Research on inspection of natural gas stations

Natural gas stations serve as a critical component in the collection, processing, and transportation of natural gas; their safety and reliability are essential to energy supply stability. However, traditional manual inspection methods exhibited limited informatization and lacked systems for real-time monitoring and data analysis, thereby preventing timely fault resolution and potentially leading to major safety incidents2. In recent years, with the rapid advancement of technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT) and cloud computing, the management model of natural gas stations gradually shifted from manual inspections to a data-driven approach incorporating real-time monitoring, automated analysis, and intelligent decision-making3.

For instance, the pipeline inspection robot system developed by Baballe et al. significantly enhanced the efficiency and reliability of pipeline inspections4. The DARTS system, proposed by Ravishankar et al., integrates drones with deep learning to improve pipeline safety through periodic image data collection5. Fischer et al. (2024) introduced an intelligent mobile robotic inspection system that integrates LiDAR, RGB/IR cameras, and acoustic sensors with 3D environmental modeling and multimodal visual processing. By enabling autonomous navigation and structural anomaly identification in complex industrial environments, the system significantly improved inspection reliability through high-density sensor fusion6. Similarly, Wang et al. (2019) developed a vision-based robotic platform for methane leak detection using optical gas imaging (OGI) and a convolutional neural network (CNN), referred to as GasNet. Automated detection was performed with an accuracy exceeding 95%, demonstrating strong robustness under small leakage volumes and long-range conditions (4–16 m). These features offered clear advantages in terms of real-time responsiveness and fine-grained leakage identification7.

Wanasinghe et al. emphasized that drone-based inspections were effective in enhancing inspection efficiency, safety, and cost control. Nonetheless, despite the progress in intelligent inspection technologies, several challenges persisted, including battery limitations, sensor accuracy issues, and poor adaptability to harsh environments8. Particularly in natural gas stations, the deployment of inspection technologies was constrained by complex terrain and diverse equipment configurations. On-site operations continued to rely heavily on manual inspections, and the necessity of manual efforts remains difficult to eliminate9.

Currently, manual inspection practices often lack scientific and efficient path planning, and fail to comprehensively account for factors such as equipment priority, risk level differentiation, and optimal resource allocation. This situation contributes to reduced inspection efficiency, increased operational costs, and an urgent need to optimize both inspection methods and route planning strategies.

Research on vehicle routing optimization models

The Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) aims to design optimal routes for multiple delivery points and vehicles, minimizing total costs while satisfying a set of operational constraints10. Initially, the VRP was solved using classical routing optimization models—such as the shortest path problem, VRP with time windows, and VRP with capacity constraints—to address single-objective problems11. As practical application demands increased, multi-vehicle scheduling and multi-load task allocation models have been further developed to incorporate additional operational constraints and energy-related objectives12. Efforts were also made to balance delivery cost, carbon emissions, and customer satisfaction through comprehensive multi-objective frameworks13. Multi-objective optimization models typically seek Pareto trade-offs among conflicting goals to address the complexity of real-world routing contexts. For example, Liang et al. introduced a bi-objective multi-period vehicle routing model for perishable goods distribution that minimized transportation cost and maximized customer satisfaction, while explicitly considering time windows and packaging/loading times in cold chain logistics14. Ahmed et al. proposed a multi-objective model that considered not only vehicle heterogeneity but also time window constraints, and required path optimization within limited timeframes15. This model was applicable to scenarios involving heterogeneous fleets and urgent delivery tasks. Sadati et al. proposed the Electric Vehicle Routing Problem with Flexibility (EVRP-FD), which optimized routing while accounting for battery life, flexibility, and timeliness to achieve a balanced trade-off between delivery cost and operational flexibility16. The cold chain delivery optimization model introduced by Li et al. (2023) focused on minimizing customer dissatisfaction and loss costs, offering theoretical insights for multi-objective path optimization in cold chain logistics17.

In addressing the practical needs of natural gas station inspections, it is essential to consider factors such as geographic complexity18, equipment diversity19, real‑time data monitoring20, and regional management approaches21 when constructing routing optimization models. Studies have shown that the integration of intelligent technologies with routing optimization frameworks effectively improved inspection efficiency, reduced operational costs, and enhanced risk management capabilities in complex, heterogeneous, and dynamic field environments.

Research on vehicle routing optimization algorithms

The Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) remained a central research focus in the domains of logistics and inspection. Early path optimization methods were initially based on classical mathematical optimization techniques, such as dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, and simulated annealing, which were suitable for small-scale and single-objective optimization problems22. With advancements in computational technologies and artificial intelligence, intelligent optimization algorithms—such as genetic algorithms23, ant colony optimization24, and particle swarm optimization25—gradually became mainstream approaches. However, in complex scenarios involving multiple objectives and constraints, traditional methods and single-type intelligent algorithms often failed to balance competing goals and were prone to becoming trapped in local optima. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed multi-objective and constraint-aware optimization algorithms capable of identifying trade-offs among conflicting goals while handling complex operational constraints effectively. For example, an Efficient Simulated Annealing (ESA) algorithm was applied to omnichannel vehicle routing scenarios to address large-scale combinatorial complexity, achieving new best-known solutions in most benchmark instances26. Moreover, Ma et al. introduced an improved Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search combined with Simulated Annealing (IALNS‑SA), which balances economic efficiency and operational safety in vehicle routing problems with time windows and simultaneous pickup-and-delivery constraints27. In the coordination and optimization of multi-objective path planning, Wardana et al. proposed an optimized A* path planning algorithm that improves the efficiency of port robot inspection by applying node simplification and path linearization techniques28. For bi-objective scenarios, Khalili-Damghani et al. developed a perishable goods delivery routing model that explicitly considers perishability, value loss, and temperature-controlled collaborative delivery, addressing VRP challenges under cold chain constraints29. Xu et al.proposed a dynamic path optimization method using a reinforcement learning-based adaptive variable neighborhood search (RL‑AVNS), coordinating multi-agent objectives and adapting routes in real time for food delivery services30. In another example, Liu et al. employed a hybrid NSGA‑II algorithm (HNSGA‑II) combined with greedy random adaptive search procedures, enabling efficient multi-objective route planning under complex urban logistics scenarios31.

Recent studies have leveraged advanced AI/ML for path optimization: deep reinforcement learning enhances dynamic routing adaptability32, hybrid deep learning-evolutionary algorithms improve multi-objective solution quality33, and fuzzy logic-aided heuristics address industrial inspection risks34. Our GA-SA algorithm complements these works by emphasizing station priority decay and practical operational constraints, tailored to natural gas station inspection needs.

Research review

Although existing research made notable progress in the field of inspection path optimization, several limitations remain. (1) From a practical application standpoint, manual inspection continued to be the primary method used in natural gas stations. However, most current research primarily focused on the application of emerging technologies such as drones and robots, emphasizing innovation while overlooking critical real-world factors such as geographic complexity and equipment-specific requirements. This oversight has resulted in difficulties in meeting the operational demands of field environments. (2) Research on vehicle routing optimization gradually introduced multi-objective optimization models that considered various factors, including cost, time, and safety. However, these models did not fully address the unique operational constraints of natural gas station inspections, particularly the diversity of equipment and task prioritization, thereby hindering effective coordination and optimization across multiple objectives. (3) In the development of path optimization algorithms, traditional techniques were gradually replaced by intelligent algorithms to achieve global optimization in complex scenarios. Nonetheless, in the context of natural gas station inspections, these algorithms continued to encounter challenges related to conflicting objectives and complex constraints, indicating the need for enhanced flexibility and adaptability.

In summary, while existing research contributed valuable insights to natural gas station inspection and vehicle routing optimization, current models and algorithms were not fully aligned with the specific operational requirements of inspection tasks. To address these gaps, this study proposes a multi-objective optimization model tailored to the actual conditions of natural gas station inspections and introduces a hybrid genetic-simulated annealing algorithm (GA-SA) for solving it. The algorithm integrates the global search capability of genetic algorithms and the local refinement capability of simulated annealing to achieve efficient and effective inspection path optimization.

Problem description and modeling

Problem description

In the daily operation and safety management of natural gas stations, inspection vehicles are dispatched from the inspection control center to visit assigned stations. These inspections aim to ensure proper equipment operation, identify and prevent potential risks, and return to the control center upon task completion. Due to variations in geographic location, equipment failure risk, operational importance, and security levels, stations are assigned different inspection priorities. This is represented by a priority parameter, where higher values indicate a greater urgency for inspection.

Given the limited number of inspection vehicles and the maximum travel distance constraint for each vehicle, efficient inspection path planning—while satisfying the demands of all inspection points—is critical for reducing total operational costs and improving inspection efficiency.

The proposed model for inspection path optimization is designed to achieve two primary objectives: (1) Minimize Total Cost: The total cost comprises fixed vehicle usage and travel distance costs. Cost efficiency is achieved by minimizing unnecessary detours and optimizing vehicle assignments. (1) Maximize Priority Satisfaction: High-priority inspection points must be inspected promptly to mitigate operational risks and ensure station safety. To this end, a decay function is introduced to quantify priority satisfaction, where time-sensitive degradation encourages early inspection of high-priority locations.

Algorithm design

Based on a comparative analysis of the strengths, limitations, and application scenarios of commonly used vehicle routing algorithms, the hybrid genetic-simulated annealing algorithm (GA-SA) was found to better balance global exploration and local refinement when solving the inspection path optimization problem in natural gas stations. GA excels in global exploration through selection, crossover and mutation, enabling extensive traversal of the solution space, while SA boasts strong local optimization capability and the ability to escape local optima via the Metropolis criterion. Their combination compensates for GA’s tendency to stagnate in local optima and SA’s inefficiency in global search, achieving a superior balance between exploration and exploitation compared to other hybrid strategies. It was suitable for handling complex constraints, including limited vehicle numbers, mileage limits, and the multi-objective model involving cost minimization and priority satisfaction, and provided stable, high-quality solutions. Regarding convergence, the GA-SA algorithm inherits GA’s directional global search and SA’s temperature-decaying probabilistic acceptance mechanism. As temperature decreases, the acceptance probability of inferior solutions gradually diminishes, guiding the population to converge toward the global optimum. Theoretical analysis and experimental validation confirm that the algorithm converges to a Pareto-optimal solution set, ensuring stable and reliable optimization results. Therefore, the GA-SA algorithm was applied in this study to solve the inspection path optimization model for natural gas stations, based on the actual operational conditions. Conclusion and recommendations

Research conclusions

This study investigated the inspection path optimization problem for natural gas stations, motivated by the practical need to balance operational cost efficiency with the safety requirements of high-priority equipment. By critically reviewing the literature, we identified that existing vehicle routing models and algorithms inadequately address the unique characteristics of station-based inspections, particularly the time-sensitive decay of equipment priority. To bridge this gap, we proposed a multi-objective optimization model that simultaneously minimizes total inspection costs (comprising fixed vehicle usage and variable travel costs) and maximizes priority satisfaction. Priority satisfaction was formulated using an exponential decay function of the arrival time, ensuring that high-priority stations are serviced as early as possible. The model incorporates realistic operational constraints, including vehicle capacity, maximum travel distance, and single-visit coverage.

Given the NP-hard nature of the problem, we developed a hybrid genetic-simulated annealing algorithm (GA-SA). This algorithm leverages the global exploration capability of Genetic Algorithms and the local exploitation strength of Simulated Annealing, enhanced by a dynamic mutation mechanism and the Metropolis acceptance criterion to escape local optima. The effectiveness of the proposed model and algorithm was validated through a simulated case study based on operational data from a real gas field. The key findings are as follows:

Superior Optimization Performance: Compared to traditional experience-based manual planning, the GA-SA optimized solution reduced total inspection costs by 20.70%, total mileage by 17.71%, total inspection time by 14.59%, and the number of vehicles deployed by 25%. Concurrently, priority satisfaction improved by 5.33%. These results demonstrate that the model successfully mediates the inherent trade-off between economic and safety objectives.

Concurrently, priority satisfaction improved by 5.33%. These results demonstrate that the model successfully mediates the inherent trade-off between economic and safety objectives.

Algorithmic Effectiveness: The hybrid GA-SA algorithm demonstrated strong global convergence and stability. By dynamically adjusting mutation rates with temperature decay and utilizing simulated annealing for offspring selection, the algorithm effectively avoided premature convergence and produced high-quality, feasible routing schemes under complex constraints.

Parameter Sensitivity Insights: Sensitivity analysis revealed that the priority decay coefficient (β) and the cost weight in the fitness function (ω1) are the most influential parameters. Increasing β significantly enhances priority satisfaction with a marginal reduction in cost, while adjusting ω1 allows decision-makers to flexibly shift the emphasis between cost efficiency and safety coverage. Despite these contributions, this study has limitations. The most significant is Assumption 10, which presumes unobstructed roads and ignores real-world disruptions such as traffic congestion, adverse weather, or temporary roadblocks. This idealization may lead to deviations between planned and actual execution in dynamic field environments.

In summary, this research provides a systematic and intelligent decision-support framework for natural gas station inspections. It not only yields quantifiable economic and safety benefits but also offers theoretical and methodological references for multi-objective path optimization problems in other industrial inspection contexts.

By leveraging national digital transformation policies, relevant technological guidance and financial incentives can be adopted to support the transition. This will ensure the proper execution of optimized routing plans, thereby enhancing both inspection efficiency and managerial capability. Additionally, the integration of artificial intelligence technologies can further improve decision-making efficiency and promote the widespread adoption of intelligent inspection practices.

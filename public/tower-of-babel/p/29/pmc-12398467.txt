Analysis of the effect of laparoscopic temporary long-tunneled external ventricular drainage in patients with post-traumatic brain injury hydrocephalus
Authors: Mingtao Chang, Jianfa Chen, Xia Qin, Hua Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo study the clinical effect of laparoscopic temporary long-tunneled external ventricular drainage(LT-LTEVD) and conventional laparoscopic ventriculoperitoneal shunt(CL-VPS) in patients with post-traumatic brain injury hydrocephalus(PTH).MethodsThis study was a prospective study. We analyzed the clinical data of 52 patients with PTH admitted to our department from October 2018 to October 2021.All cases were randomly divided into two groups, and performed LT-LTEVD and CL-VPS respectively. The catheterization time, remission time of intracranial hypertension, hospital stay, therapeutic effectiveness and complications rate were compared between groups.ResultsThe therapeutic effect of LT-LTEVD group (92.31%) was higher to that of CL-VPS group(65.38%), with statistical significance. Except the catheterization time, remission time of intracranial hypertension and hospital stay of CL-VPS group were significantly higher to those of the LT-LTEVD group, with statistical significance. The incidence of complications such as intracranial infection, shunt, obstruction and poor healing in the CL-VPS group(30.77%) was higher than that in LT-LTEVD group(7.69%), with statistical significance.ConclusionThe preliminary results of this study showed that in the treatment of patients with PTH, LT-LTEVD could potentially have a positive effect. However, future researches are needed to clarify these preliminary results.

Introduction

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a traumatic disease that causes temporary or permanent damage to brain structure and physiological function caused by external physical factors. Besides special circumstances such as war and car accidents, TBI is also more and more common in modern daily life, such as tumble, sports injuries, and family conflicts [1–3]. Given the characteristics of high morbidity, high disability rate and high mortality, it has a poor prognosis. In addition, TBI can cause disturbance of cerebrospinal fluid circulation and malabsorption of cerebrospinal fluid, which can lead to the accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid in various cavities of the brain and the subsequent development of hydrocephalus, which is one of the most common complications of TBI, be referred to as post-traumatic hydrocephalus(PTH) [4].

At present, the main treatment for PTH is shunt, which includes ventriculoperitoneal shunt(VPS), subarachnoid-peritoneal shunt, endoscopic third ventriculostomy, etc [5]. In all of these, the most frequently used method is ventriculoperitoneal shunt, but the incidence of postoperative complications is still high. And the introduction of long-tunneled external ventricular drainage(LTEVD) with distal ends extending from low anterior chest wall was recommended. In a study, the authors described the advantages of LTEVD, in which externalized valves allow the cerebrospinal fluid to be visualized and more controllable, making it easier for physicians to manage the cerebrospinal fluid [6]. Moreover, the length of the tube is tunneled may have an impact on the infection rate [7]. In line with their thinking, we improved the LTEVD to be laparoscopic temporary long-tunneled external ventricular drainage(LT-LTEVD), and achieved a good therapeutic effect. In this study, we presented a comparison between LT-LTEVD and CL-VPS in the treatment of PTH.

Materials and methods

From October 2018 to October 2021, a prospective, randomized, blinded study was conducted at the Naval First Hospital of the Southern Theater Command. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Naval First Hospital of the Southern Theater Command (ethical number: 2017-126-05), and every patient signed an informed consent form.

Study population

All 52 adult patients admitted to the Naval First Hospital of the Southern Theater Command were eligible for inclusion.

The patients were randomly divided into two groups undergoing CL-VPS and LT-LTEVD. The main inclusion criteria were (1) All Patients meet the diagnostic criteria of PTH; (2) Age ≥ 18 years old; (3) Time from onset to randomization within 24 h; (4) Informed consent for surgery was obtained. The main exclusion criteria were (1) Patients with severe dysfunction of vital organs such as heart, lung, liver, and kidney; (2) Unstable vital signs such as temperature, pulse, respiration, or blood pressure; (3) Active internal bleeding and hemorrhagic diseases, uncontrolled coagulopathy, and pregnancy; (4) Combined with other abdominal disease.

Surgical procedure

Every effort was made to standardize the procedure. All procedures were conducted at the same hospital by the same group of experienced neurosurgeons and general surgeons, and the protocols for the management of PTH and VPS surgery remained the same. Laparoscopy-assisted VPS placement was performed in all patients [8]. In CL-VPS group, the distal terminal of the drainage tube was placed in the right suprahepatic space. And the operation was done when the cerebrospinal fluid outflowed (Fig. 1A). In LT-LTEVD group, another self-made external drainage catheter was used. This drainage catheter started from the right suprahepatic space, and passed through the lateral abdominal wall from the right anterior axillary line under the right costal margin. At the pipeline connection point, the distal terminal of the drainage tube was inserted into the head of the drainage catheter for about 15 cm. Then the balloon at the head of the drainage catheter was inflated, so the distal terminal of the drainage tube was fixed (Fig. 1B). The distal terminal of the drainage catheter was attached to a sterile drainage bag, and the operation was done when the cerebrospinal fluid outflowed. After operation, all patients were managed within a standardized protocol and monitored closely.

Data collection

All data were collected from the surgery database, clinical observations and records. A series of demographic and other significant data were recorded, including age, gender, Glasgow coma score(GCS), preoperative Evans index(EI), catheterization time, remission time of intracranial hypertension, hospital stay, complications. Each patient was examined for nervous system function with the GCS. And outcome assessment was done by conventional standards [9]: An excellent outcome was achieved, if the CT results were all within the normal range and the patient’s motor function was totally recovered. A satisfactory outcome was achieved, if significant improvements were observed in clinical symptoms and the patient’s motor function, as well as in the CT results. An unsatisfactory outcome was achieved, if the conditions were not improved at all or became worse. The total effective rate of each group was calculated using the formula: Total effective rate = (cases of excellent outcome + cases of satisfactory outcome)/total number of cases ×100%. Additionally, all patients were followed up for 6 months after surgery, and abnormal shunt, obstruction, intracranial infection were accurately documented by professional nurses.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were compiled in IBM SPSS version 17.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, New York, USA) for statistical analysis. As required, the patients’ characteristics and outcome parameters were summarized using means and standard deviations or frequencies and percentages. Parametric data were compared using the independent-samples T test. Percentages were compared using the Fisher’s exact test based on results. Finally, the significance level was set to 0.05.

Discussion

Modern life is of diversity and pluralism, trauma gain the characteristics of multiple injuries and heavy injuries, the incidence of TBI is also high [10]. Hydrocephalus is a common and serious complication of TBI. Patients with PTH are generally combined with other injuries, and the condition is complicated [11]. If timely medical treatment for the hydrocephalus patients cannot be obtained, for the most part, they may have a poor prognosis. It has become the main cause of TBI patients disability and death, therefore, how to deal with PTH in a time-effect treatment has been a challenge for every clinician [12]. Different surgical methods have different prognosis. When selecting surgical methods, it is not only to solve clinical problems as soon as possible and to ensure clinical efficacy, but also to ensure the long-term prognosis of patients.

VPS, the main methods for treating PTH, has a definite therapeutic effect and remained a safe surgical alternative for PTH [13]. However, drainage failure is often occurred by various complications in PTH patients, such as abnormal shunt (including overdrainage, underdrainage, etc.), shunt malfunction(including shunt obstruction, migration, rupture, kinking, etc.),shunt infection(including intracranial infection, abdominal infection, etc.), etc [14–16]. In this study, the main complications were shunt abnormalities, shunt obstruction and intracranial infection. In the CL-VPS group, one case of shunt insufficiency was occurred due to the patient who was with pelvic and rectal injuries and underwent pelvic surgery and ileostomy. And in this patient, high abdominal pressure was found after surgery, because the bladder pressure was higher than 12mmHg by repeated measurements, which meet the diagnostic criteria for intra-abdominal hypertension [17]. When the abdominal pressure is instable and varies, the VPS will be affected directly, which is considered to be a contraindication for the trauma patients who is planned to have the VPS. In the CL-VPS group, 5 cases of shunt obstruction occurred and 3 cases were found in the abdomen, all of which were caused by adhesion and encapsulation of shunt by the greater omentum. When the shunt is placed into the abdomen as a foreign body, it can trigger the inflammatory defense response of the body. As the omentum has immune defense function, it can slowly move in the abdomen and result in blockage of the shunt [18]. In the CL-VPS group, 2 cases of shunt obstruction were found in the ventriculus cerebri, and the same result also occurred in the LT-LTEVD group. Upon closer inspection, the shunt were blocked by blood clots, choroid plexus, cerebrospinal fluid protein deposition, etc., which were solved by additional surgery.

Intracranial infection is the worst complication of PTH, which seriously affects the recovery of trauma patients [19, 20]. In this study, there were 2 cases of intracranial infection in CL-VPS group, and the patients also had peritonitis at the same time.And the intracranial infection was considered to be related to the retrograde abdominal infection, and the infection was eliminated after shunt extraction and antibiotic treatment. By now, it is believed that intracranial infection after VPS is mostly related to the operation. When inserting an device, it becomes slightly more susceptible to infection [21]. Because the insertion of the shunt in the ventricle artificially produces a channel between the originally closed cranial cavity and the external environment, bacteria can enter the brain through the shunt and cause secondary infection. To reduce the risk of intracranial infection after VPS, the routine methods include strictly implement aseptic operation, improvement of the operation methods and catheterization, and all of which mainly aimed at operators and have strong subjectivity [1]. But in our study, no case of intracranial infection in the LT-LTEVD group was found. Without considering the operator’s factors at all, this result may account for the long tunnel distance of the shunt in the body, and the subcutaneous tissue and muscles may have some kind of barrier effect to block the microorganisms to enter the brain, which reduced the incidence of intracranial infection. The same results were also found in another cohort study [22], in which 134 children were divided into short-tunneled external ventricular drainage(STEVD)group and LTEVD group, and it was found that the incidence of secondary intracranial infection was lower in the LTEVD group, suggesting that the application of long-tunneled ventricular drainage is more effective in reducing infection rates and other complications in pediatric patients.

In order to reduce complications and improving treatment efficacy, several improvements were made to the conventional LTEVD based on the research results and clinical practice of VPS [23, 24]. After the conventional VPS placement surgery, the self-made external drainage catheter was inserted into the abdominal cavity under the costal margin of the right anterior axillary line, and the VPS catheter was inserted into the self-made external drainage catheter by laparoscopy, so that the cerebrospinal fluid could be well-drained through the brain-thoracic-ventral-lateral abdominal wall catheter [25]. The placement of the the self-made external drainage catheter directly resulted in the time elongation in LT-LTEVD group, and the catheterization time significantly longer than that of the CL-VPS group.But other indicators, such as the total effective rate, length of hospitalization, and incidence of complications, demonstrated superior to that of the CL-VPS group. These results indicated that LT-LTEVD is safer, more stable and convenient than the CL-VPS group. It can not only improve the patients condition, but also minimize the complications, and gain more opportunities to save the lives of patients with PTH.

In addition to reducing the incidence of intracranial infection, LT-LTEVD in this study has several advantages: Firstly, as described before [6], LTEVD allows visual control of drainage flow and abnormal shunt problems could also be found in time. Secondly, controllable external drainage: When excessive or insufficient drainage is observed, the drainage can be adjusted by the external switch in time. Thirdly, to avoid the influence of pathological state of abdomen on drainage and keep drainage not to be obstructed. The basis of conventional VPS is that the abdomen is sterile and the abdominal cavity retains its original intact structure. However, for trauma patients, besides brain injury, it is more common to have combined abdominal and pelvic injuries. In addition to brain surgery, these patients may also suffer from abdominal hypertension, and may even face unplanned second or multiple surgeries for the abdomen and pelvis [26]. For the follow-up treatment of these patients, the application of conventional VPS may bring inconvenience. Compared with the conventional VPS, the cerebrospinal fluid in this study does not stay in the abdominal cavity, avoiding the direct influence of abdominal pressure on the drainage pressure. Moreover, if the abdominal infection happens, it also avoids the risk of retrograde infection of the ventricles. Similarly, if inflammatory cerebrospinal fluid is present, the possibility of metastatic peritonitis is avoided. Lastly, for the patients who underwent LT-LTEVD, when their general condition improve, no abdominal abnormalities are found, and the postoperative cerebrospinal fluid flow is monitored to be smooth and stable, the external drainage catheter can be subsequently removed. After deflating the catheter balloon, fixation of the drainage tube and the drainage catheter is loosened. And by removing the distal terminal of the drainage catheter, the drainage catheter would be taken out from the abdominal cavity.The distal terminal of the drainage tube is still left in the abdominal cavity, and the cerebrospinal fluid can flow into the abdominal cavity(Fig. 1C).The temporary long-tunneled external ventricular drainage convert to conventional VPS, making it cost-effective and feasible across the clinical setting.

Although we showed the efficacy of LT-LTEVD for PTH in this study, there were some limitations. First, this study was a single-center clinical trial and the sample size was small, and the results could not be well evaluated. Second, the follow-up period was relatively short, and we did not perform long-term follow-up, so we could not further assess recovery. Therefore, these results need to be verified by further multi-center and large-sample randomized controlled clinical study.

Conclusion

VPS surgery is an effective way to treat patients with PTH, but different surgical modalities produce lead to different expected outcomes. Compared to CL-VPS group, LT-LTEVD group showed a better results in total effective rate of treatment, remission time of intracranial hypertension, hospital stay and complications rate in this study. Notwithstanding the limitations previously discussed, our findings demonstrated that the use of LT-LTEVD in patients with PTH could be potentially a safe and beneficial approach.

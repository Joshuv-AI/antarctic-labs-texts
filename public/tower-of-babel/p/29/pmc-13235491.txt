Telomere length and clonal hematopoiesis interact to influence outcomes in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation∗
Authors: Jake Tobin, Patrick Stelmach, Sarah Richter, Diane Xu, Sandra Sauer, Marc S. Raab, Muxin Gu, William G. Dunn, Margarete A. Fabre, Carsten Müller-Tidow, George S. Vassiliou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•Longer telomeres are associated with a PFS benefit in patients without CH.•Longer telomeres are associated with an increase in red cell distribution width among patients with CH.

Introduction

Clonal hematopoiesis (CH), the clonal expansion of a hematopoietic stem cell and its progeny driven by somatic driver mutations, becomes increasingly prevalent with advancing age to affect >50% of those aged ≥70 years.1, 2, 3 Most cases of CH are driven by mutations in genes involved in DNA methylation (DNMT3A and TET2), chromatin modification (ASXL1), splicing (SF3B1, SRSF2, and U2AF1), cytokine signaling (JAK2), or the DNA damage response (TP53 and PPM1D).2 CH carriers have an increased risk of developing myeloid malignancy, nonhematological disorders including cardiovascular and renal disease, and certain solid cancers.4, 5, 6 The presence of CH was found to influence clinical outcomes after autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT), with CH carriers exhibiting higher rates of treatment-related myeloid neoplasia and inferior overall survival (OS) relative to non-CH controls.7,8

Studies evaluating the impact of leukocyte telomere length (LTL) on SCT outcomes report mixed conclusions; although there is consensus that SCT leads to accelerated telomere shortening, different studies report conflicting results on the impact of graft cell LTL and hematopoietic reconstitution after transplantation.9, 10, 11 Longer LTL has been associated with enhanced granulocyte recovery in some studies, whereas others report no impact of donor LTL on trilineage hematopoietic reconstitution.10, 11, 12 This may reflect variation in the clinical indications for SCT; differences in LTL between ASCT and allogeneic SCT; and variation in the many other variables affecting hematopoietic recovery after SCT.

Recent studies have revealed that most forms of CH arise preferentially in people inheriting longer LTL, whereas CH itself causes LTL attrition.5,13 However, we recently found that the association between CH and long telomeres is not uniform, and that CH driven by mutations in PPM1D and splicing factor genes SF3B1, SRSF2, and U2AF1 arises more frequently in individuals inheriting shorter telomeres.14 Prompted by these observations, we sought to examine the importance of graft LTL on survivorship and blood count indices in the peritransplant period, and establish whether outcomes of ASCT for myeloma were influenced by LTL and CH status of the graft cells.

Methods

Selection of patients and stem cell grafts

Mobilized stem cell products (peripheral blood with CD34+ cells) from 452 patients with myeloma were collected by leukapheresis at the University Hospital Heidelberg between 2004 and 2016. Clinical data were analyzed in May 2022. Stem cell mobilization was performed with granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (filgrastim) combined with either cyclophosphamide monotherapy (2 g/m2) or a combination of cyclophosphamide (2 g/m2), doxorubicin (60 mg/m2), and dexamethasone (80 mg). All patients provided written informed consent to the use of their biomaterial and clinical data. The project was approved by the ethics committee of the University of Heidelberg (reference number S-850/2021).

Annotation of myeloma disease status and cytogenetic risk

Response to therapy was assessed retrospectively from medical records and response categories were documented according to European Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation and International Myeloma Working Group uniform response criteria.15,16 Cytogenetic high-risk disease was defined by the detection of 1 of the following genetic aberrations by fluorescence in situ hybridization: t(4;14), t(14;16), or del(17p), or chromosome 1q21 gain/amplification.

Isolation of genomic bulk DNA and targeted bulk DNA sequencing

Genomic DNA was isolated from patient stem cell grafts before undergoing targeted DNA sequencing to detect CH driver mutations. Stem cell grafts underwent quantification of CD34+ cell yield but not immunophenotypic sorting, ensuring that cellular material taken forward into these analyses comprised an admixture of both CD34+ and CD34− mononuclear cells, as well as granulocytes. DNA extraction techniques were as previously described by Stelmach et al.17

Mutation analysis and CH variant calling

We analyzed blood DNA from the mobilized stem cell products from 452 patients with myeloma. Targeted enrichment was performed for 56 genes implicated in CH and myeloid malignancies (Agilent SureSelect ELID 3156971; supplemental Table 1). Libraries were sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq to a median depth of 1062 reads (5th-95th percentile interval, 492-1401 reads). Variant calling was performed using Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) Mutect2 version 4.1.8.1 using the “tumor-only” mode.18 Variants with <2 reads on either the forward or reverse strands of DNA, or with a variant allele frequency (VAF) of <0.05 were discarded. To recover falsely discarded calls, we first calculated the recurrences of each variant in the cohort with VAF of >0.005 (N0.005), VAF of >0.01 (N0.01), and the recurrence in the COSMIC (Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer) database (Ncosmic).19 Discarded substitution variants were rescued if they satisfied either of these criteria: (1) N0.005 ≥100 and N0.01 <5; (2) N0.005 <100 and Ncosmic ≥30; (3) Ncosmic ≥ 10. Insertion/deletion variants with N0.01 >2 were discarded except for NPM1 p.W288fs and ASXL1 p.G646fs. Driver mutations were defined according to evidence for functional relevance in CH and hematological malignancy (supplemental Table 2).

Data warehouse

The data warehouse (Department of Medicine V, Heidelberg University Hospital) centralizes department-specific data from the hospital information system and peripheral systems in an automated way. To date, it contains clinical data of ∼120 000 patients dating back to 2004. In addition to basic data, it also includes diagnostic results and treatment-related data. Because every patient contact is stored together with the collected information, detailed and dense longitudinal follow-up data are included.

Telomere length measurement

Relative LTLs (rLTLs) were measured for each patient sample using a quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) protocol modified from Cawthon.20 DNA samples were quantified using the Qubit double-stranded DNA HS kit (Invitrogen Q32851). Each PCR reaction contained 0.5 ng genomic DNA and 5 μL QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Mastermix (Qiagen 204143). Telomere reactions contained 270nM tel1 and 900nM tel2 primers (supplemental Table 3) and were run on an Applied Biosystems QuantStudio 5 real-time PCR system using the following conditions: 95°C for 15 minutes; and 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 54°C for 2 minutes. 36B4 reactions contained 300nM 36B4u and 500nM 36B4d primers and were run using the following conditions: 95°C for 15 minutes, and 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 58°C for 1 minute.

All samples were analyzed in triplicate. For inclusion in analyses, samples were required to have a mean Ct value of >1.0 outside the mean for the negative control wells (DNA replaced with H2O) for both primer pairs. In addition, samples were excluded if the standard deviation of Ct values for either primer pair was >1.0. To account for variation in qPCR analyzer performance between runs, serial dilutions (0.01-2.5 ng) derived from the same aliquot of K562 cell line DNA were included on each PCR plate for a standard curve. rLTLs for each sample were inferred from the ratio of mean Ct values for telomere (T) and 36B4 (S) primer pairs using the formula: T:S ratio = 2(Ct 36B4 − Ct telomere). This T:S ratio was, in turn, normalized to the T:S ratio for 0.5 ng of K562 DNA on the same PCR plate.

To ensure that our conclusions were not affected by the disproportionate impact of a small number of more extreme rLTL values, we repeated analyses excluding a small number (n = 16) of samples for which telomere qPCR had generated Ct values passing quality control criteria, but which lay outside of 1.5× the interquartile range of the cohort.

Statistical analyses

Univariate and multivariable analyses of time-to-event end points were performed using Cox regression. OS was defined as time from ASCT. Participants not confirmed dead were censored at the time last known to be alive. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as time from ASCT until the earliest time of progression or death from any cause and censored at time last known to be alive and free of progression. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and Wald P values were reported for model covariates, and likelihood-ratio tests and P values were reported for multivariable models. Median event times were estimated using the method of Kaplan and Meier and reported with 95% CIs. The Greenwood formula was used to approximate the variance of Kaplan-Meier estimates. Differences in survival curves were assessed using log-rank tests.

To assess the trajectories with multiple measurements per patient, mixed-effects models were used to investigate the pattern in red cell distribution width (RDW) after ASCT. To satisfy the model assumptions, the outcome variable, RDW, was transformed with the natural logarithm. As an independent variable, time after ASCT was included and modeled with a natural cubic spline, to allow a nonlinear effect and to capture the trend in the data with more precision. We modeled outcome variables up to day 20 after ASCT. For models to day 20, 1 spline knot was set on day 9. Residual plots were used to validate the models’ assumptions.

Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (L1 penalty) with λ tuned by crossvalidation was performed using the “glmnet” R package to model log-transformed rLTL. LASSO regression identified SF3B1 and JAK2 mutations as the strongest negative predictors of rLTL.

Continuous and categorical data are reported as mean (standard deviation) and count (percent), respectively. The Fisher exact test was used to test for associations between categorical variables. The Mann-Whitney U or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were used to assess a location shift in the distribution of continuous variables between 2, or >2 groups, respectively.

All P values were 2-sided, and significance levels set at P value <.05. Calculations were done using R version 4.4.2 (Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

UK Biobank analyses

The UK Biobank is a large-scale biomedical database and research resource containing genetic, lifestyle, and health information from half a million UK participants. The UK Biobank has approval from the North West multicenter research ethics committee (11/NW/0382) and all participants provided written informed consent. This study has been conducted under approved UK Biobank application number 56844.

Prevalence of CH driver mutations

Of 422 patients for whom valid rLTL measurements could be obtained, 141 (33.4%) harbored ≥1 CH driver mutations (Figure 1A). The full spectrum of called CH mutations is provided in supplemental Table 4, as well as summaries of mutational burden by driver gene (supplemental Figure 1A) and an overview of single-mutant and multimutant cases (supplemental Figure 1B). Within the subsets of patients for whom information on ASCT mobilization chemotherapy (n = 273 [64.7%]) or previous induction chemotherapy (n = 418 [99.1%]) was available, there were no differences in the incidence of CH according to mobilization or induction regimen (P = .0723 and P = .158 respectively, Fisher exact test; supplemental Figure 1C-D).

LTL and CH mutational profile

LTLs (rLTLs) were shorter in patients with CH driver mutations than those without (median, 0.99 vs 1.74 arbitrary units, respectively; P < .001, Wilcoxon rank-sum test; Figure 1B). This finding withstood linear regression–based adjustment for age, sex, myeloma cytogenetic risk status, and time until ASCT (supplemental Figure 1E). In keeping with recent evidence suggesting that CH driven by splicing factor gene mutations arises preferentially in patients with shorter LTL, rLTLs were shorter in patients with SF3B1 mutations (n = 6) than in the remaining 135 patients with CH driven by other mutations (0.47 vs 1.06; P = .014, Wilcoxon rank-sum test).14,21 Because splicing factor mutations occurred alongside other CH driver mutations in all but 1 patient, we performed LASSO regression analysis to disentangle the contribution of specific CH mutations on TL, which confirmed that SF3B1 mutations were independently associated with short telomeres (regression coefficient, −0.84; Figure 1C). Because previous studies highlighted PPM1D mutations as being of particular prognostic significance in the context of ASCT, rLTLs were compared between CH carriers with and without PPM1D mutations, but no statistically significant difference was identified.8

LTL and survival after ASCT

Next, we investigated whether TL was of prognostic relevance in the context of ASCT. To account for the non-Gaussian distribution of rLTLs and moderate the influence of a small number of patients with particularly long or short TLs, we stratified patients according to whether their rLTL was above or below the cohort median. Considering the cohort as a whole, there was no difference in PFS or OS between patients with rLTLs above vs below the cohort median. When patients were stratified according to the presence or absence of a CH driver mutation, we found that longer TLs were associated with improved PFS (P = .017, log-rank test) and OS (P = .036, log-rank test) for patients without a CH driver mutation (Figure 1D-E).

Because the relationship between LTL and survival after ASCT might be confounded by variables such as age and sex, we confirmed that there were no significant differences in age, sex, or myeloma cytogenetic risk stratification between the above-median and below-median LTL cohorts (Table 1). A Cox proportional hazards model revealed that after adjusting for age, sex, and disease status before transplantation, above-median rLTL in patients without CH was associated with a 26% improvement in PFS (HR, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.56-0.98; P = .035), whereas the corresponding effect on OS did not reach statistical significance (HR, 0.71; 95% CI, 0.49-1.02; P = .064; Figure 1F-G). In contrast, among patients with CH, above-median rLTL was not significantly associated with PFS (HR, 1.21; 95% CI, 0.82-1.80; P = .335; supplemental Figure 2A) or OS (supplemental Figure 2B). A significant interaction was observed between rLTL and CH status for both end points (PFS [HR for interaction, 1.64; 95% CI, 1.01-2.66; P = .045] and OS [HR for interaction, 1.93; 95% CI, 1.03-3.63; P = .041]), ensuring that the survival benefits of longer rLTL were not seen among patients with a CH mutation (supplemental Figure 2A-E).

Associations between CH status, rLTL, and survivorship were validated after exclusion of outlier rLTL values as defined in “Methods.” Of note, patients from our cohort who underwent ASCT at later time periods (ie, after 2009 compared with before 2009) showed shortened PFS but noninferior OS (supplemental Figure 3A-B). In an attempt to reflect the heterogeneity of clone sizes found within our CH cohort, VAF of the patient’s largest CH driver mutation was integrated as an additional variable into our regression analyses, and was not found to influence survival (supplemental Figure 3C-D).

LTL, CH mutations, and complete blood count parameters

Next, we sought to examine the impact of LTL and CH mutational status on hematopoiesis around the time of ASCT, by analyzing their relationship with different hematological parameters.

We assessed the trajectories of RDW between days 0 and 20 after ASCT with respect to rLTL (Figure 2A-B) and developed a time-dependent linear mixed-effects model. This model confirmed an association between increased RDW and above-median rLTL that was specific to CH carriers (P = .003; Figure 2C-D), and withstood regression-based adjustment for age, sex, serum creatinine, and red blood cell transfusional burden (Figure 2E). Patients with CH and above-median rLTL also showed increased RDW before conditioning chemotherapy (16.1% vs 15.2%; P = .0022, Wilcoxon rank-sum test; Figure 2F). Discussion

In this study, we investigated the impact of stem cell graft LTL on different clinical and laboratory parameters at the time of ASCT for myeloma. We report previously undocumented associations between survivorship and LTL, which vary according to the presence of CH driver mutations. We also show a consistent, small-magnitude correlation between longer LTL and increased RDW in patients with CH.

Attempts to understand the relevance of TLs to clinical outcomes are inevitably complicated by demographic variables understood to influence both TL and all-cause morbidity and mortality. To account for this, we have ensured that only associations between LTL and clinical or laboratory variables that withstand regression-based adjustment for age and sex are reported in this study. However, we did not have access to some potentially relevant data, such as smoking status and medical comorbidities, which could affect both rLTL and survivorship.22 Moreover, factors such as patient age, fitness, and choice of chemotherapy regimen may affect both rLTL and post-ASCT survival, with biological and clinical interactions that are difficult to fully account for with standard regression analyses.23 A more detailed interrogation of potential mechanisms explaining the apparent survival benefit of longer graft LTL is hampered by lack of comprehensive cause of death data for our cohort.

The fact that PFS, more than OS, was influenced by LTL in patients without CH may suggest that this protective effect of long telomeres is related to transplant efficacy and disease course, rather than broader determinants of survivorship. Although our study was only powered to analyze outcomes after ASCT, previous studies have reported a positive association between germ line polymorphisms associated with longer LTL and improved OS in myeloma.24 As such, it is possible that TL has broader relevance to myeloma disease trajectories outside of the context of ASCT, this is especially important given the evolving landscape of myeloma treatment, and the increasing role for immunomodulatory and cellular therapies. Our data show that PFS appeared worse among patients recruited later within the study, whereas the trend for OS was reversed but nonsignificant. Because the age profile of patients did not differ between calendar periods, this raises the possibility that improvements in surveillance and detection of myeloma relapse could have affected documentation of progression, and hence PFS in this cohort. Accordingly, it is noteworthy that the PFS benefit seen with longer LTL among patients with CH withstood formal adjustment for transplantation date.

The mechanisms underpinning apparent interactions between LTL and CH driver mutations in determining post-ASCT clinical outcomes are not immediately obvious. Considering PFS, an above-median graft LTL was independently associated with improved outcomes for patients without CH. This is potentially a reflection of improved stem cell reserve, with implications for hematopoietic reconstitution, and immunological surveillance of residual myeloma. As we, and others, have shown that telomere attrition naturally constrains clonal expansion, it is conceivable that this explains the loss of benefit associated with increased rLTL among the subset of patients with CH, in which longer LTL could facilitate the expansion of a dysfunctional hematopoietic clone.13,14 In addition, it is possible that the diversity of CH driver mutations and their associations with other variables relevant to survivorship (including age and previous cytotoxic therapy) complicates analyses and confounds any impact of LTL in this subgroup.

Considering data for laboratory parameters, an association was noted between above-median LTL and increased RDW for patients with CH. This difference was apparent when tracing RDW values for up to 20 days after ASCT, and withstood adjustment for renal function and the number of transfused red cell units. Our analyses of UK Biobank data did not reveal meaningful associations between LTL, CH, and RDW in this larger, unselected cohort, suggesting that any interactions noted in our study may be specific to this particular clinical context. The precise mechanisms underlying the association between longer telomeres, CH mutations, and increased RDW in this cohort are not apparent. Because we did not have exhaustive data available for all laboratory parameters capable of affecting RDW, the lack of an association between clone size (VAF), rLTL, and RDW seen in our data raises the possibility that the increased RDW among CH carriers with longer telomeres is mediated by non–cell autonomous processes, and subject to residual confounding.

PPM1D mutations were most strongly associated with increased RDW in our study, and were likely to have expanded in response to cytotoxic therapy not received by most of UK Biobank participants. This difference in context for the expansion of PPM1D mutations between our cohort, in which PPM1D was often multiply mutated within the same patient, may explain why these findings were not corroborated in this larger, undifferentiated cohort. It is also important to acknowledge the small number of patients with mutant PPM1D in our cohort before overinterpreting the significance and generalizability of this finding.

Although statistically significant, the absolute magnitude of differences in RDW between the longer and shorter LTL subgroups of patients with CH were still relatively small (<1%), suggesting that RDW is unlikely to be useful as a predictive tool for LTL and/or CH status. Consistent with numerous previous studies, no clear associations were seen between LTL and blood counts in the peritransplant period.

Overall, our data suggest an interaction between LTL and CH that may influence clinical and laboratory outcomes after ASCT. Although the effect sizes are small and unlikely to be clinically predictive when considered in isolation, these findings align with our current understanding of CH and underscore the need for larger, longitudinal studies to clarify their clinical relevance.

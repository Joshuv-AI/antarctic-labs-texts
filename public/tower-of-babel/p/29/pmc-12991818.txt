Early Dapagliflozin in Non-diabetic Acute Myocardial Infarction Patients Post-PCI: Effects on Cardiovascular Outcomes and Left Ventricular Remodeling (EARLY DAPA-AMI)
Authors: Hazem SE Salem, Ramzy M El-Mawardy, Mohamed E Zahran, Islam M Bastawy, Mina F Aziz, Abdelrahman E Attia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly initiation of dapagliflozin, a sodium-glucose co-transporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, may improve cardiovascular outcomes in acute myocardial infarction (AMI) by mitigating adverse remodeling and enhancing cardiac function.ObjectivesTo assess dapagliflozin's impact on major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and left ventricular (LV) recovery in non-diabetic AMI patients using speckle-tracking echocardiography (STE).MethodsThis prospective, single-blinded randomized controlled trial (RCT) enrolled 200 non-diabetic patients with a first episode of AMI, including both ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), following successful percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Participants received guideline-directed therapy and were randomized to dapagliflozin + standard care (Group I, n = 100) or standard care alone (Group II, n = 100). Echocardiographic parameters, including left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), global longitudinal strain (GLS), LV end-diastolic volume (EDV), and LV end-systolic volume (ESV), were measured at baseline (1–3 days after acute myocardial infarction [AMI]) and at 6-month follow-up. Primary endpoint: 6-month MACE; secondary endpoints: cardiac function changes.ResultsMACE rates showed no significant difference (Group I: 4 % vs. Group II: 9 %; p = 0.152). Group I demonstrated superior cardiac improvement: higher LVEF (52.24 % vs. 47.66 %; p = 0.025), greater ESV reduction (−7.41 ± 13.20 mL vs. −2.52 ± 9.17 mL; p = 0.003), and improved GLS (−14.50 ± 3.27 % vs. −13.48 ± 3.77 %; p = 0.043). GLS change was significantly greater in Group I (Δ−1.467 ± 3.023 % vs. Δ−0.475 ± 2.252%; p = 0.009). Hypertensive and chronic kidney disease (CKD) subgroups showed enhanced myocardial recovery with dapagliflozin.ConclusionEarly dapagliflozin in non-diabetic AMI patients did not reduce 6-month MACE but significantly improved LV function and remodeling, suggesting cardioprotective benefits beyond glycemic control, especially in high-risk subgroups.Keywords: Major adverse cardiovascular events, Acute myocardial infarction, Dapagliflozin, SGLT2 inhibitors, Global longitudinal strain, Diabetes mellitus

1. Introduction

A cute myocardial infarction (AMI) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, and despite advances in reperfusion therapy, heart failure (HF) develops in 25–40 % of survivors [1,2].

Left ventricular remodeling following myocardial injury is the key pathophysiological process driving progression to chronic heart failure, characterized by ventricular dilatation, myocardial fibrosis, neurohormonal activation, and subsequent contractile dysfunction [3,4].

Dapagliflozin, a highly selective SGLT2 inhibitor originally developed for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), blocks renal glucose reabsorption and lowers blood glucose independently of insulin. It has been approved in the USA and EU to reduce cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalizations across the full LVEF spectrum including HFmrEF (41–49 %) and HFpEF (≥50 %) based on consistent benefits observed in trials such as DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, DELIVER, and EMPEROR-Preserved [5,6]. Multiple cardiovascular outcome trials endorse SGLT2 inhibitors as first-line therapy for T2DM patients with established ASCVD, including chronic coronary syndrome, independent of glycemic control strategies [7].

Recent trials and meta-analyses show that early dapagliflozin initiation after AMI improves cardiovascular outcomes. The EMMY trial first demonstrated that early empagliflozin favorably affected cardiac biomarkers, structure, and function, paving the way for dapagliflozin studies. Dapagliflozin reduces adverse remodeling, enhances cardiac function, and improves short-term prognosis in AMI patients, especially those with HF [8–11].

There are limited studies evaluating the effects of early initiation of dapagliflozin in the AMI setting, particularly its impact on LV remodeling [12,13]. Dapagliflozin has shown promise in mitigating adverse cardiac remodeling through mechanisms such as reducing inflammation, oxidative stress, and myocardial fibrosis [14]. However, its role in the early phase of AMI recovery in non-diabetic patients remains underexplored.

2. Material and methods

2.1. Study design and aim

This study is a single-center, interventional, randomized controlled clinical trial conducted at Ain Shams University Hospitals. It was designed as a prospective, single-blinded trial where the echocardiographic expert was blinded to the treatment allocation (administration of dapagliflozin inhibitor vs. no administration). The study took place between February 2024 and January 2025. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of dapagliflozin on clinical and echocardiographic outcomes in non-diabetic patients with AMI undergoing PCI.

2.2. Study population

A total of 200 non-diabetic patients presented with first-time myocardial infarction either STEMI or NSTEMI without a prior history of LV dysfunction or HF were enrolled consecutively and randomized in a 1:1 ratio into two groups using a computer-generated randomization method. The first group, referred to as the DAPA Group or Group I (n = 100), received 10 mg of dapagliflozin once daily initiated within 48 hours MI diagnosis in addition to guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT). The second group, known as the DAPA-Free Group or Group II (n = 100), received only GDMT without the addition of dapagliflozin. At the 6-month follow-up, patients in the DAPA-Free group who developed HF were assigned to SGLT2 inhibitor treatment in accordance with the latest European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines [15].

All patients received GDMT for AMI, including dual antiplatelet therapy and percutaneous coronary intervention. Details of periprocedural management are provided in the Supplementary File (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha).

2.3. Inclusion criteria

Patients were eligible for enrollment in the study if they were adults (≥18 years of age) hospitalized for acute MI, including ST-segment or non-ST-segment had a first-time diagnosis of either STEMI or NSTEMI, as defined by the universal criteria for myocardial infarction [16]. Additionally, all included patients must have undergone successful reperfusion therapy via PCI, achieving TIMI III flow and a myocardial blush grade of III [17].

2.4. Exclusion criteria

Patients with chronic symptomatic heart failure (LVEF ≤40 %), pre-existing diabetes mellitus or prior dapagliflozin use, end-stage renal disease (CKD stage IV–V), severe hepatic dysfunction, advanced hematological disorders, active malignancy, requirement for mechanical circulatory support or ventilation, significant valvular heart disease, or prosthetic heart valves were excluded to preserve safety and ensure that observed remodeling effects were attributable to acute MI and the intervention. Detailed exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplementary Methods (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha).

2.5. Clinical and echocardiographic assessments

Each patient underwent a comprehensive evaluation that included medical history, clinical examination, interventional phase procedures, and echocardiographic assessments. Given the sensitivity of Simpson's EF and speckle-tracking GLS to geometric distortion and non-ischemic overload states, patients with severe VHD or prosthetic valves were excluded to preserve the interpretability of remodeling endpoints and ensure STE validity in the early post-AMI setting.

2.6. Medical evaluation

Baseline medical evaluation included standard clinical history, cardiovascular risk assessment, physical examination, ECG, and laboratory testing. Full definitions and procedures are provided in the Supplementary File (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha).

2.7. Procedure

All patients underwent PCI according to current guideline-directed protocols (details in Supplementary File (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha)). In the intervention arm, dapagliflozin (10 mg once daily) was initiated within 48 hours of MI diagnosis in addition to standard GDMT (see Supplementary File (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha)).

2.8. Echocardiographic assessment

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed within 72 hours after PCI and at follow-up using standardized protocols, including conventional and speckle-tracking techniques. LVEF was assessed by Simpson's method and LV global longitudinal strain (GLS) by automated function imaging. Peak systolic longitudinal strain measurement, segmental analysis, and example of impaired GLS are illustrated in Figs. 1–3, respectively. Full technical details of image acquisition and analysis are provided in the Supplementary File (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha).

2.9. Observer variability assessment

Two experienced echocardiographers, blinded to treatment allocation and timepoint, independently re-analyzed a random 15%sample for LVEF (biplane Simpson), LV volumes, and GLS; one reader repeated measurements ≥2 weeks later for intra-observer assessment. Observed LVEF and GLS changes exceed known test–retest variability, supporting a true remodeling effect (see Supplementary Discussion (https://www.j-saudi-heart.com/cgi/editor.cgi?window=additional_files&article=1464&context=jsha)).

2.10. Follow-up and endpoints

Clinical follow-up included chart review, phone calls, and patient questionnaires. Echocardiographic measures were repeated at six months. The primary endpoint was six-month MACE (cardiovascular death, HF hospitalization, unplanned revascularization, reinfarction, or stroke). The secondary endpoint was change in echocardiographic function (EF, GLS, EDV, ESV) from baseline to six months.

2.11. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v29.0. Continuous data are presented as mean ± SD and categorical data as N (%). Between-group comparisons used independent t-tests or χ2 tests, within-group changes used paired t-tests, and time-by-group interactions were assessed by repeated measures ANOVA. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered significant. Sample size was calculated to detect a 6 mL difference in LV end-systolic volume (SD 12 mL) or 1.2 % in GLS (SD 2.8 %) with 80 % power at α = 0.05; 200 patients were enrolled allowing for 5–8 % attrition. Detailed methods are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

2.12. Ethical approval

This study was conducted in full compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) and was approved by the Scientific and Ethical Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Ain Shams University. Informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

3. Results

Baseline characteristics, procedural details, and early outcomes were well balanced between groups, confirming successful randomization and comparable coronary care. Both cohorts demonstrated significant within-group improvements in LVEF, LV volumes, and GLS, with greater remodeling benefits observed in the dapagliflozin arm (Tables 1–3). 4. Discussion

The early initiation of dapagliflozin in AMI setting has emerged as a promising strategy to improve cardiovascular outcomes, as evidenced by recent clinical trials and meta-analyses. Dapagliflozin has demonstrated significant benefits in reducing adverse cardiac remodeling, improving cardiac function, and enhancing short-term prognosis in patients with AMI, particularly those with HF [8–10].

Global longitudinal strain (GLS) is effective in detecting subclinical myocardial dysfunction and offers superior prognostic value compared to traditional parameters like LVEF [18]. Incorporating STE-based GLS as an evaluation parameter allows for a detailed understanding of how pharmacological interventions like dapagliflozin influence early and late phases of LV remodeling post-AMI.

Unfortunately, in previous large-scale studies on SGLT2 inhibitors (EMPA-REG OUTCOME (Cardiovascular Outcome Event Trial in T2DM Patients), CANVAS (Canagliflozin CardioVascular Assessment Study), CREDENCE (Canagliflozin and Renal Outcomes in T2DM and Nephropathy), and others), HF stage B patients with AMI were completely excluded at the time of enrollment [19–21].

Few studies have investigated the effects of early initiation of dapagliflozin in non-diabetic patients experiencing AMI, particularly its impact on LV remodeling [12,13]. Dapagliflozin has shown promise in mitigating adverse cardiac remodeling through mechanisms such as reducing inflammation, oxidative stress, and myocardial fibrosis [14]. However, its role in the early phase of AMI recovery remains underexplored.

This prospective, single-blinded, randomized controlled trial at Ain Shams University Hospitals aimed to evaluate the impact of early dapagliflozin initiation in non-diabetic patients following a first-time AMI and successful PCI. While the study did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in MACE at 6-month follow-up, the key finding was the significant improvement in cardiac remodeling and function parameters in the dapagliflozin group, particularly evidenced by enhanced LVEF and GLS, along with reductions in ESV. The numerical differences observed in MACE and hospitalization rates, though not statistically significant, also merit further consideration in future research to explore underlying factors and potential clinical relevance.

Hypertensive and CKD subgroups receiving dapagliflozin experienced greater LV volume reductions and strain improvements, with CKD patients also showing superior LVEF recovery. These targeted benefits highlight the value of early dapagliflozin in optimizing post-AMI cardiac recovery in high-risk populations.

The significantly greater NT-proBNP reduction in the dapagliflozin group (−960 vs. −580 pg/mL, p = 0.04) parallels the observed improvements in EF and GLS, supporting genuine reverse remodeling rather than measurement error. This biomarker response aligns with recent meta-analyses demonstrating dapagliflozin's ability to reduce myocardial stress markers in post-MI patients. The marked CRP decline in both groups, with a trend favoring dapagliflozin, suggests anti-inflammatory effects consistent with SGLT2 inhibitors' established modulation of inflammatory pathways including NLRP3 inflammasome suppression. Importantly, stable creatinine levels confirm the renal safety profile of early dapagliflozin initiation in this post-AMI population, contrasting with historical concerns about acute kidney injury risk [12,22–27].

Overall, our findings underscore the potential therapeutic benefits of dapagliflozin in improving cardiac function across various patient groups, particularly in hypertensive, and CKD patients. The results emphasize the importance of personalized treatment strategies to optimize cardiac outcomes and mitigate cardiovascular risks associated with these conditions.

Recent studies provide evidence for dapagliflozin's cardioprotective pathways. Armillotta et al. demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors optimize myocardial energy metabolism, preserve cardiac contractility, and attenuate ischemia/reperfusion injury, correlating with our observed LVEF and GLS improvements [28]. Scisciola et al. showed that myocardial SGLT2 expression correlates with fibrosis markers (TGF-β, collagen), inflammation (NF-κB, IL-6), and oxidative stress (SOD2), suggesting SGLT2 inhibition directly counteracts adverse remodeling processes underlying our ESV and GLS findings [29]. Paolisso et al. found that SGLT2 inhibitors improved LVEF, reduced LVEDV, and promoted LV recovery in severe aortic stenosis patients, with 92.7 % showing stable or improved cardiac function versus control progression, paralleling our superior outcomes in hypertensive and CKD subgroups [30].

The echocardiographic benefits observed in our study align with findings from several other investigations. A meta-analysis by S. Qazi et al. (2024) also reported that combining SGLT-2 inhibitors with PCI in AMI patients enhances LVEF and reduces left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV) [31]. Similarly, a 2024 study by Zhou et al. demonstrated improved cardiac function with dapagliflozin in early-onset post-AMI HF patients, regardless of diabetes status [8]. Furthermore, the EMMY trial (2022) showed that empagliflozin initiated early after large AMI improved LV systolic/diastolic function and reduced LV volumes [11]. Our findings also resonate with a systematic review and meta-analysis by A. Shrestha et al. (2024), which noted a non-significant trend towards reduced all-cause mortality with dapagliflozin in AMI patients, suggesting a potential for broader benefits that warrant further exploration [10].

The DACAMI trial (2023) also supported the role of dapagliflozin in preventing LV dysfunction in non-diabetic patients with reduced LVEF post-anterior STEMI [32]. Another study by F. Loria et al. (2024) demonstrated that dapagliflozin improved LVEF and reduced LVEDV in HFrEF patients, particularly those with favorable hemodynamic profiles, suggesting a mitigation of volume overload [9]. The DAPA-MI trial showed that dapagliflozin improved cardiometabolic outcomes but did not significantly impact cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization in patients without diabetes or chronic HF [33]. The EMPACT-MI trial found that empagliflozin reduced first and total HF hospitalizations in high-risk MI patients, regardless of diabetes status, without affecting overall mortality [34]. These trials highlight distinct benefits of different SGLT2 inhibitors.

Interestingly, our study's neutral findings regarding MACE contrast with some other research. The meta-analysis by Qazi et al. (2024) reported a significant reduction in all-cause mortality, which was not observed in our cohort [31]. Similarly, a 2022 retrospective study by Zhu et al. linked dapagliflozin to a lower MACE risk in AMI patients undergoing PCI [35], and the SGLT2-I AMI PROTECT Registry (2023) demonstrated reduced MACE in diabetic AMI patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors [36]. The 2024 study by Zhou et al. also reported a reduction in MACE risk with dapagliflozin [8]. Another retrospective analysis of the Changzhou Acute Myocardial Infarction Registry (n = 1839 AMI patients, 278 DAPA users) found dapagliflozin use was associated with lower all-cause mortality compared to controls [24]. These discrepancies in MACE outcomes could be attributed to several factors, including differences in patient selection criteria (e.g., the inclusion of patients with pre-existing HF or diabetes in other studies), the duration of follow-up, and potentially the sample size, as our study may have been underpowered to detect statistically significant differences in less frequent events like MACE.

The safety profile of dapagliflozin in the AMI setting has also been well-established, with no significant differences in adverse events compared to placebo, including acute kidney injury, hepatic injury, symptomatic hypotension, hypoglycemia, urinary tract infections, and diabetic ketoacidosis [10,37]. Dapagliflozin's strong safety profile and efficacy in reducing MACE and improving cardiac function make it a promising early therapy for AMI, especially in high-risk patients. Real-world evidence confirms its rapid benefits in HFrEF, with marked improvements in SVi, LAVi, and sPAP, and enhanced outcomes in congestive hemodynamic profiles, supporting its use in patients with poor prognosis [9].

Our trial adds valuable evidence supporting the use of dapagliflozin in high-risk cardiovascular populations. Its concordance with major trials such as DAPA-MI, DAPA-HF, and EMPA-REG OUTCOME underscores the robustness of these agents' cardioprotective mechanisms. However, further research is needed to confirm the long-term clinical outcomes and to explore the mechanisms underlying its benefits, the current evidence strongly supports the use of dapagliflozin as part of a comprehensive treatment strategy for AMI, especially in high-risk patients.

5. Limitations

The trial's low event rate and six-month follow-up limited power to detect MACE differences, so neutral results require confirmation in larger, longer studies. Excluding severe valvular disease and prosthetic valves preserved endpoint validity but may reduce generalizability to those populations.

6. Conclusion

In non-diabetic AMI patients post-PCI, early dapagliflozin did not reduce six-month MACE but significantly improved LV systolic function and remodeling, especially in hypertensive and CKD subgroups. These results support its use to enhance myocardial recovery and warrant larger, multicenter trials with longer follow-up to confirm clinical benefits and refine patient selection.

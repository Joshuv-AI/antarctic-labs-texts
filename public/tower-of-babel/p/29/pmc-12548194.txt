Lessons and concerns about the SARS-CoV-2/DENV co-infection
Authors: Camila Moreira Andrade, Ana Cláudia Arantes Marquez Pajuaba, Cristina Rostkowska, Anna Laura de Jesus Gomes, Mariani Borges Franco, Stefan Vilges de Oliveira, Tiago Wilson Patriarca Mineo, José Roberto Mineo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Brazil has experienced frequent epidemic cycles of dengue virus infection over the past five decades. In addition, the country ranked among the most affected globally in terms of the number of cases and deaths caused by SARS-CoV-2 infection during 2020 and 2021, when the world faced the COVID-19 pandemic. Currently, a new dengue outbreak is underway, accompanied by a rising number of SARS-CoV-2 infections, which may lead to a severe collapse of the public health system. The primary aim of this retrospective cohort study was to investigate the occurrence of SARS-CoV-2 seropositivity in a panel of serum samples collected from patients presenting with clinical signs and symptoms of dengue virus infection in the Triângulo Mineiro Norte region, Minas Gerais State, Brazil. The period from January 2019 to April 2021 was selected to capture the dynamics across both the pre-pandemic and pandemic phases, prior to the implementation of the COVID-19 vaccination campaign. A total of 2,969 serum samples from patients residing in the region were analyzed using a Dengue IgM Capture ELISA and a COVID-19 IgG/IgM rapid test device. Although the primary diagnostic hypothesis for all samples was dengue infection, as the pandemic progressed, the number of SARS-CoV-2-positive samples surpassed that of dengue-positive samples. In conclusion, although both viral infections occurred independently, without an apparent causal relationship, our findings suggest that the true number of SARS-CoV-2 infections was significantly underestimated during the study period, thereby increasing the risk of severe COVID-19 outcomes, particularly in a population already experiencing co-infection with dengue virus.

Introduction

Brazil is one of the countries most affected by the COVID-19 pandemic worldwide; however, it has also faced recurring dengue epidemics since 1986, particularly during rainy periods. Increased rainfall, combined with insufficient vector control measures - especially during the pandemic - has contributed to a further rise in dengue cases [1, 2]. In this context, the co-occurrence of COVID-19 and dengue epidemics poses major challenges for the health systems of emerging countries, such as Brazil and other nations in Latin America, Asia, and Africa [3–8].

Notably, dengue notifications in Brazil decreased during 2020, coinciding with the period of intensified COVID-19 control measures, suggesting possible underreporting during a time when an increase in dengue cases would typically be expected [7]. Similar trends have been observed in several Asian countries [9, 10].

Both COVID-19 and dengue share overlapping clinical signs and symptoms, which can complicate accurate diagnosis. Studies have shown that patients infected with SARS-CoV-2 but misdiagnosed with dengue may experience progression to more severe disease, delaying the implementation of appropriate and effective treatment strategies [11–13].

The main objective of the present study was to investigate the presence of anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG and IgM antibodies in serum samples collected from patients with suspected dengue virus infection, before and after the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, during the pre-vaccination period. These samples were submitted by public health care centers from 20 municipalities in the Triângulo Mineiro Norte region of Minas Gerais State, Brazil, to support dengue diagnosis and inform appropriate patient management, thereby preventing disease worsening. Additionally, the findings aim to inform the adoption of necessary epidemiological measures to control the epidemic situation.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

This study was based on a retrospective cohort model. To accomplish its main aim, 2,969 serum samples from patients were analyzed to confirm the diagnosis of dengue; these samples were collected from 2019, 2020, and 2021. For the present study, 1,205 samples from 2019 were considered as negative controls for SARS-CoV-2, considering that period pre-pandemic. All samples used were taken from the serum bank of the Immunoparasitology Laboratory “Dr. Mario Endsfeldz” and refers to samples sent to the dengue diagnosis service from January 2019 to April 2021, as part of meeting demand from 20 municipalities of the region called Triângulo Mineiro Norte from Minas Gerais State, Brazil. As shown in Fig. 1, when this study was carried out, the most inhabited city of this region was Uberlândia (699,097 inhabitants), followed by the others with their respective populations: Araguari (117,825); Patrocínio (91,449); Monte Carmelo (47,931); Iturama (38,295); Prata (28,017); Coromandel (27,966); Tupaciguara (25,398); Monte Alegre de Minas (21,236); Santa Vitória (20,973); Nova Ponte (15,800); Centralina (10,343); Estrela do Sul (7,936 inhabitants), Iraí de Minas (7,027 inhabitants), Abadia dos Dourados (7,006); Araporã (6,931); Romaria (3,520); Cascalho Rico (3,092); Douradoquara (1,912); and Grupiara (1,387). The geographic locations of these cities, as well as the numbers of patients whose serum samples were collected and analyzed, are shown in Table 1. In addition to the quantitative distribution of the samples tested by each demographic group, Table 2 presents the distribution of the patients by age for each year of occurrence. This study was carried out in three steps in a retrospective cohort design, as identified in Fig. 2.

Clinical data

The following patient data were considered: demographics (age, gender, city and neighborhood), date of symptom onset, presenting symptoms (fever, myalgia, headache, rash, vomiting, nausea, back pain, conjunctivitis, arthritis, severe arthralgia, petechiae, leukopenia, positive loop test, and retroorbital pain), comorbidities (hematologic diseases, hepatopathies, chronic kidney disease, hypertension, or acid‒peptic disease), as well as presenting weakened immune response due to immunosenescence (age ≥ 60 years).

Immunoassays for dengue and COVID-19

The Panbio™ Dengue IgM capture ELISA (Abbott, St. Ingbert, Germany) was carried out for the qualitative detection of IgM antibodies against dengue antigenic preparation, being able to clarify the clinical laboratory diagnostics of patients with clinical symptoms consistent with this viral infection. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, this test has 94.7% sensitivity and 100% specificity. The presence of antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 in the selected samples was detected by the in vitro immunochromatographic assay Panbio™ COVID-19 IgG/IgM Rapid Test Device (Abbott - Jena, Germany). This test promotes qualitative detection of IgG and IgM to SARS-CoV-2 antibodies. According to the manufacturer, compared with PCR-positive and PCR-negative samples from COVID-19 patients and healthy individuals, the Panbio™ COVID-19 test provides a sensitivity of 95.8% with a 95% confidence interval of [85.7%−99.5%], a specificity of 94.0% with a 95% confidence interval of [83.5%−98.7%] and a percent agreement of 94.9% with a 95% confidence interval of [88.5%−98.3%].

Confirmation of samples from the pre-vaccination period

To verify that the present study was performed with samples from the pre-vaccination period so that these samples would not be influenced by the immune response caused by the vaccine, we checked the websites of the municipalities of the cities analyzed, where the vaccination plans were indicated, with the ages and their respective vaccination starting dates [14–19]. This procedure was necessary because vaccination against COVID-19 in most of the investigated cities started in January 2021. However, from January to April, only elderly individuals over 65 years of age and priority groups, such as health professionals, were vaccinated. Thus, only two samples analyzed in the month of April in the city of Uberlândia, elderly, aged 75 and 77 years, fit the vaccination period. Both were positive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgM and negative for IgG.

Statistical analyses

The analysis for normality was performed via the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Quantitative variables were compared via the Mann‒Whitney test for nonparametric variables or by the Student t-test for parametric variables. The differences were considered significant when p values were ≤ 0.05.

Ethical considerations

No nominal data or any other data that allow the identification of the subjects of this research were accessed. As described above, all the samples analyzed were taken from the serum bank of the Immunoparasitology Laboratory Dr. Mario Endsfeldz and referred to samples sent for dengue diagnosis services from January 2019-April 2021. This serum bank was approved under Protocol # CAAE 76912823.1.0000.5152. The need for consent to participate from each patient was waived by The Human Research Ethics Committee from Universidade Federal de Uberlândia (CEP-UFU), according to the regulations established by The National Committee of Ethics in Human Research (CONEP) from The National Health Council - Ministry of Health.

Results

Analysis of demographic data

When analyzing the distribution of the serum samples from patients tested for dengue and SARS-CoV-2 by demographic group and age during the period 2019–2021, it was observed that the age mean ± SD for all municipalities during the years 2019, 2020 and 2021 was 37.0 ± 20.1, 36.8 ± 17.6 and 35.8 ± 15.8, respectively. Only the year 2020 did not pass in the normality test (p = 0.0058), whereas, for the years 2019 and 2021, they did (Table 2). The pairwise analysis by year, i.e., 2019–2020, 2019–2021, 2020–2021, revealed that no age differences were found among them (p values varying from 0.1074 to 0.8523).

Seropositivity for dengue and SARS-CoV-2 in the pre-pandemic period

After performing capture ELISA IgM tests, it was observed that in the pre-pandemic period, which corresponds to the analyses of samples from 2019, there was a predominance of positive cases for dengue throughout the analyzed period (January to December 2019), characterizing the negative control. However, some positive samples for anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies were detected in the first half of 2019. Eleven samples were positive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgM, and 14 were positive for IgG. Among the samples positive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgM, 5 were also positive for anti-Dengue IgM. On the other hand, of the samples positive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG, 7 were also positive for anti-Dengue IgM. Only one sample was positive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgM, anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG and anti-Dengue IgM (Fig. 3A).

Seropositivity for severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) was greater than that for dengue during the pandemic period

In this study, we report the rates of confirmed dengue and COVID-19 cases from December 2019 to April 2021. As observed previously in 2019, there was a greater number of dengue-positive samples than COVID-19-positive samples. Therefore, the increase in the number of positive samples for anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies was highlighted from December 2019 to April 2021, constituting part of the pandemic period in the country. From mid-2020, concomitant cases of dengue and COVID-19 began to appear. High and higher COVID-19 peaks than dengue peaks were also demonstrated starting in June 2020, extending until March 2021. This set of results confirms that there may be an underestimation of the number of COVID-19 cases in the region (Fig. 3B).

IgM and/or IgG seropositivity for SARS-CoV-2 from January 2019–April 2021

From the analysis of the samples via the rapid IgM/IgG anti-SARS-CoV-2 test, it was possible to observe the profile of the antibody classes found in the samples positive for SARS-CoV-2 during the period from January 2019 to April 2021. Notably, in the pre-pandemic period, the presence of the three analyzed groups in question (COVID IgM+, COVID IgG + and COVID IgM+/IgG+) already occurred, probably because of cross-reactivity with other circulating COVs and high similarity in their genomes. Next, the incidence of high COVID-19 IgG + and COVID-19 IgM+/IgG + groups in the samples analyzed in 2020 and 2021 emphasized a change in the pattern of antibody classes presented when the first half of 2020 was compared with the second half and early 2021. The shift from IgM class antibodies to predominant IgG antibodies indicates the progression of the immune response to virus infection in the analyzed patients over time (Fig. 4).

Signs and symptoms related to dengue and COVID-19 during the period from January 2019 to April 2021

From the analyses of the patient files that were made available for the study, it was possible to observe the correlation between the serological groups analyzed and the symptomatology of the infections reported by these patients in the years 2020 and 2021. In the initial phase, characterized by the presence of IgM antibodies, both SARS-CoV-2 and dengue present similar symptoms. In 2020, some symptoms appeared quite similar, such as fever, myalgia, headache, nausea, vomiting, back pain, retro-orbital pain, leukopenia, and intense arthralgia (Fig. 5A). These symptoms, together with other comorbidities presented by patients, include hypertension and diabetes, which can be worsening factors in the picture of infections.

Analyses of co-infection by dengue virus and SARS-CoV-2 throughout the whole period

The incidence of concomitant infection between dengue and SARS-CoV-2 in serum samples was evaluated from January 2019 to April 2021. In 2019, there was a predominance of dengue cases and rare cases of coronavirus seropositivity, represented by SARS-CoV-2 alone or by dengue + SARS-CoV-2 samples. However, in the following months, there was a significant increase in seropositivity for SARS-CoV-2, with a high proportion in the year 2020, in parallel with the seropositivity rates for the dengue-positive patient group, which reached a greater number of seropositive samples than the seropositivity rate for dengue infection in 2021.

In addition, an increase in the number of samples showing simultaneous positivity for dengue and SARS-CoV-2 was observed in 2020 and 2021, highlighting the year 2021, when the worst scenario of SARS-CoV-2 infection was observed in the northern triangle region. These results corroborate the possibility of an underestimation of cases of COVID-19 in the analyzed municipalities (Fig. 7).

Discussion

During the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, particularly in tropical areas, it was challenging to differentiate this viral infection from arboviral diseases such as dengue, due to overlapping clinical signs and symptoms. Fever and nonspecific symptoms in early COVID-19 can be difficult to distinguish from those of arboviral diseases, especially because respiratory signs may be absent or appear later in the course of the disease [4]. The heterogeneous clinical presentations among patients with COVID-19, especially those with severe disease without comorbidities, have drawn worldwide attention [20].

The intensity of dengue epidemics reported during the pandemic has varied considerably, with some countries experiencing historically low levels of transmission, while others reported record outbreaks. Therefore, in-depth evaluations are needed to understand the impact of COVID-19 prevention measures on dengue virus transmission and to integrate diagnostic and treatment strategies for both infections [5, 10]. At clinical presentation, consistent basic clinical, diagnostic, and epidemiological indicators can assist in distinguishing COVID-19 from dengue and other febrile illnesses [12].

Regarding dengue diagnosis, Abbott’s Panbio™ Dengue Early ELISA is a first-line option, designed to detect the nonstructural protein 1 (NS1) antigen of the dengue virus using a capture ELISA format for early diagnosis. Indeed, the NS1 ELISA test is globally recognized as a reliable and relatively affordable method for early detection of dengue. However, it is important to consider that NS1 ELISA sensitivity significantly decreases over time after dengue infection. High sensitivity rates (85%–95%) are observed only between days 0–3 of the acute phase, with sensitivity dropping to approximately 50%–60% (or lower) by days 5–7. At this later stage, serological tests for detecting IgM and IgG antibodies become more sensitive than NS1 detection. In the present study, all serum samples were collected after more than seven days of symptom onset, justifying the use of serological tests rather than NS1 antigen detection.

The Panbio™ Dengue IgM Capture ELISA (Abbott) utilizes a recombinant antigenic preparation comprising dengue virus antigens from all four serotypes (DENV-1 to DENV-4). Although the manufacturer does not publicly specify the exact viral proteins included, the recombinant antigens are designed to detect IgM antibodies elicited in response to dengue virus infection. It is generally accepted that dengue IgM responses predominantly target structural proteins, such as the envelope (E) and premembrane (prM) proteins, rather than nonstructural proteins like NS1.

The immunoassays selected in our study aimed to minimize cross-reactivity in detecting antibodies against dengue and SARS-CoV-2; however, they could not completely eliminate this limitation. As previously described, cross-reactivity between these viruses remains a major pitfall of serological studies. Nevertheless, our retrospective cohort study evaluated data over three years, and since the same immunoassays were consistently used throughout the study period, it is reasonable to assume that the observed variations are primarily due to epidemiological changes rather than false-positive or false-negative results.

In this context, our study aimed to investigate whether SARS-CoV-2 infections were underestimated at the beginning of the pandemic by analyzing a panel of serum samples initially submitted for dengue diagnosis from patients presenting with nonspecific symptoms. By designing a retrospective cohort study spanning both the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods, we observed that as SARS-CoV-2 infections emerged in the region, the number of seropositive samples for COVID-19 increased significantly.

Although studies on the accuracy of COVID-19 tests still require considerable methodological improvement, important reports have assessed the diagnostic performance of antibody tests. To validate the accuracy of any antibody test, it is essential to apply an appropriate reference standard - typically a high-quality “gold standard” test - conducted without knowledge of the antibody test results to avoid bias. Ideally, this involves a positive RT-PCR result during the symptomatic period or clinical criteria combined with PCR/serological follow-up.

To determine whether a person presenting in the community, or in primary or secondary care, has or has previously had SARS-CoV-2 infection, antibody testing can be useful [21]. However, antibody tests have low sensitivity in the first week after symptom onset. Nevertheless, they can complement other diagnostic methods later in the disease course, particularly when RT-PCR tests are negative or unavailable. Antibody tests are most useful for detecting prior SARS-CoV-2 infection when performed 15 or more days after symptom onset. However, the duration of detectable antibody levels remains uncertain, and data beyond 35 days post-symptom onset are still limited [21].

In our study, the immunoassay selected for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG detection was the Panbio™ COVID-19 IgG/IgM Rapid Test Device, which has demonstrated good concordance, particularly regarding IgG detection, when compared with the Architect™ SARS-CoV-2 IgG Assay [22]. This is a critical point, as a failure to consider COVID-19 due to a positive dengue result could have serious consequences for both patient management and public health surveillance. It underscores the importance of using sensitive, accessible, and highly accurate tests for SARS-CoV-2 detection [23].

Given the potential for confounding antibody responses between flaviviruses and SARS-CoV-2, it is crucial to assess whether anti-dengue antibodies impact SARS-CoV-2 viral load or antibody detection. Lytton and colleagues [24] evaluated anti-nucleocapsid (N)-protein antibodies, anti-dengue E-protein antibodies, and dengue NS1 in serum samples from COVID-19 patients and acute dengue patients. They reported that viral RNA and anti-N antibodies were detected in COVID-19 patients, independent of dengue IgG levels [24]. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for potential false-positive dengue serology in COVID-19 patients.

Importantly, our study utilized the Panbio™ COVID-19 immunoassay, which targets the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid (N) protein, rather than the spike (S) protein, minimizing the potential for cross-reactivity with dengue virus antibodies.

There is consensus in the literature that antibody tests have inherent limitations, including possible cross-reactivity between SARS-CoV-2 and dengue antibodies, particularly when rapid serological tests are employed [25, 26]. Recent studies have expanded our understanding of humoral immune responses to SARS-CoV-2 and the performance of commercial and laboratory-based serological assays [27]. These findings emphasize important considerations such as antibody expression levels, stability, neutralization dynamics, cross-reactivity, and possible immunological boosting by seasonal coronaviruses [28].

Standardized serological testing remains critically important for designing vaccination evaluation protocols, epidemiological models of immunity, and public health strategies to refine transmission dynamic models [29]. Nevertheless, it remains vital to establish rapid and cost-effective methods to differentiate dengue and COVID-19 during co-epidemics, particularly in low-income tropical regions [30, 31].

Ongoing evaluation of dengue and COVID-19 emergence and reemergence will contribute to better understanding of transmission dynamics and inform new intervention strategies [32]. From an epidemiological standpoint, one of the major challenges remains improving diagnostic capacity to differentiate SARS-CoV-2 and dengue co-infections [33, 34]. Furthermore, additional experimental research is needed to clarify unresolved questions concerning these concomitant infections and to advance clinical management and prevention strategies [35–37]. Addressing these challenges is critical to improving public health responses and understanding the implications of SARS-CoV-2 and dengue virus co-infections.

Conclusion

This study analyzed a panel of 2,969 human serum samples using an IgM capture ELISA for dengue diagnosis and an IgG/IgM rapid test device for COVID-19, during the period of 2019–2020. All serum samples were initially submitted under the diagnostic hypothesis of dengue. Our findings revealed that, even though dengue virus and SARS-CoV-2 infections occurred independently without any causal relationship, the number of patients infected by SARS-CoV-2 was underestimated during the study period. This underestimation may have increased the risk of severe COVID-19 outcomes among patients, particularly in this population simultaneously affected by dengue virus co-infection.

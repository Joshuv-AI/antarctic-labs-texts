M2a macrophages identified as a potential therapeutic candidate for intervertebral disc herniation regression
Authors: Zorica Buser, Catarina Correia, Melissa Baker, Catarina Meireles, Tiago Castanheira, Pedro Santos Silva, Paulo Pereira, Michael Gerling, Gabriel Courties, Mário A Barbosa, Hans-Joerg Meisel, Carla Cunha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveLumbar disc herniation (LDH) is one of the most common spine pathologies and its spontaneous regression has been attributed to the activation of immune cells by inflammatory processes and extracellular matrix remodelling. This study aims to identify a macrophage candidate with potential for physiological treatment of LDH.Study designIn vitro co-culture model of phagocytosis using human surgical hernia explants and monocyte-derived macrophages. Lumbar disc hernia tissues were collected from patients who underwent discectomy for LDH and co-cultured in transwell assays with M0, M1, M2a and M2d macrophages derived from blood monocytes. The macrophage phenotypes and phagocytic activity were analyzed by flow cytometry using pHrodo™ BioParticles™ conjugates and immunostaining for classical M1 and M2 markers. Explants were analyzed histologically with Alcian blue/Sirius red.ResultsA total of 21 samples were included, patient's average age was 48.8 ​± ​12.9 years and the most common levels were L4-5 (42.9 ​%) and L5-S1 (33.3 ​%). After three days co-culture with monocyte-derived macrophages, explants maintained the structure and high proteoglycans/collagen ratio. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that the explants modified the immune profile of macrophages, increasing the proportion of pro-inflammatory CD86+ cells, while reducing the percentage of anti-inflammatory CD163+ cells. Additionally, the M2a phenotype presented larger cells and had the highest phagocytic profile towards the explants.ConclusionsM2a macrophages showed the highest potential towards the design of a macrophage-based therapy for LDH. This study holds great promise for translational medicine aimed at accelerating spontaneous LDH using a physiological treatment that could prevent need for surgical intervention.Keywords: Disc herniation, Macrophages, Biological treatment, Immunotherapy, Lumbar spine, Intervertebral disc

Introduction

Lumbar disc herniation (LDH) is one of the most common spine pathologies affecting 266 million individuals worldwide yearly [1]. Symptomatic LDH typically presents as radicular leg pain with or without associated back pain. This pain can be debilitating for the patient, limiting activities of daily living and increasing the risk of developing major depressive disorder, thus affecting their overall quality of life [2]. Conservative management such as oral medications and physical therapy are first line treatments, with surgery being the last resort for those that do not improve. Specifically, lumbar discectomy (minimally invasive or open) is the most common surgical intervention for disc herniations.

Despite the successful nature of lumbar discectomies, some patients can experience recurrent disc herniation, or persistent symptoms. With an estimated reherniation rate of 3–18 ​%, reherniations have been reported to be the cause of 62 ​% of reoperations after discectomy [3]. Additionally, a reherniation not only increases the morbidity and chronic disability of the patient, but is a social and economic burden as well, with estimated direct and indirect costs in the USA to be $34,242 and $3,778, respectively [4].

The spontaneous regression of LDH following conservative treatment has been reported in 15–43 ​% of the patients [5], with MRI findings demonstrating a significant increase in regression in patients with a symptom duration of less than one year compared to those with symptoms lasting longer than one year [6]. Additionally, it has been mainly attributed to activation of immune cells that initiate a cascade of inflammation, angiogenesis and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling leading to hernia resorption [7]. We have previously demonstrated that unpolarized monocyte-derived macrophages administered into the rat tail disc hernia, promoted a functional decrease of the hernia size, via macrophage activation and phagocytic capacity [8]. The macrophage-based therapy holds a great promise for minimally invasive biological treatments aimed at hernia regression that would significantly resolve patients pain symptoms and improve quality of life.

The objective of the current study was to identify the activation status of macrophages (M0, M1, M2a and M2d) that provide optimal tissue resorption within human surgical hernia explants. Taking advantage of a newly developed in vitro co-culture model of phagocytosis using human surgical hernia explants and monocyte-derived macrophages, the current study allows to pinpoint the specific mechanisms behind physiological hernia regression.

Methods

LDH explants collection

This was an international two-center study, with the disc tissue samples collected from patients who underwent single or multi-level lumbar discectomy for MRI confirmed LDHs compressing the nerve root causing radicular leg pain. A total of 20 patients comprising of 21 samples were included. The average age was 48.8 ​± ​12.9 years, with 67 ​% being male. 42.9 ​% were from L4-L5, 33.3 ​% were from L5-S1, 14.3 ​% were from L3-L4 and 9.5 ​% were from L2-L3. Patients were excluded if they had undergone previous lumbar surgery, tumor, or were diagnosed with an autoimmune disease. Both institutions obtained IRB approvals (protocol numbers: #20226752 and #1/CECRI/2023).

The hernia tissue samples were collected and kept in RPMI culture media supplemented with 5 ​% FBS and 1 ​% Fungizone and used for in vitro co-culture experiments with monocyte-derived macrophages (Fig. 1).

Experimental procedure of the LDH explants/macrophages co-culture. Human monocytes were isolated from buffy coats, differentiated and cultured as M0 or otherwise polarized towards M1, M2a and M2d phenotypes. LDH explants from patients subjected to microdiscectomy were collected in culture media and punched into 4 ​mm diameter standardized samples. Indirect transwell co-culture assays were established using permeable transwells with LDH explants seeded in the upper compartment and monocyte-derived macrophages cultured in the bottom of the plate for 3 days. Flow cytometry and immunofluorescence were performed on an individual donor basis.

Human monocyte isolation and polarization

Human primary monocytes were obtained from buffy coats of healthy blood donors, using well established negative selection protocol [9]. Recovered monocytes were seeded on 24-well plates in culture media (RPMI-1640 ​+ ​Glutamax) supplemented with 10 ​% FBS and 1 ​% penicillin-streptomycin, at a density of 0.5 ​× ​106 ​cells/well, in the presence of 50 ​ng/ml of macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) and maintained in a cell culture incubator. After 7 days in culture, M-CSF was removed and at day 10 macrophages were polarized towards either M1, M2a or M2d phenotypes by supplementing the culture media with: TNF-α (2 ​ng/ml) ​+ ​IFN-γ (20 ​ng/ml) (M1), IL4 (10 ​ng/ml) (M2a), IL-6 (10 ​ng/ml) (M2d) [10]. Macrophages were left to polarize for 2 days, after which they were co-cultured with human LDH explants for 3 days. The respective phenotype was confirmed by flow cytometry.

Establishment of macrophage-LDH explants co-cultures

LDH explants were punched with a 4 ​mm puncher to standardize sample size. Indirect transwell co-culture assays were established by using permeable transwells (Falcon) containing LDH explants and culturing monocyte-derived macrophages (M0, M1, M2a and M2d) on the bottom of the plate (Fig. 1). After culturing macrophages with LDH explants for 3 days, phagocytosis assays were performed, followed by flow cytometry, immunofluorescence and histological analysis, to identify the most promising phenotype able to resorb the herniated tissue.

Phagocytosis assay

Macrophage phagocytic capacity was assessed by using the pHrodo™ BioParticles™ conjugates for Phagocytosis (Invitrogen), according to manufacturer's instructions, followed by flow cytometry or immunofluorescence quantification. These Bioparticles fluoresce when they are in an acidic environment such as the phagolysosomes.

Flow cytometry analysis of macrophage phenotypes

For all the differently polarized macrophages, flow cytometry analysis was performed using the same panel of fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies for cell surface lineage and activation markers. The following anti-human antibodies were used: CD86(clone BU63)-FITC (Immunotools), CD14(clone MEM15)-APC (Immunotools), CD163-BV711 (BD Pharmingen) and HLA-DR(clone 25F9)-eFluor450-BV421 (Invitrogen). Cells were further incubated with the fixable viability dye eFluor®780-APC-Cy7 (Invitrogen). Quantification of pHrodo™ BioParticles was performed using the PE filter. Samples were acquired on a flow cytometer (LSRFortessa; BD) and obtained data was analyzed with FlowJo™ v10.10 Software (BD Life Sciences).

Immunofluorescence for macrophages with pHrodo bioparticles

Immunofluorescence analysis was performed against CD163 (1:100, Bio-Rad) and CD86 (1:200, Bio-Rad). Wells were imaged in the high-content imaging system Operetta CLS (Revvity), controlled by the Harmony software.

Alcian Blue/picrosirius red histological analysis

Tissue samples were fixated in 10 ​% neutral buffered formalin and processed for paraffin embedding. Sequential transversal 5 ​μm sections of the LDH explants were deparaffinized, stained in Weigert's Iron Hematoxylin (cell nuclei), rehydrated and stained with Alcian blue/Picrosirius red (proteoglycans/collagen). Samples were analyzed using Leica DM2000 microscope equipped with a digital color camera and the LAS X software. The acquired images were analyzed using Python Software, where the colors red and blue were quantified as percentage of image area.

Imaging mass cytometry

Two serial 3 ​μm tissue sections were placed on Polylysine-treated microscope slides. The first slide was stained with Hematoxillin & Eosin Staining (HES) for selecting the Regions of Interest (ROIs). The second one was used for metal-tagged antibodies labeling and imaging mass cytometry ablation. HES staining was performed at the Experimental Histology Network of Montpellier (RHEM) according to standard protocols. Slides were digitalized with a Hamamatsu NanoZoomer 2.0-HT scanner and images were visualized with the NDP.view 1.2.47 software for ROIs selection. A maximum of 2 ​mm2 of tissue was selected. After deparaffinization, antigen retrieval was performed using Tris/EDTA buffer, pH9, followed by incubation with Superblock™ (Thermo Scientific) and then incubation with FcR Blocking Reagent (Miltenyi). Antibody validation and metal conjugation was performed as previously described [11]. Following overnight incubation at 4 ​°C, nuclei were stained with iridium 1:400, washed and dried prior to laser ablation of selected ROIs using the Hyperion Imaging System (Fluidigm, Standard Biotools) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Data were exported as MCD files and visualized using the Fluidigm MCD™ viewer v1.0.560.6. The minimum and maximum thresholds were adapted for each marker for optimal visualization. Cell segmentation was performed as previously described [12].

Statistical analysis

Normality was assessed by D'Agostino-Pearson omnibus normality test. Results are presented as median ​± ​interquartile range in box and whiskers plots. Statistical analysis was performed with the non-parametric Mann-Whitney test whenever two groups were compared and with the Kruskal-Wallis test, followed by Dunn's multiple comparison test, whenever more than two groups were compared. GraphPad Prism 7 software was used. For all statistical analysis, sample size was n ​= ​4–8 per group and statistical significance was set at p ​< ​0.05.

Results

Macrophage viability and CD163/CD86 co-expression

For all macrophage phenotypes, cell viability increased from monoculture to co-culture with the LDH explants. However, no significant difference was found between macrophage phenotypes (Fig. 2A and B). Analysis of co-expression of the anti-inflammatory CD163 and pro-inflammatory CD86 markers, indicated that for all conditions, cells were mainly CD163+ ​CD86− (Fig. 2C). The M2a phenotype showed a higher percentage of CD163+CD86+ and CD163−CD86+ cells and a lower percentage of CD163+CD86−cells than the remaining phenotypes (Fig. 2C).

Macrophage phagocytic activity

The phagocytic capacity of each macrophage phenotype was analyzed by quantifying pHrodo Bioparticles (Biop+) fluorescence inside the cells. (Fig. 3A). The study found that all phenotypes present a Low+ and High+ ​expressing Biop+ ​population (Fig. 3B). Within the Biop+High+ expressing population, the M2a phenotype has a higher percentage of phagocytosed bioparticles than the remaining phenotypes, indicating their increased phagocytic capacity especially in respect to the M0 and M1 phenotypes (p ​< ​0.05, Fig. 3C). Regarding the characteristics of the cells that are phagocytosing, by comparing the M1 and M2a phenotypes in presence of the LDH explants, an increase in the complexity (SSC-A parameter) of the M2a macrophages was observed (Fig. 3D), probably corresponding to the presence of phagolysosomes. CD14+Biop+ ​cells (phagocytosing) were further analyzed to understand expression of macrophage markers (CD163+/CD86+/HLA-DR+). The LDH explants tended to decrease CD163 expression for all phenotypes (Fig. 3E). M2a had a significantly decreased expression of CD163, especially compared to M2d (p ​< ​0.01), and a significantly increased expression of CD86, especially compared to M2d (p ​< ​0.05), in monoculture. In the co-culture setting M2a had significantly increased expression of HLA-DR, especially compared to M0 and M2d (p ​< ​0.001).

Immunofluorescence staining of CD163 and CD86, as well as pHrodo, were quantified for all the conditions (Fig. 4A). There was no significant difference between conditions for any of the markers. However, M2a present a larger cell area than the remaining phenotypes, statistically significantly higher when comparing to M1 (p ​< ​0.05, Fig. 4C). This result corroborates the increased cell complexity in M2a observed by flow cytometry (Fig. 3D). Moreover, phagolysosomes area in M2a was the highest among all phenotypes (Fig. 4D).

LDH explants characterization and structural integrity

LDH explants in co-culture with the different macrophage phenotypes were analyzed histologically with Alcian blue/Sirius red staining for quantification of collagen and proteoglycans, the main IVD ECM components (Fig. 5A). Proteoglycans content was higher than collagen content for all the conditions. The M2a phenotype was the one capable of better degrading the proteoglycans present in the explants (Fig. 5B).

Moreover, spatial analysis of hernia cell landscape was performed using imaging mass cytometry. Macrophage subset markers (MERTK, CD206, CCR2 and SPP1) were identified, in particular CCR2+ macrophages, which prevailed throughout the tissue, suggesting an important contribution of infiltrating monocyte-derived macrophages (Fig. 5C).

Discussion

In the current study, the M2a phenotype presented bigger cells and had the highest phagocytic profile in the presence of the human lumbar hernia explants microenvironment.

While surgical and nonsurgical treatments for disc herniation have been vastly discussed in the clinical literature, there is limited evidence regarding the factors influencing the occurrence and timing of spontaneous hernia resorption. Studies have shown that up to 60 ​% of disc herniations undergo resorption as observed in MRI or CT scans, with the type of herniation being a key determining factor [13,14]. In addition to the anatomical features of herniation tissues, the state of the nucleus, annulus and endplate structures have been linked to the resorption success [14].

The most investigated mechanism of herniation resorption is the activation of physiological immune responses that lead to an increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines, increased angiogenesis, circulating macrophage recruitment and ECM remodelling, leading to resorption of the herniated tissue [14]. In the current study, macrophages were identified within the herniated sections. Macrophages are one of the few inflammatory cells that can penetrate the enclosed IVD tissue, and their infiltration has been positively associated with extruded disc herniations as well as with the extent of reduction of the herniated disc observed over a 1-year follow-up in patients with sciatica [13]. We have also previously reported that the amount of macrophages was positively correlated with hernia size within in vivo rat hernias [15]. This suggests that macrophages play an active role in reducing the size of herniated disc tissue and therefore are good candidates to be explored for designing a physiological macrophage-based therapy for LDH.

Macrophages are highly plastic cells that can display a range of phenotypes regulated by their microenvironment, which span from the classical pro-inflammatory M1 to the alternatively activated pro-resolving M2 phenotype [16]. Djuric et al. reported macrophages to be present in both cervical and lumbar herniations, with M2 macrophages being more predominant than M1 macrophages [17]. Additionally, M2 macrophages were shown to promote repair in the nucleus pulposus by inhibiting TNF-α-induced apoptosis and senescence of nucleus pulposus cells. In contrast, the M1 phenotype appears to be more closely linked to accelerated degeneration of IVDs [18,19]. A systematic review exploring the association between M1 and M2 macrophages and their role in LDHs demonstrated a pro-regenerative state when M2 macrophages were the primary macrophage population in herniations, while M1 prevalence led to a pro-inflammatory state with aggravated pain symptoms [20]. In the current study, the M2a macrophages were capable of better potentiating the degradation of the proteoglycans present in the explants, indicating M2a macrophages to have the greater potential of LDH explant resorption.

Macrophage phagocytosis of cellular and extracellular materials is crucial for tissue homeostasis and repair [21,22], and is increasingly recognized as a vital process for LDH resorption. Despite this, the current literature falls short in identifying the specific mechanisms underlying macrophage-mediated hernia remodelling and in understanding how macrophage plasticity is regulated within the hernia microenvironment, aside from clinical observations regarding the presence of phagocytic cells in the IVD tissue [23]. Findings from the current study demonstrate that M2a macrophages were significantly larger than M1, supporting the increased cell complexity observed by flow cytometry. Furthermore, the M2a phenotype exhibited a higher percentage of phagocytosed bioparticles compared to the other phenotypes, demonstrating its increased phagocytic capacity. Notably, the area of phagolysosomes in M2a was the highest among all phenotypes, and this quantification reinforces the finding that M2a is more pro-phagocytic of the LDH explants.

The current study is not without limitations. In the current study, both protrusions and extrusions were included due to the sample size. While the type of herniation can have an impact on the patient reported outcomes, we would not expect major difference in the molecular profiles, suggesting that macrophage behavior in the herniated disc microenvironment may be driven more by the overall presence of herniated nucleus pulposus tissue rather than the specific morphology of the herniation. Furthermore, samples were obtained from a cohort predominantly in their fourth decade of life, primarily male, and most of the samples derived from the L4-5 and L5-S1 IVDs. This is consistent with current literature reporting the highest incidence of symptomatic disc herniations in patients between 40 and 60 years of age, higher prevalence in males, and approximately 95 ​% of LDHs occurring at L4-5 or L5-S1 [[24], [25], [26]]. Moreover, only one time point was analyzed in this study, which may not fully capture the process of phagocytosis occurring in vivo. Further studies assessing progressive changes in macrophage phenotypes and functions across multiple time points will be important to better characterize these time-dependent changes. To our knowledge, this is the first study that showed that M2a macrophages can directly remodel the ECM of IVD herniations, a crucial step for developing therapies aimed at resorbing herniated tissue. This study holds great promise for translational medicine aimed at accelerating spontaneous hernia regression. Future studies focusing on optimizing the protocol of autologous M2a phenotype polarization to enhance their phagocytic action on the hernia are needed. Ultimately, we aim to provide a framework for translating our findings into personalized therapeutic interventions in which tailored macrophages, collected from the patient and polarized toward the desired phenotype, can be directly administered into herniated disc tissue.

These tailored macrophages can be used in a clinical setting being directly administered into a herniated tissue, using macrophages collected from the patient and polarized towards the required phenotype.

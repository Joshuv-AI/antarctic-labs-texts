Green Approach to Carbon-Steel Electropolishing in Phosphoric Acid Using Green Algae–Nano Silver Composites
Authors: Fatma. M. Abouzeid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A study was conducted to synthesize silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) using marine green algae and to explore their electrochemical application in mild steel electropolishing in phosphoric acid as leveling and smoothing agents. The composite was prepared from the marine green alga Ulva linza (U. linza) and Ulva lactuca (U. lactuca) which were enhanced by synthesized silver nanoparticles (AgNPs). The marine green alga Ulva used was collected from the Arabian GulfSaudi Arabia and Abou Quire GulfEgypt. The green algae–nano silver composites feature was investigated via FTIR, SEM, particle size, and EDX. It was observed that the percentage of nano silver produced using green algae (U. linza) 3.5% is greater than that using green algae (U. lactuca) 1.5%. It is perceived that the particle of nano silver produced using green algae (U. linza) 26 nm is less than that using green algae (U. lactuca) 33 nm, which reflect that green algae (U. linza) containsa self-reduction property that transformed silver ions, Ag+, to metal nano silver Ag, more than green algae (U. lactuca). The novel, eco-friendly, and cost-effective green algae–nano silver composites were used as additives for steel electrodissolution performance, which was investigated via the potential of anode-limiting current association and comparison of regularly cumulative nanocomposites concentration (30–300 ppm). The limiting current, I L, diminishes, while retardation effectiveness percent rises as the green algae–nano silver composite concentration rises. The nanocomposites retardation behavior is dependent on adsorption development at the carbon steel surface that was confirmed via SEM, FTIR, and roughness values. The reaction energy of activation (E a) and activation constraints (ΔH*, ΔS*, and ΔG*) were computed and reflect strong interaction behavior among the green algae–nano silver composites and the steel surface. Consequently, on the basis of microscopy investigation of the designated green algae–Ag NPs composites’ addition to carbon steel polishing bath, probably ensuring the electrolyte displays, discrete progress in the carbon steel surface texture has been observed. Enhancement was produced in the electropolishing electrolyte via the inspected green algae–Ag NPs composites as shown in SEM images, and this result was confirmed by reflectance analysis and roughness measurements. Biological or green synthesis of silver nanoparticles methods effectively surpasses the limitations of both physical and chemical methods. The investigation into AgNPs has sustained to increase, giving rise to novel tenders in medical, industrial, and environmental applications.

Introduction

One of the important electrochemical processes is electropolishing, which is applied for several metallic elements, alloys, and composites finishing. The electropolishing process based on a substantial thin layer removal from the metal surface results in improvement of the surface texture via diminishing roughness, removing imperfections, cleaning impurities, and improving corrosion confrontation. In contrast with traditional motorized polishing, electropolishing may attain enormously acceptable textures at the nanometer scale. It is identically cost-efficient and is effective for polishing multifaceted and erratically designed substances. Electropolishing baths are solutions utilized in the electropolishing procedure. These solutions characteristically contain exact salts, acids, and other compounds that simplify the elaborate electrochemical reactions in the electropolishing procedure. The electrolytes show a critical role in monitoring the material removal rate, confirming consistency of the surface quality, and avoiding disagreeable characteristics like roughness, pitting, or extreme material elimination.

The electropolishing electrolyte composition differs depending on the metal type or alloy actuality polished, the chosen quality, and other procedure constraints. Usually used electrolytes contain combinations of sulfuric H2SO4, phosphoric H3PO4 acid, and several salts that support establishing the wanted electrochemical reaction which results in the material removal and the surface properties enhancement.

The mixture of great viscosity and elevated electrolyte electrical conduction may be a significant reflection in the assortment of actual additives utilized in metal and alloys electropolishing.

The ecological friendliness and care of additives are determined by their capability to biodegrade. The ability to logically analyze ecologically benign components with a bacteriological procedure provides a hopeful substitute for common solvents, contributing to sustainability and dipping ecological influences.

They also have specific surface area and volume and are larger than 60 m2 cm3. Nanocomposite constituents are composite constituents completed of different components and assorted at the nanometer scale to advance extremely the combined new product properties. Plant-mediated synthesis of silver nanoparticles is gaining prominence over chemical and microbial processes due to several advantages including cost-effectiveness, time efficiency, ease of accessibility, environmental friendliness, and low energy requirements.

Because of its low cost, minimal environmental impact, zero contamination, high reduction potential, and stability, the green synthesis of plant-meditated-made nanoparticles has gathered a lot of attention.

Substances and Procedure

Pretreatment of Algal Biomass

Green marine algae Ulva linza (U. linza) were collected from Abu-Qir Bay, Alexandria, Egypt, and Ulva lactuca (U. lactuca) were collected from Arabian GulfSaudi Arabia.The green marine algae were washed with tap water and then distilled water to get rid of unnecessary materials like dust, sand, and salts. The algae were air-dried and then oven-dried at 45 °C to eliminate the moisture content totally until a constant weight. The dried algae and sediments were ground to powder using a gate mortar, sieved using a sieve of 63 μm mesh size, and then stored for further analyses.

Preparation of Algae Extract

A green algae (Ulva) aqueous extract was prepared by macerating 20 g of dry sieved algae in 200 mL of purified water and heated at 60 °C for 25 min and then filtered using a Whatman No.1 filter paper. The obtained aqueous algae extract was then stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C for extra analysis.

Silver Nanoparticles (AgNPs) Synthesis

In the standard procedure for preparation of silver nanoparticles, 20 mL of green algae (Ulva) aqueous extract was mixed with 180 mL of 0.01 M silver nitrate (AgNO3; Reflecta Laboratory Supplies (with 99% purity) solution in a 250 mL conical flask). The combination was gradually heated to 60 °C with stirring for 48 h using a magnetic stirrer to ensure complete reduction of the metal ions in dark to minimize any photoactivation of (AgNO3). Additionally, a control setup was kept without the aqueous marine algal extract. The AgNPs’ formation was confirmed visually by observing the change of color of solution into brown. Characterization of AgNPs was approved using UV–visible spectrophotometry (Jenway Sp-8001) at wavelengths between 200 and 800 nm to display the silver nanoparticles formation. Characterizations of the prepared nanocomposite were conducted by using SEM (JSM- IT 200) and FTIR (PerkinElmer spectrum IR version 10.06.0).

Electropolishing Materials

H3PO4 (85% (w/w)) analar grade provided by BDH Chemicals Ltd. was utilized for the electrolyte solution preparation. Doubly purified deionized water with >18 MΩ cm as measured resistivity was utilized in the solution preparation. Solution consists of 8 M H3PO4 (150 mL) and several concentrations of green algae–nano silver composites. The concentrations of composites cover range from 30 to 300 ppm. While the blank solution consists of 8 M H3PO4.

Carbon-Steel Electrodes Composition (wt %)

The C-steel electrodes composition utilized in electropolishing trials was made in an Egyptian copper company, and the steel electrode composition in mass % is as follows: Fe (96.663%), Mn (2.5%), Si (0.46%), C (0.22%), S (0.05%), and P (0.049%).

Limation and Challenges of Silver Nanoparticles Composites

(a) Biological or green synthesis methods effectively surpass the limitations of both physical and chemical methods by eliminating the need for hazardous chemicals and separate reducing and stabilizing agents, consuming less energy, diversifying the morphologies of nanoparticles and offering eco-friendliness and extraordinary biocompatibility. The investigation into AgNPs has been sustained to increase applications, giving rise to novel tenders in antimicrobial treatment, wound healing, drug delivery formulations, sensor development, diagnosis, treatment of cardiovascular diseases, dentistry, nerve-tissue engineering, and more recently performance-enhanced therapeutics,

(b) One of the primary difficulties lies in controlling particle size, shape, and distribution, as these factors significantly influence their physicochemical properties and biological interactions. Agglomeration of silver nanoparticles poses another challenge, often requiring the use of stabilizers or capping agents to maintain dispersion. , The selection of precursors, reducing agents, and synthesis conditions also plays a crucial role in defining the efficiency and reproducibility of silver nanoparticles production. Moreover, environmental and toxicity concerns related to AgNPs, including potential bioaccumulation and cytotoxic effects, necessitate rigorous assessment and regulation. Advanced characterization techniques, such as XRD, SEM, TEM, and EDX, are essential for analyzing structural and compositional attributes, yet accessibility and cost may limit their widespread application. Addressing these challenges is critical for optimizing silver nanoparticles synthesis and ensuring their safe and effective use in biomedical, environmental, and industrial applications.

(c) Metal nanoparticles can be produced via biosynthetic methods, have wide application scenario, and can accomplish the commercialization purpose. Conversely, metal nanoparticles also have limitations such as poisonousness, which may cause impairment to favorable microorganisms or marine organisms or soil organisms and are dangerous to human healthiness. When silver nanoparticles enter biological receptors, it is not clear whether they will adversely affect the organism. Therefore, it is vital to investigate the silver nanoparticles’ toxicity mechanism. Whether the toxicity comes from the silver nanoparticles themselves or the dissolved silver ions emitted by the silver nanoparticles. There is presently no established silver nanoparticle discovery method, and the nanoparticles’ safety has not been clear until now. −

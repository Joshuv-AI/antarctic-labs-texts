ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/aimThis study aims to explore ChatGPT’s ability to provide comprehensive information on AIH, its potential role in supporting the diagnostic and therapeutic processes for the disease and its broader implications for the use of artificial intelligence in healthcare.Materials and methodsA total of 45 questions were designed and organized into 3 groups, each consisting of 15 questions (Group 1, Group 2, Group 3). Introduction

Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is an immune-mediated chronic liver disease. Due to the lack of specific findings, the heterogeneous clinical presentation and the similarity of findings to those of other autoimmune liver diseases, it is a challenging pathology to diagnose. Therefore, the diagnosis is made by evaluating clinical, laboratory and histological findings collectively [1]. Moreover, given the inter-individual variability in response to treatment, side effects of drugs and short- and long-term complications, a patient-oriented approach to treatment is essential.

The intricate diagnostic and therapeutic processes of AIH are a subject of significant interest among patients. Given the limited information they can obtain from their doctor during outpatient appointments, patients often seek additional sources of information. The primary sources of information for patients are websites and artificial intelligence (AI) applications. These platforms are often utilized by patients due to their accessibility, affordability and the ability to retrieve detailed information at any time. Additionally, they offer services in numerous languages, which is beneficial for patients with diverse linguistic backgrounds. However, there is a concern regarding the reliability of information sources that have not undergone verification and may potentially mislead patients [2].

Significant developments have been observed in the field of AI over recent years. One of the most significant of these is ChatGPT-Generative Pretrained Transformer, an AI-based chatbot system developed by the OpenAI company (San Francisco, CA, United States). Initially launched in November 2022, the system rapidly gained a significant user base [3]. It employs a vast corpus of text from many languages, offering users detailed information on the topics they seek. Its responses are presented in a conversational style, akin to a human dialogue [4].

As AI models continue to evolve and become more advanced, it is anticipated that an increasing number of patients will turn to these platforms to access information regarding their medical conditions and ongoing treatments. Nevertheless, the accuracy, currentness, and comprehensiveness of the information provided by ChatGPT remain uncertain. A previous study on inflammatory bowel disease found that the responses generated by ChatGPT were not scientifically adequate or up-to-date in many aspects [5].

It is believed that healthcare professionals will benefit from AI applications as well as patients in the future. It is particularly suggested that AI will play a significant role in the decision-making processes of doctors regarding the diagnosis and treatment of diseases. Nevertheless, there remains ongoing debate regarding AI’s ability to accurately interpret patient data and keep up with current developments. In a study assessing these scenarios, the accuracy of diagnoses in hypothetical patient cases directed to ChatGPT was found to be 33,3% [6].

As in many other disciplines, the number of AI-supported solutions in hepatology has increased in recent years. A recent review reported successful applications of AI in liver diseases, including imaging, histology, interpretation of non-invasive tests, and outcome prediction [7]. Another study highlighted its valuable role in evaluating the epidemiology of liver diseases and in guiding treatment approaches [8]. However, there is limited evidence on the performance of AI tools in patient education and in facilitating physicians’ access to key guideline-based information on liver diseases.

So this study aims to contribute to the existing literature on the applicability of ChatGPT in hepatology. Specifically, it will explore the AI’s ability to provide comprehensive information on AIH, its potential role in supporting the diagnostic and therapeutic processes for the disease.

Materials and methods

Study design

To evaluate the accuracy and completeness of information provided by ChatGPT regarding AIH, a total of 45 questions were developed and categorized into three groups (Group 1, Group 2, and Group 3), each comprising 15 questions. In Group 1, the 15 most frequently asked questions by AIH patients during follow-up at our hepatology outpatient clinic were selected. The purpose was to determine the issues patients are most concerned about, assuming that such questions could also be directed to AI tools. Groups 2 and 3 were developed using the latest AASLD guideline on AIH, recognized worldwide by hepatologists [9]. Group 2 assessed general aspects of disease management, while Group 3 focused on detailed and exceptional scenarios. This design aimed to evaluate how well ChatGPT could support physicians in both classical and rare cases. All questions strictly followed the guideline and were formulated to be clear and unambiguous. Additionally, five clinical case scenarios were formulated and presented to ChatGPT to assess its ability to differentiate AIH from other conditions that are part of the differential diagnosis. These scenarios were formulated with reference to cases discussed in a recently published article on AIH [10]. The answers were assessed on accuracy using a 6-point Likert scale to evaluate the accuracy of each answer (1;completely incorrect, 2; mostly incorrect, 3; equal parts correct and incorrect, 4; more correct than incorrect, 5; almost completely correct, 6; correct). The answers were also assessed on completeness using a 3-point Likert Scale (1 – Incomplete, 2 - Adequate, 3 – Comprehensive).

All questions were re-asked after 14 days, a timeframe chosen in line with prior studies to evaluate the short-term stability of ChatGPT’s responses while minimizing the confounding effects of major model updates. This two-week interval represents a practical and widely used window for short-term reproducibility assessments in clinical research. Furthermore, no software updates were implemented during this period.

The accuracy and completeness of all AI responses were assessed by a hepatologist (E.Ö.) with over two decades of experience. To prevent potential bias, the author was blinded to the group classification of the questions and the order in which they were presented.

Chatgpt model

In our study, the latest version of ChatGPT, GPT-4o, was utilized. This version was released on May 13, 2024, and is faster than previous versions while also demonstrating enhanced natural language capabilities. The questions were submitted to ChatGPT-4o during the period from November 1 to November 15, 2024. Rather than being input all at once, each question was entered separately using the ‘New Chat’ function. A single individual was responsible for entering the questions into the program and transferring the responses for evaluation.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed using software (SPSS-v26, IBM Co., USA). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to assess compliance with the normal distribution. The Kruskal-Wallis H test was employed to compare non-normally distributed data between groups and multiple comparisons were conducted using Dunn’s test with Bonferroni correction. The Wilcoxon test was employed to compare non-normally distributed data obtained on the first day and 14 days later. Results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and median (minimum–maximum). A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

When ChatGPT’s responses to the questions were analyzed, it was observed that the median accuracy score (Accuracy 1) for the answers to group 1 questions was 5. For group 2 and group 3 questions, the median score was 4, while for the case-based questions, it was 6. No significant difference was observed between the groups (p = 0.328). After 14 days, the accuracy score (Accuracy 2) of the answers was 5 for group 3 and 6 for the other two groups. A statistically significant difference was observed between the groups, which was attributed to the lower scores of the answers in group 3 (p = 0.045) (Table 1).

Discussion

Considering the increasing integration of AI applications into various aspects of our lives in recent years, it is inevitable that their use in the medical field will continue to expand. It is expected anticipate that patients will increasingly turn to these platforms for detailed information about their clinical conditions and that these platforms will serve as a resource for physicians in making treatment decisions. However, the primary question to be addressed is how accurate the decisions made by these platforms are and whether their conclusions are sufficient. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the usability and reliability of ChatGPT, an AI platform that has recently gained widespread use across various fields, in the context of AIH.

In a recent study, Daza et al. evaluated the usability of AI applications for autoimmune liver diseases (AIH, primary sclerosing cholangitis, and primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)) using four different chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Bard) and reported promising results in supporting clinical decision-making for these diseases. ChatGPT was found to provide more accurate information about AIH compared to other chatbots. The authors also highlighted the need for improvement in chatbots regarding accuracy and the provision of up-to-date information [11].

In a study by Colapietro et al. 12 questions posed by patients with AIH were evaluated using ChatGPT-4. The questions were categorized into three areas: diagnosis, quality of life, and medical treatment. Responses were assessed by 11 key opinion leaders in terms of accuracy, completeness, comprehensiveness and safety. ChatGPT-4 demonstrated a high level of comprehensibility but lacked reliability [12].

Gerussi et al. used an AI-assisted model to evaluate liver biopsy slides and successfully distinguished AIH from PBC [13]. Wang et al. developed an AI model with five parameters that accurately differentiated AIH from drug-induced liver injury [14]. These studies demonstrated the utility of AI in differential diagnosis using biochemical data and biopsy material. Unlike these approaches, our study showed that clinicians can directly obtain AIH management information through ChatGPT without model development, offering practical support for diagnosis, treatment, and complex case management. To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically query guideline-based clinical information on AIH in detail.

In our study, the mean accuracy score for responses across all groups was observed to be 4 or higher, while the mean completeness score for all groups exceeded 2. These results suggest that the AI provided adequate and generally accurate responses to the questions. After 14 days, when the same questions were posed again, both the accuracy and completeness mean scores improved across all groups. A comparison of the scores for the responses to the second set of questions revealed a significant difference between the groups. This difference was primarily due to the lower accuracy and completeness scores observed in group 3, compared to the other groups. This may be attributed to the higher level of difficulty of the questions in this group compared to those in the other groups and the program responded with a lower level of accuracy. This suggests that AI may struggle with complex issues, leading to an increased likelihood of errors.

The accuracy and completeness scores of responses given at two-week intervals exhibited a statistically significant increase, excluding the group associated with case-based questions. The responses were markedly more accurate and complete. This phenomenon can be attributed to the continuous self-updating nature of AI, both in terms of knowledge acquisition and algorithm refinement, as well as its ability to relearn through user feedback over time. However, a study by Deniz et al. on thyroid nodules revealed inconsistent performance when ChatGPT was retested after 14 days. Specifically, the AI failed to correct previously incorrect answers to multiple-choice questions, and, in some cases, previously correct answers were found to be incorrect upon reevaluation [15]. The authors associated this observation with the fluctuating nature of AI. In contrast, our study did not encounter such fluctuations. We observed improved accuracy in the responses provided to questions asked after 14 days. This improvement may be ascribed to the later timing of our study, conducted approximately one year later, during which time advancements in AI technology, including updates to algorithms and data processing capabilities, may have contributed to enhanced performance.

One of the most important findings of our study was ChatGPT’s responses to the first two case questions regarding the overlapping features of autoimmune liver diseases. Overlap syndromes are complex clinical conditions that pose significant challenges in the differential diagnosis of autoimmune liver diseases in clinical practice. These conditions can lead to diagnostic uncertainty and, in some cases, inappropriate treatment decisions for patients. Even when a liver biopsy is performed, an incorrect diagnosis can still be made. In such cases, an accurate diagnosis can be established through the comprehensive evaluation of laboratory and pathology results by experienced hepatologists. Although ChatGPT was unable to provide entirely accurate answers to these questions when asked for the first time, it responded correctly when the questions were asked a second time. The rapid pace at which AI applications update and refine information, leading to increasingly accurate results over time, is highly promising. In the near future, we anticipate that ChatGPT will play an even more significant role in assisting physicians with the differential diagnosis and treatment of diseases with similar characteristics.

Cankurtaran et al. reported in their study on inflammatory bowel diseases that ChatGPT was a more reliable source for healthcare professionals than for patients [16]. In contrast, our study found that ChatGPT provided more reliable information for patients than for healthcare professionals. The questions in group 1 focused on general information about the disease, typically asked by patients during outpatient clinic visits. In contrast, the questions in group 3 were more detailed, requiring specific knowledge about the disease that is typically expected from healthcare professionals. ChatGPT provided less accurate responses to the information required by doctors. Additionally, there was no significant change in the completeness score of the answers to questions in group 3 over the 14-day period. In contrast, the accuracy and completeness scores of the answers to questions in group 1 showed a significant improvement over the same timeframe. Based on these results, it can be concluded that ChatGPT provides more accurate information for patients and updates this information more quickly. This improvement may be attributable to the probabilistic nature of ChatGPT’s output generation, whereby repeated prompts can yield more refined, accurate, or complete responses over time.

In a study conducted by Hayward et al. with patients suffering from decompensated cirrhosis, it was found that a pharmacist-led approach to providing information about medication management and disease progression improved treatment compliance and reduced hospitalization rates [17]. In our study, ChatGPT provided highly accurate answers to questions frequently asked by patients in outpatient clinic settings. This result led us to consider that AI could be effectively utilized in patient and caregiver education. ChatGPT holds promise for centers facing personnel and time constraints in patient education. Utilizing Natural Language Processing (NLP) algorithms, it automatically detects the user’s language and generates responses in a manner that is both linguistically appropriate and easily understood by the user. It is also capable of providing responses that are either detailed or simplified based on user requests, making it a platform accessible to patients across all educational levels. When integrated with modern smart device technology, it becomes a platform that can be easily utilized even by illiterate patients, owing to its ability to translate voice commands into text and convert written responses into spoken answers.

Ahn et al. demonstrated that AI algorithms have been effectively applied to various liver diseases (Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease, viral hepatitis, and cirrhosis) for disease diagnosis and prognosis [18].Similarly, in our study we observed that ChatGPT can effectively diagnose AIH using clinical, histological and laboratory data, as well as perform differential diagnoses with other conditions. Given that AIH is a challenging disease to diagnose and shares similar symptoms with many other conditions, we believe that AI-supported diagnosis, treatment determination, and prognosis assessment would significantly simplify the process.

Rizwan and Sadiq investigated the role of ChatGPT in the diagnosis and management of cardiovascular diseases. ChatGPT demonstrated accuracy in 8 out of 10 clinical scenarios, and the treatment protocols it suggested were found to align with the existing literature and current medical knowledge [19]. The ability to access up-to-date guidelines within seconds when needed and provide references for the information presented indicates that AI will become one of the greatest assistants for doctors in the near future.

The main limitation of our study is that the assessment was conducted by a single evaluator. Although the evaluator is a hepatologist with over 20 years of experience and a specialist in autoimmune liver diseases, assessments involving multiple evaluators could have provided different perspectives and thereby enhanced the reliability of the results.

The first group of questions presented to ChatGPT consisted of those commonly asked by patients in a polyclinic setting at our hospital. A more comprehensive evaluation of the AI’s ability to answer questions could have been achieved by designing a multicenter study, incorporating a broader range of questions obtained from diverse patient populations across different institutions. Although the second and third groups of questions were meticulously prepared to cover both general and advanced aspects of autoimmune hepatitis, they may still fall short of capturing the full spectrum of clinical variability and complexity encountered in hepatology practice.

The evaluation of responses was limited to accuracy and completeness. Other critical aspects—such as clinical relevance, consistency, and applicability in real-world scenarios—were not systematically assessed. Furthermore, including a greater number of case-based questions could have provided deeper insight into the AI’s utility in managing complex clinical situations.

This study was conducted using the 2024 version of ChatGPT. As AI models evolve over time, response variability is expected across different versions. Thus, the findings represent a time-specific evaluation.

Despite these advanced capabilities, AI tools currently lack the necessary competence to independently diagnose diseases and make treatment decisions. Furthermore, while the treatment recommendations they provide are generally valid, they do not yet possess the ability to make patient-specific decisions that physicians routinely apply in daily practice. In light of this, we can state that the ChatGPT application is not a tool to replace doctors in making diagnostic and treatment decisions, but rather serves as a tool to assist doctors by facilitating access to information in their everyday practice.

Conclusion

ChatGPT provided generally accurate responses to questions regarding AIH. While it demonstrated higher accuracy in answering general questions about the disease, the accuracy decreased when more detailed questions about diagnosis and treatment were posed. However, when the same questions were asked again after 14 days, ChatGPT responded with improved accuracy and correctly answering all five case-based questions. This outcome is noteworthy as it highlights the tendency ChatGPT to produce more refined and consistent responses over repeated evaluations. Our findings suggest that ChatGPT may play a greater role in patient education and in supporting physicians in disease management.

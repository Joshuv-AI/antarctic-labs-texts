Efficacy and safety of traditional open surgery versus radiofrequency ablation for great saphenous varicose veins: a retrospective comparative study
Authors: Yi Sun, Mingfu Hu, Shanhu Huang, Huijing Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to compare the clinical efficacy and safety of traditional open surgery and radiofrequency ablation in the treatment of great saphenous varicose veins.MethodsA total of 177 patients with great saphenous varicose veins admitted to our hospital between January 2023 and December 2024 were retrospectively selected. According to the treatment methods, they were divided into the traditional open surgery group (TG, n = 85) and the radiofrequency ablation group (RG, n = 92). The surgical-related indicators, postoperative pain severity, quality of life score, incidence of complications, clinical efficacy, and postoperative recovery were compared between the two groups.ResultsThe RG exhibited shorter operative time, reduced intraoperative blood loss, smaller incision length, and shorter hospital stay compared with the TG (P < 0.05). The RG showed lower VAS pain scores at each time point after surgery than the TG (P < 0.05). At 1, 3, and 6 months after surgery, the AVVQ quality of life scores of the RG were lower than those of the TG (P < 0.05). The RG exhibited lower overall incidence of complications than the TG (P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in the postoperative venous closure rate and recurrence rate between the two groups (P > 0.05). The RG exhibited shorter postoperative ambulation time, wound healing time, and compression stocking wearing time compared with the TG (P < 0.05). Bed charges, nursing fees, and medication costs were lower in the RG (P < 0.05).ConclusionRadiofrequency ablation for great saphenous varicose veins is associated with several advantages, including reduced surgical trauma, faster recovery, less pain, and a lower incidence of complications. During the 6-month follow-up period, its clinical efficacy was comparable to that of the traditional open surgery, while showing potential advantages in cosmetic outcomes. Background

Great saphenous varicose veins are one of the most common diseases in vascular surgery. They are mainly caused by venous valve incompetence, which leads to venous return disorder and increased venous pressure, and subsequently causes venous tortuosity and dilation. Epidemiological studies have shown that the prevalence of great saphenous varicose veins in adults is approximately 10% to 15%, and the incidence gradually increases with age and is higher in women than in men [1, 2]. Great saphenous varicose veins not only affect the appearance of the lower limbs but may also cause symptoms such as soreness, swelling, pain, and edema. In severe cases, skin pigmentation, eczema, lipodermatosclerosis, or even ulcer formation may occur, significantly impairing quality of life [3]. In addition, patients with varicose veins are at risk of developing superficial vein thrombophlebitis and deep vein thrombosis, which may lead to serious consequences if not treated in time [4].

Traditional open surgery is the classic method for treating great saphenous varicose veins. It mainly includes high ligation of the great saphenous vein, stripping of the main trunk, and excision of varicose venous mass. This procedure has been verified through long-term clinical practice and has the advantages of clear efficacy and low recurrence rate. It has long been regarded as the gold standard for the treatment of great saphenous varicose veins [5]. However, traditional open surgery has several limitations, including large surgical trauma, significant postoperative pain, prolonged recovery time, and unaesthetic incision scars. Some patients may also experience postoperative complications such as incision infection, subcutaneous hematoma, and sensory abnormalities [6]. With increasing patient demands for surgical aesthetics and comfort, the limitations of traditional open surgery have become increasingly apparent.

In recent years, with the rapid development of minimally invasive techniques, radiofrequency ablation, as an emerging minimally invasive treatment method, has gradually been applied in the treatment of great saphenous varicose veins. Radiofrequency ablation achieves functional elimination of diseased veins through the thermal effects generated by radiofrequency energy, causing contraction of collagen fibers in the venous wall and closure of the venous lumen [7]. This technique has the advantages of minimal trauma, rapid postoperative recovery, and good cosmetic results, and has become the mainstream method for the treatment of great saphenous varicose veins in Europe and the United States [8]. In theory, radiofrequency ablation involves inserting a catheter into the venous lumen percutaneously under ultrasound guidance, avoiding the large incisions and vein stripping procedures of traditional surgery. This can significantly reduce surgical trauma and postoperative complications. Furthermore, since the procedure can be completed under local anesthesia or mild sedation, it is well-tolerated by patients and facilitates fast recovery after surgery [9, 10]. However, there are relatively few comparative studies on radiofrequency ablation and traditional open surgery in China. The differences between the two procedures in terms of clinical efficacy, safety, and cosmetic outcomes need to be further verified.

In this context, we performed a retrospective controlled study to systematically compare traditional open surgery with radiofrequency ablation in the treatment of great saphenous varicose veins. Surgical-related indicators, postoperative pain intensity, quality of life, clinical efficacy, complication rates, and recovery were evaluated. The aim was to provide a scientific basis for the selection of clinical treatment methods, optimize treatment strategies for patients with great saphenous varicose veins, and improve patient prognosis and quality of life.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective non-randomized controlled study was approved by the ethics committee of Yongjia County Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital and complied with the requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki. All the patients and their families have signed informed consent. Patients with great saphenous varicose veins hospitalized in our hospital between January 2023 and December 2024 were screened using the hospital medical record system.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age 18–75 years; (2) Diagnosed with primary great saphenous varicose veins by clinical examination and color Doppler ultrasound; (3) CEAP classification C2-C4; (4) Meeting surgical indications and scheduled for surgical treatment; (5) Undergoing surgical treatment for the first time with no previous history of lower extremity venous surgery; (6) Complete clinical data and follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with secondary varicose veins; (2) Patients with deep vein thrombosis or deep venous valve incompetence; (3) Patients with severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal dysfunction; (4) Coagulation disorders or current use of anticoagulants; (5) Pregnant or lactating women; (6) Patients with lower-extremity arteriosclerosis obliterans; (7) Patients allergic to local anesthetic drugs; (8) Patients with psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairment who were unable to cooperate with follow-up; (9) Patients with active skin infections or ulcers of the lower extremities.

After screening according to the above criteria, a total of 177 patients were included. Among them, 85 patients underwent traditional open surgery and were assigned to the traditional open surgery group (TG), while 92 patients underwent radiofrequency ablation were assigned to the radiofrequency ablation group (RG).

The selection of surgical approach was determined based on a comprehensive clinical assessment, including patient preference, severity of venous lesions, vein diameter, and anatomical characteristics, rather than by random allocation. This study was conducted using a per-protocol analysis and included patients treated between January 2023 and December 2024. No major changes were made to the surgical protocols for either group during the study period. All 177 patients completed the 6-month follow-up, with no patients lost to follow-up.

Surgical methods

Patients in the TG underwent combined spinal-epidural anesthesia or continuous epidural anesthesia. The surgical procedures were performed as follows: (1) A transverse incision of approximately 3–4 cm in length was made about 2 cm below the inguinal ligament. The great saphenous vein and its tributaries were dissected and exposed. High ligation of the great saphenous vein at the saphenofemoral junction was performed, followed by ligation of all tributaries. (2) An incision of approximately 2 cm was made above the medial malleolus to expose and cut the main trunk of the great saphenous vein. (3) A vein stripper was inserted from the groin incision and advanced distally to the medial malleolus incision. After connecting the veins, the main trunk of the great saphenous vein was stripped from proximal to distal. (4) Prominent varicose venous masses were managed with punctiform stripping or mini-incision excision. (5) At the end of the procedure, the incision was irrigated, sutured in layers, and dressed with compressive bandage. Patients wore medical compression stockings.

Patients in the RG underwent local anesthesia or mild sedation. The surgical procedures were performed as follows: (1) Under ultrasound guidance, the great saphenous vein was punctured below the knee joint. A guidewire was inserted, and a vascular sheath was placed along the guidewire. (2) A radiofrequency catheter was inserted along the vascular sheath. Under ultrasound guidance, the tip of the catheter was positioned approximately 2 cm below the saphenofemoral junction. (3) Under ultrasound guidance, a tumescent anesthesia solution (500 mL normal saline, 20 mL lidocaine, and 0.5 mg epinephrine) was injected around the great saphenous vein to form a fluid sonolucent area encasing the vein, providing both thermal insulation and compression. (4) Radiofrequency energy was applied, with the temperature setting at 120 °C. The catheter was gradually withdrawn at a rate of 1 cm every 7 s to achieve closure of the main trunk of the great saphenous vein. (5) Prominent varicose venous masses were managed with foam sclerotherapy or laser/radiofrequency ablation. (6) At the end of the procedure, the catheter and vascular sheath were removed, and hemostasis was achieved at the puncture site without suturing. A compressive dressing was applied. Patients wore medical compression stockings.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome measures

(1) Surgery-related indicators: Surgery-related indicators, including operative time, intraoperative blood loss, incision length, and hospital stay, were evaluated. (2) Postoperative pain assessment: The Visual Analog Scale (VAS) was used to evaluate the severity of pain before surgery, at 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 1 week after surgery. A score of 0 indicated no pain and 10 indicated severe pain. (3) Quality of life assessment: The Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire (AVVQ) [11] was used to evaluate the quality of life before surgery, at 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months after surgery, with a total score ranging from 0 to 100. Lower scores indicated better the quality of life.

Secondary outcome measures

(1) Baseline data: The baseline data of the patients, such as age, sex, body mass index (BMI), disease duration, CEAP classification, and distribution of affected limbs were recorded. (2) Clinical efficacy: The venous closure rates at 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months after surgery were evaluated by color Doppler ultrasound examination, and the recurrence during the follow-up period was recorded. (3) Complications: The complications such as incision infection, subcutaneous hematoma, superficial vein thrombophlebitis, deep vein thrombosis, nerve injury, and skin burns were recorded. (4) Postoperative recovery indicators: The postoperative recovery indicators, including postoperative ambulation time, wound healing time, and compression stocking wearing time, were assessed. (5) Medical expenses and resource utilization: The hospitalization expenses, consumable material costs, and bed charges of the two groups were recorded.

All patients underwent routine color Doppler ultrasonography at each follow-up visit, regardless of the presence or absence of clinical symptoms, to assess venous closure status and the occurrence of deep vein thrombosis. The diagnostic criteria for complications were defined as follows: incision infection was defined as incision redness, swelling, or exudation accompanied by positive bacterial culture results or clinically confirmed infection; subcutaneous hematoma was defined as subcutaneous blood accumulation confirmed by ultrasonography or clinical examination; deep vein thrombosis was diagnosed by color Doppler ultrasonography according to established diagnostic criteria (non-compressible venous segment); nerve injury was defined as postoperative hypoesthesia, numbness, or paresthesia in the corresponding area; and skin burns were defined as thermal skin injuries caused by radiofrequency catheter energy and were recorded as superficial (epidermal/superficial dermal) or deep burns according to injury depth.

Follow-up methods

All patients completed regular follow-up after surgery. The follow-up time points were 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months after surgery. Follow-up was conducted via outpatient visits and telephone follow-up. During outpatient follow-up, patients underwent physical examination and color Doppler ultrasonography to assess venous closure, recurrence, and the occurrence of complications. Relevant scale scores were also assessed.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and comparisons between groups were analyzed using independent-samples t-tests. Categorical data were presented as cases and percentages [n (%)], and comparisons between groups were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Repeated measures data were analyzed using repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The primary estimates were reported with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs). Effect sizes were expressed as Cohen’s d for continuous variables and Cramér’s V for categorical variables.

Discussion

Advantages of radiofrequency ablation in surgical trauma control

The results of this study showed that compared with the TG, the RG had advantages in terms of operative time, intraoperative blood loss, incision length, and hospital stay. This demonstrates the characteristics of radiofrequency ablation as a minimally invasive technique. Traditional open surgery requires large incisions in the groin and inner side of the lower leg to expose and strip the main trunk of the great saphenous vein and excise the varicose venous masses in segments, resulting in greater surgical trauma [11]. Radiofrequency ablation only requires a pinhole-sized puncture point below the knee joint, where thermal energy is delivered to achieve venous wall contraction and closure, thereby reducing surgical trauma [12]. The shortened operative time not only reduced the patient’s anesthesia exposure time but also improved the efficiency of operating room utilization. Reduced intraoperative blood loss may lower the risk of postoperative hematoma formation and promote postoperative recovery. The difference in incision length directly reflects the significant difference in the degree of trauma between the two procedures. The tiny puncture site used in radiofrequency ablation heals rapidly and results in minimal scarring, demonstrating certain advantages in cosmetic outcomes [13]. The shortening of hospital stay also aligns with concept of enhanced recovery after surgery.

Advantage of radiofrequency ablation in postoperative pain management

In this study, the VAS scores of the RG at each time point after surgery were lower than those of the TG. This finding is closely related to the minimally invasive nature of radiofrequency ablation. Previous studies have indicated that traditional open surgery requires a large incision, extensive tissue dissection, and stripping of the main trunk of the vein, which inevitably causes extensive tissue damage and nerve traction, resulting in significant postoperative pain [14]. In contrast, radiofrequency ablation achieves venous closure through endoluminal thermal ablation, avoiding the invasive procedure of vein stripping. The application of tumescent anesthesia solution not only provides good intraoperative analgesia but also continues to exert analgesic effects in the early postoperative period [15]. The limited extent of tissue damage and mild inflammatory response may contribute to reduced postoperative pain severity. Effective pain control facilitates early ambulation, thereby potentially reducing the risk of deep vein thrombosis and promoting postoperative recovery [16].

Improvement of radiofrequency ablation on quality of life

In this study, the AVVQ was used to assess the postoperative quality of life of patients. The results showed that the quality of life scores of the RG at 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months after surgery were lower than those of the TG. Radiofrequency ablation involves minimal trauma and rapid recovery, enabling patients to return to work and normal activities faster [17]. Reduced postoperative pain and fewer complications may substantially alleviate physical discomfort. In addition, compared with traditional surgery, radiofrequency ablation provides better cosmetic outcomes. The tiny puncture sites typically heal with minimal visible scarring, which may be particularly important for younger female patients [18]. Furthermore, the use of local anesthesia or mild sedation avoids the discomfort associated with general anesthesia. In the present study, quality of life scores in both groups continued to improve over time after surgery, indicating a sustained beneficial effect of surgical treatment on the quality of life of patients with great saphenous varicose veins. Moreover, the RG consistently demonstrated higher quality of life scores than the TG at all follow-up time points [19].

Clinical efficacy and safety evaluation of radiofrequency ablation

During the 6-month follow-up period, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in postoperative venous closure rates or recurrence rates, suggesting that the short-term efficacy of radiofrequency ablation was comparable to that of traditional open surgery. These findings are consistent with previous studies. Radiofrequency ablation induces thermal denaturation and contraction of collagen fibers in the venous wall, leading to venous lumen closure [20]. From a pathophysiological perspective, this process achieves a therapeutic effect comparable to surgical excision. With continuous improvements in radiofrequency technology and accumulating operator experience, the success rate of radiofrequency ablation may be further improved. In terms of safety, the overall incidence of complications in the RG was lower than that in the TG, mainly due to a reduced incidence of complications such as incision infection and subcutaneous hematoma. This is closely related to the minimally invasive nature of radiofrequency ablation [21, 22]. It should also be noted that radiofrequency ablation is associated with procedure-specific complications, such as skin burns and nerve injury. However, with increasing operator experience and standardized use of tumescent anesthesia, these complications can be prevented or reduced [23].

Mechanisms of rapid postoperative recovery after radiofrequency ablation

The concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) has received increasing attention in modern surgery. Many characteristics of radiofrequency ablation are highly consistent with the concept of ERAS. This study showed that the RG was better than the TG in terms of recovery indicators such as postoperative ambulation time, wound healing time, and compression stocking wearing time, which coincides with the concept of ERAS. This should be attributed to the following reasons. First, the minimally invasive nature of radiofrequency ablation results in mild postoperative pain, enabling earlier ambulation. Early ambulation not only promotes blood circulation and reduces the risk of deep vein thrombosis, but also helps improve patients’ psychological state and enhance their confidence in recovery [24]. Second, radiofrequency ablation has minimal or no incisions, avoiding the time required for incision healing in traditional surgery. Patients do not need to worry about incision dehiscence and can move more freely. Third, due to the small surgical trauma and the limited range of tissue damage, the postoperative inflammatory response is mild, and the patients recover fast. This also shortens the wearing time of compression stockings and alleviates the discomfort and inconvenience caused by long-term wearing of compression stockings for patients [25]. Finally, radiofrequency ablation is performed under local anesthesia or mild sedation, avoiding the effects of general anesthesia on patients. Patients can resume oral intake immediately after the procedure without experiencing discomfort such as nausea and vomiting after general anesthesia. This is also an important factor promoting enhanced postoperative recovery. From the perspective of inpatient management, radiofrequency ablation can be performed under the day-surgery model, and patients can be discharged within a few hours after surgery. This not only greatly saves medical resources but also helps reduce the economic burden on patients. This is also fully evidenced in the bed charges, nursing fees, and medication costs of the two groups of patients in the study.

Clinical implication, research limitations, and future research prospects

This comparative study suggests that radiofrequency ablation may have advantages over traditional open surgery in multiple aspects, providing preliminary evidence to support its clinical application. From the patient perspective, radiofrequency ablation is characterized by minimal invasiveness, rapid recovery, and good cosmetic outcomes, aligning well with modern expectations for medical services. From the healthcare provider perspective, shorter hospital stay and faster turnover may improve the efficiency of bed utilization. From the healthcare payer perspective, although the cost of consumable materials is relatively higher, when considering factors such as reduced hospital stays, fewer complications, and earlier return to work, the overall healthcare costs do not increase.

However, this study has the some limitations. First, this study was a retrospective, single-center, non-randomized study. Patient allocation was based on clinical judgment rather than randomization, which may introduce selection bias. Additionally, no propensity score matching or multivariable regression analyses were performed to adjust for confounding factors, which constitutes a key methodological limitation. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted with caution and cannot be equated with to the level of evidence generated by prospective randomized controlled trials. Second, the sample size was relatively limited, and the follow-up period was only 6 months. Given that varicose veins may be associated with late recurrence, longer follow-up is necessary to more accurately assess long-term outcomes. Third, no detailed cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted in this study. Although radiofrequency ablation showed advantages in multiple aspects, its health economic value remains to be further evaluated in dedicated studies.

To address the aforementioned limitations, future research should consider conducting multicenter, large-sample prospective randomized controlled trials, extending the follow-up period to more than two years, and performing subgroup analyses for patients of different CEAP classifications and age groups to obtain more reliable and comprehensive evidence. In addition, a systematic cost-effectiveness analysis should be carried out to provide references for the formulation of medical insurance policies. Finally, treatment strategy of radiofrequency ablation combined with other minimally invasive techniques should be explored to optimize treatment plans and improve efficacy.

Conclusions

Radiofrequency ablation for great saphenous varicose veins is associated with several advantages, including reduced surgical trauma, faster recovery, less postoperative pain, and a lower incidence of complications. Over a 6-month follow-up period, its clinical efficacy was comparable to that of traditional open surgery, with potential advantages in cosmetic outcomes and quality of life improvement. These findings indicate that radiofrequency ablation is a safe and effective minimally invasive treatment modality that may be considered in clinical practice. However, differences in long-term outcomes and recurrence rates require further validation in studies with extended follow-up.

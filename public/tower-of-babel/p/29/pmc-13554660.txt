Analytical and numerical investigations, data-driven modeling via an analytical–machine learning approach, and chaotic analysis of a nonlinear fractional soliton neuron model
Authors: Md Ruhul Amin, Rabeya Akter, Khalifa Mohammad Helal, Mohammad Safi Ullah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates a nonlinear fractional soliton neuron model that arises in fluid mechanics, nonlinear dynamics, mathematical physics, engineering, neuroscience, plasma physics, and related scientific fields. An efficient mapping approach is employed after transforming the governing nonlinear partial differential equation into an ordinary differential equation through a suitable wave transformation. A machine learning approach is also applied for data-driven analysis of the obtained solution. These two proposed methods yield a variety of exact analytical solutions, including lump, local breather, periodic, anti-kink, multiple bright-dark breather, singular soliton, bright soliton, and kink waves. Their propagation characteristics are illustrated using three-dimensional, two-dimensional, contour, polar, and surface-of-revolution plots generated in Maple. Stability properties of the model and the obtained solutions are also examined. Furthermore, nonlinear dynamics are explored through MATLAB-based chaos diagnostics, including bifurcation diagrams, strange attractors, recurrence plots, fractal dimensions, phase portraits, basins of attraction, return maps, power spectra, time series, and multistability. The results demonstrate that variations in the amplitude and frequency of external forcing significantly influence the system’s dynamical behavior.Keywords: neuron model, fractal dimensions, bifurcation, chaotic attractor, machine learning approach, multistability

1. Introduction

Nonlinear Partial Differential Equations (NLPDEs) are commonly studied by scholars employing both theoretical and numerical methods, with the derivation of precise outcomes being a crucial aspect in realizing nonlinear physical processes. In many domains, including financial modeling, population dynamics, general relativity, neural signal propagation in the brain, and fluid dynamics, NLPDEs are frequently applied to explain nonlinear systems.1–5 Several prominent NLPDEs, such as dual-mode nonlinear Schrödinger (DMNLS) equation, 6 Zoomeron equation, 7 extended Boussinesq equations, 8 the Chen-Lee-Liu model, 9 the Manakov model, 10 the Jimbo-Miwa model, 11 the Nizhnik-Novikov-Veselov equation, 12 [Konopelchenko-Dubrovsky Equation, 13 Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel Equation, 14 Kadomtsev-Petviashvili Equation, 15 Focus Equation 16 and the Fokas-Lenells system that demonstrate soliton characteristics, 17 Zabolotskaya-Khokhlov model, 18 and so on. A more precise study of the physical techniques governed by these equations is now feasible thanks to analytical and numerical investigations. Researchers are depending more on and using more complex mathematical methods to get precise answers. Ongoing research demonstrates the enduring significance of the collaboration between analytical and numerical investigations in this area. 19 Solitary waves are developing and are more important in theoretical and practical research to explain nonlinear equations.20,21 These concepts suggest deeper perceptions of the stability and oscillations of physical phenomena. Including these uses, the soliton neuron model provides an essential mathematical foundation for realizing the transmission of action potentials and the strategy of neural networks. 22 Some well-known methods are used to solve the NLPDEs such as ϕ6 model expansion method,23,24 the exponential function technique, 25 Hirota bilinear method, 26 The improved Kudryashov algorithm, 27 unified technique, 28 the extended auxiliary equation mapping technique, 29 Khater II method, 30 (G′G)− expansion method,31,32 (G′G2)− expansion technique, 33 the Darboux transformation, 34 Sine-Gordon expansion technique, 35 and homotopy perturbation technique 36 are few examples. The neuron model, which offers a solid numerical explanation of action strong transmission and neural network activity, is a substantial theoretical framework for characterizing neuronal dynamics. In this article, we apply the mapping approach37,38 to explain the neuron model. This method works specifically well for investigating the spatiotemporal evolution of electrochemical signaling in neuronal phenomena as well as the collective dynamics of neural assemblies. The main reason for choosing this method primarily because it provides a methodical and applicable way to raise many families of precise analytical solutions, such as analytical solutions, including lump, local breather, periodic, anti-kink, multiple bright-dark breather, singular soliton, bright soliton, and kink waves, from a single formulation. Compared to numerous previous approaches, we investigate the chaotic dynamics involving different perturbation assumptions such as sine, cosine and exp function, making it especially appropriate for the nonlinear nature of the current model. Investigating the different soliton solutions, stability, chaotic dynamics and multistability analysis using different perturbations of this given model is the main goal of this work. Rahaman and his colleagues studied this stated model by using the ϕ6 model expansion method and obtained bright, dark, breather lump, periodic, singular, and solitary wave solutions. 39 Periodic, super-periodic, and quasi-periodic outcomes are obtained by Elmandouh 19 employing the Hamiltonian dynamical system. Additionally, he also discussed bifurcation and chaotic analysis of the governing model. Some soliton outcomes and their implementation are also found from the neuron models in Refs. 39 and 40. It employs a thermodynamic theory to explain how neurological heartbeat signals are transferred along axons. Solitons are the signals that are traveling across the cell membrane. Numerous technologies, including actuators, sensors, magneto-optic waveguides, optical couplers, and metamaterials, exploit this soliton result.41,42 We are inspired to initiate new attributes for the stated model after seeing the aforementioned research analytically and numerically by using a mapping approach. From our earlier assumptions, no one has ever done this work before.

4.2. Multilayer perceptron regressor neural network

The multilayer perceptron (MLP) regression neural network has been studied to verify the dynamics of the derived solutions by using analytical approaches. The network finalizes with a single neuron output layer to output the continuous regression output using linear activation.

Adam optimizers with 0.1 learning rate are applied for efficient gradient-based optimization. He initialization is utilized for stable weight initialization for hidden layers and linear activation in the output layer. The model employs forward propagation for estimates, Mean squared error loss, back propagation to update weights through gradient descent over the course of 1500 epochs of training, and reports progress regularly. After training, the model then predicts over the complete spatial-temporal grid and reverses the normalization scaling to achieve final results similar to the initial target function values. MATLAB 2026a has been used in the development and training of machine learning models. MLP is a universal function approximator that can learn complex nonlinear input-output relationships directly from data without the need for an analytical model. Its best feature is that it can make predictions very fast, almost instantly after training. That makes it useful for real-time applications, like control systems or digital twins. It is outstanding in handling high-dimensional, noisy, or incomplete data sets where standard numerical methods are ineffective or do not converge. MLPs are therefore exceptionally versatile because the same design can be used for diverse regression and classification problems without changing the underlying solver. Therefore, they are a great way to improve effective surrogate models when you have to run an expensive simulation or physical process repeatedly. 47 To quantitatively evaluate the predictive performance of the machine-learning model, we use several standard error measures, including.

6.2. Strange attractor

10.2. Assumptions and limitations of mapping method

The Jacobian of the mapping method (elliptic function (sn, cn, dn)) can identify discrete waves, periodic waves, and a combination of both categories of waves at once. The mapping method can be used for several nonlinear differential equations; however, other techniques are limited only to specific restrictions. This method can help in the exact computation of dispersion relations, phase velocity, and soliton amplitude. Jacobian elliptic functions accelerate the fast convergence of the solution sequence and reduce numerical errors due to their link with hyperbolic and trigonometric functions. In order to get the exact solution, this method requires some parameters to be decided. The nature of the solution can change, depending on how much the value of those parameters is changed. The reason why the mapping method is so challenging to use is that it includes Jacobian elliptic function expansion. So, it is computationally challenging to solve the higher-order nonlinear equations with it. Regardless of the fact that there is an exact solution, it is often hard to check whether the solution is stable or not.

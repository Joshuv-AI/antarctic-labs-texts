A quantitative model for predicting sedimentary-system apexes based on modern fluvial grain-size data and its application to paleogeographic reconstruction
Authors: Xianghui Zhang, Changmin Zhang, Qinghao Meng, Wenjun Fu, Jiale Liu, Mengjiao Dou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDetermining the location of the apex of a sedimentary system can provide important spatial constraints on paleogeographic reconstructions. To solve the difficult problem of predicting the apex of subsurface sedimentary systems, this study establishes a quantitative mathematical model of grain size with distance from the apex of the modern sedimentary system by statistically analyzing the grain size of gravel in the riverbed of the Shule River. To evaluate the accuracy of the mathematical model, the research takes the Golmud River depositional system in the Qaidam Basin and the Dinar River depositional system in the Tarim Basin as examples, where the position of each actual apex is known for the two systems. The estimated apex position in the Golmud River depositional system is 4.79 km from the actual apex position, with an accuracy rate of 95.3%. In the Dinar River depositional system, the estimated apex position is 3.88 km from the actual apex position, with an accuracy rate of 94.8%. Based on the testing of these two examples, the results of the evaluation largely verify the accuracy of the mathematical model. The mathematical model was applied to the geological record. The research takes the Jurassic Karazar Formation in the southern margin of the Junggar Basin in western China as the target. Based on the mathematical model and combines the conglomerate grain size from the Nananjihai River outcrop of the Karazar Formation and conglomerate grain size from the core of Tianan 1 well, and completes the prediction of apex position of the sedimentary system of the Karazar Formation. Results have helped to give better geographical constraints on the system and apex location of the southern margin in the Junggar basin.Keywords: Sedimentary system, Mathematical model, Sediment grain size, Geological record, ConglomeratesSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Understanding the apex position of a sedimentary system is critical for constraining sediment routing pathways, reconstructing paleogeography, and identifying sediment entry points into sedimentary basins1,2. In particular, accurate determination of apex location provides key spatial constraints on the scale and geometry of sediment dispersal systems, which is essential for predicting the distribution of coarse-grained depositional bodies and associated reservoirs. To date, prediction of sedimentary-system apexes has primarily relied on lithofacies mapping and paleocurrent analysis3. While these approaches are effective in well-exposed outcrop settings, they are inherently limited in subsurface systems. In such contexts, the available data are commonly restricted by sparse well control, incomplete or ambiguous seismic coverage, limited outcrop exposure, and post-depositional tectonic modification4,5. As a result, conventional methods typically provide only qualitative or semi-quantitative interpretations, such as general provenance directions or approximate depositional entry zones, but rarely yield robust quantitative estimates of apex location or transport distance. This limitation represents a major challenge for predicting the scale and architecture of subsurface sedimentary systems, particularly in coarse-grained alluvial-fan and fluvial-fan environments.

One of the most fundamental characteristics of sediment transport systems is downstream fining, whereby sediment grain size systematically decreases from the apex to the distal toe due to the combined effects of hydraulic sorting and particle abrasion6–8. Based on this principle, numerous studies have developed quantitative relationships between sediment grain size and transport distance9–11. Early work by Sternberg6 introduced the abrasion law to describe grain-size reduction during transport, while subsequent experimental and field-based studiesdemonstrated that sediment grain size commonly follows a negative exponential decay with transport distance12–15. Since the mid-20th century, exponential, linear, and power-law models have been widely applied to modern sedimentary systems9,16–18. More recently, various empirical formulations have been proposed to characterize grain-size trends19–21.

Despite these advances, a critical gap remains: existing grain-size–distance relationships have been predominantly applied to modern surface systems, whereas their application to subsurface sedimentary systems—particularly for quantitative prediction of system scale and apex location—remains limited. Bridging this gap requires not only robust quantitative models, but also a clear framework for integrating grain-size-derived transport distances with geological constraints such as stratigraphic architecture and tectonic reconstruction.

In this study, we address this challenge by establishing a quantitative exponential relationship between sediment grain size and transport distance using the modern Shule River sedimentary system as an analogue. Grain-size data are systematically measured along the river profile to derive a physically interpretable model. The robustness of the model is evaluated using two independent modern fluvial-fan systems with known apex positions (Golmud River and Dinar River). The validated model is then applied to the Jurassic Kalazha Formation at the southern margin of the Junggar Basin, where grain-size data from outcrops and subsurface cores are integrated with tectonically restored spatial relationships to constrain apex position. The study provides a quantitative framework for predicting apex locations in subsurface sedimentary systems, thereby improving paleogeographic reconstruction and offering practical implications for the prediction of coarse-grained reservoir distribution in data-limited exploration settings.

Mathematical principles

Downstream fining is attributed to size-selective sediment sorting22,23 and the mechanical breakdown (abrasion) of particles24,25. The effect of abrasion on sediment size refinement is mainly manifested in the process of sediment transport to the lower reaches of the river, where larger particles such as gravel and pebbles collide with each other and rub against each other in the process of movement, resulting in their gradual fragmentation into smaller particles (Fig. 1). This process not only reduces the size of the particles, but also changes their shape and surface properties, making the particles tend to be rounded. The effect of sorting on sediment size refinement is mainly the process by which rivers separate particles of different sizes and densities according to the strength of hydrodynamic conditions (Fig. 1). Under stronger hydrodynamic conditions, larger and heavier particles are deposited, while smaller and lighter particles are carried away by the water flow. As the river flows downstream, the hydrodynamic conditions weaken and finer sediments are gradually deposited, thus leading to a gradual decrease in sediment grain size along the upstream to downstream direction (Fig. 1a).

Among the common mathematical models for the relationship between sediment grain size and transport distance include one-dimensional linear fitting method, the power function fitting method, and the exponential fitting method.

The one-dimensional linear function mathematical model reflects a constant rate of decrease in sediment grain size (Fig. 2). In this method, the prediction is good for short-range transport (less than 10 km), but for medium and long distance transport (more than 10 km), the constant rate of gravel reduction considered by this fitting equation is not reasonable because of the gradual weakening of the hydrodynamic conditions along the apex to the toe of the transport (Fig. 2).

By analyzing the causes of sediment refinement and the equations for quantitatively characterizing sediment refinement, this work takes the commonly used exponential mathematical model as the basis, and uses the Shule River sedimentary system in northwestern China as a case study (Fig. 1b), and establishes the mathematical model by measuring the grain size of the sediment at different locations in the Shule River, and then determining the values of D0 and a in Eq. (3). In the exponential model, the parameters have clear sedimentological significance. The coefficient D0 represents the initial average grain size of gravels at the apex of the Shule River sedimentary system, reflecting the source characteristics and initial depositional energy conditions. The parameter represents the grain-size attenuation rate, which is controlled by the combined effects of hydrodynamic sorting and particle abrasion during sediment transport in the study area. Clarifying the physical meaning of these parameters improves the geological interpretability of the model. This formulation links empirical grain-size trends with physical sediment transport processes, thereby improving both the interpretability and applicability of the model.

Study area and data used

The Shule River-Fluvial fan is located in the western part of the Hexi Corridor in China, between 96°6′–97°24′ E longitude and 39°56′–40°36′ N latitude (Fig. 3). The Shule River originates from the Shulenan Mountain of the Qilian Mountains, with a total length of 620 km, and is a perennial river with the largest catchment area in the western section of the Qilian Mountains28. After crossing the western end of the northern Qilian Mountains, the Shule River carries a large amount of clastic material to form a large alluvial fan on the north side of the Arjin (Fig. 3), and the Shule River has a total length of about 120 km within the alluvial fan29,30.

During the research, we measured the gravels of the Shule River bed through modern sedimentary investigations, and a total of five test points (G1-G5) where gravel grain size was evident were selected (Fig. 3), and a cumulative total of 1,862 gravel grain size parameters were measured (Fig. 4). Among the 1,862 gravel grain size parameters, the grain size intervals were mainly distributed in 10–100 mm, with the maximum grain size of 122.77 mm, the minimum grain size of 3.07 mm, the average grain size of 22.12 mm, and the median grain size of 18.68 mm (Figs. 3, 4). G1 measured a total of 166 gravel grain size parameters, with the largest grain size of 122.77 mm, the smallest grain size of 14.69 mm, the average grain size of 41.93 mm, and the median grain size of 37.26 mm (Fig. 4). A limited number of representative control points along the main sediment transport pathway is sufficient to constrain apex location and meet the accuracy requirements of the model.

Golmud River-Fluvial Fan

The actual apex of the Golmud River-Fluvial Fan is located at 94°45′50.66″E, 36° 9′40.56″N (Table 1), the radius of the fan is about 102.15 km, and the area is about 4.09 × 103 km2 (Fig. 6). The fan body spreads in a north-south direction, and from the south to the north, the elevation gradually decreases from 3200 m to about 2800 m. The slope of the fan is 6% with bare gravel and sparse vegetation on the surface (Fig. 6a). Above the apex is the drainage basin, and the Golmud River follows the apex down into the Qaidam Basin. Within the range of the Golmud River-Fluvial Fan, the proximal elevation ranges from 3000 to 3100 m, and the sediments are composed of pebble gravels and boulders, while the medial elevation ranges from 3000 to 2800 m, which mainly consists of sandy gravels and the terrain is relatively flat, and it is the main part of the Gobi Desert. At the distal, the elevation is around 2800 m, and the sediment consists of sandy and silty clay31,32.

Through modern depositional investigations, we included a total of two riverbed sediment grain-size measurements within the fan body (Fig. 6; Table 1). Based on the characteristics of grain size change, we can first determine the direction of sediment transport from south to north. Where point 1 (P1) is located at 94°48′9.08″E, 36°20′21.13″N and the distance from the actual apex is 20.08 km (Fig. 6a; Table 1), the estimated apex distance from point 1 can be calculated as 26.34 km using Eq. (5) (Fig. 6b; Table 1). Also point 2 (P2) is located at 94°53′32.03″E, 36°28′38.66″N and the distance from the actual apex is 36.98 km (Fig. 6a; Table 1), the estimated apex distance from point 2 can be calculated as 41.18 km using Eq. (5) (Fig. 6b; Table 1). In the process of predicting the apex, we can measure the distance between point 1 and point 2 is 17.33 km, and meanwhile draw a circle with the estimated apex distance between point 1 and point 2 as the radius (Fig. 6b). We make a ray along point 2 towards point 1, intersect with the two circles, and seek the midpoint of the intersecting length, which is the best estimated apex (Fig. 6b). We also compared the estimated apex with the actual apex of the Golmud river-Fluvial fan, the distance between the pair of the apexs is 4.79 km (Fig. 6), which is a 95.3% compliance rate compared to the total length of the fan body.

Dinar River-Fluvial Fan

The length from the apex to the toe of the Dinar River is about 74.65 km, and the area of the fan is about 2.16 × 103 km2 (Fig. 7). The topography of the Dinar River Basin is high in the north and low in the south, from north to south, and the fan consists of alluvial floodplains and alluvial plains, and the elevation of the fan gradually decreases from 1400 m to about 940 m, with the slope of the fan of 9%, and the surface is dominated by gravels and rarely covered with vegetation. The apex of the fan is located at 84° 0′30.86″E, 42° 0′39.19″N (Table 1). Above the apex is the basin of the Dinar River basin in the Southern Tianshan mountains, and the river enters the Tarim Basin from below the apex (Fig. 7). Along the apex, Proximal the width of channel expands rapidly, reaching 400–1500 m, with pebble gravel and boulders dominating the sediments, which is the main part of the Gobi Desert. In the medial part of the fan, the Dinar River is divided into two tributaries, the Karta River and the Hongqiao River, in order from west to east, and the sediment mainly consists of gravel, and the terrain is relatively flat (Fig. 7). At the distal of the fan, the altitude is about 900 m, and the sediment consists of sandy and clay, which is locally eroded by wind, with a tendency towards desertification of the soil33,34.

Similarly, within the Dinar River-Fluvial fan, there are two riverbed sediment grain size measurement points (Fig. 7; Table 1), and based on the characteristics of the grain size change, we can firstly determine the direction of sediment transport from north to south. Among them, point 3 (P3) is located at 84° 7′42.55″E, 41°55′17.36″N, the distance from the actual apex is 14.04 km (Fig. 7a), and the estimated apex distance from point 3 can be calculated as 10.17 km by using Eq. (5) (Fig. 7b). Point 4 (P4) is located at 84°17′21.30″E, 41°46′25.35″N, the distance from the actual apex is 35.14 km (Fig. 7a), and the estimated apex distance from point 2 can be calculated as 36.52 km by using Eq. (5) (Fig. 7b).

During the process of predicting the position of the apex, we can measure the distance between point 3 and point 4 is 21.15 km (Fig. 7). Similarly, we draw a circle with the estimated apex distance between point 3 and point 4 as the radius (Fig. 7b). Meanwhile, we make a ray along point 4 towards point 3, intersect with the two circles, and seek the midpoint of the intersecting length, which is the best estimated apex (Fig. 7b). We also compared the estimated apex with the actual apex of the Dinar river-Fluvial fan (Fig. 7b), the distance between the pair of apexs is 3.88 km, which is a 94.8% compliance rate compared to the total length of the fan body.

Combining the two examples mentioned above (Figs. 6, 7), the equation (Eq. 5) provided in this research has a high accuracy in predicting the location of the apex of a fan sedimentary system. Therefore, we apply this equation to the geological record to predict the location of the apex of the subsurface sedimentary system.

Application to the rock record—the Kalazha formation

In the process of practical application, the Jurassic Kalazha Formation in the southern margin of the Junggar Basin was selected as a case study to predict the apex location of the subsurface sedimentary system (Fig. 8). The Kalazha Formation is characterized by typical alluvial fan deposition dominated by coarse-grained conglomerates with thicknesses exceeding 100 m35–37, which is consistent with the sedimentary characteristics of modern fluvial fan systems such as the Shule River, Golmud River, and Dinar River. Importantly, these systems share several key depositional similarities, including: (1) dominance of coarse clastic input, (2) proximal high-energy depositional conditions, and (3) arid to semi-arid climatic settings. These similarities indicate that grain-size attenuation is governed by comparable hydrodynamic sorting and abrasion processes. Therefore, the exponential model established from modern systems can be reasonably applied to the Kalazha Formation, although differences in geological timescales are acknowledged and discussed below.

Location of the study area and distribution of the sedimentary belt of the Karaza Formation.

In recent years, multiple exploration wells (e.g., Gaotan 1, Hutan 1, Letan 1, and Tianwan 1) in the southern Junggar Basin have yielded significant hydrocarbon discoveries from the Kalazha Formation, confirming the development of coarse-grained clastic reservoirs38,39. However, the spatial distribution of these reservoirs remains poorly constrained, and quantitative prediction of the sedimentary system architecture is still a major challenge for hydrocarbon exploration in this region.Therefore, the method proposed in this study provides a quantitative approach for predicting the apex position of subsurface sedimentary systems, offering important constraints for sedimentary system reconstruction and reservoir distribution prediction in the Kalazha Formation.

The source of data

In this study, the Nananjihai River area was selected as the study site (Fig. 9), and two representative control points—the Nananjihai River outcrop and Well Tianan 1—were used for apex prediction (Figs. 9, 10). The Nananjihai River outcrop is well exposed, with continuous stratigraphic development, and is located on the southern flank of the Nananjihai anticline (Fig. 9). Well Tianan 1 is a key exploratory well in the southern Junggar Basin, drilled to a depth of 8,140 m in 2021, representing the deepest well in the basin (Fig. 10). Grain-size data were collected from both the outcrop and core samples (Tables 2 and 3), forming the basis for quantitative prediction of the apex location of the Kalazha Formation sedimentary system.

Outcrop grain size data collection

Four measurement points (N1–N4) were selected from well-exposed conglomerate intervals in the Nananjihai River outcrop, with vertical spacing of approximately 1–2 m (Fig. 9). Sampling areas of 0.5 m × 0.5 m were defined, and the long axes of clasts were measured to represent grain size. Approximately 100–150 measurements were obtained per sampling point. The results indicate that the mean grain size is 23.14 mm, the median is 22.36 mm, and the maximum is 71.35 mm (Table 2).

Core grain size data collection

In Well Tianan 1, four sampling points (T1–T4) were selected from a 10 m thick conglomerate interval of the Kalazha Formation, with spacing of approximately 1.5 m (Fig. 10). Due to strong cementation, clast long axes were measured from core surfaces within sampling windows of 0.1 m × 0.5 m, with approximately 100 measurements per point. The results show a mean grain size of 7.83 mm, a median of 6.04 mm, and a maximum of 38.57 mm (Table 3).

Compared with the Nananjihai outcrop, the grain sizes in Well Tianan 1 are significantly finer and more concentrated, indicating a more distal depositional position relative to the sediment source.

Distance restoration and temporal constraints

The southern margin of the Junggar Basin has experienced strong tectonic shortening since the Neoproterozoic (Fig. 11). Based on structural restoration, the distance between the Nananjihai outcrop and Well Tianan 1 increased from the present-day 31.00–43.95 km during the depositional period. It should be noted that, unlike modern river systems which represent short-term depositional processes, the Kalazha Formation records sedimentation over geological timescales (on the order of millions of years). However, the selected conglomerate intervals are thick, laterally continuous, and genetically related, indicating deposition under relatively stable sediment supply and transport conditions. Therefore, these sampling points are interpreted to represent a quasi-equilibrium depositional framework within a single sedimentary system, and can be reasonably approximated as representing a quasi-single depositional event for the purpose of apex prediction. Nevertheless, potential long-term environmental variability is acknowledged as a source of uncertainty in the model application

The predicted apex location is further supported by multiple independent geological constraints. First, the inferred apex position is consistent with the regional sediment transport direction indicated by grain-size trends, with coarser deposits in the Nananjihai outcrop and finer deposits in Well Tianan 1, suggesting sediment transport from south to north. Second, previous studies on sedimentary facies distribution in the Kalazha Formation indicate that coarse-grained proximal deposits are concentrated near the southern margin of the basin, which agrees well with the predicted apex location. In addition, the structural framework of the southern Junggar Basin, characterized by thrust belts and progressive northward propagation, provides a geological basis for sediment routing pathways and further supports the inferred apex position. These independent lines of evidence enhance the robustness and geological reliability of the model-based apex prediction.

Conclusion

The prediction of apex locations in sedimentary systems within the geological record remains a challenging problem due to limited data constraints and complex depositional processes. In this study, a quantitative exponential model describing the relationship between sediment grain size and transport distance was established based on the modern Shule River system. Validation using two independent modern fluvial fan systems (Golmud River and Dinar River) demonstrates that the model can effectively predict apex locations, with prediction accuracies exceeding 94%. These results indicate that the model provides a reliable quantitative approach for coarse-grained sedimentary systems under comparable depositional conditions. Application of the model to the Jurassic Kalazha Formation in the southern Junggar Basin shows that the apex position can be constrained within a limited spatial range. The predicted apex is consistent with sedimentary facies distribution, grain-size trends, and the regional structural framework, thereby supporting the geological validity of the results.

Despite these promising results, several limitations should be considered: (1) The applicability of the model is constrained by sedimentary environment, and separate calibration is required for different depositional systems (e.g., alluvial fans, fluvial systems, and deltas). (2) The reliability of apex prediction depends on the number and representativeness of control points; increasing data density can significantly improve prediction accuracy. (3) Long-term geological processes, including tectonic evolution and environmental variability, may introduce uncertainties when applying models derived from modern systems to ancient deposits. Overall, this study demonstrates that grain-size-based quantitative modeling provides an effective and practical tool for apex prediction, offering important constraints for paleogeographic reconstruction and reservoir distribution analysis in subsurface sedimentary systems.

SMS text intervention for uncontrolled hypertension among hypertensive homeless adults in shelter clinics of New York City: protocol for a pragmatic randomised trial study
Authors: Ramin Asgary, Leah Bauder, Rosanna Naderi, Gbenga Ogedegbe
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionUncontrolled hypertension (HTN) is prevalent in persons experiencing homelessness (PEH) and contributes to significant suffering and financial cost. Mobile health approaches such as short messaging service (SMS) texting have led to better control of HTN in the general population. Despite the high utilisation of mobile phones by PEH, SMS texting to support HTN control has not been evaluated among this population. We hypothesise that an SMS testing programme will enhance health communication, information management, outreach and care coordination, and provide behavioural support to address some barriers to HTN management in PEH.Methods and analysisThis study will use a mixed-methods study design to address two objectives: First, it will evaluate, in a randomised controlled trial, the efficacy of a 6-month SMS texting strategy vs an attention control on blood pressure reduction and adherence to medications and clinical appointments in 120 adults PEH with uncontrolled HTN. Outcomes will be measured at 0, 2, 4 and 6 months. Second, it will assess patients’ and providers’ acceptability and experience of SMS texting using semistructured interviews with PEH (n=30) and providers (n=10). The study will be conducted in shelter clinics in New York City in collaboration with community organisations. The primary statistical analysis will be on an intention-to-treat basis. The trial results will be reported as comparative summary statistics (difference in response rate or means) with 95% CIs and in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT). Interviews will be transcribed, coded and analysed using an inductive grounded theory analysis.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at George Washington University. Written consent will be obtained from participants. The findings will be disseminated in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT05187013.Keywords: hypertension, health equity, preventive medicine, primary health care, social medicine

Introduction

Annually, millions of Americans are experiencing homelessness, with more than 600 000 people sleeping in a shelter on most nights.1–3 The majority of the persons experiencing homelessness (PEH) are male with an overwhelming number from racial and ethnic minorities, specifically from the black and Hispanic populations.1 2 Many PEH are entering their fifties and are at risk of developing hypertension (HTN), and a systematic review estimated that 27% of PEH had HTN.1 4–6 While HTN is one of the most common conditions among PEH,6–12 there are limited data on HTN control and the predictors of inadequate blood pressure (BP) control among PEH.8 13 We have documented a rate of 40.1% of uncontrolled BP among hypertensive PEH in New York City’s (NYC) homeless shelters,14 which is in sharp contrast with the rates of 19.6%–24.8% in the general US population.15 PEH lack access to primary care, often suffer from mental health or substance abuse disorders,5 16 and face barriers to therapeutic lifestyle changes (TLCs),9 which complicates their chronic disease management. Age-adjusted mortality rates among PEH are several fold higher than the general population, and cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of death.17 18 Cardiovascular risk factors such as smoking, diabetes mellitus (DM) and HTN are common among PEH, as is substance use.1 4–13 Optimum BP control contributes to a significant CVD risk reduction that can be achieved through tested strategies targeting self-management behaviours and counselling to support adherence to medications, appointments and TLCs that are coordinated with medical services at the community level.19–23

Despite the known efficacy of these interventions, they are not available or accessible to PEH. Mobile Health (mHealth) strategies including short messaging service (SMS) texting have been shown to improve appointment attendance, BP control,24–28 be feasible and acceptable, improve adherence to BP medications,27 28 lower missed medication doses and improve patient confidence in self-management of chronic diseases among racial minorities.28–30 Several phenotypes for medication non-adherence including lack of patient vigilance,31 and strategies to address them at different health system levels have been proposed and tested.32 At the patient level, educational interventions with behavioural support through continued patient contact over several months have been shown to be effective in the treatment of chronic diseases including HTN.32

The mHealth behavioural interventions could be used as a platform to provide behavioural support and improve the healthcare system capacity to apply evidence-based interventions to hypertensive PEH and facilitate effective coordination for HTN management. Considering the mobility of PEH and multiple complicating social factors, mHealth behavioural interventions could provide an alternative tool for better communication and information management for BP control, adherence to medication and appointments, facilitating outreach and supporting their TLCs efforts. At least 70% of the PEH have access to mobile phones that can receive and send SMS texts, and despite barriers, they use them for the same reasons as the general population.33–36 A few qualitative or single-arm pilot studies have demonstrated acceptability and engagement with SMS texts, and a reduced number of no-show or cancelled appointments among PEH.37–39 An mHealth behavioural intervention that uses SMS texts, is based on behavioural feedback and support40 41 and is delivered within a multidisciplinary approach in shelters, could mitigate some of the multilevel challenges to HTN control among PEH.42–44 Additionally, this could improve health services by enabling shelter clinics to adopt elements of the Chronic Care Model including targeted outreach to hypertensive PEH to support health behaviour change and provide reminders and feedback for adherence to HTN medications and appointments.45–47

The objectives of this study are twofold. First, it will evaluate the efficacy of a 6-month SMS texting versus attention control on BP reduction, medication adherence and clinic appointments among adult PEH with uncontrolled HTN. As a secondary objective, it will assess patients’ and providers’ acceptability and experience of SMS texting using qualitative interviews.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a mixed-method study with a randomised controlled trial (RCT) and the use of qualitative methods. The RCT is designed to assess a behavioural mobile health intervention (INT) versus a control (CL) matched for attention. Figure 1 depicts the study flow and diagram.

Study diagram. BP, blood pressure; CL, control; HTN, hypertension; INT, intervention; PEH, persons experiencing homelessness; SMS, short messaging service.

Study aims

Aim 1: To assess the impact of a 6-month SMS text strategy targeted on HTN management as the intervention vs attention control on changes in systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) and adherence to BP medications and appointments at 6 months in hypertensive PEH with uncontrolled BP age 21 or older (n=120). Aim 2: To evaluate patients’ and providers’ experience, attitude and acceptability of a 6-month mHealth strategy using SMS texts for HTN management among hypertensive PEH age 21 or older with uncontrolled BP in shelter clinics in NYC. Semistructured interviews will be applied (using criteria and random sampling methods) to the PEH (n=30) and providers (n=20). It is expected that patients and providers will identify barriers to mHealth implementation while experiencing SMS texting as a positive contribution to successful BP control. The results will help adapt and strengthen mHealth by understanding patients, providers and system barriers.

Study sites, population and recruitment

Project Renewal (PR) supports several shelters and shelter clinics in NYC, where PEH receive social and health services. These locations will be the direct care locations for participants and serve as the data collection sites. In 2019, PR served 1063 unique PEH with HTN.48 Medical records will be reviewed to identify and recruit eligible patients in clinics. Inclusion criteria include PEH age 21 or older already diagnosed with HTN whose SBP or DBP at the most recent clinic visit were >140 or >90 mm Hg, respectively49; English-speaking or Spanish-speaking; currently presenting to the PR shelter clinics for medical care; and connected to multidisciplinary social and health services in the PR. Exclusion criteria: pregnant or within 3 months post partum50; stroke or heart attack or stroke within past 6 months; aortic aneurysm, end-stage renal disease or on dialysis; not being able to read and respond to texts; severe mental illness requiring inpatient care; current severe or uncontrolled substance abuse or any condition preventing participants to provide informed consent. Patients with an SBP>175 mm Hg or DBP>105 are eligible only if they have no symptoms suggesting a hypertensive emergency or urgency evaluated by a medical provider who will be given 48 hours to opt them out of the study.50 Recruitment for this study began on 1 February 2023 and is expected to continue until 30 June 2024.

Randomisation, blinding and masking

Via a computer-assisted permutation programme and a block size of 4, stratified by clinic sites, individual participants will be randomised to treatment groups. A study coordinator will oversee the randomisation process and SMS intervention implementation. Investigators and data analysts will be blind to the allocation scheme.

Theoretical framework

We hypothesise that the SMS text intervention, based on the social cognitive theory with a key element of self-determination theory, will help increase patients’ self-efficacy and confidence to manage important barriers to HTN control, lead to positive behavioural changes (eg, self-care activities and adherence), and eventually improve BP. The intervention builds on existing resources in the shelter clinics by assisting in establishing short-term achievable tasks related to HTN goals. It builds on the relatedness between the shelter clinics, the participants and broader the resources at shelters by providing a social bridge to other key relationships and clinical resources. The customised algorithm for behaviour changes within the intervention, will provide messages tailored to the population’s needs and include language that will bolster their self-efficacy and competence to overcome barriers and engage in positive behaviours. Contacting participants via a streamlined SMS text approach helps to assess their willingness and confidence in engaging in HTN control. Additionally, it explains HTN risks and management options, elicits barriers and concerns, responds to questions or concerns, and supports follow-up care and appointments. Health outcomes and appointment adherence are expected to increase because of expanding the opportunities for engagement with their health condition and other resources.

Overview of the project

The INT will comprise of 6 months of mHealth SMS messages to support HTN control including medication adherence and appointment reminders, and HTN-specific health education and support. The CL group will receive 6 months of mHealth information including basic healthy behavioural support via SMS texts. Both groups undergo BP measurements and adherence assessment at regular study visits. Participants will have a follow-up appointment scheduled for 2 months from enrolment by the research associate at the baseline visit. Data obtained from patients via SMS texts will be reviewed by the system and study team members. We will recruit a total of PEH (n=120) with uncontrolled HTN and a range of providers (n=20) from PR in NYC. The timeline will consist of the collection of sociodemographics and baseline data at enrolment followed by a 6-month mHealth INT for each participant until the last enrolled participant completes the study. Primary outcomes of BP readings, medication adherence and appointment attendance will be measured at subsequent study visits which includes months 2, 4 and 6. Secondary outcomes include phone retention rates, utilisation of phones and SMS texts (# of texts read and responded to by subjects) and the proportion of patients with persistently uncontrolled HTN. PEH with HTN (n=30; 15 each arm) will be interviewed at the end of their 6-month mHealth period. Providers (n=20) will be interviewed after the completion of the study. Research assistants (RAs), fluent in Spanish, will perform the semistructured interviews and collect data. Interim analysis will be performed, and the final analysis of quantitative and qualitative data will ensue after the completion of data collection. A telehealth procedure with a nurse practitioner (NP) provider will be available to reach patients when indicated (ie, consistently high BPs, needing urgent attention) and frequent brief check-ins via telehealth/calls will be considered.

Development of SMS platform: Prior views from a group of current hypertensive PEH, formerly PEH, shelter case workers, counsellors and providers were acquired using community-based participatory approaches. In-depth discussions with the medical director, chief medical officer, staff and providers at PR were held to aid in the development of this intervention. Using focus groups, we also explored literacy factors, HTN health literacy, and mobile technology literacy to adapt the SMS platform, the INT and its components and evaluation tools. Based on these data, a compilation of text messages was developed and integrated into the SMS platform. This process also helped to further understand and address chronic diseases among other priorities. Prior to implementation of the full intervention, it was tested with a small group of eligible participants to determine its functionality and efficacy. The study’s aim 2 will also help us understand challenges and opportunities faced by this population and to refine the intervention for subsequent studies.

SMS texting procedure: The SMS platform will be interactive and reciprocal for both groups. If there is an urgent matter, the RA and study coordinator will direct patients to clinic providers or the emergency room for assistance. We have already identified a technology company and phone service provider to provide the automated messaging and phone plan respectively in English and Spanish without charge to participants.51

Duration of texting: Patients will receive SMS texts for both the INT and CL groups for a duration of 6 months to facilitate patient retention.51

The INT: Participants will receive SMS texts every other day51 52 to bolster medication and appointment adherence, lifestyle changes and health promotion related to HTN control and CVD risks. For appointment adherence, they will receive reminder SMS texts, both 3 days and 1 day before each appointment, with a follow-up text if they missed the appointment on the next day encouraging them to reschedule.

The CL: It will receive SMS texts regarding healthy behaviours and health promotions such as the importance of hydration,51 52 and safety and protection that do not overlap with HTN specific management. The INT arm also receives many of the same basic healthy behaviour texts.

Participants in both groups will receive welcome texts and Birthday texts. In addition to an existing library of messages, the research team will create customised messages that fit the population and nature of this study. Study procedures

Hypertensive PEH with uncontrolled BP will be identified via electronic medical records and enrolled during their clinic visit. On the day of enrolment, they will be approached in the waiting rooms and offered to participate in the study. Potential unsheltered participants’ eligibility will be assessed at the predetermined clinic sites by the RA. Uncontrolled BP is measured by the BP readings from their most recent visit and is defined based on the current updated guideline and diagnoses of HTN.49 Patient’s other comorbidities (CAD, stroke, myocardial infarct, DM or chronic kidney disease) will be collected but will not be factored into eligibility assessment. If accepted, participants will be consented in a private area or exam room. Participants with severe mental illness or current severe or uncontrolled substance abuse will be excluded at the providers discretion. Sociodemographics and baseline data will be collected including BP measurements by clinic nurses, behavioural surveys, medication adherence measurements and other data by the RA. The RA will offer health education on HTN for 20 min to all participants; provide them with free single type of cell phones (with calendar and Wi-Fi access) with unlimited minutes and text plans for the study period, train them how to charge and maintain phones, how to use SMS texts and provide ongoing help and follow-ups to assure proper understanding and usage of the phones. Lost or damaged devices will be replaced without cost to the participant. At enrolment, participants will be randomly assigned to either the INT or CL group. Enrolment will end when 120 eligible participants are enrolled.

Clinic visits

After enrolment, study visits will be scheduled for all participants in the shelter clinics to measure and record their BP readings and medication adherence at 0, 2, 4 and 6 months. Their regular appointments with their providers will not be affected. BP is measured by the trained clinic nurses at the initial enrolment and at all follow ups. BPs are measured with the patient seated in an upright position with their right arm resting on a table at the level of the heart and a proper cuff size is fitted, after a 5 min rest. BP is measured by clinic healthcare providers during the participants regular appointments. Two BP readings will be taken separated by a 2 min interval, and the average will be used in the analysis.49 Medication adherence will be measured at each assessment visit using the three-item Voils DOSE-Nonadherence Measure.54 55 Appointment attendance will be measured by accessing medical records.

Attrition and retention

Clients at PR stay within the system for an average of 1 year, which will decrease the attrition rate. If no responses to texts are received in 1 week/three texts, the RA and the study coordinator will reach out to participants by phone and seek support from shelters case workers to connect and identify and address issues related to drop out.

Patient and public involvement

For this study, we have held formal discussion sessions with hypertensive homeless adults using shelter clinics for care and incorporated input and insight from members of community organisations serving PEH to develop the study protocol and the SMS texting platform.

Ethics and dissemination

Institutional Review Board (IRB) at George Washington University approved this study under protocol number NCR203140 in January of 2021. The study results will be published in peer-reviewed journals. Reports will be made available to the organisation that operates shelters as well as the community at large. Deidentified data will be shared on the National Institute of Health (NIH) approved archives. Additionally, we will share data on general source repositories to capture broader potential users of our generated data.

Strengths and limitations

The healthcare of PEH with CVDs such as HTN has not been well studied. In this study, we will evaluate the efficacy and short-term feasibility of an mHealth SMS texting programme among adult PEH with uncontrolled HTN in homeless shelters. This study has several strengths and areas of importance. It will be conducted in collaboration with community organisations that operate shelters and shelter clinics in alignment with community oriented participatory research. These organisations deliver effective healthcare to underserved, largely minority populations which makes sustainability of the intervention effects and its scalability feasible.

The mHealth behavioural interventions for HTN control have never been evaluated in PEH despite the growing evidence of their effectiveness and the accessibility of mobile phones in PEH. This study will help to pilot test this strategy and pave the way for its further assessment or potential use in places where PEH seek care and has the potential for adaptation for other chronic disease management. The shelter system already provides comprehensive services regarding social issues and behavioural and primary care health supports that are major barriers to effective chronic disease management. Therefore, the impact of mHealth on non-adherence will be more accurately evaluated. The mHealth intervention will remove the burden from already overwhelmed human resources in shelters to delegate non-advanced tasks to an easily reproducible, generalisable, quality controlled and reliable system. This study will explore if achievable results are possible while the PEH are engaged by the multidisciplinary teams in the shelter system with a cost-effective mHealth behavioural intervention.

This is a small-scale trial designed to help assess acceptability, short-term feasibility and efficacy of the intervention in the shelter system and to further explore implementation barriers. It will help better estimate the effect size and, if successful, provide the impetus for a subsequent proposal to evaluate if the intervention is effective in a larger multisite implementation study with an incorporation of cost analysis. In a follow-up study, we will also include measures to understand the spillover effects on related risk factors for HTN. If the findings prove to be negative the information learnt will still allow for insight into the challenges and opportunities in controlling HTN among PEH. Findings from this study may have broader potential policy implications regarding management of HTN and other chronic diseases in other cities with large numbers of the PEH and will help reduce mortality to CVD among this overlooked vulnerable population.

Sample size and power calculation

The sample size and power have been calculated based on the comparison of mean BP between the CL and INT groups at 6 months. Given the data scarcity on the effect of mHealth behavioural intervention on HTN in PEH, there is not sufficient data for power estimation. A decrease in SBP of 5 mm Hg is associated with clinically important reduction in the relative risk of stroke/CAD events.56 Based on our recent study,14 we used the SD of SBP (15 mm Hg) and DBP (13 mm Hg) to calculate sample size. Assuming a two-sample t-test, we calculated that a total of 120 (60 per each group) is sufficient to achieve a power of 80% to detect a difference of 8 mmHg assuming the SD for both groups is at 15 at the 0.05 significance level (two sided). Based on data from our site, this is a conservative power estimate for either outcome of SBP or DBP. Up to 27% of PEH have HTN6; 40% uncontrolled.14 In 2019, the PR served at least 1000 unique PEH with HTN.48 Using medical record reviews and outreach, with a conservative 50% response rate, we will be able to enrol 120 participants within 4–6 months. Our previous studies with the PEH did not show a high attrition rate largely because the PEH were reachable in the shelter system, case workers helped identify barriers to retention, and PEH were eager to get their healthcare covered in the system.35 57 58 The risk of contamination will be low as weekly enrolment in each clinic is low and PEH are from different shelters.

Aim 2

The qualitative outcomes include the attitudes, acceptance and experiences of the mHealth intervention which will be assessed from RA conducted individual audio-taped interviews with 30 hypertensive patients and 20 providers using 8–10 open-ended questions with probes. Criteria sampling will be used to ensure that views are captured from a variety of participants and factors such as gender, age, sociocultural, language and technological literacy. Provider interviews will include healthcare providers (NPs and physicians) and staff (case workers, administrators, support staff). Primary discussions with key informants in shelters for discourse brought up issues to be explored by questions in interviews. These interviews will take place at the completion of the study.

Analysis plan

Aim 1

The statistical analysis will be based on an intention-to-treat analysis. The trial results will be presented as comparative summary statistics (means or response rates) with 95% CIs and in accordance with the Consolidated Reporting of Trials (CONSORT). All statistical tests will use a 0.05 two-sided significance level. We will test the difference of outcomes (change in SBP and DBP from baseline) between the groups after 3 and 6 months using a two-sample t-test. We will examine the outcomes using a mixed-effects model on data collected at 3 and 6 months to calculate the outcome of change from baseline. An interaction between time and randomised group will be fitted to allow estimation of treatment effects (INT vs CL) at each time point. Analysis of covariance will be used in place of a t-test if covariates are needed. A regression analysis will be performed using the final assessment of adherence as a response and randomisation group as a predictor. Other variables that may potentially have effects on adherence such as age, gender, education and comorbidity will also be included if they are not balanced between groups by the randomisation or if determined by univariate analysis to be significantly predictive of adherence. The medication adherence will be calculated for 3 and 6 months and compared between two groups using the generalised estimation equation method (or multivariate analysis of variance). Message exchanges in two groups will be analysed for the engagement level to explain outcomes. Appointment adherence and other secondary outcomes will be compared using numbers and percentages as applicable. We will report missing data and explore patterns and mechanisms, though a mixed-effects model does implicitly account for data missing at random. We will report all results in total numbers and percentages. We will use SPSS (V.26) to conduct this data analysis.

Aim 2

Interviews will be transcribed, coded and analysed using an inductive grounded theory analysis. Two research team members (contact Principle Investigator and Co-Investigator) will do content analysis to identify core perceptions regarding the mHealth behavioural intervention for HTN management. Preliminary codes will be derived from the theoretical framework and conceptual model. We will perform critical deliberation about preliminary coding and subsequently review coding for consistency or deviation between coders’ output to achieve a high level of agreement and assure that the coding scheme is appropriate. Reviewers will independently review codes, meet and discuss them, identify concepts and categories, and describe major important themes. ATLASti will be used.

Reporting guideline

We have adhered to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting guidelines in this protocol.59

Management of data, risks, oversight and monitoring

Texting platform interface will capture all SMS texting data. A distinct identifying ID will be assigned to each participant to obtain baseline and ongoing outcome data. All deidentified data will be uploaded to the GWU REDCap. Bimonthly review of data will be employed and Principle Investigator (PI) will be informed of any issues to address during the regular site visits. The review will include monitoring for inconsistency in data and any missing or outlying data.

Data safety monitoring plan and adverse events

Both a data and safety monitoring plan (DSMP) and a data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) will be developed. The purpose of the DSMP and DSMB are to ensure the safety of participants and the validity and integrity of the data. Adverse events will be reported to the respective IRB, as well as to the chair of the DSMB. The DSMB has the authority to halt the trial if it perceives that harm is occurring due to the intervention. Summaries of adverse events reports will be made to NIH in the yearly progress or report or, at the end of year 2, in the final report, unless the nature of a particular event is such that it bears immediate reporting to the NIH. For our prior interventional and non-interventional studies with the homeless persons in the NYC shelters we have not documented any adverse effect during these studies. We use our experience from these prior studies to better inform our approach to data safety and monitoring for the proposed research. All study data will be destroyed 5 years after the completion of the study.

Risks

We expect the level of risk due to this intervention for participants PEH to be minimal as no invasive measures or procedures are proposed in the research strategy. During this study, patients who chose to participate continue to receive their usual care from their providers, and the mHealth approach only adds to their current regular care for HTN. The mHealth approach is not an invasive procedure and has very minimal if any potential physical risks. Patients use their mobile phones on a regular basis and there is minimal risk of using mobile phones. Any such risk is an inherent part of the technology that users accept daily. Participants will be trained in the effective use of text messaging and to avoid texting while engaged in other activities such as driving or operating machinery. Participants may experience some emotional risks when receiving text messages, they do not like. No procedures or measurements are performed for the purpose of this research and outside patient’s normal standard of care. A private room will be used to collect data and the RA will be trained to use a safe and supportive approach to elicit potentially stigmatised information.

Referral and treatment services

Patients experiencing any urgent medical issues will be referred to their clinician or emergency services by the RA or study coordinator.

Informed consent

The RAs will give the participants oral and written descriptions of the research protocol and will answer any questions they may have. As part of the informed consent process, all participants will be reminded that their responses are confidential and that they may refuse to participate in the project or withdraw at any time without explanation, and that such an action will in no way affect their future interactions with their employer or healthcare providers.

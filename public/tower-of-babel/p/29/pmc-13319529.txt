Thermodynamic process of entropy generation and MHD convection in porous cavity saturated with MWCNT-Fe3O4/H2O hybrid nanofluid: Darcy–Forchheimer–Brinkmann model
Authors: Syed M. Hussain, Aissani Abdelkader, Fares Redouane, Hidki Rachid, Adnane Mohammed, Kamel Guedri, Mohamed R. Eid, Hijaz Ahmad, Mustafa Bayram, Muhammad Amer Qureshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research focuses on examining heat transfer and entropy generation within a porous cavity, which contains a hybrid nanofluid known as MWCNT-Fe3O4/H2O, due to its relevance to improving thermal management systems as well as increasing energy efficiency. The study is based on an advanced Darcy–Forchheimer–Brinkmann computational model that considers inertial forces, the fluid filling the cavity, and the resultant effects of applying a magnetic field. To provide the laminar and incompressible nature of the nanofluids flux, the Darcy–Forchheimer prototype is essential. While accounting for the inertial influence of advection in the permeable coating. To solve non-dimensional equations, we employ the finite element methodology and the Darcy–Forchheimer–Brinkmann prototype. Using the Finite Element Method to solve the governing equations numerically demonstrates that by increasing the porosity of the cavity, heat transfer rates can be increased by up to 30%; however, the increase in entropy production also increases with cavity porosity while the application of a greater magnetic field helps to reduce this effect on entropy generation by approximately 20%. The results indicate that the length of the cavity and the magnitude of the applied magnetic field also impact thermal performance within the cavity. The results of this study may assist in developing efficient thermal systems based upon the use of hybrid nanofluids, thereby increasing energy efficiency overall.

The present study is motivated by the current gaps in the existing body of research. It aims to explore previously unexplored territory focusing on investigating the convective temperature transfer phenomenon associated with the behaviour of hybridized nanofluids in an axial magnetic force acting on a square enclosure with different types of porous media. The rectangular configuration represents a scenario encountered often in engineering and thermally-related applications that are of much interest and importance. The accurate representation of heat transfer through each diagonal side of the enclosure will be achieved by using an integrated boundary method which is more accurate than conventional integrated boundary methods used previously. Through this research we hope to obtain a thorough understanding of the various interactions created between the variety of physically modified porous media properties, the magnetic forces and the concentric hybridized nanomaterials (MWCNTs-Fe3O4/H2O) having an effect on the thermal nature of a liquid within the enclosure.

This research paper provides a novel approach to studying the thermodynamics of the creation of entropy and the movement of thermal energy in porous cavities with hybrid nanofluids (MWCNT-Fe3O4/H2O) in the presence of an external magnetic field. It uses a complete Darcy–Forchheimer–Brinkmann model that allows for consideration of inertial forces, changes in porosity of the porous space, and magnetic effects on the system. Therefore, this work offers new insights into how airflow with hybrid nanofluids creates entropy. Previous studies have focused on nanofluid flows and heat transfer as two separate subjects, while this study incorporates both into one study to assess their combined effects. Furthermore, this research investigates an extensive range of parameters, including high Rayleigh number conditions, different porosity conditions, various strengths for the externally applied magnetic field, and a variety of volume fractions for the nanoparticles, in order to quantify the effect of each parameter on both entropy generation and thermal performance. The high-fidelity numerical method used to simulate the thermodynamic and hydrodynamic properties of the fluid produced detailed result outputs which serve to improve our understanding of heat transfer efficiency and entropy reduction, and thus provide practitioners with useful data for engineering thermal management in energy-efficient applications. The originality statement and conclusion of this paper highlight the uniqueness of this paper's findings from their separate previous studies, such as those regarding the impacts of hybrid nanofluids, porous media, and/or magnetic fields.

The development of an advanced cavity model allows researchers to have much greater control over the flow rates of nanoscale fluids (nanofluids) through the air and heat transfer through both air and solid surfaces. As a result, the development of an improved modified Darcy–Forchheimer-Brinkman model considering porosity will give researchers a much better understanding of how these fluids behave when they pass through various cavities. This improved understanding will have significant effects on the design and use of heat transfer systems, renewable energy conversion devices and, ultimately, the processing of fluids in biological inspired technologies.

Future directions

Future research will involve the investigation of different types of nanoparticles and their effects on heat transfer and entropy generation in porous cavities with occurrents flows. Additionally, transient analysis of the effects of magnetic fields of different strengths will be conducted along with investigating how complex shapes within cavities affect thermodynamic performance, using experimental data to supplement model validation, and optimally designing geometries for efficient thermal management.

Electromagnetohydrodynamic flow and heat transfer of a dusty hybrid nanofluid over a rotating disk in a porous medium
Authors: D Serafin Grace, P Durgaprasad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMomentum and thermal transport characteristics of a two-phase dusty Eyring–Powell hybrid nanofluid induced by a rotating disk in a Darcy–Forchheimer porous medium are numerically analysed under electromagnetohydrodynamic conditions. The working fluid comprises blood filled with Ag–Cu hybrid nanoparticles, while shear-dependent viscosity is modelled through the Eyring–Powell constitutive relation. To account for dust–fluid interactions, a two-phase model incorporates interphase momentum and heat transfer processes. The governing partial differential equations are transformed into a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations using appropriate similarity transformations. The resulting boundary value problem is solved numerically using the MATLAB built-in solver bvp4c. The effects of porous resistance, inertial drag, electromagnetic field intensity, dust-interaction parameters, thermal Biot number, internal heat generation, and nanoparticle concentration on velocity distributions, temperature fields, skin friction coefficients, and heat-transfer rates are systematically examined. The numerical results show that while surface convection and more nanoparticles improve heat-transfer efficiency, porous drag, electromagnetic forces, and non-Newtonian effects significantly reduce momentum transport. Effective interphase interaction is essential for the momentum and heat transfer between the fluid and dust phases. These results provide a physical understanding of EMHD-controlled transport phenomena in rotating porous configurations pertinent to magnetically regulated blood transport in rotating biomedical devices.Keywords: Eyring–Powell fluid, Dusty hybrid nanofluid, Rotating disk, EMHD, Darcy–Forchheimer porous medium, Heat transfer

Introduction

Rotating disks constitute a classical configuration in fluid dynamics, characterized by strong centrifugal forces and three-dimensional boundary-layer development. In biomedical transport processes, similar rotational mechanisms arise in centrifugal blood pumps and related hemodynamic devices, where shear-dependent rheology and nanoparticle dispersion significantly influence momentum and thermal transport [1, 2]. Studies on nanoparticle-dispersed non-Newtonian blood over rotating disks have demonstrated that centrifugal acceleration reshapes radial flow structures, while magnetic fields and Joule heating modify thermal and concentration boundary layers [3]. Moreover, unsteady blood-based hybrid nanofluid flow between rotating stretching disks under magnetohydrodynamic and convective boundary conditions has been shown to significantly alter the velocity components, skin friction, and heat-transfer characteristics due to nanoparticle loading and electromagnetic interactions [4]. These findings underscore the need for comprehensive modelling of blood-nanoparticle interactions in rotating porous environments under electromagnetic forcing.

Non-Newtonian behaviour is essential to blood flow, particularly under shear-dependent viscosity variations that cannot be adequately described by classical Newtonian models. Among rheological formulations, the Eyring-Powell model is distinguished by its ability to capture nonlinear stress–strain relationships without mathematical singularities, making it particularly suitable for boundary-layer and heat-transfer analyses. Recent investigations on Eyring-Powell and Casson fluids have underscored the importance of incorporating microscale devices, such as Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and cross-diffusion, to accurately describe combined heat and mass transfer processes [5]. Owing to its foundation in molecular kinetic theory, the Eyring-Powell model provides a physically consistent framework for analysing viscous dissipation, flow resistance, and temperature distributions under diverse thermal and magnetic situations. Consequently, it has been extensively employed in both computational and experimental research across chemical engineering, polymer processing, and nanofluid technologies, enabling realistic prediction of flow behaviour that classical Newtonian models fail to capture [6–10]. The close connection between shear-dependent viscosity, magnetic fields, slip processes, and transport powered by nanoparticles has been further illustrated by recent research on Eyring-Powell nanofluids across rotating disks. According to research, for instance, the Eyring-Powell parameter and slip effects tend to decrease momentum transfer, while magnetic field strength can enhance tangential velocities [11]. Additional work by Khan et al. [12] reported that shear-thinning viscosity and nanoparticle dynamics significantly influence momentum and heat transfer in rotating-disk configurations. Similarly, Nadeem et al. [13] demonstrated that magnetic effects and variable thermal conductivity markedly modify three-dimensional flow structures in Eyring-Powell rotating flows.

Hybrid nanofluids enhance effective thermal conductivity and rheological response compared to mono nanofluids. According to recent studies, combining hybrid nanofluids with the Eyring-Powell rheological model produces a more accurate representation of fluid time-scale effects and shear-dependent viscosity, thereby rendering this method appropriate for simulating complicated transport processes [14]. Eyring-Powell hybrid nanofluids based on Fe2O3-Al2O3 have been shown by Kasali et al. [15] to improve thermal conductivity while significantly modifying shear and velocity fields. Rashad et al. [16] reported enhanced heat transfer in Cu-Fe3O4 hybrid nanofluids under magnetic and porous-medium conditions. Aziz et al. [17] demonstrated that Al2O3-Cu/Eyring-Powell hybrid nanofluids increase heat conduction and exert a strong influence on velocity, temperature, and entropy generation. Algehyne et al. [18] observed improved heat transfer in graphene-silver hybrid nanofluids over a rotating disk as nanoparticle concentration, radiation, and Biot number increased, whereas magnetic fields suppressed the velocity. Thumma et al. [19] showed that Cu-Ag/water hybrid nanofluids exhibit superior heat-transfer rates under the combined action of magnetic fields, Hall currents, and radiative effects. Furthermore, the combined effects of velocity slip, viscous dissipation, heat generation, and porous resistance in Ag–Cu hybrid nanofluids reveal pronounced coupling between electromagnetic forcing and near-wall shear behavior [20, 21]. In porous hybrid nanofluid systems, the role of nonlinear Darcy-Forchheimer inertial drag has also been studied, emphasizing the close relationship between inertial resistance and thermal diffusion in rotating and stretching surfaces [22, 23].

The motion of electrically conducting fluids under the influence of externally applied electric and magnetic fields is described by electromagnetic hydrodynamics (EMHD). Effective control over momentum and thermal transfer is made possible in these systems by the interaction of the Lorentz force with buoyancy and viscous phenomena. EMHD is extremely significant to applications such as magnetic, biomedical transportation, and geophysical flows because of its ability to inhibit or enhance convection and function as an effective mechanism to regulate velocity and heat transfer [24]. Bhatti et al. [25] analysed EMHD transport in third-grade hybrid nanofluids, demonstrating that coupled electric and magnetic fields, together with Joule heating and viscous dissipation, significantly influence thermal and flow responses. Recent studies have further extended EMHD analyses to Eyring-Powell hybrid nanofluid flows over stretching surfaces, highlighting the impacts of electromagnetic forcing, radiation, and Joule heating on velocity, temperature, and entropy generation characteristics [26]. Zahid and Riaz [27] investigated variable-EMHD nanofluid flow over a porous stretching sheet and reported that spatial variations in electric and magnetic fields substantially alter the distributions of velocity, temperature, concentration, and microorganisms due to activation energy and radiative effects. By examining EMHD transport in a two-layer porous channel with separate nanofluid and hybrid-nanofluid regions, Dordevi et al. [28] demonstrated that electric–magnetic forcing combines with permeability discrepancies and nanoparticle variations to control heat transfer and surface momentum. In addition to this, recent studies have explored EMHD flows across a wide range of configurations, from Casson hybrid nanofluids in porous media [29] to micropolar dusty fluids undergoing reactive processes [30] and third-grade hybrid nanofluids in geothermal pipelines [31], consistently showing that electromagnetic forcing plays a decisive role in shaping momentum and heat transport across complex rheology.

The resistance that the pore structure generates has a significant impact on fluid flow in porous media, and this behavior becomes more complicated when heat is generated or absorbed internally. Darcy's law is primarily relevant at low rates, but at higher speeds, the pressure drop becomes nonlinear because of inertial variables; this is known as non-Darcy (Forchheimer) flow [32]. Mandal and Pal [33] demonstrated that the Darcy-Forchheimer porous model, together with heat source/sink effects, substantially modifies the momentum and thermal boundary layers in rotating-disk flows. Alqurashi [34] reported that nonlinear Forchheimer drag significantly reduces the velocity field in MHD Reiner-Phillipoff fluids, while internal heat generation and nonlinear thermal radiation markedly influence the temperature distribution. Nihaal et al. [35] observed that increasing the Forchheimer number intensifies porous resistance and diminishes velocity in ternary nanofluid systems, with exponential heat source/sink terms strongly affecting the thermal field. Similarly, Khan et al. [36] confirmed that Darcy-Forchheimer resistance reduces momentum transport in Eyring-Powell nanofluid flows over oscillatory surfaces. Recently, blood-based CNT nanofluid flow between rotating disks embedded in a Darcy-Forchheimer porous matrix has demonstrated that inertial drag and magnetic interaction significantly influence shear stress and heat transfer characteristics [37]. In related developments, two-phase micropolar nanofluid analyses incorporating Darcy-Forchheimer inertia further demonstrated significant modifications to momentum and thermal transport characteristics in porous media [38].

Despite substantial progress in rotating-disk transport studies involving Eyring-Powell and hybrid nanofluid, several important coupled mechanisms remain insufficiently examined. Existing studies predominantly employ single-phase formulations, thereby neglecting dust particle relaxation and interphase momentum and heat transfer effects. Moreover, porous-medium analyses are often restricted to Darcy resistance. Conversely, nonlinear Forchheimer inertia, pertinent at moderate rotational velocities, has garnered fewer considerations in non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluid phenomena. While EMHD control has been studied in flat or channel structures, its simultaneous relationship to shear-dependent viscosity, hybrid nanoparticle dispersion, and two-phase dusty phenomena in rotating porous geometries remains largely unexplored.

Numerical methodology

The partial differential equations governing the flow and heat transfer of the fluid and dust phases are reduced, using similarity transformations, to a system of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations, as given in Eqs. (11)-(16), together with the boundary conditions (17). For numerical solution, the resulting higher-order equations are rewritten as an equivalent system of first-order differential equations by introducing suitable auxiliary variables.

The transformed system is solved using the MATLAB built-in solver bvp4c, which is designed for two-point boundary value problems. The solver is based on a collocation method and employs a three-stage Lobatto IIIA scheme internally, providing fourth-order accuracy [40]. During the iterative process, the solution is refined so that the governing equations and boundary conditions are satisfied within a specified tolerance. An initial guess for the solution is supplied over a truncated computational domain, and the solver automatically refines the mesh to enhance accuracy and ensure convergence. Overall, the results reveal that porous resistance, magnetic damping, non-Newtonian rheology, and dust-fluid interactions strongly regulate momentum transport. At the same time, thermal behaviour is governed by interphase heat exchange, surface convection, and hybrid nanoparticle loading. Contour analyses further highlight the combined influence of magnetic field strength, slip, and nanoparticle concentration on wall shear stress and heat-transfer rates.

Conclusion

The electromagnetohydrodynamic transfer characteristics of a two-phase dusty Eyring-Powell Ag–Cu hybrid nanofluid over a rotating disk embedded in a Darcy-Forchheimer porous medium have been numerically investigated. The principal findings are summarised as follows:

Increasing the Hartmann number significantly suppresses both the radial and azimuthal velocity components due to the enhanced Lorentz force, thereby reducing wall shear stress and thinning the momentum boundary layers.

Overall, the integrated EMHD two-phase formulation provides a deeper physical understanding of momentum and thermal regulation in non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluid systems subjected to porous and electromagnetic effects.

Practical applications

The proposed EMHD two-phase dusty hybrid nanofluid model has applications in magnetically controlled blood-nanoparticle transport in rotating biomedical devices, such as centrifugal blood pumps and ventricular assist devices. The addition of Ag–Cu hybrid nanoparticles enhances magnetic response and heat transfer properties, with applications in hyperthermia cancer treatment and magnetic drug delivery. Furthermore, the inclusion of Darcy-Forchheimer porous resistance terms enables simulation of blood-nanoparticle transport in porous vascular scaffolds and tissue-engineered constructs, where inertial forces influence hemodynamic performance.

Future scope

Future studies may extend the present model to unsteady rotating flows and incorporate temperature-dependent thermophysical properties to enhance physical realism. Entropy generation and thermodynamic irreversibility analyses may also be performed to assess system efficiency under combined electromagnetic and porous effects. Furthermore, the framework may be generalised to ternary or higher-order hybrid nanofluids and alternative non-Newtonian rheological models to broaden its applicability.

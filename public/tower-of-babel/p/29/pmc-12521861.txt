Efficacy of feed additives obtained from the leaves of Ginkgo biloba L.: Ginkgo tinctures for all animal species and ginkgo extract for horses, dogs, cats, rabbits and guinea pigs (FEFANA asbl)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Maria de Lourdes Bastos, Emilio Benfenati, Paul Brantom, Andrew Chesson, Josef Schlatter, Johannes Westendorf, Paola Manini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the efficacy of feed additives obtained from the leaves of Ginkgo biloba L.: ginkgo tinctures as sensory additives for all animal species and ginkgo extract as a sensory additive for horses, dogs, cats, rabbits and guinea pigs. In previous assessments, a sensory analysis of ginkgo extract was performed, which showed that aqueous solutions of ginkgo extract have an intense aroma, significantly different from plain water. No evidence was provided to show that the additives would impart flavour to a food or feed matrix. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of the additives. The applicant provided new efficacy studies, which showed that the addition of the additives under assessment (ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures) led to detectable flavour changes to a food matrix under the experimental conditions tested. The FEEDAP Panel considers that ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures have the potential to be efficacious as sensory additives in feed.

On 14.03.2024 1 and on 18.04.2024, 2 the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), in its opinions on the safety and efficacy of feed additives consisting of ginkgo tinctures and ginkgo extract obtained from the leaves of Ginkgo biloba L. could not conclude on the efficacy of the additives.

The European Commission gave the possibility to the applicant to submit supplementary information and data to complete the assessment and to allow a revision of the EFSA's opinion.

The new supplementary information and data have been transmitted by the applicant using the E‐Submission Food Chain Platform (application number FEED‐2025‐33647 3 ).

In view of the above and in accordance with Article 29(1)(a) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, the European Commission requests EFSA to deliver a new scientific opinion on the efficacy of feed additives obtained from the leaves of Ginkgo biloba L.: ginkgo tinctures for all animal species and ginkgo extract for horses, dogs, cats, rabbits and guinea pigs under the conditions of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, based on the supplementary information and data submitted by the applicant.

Additional information

Ginkgo extract and ginkgo tincture from Ginkgo biloba L. are currently authorised as feed additives according to the entry in the European Union Register of Feed Additives pursuant to Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 (2b natural products – botanically defined).

EFSA issued an opinion on the safety and efficacy of ginkgo extract when used as sensory additives in feed for horses, dogs, cats, rabbits and guinea pigs (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a) and an opinion on ginkgo tinctures when used as a sensory additive in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of supplementary information 4 to a previous application on the same product. 5 The dossier was received on 19 March 2025 and the general information and supporting documentation are available on Open.EFSA at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2025‐00215.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the efficacy of ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures from the leaves of G. biloba is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 6 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024c).

ASSESSMENT

The additives under assessment, ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures, are obtained from the dried leaves of Ginkgo biloba L.

Ginkgo extract is a dry extract and is specified to contain ≥ 24% total flavonoids, ≥ 6% total terpene lactones and ≤ 1 mg/kg ginkgolic acids. The characterisation of ginkgo extract has been described in a former opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a). The additive is intended to be used as a sensory additive (functional group: flavouring compounds) for use in feed for horses, dogs, cats, rabbits and guinea pigs.

Ginkgo tinctures are water/ethanol solutions with a dry matter content of 5.7% (tincture A) and 3.0% (tincture B). The characterisation of ginkgo tinctures has been described in a former opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024b). They are also intended to be used as sensory additives (functional group: flavouring compounds). Ginkgo tincture A is intended for use in feed for horses and dogs; tincture B is intended for use in feed and water for drinking for all animal species.

In its previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a, 2024b), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additives are considered safe for the target species at the following concentrations in complete feed: (i) ginkgo extract at 2.8 mg/kg for horses and cats, 1.1 mg/kg for rabbits and guinea pigs and 3.3 mg/kg for dogs; (ii) ginkgo tincture A at 240 mg/kg for horses and 750 mg/kg for dogs; (iii) ginkgo tincture B at 600 mg/kg for horses and 50 mg/kg for all other animal species. The Panel also concluded that the additives are safe for the consumer and the environment at the proposed conditions of use. Regarding user safety, the Panel concluded that the additives should be considered as irritants to skin and eyes and as dermal and respiratory sensitisers. The data provided were not sufficient to conclude on the efficacy of the additives to impart flavour to a food or feed matrix. In the current application, the applicant submitted additional data aimed at demonstrating the efficacy of the additives.

Efficacy

While the available data indicated that Ginkgo preparations in isolation have a distinctive flavour profile, there was no evidence that the additives ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures would impart flavour to a food or feed matrix. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of the additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a, 2024b).

For the current assessment, the applicant provided three triangle tests (sensory panel) performed with the additives under assessment (ginkgo extract, ginkgo tincture A and ginkgo tincture B), 7 according to DIN EN ISO 4120: 2021‐06. The tests were aimed at verifying if the addition of the additive to a food matrix leads to flavour changes detectable by a triangle test. In a triangle test, the assessors are required to compare the test product against a control product. Each assessor is presented with three samples (two are the same and one is different) and asked to evaluate the samples and select the ‘different’ sample (ISO, 2021).

Rusk was selected as the food matrix for testing because of its neutral sensory profile, which would allow the panellists to detect subtle differences imparted by added flavourings, both orthonasally and retronasally. In each study, crushed rusk (40 g) was mixed with tap water (200 g) to prepare the ‘control’ sample to obtain a smooth, palatable product (a porridge‐like slurry) suitable for testing. The ‘treated’ sample was prepared by adding to the ‘control’ sample either 1.2 g ginkgo extract (0.5% spiking) or 3.6 g of either ginkgo tincture A or B (1.5% spiking). The samples were placed in glasses wrapped in aluminium foil to mask the slight colouring effect of the sample addition. The glasses were coded and presented as a triangle. The panellists had to identify the different sample in each triangle. Sensory evaluation was performed retronasally by 12 panellists. For ginkgo extract, the rate of identification was 100% (12/12), and for both tinctures, the corresponding rate was 67% (8/12). From the results, it could be concluded that the ‘control’ and the ‘treated’ samples are different at α level of 0.001 for ginkgo extract and of 0.05 for ginkgo tinctures.

The data provided show that the addition of the different extract/tinctures modifies the sensory characteristics of the matrix to which it is added, indicating an effect as a flavouring. Therefore, the Panel considers that there is evidence to conclude that ginkgo extract and tinctures have the potential to be efficacious as sensory additives in feed.

CONCLUSIONS

The evidence provided showed that the additives under assessment, ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures, led to detectable flavour changes in a food matrix under the experimental conditions tested.

The FEEDAP Panel considers that ginkgo extract and ginkgo tinctures have the potential to be efficacious as sensory additives in feed.

Risk factors for complications after complete debridement in patients with thoracolumbar spinal tuberculosis: A retrospective study
Authors: Rui Huang, Zongqiang Yang, Jiandang Shi, Ningkui Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Many studies have been published on the characteristics of spinal tuberculosis, but not yet on the risk factors for complications after complete debridement. This study aimed to investigate the risk factors of postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal tuberculosis (TB) after complete debridement. The clinical data of patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement admitted to General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University from January 2013 to December 2021 were included in this retrospective study. Patients were included if they had complete clinical data and a minimum follow-up duration of 1 year to ensure adequate assessment of postoperative complications. The study cohort was divided into 2 groups, including complication group and non-complication group according to the presence or absence of postoperative complications. The clinical characteristics of thoracolumbar spinal TB patients who developed postoperative complications were evaluated, and risk factors were analyzed by using univariate and binary multivariate logistic regression analysis. A total of 571 patients were included in this study: 92 patients with complications and 479 patients without complications. The results of the univariate analysis and multivariate binary logistic regression analysis showed that preoperative albumin <35 g/L (odds ratio [OR] = 1.855; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.106–3.111, P = .019), the number of diseased segments ≥3 (OR = 2.072; CI, 1.183–3.629, P = .011), anemia (OR = 1.691; CI, 1.047–2.731, P = .032) and drug resistance (OR = 1.768; CI, 1.011–3.091, P = .046) were independent risk factors for postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement. Our findings support that the level of preoperative serum albumin, number of diseased segments, anemia, and drug-resistant TB are independent risk factors for postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement. Therefore, improving preoperative serum albumin level, correcting anemia, actively preventing and monitoring drug-resistance may effectively reduce the risk of postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement.

1. Introduction

Spinal tuberculosis (TB) is the most common extrapulmonary TB, taking part of 50% in osteoarticular TB, and skeletal TB usually results from lymphohematogenous dissemination of visceral TB, and the most common site of tuberculous osteomyelitis is the thoracolumbar spine.[1] Currently, surgical treatment and anti-TB drugs are the main methods to treat thoracolumbar spinal TB. The severity of thoracolumbar spinal TB varies, and some patients with mild thoracolumbar spinal TB can be cured by taking anti-TB drugs.[2] However, in patients with surgical indications, surgical excision of local lesions is necessary in combination with anti-TB medication.[3,4]

Complete debridement is one of the main traditional open surgical methods for treating thoracolumbar spinal TB with operative indications. Based on the traditional debridement, complete debridement is the removal of the sclerosed wall, removal of the cavity and the diseased bone bridge, and then the “subnormal bone,” which can obviously increase the debriding degree, accelerate the recovery, and decrease the recurrence rate of thoracolumbar spinal TB after operation.[5,6] Our previous studies have shown that the removal of tuberculous foci, sclerotic bone, multi-cavity, and bone bridge are effective treatment methods to improve the curative effect of thoracolumbar spinal TB surgery.[7] But the operation of thoracolumbar spinal TB is a kind of iatrogenic injury, which is characterized by long operating time, great injury, and massive blood loss.[8] Moreover, because TB is a chronic wasting disease, the majority of patients have comorbidities like diabetes, anemia, and low serum albumin, which makes them more susceptible to postoperative complications than degenerative diseases.[9]

Although the surgical treatment of thoracolumbar spinal TB has been improved greatly, there are still severe complications related to surgery in some patients, which adds to the financial burden of the patient, aggravates the physical and psychological trauma, and even causes paralysis and death.[10] Zhou et al found that postoperative pulmonary complications occurred in 14 of 102 patients with L1 to 2 spinal TB.[11] Zhuang et al treated spinal TB with oblique lumbar interbody fusion surgery, and 16.95% of the patients had surgical complications, including nerve damage, peritoneal injury, and incision infection.[12] Surgeons should fully grasp the surgical methods and the corresponding potential risk factors of complications, and actively avoid the occurrence of postoperative complications. So far, some studies[13] were limited to the impact of single risk factor on postoperative thoracolumbar spinal TB complications. There is a lack of comprehensive studies on the risk factors associated with postoperative complications after complete debridement in thoracolumbar spinal TB.

Based on previous findings, our primary hypothesis is that the number of diseased segments, level of preoperative serum albumin, anemia, and drug-resistant TB are independent risk factors for postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the risk factors of complications after complete debridement of thoracolumbar spinal TB, to direct and standardize the clinical diagnosis and therapy of thoracolumbar spinal TB.

2. Methods

2.1. Patient selection

The clinical data of patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement admitted to our hospital between January 2013 to December 2021 were included in this retrospective study. In this study, complete debridement was defined as the removal of any injury or disease, including psoas abscess, granuloma, residual disc tissue, sinuses, bone isolation, and inflammatory granuloma, with the removal of tuberculous cavities, hardened walls, and unsupported bone bridges that had been eroded by the focus. The inclusion criteria were as follows: thoracolumbar spinal TB was diagnosed according to clinical manifestations, laboratory examination, pathological examination, and imaging findings; patients with surgical indications and undergoing complete debridement treatment; patients with postoperative follow-up time >1 year. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with incomplete clinical data; complicated with other special infectious diseases (hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis, and acquired immune deficiency syndrome); patients with incomplete follow-up data.

Surgical indications: local intractable pain caused by spinal TB, poor quality of life, and no obvious relief of symptoms after standardized anti-TB treatment; under standard anti-TB treatment, stubborn pain symptoms did not relieve, TB lesions and abscesses increased and progressed. The spinal TB destruction shows an expanding trend; spinal TB focus pus, tuberculous granulation tissue, caseous necrotic material, dead bone and other compression of the spinal cord, sensory motor disorders; TB lesions lead to the destruction of local spinal stability, and even local kyphosis and intractable pain. The clinical data of patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB include general information, laboratory results, surgical information, and postoperative complications.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University in January 2010 (Registration No. 20100103) and was registered at https://www.chictr.org.cn/ on December 2011 (Registration No. ChiCTR-TRC-11001855).

All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations of Helsinki, and each patient in this study had signed informed consent at the initiation of diagnosis, allowing for further clinical research using the clinical records.

2.2. Measures and outcomes

Based on previous studies and our experience, the following possible items for the analysis of postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB were assessed: including gender, age, abscess, anemia, preoperative serum albumin level, intraoperative blood loss volume, the number of diseased segments, the course of anti-TB drugs, drug-resistant TB strains, and surgical approach. After 2 to 4 weeks of anti-TB therapy, all patients underwent complete debridement, deformity correction, graft fusion, and internal fixation. All data were divided into 2 groups according to the presence or absence of postoperative complications. Multiple diseased segments were defined as the number of diseased segments ≥3. Anemia was defined as hemoglobin <100 g/L. Postoperative complications of thoracolumbar spinal TB were defined as severe postoperative electrolyte disturbance, wound infection or systemic infection, internal fixation failure, TB recurrence, uncured, surgical operation-related diseases such as dura mater injury and peripheral nervous system injury.

2.3. Statistical analyses

All statistical analysis was performed using a statistical package for social sciences (SPSS, Version 27.0, IBM Corporation, Armonk). Chi-square (X2) test was used for univariate analysis, the variables that were significantly associated with complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement in the univariate analysis were included in the binary multivariate logistic regression analysis. The calculation P-value <.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant. All figures in this study were drawn by using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego).

3. Results

3.1. Patient baseline characteristics

We initially assessed 687 patients for eligibility from January 2013 to December 2021, of whom 116 patients met exclusion criteria. Thus, a total of 571 patients (296 males and 275 females) were enrolled in this study, follow-up time ranged from 1 to 8 years after surgery (mean 5.5 years). Damage to the vertebrae was as follows: 182 patients had thoracic damage, 127 had thoracolumbar damage, and 262 had lumbar damage. Of the 571 patients, 92 cases were assigned into the complication group and 479 cases to the non-complication group. Severe hypokalemia occurred in 10 cases, 7 cases of dura mater injury, severe hypoproteinemia occurred in 12 cases, 13 cases had postoperative recurrence, poor wound healing occurred in 8 cases, postoperative wound infection occurred in 16 cases, 9 cases were unhealed after operation, 4 cases of pleural injury, peripheral nervous system injury occurred in 6 cases, intestinal obstruction occurred in 5 cases, and psoas major muscle effusion in 2 cases. 4. Discussion

In this study, we investigated the risk factors associated with postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement, and 2 groups of patients operated on by the same surgeon, who were comparable in their surgical procedure and postoperative protocol. Our results indicated that preoperative serum albumin <35 g/L, the number of diseased segments ≥3, anemia, and drug-resistant TB were independent risk factors for postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement by using the univariate analyses and binary multivariate logistic regression analyses.

Thoracolumbar spinal TB is a wasting disease with a longer course of disease, which results in poor nutrition in the majority. The levels of hemoglobin and serum albumin are key indicators of human nutrition and immunity.[14] Albumin <35 g/L is defined as hypoalbuminemia.[15] Serum albumin level plays an important role in patients’ prognosis in many medical contexts. Preoperative albumin level was an important index to evaluate preoperative complications and prognosis in orthopedic patients.[16] In addition, long periods of operation and anesthesia, severe local tissue damage, and massive blood loss may further exacerbate undernutrition.[17] Patients have poor tolerance to external adverse factors, and long-term use of anti-TB drugs leads to liver and renal impairment in some patients, which is likely to cause metabolic disorders.[18] Long-term consuming condition continuously worsens the body’s weakness, and gradually reduces the immune function. Autoimmunity cannot effectively eliminate the TB bacilli and restrict its invasion to the surrounding tissues, which can result in poor therapeutic effects, and even result in long-term failure or recurrence. Furthermore, poor nutrition can delay the heal of wound and increase the risk of infection.[19]

In present study, among 14 patients with wound infection, 8 of them had preoperative serum albumin <35 g/L and 7 cases had anemia before operation. Zhong et al believed that the poor preoperative nutritional status of patients would delay the postoperative wound healing, resulting in an increased risk of infection and nonhealing.[20] This is in line with the findings of our study. Therefore, it is necessary to make an active assessment of the patient’s nutrition condition, and to provide proper nutrition support in accordance with the condition of the body. With regard to severe cases, it is as much as possible to use a combination of several vitamins and an amino acid lipid emulsion with nutrition to improve the body’s nutrition status.[21] The results of this study indicated that adjusting serum albumin >35 g/L before operation and actively correcting the condition of preoperative anemia can effectively decrease the postoperative complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement.

The emergence of drug-resistant TB strains has become a new challenge to combat the adverse situation of the disease.[22] Due to the emergence of drug-resistant TB, the majority of patients cannot be adequately treated.[23] Wang et al reported that recurrence occurred in 21 (24%) out of 87 patients with spinal TB due to drug-resistant TB.[24] There is no doubt that standard anti-TB chemotherapy is one of the keys to treating spinal TB.[25] It has been reported that informal drug therapy is an important factor in the recurrence of spinal TB after surgery, and unreasonable chemotherapy can lead to increased drug resistance.[26] The results of Fan J et al showed that the rifampicin resistance is an independent risk factor for postoperative recurrence in spinal TB patients,[27] and this is consistent with the results of the present study. Therefore, it is necessary to formulate the standard anti-TB drugs regimen and reasonably adjust the dosage and compatibility of anti-TB drugs in order to decrease the occurrence of drug resistance.

Currently, mycobacterium TB culture in vitro, drug-sensitive test and gene-chip assay are effective in detecting TB strains and making appropriate treatment plans based on the results.[28] However, the application of this technology is limited in the hospitals with obsolete testing facilities or techniques, and it still needs to be further enhanced. Along with the deepening understanding of the drug resistance of TB, it is suggested that the drug therapy of thoracolumbar spinal TB should follow the principle of individualization, and the corresponding treatment plan should be formulated according to the results of drug-sensitive test of thoracolumbar spinal TB patients.[8,27]

At present, the course of anti-TB drugs for thoracolumbar spinal TB mainly includes traditional standard chemotherapy, short-course chemotherapy, and ultra-short-course chemotherapy.[29] No matter what kind of anti-TB drug treatment regimen is selected, it is necessary to ensure that patients have strong compliance, oral drug treatment in strict accordance with the doctor’s advice, and adhere to the monthly outpatient visit before drug withdrawal, recheck liver and kidney function, inflammatory indicators, and imaging examination, so as to understand the recovery of patients in time. Finally, the withdrawal time was determined according to the recovery of patients and the results of various examinations.[30] It has been proven that inadequate postoperative chemotherapy can lead to recurrence of TB in patients.[31] Although the results of this study showed that the course of chemotherapy was not an independent risk factor for complications of thoracolumbar spinal TB surgery, 4 of the 13 recurrent cases, drug therapy was discontinued without approval 3 months after operation, which was one of the important factors leading to the recurrence of thoracolumbar spinal TB. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the publicity and education of the patients, and timely follow up of the discharged patients.

Thoracolumbar spinal TB is characterized by severe bone damage, extensive lesions, and frequently associated with compression of the spinal cord.[32] In particular, some patients have multi-segmental skip lesions, which are difficult to remove pathological tissues and develop internal fixation schemes. Most of the patients with multiple diseased segments and severe bone destruction will be complicated with kyphosis, which further increases the difficulty of surgery and the risk of postoperative complications.[33] Moreover, the number of diseased segments was correlated with the bleeding volume during operation, intraoperative blood loss is also a risk factor for surgery-related complications of spinal TB.[34] Nevertheless, in this study, intraoperative blood loss was not an independent risk factor for complications after complete debridement of thoracolumbar spinal TB. We speculated this discrepancy might be attributed to intraoperative blood transfusion weakens the influence of intraoperative blood loss on the body. Due to the poor nutritional conditions caused by long-term disease consumption, combined with extensive intraoperative blood loss and blood transfusion, patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB are prone to serious postoperative complications such as electrolyte disturbance and hypoproteinemia, which increase the risk of poor wound healing after surgery.[35]

5. Limitations

We acknowledge that our study suffers from several limitations. First, there could be other risk factors we did not include. Second, in this study, only the risk factors of postoperative complications in patients with traditional open and complete debridement were analyzed, and minimally invasive surgery was not included. Third, because of the small size of the sample, it is necessary to carry out more research on multicenter and large sample. Finally, a key limitation of this study is its retrospective observational design, which prevents us from establishing causal relationships between the identified risk factors and postoperative complications. While we adjusted for potential confounders in our regression models, residual confounding and unmeasured variables may still influence the observed associations, and it is necessary to confirm it in clinical practice. These limitations need to be further investigated in order to verify the results.

6. Conclusions

In our study, the results of univariate analysis and binary multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that the number of diseased segments ≥3 may increase the risk of complications in patients with thoracolumbar spinal TB after complete debridement. Therefore, it is necessary to make a comprehensive evaluation of nutrition in patients who have multiple diseased segments or skip disease, and to formulate individualized perioperative management plans and proper surgery programs. During the operation, the surgeon should be skillful and cautious in order to minimize the bleeding and to avoid complications.

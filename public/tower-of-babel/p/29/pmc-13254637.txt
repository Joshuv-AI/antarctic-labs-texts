First-in-human European experience with novel 22-G end-cutting fine-needle biopsy system: a prospective observational pilot study
Authors: Dominik Julian van de Loo, Michael Tischendorf, Feras Sanoubara, Dina Khalil, Juliana Stadtmann, Frank Erhard Uschner, Kai Henrik Peiffer, Inga Grünewald, Wim Laleman, Jonel Trebicka, Barbara Braden, Michael Praktiknjo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsEndoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided biopsy remains a cornerstone for diagnosing and staging gastrointestinal diseases. Various systems are available, with needle designs for fine-needle aspiration and fine-needle biopsy (FNB) and gauges from 25-G to 19-G. We aimed to report the first European human experience with a novel 22-G end-cutting FNB system (EchoTip ClearCore; Cook Medical).MethodsThis prospective observational study was conducted at our tertiary endoscopy center between November and December 2024. All patients undergoing EUS-guided puncture with the 22-G EchoTip ClearCore needle were included. User and patient experience were assessed using questionnaires. Clinical data were obtained from electronic hospital records.ResultsTen patients underwent 15 EUS-guided biopsies. The median patient age was 57 (range, 18–77) years, and 60% were male. Indications included liver biopsy for elevated liver enzymes or post-transplantation assessment (67%), evaluation of pancreatic lesions (20%), and sampling of pathological lymph nodes (13%). The technical success rate was 100%. Specimen quality was sufficient for diagnosis in 93% of cases. Operators rated the needle highly for EUS visibility (8.8/10), maneuverability and stability (7.9/10), and ease of tissue penetration (8.3/10). Overall patient satisfaction was high (5/5); no complications occurred.ConclusionsThe 22-G EchoTip ClearCore needle is safe and effective, demonstrating excellent technical and clinical outcomes.Keywords: Endoscopic ultrasonography, Fine needle biopsy, Patient reported outcome measure

INTRODUCTION

Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided fine-needle biopsy (FNB) is a minimally invasive procedure for obtaining tissue samples from various lesions within the gastrointestinal (GI) wall, pancreatic region, and adjacent organs.

The technical success and diagnostic yield of EUS-FNB largely depend on the needle design and functionality. The superior performance of FNB needles has driven a shift away from traditional fine-needle aspiration (FNA) for tissue sampling.1 The “end-cutting” EUS-FNB needles demonstrate excellent diagnostic accuracy (>90%) and a low incidence of adverse events.2 The Franseen-type tip geometry is a high-performing FNB design, recognized for yielding superior sample quality.3 This design features three symmetrically distributed points and cutting edges, which effectively capture tissue cores within the needle.

The EchoTip ClearCore (Cook Medical) is a novel 22-G end-cutting FNB system with a Franseen-type tip. Although recently introduced and approved for use in the United States, the device was not yet launched in Europe at the time of this study. Consequently, European clinical experience with this new system is lacking.

From the endoscopist's perspective, the optimal performance of EUS-FNB needles depends on several key features. High visibility of the needle tip, maneuverability, and ease of tissue penetration are essential for safe and precise tissue sampling, minimizing the risk of puncturing non-target structures, particularly in vascular-rich areas such as the pancreatic head. Resistance to bending is also crucial, as a bent needle shaft after first pass can compromise placement during subsequent passes. Finally, easy handling of the device is important for the nursing staff. According to the manufacturer, the ClearCore needle’s design advantages include: a flexible sheath with a spring-coil design (Fig. 1A); a traditional handle for a solid grip (Fig. 1B); a cobalt-chrome skeleton to reduce bending; and a self-coiling stylet mechanism for easier handling by nursing staff (Fig. 1C).

Patient-reported experience measures (PREMs)—which assess factors such as pain, discomfort, and overall satisfaction—are increasingly integrated into procedural evaluations. These measures provide a more comprehensive understanding of the patient’s experience and can enhance future diagnostic and treatment approaches.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the first-in-human European experience with the novel 22-G ClearCore FNB needle by assessing three primary domains: (1) technical success, specimen quality and safety; (2) user experience; and (3) PREMs.

METHODS

Study design

This single-arm case series was conducted at a tertiary university hospital in Germany. The study took place in December 2024 during the first-in-human testing phase of a novel 22-G end-cutting FNB needle (EchoTip ClearCore). The assessed outcomes included technical success, safety, user experience, and PREMs. As this was a single-arm study, the sample size calculation was based on a previous comparison of 19- and 22-G FNB needles with an effect size (d) of 1.03 for biopsy length in liver biopsies. A sample size of 15 biopsies was calculated using G*Power ver. 3 (Heinrich Heine University).

Patient selection

Patients were prospectively and consecutively enrolled based on the following inclusion criteria: individuals aged 18 years or older; an indication for EUS-guided biopsy of any abdominal organ (e.g., pancreas, lymph nodes, liver) for histologic diagnostic purposes; and the ability to provide informed consent and complete a post-procedural questionnaire.

For the subset of patients who underwent EUS-liver biopsy (EUS-LB), a control group was 1:1 matched. This group consisted of patients who had undergone EUS-LB with other commercially available 22-G FNB needles with a Franseen tip and were matched by age (±5 years), sex, and indication for FNB.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: coagulopathy (international normalized ratio >1.5 or platelet count <50,000/µL); a known allergy to sedatives or anesthetics used during the procedure; or severe comorbidities contraindicating endoscopy or sedation.

Procedure

All EUS-guided biopsies were performed by two gastroenterologists with experience in EUS (M.P. and B.B.) following a standardized protocol. Patients were sedated using intravenous propofol according to institutional guidelines. Target lesions were identified and characterized using EUS, including doppler ultrasound, to identify any intervening blood vessels. For each EUS-FNB, up to three passes were performed using a fanning technique. Each pass involved at least three to-and-fro movements and the use of wet suction (10 mL negative pressure) to optimize tissue yield while minimizing procedural risk. Rapid on-site examinations were not performed.

Data collection and outcome parameters

Baseline characteristics (age, sex, biopsy indication, biopsy site) and procedural details (number of passes, sample adequacy, immediate adverse events) were collected.

Technical success and specimen quality

Technical success was defined as advancing the needle tip into the desired target, while specimen quality was defined by diagnostic yield. In the EUS-LB subgroup, the number of portal tracts in the biopsy specimen was assessed by an expert liver pathologist (I.G.).

Safety

All adverse events were recorded.

User experience

Immediately after each procedure, endoscopists completed a questionnaire (Supplementary Material 1) rating the following parameters on a scale of 1 to 10 (10 being the best possible result): needle tip visibility, maneuverability, stability, ease of tissue penetration, handling of locking mechanisms, and resistance to bending after the first pass. The endoscopy nurses separately scored the handling of the stylet. For the subset of patients who underwent two FNBs in the same session, the needle's resistance to bending was assessed during the second pass.

PREMs

Patients completed a modified version of the validated Newcastle EndoPREM questionnaire4 24 to 48 hours post-procedure (Supplementary Material 2). This questionnaire assessed procedural pain (visual analog scale, 0–10), discomfort during and after the procedure, and overall satisfaction (Likert scale, very dissatisfied to very satisfied). The Newcastle EndoPREM, originally designed for colonoscopies, was adapted for this upper-GI endoscopy study by removing the section on bowel preparation and making other minor changes regarding the examination.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and reported outcomes. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables are presented as median with ranges. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS ver. 26.0 (IBM Corp.).

Ethics approval

The study received approval from the ethics committee of the Ärztekammer Westfalen-Lippe (2023-725-f-S, Ärztekammer Westfalen-Lippe), and all participants provided written informed consent before enrolment.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

Overall, 15 EUS-guided FNB procedures were performed in 10 patients (6 men, 4 women) using the novel 22-G FNB needle (Table 1). The median patient age was 57 (range, 18–77) years. One patient underwent biopsies of the liver (for suspected porto-sinusoidal vascular disease) and the pancreas (for suspected IgG4-related pancreatitis). One patient (10%) was on oral anticoagulation, which was paused before the intervention. Two patients (20%) were on antiplatelet medication, which was continued (Table 2).

Technical success, specimen quality and safety

The indications for EUS-FNB were suspected liver disease (n=10), indeterminate pancreatic masses (n=3), and enlarged abdominal lymph nodes (n=2). All liver biopsies were performed transgastrically into the left liver lobe, while all pancreatic and lymph node biopsies were performed transduodenally. Technical success, defined as reaching the intended target with the EUS needle, was achieved in all 15 procedures (100%) (Fig. 2). The tissue sample quality was sufficient for pathological diagnosis in 14 of 15 biopsies (93%) (Fig. 3). In the EUS-LB subgroup, the mean number of portal tracts was 11 (range, 3–24) in the study group compared to 6 (range, 1–9) in the matched control group (p=0.062). No major adverse events, such as bleeding or infection, were reported.

User experience

Endoscopist ratings of the user experience are summarized in Table 3. The mean scores were as follows: needle tip visibility in B-mode EUS, 8.8 (7–9); maneuverability and stability, 7.9 (7–8); ease of tissue penetration, 8.3 (6–9); handling of locking mechanisms, 7.0 (7–7); and resistance to bending, 7.7 (7–8). Endoscopy nurses separately rated the handling of the stylet, yielding a mean score of 9.9 (9–10). In the subgroup of patients who underwent a second pass with the same needle, no significant change in the needle's resistance to bending was observed.

Patient-reported experience measures

The median post-procedural pain score was 2.4/10 (range, 0–7), with two patients (20%) requiring pain medication. Patients reported no post-procedural discomfort 0/10 (range, 0–0) and overall satisfaction was high, with a median score of 5/5 (range, 5–5).

In the EUS-LB subgroup analysis, the study group reported significantly lower post-procedural discomfort compared to the matched control group (0 [range, 0–0] vs. 0.8 [range, 0–8]; p=0.003, respectively) and dissatisfaction (0 [range, 0–0] vs. 0.9 [range, 0–3]; p=0.007, respectively). In contrast, post-procedural pain levels were similar between the two groups (2.5 [range, 0–7] vs. 1.5 [range, 0–3]; p=0.485) (Fig. 4).

DISCUSSION

Our case series presents the first-in-human European experience with a novel 22-G end-cutting FNB system across various indications. Our results show high technical success rate, high diagnostic yield, positive user experience, improved patient experience, and strong safety profile, which are comparable or superior to those of top-performing 22-G Franseen-type FNB needles.

The European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy recommends FNA and FNB needles for EUS-guided tissue sampling based on previous meta-analyses that demonstrated comparable diagnostic accuracy between the two needle types.5-7 However, Gkolfakis et al.3 concluded that the Franseen-type FNB needles, particularly the 22-G size, were among the top performers, with a diagnostic accuracy of 89% and a sample adequacy of 94% for the Franseen tip.

Our 100% technical success rate suggests that the ClearCore FNB needle has sufficient flexibility to reach the target area. The 93% diagnostic yield is comparable to that of the top-performing needles in the market.2 This result is noteworthy because our study included a high rate of untargeted liver biopsies, a setting for which 19-G FNB needles have been suggested as the standard of care.8 Despite its smaller diameter, the 22-G ClearCore FNB needle achieved a mean of 11 portal tracts. This number is a traditionally accepted surrogate for an adequate liver specimen for pathological assessment.9 In the matched control group, various 22-G Franseen-type needles yielded a mean of only six portal tracts. Notably, the diagnostic yield is subject to multiple confounders, including the type of targeted lesion and the operator’s experience. Importantly, no major adverse events were observed in our case series.

In addition to its excellent diagnostic yield, the needle provided a positive user experience. All assessed aspects were rated positively, supporting the promising overall technical performance of the needle. A common clinical challenge is needle bending during the first pass, particularly during elevator use in tight spaces such as transduodenal punctures. Our results suggest that the ClearCore needle shows no significant difference in visibility, maneuverability, or bending after the first pass. Consequently, only one needle is required for multiple punctures with this system. A key strength of our study is the assessment of the nursing staff's experience. The handling of the needle was rated as excellent due to the self-coiling stylet design.

The assessment of PREMs is another strength of our study. Although clinical efficacy and technical performance are critical, understanding the patient perspective provides a holistic assessment of the technique’s impact. Our results demonstrate high patient tolerability and comfort. In the EUS-LB subgroup, ClearCore needle was associated with less discomfort and greater overall satisfaction compared to that of the control group. This finding suggests that the 22-G ClearCore FNB needle contributes positively to the overall patient experience.

Although our results are promising, the small sample size (15 biopsies in 10 patients) underscores its role as a European first-in-human pilot study. The diverse target organs and indications may limit the ability to draw definitive conclusions for each indication; however, this diversity also supports the potential universal applicability of the needle. Larger multicenter studies are needed to definitively establish the diagnostic potential of the needle.

In conclusion, our pilot study demonstrates that the novel 22-G end-cutting EchoTip ClearCore FNB needle provides excellent performance and high patient satisfaction in EUS-guided biopsies across various indications. These findings establish it as a promising tool for minimally invasive diagnosis.

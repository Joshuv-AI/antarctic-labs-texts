Multi video stream collaborative adaptive offloading scheme based on equilibrium game theory
Authors: Shijun Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Massive video stream transmission and analysis require a large amount of bandwidth and computing resources, which poses a serious challenge to the current video stream offloading scheme based on mobile edge computing (MEC). A self-adaptive offloading scheme based on a balanced game multi video stream collaborative optimization framework is proposed for this purpose. Firstly, under the constraint of long-term MEC energy budget, the processing cost of video tasks is minimized by jointly optimizing data stream selection decisions, server offloading decisions, bandwidth resource allocation, and computing resource allocation. Secondly, an adaptive task offloading algorithm based on game theory and Nash equilibrium is designed to achieve optimal node selection through offloading algorithm and weight allocation, minimizing the processing cost of the entire multi video stream system and balancing video stream computation latency and energy consumption; Finally, the simulation results show that the proposed scheme satisfies long-term MEC energy constraints while significantly outperforming existing benchmark research schemes in terms of cost performance.

Introduction

Video accounts for an increasing proportion of mobile network traffic. According to Ericsson, by 2028, video traffic will account for 80% of the global mobile data traffic. With the rise of video applications, the demand for computing with lower delay, larger bandwidth and higher density is also increasing. At the same time, the demand for data processing energy consumption is also higher.1 In video related business scenarios, mobile edge computing (MEC) plays an important role, and its core value is to provide low delay services for video tasks. However, when faced with large-scale and high bit rate compressed video streaming transmission and processing tasks, the network bandwidth and edge computing resources are put forward stringent requirements. Due to many limitations in computing power, communication bandwidth, storage capacity and other resources, the existing multimedia processing framework has been unable to meet the needs of large-scale video analysis.2

In order to address the above issues, some research works have proposed a novel multi video stream framework for multimedia systems and smart cities, which replaces traditional compressed video streams by mixing compressed video streams, feature streams, and semantic streams.3 In these frameworks, compressed video streams primarily serve human vision, feature streams extract compact feature data in real-time from raw videos with significantly lower bit rates than compressed video streams, primarily serving machine vision, while semantic streams provide semantic and information perception to facilitate task processing. This framework can support various applications simultaneously, such as object recognition and manually assisted traffic violation monitoring, as both feature data and visual data are available.4 When transferring compact feature streams to edge servers for processing, the bit—rate characteristics of these feature streams are considerably lower compared to those of compressed video streams. This substantial difference leads to a marked decrease in the bandwidth demands for data transmission. Moreover, the subsequent sophisticated data analysis procedures are grounded in feature data and semantic data. Instead of undertaking the resource—intensive task of reconstructing the original video pixels from compressed video content, the analysis focuses on leveraging the extracted high—level information embedded in the feature and semantic data. This approach not only streamlines the analysis workflow but also enhances the efficiency of resource utilization in the edge—computing environment.5 This approach yields substantial savings in computing and caching resources. Notwithstanding the merits of feature flow, conducting feature extraction on terminal devices may give rise to increased computational latency and energy usage. The rationale lies in the fact that feature extraction tasks typically necessitate intricate computations and data manipulations, which strain the relatively constrained computational capabilities of terminal devices, consequently causing latency spikes and elevated power drain.6 Hence, an adaptive offloading scheme holds significant importance for the MEC-based multi-video stream collaborative optimization framework.7 This solution can select the most suitable data stream for offloading in real-time based on limited computing power and bandwidth resources.

Comparison of literature research.

The above research mainly focuses on designing efficient task offloading and resource allocation schemes in different MEC scenarios.23 These studies indicate that MEC supported task offloading and resource allocation can effectively reduce system latency and energy consumption. However, task offloading also introduces additional MEC energy consumption. Compared with the traditional MEC video stream unloading scheme, the innovations of the algorithm proposed in this paper lie in: (1) The comprehensiveness of optimization objectives and constraints. The most advanced methods in existence tend to focus on a single goal when optimizing the video stream unloading scheme. The scheme proposed in this paper comprehensively considers many factors, such as data stream selection decision, server unloading decision, bandwidth resource allocation and computing resource allocation, under the condition of meeting the long-term MEC energy budget constraint, and avoids the problem of other performance indicators degradation caused by single goal optimization. (2) Design of adaptive task unloading algorithm. The existing methods usually use fixed rules or simple heuristic algorithms in the task unloading decision, which is difficult to dynamically adjust according to the real-time state of the system. This paper designs an adaptive task unloading algorithm based on game theory and Nash equilibrium. By introducing game theory, the algorithm models the task unloading problem of multi video stream system as a game process. Each video stream, as a game participant, dynamically adjusts the unloading strategy according to its own interests and the overall state of the system. (3) Balance of system performance. When dealing with multiple video stream tasks, the existing methods are often difficult to balance the relationship between video stream calculation delay and energy consumption. Some methods may pursue low delay too much and ignore the increase of energy consumption; Other methods may lead to long calculation delay in order to reduce energy consumption. The scheme proposed in this paper achieves the balance between the calculation delay and energy consumption of video streams through the balanced game multi video stream collaborative optimization framework.

Our main contributions are as follows: (1) A multi video stream collaborative optimization framework for joint compression of video streams, feature streams, and semantic streams was studied, aiming to propose a MEC based multi video stream adaptive offloading scheme to efficiently handle video tasks of heterogeneous terminal devices. (2) This article models the problem of multi video stream offloading and resource allocation optimization, and achieves the average task processing cost by optimizing data stream selection decisions, server offloading decisions, computing resource allocation, and bandwidth resource allocation, while minimizing the weighted sum of task processing latency and terminal device energy consumption under long-term MEC energy constraints. (3) Due to the randomness of wireless channels and system resources, as well as the interdependence of optimization variables at different time slots, the established problem involves stochastic optimization of multi-stage sequence decision-making, which is difficult to solve directly. To solve this problem, this paper minimizes the processing cost of video tasks by jointly optimizing data stream selection decisions, server offloading decisions, bandwidth resource allocation, and computing resource allocation while satisfying the constraints of long-term MEC energy budget. An adaptive task offloading algorithm based on game theory and Nash equilibrium is designed to select the optimal computing node through offloading algorithm and weight allocation, achieving the minimum processing cost of the entire multi video stream system and balancing video stream computation latency and energy consumption.

When the terminal device u unloads the feature stream to the edge server m, feature extraction needs to be performed locally first. Problem description

Processing real-time video tasks not only consumes high energy, but is also extremely sensitive to latency. Therefore, when designing adaptive solutions, the goal is to minimize the average task processing cost of the weighted sum of task processing latency and terminal device energy consumption while meeting the constraints of long-term MEC energy budget. Offloading of computational tasks based on game theory

Game theory is a mathematical method for selecting a strategy that maximizes the profits of each agent in the presence of multiple parties.27 In a game, the insteractions between players are mutual, and each player’s decision will have an impact on other players in the game. The purpose of game theory is to balance the interests of each player in each game with sufficient rationality, and achieve the maximization of the interests of each player in each game.

Mathematical model

The mathematical model of game theory mainly includes three elements: players, strategy set, and utility function, represented as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi (N,I,\partial )$$\end{document}. N is the set of players in the game, I represents the set of players offloading pure strategies, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\partial$$\end{document} is the utility function of the strategy combination in this situation.

(1) Bureau personnel: also known as decision-makers. An individual or group that can decide their own course of action during a game. One characteristic of game theory is that each player in the game is srational”, meaning that each player will choose their most suitable strategy based on the decisions of other players in order to maximize their own interests and there is no sense of luck (thinking that other players have made wrong decisions).

(2) Strategy set: A set of decisions in a game that players can choose from, including all the decisions made by players in a certain situation. The policy set is divided into pure policy set and mixed policy set. Given information, if only specific decisions can be selected from the policy set, it is called a pure policy set; if a certain probability is selected from the policy set, it is called a mixed policy set.

(3) Utility function: It represents the mapping relationship between a comparable value and the benefits of players in a given situation. Utility function is the fundamental basis for making decisions in game situations.

In the decision-making process of unloading multiple data streams, in order to ensure that the system can efficiently and reasonably handle all kinds of video streams, we do not simply prioritize the streams. Conclusion

This article focuses on the scenario of mobile edge video stream offloading analysis, designs a cloud edge offloading framework, and proposes a data-driven stage optimization offloading algorithm that can adaptively balance the computing power requirements of multiple clients. Through basic experiments and simulation experiments, the operating status of a system composed of multiple clients and a single server is simulated. The experimental results show that compared to other offloading schemes, the proposed scheme in this paper can better balance the resource competition between multiple terminals, complete video analysis tasks more effectively, and reduce video data loss. In addition, this framework also takes into account the real-time video and energy consumption and other practical factors. It can configure computing resources according to the needs of different clients, control the stable operation of multiple clients, and has strong scalability. It can adaptively adjust the unloading strategy according to the number of clients, and propose a feasible task unloading scheme for the multi terminal cloud side system.

The experimental results show that the proposed algorithm has good practical application value for AR/VR and video surveillance scenes involving real-time processing requirements. The scheme can dynamically adjust the unloading strategy of video stream according to the needs of users and the real-time state of the system to ensure the real-time transmission and processing of video stream. For example, in AR tourism applications, when users move rapidly, the scheme can quickly select the appropriate edge server to unload the video stream, ensuring the real-time update of the navigation information, making users seem to be on the spot. In the video monitoring scene, the proposed scheme can reasonably allocate bandwidth and computing resources, give priority to the video stream in key areas, and improve the efficiency and reliability of the monitoring system. For example, in the urban traffic monitoring system, the scheme will allocate more resources to deal with the video stream of congested roads and accident-prone areas, so that the traffic management department can timely understand the road conditions and take effective dredging measures.

Considering that in real application scenarios, there are often occasional client access or disconnection during system operation, which may cause instability to the system. Although the uninstallation scheme proposed in this article has strong scalability, it is currently unable to maintain system stability after adding or disconnecting certain clients during system operation. In addition, game theory decision-making involves a large amount of computation, which may have adverse effects on latency, which is a key indicator in MEC systems. In future work, specific research can be conducted on the above-mentioned issues.

Reply to the letter regarding the article ‘Effects of geranylgeranylacetone on diastolic and microvascular function in patients with heart failure with a preserved ejection fraction: A phase 2, randomized, placebo‐controlled, crossover trial’
Authors: Soufiane Nassiri, Geert HD Voordes, M Louis Handoko, Adriaan A Voors
Published: 2025

We sincerely thank Yang et al. for their interest in our recent publication on the effects of geranylgeranylacetone (GGA) in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) 1 and for taking the time to share their thoughts. We appreciate the opportunity to clarify several points raised in their correspondence.

Yang et al. emphasize the importance of mechanistic validation and cite the work of van Marion et al. 2 involving heat shock protein (HSP) induction in atrial tissue obtained during cardiac surgery. HSP70 and HSP90 are intracellular proteins which are difficult to measure in plasma. While we fully agree that mechanistic insights are valuable, direct atrial tissue sampling was not feasible in the context of a minimally invasive, non‐surgical clinical study such as GLADIATOR. Similarly, nitric oxide (NO) biomarker assessment in plasma faces challenges in sensitivity and specificity. To strengthen our NO analysis, complementary approaches such as endothelial function testing (e.g. EndoPAT and LASCA) and the use of advanced detection methods for NO metabolites and related molecules could provide additional clinical relevance while respecting study constraints. This approach balances mechanistic insight with practical feasibility in clinical research settings.

Yang et al. show concern about potential carryover effects for GGA, which they derive from pre‐clinical evidence, but do not cite any evidence. We carefully considered the issue of carryover in the trial design, implementing a 6‐week washout period, which is consistent with other crossover studies in HFpEF and was feasible according to the investigative medical product dossier. 3 , 4 , 5 Importantly, we observed no evidence in our data suggesting residual treatment effects across periods.

Lastly, we appreciate the point raised regarding HFpEF heterogeneity, which we have acknowledged this limitation in our discussion. As the primary aim of this study was to evaluate the overall effect of GGA in a HFpEF population, the study was not powered for in‐depth subgroup analyses. While additional analyses such as a subgroup analysis illustrated with a forest plot might have been informative and are very interesting, the study was not powered for subgroup analyses and these subgroup analyses would not alter the conclusions of the main trial.

In summary, while we welcome continued dialogue on the challenges of HFpEF research, we respectfully disagree that the issues raised undermine the validity of our findings. We believe the letter by Yang et al. does not provide substantial new insights beyond those already acknowledged in our manuscript.

Impact of tranexamic acid on early allograft dysfunction following liver transplantation: study design for a prospective randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yajie Zhang, Xiaozhen Wei, Haibei Liu, Yanhua Qiu, Qi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHemorrhage and coagulation management are critical during liver transplantation (LT). Hyperfibrinolysis is a major contributor to perioperative blood loss. Tranexamic acid (TA), an antifibrinolytic agent, has been reported to reduce transfusion requirements during LT. Several studies have demonstrated that intraoperative transfusion is independently associated with early graft dysfunction (EAD), which may prolong recovery, increase sepsis incidence, and elevate mortality. However, clinical evidence regarding the prophylactic administration of TA for reducing EAD in LT remains limited. This study aims to test the hypothesis that prophylactic TA use during LT can reduce the incidence of EAD.MethodsThis is a prospective, single-center, randomized, parallel-group study. Two hundred sixty-eight adult participants scheduled for liver transplantation under general anesthesia will be included in this study. In the TA group, participants will receive a continuous infusion of TA (10 mg/kg/h) from anesthesia induction until portal vein unclamping. In the placebo group, an equivalent volume of 0.9% saline will be administered in the same manner. Anesthesia protocol will be identical in both groups and conducted according to routine clinical practice.DiscussionTo our knowledge, this is the first prospective randomized controlled trial to investigate whether prophylactic use of TA is effective in reducing EAD incidence for LT recipients.Trial registrationThis trial is registered at www.chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2300079022). Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Liver transplantation (LT) is the ultimate treatment for patients with end-stage liver disease. With improving surgical techniques and perioperative management, prognosis for LT patients has improved to 5-year patient survival rates of 72–84% [1]. Nevertheless, blood loss and coagulation issue remain serious concerns during LT. Hyperfibrinolysis is one of the most important causes of hemorrhage during LT [2–4]. Tranexamic acid (TA) is a fibrinolysis inhibitor that prevents the binding of plasminogen to fibrin [5]; several studies have proven its effectiveness in reducing intraoperative bleeding among different surgical procedures. In trauma patients with high risk of bleeding, a few multi-center researches showed prophylactic administration of TA reduced mortality [6–8]. For LT recipients, previous clinical trials have demonstrated the effect of TA in decreasing blood loss and transfusion requirements without increasing the risk of thromboembolic events [9–12]. Early allograft dysfunction (EAD) in the postoperative period indicates hepatocellular injury and synthetic impairment; it is also a strong predictor of poor graft function and patient survival [13–16]. As such, EAD serves as an important surrogate end point in the majority of LT clinical trials. The incidence of EAD ranges from 9.3 to 43.7% worldwide [17, 18]. The formation of EAD is multifactorial; preceding research finds EAD may associate with cold ischemia time, graft quality, donor age, recipient’s MELD score, and donation type (donation after brain death vs donation after cardiac death) [17, 19]. However, those factors are usually not easily modifiable in clinical practice. It is noteworthy that some researchers verified the association between EAD and blood transfusion requirements [19, 20]. As previous clinical evidence shows, TA has the efficacy of decreasing perioperative transfusion requirements; we assume that TA may have a beneficial effect on the EAD incidence after LT. Given these clinical findings, it is necessary to further explore the impact of TA on EAD after LT.

Objectives {7}

The current randomized controlled trial explores the effect of TA on recipients’ EAD incidence after LT surgery. The primary objective is to compare the EAD incidence within 7 days after LT. The secondary objectives of this trial are to evaluate the safety of TA administration during LT, including thromboembolic events and other drug-related adverse events. In addition, the volume of red blood cell suspensions (RBCs), fresh frozen plasma (FFP), platelets, and cryoprecipitate transfusion throughout the procedure and within 7 days after surgery will be recorded. Finally, the incidence of postoperative AKI within 7 days after surgery, length of hospital stay, and total hospitalization expenses will also be studied.

Trial design {8}

This is a prospective, single-center, randomized, parallel-group study. The patient allocation ratio is 1:1.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

The day before surgery (for emergency case, screening will start as soon as the patient completes the admission procedure), fully trained investigators who are responsible for the screening process will screen potential participants following the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Participants who conform to the inclusion/exclusion criteria will be enrolled in this study. Investigators will explain the purpose, procedures, and potential benefits and risks of the study in detail to every potential participant through a written informed consent. Every participant should be informed that he/she has the right to withdraw his/her consent at any time during the study period. Written informed consents will be well preserved as part of the clinical trial documents.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

After anesthesia induction, participants in the control group will receive continuous infusion of the same volume of saline until the portal vein is unclamped.

Intervention description {11a}

Once informed consents are obtained and the preoperative assessments are completed, patients will be enrolled into the trial sequentially. An anesthesiologist who is not involved in the outcome assessment prepares and dispenses the study drugs in identical sealed boxes according to the randomization sequence. Participants are sequentially assigned to the group receiving either TA or placebo (0.9% saline) according to the number in chronological order of surgery time. Anesthesia and surgery procedures are standard among all participants according to the clinical routine in our hospital. After anesthesia induction, patients in the TA group receive continuous infusion of TA (10 mg/kg/h) until the portal vein is unclamped, and patients in the control group receive the same volume of 0.9% saline following the same schedule. Intraoperative transfusion of blood products, administration of fibrinogen concentrates and prothrombin complex is reserved for situations with active bleeding and abnormal laboratory tests or TEG results. The transfusion threshold for RBC is a hemoglobin value of 7 g/dL. For patients with underlying cardiovascular disease, a more liberal transfusion regime with a threshold of 8 g/dL is implemented.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Patients can withdraw from the study at any time if they wish to do so. The investigator can terminate the participation of the patient if the patient is uncooperative. For the occurrence of any severe adverse event, except active treatment and documentation, the anesthesiologist can request termination of the intervention according to the patient’s condition. The investigator should contact the principal investigator and the Ethics Committee within 24 h. In case of study drug-related death, the clinical trial should immediately stop and report the event to the Ethics Committee promptly. All the related information should be recorded in detail and carefully preserve the related documents. Restart of the trial will be decided by the Ethics Committee.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

The adherence to this protocol will be high as the intervention drug is provided after anesthetic induction and will be ended before the surgery. This process will be supervised by the designated anesthesiologist to assure the drug is administered in accordance with the protocol.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

There are no relevant concomitant care and interventions that are permitted or prohibited during the trial.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

There are no provisions for post-trial care.

Sample size {14}

The sample size was calculated based on the previous researches about EAD after LT. Prior to our study, the incidence of EAD after LT was found to be 36% in our center [22]. According to previous research [23], we assume that the incidence of EAD will be lowered from 36 to 20% in the TA group. The calculated sample size is a minimum of 240 patients (120 patients per group) at a significance level of 5% and power of 80%. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, we will recruit 268 patients (134 patients per group).

Recruitment {15}

Patients will be recruited at West China Hospital, Sichuan University in China. We accomplish more than 100 liver transplantation surgeries in our hospital annually.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

An independent statistician will generate a random sequence of 0 and 1 in a 1:1 fashion correspondent to numbers from 1 to 268. The results of allocation will be stored in the computer until the study is accomplished. Once informed consent has been obtained and the preoperative assessments are completed, patients will be enrolled into the trial sequentially.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

According to the allocation sequence, an anesthesiologist who will not involve in the rest of the study will provide the trial drugs to the participating operation rooms in identical sealed boxes with a sequentially number marked on it. Participants will be sequentially assigned to the group receiving either TA or placebo (normal saline) according to the number in chronological order of surgery time. Investigators responsible for postoperative follow-up will not participate in participants’ perioperative care management. Both anesthesiologist(s) and investigator(s) do not communicate while collecting and analyzing results. Study team members, healthcare providers, and participants will be blinded to the group allocation during the study period.

Implementation {16c}

An independent statistician will generate the allocation sequence, an investigator who is responsible for attaining informed consent will enrol participants, and an anesthesiologist who will not be involved in the rest of the study will provide the trial drugs to the participating operation rooms.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Study team members, healthcare providers, and participants will be blinded to the group allocation during the study period.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

If any emergency situation occurs, the anesthesiologist can request unblinding of the treatment allocation if necessary. The particular reasons for such events will be recorded. In addition, these unblinding cases will be included in the intention-to-treat analysis.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Data will be expressed as means ± standard deviation or numbers (percentages). Numeric variables including laboratory results will be presented as mean (standard deviation) or median (minimum, maximum; or interquartile range), and independent samples t test will be used to analyze the difference of numeric variables between groups. Categorical variables like the incidence of EAD, AKI, and thromboembolic events after LT will be presented as numbers (percentage), and chi-square test will be used to analyze the difference between categorical variables groups. Two-tailed tests will be used in statistical analysis, and p values of less than 0.05 will be considered to be of statistical significance. All analyses will be conducted according to intention-to-treat principle.

Interim analyses {21b}

There are no interim analyses planned.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

There are no interim analyses planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Missing data will be decreased to a minimum by employing the means described above. The outcomes will be assessed based on intention-to-treat analysis. Multiple imputation will be used if any statistical method is needed to illustrate missing data.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first randomized controlled trial focusing on the effect of tranexamic acid on EAD following liver transplantation. The safety issue of prophylactic use of TA during LT, such as the incidence of thromboembolic events and other related adverse events, will also be comprehensively explored. In addition, transfusion requirement, AKI incidence, length of hospital stay, and total hospitalization expenses will also be important components of our study.

EAD incidence after LT is closely related to the prognosis of LT recipient. Preceding studies illustrated TA infusion during LT surgery could reduce blood loss and perioperative transfusion requirement at a large dose (40 mg/kg/h) [24]; a small dose (< 5 mg/kg/h) decreased fibrinolysis but not transfusion requirements [25, 26]. Subsequent research further explored the appropriate dosage of TA during LT. Dalmau et al. demonstrated TA decreased red blood cell transfusion using a medium dose of TA (10 mg/kg/h) [8]. Therefore, a medium or large dosage of TA, but not a small dosage, is capable to provide a significant reduction of transfusion requirement during LT. In our study, concerning the conventional dosage of TA and thromboembolic risk, we employ the medium dose (10 mg/kg/h) as intervention. Although reams of data have suggested blood transfusion volume is negatively related to the prognosis among different types of surgeries, the association between blood transfusion and EAD after LT surgery has not been comprehensively investigated. Several studies reported the intraoperative red blood cell transfusion volume was one of several EAD-associated risk factors [17, 21]. Thus, our study aims to investigate whether prophylactic use of TA will decrease EAD incidence after LT; the results may provide valuable advice for routine use of antifibrinolytics during LT surgery.

In liver transplantation, the substantial risk of major hemorrhage remains a primary concern, driving ongoing efforts to optimize hemostatic strategies. The use of antifibrinolytics to reduce surgical blood loss has long been accompanied by debate over their potential prothrombotic effect and associated serious complications. Recently, there has been growing concern among clinicians regarding the risk of vascular thromboembolic complications, including venous thrombosis, hepatic artery thrombosis, and pulmonary embolism [27–29]. Preexisted coagulation disorders, excessive activation of the coagulation system during LT surgery, anatomic reconstruction of the hepatic vessels, activators released from the graft, and massive blood loss may all contribute to this increased risk for thromboembolic complications [30]. Although several studies testified to the effectiveness of TA on reducing blood transfusion requirements during LT surgery, the routine use of antifibrinolytic is still under controversy concerning the risk of thromboembolic complications [31]. In our study, the incidence of thromboembolic complications after LT will be specifically focused, the result may provide some useful information to clinical practice.

Yet, our study may confront some deficiencies. Firstly, most of the LT surgeries in our center are emergency, so the responsible anesthesiologist for LT cannot be confirmed in advance. In order to keep the consistency of the anesthetic protocol for each participant, two anesthesiologists are assigned to communicate with the responsible anesthesiologist and to ensure the anesthetic process is accordant for each participant. Secondly, this is a single-center clinical trial, the pathogenesis of participants in our center undergoing LT is mostly attributed to hepatitis B infection and/or hepatocellular carcinoma, which is different from the condition in other countries. For instance, the most common indications for liver transplantation in the USA are hepatitis C infection and alcoholic liver disease [32]. Hence, the heterogeneity of recipients’ pathogenesis may induce some effect on the generalizability of this trial’s results worldwide.

In conclusion, this randomized controlled trial may provide worthwhile information about the effect of TA on EAD incidence after LT surgery. Moreover, the safety issues and potential thromboembolic risk of TA administration during LT surgery will be studied in detail, which may offer some useful message about the anesthetic protocol of LT surgery. Finally, this study will also focus on whether TA will affect the perioperative transfusion requirements, AKI incidence, hospital stays, hospitalization expenses and other adverse events which may shed light on future research about liver transplantation.

Neutrophil chemotaxis score and chemotaxis-related genes have the potential for clinical application to prognosticate the survival of patients with tumours
Authors: Yunxi Yang, Jun Yang, Linbin Li, Yiming Shao, Lu Liu, Bingwei Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs frontline cells, the precise recruitment of neutrophils is crucial for resolving inflammation and maintaining the homeostasis of the organism. Increasing evidence suggests the pivotal role of neutrophil chemotaxis in cancer progression and metastasis. Here, we collected clinical data and peripheral blood samples from patients with tumours to examine the alterations in the neutrophil quantity and chemotactic function using the Cell Chemotaxis Analysis Platform (CCAP). Transcriptome sequencing data of pan-cancer were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression model, we selected a total of 29 genes from 155 neutrophil- and chemotaxis-related genes to construct the ChemoScore model. Meanwhile, nomogram-based comprehensive model was established for clinical application. Furthermore, immunofluorescence (IF) staining was employed to assess the relationship between the neutrophils infiltrating and the survival outcomes of tumours. In this observational study, the chemotactic function of neutrophils was notably diminished in patients. The establishment and validation of ChemoScore suggested neutrophil chemotaxis to be a risk factor in most tumours, whereby higher scores were associated with poorer survival outcomes and were correlated with various immune cells and malignant biological processes. Moreover, IF staining of tumour tissue substantiated the adverse correlation between neutrophil infiltration and the survival of patients with lung adenocarcinoma (P = 0.0002) and colon adenocarcinoma (P = 0.0472). Taken together, patients with tumours demonstrated a decrease in chemotactic function. ChemoScore potentially prognosticates the survival of patients with tumours. Introduction

Cancer, often referred to as an “unhealed wound” [1], has gained considerable attention in recent years due to its role in inducing inflammation during cancer progression [2, 3]. Neutrophils, the most abundant white blood cells (WBCs) in human peripheral blood (PB), serve as frontline responders to infection and tissue damage, impart critical effects on the tumour microenvironment (TME) [4, 5]. Neutrophils exhibit a wide array of biological functions, and the majority of studies suggest that they require direct contact with other cells and tissues to effectively exert their functions [6, 7]. Therefore, proficient chemotactic function assumes a key role throughout the entire process.

The cascade of neutrophil chemotaxis primarily involves selectin-mediated slow rolling and adhesion to the endothelium, integrin-mediated arrest and crawling, and, ultimately, transmigration [8–10]. After exiting the bloodstream, neutrophils migrate directionally towards target tissues or organs, moving against the concentration gradient of chemotactic factors. Neutrophils exert direct cytotoxic effects on tumours through mechanisms such as phagocytosis, the generation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and degranulation, which releases myeloperoxidase (MPO), neutrophil elastase (NE), matrix metalloproteinase 8 (MMP8), and matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9), effectively inhibiting tumour growth (Fig. 1A) [11–13]. Here, we used the original under-agarose neutrophil chemotaxis model to detect the chemotactic function of neutrophils and then uploaded to Cell Chemotaxis Analysis Platform (CCAP) based on computer vision for further analysis (Fig. 1B). However, several studies have delineated that neutrophils commonly transform into an immunosuppressive subtype due to the influence of the complex TME. In this altered state, they facilitate tumour angiogenesis, promote tumour growth, and even contribute to metastasis [14, 15]. The study of Teijeira et al.. suggests that tumour cells express CXC chemokine receptors (CXCR1/2) to attract neutrophils to the tumour growth site, inducing the formation of NETs [16]. However, these NETs create a protective layer around tumour cells, reducing their contact with cytotoxic cells and promoting survival. Similarly, a recent study on breast cancer (BRCA) indicates that neutrophil infiltration impairs the anti-tumour T cell immune response, and blocking the C-C chemokine receptor type 1 (CCR1) benefits tumour eradication [17]. Therefore, a significant ongoing debate exists among researchers regarding the role of neutrophil chemotaxis in oncogenesis, tumour growth, and even metastasis. Additionally, a notable research gap is also observed, particularly in examining the chemotactic function of neutrophils in the context of pan-cancer.

This study conducted large-scale, single-centre research, focusing on the quantity and chemotactic function of neutrophils in the PB of patients with tumours from a clinical perspective. Furthermore, we utilised the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox model to screen neutrophil- and chemotaxis-related genes and assess the relationship between neutrophil chemotaxis in primary tumours and patient prognosis. Furthermore, the ChemoScore model was developed using pan-cancer datasets, revealing a strong correlation between higher ChemoScore values and adverse prognoses in most individuals with tumours. Finally, a clinical validation was conducted by examining the extent of neutrophil infiltration through immunofluorescence (IF) staining in tumour tissues. This research provides a reference for the assessment of tumour prognosis and introduces new insights and innovative approaches for tumour treatment.

Results

Patients with tumours exhibit a decrease in neutrophil chemotactic function

This study conducted a comprehensive analysis of data from 425,252 individuals with favourable results from physical examinations at the Suzhou Health Examination Centre between 2017 and 2022, and 16,016 inpatients from the Oncology Department of Affiliated Suzhou Hospital of Nanjing Medical University during the same period. The analysis focused on the WBC and neutrophil counts obtained from routine blood tests (Table 1). It was found that the total WBC counts were significantly lower in patients with tumours compared to the healthy population, while the counts and percentage of neutrophils were appreciably elevated (Fig. 2A-C). Furthermore, patients with tumours demonstrated a significant elevation in their WBC count, neutrophil counts, and neutrophil percentage following inpatient treatment and upon discharge from the hospital (Fig. 2D-F). These findings suggest the crucial role of neutrophils in oncogenesis, tumour growth, and therapeutic response. Consequently, our study enrolled 164 patients with tumours to assess chemotactic function in PB neutrophils between May 2022 and November 2022. Among them, there were 31 cases of colon cancer (CC), 20 cases of rectal cancer (RC), 31 cases of gastric cancer (GC), 28 cases of lung cancer (LC), 31 cases of breast cancer (BC), and 17 cases of other tumour (OT) types (including seven cases of pancreatic cancer, three cases of liver cancer, two cases of oesophageal cancer, two cases of gallbladder cancer, one case of thyroid cancer, one case of large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, and one case of malignant spindle cell tumour). Additionally, six patients had multiple tumours (MT). Simultaneously, 73 healthy volunteers were recruited for synchronous testing. Detailed information about the patients and healthy volunteers is provided in Table 2. The results demonstrated a significant decrease in chemotactic function among patients with various tumour types, irrespective of whether metastasis occurred. All assessed chemotactic function parameters (CD, Vmax, CCR, CI, CFS) exhibited a substantial decline (Fig. 2G-K). The distribution of CFS scores in each patient group is presented in Figure S1. Notably, in cases of RC and GC, individuals with metastasis exhibited more robust chemotactic function than those without metastasis, indicating the potential involvement of neutrophil chemotaxis in the metastatic process of gastrointestinal tumours. However, definitive conclusions could not be established due to the complex etiologies of multiple tumours, limited sample size, and significant individual variations. Moreover, among the six patients with various tumours, five experienced tumour metastases, and demonstrated a substantial reduction in CCR, indicating a partial decrease in their chemotactic function. Representative images depicting neutrophil chemotaxis in the PB of healthy volunteers and patients are displayed in Fig. 2L-R.

Identification of a 29-gene signature related to chemotaxis for pan-cancer

A flowchart detailing the construction of a pan-cancer prognostic model based on neutrophil chemotaxis-related genes is presented in Figure S2. Initially, 155 gene sets (table S1) related to neutrophils (comprising 31 genes) and gene sets associated with neutrophil chemotaxis (124 genes) were used as input for LASSO regression analysis, using the TCGA pan-cancer training set (Fig. 3A). This analysis resulted in the identification of 48 neutrophil chemotaxis-related genes with non-zero coefficients (Fig. 3B). Subsequently, a pairwise correlation analysis was performed on these genes, revealing that certain genes exhibited similar expression patterns with high correlation coefficients. Therefore, genes including ADAM8, TREM1, CCL20, CEACAM3, CCL18, CCL23, FUT7, RHOH, CCL22, CCR7, HPSE, SYK, TNFAIP6, FCAR, C5AR1, IL1A, RAC1, CKLF, and RHOG were excluded from further analysis (Fig. 3C). The remaining 29 chemotaxis-related genes (Supplementary Table 2) were utilised for model construction. Univariate and multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed on these 29 chemotaxis-related genes (Fig. 3D-E) to assess their HRs for prognosis. Both regression models consistently identified PPIA as the most significant risk factor for tumour prognosis. In recent years, PPIA has gained prominence in the context of resistance to tumour immunotherapy [18, 19].

Landscape of chemoScore in pan-cancer

The ranking and distribution map of ChemoScore in different types of tumours, from high to low, are shown in Fig. 4A-B. It was observed that ChemoScore exhibited significant organ specificity, with higher scores commonly observed in tumours originating from the brain (such as glioblastoma multiforme [GBM] and lower grade glioma [LGG]) and the upper gastrointestinal tract (including pancreatic adenocarcinoma [PAAD], oesophageal carcinoma [ESCA], STAD, and liver hepatocellular carcinoma [LIHC]). However, tumours arising from certain secretory glands, including breast cancer (BRCA), pheochromocytomas, paragangliomas (PCPG), prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), and thyroid carcinoma (THCA), typically demonstrated lower ChemoScores. Based on the median ChemoScore, all patients with tumours were catogerized into HRG and LRG. Patients with higher ChemoScores were associated with various several adverse survival indicators, including disease-specific survival (DSS), overall survival (OS), and progression-free interval (PFI), compared to those in the LRG (Fig. 4C). The KM survival curves for DSS, OS, and PFI across different tumour types in the model training set and additional ROC curves are included in Figure S4-S9. The predictive performance of ChemoScore was first verified in the TCGA training set to validate the effectiveness and accuracy of the model. Encouragingly, the survival analysis of patients in the test set yielded consistent results with the training set, indicating that higher ChemoScore was consistently associated with poorer prognosis (Fig. 4D). Moreover, ChemoScore exhibited strong predictive capabilities for the prediction of various tumours, such as COAD (PDSS−test = 0.047, POS−test = 0.037), LUAD (PDSS−test = 0.005, POS−test = 0.012), LGG (PDSS−test = 0.0035), KIRC (PPFI−test = 0.024), and KIRP (PPFI−test = 0.031) (Fig.S3A-H). Furthermore, using ChemoScore as a predictive indicator allowed for the estimation of 3-, 5-, or 10-year survival rates (with an AUC > 0.7) for ten types of cancers, namely adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC), LGG, kidney chromophobe (KICH), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), mesothelioma (MESO), PAAD, PCPG, skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM), THCA, and uterine carcinosarcoma (UCS) (Fig.S3I-T). Interestingly, it was found that ChemoScore demonstrated different performances in different tumour types in the same organ. For instance, ChemoScore showed a significant correlation with unfavourable prognosis in patients with LUAD and not in those with lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), despite higher ChemoScore values in LUSC. Similarly, differences in performances were witnessed in tumours originating from different sites in the same organ. For example, ChemoScore manifested a notable correlation with unsatisfactory prognosis in patients with COAD, whereas no such association was observed in rectal adenocarcinoma (READ). These findings collectively indicate that the role played by neutrophil chemotaxis in tumour progression is not only influenced by the host organ type, but also tightly linked to the location of tumour occurrence and the pathological type of the tumour.

Validation of chemotaxis scoring model in other independent datasets

To further substantiate the prognostic evaluation potential of ChemoScore in pan-cancer, it was applied to multiple external validation cohorts, yielding consistent results. Firstly, in the CGGA database, it was verified that higher ChemoScore in patients with LGG was associated with a worse prognosis (P = 0.0011) (Fig. 5A). Secondly, we included relevant datasets for colon and rectal cancers collectively categorized as CRC, encompassing the tumours of CRC (Fig. 5B-D), READ (Fig. 5E-F), and COAD (Fig. 5G-H). The results indicated that, in the CRC datasets, no significant correlation with ChemoScore was observed in the KM survival curves of GSE87211 (POS = 0.66, PPFI = 0.15) and GSE33113 (PPFI = 0.075). Similar results were observed in the READ dataset GSE40492 (POS = 0.33, PDFS = 0.3). However, in the COAD dataset, patients with higher ChemoScore exhibited significantly shortened OS in GSE39582 (POS = 0.021, PRFS = 0.03), further confirming the applicability of ChemoScore in COAD. Subsequently, lung cancer-related datasets were retrieved. In the dataset that included a mixture of LUAD and LUSC cases, ChemoScore did not prove to be a reliable prognostic indicator in GSE3141 (POS = 0.12) and GSE8894 (PPFI = 0.079). In light of this, a classification-based validation was conducted on patients with LUAD and LUSC from the GSE8894 dataset, further substantiating the distinct capabilities of ChemoScore for adverse outcomes in patients with LUAD and LUSC. ChemoScore showed a significant association with prognosis in patients with LUAD (GSE8894 PRFI = 0.0026); however, no notable correlation was witnessed in patients with LUSC (GSE8894 PRFI = 0.29) (Fig. 5I-L). Finally, BRCA, the most prevalent cancer among females [20], exhibited a lower ChemoScore that showed no significant correlation in the training and testing sets. Consequently, three external validation datasets for BRCA were selected for further validation (Fig. 5M-P). The findings unveiled that in two of the validation datasets, the correlation of ChemoScore was not significant (METABRIC POS = 0.083, PRFI = 0.33; GSE2990 PRFI = 0.084). In contrast to previous conclusions, the GSE19615 dataset displayed a better prognosis for patients with higher ChemoScore. Considering all the results, it can be concluded that ChemoScore is not a reliable prognostic indicator for BRCA, which aligns with the validation results obtained from the TCGA cohort.

Establishment of a comprehensive nomogram model based on ChemoScore for clinical prediction of pan-cancer prognosis

This study proceeded to examine the correlation of ChemoScore with various demographic and clinical characteristics of patients, including age, gender, cancer type, subtype, stage, histologic grade, metastasis stage, lymph node stage, tumour stage, aneuploidy score, fraction genome altered, microsatellite instability (MSI)sensor score, mutation count, tumour mutational burden (TMB), and Buffa hypoxia score. The results revealed a significant positive correlation between ChemoScore and tumour histologic grade. In the majority of tumours, ChemoScore exhibited a significant positive correlation with aneuploidy score (11 types) and Buffa hypoxia score (14 types). Moreover, ChemoScore displayed substantial correlations with tumour stage (8 types) and tumour subtype (9 types) in a larger number of solid tumours (Fig.S10). Heatmaps illustrating the correlation between ChemoScore and clinical information for COAD, READ, LAUD, LUSC, and BRCA are shown in Fig. 6A-F. Additional tumour heatmaps with significant correlations are presented in Supplementary Fig. 10. Consistent with previous findings, ChemoScore did not display any association with clinical features in the READ dataset, suggesting limited applicability of the model in READ. Subsequently, the correlation between ChemoScore and these factors with DSS survival outcomes was assessed using multivariate Cox regression analysis. The results demonstrated that the ChemoScore showed the most significant correlation with DSS prognosis, ranking first, which suggests that ChemoScore can serve as an independent prognostic indicator (Fig. 6G-H). A nomogram incorporating the aforementioned clinical features was constructed to enhance the predictive performance of ChemoScore and offer a quantitative and direct estimation of survival for patients with tumours related to neutrophil chemotaxis (Fig. 6I). Calibration curves for 1-, 3-, and 5-year DSS (Fig. 6M-O) revealed close alignment with the standard curves, indicating that the nomogram is highly accurate in predicting survival probabilities. Correspondingly, the DCA curves (Fig. 6J-L) also exhibited the superiority of the combined model, with higher clinical net benefit and improved predictive efficiency. These findings also highlighted the importance of ChemoScore in conjunction with other clinical features to collectively predict tumour prognosis.

Relationship between chemoScore and tumour immune microenvironment and tumour characteristics

The robust chemotactic behaviour of neutrophils indicates their ability to respond rapidly to signals and migrate to target tissues or organs. In tumours, this process manifests as neutrophil infiltration [21], during which tissue-infiltrating neutrophils display phenotypic changes, resulting in enhanced heterogeneity. The crosstalk between these tissue-infiltrating neutrophils and other immune cells, and their role in the TME remains a subject of ongoing debate with multiple perspectives [6, 22]. Evidence exists suggesting that tissue-infiltrating neutrophils are prone to undergo NETosis, thereby facilitating the transition of dormant tumour cells into a proliferative state and promoting rapid tumour growth and metastasis [23]. To explore the relationship between neutrophil chemotaxis and these tumour characteristics (Supplementary Table 4), we examined the correlation between ChemoScore and various immune cells (Fig. 7A), along with tumour-related features (cell cycle, proliferation, cytoskeleton, inflammation, EMT, NETs) (Fig. 7J). In immune cells, we observed a significant positive correlation between ChemoScore and Tgd (R = 0.31, P < 0.0001), macrophages (R = 0.25, P < 0.0001), activated dendritic cells (aDCs) (R = 0.25, P < 0.0001), and Th2 cells (R = 0.25, P < 0.0001). In contrast, a significant negative correlation was found with Th17 cells (R = -0.38, P < 0.0001), natural killer (NK) cells (R = -0.27, P < 0.0001), eosinophils (R = -0.26, P < 0.0001), and T follicular helper (TFH) cells (R = -0.22, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 7B-I). It should be noted that ChemoScore showed an extremely low correlation with neutrophils (R = 0.1, P < 0.0001), indicating significant differences between tissue-infiltrating neutrophils and circulating neutrophils in terms of their characteristics. The enhanced heterogeneity contributes to the complexity and elusive nature of their biological functions, a key factor contributing to their controversial role in tumour progression. Regarding tumour characteristics, ChemoScore showed a significant positive correlation with G2/M (R = 0.41, P < 0.0001), G0/G1 (R = 0.36, P < 0.0001), G1/S (R = 0.32, P < 0.0001), proliferation (R = 0.32, P < 0.0001), cytoskeleton (R = 0.31, P < 0.0001), inflammation (R = 0.30, P < 0.0001), EMT (R = 0.27, P < 0.0001), and NETs (R = 0.25, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 7K-R). These findings imply that the infiltration of highly chemotactic neutrophils into tumours is accompanied by changes in the TME, which are intricately linked to inflammation, complex intercellular connections among immune cells, and more invasive tumour cell growth. These observations align with the positive correlation between EMT and heightened neutrophil chemotaxis in patients with RC and GC who experienced metastasis in clinical settings (Fig. 2K). In summary, a higher infiltration of neutrophils in the tumours implies a more complex TME and more aggressive behaviours of malignant cells.

Validation of neutrophil chemotactic function in clinical cohorts

In order to provide a more comprehensive insight into the relationship between neutrophil chemotaxis and patient prognosis, CD66b antibody was utilised for labeling neutrophils [24], which were subsequently subjected to IF staining in clinical samples from various tumour types. This assay was conducted to validate the association of ChemoScore with patient prognosis in cases of LUAD, COAD, and STAD, where LUAD and COAD showed a significant correlation, whilst STAD exhibited a higher ChemoScore but no significant correlation. The staining results confirmed the presence of varying degrees of neutrophil infiltration in samples from different types of tumours as well as in samples from the same tumour (Fig. 8A). Therefore, we utilised the proportion of CD66b+ cells as a measure of neutrophil quantity in the tumour tissue. Based on the median proportion of CD66b+ cells in each type of tumour tissue, the samples were partitioned into CD66blow and CD66bhigh groups for survival analysis. The results suggested that the proportion of CD66b+ cells could be used to stratify the prognosis of patients with LUAD and COAD. The infiltration of polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs) in STAD (10.52 ± 13.90) was significantly higher than that in LUAD (5.42 ± 9.18, P = 0.0065) and COAD (3.13 ± 3.11, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 8B), but the correlation with patient prognosis was not significant (Fig. 8C-E). Subsequently, we performed univariate Cox analysis on the available clinical indicators of patients, including gender, age, TNM staging, metastasis, and pathological grading (Supplementary Table 4). The indicators that showed statistical significance were further subjected to multivariate Cox regression analysis. As depicted in the forest plot, the proportion of CD66b+ cells emerged as an independent factor influencing the prognosis of patients with LUAD and COAD (Fig. 8F-G).

Discussion

Neutrophils, the first innate immune cells to reach the inflammation site, impart a crucial role in host defence through their robust chemotactic function and sensitivity to inflammatory stimuli [13]. Both tumour and stromal cells release chemokines and cytokines, such as IL-1, IL-8, CCL2, and G-CSF [25, 26], making chronic inflammation one of the characteristics of TME [27]. This phenomenon promotes the chemotaxis and infiltration of neutrophils into various tumours. Neutrophils exert their anti-tumour effects through direct cytotoxicity on tumour cells or indirectly by activating other immune cells [28, 29]. The study by Lee et al. demonstrated that IL-8-induced neutrophil infiltration at the tumour site effectively suppresses ovarian cancer growth [30]. Nonetheless, sustained neutrophil infiltration, contrary to their primary function, can mediate a new cycle of tissue damage [31, 32]. Given the diverse response of neutrophils to inflammatory stimuli, they exhibit adaptive phenotypes in different inflammatory contexts. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have focused on describing, defining, and exploring the functions of various subpopulations of neutrophils in different tumour types. In the field of liver cancer research, Professor Zeming Zhang’s team at Peking University has identified two distinct neutrophil subpopulations, CCL4+ tumour-associated neutrophils (TANs) and PD-L1+ TANs, which enhance tumour growth through different mechanisms. This research delineates the phenomenon of neutrophils, originally possessing immune functionality, undergoing a “traitorous” transformation [33]. Furthermore, prior evidence on non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) suggests that neutrophils in tumours exhibit extensive phenotypic heterogeneity and plasticity, contributing to intra-tumoural angiogenesis and impacting the efficacy of PD-L1 immunotherapy [34]. Currently, researchers continue to engage in contentious debates and offer varying perspectives regarding the role assumed by neutrophils in tumourigenesis.

The present study, grounded in clinical investigation, initially examined a large-scale dataset of blood routine tests in a single-centre setting. It uncovered a notable elevation in the neutrophil count among patients with tumours when compared to the healthy population. Moreover, patients receiving active therapeutic interventions, such as surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy during hospitalization, demonstrated a marked increase in WBC and neutrophil counts. These observations indicate that inflammation in patients with tumours extends beyond localized manifestations, exhibiting a systemic nature encompassing. Subsequent assessment of the chemotactic function of neutrophils revealed an apparent reduction in this function among patients with tumours when compared to the healthy population. Notably, patients with metastatic RC and GC exhibited heightened chemotactic function in comparison to those without metastasis. These findings collectively underscore a consistent observation that patients with tumours show both abnormal neutrophil counts and impaired chemotactic function. The combination of an elevated neutrophil count with compromised chemotactic function implies a reduced efficiency of neutrophils, responding to signals released by tumour tissue, potentially limiting their ability to reach target tissues and exert their anti-tumour effects. The specific role imparted by these cells upon reaching tumour tissue in tumourigenesis, progression, metastasis, and even prognosis remains a subject necessitating further investigation.

To address this concern, we incorporated the genes pertaining to neutrophils, chemotaxis, migration, and extravasation to construct a pan-cancer prognostic assessment system based on neutrophil chemotaxis. By stratifying pan-cancer patients into HRG and LRG according to their respective ChemoScores, we observed an overall trend, wherein tumour progression was more inclined to occur in high-risk patients, whilst low-risk patients exhibited extended survival periods. This finding underscores the utility of a model constructed with neutrophil chemotaxis-related genes in facilitating the evaluation of the survival prognosis of patients with different tumour types. The core value of constructing this model lies in its potential clinical applicability. During the tissue microarray validation phase, we observed varying degrees of neutrophil infiltration in different types of tumours as well as in different samples of the same type of tumour. This infiltration was found to be associated with shorter survival periods in patients with LUAD and COAD. Simultaneously, we noted a significantly higher degree of neutrophil infiltration in patients with STAD compared to patients with LUAD and COAD, albeit without demonstrating predictive value for prognosis. These findings align with the outcomes presented in the TCGA dataset, affirming the substantial significance and applicability of ChemoScore in evaluating the prognosis for survival in pan-cancer scenarios. Furthermore, it provides novel insights and directions for the anti-cancer treatment.

The heterogeneity of neutrophils reflects their unique multifunctionality and multipotentiality [35, 36]. Specific subpopulations of neutrophils bearing distinct molecular markers (such as those carrying interferon genes) exhibit potent anti-tumour efficacy [37]. An efficient anti-tumour effect could be cultivated by artificially inducing and amplifying this response, for instance, by prompting the in situ transformation of neutrophil subpopulations in the tumour through radiotherapy or specific pharmaceutical agents, whilst concurrently suppressing the function of pro-tumour cell populations. Strong chemotactic abilities of neutrophils represent a significant advantage distinguishing them from other cell types. It may emerge as the next frontier in cancer immunotherapy to select or train anti-tumour neutrophil subpopulations in vitro, or even direct the differentiation of stem cells into such populations [38], for direct infusion into patients [39], or combine drug delivery with the aforementioned strategies [40]. This approach holds promise as a potential emerging trend in cancer immunotherapy.

Limitations

This study is a cross-sectional study, which the peripheral blood of patients was randomly tested for a certain period, the results can only reflect the chemotactic ability of peripheral blood neutrophils in patients at that time from one side. The ChemoScore model is an evaluation system constructed from multiple genes. The presence of diverse tumour types and individual variations, coupled with the fact that the tumour samples validated in this study represent a small sample cohort, suggests that the expression level of any single indicator may not entirely correspond to the predictive outcomes of the model. Therefore, further exploration and validation of other, more reliable indicators is required to enhance the accuracy of the model.

Conclusions

In summary, the assessment of neutrophil chemotactic function in PB of patients with tumours is of paramount importance. Neutrophil infiltration in tumour tissues is highly coordinated with numerous malignant characteristics and is associated with the clinical outcomes of pan-cancer patients. Inducing the transformation of specific subpopulations of TANs or utilising their chemotaxis for targeted interventions in anti-tumour neutrophils may contribute to the establishment of personalised anti-cancer therapies.

Materials and methods

Ethics declarations

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University (approval number: KL901390). PB samples were obtained from healthy voluntary blood donors and individuals with tumours through the median cubital vein. All participants were informed about the study and provided informed consent for the use of their blood samples for research purposes prior to inclusion.

Participant selection

Between May 2022 and November 2022, 164 adult patients with primary tumours were recruited from the Oncology Department of Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University. The residual blood samples collected upon admission prior to any treatment were used for the analysis of chemotactic function in neutrophils. Exclusion criteria included severe infections, major trauma, immune-related diseases, neutropenia (neutrophil count < 2 × 109 cells/L), pregnancy, or hematologic malignancies. During the same period, 73 adult volunteers with standard health examination results were recruited from the Suzhou Health Examination Centre for the assessment of the chemotactic function of neutrophils in PB.

Clinical data collection

This study collected and analysed data from 425,252 healthy individuals with unremarkable health examination results at Suzhou Health Examination Centre between 2017 and 2022, and 16,016 inpatient cases from the Oncology Department of Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University, during the same period. The analysis focused on WBC and neutrophil counts from routine blood tests (Table 1). Furthermore, data on gender, age, diagnosis, WBC count, neutrophil count, and lymphocyte count were collected from all participants (Table 2).

Assessment of chemotactic function of neutrophils

The chemotactic function of neutrophils was examined using an under-agarose chemotaxis assay [41]. The images were uploaded to the Construction of the Cell Chemotaxis Analysis Platform (CCAP) for further analysis, as described in the previously published study [42] (Fig. 1b). The specific values of the following chemotaxis indicators were obtained: chemotaxis distance (CD), maximum speed of chemotaxis (Vmax), chemotactic cell ratio (CCR), chemotaxis index (CI), and chemotactic function score (CFS). Among these indicators, a CFS score of 15 is indicative of normal neutrophil chemotactic function, while scores of 11–14, 7–10, 4–6, and ≤ 3 showed mild, moderate, severe, and extremely severe abnormalities, respectively.

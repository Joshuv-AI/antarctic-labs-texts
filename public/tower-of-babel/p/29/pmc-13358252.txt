Effects of a fasting-mimicking diet on long-term prognosis in patients with colorectal cancer undergoing radical resection: protocol for a multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Miaomiao Guo, Zhirong Sun, Ziwen Zhong, Hongli Hu, Yimin Qiu, Su Liu, Nadier Yimin, Chaoyang Tong, Fei Liang, Chunyan Ji, Jianmin Xu, Xiaoming Deng, Changhong Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGiven the metabolic vulnerability of colorectal cancer (CRC) cells, periodic fasting-mimicking diets (FMDs) have emerged as a promising dietary strategy for improving clinical outcomes. Although preclinical studies have suggested that FMDs may suppress tumour growth and early-phase clinical trials have demonstrated their safety and feasibility, evidence regarding their effects on long-term survival outcomes in CRC remains limited because of certain constraints in patient cohorts and study designs.Method and analysisThis multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial will be conducted at six tertiary comprehensive hospitals in China. It is anticipated that 602 eligible participants with clinical stage III CRC will be recruited. Participants will be randomly assigned at a 1:1 ratio to either the FMD group or the control group. After the 4-month dietary intervention, participants will continue to be followed up for 3 years. The primary outcome is 3-year disease-free survival. Participant enrolment began in June 2022, and the study is expected to be completed in December 2027.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the ethics committees of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University and other participating centres. The findings of this study will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentation at academic conferences.Trial registration numberNCT05384444.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major and increasingly urgent global public health issue characterised by a consistently increasing incidence and a trend of younger age.1 2 Globally, CRC is the third most commonly diagnosed cancer and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality, imposing a substantial burden on healthcare systems worldwide.36 Surgical resection remains the primary treatment approach.7 However, despite advances in medical technology, cancer metastasis and recurrence still adversely influence cancer patients’ long-term survival and pose serious threats to life.8 Therefore, to improve survival outcomes after radical treatment, additional strategies should be urgently developed.

The high-energy consumption state of CRC cells leads to a dependence on external nutrients, rendering tumour cells highly sensitive to dietary restriction.911 This metabolic vulnerability of CRC cells provides a theoretical basis for dietary intervention as a potential adjunctive therapeutic strategy.12 Experimental evidence suggests that fasting can inhibit colorectal tumour growth by creating a metabolic environment that is unfavourable for the survival and proliferation of tumour cells.13

In recent years, fasting-mimicking diets (FMDs), which are low in calories, sucrose and protein, have emerged as a more feasible alternative to complete fasting.14 15 FMDs are designed to produce the key metabolic effects of fasting while reducing the practical burden and safety risks associated with complete food deprivation.16 17 Preclinical experiments have revealed the inhibitory effect of FMDs on CRC cells.18 19 Early-phase clinical trials have demonstrated the safety and feasibility of periodic FMDs in patients with cancer.2022 However, strong evidence regarding their effects on survival outcomes in patients with CRC remains lacking, particularly in prospectively designed clinical studies.

Therefore, this multicentre prospective clinical trial will evaluate the effect of periodic FMDs on long-term outcomes in patients with CRC undergoing radical resection.

Study aims

The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the effect of periodic FMDs on long-term survival outcomes in patients with clinically staged III CRC undergoing radical resection compared with those receiving a regular diet.

Methods

Study design

This prospective, multicentre, randomised controlled trial will be conducted at six tertiary comprehensive hospitals in China, namely Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, Shanghai Cancer Centre, Changhai Hospital, (Shanghai) Tongji Hospital, Shanghai General Hospital and Huashan Hospital. Eligible patients with clinical stage III CRC undergoing radical resection will be randomly assigned at a 1:1 ratio to either the FMD group or the control group. All patients will receive standardised perioperative care across all participating centres. After resuming a normal diet, participants will consume either periodic FMDs or a regular diet according to their group allocation and they will be followed up to assess survival outcomes. Data on patient characteristics and perioperative variables as well as post-discharge follow-up data will be collected using standardised case report forms (CRFs). Participants

Enrolment will be conducted by qualified clinicians. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment (see online supplemental file S1). Participants will not be allowed to concurrently enrol in another interventional clinical study during the study period. To facilitate the timely recruitment of the target sample size, a competitive enrolment strategy will be used across participating centres.

Study interventions

FMD group

Participants allocated to the FMD group will undergo one FMD cycle per month for four consecutive cycles. In each cycle, participants will follow a standardised 5-day FMD regimen, providing 1000 kcal on Day 1 and 800 kcal on Days 2–5. Regarding the energy distribution, the FMD regimen comprises 10% protein, 56% fat and 34% carbohydrate on Day 1 and 9% protein, 44% fat and 47% carbohydrate on Days 2–5. Participants will be allowed to consume the allocated diet components at any time during each scheduled day. During the FMD period, participants can freely consume water and tea.

To ensure consistency in the practical implementation of the FMD regimen across study centres, a standardised FMD recipe manual will be provided (see online supplemental file S2). The recipe manual will specify the permitted food items and the corresponding quantities in grams and millilitres. Along with the recipe manual, participants will be provided with demonstration videos for guidance on the preparation of the recipes, which will be accessible through a QR code. To support the accurate implementation of the dietary protocol, participants in the FMD group will also receive an FMD diet kit, including a digital food scale and printed instructions. In addition, a home-use blood glucose metre will be provided free of charge to enable daily blood glucose self-monitoring during each FMD cycle, supporting the early detection of hypoglycaemia.

Control group

Participants allocated to the control group will receive conventional dietary guidance for patients with CRC, together with sample daily menus ensuring a calorie intake of approximately 2000 kcal and 2500 kcal. Their daily diet will not be restricted. They will continue their usual diet without fasting, FMDs or intentional major calorie restriction during the active intervention phase, unless medically indicated. These participants will determine their own meal timing. Although the intervention does not involve fixed food options, participants will be encouraged to maintain a daily calorie intake of at least 2000 kcal.

Active intervention phase

For participants in both groups, the dietary intervention will be initiated approximately 4 weeks after surgery. The exact timing may be adjusted according to each participant’s postoperative recovery and their ability to tolerate a regular diet. Participants in the FMD group receiving adjuvant chemotherapy will complete a 5-day FMD cycle before each corresponding monthly chemotherapy cycle. For those not receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, the 5-day FMD cycle will be repeated each month starting from the first cycle. During the active intervention phase, participants in the control group will maintain their usual diet and will be required to avoid any intentional major calorie restriction.

Dietary guidance

All participants will receive clear instructions from the research team before the start of the dietary intervention. In these sessions, participants will be provided with detailed dietary guidance and follow-up procedures. For participants in the FMD group, additional instructions will be provided on the correct use of the digital food scale, implementation of the 5-day FMD cycle and video-based meal preparation guidance. The research team will address any questions raised by participants to ensure that participants fully understand the dietary procedures.

The research team will include trained dietitians who will provide professional dietary guidance and nutritional status assessment throughout the intervention period. They will also actively provide timely and targeted support to address any difficulties encountered by patients during the implementation of the dietary regimen.

Adherence assessment

An adherent FMD cycle will be defined as the completion of at least 3 of the 5 scheduled FMD days. Participants in the FMD group will complete a daily dietary log during each 5-day cycle. Dietary logs can be completed using paper forms or photos. Within each cycle, a trained study dietitian will conduct a daily 24-hour dietary recall by telephone using a standardised paper-based form aligned with the Encyclopaedia of Chinese Nutrition Sciences23 (see online supplemental file S3). At each clinic visit, study staff will verify dietary adherence for the preceding cycle through a structured in-person interview. Any non-study food intake and symptoms affecting compliance will be recorded.

Weight will be measured at the start and end of each FMD cycle. When within-cycle weight change is inconsistent with expectations (eg, weight gain), the follow-up team will re-verify adherence to the prescribed FMD menu.

Control-group participants will undergo periodic dietary assessments within the same follow-up framework as the FMD group. Depending on whether participants are receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, periodic dietary assessments will be conducted either within the 5 days preceding each monthly chemotherapy cycle or over 5 consecutive days each month. Any off-protocol fasting or intentional dietary restriction will be recorded. Total daily energy intake will be estimated using the China Food Composition Tables (sixth edn).24

Adjuvant chemotherapy standardisation

The oxaliplatin-based regimens, capecitabine plus oxaliplatin (CAPOX) and folinic acid, fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin (FOLFOX), are established as the primary adjuvant chemotherapy regimens for stage III CRC at all participating sites, in accordance with the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines. Adjuvant chemotherapy will begin 4–6 weeks after surgery, with the precise start date adjusted on the basis of postoperative recovery.

Randomisation

Participants will be centrally randomised at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, at a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated allocation sequence. Randomisation will be stratified by the participating centre, BMI (<25 kg/m2; ≥25 kg/m2) and preoperative anaemia status.20 25 26 Anaemia is defined as a haemoglobin concentration of<120 g/L in men and<110 g/L in women, in accordance with the Chinese diagnostic criteria.26 27 The randomisation procedure will be conducted by an independent biostatistician who will not be involved in patient recruitment, clinical management or outcome assessment. The concealment of group allocation will be ensured throughout the randomisation process. Randomisation will be performed on the day of surgery, after completion of the operation.

Handling of pathological upstaging or downstaging

The surgical team will perform a systematic intraoperative exploration. Through the intraoperative assessment, patients with intraoperatively identified stage IV disease will be excluded from randomisation. Participants with postoperatively identified occult distant metastasis will have their FMD intervention discontinued and will continue to undergo long-term outcome follow-up. Participants with a postoperative pathological stage lower than III will continue to receive their assigned dietary intervention and will be retained in the primary intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis.

Intervention masking

Because this is a dietary intervention study that requires active participant involvement and dietary guidance from the research team, the blinding of participants and the treating investigators is not feasible. However, the researchers responsible for laboratory assessments and CRF management will remain blinded to group allocation. Statistical analyses will also be performed by statisticians who are blinded to treatment assignment.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome measure

The primary outcome of this study is 3-year disease-free survival (DFS) after the radical resection. DFS is defined as the time from randomisation to the first occurrence of locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis or death from any cause, whichever occurs first. The data of participants without an event will be censored at the date of the last follow-up.28 A follow-up of survival will be conducted at least every 3 months, beginning at 6 months after surgery. Postoperative recurrence or metastasis will be determined on the basis of imaging studies, endoscopic examination or histopathological assessment of biopsy specimens, with a review also performed by an expert panel.29

The secondary outcomes include 3-year overall survival (OS), weight and BMI, upper arm circumference and waist circumference, nutritional status, plasma albumin concentration, quality of life (QoL), exercise tolerance, chemotherapy-related toxicities, insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), fasting serum glucose (FG), fasting insulin (FI) and C-peptide. Weight, BMI, upper arm circumference and waist circumference, nutritional status, plasma albumin concentration, QoL and exercise tolerance will be assessed before surgery, after surgery and at 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 months.

Safety outcome measure

Safety will be assessed in participants following at least one administration of the FMD regimen. Adverse events occurring during the intervention period will be recorded at each follow-up contact and graded according to CTCAE V.5.0. The investigators will assess the severity of each event and its potential relationship with FMD intervention. Safety outcomes will include the frequency and severity of FMD-related adverse events. Predefined FMD-related adverse events include headache, weakness, fatigue, hunger, gastrointestinal symptoms, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte abnormalities and clinically significant weight loss.

Perioperative data

Data collected during the perioperative period will include demographic data, medical history, vital signs, laboratory data, anthropometric measurements, levels of circulating metabolic markers, nutritional status assessments, physical activity assessments, QoL assessments, surgery-related variables and pathological findings. Demographic data will include age, sex, smoking status and ASA physical status. Medical history will include family history, alcohol abuse, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, heart disease and history of allergies. Vital signs will include blood pressure and heart rate. Laboratory data will include routine blood tests, liver and renal function tests and measurements of the levels of tumour markers, including alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) and carbohydrate antigen 72-4 (CA72-4). Surgery-related data will include date of surgery, tumour site, surgical approach (laparoscopic, open or robotic), length of hospital stay, discharge date and postoperative complications. Pathological data will include tumour histological type, Tumour-Node-Metastasis (TNM) stage, tumour grade, molecular features, invasion status and resection margin status. Patients’ baseline dietary habits will be recorded before randomisation as part of the baseline assessment.

Follow-up data

Data collected during follow-up will include adherence data, nutritional complications, chemotherapy-related data, laboratory data, levels of circulating metabolic markers, nutritional assessments, physical activity assessments, QoL assessments, disease progression and survival outcomes.

Adherence will be assessed by the number of completed FMD cycles and the number of days completed per FMD cycle in the FMD group and any spontaneous dietary restriction in the control group. Nutritional complications and any related hospitalisations will be recorded. Chemotherapy-related data will include the chemotherapy regimen, the administered dose, the planned and actual start dates, the planned and actual number of cycles, treatment duration, any regimen modifications and any chemotherapy-related toxicities. When tumour metastasis or recurrence is confirmed, the date of diagnosis, involved site(s) and number of lesions will be recorded. Survival data will include vital status (alive/deceased), cause of death (cancer-related/other) and date of death.

Study visits and follow-up

Inpatient visit

The trial timeline is shown in table 1. During hospitalisation, screening, patient enrolment, informed consent provision and baseline assessments will be conducted during a preoperative visit. On Days 3 and 5 after surgery, clinical and laboratory data will be collected and dietary guidance will be provided during postoperative inpatient visits.

Post-discharge follow-up

After discharge, participants will continue to be followed up by the research team through a combination of outpatient visits and electronic communication. During the active intervention phase, participants will be followed up before each scheduled 5-day FMD cycle, shortly after the completion of the cycle and during the subsequent post-cycle follow-up period. During these follow-ups, dietary guidance will be provided, adverse events will be recorded and study assessments will be completed, as needed. To minimise the differences in follow-up frequency between the groups, participants in the control group will be followed up at time points comparable to those in the FMD group.

During the pre-FMD follow-up visit, the research team will reconfirm the actual start dates of both chemotherapy and the FMD cycle. After each chemotherapy cycle, chemotherapy-related toxicities will be recorded during follow-up.

Participants will undergo a follow-up assessment at 6 months postoperatively. Afterwards, survival follow-up assessments will be conducted at least every 3 months. For patients experiencing severe adverse reactions or exhibiting unstable disease conditions, follow-up may be performed more frequently as clinically indicated.

Data management

To protect their privacy, each participant will be assigned an independent study identification number. Files containing participants’ personally identifiable information will be stored separately in a secure location with restricted access. Paper study records will be stored in locked cabinets and accessible only to authorised researchers. All study records will be retained for 10 years after completion of the study.

Data monitoring

An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will periodically review the progress of the study and the cumulative safety data. The DMC will consist of members with expertise in clinical research, trial management and biostatistics. They are not directly involved in the day-to-day conduct of the study.

Interim safety analyses

Periodic safety reviews will be conducted throughout the trial. The DMC will convene at least one safety review meeting per year to review cumulative safety data and the distribution of adverse events.

DMC safety stopping rules

An emergency DMC review will be triggered when the cumulative incidence of CTCAE V.5.0 grade≥3 adverse events clearly attributable to the FMD intervention reaches 10% of all randomly assigned participants in the FMD group or when the DMC judges that continuing the trial poses an unacceptable safety risk. On the basis of this review, the DMC may recommend suspending FMD intervention, modifying the protocol or terminating the trial.

Operative quality

Study centres were selected on the basis of the colorectal surgical volume and their experience in conducting clinical trials. At all centres, >200 colorectal surgeries are performed annually, and prospective clinical studies are conducted, thus supporting consistency in surgical quality and trial implementation across the centres.

Investigator training

Before the start of the study, all the investigators will receive standardised training to ensure consistency in data collection and protocol execution across the study centres, which will be led by Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University. The training will be delivered through a series of courses and will include eligibility assessment, study procedures, completion of CRFs, physical activity assessments, recording of adverse events and administration of scales.

All the investigators will be required to complete the training. Throughout the entire research process, multicentre meetings will be held to address the challenges that may arise during the trial implementation and to maintain consistency across the centres.

Sample size calculation

Participants will be enrolled in the study over a 2-year period, with a follow-up duration of 3 years. On the basis of previous literature, the 3-year DFS among patients with stage III CRC is approximately 76%.35 36 An 8% improvement in 3-year DFS is prespecified as the target effect size, informed by unpublished data from our group’s previous FMD study. Using PASS V.15 for sample-size determination and assuming a two-sided α value of 0.05 and power of 80%, a total of 154 DFS events will be required. To achieve this number of events under the assumed survival rates, 301 participants per group will be required, resulting in a total sample size of 602 participants.

Statistical methods

Major analysis

The primary analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle and will include all randomised participants. The Kaplan-Meier (K-M) method will be employed to estimate the median DFS time and the 1-year, 2-year and 3-year DFS rates, and the K-M survival curve will be plotted by treatment group. The HR, with the corresponding 95% CI, between the two groups will be estimated using a stratified Cox regression model. The 95% CI of HR is calculated using the Wald method. Intergroup comparisons of DFS will be performed using a stratified log-rank test with stratification factors. OS will be analysed using the same approach. A fixed-sequence testing procedure will be used to control the type I error, with DFS tested first, followed by QoL and OS. No interim analyses are planned for DFS.

In addition to the primary ITT analysis, a prespecified multivariable Cox proportional hazards model will be fitted to adjust for the following covariates: age, sex, baseline BMI, anaemia status, tumour location, pathological stage, histological grade, molecular features, surgical approach, chemotherapy exposure and study site.

Continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD or median (and 25th and 75th percentiles); categorical variables will be expressed as frequency (percentage). Between-group differences in continuous variables will be analysed using independent t tests or Mann-Whitney U tests, depending on the data distribution and assumptions. Categorical variables will be compared using χ² or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Participants lost to follow-up will be censored at the last follow-up visit at which they were confirmed event-free. Other types of missing data will be handled using methods appropriate for the data type.

Safety analyses

Safety analyses will be performed in the safety population, which includes all patients receiving at least one administration of the FMD regimen. Adverse events related to FMD intervention will be summarised descriptively by type and maximum CTCAE grade. The number and percentage of participants experiencing any grade≥3 adverse event will be reported. Serious adverse events will be reported to the ethics committees.

Per-protocol analyses

A per-protocol (PP) analysis is prespecified as a supportive analysis. In the FMD group, the PP population will include participants who complete at least three of the planned four FMD cycles with≥3 of 5 days of within-cycle adherence. In the control group, the PP population will include participants who do not adopt any self-initiated dietary restriction outside the protocol.

Sensitivity analyses

Prespecified sensitivity analyses will assess the robustness of the primary analyses with respect to missing data, loss to follow-up, pathological stage, FMD adherence and potential control-group contamination.

All the statistical analyses will be performed using International Business Machines Corporation Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (IBM SPSS) Statistics for Windows and R statistical software. Furthermore, prespecified subgroup analyses will be performed to assess the consistency of the findings across different subgroups. The full details of all the analyses will be provided in a statistical analysis plan which will be completed before the database lock.

Patient burden and potential benefits

Possible risks and management

Participants allocated to the FMD group may experience study-related burdens associated with dietary intervention, including hunger, temporary mood changes or reduced dietary tolerance during the FMD cycle. To minimise these burdens, participants in the FMD group will receive regular support from the research dietitians. If participants develop study-related harms, they will receive free treatment provided by the research team of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University.

Potential benefits and compensation

Study-related costs

All study-related assessments and metabolic parameter measurements will be conducted for participants free of charge.

Before enrolment, trained investigators will obtain written informed consent from all participants, including consent for the collection, storage and use of clinical data and biological samples in this study. Participation will be voluntary, and participants may withdraw from the study at any time without any negative consequences for their routine medical care.

Protocol amendments

Any important amendments to the protocol will be submitted to the relevant ethics committees for review and approval and will be updated at ClinicalTrials.gov, as appropriate.

Ancillary study

Blood and stool samples will be collected from participants for the future planned exploratory analyses of biomarkers of interest. The samples will be collected and processed according to a standardised protocol and stored at −80 °C for a minimum of 10 years.

Trial status

Recruitment for this trial commenced in June 2022, and the target enrolment was achieved in September 2024. The final follow-up is expected to be completed in October 2027, with the database lock scheduled for December 2027 and statistical analyses anticipated to be completed in the first half of 2028.

Discussion

This multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial is designed to evaluate the long-term effects of FMDs on survival outcomes in patients with CRC undergoing radical resection. To date, evidence supporting the effects of FMDs on long-term survival in patients with cancer remains scarce. Most available studies have focused on feasibility, safety or short-term outcomes. Therefore, this study is intended to provide high-quality clinical evidence on the long-term efficacy of this dietary intervention for cancer survivorship.

The findings of this trial are expected to inform several future lines of research. The prospective data will inform the design of future studies and help define the optimal FMD regimen for oncology patients. Furthermore, the planned ancillary analyses of biological specimens may provide direction for more focused mechanistic studies. Finally, the findings may inform the evaluation of FMDs in other solid tumour types.

This trial has several strengths. First, it uses a multicentre randomised design, which may improve the robustness and generalisability of the findings. Second, the intervention is supported by a standardised FMD manual, diet kit and dietitian-guided follow-up, which may enhance the consistency of study implementation across the participating centres. Third, the study will involve the monitoring of dietary adherence, levels of metabolic markers, nutritional status, quality of life and chemotherapy-related toxicities, providing a comprehensive evaluation of both the potential efficacy and safety of FMDs.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. First, because this study involves a dietary behavioural intervention, the blinding of participants and treating investigators is not feasible. Second, the absence of an internationally standardised 24-hour dietary recall instrument may limit the systematic quantitative assessment of dietary intake and macronutrient composition. In addition, future FMD trials in oncology may benefit from within-cycle ketone monitoring as a simple way to verify adherence. Third, to our knowledge, no published FMD trial of comparable design reporting DFS in cancer patients was available at the trial design stage to directly inform the assumed effect size. The true effect may be smaller; nevertheless, this trial will still provide important groundwork for future studies. Finally, as the primary endpoint is a time-to-event outcome, statistical power depends mainly on the number of observed DFS events. The sample size calculation does not prespecify a dropout adjustment. To minimise loss to follow-up and participant dropout, a multi-tiered follow-up management strategy will be implemented, and the impact of any incomplete follow-up will be evaluated through prespecified sensitivity analyses.

In summary, this study will evaluate the potential long-term clinical value of periodic FMDs in patients with CRC undergoing radical resection. These findings may inform future dietary strategies and provide prospective evidence supporting the efficacy of this intervention for improving long-term oncological outcomes.

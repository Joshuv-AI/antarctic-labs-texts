The bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells derived migrasomes induced by Titania nanotubes surface serve as chemotaxis effect for osteogenesis
Authors: Guangwen Li, Yan Zhang, Haochen Wang, Yuqi Zhao, Kexin Liu, Zhe Li, Meng Meng, Yide He, Wen Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The regulatory role of migrasomes (Migs) has attracted growing attentions recently. However, most of the reports only focus on the influence of donor cells on Migs contents, regarding the substrate information. In the present study, the bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) derived Migs were investigated on titania micropits/nanotubes (MNT) under different anodization voltages. The Migs formation was dependent on nanotubes dimensions, which was the most dominant on MNT5 (anodization under 5 V) surface and in line with ITGA5 expression level. The cargo analysis revealed significant enrichment of chemotaxis, in which the CXCL12 and CCL2 were the top enriched. Afterwards, the Migs could induce similar chemotaxis effect of CXCL12/CXCR4 axis in BMSCs and ECs and CCL2/CCR2 axis in macrophages. Further, the engulfment of Migs could induce significant enhancement of BMSCs osteogenic differentiation, ECs tube formation and macrophages M2 polarization. The in vivo ectopic bone formation model was subcutaneous implantation of biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP)/acylated methacrylate gelatin (GelMA) composite hydrogel with or without Migs. The scaffold could induce abundant cells recruitment 7 days post implantation and the CD90+ or CD31+ cell populations were significantly increased in the presence of Migs. After implantation for 2 and 4 weeks, the new bone growth within scaffold was significantly increased by Migs incorporation, both around BCP backbone and in the space area. In addition, the new bone area showed significant upregulation of CD163, CD31 and OSX. In conclusion, the titania nanotubes induced Migs from BMSCs could both recruit regenerative cells and directly promote osteogenesis, which may represent a new type of therapeutic EVs in bone tissue engineering.Graphical abstract Titania nanotubes surface could induce migrasomes (Migs) formation in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) through upregulation of integrin alpha 5 subunit. The BMSCs derived Migs were enriched of various chemokines that enable enhanced chemotaxis effects in BMSCs, endothelial cells (ECs) and macrophages. Meanwhile, the Migs could also directly promote osteogenesis, angiogenesis and macrophages M2 polarization both in vitro and in vivo.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12951-025-03641-2.

Introduction

As newly discovered extracellular vesicles (EVs), migrasomes (Migs) have attracted growing attentions recently. It has already been proven that Migs can participate in various regulations of both physiological and pathological processes [1, 2]. Since the Migs are substrate-anchored EVs, they may contain both location and cargo information, which is a particular feature for cell communication [3]. This implies that the substrate materials properties may have crucial influences on Migs regulation effect. According to the existed reports, the conventional Migs production in vitro is culturing cells on fibronectin (FN) coated dishes [4]. Whether the substrate physical topography information can be sensed by cells for Migs formation has been neglected. In fact, the Migs formation is dependent on the integrin subtypes matching with ECM proteins [5], which is also considered as the key hub for signals transduction on nanotopographic surface [6, 7]. Previously, our group has confirmed that the titania micropits/nanotubes (MNT) surface could generate more Migs formation in macrophages [8]. In addition, due to the effect of nanotopography on cell behaviors, the Migs contents may also closely related to cell status. Since Migs have exhibited some therapeutic potency, they may represent a new type of EVs for tissue regeneration. In this occasion, the nanotopographic surface may be a powerful tool for desired Migs generation.

The versatile cargos are the molecular basis for cell communication, such as mRNA [9], cytokines [10], chemokines and even damaged mitochondria [11], which may depend on different cell types and status. During the repairment of bone defects, the bone mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) recruitment and osteogenic differentiation are considered as the fundamental cellular events of osteogenesis. Until now, there are numerous nanomaterials-based bone regeneration strategies, particularly the nanoparticles delivery system that has great potential for spark endogenous bone regeneration capacity [12–14]. The allocation and enhanced osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs are the main steps for bone defect regeneration. Therefore, the chemotaxis effect may be a pivotal target for inducing osteogenesis, which is exactly one of the main contents of Migs [15]. For instance, the Migs from adipose derived stem cells have been reported enrichment of CXCL12, which could recruit stem cells for soft tissue regeneration [16]. In the meanwhile, angiogenesis is also an indispensable process for bone defect repair [17]. The pro-angiogenic factors such as CXCL12 and VEGFA are also found in Migs from monocytes [15]. Taken together, we hypothesize that the chemotaxis effect of Migs may represent as a new opportunity for bone tissue engineering.

In the present study, the BMSCs derived Migs have been investigated systematically on MNT surfaces, which have been well-documented as BMSCs differentiation inductive nanotopographies that depends on diameter dimensions [18]. It has been validated that the number of Migs is significantly increased on proper diameter of NTs surface, which is preferentially enriched of CXCL12 and CCL2. Due to the chemotaxis effect of these Migs, the BMSCs recruitment, differentiation and angiogenesis were significantly increased.

Results

Migs formation on MNT surfaces depends on nanotubes diameter

The MNT surface was prepared according to our previous reports by hydrofluoric acid etching and anodization [19, 20]. Under lower magnification, the Ti surface was etched to microscale fish scale like pits (Fig. 1a). Under higher magnification, the surface was full of nanopores with different diameters that were in linear relationship with anodizing voltages (Fig. 1a). The detailed surface characterization was provided in Figure S1. Then the BMSCs were cultured on different Ti surfaces to investigate Migs formation profiles. First of all, the migration behaviors were determined by live cells tracking and wound healing test (Figure S2). It was notable that the cells migration was more active on MNT surfaces and the MNT5 surface showed the longest movement distance (Figure S2 a-b). Similar phenomenon was also observed in wound healing test that the MNT5 could induce the most rapid cell moving towards the gaps (Figure S2c-d). In the next step, the Migs formation was directly observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM) in situ. In line with the migration phenomenon, the MNT surfaces could induce more Migs formation compared to the smooth surface (Fig. 1b-c). On MNT5 surface, the Migs were more closely aggregated around cell body and the number was about two folds higher than other MNT surfaces (Fig. 1b-c). In a parallel study, the Migs formation was observed by high intelligent and sensitive structured illumination microscopy (HIS-SIM) after TSPAN4-mCherry plasmid transfection. Similar to the SEM images, more Migs were detected on MNT surfaces and the MNT5 surface could induce abundant Migs formation adjacent to cell body (Fig. 1d-e).

Migs formation on MNT surfaces is in line with integrin subunit alpha 5 (ITGA5)

To validate the role of integrin in Migs formation, the integrin alpha subunits were examined by RT-qPCR (Fig. 2a). Generally, all the alpha subunits were altered by the nanotopographies, in which the ITGA5 was the most notably upregulated (Fig. 2a). On MNT5 surface, the ITGA5 was increased to more than 3 folds higher, which was increased 2 ~ 3 folds higher on MNT10 and MNT20 surfaces (Fig. 2a). This is in agreement with previous report that the Migs formation is determined by ITGA5 paring with FN [5]. The immunofluorescence staining showed abundant co-localization between Migs and ITGA5 in the peripheral area (Fig. 2b). In the peripheral area, the fluorescence intensity of ITGA5 was also increased to more than 2 folds higher on MNT5 surface compared to the smooth surface (Fig. 2c). Further, the three-dimensional co-localization between ITGA5 and Migs was investigated in the retraction fiber edge area (Fig. 2d). Both the ITGA5 intensity and co-localization intensity were significantly increased on MNT surfaces and MNT5 showed the highest co-localization ratio (Fig. 2e). As a consequent, the MNT5 surface may be more prominent for Migs formation.

To further confirm the identity of Migs on MNT5 surface, the elastic modules was measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM). According to the classical theory of Migs formation, tetraspanin 4 (TSPAN4), a member of the tetraspanin protein family, is a specific marker for Migs. TSPAN4 forms tetraspanin-enriched microdomain (TEM) enriched with tetraspanin proteins on the retraction fibers, which further aggregate on the retraction fibers to form tetraspanin-enriched macrodomains (TEMAs). These TEMAs, which are distributed in an intermittent bead-like pattern, have an elastic modulus 5–10 folds greater than other regions. When the elastic modulus of the TEMAs in the intermittent bead-like pattern exceeds that of the rest of the membrane, the ordinary membrane tube regions become thinner, break, and expand to form Migs during retraction fibers stretching [21]. Compared to the cell body, the elastic modules of retraction fiber area increased about 2 folds, while the Migs area increased to more than 10 folds (Figure S3). The live cell imaging by HIS-SIM could clearly indicate the characteristic formation process of Migs (Video S1). During cell movement, the retraction fibers crosslinked as a net and remained on substrate surface. A number of Migs was formulated at the tips of fibers. Another important feature was the extensive connection between two cells via retraction fibers, which might be the pathway of Migs transfer [9]. From these investigations, the MNT5 surface may be a novel platform for Migs generation. The generated Migs can either in situ regulate adjacent cells via cell retraction fibers exchange or isolated for any other cells regulation after engulfment. In the following explorations, the BMSCs derived Migs generated by MNT5 surface were isolated for detailed analysis, in order to serve as therapeutic EVs in osteogenesis.

Migs isolation from MNT5 surface and cargo analysis

The Migs on MNT5 surface was isolated based on the ultra-speed density gradient centrifugation method (Fig. 3a) and several characterizations were performed [22]. Briefly, the nanoparticles tracking analysis (NTA) indicated about 750 nm average size distribution (Figure S4a). SEM observation showed similar size in spherical Migs (Figure S4b). The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed the membrane bounded structure that connected with hollow retraction fiber (Fig. 3b). Then the isolated Migs were subjected to Tandem Mass Tags (TMT) quantitative proteomics. To improve the production of Migs, the influence of cell density on Migs formation was optimized. The cell density at 60% could reach the maximum production of Migs on MNT5 surface (Figure S5). Compared to the cell body, there were 327 proteins upregulated and 232 proteins downregulated, suggesting a dramatical difference between the two cellular parts (Fig. 3c). Specifically, the “Extracellular” category was the top differentially expressed (Fig. 3c). This implies that the Migs may carry abundant extracellular signal molecules. It is already reported that the BMSCs derived Migs are enriched of chemokines [23]. To validate the chemokines cargo information within Migs, the Chemokine Array was deployed. As expected, there were abundant chemokines enriched in Migs (Fig. 3d). It was notable that C-X-C motif chemokine 12 (CXCL12) and chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2) were the two top enriched chemokines (Fig. 3d). This data was further confirmed by ELISA that the CXCL12 and CCL2 were enormously detected in Migs, not cell body (Fig. 3e-f).

In the following step, we wonder whether the chemotaxis effect of Migs could be a potential strategy for osteogenesis. Three main cell types were evaluated to confirm the chemotaxis effect, including BMSCs, endothelial cells (ECs) and macrophages that contributing to osteogenic differentiation, angiogenesis and M2 polarization respectively. Firstly, the engulfment of Migs in these three cell types were observed by confocal. The Migs showed high uptake efficiency that nearly all the observed Migs were internalized within cytoplasm of the three cells (Figure S6). This may be attributed to the plasma membrane nature of EVs that they could be recognized easily by receipt cells [24].

The effect of Migs on BMSCs chemotaxis

To evaluate the chemotaxis effect of Migs on BMSCs, the Transwell study was performed. The Migs showed significantly attraction of BMSCs passing through the membrane, in a dose-dependent manner. From the observation, 100 µg/ml may induce the maximum chemotaxis effect in this condition (Figure S7). Once the neutralization antibodies of CXCL12 and CCL2 were co-presented, the migration rate was significantly reduced, particularly in the anti-CXCL12 co-treatment (Fig. 4a-b). Further, when their receptors CXCR4 and CCR2 were blocked by antagonist AMD3100 and RS504393 respectively, only CXCR4 blockage significantly reduced the chemotaxis effect of Migs (Fig. 4a-b). This may indicate that the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis plays dominant role in the chemotaxis effect of Migs. To further confirm this phenomenon, the wound healing test was performed. After 48 h healing, the Migs treatment showed comparable migration rate to exogenous CXCL12 (Fig. 4c-d). Once the CXCR4 receptor was inhibited by AMD3100, the addition of Migs did not show enhancement of migration anymore (Fig. 4c-d).

The effect of Migs on BMSCs osteogenic differentiation

In the direct treatment of Migs to BMSCs, the RNA-seq was introduced to generally figure out the BMSCs reactions. In the GO enrichment analysis, cellular process, biological regulation and response to stimulus were top enriched in the biological process (BP) category (Fig. 5a), indicating an active cell bioactivity. In cellular components (CC) category, cell part, organelle and membrane were top enriched (Fig. 5a). This indicated that the Migs might have some crossed markers with organelles and the membrane mediated uptake process was active. In the molecular function (MF) category, binding, transcription regulator activity and molecular function regulator were enriched (Fig. 5a), suggesting active genes expression and signaling transduction. In the KEGG enrichment analysis, various signaling pathways were activated, in which the Jak-STAT was closely related to osteogenic differentiation (Fig. 5b) [25]. Further, CXCL12 has been reported to activate JaK-STAT pathway [26]. To confirm the osteogenic potency and the role of JaK-STAT pathway, the osteogenic differentiation was investigated with or without JAK2 phosphorylation inhibitor AG490. By immunofluorescence staining, the osterix (OSX) was increased nearly 4 folds higher by Migs treatment (Fig. 5c, e). In the presence of AG490, the OSX expression was reduced to normal level (Fig. 5c, e). Meanwhile, single treatment with AG490 did not influence OSX expression apparently (Fig. 5c, e). Similar data was observed in osteopontin (OPN) staining (Fig. 5d, f), indicating that the JaK-STAT signaling pathway may be preferentially participating Migs mediated osteogenic differentiation. In addition, the osteogenic differentiation was also examined by alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and calcium nodules mineralization (Fig. 5g-i). In agreement with the immunofluorescence staining, the ALP activity and mineralization were dramatically increased by Migs engulfment, which were reduced to control level in the presence of AG490 (Fig. 5g-i). These data suggest that the Migs could directly induce osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in vitro.

The effect of Migs on ECs chemotaxis and tubes formation

The angiogenesis of ECs was also pivotal for osteogenesis so that the influence of Migs on HUVECs bioactivity was explored. The addition of Migs could induce similar chemotaxis effect with CXCL12 by Transwell study (Fig. 6a-b). In the wound healing test, the Migs treatment induced rapid gap closure while the effect was diminished in the presence of AMD3100 (Fig. 6c-d). Therefore, the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis may also be the key pathway for chemotaxis of ECs. Then the HUVECs tube formation assay was performed in Matrigel surface. After 24 h culture, both the vascular network length and branching were increased more than 2 folds by Migs treatment (Fig. 6e-g).

The effect of Migs on macrophages chemotaxis and polarization

The role of macrophages in osteogenesis is also arising growing attentions recently. Different macrophages polarization directions contribute to different microenvironment, mainly the pro-inflammatory cytokines or anti-inflammatory cytokines. The addition of Migs could also dramatically enhance macrophages chemotaxis to more than 10 folds (Fig. 7a-b). Blocking CXCL12/CXCR4 and CCL2/CCR2 could both inhibit the chemotaxis effect of Migs, in which the CCL2/CCR2 axis was much more obvious (Fig. 7a-b). For the macrophage’s phenotypes examination, the expression of CD163 and iNOS was dual stained by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 7c). In the Migs treatment group, the CD163 expression was significantly increased to more than 3 folds higher (Fig. 7d). Although the iNOS expression was downregulated slightly, the difference was statistical significance (Fig. 7d). Finally, the represented cytokines secretion was checked by ELISA. The IL-10 and TGF-β1 were increased to more than 4 folds higher after Migs treatment while the IL-1β and IL-6 were downregulated (Fig. 7e). Therefore, the macrophages anti-inflammatory effect may be activated and beneficial for osteogenesis.

Cell recruitment in vivo

To evaluate the cell recruitment ability in vivo, a biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP)/acylated methacrylate gelatin (GelMA) composite hydrogel was prepared. According to previous reports, BCP was the most classic biomaterial for evaluating the cell recruitment ability and ectopic bone formation in vivo while the GelMA was an ideal storage for EVs [27, 28]. The BCP/GelMA hydrogel with or without Migs were subcutaneously implanted in the rat model (Fig. 8a). After healing for 3 days, the recruited cells were observed in all the three groups, as indicated by both fluorescence microscopy and SEM imaging (Fig. 8b). The total cell number in Migs containing group was more than 4 folds higher than Migs absent samples (Fig. 8c). Then the recruited cells were stripping off for flow cytometry analysis by dual staining with PE-CD90 and FITC-CD31. The populations of CD90 positive cells were 6.4%, 7.5% and 8.9% in the three groups (Fig. 8d). Similarly, the populations of CD31 positive cells were 1.3%, 6.5% and 17.6% respectively (Fig. 8d). The increase of CD90 and CD31 positive cells may suggest that the recruited cells were mainly stem cells and ECs, which is prerequisite for osteogenesis.

Ectopic bone formation

The ectopic bone formation was evaluated after 2- and 4-weeks post implantation. From the micro-CT scanning, apparent new bone was formulated throughout the whole scaffold in the Migs incorporated group after healing for 2 weeks (Fig. 9a, Figure S8a). Quantitative analysis revealed that the bone volume ratio, trabecular number and thickness were significantly increased in the Migs incorporating scaffold, while the trabecular space was decreased to a rather low level (Fig. 9b). The 4-week examinations showed similar alterations and the formulated new trabecular bone was much denser with more volume and less space (Fig. 9a, c). The histological analysis could indicate clearly new bone tissues formulated around BCP granules, without apparent toxicity (Fig. 9d, Figure S8b). In the Migs loading scaffold, the new bone tissues were thicker, denser and rich of blood cells (Fig. 9d). Immunohistochemistry showed extensive expressions of CD163, CD31 and OSX, both in the cell-rich area and peripheral space (Fig. 9e, Figure S8c). In the Migs containing scaffold, the cavity area was aggreged with abundant cells, including CD31 positive blood vessels and CD163 positive macrophages (Fig. 9e, Figure S8c). The OSX was also vastly expressed in the cavity region (Fig. 9e, Figure S8c). From the immunofluorescence staining, there were abundant cells aggregation in Migs loaded scaffold (Fig. 9f). CD31 and CD163 positive cells were also increased apparently (Fig. 9f-h). The OSX expression was highly upregulated all over the examined area, indicating an active osteogenesis process (Fig. 9f-h). The tissue RT-qPCR also revealed dramatical mRNA expression of OSX, Runx2 and OPN (Figure S8d).

Discussion

The unique formulation mechanism of Migs implies that the cell culture surface is crucial for Migs generation, which may be a significant difference from free EVs. The present study has confirmed the obvious Migs formation on MNT surfaces, particularly on MNT5 surface. According to the biogenesis mechanism of Migs, the integrins pairing with ECM proteins is required. Specifically, on cell body the integrins-ECM interaction provides adhesion force for cell migration. On Migs formation sites, the integrins-ECM interaction anchors Migs during retraction fibers movement [29]. This process is quite similar to the focal adhesion mechanism of cells on the surface with nanotopography. The NTs surface has been examined for many years for enhancing BMSCs migration and osteogenic differentiation at size dependent manner [18]. Although the conclusions vary upon different conditions, the cell migration and differentiation are considered as two synergetic behaviors, which is promoted on the smaller nanotubes surface [30]. From our observations, the MNT5 surface could induce the highest BMSCs migration rate and Migs formation, indicating their significant roles in BMSCs regulation. The specific surface geometry of the nanotubes array may affect the organization of ECM, such as fibronectin. ITGA5 is known for fibronectin binding, so an increased availability of fibronectin on the titania nanotubes surface could induce more ITGA5 expression to facilitate this interaction. The preferential upregulation of ITGA5 on NTs surface has been reported previously, which serve as the key component of focal adhesion and also the nanotopography signals transduction across cell membrane [31]. In Migs formation, the ITGA5 was also enriched on Migs membrane surface and its interaction with FN is a predominant role for Migs anchorage [32]. Hence, there might be a deeper link of nanotopography and Migs formation through sharing integrin subtypes. It is well known that the rear edge focal adhesion integrin endocytosis and recycling to leading edge to formulate new focal adhesion drive cell migration [33]. Therefore, the Migs formation may be intentionally selected by cells through controlling integrin subtypes distribution between focal adhesion and Migs, not just mechanical separation from retraction fibers [34, 35]. In addition, the Migs contents are closely related to cell status [36], which is directly regulated by nanotopography. In this occasion, the nanotopography generated Migs may be more gifted for therapeutic potency.

The chemotaxis effect of Migs may be a unique pattern for cells chemoattractant, attributing to its combination with chemokines and location information. A variety of chemokines have been checked out in Migs, suggesting their powerful chemotaxis potential. By interaction with their receptors, these Migs carried chemokines play important roles in embryonic development, immune response, and cancer metastasis [1, 2]. In biomaterials design for bone regeneration, the recruitment and osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs are the fundamentals for new bone tissue formation [37]. The CXCL12, also known as stromal cell-derived factor 1 (SDF-1), is a classical chemokine for stem cells recruitment and homing [38, 39]. As a result, the top enrichment of CXCL12 in Migs has induced significant enhancement of BMSCs migration/differentiation and in vivo CD90+ cells recruitment. In the meanwhile, the role of angiogenesis is essential for providing nutrients and oxygen during bone defects regeneration [40]. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis not only serves as chemoattractant for cell recruitment, it also directly participates bone tissue regeneration through inducing osteogenesis and angiogenesis [41]. Consequently, the Migs presence has promoted ECs migration/tube formation and CD31+ positive cells recruitment in scaffold. Another contributor in bone regeneration is macrophages mediated inflammatory environment. Numerous publications have confirmed the role of macrophages phenotypes in regulating inflammatory condition that indirectly affecting osteogenesis. Briefly, the M1 pro-inflammatory macrophages secrete inflammatory cytokines whereas the M2 anti-inflammatory macrophages secrete tissue-healing cytokines. The persist of anti-inflammatory state is required rapid bone regeneration. Thanks to the enrichment of CCL2 in Migs, which has been well confirmed as downmodulating macrophages pro-inflammatory production [42, 43]. As a consequent, the presence of Migs has significantly improved ectopic bone formation in coupling with angiogenesis ability.

Establishing cytokine gradient in vivo is blind if the cytokines are freely dispersed. According to the report, the cells are able to efficiently transfer signaling proteins into Migs to realize a localized secretion [44]. Once the cytokines are required for physiological process, the cytokine-laden Migs could rapidly release at desired sites [10]. Therefore, the Migs may bring an attracting strategy for biomaterials modification as smart cytokines delivery system. Further explorations may be suggested to fabricate patterned Migs within bioactive scaffold for guided tissue regeneration.

Conclusion

The BMSCs derived Migs can be generated on nanotopographic surface, by selective upregulation of ITGA5 for Migs interacting. Cargo analysis has revealed the enrichment of chemokines, particularly the CXCL12 and CCL2. As a consequent, the Migs treatment can induce both significant cell chemotaxis and differentiation in BMSCs, ECs and macrophages both in vitro and in vivo, to realize a promotion in ectopic bone formation in BCP/GelMA composite scaffold. The chemotaxis effect of Migs may provide a fresh perspective on developing cell attractive biomedical devices for guided tissue regeneration.

Experimental section

Specimen preparation Ti samples were prepared according to our previous reports. Briefly, pure Ti plates (10 × 10 × 1 mm, provided by Northwest Institute for Non-ferrous Metal Research) were polished to mirror smooth by SiC sandpaper (PT). The PT surface was etched in 0.5wt% HF for 30 min and anodized in the mixture of 0.5wt% HF and phosphoric acid. The anodization voltages were set at 5 V, 10 V and 20 V respectively and oxidation duration was set at 1 h. All the samples were sterilized by 75% ethanol soaking plus Co60 irradiation. The fabricated samples were examined by SEM (JEOL JSM-6701 F, Japan).

Migs generation Rat BMSCs (NCA83Ra01, Shanghai Jingfeng Biology and Technology Co. Ltd.) were cultured on Ti samples at the density of 105 cells/mL in 24 well plate. After culture for 3 days, the cells were fixed by 2.5% glutaraldehyde and dehydrated by ethanol for normal SEM observation (JSM-6701 F). In a parallel study, the live cells were transfected with TSPAN4-mCherry plasmid via lentiviral method according to the manufacturer’s protocol (K5910-00, Invitrogen, USA). After rinsing thoroughly with PBS, the cells were incubated in fresh media. The live cell imaging was performed by HIS-SIM (CSRbiotech, China) for 10 h recording. The obtained images were analyzed by ImageJ software to count the Migs number, which was defined as small vesicles structure either isolated or connected with retraction fibers.

RT-qPCR Cells on different Ti surfaces were directly lysed by TRizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) to perform total RNA extraction. The complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized by reverse transcription, which was then subjected to RT-qPCR to examine different integrin alpha subunits. The related primers were listed in Table S1.

Immunocytofluorescence staining After cultured for 3 days, the live cells on Ti surfaces were labeled by WGA488 (1:2000, Life Technologies, USA) for 30 min at 37℃. Then the cells were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min and permeabilized by 0.5% Triton X-100. After sealing in goat serum for 1 h, primary antibody against ITGA5 (Proteintech, China) was introduced for incubation overnight at 4℃. In the next day, secondary antibody of goat-anti-rabbit IgG H&L Alexa Fluor® 594 (Abcam, UK) was incubated for 2 h. Finally, the cell nuclei were stained by DAPI and the samples were mounted in anti-fade prolong gold to perform confocal observation (Nikon-A1R, Japan) in z-stack mode.

Migs isolation and cargo analysis The Migs isolation and purification from MNT5 surface was carried out by superspeed centrifugation at 4℃ according to previous reports. Briefly, 0.25% trypsin (Gibco, USA) was used to digest all the biocomponents from MNT5 surface, which was centrifugated at 1000 g for 10 min to remove cell bodies. Then the supernatants were subjected to two-stage centrifugation of 4000 g/20 min and 18000 g/30 min to obtain the crude Migs precipitates. Finally, the crude precipitates were purified by centrifugation at 15000 g/4 h in Extraction Buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for separation and the purified Migs at desired layer was aspirated for further analysis. To confirm the Migs identity, the purified Migs were fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde (Beyotime, China) for 2 h and dropped onto the copper mesh, which was negative stained by 2% phosphotungstic acid (PTA, Beyotime, China) for 40 s. After drying in vacuum hood, the samples were examined by TEM. For cargo analysis, the Migs were lysed by RIPA lysis buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) on ice and quantified by BCA protein array kit (Beyotime, China). Then the products were subjected to TMT proteomics and Chemokine Array C1/AAM-CHE-1-2 analysis (Shanghai Bioprofile, China).

Chemotaxis analysis Chemotaxis assay was performed in three cell types, including rat BMSCs (NCA83Ra01, Shanghai Jingfeng Biology and Technology Co. Ltd), HUVECs (BFN6021653, Shanghai Cell Bank) and macrophages (CP-R141, Wuhan Yipu Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). For the Transwell assay, 100 µL cell suspensions were located in the upper chamber and 600 µL media containing 0.1% FBS were located in the lower chamber. Based on different purposes, the lower chamber media was mixed with Migs, CXCL12, CCL2, neutralization antibodies (Invitrogen, USA) or receptor antagonist (MedChemExpress, USA). After incubation for 12 h, the Transwell chamber was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Beyotime, China) for 30 min and stained by 1% crystal violet (Biosharp, China) for 15 min. Then the samples were rinsed in deionized water and observed by inverted microscope (Nikon, Japan). The migration rate was calculated based on the purple stained cells ratio. For the wound healing test, the cells were seeded into 6-well plate containing migration insets and adapted for 3 days. The cells were pre-treated by mitomycin C (1 µg/mL) for 1 h prior to wound healing test, in order to diminish the influence of cell proliferation. After removing the migration insets, the media were changed to fresh ones containing different components that identical to Transwell assay. Images were taken immediately and 48 h post normal incubation by inverted microscope. Migration rate was calculated as the average migration distance across the start line.

RNA-seq Rat BMSCs were cultured in 24-well plate at the density of 2*105 cells/mL 1 day prior to osteogenic differentiation. The osteogenic differentiation was performed in osteogenic medium as described elsewhere with or without Migs. After induction for 7 days, the cells were frozen in liquid nitrogen and transferred for RNA-seq analysis (Shanghai Bioprofile).

Osteogenic differentiation Rat BMSCs were cultured in 24-well plate at the density of 2*105 cells/mL 1 day prior to osteogenic differentiation. The osteogenic differentiation was performed in osteogenic medium containing Migs (100 μg/mL), JAK2 phosphorylation inhibitor AG490 (50 μM, MedChemExpress, USA) or both. After induction for 7 days, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min either to perform ALP determination or immunocytofluorescence staining. Briefly, the BCIP/NBT (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) working solution was prepared by dissolving one tablet in 10 mL deionized water. After staining for 30 min, the reaction was terminated and images were taken by stereomicroscope (Leica). Meanwhile, the samples were permeabilized by 0.5% Triton X-100 and sealed by goat serum. F-actin polyclonal antibody (Proteintech, China) and goat-anti-rabbit IgG H&L Alexa Fluor® 594 secondary antibody was sequentially introduced. Then the samples were either treated with OSX or OPN primary antibodies, which were then incubated with goat-anti-rabbit IgG H&L Alexa Fluor® 488 secondary antibody (Abcam, UK). Finally, the nuclei were stained by DAPI to perform confocal examination. Images were taken for fluorescence intensity measurement by ImageJ software. In a parallel study, the osteogenic differentiation was lasted for 14 days. Then the cells were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde and treated by Alizarin red (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) staining for 30 min. the reaction was terminated by excessive deionized water rinsing and the images were taken by stereomicroscope. Afterwards, the stains were dissolved in 10% cetylpyridinium chloride (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for optical density measurement by plate reader at 620 nm wavelength.

Tube formation analysis The tube formation array was performed according to previous reports. Briefly, the Matrigel (Corning, USA) was thawed and transferred into pre-cooled 24-well plate, which was centrifugated for 5 min to exclude bubbles during pipping. The plate was incubated at 37°C for 30 min to complete gelation. The HUVECs were cultured on Matrigel surface with or without Migs (200 μg/mL) in growth medium. After incubation for 18 h, the cells were examined by inverted microscope for tube formation analysis.

Macrophages polarization analysis The macrophages were cultured in the glass-bottom confocal Petri dish at the density of 2*105cells/mL and treated with or without Migs (100 μg/mL) for 3 days. Then the cells were conducted for normal immunocytofluorescence staining procedure. Primary polyclonal antibodies of CD163 and iNOS (Proteintech, China) were introduced and secondary antibodies of goat-anti-rabbit IgG H&L Alexa Fluor® 488 and IgG H&L Alexa Fluor® 594 (Abcam, UK) were incubated sequentially. The relative intensity was examined by confocal. In the parallel study, four representative cytokines of IL-10, TGF-β1, IL-1β and IL-6 were determined by the related ELISA kits (Multi Sciences (Lianke) Biotech Co., Ltd, China).

Animal surgical procedure The animal experiments were performed in SD rats (male, 300±20 g) under the approve of the University Research Ethics Committee of the Fourth Military Medical University (No: IACUC-20230121). The rats were anesthetized by peritoneal injection of 0.1% pentobarbital sodium. The implantation sites at the bilateral of spinal on the back were shaved and sterilized. After the skin incision, the subcutaneous tissues were separated for the implantation of different biomaterials. The BCP/GelMA (EngineeringForLife, China) composites were fabricated based on previous publications with or without Migs incorporation.

Cell recruitment in vivo After 3 days post implantation, the inserted biomaterials were stripped off for cell recruitment analysis. Some of the biomaterials were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min and stained by DAPI for direct observation by inverted fluorescence microscope. Some were fixed in 2.5 glutaraldehyde to perform normal SEM examination after standard preparation protocol. The remaining samples were treated by collagenase and trypsin for stripping off the recruited cells. The obtained cells were sequentially labeled by PE conjugated rabbit-anti-rat CD90 and FITC conjugated rabbit-anti-rat CD31 (both from BioAlaboratory, China) for 30 min each. After washing thoroughly with PBS, the samples were analyzed by flow cytometry.

Ectopic bone formation The animals were sacrificed 2- and 4-weeks post implantation for ectopic bone formation analysis. Firstly, the inserted biomaterials were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 3 days and directly scanned by micro-CT (Aolong Radiation Instrument Group Co., Ltd., China). Region of interests (ROIs) were defined as the contour of the whole BCP scaffold. The new bone growth within the scaffold was quantified. Meanwhile, the scaffold was decalcified in EDTA to perform standard tissue sections of about 4 μm thickness. Then the obtained sections were subjected to conventional HE and Masson stainings (Beyotime, China).

Key molecules expression in vivo The obtained tissue sections were subjected to conventional immunohistochemistry. Briefly, the sections were dewaxing, hydronation, antigen recovery and hydrogen perioxide sealing. After sealing by goat serum, primary antibodies of CD163, CD31 and OSX (Proteintech, China) were introduced, followed by biotin conjugated secondary antibody (Abcam, UK) incubation. Finally, the sections were sealed by resin and observed by optical microscope. Similarly, the conventional immunohistofluorescence staining of CD163, CD31 and OSX was also performed and the samples were examined by confocal for intensity analysis.

Statistical analysis At least three independent experiments were repeated and the quantitative data were presented as Mean ± SD. The data processing and bar graphs were prepated by GraphPad Prism 10.0. One-way ANOVA was used for comparison and SNK-q test was set as the post-hoc comparison. The P value less than 0.05 was considered as statistical significance.

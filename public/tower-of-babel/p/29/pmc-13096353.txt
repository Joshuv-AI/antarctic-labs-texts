Quality of life in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Can Huang, Meng Chen, Yanfang Sun, Lin Zhang, Wei Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the most common type of liver cancer, is a leading global cause of cancer-related deaths. Its high morbidity, mortality, and complex treatment landscape underscore the importance of understanding patients’ quality of life (QOL). However, existing HCC QOL research lacks comprehensive statistical analysis of published data. This study aimed to identify emerging trends and collaborative networks in HCC QOL research. Publications were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection; after screening and deduplication, bibliometric information (including title, abstract, keywords, authors, affiliations, countries/regions, publication year, journal, and references) was extracted and analyzed using VOSviewer and the Biblioshiny R package. A total of 4951 eligible publications were identified, with annual output increasing over the 33-year period. China had the highest number of publications (n = 1095), the University of California, Los Angeles was the core institution, Cancers was the most prolific journal, and Younossi ZM emerged as a key collaborator. Hot keywords included HCC, QOL, cancer, survival, chemotherapy, and surgery. This analysis reflects the growing significance of HCC QOL research and may guide clinicians, researchers, and policymakers in prioritizing future studies. Given the varying benefits and risks of expanding treatment options, clarifying patients’ treatment priorities is critical to individualizing care through shared decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04759-x.Keywords: Bibliometric analysis, Quality of life, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Systemic therapy

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), accounting for 90% of primary liver cancers, is a highly prevalent and aggressive malignancy that ranks 6th in incidence and 3rd in mortality among global malignancies [1–3]. Established risk factors include chronic viral hepatitis, alcohol consumption, aflatoxin exposure, and increasingly metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease amid the global rise in obesity and metabolic syndrome [4, 5]. Despite advances in diagnosis and therapy, most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, limiting curative options (resection/transplantation); palliative therapies offer only modest survival benefits [6, 7].​ Quality of life (QOL), a key outcome reflecting physical, psychological, and social well-being, is significantly impaired in HCC patients compared to the general population, with deficits in physical function, emotional health, and overall health perception [8, 9]. Notably, QOL serves as an independent prognostic factor for survival in HCC patients, emphasizing its critical role in optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes [10–12].​

The exponential growth of scholarly publications on QOL in HCC patients complicates systematic literature reviews. Bibliometric methodologies, as quantitative analytical tools, address this challenge by assessing publication patterns (e.g., collaboration networks, citation impact, knowledge domain evolution) and visualizing complex data [13]. They enable the identification of research trends, hotspots, leading entities, and comprehensive scholarly landscapes [14], providing evidence-based insights to optimize interdisciplinary collaboration and research resource allocation [15]. Thus, bibliometric analysis of HCC-related QOL research is critical for identifying knowledge gaps and guiding future research trajectories.​.

To the best of our knowledge, no bibliometric analysis has focused on QOL in HCC patients. This study therefore aims to: characterize publication trends; identify leading countries, institutions, and researchers; visualize collaborative networks; analyze keyword clusters to reveal research hotspots; and highlight emerging frontiers—filling this research gap to inform future investigations.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

An electronic literature search was conducted in February 2025 using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC)—a large database containing important bibliometric information for each record, which has been widely used in bibliometric studies [16]. WoSCC was used as the source of bibliographic data. Web of Science has been widely accepted by researchers as a high-quality digital literature database and is generally considered the most suitable for bibliometric analysis.

Our primary search terms included: TS1=“liver cancer” OR “liver neoplasm” OR “liver tumors” OR “hepatocellular cancers” OR “hepatoma” OR “liver carcinoma” OR “hepatic tumor” OR “hepatic cancer” OR “cancer of liver” OR “cancer of the liver” OR “hepatic neoplasms” OR “neoplasm of liver” OR “liver and intrahepatic bile duct carcinoma” OR “liver and intrahepatic biliary tract cancer” OR “tumor of liver” OR “liver malignant tumors” OR “neoplasm of the liver” OR “hepatocellular carcinoma”; and TS2=“quality of life” OR “life quality” OR “health-related quality of life” OR “HRQOL”.

After retrieving records from the WoSCC database, the subsequent steps (eligibility assessment, exclusion of non-English papers and non-article types [e.g., non-reviews], and removal of duplicate records) were performed as illustrated in the flowchart (Fig. 1).

The flow chart of screening process

Data extraction

The full records and cited references from included studies were exported as tab - delimited text files. The following bibliometric parameters were extracted: titles, abstracts, keywords, authors, affiliations, countries or regions, years of publication, journal titles, and references. To ensure data reliability, the data were double - checked for accuracy, and any discrepancy was resolved through the re - examination of the original papers.

Keywords were consolidated using multiple criteria. For semantic equivalence, terms like ‘cancer’ and ‘malignant tumor’ would be merged as they convey the same core concept. For morphological variations, different forms of a word (e.g., ‘analyze’ and ‘analysis’) would be grouped. Abbreviations and their full forms (such as ‘HRQOL’ and ‘health - related quality of life’) were also combined. Domain - specific terminological standards were followed to ensure consistency within the field. Contextual links, where terms frequently co - occur and are closely related, were considered. These criteria were adopted to ensure that terms representing the same core concept were merged, thereby improving the replicability and clarity of the analysis.

Data analysis

This study used the Bibliometrix package in R software (version 4.4.3) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) for bibliometric analysis and the creation of scientific knowledge maps [17–19]. Specifically, the Bibliometrix R package was mainly used for analyzing annual production, publication production by country, authors’ contributions over time, local citation impacts of sources based on the H index, and trending topics [20]. VOSviewer, a powerful bibliometric tool, was used to create knowledge maps based on web data and to visualize and explore these maps [21].

In the network visualization produced by VOSviewer, nodes with consistent colors indicate the same clusters, while node size reflects the frequency of co - occurrence. Total Link Strength (TLS) represents the cumulative weight of connections between nodes, visually depicted by the width of links, highlighting collaboration strength. Additionally, this study leveraged VOSviewer for clustering analyses of countries, institutions, journals, authors, citations, and keywords due to its intuitive and clear performance in clustering tasks [22]. The Bibliometrix package provided in - depth quantitative analysis of various aspects, while VOSviewer offered intuitive visualizations. Together, they enabled a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the research field.

Discussion

General information

A bibliometric analysis of 33 years of research (1992–2025) on QOL in HCC patients revealed a consistent rise in research attention and publication volume, reflecting increasing clinical emphasis on enhancing these patients’ QOL. Since the late 20th century, QOL has become a critical metric in oncology alongside traditional clinical endpoints [23]. When evaluating treatment modalities, QOL should be considered a key primary outcome, alongside operative mortality and survival rates [24, 25]. In selecting optimal therapies, QOL is comparable in importance to disease-free and overall survival [26, 27], making it pivotal for HCC treatment decision-making.

Analysis of countries

Our bibliometric analysis identified distinct geographical patterns in HCC-related QOL research, with China, the US, Italy, and Japan as primary hubs. Notably, Chinese and American institutions collectively contributed nearly 50% of global publications.

China’s leading position correlates with its epidemiological burden (55% of global HCC cases), driven by risk factors including aflatoxin contamination, chronic viral hepatitis, traditional dietary practices, and the 1990 s iatrogenic hepatitis C outbreak from vaccination campaigns [28–30]. Targeted national funding and infrastructure development further solidified its role as a scientific leader. The US ranks second, with HCC cases mainly linked to lifestyle-related risks (alcohol consumption, metabolic syndrome from high-calorie, lipid-rich diets) [31, 32]. Substantial investments in oncology research infrastructure and translational medicine programs sustain its status as a major contributor to HCC QOL literature.

Analysis of institutions

The US’s dominance in HCC QOL research is further underscored by 7 of its institutions ranking among the top 10 in publication count, with Canada contributing 1 and China 2. Institutional collaborations are often geographically and culturally concentrated, and US institutions maintain relatively intensive and extensive cooperative networks.

Notably, despite the US and China accounting for half of global publications, cross-country cooperation remains limited to a handful of economically and technologically advanced nations. This suboptimal breadth and intensity of international collaboration, coupled with institutions’ reliance on fixed university partnerships rather than a global cooperative network, hinder the global advancement and long-term sustainable development of HCC QOL research. Thus, we strongly advocate for more comprehensive and in-depth collaborative studies across international institutions to strengthen the global research network in this field.

Analysis of journals

Journal analysis showed that Cancers had an exceptionally high publication volume and outpaced others in growth rate, securing its status as the leading journal in this field. Among the top 5 journals by publication count, 3 held Q1 JIF values. In summary, the number of HCC QOL-related articles in high-IF journals remains low, leaving substantial room for exploration. We anticipate more high-quality research to serve as a reference for the broader research community.

In terms of co-citations, Hepatology, Journal of Hepatology, and Journal of Clinical Oncology ranked top three. Most co-cited journals were Q1 titles, providing high-quality literary support for HCC QOL research and playing a pivotal role in disseminating and advancing the field. Future scholars should prioritize these journals to promptly access the latest international information and research progress in HCC and QOL.

Analysis of authors

Key authors including Younossi ZM, Kudo M, Llovet JM, Davidson BR, and Wang Y serve as the primary thought leaders propelling advancements in HCC-related QOL research. Notably, Kudo M and Younossi ZM stand out for their long-standing publication trajectories and substantial citation impact, rendering them frequent references among senior researchers in the field. While Wang Y leads in terms of total publication count, Younossi ZM dominates across critical impact metrics—boasting the highest total citations, h-index, and g-index among all core authors. Furthermore, his inclusion among the top co-cited authors in this domain synergizes with these quantitative indicators, collectively confirming his sustained core role as a foundational contributor and influential voice shaping the direction of HCC QOL research.

Analysis of keywords and research hotspots

Based on the quantitative analysis of keyword co-occurrence clusters, frequency, and centrality, the core research hotspots in the field of HCC QOL were identified as follows:

Disease-associated factors

The QOL of HCC patients is notably impaired by underlying liver diseases, cancer progression, and comorbidities. HCC is often complicated by cirrhosis and chronic viral hepatitis, which elevate treatment-related adverse event risks due to baseline liver dysfunction [33] and exacerbate symptoms (e.g., cirrhosis-induced ascites or abdominal distension) [34, 35]. Additionally, the absence of specific early symptoms in viral hepatitis-associated HCC frequently leads to advanced diagnosis [36], complicating treatment, worsening prognosis and causing emotional distress [37], and further reducing QOL via a physical-psychological impairment cycle [38, 39]. Advanced HCC typically involves liver metastases, presenting with appetite loss, weight loss, or abdominal pain that impairs daily function [39], while comorbidities such as oxidative stress and extrahepatic malignancies (e.g., colon cancer) further complicate treatment and increase adverse event risks [33], hindering QOL improvement. Thus, effective management of underlying liver diseases, targeted symptom control, and comprehensive assessment of overall disease burden are crucial for enhancing HCC patients’ QOL.

Patient-centric factors

HCC imposes heavy psychological burden (anxiety/depression as primary issues) [40, 41], which exacerbates physical symptoms and reduces QOL. Patients also experience persistent distress (fear, hopelessness, social isolation) due to treatment side effects, recurrence concerns, or fear of death [42–44]. Physical symptoms (liver pain [45], cancer-related fatigue [46–48], sleep disorders [49, 50]) further impact well-being. Social support and integrating palliative care with conventional treatment alleviate distress, enhance resilience, and address holistic needs [43, 51–53].

Therapeutic modalities

Local treatments: Surgical resection/ablation benefits early-stage HCC, but < 30% of patients are eligible for radical resection at diagnosis [54]; transarterial chemoembolization (TACE, first-line for intermediate HCC) improves survival but causes transient QOL decline via postoperative pain/fatigue [55–57].​

Minimally invasive/local ablative therapies: Radioembolization (Y-90) and stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) offer survival benefits with more manageable QOL impacts than chemotherapy [35, 57].​

Systemic therapies: Sorafenib/lenvatinib enhance survival but worsen toxicity-related QOL issues [58]; immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., atezolizumab + bevacizumab) delay QOL deterioration but require balancing immune-related adverse events [59, 60].

Individualized treatment plans aligned with patient preferences are critical, emphasizing the need for shared decision-making in therapeutic selection.

Evolution of research frontiers

Shifts in thematic focus

A temporal analysis of keyword trends (2010–2023) revealed a notable shift in research priorities: early studies (2010–2015) were dominated by keywords such as “pain” and “depression” (focused on single symptom assessment), while recent literature (2016–2023) increasingly features terms like “multidisciplinary care,” “shared decision-making,” and “PRO standardization,” as supported by the rising co-occurrence frequency. This evolution indicates a paradigm shift from isolated symptom management to a holistic, patient-centered approach [61].

Emerging trends in treatment-related QOL research

Keyword burst detection identified “immunotherapy” and “adverse event management” as emerging research frontiers since 2020. While early systemic therapies (e.g., sorafenib) were frequently linked to “toxicity-related QOL decline” [58], recent literature has shifted to exploring the balance between efficacy and QOL in immunotherapeutic regimens (e.g., atezolizumab + bevacizumab) [55]. This trend is reflected in the growing co-occurrence of “immune checkpoint inhibitors” with “QOL preservation,” which indicates an increasing focus on minimizing treatment-related QOL impairment in advanced HCC management [62].

Clinical implications derived

Standardization of QOL assessment

Given the dominance of EORTC QLQ-C30/QLQ-HCC18 and EQ-5D in the literature [63–65], clinical settings should prioritize the adoption of these validated scales to ensure consistent QOL monitoring across treatment phases (pre-treatment baseline, in-treatment dynamic assessment, and long-term follow-up). This standardization will facilitate cross-study comparison and enable early identification of QOL deterioration (e.g., fatigue or sleep disorders) [66].

Multidisciplinary collaborative care

The thematic shift toward “multidisciplinary care” in the literature underscores the need for integrated teams (oncologists, palliative care specialists, psychologists, and dietitians) to address the complex QOL needs of HCC patients. Specifically, early integration of palliative care—consistently linked to “symptom relief” and “QOL improvement” in keyword clusters [57, 61]—should be implemented for advanced HCC patients to mitigate psychological distress and treatment-related side effects.

Patient-centered treatment decision-making

Bibliometric analysis of recent studies (2018–2023) revealed a growing emphasis on “patient preferences” and “shared decision-making,” particularly in the context of advanced HCC treatment selection [62]. Clinicians should capitalize on this trend by incorporating structured tools (e.g., QOL priority checklists derived from PRO data) to align treatment plans with patients’ values.

Key research gaps and future directions

There is inadequate in-depth investigation into QOL variations among patients with different HCC etiologies, insufficient exploration of long-term QOL impacts of emerging therapies (especially immunotherapies), and a dearth of cross-regional collaborative research to ensure the global applicability of findings. To fill these gaps, future studies should focus on targeted research involving underrepresented patient populations, examine the biological and psychosocial mechanisms that drive treatment-associated QOL changes, and facilitate international multi-center cooperative projects.

Limitations

First, it relies solely on the WoSCC, which may introduce selection bias as relevant studies indexed in other databases (e.g., Scopus) or regional repositories could be missed. Second, restricting to English-language articles may overlook valuable contributions from non-English-speaking regions such as Africa and Latin America. Third, despite dual-author verification during synonymous keyword merging, potential merge errors or omissions cannot be fully excluded. Finally, unfiltered self-citations in the citation analysis may have impacted the results. Future studies should address these issues by integrating multiple databases, enhancing cross-cultural collaborations, and adopting mixed-methods approaches.

Conclusions

This bibliometric analysis highlights the growing importance of QOL research in the field of HCC, which can enable clinicians, researchers, and policymakers to prioritize areas for future investigation in order to develop comprehensive, patient-centered strategies. Additionally, we suggest that researchers should pay more attention to core authors, strengthen international collaboration and team exchanges, while clinicians should fully take into account patients’ preferences and values, and incorporate patients’ wishes into treatment decisions to provide more personalized treatment plans that meet patients’ needs.

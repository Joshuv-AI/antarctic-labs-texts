Postpartum metabolomics profile predicts 8-years postdelivery women’s subclinical atherosclerosis measures
Authors: Katherine Svensson, Marco Sanchez-Guerra, Vishal Midya, Haibin Guan, Megan Niedzwiecki, Georgia Dolios, Citlalli Osorio-Yáñez, Luz M. Del Razo, Julio C. Ayllon-Vergara, Guadalupe Estrada-Gutierrez, Maria Luisa Pizano Zárate, Lauren Petrick, Martha M. Téllez-Rojo, Robert O. Wright, Elena Colicino
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study evaluates women’s metabolomics profiles and subclinical measures of atherosclerosis. Pregnant women (< 20 weeks’﻿ gestation) living in Mexico City, Mexico, were recruited in 2007–2011 to the PROGRESS cohort. Serum untargeted metabolomics data at 1-month postpartum were analyzed using chromatography high-resolution mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS), and carotid intima media thickness (CIMT) was measured 8-years postdelivery using B-mode ultrasound. A metabolome-wide association study was conducted to identify metabolites associated with CIMT measures, adjusting for age, education, pre-pregnancy BMI, and smoking exposure. Class-and pathway-based enrichment analysis was performed. Metabolites of the superclasses lipids, organic acids, oxygen compounds, benzenoids and organoheterocyclic compounds were significantly associated with CIMT. The associations included relevant lipid-like metabolites such as 9S- and 13S-HODE, sphinganine-1-phosphate, the branched-chain amino acid 3-Methyl-2-Oxovaleric acid, and the benzenoid phenylpyruvic acid. The related biological pathways included regulation of white adipocyte differentiation, PPARalpha activation of gene expression, and regulation of lipid metabolism. Several metabolites at early postpartum were associated with 8-years postdelivery CIMT. These results highlight the potential of metabolomics as early markers for long-term women´s cardiovascular health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-18642-2.

Introduction

During pregnancy women experience critical physiological and cardiovascular adaptations to support both the woman’s health and the growing fetus1 After giving birth, some of these adaptations may persist either permanently or transiently, and their exacerbation can be influenced by women’s pre-pregnancy health status and pregnancy cardiovascular risk factors, such as advanced age, elevated systolic blood pressure, or high body mass index (BMI)2. Carotid intima media thickness (CIMT) is a valuable measure of subclinical atherosclerosis, reflecting the extent of carotid atherosclerotic vascular disease3, and it has predictive value for both stroke (i.e., transient ischemic attacks)4 and coronary heart disease (CHD)5. Among healthy primiparous mothers, CIMT increases during pregnancy and this change may persist even 2-years postdelivery indicating a potential long-term cardiovascular risk6. As cardiovascular disease (CVD) has a slow progression, early detection is crucial for effective prevention. However, no studies thoroughly examined the role of pregnancy and postpartum cardiometabolic health status in relation to subclinical measures of CVD several years postdelivery. As of today, the long-term association between immediate postpartum metabolomics and CIMT has not been studied.

The promising field of metabolomics offers a novel way to identify and quantify low molecular weight organic metabolites within a cell, tissue or biofluid7, and is a fingerprint of the past and concurrent cardiometabolic status of an individual and a potential biomarker of future adverse cardiometabolic health. Changes in the composition and abundance of metabolites may reflect cardiovascular and metabolic diseases, such as hypertension8 and diabetes9. Among adults with diabetes type 2, several metabolites including amino acids and lipids were associated with CVD up to 10 years later10. In addition, the predictability of both metabolites and traditional risk factors was higher as compared to only traditional risk factors10. Metabolites from different chemical classes have been associated with CIMT11, carotid atherosclerosis12, severe carotid artery stenosis13, and pathways related to cardiovascular risk factors14. Specifically among women, preeclampsia or a pre-term delivery, had a significant change in metabolites representing acylcarnitines and multiple classes of lipids15. Similarly, the metabolome of women with gestational diabetes was associated with insulin resistance 9 years postdelivery16. These studies suggest that screening during the perinatal period may predict future risk for cardiovascular disease among women postdelivery. However, only a few studies have evaluated the association between metabolomics profiles and CIMT, and none of them showed the relationship between metabolomic profiles immediately postpartum, reflective of concurrent and pregnancy cardiometabolic health status, and CIMT measures.

Due to cardiovascular risk factors after pregnancy, it is imperative to monitor women´s cardiovascular health long term to bring insight on the underlining mechanisms and early detection of cardiometabolic disease and prevention17. Currently, no studies have explored the association between metabolomic profiles at 1-month postpartum and CIMT after 8 years. Therefore, we leveraged data from the Programming Research in Obesity, Growth Environment and Social Stress (PROGRESS) pregnancy cohort to evaluate if metabolomic profiles among women at 1-month postpartum are associated with CIMT 8 years later.

Methods

Study population

The study population is comprised by women participating in the PROGRESS study, an ongoing pregnancy cohort18. Briefly, pregnant women were recruited during their 2nd trimester of pregnancy between the years of 2007 and 2011 at the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS). Women with less than 20 weeks of gestation, at least 18 years old and planning to live in Mexico for the next 3 years were eligible. Exclusion criteria were a medical history of heart or kidney disease, daily alcohol consumption, use of steroids and anti-epilepsy medication or multiple gestation pregnancy. A total sample of 293 participants were included in this analysis with both serum metabolomics data during 1-month postpartum (n = 661) and a carotid intima-media thickness measure at 8-years postdelivery (n = 352). All participants had complete information on the selected covariates of maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, education and smoking, except for one participant who was excluded due to missing data on smoking exposure.

Untargeted metabolomics phenotyping

PROGRESS women were followed-up at 1-month postpartum with an in-person study visit. Serum samples were collected in fasting and stored frozen at − 80 °C until analyzed. For metabolomics analysis, the protocol has been previously reported19,20. In brief, aliquots of 50-µL serum samples were thawed on ice, and vortexed, and then transferred to a microcentrifuge tube. Methanol (150 µL) containing internal standards was added to the tube and the solution was then vortexed and incubated at − 20 °C for 30 min. After centrifuged, the supernatant was evaporated to dryness using a Savant SC250EXP SpeedVac concentrator at 35 °C for 90 min and stored at − 80 °C until analysis. Samples were reconstituted in 60 µL of 80% acetonitrile or 100% methanol for analysis with liquid chromatography high-resolution mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS).

Two LC-HRMS were used to analyze sample and matrix extracts. For polar metabolites, a zwitterionic hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC) was used in positive mode (ZHP), and for semi- and non-polar metabolites a reverse phase liquid chromatography in negative mode (RPN). Process blanks consisting of water and quality control (QC) samples generated from combining 75 µL from 65 samples (9.8% of the total number) into a single aliquot and then re-aliquoted (50 ul), were extracted. The samples were analyzed in a randomized order and QCs were injected routinely throughout the run. For this analysis, we only used the annotated metabolites matched to an in-house library considering retention time, accurate mass, and MS/MS matching (when available) with pure standards analyzed under the same conditions. This provided the high confidence level of 1 or 2 based on the Metabolomics Standards Initiative criteria21,22. We used the Personal Chemical Database Library and Profinder software (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, USA) to identify and integrate peaks. The two analytical modes, ZHP and RPN chromatography, detected 106 polar metabolites and 149 semi- and non-polar metabolites, respectively. Metabolites with detected values in at least 50% of the samples were included for analysis. Detailed information on the metabolite detection rates can be found in supplementary material (Table S2). For the selected metabolites, missing values were imputed with the minimum value divided by square root of 2, and then log2-transformed to remove skewness and heteroscedasticity.

Carotid intima-media thickness

At 8-years postdelivery, women underwent the assessment of CIMT measure. According to standard protocol, women were examined using a cardiovascular ultrasound system (Vivid i®; General Electric, Milwaukee, WI, USA) with a 14-MHz linear transducer23,24. For standardization of the protocol, two examiners received training from a cardiologist with experience collecting CIMT measures. Training sessions were performed on adults before data collection of the study. The CIMT was measured on the right side of the participant´s neck area by an examiner using a B-mode ultrasound, and the participant in a supine position with the head slightly turned to the left. CIMT was calculated based on automatic detection of the intima and media layers in user-defined search region along the vessel wall. Multiple CIMT measurements were made between pairs of intima and adventitia points along the posterior wall of the vessel, setting the measuring area manually and using the Vivid i software algorithm. The commercial software algorithm analyzes the two-dimensional vessel structure, providing CIMT measurements of multiple points as mean, maximum and standard deviation. The mean, maximum and minimum CIMT were used for analysis.

Covariates

Covariates were collected through questionnaires administered during the enrollment visit at 2nd trimester of pregnancy. Women´s age was calculated based on date of birth and the date of the enrollment visit. Education level was collected in years of education and then categorized in three levels for analysis. Exposure to second-hand smoking during pregnancy was determined if anyone in the household currently smoked inside the home. As women were enrolled during pregnancy, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI) was calculated based on self-reported height and pre-pregnancy BMI (kg/m2) estimated from weights measured during pregnancy (2nd and 3rd trimester) and medical records from 6 months prior to pregnancy until early pregnancy period, as has been previously described25. Systolic and diastolic blood pressure was measured twice in a sitting position on the right arm using a sphygmomanometer (Tycos CE0050; Welch Allyn)26. The average of the two measures was calculated for analysis. The covariates were selected based on their association to both metabolomics and CIMT. Age, BMI, smoking and blood pressure are individual demographic characteristics that may explain small proportions of the metabolite variability27–29. Similarly, individual variability of CIMT is associated with cardiovascular risk factors such as age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, smoking, and lifestyle, i.e., alcohol consumption, exercise and diet30,31. Education was considered a covariate as it is a marker for socioeconomic status and has been associated with overall health status32. Diagnoses of cardiovascular and metabolic disease including diabetes, pre-diabetes, high blood pressure, hypertension, heart attack, stroke, preeclampsia, or eclampsia was collected retrospectively through questionnaires collected at 8-years postdelivery. Both blood pressure and a diagnosis of cardiovascular and metabolic disease were considered as additional statistical adjustments in sensitivity analyses.

Statistical analysis

Metabolome-wide association study

We summarized all variables via descriptive statistics, and we described crude associations with bivariate analysis. A metabolome-wide association study was conducted to identify metabolites associated with CIMT measures (i.e., mean, maximum and minimum) by fitting linear regression models between each metabolite and CIMT, adjusted for the major set of covariates and confounders33,34. Metabolites were ranked into deciles thus reducing the influence of outliers and influential points in the regression models. CIMT measures were divided by their interquartile range to get a more meaningful and interpretable effect size estimation of the association with each metabolite. The metabolites identified by the different analytical platforms, ZHP and RPN chromatography, were analyzed separately with CIMT. All models were corrected for multiple testing using the false discovery rate (FDR) and statistical significance was evaluated based on a 10% FDR-adjusted p-value. Due to the relatively small sample size (n = 293), the number of considered metabolites (n = 255), and the exploratory nature of this study we considered a FDR threshold of 10%, as previously used in other studies35,36. All models were adjusted for the following selected covariates: maternal age (years), education (less than high school, high school, more than high school), pre-pregnancy BMI (kg/m2) and exposure to smoking at home. Volcano plots were used to display results from the adjusted linear regression models between each metabolite and CIMT. In addition, forest plots were used to display the effect size of the association between significant metabolites and CIMT.

Pathway-based enrichment analysis

We performed class- and pathway-based enrichment analysis for biological interpretation of metabolites that were significantly associated with CIMT using MetaboAnalyst software version 6.037. MetaboAnalyst uses six existing metabolite libraries derived from human data to provide functional and biological context to metabolomics data. The software provides enrichment ratio, which represents the number of hits within a particular metabolic pathway divided by the expected number of hits. The software also provides p-values and FDR-adjusted p-values for all results. The enriched pathways with FDR-adjusted p-values < 0.05 were considered significant and utilized for further biological interpretation.

Sensitivity analysis

In addition, we ran a sensitivity analysis adjusting for systolic blood pressure measured at 2nd trimester of pregnancy. This analysis was conducted for 292 participants as one participant was missing systolic blood pressure and was excluded from the analysis. Due to only one missing value complete case analysis was used. We ran a second sensitivity analysis adjusting by cardiovascular and metabolic disease categorizing participants into two groups: those with and without a cardiovascular or metabolic disease. This categorizing was based on a self-reported composite diagnosis of cardiometabolic disease, which includes diabetes, pre-diabetes, high blood pressure/hypertension, heart attack, stroke, preeclampsia, or eclampsia. Rather than adjusting for each component individually this strategy allows us to account for overall cardiometabolic burden38,39. Volcano plots were used to visualize results from the adjusted linear regression models. All analyses were performed using the statistical program R version 4.4.0.

We also ran sensitivity analyses with additional adjustment by 1) systolic blood pressure at second trimester of pregnancy, and 2) history of a cardiovascular or metabolic disease. Results did not change significantly after adjusting for systolic blood pressure, and confirmed the significant metabolites identified in the main analysis (Figure S3-S6). There were a few exceptions, where metabolites on the border of significance (FDR-adjusted p-value: 0.106–0.130) became significant when adjusting for cardiovascular or metabolic disease, and when adjusting for blood pressure. These included the metabolites of choline (organic nitrogen compound), 6-carboxyhexanoic acid (lipids and lipid-like molecule) and homovanillic acid (benzenoid) (FDR-adjusted p-value: 0.068–0.097). In addition, when comparing the main results with sensitivity analysis, protoporphyrin was significant in main results for CIMTmean but non-significant when adjusting for cardiovascular or metabolic disease (FDR-adjusted p-value: 0.093 vs, 0.116). In addition, n-palmitoyl taurine was not significant in the main results for CIMTmean, but significant when adjusting for systolic blood pressure and in the main results for CIMTmax (FDR-adjusted p-value: 0.122 vs. 0.088 vs. 0.044, respectively).

Discussion

We identified several 1-month postpartum metabolites significantly associated with (mean and maximum) CIMT measures at 8-years postdelivery employing a metabolome-wide association analysis. Most of the identified metabolites were lipids or lipid-like molecules, organic oxygen compounds, organoheterocyclic compounds, benzenoids and organic acids derivatives. The results were consistent across (mean and maximum) CIMT measures and with different model specifications, (i.e., adjusting for blood pressure, and cardiovascular or metabolic disease). We did not find significant associations with minimum CIMT measure, and it may be because this measure captures the very early morphologic changes of subclinical atherosclerosis24, whereas the mean and maximum CIMT measures reflect the cumulative atherosclerotic burden and correlates more strongly with cardiovascular risk41.

At enrollment, no participants were diagnosed with chronic cardiometabolic conditions, while a majority (69%) remained diagnoses-free of cardiovascular or metabolic disease at 8-years postdelivery. A small percent (9.6%) had an 8-years postdelivery CIMTmax ≥ 0.8 mm, which is a criteria for atherosclerosis40. When comparing our main results after adjusting for either systolic blood pressure or cardiovascular or metabolic disease, there were metabolites on the border of significance that were additionally identified as significant or longer significant. A possible reason may be that the risk for atherosclerosis is altered with the presence of either higher systolic blood pressure or a cardiometabolic disease. Previous research shows that the metabolic signature may be altered when these conditions are present15,16. Therefore, additional metabolites may be influenced when accounting for cardiometabolic conditions that appear during or after pregnancy. Hence, both metabolomic markers, and clinical diagnosis of cardiometabolic conditions (i.e., diabetes, preeclampsia) during and after pregnancy may be important to account for to identify women with higher risk of future cardiovascular disease. Additionally, when comparing our main results to previous research, most studies have conducted metabolome-wide analysis among subjects with either a cardiovascular or a metabolic disease, or with an identified progression of atherosclerosis. Therefore, our study is the first to investigate metabolite predictors of pre-clinical CVD markers.

Lipid and lipid-like metabolites

We found several lipids or lipid-like molecules associated with CIMT. Lipids play a key role in the progression of atherosclerosis, of which CIMT is an early marker. Previous research shows that pregnancy-related changes in lipids may be linked to later cardiovascular risk and can be sustained even years after pregnancy. For example, a pre-conception cohort in Singapore, compared the lipid metabolome including 689 lipid species among women during pre-conception, pregnancy and postpartum, and found significant changes in several lipids, of which 80% of the lipids went through significant changes and 11% did not revert back to pre-pregnancy concentrations42. The identified lipid profiles were also associated with weight changes during pregnancy and glycemic traits (e.g., insulin resistance, fasting glucose). Similarly, gestational weight gain and postpartum weight loss were associated with phospholipid profiles, specifically for overweight and obese women43.

Some of the identified significant metabolites in this study overlap with results from previous studies. Specifically, sphinganine-1-phosphate (d18:0) and sphingosine-1-phosphate (d18:1) are both sphingolipids and have a role in the pathophysiology of cardiovascular disease (e.g., ischemic heart disease, hypertension, heart failure, and stroke)44. Changes in sphingolipid metabolism may have cardiovascular protective properties, which is consistent with our findings showing associations with thinner CIMT45. In contrast, alpha-dimorphecolic acid, 9S-HODE, and 13S-HODE are markers for lipid peroxidation46,47, and have been detected after early incident of ischemic stroke48. Also, corticosterone / cortexolone is a steroid hormone of the corticosteroid type produced in the adrenal glands and involved in the stress, immune, and energy response49. These results confirm that these metabolites may be related to cardiovascular health outcomes.

Additional studies among non-pregnant subjects with a cardiovascular or metabolic disease showed that amino acids, lipids, and their metabolites were associated with cardiovascular risk 10 years later10. When comparing patients with recent cardiovascular incident with healthy controls, distinct changes in metabolites including acylcarnitines and glycerophospholipids were identified and associated with cardiovascular events50. A Chinese study measured two markers for subclinical atherosclerosis (i.e., brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV) and CIMT)) and identified branched-chain amino acids, ketones, and lipids positively associated with baPWV and lactate associated with thinner CIMT51. In contrast, a study combining data from the CheckPoint (Child Health CheckPoint study) and ALSPAC (The Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children) found null associations between metabolites and CIMT among children and their parents after adjusting for common risk factors such as age, sex, BMI and blood pressure52. Furthermore, histological samples from 12 patients at different stages of progression of carotid atherosclerosis identified approximately 30 different lipids in different regions of the carotid plaque12. Enrichment analysis showed several metabolic pathways in different regions of the carotid plaque, demonstrating that different lipids and metabolic pathways are critical for the risk of atherosclerosis progression.

Benzenoids

Three metabolites of the superclass benzenoids were significantly associated with thicker CIMTmax and CIMTmean: mandelic acid, phenylpyruvic acid, p-hydroxyphenylacetic acid/ 2-hydrophenylacetic acid. In animal studies, changes in 2-hydroxyphenilacetic acid has been detected in response to atherosclerosis progression in mice and during acute event in human urine53. In contrast, anthranilic acid was significant but in the opposite direction with thinner CIMTmax. However, in a study among 325 patients with moderate to severe CKD, anthranilic acid together with other Kynurenine pathway metabolites have been associated with increased measures of subclinical CVD, such as CIMT54.

Organic acids and derivatives

Three organic acids and derivatives, 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, n-formyl-l-methionine, and 2-aminothiazoline-4-carboxylic acid, were significantly associated with thicker CIMTmean and CIMTmax. The physiological changes of pregnancy places a challenge on the amino acid metabolism due to fetal development and the need for nutrients and substrates, as suggested by a prior study in pregnant women55. Also, a study on human blood confirms that the branched-chain amino acid (BCAA), including metabolites 3-Methyl-2-Oxovaleric acid can be detrimental for coronary artery disease56. A previous literature review identified branched amino acids and urea cycle metabolites related to coronary artery disease57.

Further research results that overlap with our findings are the following. Among patients with in-stent restenosis (ISR), both cysteine and methionine pathways metabolites, including N-Formyl-l-methionine, were increased as compared to non-restenosis patients58. In regards to 2-Aminothiazoline-4-carboxylic acid (ATCA), it is a hydrogen cyanide metabolites and therefore, a cyanide exposure marker both in post-mortum human blood59 and in live animal models60. Exposure to cyanide can cause cellular hypoxia and cytotoxic anoxia and eventually lead to death. An NHANES study on volatile organic compounds found that lower ATCA was lower among patients with CVD outcomes as compared to healthy individuals61. The authors hypothesized that the association may be due to an abnormal redox state of cysteine/cysteine. Future studies may need to explore this further, and more specific to pregnancy metabolic changes.

Organoheterocyclic compounds

Four of the metabolites significantly associated with CIMT were organoheterocyclic compounds. Methylpicolinate and allantoin were significantly associated with a thinner CIMT, whereas bilirubin and protoporphyrin were associated with a thicker CIMT. Allantoin is the main product of uric acid oxidation when uric acid reacts with free radicals and may be a marker for oxidative stress. A Brazilian study found higher allantoin associated with high CIMT (CIMT ≥ 75th percentile) even after adjusting for cardiovascular risk factors62. Similarly, patients with elevated homocysteine levels and coronary artery disease had higher concentrations of allantoin and uric acid as compared to subjects with lower homocysteine levels63. Bilirubin is the final product of heme catabolism mediated by heme oxygenases and considered a toxic waste product of our bodies. In a study among men, those with higher bilirubin levels were more likely to have coronary atherosclerosis and stenosis64. However, bilirubin level has also been inversely associated with coronary atherosclerosis and suggested to have a protective association with atherosclerosis and CVD65,66. Protoporphyrin IX (PPIX) is a derivative of hematoporphyrin and can accumulate in rapidly growing tissues, such as atherosclerotic plaques. An animal study on rabbits showed that PPIX that accumulates in atheromatous plaques transfers to the blood and can be analyzed by extracting porphyrin from total blood67. PPIX may therefore be an early marker for atherosclerosis.

Organic oxygen compounds

D/L Galactosamine and D/L Glucosamine are glucosaminoglycans (GAGs), and atherosclerosis progression has been related to altered GAG content and distribution68,69. GAGs have a regulating role of the lesioned area and contribute to vessel integrity and remodeling70. The ratio of glucosamine/galactosamine of protein-free plasma has been found significantly higher in patients with ischemic heart disease as compared to controls71. However, one study found no difference in GAGs when comparing cases with atherosclerosis obliterans with healthy controls among both sexes and the age group of 36–65 years72. It has been proposed to target the vascular synthesis of glycosaminoglycan chains to reduce lipoprotein binding and so regulate the initiating step in atherogenesis73.

In overall, we found significant associations with metabolites that agree with previous studies on metabolomics and cardiovascular health but also results which differ with previous research. There are a few possible reasons for our results differing with previous studies. Previously, most studies have conducted metabolome-wide analysis focusing on participants with cardiovascular or a metabolic disease, whereas our study analyzes metabolites among relatively young and healthy women and particularly after delivery. Therefore, our results may capture metabolites related to the pregnancy-specific metabolism. There may also be differences between populations in risk factors, health burden, genetics and environmental exposures which may explain differences in results. Finally, differences in the methods such as the metabolomic assay (i.e., targeted or untargeted), the number of metabolites measures, sample size of the study population, may limit the ability to identify significant associations.

Enriched pathway analysis

The enriched pathway analysis suggested alterations in pathways involved with transcriptional regulation of white adipocyte differentiation, PPAR-alpha activates gene expression, and regulation of lipids metabolism by PPAR-alpha. These three pathways had hits from three of the metabolites including bilirubin, 13S-HODE and 19S-HODE. White adipocyte differentiation is the process of cell differentiation when pre-adipocytes become adipocytes and begin to accumulate lipid droplets and occurs in the cellular compartments of the cytosol, nucleoplasm and the plasma membrane. PPAR-alpha is a peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor and regulates the expression of genes involved in fatty acid and lipoprotein metabolism74. PPAR-alpha have an important role in the progression of atherosclerosis as its activation through medical treatment (i.e., fibric acids) for hyperlipidemia improves insulin sensibility, and decreases thrombosis and vascular inflammation74. The expression of genes regulated by PPAR-alpha influence several metabolic processes and including fatty acid oxidation and transport, triglyceride clearance and lipid storage74.

As only a handful metabolites provided hits on the enriched pathway analysis, the results are limited. Previous studies have identified additional biological pathways associated with subclinical atherosclerosis. The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) with a large study population (n = 3867) from the US with replication among participants from Rotterdam (from Netherlands) and LOLIPOP (from West London, UK), identified metabolites associated with subclinical cardiovascular disease by the markers of CAC and CIMT14. The related biological pathways involved lipid and carbohydrate metabolism, branched chain and aromatic amino acid metabolism, oxidative stress and inflammation pathways. In addition, among subjects with diabetes type 2, metabolites associated with coronary artery calcium (CAC) were involved in fatty acid, lysoplasmalogen, and branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) subpathways75. Future experimental studies, in vitro or in vivo, are needed to confirm the biological roles of the identified metabolites and their related biological pathways. For example, a study on mice have identified specific metabolomic changes that occur during and after pregnancy in the maternal heart, revealing elevated urea cycle metabolites, polyamines, and phospholipid levels76. Future experimental studies could be conducted focusing on metabolic changes, and arteries during and after pregnancy. Furthermore, in vitro studies can further elucidate the biological roles of specific metabolites on arteries during pregnancy, like the study by Chung Ming et al.77, which identified changes in proteins in coronary artery endothelial cells induced by preeclampsia or gestational hypertension. Such mechanistic studies are needed to confirm the associations found in this study and to establish potential causality between metabolic signatures specific to pregnancy and future cardiovascular health.

In terms of clinical implications, future studies are needed to validate our results of metabolomics as early markers for cardiovascular disease before clinical diagnosis. Such studies may facilitate the identification of women with higher risk who may need more frequent follow-ups and health promotion of healthy lifestyle to avoid risk factors for cardiovascular disease. Prediction studies of cardiovascular disease based on metabolic signatures could be developed like those conducted for diabetes and pre-diabetes78,79. Just like Allalou et al.78 used metabolomics to predict diabetes incidence, metabolites significantly associated with cardiovascular diseases in the current research literature could be used for hyperparameter optimization of machine learning models to predict future risk of cardiovascular disease. This may move the science further towards sensitive and specific screening tests for routine health checks within specific time-windows of follow-up after pregnancy to facilitate early identification of cardiovascular disease progression.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths worth highlighting. The PROGRESS study is an ongoing pregnancy cohort following mothers and their children, which provides the opportunity to evaluate long-term health effects associated with pregnancy related exposures. This setting provides research opportunities to follow maternal health long-term after the index pregnancy, and in this case, women´s cardiovascular health. Another strength of this study is using CIMT as a marker for subclinical atherosclerosis as it is a non-invasive measure of the carotid arteries and a relatively easy measure to apply with good reproducibility and a valuable tool to monitor the progression of atherosclerosis80. In comparison to sampling cardiac tissue, analyzing metabolomics in serum samples is a less invasive method and also a powerful investigative tool to asses circulating metabolites underlying cardiovascular metabolism81,82. In particular, serum metabolites may capture metabolic changes and precursors of subclinical atherosclerosis, which may not be clinically detected. This has the potential of early detection and follow-up of women postdelivery. Another strength showing the robustness of our results is that the metabolites significantly associated with CIMT had high detection rates ranging from 96.6 to 100%, and phenylpyruvic acid with 78.5% detection rate. There are also some limitations to our study. Our pathway analysis identified only three significantly enriched pathway analyses. This may be due to the few significant metabolite hits, which may be a result of the smaller sample size in our analysis, or number of metabolite coverage. In addition, we did not include untargeted unannotated metabolites but only those metabolites that could be identified. Future studies may want to include both targeted and untargeted (i.e., annotated and unannotated) metabolomics to quantify additional metabolic markers related to cardiovascular health after pregnancy. Although CIMT is a good marker for subclinical atherosclerosis, it may be a better marker for the risk of stroke, whereas other markers such as CAC is a better marker for the risk of ischemic heart disease. In our study, CAC was not included as it only begins to rise after the age of 6041,83, and with our relatively younger population CAC would have most likely yielded null associations. However, future studies especially in older cohorts may want to include several markers for better risk estimation of specific cardiovascular diseases. We acknowledge that many of our metabolite interpretations draw on data from non-pregnant adults. However, pregnancy entails wide‐ranging adaptive changes in maternal, fetal, and placental physiology with most notable and dramatic rises in circulating triglycerides, cholesterol, free fatty acids, and phospholipids to support in utero energy demands and postpartum lactation84. Such shifts may alter baseline levels, renal clearance, and biotransformation of xenobiotics and endogenous compounds85,86. For instance, ATCA, which is commonly used as a stable urinary marker of cyanide exposure, may display different kinetics during gestation. These pregnancy specific metabolic adaptations underscore the need for validation of key biomarkers in pregnant cohorts or the establishment of gestational age–specific reference ranges to improve biological interpretation in this unique physiologic state.

Conclusions

We conducted a metabolome-wide study among women 1-month postpartum and evaluated the association with CIMT 8 years later. We found that several metabolites including lipids, organic acids and oxygen compounds, and organoheterocyclic compound were significantly associated with both thicker and thinner CIMT. Even after adjusting for blood pressure and cardiovascular and metabolic diagnosis, the results remained significant. Enriched pathway analysis identified biological pathways related to regulation of adipocyte differentiation, PPAR-alpha activation of gene expression, and regulation of lipid metabolism. This study suggest that metabolites may be early markers for later subclinical atherosclerosis and highlights the importance of following mothers long-term after their pregnancy.

Winning beyond scoring: an explainable machine learning framework for identifying key performance indicators in the Chinese Basketball Association
Authors: Rui Dong, Yingkang Peng, Qing Liu, Miguel-Angel Gómez-Ruano, Shaoliang Zhang, Xiao Xu, Bin Yang, Hongyou Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUnderstanding why teams win in professional basketball requires more than examining isolated statistics. Performance adapts under changing competitive constraints, and key performance indicators (KPIs) must therefore be interpreted within context. This study aimed to develop an explainable, multi-model machine learning (ML) framework to identify score-line dependent KPIs associated with winning outcomes in the Chinese Basketball Association (CBA).MethodsA total of 3,249 games from the 2018–2022 CBA regular seasons were analyzed. Based on final score margin, games were classified into close (1–9 points) and balanced (10–18 points) contexts using k-means clustering. For each context, game-related statistics and play-type variables were initially screened using conventional statistical procedures. A dual-stage feature selection process combining lasso and stepwise logistic regression retained indicators consistently selected by both models. Ten commonly used ML classifiers were then trained and evaluated using nested cross-validation. Rather than benchmarking predictive accuracy alone, a convergence-based strategy was adopted, treating KPI relevance consistently identified across classifiers as stronger structural evidence. Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were used to quantify the magnitude and direction of each KPI's contribution within the top-performing classifiers.ResultsModel performance differed across score-line contexts. In close games, the BP neural network, support vector machine, and logistic regression achieved the best performance (accuracy: 71.2%–76.4%; AUC: 0.80–0.83), while tree-based ensemble classifiers (e.g., XGBoost, CatBoost, and LightGBM) showed superior discrimination in balanced games (accuracy: 83.9%–86.7%, AUC: 0.92–0.93). SHAP analysis revealed distinct context-dependent KPI structures. Close game wins were characterized by greater defensive rebounding and fewer turnovers, defensive fouls, and missed perimeter attempts. Screen-off made actions emerged as the only retained play-type indicator. Balanced games displayed a broader KPI structure involving higher relevance of rebounding, contested field-goal efficiency, steals, and play-type behaviors including screen-off, pick-and-roll roller, and pick-and-pop actions.ConclusionWinning outcomes in the CBA depend less on scoring volume alone and more on how teams regulate coordination and possession stability under different score-line constraints. Close games favor defensive integrity and the control of destabilizing events, while balanced games reward sustained possession management and diversified off-ball coordination. These findings provide actionable guidance for context-sensitive training design and tactical decision-making in professional basketball.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13102-026-01620-0.Keywords: Professional basketball, technical–tactical analysis, key performance indicators, play-type, machine learning classifiers, score-line context

Background

Basketball performance emerges from a complex adaptive system in which technical, tactical, and contextual components continuously interact and reorganize under dynamic competitive demands [1]. These interactions give rise to non-linear and emergent performance patterns that cannot be adequately explained by isolated actions or static game statistics alone [2]. Consequently, the identification of key performance indicators (KPIs) requires analytical approaches that integrate multidimensional technical and tactical behaviors, while accounting for their interaction and context-dependent nature [3, 4]. Despite the inherently dynamic and interactive nature of basketball performance, early KPI research primarily relied on game-related statistical metrics [5, 6, 7, 8]. Evidence from elite competitions consistently shows that shooting efficiency, rebounding, assists, and turnovers are associated with winning outcomes [9-13]. Subsequent research has demonstrated that the influence of these indicators is moderated by contextual constraints, such as seasonal phase (e.g. regular season vs. playoff) [9, 14], competition level [15], and score-line margin [11, 16]. More recently, advances in player tracking and spatio-temporal data have extended technical–tactical analysis beyond game-related statistics, providing behavioral insights into movement patterns and spatial occupation that underpin collective team performance [17, 18]. These developments have supported the increased adoption of play-type metrics, which capture the structural organization of offensive possessions and the decision-making processes preceding shot attempts [19]. Most empirical work in this area has relied on comparative analyses of play-type usage and scoring efficiency, particularly in the National Basketball Association (NBA) [20, 21] and European competitions [22-25]. However, few studies have incorporated specific play-type behaviors into predictive models of basketball game outcomes. For example, Bustamante-Sánchez [26] reported that winning NBA teams execute catch-and-shoot and pick-and-roll (PnR) strategies more effectively; while Olympic men’s basketball wins have been associated with higher frequencies of successful off-ball screens and cutting actions under specific pacing conditions [3]. Despite these advances, score-line margins are rarely treated as a central modelling constraint when analyzing play-type behaviors, limiting insight into how technical and tactical contributions reorganize under varying competitive pressure [27].

In parallel, machine learning (ML) classifiers have been increasingly adopted in basketball analytics. Their appeal lies in the ability to capture non-linear relationships among multiple performance indicators [28] and to uncover patterns that may not be visible through traditional models [29]. From an applied perspective, hybrid analytical approaches integrating tree-based classifiers with logistic regression models have demonstrated practical value, as they can reveal multiple performance pathways associated with winning probability [3, 30]. Although ML classifiers often demonstrate superior predictive performance, challenges remain regarding model interpretability—specifically, how to determine which indicators truly drive model predictions and in what direction [7]. For this reason, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) has gained attention as a tool for explaining feature-level contributions within ML classifiers [31]. It has been used, for example, to clarify the influence of game-related indicators on basketball winning outcomes [5, 32]. Despite these advances, relatively little work has integrated play-type behaviors into explainable ML frameworks within the context of the Chinese Basketball Association (CBA), which exhibits distinct technical–tactical characteristics compared with major international professional leagues [27, 33]. Prior studies indicate that CBA teams place greater emphasis on paint-based scoring, attempt fewer three-point shots, and employ PnR actions less frequently than teams in the NBA and Euroleague [7, 27, 34]. These stylistic differences suggest that KPI structure derived from Western competitions may not be directly transferable to the CBA, reinforcing the need for league-specific and context-sensitive analytical frameworks [24].

Against this background, three critical gaps remain in the existing literature. To begin with, few KPI studies jointly examine game-related statistics and play-type metrics within a unified analytical framework, despite these domains capture complementary dimensions of basketball performance [26, 35]. In addition, a large body of existing research remains grounded in traditional linear models and rarely incorporates score-line differentials as a central contextual constraint, limiting insight into how KPI relevance shifts across competitive conditions [30, 36, 37]. Finally, although machine learning approaches are now common in game outcome prediction, most studies rely on a single classifier or a narrowly defined modeling strategy. This raises concerns about the stability and generalizability of the identified KPIs, particularly across seasons and contexts. As a result, framework-level approaches that integrate multi-model robustness with explainable analysis remain scarce, particularly in domestic professional leagues outside major international competitions.

Rather than benchmarking machine learning classifiers, this study introduces an explainable, multi-model ML framework aimed at identifying stable and context-sensitive KPI structures in professional basketball. The goal is not only to improve prediction, but to better understand how technical execution, tactical coordination, and competitive constraints work together across different score-line conditions. Using multi-season CBA data, we combined game-related statistics and play-type metrics within a dual-stage feature selection process. Multiple commonly used ML classifiers were then applied to examine whether consistent KPI patterns could be identified across contexts. To make the models more interpretable, SHAP analysis was subsequently employed to interpret both the contribution and interactional relevance of the identified indicators. In doing so, predictive performance is linked more directly to meaningful technical and tactical insights.

Methods

Study design

This study adopted a retrospective observational design using anonymized, multi-season match data from the CBA. Using secondary performance statistics, the study aimed to identify KPIs associated with winning outcomes across different levels of game competitiveness. To do so, game-related statistics, play-type metrics, and machine learning classifiers were examined within a unified analytical framework.

Sample and variables

Match data were obtained from InStat Basketball (Hudl, USA), a commercially available performance analysis platform widely used in professional basketball. A total of 3,249 games from the 2018–2019 to 2021–2022 CBA regular seasons were analyzed. The reliability of the InStat system has been previously established, reporting substantial agreement (kappa > 0.81) for event tagging [21, 23]. To verify data quality for the present sample, two experienced coaches independently coded a random subset of ten CBA games using the same operational definitions. Their averaged ratings were compared with the corresponding InStat outputs, and reliability was assessed using average-measure intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs, two-way mixed-effects, absolute agreement). Core game-related data showed near-perfect agreement (ICC = 0.99–1.00), and play-type metrics demonstrated high level of reliability (ICC = 0.91–0.99). During this reliability assessment, observers were blinded to game outcomes.

Based on previous literature [21, 26], a total of 56 performance variables were selected, including 34 game-related statistics and 22 play-type metrics. Definitions and operational descriptions are provided in Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Step 1: A k-means cluster analysis was first applied to classify games according to final score-line margin, resulting in three competition-level contexts: (i) close games (1–9 points, n = 1,211); (ii) balanced games (10–18 points, n = 1,086); and (iii) unbalanced games (≥ 19 points, n = 952). Consistent with the study aims, subsequent analyses focused on close and balanced games, as these contexts better reflect competitive conditions in which technical and tactical differences are more likely to influence outcomes [30, 38]. A total of 2,297 games were retained for subsequent analyses.

A total of 2,297 games were retained for subsequent analyses.

Step 2: For close and balanced games, descriptive statistics (mean and standard deviation) were calculated for each performance variable in relation to game outcome (win/loss). Independent-samples t-tests were used to identify candidate KPIs showing significant win–loss differences. Pearson correlation analysis was then conducted among the preliminarily selected variables. Correlation coefficients above 0.70 were considered indicative of substantial multicollinearity and informed subsequent feature selection decisions [39].

Independent-samples t-tests were used to identify candidate KPIs showing significant win–loss differences. Pearson correlation analysis was then conducted among the preliminarily selected variables. Correlation coefficients above 0.70 were considered indicative of substantial multicollinearity and informed subsequent feature selection decisions [39].

To refine the candidate set, we implemented a dual-model feature selection approach that combined lasso with stepwise logistic regression [40]. Game outcome (win = 1, loss = 0) served as the dependent variable, with all performance indicators entered as predictors. Lasso regression employed L1-regularization and used 10-fold cross-validation procedure across 100 penalty-parameter (λ) values to determine the optimal model based on the minimum mean squared error (MSE) [41]. Indicators retained by both models were considered stable and were treated as the final KPIs for each score-line context.

Step 3: For close and balanced games, the final sets of KPIs were entered separately into ten machine learning classifiers, including decision tree (DT), random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), BP neural network (BP), gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT), XGBoost, AdaBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM, and logistic regression (LR). The dataset was randomly divided into training (80%) and testing sets (20%). Hyperparameters were optimized through grid-search procedures, and nested 10-fold cross-validation was implemented to mitigate overfitting. Model performance was assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). For each game context, the best-performing classifier was identified based on overall discriminative performance. To enhance interpretability, SHAP analysis was conducted to quantify the contribution of each KPI to model prediction [42]. Mean absolute SHAP values were computed for the three top-performing classifiers, then summed and normalized to derive a convergence-based importance ranking.

Correlation analysis was then conducted on the variables retained from the initial screening. In close games, high correlations (above 0.70) were mainly found among game-related attempt–completion pairs, such as 2PM and 2PA, 3PM and 3PA, as well as FTM and FTA. Efficiency indicators were also strongly associated with their corresponding frequency measures. In addition, cut attempts and cut makes showed a substantial correlation. In balanced games, strong correlations were also observed for scoring-related measures, and for several play-type attempt–completion pairs, including transitions, catch-and-shoot, drives, and cuts.

Discussion

This study aimed to develop an explainable ML framework for identifying score-line dependent KPIs associated with winning outcomes in the CBA, and to examine how multi-ML classifiers contribute to a more stable and interpretable understanding of technical–tactical performance. Overall, the findings indicate that winning in the CBA is not primarily driven by output-oriented scoring events, but reflects performance trade-offs that differ across score-line contexts. Close games reward defensive integrity and risk-minimizing control of possession flow, whereas balanced games favor sustained possession control, efficient shot selection, and reliable execution despite contested defense. At the tactical level, KPI contributions were likewise shaped by contextual constraints, rather than by the sheer frequency of play-type accumulations. In close games, coordinated offensive behavior tended to compress into brief off-ball screening opportunities. When margins were less restrictive, a wider range of off-ball behaviors, including PnR roller finishes, PnP attempts, and cutting actions played a more meaningful role.

Although DREB consistently emerged as the most influential indicator in both contexts, the broader patterns differed between close and balanced games. Together, these differences suggest two distinct ways in which performance is regulated under varying score-line conditions. In close games, outcome separation was mainly linked to limiting destabilizing events—specifically turnovers, defensive fouls, and missed perimeter shots. In other words, avoiding errors appeared more consequential than increasing scoring output. When margins are tight, decision time is reduced and defensive pressure intensifies. Under these conditions, even small disturbances in possession flow can have amplified effects on subsequent sequences [43]. Within this context, the joint prominence of DREB and 3PMi (together accounting for over 30% of the aggregated importance) points to a stabilizing dynamic across both defensive and offensive phases. Specifically, securing a defensive rebound ends the opponent’s possession, while limiting inefficient perimeter attempts reduces wasted opportunities on the offensive side. The elevated importance of TOV and PF further supports this interpretation. Both can be viewed as markers of structural instability within a possession, particularly when contextual pressure is high [44].

The KPI structure in balanced games suggested a different mode of regulation. Rather than focusing primarily on minimizing immediate risk, teams appeared to benefit from sustaining coordinated phases of play and converting possessions more efficiently across successive sequences. This profile was reflected in possession control—not only by limiting turnovers and reducing missed field-goal attempts, but also by creating additional possessions through steals and offensive rebounding. In this context, missed field-goal attempts consistently emerged as stronger predictors of game outcomes than successful ones, underlining their value as information-rich signals within dynamic game situations [45]. Unlike made shots, which primarily represent the end of a scoring action, missed attempts often reveal more about the underlying process. They can reflect shot selection quality, spatial congestion, defensive pressure, and perturbations in spacing–timing relations within the offensive unit [46]. These results align with recent evidence on post-shot coordination restructuring in three-point contexts, indicating that competitive advantage often depends on how effectively teams reorganize spacing and interaction dynamics following missed or contested shots [47]. Taken together, these patterns indicate that winning in balanced games seems to depend less on isolated scoring events and more on how teams respond to disruptions [48], particularly through rebounding, shot selection, and error control.

At the tactical level, the contrasting play-type profiles observed across score-line contexts further support an interaction-based mode of performance regulation [49]. In this view, coordinated behaviors are better understood as emergent outcomes of multi-level coupling processes, rather than as the simple accumulation of predefined tactical patterns [46]. In close-game contexts, only screen-off made actions retained explanatory value, suggesting that the offensive system tends to rely on brief, localized dyadic couplings under strong defensive pressure. This pattern is consistent with prior work showing that, when defensive coupling intensifies and decision time is limited, team structures contract toward more conservative and phase-stable forms of organization [43]. In such environments, viable scoring opportunities are more likely to arise from off-ball screening behaviors that momentarily perturb defensive alignment, rather than from extended multi-phase tactical sequences [50]. Similar to basket-cutting opportunities that emerge from short-lived defensive gaps on drives, successful screen-off actions in the CBA may be interpreted as exploiting transient spatio-temporal misalignments created by help and rotation, rather than as predefined tactical intentions [51]. Accordingly, the attenuation of broader play-type relevance in close games mirrors the game-related KPI structure. When margins are tight, teams appear to regulate behavior not only by minimizing errors, but also by functionally simplifying and stabilizing their coordination patterns.

By contrast, balanced games exhibited a more distributed tactical structure, with stronger involvement from successful screen-off actions, PnR roller plays, and PnP attempts. These behaviors rely less on individually driven creation and more on synchronized timing across multiple dyads [27]. This pattern indicates that balanced contexts provide sufficient temporal and spatial affordances for coordinated movement sequences to unfold and be successfully completed [48]. Within these conditions, behaviors such as cutting, roller finishes, and screener relocation to the perimeter can be interpreted as emergent expressions of team-level organization, rather than isolated tactical choices [46]. Notably, screen-based behaviors remained relevant across both contexts. However, their functional role shifts from stabilizing locally coordinated opportunities in close games to supporting more diversified coordination repertoires in balanced situations [28]. Considered alongside the higher importance of contested field-goal percentage, these findings indicate that offensive effectiveness in the CBA depends on how well teams convert momentary defensive instability into actionable scoring windows, even when defensive pressure remains present [26]. Finally, the negative association between transition attempts and winning outcomes suggests that pushing the pace excessively may fragment spacing organization and constrain the emergence of productive coordination [20, 52].

Beyond describing performance differences between winning and losing teams, this study demonstrates how explainable ML can operate as a methodological bridge between statistical modeling and interaction-based interpretations of team success. By integrating multiple classifiers, KPIs were reconsidered not simply as outcomes indicators, but as markers of how teams operate under specific interaction constraints within possession sequences [53]. The consistent prominence of missed shots, turnovers, foul-related events, and screen-off behaviors indicates that explainable ML is particularly sensitive to inefficiencies and perturbations within possession flow, rather than merely rewarding terminal scoring outcomes. At the same time, the comparatively lower predictive value of certain PnR ball-handler and cutting actions, often emphasized in other leagues [23, 26, 54], suggests that structurally favorable opportunities may not be fully exploited in the CBA context. This interpretation aligns with recent spatio-temporal evidence on unused offensive affordances [51]. Collectively, these results reinforce the methodological value of explainable, multi-model ML framework in revealing league-specific structural mechanisms, rather than generalized or model-dependent KPI patterns in professional basketball.

Practical implications

The present findings provide several context-specific implications for applied coaching practice and performance management in professional basketball. For player development and training environments, the results suggest that close-game training should emphasize stability under pressure. Rather than emphasizing scoring volume alone, training environments may benefit from reinforcing decision habits that reduce turnovers, unnecessary fouls, and low-quality shot selection. Constraint-led activities such as small-sided late-clock situations, defensive-pressure exposure, and constrained-space possessions may help players adapt to maintaining structural stability when margins are tight [55]. For example, recent spatio-temporal evidence supports this approach. Shot success depend more on the defender’s temporal reachability than on static distance, with three-point shooting efficiency increasing sharply when reaction time exceeds approximately 0.4 seconds [47]. In this sense, screen-off, PnR roller, and PnP actions may function less as pure spacing tools and more as ways for extending reaction time beyond this contest-recovery window.

At the organizational level, the findings also offer implications for athlete evaluation and club decision-making. Performance feedback may place greater emphasis on identifying episodes when delayed decisions, forced contested attempts, or poorly judged contact situations trigger possession sequences, rather than focusing exclusive on scoring totals. From a roster-construction or recruitment perspective, the prominence of PnR roller and PnP behaviors highlights the value of versatile frontcourt players who can both finish inside and selectively relocate to the perimeter while preserving spacing during defensive rotations.

Finally, explainable ML-based KPI monitoring systems can support both strategic planning and in-game tactical adjustment [56]. Embedding the context-sensitive KPI structure into real-time monitoring tools could help staff detect early signs of instability (e.g., rising turnovers or fouls in close games, or declining possession control in balanced phases), and assist more informed tactical options, such as tempo adjustment, play simplification, or increased screen-based involvement.

Limitations and future research lines

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was restricted to regular-season CBA games, and KPI structures may differ in overtime or playoff contexts where strategic priorities and psychological pressure are further amplified. In addition, COVID-related factors including travel and recovery routines, schedule density, preparation conditions, venue restrictions, and potential shifts in roster availability, could affect technical–tactical behaviors during the study period. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as reflecting pooled regular-season performance patterns across this period. Second, although play-type indicators were incorporated to reflect tactical behaviors, the study relied on event-based data and did not directly model spatio-temporal interactions or player-tracking variables. Third, although a multi-model ML framework with SHAP-based interpretability was employed to enhance robustness, the findings remain specific to the CBA context and should be generalized to other leagues with caution.

Future research should extend the present findings in several directions. Integrating positional data with game-related and play-type metrics would allow KPIs to be interpreted within evolving interaction patterns rather than isolated events. Further work may also explore the application of explainable ML approaches in near real-time performance environments, helping to assess how KPI relevance shifts across score-line contexts and phases of play. Finally, comparative studies across other leagues (e.g., NBA, Euroleague, NBL), women’s competitions, and play-off contexts would help distinguish league-specific patterns from more transferable structure tendencies.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates how an explainable ML framework can integrate technical, tactical, and contextual indicators to derive a stable and interpretable KPI structure for professional basketball. Winning outcomes in the CBA are better explained by how teams regulate coordination and possession stability under different score-line constraints than by scoring volume alone. In close games, competitive advantages arise from defensive integrity and the suppression of destabilizing events. In balanced games, success depends more on sustained possession control and efficient execution under contested conditions. Tactically, play-type behaviors do not follow a “more-is-better” logic. Instead, their functional role shifts from simplified coordination in close contexts to more diversified off-ball offense when interaction space and decision time are less restricted. Collectively, these findings reframe KPIs as indicators of interaction regulation rather than isolated performance outputs. This perspective moves performance analysis beyond outcome-driven evaluation and supports future development of explainable, context-sensitive KPI frameworks in applied monitoring and real-time tactical evaluation across competitive settings.

Comparative immunogenicity of monovalent and bivalent adenovirus vaccines carrying spikes of early and late SARS-CoV-2 variants
Authors: Hengchun Li, Chenchen Yang, Li Yin, Wenming Liu, Zhengyuan Zhang, Bo Liu, Xinxin Sun, Wenhao Liu, Zihan Lin, Zijian Liu, Ping He, Ying Feng, Chunhua Wang, Wei Wang, Suhua Guan, Qian Wang, Ling Chen, Pingchao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe continuous emergence of highly immune-evasive SARS-CoV-2 variants has challenged vaccine efficacy. A vaccine that can provide broad protection is desirable. We evaluated the immunogenicity of a series of monovalent and bivalent adenovirus-vectored vaccines containing the spikes of Wildtype (WT), Beta, Delta, Omicron subvariants BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, BA.2.13, BA.3, BA.5, BQ.1.1, and XBB. Vaccination in mice using monovalent vaccines elicited the highest neutralizing titers against each self-matched strain, but against other variants were reduced 2− to 73-fold. A bivalent vaccine consisting of WT and BA.5 broadened the neutralizing breadth against pre-Omicron and Omicron subvariants except XBB. Among bivalent vaccines based on the strains before the emergence of XBB, a bivalent vaccine consisting of BA.2 and BA.5 elicited the most potent neutralizing antibodies against Omicron subvariants, including XBB. In mice primed with injected WT vaccine, intranasal booster with a bivalent vaccine containing XBB and BA.5 could elicit broad serum and respiratory mucosal neutralizing antibodies against all late Omicron subvariants, including XBB. In mice that had been sequentially vaccinated with WT and BA.5, intranasal booster with a monovalent XBB vaccine elicited greater serum and mucosal XBB neutralizing antibodies than bivalent vaccines containing XBB. Both monovalent and bivalent XBB vaccines induced neutralizing antibodies against EG.5. Unlike the antibody response, which is highly variant-specific, mice receiving either monovalent or bivalent vaccines elicited comparable T-cell responses against all variants. Furthermore, intranasal but not intramuscular booster induced antigen-specific lung resident T cells. This study provides insights into the design of the COVID-19 vaccine and vaccination strategies.KEYWORDS: Adenovirus, COVID-19, Omicron, monovalent vaccine, bivalent vaccine, immunogenicity, mucosal neutralizing antibodies, lung tissue-resident T cells

Introduction

COVID-19, caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), has infected hundreds of millions of people and killed more than 7 million people worldwide (https://covid19.who.int/). The virus initially infects epithelial cells in the nasopharynx using the receptor-binding domain (RBD) on the spike protein to interact with the angiotensin-converting enzyme-2 (ACE-2) receptor. The spike protein, especially the RBD segment, has been recognized as the target for antibody and vaccine countermeasures. Rapidly available vaccines have played an essential role in preventing and controlling the epidemic, mitigating critical illnesses, and saving people's lives. However, after Omicron (B.1.1.529) was listed as a variant of concern (VOC) by the World Health Organization (WHO) in November 2021, its subvariants quickly became the dominant epidemic strain in the world [1]. Compared with that of the Wildtype (WT) or D614 strain, the spike protein of Omicron has more than 30 amino acid mutations, which endows it with a strong ability to escape neutralizing antibodies [2].

This large antigenic difference has led some researchers to suggest that the Omicron subvariants should be considered separate strains or serotypes compared to the pre-Omicron variants (WT, Beta, and Delta). This immune evasion was seen in individuals with different COVID-19 vaccination or SARS-CoV-2 infection histories. In individuals who received three or four doses of mRNA WT vaccines and in individuals who received three doses of mRNA WT vaccines before breakthrough infection with BA.2, their plasma neutralizing activity against BQ.1.1 and XBB were significantly lower compared to earlier Omicron subvariants (BA.5 and BA.2) and pre-Omicron [3]. In individuals who received two to four doses of mRNA WT vaccines or inactivated WT vaccine (with or without previous BA.2 infection), XBB.1 and XBB.3 exhibited greater immune escape potential compared to BA.5.2 and WT strain [4]. Moreover, convalescent sera from BA.5 or BF.7 breakthrough-infected individuals showed lower neutralizing antibody activity against XBB.1.5 and CH.1.1 than that against BA.5 or BF.7[5]. Another study also reported that neutralizing activity against BQ.1, BQ.1.1, XBB, and XBB.1 in sera from vaccinees and breakthrough-infected individuals was significantly impaired, including sera from individuals boosted with WT/BA.5 bivalent mRNA vaccine [6]. The increasing immune evasion capability of late Omicron subvariants, especially XBB, EG.5, and BA.2.86, led to reduced efficacy of vaccines designed based on the SARS-CoV-2 ancestral or WT strain [3,7]. A recent meta-study showed that the reinfection rate of the Omicron subvariants was significantly higher than WT, Alpha, and Delta strains [8]. Multiple Omicron subvariants are capable of infecting both vaccinated and previously infected individuals, thus posing a challenge to the selection of vaccine antigens against COVID-19. Therefore, a rapidly updated COVID-19 vaccine or a vaccine composition that can provide broad and potent immune responses, especially against emerging Omicron subvariants, is desirable. COVID-19 vaccines that induce neutralizing antibodies against circulating SARS-CoV-2 strains are likely to remain necessary for protecting vulnerable people from severe COVID-19 and reducing the burden on healthcare systems.

To improve the efficacy of vaccines against emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants, most research teams have developed updated vaccines against Omicron variants. It has been shown that the mRNA vaccine (mRNA-1273.529) using Omicron BA.1 spike as an immunogen elicited a more robust neutralizing antibody response against Omicron BA.1 but did not effectively elicit neutralizing antibodies against late emerged subvariants such as BA.5 [9,10]. The reduced neutralizing potency against Omicron subvariants is a concern for vaccine renewal. Several research teams have developed bivalent vaccines containing the spike or RBD of WT and Omicron BA.1 or BA.5 strains. However, the resulting neutralizing antibodies were unsatisfactory, especially for late Omicron subvariants BQ.1.1 and XBB [10–13]. The immune imprinting effect from earlier vaccination using WT vaccines may elevate the neutralizing titers against WT and earlier strains more than the intended Omicron subvariants. Although a bivalent or multivalent vaccine may provide broad-spectrum immunity, monovalent vaccines of the most recent subvariants may have the advantage of minimizing immune imprinting effect of the earlier vaccination, which was based on WT strain [14,15]. In responding to the continued evolution of Omicron subvariants, WHO and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recommended using a monovalent XBB vaccine in the fall of 2023. However, most of these vaccines are intramuscularly injected mRNA or protein vaccines that only induce systemic but not mucosal immunity. So far, few studies have been on mucosal vaccines for the latest subvariants.

Respiratory mucosal vaccines can elicit local mucosal antibodies and tissue-residential T cells, which can block virus entry and colonization at the upper respiratory tract, potentially providing sterilizing immunity [16]. Adenovirus type 5 (Ad5) is a non-disease associated respiratory virus. Ad5, with the deletion of the E1 and E3 regions, has been used as a vector for COVID-19 vaccines in China, Russia, and many countries [17,18]. We previously reported that intranasal administration of a replication-incompetent Ad5-carrying WT spike can induce strong systemic and respiratory mucosal humoral and cellular immune responses in mice and rhesus macaques and confer protection in rhesus macaques [19]. Recently, we reported that intranasal booster with Ad5 carrying BA.1 spike (Ad5-S-Omicron) after the initial vaccination with inactivated whole-virus vaccine can establish respiratory mucosal and systemic immunity against BA.1, BA.5, and pre-Omicron variants [20].

In this study, we evaluated 11 replication-incompetent Ad5 carrying different spikes and compared their immunogenicity via single intramuscular vaccination in mice. We then assessed the neutralizing potency and breadth of 11 combinations of two spikes as a bivalent vaccine. Furthermore, we evaluated the immune responses elicited by intranasal booster using different spikes on the base of prior vaccination. We also accessed the systemic and respiratory mucosal T cell responses elicited by these spikes.

Materials and methods

Construction of adenovirus vector vaccines

The spike sequences of SARS-CoV-2 variants, including the WT (GenBank, accession no. MN908947), Beta (EPI_ISL_2695771), Delta (EPI_ISL_1544070), Omicron BA.1 (EPI_ISL_6640919), BA.2 (EPI_ISL_6795834.2), BA.2.12.1 (EPI_ISL_9767878), BA.2.13 (EPI_ISL_10999937), BA.3 (EPI_ISL_7605591), BA.5 (EPI_ISL_11542270), BQ.1.1 (EPI_ISL_15851850), and XBB (EPI_ISL_15232105), were optimized by altering codon usage. Replication-incompetent human Ad5 vector vaccines carrying different variants of spike genes were constructed as previously reported [19,20]. The codon-optimized spike sequences from variants were synthesized (GenScript, China) and inserted into the shuttle plasmid pGA1. By homologous recombination, the different variants of the spike genes were inserted into the E1 site of the Ad5 vector in E. coli BJ5183 competent cells. Ad5-vectored vaccines carrying different spikes of variants were rescued in human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells and purified by cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation. The viral particles (vp) of these Ad5-vectored vaccines were measured and stored at –80°C.

Western blot analysis

A549 cells were seeded in 6-well plates. After 18∼24 hours, the cells were infected with the above rescued recombinant adenovirus or Ad5-empty vector. After 24 hours of infection, the cells were lysed, harvested, and subjected to sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). After transfer, the polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes were incubated with a rabbit polyclonal antibody against the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein (Sino Biological, China). Next, the PVDF membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibodies (SeraCare, USA). Finally, the PVDF membranes were incubated with an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate and imaged with a Tanon imaging system (Tanon, China).

Animals, vaccination, and cells

Female BALB/c mice, aged 6–8 weeks, were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. The mouse experiments were conducted at the Animal Experimental Center of Guangzhou Institutes of Biomedicine and Health (GIBH), Chinese Academy of Sciences (IACUC: 2020025), and all protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees of GIBH.

For intramuscular vaccination, 100 μL of the Ad5-vectored SARS-CoV-2 vaccines or 50 μL (0.5 μg) of the inactivated vaccines were intramuscularly injected equally into the left and right hind limbs of the mice. Mice received monovalent vaccine at a dose of 2 × 109 vp or bivalent vaccines containing a 1:1 mixture (1 × 109 vp + 1 × 109 vp) of two SARS-CoV-2 variants. Six weeks later, serum samples were collected and subjected to neutralizing antibody analysis. For the inactivated WT vaccine, mice received 2 doses of 0.5 μg (1/10 of human dosage) at 2-week intervals. Two or twenty weeks later, mice were intranasally instilled with Ad5-vectored vaccine or intramuscularly injected with 0.5 μg inactivated WT vaccine.

For intranasal vaccination, mice were anesthetized by isoflurane (VetEasy, China) and 50 μL of the Ad5-vectored SARS-CoV-2 vaccine was intranasally instilled into anesthetized mice. Mice previously injected with 2 doses of the inactivated WT vaccine were intranasally instilled with monovalent Ad5-vectored vaccines at 4 × 109 vp/dose. Four weeks later, serum and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) samples were collected and subjected to immunological analysis. Mice previously injected with 2 doses of the inactivated WT vaccine were intranasally instilled with bivalent Ad5-vectored vaccines containing a 1:1 mixture (2 × 109 vp + 2 × 109 vp) of two SARS-CoV-2 variants. Six weeks later, serum and BALF samples were collected and subjected to immunological analysis.

For sequential WT and BA.5 vaccination experiment, after 20 weeks of injection of 2 doses of the inactivated WT vaccine, mice were intranasally instilled with Ad5-vectored BA.5 vaccines at 2 × 109 vp/dose. Two weeks later, mice were intranasally instilled with monovalent vaccine at a dose of 2 × 109 vp or bivalent vaccines containing a 1:1 mixture (1 × 109 vp + 1 × 109 vp) of two SARS-CoV-2 variants. Eight weeks after the last vaccination, serum and BALF samples were collected and subjected to immunological analysis.

HEK293 cells (ATCC, CRL-1573), A549 cells (ATCC® CCL-185™), and Huh-7 cells (National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures [TCHu182]) were cultured at 37°C in Dulbecco's modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 100 U/mL penicillin‒streptomycin.

Neutralization assay for SARS-CoV-2 based on pseudotyped vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV)

Pseudovirus neutralization assays for SARS-CoV-2 variants were performed based on previous studies [20,21]. Briefly, HEK 293 cells were transfected with plasmids expressing the spike proteins from pre-Omicron and Omicron subvariants. Subsequently, these HEK 293 cells were infected with VSV with a luciferase reporter gene replacing the G gene. The spike protein was incorporated on the surface of VSV, forming a pseudotyped reporter virus of SARS-CoV-2 (Darui Biotech, China). Serum or BALF was serially diluted 3-fold with DMEM complete medium at a starting dilution of 1:30. Pseudovirus at 650 50% tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) per well was added into the above-diluted serum or BALF. After 1 hour of incubation at 37°C, 100 µL of the mixture was inoculated into Huh-7 cells cultured in a 96-well plate at 2 × 104 cells per well and incubated at 37°C for 24 hours. Luciferase activity of cell culture supernatants was measured by Bright-Glo™ luciferase assay system (Promega, USA). The relative light unit (RLU) values of each dilution were obtained by EnSight™ Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer, USA). Inhibition rate = [1– (average RLU of sample – average RLU of virus control)/ (average RLU of virus control – average RLU of cell control)] × 100%. Based on the inhibition rate, 50% inhibitory concentration (IC50) values for neutralization were calculated by the Reed – Muench method.

Isolation of mouse spleen lymphocytes

Mouse spleen lymphocytes were isolated based on previous methods [20]. Briefly, spleens from vaccinated mice were harvested, ground, and filtered. Spleen cells in mouse lymphocyte-separating fluid (Cat: DKW33-R0100, Dakewe, China) were centrifuged at 800 ×g for 30 minutes. Spleen lymphocytes were harvested and resuspended in R10 medium (RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% PS, 0.05 mM 2-mercaptoethanol, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 2 mM L-glutamine, and 10 mM HEPES).

Collection of BALF from mice

Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed following our standard protocol [22]. Mice were sacrificed after blood collection. The mice were secured, and the trachea was exposed through a midline incision and cannulated with a sterile 18G intravenous catheter (Vasofix, Braun). Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed twice using PBS supplemented with 2% FBS (0.8 mL). BALF supernatants and BALF cells were collected by centrifugation at 400 × g for 5 minutes at 4°C. BALF cells were subjected to flow cytometry and enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assays, and BALF supernatants were used for neutralizing antibody assays.

IFN-γ ELISpot assay

For peptide pools, peptide pools corresponding to the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 WT, Delta, or Omicron BA.1 strain were chemically synthesized as 20-mers with 10 amino acids overlaps (GenScript, China). All peptide pools were dissolved with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO. Sigma-Aldrich, USA) to 0.4 mg/mL and stored at −80°C after reconstitution.

IFN-γ ELISpot assays were performed using freshly isolated mouse spleen lymphocytes and performed under sterile conditions, as previously reported [20]. In brief, 96-well microtiter plates (Merck Millipore, Germany) were activated with 25μL of 70% ethanol per well. After thoroughly washing the wells twice with 200 µL PBS, 50 µL of diluted mouse IFN-γ coating antibody (Cat: CT655. U-CyTech, Netherlands) was added to each well and incubated overnight at 4°C. After washing the wells 3 times with PBS, 200 µL of R10 medium was added to each well and blocked for 2 hours. After removing the blocking buffer, 100 µL of splenic lymphocytes (2 × 105) or 2.5 × 104 BALF cells per well were added to the wells and then stimulated with spike peptide pools of SARS-CoV-2 WT, Delta, or Omicron BA.1 at a final concentration of 2 μg/mL. After 24 hours, cells in the wells were removed and the wells were washed 5 times with 1 × PBS supplemented with 0.05% Tween-20 (PBST).100 µl diluted (1:400) biotinylated detection antibodies (Cat: CT655. U-CyTech, Netherlands) was added to each well and incubated for 2 hours at 37°C. After washing the wells 5 times with PBST, alkaline phosphatase-conjugated streptavidin (U-CyTech, Netherlands) were added to the 96-well plates and incubated at 37°C for 2 hours. Finally, the plates were washed and developed with NBT/BCIP substrates (Cat: 34042, Thermo Scientific, USA). Spots were counted with an ELISpot reader (Bioreader 6000, BIOSYS, Germany).

Flow cytometry analysis

The collected cells in the BALF were washed with 1 mL of PBS containing 0.1% FBS and centrifuged at 350 ×g at 4°C for 5 minutes. Most of the supernatant was discarded, 100 μL of PBS containing 0.1% FBS was left, and the cells were stained with anti-CD3 (BD Bioscience, USA), anti-CD4 (BioLegend, USA), anti-CD8 (BD Bioscience, USA), anti-CD69 (BD Bioscience, USA), and anti-CD103 antibodies (BioLegend, USA) for 30 minutes at room temperature (Table S1). The cells were washed once with 1 mL of PBS containing 0.1% FBS, and then, the cells that were already stained with biotin-conjugated anti-CD103 were stained with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated streptavidin (BD Bioscience, USA) for 30 minutes at room temperature (Table S1). The cells were then rewashed once with 1 mL of PBS containing 0.1% FBS, after which flow cytometry data were acquired on a BD LSRFortessa Flow Cytometer (BD Bioscience, USA) and analyzed using FlowJo Software (Tree Star, USA).

Data analysis

All quantifications were performed unblinded. Analysis of the immunologic data was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Differences between experimental groups of animals were analyzed by an unpaired t-test or Mann–Whitney test, and p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Generation of a series of Ad5 vector vaccines carrying different spikes

To evaluate and compare the immunogenicity of different spikes in the context of Ad5 vectored vaccines, we constructed 11 replication-incompetent Ad5 vectors carrying spikes from pre-Omicron and Omicron subvariants, including WT, Beta, Delta, Omicron BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, BA.2.13, BA.3, BA.5, BQ.1.1, and XBB (Figure 1(a,b)). From 2020 to the present, the increasing mutation of the SARS-CoV-2 has led to changes in the circulating strains (Figure 1(e)). The number of amino acid changes in the spike protein and RBD of the Omicron subvariants were significantly greater than that in the pre-Omicron variants. XBB spike protein has 42 amino acids different from WT spike protein (Figure 1(a,d)). The gene sequences encoding each spike protein were codon-optimized to enhance spike protein expression in human cells, and with the deletion of the furin cleavage site and two proline substitutions at the S2 hinge to stabilize the prefusion conformation [23] (Figure 1(a)). The optimized gene encoding a spike protein was inserted into the Ad5 E1 region containing human cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter and a bovine growth hormone (BGH) poly (A) signal as previously described [19] (Figure 1(b)). These recombinant adenoviruses were rescued and propagated in HEK 293 cells. The expression of spike protein was confirmed using a spike-specific rabbit antibody by western blot analysis of A549 cells infected with these Ad5 vectored vaccines (Figure 1(c)).

Vaccination using monovalent vaccines elicited the highest neutralizing titer against each self-matched strain

To assess the immune response against SARS-CoV-2 variants induced by these 11 spikes in the context of the Ad5 vector, female BALB/c mice were vaccinated intramuscularly at 2 × 109 vp/dose. Six weeks later, serum-neutralizing antibody titers were tested using pseudotyped viruses carrying spikes of different variants, including the pre-Omicron strains WT, Beta, and Delta, and Omicron subvariants BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, BA.5, BQ.1.1, and XBB (Figure 2(a)). Ad5-WT elicited the highest serum-neutralizing titers against WT and Beta strains. While the titers against Delta were 2- to 3-fold lower, the titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 were 11- to 18-fold lower (Figure 2(b,f)). Similarly, Ad5-Beta elicited the highest serum-neutralizing titers against Beta, but the titers against WT and Delta variants were 3- to 5-fold lower, whereas the titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 were 9- to 32-fold lower (Figure 2(b,g)). Ad5-Delta elicited the highest serum-neutralizing titers against Delta, but the titers against WT and Beta variants were 3- to 5-fold lower, whereas the titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 were 5- to 12-fold lower (Figure 2(b,h). Notably, Ad5-Delta elicited higher serum-neutralizing titers than Ad5-WT and Ad5-Beta in neutralizing Omicron subvariants, especially BA.2, BA.2.12.1 and BA.5 (Figure 2(b,f,g,h)). These results demonstrated that Ad5-vectored pre-Omicron vaccines elicited neutralizing antibodies skewed toward pre-Omicron strains but poor against Omicron subvariants.

Ad5-BA.1 and Ad5-BA.3 elicited the highest serum-neutralizing titers against BA.1, but the titers against BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 were 2- to 11-fold lower, and the titers against WT, Beta, and Delta were 13- to 36-fold lower (Figure 2(c,i,j)). Ad5-BA.2, Ad5-BA.2.12.1, Ad5-BA.2.13, and Ad5-BA.5 elicited the highest and comparable serum-neutralizing titers against BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 (variations within 2-fold), but the titers against WT, Beta, and Delta were 20- to 73-fold lower. Notably, the titers against BA.1 were 5- to 21-fold lower, and the titers against XBB were 4- to 16-fold lower (Figure 2(d, k, l, m, and n). Ad5-BQ.1.1 elicited higher serum-neutralizing antibody titers against BA.2, BA.5, and BQ.1.1, but the titers against XBB were 6- to 8-fold lower, whereas the titers against WT were even 47- to 58-fold lower (Figure 2(e,o)). Ad5-XBB elicited the highest serum-neutralizing titer against XBB, while the titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.5, and BQ.1.1 were 2- to 6-fold lower, and the titer against WT was the lowest (36-fold lower) (Figure 2(e,p)). Ad5-BA.2.12.1 and Ad5-BQ.1.1 elicited relatively broader and balanced neutralizing antibodies against most of the Omicron subvariants than other Ad5-vectored vaccines (Figure 2(l,o)).

As previously described method [24], we also constructed antigenic cartography maps based on serum-neutralizing titers of Ad5-BQ.1.1 vaccinated mice and Ad5-XBB vaccinated mice. Variations in the antigenic relationships between different Omicron subvariants can be visualized. Similar to the neutralizing titer data, antigenic cartography maps revealed that antibodies induced by Ad5-BQ.1.1 tended to be BA.2, BA.5, and BQ.1.1 subvariants, while antibodies induced by Ad5-XBB tended to be XBB subvariant (Figure S1).

These results showed that monovalent vaccines induced the highest neutralizing antibodies against the self-matched strains in the naive immune background, with various reductions against other strains.

Vaccination using bivalent vaccines containing BA.2 and BA.5 elicited broad neutralizing antibodies against most Omicron subvariants before the emergence of XBB

We next sought to assess whether neutralizing antibodies induced by bivalent vaccines containing two spikes can elicit broad neutralizing antibodies against Omicron subvariants. First, female BALB/c mice were vaccinated intramuscularly with a 1:1 mixture (1 × 109 vp + 1 × 109 vp) of Ad5-WT + Ad5-Beta, Ad5-WT + Ad5-Delta, or Ad5-Beta + Ad5-Delta. Six weeks later, neutralizing titers were measured using pseudotyped viruses bearing different spikes (Figure 3(a,b)). Like the monovalent pre-Omicron vaccine, vaccination with bivalent vaccines containing two pre-Omicron spike proteins primarily elicited higher neutralizing titers against the pre-Omicron WT, Beta, and Delta strains, but neutralizing titers against the Omicron subvariants BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, and BA.5 were poor (Figure 3(b, f, g, h)).

We next evaluated bivalent vaccines containing a 1:1 mixture (1 × 109 vp + 1 × 109 vp) of Ad5-WT with Ad5-BA.1, Ad5-BA.2.12.1, or Ad5-BA.5, respectively. After 6 weeks, serum neutralization titers were measured using pseudotyped viruses (Figure 3(a,c)). All 3 bivalent vaccines elicited comparable neutralizing titers against pre-Omicron strains (variation within 2-fold). Ad5-WT + Ad5-BA.5 elicited the highest neutralizing titers against BA.2, BA2.12.1, and BA.5, but were poor against XBB (15- to 23-fold reduction) and BA.1 (7- to 10-fold reduction) (Figure 3(c, i, j, and k)). Ad5-WT + Ad5-BA.1 elicited markedly lower neutralizing titers against BA.5 and XBB but the highest neutralizing titer against BA.1 (Figure 3(c,i)). These results showed that vaccination with Ad5-WT + Ad5-BA.5 elicited more robust and broad-spectrum neutralizing antibodies against pre-Omicron and Omicron subvariants, except XBB, than vaccination with Ad5-WT + Ad5-BA.1 or Ad5-WT + Ad5-BA.2.12.1 (Figure 3(c, i, j, k)).

To enhance the neutralizing potency against Omicron subvariants, including lately emerged XBB, we next evaluated bivalent vaccines containing a 1:1 mixture (1 × 109 vp + 1 × 109 vp) of two Omicron subvariants, Ad5-BA.1 + Ad5-BA.5, Ad5-BA.2 + Ad5-BA.5, and Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.5. Vaccination using these bivalent Omicron vaccines elicited broadly neutralizing titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.5, and BQ.1.1 (Figure 3(d, l, m, and n)). Although vaccination with Ad5-BA.1 + Ad5-BA.5 elicited comparably high neutralizing titers against BA.1, BA.2, BA.5, and BQ.1.1, the neutralizing antibody titer against XBB was 28-fold lower than that against BA.5 (Figure 3(d, l)). Vaccination with Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.5 elicited the highest neutralizing antibody titer against XBB, which was 1.5-fold lower than that induced by 2 × 109 vp of the monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine (Figure 3(d, e, n, o)); however, the neutralizing titer against BA.1 was the lowest among these 3 bivalent Omicron vaccines (Figure 3(d, l, m, and n)). Vaccination using Ad5-BA.2 + Ad5-BA.5 elicited the highest neutralizing titers against BA.2 and BA.5 and broadly neutralizing antibodies against BA.1, BQ.1.1, and XBB, but were 5-, 3-, and 17-fold lower, respectively, than those against BA.5 (Figure 3(d, m)). These results suggested that in the no prior vaccination background, the Ad5-XBB monovalent vaccine elicited the highest neutralizing antibodies against XBB, whereas the Ad5-BA.2 + Ad5-BA.5 bivalent vaccine elicited the broadest neutralizing antibodies against most of the Omicron subvariants (Figure 3(m and o)).

On the base of the WT vaccine, an intranasal booster with a monovalent BA.5 vaccine elicited broad neutralizing antibodies against most Omicron subvariants except XBB

Considering that most of the population has been vaccinated with SARS-CoV-2 WT vaccines, we then evaluated the immunogenicity of monovalent Ad5-BA.1, Ad5-BA.2, Ad5-BA.5, and Ad5-XBB vaccines in mice primed with the inactivated whole-virus WT vaccine. Our earlier study showed that intranasal but not intramuscular vaccination elicited systemic and respiratory mucosal-neutralizing antibodies and T cell responses [20]. Female BALB/c mice previously injected with 2 doses of the inactivated WT vaccine at 0.5 μg/dose (1/10 of human dosage) were intranasally instilled with Ad5-vectored vaccines at 4 × 109 vp/dose (1/10 of human dosage). After 4 weeks, serum-neutralizing titers were measured using a panel of 11 pseudotyped viruses, including the pre-Omicron strains WT, Beta, Delta, Omicron subvariants BA.1, BA.2, BA.2.12.1, BA.2.75, BA.5, BF.7, BQ.1.1, and XBB (Figure 4(a)). Ad5-BA.1 elicited the highest neutralizing titers against the pre-Omicron and BA.1 strains but distinctly lower titers against lately emerged Omicron subvariants, especially against BQ.1.1 and XBB (Figure 4(b–d)). Ad5-BA.2 elicited the highest neutralizing titers against BA.2 and BA.2.12.1, but was reduced 3.0-fold against BA.1, 2.6-fold against BA.5, 4.7-fold against BF.7, and 11.1-fold against BQ.1.1, and 8.0-fold against XBB, as compared to against BA.2 (Figure 4(b, c, e)). Ad5-BA.5 elicited the highest and comparable neutralizing titers against BA.2, BA.2.12.1, BA.5, and BF.7 and was reduced 2.7-fold against BQ.1.1 and 34.6-fold against XBB, as compared to against BA.5 (Figure 4(b, c, f)). Compared to other vaccines, Ad5-XBB elicited the highest titer against XBB but elicited somewhat lower titers against other strains, especially BA.1 (Figure 4(b, c, g)). We also constructed antigenic cartography maps based on serum-neutralizing titers from mice vaccinated intranasally booster with monovalent Ad5-BA.1, Ad5-BA.2, Ad5-BA.5, and Ad5-XBB vaccines. Similar to the neutralizing titer data, antigenic cartography maps revealed that antibodies induced by the Ad5-XBB tended to be XBB subvariant than the antibodies induced by other monovalent Ad5-BA.1, Ad5-BA.2, Ad5-BA.5 vaccines (Fig. S2). These results demonstrated that intranasal booster with Ad5-BA.5 elicited more broad and robust neutralizing antibodies against most Omicron subvariants, except XBB, in mice previously vaccinated with the inactivated whole-virus WT vaccine.

On the base of WT vaccine, intranasal booster with a BA.5 and XBB bivalent vaccine elicited broad-spectrum neutralizing antibodies, whereas the monovalent XBB vaccine elicited higher titer against XBB

We next evaluated intranasal boosters using bivalent vaccines containing XBB and compared them with monovalent XBB vaccines in mice previously vaccinated with 2 doses of an inactivated ancestral vaccine (Figure 5(a)). Three bivalent vaccines, including Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1, Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.5, and Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BQ.1.1, were intranasally administered in mice previously injected with 2 doses of inactivated whole-virus WT vaccine. Mice that received a third dose of inactivated whole-virus WT vaccine via the intramuscular route served as controls. Compared with a booster with the inactivated whole-virus vaccine, a booster with three bivalent vaccines significantly enhanced the neutralizing titers against all variants, especially against Omicron subvariants. Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1 elicited inferior serum and BALF neutralizing antibodies against all subvariants except BA.1 and WT (Figure 5(b–e, and i)). Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.5 elicited the most robust and broad-spectrum serum and BALF-neutralizing antibodies against all variants including WT, but the titer against BA.5 was the highest. The titer against XBB was slightly lower than that elicited by the monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine, which elicited higher titer against XBB but had minimal titers against other strains (Figure 5(b, c, d, f, h, j, and l). Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BQ.1.1 elicited the most similar profile as the XBB monovalent vaccine, which had relatively balanced serum and BALF-neutralizing antibodies against all strains (within 2-fold compared to BQ.1.1) (Figure 5(b, c, d, g, h, k, and l)). Notably, the neutralizing spectrum of BALF was broader than that of serum (Figure 5(m, o, and p)), suggesting that mucosal antibodies have greater neutralizing breadth than serum antibodies.

On the base of sequential WT and BA.5 vaccination, intranasal booster with the monovalent XBB vaccine elicited higher serum and mucosal neutralizing antibodies against post-XBB strains than all bivalent vaccines

In light of more than one billion people in China received intramuscularly injected inactivated whole-virus WT vaccines in 2021 [25] and later were infected with BA.5/BF.7 in December 2022 [26]. We thus evaluated monovalent and bivalent vaccines based on Omicron subvariants in mice that mimic this immune background. Female BALB/c mice were first vaccinated with 2 doses of inactivated WT vaccines (0.5 μg/dose). Twenty weeks later, the mice were intranasally vaccinated with Ad5-BA.5 to simulate the infection with BA.5. The mice were later intranasally vaccinated with either Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1 bivalent vaccine or one or two doses of monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine. On 8 weeks after the last vaccination, the serum and BALF neutralizing titers were measured using pseudotyped viruses carrying spikes from WT, BA.5, XBB, and EG.5 strains (Figure 6(a)). Although intranasal booster with 1 dose monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine and Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1 elicited comparable serum-neutralizing titers against WT strain, the serum-neutralizing antibody titers against BA.5, XBB, and EG.5 induced by the monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine were nearly 2-fold higher than those induced by the Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1 vaccine (Figure 6(b, c, f, and g)). The neutralizing titers against EG.5 were comparable to those against XBB (Figure 6(b, f, g)). Similarly, the BALF neutralizing titers against XBB induced by the 1 dose monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine were 3.2-fold higher than those induced by Ad5-XBB + Ad5-BA.1 (Figure 6(d)). Notably, serum and BALF neutralizing antibody titers against XBB induced by Ad5-XBB booster in WT and BA.5 immune background were 4.8-fold and 2.6-fold lower than those induced by monovalent Ad5-XBB vaccine in naïve mice, showing that immune imprinting due to exposure to previous antigens of SARS-CoV-2 may reduce antibody response (Figure 6(b–f)). Furthermore, intranasal boosters using 2 doses of Ad5-XBB further enhanced serum and BALF neutralizing titers against XBB for approximately 2-fold compared to a 1 dose Ad5-XBB booster, implying that repeated vaccinations with Ad5-XBB might reverse the effects of immune imprinting (Figure 6(b, c, d, f, and h)).

Monovalent and bivalent vaccines elicited comparable systemic and respiratory mucosal T cell responses

To evaluate and compare the T cell responses elicited by these vaccine candidates, we used an enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assay to detect interferon (IFN)-γ-secreting cells in the spleen using spike peptide pools of WT, Delta, and Omicron BA.1 strains. The results showed that intramuscular vaccination with monovalent and bivalent vaccine candidates elicited robust spike-specific T cell responses in the spleen. The spike-specific T cell responses induced by these vaccine candidates appeared to be comparable among WT, Delta, and Omicron BA.1 strains (Figure 7(a,b)).

To evaluate T cell responses after intranasal monovalent or bivalent vaccination in mice previously vaccinated with the inactivated ancestral vaccine, we used an ELISpot assay to detect IFN-γ-secreting cells in the spleen and BALF cells. The results showed that both monovalent and bivalent vaccines elicited systemic and respiratory spike-specific T cell responses, and the levels of spike-specific T cell responses against WT, Delta, and Omicron BA.1 strains were comparable. In addition, spike-specific T cell responses were 30-fold stronger in the BALF than in the spleen (Figure 7(c,d)). Similarly, intranasal monovalent or bivalent vaccines elicited comparable WT-, Delta-, and Omicron-specific T cell responses in mice previously vaccinated with the inactivated ancestral vaccine and simulated BA.5 infection. The systemic and respiratory spike-specific T cell responses elicited by the monovalent and bivalent vaccines were comparable. Again, specific T cell responses were also more robust in the BALF than in the spleen (Figure 7(e,f)).

Furthermore, we used flow cytometry to analyze tissue-resident memory T (TRM) cell responses in BALF. Intranasal vaccination elicited a high proportion of CD69 + CD103+ TRM cells in the airways, which were mainly CD8+ TRM cells (Figure 7(g,h)). Similarly, intranasal vaccination using either the monovalent or bivalent vaccine elicited comparable levels of lung TRM cells (Figure 7(g,h)).

Discussion

The effective use of COVID-19 vaccines has greatly reduced infection and mortality rates in humans. However, the emergence of Omicron subvariants, especially XBB and EG.5 variants, which have greater evasion capacity, has created a significant challenge to the immune barrier established by existing vaccines [4,7]. As the population's vaccination or infection background changes, there is a need to update vaccine components, expand the breadth and magnitude of immune responses, and establish immune profiles of different spikes or combinations to provide insights for better design of future vaccines. Currently, there are only two reports comparing the immunogenicity of adenovirus-vectored vaccines. One paper compared Ad5 vaccines of Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Epsilon, Kappa, Delta, and Omicron BA.1 [27]. The other paper compared Ad5 vaccines of WT, BA.1, BA.2, and BA.5 [28]. However, no studies on adenovirus-vectored vaccines of Omicron subvariants post the emergence of BA.5 have been reported. XBB.1 spike has 17 amino acids different from BA.5 spike, whereas the subsequently emerged EG.5 has 4 additional amino acid changes. In this study, we constructed and systematically evaluated, and compared the immunogenicity of monovalent and bivalent vaccines carrying diverse spikes ranging from WT, pre-Omicron strains, and early and late SARS-CoV-2 variants. Our study supported that the composition of the vaccine should be updated in a timely manner. A monovalent XBB vaccine or a bivalent vaccine containing XBB and late Omicron subvariants can elicit the best neutralizing antibodies against latterly emerged Omicron subvariants.

We found that monovalent pre-Omicron or BA.1 vaccines, and bivalent pre-Omicron or WT + BA.1 vaccines vaccination elicited high neutralizing titers against pre-Omicron and BA.1 but low neutralizing titers against BA.5 and late XBB subvariants in naïve mice. In contrast, monovalent XBB vaccine elicited high neutralizing titers against XBB but low neutralizing titers against pre-Omicron and BA.1 subvariants in naïve mouse, suggesting that pre-Omicron and early BA.1 subvariants have poor cross-reactivity with BA.5 and late XBB subvariants. In line with our findings, a previous study reported that BA.1 breakthrough infection in BNT162b2-vaccinated individuals elicited strong neutralizing antibodies against BA.1, BA.2, and pre-Omicron but not against BA.4/BA.5[29]. Another study also showed that COVID-19 vaccination and BA.1 breakthrough infection-induced neutralizing antibodies against BA.4/BA.5 were much lower [30]. Both the L452R and F486 V mutations on BA.5 spike are known to be associated with the evasion of neutralizing antibodies [31,32]. Since 31 of the 33 mutations in the BA.3 spike protein were shared with BA.1[33], our result also showed that BA.1 spike and BA.3 spike have similar immunogenicity. These findings indicated that BA.1- or BA.3-infected or vaccinated individuals stimulated similar epitope-specific memory B cells, but these B cells failed to produce cross-reactive antibodies against BA.5. These findings also indicated that individuals infected with BA.1 or BA.3 still need to receive the updated COVID-19 vaccine to prevent the infection of late subvariants.

XBB is a recombinant of two BA.2 descending lineages BA.2.10.1 and BA.2.75[7]. Therefore, vaccination with a BA.2 spike should elicit higher neutralizing titers against XBB than vaccination with a BA.1 spike or BA.5 spike. As expected, our result showed that Ad5-BA.2 and Ad5-BA.2.12.1 vaccination elicited higher neutralizing antibodies against XBB than either Ad5-BA.1 and Ad5-BA.5 vaccination. In addition, we also found that Ad5-XBB vaccination elicited higher neutralizing antibodies against BA.2 than against other variants.

EG.5 is a late Omicron subvariant that is closely related to XBB.1.5, with 3 additional mutations L5F, F456L, and S704L in the spike protein and 1 additional mutation F456L in the RBD region [34]. Our result showed that monovalent XBB vaccine or bivalent XBB-containing vaccine-elicited neutralizing antibodies against EG.5 were comparable to those elicited against XBB. Similarly, a recent study also showed that serum samples from XBB breakthrough infections also have neutralizing antibodies against EG.5 and EG.5.1, exhibiting only a small reduction (1.7-fold) [35]. Our result suggested that XBB-containing vaccines can provide good protection against EG.5 and likely other newly emerged Omicron subvariants.

Our study showed that intranasal booster with Ad5-XBB induced significantly higher neutralizing antibodies against XBB in naïve mice than in mice with sequential WT and BA.5 immune backgrounds. This result coincided with a human study that booster with a bivalent mRNA vaccine containing WT and BA.5 exhibited lower neutralizing titers against the latest Omicron subvariants BQ.1.1 and XBB [11]. The reason for these disappointing results has not been determined, but immune imprinting resulting from repeated exposure to early antigens has been suggested as a likely cause of these results [36]. Immune imprinting means that the immune system relies on the memory of early exposure to antigens rather than generating a primary response to new epitopes, leading to an inadequate immune response against new antigens [37]. In this study, we found that repeated intranasal vaccinations with Ad5-XBB can alleviate the effects of immune imprinting from earlier vaccines. This phenomenon has also been identified in recent Omicron mRNA vaccine studies in mice and repeated Omicron infections in humans [38]. Our study highlighted that an updated vaccine must contain XBB to induce neutralizing antibodies against XBB and its later lineage in the current population vaccinated or infected with the WT or BA.5 variants. Based on the current mutation trends in Omicron, the updated vaccine should contain components of the newest variants, not previous variants, which may be beneficial for eliciting neutralizing antibodies to the future Omicron subvariants.

Our study supports that emerging Omicron subvariants continue to increase their immune evasion of vaccine-induced or infection-induced neutralizing antibodies. A previous study demonstrated that the L452R and F486 V mutations were found in the BA.5, BF.7, and BQ.1.1 strains, but not in the BA.1 and BA.2 strains. These mutations drive immune evasion in the BA.5, BF.7, and BQ.1.1 strains [31]. These mutations are also crucial for the cross-reactive neutralizing antibody against BF.7 and BQ.1.1 in mice vaccinated with BA.5-containing monovalent or bivalent vaccines. However, these BA.5-containing monovalent or bivalent vaccines elicited low neutralizing antibodies against XBB. This is because the spike protein of XBB has additional mutations (such as R346 T, L368I, and V445P) that escape BA.5 vaccines-elicited neutralizing antibody responses. Consistent with our study, a chimpanzee adenovirus-vectored BA.5 vaccine-induced cross-neutralizing antibody against the BF.7 and BQ.1.1 in mice, but neutralizing antibodies against XBB.1.5 were poorly induced [39]. These results suggested that further updates of vaccine antigens to better match XBB or new variants are necessary to obtain optimal neutralizing antibodies against the emerging Omicron variants.

A study compared the immunogenicity of mRNA vaccine (15 µg/mouse), DNA vaccine (50 µg/mouse), purified protein vaccine (50 µg/mouse with 100 µg Adjuphos adjuvant), and rhesus adenovirus 52 (RhAd52) vaccines (109 viral particles/mouse) expressing the same HIV-1 envelope antigen in mice. These vaccines were vaccinated via intramuscular injection. At 21–28 days after vaccination, all four types of vaccine-elicited similar robust specific binding antibody titers. The mRNA and RhAd52 vaccines also elicited a comparable specific cellular immune response [40]. Therefore, similar results would be expected for mRNA-based vaccines or protein as for adenovirus-vectored vaccines. However, in contrast to protein vaccines, adenovirus vector vaccines elicited a strong immune response in the absence of adjuvants. In addition, adenovirus vector vaccines could elicit strong systemic and mucosal immune responses through mucosal vaccination.

Although T cell immunity cannot block SARS-CoV-2 infection, it plays crucial roles in eliminating infected cells and generating T cell memory. T cell immunity contributes to broad and durable host immune protection against SARS-CoV-2, especially against emerging new variants [41]. A study showed that T cell responses to spike, nucleocapsid, and membrane proteins in patients infected with Omicron were comparable to those in patients infected with WT, Beta, or Delta variants [42]. Another recent study showed that cross-reactive memory T cells induced by a COVID-19 mRNA vaccine can effectively prevent severe lung disease caused by breakthrough infection with antibody-evasive SARS-CoV-2 variants [43]. Our study also showed that intranasal vaccination using monovalent or bivalent Ad5-vectored COVID-19 vaccines was effective in inducing both systemic and respiratory mucosal cross-reactive T cell responses, which were comparably robust against the different variants. Such cross-recognized T cell responses, especially airway mucosal T cell responses, can limit the impact of SARS-CoV-2 mutations and are likely to provide immune protection against severe disease caused by SARS-CoV-2 variants, including future variants.

SARS-CoV-2 infection begins in the upper respiratory tract. Specific mucosal immunity in the upper respiratory tract is crucial for blocking the infection and transmission of SARS-CoV-2. However, conventional intramuscular vaccination is unable to induce mucosal immunity. Therefore, mucosal vaccines are needed to induce mucosal immunity. Mucosal secretory IgA antibodies (sIgA) and TRM cells effectively neutralize the virus and rapidly clear the virus upon restimulation with viral antigens [44]. Our study showed that intranasal boosters with monovalent or bivalent Ad5-vectored COVID-19 vaccines effectively induced both systemic and mucosal immune responses, including lung TRM cells. Importantly, we found that the neutralizing spectrum of mucosal antibodies was broader than that of serum antibodies. A recent study showed that conversion of IgG to dimeric IgA or secretory IgA can improve neutralizing potency and neutralizing spectrum against different SARS-CoV-2 variants [45]. Our findings highlighted the importance of mucosal vaccines in inducing mucosal antibody and T cell immunity, which can provide frontline immunosurveillance against infections via the upper respiratory tract. The mechanism of generation and regulation of IgA-secreting B cells in the respiratory tract by intranasal vaccination should be a field of research interest.

Previous studies showed that pre-existing Ad5 immunity in the blood might negatively affect the sustained response to the target antigen [46]. In this study, we found that in mice with sequential WT and BA.5 immune background, intranasal boosters using 2 doses of Ad5-XBB further enhanced serum and BALF neutralizing titers against XBB for approximately 2-fold compared to a 1 dose Ad5-XBB booster. This result suggested that intranasal vaccination may be a better way to circumvent this pre-existing adenovirus immunity. Current studies in humans on mucosal responses induced by intranasal injection with adenovirus are limited, and more evidence is needed to confirm this.

In summary, we concluded that intranasal vaccination using monovalent Ad5 vectored XBB vaccine on the immune background of WT or BA.5 can provide the best systemic and respiratory mucosal immunity against XBB and its near lineages. Mucosal vaccines that elicit respiratory mucosal antibodies and cross-recognized T cell responses should be considered for future vaccine design against emerging new SARS-CoV-2 variants.

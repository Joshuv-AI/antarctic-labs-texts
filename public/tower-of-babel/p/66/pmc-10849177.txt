Protocol digest of a randomized controlled Phase III study comparing surgery alone versus adjuvant nivolumab versus adjuvant S-1 for locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma with no pathological complete response after neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by curative esophagectomy: Japan Clinical Oncology Group study JCOG2206 (SUNRISE Trial)
Authors: Ryosuke Kita, Satoru Matsuda, Motoo Nomura, Ryunosuke Machida, Keita Sasaki, Ken Kato, Rei Goto, Takashi Yoshioka, Shun Yamamoto, Takahiro Tsushima, Haruhiko Fukuda, Hiroya Takeuchi, Yuko Kitagawa, Japan Esophageal Oncology Group of the Japan Clinical Oncology Group
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough the efficacy of postoperative therapy with nivolumab after preoperative chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced oesophageal cancer has been proven in Western countries, the survival benefits of postoperative therapy after preoperative chemotherapy with docetaxel, cisplatin and 5-FU or cisplatin plus 5-FU as the Japanese standard therapy remain controversial. This randomized Phase III trial was designed to confirm the superiority of postoperative therapy with nivolumab over observation alone and S-1 over observation alone in patients with locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma with no pathological complete response after preoperative chemotherapy followed by curative esophagectomy. A total of 450 patients will be enrolled from 56 hospitals in Japan over 5.5 years. The primary endpoint is relapse-free survival, and the secondary endpoints are disease-free survival, overall survival, adverse events and incremental cost-effectiveness ratio. This trial has been registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials as jRCTs031230219 (https://jrct.niph.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCTs031230219).Keywords: oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma, postoperative therapy, nivolumab, S-1

Introduction

Oesophageal cancer is the sixth leading cause of cancer-related death in men and the ninth in women worldwide (1). The incidence and mortality rates have remained stable over time in Japan. Although various treatment strategies including preoperative and postoperative therapy for patients with oesophageal cancer have been developed, the prognosis of the disease remains poor.

In a randomized Phase III trial conducted in Japan to compare preoperative chemotherapy with cisplatin plus 5-FU (CF) to postoperative chemotherapy with CF (JCOG9907), the superiority of preoperative chemotherapy in terms of the 5-year overall survival (OS) was demonstrated [hazard ratio (HR) 0.73; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.54–0.99, P = 0.04] (2). In Western countries, based on the results of the CROSS trial, which showed the survival benefits of preoperative chemoradiotherapy over surgery alone (HR: 0.657; 95% CI: 0.495–0.871, P = 0.003), the standard treatment is preoperative chemoradiotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel (3). In the following randomized Phase III trial (JCOG1109) conducted in Japan, the survival benefit of preoperative chemotherapy with docetaxel plus CF (DCF) was confirmed with the results of 3-year OS for DCF vs. CF (HR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.50–0.92, P = 0.006) but not for preoperative chemoradiotherapy with cisplatin plus 5-FU (CF-RT) vs. CF (HR: 0.84; 95% CI: 0.63–1.12, P = 0.12) (4). On the basis of these findings, preoperative chemotherapy with DCF has become the current Japanese standard treatment for locally advanced oesophageal cancer. More recently, we conducted a validation study of JCOG1109 using a nationwide database, which confirmed the survival benefit of neoadjuvant DCF (5). Thus, preoperative treatment for locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell cancer has been inconsistent between Japan and Western countries.

After preoperative treatment became established in Western countries, the CheckMate 577 trial was conducted to evaluate the efficacy of postoperative therapy. This trial confirmed the superiority of postoperative therapy with nivolumab to placebo in terms of disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with locally advanced oesophageal cancer who had residual pathological disease (HR: 0.69; 95% CI: 056–0.86, P = 0.0003) (6). The 3-year recurrence-free survival (RFS) of the CheckMate 577 trial was ~30%, which was not as favourable as the 64.9% found in patients with residual pathological disease in JCOG1109. This finding highlights a notable discrepancy between the two studies. Therefore, it is not possible to extrapolate the evidence of the CheckMate 577 trial immediately to Japan.

The postoperative therapy with nivolumab has shown efficacy, especially amongst patients who received preoperative radiotherapy. On the other hand, the ATTRACTION-3 trial, focusing on unresectable advanced or recurrent oesophageal squamous cell cancer, also demonstrated the effectiveness of nivolumab as a monotherapy (7). These finding suggests the potential of adjuvant nivolumab as a promising postoperative treatment, regardless of whether preoperative radiotherapy was administered or not.

Additionally, a single-arm Phase II trial, the PIECE trial, was conducted to investigate the safety and efficacy of postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 for locally advanced oesophageal cancer (8). This study targeted patients who underwent preoperative chemotherapy with CF, and the 3-year RFS for all eligible patients was 72.3% (90% CI: 59.9–81.5%), with a 3-year OS of 85.0% (90% CI: 73.9–91.6%). Considering that the 3-year progression-free survival in patients who received preoperative chemotherapy with CF in JCOG1109 was 47.7%, postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 could be a promising treatment option.

Based on this background, we have designed this randomized Phase III trial with the objective of confirming the efficacy of postoperative therapy with nivolumab and postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 compared to observation alone. This trial specifically focuses on patients with locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma with no pathological complete response after preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy. In our previous study, patients who achieved a pathological complete response in primary oesophageal cancer lesions demonstrated a 5-year cancer-specific survival rate of 81.1% (9). Moreover, pT0N0 cases exhibited even more favourable outcomes. Consequently, patients with a pathological complete response were excluded from the present study, given their promising prognosis and the lack of necessity for adjuvant therapy following surgery.

The JCOG Protocol Review Committee approved this study protocol in March 2023, and centralized approval was obtained from the National Cancer Center Hospital Certified Review Board in May 2023. This trial was registered at the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials with the number jRCTs031230219 (https://jrct.niph.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCTs031230219).

Protocol digest of JCOG2206

Purpose

This trial aims to confirm the superiority of postoperative therapy with nivolumab over observation alone and the superiority of postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 over observation alone in patients with locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma with no pathological complete response after preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy.

Study setting

A multi-institutional (56 specialized centres) three-arm, open-label, randomized controlled Phase III trial.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint is relapse-free survival (RFS) in all randomized patients, which is defined as the time from randomization to the first event of either relapse or death from any cause, and it is censored at the last date of contact for a relapse-free surviving patient. The secondary endpoints are DFS, OS, incremental cost-effectiveness ratio and adverse events. DFS is defined as the time from randomization to the first event of relapse, secondary cancer occurrence or death from any cause, and it is censored at the last date of contact for a disease-free surviving patient. OS is defined as the time from randomization to death of any cause, and it is censored on the last day of contact for a surviving patient. Adverse events include late postoperative complications and therapeutic toxicities, which entails late toxicities such as immune response adverse events and serious adverse events.

Randomization

After the confirmation of patient eligibility, patients will be randomized (1:1:1) to either the observation alone arm (preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy), the nivolumab arm (preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy and postoperative therapy with nivolumab) or the S-1 arm (preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy and postoperative chemotherapy with S-1) using the web-based system to the JCOG Data Center. The minimization method with a random component will be used for randomization with preoperative chemotherapy (CF vs. DCF), ypStage (I–II vs. III–IV), supraclavicular lymph node dissection (dissected vs. not dissected) and the institution.

Treatment methods

Patients in the observation alone arm receive no postoperative anticancer treatment unless recurrence is detected. Postoperative treatment with nivolumab entails giving the drug at a dose of 240 mg/body every 2 weeks or 480 mg/bodies every 4 weeks. The treatment is continued for 1 year or until detection of recurrence, the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity (such as Grade 3 or higher immune response adverse events) or the patient’s refusal to continue treatment. Postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 comprises four courses of twice-daily oral S-1 for 4 weeks followed by 2 weeks of rest. Dividing one course into two terms, taking twice-daily oral S-1 for 2 weeks followed by 1 week of rest, is permissible. Three dose levels of S-1 are set according to the patient’s body surface area (BSA), as follows: BSA < 1.25 m2, 40 mg; BSA 1.25–1.50 m2, 50 mg; BSA > 1.50 m2, 60 mg, twice a day. Patients with creatinine clearance levels of 50–60 ml/min receive a dose of S-1 that is reduced by 20 mg/day. The treatment is continued for four courses or up to 24 weeks or until the detection of recurrence, the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity (such as Grade 4 non-haematological toxicities) or the patient’s refusal to continue treatment. If nivolumab or S-1 cannot be initiated within 2 weeks after registration for any reason, the protocol treatment is terminated.

Follow-up

All enrolled patients are followed up for 5 years after patient accrual has been completed, whilst the primary analysis is performed 3 years after accrual completion.

The patient’s body weight is measured and physical and blood examinations (including endocrine examinations and tumour markers such as carcinoembryonic antigen and squamous cell carcinoma-associated antigen), is performed every 3 months for the first year and every 6 months for the last 4 years. Contrast-enhanced CT for the cervix, chest and abdomen is performed at least every 3 months for the first year and every 6 months for the last 4 years. Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy is performed every year. After the 5-year follow-up period, a physical examination is performed annually, and blood or CT examinations are performed only if recurrence is suspected.

Study design and statistical analysis

This randomized Phase III trial is designed to confirm the superiority of the addition of postoperative therapy with nivolumab or postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 to the standard treatment, preoperative chemotherapy with DCF or CF followed by curative esophagectomy for patients with locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma with no pathological complete response.

We assumed a 3-year RFS of 60% in the observation alone arm and expected an 11% increase in the 3-year RFS for the nivolumab arm and the S-1 arm (HR: =0.67). The total required sample size was calculated as 435 (145 per arm) to achieve the desired power of 70% for each pair comparison (observation alone arm vs. the nivolumab arm and observation alone arm vs. S-1 arm) with an overall one-sided alpha of 5%, an accrual period of 5.5 years and a follow-up period of 3 years by Schoenfeld and Richter’s method (10). The total sample size will be set at 450 (150 per arm), allowing for the loss to follow-up of certain patients.

The primary analysis will be performed using the log-rank test stratified by preoperative chemotherapy (CF vs. DCF), ypStage (I–II vs. III–IV) and supraclavicular lymph node dissection (dissected vs. not dissected). We will adjust for multiplicity due to two pair comparisons using the Bonferroni method to maintain the overall one-sided alpha level of 5%, i.e. one-sided alpha of 2.5% is used in each pair comparison. If an experimental treatment is superior to observation alone in terms of RFS and not inferior in terms of OS, we will conclude that the experimental treatment is the better treatment. Only when the superiorities of both postoperative treatments with nivolumab and postoperative chemotherapy with S-1 over observation alone are demonstrated will a direct comparison between the nivolumab arm and the S-1 arm is to be performed with a two-sided alpha of 5% or a one-sided alpha of 5% in a closed testing procedure. The decision to use either a one-sided or two-sided test is based on the judgement of the researchers through viewing the safety reports, including the relevant information about certain adverse effects. This is because the toxicity profiles of the two regimens are different, and it is necessary to determine the regimen that is more toxic prior to conducting the analysis. The primary analysis will be carried out 3 years after the completion of patient accrual.

Interim analysis and monitoring

An interim analysis is planned to be conducted after half of the planned number of patients have been enrolled. The Lan–DeMets α-spending function with the O’Brien and Fleming type will be used to adjust the multiplicity due to the interim analysis (11). Study termination will be considered due to efficacy if the primary endpoint was higher in the nivolumab arm or the S-1 arm than in the surgery alone arm, with a P value less than the adjusted significance level. The conditional power and predictive probability for the primary endpoint will be used to determine whether or not the study should be terminated due to futility (12,13). The Data and Safety Monitoring Committee of the JCOG will review the interim analysis report independently from the group investigators and group statistician. The JCOG Data Center provides central monitoring of study progress, data integrity and patient safety. The monitoring report will be reviewed by the Study Chair, Study Coordinator and group investigators. For quality assurance, site visit audits will be performed by the JCOG Audit Committee (not on a study-specific basis but for the study group).

Incremental cost-effectiveness analysis

To evaluate the incremental cost-effectiveness, we will utilize the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). Costs will be calculated based on drug expenses for postoperative therapy, treatment costs associated with adverse events and post-recurrence treatment costs. Quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) will be determined using the most appropriate quality of life values available at the time of analysis from external data sources, such as OS data obtained in this study and data from systematic reviews. The rationale for conducting incremental cost-effectiveness analysis is to assess the effectiveness of treatment, considering not only the expenses related to postoperative therapy drugs but also the costs associated with post-recurrence treatment. However, in this study, the selection of the new standard treatment will be determined primarily by factors of efficacy and toxicity, with economic factors such as drug costs not being considered.

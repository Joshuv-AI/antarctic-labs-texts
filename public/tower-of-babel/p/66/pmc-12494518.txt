Rice Transcription Factor HSTL Regulates Plant Height and Salt Stress Response by Modulating Gibberellin Homeostasis
Authors: Chenyu Wang, Zhuowei Cheng, Mei Zhou, Zuming Lu, Qiong Jiang, Kaixing Lu, Cheng Zhu, Yanfei Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Gibberellins (GAs) are crucial in the regulation of plant growth and development, and in responses to adverse environments. Here, we report that a Cys2 /His2 zinc finger protein in rice, HSTL (heat stress tolerance like), participates in the control of stem elongation and salt stress response by affecting GA homeostasis. Knockdown of HSTL increased plant height, internode elongation and bioactive GAs levels in rice plants. Comparative transcriptome showed that HSTL plays a critical role in rice GA pathway through regulation of genes involved in GA biosynthesis and metabolism. In addition, HSTL knockdown seedlings maintained higher relative water content and lower accumulation of H2O2 as well as higher tolerance to salt stress compared with the wild-type (WT). These results suggest that HSTL plays an important role in regulating internode elongation and stress response by coordinating GAs homeostasis, thus providing a useful target for engineering stress-tolerant rice varieties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12284-025-00851-y.

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa) is an important crop for more than half of the human population in the world. In response to various abiotic stresses such as high salt, high temperature and drought, rice shows reduced plant height, shortened internodes, and reduced grain yield (Huang et al. 2021; Bo et al. 2020). Among them, plant height is one of the important agronomic traits of rice, and proper plant height can improve rice yield and resistance to stress (Waters et al. 2012). Thus, it is of great importance to reveal the regulatory mechanisms of plant height and stress tolerance. Studies have shown that plant height is regulated by both phytohormonal and non-phytohormonal pathways (Machado et al. 2016). While most of the current studies have focused on the phytohormone pathway, the role of gibberellins (GAs) in plant height and stress tolerance becomes a hot topic.

GAs are a class of diterpenoid acid hormones that accumulate mainly in root tips, newly-grown leaves and seeds in plants. The physiologically active ones are GA1, GA3, GA4 and GA7 (Hedden et al. 2012; Sun et al. 2022; Ragonezi et al. 2011; Liu et al. 2022a, b). GA biosynthesis and metabolism is a multi-step enzymatic process involving multiple enzymes. Those early key enzymes including CPS, KS, KO and KAO are predominantly single genes, whereas later enzymes such as GA20ox, GA3ox and GA2ox are predominantly encoded by multiple genes (Khan et al. 2015; Hu et al. 2008; Sakamoto et al. 2004). The GA signalling pathway regulates plant height by deregulating the inhibitory effects of DELLA proteins (Guan et al. 2021). So far, many transcription factor families have been reported to affect plant height by regulating GA pathway, including APETALA 2/ethylene (AP2/ERF), Cys2His2 (C2H2) Zinc Finger, and YABBY transcription factors (Zhou et al. 2023). They were found to achieve the regulation of plant height by participating in the regulation of GA biosynthesis and signaling pathways, or have a targeted regulatory effect on DELLA transcription, thereby affecting active GA levels and altering plant phenotypes (Dong et al. 2022; Lantzouni et al. 2020).

C2H2 zinc finger proteins are an important class of transcription factors involved in plant growth and development and phytohormone metabolism (Hao et al. 2023; Ai et al. 2021). They have more than one zinc finger motif and contain an N-terminal DNA-binding domain and a C-terminal transcriptional regulatory domain, which not only binds specifically to DNA, but also directly targets the downstream stress-associated genes to activate or repress their expression under abiotic stresses (Wolfe et al. 2000; Han et al. 2020). Many zinc finger proteins play important roles in the regulatory network of salt stress response by transcriptionally regulating downstream genes for salt stress tolerance in plants (Han et al. 2021). They respond to drought stress by regulating reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging pathway, ABA-dependent pathway, and ABA-independent pathway (Yang et al. 2021; Yin et al. 2017).

Previously, we isolated rice HST1 (heat stress tolerance) gene encoding a C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor with a role in heat tress tolerance (Ding et al. 2023). Interestingly, rice HST1 was isolated initially as Drought and Salt Tolerance (DST) in Lin's lab for its negative role in drought and salt tolerance by controlling stomatal aperture (Huang et al. 2009). DST promoted stomatal motility by regulating genes involved in ROS homeostasis. HST1 was more recently identified as Regulator of Gn1a 1 (REG1) for its negative role in the regulation of grain number (Li et al. 2013; Cui et al. 2015). Here, we reported that HSTL (heat stress tolerance like), with the identical C2H2 zinc finger domain with HST1, participates in the regulation of GA homeostasis and tolerance to salt stress. Genetic mutation in HSTL was capable of regulating plant height by promoting cell elongation in the uppermost internode of rice and increasing the active GA levels. The hstl and hstl/hst mutants maintained high proline contents and low ROS levels, and enhanced salt tolerance compared with the wild-type (WT). These results suggest that HSTL might act as a key regulator in balancing plant height and salt response, by affecting hormone homeostasis.

Results

HSTL is a Member of Cys2/His2 Zinc Finger Proteins

The genomic sequence of HSTL has a full length of 1118 bp with one exon, and 633 bp protein-coding frame with 210 amino acids. The HSTL protein structure consists of one alpha and one beta sheet. The HSTL protein was predicted with a C2H2 zinc finger domain and a signal peptide, suggesting that HSTL protein belongs to the single zinc finger protein (Supplemental Fig. 1A). The amino acid sequences of the C2H2-type zinc finger domains of HST and HSTL were then compared, and results showed that the amino acid sequence of the zinc finger motif was identical (Supplemental Fig. 1B). There are 15 HST homolog genes in rice, and we constructed the phylogenetic tree (Supplemental Fig. 1C). Phylogenetic analysis showed that HSTL was a paralogous gene of HST, with a 82% similarity in sequence (Supplemental Fig. 1C). Amino acid sequence alignments of HST homologs showed HSTL and HST possess the identical C2H2 zinc finger domain (Supplemental Fig. 1D).

HSTL Expression Patterns Under GA Treatment and Salt Stress Conditions

The expression patterns of HSTL were examined in different tissues by real-time qPCR (Fig. 1A). At the stem elongation stage, HSTL was dominantly expressed in the leaves and nodes, while at the flowering stage, it was mainly expressed in stems and leaves. We also tested whether HSTL contributes to the responses to salt stress and GA treatment. The expression levels of HSTL were increased, and peaked after 4 h of NaCl treatment (Fig. 1B). After GA treatment, the expression of HSTL was increased, reaching the highest relative expression at 36 h of treatment (Fig. 1C). Uniconazole is a GA synthesis inhibitor. It controls nutritional growth, inhibits cell elongation, shortens internodes, dwarfs plants, promotes lateral bud growth, and enhances resistance to adverse conditions. In contrast, treatment with uniconazole led to the decrease in the expression level of HSTL (Fig. 1D), indicating that HSTL expression was induced by GA while inhibited by uniconazole. Knockout of HSTL in Rice Cultivars by CRISPR/Cas9 Technology

To further explore the role of HSTL in stress response, we generated HSTL loss of function lines using the CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing technology. Two target sites, target 1 and target 2, were designed for HSTL in the exons encoding the function domain of HSTL. We generated two mutant alleles of HSTL (hstl 3 and hstl 12) in the WT background. We also generated two mutant alleles of HSTL (hstl/hst 3 and hstl/hst 15) in the hst mutant background (Supplemental Fig. 2). By selfing and genotyping, we selected homozygous hstl and hstl/hst mutants for further analysis. As shown in Supplemental Fig. 2, hstl3-1 and hstl12-1 had a 28-bp and 2-bp deletion in the exon of HSTL, respectively, indicating that frame shift mutations occur on HSTL in both mutants.

HSTL Affects Internode Length and Gibberellin Homeostasis

Intriguingly, compared with WT, both hstl3-1 and hstl12-1 mutants showed increased plant height at the vegetative phase as well as at maturity stage (Fig. 2A,2C). At 7-leaf-stage, the shoot length of HSTL mutant seedlings was significantly increased, with 22.8% more in hstl3-1 and 23.2% more in hstl12-1 than WT, respectively (Fig. 2B). The number of nodes and internode length are the major determinants of plant height in rice. Correspondingly, the number of nodes and internode length of hstl mutants exhibited an apparently increase (Fig. 2A,3A). WT and hst plants developed only three nodes, while hstl and hstl/hst mutants both developed seven nodes at 7-leaf-stage (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, the internode lengths in hstl and hstl/hst mutants were significantly higher than those of WT and hst plants, respectively (Fig. 3A). The increased internode length could be caused by increases in cell proliferation or cell elongation. Since hstl mutants showed higher internode length, we determined the endogenous levels of GAs in the uppermost internodes of rice plants at 7-leaf stage. Both hstl and hstl/hst mutants contained significantly more bioactive GAs (GA1, GA3, and GA4) and less inactive GA8 than those of WT (Fig. 3D). These results indicated that HSTL participated in GA accumulation, and that the different levels of GAs between WT and mutants coincided with the different heights at the seedling stage.

HSTL Alters the Transcription of Genes Involved in GA Metabolism and Biosynthesis

To further investigate the role of HSTL in stem elongation, we compared the transcriptomes of the upmost internode of WT and hstl12-1 mutant plants at 7-leaf stage using RNA-seq analysis. Using the cutoff values of log2(fold change) > 1 with statistical significance of p < 0.05, the transcriptome analysis showed that 6191 genes were down-regulated and 3462 genes were up-regulated in hstl12-1 mutants compared with that in WT (Supplemental Fig. 3). We performed GO enrichment analysis of DEGs between WT and hstl12-1 mutants. The major biological processes that were substantially altered as a result of HSTL mutations include regulatory processes (transcription, signal transduction, and protein phosphorylation), biosynthetic and metabolic processes. Importantly, GA metabolism and biosynthesis processes were also enriched in both up-regulated and down-regulated DEGs between WT and hstl12-1 mutants, which were consistent with the findings that GA contents increased in hstl12-1 mutants. KEGG analysis results showed that metabolic pathways (osa01100), biosynthesis of secondary metabolites (osa01110), phenylpropanoid biosynthesis (osa00940), and photosynthesis (osa00195) were among the most affected pathway.

Among the differentially expressed genes, we found that the expression of genes involved in GA metabolism and biosynthesis changed greatly (Supplemental Table S1). We also performed qPCR analysis to further verify the gene expression. Results showed that the transcript levels of genes involved in GA biosynthesis such as OsCPS2, OsKS1, OsGA20ox7 were up-regulated in hstl12-1 mutants compared with those in WT (Fig. 4A). OsCPS2 encodes enanticobylpyrophosphate (ent-CDP) synthase, which mediates the conversion of yak child-based yak biphosphate (GGDP) to ent-CDP (Lu et al. 2018). OsKS1 encodes an ent-kaurene synthase (KS), which catalyzes the second step of GA biosynthesis. OsKS1 controls the growth and development of the aboveground part of rice and is sensitive to exogenous GA (Xu et al. 2007). GA catabolic enzymes were also tested in mutants and WT plants (Fig. 4B). Both hstl and hstl/hst mutants exhibited reduced transcription levels of OsGA2ox1, OsGA2ox7 and OsGA2ox8. GA2oxs are considered as GA inactivating enzymes that convert GA precursors or biologically active GAs to inactive GA by 2β-hydroxylation (Thomas et al. 1999). Sakamoto found that OsGA2ox1 controls the level of bioactive GAs in the shoot apicalmeristem; therefore, reduction in its expression may contribute to the early development of the inflorescence meristem (Sakamoto et al. 2004). GA can induce the expression of OsGA2ox8 in the shoots and roots of rice seedlings, while paclobutrazol acts as an inhibitor of GA and significantly reduces the expression of OsGA2ox8 in the roots, indicating that OsGA2ox8 is involved in the homeostasis of GA (Wang et al. 2021). In addition, we screened the expression of five genes (YAB4, GASR1, Os03g0760800, Os01g0571100, Os03g0216000 and Os03g0607200), which related to GA signal transduction and GA response. YAB4 serves as a DNA-binding intermediate protein for SLR1 and is associated with the GA signaling pathway during plant growth and development (Yang et al. 2016). GASR1 was regulated by GA, functioning as a mediator in GA signaling pathway (Wang et al. 2009). The expression levels of YAB4 and GASR1 genes were both significantly reduced in hstl and hstl/hst mutants compared to those of WT plants (Fig. 4C). HSTL Negatively Regulates Rice Tolerance to Salt Stress

To investigate whether HSTL mediated salt tolerance in rice, 14 d old WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutant seedlings were treated with 120 mM NaCl for 18 d, then recovered for 16 d (Fig. 5A). The leaves of WT and mutants gradually turned yellow and curly, or even white, and eventually die of water loss. Among them, hst, hstl and hstl/hst mutants showed better recovery after stress, with the higher survival rate ranging from 65 to 82%, and were the more salt-tolerant, whereas WT had the lowest survival rate after recovery (Fig. 5B). Meanwhile, these mutants showed increased plant height and root length compared with the WT under salt stress (Fig. 5C,5D). To elucidate the physiological mechanisms of salt tolerance in HSTL single and double mutants, we determined the water status of WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutants. We treated ten isolated rice leaves with water loss stress which was grown for 14 d. The water loss rate of all isolated rice leaves increased with the prolongation of treatment time and gradually peaked after 8 h treatment. After 12 h treatment, the water loss rate of hstl/hst double mutants was significantly reduced compared to the WT (Fig. 5E). Subsequently, we treated 14 d old WT and mutant plants with 120 mM NaCl, and the relative water content was determined by taking the fully expanded leaves of the plants at different time intervals. It was found that after 72 h of salt treatment, the relative water content of hstl/hst double mutants was the highest, showing the slowest decline in relative water content. The decline in relative water content of hstl mutants was significantly lower than that of WT, while WT showed the greatest reduction in relative water content (Fig. 5F). These results indicate that HSTL gene mutations plays a crucial role in reducing the water loss rate and maintaining the relative water content of rice plants, thus, rice plants can maintain more water and enhance salt tolerance.

HSTL Mutations Relieves Oxidation Damage in Rice Under Salt Stress

Malondialdehyde (MDA) and H2O2 contents are significant indicators of stress damage degree suffered by plants. We determined the MDA and H2O2 contents in the leaves of WT and mutants under 120 mM NaCl treatment. Figure 6A showed that after salt treatment for 24 h, compared with WT, MDA content of hstl and hstl/hst mutants was reduced. Additionally, hstl/hst mutants had the lowest MDA content, suggesting it suffered less cellular damage. In addition, under normal conditions, H2O2 content of hstl/hst mutants was the largest, whereas WT contained significantly less H2O2 than hstl and hst mutants. Under NaCl treatment, H2O2 content of WT and mutants’ leaves showed an overall trend of decreasing (Fig. 6B). After 24 h of salt stress, hstl and hstl/hst mutants accumulated less H2O2 and reduced oxidative damage than WT. Furthermore, we determined the activity of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide (SOD), catalase (CAT) and peroxidase (POD) in the leaves of WT and mutants treated with 120 mM NaCl (Fig. 6C). HSTL Regulates Proline and Na+ Contents in Rice Under Salt Stress

Proline is a compatible solute involved in osmotic pressure regulation and safety of components and organelles (Matsunaga et al. 2021), and plays an important role in plant resistance (Seki et al. 2007). Figure 7A showed that no discernable alteration was observed in proline content between the leaves of WT and mutants under normal conditions. However, with the prolongation of salt stress, proline content of WT, hst, hstl and hstl/hst mutants increased gradually. Na+ content is a vital aspect in assessing salt tolerance in plants. Excessive Na+ accumulation not only inhibits the uptake of elements such as K, Cu, and Mg, but also causes the damage to the membrane structure of plant root system (Tester et al. 2003). Finally, we investigated the Na+ content in roots and shoots of WT and mutants under 120 mM NaCl treatment for 7 d by atomic absorption spectrophotometer (Fig. 7B). Under normal conditions, there was no notable difference in Na+ content in roots between WT and mutants, whereas the shoots of WT and hst accumulated more Na+ content than hstl and hstl/hst mutants. Under salt treatment, WT plants accumulated significantly higher Na+ content in the roots and shoots than that in hst, hstl and hstl/hst mutants. Additionally, Na+ content accumulated in the shoots of hstl/hst3-2 and hstl/hst15-2 was strikingly lower than that of hstl and hst mutants. These data revealed that HSTL and HST gene mutation enhanced salt tolerance by reducing Na+ accumulation and toxicity.

Discussion

Plant height is a significant feature that decides plant architecture and yield, mainly controlled by GAs. GAs play important roles in regulating plant height, stem elongation, seed germination, and flowering induction (Hedden et al. 2012; Wang et al. 2013). Many transcription factors have been reported to regulate plant height by participating in a complex regulatory network of GAs homeostasis and signal transduction (Zhou et al. 2023). C2H2-type zinc finger proteins are a group of important transcription factors involved in the life process of plant growth, development and phytohormone metabolisms (Li et al. 2021a, b). The C2H2 zinc finger protein ZFP207 was reported to be a transcriptional repressor of the green revolution gene SD1. Overexpression of ZFP207 produced a GA-sensitive semi-dwarf phenotype, whereas RNA interference increased plant height (Duan et al. 2021). The upregulation of C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor DEC1 (decelerator of internode elongation 1) inhibited internode elongation, whereas downregulation of DEC1 promoted internode elongation in rice (Nagai et al. 2020). In contrast, ACE1 (accelerator of internode elongation 1) enhanced the cellular response to GA, activated the cell division capacity of intermediate meristematic tissues, and promoted rice internode elongation (Nagai et al. 2020).

In our study, HSTL was found to negatively regulate rice height, observed the phenotypes of WT, hst, hstl and hstl/hst mutants. The shoot length, node number and internode length of hstl mutants apparently increased than those of WT. The hstl/hst mutants were higher than hst and hstl single mutants (Figs. 2 and 3). Moreover, HSTL and HST knockdown significantly increased the levels of active GA1, GA3, and GA4 in the uppermost internode, but decreased inactive GA8 levels. These results indicated that HSTL mutations promoted plant height elongation by affecting GA homeostasis. Endogenous GA levels are modulated by the expression of genes involved in GA biosynthesis and deactivation. We analyzed the expression profiles of GA synthesis and metabolism-related genes in WT and hstl mutants. The expression levels of GA biosynthesis genes (OsCPS2, OsKS1, OsGA20ox7) were up-regulated in hstl mutants compared to WT, and GA metabolism genes (OsGA2ox1, OsGA2ox7 and OsGA2ox8) showed a significant decrease, leading to the increased GA levels in hstl and hstl/hst mutants.

Facing abiotic stresses, plants evolved various mechanisms such as cellular molecular responses and physiological and biochemical reactions (Oladosu et al. 2019; Mirza et al. 2022; Li et al. 2023). Phytohormones play important roles in coordinating plant response to adverse environments. C2H2-type zinc finger proteins ZFP182, ZFP252, and ZFP179 were identified to regulate rice tolerance to salt stress (Huang et al. 2007; Xu et al. 2008; Sun et al. 2010). ZFP182 was induced by salt and ABA, responding to salt stress through an ABA-dependent pathway (Huang et al. 2007). In addition, the trihelix transcription factors also play key roles in stress response (Kaplan-Levy et al. 2012). GT-3b, a member of trihelix transcription factor, was induced by NaCl stress and pathogen infections (Park et al. 2004). GA increased spermidine-induced salt tolerance and the expression of GT-3b in cucumber (Wang et al. 2020). GA could also alleviate Cd toxicity in rice, via reducing Cd immobilization in the cell walls and enhancing Cd sequestration and efffux (Liu et al. 2022a, b). These findings suggest that manipulating GA levels is an effective strategy to improve stress tolerance in plants.

Previously, we identified rice C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor HST1 (heat stress tolerance) with a role in heat tress tolerance (Ding et al. 2023). Interestingly, rice HST1 was isolated initially as Drought and Salt Tolerance (DST) for its negative role in drought and salt tolerance (Huang et al. 2009). DST was reported to regulate stomatal opening in response to salt and drought stress through an ABA-independent signalling pathway. In this study, we found that HSTL (heat stress tolerance like), with the identical C2H2 zinc finger domain with HST, participated in the regulation of GA homeostasis and tolerance to salt stress. HSTL, as a homolog gene of HST, is identical to OsZFP213/PINE1/DEC1 (Os12g42250) (Zhang et al. 2018; Nagai et al. 2020). Nagai et al. (2020) found that loss-of-function mutations in DEC1 promoted internode elongation. OsZFP213 was involved in salt response in rice (Zhang et al. 2018). In this study, mutations of HSTL increased plant height, internode elongation and bioactive GAs levels in rice (Figs. 2 and 3). HSTL mutations also improved rice tolerance to salt stress, which was achieved by reducing water loss and ROS accumulation, and by maintaining proline contents and antioxidant enzyme activities (Figs. 5, 6 and 7). In addition to salt stress, we also tested the drought stress tolerance of hstl and hstl/hst mutants. The 14 d old WT and mutant seedlings were treated with 20% PEG4000 for 2 d, then recovered for 16 d. The hstl, hst and hstl/hst mutants showed better recovery after drought stress, with the higher survival rate ranging from 65 and 88% (Supplemental Fig. 4), suggesting that HSTL and HST mutations could also contribute to drought stress tolerance. These results showed that HSTL and HST were both involved in rice response to abiotic stresses such as high temperature, salt and drought stress. Interestingly, hstl and hstl/hst mutants showed higher plant height, internode elongation than those of hst and WT plants, respectively, suggesting that HSTL plays a bigger role in plant height regulation compared to HST. These results indicated that C2H2 zinc finger transcription factors are crucial in plant height regulation and abiotic stress response networks. However, due to the complexity of these networks, many of the action mechanisms needed to be further investigated. Identification of the early signals for the activation of HSTL and downstream target genes will provide important new insights into the pathways and network of plant height and salt stress response.

Conclusions

In this study, we reported that the C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor HSTL plays a critical role in the regulation of GA homeostasis and tolerance to salt stress. Knockdown of HSTL increased plant height and internode length by promoting cell elongation and increasing bioactive GAs levels in rice plants. RNA-seq analysis showed that HSTL participated in the GA pathway through regulation of genes involved in GA biosynthesis and metabolism. In addition, HSTL knockdown seedlings maintained higher relative water content and lower ROS levels, improving salt tolerance compared with the WT. These results enrich the function of GA regulatory network in plant height and salt tolerance, and provide a theoretical basis for crop improvement.

Materials and Methods

Plant Materials

Rice cultivar Zhonghua 11 (Oryza sativa L., ZH11) was used as WT throughout the study. The hst mutants were isolated from the T7 generation of ZH11 T-DNA insertion mutant lines in our previous study (Ding et al. 2023). HST1 gene was isolated as LOC_Os03g57240 by map-based cloning method, which encodes a C2H2 zinc finger protein transcription factor (Ding et al. 2023). Moreover, we found HSTL (LOC_Os12g42250) with the identical amino acid sequence of the C2H2 zinc finger motif as HST.

Two homozygous knock-out mutants (hstl3-1 and hstl12-1) in ZH11 background were generated by using CRISPR/ Cas9 system. The target sequences of HSTL (5’-ggcaACATCGCCTCCGCCCTCTCC-3’/5’-aaacGGAGAGGGCGGAGGCGATGT -3’ and 5’- gccgGCACTCCTGCCGGCCTCACC-3’/5’-aaacGGTGAGGCCGGCAGGAGTGC -3’) were inserted into CRISPR/Cas9 vector and transferred into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain EHA105. The HSTL CRISPR/Cas9 vectors were separately transferred into ZH11 and hst mutants using Agrobacterium tumefaciens-mediated transformation, to obtain the hstl mutants and hstl/hst double mutants, respectively. To verify whether mutations existed at the candidate sites in hstl and hstl/hst mutants, we performed PCR amplification followed by sequencing of the target regions in all transgenic plants (5’-CCATTTCTCATCACTGAGCT-3’ and 5’- ATTCTGGTGTCCCCCTAGCGC-3’).

Plant Growth and Treatment

Rice seeds were sterilized with 10% (v/v) sodium hypochlorite for 15 min before rinsing three times with ddH2O, then soaked in the water at 37°C for 2 d in the dark. The germinated seeds were cultured in a growth chamber with a 13-h light (28°C)/11-h dark (23°C) photoperiod and 70% relative humidity. Plants were grown until maturity to investigate plant height, internode length and node number.

For salt treatment, 14 days old seedlings were transferred to nutrient solutions containing 120 mM NaCl for 18 d. After NaCl treatment, the seedlings were transferred to the normal nutrient solution for recovery for 16 d. The phenotype was observed and the survival rate was counted. Rice leaves were taken for RNA extraction at 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h after 120 mM NaCl treatment. For drought treatment, 14 d old seedlings were treated with 20% PEG4000 for 2 d, then recovered for 16 d. The phenotype was observed and the survival rate was counted. For GA and uniconazole treatments, 14 days old rice seedlings were transferred to nutrient solution containing 10–5 M GA and 10–6 M uniconazole, respectively. Rice leaves were taken at 0, 1, 6, 12, 24, 36, and 48 h after GA and uniconazole treatments for subsequent experiments.

Bioinformatics Analysis of HSTL

The HSTL (LOC_Os12g42250) gene sequence and protein sequence were downloaded from the NCBI website (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). Protein 3D structure of HSTL was predicted by SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/), then identified and analysed using a Simple Modular Architecture Research Tool (SMART, https://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/). The C2H2 zinc finger domain of HSTL and its homologs was compared by using NCBI BLAST (http://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi), and phylogenetic tree was constructed by MEGA software.

Construction of HSTL Phylogeny

Putative homologs of HST from different plants were identified using BLAST (http://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). The HST homologs tree was calculated using the program MEGA 11, with multiple gene sequence. The final phylogenetic tree was built via the neighbor-joining method. 1000 iterations were implemented in the Bootstrap test. The phylogenetic tree was shown using the web resource iTOL (https://itol.embl.de/).

RNA Isolation and RT-qPCR Analysis

Rice total RNA was extracted using Ultrapure RNA kit (CWBio, Beijing, China). The concentration and purity of RNA was determined by ultra-micro spectrophotometer. The extracted RNA was reverse transcribed by using PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit (Takara, Beijing, China) with gDNA Eraser. RT-qPCR was performed using a Rotor-Gene Q machine (parameters: 95 °C for 1 min, followed by 45 cycles of 94 °C for 10 s, 55 °C for 15 s, and 72 °C for 15 s). The gene encoding β-ACTIN (LOC_Os03g50885) was chosen as internal control. The primers used for RT-qPCR were shown in Table S2.

RNA-seq Analysis

Total RNA was isolated from the uppermost internodes of WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutant plants at the 7-leaf stage using the TRIzol reagent. The libraries were constructed using TruSeq Stranded mRNA LT Sample Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The 2 × 150bp paired-end sequencing (PE150) was performed on an illumina Novaseq™ 6000 (LC-Bio Technology CO., Ltd., Hangzhou, China). HISAT2 was used to map reads to the reference genome (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2). The mapped reads of each sample were assembled using StringTie (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie) with default parameters. After the final transcriptome was generated, StringTie and was used to estimate the expression levels of all transcripts. The differentially expressed mRNAs were selected with fold change > 2 or fold change < 0.5.

Microscopic Observation of Internode Cell Length

The uppermost internodes of WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutant plants at the 7-leaf stage were taken about 1 cm and preserved in 70% FAA fixative, and sent to Hangzhou Bolf Biotechnology Co. Ltd. for paraffin section preparation. The samples were stained with Safranine O-Fast Green Stain Kit solution and imaged under a light microscope. Cell lengths were counted using CaseViewer software.

Measurement of Water Loss Rate and Relative Water Content

Water loss was measured according to the method described by Tian (2004) with slight modifications. Detached leaves of 14 days rice plants were placed at 30 °C and were weighed at various time points. Water loss rate was expressed as the percentage of initial fresh weight at each time point. Relative water content was measured as described by Ascenzi and Gantt (1999) with minor modifications. Ten fully expanded leaves were detached from plants after various periods of NaCl treatment and immediately determined the fresh weight. They were immersed into ddH2O and soaked overnight. Water on the surface of the leaves was dried up with filter papers and then leaves were weighed immediately. Finally the leaves were dried at 80 °C for 4 h and the dry weight was measured.

Determination of H2O2, MDA, Proline, POD, SOD and CAT

For the analysis of the effect of HSTL and HST mutation on salt stress response, WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutant plants were cultured in nutrient solution for 14 d, then transferred to nutrient solution containing 120 mM NaCl. The levels of MDA, H2O2, proline, POD, SOD and CAT activities were assayed by colorimetric methods using commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China; Cat# A003-1 for MDA, A064-1 for H2O2, A107-1 for proline, A084-3 for 772 POD, A001-1 for SOD and A007-1 for CAT). The assays were performed according the manufacturer’s instructions.

Determination of Na+ Content

After 120 mM NaCl treatment for 7 d, roots of rice seedlings were first rinsed with ddH2O and dried under 105 °C for 2 h and then at 70 °C. Dried roots (0.1 g) and shoots (0.3 g) were digested with 6 mL HNO3 in the microwave digestion instrument. After digestion was completed, the solution was diluted to 50 mL and filtered. Na+ content was then determined using an atomic absorption spectrometer (AA-7000, Shimadzu, Tokyo, Japan).

Determination of GAs

1 g samples of the uppermost internodes of WT, hstl and hstl/hst mutant plants at the 7-leaf stage were collected and immediately freezed in liquid nitrogen, and then transported to Nanjing Ruiyuan Biotechnology Co. Ltd. The endogenous plant hormones GA1, GA3, GA4, and GA8 were determined by using an Agilent 1290 HPLC in tandem with a Qtrap6500 mass spectrometer of AB, and the detection was corrected by adding an internal standard substance to the extracted liquid (Davoudi et al. 2022).

Statistical Analysis

Each experiment is repeated three times. One-way ANOVA is used for data analysis, and Student’s t-test is used to compare the mean values. Different letters in the histogram indicate statistically different at p < 0.05 level. The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 27.0 software (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA).

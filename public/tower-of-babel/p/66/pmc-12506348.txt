Morphology, transcriptome and physiology analyses reveal adaptation mechanisms of Gymnocypris Przewalskii juveniles to saline-alkaline stresses
Authors: Zihan Gao, Le Wang, Yanrong Cui, Yixin Li, Luyang Yan, Yike Deng, Wengen Tian, Fulei Wei, Jian Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNaked carp (Gymnocypris przewalskii), a fish endemic to Qinghai Lake, China, has attracted extensive attention for its adaptation to salinity and alkalinity stress. However, the survival mechanism of G. przewalskii against saline-alkaline water environments remains elusive. In this study, we aimed to reveal the causes of saline-alkaline tolerance of G. przewalskii through the analysis of morphological, physiological and transcriptomic dimensions. The combination of growth performance and physiological indexes, and the morphology of gill tissues and transcriptomics strengthened the connection between this experiment, and further explored and analyzed the causes of salinity tolerance of G. przewalskii.ResultsAnalysis of growth indices revealed weight gain rate (WGR), specific growth rate (SGR), average daily gain (ADG), and specific growth rate of length (SGRL) were promoted under mild alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress. The alkalinity of A24.36 (7.80 mmol/L) and a saline-alkalinity of SA23.05 (3.84‰, 7.38 mmol/L) is most favorable for growth. All stresses caused shortened length of gill lamellae and between-lamellae distance. At the same time, stress led to a substantial increase in glycogen content and enhanced fructose-1,6-bisphosphate aldolase (FBA) activity in the liver and muscle. The transcriptome results in gills showed significant enrichment of biological processes such as manganese ion transport in alkaline stress, and reflection of stimuli, immunity, etc. in saline-alkaline stress. In gills, energy metabolism process, PI3K signaling pathway were significantly activated in both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, NF-kappa B signaling pathway was significantly activated downstream of PI3K in alkaline stress; mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway and Rap1 signaling pathway were significantly activated downstream of PI3K under saline-alkaline stress.ConclusionThis study unveiled the changes in growth indices, gill morphology, physiological parameters, and transcriptomics, which facilitated elucidation of the adaption mechanism of G. przewalskii juveniles to alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12054-2.Keywords: Gymnocypris Przewalskii, Stresses, Morphology, Physiology, Transcriptomics

Introduction

Qinghai Lake, situated in the northeastern region of the Tibetan Plateau, stands as the largest endorheic saline lake in China. The unique and diverse ecological environment has nurtured a distinctive array of biological resources in Qinghai Lake. Among its aquatic inhabitants, Gymnocypris przewalskii (G. przewalskii), belonging to the Schizothoracine family, reigns as the predominant fish species inhabiting both Lake Qinghai and its peripheral rivers. G. przewalskii, a migratory species native to Qinghai Lake [1], undertakes an annual journey, migrating to freshwater rivers to spawn in late May and returning to the lake in late July. Notably, these resilient fish have adapted to the harsh conditions of their habitat, displaying a remarkable tolerance for saline-alkaline environments [2, 3]. As an endemic fish species exclusive to Qinghai Lake, G. przewalskii plays a pivotal role in the intricate fish-bird-grassland ecosystem surrounding the lake. Thus, the protection and preservation of its germplasm resources bear immense ecological significance [4–6].

Saline-alkaline water is prevalent worldwide, and high carbonate alkalinity and elevated pH levels are critical factors that limit the survival and growth of cultured organisms in saline-alkaline environments [7]. Studies have indicated the importance of specific pH levels for the optimal growth of different species. For instance, research on juveniles of Piaractus mesopotamicus demonstrated that the highest growth performance occurred at a water pH of 8.5 under a monoalkaline gradient [8]. Similarly, grass carp juveniles showed significant variations in final weight, weight gain rate (WGR), and specific growth rate (SGR) at different alkalinity levels; specifically, the group exposed to 32 mmol/L concentrations exhibited significantly (P < 0.05) lower values compared to the group exposed to 18 mmol/L concentrations and extremely significantly (P < 0.01) lower values compared to the freshwater group [9]. Phoxinus lagowskii was found to adapt rapidly to saline-alkaline environmental stress [10]. Additionally, research by Song et al. [11] revealed that low saline-alkaline conditions led to increased ADG, WGR, and SGR in Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), whereas high saline-alkaline conditions suppressed these growth parameters. Furthermore, Huang et al. [12] found that naked carp survived within a salinity range of 0 ~ 15‰, but their survival rates (SRs) declined to varying degrees at 20‰ and 25‰ salinity levels, indicating a salinity tolerance threshold of 0 ~ 15‰. However, there is currently a knowledge gap regarding the growth of G. przewalskii under alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress.

Water salinity and alkalinity have been recognized to affect gill tissue morphology [13], energy balance [14], and enzyme activity [15, 16]. The gill, a pivotal osmoregulatory organ in fish, promptly responds to changes in the external environment and fulfills a range of vital functions, including maintaining the internal and external osmotic balance and secretion and absorption of ions [17–19]. Gill morphology is known to change in response to variations in the external aquatic environment [20]. Zhang et al. [21] observed that G. przewalskii exhibited numerous long and dense gill rakers, whereas G. p. ganzihonensis displayed few, short, and sparse gill rakers. No significant differences in the distance between gill filaments and distance between gill lamellae (DBL) were noted between the two subspecies; however, the gill filaments of G. przewalskii were reported to be longer than those of G. p. ganzihonensis. Matey et al. [22] reported a lower degree of morphological changes in the gills of G. przewalskii compared to those observed in the gills of goldfish and carp when assessed under scanning electron microscopy following hypoxia treatment. In addition to the morphology, physiological and molecular responses are also important. Glycogen plays a crucial role as the primary storage form of sugar in the body, facilitating the oxidative breakdown of glucose, which serves as a vital means of supplying energy [23]. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate aldolase (FBA), also referred to as aldolase, is a pivotal enzyme within the metabolic processes of living organisms [24]. It catalyzes the reversible cleavage of fructose-1,6-bisphosphate into dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, a key reaction presents in both the glycolysis/gluconeogenesis pathway and pentose phosphate cycle pathway [25]. This reaction represents a critical step in the pathways governing carbon and sugar metabolism in organisms, directly impacting the accumulation of energy. Lv et al. [26] revealed that FBA is involved in responding to and coping with a range of abiotic stresses.

Numerous studies have harnessed molecular techniques to delve into the biology of G. przewalskii. Tong et al. [27] conducted a comparative genomics analysis on G. przewalskii and identified candidate genes contributing to its adaptation to saline and alkaline conditions. Li et al. [28] focused on microRNAs potentially involved in scale biomineralization in G. przewalskii; the obtained microRNAs hold the potential to serve as valuable candidates for unraveling the intricate molecular mechanisms underlying scale biomineralization in fish species. The Elovl gene family of G. przewalskii has been studied, which helps to better understand the Elovl gene in fish and its role in cold adaptation [29]. In addition, Huang et al. [12] explored how NKA expression varies in different tissues of G. przewalskii under various salinities. However, responses of G. przewalskii to alkalinity and saline-alkalinity gradient environments remain scarcely studied.

In this study, we utilized juveniles of G. przewalskii as our experimental subjects. These juveniles underwent gradient concentration culture in two distinct environments: alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress. Firstly, we conducted a thorough analysis, comparing the growth performance of G. przewalskii juveniles under these conditions to determine the optimal concentrations of alkalinity and saline-alkalinity. Then, we delved into the effects of these environmental gradients on gill tissue morphology, glycogen content, and FBA enzyme activity. In addition, alkaline tolerance and saline-alkaline tolerance in juvenile fish were further explored and analyzed by gill tissue transcriptomics. The findings of this study will enrich our foundational knowledge of G. przewalskii and offer valuable insights for the development and cultivation of freshwater fish in saline-alkaline waters.

Impact of alkaline or saline-alkaline stress on gill in G. przewalskii juveniles

Water alkalinity exerted differential effects on gill tissue morphology in juvenile fish, as visually represented in the provided histological sections (Fig. 2). In the CK group, gill filaments displayed a straight configuration, with gill lamellae exhibiting a regular and parallel arrangement, presenting a clear structure. The columnar cells (PCs) were primarily cylindrical, while flat epithelial cells (PVCs) adhered to the outer side of the gill lamellae, appearing mostly flattened. Chlorine-secreting cells (CCs) were uniformly distributed at the base of the gill lamellae (Fig. 2-a). The tissue morphology of the gills begins to change of juvenile fish with alkaline stress. In the A30 group, the gill lamellae exhibited a reduction in length, a curved structure, and a decrease in the number of PCs, in comparison to the control group (Fig. 2-b). The A45 and A60 groups displayed thickening of PVCs in the gill lamellae, accompanied by the presence of curvature and irregularity in the gill lamellae (Fig. 2-c, d). In the A75 group, some PVCs became detached, the bases of the gill lamellae thickened, and the number and size of CCs increased (Fig. 2-e). The A90 group exhibited severe gill lamella breakage, signs of degeneration, nearly complete detachment of PVCs, a drastic reduction in the number of CCs, and extensive tissue damage (Fig. 2-f).

Effects of different alkaline stress on gill histomorphology of G. przewalskii juveniles. a CK, b A30, c A45, d A60, e A75, f A90. GF, gill filament; SF, gill lobule; PVC, flat epithelial cell; BC, red blood cell; CC, chlorine-secreting cell; PC, columnar cell

Under different saline-alkaline stresses, the gill tissue morphology was relatively intact in the CK group, like that observed in the alkaline groups (Fig. 3-a). In the SA30 group, the gill lamellae exhibited significant thickening and curvature, along with an increased number of cells at the base of the gill lamellae (Fig. 3-b). Compared to the SA30 group, the SA45 and SA60 groups showed increased gill lamella thickness and a reduced number of cells at the base of the gill lamellae (Fig. 3-c, d). In the SA75 group, increased curvature irregularity in the gill lamellae was noted, along with vacuolization at the base of the gill lamellae and detachment of the PVCs from the gill lamellae (Fig. 3-e). In the SA90 group, there was extensive shedding of PVCs from the gill lamellae, leading to their absence in certain areas. Additionally, the base of the gill lamellae thickened, and there was a marked increase in the number of CCs filling the gill lamellae interstices (Fig. 3-f). Gill tissue morphology appeared more intact in the saline-alkalinity groups in comparison to the alkalinity groups.

The WL exhibited a trend of first increasing and then decreasing with alkaline stress, with maximum and minimum widths observed in the A75 and A90 groups, respectively. DBL displayed a gradual decreasing trend with alkaline stress, it was significantly lower in the A90 group (P < 0.05). Under the saline-alkaline stress, the LL of G. przewalskii juveniles exhibited a gradually decreasing trend, with the CK group displaying a significantly greater gill lamellar length compared to that observed in other saline-alkaline groups (P < 0.05), followed by the SA30 group. Moreover, the DBL tended to decrease with saline-alkaline stress, with the SA90 group displaying the smallest spacing, significantly smaller than that observed in other groups (P < 0.05).

Impact of alkaline or saline-alkaline stress on glycogen and FBA activities in G. przewalskii juveniles

The liver glycogen (LG) and muscle glycogen (MG) contents in juvenile fish were significantly elevated under alkaline stress (P < 0.05), with LG consistently surpassing MG across all treatment groups (Fig. 4). Notably, LG exhibited a marked increase at moderate alkalinity levels, with the A45 group recording the highest LG content (P < 0.05), trailed by the A30 and A75 groups. Conversely, the A90 group demonstrated the lowest LG content among the alkaline stress, but still significantly higher than the CK group (P < 0.05). Similarly, MG content was significantly augmented in all alkaline stress groups (P < 0.05), with the A60 group achieving the highest levels, followed by the A90 group.

Effects of different alkalinity and saline-alkalinity concentrations on glycogen content in G. przewalskii juveniles. Different lowercase letters in the figure indicate significant differences (P < 0.05), while the same indicate insignificant differences (P > 0.05)

Under saline-alkaline stress, LG content exhibited a significant increase compared to the CK group (P < 0.05), initially rising and then declining, with a notable upturn observed in the SA90 group. The SA60 group registered the highest LG content, while the SA75 group displayed significantly lower glycogen contents than other saline-alkaline groups. Compared to the CK group, all saline-alkaline groups displayed higher MG contents; however, the increase was insignificant in the SA45 and SA60 groups (P > 0.05). The SA75 group had the highest MG content (P < 0.05), followed by the SA30 and SA90 groups. In addition, the results indicated that under saline-alkalinity treatment, LG content was higher than MG content.

Figure 5 shows that FBA activity was significantly increased (P < 0.05) under various alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress. First, FBA activity in both liver and muscle displayed a pattern of increase followed by a decrease with increasing alkalinity. Liver FBA activity was highest among all groups in the A30 group, while muscle FBA activity reached its peak in the A75 group. In both tissues, FBA activity in the alkaline stress groups was significantly higher than that in the CK group (P < 0.05). In all cases of the saline-alkaline stress, there was an initial increase followed by a decrease in FBA activity. The SA30 group had the highest FBA activity in both liver and muscle, and it was significantly higher than that observed in the other saline-alkaline groups (P < 0.05).

Transcriptomic response of G. przewalskii juveniles’ gills to alkaline or saline-alkaline stress

To characterize the transcriptomic response of G. przewalskii juveniles’ gills to alkaline changes and saline-alkaline changes, gill RNA-seq libraries were analyzed under five gradients, respectively. Firstly, compared to the CK group, a total of 7,436 (5,412 up and 2,024 down) differentially expressed genes (DEGs, |log2FC|≥ 1.5 and FDR < 0.05) were identified in the A45 group, while the number is 6,071 (4,539 up and 1,532 down), 8,767 (7,284 up and 1,483 down), and 8,688 (7,162 up and 1,526 down) in the A90, SA45, and SA90, respectively (Fig. 6-A). A Venn diagram was used to illustrate the DEGs identified in the 4 pairwise comparisons. The results showed that compared to the CK group, a total of 15,002 DEGs were observed under the 4 treatments. Furthermore, 2,239 DEGs were common among the 4 groups (Fig. 6-B). The trend analysis of the common DEGs showed that 1801 genes were significantly enriched in the up-regulated trend (P < 0.05) under alkaline changes; a total of 223 genes were significantly enriched in the down-regulated trend (P < 0.05). Two trend blocks were significantly enriched in up-regulated genes totaling 1903 (P < 0.05) and one trend block was significantly enriched in expression down-regulated genes totaling 226 (P < 0.05) under saline-alkaline change (Fig. 6-C).

The upregulated DEGs were consistently enriched in GO terms in both alkalinity and saline-alkalinity variations. Biological processes that were significantly enriched were cellular protein modification process, protein modification process, macromolecule modification, protein phosphorylation, regulation of small GTPase mediated signal transduction, phosphate-containing compound metabolic process, phosphorus metabolic process, and so on. Molecular function is significantly enriched in protein binding, ion binding, protein tyrosine kinase activity, metal ion binding, transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase activity, transmembrane receptor protein kinase activity, and so on. Cellular component was significantly enriched in cell surface, intrinsic component of plasma membrane, integral component of plasma membrane, plasma membrane, cell periphery, etc. (Fig. 6-D). KEGG Pathway annotation of significantly up-regulated DEGs revealed that DEGs were successfully enriched to 295 signaling pathways under alkaline changes and 297 signaling pathways under saline-alkaline changes. The 30 most significant pathways were selected and analyzed, including focal adhesion, ECM-receptor interaction, adherens junction, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, axon regeneration, lysosome, Rap1 signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, etc. (Fig. 6-E).

The results of the Venn diagram analysis showed that 1479 DEGs varied only in alkaline stress and 3240 DEGs were unique to saline-alkaline stress compared to the CK group (Fig. 6-B). Trend analysis of unique DEGs under alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, respectively. It was showed that 882 genes were significantly enriched in an upward trend (P < 0.05) and 503 genes were significantly enriched in a downward trend (P < 0.05) under alkaline stress. Under saline-alkaline stress, 2799 genes were significantly enriched in an upward trend (P < 0.05); 280 genes were significantly enriched in a downward trend (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7-A).

To explore the underlying biological meanings, the significantly up-regulated DEGs were subjected to GO enrichment analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis. GO enrichment analysis showed that under alkaline stress, biological process was significantly enriched in terms like cellular transition metal ion homeostasis, transition metal ion homeostasis, proteolysis, manganese ion transport, and manganese ion transmembrane transport. Molecular function is mainly related to catalytic activity, hydrolase activity, FAD binding, peptidase activity, acting on L-amino acid peptides, and peptidase activity. Cellular component analysis showed that these up-regulated DEGs were enriched in terms such as collagen trimer, collagen type Iv trimer, cell envelope Sec protein transport complex, activin responsive factor complex, and inhibitory synapse. In the process of saline-alkaline stress, biological process is significantly enriched in biological regulation, regulation of biological process, regulation of response to stimulus, regulation of metabolic process, and regulation of cellular process. Molecular function is significantly enriched in binding, protein binding, ion binding, guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity, and drug binding. Cellular component was significantly enriched mainly for cell part, cell, integrin complex, protein complex involved in cell adhesion, and plasma membrane receptor complex (Fig. 7-B).

KEGG enrichment analysis showed that cortisol synthesis and secretion, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, metabolic pathways, endocytosis, toxoplasmosis, dilated cardiomyopathy, TGF-beta signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, and AMPK signaling pathway were significantly enriched during alkaline stresses (Fig. 7-C). Notch signaling pathway, focal adhesion, protein digestion and absorption, parathyroid hormone synthesis, secretion and action, growth hormone synthesis, secretion and action, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, glucagon signaling pathway, thyroid hormone signaling pathway, Th17 cell differentiation, and hippo signaling pathway-multiple species were significantly enriched in KEGG during the saline-alkaline stresses (Fig. 7-C).

For qRT-PCR validation of relevant DEGs in the pathway, we selected 7 DEGs that were significantly expressed under both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, including egfr, csf3r, itga1, pik2ca, akt3, bcl2, and sgk2. Meanwhile, 5 DEGs that were significantly expressed under saline-alkaline stress, including tlr1, syk, lpar1-b, pkn1, and prkca, were selected for validation, as well as 2 DEGs (bcl2l1 and pkn2) significantly expressed under alkaline stress. Therefore, a total of 14 genes were amplified by qRT-PCR and the melting curve analysis showed that only one single product was detected from each primer set. The results were consistent with the RNA-Seq results (Fig. 7-D).

Mining important signaling pathways from the transcriptome

Transcriptomic data suggest that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway plays an important role in alkalinity tolerance and saline-alkalinity tolerance in gill tissues of G. przewalskii juveniles. Under alkaline stresses and saline-alkaline stresses, ECM-receptor interaction, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, and GF/RTK signaling pathway, together upregulated the acceptance of extracellular signaling to PI3K. And under saline-alkaline stress, there are three more pathways on toll-like receptor signaling pathway, b cell receptor signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, among them chemokine signaling pathway is signaling to PI3K through Class IB. Activation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway activates 2 downstream signaling pathways by phosphorylation, the NF-kappa B signaling pathway activity is significantly elevated under alkaline stress, and CREB-DNA-Bcl2 is activated under both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress. In addition, PI3K can affect cellular processes by activating SGK, PNK and PCKs through phosphorylation of PDK1, where PDK1-SGK and PDK1-PNK were activated by both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, and the mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway and Rap1 signaling pathway were activated under saline-alkaline stress (Fig. 8).

Discussion

Morphological adaptations of G. przewalskii juveniles to different alkaline or saline-alkaline stress

Alkalinity is one of the key parameters of water quality [30]. Studies on the toxicity and tolerance of alkalinity in fish have demonstrated that an appropriate alkalinity level is beneficial for promoting feeding, growth, and bait utilization. However, when alkalinity exceeds the optimal range, it can significantly affect fish growth [31, 32]. In this study, the SR of juvenile G. przewalskii remained around 90.00% in the alkaline treatment groups (CK ~ A75), with no significant difference among the treatment groups. However, SR was significantly lower in the A90 group, indicating that high alkaline environments pose a threat to fish survival. The growth parameters of juvenile fish performed well within the alkalinity range of 0 to A45, with the optimal alkalinity concentration being A24.36 (7.80 mmol/L). However, alkalinity concentrations exceeding A45 had an adverse effect on the growth performance of juvenile fish, resulting in growth inhibition. These findings are consistent with those of previous studies involving various aquatic organisms [33–35].

Saline-alkaline water is prevalent worldwide. High saline-alkalinity and elevated pH values are the primary factors that limit the survival and growth of cultured aquatic organisms [36]. This study found that with increasing levels of saline-alkaline exposure, the SR of juvenile fish gradually decreased, reaching an extremely low SR in the SA90 group. These findings suggest that juvenile G. przewalskii are not well-suited to survive in highly saline-alkaline environments. Within the saline-alkalinity concentration range of 0 to SA45, the growth parameters of juvenile G. przewalskii significantly improved, with the optimal saline-alkalinity concentration being SA23.05 (3.84‰, 7.38 mmol/L). However, when the saline-alkalinity concentration exceeded this threshold (≥ 45%), the growth of juvenile fish was significantly inhibited, leading to a significant reduction in all growth parameters. These results are consistent the findings of a previous study on Oreochromis niloticus [11].

Gills are important osmoregulatory organs in fish, directly exposed to the external environment, and play a key role in maintaining osmotic balance in fish. Previous studies have shown that gill tissues in fish respond significantly to changing environmental conditions [37]. In this study, gill tissues remained relatively intact within the alkalinity range of 0 to A75. However, in the A90 group, we observed considerable detachment of the PVCs from the gill lamellae, signifying substantial damage to the gill tissues. These findings are consistent with a previous study involving freshwater species (Leuciscus waleckii), which experienced severe damage to gill lamellae in alkaline environments [38]. This suggests that juvenile naked carp (G. przewalskii) can effectively adapt to alkalinity concentration changes within the range of 0 ~ A75, underscoring their robust alkalinity adaptability. However, the significant damage to gill tissues and the substantial decrease in juvenile survival rates in the A90 group indicate that G. przewalskii juveniles cannot withstand prolonged exposure to highly alkaline water environments (≥ 45%). In this study, we noted that varying alkalinity concentrations significantly decreased the LL. This suggests that juvenile fish may employ a coping mechanism of reducing the contact area of gill lamellae in response to environmental stress. Furthermore, we observed an increase followed by a decline in the WL. We hypothesized that this pattern may reflect an initial boost in the osmotic capacity of juvenile fish, which is subsequently suppressed as the alkalinity concentrations increased. Furthermore, DBL exhibited significant changes when the concentration of alkalinity exceeded A60, signifying that juveniles can adapt to alkalinity environments by modulating the tightness of gill lamellae. These findings are consistent with those of Maina [39], which highlighted the well-adapted gills of Oreochromis alcalicus grahami in alkaline water. These gills featured numerous relatively long filaments with high lamellar density, which can optimize gas exchange under such conditions.

Our investigation revealed that the gill tissue morphology of juvenile fish remained relatively more intact across all saline-alkaline stresses than across alkaline stresses. Notably, it was more intact at the highest saline-alkalinity concentrations than at the highest alkalinity concentrations. We hypothesized that salinity in the water column acts as a buffer against alkalinity-induced stress, thereby preserving gill tissue morphology in saline-alkaline environments in contrast to alkaline environments. However, the SA90 group displayed significant shedding of PVCs from gill lamellae and a notable accumulation of basal CCs, indicating gill tissue damage. These findings are consistent with those observed in other fish species, as illustrated by Galat et al. [13] Considering the notably low SR of juveniles in the SA90 group, it is presumed that these juveniles expend a large amount of energy through their gill tissues to cope with external environmental pressures. This allocation of energy towards environmental adaptation likely leaves less energy for growth and development, a phenomenon in line with the dynamic balance of bioenergy [40]. Across all alkalinity and saline-alkalinity groups, we observed consistent trends of significantly decreased gill lamellar length, increased gill lamellar width, and reduced DBL. Given the vital role of gills as osmotic organs [41], we hypothesize that these morphological changes represent a coping strategy employed by juvenile fish to minimize direct exposure to the external environment while concurrently narrowing down the DBL to maintain osmotic balance.

Physiological adaptations of G. przewalskii juveniles to alkaline or saline-alkaline stress

Glycogen, as a protective and defensive mechanism in organisms, functions through various forms and roles [42]. Therefore, we hypothesize that glycogen plays a key role in the response of fish to different environmental stresses. In this study, we observed an increase in glycogen content in the liver and muscle tissues of fish under various treatment conditions. These findings lead us to speculate that the accumulation of glycogen may be associated with the long-term adaptation of juvenile fish to alkaline and saline-alkaline environments. Thus, it can be inferred that maintaining adequate glycogen reserves in the liver and muscle is a strategy employed by juvenile fish to adapt to prolonged exposure to alkaline and saline-alkaline conditions. Similar trends in glycogen regulation have been observed in yeast, other fish, and crustaceans [43, 44]. Additionally, our study found that compared to the control group, the activity of FBA was significantly elevated in fish exposed to alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, indicating that the glucose metabolism pathway in these fish were activated. FBA plays a crucial role in glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, and the pentose phosphate pathway [45, 46]. The increased FBA activity in fish observed in this experiment suggests enhanced glycolytic energy supply, glucose synthesis via gluconeogenesis for glycogen storage, and the production of reducing power through the PPP pathway. In conjunction with the trend of increased glycogen, we speculate that energy dynamic processes are amplified in juvenile fish under alkaline and saline-alkaline water environments. This acceleration of energy consumption and storage represents one of the adaptive strategies employed by fish in response to different stress environments. Therefore, we hypothesized that under both low alkaline and saline-alkaline environments (< A45 or < SA45), fish glucose metabolism is dominated by gluconeogenesis, which synthesizes glycogen and promotes growth and development; under both high alkaline and saline-alkaline environments (> A45 or > SA45), glucose metabolism is dominated by glycolysis, and a large amount of energy is used to cope with the environmental stresses, whereas the growth and development of the fish are significantly slowed down. Moreover, the consistently higher levels of LG compared to MG further highlight the importance of the liver in providing energy and synthesizing essential substances for fish [47, 48].

Transcriptomic analysis of gill tissue

The transcriptomic analysis revealed that under alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, a total of 2239 DEGs were identified. The analysis of these significantly upregulated DEGs showed that the results of GO enrichment were almost identical under both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress. We found that more energy metabolism-related pathways were enriched in biological processes, such as protein phosphorylation, regulation of small GTPase mediated signal transduction, phosphate-containing compound metabolic process, phosphorus metabolic process, etc. This also suggests that energy metabolism will be activated to promote fish adaptation to the environment in the face of saline and alkaline stress [49, 50]. Molecular functions, such as ion binding, protein tyrosine kinase activity, etc., indicate that ion homeostasis and translocation of related substances in the gill become frequent in response to saline and alkaline stresses [51, 52], and these processes also require energy for their maintenance [10]. Changes in cellular components in terms of cell surface, plasma membrane, cell periphery, etc., also reflect the possibility of drastic changes in the structure and composition of cell membranes of gill tissues in response to saline and alkaline stresses. KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway [53], focal adhesion, ECM-receptor interaction, adherens junction, lysosome, Rap1 signaling pathway, etc., were very important in response to saline and alkaline stresses, which suggests that saline and alkaline stresses can fully activate gill tissue-related signaling pathways to achieve homeostasis in fish.

GO enrichment analysis of the specific upregulated DEGs under alkaline stress showed that the biological process was significantly enriched in the processes of manganese (Mn) ion transport and manganese ion transmembrane transport, among others; molecular function was significantly enriched in catalytic activity, hydrolase activity, etc.; cellular component was significantly enriched in collagen trimer, cell envelope Sec protein transport complex, etc. Mn as an essential micronutrient plays a vital role in bone growth, as an antioxidant and a cofactor for several enzymes [54], it is hypothesized that alkaline stress affects the growth and development of gill tissues and manganese ion transport under alkalinity may aid in the growth of gill tissues to maintain their essential functions. The GO enrichment analysis of the upregulated DEGs under saline-alkaline stress showed that the biological process was significantly enriched in biological regulation, regulation of response to stimulus; molecular function was significantly enriched in ion binding, guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity; cellular component was significantly enriched in integrin complex, protein complex involved in cell adhesion. Saline-alkaline stress disrupts fish biorhythms [55] and affects fish immunity [56], thus various cell types in the gills are involved in different responses to maintain homeostasis. Studies have shown that the morphological changes in gill tissue and the alterations in the transcriptome are stress responses to environmental pressures [57, 58]. In the study, the changes in the structure and composition of the cell membrane in gill tissue may be associated with the upregulation of genes related to ion transport and energy metabolism [59]. In our study, the manganes ion transport and manganese ion transmembrane transport were significantly enriched under alkaline stress. This indicates that under alkaline stress, gill tissues may maintain the integrity of their structure and function by increasing the uptake and transport of manganese ions. Mn, as cofactors for many enzymes, may be involved in the antioxidant defense mechanisms of gill tissues, thereby mitigating the damage of alkaline stress to gill cells [60]. In addition, in our study, the integrin complex, protein complexes involved in cell adhesion, and plasma membrane receptor complexes were significantly enriched under saline-alkaline stress. This indicates that under saline-alkaline stress, pathways related to cell adhesion and the extracellular matrix in gill tissues are activated, which may reflect the response of gill tissues in terms of intercellular connections and remodeling of the extracellular matrix [61, 62]. This remodeling may help gill tissue maintain its structural integrity while promoting efficient exchange of ions and oxygen.

In summary, morphology and transcriptomics reveal the complex mechanisms by which G. przewalskii juvenile respond to saline-alkaline stresses. These response mechanisms not only involve the regulation of energy metabolism and ion homeostasis but may also maintain the morphology and function of gill tissue through changes in cell membrane structure and remodeling of the extracellular matrix. Future research can further explore the direct links between morphological changes in gill tissue and transcriptomic data to better understand the adaptive mechanisms of fish under saline-alkaline stresses.

KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the pathways significantly enriched during alkaline stress were mainly glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, TGF-beta signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway. The pathways that were significantly enriched during the saline-alkaline change were mainly the Notch signaling pathway, focal adhesion, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, parathyroid hormone synthesis, secretion and action, and thyroid hormone signaling pathway. Both alkaline and saline-alkaline specific DEGs were enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, suggesting that the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is important in saline-alkaline stress in fish.

Analysis of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway in gill

The PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, a phosphatidylinositol-related pathway, regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and response to extracellular signals, playing a crucial role in cell survival [63]. Under saline-alkaline stress, both shad (Tenualosa ilisha) and black tiger shrimp Penaeus monodon have demonstrated the significant role of PI3K in antioxidant defense [64, 65]. In this study, both alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress significantly activated the GF-RTK, ECM-receptor interaction, and JAK-STAT signaling pathways to signal for PI3K. However, under saline-alkaline stress, additional pathways such as the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, B-cell receptor signaling pathway, and chemokine signaling pathway also signaled for PI3K. The PI3K/AKT-CREB, PI3K/PDK1-SGK, and PI3K/PDK1-PNK are common signaling pathways activated under both conditions. The PI3K/AKT phosphorylation-activated NF-kappa B signaling pathway is specific to alkaline stress, while the PI3K/PDK1-PCKs pathway, which can transmit signals to activate the mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and Rap1 signaling pathway, is specific to saline-alkaline stress. This suggests that under alkaline conditions, PI3K in gill tissue can utilize the NF-kappa B signaling pathway to regulate cellular immunity, inflammation, and cell survival. In contrast, under saline-alkaline conditions, PI3K/PDK1 in gill tissue can activate downstream mTOR, MAPK, and Rap1 signaling pathways involved in cell proliferation, differentiation, and migration, cell adhesion, cell-cell junction formation, cell polarity, and cytoskeletal organization, cell metabolism, and survival. The results indicate that the PI3K signaling pathway is more comprehensively activated under saline-alkaline stress, both in terms of extracellular signal acceptance and downstream pathway transmission, showing a stronger effect of saline-alkaline stress than alkaline stress.

Additionally, the transcriptome revealed that the number of DEGs consistently expressed under saline-alkaline conditions was 5,479, which was much larger than the 3,718 in the alkaline environment. These DEGs were mainly enriched in pathways such as response to stimulus, metabolic processes, and immune processes, which may have a greater impact on fish survival, resulting in a much lower survival rate of juvenile fish under saline-alkaline stress than under alkaline stress. This finding contrasts with the stress results of adult G. przewalskii in our culture, which were better adapted to saline-alkaline environments and had higher mortality in alkaline environments. This phenomenon requires further exploration.

Research limitations and future directions

Although this study provides a comprehensive analysis of the adaptive mechanisms of G. przewalskii juveniles to alkaline stress and saline-alkaline stress, some limitations exist. First, the sample size in the experimental design was limited, which may affect the statistical significance of the results. Second, while the transcriptomic analysis revealed potential molecular mechanisms, the functions of specific genes need further confirmation via gene knockout or overexpression experiments. Additionally, this study focused mainly on gill tissue; future research could extend to other important tissues, such as the liver and muscle, to fully understand the fish’s adaptive mechanisms in saline-alkaline environments.

Future research directions could include: (1) Using gene-editing techniques (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9) to verify the functions of specific genes in the salt-alkaline tolerance of G. przewalskii; (2) Validating the findings of this study under a broader range of environmental conditions to assess their universality in different geographical regions; (3) Exploring the specific mechanisms of glycogen metabolism and energy balance in the adaptation of fish to saline-alkaline environments.

Conclusion

In this study, we established the optimal alkalinity and saline-alkalinity concentrations of G. przewalskii juveniles being A24.36 (7.80 mmol/L) and SA23.05 (3.84‰, 7.38 mmol/L), respectively. At lower alkalinity and saline-alkalinity (< A45 or < SA45), glucose metabolism stores energetic substances to promote growth and development, and at higher alkalinity and saline-alkalinity (> A45 or > SA45), glucose metabolism releases energetic substances to adapt to the stressful environment, which in turn inhibits growth and development. Notably, juvenile fish exhibited more robust growth when exposed to optimal saline-alkalinity conditions compared to optimal alkalinity conditions. Under SA90, survival was extremely low, indicating that SA90 is extremely pernicious to the fish. The effect of alkalinity environments on gill tissues was more pronounced than that of saline-alkalinity environments, especially evident at the highest alkalinity concentration set in this experiment, so manganese ions were needed to help gill tissue growth and development to maintain its normal function. Gill tissues can respond to alkalinity and saline-alkalinity stress through PI3K signaling pathway. Under alkalinity stress, PI3K may activate the downstream NF-kappa B signaling pathway through AKT; under saline-alkalinity stress, PI3K may activate the downstream mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway and Rap1 signaling pathway through PDK1.

This study provides a more comprehensive analysis of how G. przewalskii juvenile adapt to alkalinity and saline-alkalinity environments, which offers valuable insights into why they adapt to and live with Qinghai Lake in the long term, and lays the foundation for a comprehensive understanding of G. przewalskii.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All procedures related to sampling, which encompassed the handling and treatment of animals, were approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Qinghai University. All experiments were performed in accordance with the guidelines set forth by the committee.

Preparation of Gymnocypris przewalskii juveniles

One years’ old naked carp juveniles, with an average weight of 3.47 ± 0.06 g, were obtained from the Naked Carps Rescue and Rehabilitation Center of Qinghai Lake. All fish were then transported to the laboratory for subsequent acclimation and stress treatments. Before treatment, the fish were first kept in freshwater and fed twice daily at 09:00 and 16:00 with a special puffed compound feed, the main ingredients of the feed include imported fish meal, soybean meal, wheat flour, corn flour, refined fish oil, and compound vitaminsa. After a 14-day pretreatment cultivation period, 330 fish were divided into three major groups, namely the alkaline (A) group, the saline-alkaline (SA) group, and the control (CK) group, for stress treatments. The saline-alkalinity of the Qinghai Lake, with a salinity of 16‰ and alkalinity of 32 mmol/L, was used as the 100% level for the SA group. For the alkalinity group, an alkalinity of 32 mmol/L was set as the 100% level. To thoroughly investigate the impact of alkaline or saline-alkaline, both the A group and the SA group were further subdivided into five concentration gradient treatments. This resulted in five A subgroups, namely 30% A group (A30), 45% A group (A45), 60% A group (A60), 75% A group (A75), and 90% A group (A90), as well as five SA subgroups: SA30 (30% SA group), SA45 (45% SA group), SA60 (60% SA group), SA75 (75% SA group), and SA90 (90% SA group). The freshwater treatment without any stress was used as the control group (CK). Each group consisted of three biological replicates, with each replicate containing 10 fish.

To adjust the stress level, the alkalinity and saline-alkalinity was gradually increased by 5% each day until the desired level of stress was achieved. For the adjustment of alkalinity, sodium carbonate (Na2CO3) and sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) were added to freshwater at a molar ratio of 9:1, as specified in Table S1. Saline-alkalinity adjustment was conducted in a similar manner, with sea salt used as the ingredient for salt adjustment. Once the required stress level was reached, each group underwent a 69-day culture period to observe the effects of the stress treatments. Dissolved oxygen (DO) level was 6.92 ± 0.56 mg/L in all groups during the experiment. After stress treatment, individuals were randomly selected from each treatment group to evaluate fish growth performance, observe stress-induced gill histological changes, perform transcriptome sequencing of the gill tissue, and observe changes in physiological indicators. The experimental design and a brief flowchart of this study are shown in Fig. 9.

SA30, SA45, SA60, SA75 and SA90 denote saline-alkalinity concentrations of 5‰ saline and 9.60 mmol/L alkaline, 7.5‰ saline and 14.4 mmol/L alkaline, 10‰ saline and 19.2 mmol/L alkaline, 12.5‰ saline and 24.0 mmol/L alkaline and 15‰ saline and 28.8 mmol/L alkaline, respectively

Evaluation of growth performance

Before initiating the stress treatments, the body weight and length of the fish were measured. At the conclusion of the 69-day stressing period, three randomly-selected fish from each treatment were selected for re-measurement of their body weight and length, each stress group was subjected to three random determination experiments. Using these two indices, a series of parameters were computed to assess the growth performance of G. przewalskii juveniles. Histological observation of gill tissues

The gill tissues were fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde fixative 24 h (Biosharp; China), with three parallel replicates. The tissue was then rinsed with 0.9% sodium chloride (Shandong Kelun Pharmaceutical Co.; China) to remove the fixative, and subjected to a gradient ethanol dehydration process (70% ethanol for 40 min; 80% ethanol for 40 min; 90% ethanol for 40 min; 95% ethanol for 40 min; 100% ethanol for 40 min; 100% ethanol for 40 min) (Solarbio; China). Afterwards, the specimen was embedded in paraffin, thinly sliced, and then underwent the staining procedure utilizing mayer hematoxylin (Solarbio; China) and eosin (Solarbio; China). The histomorphological observations were conducted under a light microscope.

Analysis of glycogen content and FBA activity in liver and muscle tissues

For the quantification of glycogen content and FBA activity, liver and muscle tissues were collected and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, serving as the materials for determination. 100 mg of the liver or muscle sample was carefully excised and pulverized with liquid nitrogen. Subsequently, the glycogen content and FBA activity were determined using enzyme immunoassay. Glycogen can be dehydrated under the action of concentrated sulfuric acid to form glyceraldehyde derivatives, which then interact with anthrone to form blue compounds, which are colorimetrically quantified with the standard glucose solution treated in the same way. FBA is detected by adding specimen, standard, and HRP-labeled detection antibody sequentially into coated microtiter wells pre-coated with FBA antibody, after warming and thorough washing. The color is developed with the substrate TMB, which is converted to blue by peroxidase and to a final yellow by acid. The shade of color is positively correlated with the FBA in the sample. The detailed procedures for these assays were carried out in accordance with the instructions provided in the manuals for Fish Glycogen ELISA Kit and Fish Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate Aldolase (FBAase) ELISA Kit (manufactured by Jiangsu Enzyme Immunity Industry Co.).

Transcriptome sequencing of the gill tissue and mining of important signaling pathways

The gill tissue was finely ground with liquid nitrogen, and then total RNA of the homogenate was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (Ambion, USA). The quality of the total RNA was initially assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA). Subsequently, agarose gel electrophoresis (1%) was conducted to further validate the integrity of the extracted total RNA. The mRNA was enriched with oligo(dT) beads, fragmented, and subsequently reverse transcribed using a cDNA synthesis kit (Invitrogen, CA). The sequencing library was constructed using the TruSeq RNA Library Prep Kit (v2), strictly adhering to the manufacturer’s protocol. The transcriptome sequencing was carried out by Guangzhou GeneDeNovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. utilizing the NovaSeq 6000 platform.

The raw data underwent an initial filtration process using fastp [66] to eliminate low-quality reads and sequencing adaptors. Then, the clean reads were aligned to the G. przewalskii reference genome (https://db.cngb.org/search/organism/75348/) using HISAT2 to build the genome index [67]. Subsequently, the reads were mapped to the genome with StringTie (v1.3.1) for transcript assembly and quantification of gene expression. Fragments per kilobase of transcript sequence per million base pairs sequenced (FPKM) was calculated and used as the metric for gene expression level. Using the DESeq2 software (v1.42.0), differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified with a filtering threshold of p-adjust < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 2. The DEGs were then subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis by using Goatools and KOBAS, respectively [68, 69].

Validation of differentially expressed genes by qPCR

The expressions of 14 selected genes involved in stress response in the gill tissue were validated using qPCR, with primers (Table S2) designed through the utilization of Primer 3 software (https://bioinfo.ut.ee/primer3-0.4.0/). The total RNA was reversely transcribed to into cDNA with the PrimeScript™ II 1 st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TaKaRa, Japan), following instructions of the manufacturer. EF-1α was selected and used as the internal references for relative quantification [70]. The LightCycler 480 System (Roche, Switzerland) were used for used for thermocycling. qRT-PCR experiments were performed using the TB Green™Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNaseH Plus) kit (TaKaRa, Japan), with three replicates set for each sample. The reaction mixture (20 µL) contained cDNA 2 µL, upstream and downstream primer 0.8 µL for each, TB Green Premix 10 µL, ddH2O 6.4 µL. The following thermal condition was used for the reaction: 95 °C for 5 s, 55 °C for 30 s and 72°C for 30 s (40 cycles). The relative expression level of the target gene was quantified using the 2−ΔΔCt method [71].

Statistical analysis

The data analysis was carried out using IBM SPSS Statistics 27, and the results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. To assess differences between treatments, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied, followed by Duncan’s test for post hoc comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Additionally, a quadratic regression model analysis was employed to identify the optimal alkalinity and saline-alkalinity ranges for G. przewalskii. Plots illustrating the results were generated using Origin 2021.

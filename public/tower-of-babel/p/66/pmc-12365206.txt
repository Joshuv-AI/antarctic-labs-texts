Hybrid harbor seal and jellyfish optimization technique for selecting the optimal location of hybrid renewable energy system
Authors: Radwa S. A. Gad, Hegazy Zaher, H. Al-Wahsh, M. H. Eid, Wael M. AlMetwaly, Islam M. Abdelqawee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Availability of a dependable electricity source is essential for improving living standards. When designing a micro-grid system, two key factors must be considered: cost-effectiveness and service quality. A major challenge in hybrid system design is balancing cost and reliability. To address this, a Renewable Energy System (RES) is optimized using a hybrid PV/Wind system with a diesel generator and battery storage. A novel hybrid harbor seal jellyfish searching (HHSJS) method is introduced for system configuration and component sizing. HHSJS seeks to address the problem with the investigation of the current harbor seal whiskers optimization algorithm (HSWOA). A temporal control technique is used to switch between JS and HSWOA operators throughout the search operation. The effectiveness of HHSJS is tested using 50 benchmark functions. Recent hourly wind speed data from two meteorological stations in Egypt: the Northwest Coast area and the New Valley Governorate. Despite the complexity of RES design, the optimal component sizing has been effectively determined for each location using HHSJS. This suggests that RES can be a viable solution for electrification projects, improving energy access in remote Egyptian regions and other developing areas with similar climates.

Introduction

In rural areas and on islands with significant renewable energy sources, electricity scarcity remains a significant problem despite the significant global improvements in renewable energy technologies. The utilization of renewable energy systems (RES) as a cost-effective electric energy source for a variety of applications has been made easier in recent years by advancements in power electronics. The two main challenges to the widespread implementation of renewable energy systems are the elevated specific costs of the units produced from these systems compared to those from conventional power plants, as well as the accessibility of renewable energy sources, as noted in1.

Furthermore, by taking into account an energy mix of various renewable resource types and the use of energy storage systems, the viability of high RES sharing may be strengthened due to the intermittent nature of almost renewable sources. The aforementioned obstacles prompt policymakers to consider integrating RES with conventional energy sources to create a hybrid RES, hence augmenting the proportion of RES in the energy blend2. RES, which combine solar, wind, and hybrid energy with diesel generators, are being researched as a viable and economically viable energy source.

In developing nations, isolated populations with poor access to electricity are frequently found on islands, in rural regions, and in some metropolitan areas. Due to the high levels of environmental pollution and fuel costs, diesel generators are typically used to provide the demand for energy in these areas, which creates a great deal of unpredictability3–5.

As a result, hybrid RES may be a very good option for remote communities; yet, further investigation, feasibility testing, and technology advancements are still needed in this field. Considering the national energy sector’s goal, which calls for increasing the use of renewable resources to 20% by 20276. In Egypt, photovoltaics and wind turbines are the two most promising energy sources. As a result, several feasibility studies and research projects pertaining to the evaluation of energy potentials and the best distribution of these sources in various places around the nation have been presented6–8.

Currently, much of the research focusses on optimizing standalone electrification systems rather than RES design9. Software tools are commonly used to simulate, optimize, and scale systems of this type. The software tools used included HOMER, HYBRID2, HYBRIDS, HOGA, PVSYST, SOMES, RAPSIM, SOLSIM, INSEL, PV-DESIGN PRO10, RSHAP, and ORIENTE. HOMER (hybrid optimization model for electric renewables) is the most widely used tool for cost calculation, sensitivity analysis, and validation of hybrid stand-alone system designs. These programs, however, have some downsides, such as black box utilization11.

In recent years, there has also been a considerable advancement in computational optimization techniques utilizing bio-inspired technology. By determining the ideal configuration to maximize the technical and financial requirements, they may significantly raise the efficiency of hybrid systems. In complicated systems where many characteristics need to be taken into account, genetic algorithms (GAs) are an effective way to optimize hybrid system sizing. Although the GA is difficult to write, particularly for RES optimization, it offers a range of hybrid systems with various component sizes to meet the load requirement in a given area and assesses them in accordance with the stated fitness function11–13.

Another approach that stands out for its straightforward concept, ease of coding implementation, robustness to control parameters, and computational efficiency is particle swarm optimization (PSO), which produces high-quality solutions with stable convergence characteristics and shorter calculation times11–14. PSO performance is similar to that of genetic algorithms, but it is less complex and quicker, and it has been effectively used to solve a wide range of issues15. In16, Improved grey wolf optimizer (IGWO), grey wolf optimizer (GWO) and Salp Swarm Algorithms (SSA) have been put to use, put to the test, and examined in order to optimize and build a hybrid energy system.

A hybrid renewable energy system comprising wind turbines (WT), photovoltaic (PV) panels, and backup diesel generators (DGs) was developed in conjunction with a battery storage system. In16, the ideal size of a PV/wind/diesel hybrid RES with battery bank storage is demonstrated by a thorough comparative analysis of Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), Moth-Flame Optimizer (MFO), Water Cycle Algorithm (WCA) and Hybrid particle swarm-gravitational search algorithm (PSOGSA).

The aim of this research is to determine which RES components are necessary to create a dependable and affordable system that can serve several families. In order to do this, a new Hybrid Harbor Seal Jellyfish Search (HHSJS) optimization method is evaluated by fifty benchmark functions and the results are compared to those of many other meta-heuristic techniques, including JS GWO, PSO, Hybrid grey wolf optimizer and Cuckoo Search (HGWOCS), Whale-Bat Optimization algorithm (WOABAT), hybrid Particle Swarm Optimization-Grey Wolf Optimization (PSOGWO), HSWOA, and Hybrid Jellyfish Searching Particle Swarm Optimizer (HJSPSO) to verify the superiority of HHSJS over the comparative techniques. The importance of introducing HHSJS is that solving insufficient ability to explore in some HSWOA applications. HHSJS is used to select the best promising place from two promising areas in Egypt; The New Valley Governorate and The Northwest coast region by identify the lowest Cost of Electricity (COE) and the lowest Loss of Power Supply Probability (LPSP) by satisfying specific requirements.

System description

A RES is typically composed of three subsystems: the production, distribution, and demand subsystems. These subsystems might differ significantly based on a number of factors, including the accessibility of sustainable resources, the intended services to offer, and subsystem of demand17. These factors significantly affect decision-making, which in turn affects the system’s dependability and cost. This section looks at the design of three specific stations’ wind, solar, diesel, and battery production subsystems. It also studies the load profile, which makes up the majority of the demand subsystem. Figure 1 shows how the RES is configured as the distribution subsystem. The hybrid RES optimal sizing

Sizing the system components should be the first focus in order to create a low-cost and highly efficient RES. The lifespan of the system is significantly impacted by the mix of high-quality components and generation sources, which can also lower the cost of electricity for end users in remote locations.

Cost analysis

The cost of electricity (COE) is one of the most often used and well-known indicators of the economic feasibility of hybrid renewable energy systems24. It is defined as the price per unit of energy or the fixed cost per unit of power. Identification of the areas of interest

When Egypt began electrifying the country, the remote coastal and desert cities were the last to be included in the Ministry of Electricity’s plan in this regard. The city of El-Kharga, the capital of New Valley Governorate for example, was ranked last among Egyptian cities in terms of the date of the introduction of electricity; construction convoys began there in October 1959, while the introduction of electricity to the city of Cairo began in 1892 (General Authority for Rural Electrification, 1973).

Accordingly, the study areas were selected within the border governorates and coastal areas, which were late to receive electricity; they also suffer from relative isolation, disparity in development levels between rural and urban areas, and imbalance between the inhabited and the uninhabited.

Optimization techniques

Harbor seal whiskers optimization algorithm

The most recent optimization algorithm, called HSWOA32, is predicated on the seals’ exceptional sense of smell and their ability to track their prey. Harbor seals track their prey by using their whiskers to detect underwater disturbances, which take the shape of oscillating spheres. Harbor seals explore the search space for attacking their preys using their whiskers at a certain sensing velocity. Jellyfish searching optimization algorithm

JS mimics the foraging behavior of jellyfish in the ocean as they look for food. It is also derived from the swarm intelligence technique33. JS is made up of two primary movements: (i) travelling with the flow of the ocean and (ii) moving within the jellyfish swarm. To transition between the movements, a time control mechanism is employed. A logistic map is one of the chaotic maps that is used to randomly start the population, or swarm, of jellyfish at the beginning. In order to guarantee that jellyfish are not trapped in the local optimal and to increase the accuracy of convergence, the logistic map distributes them throughout the search space to provide better initialization. Conversely, there are two categories of motions that occur within a swarm: passive and active motions. When in passive motion (type A), jellyfish use the following expression33 to move about in their own positions in search of better positions:30\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X_{i}^{t + 1} = X_{i}^{t} + rand1\gamma (UB - LB){ 0} \le i \le N$$\end{document}where γ, which is typically at 0.1, is the motion coefficient related to the length of motion around the jellyfish locations.

When a jellyfish is moving in an active motion type B, its direction of movement is determined by comparing its current position with a randomly chosen position of the j-th jellyfish, which is based on the quality of the food. In the event that the j-th jellyfish has higher-quality food, the i-th jellyfish will move in that direction; in the opposite direction, it will move away. A time control mechanism is employed to alternate between travelling inside the swarm and following the ocean current, as was discussed in Section “Reliability analysis”. A constant co and a time control function Tc make up this mechanism. While co is a mean value between 0 and 1, where co = 0.5, Tc provides a random value that varies between 0 and 133. The following expression33 is used to calculate the time control function Tc:33\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{c} = \left| {\left( {1 - \frac{t}{T}} \right) \times (2rand - 1)} \right|$$\end{document}

The variable r is a random number with a uniform distribution within the interval [0, 1], and T represents the total number of iterations. When Tc is greater than co, the chosen movement is to follow the ocean current; if not, it is to move inside the swarm. The swarm’s active and passive movements are alternated at the same time via the time control function. A function of 1−Tc is compared to a random variable in the interval [0, 1]. If 1−Tc is greater than the random variable, (type A) passive motion is performed; if not, (type B) active motion is performed. From 0 to 1, the value of 1−Tc rises gradually. Stated differently, initially, there is a greater likelihood of passive motion (type A) than active motion (type B); however, over time, there is a greater likelihood that type B will be chosen33. In JS, travelling through a swarm of jellyfish symbolizes the exploitation (local search), while following the ocean current represents the exploration (global search).

Proposed hybrid harbor jellyfish searching optimization algorithm (HHSJS)

This study’s primary objective is to address the inadequate exploration capability in some applications of the current HSWOA in order to combine JS with it to create a hybrid algorithm known as HHSJS that strikes a suitable balance between exploration and exploitation. Furthermore, the HHSJS is able to avoid premature convergence and break free from the local optimum through the fusion of HSWOA and JS. In optimization techniques, an inertia weight is typically employed to modify the treatment between exploration and exploitation. Low exploration and high exploitation are associated with a low inertia weight. Conversely, a high inertia weight results in both high and low exploitation. In the original HSWAO32, the transition from exploration to exploitation was linear and could not be changed. A nonlinear decreasing inertia weight, on the other hand, allows for adjustment to prioritize exploration or exploitation.

The nonlinear decreasing inertia weight is introduced to help improve the exploration, which is lacking in JS, and balance it with the current strong exploitation. In addition, HSWOA uses social and cognitive coefficients to manage the impact of exploitation and exploration, respectively. Time-varying cognitive and social coefficients, like inertia weight, contribute to high diversity for early global exploration and later global solution exploitation.

Case study: 50 benchmark functions.

This test runs 50 benchmark functions, details of these functions can be found in33. This test covers four types of functions: separable, non-separable, multimodal, and unimodal. Algorithms can easily discover the local optimum for multimodal functions because they contain multiple local extrema, whereas unimodal functions only have one. The non-separable characteristic prevents the division of the function’s variables into a product of each variable of functions, which typically makes it challenging to determine the global optimum. In contrast, the separable property shows that the function’s variables may be divided into a product of every variable of functions. There are seventeen unimodal, thirty-three multimodal, fourteen separable, and thirty-six non-separable functions. The outcomes of eight distinct swarm-based optimizers—JS GWO, PSO, HGWOCS, WOABAT, PSOGWO, HSWOA, and HJSPSO—are contrasted with those of HHSJS. For every technique under consideration, the population size and iterations are set at 50 and 1000, respectively, and each function is executed 50 times.

Conclusions

The best option for electricity in remote and rural areas may be to implement a hybrid renewable energy system (RES). A simulation model based on PV/wind/diesel and battery storage has been examined to explain how a hybrid RES operates while taking into consideration the site’s meteorological data. This study proposes a novel Hybrid Harbor Seal Whisker Jellyfish (HHSJS) optimization approach to determine the best place for RES between two prospective places: Egypt’s North Coast and New Valley region. The introduction of HHSJS is crucial since it addresses the lack of exploration capability in certain HSWOA applications. The suggested algorithm HHSJS, which combines JS and HSWOA, finds a good balance between the processes of exploration and exploitation. To evaluate the efficiency and superiority of HHSJS, it is compares with eight well-known and modern optimization algorithms using 50 benchmark functions. The results obtain the superiority of HHSJS over the comparative techniques. The outcomes of the HHSJS demonstrate that the Northwest Coast area achieves the maximum reliability at the lowest cost and with a significant contribution from renewable energy, and there is a great chance that a hybrid RES will be used at this place in order to confirm the superiority of the suggested method. The objective of this study is to provide high power supply dependability and a high renewable factor to meet the load demand at the proposed location while minimizing energy costs. In reality, this study will help North Coast, Egypt’s decision-makers consider the installation of such hybrid systems as a practical electrification alternative to lessen reliance on fossil fuels.

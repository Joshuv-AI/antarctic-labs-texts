Synergistic effects of platelet-rich plasma and teriparatide on osteoporotic fracture healing in OVX rats
Authors: Li Shuwei, Wang Zhi, Wu Kaidong, Zeng Lingyuan, Wang Qiang, Bai Xiaoyu, Yang Xiaoqiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate the combined efficacy of locally injected platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and systemically administered teriparatide (PTH) on fracture healing in osteoporotic rats. Thirty female Sprague-Dawley rats underwent ovariectomy (OVX) to induce osteoporosis, followed by a left femoral osteotomy with Kirschner wire fixation. Rats were randomized into five groups (n = 6 per group): Sham, Model (OVX + Fx), PRP (local injection of 0.3 mL autologous PRP biweekly), PTH (subcutaneous teriparatide 10 µg/kg/day), and Combination (PRP + PTH). Healing was assessed 6 weeks post-treatment via serum ELISA (measuring osteocalcin (OCN), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1)), radiography, biomechanical testing (ultimate load, stiffness), histology (H&E staining), and micro-computed tomography (BV/TV, Tb.Th, Tb.N, Tb.Sp). The Combination group exhibited significantly higher serum levels of OCN, PDGF, and TGF-β1 compared to the monotherapy groups (P < 0.05). Radiographic evaluation indicated nearly complete fracture bridging at 6 weeks in the Combination group, with superior mineralization density observed as early as 4 weeks. Biomechanically, the Combination group demonstrated the greatest increase in ultimate load (135.7%) compared to the Model group, surpassing the improvements seen with either PRP (90.5%) or PTH (78.6%) monotherapy. Micro-CT analysis confirmed the highest bone volume fraction (BV/TV) in the Combination group (0.337 ± 0.017), significantly greater than in the PRP (0.280 ± 0.012) and PTH (0.263 ± 0.015) groups. H&E staining revealed optimal trabecular continuity and minimal adipocyte infiltration in the Combination group. PRP (promoting local callus augmentation) and PTH (enhancing systemic mineralization) demonstrated complementary actions. Their combination synergistically restored bone microarchitecture and biomechanical strength in osteoporotic fractures, yielding outcomes superior to those achieved with monotherapies.Keywords: Osteoporotic fracture healing, Platelet-rich plasma (PRP), Teriparatide, Synergistic effect, OVX rat modelSubject terms: Diseases, Endocrinology, Medical research

Introduction

Osteoporosis, a prevalent metabolic bone disorder associated with dietary factors, results from an imbalance between bone formation and resorption. It is characterized by progressive bone loss, deterioration of bone microarchitecture, and increased fragility1,2. Osteoporotic fractures predominantly occur in elderly postmenopausal women, which underscores that osteoporotic fractures represent a significant health burden. Although clinical studies have presented inconsistent findings, current evidence indicates that osteoporosis may impair biomechanical recovery during fracture repair in animal models and could potentially delay the healing process3. Among the FDA-approved pharmacological treatments, bisphosphonate therapy is a primary choice for reducing the hip fracture risk by approximately 50%. However, this therapy is associated with substantial adverse effects, such as osteonecrosis of the jaw, dysphagia, esophageal inflammation, gastric pain, and renal dysfunction4–6. Moreover, other drug interventions can effectively mitigate the impact of osteoporosis on bone structure; however, their side effects, such as biliary issues, myocardial infarction, deep vein thrombosis, muscle cramps, nausea, and diarrhea, must be considered before initiation4. Therefore, despite satisfactory outcomes of the current treatment strategies, their adverse effects and mechanisms of action drive the search for novel therapeutic approaches for osteoporosis and related fractures.

Osteoporosis management should follow the principles of tissue regeneration7–9. Accumulating evidence suggests that platelet-derived biomaterials (PDBs), also known as platelet concentrates, may support the treatment of osteoporosis-associated injuries10–13. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is a platelet concentrate, containing platelets at 4–6 times the concentration of whole blood, along with abundant growth factors and highly active fibrin. It plays a crucial role in angiogenesis and bone metabolism for patients with osteoporosis and osteoporotic fractures, promoting new bone formation and exhibiting significant tissue repair capabilities14. A study demonstrated that moderate concentrations of PRP could effectively induce proliferation and osteogenic differentiation of osteoporotic bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells, thereby accelerating fracture healing15. Despite its regenerative promise through local bone repair enhancement, PRP’s clinical application is limited by the transient release of growth factors and its minimal impact on systemic bone metabolism.

Teriparatide, a synthetic parathyroid hormone (PTH) analog, can effectively stimulate new bone formation16. PTH can enhance osteoblast proliferation and activity, promote bone matrix synthesis and mineralization, and activate the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby amplifying osteoblast function17. Teriparatide is the first FDA-approved anabolic agent for the treatment of osteoporosis and other skeletal disorders18; therefore, its combination therapy is an active area of research. However, its key limitation is its systemic mode of administration, which may provide suboptimal stimulation for localized early-stage callus formation.

Thus, this study proposed that the combination of locally acting, growth factor-releasing PRP with systemically anabolic teriparatide might yield synergistic effects, thereby overcoming the limitations of monotherapy. Based on this, it may represent a potential therapeutic approach for addressing such impaired healing.The current study systematically evaluated this PRP + PTH combination for the first time in an ovariectomized (OVX) rat model, aiming to provide a novel strategy for clinically challenging fractures.

Materials and methods

Preparation of autologous platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

After anesthetizing the rats by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg body weight), their venous blood was collected from the lateral tail vein, transferred into anticoagulant-containing tubes, and gently mixed. The tubes were centrifuged at 500 rpm for 10 min. Then, the upper layer and a small portion of the intermediate layer were aspirated using a 5 mL syringe connected to a long needle. This mixture was then transferred into a tube without an anticoagulant and centrifuged again at 1500 rpm for 8 min. Centrifugation separated the tube contents into three layers: a bottom layer containing residual red blood cells, a top layer of platelet-poor plasma (PPP), and an intermediate PRP layer. The intermediate PRP layer was collected for subsequent experiments. Using a hemocytometer, the platelet concentration in the PRP prepared in this study was measured to be (1200 ± 150) × 10⁹/L, which is approximately 4.8 times the baseline of whole blood concentration [(250 ± 30) × 10⁹/L].

Animal husbandry and procedures

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, and the study is reported in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines (https://arriveguidelines.org). The experimental protocol was reviewed and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Zhonghong Boyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Approval No.: LL-202403290006).A total of 30 12-week-old female Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (SPF grade) were obtained from Beijing Huafukang Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (License No.: SCXK (Jing) 2019-0008). The animals were housed at 20–26 °C temperature with 40%–70% humidity and ad libitum access to food (standard rat breeding chow) and water. After a 7-day acclimatization period, the rat OVX models (bilateral OVX followed by femoral fracture) were established. For all surgical procedures (OVX and femoral osteotomy), rats were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg body weight).For OVX, the rats were anesthetized, and bilateral dorsal incisions (~ 1 cm) were made. The ovaries that appeared pink and cauliflower-like within the fat pad were located. The uterine horn and fallopian tube were ligated using 4 − 0 suture, and the entire ovarian tissue was excised. Hemostasis was achieved, followed by closing the incisions layer by layer.Previous studies have demonstrated that an 8-week period after ovariectomy is sufficient to induce significant osteoporotic changes in rats, including decreased bone mineral density and deterioration of trabecular bone microarchitecture19. Therefore, eight weeks after osteoporosis induction, femoral fractures were induced in the anesthetized rats; their left hind limbs were shaved and disinfected.An incision was made over the femur, and muscles were separated along fascial planes to expose the bone. A groove was created at the femoral midshaft using an electric drill, and the femur was manually fractured along this groove. A 1.0 mm Kirschner wire was inserted perpendicularly into the intercondylar notch of the left femur and advanced into the medullary cavity to fix the fracture ends. The wound was closed layer by layer, disinfected, and the animals were returned to their cages; drug treatment was initiated on the following day.At the experimental endpoint (6 weeks post-treatment), all rats were euthanized by an overdose of the same anesthetic agent, sodium pentobarbital (150 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection).

Animal grouping and administration

The PRP dosage (0.3 mL) was selected based on preliminary experiments and previous studies demonstrating that local PRP injection can enhance bone regeneration and fracture healing without causing excessive local pressure or tissue damage. Similar PRP volumes have been used in experimental fracture models in rodents to promote osteogenesis and bone repair20. The teriparatide dosage (10 µg/kg/day) was selected according to previously established protocols demonstrating effective stimulation of bone formation and fracture healing in ovariectomized (OVX) rat models21. After establishing OVX models, the rats were divided into five groups (n = 6 per group): (1) Control group (sham): rats underwent sham surgery (no OVX and no fracture); (2) Model group (OVX + Fx): rats underwent OVX and fracture fixation but received no specific treatment; (3) PRP group (OVX + Fx+PRP): rats underwent OVX and fracture fixation and received 0.3 mL autologous PRP injections locally at the fracture site every 2 weeks (total of 3 injections); (4) Teriparatide group (OVX + Fx+PTH): rats underwent OVX and fracture fixation and received daily subcutaneous teriparatide injection (10 µg/kg body weight); (5) Combination group (OVX + Fx+PRP + PTH): rats underwent OVX and fracture fixation and received both local PRP injection (0.3 mL every 2 weeks) and daily subcutaneous teriparatide (10 µg/kg body weight).The treatment duration was 6 weeks for all groups.

X-ray examination

X-ray imaging was performed using a digital radiography system (parameters: 45 kV, 5 mA, exposure time 0.1 s). Images were acquired in the anteroposterior direction under general anesthesia.X-ray examination of the left femur was performed for all rats at 2, 4, and 6 weeks after treatment initiation to assess fracture healing progression.To address the limitations of qualitative assessment based solely on representative images, quantitative analyses of callus formation and fracture healing were performed to complement the radiographic observations.Callus area (mm²) at the fracture site was measured using ImageJ software (Version 1.8.0). Fracture healing was evaluated using a modified Radiographic Union Score for Tibia (RUST) system (range: 0–12 points, with higher scores indicating more advanced healing).Radiographic scoring was independently performed by two experienced investigators who were blinded to the experimental group allocation. The final score for each specimen was calculated as the mean value of the two observers.Additionally, callus mineralization density was quantified as the mean gray value (normalized to the cortical bone density of the contralateral intact femur) using ImageJ’s densitometric analysis tool.

ELISA biochemical assay

Blood samples for ELISA were collected from the abdominal aorta under deep anesthesia, and serum was obtained by centrifugation. Whole blood cells were collected before treatment initiation (post-modeling) and at the end of the 6-week treatment period. Serum was separated by centrifugation. The levels of rat osteocalcin (OCN/BGP) (E-EL-R0243, Elabscience), transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) (E-EL-0162, Elabscience), and rat platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) (MM-0076R1, Jiangsu Enzyme Immunity), and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) were measured using their respective ELISA kits, following the manufacturers’ instructions.

Measurement of bone mineral density in rats

Bone mineral density (BMD) was measured in vivo using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA). A dedicated small-animal DXA scanner (GE Lunar PIXImus, GE Healthcare, USA) was used for all measurements. Prior to scanning, rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital and positioned in a standardized prone posture with the limbs extended on the scanning platform.BMD measurements were obtained at the lumbar vertebrae (L4–L6) as the primary region of interest. Whole-body scans were also acquired to ensure consistent positioning and image quality. Calibration was performed before each scanning session using a manufacturer-provided phantom.Acquired images were analyzed using the accompanying analysis software. Regions of interest (ROIs) were manually defined based on consistent anatomical landmarks, and the system automatically calculated areal BMD values (g/cm²).All measurements were performed by a single investigator who was blinded to the experimental group assignments.

Compression testing of left tibia-fibula complex

The left tibia-fibula complex tissue was harvested. The specimen ends were embedded and fixed in a universal material testing machine (CMT 6104, MTS Industrial Systems (China) Co., Ltd.). The bone was aligned with the compression axis. Compression was applied at a rate of 1 mm/min until a bone fracture occurred. Maximum load (N) and stiffness (N/mm) (slope of the stress-strain curve) were recorded.

Micro-CT scanning and bone microstructural quantitative analysis

After treatment completion, the distal portion of the left femur containing the fracture healing region was harvested and immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. For three-dimensional microarchitectural analysis, the fixed samples were scanned using a high-resolution micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) system with a voxel size of 10 μm. Reconstruction and analysis were performed using the manufacturer-provided software. The region of interest (ROI) was defined as a 5-mm segment centered on the fracture line, extending 2.5 mm proximally and 2.5 mm distally from the osteotomy site.The following trabecular bone microstructural parameters were calculated from binarized images (threshold: gray value 80–255, validated using Otsu’s algorithm): bone volume fraction (BV/TV), trabecular number (Tb.N, 1/mm), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp, mm), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th, mm), and connectivity density (Conn.D, 1/mm³). Connectivity density was included to evaluate the degree of trabecular network connectivity within the fracture region. Intergroup ratios were calculated by normalizing each treatment group value to the model group mean.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of femur tissues

Subsequently, for histomorphological examination, the same samples were decalcified. They were transferred to a 0.5 M ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) decalcification solution (pH 7.4), which was refreshed every 48 h until complete softening (approximately 4 weeks). Following decalcification, the samples were rinsed three times with PBS. They were then processed through paraffin embedding, sectioning, baking, deparaffinization, and rehydration. The sections were stained with hematoxylin for 3–5 min, rinsed under running water, differentiated in 1% acid alcohol, blued, stained with eosin for 3–5 min, dehydrated, and mounted. Finally, the tissue sections were examined under a light microscope (BX43, Olympus).

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software was used for statistical analysis and graph generation. Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD).Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Student–Newman–Keuls (SNK) post hoc test.Pearson correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationship between BMD and BV/TV values. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data analyses were conducted by two investigators blinded to the group allocation.

Results

Biochemical parameters before treatment

Eight weeks after bilateral ovariectomy, when osteoporotic changes were considered established, femoral fracture fixation was performed and blood samples were collected prior to treatment. Serum levels of osteocalcin (OCN), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) were measured by ELISA.

As shown in Fig. 1, the model group exhibited significantly lower serum levels of OCN, PDGF, TGF-β1, and IGF-1 compared with the control group (P < 0.05). These findings suggest that ovariectomy may influence systemic bone metabolic and growth factor profiles prior to treatment. However, the biological significance of these changes should be interpreted with caution, as bone turnover dynamics following OVX may vary depending on age, experimental conditions, and the time point of evaluation.

Biochemical parameters after treatment

The serum levels of OCN, PDGF, TGF-β, and IGF-1 were measured using ELISA at the end of the 6-week treatment period. The results are shown in Fig. 2. As compared to the model group, the PRP and PTH groups showed significantly increased levels of OCN, PDGF, and TGF-β (P < 0.05), and the combination (PRP + PTH) group showed the most significant increase in these indicators (P < 0.01). As compared to the monotherapy (PRP or PTH alone) groups, the combination (PRP + PTH) group exhibited significantly higher levels of OCN, PDGF, and TGF-β (P < 0.05), indicating a synergistic effect. There were no statistically significant differences in IGF-1 among the groups (P > 0.05).

Femoral X-ray examination

The X-ray examination results are shown in Fig. 3. The control group exhibited normal left femoral structure throughout the experiment, while the model group showed a severe femoral fracture that persisted throughout the experimental period. All the treatment groups showed progressive healing over time. Specifically, the Model + PRP and Model + PTH groups demonstrated comparable healing effects overall. The PRP group exhibited increased callus volume during the early phase (2–4 weeks); however, mineralization was slightly lower than in the PTH group. The PTH group showed more significant optimization of callus mineralization density and trabecular structure during the mid-late phase (4–6 weeks). The Model + PRP+PTH group demonstrated the most significant healing with significantly higher callus mineralization density than other groups at 4 weeks; the fracture line was nearly absent at 6 weeks.

X-ray evaluation of left femoral fracture healing in rats. Representative X-ray images of the left femur from the control, model, and treatment groups at 2, 4, and 6 weeks after fracture establishment and treatment.

Quantitative evaluation

Quantitative results

Callus Area: At 2 weeks post-treatment, the Model + PRP group exhibited a significantly larger callus area (3.21 ± 0.35 mm²) compared to the Model group (1.89 ± 0.27 mm², P < 0.05) and the Model + PTH group (2.34 ± 0.31 mm², P < 0.05), reflecting PRP’s early pro-callus formation effect. By 4 weeks, the Model + PRP+PTH group showed the largest callus area (4.12 ± 0.42 mm²), which was significantly greater than the Model + PRP (3.56 ± 0.38 mm², P < 0.05) and Model + PTH (3.01 ± 0.33 mm², P < 0.01) groups. At 6 weeks, callus area in the Model + PRP+PTH group (3.87 ± 0.39 mm²) remained larger than the monotherapy groups, but the difference was attenuated as callus remodeling progressed (Model + PRP: 3.42 ± 0.36 mm²; Model + PTH: 3.15 ± 0.34 mm²; P < 0.05 for both vs. Combination).

Callus mineralization density

At 4 weeks, the mean gray value of callus in the Model + PRP+PTH group (0.82 ± 0.06) was significantly higher than that in the Model + PRP (0.65 ± 0.05, P < 0.01) and Model + PTH (0.71 ± 0.04, P < 0.05) groups, indicating superior early mineralization. By 6 weeks, the mineralization density of the Combination group (0.91 ± 0.05) approached that of the Control group (0.95 ± 0.03), while the monotherapy groups remained lower (Model + PRP: 0.78 ± 0.04; Model + PTH: 0.83 ± 0.05; P < 0.05 for both vs. Combination).

RUST scoring

The Model + PRP+PTH group achieved the highest RUST scores at all time points: 4.2 ± 0.5 (2 weeks), 8.7 ± 0.6 (4 weeks), and 11.3 ± 0.4 (6 weeks). At 6 weeks, the Combination group’s score was significantly higher than the Model + PRP (9.5 ± 0.5, P < 0.01) and Model + PTH (10.1 ± 0.4, P < 0.05) groups, with 5 out of 6 rats in the Combination group reaching a score ≥ 11 (indicating near-complete union). In contrast, only 2 rats in the Model + PRP group and 3 rats in the Model + PTH group achieved scores ≥ 11. The fracture line visibility, quantified as the ratio of fracture line length to femoral diameter, was reduced to 5.2 ± 1.8% in the Combination group at 6 weeks, which was significantly lower than the Model + PRP (18.3 ± 3.2%, P < 0.01) and Model + PTH (12.5 ± 2.6%, P < 0.05) groups, confirming that the fracture line was nearly absent in the Combination group.

Integrated radiographic conclusion

Combined qualitative and quantitative radiographic analyses demonstrated that the Model + PRP+PTH group exhibited the most significant healing progression. Quantitative measurements of callus area, mineralization density, and RUST scores confirmed that the Combination group had superior early callus formation (2–4 weeks) and accelerated mineralization (4–6 weeks) compared to monotherapy. At 6 weeks, the significant reduction in fracture line length (to < 10% of femoral diameter) and the highest RUST scores (≥ 11) in the Combination group directly support the conclusion that the fracture line was nearly absent, with healing outcomes surpassing those of PRP or PTH alone.

Compression testing results of left tibia-fibula complex

This study systematically evaluated the effects of different interventions on the biomechanical properties of injured tissue through left tibial mechanical testing (Fig. 4). The results showed that compared with the Control group, the maximum load (9.4 ± 1.3 N) and stiffness (63.2 ± 7.0 N/mm) in the Model group were significantly reduced, reaching only about 47% and 51% of the control values, respectively. This indicates the successful establishment of the injury model and significant impairment of tissue strength and stability.

Left tibia–fibula compression test. (A) Maximum load of the left tibia–fibula complexes in the control, model, and treatment groups. (B) Stiffness of the left tibia–fibula complexes in the control, model, and treatment groups.Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 6). *P < 0.05 vs. Model.

Both the Model + PRP group and the Model + PTH group effectively improved the mechanical performance of the injured tissue. The Model + PRP group exhibited an increased maximum load of 15.7 ± 1.4 N and stiffness of 94.4 ± 8.0 N/mm, while the Model + PTH group showed a maximum load of 16.9 ± 1.2 N and stiffness of 96.1 ± 7.6 N/mm. Both monotherapies were significantly superior to the Model group (P < 0.05).

Notably, the Model + PRP+PTH group demonstrated a pronounced synergistic repair effect. Its maximum load (18.6 ± 1.6 N) and stiffness (119.6 ± 9.8 N/mm) recovered to approximately 92% and 96% of the control levels, respectively, and the extent of mechanical restoration was significantly greater than that of either monotherapy group (P < 0.05). The morphological changes in the stress-time curves were highly consistent with the quantitative data, further confirming the superiority of the combined intervention in enhancing tissue strength, stiffness, and stress-bearing capacity.

The Model + PTH group showed partially restored trabecular structure with reduced adipocyte accumulation. Femoral micro-CT quantitative analysis results

Micro-CT quantitative analysis (Fig. 7) demonstrated that all treatment groups exhibited significant improvements in trabecular bone microarchitecture compared with the model group (P < 0.05).Consistent with these structural changes, DXA analysis showed that BMD was significantly decreased in the model group (0.142 ± 0.012 g/cm²) compared with the control group (0.212 ± 0.014 g/cm²). Treatment with PRP (0.178 ± 0.011 g/cm²) or PTH (0.183 ± 0.013 g/cm²) significantly increased BMD relative to the model group (P < 0.05), while the combined PRP + PTH therapy produced the highest BMD among the treatment groups (0.198 ± 0.012 g/cm²). The combination group showed a significantly higher bone volume fraction (BV/TV: 0.337 ± 0.017) than the PTH (0.263 ± 0.015; P = 1.10 × 10⁻⁶) and PRP (0.280 ± 0.012; P = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵) groups. Bone mineral density (BMD) changes were strongly correlated with BV/TV at the individual sample level (r = 0.893, P < 0.001). PRP treatment resulted in significantly greater trabecular thickness (Tb.Th: 0.296 ± 0.014 mm) compared with the PTH group (0.247 ± 0.012 mm, P = 3.5 × 10⁻⁶). In contrast, PTH treatment significantly reduced trabecular separation (Tb.Sp: 0.273 ± 0.018 mm vs. PRP 0.310 ± 0.013 mm) and increased trabecular number (Tb.N: 2.004 ± 0.090 /mm vs. PRP 1.885 ± 0.086 /mm). The combination therapy produced the highest overall BV/TV, representing a 135.7% increase relative to the model group, exceeding the increases observed with PRP (90.5%) or PTH (78.6%) monotherapy. Furthermore, we introduced the parameter of connection density (Conn.D) (Fig. 8) to demonstrate this synergistic effect.

Discussion

The characteristics of osteoporosis, including reduced bone mass, abnormal bone structure, and increased bone fragility, are major contributors to elevated fracture incidence3. During fracture healing, platelets accumulate at the fracture site and release various growth factors, including platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), which promote osteoblast proliferation and regulate osteogenic cell activity. Osteocalcin (OCN), in contrast, is mainly synthesized by osteoblasts and is commonly used as a biochemical marker of bone formation22,23. In classical ovariectomized rodent models, estrogen deficiency is generally associated with increased bone turnover, and several studies have reported increased serum osteocalcin during the early to intermediate stages after OVX24. In the present study, serum OCN, PDGF, TGF-β1,andIGF-1levelswere lower in the model group than in the control group before treatment. These findings should be interpreted with caution. The behavior of biochemical markers after OVX is not uniform across studies and may vary according to animal age, strain, sampling time, and concomitant pathological conditions. A literature analysis of the mature OVX rat model indicates that although bone turnover generally increases after ovariectomy, the magnitude and persistence of osteocalcin changes are time-dependent, and long-term differences may become less evident. In addition, studies in diabetic OVX rats have shown altered baseline osteocalcin levels and a response pattern different from that of standard control rats, suggesting that systemic metabolic context can modify circulating bone markers25. Therefore, the reduced serum OCN and growth factor levels observed in our study may reflect a model-specific systemic response under the present experimental conditions. To avoid overstating this point, we now regard them primarily as descriptive baseline observations before treatment. However, after 6 weeks of treatment, ELISA showed an increase in these factors compared to pre-treatment levels, demonstrating the animals’ inherent, albeit limited, self-healing capacity involving a gradual restoration of growth factor levels. This underscored the need for exogenous therapeutic agents to augment the repair process. The combination of PRP and teriparatide demonstrated significant synergistic effects in promoting fracture healing in osteoporotic rats, outperforming the monotherapies across multiple parameters, such as serum biomarkers, radiographic healing speed and quality, biomechanical strength, and bone histomorphology. PRP is rich in growth factors, including PDGF, IGF-1, and offers advantages, such as simple autologous preparation, high feasibility, low cost, excellent biocompatibility, and minimal complications. Studies have shown that it can accelerate osteocyte activation, promote bone defect repair, and induce bone regeneration, thereby holding broad application prospects in fracture treatment26,27. Numerous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of PRP in addressing distinct osteoporosis-related conditions: its application has shown therapeutic potential in mitigating osteoporosis-associated bone loss in animal models (e.g., ovariectomized mice11,12, rats10, and rabbits31, promoting fracture healing in osteoporotic bone10,15, and enhancing implant stability in osteoporotic milieus28,29,31. Specifically, systemic or local administration of PRP alone, or in combination with osteoinductive/osteoconductive biomaterials, has been shown to alleviate bone loss in ovariectomized rodent models10–12,30; PRP local delivery has also accelerated the healing of osteoporotic fractures by promoting callus formation and osteogenic differentiation10,15; additionally, PRP combined with implant surface modifications has improved osseointegration and implant stability in osteoporotic animals28,29,31. However, studies specifically focusing on the synergistic effects of PRP and teriparatide in osteoporotic fracture healing remain limited.The current study confirmed that PRP significantly increased key growth factors in osteoporotic fracture rats, particularly elevating OCN levels more effectively compared to the PTH group alone. This indicated the potential of PRP in promoting bone-related growth factors. Mechanistically, based on previous studies, it is reasonable to speculate that growth factors locally released from PRP (e.g., PDGF and TGF-β1) may facilitate early soft callus formation through BMP/Smad-related osteogenic signaling, while teriparatide may systemically promote callus mineralization and maturation via PTH1R-mediated activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. However, these molecular pathways were not directly examined in the present study. The observed synergistic effects might originate from the molecular crosstalk between these two critical signaling cascades. It is worth noting that the precise mechanisms of interactions among the growth factors released upon platelet activation warrant further investigation. Furthermore, the H&E staining and CT scan clearly demonstrated the significant efficacy of PRP in repairing trabeculae and increasing bone density in osteoporotic fracture rats. Currently, teriparatide is the only FDA-approved anabolic agent for osteoporosis treatment, which can effectively stimulate bone formation and increase bone mass in patients32. The current study results also unequivocally showed the therapeutic effects of teriparatide on model rats, particularly excelling in tibia-fibula compression strength as compared to PRP. However, it is crucial to note that the FDA recommends limiting teriparatide treatment to 2 years for most patients due to concerns about potential risks, including osteosarcoma. Treatment beyond 2 years may be considered only if a patient remains at or returns to a high fracture risk33. Therefore, the combination of teriparatide with other therapeutic agents represents a promising approach. Etani et al.34 investigated and compared the effects of teriparatide combined with an anti-RANKL monoclonal antibody and teriparatide alone on bone defect regeneration in osteoporotic mice. They demonstrated that the combination significantly increased lumbar spine bone density and cortical bone strength as compared to monotherapy. Inspired by this strategy, the current study explored the PRP and teriparatide combination therapy. This combination covered and reinforced different stages of fracture healing; PRP can promote early soft callus formation, while PTH can powerfully drive the conversion of soft callus to hard callus, thereby achieving abundant early callus formation with rapid mineralization and ultimately optimal mechanical strength (highest stiffness). Both PRP and PTH monotherapies demonstrated significant osteogenic efficacy. However, H&E staining and X-ray results consistently showed that the combination therapy exhibited a superior effect over either monotherapy. Micro-CT quantitative analysis revealed the complementary mechanisms of PRP and PTH; PRP preferentially thickened trabeculae by releasing local growth factors, while PTH systemically drove trabecular network densification. Their synergy significantly surpassed monotherapy efficacy and circumvented the limitations of PRP monotherapy (insufficient mineralization) or PTH monotherapy (insufficient early callus volume). The combination therapy most effectively reversed the deterioration of osteoporotic bone microstructure (restoring trabecular number, thickness, and continuity) and significantly reduced bone marrow adiposity (fewest adipocytes), thereby forming the structural basis for improved bone density and mechanical strength. This reflected the synergistic action of PRP locally improving the microenvironment and PTH systemically promoting bone formation/inhibiting adipogenic differentiation, thereby targeting the imbalance in bone remodeling.

Although surgical injury is known to transiently elevate circulating growth factors, blood samples in the present study were collected during the chronic phase following ovariectomy and fracture fixation, rather than immediately after surgery. At this stage, estrogen deficiency–induced suppression of osteoblastic activity and impaired bone regeneration predominate, which may account for the observed reductions in OCN, PDGF, TGF-β1, and IGF-1 levels in the model group. Similar decreases in osteogenic markers during the chronic phase of osteoporosis have been reported previously. Therefore, the reduced serum levels observed in the model group are more likely reflective of impaired bone metabolism rather than acute surgical stress.It should be noted that although osteocytes are known to play critical roles in bone remodeling and mechanotransduction, the present study did not directly evaluate osteocyte-related parameters. Therefore, any potential involvement of osteocytes is inferred from existing literature rather than directly demonstrated.This study provided a novel perspective for clinically addressing the challenging microenvironment of osteoporotic and other difficult-to-heal fractures using a combination of PRP with PTH. The synergistic effects observed in the OVX rat model—including enhanced bone microstructural restoration, accelerated mineralization, and superior biomechanical strength—support the translational potential of this combination therapy for clinical applications. Future clinical studies are warranted to validate its efficacy in human patients with osteoporotic fractures or non-union, building on the preclinical evidence established herein.

Study limitations and future perspectives

In summary, this study demonstrated that the combination of PRP and teriparatide produced a marked synergistic effect in promoting osteoporotic fracture healing, thereby outperforming either monotherapy. The complementary mechanisms, where PRP mediates localized trabecular thickening by releasing local growth factors, while teriparatide drives systemic trabecular network densification, collectively enhanced bone microstructural restoration and mechanical strength. These findings positioned the PRP-teriparatide combination as a promising novel strategy for managing challenging fractures in an osteoporotic milieu.

Notwithstanding these promising results, there were several limitations to this study. In the present study, femoral fractures were induced eight weeks after ovariectomy, a time point that has been widely reported to produce significant osteoporotic changes in rats, including reduced bone mineral density and trabecular deterioration. But baseline micro-CT or BMD measurements prior to fracture induction were not performed in this study. Future studies incorporating baseline structural validation may further strengthen the model characterization.The sample size was relatively small, and the observation period remained short, thus precluding assessment of long-term outcomes. These analyses were primarily oriented toward phenotypic observations, such as serum biomarkers, imaging, biomechanical properties, and histomorphology, without investigating the underlying molecular signaling cascades, such as Wnt/β-catenin, BMP/Smad, or downstream PTH1R pathways and their crosstalk with PRP-derived factors. Moreover, the current evidence for PRP in osteoporosis management relies substantially on in vitro and rodent models. Given the substantial interspecies differences in bone metabolism and healing capacity between rats and humans, the lack of robust clinical evidence represents a major constraint.Radiographic fracture healing was evaluated using a modified RUST scoring system by two blinded observers. However, interobserver reliability analysis (e.g., intraclass correlation coefficient) was not formally assessed in the present study, which may represent a limitation and should be addressed in future investigations.Thus, the extrapolation of these animal findings to human applications must be approached cautiously.And the term ‘synergistic’ is used herein to denote that the combined treatment produced effects significantly greater than either monotherapy alone, rather than implying a formal interaction analysis.

Therefore, future studies should prioritize mechanistic investigations using techniques such as RNA sequencing, Western blot, and immunohistochemistry to delineate the signaling pathways modulated by this combination treatment. Subsequent translational studies should include validation in large animal osteoporotic fracture models (such as sheep or pigs) and well-designed clinical studies to evaluate safety and preliminary efficacy in human patients.

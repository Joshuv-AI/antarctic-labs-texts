From Theory to Practice: A Sustainable Solution to Water Scarcity by Using a Hybrid Solar Distiller with a Heat Exchanger and Aluminum Oxide Nanoparticles
Authors: Dharamveer Singh, Satyaveer Singh, Ashok Kumar Yadav, Osama Khan, Ashish Dewangan, Meshel Q Alkahtani, Saiful Islam
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study presently conducted focused on analyzing a solar-powered desalination setup that had a double slope. It can recycle blackish water into drinking water with solar energy. Not only does this result in a significant decrease in carbon emissions but it also represents an environmentally beneficial alternative that is particularly suited for arid locations that are lacking of electrical infrastructure. This system was equipped with a PVT system which makes the system self-sustainable and a CPC collector and implemented the use of aluminum oxide (Al2O3) nanoparticles to enhance its energy efficiency. Energy matrices, economic analysis, and life cycle conversion efficiency were evaluated. The study was conducted annually in New Delhi, with input data provided by IMD in Pune, India. MATLAB was used for the analytical calculations. Energy and exergy were utilized to determine the average annual energy output, which was found to be 8.5%. Additionally, the average energy payback time was calculated to be 16.16%, the average energy payback factor was 13.91%, and the average life cycle cost conversion efficiency was 7.15% higher. The proposed system demonstrated superior performance compared to the previous system in terms of annual yield, energy payback time (EPBT), efficiency of life cycle cost (LCCE), and factor of energy payback (EPBF). The hybrid system has the potential to meet the future demand for potable water and become self-sustainable.

Introduction

Water pollution and water scarcity are two major issues that pose a severe threat to mankind in the near future, especially in the face of a growing population. The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that more than 2 billion individuals across the globe do not have access to safe drinking water, and around 1.8 billion people rely on drinking water from sources that are contaminated. In addition, around 40% of the world’s population is impacted by water scarcity, and it is estimated that by 2050, over 1.6 billion individuals will be affected by this issue. This problem is further compounded by water pollution, which leads to the contamination of freshwater sources, making them unsuitable for consumption. Exposure to polluted water can lead to a range of health problems, such as diseases of the digestive system, issues with reproductive health, and disorders of the nervous system, among others. The combination of these issues is a serious threat to the health and well-being of the growing population, and urgent action must be taken to address them. As the world faces mounting challenges in terms of water scarcity and pollution, it is more critical than ever that we develop sustainable solutions to address these issues. Solar distillation, which uses the power of the sun to purify water, is one such solution that has great potential in this regard. However, it is vital that we approach solar distillation with a sustainable mindset, utilizing materials and practices that minimize waste and reduce our environmental impact. Sustainable solutions in solar distillation could include the use of recycled materials, renewable energy sources, and low-impact processes. By embracing sustainability in solar distillation, we can ensure that this technology is not only effective in providing clean water but also helps to create a more resilient and sustainable future for all. Solar distillation system utilization can help in achieving sustainable development goals, including SDG6, which ensures clean water availability for all; SDG3, which provides good health and promotes the well-being of all ages; SDG7, as the system utilizes solar energy which is a sustainable, renewable, reliable, and modern clean energy source for water distillation process; SDG8, which ensures economic growth as the technology creates new job opportunities and entrepreneurship; SDG9, as the system provides innovative and alternative infrastructure in remote areas; SDG13 as the system aligns with climate action effort and promote sustainable practices; and SDG17, as it requires the collaboration of various agencies such as government, community, and other organizations for its successful implementation.

A solar desalination system incorporating a PVT system and a CPC collector is an innovative solution to the challenges of water scarcity and pollution. This system utilizes the power of the sun to purify seawater or brackish water, producing clean, drinkable water in an efficient and sustainable manner. The PVT system combines photovoltaic (PV) and thermal technologies to maximize energy efficiency, while the CPC collector focuses sunlight to further increase the energy output. The incorporation of aluminum oxide nanoparticles (NPs) in this system can also enhance energy matrices and efficiency, making it even more sustainable. By utilizing renewable energy sources and minimizing waste, this solar desalination system can provide a reliable source of clean water while reducing our impact on the environment. The system can be particularly useful in arid regions where water scarcity is a major concern, providing a sustainable solution to this pressing issue.

Incorporating NPs such as aluminum oxide (Al2O3) into a solar desalination system can significantly enhance its energy efficiency. NPs have a large surface area-to-volume ratio, which increases their ability to absorb and scatter light, allowing for more efficient use of the sun’s energy. The introduction of Al2O3 NPs into the system can enhance the system’s thermal conductivity, allowing for more efficient heat transfer between the water and the system. This results in increased energy output and higher conversion efficiency. Moreover, the utilization of NPs can enhance the stability and longevity of the system as well, reducing maintenance and repair costs. Overall, the incorporation of NPs such as Al2O3 in a solar desalination system can result in a more sustainable and efficient technology, contributing to the mitigation of water scarcity and pollution. Energy metrics measurement and investigation are crucial in ensuring the optimal performance of any energy system. By measuring and analyzing the system’s energy output, efficiency, and performance, we can identify the areas for improvement and make informed decisions regarding the design and operation of the system. This information can then be used to optimize the system, improve its energy efficiency, and reduce its environmental impact.

A thorough literature review on solar desalination nanofluid systems can help identify research gaps in the field, such as areas where further investigation is needed, as well as potential areas for improvement and optimization of the technology. Ho et al.1 conducted a quantitative analysis on the effects of uncertainty on the viscous and thermal conductivity of a nanofluid in a square enclosure. Otanicar and Golden2 used a nanofluid to analyze the environmental and economic aspects of collectors and found that it neutralizes 74 kg over 15 years. Khullar and Tyagi3 researched the reduction of household emissions per year for a solar heating device based on the cost of flat plate collectors employing various oxides. The CuO nanofluid was found to be the most appropriate for performance by Faizal et al.4 Tiwari et al.5 analyzed the exergo-economic and environmental economics of incorporating a flat plate with PVT to meet daily drinkable water requirements. Sharon and Reddy6 performed an annual economic analysis for an active solar still using saline water. Dhivagar et al.7 analyzed a single slope grate crude shrewd solar distiller unit for energy, efficiency, and financial considerations. Finally, Arora et al.8 examined a double-slope solar still N-CPC-PVT using carbon nanotubes for water generation. Shatar et al.9 studied the effects of varying thermoelectric cooling power (12 and 36 W) on reference solar-still under Malaysia’s tropical weather conditions. The performance of the solar still was thoroughly examined on the basis of freshwater yield, energy, exergy, and environmental and economic factors. Muraleedharan et al.10 used a modified solar distillation system with a heat exchanger, an evacuated tube, and concentrator lens. The addition of 0.1% Al2O3 with Therminol-55 results in improved heat-transfer rate, along with 3.5 times enhanced water production rate than a conventional system. The above study presents a comprehensive review of various studies related to solar desalination systems that incorporate nanofluids and other materials to enhance their energy efficiency and performance. Numerous studies on passive and active solar stills have been conducted according to the current literature survey. However, seldom investigation on the active solar still filled with water-based nanofluids has been so far studied. Furthermore, no such research has ever been conducted for a CPC collector and an ETC double-slope basin-type solar distiller using a nanofluid, thus establishing its novelty. The novelty of this study lies in its approach to the proposed study which will examine the impacts of the active solar still double slope with the CPC and filled with a water-containing Al2O3 nanofluid based on energy matrices. Henceforth, the present study seeks to examine or explore the effects of incorporating Al2O3 nanofluid into an active solar still double-slope system with a CPC collector. The study will focus on evaluating the energy matrices, including the efficiency of life cycle cost, factor energy payback, energy payback time, and productivity of solar desalination systems. In doing so, the study will provide a comprehensive analysis of the proposed approach and compare its effectiveness to that of previous studies. Through this investigation, the research aims to contribute to the development of sustainable and efficient solar desalination systems.

Working of double-slope solar desalination incorporating PVT with a CPC collector using NPs (N-PVT-CPC-DS-HE) is shown. Representation of a solar still is followed by the greenhouse principle. Solar radiation receives on glass cover transferred to water surface, thereafter absorbed by surface. Later on, reflected water received heat on the top cover and rest portion and then moved to the liner where the maximum amount absorbed and the liner temperature increases and transfers this heat to water. Thus, the temperature water in the basin is increased and water is evaporated. Collectors also heat the water in the basin. Water is heated and evaporates in this manner. The distillate trickles forward to the passage attached to the bottom side as the vapor reaches the interior face of the condenser, where filmwise condensation takes place. Then, the beaker receives the distilled water that was syphoned off.

Conclusions

The urgent need for sustainable and affordable solutions for water scarcity, especially in developing countries, makes the use of NPs in solar distillation even more crucial. With the increasing demand for freshwater and the limitations of traditional water treatment methods, the adoption of innovative and efficient technologies like NP-enhanced solar distillation can play a significant role in addressing the global water crisis. The proposed system and previous system performance have been studied using characteristic equations and Al2O3 NPs and found better than the previous system.

The following conclusions are made by annual analysis of the proposed systems with Al2O3 NPs.

The possible limitations include the lack of standardization in the synthesis and characterization of NPs, which can affect the comparability of the results and the complexity of modeling the behavior of NPs in real-world conditions, which can lead to uncertainties in the assessments of their performance and impacts. This work can be further expanded using research with the PCM material in CPC up to a specific level. Future research studies on the energy and exergy potential of various NPs can be conducted, along with studies on the energy matrices, EPBT, EPBF, and LCCE, to assess their economic and environmental benefits; furthermore, the impact of different NP sizes and shapes can be investigated, and the effectiveness of partial coverage at 50, 75, and 100% can be studied to explore the full potential of these innovative materials.

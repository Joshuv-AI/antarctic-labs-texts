Efficacy and safety of tislelizumab plus anlotinib and irinotecan as second-line treatment for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer: a single-arm, open-label, phase II trial
Authors: Xian Chen, Jiao Wang, Lirong Liu, Xiaoshu Chai, Wenzhu Li, Rui Zhou, Xin Qu, Yanchun Qu, Yong Li, Yanjuan Zhu, Hongxi Hong, Haibo Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundExtensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) often recurs after platinum-based chemotherapy, with limited second-line options. This phase II trial evaluated the efficacy and safety of tislelizumab combined with anlotinib and irinotecan as second-line therapy for ES-SCLC.MethodsIn this single-arm, open-label phase II trial (NCT05027100) conducted in China, patients with ES-SCLC who progressed after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy were enrolled. Patients received tislelizumab (200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks), anlotinib (10 mg orally, days 1–14), and irinotecan (100 mg/m2 intravenously, days 1 and 8) for two 21-day cycles, followed by maintenance with tislelizumab and anlotinib until progression or intolerable toxicity. Tumor response was assessed every six weeks using the response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) v1.1. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), with secondary endpoints including progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), disease control rate (DCR) and safety.FindingsBetween September 2021 and March 2024, 32 patients were enrolled. At a median follow-up of 9.6 months (IQR: 7.4–21.9), ORR was 50% (16/32, 95% CI: 32%–68%), median PFS was 7.1 months (95% CI: 3.1–9.9), and median OS was 7.5 months (95% CI: 5.3–11.0). Research in context

Evidence before this study

We systematically searched PubMed and conference proceedings (ASCO, ESMO, WCLC) from January 2010 to March 2024 using terms such as “extensive-stage small cell lung cancer,” “second-line therapy,” “immune checkpoint inhibitors,” “antiangiogenic therapy,” and “irinotecan,” without language restrictions. Existing evidence indicates that second-line chemotherapy (topotecan) achieves limited efficacy while preclinical and early-phase trials suggested synergistic potential for combining ICIs with antiangiogenics or chemotherapy. However, no prior studies evaluated triplet regimens combining PD-1 inhibitors (tislelizumab), antiangiogenics (anlotinib), and irinotecan in second-line ES-SCLC, particularly in high-risk subgroups like patients with brain metastases or ICI-resistant disease.

Added value of this study

This phase II trial is the first to demonstrate the effects of tislelizumab-anlotinib-irinotecan triplet therapy in second-line ES-SCLC (ORR 50%, 16/32; median PFS 7.1 months, 95% CI: 3.1–9.9). The regimen showed enhanced activity in traditionally underserved populations, including patients with brain metastases (ORR 60%, 9/15) and ICI-resistant disease (DCR 89%, 8/9), while maintaining manageable toxicity (25% grade ≥3 adverse events, 8/32). These findings address a critical gap in second-line options and provide a mechanistic rationale for combining PD-1 inhibition, antiangiogenesis, and short-course chemotherapy.

Implications of all the available evidence

When integrated with prior evidence on tislelizumab's first-line efficacy (RATIONALE-312) and anlotinib's role in relapsed SCLC, this study supports the therapeutic potential of dual-pathway targeting (PD-1 + VEGFR) alongside chemotherapy in ES-SCLC. These results warrant validation in randomized phase III trials and highlight the need for biomarker-driven strategies to identify patients most likely to benefit from this approach.

Introduction

Extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) with five-year survival rate of merely 3% is characterized by short doubling time, high proliferation rate and early widespread metastasis, leading to present a formidable challenge in oncology.1,2 In patients with ES-SCLC, first-line platinum-based chemotherapy or optionally combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) pathway has been established as the standard treatment for patients with better results.3, 4, 5 Despite the high initial response, disease progression and drug resistance are almost inevitable, leading to a critical need for efficacious treatment options for patients with relapsed disease after first-line therapy.

Second-line therapy for ES-SCLC primarily relies on chemotherapy; however, outcomes remain poor, especially for patients with rapid relapse within six months, who also face a higher incidence of grade 3–4 hematological adverse events.6,7 With growing interest in ICIs for multi-line treatment, selective PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors have been explored in ES-SCLC.8, 9, 10 Tislelizumab, an investigational humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody, demonstrates high affinity for PD-1 while minimizing binding to FcγR on macrophages.11,12 Clinical trials have shown tislelizumab's benefit and acceptable safety profile across various solid tumors.13, 14, 15, 16 The phase 3 RATIONALE-312 trial revealed that tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy significantly improved clinical outcomes compared to placebo plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced ES-SCLC (median overall survival: 15.5 vs 13.5 months).17 However, its efficacy as a second-line therapy for ES-SCLC remains unexplored, with outcomes yet to be determined.

ICIs have shown promise in second-line and later treatment of small cell lung cancer (SCLC), but single-agent efficacy has been limited, as demonstrated by trials of nivolumab (CheckMate 331) and atezolizumab (IFCT-1603).18,19 Consequently, research has shifted towards combining immunotherapy with antiangiogenic agents or chemotherapy for relapsed SCLC, yielding mixed results. Anlotinib, a multitargeted antiangiogenic agent, has demonstrated potential synergy with ICIs. The ALTER1202 study established anlotinib's efficacy in third-line and later SCLC treatment, significantly extending median progression-free survival (PFS) by 3.4 months, along with improvements in overall survival (OS) and disease control rate (DCR).20 Anlotinib monotherapy also showed promise in relapsed SCLC, with median PFS of 4.1 months and median OS of 6.1 months.21 Recent studies combining anlotinib with ICIs have yielded encouraging results.22, 23, 24

Building upon the concepts of combining ICIs with both antiangiogenic agents and chemotherapy, and informed by the limited-course chemotherapy approach pioneered in studies like CheckMate-9LA,25,26 we hypothesized that a triple combination of tislelizumab, anlotinib, and a short course of irinotecan could offer superior efficacy in relapsed ES-SCLC. The rationale for this specific combination is multi-faceted. As noted, anlotinib is postulated to remodel the tumor microenvironment (TME), potentially overcoming primary or acquired resistance to ICIs and creating a more favorable setting for tislelizumab to reactivate antitumor immunity.27, 28, 29 Irinotecan, a standard chemotherapeutic agent in SCLC, can induce immunogenic cell death, releasing tumor antigens and potentially priming the immune system for enhanced response to ICIs like tislelizumab.30,31 In China, irinotecan's approval, accessibility, and inclusion in treatment guidelines made it the feasible backbone for this trial. Unlike amrubicin (unapproved) or topotecan (unavailable during study initiation), irinotecan is routinely used nationwide in China, with clinicians well-versed in managing its primary toxicity. Its selection also aligns with preclinical and clinical evidence supporting synergy with ICIs and antiangiogenic agents.30,32,33 The short-course design aims to leverage this potential immunostimulatory effect while minimizing long-term chemotherapy toxicity and immunosuppression, aligning with the strategy validated in CheckMate-9LA. Antiangiogenic agents like anlotinib may improve drug delivery by normalizing tumor vasculature, potentially enhancing the efficacy of concurrently administered chemotherapy such as irinotecan. This regimen simultaneously targets tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune evasion, addressing multiple critical pathways in ES-SCLC progression. While the efficacy of anlotinib combined with tislelizumab remains unexplored in solid malignancies, the strong mechanistic premise for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor and antiangiogenic agent synergy,24,34 combined with the established roles of both irinotecan in SCLC and short-course chemo-immunotherapy, provides a compelling rationale for evaluating this novel triplet combination.

Therefore, we designed a regimen of tislelizumab, anlotinib, and two-cycle irinotecan as a second-line treatment strategy. This phase II clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of this combination in ES-SCLC patients. The primary objective was to address the limitations of current second-line chemotherapy options, which are often associated with high recurrence rates and rapid disease progression. By evaluating this innovative approach, the study sought to provide a potentially more effective treatment alternative for ES-SCLC patients, ultimately improving outcomes in this challenging disease setting.

Methods

Study design and patients

This was a single-arm, open-label, phase II trial (NCT05027100). The enrolled patients aged 18–75 years, had histologically or cytologically confirmed ES-SCLC. Eligible patients included those with measurable target lesions in whom prior first-line therapy had failed, including chemotherapy or chemotherapy plus PD-L1 inhibitors according to National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guideline. All patients had a life expectancy of 3 months or more, an ECOG score of 0–1, and at least one measurable lesion according to the response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. Patients were excluded if they had previously undergone tislelizumab injection or treatment with virus-based therapy or antibodies that modulate T-cell function or checkpoint pathways, anlotinib or other antiangiogenic drugs, ≥2 systemic platinum-containing two-drug chemotherapy.

Ethics

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (NO: BF2021-091-01), was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and the local laws and regulations of the People's Republic of China. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients; Subjects volunteered to participate in this study and signed the informed consent, with good compliance and follow-up.

Treatment and assessments

Eligible patients received intravenous tislelizumab (200 mg) every 3 weeks, oral anlotinib (10 mg) once daily (QD) on days 1–14, and irinotecan (100 mg/m2) intravenously on day 1 and day 8 of each 21-day cycle for two cycles. Subsequently, patients received maintenance treatment with tislelizumab (200 mg) and anlotinib (10 mg) every 21 days until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent (Fig. 1). The dosing was chosen to balance efficacy and tolerability in this triple-combination, minimizing overlapping myelosuppression risks. By anchoring the regimen to a familiar, guideline-recommended agent, this trial addresses a critical unmet need for feasible second-line options in China's real-world practice.

Participant flow diagram and trial plan. (a) Participant flow diagram. (b) Trial plan; ES-SCLC, extensive-stage small cell lung cancer; ECOG, eastern cooperative oncology group; ORR, objective response rate; PFS, progression free-survival; OS, overall survival; DCR, disease control rate.

Tumor assessments were evaluated by investigators every six weeks per RECIST v.1.1 until disease progression or study discontinuation. The primary endpoint was ORR, which was defined as the percentage of patients who had confirmed as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR). The secondary endpoints included the following: PFS, defined as the time from entry to the first documentation of tumor progression or death from any cause (whichever occurred first); OS, defined as the time from entry to death for any cause; DCR, defined as the proportion of patients with CR, PR, or stable disease (SD); 12-month PFS rate; 12-month OS rate; and safety. Exploratory endpoint was median OS from first-line therapy.

The investigators conducted regular follow-up visits to assess safety. Adverse events (AEs) were monitored from the time patients signed informed consent until the data cutoff date or initiation of a new treatment regimen; whichever came first. AEs were graded in severity using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE), version 4.03. All AEs occurring throughout the study were recorded, including AEs, serious AEs, laboratory test abnormalities and changes in vital signs.

Statistics

Efficacy was evaluated using an expected ORR of 35% for the tislelizumab, anlotinib, and irinotecan combination as a second-line treatment for ES-SCLC, compared to a historical control of 16% (based on the chemotherapy arm of the CheckMate-331 trial). The study was designed with a one-sided alpha of 0.05 and a power of 80%, resulting in a planned effective sample size of 32 patients. The full analysis set (FAS) included all eligible patients who were screened and received the treatment regimen, while the per-protocol set (PPS) consisted of patients in the FAS who had at least one post-baseline tumor assessment. Response endpoints were analyzed in both the FAS and PPS, whereas survival endpoints and safety were primarily assessed in the FAS.

Continuous variables were presented as median with interquartile range (IQR; 25th–75th percentiles). ORR and DCR were calculated along with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) by the Clopper-Pearson method. Time-to-event endpoints, including median PFS and OS, were determined using the Kaplan–Meier method, with 95% CIs estimated via the Brookmeyer-Crowley method. Additionally, 6-month and 12-month PFS and 12-month OS rates were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and 95% CIs were derived using the point–wise method based on log–log transformation. The median follow-up duration was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Differences between two groups were assessed using the log-rank test. Post hoc subgroup analyses of efficacy were conducted based on patient characteristics at baseline, including the presence of brain metastases (yes/no), and first-line therapy (chemotherapy alone or PD-L1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy). All statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Analysis System version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina).

Role of the funding source

The funder had no role in the design and conduct of the study; collection, management, analysis, and interpretation of the data; preparation, review, or approval of the manuscript; nor the decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

Results

Patient baseline characteristics

Between September 30, 2021, and March 1, 2024, 32 eligible patients were enrolled and received tislelizumab plus anlotinib and two-cycle irinotecan (Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 64 years (IQR: 59.0–70.5) and most patients were male (91%, 29/32) and past or current smokers (91%, 29/32). The majority of patients had ECOG performance status (PS) of 0/1 (84%, 27/32; PS 2: 16%, 5/32). Of the 32 patients, 15 patients (47%) had asymptomatic brain metastases at enrollment. 23 patients (72%) only received one line of chemotherapy including etoposide and platinum (EP) or etoposide and carboplatin (EC) therap. Nine patients (28%) received ICI inhibitors plus EP or EC therapy as first-line treatment. As of data cutoff (March 1, 2024), the median follow-up duration was 9.6 months (IQR: 7.4–21.9). A total of 15 PFS events were observed, followed by 17 patients who were censored due to ongoing maintenance therapy (11/17, 65%), adverse events (3/17, 18%), and SARS-CoV-2 infection (3/17, 18%) during the COVID-19 pandemic (Supplementary Table S1).

Efficacy

In the FAS, 16 of 32 patients (50%, 95% CI: 32%–68%) achieved an objective response, and all had a PR. At data cutoff (March 1, 2024), DCR was 88% (28/32, 95% CI: 71%–97%), with PR in 16 patients (50%) and SD in 12 patients (38%; Table 2). In the PPS, the ORR and DCR was 55% (16/29 patients; 95% CI: 36%–74%; Table 2) and 97% (28/29; 95% CI: 82%–100%), respectively. The median time to PR in the 29 assessable patients was 1.7 months (95% CI: 1.5–5.5 months, Supplementary Figure S1).

Safety

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 25 of the 32 patients (Table 3). Grade 3 or higher TRAEs were observed in 8 patients (25%), with the most common being white blood cell count decrease (41%, 13/32), platelet decrease (41%, 13/32), inappetence (34%, 11/32), and neutrophil count decrease (34%, 11/32; Table 3). Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) occurred in 32 patients, with grade 3 or higher irAEs observed in 2 patients. The most common irAEs of any grade were pruritus (13%, 4/32) and hyperglycemia (13%, 4/32), all of which were grade 1 or 2. Serious TRAEs were reported in two of the 32 patients, including one case each of immune-related hyperglycemia and hyperthyroidism.

Discussion

Finally, the primary endpoint was met, with a confirmed ORR of 50% (16/32, 95% CI: 32%–68%) in the FAS, exceeding the expected value of 35%. The DCR in the FAS was 88% (28/32, 95% CI: 71%–97%). Median PFS was 7.1 months (95% CI: 3.1–9.9 months), with 6- and 12-month PFS rates of 53.3% (95% CI: 28.9%–72.6%) and 15.2% (95% CI: 2.6%–37.9%), respectively. Median OS was 7.5 months (95% CI: 5.3–11.0 months), with a 12-month survival rate of 25.0% (95% CI: 8.7%–45.6%). Grade ≥3 TRAEs were observed in 25% (8/32) of patients, and only two patients experienced irAEs of grade 3 or higher. Overall, the regimen appeared to be reasonably tolerated. These findings indicate promising efficacy and manageable safety of tislelizumab plus anlotinib with two-cycle irinotecan for this therapeutic regimen in second-line settings.

While front-line treatment for ES-SCLC has seen modest improvements with the introduction of immunotherapy, the second-line treatment landscape for recurrent ES-SCLC remains relatively stagnant, with limited effective options. For many years, topotecan, with an ORR of 22%–24% and a PFS of 2–3 months, was the primary approved second-line agent, despite its limited efficacy and unfavorable safety profile.6 Lurbinectedin, which initially showed promise with a reported 35% ORR leading to FDA approval in the second-line setting,7 ultimately failed to improve OS in the subsequent phase III ATLANTIS trial.35 Furthermore, ICI monotherapies and ICI plus chemotherapy combinations have demonstrated limited efficacy in ES-SCLC, particularly in patients who have progressed on first-line treatment. Thus, in our study, we explored the combination of the anti-angiogenic agent anlotinib with tislelizumab and short-course irinotecan in the second-line setting, followed by anlotinib plus tislelizumab maintenance. Other multi-drug combination studies have shown phased results: for example, sintilimab plus anlotinib as second or further-line therapy for ES-SCLC demonstrated a median PFS of 6.1 months and an ORR of 56.8%.24 In another phase II trial of 25 patients with ES-SCLC relapse, a combination therapy consisting of sintilimab plus anlotinib and nab-paclitaxel resulted in an ORR of 60%, a DCR of 76%, and a median PFS of 6 months.23 While similar in design and purpose to these prior studies, our three-drug strategy incorporating short-course chemotherapy achieved a numerically longer median PFS (7.1 months) and a higher DCR (88%) in the second-line setting for ES-SCLC patients.

Our subgroup analysis of patients with brain metastases showed encouraging results. Relapsed ES-SCLC patients with brain metastases had a numerically higher ORR and DCR (60% and 93%, respectively) compared to those without brain metastases (41% and 82%), with a median PFS of 7.1 months. This observation may be attributed to the intracranial antitumor activity of both anlotinib and tislelizumab. Anlotinib has demonstrated efficacy in managing intracranial lesions in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) or EGFR-mutated NSCLC with brain metastases, and recurrent glioblastoma.36, 37, 38 Similarly, tislelizumab plus chemotherapy has shown comparable ORRs for systemic (43.8%) and intracranial responses (46.7%) in NSCLC, with one-year systematic PFS and intracranial PFS rates of 36.8% and 55.8%, respectively.39 The intracranial antitumor activity of anlotinib and tislelizumab induced the high response for brain-metastatic ES-SCLC patients. However, the high proportion of censored cases (17/32, 53%) warrants careful interpretation of PFS results, with the majority attributed to continuation of maintenance therapy (11/17, 65%), followed by treatment-related adverse events (3/18, 18%) and COVID-19-associated SARS-CoV-2 infections (3/18, 18%) during the pandemic period. Despite demonstrating higher ORR and DCR, ES-SCLC patients with brain metastases exhibited a shorter mPFS and mOS of 7.1 and 7.2 months compared to 8.4 and 9.0 months in patients without brain metastases, indicating no significant survival benefit in this subgroup. Given the limited small cohort size where a single event alters percentage estimates by >3%, combined with the ambiguity in pathological and molecular subtyping that mainly determines the therapeutic outcomes, the observed dissociation between high response rates and short survival durations in brain-metastatic ES-SCLC patients warrants validation through larger cohorts and refined subtyping approaches.

Limited data exist regarding the efficacy of ICI rechallenge in ES-SCLC patients who have progressed on first-line ICI-containing regimens. In our study, nine patients who had previously received ICIs plus EP/EC but experienced relapse were treated with our three-drug strategy, including tislelizumab and maintenance with anlotinib plus tislelizumab. Encouragingly, 89% of these patients achieved tumor control, with three achieving a PR and five achieving stable disease. These results suggest that this approach may offer a potential treatment option for patients with prior immunotherapy resistance. The observed activity in this subgroup may be attributed to key features of our regimen. Firstly, given the heterogeneity of ICI resistance mechanisms, progression on initial anti-PD-L1 therapy does not preclude potential benefit from a different ICI like tislelizumab, an anti-PD-1 antibody engineered with Fc modification to minimize FcγR binding, potentially offering distinct immune activation.12,40 More critically, the core rationale lies in synergistic interaction: anlotinib may reverse ICI-resistant immunosuppression by modulating the TME,32,41 while irinotecan induces immunogenic cell death, releasing tumor antigens.30,31 We propose that anlotinib optimizes the TME and irinotecan provides antigenic stimulus, enabling tislelizumab to more effectively reactivate anti-tumor T-cell responses. This synergy may help overcome limitations of prior ICI therapy. However, given the very small sample size in this subgroup (n = 9), these findings should be considered preliminary. Larger prospective trials are needed to validate these observations and to fully define the role of ICI rechallenge in this setting.

While conventional chemotherapeutics can induce immunostimulatory effects by increasing cancer cell antigenicity or adjuvanticity, prolonged cytotoxic chemotherapy may impair the immune system, potentially limiting the efficacy of immunotherapy.42,43 Drawing inspiration from the CheckMate 9LA study, which demonstrated the benefits of combining short-course chemotherapy with immunotherapy,26,44 and from the concept of chemo-holiday regimens in advanced NSCLC,45 our study incorporated short-course irinotecan into our combined therapy to enhance the initial immunogenicity while minimizing long-term immunosuppression. Furthermore, we employed a chemo-free maintenance strategy with tislelizumab plus anlotinib after the initial induction phase, aiming to sustain the response while reducing the risk of cumulative toxicities. Given the limited efficacy of ICI monotherapies in ES-SCLC, the addition of the anti-angiogenic agent anlotinib was deemed crucial and potentially synergistic anlotinib can promote antitumor immunity and enhance sensitivity to ICIs through multiple mechanisms affecting both innate and adaptive immune cells, as well as tumor vasculature.41

Our treatment strategy, which incorporated short-cycle irinotecan combined with tislelizumab plus anlotinib in the induction phase, followed by a chemo-free maintenance phase, demonstrated a favorable safety profile in this second-line ES-SCLC study. The rate of grade ≥3 TRAEs was 25% (8/32), which appears to be lower than that reported for other ES-SCLC regimens, such as 6-week-cycle irinotecan (42%),33 tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (86%)17 and benmelstobart combined with anlotinib plus etoposide/carboplatin (93.1%) or anlotinib plus etoposide/carboplatin (94.3%).22 Furthermore, most TRAEs with a potential immunological cause were low grade and resolved. Only two patients (6%) experienced grade 3 or higher irAEs, with one instance each of immune-related hyperglycemia and hyperthyroidism. This occurrence rate also compares favorably to that observed with sintilimab plus anlotinib (26%) grade ≥3 irAEs, potentially due to the limited duration of irinotecan exposure in our regimen. Overall, the observed safety profile was consistent with expectations for a combination of agents with distinct mechanisms of action and short-course chemotherapy, supporting the feasibility of using tislelizumab plus anlotinib with chemotherapy as second-line treatment in patients with ES-SCLC.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the trial was a single-arm, open-label study without a control group, which limits the ability to directly compare efficacy and safety outcomes with standard second-line regimens. Second, the relatively small sample size and short median follow-up reduce the precision of survival estimates and restrict the power to detect less common adverse events. Third, the study enrolled patients exclusively from Chinese centers, raising questions about the generalizability of the results across broader, more diverse populations with different clinical characteristics. Finally, while safety appeared manageable overall, longer follow-up is needed to fully characterize the risk of immune-related adverse events and cumulative toxicities associated with prolonged therapy.

In conclusion, the combination of tislelizumab and anlotinib, along with two cycles of irinotecan, demonstrated promising antineoplastic efficacy and a manageable safety profile in ES-SCLC patients who have progressed on first-line therapy. It is warranted to further evaluate this regimen in larger, randomized clinical trials to confirm its efficacy and establish its potential role in the treatment landscape for ES-SCLC.

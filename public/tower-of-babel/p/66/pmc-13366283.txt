Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection Using a Finger‐Assist Device
Authors: Mafu Tsunemi, Ippei Matsuzaki, Kiyokazu Nakajima
Published: 2026

Brief Explanation

Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) require precise control of insertion and withdrawal of treatment devices. Using the two‐fingers method and the right pinkie maneuver allows the right hand to remain on the endoscope, ensuring a stable operative field [1, 2]. However, treatment devices are thin to pass through the forceps channel and are not ergonomically designed for finger manipulation or the two‐fingers method. To address this, devices for simultaneous manipulation of an endoscope and a treatment device, automated device for insertion and transfer, and the “Nelaton Attachment” have been reported to facilitate handling [3, 4, 5].

We developed a novel reusable assistive device (Finger Assister; CASTEM Co. Ltd., Hiroshima, Japan) within Project ENGINE. The device weighs 6 g, measures 6 cm in length, and has a maximum diameter of 1.5 cm. This device can be easily attached or detached laterally to EMR snares and ESD knives, and features an ergonomic surface adaptable to various finger sizes (Figure 1). Between procedures, the device can be cleaned using a neutral or enzymatic detergent and is compatible with ethanol, sodium hypochlorite, and ortho‐phthalaldehyde disinfectants.

Attachment of the Finger Assister to the EMR snare.

The patient had a 30‐mm laterally spreading tumor with fibrosis due to recurrence after ESD in the sigmoid colon. This assist device was attached to an ESD knife (Endosaber Yamashina Seiki Co. Ltd., Shiga, Japan). The device remained stable throughout the procedure, all manipulations were performed with the two‐fingers method, and the procedure was safely completed within 30 min (Figure 2, Video 1).

The pathological diagnosis was superficially serrated adenoma, and the tumor was completely resected. The patient was discharged without adverse events.

Further studies should evaluate its effects on musculoskeletal disorders, procedural efficiency, and endoscopic training. Nevertheless, its reusable design offers cost and environmental advantages and could contribute to improving endoscopists' performance.

A cross-cultural adaptation and validation of the Chinese version of American orthopaedic foot and ankle society hallux metatarsophalangeal-interphalangeal scale (AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP) in patients with hallux valgus
Authors: Qiong Wang, Jin Tong, Jiabao Liang, Dongdong Ji, Zhe Wang, Jingqi Liang, Hongmou Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHallux valgus (HV) is a common deformity of the hallux, which affects the functional ability of patients and reduces their quality of life. The American Orthopaedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) Hallux Metatarsophalangeal (MTP)-Interphalangeal (IP) scale is a questionnaire widely used to comprehensively assess the condition of hallux deformities. The purpose of this study was to translate the AOFAS-MTP-IP scale into a Chinese version (AOFAS-MTP-IP-CV), conduct cross-cultural adaptation, and simultaneously evaluate its validity and reliability.MethodsTranslate the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV according to general guidelines and conduct cross-cultural adaptation for it. From November 2024 to June 2025, a total of 120 patients with HV deformity who could communicate in Chinese were included. They successfully completed the demographic questionnaire, the Manchester-Oxford Foot Questionnaire-Chinese version (MOXFQ-CV), and the EuroQoL Group’s five‑dimension questionnaire (EQ‑5D). The reliability of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was determined by internal consistency, inter-rater reliability, intra-rater reliability, and test-retest reliability. Two orthopedic surgeons were responsible for the collection of questionnaires. The intra-rater reliability and inter-rater reliability were evaluated by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). The internal consistency of the scale items was assessed by calculating the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. The Bland-Altman analysis was used to test the consistency between test-retest assessments. The construct validity of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was determined by MOXFQ-CV and EQ-5D. Floor/ceiling effects were analyzed to assess scale responsiveness, with < 15% of scores at floor/ceiling levels indicating adequate range.ResultsThe AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was well accepted with no ceiling or floor effect. The intra-rater reliabilities of the total score of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV and the pain, function, and alignment dimensions were 0.941, 0.925, 0.945, and 0.922, respectively. The inter-rater reliabilities were 0.916, 0.877, 0.885, and 0.896, respectively. The Bland-Altman plots showed good consistency in test-retest measurements. The total score of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV showed moderate to strong correlations with the pain dimension (r = -0.676) and the walking/standing dimension (r = -0.757) of the MOXFQ and the EQ-5D (r = -0.601).ConclusionThe AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV has good feasibility, reliability and validity. It can be recommended for the comprehensive assessment of the clinical conditions of patients with hallux valgus (HV) deformity in Mainland China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-025-06196-y.

Introduction

Hallux valgus (HV) is one of the most common structural deformities of the foot in adults, and it is particularly more prevalent among women [1]. It is closely related to factors such as genetics, shoe-wearing habits (such as high-heeled shoes and narrow-toed shoes), and abnormal foot biomechanics [2, 3]. Since HV often leads to pain, difficulty in wearing shoes, and foot functional impairment, and may also give rise to secondary deformities of other toes (such as claw toes and hammer toes), it is regarded as the primary concern in the lesions of the big toe in clinical practice [3]. Therefore, it is necessary to regularly evaluate the clinical condition of HV to provide timely and effective treatment.

The term “hallux valgus” was first proposed by Carl Hueter in 1870 [4]. HV is characterized by dislocation of the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, accompanied by lateral deviation of the big toe and medial deviation of the first metatarsal bone. The bunion and first metatarsophalangeal arthritis are the primary pathological changes in HV [5]. A recent meta-analysis indicates that the prevalence of HV deformity varies from around 15–65% across different populations [6]. Increased severity of HV is associated with changes in gait patterns, reduced balance ability, increased fall risk, and declines in overall health status and self-perceived functional ability [7, 8].

Currently, multiple clinical scoring systems are used to evaluate patients with HV deformity, such as the Musculoskeletal Outcomes Data Evaluation and Management System Foot Questionnaire (MOXFQ), Foot Function Index (FFI), Foot and Ankle Ability Measure (FAAM), Foot and Ankle Disability Index, and the American Orthopaedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) clinical rating scale. The assessment covers multiple aspects including pain, function, daily work, and social activities [9, 10].

The AOFAS scale, developed in 1994, serves as a tool for evaluating various foot and ankle disorders and treatment outcomes. Although some researchers have highlighted its limitations, it remains the most frequently used scale for assessing foot and ankle conditions in recent years, with a usage frequency far exceeding that of the second most common scale [10].

The AOFAS scale is designed to evaluate the status of four distinct anatomical regions of the foot and ankle: the ankle-hindfoot, midfoot, MTP-IP of the hallux, and the MTP-IP of the lesser toes. AOFAS scale has developed separate questionnaires for these four anatomical regions, each of which can be used independently. As a commonly used scale for comprehensive assessment of HV, the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP scale assists clinicians in evaluating the disease progression and quality of life of patients with HV deformity from both subjective and objective perspectives [11].

The AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP scale has been translated into Turkish [12], Colombian Spanish [13], Persian [14], Arabic [15] and Italian [16], with verified validity. However, there is no traditional Chinese version of the AOFAS-hallux-Hallux-MTP-IP scale (AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV) for the assessment and monitoring of patients with HV. The purpose of this study is to perform the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV through a cross-cultural adaptation and validation of the original questionnaire.

Methods

Translation and cross-cultural adaptation

We translated and adapted the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP scale following a five-stage approach. This approach aligns with the guidelines proposed by Beaton et al. [17]. Step 1: Two bilingual translators, one being an orthopedic surgeon and the other a professional translator without a medical background, independently translated the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP scale from English into Simplified Chinese. Both translators were fluent in English and native Chinese speakers. Step 2: For translation integration, a consensus meeting was conducted, attended by the two translators and several authors of this study. The meeting aimed to reconcile discrepancies arising from linguistic expressions and cultural differences, leading to the formation of the initial version of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV. Step 3: Two bilingual translators, who were neither familiar with this study nor did they have a medical background, respectively back-translated the initial version of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV into English. Step 4: A consensus meeting was convened, involving all participants of the study, to address any discrepancies, ambiguities, or issues related to verbal expressions. This process ultimately led to the finalization of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV. Step 5: The pretest was conducted to determine the comprehensibility of the Simplified Chinese version. The pretest was carried out among 35 patients who met the inclusion criteria of this study [29 females (82.9%); age, 48.2 ± 18.3 years (range: 20–71 years); BMI: 25.7 ± 2.8 kg/m²]. The aim was to assess the comprehensibility of the Simplified Chinese version and conduct further cultural adaptation. After each patient completed the questionnaire, the researchers interviewed them to identify any difficulties they encountered in understanding the questions. If more than 10% of the patients considered a question “difficult to understand”, it had to be revised. The question was recorded, and patients were asked for suggestions on revision. After in-depth discussions among all researchers, which addressed the issues encountered during the pretest, the final version of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was determined (Supplementary File 1). Patients recruited for the pretest were not included in the remaining parts of this study. Notably, during the translation and integration phase, the expert group reached a consensus on adjusting the options under the item “IP joint motion (plantarflexion)”. The original options “No restriction” and “Severe restriction (less than 10°)”, which were assigned scores of “+5” and “0” respectively, were revised to “No restriction”, “Mild to moderate restrictions”, and “Severe restriction (less than 10°)”, with scores of “+5”, “+3”, and “0” assigned accordingly. This modification was implemented due to the fact that, in clinical practice, a significant proportion of patients demonstrate hallux plantarflexion range of motion falling between the unrestricted and severely restricted levels.

Patients and data collection

The sample size for this research was calculated in accordance with the recommended principle of “at least 10 respondents per scale item“ [18]. Considering that the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV scale consists of eight items, we aimed to enroll no fewer than 80 participants.

This prospective observational study recruited 120 patients diagnosed with HV deformity from November 2024 to June 2025 at the Outpatient Clinic of the Foot and Ankle Surgery Department, Honghui Hospital, Xi’an, Shaanxi Province. Inclusion criteria: (a) age ≥ 18 years; (b) cognitive ability sufficient to complete questionnaires; (c) fluent Mandarin speaking ability. Exclusion criteria: (a) history of other vascular, neurological, or musculoskeletal diseases affecting mobility or causing pain symptoms; (b) presence of severe diseases impacting daily life, such as heart disease, dyspnea, or psychiatric disorders; (c) individuals with cognitive impairments, mental or psychological conditions, systemic diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis). The patient characteristics are presented in Table 1. All participating subjects signed the informed consent form, and the study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Honghui Hospital (No. 2025-KY-054-01).

Instruments

Demographic information

The information to be recorded when patients seek medical treatment includes: age, gender, weight, height, duration of illness, educational background, living conditions, etc. Each patient completed three questionnaires, including AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV, MOXFQ-CV, and EQ-5D-5L. All questionnaires were administered by well-trained interviewers.

Questionnaires

AOFAS hallux Metatarsophalangeal-Interphalangeal (AOFAS-Hallux MTP-IP)

This scale was developed in 1994 and is used to evaluate hallux-related functions, symptoms, and the degree of deformity [11]. It contains eights items and is divided into three dimensions: pain (40 points), function (45 points), and alignment (15 points), with a total score ranging from 0 to 100. A score of 100 indicates the absence of symptoms or functional impairments, no pain, complete normal function, and good alignment, representing the optimal state. Conversely, a score of 0 represents the worst state. The scale combines subjective and objective questions for scoring. The subjective scores are self-assessed by patients and include questions regarding pain, activity limitations, and footwear requirements. The objective scores are evaluated by clinical doctors, covering the assessment of the range of motion of the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) and interphalangeal (IP) joints, the stability of the MTP or IP joints, and the alignment. This scale helps doctors and researchers understand the condition of patients’ hallux and assist in formulating diagnosis and treatment plans.

The Manchester–Oxford foot questionnaire (MOXFQ)

The MOXFQ was initially developed in 2006, compiled and revised with reference to the Manchester Foot Pain and Disability Index, and validated in patients with HV deformity [19]. The MOXFQ is a validated and reliable assessment tool widely used to evaluate patients with HV deformity. It serves as a key instrument for monitoring the progression of HV deformity, measuring the effectiveness of interventions, and guiding treatment decisions. The questionnaire underwent Chinese translation and cross-cultural adaptation in 2019, leading to the development of the MOXFQ-CV [20]. The MOXFQ-CV consists of 16 items divided into three dimensions: pain, walking/standing, and social interaction. Each question is scored on a scale from 0 to 4, where 0 represents the best condition and 4 indicates the worst symptoms or most severe health state. The maximum possible total score for the questionnaire is 64 (representing the most severe scenario), and it is typically converted to a 0-100 scale using the following formula: (Total Score / 64) × 100.

The EuroQoL group’s five‑dimension questionnaire (EQ‑5D)

The EQ-5D is a multidimensional health-related quality of life (HRQoL) measurement scale [21]. The EQ-5D tool has become a widely accepted instrument for assessing HRQoL in the Chinese population, demonstrating good reliability and validity [22]. The scale consists of five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Each question in EQ-5D is divided into five levels: 1 = “no problems”, 2 = “slight problems”, 3 = “moderate problems”, 4 = “severe problems”, and 5 = “extreme problems”. The calculation of EQ-5D utility values follows methods validated in the Chinese population.

Feasibility assessment

Document all the challenges that every participant faced during the process of answering the questions. Measure and record the duration needed for each individual to finish the questionnaire.

Floor and ceiling effect analysis

The distribution of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV scores underwent examination to search for floor and ceiling effects. It was concluded that an effect existed whenever more than 15% of the participants attained the lowest or highest scores that could be achieved [23].

Reliability

Reliability reflects the ability of a scale to provide consistent results when repeatedly measured under the same conditions. In this study, four elements of reliability were assessed: internal consistency, inter-rater reliability, intra-rater reliability, test-retest reliability. Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient (α), with α > 0.70 considered acceptable [12]. The inter-rater and intra-rater reliabilities of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV score were calculated based on evaluations conducted with a 1-hour interval on the same day. Inter-rater reliability was determined through repeated measurements by different examiners, while intra-rater reliability was calculated via repeated measurements by the same examiner. During the reliability assessment of this study, all visiting patients alternately underwent inter-rater reliability and intra-rater reliability assessments in a predetermined order. Specifically, statistics after the study showed that among a total of 120 participants, 51 patients were involved in the intra-rater reliability assessment, and another 50 patients participated in the inter-rater reliability assessment. Test-retest reliability reflects the performance of a measurement tool when measuring the same patient without intervening treatment. Test-retest reliability was evaluated in the study by randomly selecting 40 patients from 120 participants. Questionnaires were resent after a 5–7-day interval. Inter-rater, intra-rater and test-retest reliability were assessed by calculating the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). The stratification criteria for ICC were as follows: excellent: ICC ≥ 0.80; good: 0.60 ≤ ICC < 0.80; moderate: 0.40 < ICC < 0.60; poor: ICC < 0.40 [24]. (Notably, the pain and alignment dimensions of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV each contain only one item, so their internal consistency could not be evaluated.)

The degree of agreement for repeated test-retest measurements of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was calculated using the following indices: Standard Error of the Mean (SEM = SD × √(1 − ICC)), Minimal Detectable Change (MDC95 = 1.96 × √2 × SEM) and MDC95% (MDC / Mean of all scores) × 100%), where standard deviation (SD) is the pooled SD of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV scores, and ICC is the test-retest reliability. To further assess the test-retest reliability, we utilized Bland-Altman plots, which illustrate the agreement between the measures [25].

Validity

The Chinese versions of the MOXFQ and EQ-5D have been extensively utilized in mainland China. Their validity and reliability have undergone rigorous testing, as reported in previous studies [20, 22]. To evaluate the construct validity of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV, we calculated the correlation coefficients(r) between the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV scores and those of the MOXFQ-CV and EQ-5D. The strength of correlations was categorized as follows: none or very weak (0-0.2), weak (0.21–0.4), moderate (0.41–0.60), strong (0.61–0.80), and very strong (0.81-1.0) [26]. Before this analysis, we hypothesized that EQ-5D and the physical component scores of the MOXFQ (walking/standing and pain) have a good correlation with the functional interference dimension of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV.

Statistical analysis

The normality of variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Non-normally distributed data were first subjected to normal score transformation. Continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (X̄ ± SD), while categorical data are expressed as numbers(n) and frequencies (%). As described below, the reliability and validity of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV Scale were tested, including internal consistency, inter-rater and intra-rater reliability, test-retest consistency, construct validity, and floor/ceiling effects. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). A P-value < 0.05 indicates statistical significance.

Feasibility

All items of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV and the other two scales had complete data. The time required to complete the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was around 5.5 min (SD = 0.63).

Floor and ceiling effects

We examined the floor and ceiling effects of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV. Since no participant in this study achieved the minimum score of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV, and only 2 patients (1.7%) achieved the maximum score in the functional dimension, it can be concluded that neither floor nor ceiling effects were found (Table 2; Fig. 1).

Reliability

Internal consistency

The Cronbach’s α coefficient for the total score of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was 0.834, and that for the function dimension was 0.807. Since both the pain and alignment dimensions each contained only one item, their internal consistency was not evaluated (Table 2).

Intra-rater and Inter-rater reliability

The intra-rater reliability of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was excellent. The ICC for the total score and the scores of pain, function, and alignment dimensions were 0.941, 0.925, 0.945, and 0.922, respectively. The inter-rater reliability of the total score was 0.916, while that of the other dimensions ranged from 0.877 to 0.896 (Table 3).

Test-retest reliability

Meanwhile, the SEM was 3.67, MDC was 10.19 and MDC% was 7.14% for the overall score of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV. It was also demonstrated that the level of agreement between repeated measurements was excellent (Table 4), according to the previous criteria [12]. The Bland-Altman plot indicated the mean difference values between test and retest measurements was − 0.45, which is close to zero lies, indicating negligible directional bias (Fig. 2).

Construct validity

The correlations of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV with MOXFQ and EQ-5D are shown in Table 5. The total AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV score exhibits high correlation with the MOXFQ total score(r=-0.763), MOXFQ walking/standing score (r=-0.757), MOXFQ pain score (r=-0.676), and EQ-5D-5 L(r=-0.601). Additionally, the pain dimension of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV and MOXFQ showed high correlations(r=-0.678). And, the function dimension of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV had strong correlations with the walking/standing dimension of the MOXFQ(r=-0.739) (Table 5).

Discussion

The AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale, a validated and widely adopted instrument in foot and ankle medicine, is specifically developed for the comprehensive evaluation of hallux pathologies and their associated functional impairments [11]. Through a standardized, systematic assessment of pain intensity, functional capacity, and joint alignment, this scale serves as a fundamental tool for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient management. The standardized scoring system of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale ensures consistency and comparability of data across diverse healthcare institutions and research cohorts. This standardization is essential for conducting multicenter clinical trials, benchmarking treatment outcomes, and advancing the field of foot and ankle medicine through evidence-based research. As such, the scale has become an indispensable instrument for foot and ankle specialists, providing a robust and objective means of evaluating the therapeutic outcomes of hallux-related conditions [10].

In clinical practice, besides the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale, there are several other scoring systems for evaluating hallux disorders [17]. The AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale has two significant advantages: one is its stronger specificity for hallux conditions, and the other is its inclusion of considerations for toe appearance aesthetics. In terms of evaluation methods, the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale balances the objective evaluation by clinicians with the subjective feedback from patients. In contrast, some other outcome assessment tools (such as MOXFQ [20], FFI [27], and FAAM [28]) are based solely on patient self-reported questions. This diverse range of assessment tools can better adapt to the needs of clinical work.

This study successfully completed the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV and adapted the scale to Chinese local cultural characteristics. To test the reliability and validity of the scale in clinical application, a group of patients with HV deformity was selected. The results showed that the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV met acceptable standards for internal consistency, had excellent test-retest reliability, good construct validity, and no floor or ceiling effects. These findings are consistent with the results of the Turkish version of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP [12]. Overall, the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV has sufficient psychometric properties to scientifically assess the pain level, foot function, and hallux alignment of patients with hallux diseases in the local Chinese population.

The translation and cross-cultural adaptation of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP scale have been carried out in numerous countries, and its reliability and validity have also been tested [12–16]. In the present study, we used multi-stage guidelines for translation and cultural adaptation, as in all other versions. An acceptable level of internal consistency was obtained for the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP, with Cronbach’s α values of 0.834 and 0.807 for the total and function dimension scores, respectively. The results were generally consistent with the Turkish version (0.704, 0.706) [12], the Colombian Spanish version (0.70) [13], the Persian version (0.72) [14], and the Arabic version (0.981, 0.856) [15] of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP. All coefficient values above indicated good internal consistency.

The AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV demonstrated excellent intra-rater reliability and inter-rater reliability for the overall score (0.941, 0.916). Additionally, the ICCs for the pain, function, and alignment dimensions were all greater than 0.8. The results were basically consistent with the Turkish version (0.971, 0.913) [12], the Persian version (0.86, 0.97) [14], the Italian version (0.811, 0.881) [16] and the Arabic version (0.981,0.983) [15]. In our study, the excellent MDC% for the total score of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV was 7.14%. In the Bland-Altman analysis, the mean difference between the test-retest assessment for HV patients was close to zero, indicating negligible directional bias. These results confirm that when the clinical condition of patients remains unchanged, the measurement results of AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV are stable, and consistent outcomes can be obtained by different assessors.

In terms of construct validity, the results of the present study showed that the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV (except for the alignment dimension) was moderately to highly correlated with the pain dimension (r=-0.630–-0.678) and the walking/standing dimension (r=-0.580– -0.757) of the MOXFQ-CV and weakly correlated with the MOXFQ-CV social interaction dimension (r=-0.094– -0.362). There was a moderate correlation between the total AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV and EQ-5D-5 L (r=-0.601). All these results indicate that the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP-CV has qualified construct validity. Currently, multiple studies have been conducted on the construct validity of the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP by analyzing its correlations with various other outcome assessment measures. These measures include the Short Form 12 Health Survey (SF-12), Short Form 36 Health Survey (SF-36), Visual Analog Scale (VAS), FFI and MOXFQ [12–16]. Research has revealed that the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP exhibits moderate to high correlations with specific dimensions of these instruments. The moderate to high level of correlation was found to be with the physical summary score of SF-36 (r=-0.50 [14], r=-0.485 [12]), physical summary score of SF-12 (r=-0.504 [15]), general health component of SF-36 (r=-0.584 [16]), pain domain of FFI (r=-0.720 [13]), pain dimension of MOXFQ ( r=-0.732 [12]) .

In this study, we examined the construct validity of the AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV by evaluating its correlations with the MOXFQ-CV [20] and the EQ-5D-5L [21, 22]. All these results indicate that the AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV has qualified construct validity. In addition, there were no ceiling or floor effects in AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV in the HV patients, which is consistent with the results in Turkish [12], and indicates that the AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV is a questionnaire with good discriminatory ability.

Nevertheless, this study still has some limitations. First, patients were selected from a single center, which may introduce selection bias. Second, although the AOFAS-Hallux-MTP-IP Scale can be used to evaluate the clinical treatment outcomes of various hallux deformity patients, our study sample was limited to patients with HV. Therefore, future studies with multi-center, large sample sizes, and involving various types of hallux diseases are needed to validate the results of this study. Additionally, the patient sample primarily came from the central and western regions of China, which may not fully represent the entire Chinese population. To overcome these limitations, we plan to conduct a multi-center study in future research.

In conclusion, our study shows that the AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV has been developed, culturally adapted, and tested for psychometric properties. Based on these results, the AOFAS-hallux-MTP-IP-CV can serve as an effective and reliable tool for the clinical evaluation of native Chinese patients with HV deformity.

Transrectal ultrasound-guided optimization of needle insertion for interstitial brachytherapy in the treatment of vaginal stump recurrence of cervical cancer
Authors: Mingyuan Wu, Zirui Jiang, Xinxin Xian, Man Lu, Tingting Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the clinical value of real‑time transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) guidance in interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) for patients with vaginal stump recurrence after hysterectomy for cervical cancer. MethodsThis retrospective, non-randomized study included 32 patients receiving ISBT who were categorized into two groups: Group A (TRUS‑guided ISBT, n=16) and Group B (non‑TRUS‑guided ISBT, n=16). A total of 39 procedures were performed in Group A, involving 137 implanted interstitial needles, while Group B underwent 37 procedures with 145 needles implanted. Postoperative computed tomography (CT) was used to evaluate vaginal stump unintended perforation rates, penetration depth, and rectal or bladder perforation. Results A total of 282 interstitial needles were implanted across both groups.The vaginal stump unintended perforation rate in Group A was significantly lower than in Group B (16.06% vs. 40.69%, P < 0.05). Furthermore, In Group A, 22 (22/137) needles perforated the vaginal stump, with a mean penetration depth of 3.36 ± 1.32 mm, while in Group B, 59 (59/145) needles perforated the vaginal stump, with a mean penetration depth of 5.29 ± 3.51 mm; The mean penetration depth in Group A was significantly lower than that in Group B (t = -3.576; P < 0.05). Additionally, the incidence of rectal and bladder perforation was significantly lower in Group A compared to Group B (2.92% vs. 12.41%, P < 0.05). ConclusionThe use of TRUS guidance in ISBT for vaginal stump recurrence significantly reduces the incidence and depth of unintended perforations and is anticipated to lower the risk of fistula formation. These findings support TRUS as a promising technique for enhancing the safety of ISBT in patients with recurrent cervical cancer.Keywords: Transrectal Ultrasound Guided, Interstitial Brachytherapy, Vaginal Stump Cancer, Optimization of Needle Insertion

Introduction

Vaginal stump cancer is a recurrent malignancy that develops in women who have undergone hysterectomy for cervical cance [1]. It represents one of the most common sites of recurrence after surgery, typically emerging within two years postoperatively [2]. Vaginal stump recurrence is a significant prognostic factor in cervical cancer outcomes, and disease relapse remains a major contributor to patient mortality [3, 4]. Interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) is an effective treatment for managing vaginal stump recurrence, offering significant improvements in local tumor control [5]. The procedure involves transvaginal insertion of multiple interstitial needles into the tumor site, enabling precise delivery of radiation while minimizing damage to surrounding healthy tissues. However, improper needle placement can lead to complications including perforation of adjacent organs, hemorrhage, and compromised therapeutic efficacy [6]. Furthermore, beyond such immediate procedural risks, the long-term quality of life for cancer survivors is a critical concern. Treatments for locally advanced cervical cancer, including radiotherapy and chemotherapy, are associated with a significant burden of urinary, gastrointestinal, and sexual dysfunction, which profoundly impacts patient well-being [7].

Several imaging modalities—including ultrasound, X-ray, and CT—are used to guide ISBT procedures. X-ray and CT guidance, however, are limited by their inability to provide real-time intraoperative monitoring, often requiring repeated scans and increasing radiation exposure. In contrast, ultrasound offers real-time guidance, ease of use, and absence of ionizing radiation, which makes it a promising technique for improving the safety and accuracy of ISBT in vaginal stump recurrence.

Due to the absence of the uterus, altered pelvic anatomy, shortened vaginal stump, and frequent inflammatory adhesions and fibrosis around the lesion, patients with vaginal stump cancer face elevated risks during ISBT. The bladder, rectum, and sigmoid colon lie in close proximity to the tumor site, rendering them more vulnerable to radiation exposure compared to patients with an intact uterus. Without image guidance, interstitial needles are prone to unintended perforation of the vaginal stump, which may lead to suboptimal dosimetry, hemorrhage, and injury to nearby organs.

Preventing unintended needle perforation remains a critical challenge in clinical practice. Previous studies have shown that image-guided ISBT improves needle placement accuracy in cervical cancer treatment [8]. However, no studies to date have specifically investigated the advantages of transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) guidance for ISBT in vaginal stump cancer. This study is the first to evaluate the feasibility and efficacy of TRUS-guided ISBT in this patient population. Leveraging our department’s extensive experience with TRUS, we aim to report the clinical outcomes of TRUS-guided ISBT in patients with vaginal stump recurrence.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This study retrospectively analyzed 32 patients with vaginal stump recurrence following cervical cancer surgery who underwent interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) at our radiation oncology center between January 2021 and October 2024. Participants were allocated into two groups consecutively based on the time of treatment, reflecting the introduction timeline of the TRUS-guidance technique at our center. Group A (TRUS-guided ISBT, n = 16) comprised consecutive patients treated after [Start Date, March 2023], following the routine adoption of TRUS guidance. Group B (non-TRUS-guided ISBT, n = 16) comprised consecutive patients treated before [Start Date, March 2023] under the conventional protocol. This was a non-randomized, consecutive allocation. The cohort consisted of female patients aged 42–75 years (mean age: 57.78 ± 9.21 years), including 31 cases of squamous cell carcinoma and 1 case of adenocarcinoma. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age > 18 years; (2) histopathologically confirmed diagnosis; (3) prior total or subtotal hysterectomy; (4) documented vaginal stump recurrence after cervical cancer surgery; and (5) normal hematologic, hepatic, and renal function parameters. Exclusion criteria included: (1) non-compliance with treatment protocols; (2) psychiatric disorders or severe systemic comorbidities; (3) coagulation abnormalities; and (4) incomplete medical records. All 32 patients met the inclusion criteria and were included in the final analysis.This retrospective study was granted approval (SCCHEC−03−2017−013) by the Institutional Review Board, which waived the requirement for informed consent specifically for the retrospective analysis of the anonymized clinical data. Written informed consent had been obtained from all patients prior to the brachytherapy procedure for the treatment itself, which included the specifics of the image-guidance method (TRUS or CT-based) to be used.

Instruments and methods

Equipment

The study utilized the BK Pro Focus 2202 ultrasound diagnostic system(Denmark) equipped with an endocavitary probe(4−12 MHz) for imaging acquisition. Additionally, a 64-slice spiral computed tomography (CT) scanner (Philips BIG Bore) was employed, with a slice thickness of 3 mm for imaging reconstruction and analysis.

Transrectal ultrasound guided technique

Prior to the procedure, all interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) needle implantation procedures for both Group A (TRUS-guided) and Group B (non-TRUS-guided) were performed by the same senior radiation oncologist, who has over 12 years of experience in gynecologic brachytherapy. Initially, a transrectal probe was used to examine and measure the recurrent tissue at the vaginal stump, assessing its position, size, and echogenicity, as well as its spatial relationship with adjacent structures such as the rectum and bladder. In collaboration with the clinical team, decisions regarding the number of implantation needles, the insertion sites, and the penetration depth were made. Subsequently, real-time transrectal ultrasound guidance was employed to monitor and adjust the needle’s position, depth, and angle (Fig. 1). Interstitial Brachytherapy (ISBT) Treatment Protocol

Patients were positioned in the lithotomy position, and standard aseptic preparation was performed, including disinfection of the perineal region, sterile draping, and urinary catheterization. General intravenous anesthesia was administered prior to the procedure. Vaginal expansion was achieved using a speculum to facilitate needle placement.In Group A, implantation needles were inserted under real-time TRUS guidance, allowing for continuous visualization and adjustment of needle positioning. In Group B, needle insertion sites and depths were determined based on preoperative computed tomography (CT) imaging and the operator’s clinical experience. Immediately following implantation, a pelvic CT scan was performed to assess needle placement and confirm the spatial relationship between the implanted needles and surrounding structures (Figs. 3 and 4). Perforation depth was defined as the shortest distance from the inner mucosal contour of the vaginal stump (the anatomical baseline) to the needle tip, as identified on post-procedural CT scans. This measurement was performed digitally within the treatment planning system, with a positive value recorded exclusively when the needle tip was confirmed to be outside the vaginal stump lumen. In this study, ‘vaginal stump unintended perforation’ was defined as an uncontrolled and unintended needle passage through the Vaginal Stump, as confirmed on post-procedural CT imaging.

Post-procedural coronal CT validation of needle placement. e Coronal CT reconstruction demonstrating an uncontrolled needle perforation (blue arrow) through the vaginal stump (outlined by the dashed yellow line) in a patient from the non-TRUS-guided cohort. f Coronal CT reconstruction from a TRUS-guided patient showing an optimally positioned interstitial needle (green arrow) entirely within the target volume, confined by the vaginal stump contour (yellow dashed line)

Post-procedural axial CT validation of needle placement. g Axial CT image from the TRUS-guided group illustrating a correctly placed needle (green arrow) within the confines of the vaginal stump (yellow dashed line). h Axial CT image from the non-TRUS-guided group showing a needle (blue arrow) that has perforated through the vaginal stump wall (yellow dashed line), entering the perirectal space

The acquired pelvic CT images were transferred to the Oncentra Brachytherapy treatment planning system, where incorrectly positioned needles were excluded from the treatment plan. A medical physicist developed the brachytherapy treatment plan based on correctly positioned needles to ensure optimal dose distribution. The patient was then transferred to the treatment room, where the brachytherapy system was connected, and the prescribed treatment was administered.

Upon completion of the treatment, the implantation needles were carefully removed, and hemostasis was achieved through compression. Patients were closely monitored for 30 min to assess for any immediate complications. In the absence of adverse events, patients were escorted back to the ward under supervised care. All ISBT were performed by the same radiation oncologist (thereby eliminating interapplier variability).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 software. Continuous variables following a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (±s). Comparisons between groups for continuous variables were conducted using the independent sample t-test. Categorical variables were presented as frequency (n) and percentage (%), and comparisons were performed using the chi-square test (χ² test). A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Between January 2021 and September 2024, a total of 32 patients with postoperative vaginal stump recurrence of cervical cancer underwent 76 sessions of interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT), with 282 implantation needles inserted. Among them, 16 patients (Group A) underwent 39 ISBT sessions with a total of 137 implantation needles, of which 22 needles (16.06%, 22/137) were positioned outside the vaginal stump as confirmed by CT imaging. In contrast, 15 patients (Group B) underwent 37 ISBT sessions with 145 implantation needles, with 59 needles (40.69%, 59/145) identified on CT as extending beyond the vaginal stump. The difference between the two groups was statistically significant (Risk Difference [RD]:−24.63%, 95% CI:−33.89% to−15.37%; P < 0.05). Only one patient developed a rectovaginal fistula. In the non-TRUS-guided group, 16 instances of rectal needle penetration were recorded, with three patients developing postoperative rectovaginal fistulas. Additionally, two instances of bladder penetration occurred, one of which resulted in a vesicovaginal fistula.

Discussion

Interstitial brachytherapy (ISBT) for vaginal stump recurrence presents distinct technical challenges due to altered anatomy post-hysterectomy. While computed tomography (CT) remains a common guidance modality [9, 10], its limitations in real-time visualization can lead to unintended needle placements. Although transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) has proven utility in cervical ISBT for real-time imaging [11, 12], its specific role in mitigating unintended perforations in the unique context of vaginal stump recurrence had not been established prior to this study.

The unintended perforation rates for TRUS-guided group and non-guided group were 16.06% and 40.69% separately. J. Knoth et al. reported that real-time TRUS-guided interstitial needle placement reached high visualisation rate of 99.1% [9], and this supports that TRUS guidance could reduce the unintended perforation significantly. Previous studies have also examined the perforation rates of trans-abdominal guidance and non-guided for cervical cancer patients. Lucas et al. [10] mentioned that. The unintended perforation rate without ultrasound guidance was 10.54% versus 1.06% with ultrasound guidance. It aligns with our findings; however, our perforation rate was observed to be higher than reported in their study. This discrepancy may potentially be attributed to our study cohort consisting of patients who had previously undergone hysterectomy.The shortened vaginal vault in these patients likely contributed to increased surgical complexity. The interpretation of our findings on vaginal stump unintended perforation must be considered within its specific definition, which excluded clinically intentional needle placements. In our cohort, three patients with challenging tumor geometries (one deep-seated, two bulky) underwent planned needle extensions to ensure target coverage. Such maneuvers, which are integral to high-quality interstitial brachytherapy, are fundamentally different from the uncontrolled perforations that were the focus of this study. This distinction reinforces that the primary clinical benefit of TRUS guidance is the prevention of unintended perforations, thereby enhancing the safety profile of the procedure without compromising the ability to perform necessary intentional extensions.

In our study, the average unintended perforation depth of the interstitial needle were 3.36 ± 1.32 mm under TRUS-guided, compared to 5.29 ± 3.51 mm when performed without TRUS-guided. The use of TRUS guidance was associated with a significant reduction in the mean depth of needle unintended perforation beyond the vaginal stump. This result is consistent with a previous study by Tugana et al. [11, 12], they mentioned that real-time ultrasound can be used to improve the tandem length of implants and has the potential to decrease misplacement of applicators.

Previous studies have indicated that the close proximity of the tumor to the bladder, rectum, and sigmoid colon means that the high radiation doses required for tumor control can lead to fistula formation [13, 14]. Consistent with these reports, our findings demonstrate that TRUS guidance significantly reduced such complications, with only four rectal penetrations and no bladder perforations observed in the TRUS-guided group. Only one patient in this group developed a rectovaginal fistula. By contrast, the non-TRUS-guided group exhibited 16 instances of rectal needle penetration, three of which progressed to postoperative rectovaginal fistulas. Additionally, two bladder penetrations occurred in this group, one resulting in a vesicovaginal fistula. These results suggest that the routine use of TRUS-guided ISBT for vaginal stump recurrence may substantially reduce the incidence of both vesicovaginal and rectovaginal fistulas. All fistula cases in both groups were diagnosed within 3 weeks post-treatment. As established in the studies of Eifel and Mansha, fistulas primarily caused by radiation toxicity typically manifest after a latency period of several months to years due to delayed vascular and fibrotic changes [15, 16]. In alignment with this principle, findings by Wit et al. indicate that fistulas presenting within the first few months are highly suggestive of a significant contribution from procedural trauma or surgery [17]. Consistent with reports that early-onset fistulas are often linked to procedural trauma [13],the extremely early onset in our cohort strongly implicates uncontrolled needle perforation as a primary precipitating factor. TRUS-guided group (1 rectovaginal fistula): This patient underwent a transvaginal flap repair, which resulted in a 4-week delay in the completion of her overall treatment plan. Non-TRUS-guided group (3 rectovaginal fistulas, 1 vesicovaginal fistula): One patient with a rectovaginal fistula experienced mild symptoms and was managed conservatively; nonetheless, this still led to a 3-week delay in treatment.The remaining two rectovaginal fistula patients required surgical repair, each resulting in a 4-week treatment delay.The patient with a vesicovaginal fistula underwent surgical intervention, which was the most complex, causing a 6-week delay in their treatment schedule.

The demonstrated improvement in procedural precision with TRUS guidance is a critical step toward optimizing long-term clinical outcomes. In brachytherapy for patients with an intact uterus, studies by Bikramjit Chakrabarti and Xiujuan Zhao have established a clear link between suboptimal needle placement and worse tumor control as well as increased toxicity, primarily due to resultant inadequate dose distributions [18, 19]. While the anatomical context of vaginal stump recurrence precludes direct extrapolation, the fundamental principle that geometric accuracy underpins dosimetric quality and clinical success remains paramount.

Our findings, which confirm that TRUS significantly enhances needle placement accuracy in this unique population, therefore lay a necessary foundation. We posit that this technical advancement holds considerable promise for ultimately improving local control and reducing late toxicity. Future studies with larger cohorts and extended follow-up are now warranted and highly anticipated to directly quantify the translation of this precision into tangible patient benefits.

The findings of this study, which demonstrate a quantifiable improvement in needle placement precision with TRUS, should be interpreted within the specific context of its methodology and objectives. Our analysis focused on an imaging-derived metric of procedural accuracy—the rate and depth of unintended over-penetration on post-operative CT—which provides a reproducible, albeit partial, view of the implant process. This technical focus may account for apparent discrepancies with the rich, integrated clinical judgment developed through extensive experience in routine ISBT practice, where intentional needle advancement for target coverage is paramount. The primary aim of this work was not to challenge established clinical paradigms but to introduce and validate an objective endpoint for precision in a complex anatomical setting. We propose that the reduction in unintended deviations documented here represents a foundational step towards optimizing safety. These preliminary findings highlight the need for and help to design future multi-center, prospective studies with larger cohorts to confirm whether this technical enhancement translates into improved long-term clinical outcomes across diverse practice settings.

This study is a single-center retrospective analysis, and its relatively small sample size (n = 32) limits the statistical power of the findings. Furthermore, the interpretation of the statistically significant results should consider the potential for Type I error due to the multiple comparisons performed.Therefore, the conclusions, while promising, should be interpreted as preliminary and require validation in larger cohorts. Future research should include larger, prospective, multicenter studies to further evaluate the long-term impact on oncological outcomes, toxicity, and chronic adverse effects. It must be emphasized that, given the retrospective nature of this study, the observed association between TRUS guidance and the reduction in perforations and complications—while statistically significant and clinically important—should be interpreted as a strong correlation rather than definitive causation. Establishing causality requires evidence from future prospective, randomized controlled studies.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that transrectal ultrasound guidance in interstitial brachytherapy for vaginal stump recurrence significantly mitigates the risk of unintended perforations, a key factor in preventing fistulas. The clinical value of TRUS extends further by enabling real-time, adaptive planning during the procedure. This direct visual feedback ensures accurate needle placement, which is the fundamental prerequisite for achieving excellent dosimetry—maximizing dose to the target while minimizing exposure to critical structures. Therefore, the integration of TRUS represents a pivotal advancement for elevating the standard of care in this setting.

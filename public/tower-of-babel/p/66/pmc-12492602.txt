Efficacy and safety of treprostinil in pediatric pulmonary hypertension following fontan surgery: a real-world analysis
Authors: Mingjie Zhang, Xi Chen, Yahe Xu, Yijun Chen, Zhiyue Zhang, Jingru Zhou, Zhuoming Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of treprostinil in managing pulmonary hypertension (PH) following Fontan surgery in children. The hypothesis was that early postoperative administration of treprostinil could reduce pulmonary arterial pressure and improve oxygenation, utilizing real-life data to better reflect clinical practice.MethodsA single-center, retrospective study was conducted at the Shanghai Children’s Medical Center. The study included patients who underwent Fontan surgery between January 2015 and December 2023, comparing those treated with treprostinil (T Group) and those not treated (NT Group). Propensity-score matching was applied to adjust for baseline differences. Extracted demographic and clinical data included age, weight, height, type of cardiac defect, and surgical details. Postoperative variables included hemodynamic data, inotropic scores(IS), mean pulmonary arterial pressure(mPAP), pulmonary to systemic pressure ratio (Pp/Ps), PaO2/FiO2(P/F), volume of drainage and fluid balance. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software, with significance set at p<0.05.ResultsA total of 264 patients were identified, with 126 in the T Group and 138 in the NT Group. After matching, 154 patients were enrolled in (77 patients in each group). Mortality rates were similar (T: 0% vs NT: 1.3%, p=0.375). The T group showed significantly higher effective rates at both POD1 (90.9% vs.75.3%, p=0.010) and POD2 (93.5% vs. 79.2%, p=0.005). In patients receiving treprostinil ≥20 ng/kg/min, both mPAP and Pp/Ps showed significant reductions (p<0.001). Compared with baseline, the T group had significantly lower mPAP (P=0.045) and Pp/Ps (P=0.009), and a higher P/F ratio (P=0.02) on POD1. Significant main effects of treprostinil were observed for Pp/Ps (p<0.001), IS (p = 0.012), and P/F (p<0.001).T group had longer mechanical ventilation (p = 0.001) and ICU stay (p = 0.031). No major adverse events were observed.ConclusionTreprostinil treatment significantly reduced mPAP and Pp/Ps in the early postoperative period following Fontan surgery, with higher doses of treprostinil yielding more substantial hemodynamic improvements. But it did not significantly alter short-term clinical recovery, with no major adverse reactions observed.Keywords: Treprostinil, Pulmonary hypertension, Fontan surgery, Hemodynamics

Introduction

For single-ventricle defects, the Fontan procedure establishes a total cavopulmonary connection without a subpulmonary ventricle to alleviate cyanosis, resulting in passive pulmonary perfusion and unique hemodynamics [1–5].Due to the vulnerability of the Fontan physiology to elevated pulmonary artery pressure (PAP), even modest increases in pulmonary vascular resistance can significantly reduce cardiac output and precipitate Fontan failure [6–8].Accordingly, reactive pulmonary hypertension(PH) in the immediate postoperative period is a critical determinant of early postoperative success [3, 5]. Mean PAP (mPAP) exceeding 15mmHg is considered clinically significant and indicative of “Fontan-associated” PH [9]. Notably, an mPAP exceeding 15mmHg in Fontan patients is a known risk factor for adverse long-term outcomes underscoring the importance of this lower threshold [9].

The impact of PH on Fontan patients is also noticeable in the early postoperative period. On the one hand, the inflammatory factors released after extracorporeal circulation to some extent increase the pulmonary vascular resistance, so patients often experience an increase in pulmonary artery pressure in the early postoperative period, i.e., reactive PH [10]. On the other hand, the lack of right ventricular drive function after the operation leads to passive systemic venous pressure of the vena cava. Therefore, in the early postoperative period, pulmonary vasodilators are crucial for stabilizing the single ventricle patients. Currently, due to the problem of drug supply, the widely used acute pulmonary vasodilator in China is treprostinil.

As a stable prostacyclin analogue, treprostinil can dilate blood vessels, inhibit platelet aggregation and inhibit the proliferation of smooth muscle cells [11, 12]. Consequently, treprostinil has been identified as potential therapy in patients with Fontan physiology. Although treprostinil has been administered in clinical trials on PH, no consensus has been reached regarding the optimal drug to treat PH after Fontan surgery. Hence, it’s worth determining whether treprostinil use should be recommended in the clinical setting.This study aimed to evaluate efficacy and safety of treprostinil treatment in PH after Fontan surgery by investigating real-life data.

Materials and methods

Study design and study population

This study was designed as a single-center, retrospective analysis conducted by reviewing the medical records of patients at the Shanghai Children’s Medical Center. The study was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Shanghai Children’s Medical Center and was carried out in accordance with the ethical standards(SCMCIRB-YG2024056). As the study employed a retrospective design and involved the use of existing patient data, written informed consent was waived.

All patients who underwent Fontan operations in our center between January 1, 2015, and December 31, 2023, were identified from a preexisting institutional database. The criteria for administering treprostinil (Zhaoke Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. China) postoperatively in our center were as follows: mPAP ≥ 15 mmHg and hypoxemia (Fenestration: SpO2 < 90%; Non-Fenestration: SpO2 < 95%) with hemodynamic instability. For patients meeting these inclusion criteria, electronic medical records were retrospectively reviewed. Data extracted from these records included demographic and clinical information, such as age, weight, height, type of cardiac defect, duration of cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and aortic cross-clamp (ACC) times, as well as surgical details. Postoperative variables included hemodynamic data, inotropic scores (IS), PaO2/FiO2 (the ratio of arterial partial pressure of oxygen to the fraction of inspired oxygen, P/F), volume of drainage, and fluid balance (total output minus input). Cases with missing data were excluded from the analysis.

All patients were monitored for hemodynamic parameters, including mPAP and mean arterial blood pressure (mABP), with the pulmonary to systemic pressure ratio (Pp/Ps) calculated as the ratio of mPAP to mABP. Patients receiving treprostinil treatment were administered the drug intravenously via a central venous line, at dosages ranging from 5 to 80 ng/kg/min. The infusion was typically continued through the early postoperative period and tapered once hemodynamic stability was achieved. Dosing was individualized: all patients started at 5 ng/kg/min and the dose was escalated in 5–10 ng/kg/min increments based on clinical response and tolerance, which resulted in a broad range of doses (5–80 ng/kg/min) based on previous related studies [13, 14]. Finally, the drug was weaned off once the patient’s hemodynamics stabilized (e.g., mPAP fell below 15 mmHg and oxygenation improved).

Outcomes: The primary outcome was defined as the proportion of patients achieving a ≥ 20% reduction in mPAP or Pp/Ps within 48 h post-Fontan operation as effective response. Secondary outcomes included changes in hemodynamic parameters (mPAP, Pp/Ps, and P/F ratio over postoperative days 0–2), clinical recovery measures (e.g., duration of mechanical ventilation, ICU length of stay, chest drainage), and safety outcomes (adverse event incidence).

All other aspects of postoperative management were consistent between the two groups. Patients were managed according to our standard Fontan postoperative protocol (including ventilation, inotropic support, fluid management, etc.), with the only difference being the administration of treprostinil in the T group.

Adverse events were also captured by reviewing postoperative records for any complications or side effects, including injection-site reactions, phlebitis, thrombocytopenia, diarrhea or nausea, and flushing.

Statistical analysis

Patients were categorized into two groups based on the medication received: the Treprostinil Group (T Group) and the Non-Treprostinil Group (NT Group). To account for baseline differences between the groups, propensity score matching was performed to create a cohort with similar baseline characteristics with the matching ratio of 1:1.The propensity model included variables such as age, sex, weight, height, baseline SpO₂ and fenestration status, primary cardiac diagnosis, and operative variables (CPB and ACC durations). A caliper of 0.2 was used to ensure close matches on the propensity score.Post-matching baseline characteristics were well balanced (standardized mean differences < 0.1 for all variables).

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 25). Categorical data were presented as counts and percentages, while continuous variables were summarized as means with standard deviations (SD) for normally distributed data, and medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for skewed data. Fisher’s exact test was used for comparing categorical variables, and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for continuous variables. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For comparisons of hemodynamic changes between post-operation day (POD)0, POD1, and POD2, a one-way repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used. The assumption of sphericity was checked with Mauchly’s test, and the Greenhouse-Geisser correction was applied when necessary. A two-tailed significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 264 patients were identified who met the inclusion criteria. Among these, 126 patients (47.7%) received intravenous treprostinil, while 138 patients (52.3%) did not. After applying propensity score matching, not all patients could be matched due to differences in characteristics, and 154 patients were enrolled in (77 patients for each) (Table 1). The mean age of the two groups was 4.3 ± 1.8 years in the T group and 4.4 ± 1.7 years in the NT group. The mean weight was 15.9 ± 5.4 kg in the treprostinil group and 15.9 ± 4.0 kg in the non-treprostinil group. The most common diagnoses in both groups were univentricular heart, followed by double outlet right ventricle.

Mortality did not differ significantly between the NT and T groups (0% vs. 1.3%, χ² = 1.01, p = 0.375). However, the T group showed significantly higher effective rates at both POD1 (90.9% vs. 75.3%, χ² = 6.871, p = 0.010) and POD2 (93.5% vs. 79.2%, χ² = 7.836, p = 0.005), with decrease greater than 20% in mPAP or Pp/Ps from baseline. Furthermore, the median dose of treprostinil was 30ng/kg/min (range: 20–62.5 ng/kg/min), and the median time to initiation of treprostinil was 1 h (range: 0.5–3.0 h) post-operation.

In patients receiving a dose of < 20 ng/kg/min of treprostinil, there were no significant changes in either the Pp/Ps ratio (from 0.25 ± 0.06 to 0.18 ± 0.01, p = 0.150) or mPAP (from 14.76 ± 2.06 to 12.61 ± 0.64, p = 0.163). In contrast, for those receiving ≥ 20 ng/kg/min, both the Pp/Ps ratio and mPAP showed significant decreases, with the Pp/Ps ratio dropping from 0.32 ± 0.07 to 0.19 ± 0.07 (p < 0.001) and mPAP decreasing from 16.52 ± 2.50 to 13.65 ± 3.11mmHg (p < 0.001). These results suggest that higher doses of treprostinil (≥ 20 ng/kg/min) are associated with a more substantial reduction in both Pp/Ps ratio and mPAP compared to lower doses(Table 2).

Discussion

The results of this retrospective study provide valuable insights into the efficacy and safety of treprostinil in treating PH following the Fontan operation. Despite its use in clinical practice, there remains a lack of consensus regarding its optimal role in treating PH after Fontan surgery. This study aimed to address that gap by evaluating the real-life data from a cohort of pediatric patients undergoing Fontan surgery.

Our study demonstrates that treprostinil treatment significantly improves hemodynamic parameters such as mPAP and the Pp/Ps in the early postoperative period. At both POD 1 and POD 2, patients who received treprostinil exhibited greater reductions in mPAP and Pp/Ps compared to those who did not receive the drug. These findings are consistent with previous studies that have shown that pulmonary vasodilators, including prostacyclin analogues, can help mitigate the elevated pulmonary vascular resistance that characterizes PH after Fontan surgery [15–17]. Our findings are consistent with emerging reports in the literature. Kavgacı et al.documented improved outcomes with subcutaneous treprostinil in pediatric PH patients, and Wang et al. reported that intravenous treprostinil improved hemodynamics after the Fontan procedure [18, 19].

Importantly, we found that a higher dose of treprostinil (≥ 20 ng/kg/min) was associated with more pronounced improvements in these hemodynamic measures, suggesting that dose-dependent responses are a critical consideration in optimizing treatment strategies for PH in Fontan patients [20–23].A dose of 20 ng/kg/min administered intravenously was commonly recommended in the pediatric literature as an initial therapeutic target and was considered a threshold for achieving effective plasma concentrations and clinical benefit [13].While we did not have a rigid written protocol beyond the initiation criteria, in practice our clinicians followed a similar approach for all: early start, cautious uptitration, and weaning off gradually if the hemodynamics was stable. The feasibility and safety of higher treprostinil doses have been supported by published studies and were also confirmed in our cohort [13, 14, 19].

In terms of clinical outcomes, patients in the treprostinil group showed a higher effective rate at both POD1 and POD2, with over 90% of patients achieving a reduction of more than 20% in mPAP or Pp/Ps. This highlights the potential of treprostinil in offering therapeutic benefit, particularly in those with more severe pulmonary hypertension or hemodynamic instability in the early postoperative period [24]. While statistically significant, the 20% reductions in mPAP and Pp/Ps were also clinically meaningful. The clinical impact of these changes is likely incremental, and a slight reduction in the loading conditions for the single ventricle, which may help cardiac output.Literature has shown that even the Fontan pressure decreased by ~ 1 mmHg while cardiac flow increased by 0.97 L/min during rest [25].

While mortality rates were not significantly different between the two groups, and no major differences were noted in complications such as arrhythmias, hepatic injury, or RRT, the increased duration of mechanical ventilation and ICU stay in the treprostinil group could reflect the more intensive care needs of these patients. It could be attributed to the more critical clinical condition of the patients receiving treprostinil, as this treatment was typically initiated for those with more severe PH and hypoxemia.

The higher inotropic support observed in the treprostinil group at all time points suggests a higher degree of hemodynamic instability in this cohort, further supporting the idea that these patients may have been at greater risk for complications due to their underlying condition or surgical procedure. However, it is important to note that despite this higher level of inotropic support, the treprostinil group did not experience worse clinical outcomes in terms of mortality and early Fontan failure. Notably, no significant adverse reactions such as phlebitis, thrombocytopenia, or gastrointestinal side effects were reported, indicating that treprostinil was well-tolerated, with no major complications arising from its administration [26, 27]. Additionally, the sample size (77 patients per group) may not have been sufficient to detect differences in very low-frequency adverse events. For example, although flushing occurred in 3 patients in the T group versus 1 patient in the NT group (3.9% vs. 1.3%), the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.67). Moreover, because adverse events were retrospectively collected from medical records, minor or transient events may have been underreported. As a result, the study was likely underpowered to reliably identify rare complications.

Another interesting finding was the significant improvement in the P/F ratio in the T group at POD1 (T:73.9 ± 69.8 vs. NT:58.5 ± 51.9 mmHg, P = 0.02) compared with baseline, suggesting that the use of treprostinil may contribute to better oxygenation. However, we note that the absolute P/F ratio remained lower in the T group compared to the NT group on POD1, likely reflecting the more compromised respiratory status of patients who required treprostinil. In other words, the T group started with consistent and worse oxygenation from the baseline, although their P/F improved, it was still below that of the NT group at the same time point. This finding underscores that the treprostinil treated patients had higher initial severity, which could account for prolonged support needs despite improved oxygenation.Although the P/F ratio is not a direct measure of pulmonary artery pressure, it is indicative of the overall respiratory function, and improvements in oxygenation are critical in the management of Fontan patients, who often have impaired oxygen exchange due to the altered circulatory physiology post-surgery.

Despite these positive findings, there are limitations to this study that should be considered. As a retrospective design, the study is inherently subject to biases such as selection bias, where patients who were more severely affected by PH may have been more likely to receive treprostinil, potentially confounding the results. In our analyses, we adjusted for baseline hemodynamics (as discussed earlier), and while we did not adjust for IS directly, we acknowledge it as a limitation/interpretation point. Additionally, because data on complications were collected retrospectively from charts, any adverse events not documented would be missed. This means some milder side effects could be underreported. We have likely captured all major complications, but the retrospective design may underestimate the true incidence of minor events.The absence of a randomized controlled trial (RCT) means that while our results suggest a benefit from treprostinil, they cannot definitively establish causality. Additionally, the sample size, though relatively large, may not be sufficient to detect rare adverse events or long-term outcomes. Further studies, particularly multicenter RCTs, are needed to confirm these findings and refine treatment strategies for managing PH after Fontan surgery.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study supports the use of treprostinil as an effective and safe option for managing pulmonary hypertension in the early postoperative period following Fontan surgery. Treprostinil was associated with significant improvements in hemodynamic parameters and oxygenation, and its administration was well-tolerated without major adverse events. Given the lack of consensus on the optimal pharmacological management of PH after Fontan surgery, these findings contribute valuable evidence to guide clinical decision-making and provide a foundation for further research into the role of pulmonary vasodilators in this challenging patient population.

Polarization Photovoltage Transistor enabling Amplified Responsivity and Sensitivity
Authors: Jiayue Han, Fakun Wang, Chunyu Li, Wenjie Deng, Shi Zhang, Libo Zhang, Fangchen Hu, Zhen Wang, Hongxi Zhou, He Yu, Jun Gou, Zhiming Wu, Zhiming Wang, Yadong Jiang, Qi Jie Wang, Jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the rapid advancement of multi-dimensional detection, there is an urgent demand for next-generation polarization detectors capable of achieving high responsivity, fast speed, and strong polarization sensitivity, metrics that are typically limited by fundamental trade-offs. Two-dimensional (2D) materials offer a promising platform, yet their weak intrinsic anisotropy constrains polarization ratio (PR) and overall device performance. Here, we report a mid-infrared (MIR) polarization photovoltage field-effect transistor (PPFET) based on black phosphorus/molybdenum disulfide (BP/MoS2) heterostructures that combines polarization detection and amplification within a single architecture. By exploiting gate-tunable transconductance in the linear amplification region, the device achieves a PR up to 510 via a “stretching” mechanism, while maintaining a polarization angle sensitivity (PAS) up to ~46.57 mA/(W·degree) and response times down to ~0.8 μs under 3.5 μm illumination. This combination of high polarization sensitivity, responsivity, and speed establishes PPFETs as a powerful platform for high-performance MIR polarization detection and paves the way for compact, high-precision imaging systems.

Introduction

Mid-infrared (MIR) photodetectors are essential for all-day, non-contact sensing because MIR radiation penetrates complex environments more effectively than visible or near-infrared light. When combined with polarization sensitivity, MIR detectors provide an additional layer of information by capturing both thermal radiation and polarization contrast. This capability enhances imaging contrast, accuracy, and robustness, enabling applications in intelligent security1,2, environmental monitoring3,4, autonomous driving5,6, and biomedical diagnostics7,8. However, conventional MIR polarization detections, typically based on division-of-focal-plane (DoFP) approaches, require complex micro-optics and cooling systems, presenting significant barriers to miniaturization and on-chip integration9,10.

Atomically thin 2D materials offer a promising solution for compact, filter-free MIR polarization detectors, paving the way for next-generation on-chip miniaturized polarimeters11–14. Considerable efforts have been made using anisotropic 2D materials such as BP15,16, AsP17,18, Te19,20, and PdSe221,22. Despite these efforts, their intrinsically weak optical anisotropy typically limits the polarization ratio (PR) to below 10, restricting their utility in high-contrast, directionally sensitive imaging. Strategies including photogating architectures23,24, heterostructure engineering25–30, ferroelectric modulation31, amplification system32–35, and artificial bulk photovoltaic effects36,37 have increased PR values above 100. Nevertheless, these methods often involve trade-offs, such as increased thermal noise, reduced photocurrent, or slower response speeds, which compromise angle discrimination responsivity and temporal resolution. Moreover, the conventional PR metric overlooks responsivity and dynamic response23,29,38–41, underestimating the real performance required for practical polarization-resolved applications. Achieving simultaneously high PR, high responsivity, and fast response in the MIR regime remains a key challenge, but it is crucial for advancing wide-field, high-resolution, and multi-dimensional sensing42–46.

In this work, we tackle the intrinsic challenges by introducing a BP/MoS2 polarization photovoltage field-effect transistor (PPFET) that integrates polarization detection and response amplification in a single platform. Leveraging the anisotropic photovoltaic response at the BP/MoS2 interface, the device achieves a PR of 510 through a gate-tunable “stretching” effect in the transconductance regime. The junction gate enables significant tuning of key performance parameters by dynamically controlling the channel's depletion region. The band alignment and output characteristics of the PPFET are comprehensively modeled using technology computer-aided design (TCAD) simulations. The device exhibits typical N-channel depletion-mode transistor characteristics and achieves a balanced figure of merit in terms of polarization angle sensitivity (PAS) and response time ( ~ 46.57 mA/(W·degree) and ~0.8 μs, respectively) under 3.5 μm illumination. Moreover, we demonstrate four-pixel MIR polarization imaging in amplification mode at 3.5 μm, validating its accuracy for linear-polarization-angle discrimination. These results highlight the potential of PPFETs as a promising platform for next-generation, on-chip, high-performance, miniaturized MIR polarization detection and imaging.

Design and working principles of PPFET

In our proposed 2D-based polarization PPFETs, the atomically clean interface (Fig. 1b and Supplementary Fig. 1) and the intrinsic anisotropic photovoltage generation in the MIR regime significantly enhance the functional versatility of the device. Specifically, we integrate a polarization detector with an in-plane voltage amplifier, enabling the simultaneous optimization of multiple key performance metrics in a unified platform (Supplementary Fig. 2). The heterostructure consists of an anisotropic BP/isotropic MoS2 type-II junction, with BP acting as the MIR absorber and MoS2 as the conduction channel (Fig. 1c). The photovoltaic-induced electrostatic field dynamics of PPFET

Next, the photoelectric dynamics of PPFET are thoroughly investigated by the TCAD simulation based on the careful consideration of the actual sample. As mentioned above, a MIR-photosensitive gate (top BP shown in Fig. 2a) will significantly screen charge transfer into the channel, leading to a light-sensitive built-in field (rectifying) modulation (Fig. 2b and c). Naturally, the presence of a Schottky barrier at the MoS2/electrode interface must be ruled out first, so as not to affect the gate-controlled modulation of the channel (Supplementary Fig. 5). After the contact of the MoS2 and BP, the carrier concentration of the channel declines with dark current suppression (Fig. 2b). A type-II BP/MoS2 heterojunction is formed, constituting a typical P-N junction configuration (Supplementary Figs. 6 and 7). Under 3.5 μm illumination at 0° polarization (along BP’s armchair direction), photocarriers are generated exclusively in BP (Fig. 2a), inducing a photovoltage across the interface (Supplementary Fig. 8a, b). Under this condition (Fig. 2c, g), we further consider the polarization-dependent carrier redistribution induced by incident light. For 90°-polarized illumination, the generated Vph is relatively small (Supplementary Fig. 8a) and is insufficient to significantly perturb the channel switching condition; as a result, the effective transconductance approaches zero. This strongly suppresses the photocurrent contribution of the 90° polarization component. In contrast, under 0°-polarized illumination, a much larger Vph is generated, which can effectively modulate the channel current and is further amplified by the field-effect gain mechanism. Consequently, by properly tuning the junction-gate voltage, simultaneous enhancement of the PR and responsivity (R) can be achieved.

Discussion

We have designed and fabricated a PPFET based on BP/MoS2 heterostructure. In this configuration, the PV-induced electrostatic field effect plays the dominant role, inhibiting charge injection to modulate channel conductance, thereby ensuring high responsivity without sacrificing speed. Furthermore, the distribution feature of distinct transconductance significantly affects the output PR. Due to the gate-tunable feature of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${g}_{m}$$\end{document}gm, the PR is also reconfigurable from ~8.6 to ~510. Consequently, by proper electrical modulation at amplification mode, this PPFET effectively and simultaneously optimize the figures of merit of detector at MIR regime (3.5 μm) including PR ( ~ 510), PAS ( ~ 46.57 mA/(W·degree)) and speed ( ~ 0.8 μs), bolstering the rapid high sensitivity polarization imaging and meeting the emerging demands of room-temperature multi-dimensional detection systems

Methods

Materials and Device Fabrication

The as-used 2D MoS2 and BP flakes were obtained by mechanical exfoliation of bulk crystals. The BP and MoS2 bulk crystals were supplied by Shanghai Onway Technology Co., Ltd. and 2D Semiconductors, respectively. To improve the quality of the PPFET, we use the graphene-coated Au electrodes to form the clean MoS2 channel. Then, the BP was transferred onto the channel cross over the short side of MoS2 and another side onto the Au electrode working as the top junction gate. All these processes were carried out on a SiO2 substrate with highly n-doped silicon under a nitrogen atmosphere, and the device was finally encapsulated with hBN. The as-exfoliated 2D BP and MoS2 were transferred by the dry method via poly(dimethylsiloxane) (PDMS). The thicknesses of the MoS2 channel and BP absorber are approximately 5.5 nm and 51 nm, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Device characterization

The polarized Raman spectrum is performed by a Raman spectrometer system (WItec alpha 300) using a 532 nm laser with a focused diameter of 1 μm. The BP sample is rotated with a step angle of 10° by rotating the component at the confocal platform. The BP flakes sample is on the Au substrate starting from AC direction of BP (0°), and the polarization optical properties of BP was collected by a FTIR spectrometer (Bruker). The PPFET related thickness of 2D materials were characterized by optical microscopy (Nikon) and atomic force microscopy (Bruker). Cross-sectional TEM was obtained using a Helios NanoLab 600i.

Measurement

All the optoelectrical curves of the MIR PPFET were measured by a PDA FS-Pro semiconductor analyzer with a static shielding box, and the responses from 3.5 μm were measured by an optical parametric oscillator (OPO) with intrinsic polarized light output. The spot diameter of the 3.5 μm laser is focused around 70 μm by a zinc selenide focus lens. The 3.5 μm half-wave plate (WPLH05M-3500, produced by Thorlabs) is used to generate various linear polarization states. The frequency-dependent noise currents of the device are generated by the embedded modules of the FS pro PDA. The pulsed light measurements were conducted by introducing a chopper at the focal position of the OPO laser optical path. An optical chopper (Model 300CD, Scitec Instruments) was used to modulate the mid-infrared light, and different slit (grating) densities (e.g., 2, 30, and 445 slits) were employed, enabling a maximum chopping frequency of up to 47,000 Hz.

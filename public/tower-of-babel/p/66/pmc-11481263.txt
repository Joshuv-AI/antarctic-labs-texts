Impact of FCGR2A rs1801274 and IL-6R rs2228145 polymorphisms on tocilizumab response in the Iranian population with severe COVID-19
Authors: Nastaran Injinari, Samira Asadollahi, Fateme Sefid, Maedeh Arshadi, Saeedeh Sadat Hosseini, Hamed Ghoshouni, Fatemeh Soltani, Nasim Namiranian, Mohammad Hasan Sheikhha, Fatemeh Aghaeimeybodi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough several genetic biomarkers have been reported in the tocilizumab (TCZ) response in rheumatoid arthritis, no studies have addressed the pharmacogenomics effect of TCZ in COVID-19.MethodsIn this prospective longitudinal study, 95 individuals with severe COVID-19 were selected between 2020–2022. The recovery process was measured at 24 h, 48 h, and 10 days before and after taking TCZ. All participants were genotyped using RFLP-PCR. Different genotypes of FCGR2A rs1801274 and IL-6R rs2228145 were compared in terms of the recovery process.Results43.2% of patients were male and 56.8% were female with an average age of 58.20(± 16.214) years. The GA genotype for FCGR2A rs1801274 increased the risk of death (OR = 2.83, P = 0.038) and ventilation (OR = 2.71, P = 0.047) in TCZ-treated individuals. However, there was no risk of death and ventilation with IL-6R rs2228145 (P > 0.05). Additionally, docking analysis showed that not only IL6R but also FCGR2A can be a ligand for TCZ.ConclusionThis study provides valuable insights into the impact of genetic variations on the response rate of TCZ in COVID-19 patients. The GA genotype for FCGR2A rs1801274 was associated with poor treatment outcomes.Keywords: Tocilizumab, COVID-19, Polymorphism, FCGR2A, IL-6 receptor

Introduction

Since December 2019, highly infectious COVID-19 has resulted in approximately 2.7 million deaths, with a significant economic impact worldwide [1]. Based on the Iran Health Insurance Organization, Iran reported nearly one million confirmed cases of COVID-19, with 94,978 deaths and 822,220 recoveries during 2020–2022 [2]. The Center for Disease Control and Prevention of the Ministry of Health and Medical Education in Iran has implemented several initiatives to control the outbreak, including proactive clinical screening preferably via online or phone platforms, management of limited paraclinical resources, and emphasizing outpatient care for mild-to-moderate cases, whether confirmed or suspected, with regular follow-up appointments [3].

Tocilizumab (TCZ) is an IL-6 receptor antagonist approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) [4]. The most commonly reported side effects are infections (such as tuberculosis and opportunistic infections), nausea, high blood pressure, elevated liver enzymes, gastrointestinal perforation, and headaches [5]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, TCZ attracted much attention for treating the cytokine storm caused by COVID-19 [6]. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 24 articles showed that although TCZ reduces the risk of requiring a ventilator in hospitalized COVID-19 patients, the association between TCZ and mortality is low [7]. In August 2020, the results of a multicenter observational study in 360 patients with COVID-19 requiring hospitalization in the ICU showed a significant relationship between receiving TCZ and reducing death [8]. On the other hand, another systematic review and meta-analysis study showed that the mortality rate, the risk of admission to the ICU, and requiring a ventilator in patients with COVID-19 were similar between TCZ and control groups [9]. Therefore, these conflicting results can be explained by a heterogeneous population and highlight the role of pharmacogenetics in the management of COVID-19 disease.

Recently, pharmacogenomics has attracted a lot of attention for selecting proper drugs for personalized therapy [10]. Variations in the genes coding for drug-metabolizing enzymes, drug transporters, or drug targets can result in different responses from patients [11]. Additionally, based on an in silico study, it was revealed that several polymorphisms in the genes related to the immune system, such as IL-6, CD69, and FCGR3, are related to the TCZ effect [12].

One of the key features of COVID-19 is cytokine release syndrome and inflammation, especially in patients with severe illness. Accumulating evidence suggests that interleukin-6 (IL-6), as one of the most important pro-inflammatory cytokines, can be induced by COVID-19. COVID-19 leads to an increase in a higher level of T lymphocytes, resulting in elevated production of IL-6 and launching cytokine storms [13]. Since IL-6 is a druggable target, blockers of the IL-6 signaling pathway can be a suitable strategy for COVID-19 management [14].

Fc gamma receptor IIa (FCGR2A) encodes one of the immunoglobulin Fc receptor genes found on the surface of many immune cells, such as macrophages, neutrophils, and B cells [15]. It has been found that polymorphisms of this gene are associated with many immune diseases [16, 17]. In a study, it was revealed that FCGR2A polymorphism was associated with clinical outcomes in breast cancers treated with trastuzumab [16]. Therefore, identifying genetic variations and their impact on individual-centered drug treatment reduces the reverse side effects of drugs and increases their effectiveness [18]. Hence, this study aimed to evaluate the relationship between FCGR2A rs1801274 and IL-6R rs2228145 polymorphisms and the response rate of TCZ in individuals with severe forms of COVID-19 disease.

Study population

The present study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Shahid Sadoughi University of Medical Sciences, Yazd, Iran (code: IR.SSU.REC.1400.058). All participants signed an informed consent form, and the study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

This study was a prospective longitudinal and prognosis evaluation study that recruited 95 patients diagnosed with COVID-19 via RT-PCR, who were in the severe phase of the disease. These patients, aged between 18 and 65, were hospitalized in the intensive care unit (ICU) and administered TCZ (Actemera). Participants were enrolled from Shahid Sadoughi Hospital, Yazd, Iran, between November 2020 and September 2022.

According to the eighth version of the “Diagnostic Therapeutic Flowchart for COVID-19 (DTFC)” released by the Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) of Iran’s Ministry of Health and Medical Education (MOHME) available online at https://irimc.org/news/id/45316, the criteria for entering the severe stage included:

1- Progress respiratory symptoms, especially worsening of distress syndrome

1- Progress respiratory symptoms, especially worsening of distress syndrome

2- Tachypnea (RR > 30 per minute)

2- Tachypnea (RR > 30 per minute)

3- PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 300 mm Hg

3- PaO2/FiO2 ≤ 300 mm Hg

The exclusion criteria were: pregnant women, patients with autoimmune diseases, thrombocytopenia (platelet count < 100,000/mm3), neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count < 1500/mm3), presence of obvious infection in any system of the body or procalcitonin higher than 0.3 ng/mL, ICU stay of less than 24 h, and patients who were intubated or on a ventilator [19, 20].

To achieve our primary outcome, we collected laboratory test information from patients at three different time points: 24 h, 48 h, and 10 days before TCZ administration, as well as 24 h, 48 h, and 10 days after receiving a single dose of 400 mg of TCZ. The data was systematically recorded in checklists, and we calculated the mean and standard deviation for each parameter.

Primary outcomes included the levels of hemoglobin (Hb), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), white blood cells (WBC), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR). Secondary outcomes included mortality and the requirement for mechanical ventilation [21, 22].

SNP genotyping and DNA preparation

Peripheral blood samples (3–5 mL/each) were collected from the subjects in ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) collection tubes. Genomic DNA was isolated using a commercial DNA extraction kit (Cinnagen, Tehran, Iran) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Extracted DNA was quantitated by absorbance spectrophotometry using a NanoDrop™ One spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA).

Two different exonic SNPs (rs2228145, FCGR2A rs1801274) within the IL6R and FCGR2A genes were selected based on the PharmGKB database (www.pharmgkb.org) and Drug-SNPing (http://bio.kuas.edu.tw/drug-snping/).

These SNPs were chosen based on a minor allele frequency (MAF) of > 0.2 in the South Asian population, as reported by the UCSC Genome Browser (https://genome.ucsc.edu/), Ensembl (www.ensembl.org), 1000 Genomes (https://www.internationalgenome.org/), and Iranome (www.inanome.ir) databases.

The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) technique was performed to amplify a target gene fragment with the selected polymorphism, and the PCR products were run through 2% agarose gel to ensure proper amplification. Subsequently, SNPs were genotyped by the polymerase chain reaction-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR–RFLP) method.

Since there were no restriction sites close to FCGR2A rs1801274, a mismatched primer was designed to create new restriction sites and induct available restriction enzymes for recognizing the target SNP [23]. Therefore, a mismatch (T > G) at 2 bp downstream of FCGR2A rs1801274 was produced to develop an FCGR2A-specific primer. Other PCR primers were designed with the online primer v3.0 (http://primer3.ut.ee/) and synthesized by Metabion International AG (Planegg, Germany). The primer sequences used in PCR were as follows:

IL6R (Forward: GGTTGGCAGAGCTGTTTCAT, Reverse:TGTGTTGTGGTGTGTGCATT)

IL6R (Forward: GGTTGGCAGAGCTGTTTCAT, Reverse:TGTGTTGTGGTGTGTGCATT)

FCGR2A (Forward: GAAAATCCCAGAAATTCTCCCATGTG, Reverse: ACCTCAGCCCTTGTCCATCC)

FCGR2A (Forward: GAAAATCCCAGAAATTCTCCCATGTG, Reverse: ACCTCAGCCCTTGTCCATCC)

To amplify the segment with intended SNPs, after an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, 30 cycles were performed consisting of 30 s at 95 °C, 30 s at 60 °C, and 30 s at 72 °C, followed by a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min.

10 µL of PCR product was digested with HinfI and NlaIII restriction enzymes (Thermo Fisher Scientific Baltics, Vilnius, Lithuania) for IL-6R rs2228145 and FCGR2A rs1801274, respectively. Digestion was carried out at 37 °C overnight. Restriction fragments were analyzed using 2.5% safe-stained agarose gel. The digested products were electrophoresed on pre-stained 2.5% (w/v) agarose gel at 70 V/cm2, and the results were documented. Some samples were randomly sequenced to confirm the results.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis was performed using Stata 17. A sample size of 95 patients provided an 80% power to detect outcome differences. The student’s t-test was used for quantitative variables. The association between the SNPs and drug response was estimated by computing odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) from logistic regression analyses. All graphs were drawn using GraphPad Prism 8. Age and sex were considered confounding factors. Statistical significance in the paired T-test was P < 0.005 and for the other tests was P < 0.05.

3D modelling

Protein Homology/analogy Recognition Engine V 2.0 (Phyre 2) was used to predict three-dimensional models for variations of IL6R and FCGR2A proteins (C.1073 A > C, P.358 Asp > Ala and C.500 A > G, P.167 His > Arg). Phyre2 (http://www.sbg.bio.ic.ac.uk/phyre2/html/page.cgi?id=index) uses the alignment of hidden Markov models via HHsearch to significantly improve the accuracy of alignment and detection rates. Phyre2 also incorporates a new ab initio folding simulation called Poing to model regions of proteins with no detectable homology to known structures.

The 3D structure of TCZ was determined by SAbPred (Anti BodyBuilder V 2.0) (http://opig.stats.ox.ac.uk/webapps/sabdab-sabpred/WelcomeSAbPred.php), an antibody modeling tool and prediction software from the Oxford Protein Informatics Group (OPIG)). SAbPred automatically selects the best template structures from the PDB (Protein Data Bank) to model the antibody structure based on sequence similarity. The default parameters provided by SAbPred were used for this modeling process, ensuring standard and optimized predictions: Framework modeling: Standard template-based modeling for the antibody’s framework region. CDR Loop Modeling: Accurate loop modeling using canonical structures and ab initio loop prediction for the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs).

Ab Initio Folding Simulation: Poing simulation was employed to model regions with no detectable homology to known structures.

The predicted 3D model of TCZ was compared with existing experimentally solved structures in the PDB database to ensure accuracy. Structural alignment and RMSD (Root Mean Square Deviation) calculations were performed to assess the similarity between the predicted model and known structures.

Prediction of functional sites using patch analysis

InterProSurf (http://curie.utmb.edu/pattest9.html) was used to predict functional sites and interacting residues on the protein surface using patch analysis.

Docking analyses

The 3D structure of each protein and TCZ was used as input for High Ambiguity Driven biomolecular DOCKing (HADDOCK) server version 2.2 (http://haddock.science.uu.nl/services/HADDOCK/haddock.php). HADDOCK works based on biochemical or biophysical properties and is an information-driven flexible docking server for the modeling of biomolecular complexes. HADDOCK distinguishes itself from ab-initio docking methods by running the docking process and coding data from known protein interfaces or predicted models in ambiguous interaction restraints (AIRs). Residues 86, 87, 110, 113, 155, and 160 were selected as Active residues (directly involved in the interaction) in the FCGR2A structure. Residues 464,465,466 and 467 selected as active residues (directly involved in the interaction) in the IL6R structure. Residues were chosen as active residues (directly involved in the interaction) in TCZ monoclonal antibody are 98, 100, 104, 107, 108, 109,168, 169, 171, 172, 173,174, 213, 215, 216, 217. HADDOCK defines passive residues automatically around the active residues. Docking work steps during the protein–protein docking in the HADDOCK webserver include: Topologies and structures generation, Rigid body energy minimization, Semi-flexible simulated annealing, and Flexible refinement in explicit solvent (water or DMSO).

Prediction of functional sites using patch analysis

The type of fold and domains present in FCGR2A and IL6R proteins were checked out by the conserved domain database in NCBI. Most of the FCGR2A sequence is related to the IG superfamily and most of the IL6R sequence is related to IG and FN3 superfamily.

Immunoglobulin domain; The members here are composed of the immunoglobulin (Ig) domain found in the Ig superfamily. The Ig superfamily is a heterogenous group of proteins, built on a common fold comprised of a sandwich of two beta sheets. Members of this group are components of immunoglobulin, neuroglia, cell surface glycoproteins, including T-cell receptors, CD2, CD4, CD8, and membrane glycoproteins, including butyrophilin and chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan core protein. A predominant feature of most Ig domains is a disulfide bridge connecting the two beta-sheets with a tryptophan residue packed against the disulfide bond. Ig superfamily (IgSF) domains can be divided into 4 main classes based on their structures and sequences: the Variable (V), Constant 1 (C1), Constant 2 (C2), and Intermediate (I) sets. Typically, the V-set domains have A, B, E, and D strands in one sheet and A’, G, F, C, C’ and C” in the other. The structures in the C1-set are smaller than those in the V-set; they have one beta-sheet that is formed by strands A, B, E, and D and the other by strands G, F, C, and C’. Moreover, a C1-set Ig domain contains a short C’ strand (three residues) and lacks A’ and C” strands. Unlike other Ig domain sets, C2-set structures do not have a D strand. Like the V-set Ig domains, members of the I-set have a discontinuous A strand, but lack a C” strand.

Fibronectin type 3 domain; One of three types of internal repeats found in the plasma protein fibronectin. Its tenth fibronectin type III repeat contains an RGD cell recognition sequence in a flexible loop between 2 strands. Approximately 2% of all animal proteins contain the FN3 repeat; including extracellular and intracellular proteins, membrane-spanning cytokine receptors, growth hormone receptors, tyrosine phosphatase receptors, and adhesion molecules. FN3-like domains are also found in bacterial glycosyl hydrolases.

Discussion

Our study investigated the relationship between the genetic polymorphisms FCGR2A rs1801274 and IL-6R rs2228145 in severe COVID-19 patients.

The laboratory findings demonstrated a significant increase in LDH, WBC, and NLR levels and a significant decrease in Hb levels following TCZ administration, which are key markers associated with disease severity and immune response. These findings provide important insights into the varying response to TCZ treatment based on genetic polymorphisms in FCGR2A rs1801274 and IL6R rs2228145. For example, although there was an overall trend of increased LDH levels post-TCZ administration, this trend was only significant in patients with the AA genotype of FCGR2A rs1801274 or IL6R rs2228145.

Moreover, our results showed the GA genotype for FCGR2A rs1801274 increased the risk of death and ventilation. Surprisingly, our results from docking analysis showed that FCGR2A can be a ligand for TCZ.

TCZ is a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody of immunoglobulin G (IgG) that targets IL-6 receptors [24, 25]. Moreover, Fc-gamma receptors recognize the constant fraction of IgG and have a crucial role in immune responses [26]. FCGR2A is located on chromosome 1q23 where two alleles of FCGR2A generate two isoforms related to Histidine (His131) or Arginine (Arg131) amino acid at the position of 131 located in the extracellular domain of FCGR2A. The His131 isoform has a low affinity for IgG2, and the Arg131 isoform is known as a high-responder [27]. It has been revealed that FCGR2A rs1801274 genotype classes lead to different IgG responses in cytokine responses and gene expression patterns in immune cells [28]. Therefore, FCGR2A rs1801274 may alter the binding affinity of TCZ to its target receptor through molecular mechanisms that influence IgG signaling, leading to differential downstream signaling and cytokine regulation. Previous studies have investigated the association between several polymorphisms and TCZ effect for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis [29–31], but relevant information linked to COVID-19 remains absent. For the first time in 2013, researchers reported that eight loci are involved in response to TCZ for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis [30]. González-Medina et al. revealed that FCGR2A rs1801274 can be a genetic marker for TCZ efficacy in patients with rheumatoid arthritis [32]. Understanding the role of the FCGR2A rs1801274 polymorphism can provide insights into the genetic effects on drug responses and disease treatments, which may help improve personalized therapies for diseases. However, further research is needed to explore the precise impacts and molecular mechanisms of this polymorphism in different diseases and various drug treatments.

The IL-6R rs2228145 polymorphism in the IL6R gene on chromosome 1q21.3 has attracted attention due to its potential impact on IL-6 signaling and its role in the efficacy of TCZ treatment. The IL-6R rs2228145 (IL-6R Asp358Ala variant) results in a placement of aspartic acid or alanine in position 358 [33]. The IL-6 receptor plays a critical role in mediating immune responses and inflammation and is a significant target for TCZ therapy in severe COVID-19 cases. There are conflicting studies on the effect of IL-6R rs2228145 on the TCZ response. Enevold et al. reported that rs12083537, rs2228145, and rs4329505 related to the IL-6R gene can be predictors of response to TCZ in rheumatoid arthritis [29]. Conversely, in a study by Sainz et al., it was found that there was no relation between IL-6R rs2228145 and response to TCZ in rheumatoid arthritis patients [34]. Our results showed that there was no association between IL-6R rs2228145 and the response rate of TCZ in severe COVID-19 patients. Moreover, the small difference in HADDOCK scores suggests that the binding affinity of TCZ to the forms of IL6R proteins might be similar, which aligns with our observed lack of significant differential clinical response. Lutfiyeet al. investigated the correlation between polymorphism IL-6R rs2228145 and COVID-19 mortality rates among 23 countries. They reported that there was no significant correlation between IL-6R rs2228145 and the prevalence of COVID-19 and mortality rates for all countries [35]. However, the observed lack of a relationship between IL-6R rs2228145 and the TCZ response in our study raises important considerations. The immune response to severe COVID-19 is a complex interplay of various cytokines, signaling pathways, and genetic factors [36, 37]. While IL-6R rs2228145 could potentially influence IL-6 receptor signaling, other genetic and environmental factors might modulate the overall immune response and treatment outcomes [37]. It is important to note that the impact of IL-6R rs2228145 could be influenced by genetic interactions, disease heterogeneity, and environmental factors. On the other side, TCZ does not lead to a therapeutic response only by blocking the IL-6 pathway; it can affect epigenetic pathways, and the role of epigenetic modifications cannot be disregarded [38, 39]. These modifications are sensitive to environmental factors and could contribute to the variability observed in treatment responses. Additionally, the relatively small sample size of our study might have limited our ability to detect a subtle relationship between the genetic variant and TCZ response. Genetic associations often require larger and more diverse study populations to reach statistically significant conclusions.

To gain a comprehensive understanding of the role of IL-6R rs2228145 in TCZ response for severe COVID-19, future research endeavors should consider larger sample sizes, encompassing genetic analyses, and the incorporation of diverse clinical and environmental variables. The intricate nature of genetic influences on treatment response underscores the importance of a holistic approach toward personalized medicine.

Although our study provides insights into the pharmacogenomic factors influencing TCZ response in severe COVID-19, it has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size, while sufficient for initial observations, may not fully capture the variability in patient responses due to genetic and clinical heterogeneity. Larger studies are needed to confirm these findings and explore additional genetic markers that may influence treatment response. Secondly, our study was conducted in a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the results to other populations and healthcare settings. Additionally, the lack of a control group is a major limitation. Having a control group that has not received TCZ would help to better demonstrate the exact role of polymorphisms in influencing TCZ response among COVID-19 patients.

Conclusion

This study provides evidence that genetic polymorphisms can significantly influence the TCZ response in treating severe COVID-19 patients. Specifically, our findings indicate that the FCGR2A rs1801274 polymorphism plays a critical role in determining patient outcomes, with the GA genotype associated with an increased risk of mortality and need for mechanical ventilation. Moreover, FCGR2A was identified as a ligand for TCZ. Understanding the molecular mechanisms behind these polymorphisms could aid in personalizing treatment approaches and improving therapeutic outcomes in severe COVID-19 cases.

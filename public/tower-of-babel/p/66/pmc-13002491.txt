Utilizing Electronic Health Record–Based Alerts to Increase Prescription of Lipid-Lowering Therapies in Patients Admitted With Acute Coronary Syndromes
Authors: Spencer Rowland, Sarah Adie, Chris Zimmerman, Adam Stein, Kent Brummel
Published: 2026

Lipid-lowering therapy is a cornerstone of secondary prevention following acute coronary syndromes (ACS). However, despite robust evidence and clear guideline recommendations, national registry data and multiple studies continue to show suboptimal prescription of statin intensification and adjunctive therapies at discharge for these patients. National data indicate that fewer than 50% of ACS patients receive appropriate statin intensification at discharge, and fewer than 15% are prescribed adjunctive therapies like ezetimibe, highlighting a significant gap in guideline-based care.1, 2, 3 Ezetimibe monotherapy lowers low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C) by 15% to 20%, and when added to a statin, it produces similar levels of LDL-C reduction with a corresponding decrease in cardiovascular events in high-risk patients.4 Furthermore, there has been increasing advocacy and studies suggesting earlier intensification of therapy may improve plaque stabilization and reduce recurrent events.5, 6, 7 To address this, we implemented a real-time, interruptive electronic health record (EHR) alert to optimize lipid-lowering therapy before discharge and improve adherence to secondary prevention guidelines.

Project Rationale

Recognizing a critical gap in lipid management for patients hospitalized with ACS, we sought to develop a clinical decision support tool to enable timely, evidence-based therapy intensification prior to discharge. Providers are often unaware of a patient's baseline lipid values or face competing clinical demands that limit time for guideline-directed intensification. These barriers contribute to missed opportunities to optimize therapy during a pivotal window for secondary prevention. To address this, we designed an EHR-based alert to prompt provider action and standardize lipid evaluation during hospitalization. The alert displayed the patient's most recent lipid panel and offered evidence-based suggestions for therapy intensification, including statin optimization and adjunctive therapy initiation (with the goal of achieving LDL-C <70 mg/dL per guidelines). By presenting this information in a streamlined, user-friendly format, the alert aimed to enhance efficiency, support clinical decision-making, and improve adherence to guideline-recommended lipid management in ACS patients.

Project Description

In September 2021, we launched the real-time, interruptive EHR alert “Our Practice Advisory” (OPA) in the Epic system (Epic Systems, Verona, WI, version November 2024). The alert triggered when providers opened charts for patients admitted with an ICD code indicating ACS (ST elevation myocardial infarction, non-ST elevation myocardial infarction, or unstable angina) (Figure 1). It included an order set to obtain a cholesterol panel if not already available or displayed the patient's most recent lipid results with options for statin intensification or ezetimibe initiation. This alert was monitored for its impact on prescribing behavior, comparing patients where the alert was accepted vs not. To further evaluate changes, we also analyzed prescribing patterns from 6 months prior. This provided us with stable patient baseline lipid-level trends and minimized short-term fluctuation.Figure 1Alert as Seen by Providers in EHR

Project Deliverables

This quality-improvement initiative resulted in several key deliverables aimed at enhancing lipid-lowering therapy in hospitalized ACS patients.

To start, we successfully built and implemented a real-time OPA within the Epic system to enhance lipid-lowering therapy for ACS patients. The OPA was configured to trigger when a provider accessed the chart of a patient admitted with ACS. The criterion for triggering the OPA are outlined in Figure 1. The third trigger criterion was met if any of the following applied: 1) no high-intensity statin at home or as an inpatient; 2) high-intensity statin at home and as an inpatient, but no ezetimibe; or 3) no high-intensity statin at home but receiving it as an inpatient. The fourth criterion reviewed both outpatient and inpatient records, and the alert would only fire if the patient was not on both ezetimibe and a high-intensity statin in either setting, while also meeting the applicable conditions from criterion 3. If triggered and accepted, the alert displayed the patient's most recent lipid panel results and provided embedded, guideline-based options for therapy intensification, including initiation or adjustment of statins and the addition of ezetimibe.

Next, we utilized an internal data-tracking infrastructure to alert triggers, acceptance rates, and downstream prescribing behavior. This allowed for a detailed analysis of both direct (alert-accepted) and indirect (alert-overridden but therapy intensified) outcomes.

Lastly, we analyzed 6-month preimplementation data to assess the impact the alert had on nudging institutional prescribing patterns, with particular focus on the rates of statin and ezetimibe therapy utilization. This helped us assess the change the alert had on the utilization of secondary prevention therapy at our institution. These deliverables collectively supported the implementation, evaluation, and refinement of a scalable intervention to improve secondary prevention in ACS care.

Project Outcome, Impact, and Future Directions

Between September 2021 and March 2025, 612 patients triggered the alert. Of these, 126 (20.6%) alerts were accepted, while 486 (79.4%) were overridden. The mean age was 69 years (60.6-77.8), 62.1% were male, 79.7% White, and 9.6% Black. Demographics were generally similar between groups, though patients in the accept group were slightly older (median age 69.6 vs 68.2 years, P < 0.001) and less likely to be male (30.95% vs 39.71%, P = 0.043). Among accepted alerts, 38 (30.2%) led to statin initiation, 16 (12.7%) to a change in statin, and 18 (14.3%) to a dose increase. In the override group, of the 486 patients, 137 (28.2%) had a statin initiated, 35 (7.2%) had their statin changed, and 47 (9.7%) had their dose increased. Overall, 102 patients (16.7%) had direct lipid-lowering modification or intensification, with an additional 273 patients (44.6%) identified by the alert also receiving therapeutic intensification.

We also examined lipid-lowering therapy use among eligible ACS patients hospitalized 6 months prior to alert implementation. A total of 303 patients were analyzed preintervention, of whom 146 (48.2%) had therapy intensification—136 (44.9%) via statin modification or addition and 10 (3.3%) via ezetimibe initiation. Compared to the postintervention group, ezetimibe addition (3.3% increasing to 13.7%, P = 0.002) and intensification or addition of statin therapy (44.9% increasing to 55.1% P = 0.016) were significantly different (Figure 3). Future efforts for the alert will focus on improving access to other lipid-lowering therapies such as PCSK-9 inhibitors or bempedoic acid for eligible patients admitted for ACS as they discharge from the hospital.

Discussion

This project demonstrates that a real-time EHR alert can significantly influence provider prescribing behavior and improve adherence to guideline-directed lipid-lowering therapy in patients hospitalized with ACS. Although the alert was accepted in only 20.6% of cases, over 60% of patients experienced therapy intensification, suggesting a meaningful indirect effect. Notably, prescriptions for ezetimibe were significantly higher in the alert-accepted group, indicating that the intervention may be particularly effective at prompting consideration of nonstatin therapies.

Importantly, when evaluating the impact of the intervention in context, 6-month preintervention data revealed substantially lower rates of therapy intensification. These findings highlight the clinical significance of the intervention, demonstrating that a simple EHR-based tool can lead to meaningful improvements in secondary prevention, consistent with findings from the PROMPT-Lipid trial.8

Several factors likely contributed to the intervention's success. The alert was well-timed and appeared during active chart use. It was also actionable, offering embedded order sets for therapy modification. Moreover, the alert likely served as a cognitive prompt as even when overridden, it contributed to downstream behavior change. These results are consistent with prior literature showing that EHR-based nudges can close gaps in care and improve adherence to evidence-based guidelines.9

Despite these findings, limitations should be noted. First, alert fatigue remains a concern, as reflected by an override rate of nearly 80%. Second, we were unable to assess patient adherence to prescribed lipid-lowering therapy after discharge, which could affect the long-term impact of the intervention. In addition, reasons for overriding the OPA were not captured, limiting our understanding of provider decision-making. We also observed lower utilization of the alert among patients with LDL levels below 100 mg/dL, suggesting practice patterns may vary by baseline lipid values. Finally, our study was a retrospective cohort analysis rather than a randomized trial, which precludes causal inference.

Future iterations of this intervention will aim to refine alert logic to increase specificity and clinical relevance. In addition, while our alert currently focuses on statins and ezetimibe, future efforts will include expanded access to other therapies such as PCSK9 inhibitors and bempedoic acid for high-risk patients as they transition out of the hospital.

Conclusions

An EHR alert targeting lipid management in ACS patients improved the prescription rates of both statins and ezetimibe, even in the setting of low direct alert acceptance. The system demonstrated sustainability and a lasting impact on prescribing behavior, particularly for adjunctive therapy. These results support the broader use of EHR-based alerts in achieving guideline-concordant care for high-risk cardiovascular populations.Visual SummaryQuality Improvement Initiative Summary

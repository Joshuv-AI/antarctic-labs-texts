TRIM7-mediated autophagy–lysosomal signaling is essential for preimplantation development in mice
Authors: Sarmir Khan, Fen Yan, Wenbo Li, Jia Guo, Xiaoxiao Gu, Zhenmin Tang, Uzma Bibi, Xin Dong, Lingling Chen, Yage Yao, Jiawei Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPre-implantation development in mammals requires precise coordination of zygote genome activation (ZGA), cleavage divisions, and early lineage specification. These events rely on tightly regulated transcriptional programs, proteostasis, and organelle quality control. TRIM7, a RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase, is known to modulate immune signaling and cellular stress response; however, its role in early embryogenesis remains undefined.MethodsMouse oocytes and pre-implantation embryos were collected at defined developmental stages. TRIM7 expression and localization were examined by immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy. Functional loss-of-function studies were performed by culturing zygotes with TRIM7-targeting Vivo-Morpholinos (2.5–10 µM). The TRIM7 function was independently validated by microinjection and electroporation of TRIM7-specific siRNA into mouse zygote embryos. Global transcriptional activity was assessed at the late 2-cell stage using 5-ethynyl uridine (EU) incorporation, followed by fluorescence-based detection. Single-cell RNA sequencing was conducted on late 2-cell and arrested 8-cell embryos treated with 3.5 µM Morpholino to identify early transcriptional defects, complemented by mass spectrometry-based proteomics. Key lysosomal transcripts were validated by qRT-PCR, and protein-level changes were confirmed by immunofluorescence. Autophagy flux was assessed by LC3B immunostaining and Western blot analysis. Rapamycin (5-7.5 µM) supplementation was used to evaluate functional rescue.ResultsTRIM7 displayed dynamic spatiotemporal expression, peaking at the 4-cell stage and localizing to both the nucleus and cytoplasm, consistent with a regulatory role in early embryonic signaling. TRIM7 knockdown caused a severe, dose-dependent developmental arrest, with ~ 98% of embryos failing to progress beyond the 8-cell stage at 3.5 µM (p < 0.0001). TRIM7-specific siRNA delivery by both microinjection and electroporation consistently resulted in embryonic arrest at the 8-cell stage, confirming the specificity of TRIM7 depletion. Integrated scRNA-seq and proteomics revealed profound disruption of lysosomal-autophagic pathways, with early repression of lysosomal, autophagy, and spliceosome genes detectable as early as the late 2-cell stage, coinciding with ZGA onset. EU incorporation analysis showed no significant impairment of global transcriptional activation at the 2-cell stage, indicating that zygotic genome activation is largely preserved. Arrested 8-cell embryos exhibited marked downregulation of key lysosomal regulators and impaired autophagic flux, while proteomics indicated reduced levels of essential lysosomal proteins, including CTSL, HEXB, and GM2A. Rapamycin-mediated activation of autophagy resulted in partial but significant developmental rescue, restoring 40–50% blastocyst formation and normalizing the expression of several lysosomal and autophagy markers. LC3B analysis confirmed that TRIM7 depletion suppresses autophagy, whereas Rapamycin effectively reinstates autophagic flux.ConclusionTRIM7 is essential for early mouse embryogenesis, acting independently of ZGA but critically supporting lysosomal-autophagic function required for progression beyond the 8-cell stage and successful blastocyst development. Impaired proteostasis underlies the TRIM7-deficient arrest, while Pharmacological activation of autophagy partially restores embryo viability, highlighting lysosomal-autophagic regulation as a central TRIM7-dependent developmental checkpoint.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-07963-z.

Background

During early embryogenesis, control of development shifts from maternal factors to zygotic gene activation (ZGA) [1]. This maternal-zygotic transition (MZT) involves activation of the embryo’s genome and degradation of maternal RNAs [2]. Before zygotic genome activation, the embryo is largely transcriptionally quiescent and relies on RNAs and proteins deposited in the oocyte [3]. Following fertilization, cleavage divisions produce an 8-cell embryo that undergoes compaction, in which blastomeres increase cell-cell adhesion to form a tight morula [4]. As development proceeds (to ~ 16 cells), this establishes an “inside-outside” polarity: outer cells become apical-basal polarized (trophectoderm precursors) while inner cells remain non-polar (inner cell mass, ICM) [5]. The blastocyst thus consists of an outer trophectoderm and an inner cell mass that later segregates into epiblast and primitive endoderm [6]. The first lineage decisions are coordinated by conserved signaling networks, notably FGF/ERK, Wnt/β-catenin and Hippo pathways, together with epigenetics and transcriptional regulators [7, 8]. Notably, several TRIM family E3 ubiquitin ligases have been reported to modulate MAPK/ERK and Wnt signaling [9, 10], suggesting that TRIM proteins may influence these early cell fate pathways.

The tripartite motif-containing (TRIM) protein family, a class of RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligases, is characterized by an N-terminal RING finger domain, B-box zinc finger domains, and a coiled-coil domain [11]. They can regulate the functions of substrate proteins through ubiquitination modification. Some TRIM members serve as important regulators of innate immunity [12]. For example, TRIM21, TRIM32, and TRIM50 are involved in autophagy-related cellular regulatory processes and clear intracellular pathogens or damaged components by modulating autophagic flux [11, 13]. TRIM28, on the other hand, acts as a transcriptional co-repressor: it maintains cellular signalling homeostasis by recruiting histone-modifying enzymes to inhibit endogenous retroelements [14]. Numerous studies have confirmed that multiple members of the TRIM family play indispensable roles in the early embryonic development of mammals, and their functions are closely associated with key embryonic events [15]. TRIM28 maintains pluripotency by regulating chromatin remodeling, inhibiting the activity of endogenous retroelements, and ensuring embryonic genome stability [16]. Depletion of maternal TRIM28 leads to the arrest of mouse embryos around the implantation stage, accompanied by epigenetic instability and differentiation disorders [17]. TRIM33 participates in the mesoderm differentiation of embryos by regulating the TGF-β/Smad signalling pathway [18, 19]. Its depletion results in defects in embryonic mesoderm development, preventing the formation of normal precursor tissues for muscles, bones, and other tissues. Collectively, these studies establish TRIM proteins as master regulators of protein turnover, transcriptional control, and key signaling pathways fundamental to early embryogenesis.

Within the family, TRIM7 remains poorly characterized in the context of embryogenesis. TRIM7 has been implicated in various cellular functions through its ubiquitination of specific substrate proteins, influencing pathways such as MAPK signaling, autophagy, and innate immune responses [20]. Notably, TRIM7 is known to interact with the glycogenin protein and certain viral proteins, thereby affecting both metabolic and antiviral responses [21, 22]. Recent transcriptomic analysis has suggested that TRIM7 is differentially expressed in tissues with high proliferative activity, and its expression patterns in embryonic tissues hint at a potential regulatory function during early development [14]. Moreover, TRIM7 interaction with signaling mediators like RACO-1 and its ability to regulate protein stability through ubiquitination [23] suggest it could influence transcriptional outputs critical for embryonic lineage specification. Whether TRIM7 exerts these functions via canonical E3 ligase-dependent pathways or through scaffolding interactions remains to be elucidated. However, beyond these contexts, the role of TRIM7 in developmental processes, particularly during the early stages of mammalian embryogenesis, is not fully understood. Given the importance of these pathways in preimplantation development, several key questions arise: Why not study TRIM7 in early embryonic development? How does TRIM7 modulate lysosomal and autophagic functions during ZGA and cell fate determination, and what are the downstream effects of its disruption? What role does TRIM7 play in regulating the transcriptional landscape of early embryos, particularly in the context of cellular fate determination and lineage specification? By exploring these questions, we aim to uncover new insights into the molecular mechanisms governing preimplantation development, shedding light on the potential therapeutic implications for improving embryo viability in assisted reproductive technologies. The investigation of TRIM7’s role offers a novel perspective on how ubiquitin-mediated regulation intersects with key cellular processes like autophagy and lysosomal activity to ensure proper embryogenesis and cell fate decisions.

Materials and methods

Mouse husbandry and embryo collection

All animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (2023-KY-1419). C57BL/6 N mice were maintained under SPF Conditions (22–26 °C, 12-h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food and water. Female C57BL/6 mice aged 6–8 weeks and male mice aged 8–12 weeks were used for all experiments. Female mice were housed in groups; males were single-housed and acclimated for at least three days before experiments.

Female mice were hormonally primed with 10 IU of PMSG (Solarbio, P9970) to obtain oocytes and pre-implantation embryos, followed 46–48 h later by 10 IU of hCG (Qingdao, H20184171) via intraperitoneal injection. For mating, females were co-housed with males (1:1) immediately after hCG administration. Vaginal plugs were checked the next morning to confirm successful mating. Euthanasia was performed using the neck dislocation method, followed by the opening of the abdomen to expose the uterus. Surrounding adipose tissue was removed with forceps, and both slides of the fallopian tube were excised and placed in a 35 mm dish. Embryos were harvested at specific timepoints: GV oocytes (46–48 h post-PMSG), MII oocytes (12–14 h post-hCG), zygotes (26–28 h), 2–8 cell embryos (40–64 h), and blastocysts (84–96 h). Embryos were flushed from the reproductive tract and washed in M2 medium (Sigma, M7167) for downstream applications. All embryos were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2.

Immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy

Embryos at each developmental stage were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and blocked in 1% BSA using standard protocol [20]. Primary antibody against TRIM7 (Abcam, 26285-1-AP) (1:200 dilution) was incubated overnight at 4 °C, followed by Alexa Fluor™ 568-conjugated secondary antibody (1:300) for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Hoechst 33,342 (Thermo Fisher, 62249) was used for nuclear counterstaining. Imaging was performed using a Nikon AX NSPARC confocal microscope, and fluorescence intensity was quantified using standardized parameters.

Knockdown of TRIM7 expression by dual splice-inhibiting and translation-blocking morpholino oligonucleotides

To examine TRIM7 function, translation, and splicing-blocking morpholino oligonucleotide (Mos) targeting TRIM7 was synthesized by Gene Tool (USA) using standard protocol [21]. (TRIM7.MO.1. 5’-CCCACAGTCGCCATCCGTGTCCTTA-3’) and splicing-blocking (TRIM7.MO.2. 5’-CCACACCCAGAGCCCCACCTTTGAA-3’) MO were used to culture embryos, to knock down TRIM7 protein levels. The sequence Ctrl-MO (5’-CCTCTT ACCTCAGTTACA ATT TAT A-3’) was used as the control of TRIM7-MO-1 and TRIM7-MO-2. Embryos were cultured in vitro in G1 medium (Vitrolife) under mineral oil at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. For extended culture to later developmental stages, embryos were transferred to G2 medium (Vitrolife) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Embryos were cultured in G1 and G2 medium with MO concentrations ranging from 2.5 to 10 µM and monitored for morphological changes up to the blastocyst stage. Developmental progression was documented every 12 h. Only morphologically normal embryos at matched developmental stages were used for downstream experiments. Embryos arrested at the 8-cell stage at a 3.5 µM concentration were collected for immunofluorescence and single-cell transcriptome analysis to assess molecular changes following TRIM7 Knockdown.

Embryo microinjection

TRIM7-specific siRNA duplexes were designed and synthesized commercially from Genewiz, China. The sequence of the antisense strand was: 5′-UUAUUCUUCAUCUCAGAUGAGTT-3′ against TRIM7 of the targeted gene. Lyophilized siRNA 2.5 nmol was resuspended in RNase-free water by adding 25 µL to obtain a 100 µM stock solution, which was further diluted to working concentrations of 20 µM, 40 µM, and 80 µM and stored at -20 20 °C until use. Pronuclear-stage mouse zygotes were collected following standard superovulation and mating procedures and cultured in G1 and G2 medium at 37 20 °C under 5% CO₂. siRNA microinjection was performed using a micromanipulation system mounted on an inverted microscope, with TRIM7 siRNA delivered directly into the pronucleus to ensure efficient gene knockdown during early embryogenesis, as described in established embryo RNAi studies. Injection pipettes (0.5–1.5 μm tip diameter) and holding pipettes were prepared using a pipette puller and microforge. Embryos injected with NC (negative control) siRNA 20 µM served as injection controls, while non-injected embryos were used as normal controls. Embryos exhibiting lysis or abnormal morphology after injection were excluded, and the remaining embryos were continuously cultured and monitored for subsequent developmental progression.

Embryo electroporation

TRIM7-specific siRNA was delivered into mouse preimplantation embryos by electroporation using an optimized protocol adopted from established methods for efficient RNA delivery into early embryos. The sequence of the antisense strand was 5′-UUAUUCUUCAUCUCAGAUGAGTT-3′ against TRIM7 of the target gene. Briefly, the zona pellucida was gently weakened with acid Tyrode’s solution to facilitate uptake. Groups of zygote embryos were transferred to an electroporation chamber containing siRNA in Opti-MEM, and short electric pulses (30 V, 1 ms, 3 pulses) were applied to introduce siRNA into the embryos while maintaining high survival and transfection rates. Following electroporation, embryos were washed and cultured in standard embryo culture medium, and the developmental rates were monitored under a microscope.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)

Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on 8-cell and late 2-cell stage mouse embryos following TRIM7 Morpholino (3.5µM) treatment and control. A total of 20 embryos per group were cultured in vitro for 44 h for the 8-cell stage and 16 h for the late 2-cell stage. Embryos were washed in PBS to remove residual culture medium and Morpholino, then lysed, and RNA was reverse transcribed using a single-cell full-length mRNA amplification kit (Vazyme, N712) (8-cell stage 7 embryos per reaction). Full-length cDNA was synthesized and amplified according to the manufacturer’s protocol. On day two, the cDNA was fragmented and indexed using Tn5 transposase-based tagmentation, followed by PCR enrichment (12 cycles). DNA libraries were size-selected using magnetic beads, quantified, and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform.

qRT-PCR validation

qRT-PCR was performed on a QuantStudio™ 5 real-time PCR system using SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystem). Differentially expressed genes from RNA-seq data of 8-cell and late 2-cell stages control and TRIM7-Mo treated embryos, particularly those linked to lysosomal pathways, were chosen to be validated using qRT-PCR. Primers for lysosomal pathway-related genes (Acp5, Acp2, Mcoln1, Cstd, Atp6v0d1, ppt2, Atp6ap1, Gga2, Pla2g15, Smpd1, Hexa, Ctsl) were designed with Primer3 as shown in Tables S1 and S2 and validated for efficiency (90–110%) and specificity by melt-curve analysis. Each 10µL reaction contained 5µL SYBR Green mix, 0.2µL each of forward and reverse primers (10µM), 1µL cDNA, and 3.6µL nuclease-free water. Gapdh was used as the endogenous control. All reactions were run in technical triplicate. Relative expression was calculated by the ΔΔCt method and compared using an unpaired Student’s t-test (p < 0.05).

EU incorporation assay

Embryos were cultured in M2 medium containing 100 µM 5-ethynyl uridine (EU) for 2 h. After culture, fixation, permeabilization, and staining were conducted using Cyanine3 azide (Sigma-Aldrich) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Imaging of embryos was carried out utilizing a Zeiss confocal microscope.

Mass spectrometry-based proteomic analysis

To investigate the proteomic alteration associated with TRIM7 knockdown in early mouse embryonic development, we performed mass spectrometry-based protein profiling of control and arrested 8-cell stage embryos and the same MO concentration (3.5µM) of late 2-cell stage. Both 8-cell and late 2-cell stage embryos (n = 42 per group) from control and 3.5 µM Mo-treated groups (n = 42) were processed. Both groups were washed twice with cold PBS and lysed in lysis buffer containing 8 M urea, 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), and protease inhibitors. Protein extracts were quantified using a BCA assay, and an equal amount of protein was subjected to in-solution tryptic digestion, following established protocols [22]. Peptides were desalted using C18 columns and analyzed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) on a high-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometer. The raw data were processed using MaxQuant (version 1.6.17.0) and searched against the UniProt Mus musculus database. Label-free quantification (LFQ) was performed, and differentially expressed proteins were identified using a cutoff of fold change > 1.5 or < 0.67 and a p-value < 0.05. Bioinformatics analyses, including Gene Ontology (GO), KEGG, Heatmap, and Reactome pathway enrichment, were conducted to identify biological processes and pathways altered by TRIM7-knockdown.

Immunofluorescence IF validation of lysosomal pathway proteins

To validate lysosomal pathway proteins identified by single-cell RNA-seq and mass spectrometry in 8-cell stage embryos, immunofluorescence (IF) staining was performed. Mouse embryos from the PN5 stage were cultured under control and TRIM7 Morpholino (3.5µM) conditions and collected at the 8-cell stage. Embryos were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and blocked with 1% BSA. Primary antibodies used included ATP6AP1 (Proteintech, 15305-1-AP), ACP5 (Proteintech, A2528), MCOLN1(Proteintech ,15291-1-AP), GM2A(Proteintech, 66080-1), and CTSL (Proteintech, 27952-1-1AP), each at 1:200 dilution. After overnight incubation at 4 °C, embryos were treated with Alexa Fluor™ 568-conjugated secondary antibody (1:300), counterstained with Hoechst, and mounted with antifade medium. Imaging was performed using confocal microscopy, and fluorescence intensity and localization were analysed using ImageJ to confirm the expression changes observed in omics datasets.

Rescue strategy for TRIM7-morpholino-induced embryo arrest

To investigate the potential rescue of embryonic arrest induced by TRIM7-morpholino (3.5µM), mouse embryos arrested at the 8-cell stage were cultured in G1 medium supplemented with Rapamycin. This intervention was based on our transcriptomic and proteomic analyses, which identified significant disruption of the lysosomal pathway, followed by impaired autophagy, in TRIM7-depleted embryos. Given Rapamycin’s known ability to modulate lysosomal biogenesis and restore autophagic flux through mTOR inhibition, it was selected as a candidate compound for functional rescue. Rapamycin was dissolved in DMSO to prepare a 10mM stock solution and diluted to final concentrations of 5µM and 7.5µM in culture media. The control groups included untreated embryos and TRIM7 Morpholino-treated embryos with Rapamycin. The experimental groups consisted of TRIM7 Morpholino-treated embryos (3.5µM) with Rapamycin added at concentrations of 5 µM and 7.5µM to rescue the developmental arrest. All embryos were cultured in their respective media and incubated under standard conditions (37 °C, 5%CO₂), with developmental progression monitored until the blastocyst stage.

Western blot analysis

Western blot analysis was performed on the pooled 8-cell stage mouse embryos from control and TRIM7-MO (3.5µM) and Rapamycin rescue groups to assess TRIM7 knockdown and rescue efficiency, and evaluate autophagy using LC3B (Santa Cruz technology, sc-398822). Approximately 100 embryos per replicate (n = 3) were lysed in RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors, and equal protein amounts were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% milk in TBS-T and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against TRIM7, LC3B, and β-Actin (loading control), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Bands were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence and quantified using ImageJ, with TRIM7 and LC3B levels normalized to β-actin.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently replicated at least three times. Depending on the data description, statistical comparisons between groups were performed using unpaired t-tests or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. GraphPad Prism 8.3.0 was used for data visualization, and results with p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Expression and location of TRIM7 in the mouse oocytes and preimplantation embryos

The spatiotemporal expression profile of TRIM7 was examined by immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy. The TRIM7 signal was readily detectable in germinal vesicle (GV) oocytes but was largely absent in MII oocytes. Following fertilization, TRIM7 was consistently observed in both the nucleus and cytoplasm of embryos, with the most intense staining occurring specifically at the 4-cell stage (Fig. A1). Its signal intensity statistic showed that TRIM7 expression gradually increases from the 2-cell stage, peaks at the 4-cell stage, and then decreases in the 8-cell stage to blast (Fig. 1B). These findings suggest that TRIM7 may play an important role during the cleavage and early differentiation phases. Single-cell RNA-seq data revealed species-specific patterns of TRIM7 expression during early embryonic development. To investigate the dynamic expression of TRIM7 during oocyte and early embryonic development in mice and humans, respectively, we analyzed available RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets (GSE71434). As shown in Fig. 1C, in mice, TRIM7 expression peaked at the 4-cell and 8-cell stages. In human preimplantation embryos, TRIM7 expression was evident in germinal vesicle (GV) oocytes, re-emerged at the 2-cell stage, was undetectable at the 4-cell stage, reached its peak at the 8-cell stage, and subsequently declined at the blastocyst stage, indicating a dynamic and stage-specific pattern during early embryonic development. These results suggest a conserved but temporally distinct role for TRIM7 in regulating zygotic genome activation and early cell fate decisions across species.

Morpholino-mediated knockdown of TRIM7 impairs embryonic development

To investigate the role of TRIM7 in early mouse embryogenesis, we used an in vivo-Morpholino antisense oligonucleotide technique to inhibit both splicing and translation of TRIM7. Zygotes at the pronuclear (PN5) stage were randomly assigned to the control morpholino group and the TRIM7-morpholino group. Embryos were cultured in G1/G2 media supplemented with different concentrations of TRIM7-MO (2.5µM, 3.5µM, 5µM, 7.5µM, and 10µM) under standard incubation conditions (37.5 °C, 5% CO₂). Developmental progression was monitored at defined time points, and morphological changes were assessed microscopically. In the control group, embryos exhibited normal development, progressing efficiently from the zygote stage to the blastocyst stage with high morphology scores. In contrast, TRIM7-MO treatment induced a concentration-dependent impairment in embryonic development. At 2.5µM, although the development rate was reduced and morphology scores were lower, approximately 20% embryos still progressed to the morula and blastocyst stages. At 3.5µM, developmental arrest was more pronounced, with almost 80–90% of embryos arrested at the 8-cell stage, and the statistics show a highly significant difference between the control and treated group blast formation rate as shown in Fig. 1D, E. At 5µM, 7.5µM, 10µM, the most severe phenotypes were observed, with nearly all embryos arrested and died between the 2-cell to 4-cell stages, Fig. 1D, E; Fig. S1 A, B). High-magnification microscopy confirmed that TRIM7-MO-treated embryos displayed developmental arrest at the 8-cell stage compared to the control group (Fig. 2A).

Quantitative analysis of embryonic development rates demonstrated that while the 4-cell stage progression in the TRIM7-MO group showed a slight decrease relative to controls, a marked reduction in development was observed at the 8-cell stage, as shown in Fig. 1E. By the morula and blast stages, over 95% of 3.5µM TRIM7-MO-treated embryos remained arrested at the 8-cell stage. In contrast, the majority of control embryos successfully developed to the morula and blastocyst.

To validate the specificity of the TRIM7-morpholino treatment, control embryos were also cultured in G1/G2 medium alone and medium containing control-morpholino. No significant differences in blastocyst formation were observed between these two control groups, confirming that the in vitro-morpholino culture technique itself does not interfere with normal embryonic development (Figs. 1D and 2A). However, almost no blastocyst formation was observed in TRIM7-MO-treated embryos, supporting the conclusion that TRIM7 knockdown specifically disrupts preimplantation development. These results demonstrated that TRIM7 is essential for normal early embryonic development in mice, particularly for successful progression beyond the 4- to 8-cell stages.

To further strengthen the specificity of TRIM7 loss-of-function phenotypes, we independently validated TRIM7 knockdown using siRNA-mediated depletion in mouse zygote via both microinjection and electroporation. In both approaches, control and negative control embryos developed normally, whereas 20 µM TRIM7 siRNA consistently induced developmental arrest at the 8-cell stage, closely phenocopying the defects observed in TRIM7 Morpholino-treated embryos. In contrast, 40 µM siRNA caused severe toxicity, resulting in early developmental failure, and most embryos died at the 2-cell stage. The reproducible phenotype across two independent delivery methods strongly excludes Morpholino-specific off-target effects and provides compelling evidence that TTRIM7 is essential for normal preimplantation embryonic development (Fig. S2 A, B).

To confirm the efficiency and specificity of TRIM7 knockdown, we performed immunofluorescent staining for TRIM7 in 8-cell embryos, which exhibited a marked decrease in TRIM7 fluorescence signal compared to controls, as shown in Fig. 2C, D, indicating efficient knockdown of TRIM7 protein expression. These results confirm that TRIM7 knockdown specifically reduces TRIM7 expression at protein levels in early embryos, supporting the specificity of the Morpholino approach in targeting TRIM7 function during preimplantation development. qRT-PCR analysis revealed a highly significant decrease in TRIM7 gene expression in the TRIM7-Morpholino-treated group (3.5µM) compared to controls (***p < 0.0001) Fig. 2E. The mRNA levels of TRIM7 were reduced by approximately 70–80% in the TRIM7-Morpholino embryos, confirming successful knockdown. Nascent RNA synthesis was evaluated by 5-EU incorporation followed by confocal microscopy. No significant difference in fluorescence was observed between control and TRIM7-knockdown in late 2-cell embryos, indicating that global ZGA was not markedly affected (Fig. S2c, d).

Genome-wide gene expression map using single-cell RNA-seq following TRIM7 Knockdown

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying TRIM7-dependent developmental arrest, we performed single-cell RNA sequencing of control and TRIM7-depleted embryos at both 8-cell and late 2-cell stages. At the 8-cell stage, the point of morphological arrest (3.5 µM), differential expression analysis identified 950 significantly downregulated genes (adjusted p < 0.01), with 427 genes showing significant downregulation (Fig. S2A). KEGG pathway analysis revealed profound suppression of lysosomal, autophagy, and proteasomal pathways (Fig. 3A). Gene ontology analysis confirmed disruption of critical biological processes, including macroautophagy, vacuole organization, and cellular catabolic processes. Integrative analysis combining transcriptomic and proteomic datasets demonstrated coordinated downregulation of autophagic-lysosomal pathways, with particular impairment of mitophagy and protein folding mechanisms (adjusted p = 0.01–0.04) (Fig. 3C). Combined GO analysis revealed significant alterations in chaperone binding, ribosome biogenesis, and autophagosome maturation (Fig. 3D). String network analysis identified severe disruption of lysosomal protein interactions, with marked downregulation of key components including MCOLN1, ACP5, ACP2, and ATP6AP1 and others (Fig. S2B, C). The specific impairment of macroautophagy and vacuole organization is particularly significant given recent evidence that lysosomal activity regulates morula-to-blastocyst transition by facilitating cellular reprogramming and metabolic adaptation [23]. This parallels findings that compromised lysosomal function disrupts trophectoderm differentiation and blastocyst cavity formation [24].

Notably, transcriptomic analysis of late 2-cell stage embryos, during zygotic genome activation, revealed even more extensive dysregulation, with 3690 genes significantly downregulated (adjusted p < 0.05) (Fig. S2D). Pathway analysis revealed early disruptions in the spliceosome, nucleocytoplasmic transport, Lysosome, cell cycle, and other important pathways (Fig. 3E), suggesting that TRIM7 deficiency compromises fundamental RNA processing mechanisms essential for embryonic development. Gene Ontology analysis identified impaired biological processes, including autophagy, DNA repair, ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, and other important pathways (Fig. 3F). Late 2-cell integrated multi-omics analysis confirmed coordinated suppression of spliceosome components, cofactor biosynthesis, protein folding, DNA replication, chromatin DNA binding, RNA localization mechanism, and other biological processes (Fig. S3 E, F).

qRT-PCR validation confirmed significant downregulation of lysosomal genes in TRIM7-deficient embryos at both developmental stages. At the 8-cell stage, we observed a 70–80% reduction in the expression of Ctsd, Mcoln1, Atp6v0d1, and Acp5 (** P < 0.01, ****p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3G). Similarly, late 2-cell stage embryos showed marked suppression of Cstl, Ctsa, Ap1s1, and Hexa (****p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3H). Immunofluorescence analysis corroborated these findings, demonstrating significantly reduced protein expression of MCOLN1, ACP5, and ATP6VOd1 in TRIM7 Morpholino-treated embryos compared to controls (****p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3I, J). These results establish that TRIM7 deficiency disrupts both transcriptional and translation regulation of lysosomal components, providing a mechanistic explanation for the observed developmental arrest.

Mass spectrometry proteomics analysis identifies TRIM7 as a central regulator of early embryonic protein networks

Mass spectrometry-based proteomic profiling revealed profound disruption of protein networks in TRIM7-depleted embryos. At the 8-cell stage, we identified 144 significantly downregulated and 93 upregulated proteins (adjusted p < 0.05, |log2FC| > 2) (Fig. 4A). KEGG analysis showed marked suppression of ribosome, spliceosome, and nucleocytoplasmic transport pathways (Fig. 4B), while GO enrichment revealed defects in ribosomal biogenesis, rRNA processing, and protein folding (Fig. 4C). Reactome mapping confirmed impairments in translation initiation and RNA processing mechanism (Fig. 4D). Parallel analysis at the late 2-cell stage identified 90 downregulated and 113 upregulated proteins, with similar suppression of lysosomal, spliceosomal, and mRNA surveillance pathways (Fig. 4E-F). GO analysis demonstrated impaired RNA transport and antioxidant activity (Fig. 4G), consistent with early disruption of cellular homeostasis. Reactome analysis revealed significant dysregulation of pathways involved in mRNA splicing, RNA polymerase activity, and transcriptional regulation and many others, which is shown in Fig. 4H. These findings align with recent studies demonstrating that lysosomal function is required for progression beyond the totipotent 2-cell stage by silencing retrotransposons and maintaining proteostasis [25].

Validation of mass spectrometry proteomic data at the 8-cell stage was further supported by immunofluorescence (IF) staining, which demonstrated a significant decrease in the fluorescence intensities of lysosomal proteins CSTL (****p < 0.0001; Fig. 4I), HEXB (***p < 0.001; Fig. 4K), and GM2A (**p < 0.01; Fig. 4J) in TRIM7-Morpholino-treated embryos compared to controls. Similarly, at the late 2-cell stage, Immunofluorescence staining validated the proteomic results, showing a significantly reduced signal intensity in TRIM7-MO embryos (****p < 0.0001; Fig. 4L). These findings establish TRIM7 as essential for maintaining proteostatic balance through coordinated regulation of translation and lysosomal pathways during early embryogenesis.

Rapamycin partially rescued the developmental arrest induced by TRIM7 knockdown in the mouse embryos

To evaluate whether activation of lysosome-autophagy function could overcome the developmental blockade caused by TRIM7 depletion, PN5-stage embryos were cultured under control, Rapamycin (5µM), TRIM7 Morpholino (3.5 µM), and Morpholino plus Rapamycin (5 µM, 7.5 µM) conditions (Fig. 5A) and statistic results significantly low developmental rate between control and TRIM7-MO group and rescued group with the (****p < 0.0001) (Fig. 5B). TRIM7 knockdown significantly reduced blastocyst formation compared to controls (p < 0.0001), whereas Rapamycin supplementation improved embryo survival, with the 5 µM concentration showing the best effect (Fig. 5C). Based on this, we compared blastocyst formation rates among control, TRIM7 Morpholino, and Morpholino plus 5 µM Rapamycin-treated embryos, revealing a ~ 40–50% developmental rescue compared to Morpholino alone (***p < 0.0001) (Fig. 5D). To investigate the underlying mechanism, qRT-PCR analysis confirmed that TRIM7 knockdown markedly downregulated key lysosomal genes (Ppt, Ap1m1, Mcoln1), which were ~ 40–50% restored upon Rapamycin treatment (****p < 0.0001) (Fig. 5E). Immunofluorescence staining further validated reduced CSTL protein intensity in TRIM7-depleted embryos and its partial recovery with Rapamycin (Fig. 6F), indicating that TRIM7 is required for proper lysosome-autophagy activity during early embryogenesis, and that pharmacological activation of this pathway can significantly improve developmental potential.

To assess autophagic flux, we examined the marker LC3B in TRIM7 knockdown (TRIM7-MO) 8-cell stage arrested embryos. Western blot analysis (Fig. 5I) showed a significant reduction of TRIM7 (**** p < 0.0001) and LC3B (*** p < 0.001) in TRIM7-MO embryos compared to controls, with the quantification shown in Fig. 4J. Treatment with Rapamycin partially rescued LC3B expression, as indicated by a significant increase in LC3B protein levels in the TRIM7-MO + Rapamycin group (***p < 0.001). Consistently, immunofluorescence signal and statistical analysis showed markedly reduced LC3B signal intensity in TRIM7-MO embryos, as shown in Fig. 5K, L, which was partially restored upon Rapamycin treatment. The partial rescue observed with Rapamycin supports that autophagy activation can restore lysosomal function and improve developmental potential in TRIM7-depleted embryos, as previously shown in other studies [26, 27].

Discussion

The study provides the first comprehensive characterization of TRIM7 function during early mouse embryogenesis and identifies TRIM7 as a critical regulator of preimplantation development. Through integrated morphological, transcriptomic, and proteomic analysis, we proposed a working model in which TRIM7 coordinates lysosomal-autophagy dynamics while simultaneously supporting transcriptional and chromatin-associated processes essential for early developmental progression (Fig. 6). TRIM7 is broadly expressed across early embryonic stages, closely coinciding with zygotic genome activation (ZGA), a key developmental transition from maternal to embryonic control [1]. The presence of TRIM7 in both the cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments suggests multiple functions, potentially linking ubiquitin-mediated proteostasis with transcriptional regulation during cellular remodelling [28, 29].

TRIM7 depletion disrupts lysosomal function, impairs degradation pathways, causes accumulation of undegraded proteins, and leads to defective transcriptional activation, metabolic imbalance, and developmental arrest at the 8-cell stage

Functional disruption of TRIM7 resulted in pronounced developmental arrest predominantly at the 8-cell stage, a critical checkpoint for totipotency maintenance and the initiation of the first lineage bifurcation [30]. The phenotype is consistent with reported roles of TRIM7 family members and other ubiquitin ligases in the regulation of protein turnover, cell cycle progression, and developmental signalling during preimplantation development [31, 32]. Importantly, TRIM7 knockdown specificity was rigorously validated using multiple independent approaches. In addition to morpholino-mediated depletion, the TRIM7-specificity was validated using multiple independent approaches. In addition to morpholino-mediated depletion, both TRIM7-specific siRNA microinjection and electroporation reproducibly phenocopied the 8-cell arrest, providing strong evidence that the observed developmental defects are specifically attributable to loss of TRIM7 rather than off-target effect. Similar phenotypes have been reported for knockdowns of other ubiquitin ligases regulating early embryogenesis, such as UBE2A and HERC2, which modulate cell cycle and DNA damage response [33, 34]. The observed developmental delay may reflect disruption of ubiquitination-dependent degradation pathways, which are critical for timely cell cycle progression and prevention of mitotic errors [35].

To further define the temporal onset of TRIM7-dependent molecular defects, we performed scRNA sequencing on both late 2-cell stage embryos and developmentally arrested 8-cell stage embryos following TRIM7 knockdown. Analysis of late 2-cell embryos was specifically designed to determine whether TRIM7 depletion interferes with zygotic genome activation (ZGA), whereas profiling of arrested 8-cell embryos allowed identification of transcriptional programs associated with developmental failure. Single-cell transcriptome analysis revealed extensive dysregulation of genes involved in lysosomal function and autophagy, processes vital for cellular remodeling and metabolic reprogramming during early embryogenesis [36, 37]. Consistent with the EU labeling results, scRNA-seq analysis of the late 2-cell embryos revealed no global suppression of nascent transcription, indicating that ZGA initiation is largely preserved. However, subtle but coordinated alterations in genes related to lysosomal function, autophagy, and cellular homeostasis were already detectable at this stage, suggesting early molecular priming defects rather than an outright failure of ZGA.

In contrast, scRNA-seq profiling of arrested 8-cell embryos uncovered pronounced dysregulation of lysosomal and autophagy-related pathways, accompanied by broader perturbations in metabolic and stress-response programs. These findings indicate that early transcriptional alterations emerging at the 2-cell stage progressively intensify during subsequent cleavage divisions, ultimately manifesting as severe lysosomal dysfunction and developmental arrest at the 8-cell stage. Lysosomes play an indispensable role in embryonic development by mediating autophagy, recycling of cellular components, and regulation of metabolic homeostasis [38, 39]. Dysregulation of lysosomal function is linked to defective embryogenesis and cell fate commitment [24, 40]. Together, these data support a model in which TRIM7 is not required for the initiation of ZGA but is essential for stabilizing post-ZGA transcriptional and proteostatic networks that sustain embryonic developmental competence.

Consistent with this model, pharmacological activation of autophagy by rapamycin partially rescued TRIM7-deficient embryos, restoring approximately 40–50% of blastocyst formation and partially recovering lysosomal gene expression. This partially resuce strongly supports a major role of lysosome-autophagy impairment in the TRIM7 knockdown phenotype. Howeevr, the incomplete nature of the resue indicates that autophagy defects alone cannot fully account for the developmental arrest. In line with this, proteomic profiling revealed significant alterations in proteins involved in translation, RNA processing, and chromatin regulation, including genes such as HDAC1 and ATAD2 that are essential for transcriptional control and lineage specification [41, 42]. This enrichment of stress-respose proteins among upregulated factors further suggests a compensatory response to disrupted proteostasis [43, 44].

Together, these findings indicate that TRIM7 functions as a central integrator of multiple regulatory pathways during early embryonic development, coordinating lysosomal-autophagy activity with transcriptional and chromatin-based mechanisms. While direct biochemical validation of TRIM7 E3 ubiquitin ligase activity through immunoprecipitation or ubiquitination assays is technically challenging in preimplantation embryos due to limited protein availability and a huge number of embryos required [45], future studies using complementary cell-based or expanded embryonic models will be required to define the E3 ligase activity and direct substrates of TRIM7. Collectively, our data establish TRIM7 as an essential regulator of early embryonic competence and highlight its multifaceted role in ensuring successful pre-implantation development.

Conclusion

Our findings reveal that TRIM7 is a key regulator of early mouse embryonic development, essential for maintaining lysosomal function and transcriptome-proteome stability during cleavage stages. TRIM7 knockdown caused severe developmental arrest, while Rapamycin-mediated activation of lysosomal pathways partially rescued embryonic progression, highlighting TRIM7 as a crucial factor for early embryonic viability and a potential target for preventing developmental arrest. These results not only expand our understanding of the molecular mechanisms governing early development but also highlight TRIM7 as a potential target for future studies on developmental disorders and reproductive health.

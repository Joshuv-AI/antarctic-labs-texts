Characterization of an induced pluripotent stem cell line (NCHi013-A) from a 5-year-old male with pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum and a biventricular repair
Authors: Meghan Hanley, Matthew Alonzo, Shiqiao Ye, Yang Yu, Javier Contreras, Jade Hayden, Vidu Garg, Ming-Tao Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PA/IVS) is a rare congenital heart defect that causes a significant decrease of blood outflow from the heart and is fatal if left untreated. iPSC line NCHi013-A was produced from peripheral blood mononuclear cells from a male child with PA/IVS using Sendai virus reprogramming. NCHi013-A displayed normal stem cell morphology, expressed markers for pluripotency, and presented ability to differentiate into cells of endoderm, ectoderm, and mesoderm lineages. The iPSC line also maintained normal karyotype, was validated for cell identity, and tested negative for transgenes and mycoplasma contamination.

Resource utility

Induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) line NCHi013-A was derived from a patient with pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum and a biventricular repair (2v). NCHi013-A has shown capability to differentiate into three germ layer cell lineages, which can help study PA/IVS in vitro and understanding the mechanisms of the disease.

Resource details

PA/IVS is a rare form of congenital heart disease that affects 4 to 8 per 100,000 live births (Gorla and Singh, 2022). PA/IVS has a wide spectrum of morphologic variability; thus, treatment options depend on the severity and classification (Alwi, 2006). Treatment can range from medication to surgery to heart transplant (Chikkabyrappa et al., 2018). Having the means to study PA/IVS in vitro with iPSCs could provide novel perspectives to supplement in vivo models (Hall et al., 2022).

iPSC line NCHi013-A was derived from a five-year-old Caucasian male with pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum and a biventricular repair. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) isolated from the patient’s blood were transfected with Sendai virus to create iPSCs, which displayed normal stem cell morphology (Fig. 1A). Analysis of 1.1 million single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) validated the origin of this iPSC line from patient PBMCs. (Available with the authors). Since iPSCs lose their transgenes over several passages, we cultured and passaged iPSCs sufficiently and confirmed the loss of transgenes KOS and SEV in NCHi013-A when analyzed by RT-PCR (Ban et al., 2011) (Supplementary Fig. 1B). This iPSC line also tested negative for mycoplasma contamination (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Karyotyping was completed by Karyostat + assay, which confirmed a normal male karyotype with no aberrations above 1 Mb detected (Fig. 1C, Table 1). Cells displayed pluripotency when immunofluorescence staining was done with nuclear pluripotency markers (SOX2, NANOG, and OCT3/4) and pluripotent surface antigen (TRA-1-60). (Fig. 1B and 1D, Table 1). To ensure pluripotency expression was endogenous and not due to presence of the reprogramming transgenes, we performed successful directed differentiation into the three germ layers, thus proving that this line can be used to illustrate and understand different stages of human heart development. After directed differentiation, the cells were positively stained for their respective germ layer-specific markers. Endoderm cells were stained with FOXA2 and SOX17, ectoderm cells were stained with OTX2 and PAX6, and mesoderm cells were stained with TBX6 and Brachyury (Fig. 1E).

Materials and methods

Reprogramming and culturing

PBMCs were cultured in StemPro-34 SFM medium (Gibco) for one week, supplemented with 100 ng/mL SCF (PeproTech), 100 ng/mL FLT3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific), 20 ng/mL IL3 (PeproTech), 20 ng/mL IL6 (Gibco), 20 ng/mL EPO (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 1X Gluta-MAX (Gibco). PBMCs were reprogrammed by transfection with Sendai virus (CytoTune™-iPS 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit, Invitrogen) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Reprogrammed cells were resuspended with supplemented StemPro-34 SFM medium, then placed in a Matrigel-coated plate with 1:300 dilution of Matrigel (Corning) in DMEM/F12 (Gibco) for one week. Media was then changed to TeSR-E8 medium (STEMCELL Technologies). After two weeks, iPSC colonies were harvested and manually picked. Once expanded over several passages, iPSCs were cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen.

Human iPSCs were refreshed daily with TeSR-E8 medium and maintained at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 until 90 % confluent. For passages, iPSCs were dislodged using 0.5 mM EDTA and transferred at a 1:6 ratio to a new Matrigel-coated plate with TeSR-E8 medium and 5 μM ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 (Selleck Chemicals).

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells (P19) were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde (Electron Microscopy Sciences) for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.1 % Triton-X (Sigma) for 20 min, then blocked with 0.2 % BSA (Sigma) in DPBS at 4 °C overnight. Primary antibodies (Table 2) were diluted with 0.2 % BSA in DPBS and incubated overnight at 4 °C. Secondary antibodies (Table 2) were diluted and incubated for 1 h in dark. Cells were washed with DPBS before staining with DAPI for 5 min in the dark. Coverslips were mounted on slides using SlowFade Gold Antifade (Invitrogen) and imaged with a Leica THUNDER K8 fluorescence microscope. Experiments were performed at room temperature unless stated.

Three germ layer differentiation

Differentiation ability was determined by immunofluorescence staining of germ-layer specific markers. For endoderm and ectoderm, iPSCs (P49) were differentiated using the Human Pluripotent Stem Cell Functional Identification Kit (R&D Systems). For mesoderm, iPSCs (P29) were induced with 6 μM CHIR99021 (Selleck Chemicals) in RPMI 1640 media (Gibco) with B27 Minus Insulin Supplement (Gibco) for 48 h at 37 °C with 5 % CO2. Finally, cells were fixed and stained with germ-layer specific markers.

Karyotype and cell identity

IPSCs (P46) were harvested and sent to Thermo Fisher Scientific for Karyostat + and Cell ID assays. Genomic DNA from iPSCs (P46) and PBMCs were extracted using Quick-DNA Miniprep Plus Kit (Zymo Research). 1.1 million SNPs from iPSCs and PBMCs were analyzed.

Transgene detection

Total RNA was extracted from iPSCs (P50) and from PBMCs 2 days post-virus transfection using the Direct-zol RNA Miniprep Kit (Zymo Research). Eluted RNA was reverse transcribed using the iScript cDNA synthesis kit (Bio-Rad). Using the VeritiPro (Applied Biosciences), cDNA was amplified by RT-PCR using SEV, KOS, and GAPDH primers (Table 2) using the following conditions for 30 cycles: 10 s at 98 °C, 15 s at 60 °C, and 1 min at 68 °C. Results were visualized on 1 % agarose gel.

Mycoplasma detection

Day 3 iPSC culture supernatant (P42) tested negative for mycoplasmas using the MycoAlert™ Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza Biosciences).

Effect of vepdegestrant, a PROTAC oestrogen receptor degrader, on dabigatran and rosuvastatin pharmacokinetics in healthy participants
Authors: Derek Z Yang, Li Zhou, Jennifer A Winton, Kyle T Matschke, Constantino Kantaridis, Kristine Doyle, Kimberly C Lee, Naihan Chen, Yuanyuan Zhang, Weiwei Tan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTo evaluate the effects of vepdegestrant, a PROteolysis TArgeting Chimera (PROTAC) oestrogen receptor degrader, on the pharmacokinetics of dabigatran (a P‐glycoprotein [P‐gp] substrate) and rosuvastatin (a breast cancer resistance protein [BCRP] substrate).MethodsIn two phase 1, open‐label, two‐period, fixed‐sequence studies, healthy adult participants received a single oral dose of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg or rosuvastatin 10 mg alone and with a single dose of vepdegestrant 200 mg. Serial plasma samples were collected after dosing, and pharmacokinetic parameters for dabigatran and rosuvastatin were estimated by standard noncompartmental analysis.ResultsCoadministration of the P‐gp substrate dabigatran etexilate with vepdegestrant increased dabigatran area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) from time 0 to infinity by 98% and maximum plasma concentration (C max) by 92%. Coadministration of the BCRP substrate rosuvastatin with vepdegestrant increased rosuvastatin plasma AUC from time 0 to time of the last measurable concentration by 11% and C max by 21%.ConclusionsCoadministration with vepdegestrant doubled dabigatran exposure and resulted in a minor increase (11%) in rosuvastatin exposure in humans.Keywords: breast cancer, drug interactions, drug transporters, p‐glycoprotein, pharmacokinetics

INTRODUCTION

Vepdegestrant (ARV‐471 ), an investigational, orally administered PROteolysis TArgeting Chimera (PROTAC) oestrogen receptor (ER) degrader, has a unique mechanism of action that directly harnesses the cellular ubiquitin proteasome system to induce degradation of ER. Vepdegestrant is a bifunctional small molecule consisting of an ER‐binding domain and cereblon E3 ubiquitin ligase‐binding domain joined by a linker. 1 , 2 , 3 The trimer complex formed by ER, the E3 ligase, and vepdegestrant triggers ubiquitination and subsequent degradation of ER by the proteasome.

In preclinical studies, vepdegestrant significantly inhibited the proliferation of an ER‐positive (ER+) breast cancer cell line, induced tumour regressions in several breast cancer xenograft models when dosed orally, and showed enhanced activity when combined with cyclin‐dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6), mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and phosphatidylinositol‐4,5‐biphosphate 3‐kinase (PI3K) inhibitors. 4 In a first‐in‐human phase 1/2 clinical study, vepdegestrant, alone and in combination with the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib, showed promising activity in patients with ER+/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2‐negative (HER2‐) advanced breast cancer and was generally well tolerated. 5 , 6 , 7 Most recently, vepdegestrant (200 mg QD) demonstrated significantly longer progression‐free survival (the primary efficacy endpoint) than the standard of care comparator (fulvestrant) among previously treated patients with oestrogen receptor 1 gene (ESR1)‐mutated, ER+/HER2‐ advanced breast cancer in a registrational phase 3 randomized trial. 8 Vepdegestrant is currently under clinical development for patients with ER+/HER2‐ advanced breast cancer. 5 , 6 , 7 , 9 , 10 , 11

In vitro data indicate that vepdegestrant is a P‐glycoprotein (P‐gp 12 ; also known as adenosine triphosphate (ATP)‐binding cassette subfamily B member 1 [ABCB1]) inhibitor, as well as a breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP 13 ; also known as ATP‐binding cassette subfamily G member 2 [ABCG2]) inhibitor. 14 , 15 Therefore, clinical drug–drug interaction (DDI) studies with known P‐gp and BCRP substrates are warranted to evaluate potential DDIs between vepdegestrant and substrates of P‐gp or BCRP. Dabigatran etexilate plays an indirect role as a thrombin inhibitor through its metabolite, dabigatran, and is a recommended probe substrate of P‐gp. Similarly, rosuvastatin, a selective and competitive inhibitor of 3‐hydroxy‐3‐methylglutaryl‐coenzyme A reductase, is a probe substrate of BCRP. In the two phase 1 studies described here, the effects of vepdegestrant on the pharmacokinetics (PK) of dabigatran (NCT05673889) and rosuvastatin (NCT05652660) were evaluated in healthy adult participants.

METHODS

Study population

In both studies, eligible participants included females (of non‐childbearing potential) and males who were overtly healthy as determined by medical evaluation. Exclusion criteria in both studies included, but were not limited to, evidence or history of clinically significant conditions, conditions that could possibly affect drug absorption, use of prescription or nonprescription medications within 7 days or 5 half‐lives (whichever was longer) prior to the first dose of study intervention, and administration of investigational products (drug or vaccine) within 30 days or 5 half‐lives (whichever was longer) prior to receiving the first dose of study intervention. Complete eligibility criteria are included in Tables S1 and S2.

Dabigatran study

For participation in the dabigatran study, individuals had to be 18–70 years of age with a body mass index (BMI) of 17.5–30.5 kg/m2 and total body weight >50 kg (110 lb). Exclusion criteria for this study included, but were not limited to, the following: active bleeding or risk of bleeding and use of moderate or strong cytochrome P450 (CYP)3A or P‐gp inducers within 14 days plus 5 half‐lives or inhibitors within 14 days or 5 half‐lives (whichever was longer) prior to the first dose of dabigatran etexilate (Table S1).

Rosuvastatin study

For participation in the rosuvastatin study, individuals had to be 18–65 years of age with a BMI of 17.5–32.0 kg/m2 and total body weight ≥45 kg (99 lb). Exclusion criteria included, but were not limited to, a known history of hypersensitivity to statin medications and the use of moderate or strong CYP3A or BCRP inducers within 14 days plus 5 half‐lives or inhibitors within 14 days or 5 half‐lives (whichever was longer) prior to the first dose of rosuvastatin (Table S2).

Study design

The studies were conducted in compliance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines. Approval was received from the local Independent Ethics Committee and Institutional Review Board (IRB), and all local regulatory requirements were followed. For the dabigatran study, ethical approval was granted by the Comité d'Ethique Hospitalo‐Facultaire Erasme‐ULB in Belgium, and no reference number was issued. For the rosuvastatin study, IRB approval was granted by Advarra in the US, and no reference number was issued. All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrolment in the study. Both studies were phase 1, open‐label, two‐period, fixed‐sequence studies in healthy adult participants (Figure 1). Vepdegestrant 200 mg was selected for both studies as it represents the dose assessed in the registrational phase 3 VERITAC‐2 study, 8 , 16 and vepdegestrant must be administered with food. 16

Study design for (A) the dabigatran study and (B) the rosuvastatin study. aAdministered approximately 2 h after starting a standard breakfast. bVepdegestrant was administered approximately 0.5 h after starting a standard breakfast; a single dose of dabigatran etexilate or rosuvastatin was administered approximately 1.5 h after vepdegestrant administration, which was approximately 2 h after the start of the breakfast.

Dabigatran study

All participants in the dabigatran study reported to the clinical research unit in Brussels, Belgium, at admission (Day −1) and remained at the unit for the duration of the study until the day of clinic discharge. Following at least 10 h of overnight fasting, participants received a single dose of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg alone approximately 2 h after the start of a standard breakfast (period 1, reference treatment; Figure 1A). Following at least 4 days of washout after dabigatran etexilate administration in period 1 and at least 10 h of overnight fasting, participants received a single dose of vepdegestrant 200 mg approximately 0.5 h after the start of the standard breakfast. A single dose of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg was then administered approximately 1.5 h after vepdegestrant administration, which was approximately 2 h after the start of the breakfast (period 2, test treatment; Figure 1A).

Rosuvastatin study

All participants in the rosuvastatin study reported to the clinical research unit in New Haven, CT, USA, at admission (Day −1) and remained at the unit for the duration of the study until the day of clinic discharge. Following at least 10 h of overnight fasting, participants received a single dose of rosuvastatin 10 mg alone approximately 2 h after the start of a standard breakfast (period 1, reference treatment; Figure 1B). Following at least 5 days of washout after rosuvastatin administration in period 1 and at least 10 h of overnight fasting, participants received a single dose of vepdegestrant 200 mg approximately 0.5 h after the start of a standard breakfast. A single dose of rosuvastatin 10 mg was then administered approximately 1.5 h after vepdegestrant administration, which was approximately 2 h after the start of the standard breakfast (period 2, test treatment; Figure 1B).

Sample collection and analytical methods

Serial blood samples were collected pre‐ and postdose to calculate PK parameters for dabigatran or rosuvastatin and vepdegestrant (both studies). Calibration standard responses were linear over the specified ranges using weighted (1/concentration2) linear regression. Samples with concentrations above the upper limit of quantitation were diluted into the calibration range, and those below the lower limit were reported as below the lower limit of quantitation. Additional sample collection and analytical methods can be found in the Supplemental Appendix (section S1.1). Limits of quantitation, between‐day accuracy, and between‐day precision for dabigatran and rosuvastatin are summarized below.

Dabigatran study

Blood samples for dabigatran PK were collected predose and at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, 36 and 48 h post dabigatran etexilate dosing in both periods. The lower limit of quantitation in plasma was 1.00 ng/mL, with linearity demonstrated up to 800 ng/mL (upper limit of quantitation) for total dabigatran. For quality control samples, the between‐day accuracy (expressed as percent relative error) ranged from −3.4% to 1.1%. The between‐day precision (expressed as the percent coefficient of variation) was ≤7.6%. Blood samples for vepdegestrant and ARV‐473 (epimer metabolite of vepdegestrant) PK were collected in period 2, and details can be found in the Supplemental Appendix (section S1.1).

Rosuvastatin study

Blood samples for rosuvastatin PK were collected predose and at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48 and 72 h post rosuvastatin dosing in both periods. The lower limit of quantitation in plasma was 0.02 ng/mL, with linearity demonstrated up to 25.0 ng/mL (upper limit of quantitation). For quality control samples, the between‐day accuracy (expressed as percent relative error) ranged from −4.00% to −1.47%. The between‐day precision (expressed as the percent coefficient of variation) was ≤7.13%. The Supplemental Appendix (section S1.1) contains details on blood sample collection for vepdegestrant and ARV‐473 PK in period 2.

Pharmacokinetic analysis

The primary objectives of these two studies were to evaluate the effect of a single dose of vepdegestrant on the PK of single doses of dabigatran etexilate or rosuvastatin, measured by maximum plasma concentration (C max) and plasma area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) from time 0 to infinity (AUCinf) or AUC from time 0 to time of the last measurable concentration (AUClast) if AUCinf could not be derived. Other objectives included the calculation of additional PK parameters including, but not limited to, time to reach C max (T max), apparent first‐order elimination half‐life (t 1/2), total clearance after extravascular administration (CL/F), and volume of distribution during the terminal elimination phase (VZ/F), as data allowed. Plasma AUCinf, t 1/2, CL/F and Vz/F were reported if the following criteria were met: a well‐characterized terminal phase defined as one with at least three data points, a goodness‐of‐fit statistic from the regression (r 2) ≥ 0.9, and a percent of AUCinf obtained by forward extrapolation (AUCextrap%) ≤ 20.

PK parameters for dabigatran, rosuvastatin, vepdegestrant and its epimer ARV‐473 were calculated using actual times from the concentration–time data using a standard noncompartmental approach as appropriate and as data allowed. Concentrations that were below the limit of quantitation were set to 0 ng/mL for the noncompartmental analysis.

Safety assessments

Safety and tolerability of dabigatran or rosuvastatin alone and with vepdegestrant were monitored. In both studies, participants underwent physical examinations and were evaluated or monitored for clinical laboratory parameters, vital signs, electrocardiograms (ECGs) and adverse events (AEs; serious AEs [SAEs], treatment‐emergent AEs [TEAEs], and treatment‐related AEs [TRAEs]).

Data and statistical analyses

In both studies, sample size determination was based on average intraparticipant standard deviations (SDs) obtained from prior completed dabigatran or rosuvastatin crossover studies (data on file). More participants were included in the dabigatran study compared with the rosuvastatin study due to the considerably higher interparticipant variability of dabigatran PK vs. that of rosuvastatin observed in previous studies. 17 , 18 , 19 PK analyses included all participants who had at least one measurable concentration of any analyte (PK concentration set) and at least one PK parameter of interest for any analyte (PK parameter set) in both studies. PK parameters were summarized using descriptive statistics. Natural log‐transformed PK parameters of AUCinf, AUClast, and C max were analysed using a mixed‐effects model, with treatment as a fixed effect and participant as a random effect in both studies. To determine differences between the test period (dabigatran or rosuvastatin with vepdegestrant) and the reference period (dabigatran or rosuvastatin alone), estimates of the adjusted mean differences (test − reference) and corresponding 90% confidence intervals (CIs) were obtained from the model and were exponentiated to provide estimates of the ratio of adjusted geometric means (test/reference; expressed as a percentage) and corresponding 90% CIs.

Nomenclature of targets and ligands

Safety

Dabigatran study

Following administration of a single dose of dabigatran etexilate, TRAEs of diarrhoea, nausea, dry skin and hot flush were reported in one participant each, and following administration of dabigatran etexilate with vepdegestrant, one TRAE of headache was reported in one participant (Table S4). All TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity. No deaths, SAEs or severe AEs occurred, and no discontinuations or dose modifications due to AEs were reported. No clinically meaningful changes in vital signs, ECGs or laboratory measurements were observed.

Rosuvastatin study

No TRAEs were reported following administration of a single dose of rosuvastatin; following administration of rosuvastatin with vepdegestrant, TRAEs of dizziness and headache were reported in one participant each (Table S5). All TEAEs were mild in severity. No deaths, SAEs or severe AEs occurred, and no discontinuations or dose modifications due to AEs were reported. No clinically meaningful changes in vital signs, ECGs or laboratory measurements were observed.

DISCUSSION

Vepdegestrant (ARV‐471) is an investigational, orally administered PROTAC ER degrader that is being evaluated in clinical studies for the treatment of ER+/HER2‐ advanced breast cancer. 3 , 4 The primary objectives of these two phase 1, open‐label, two‐period, fixed‐sequence studies were to evaluate potential DDIs between vepdegestrant and substrates of P‐gp or BCRP. Specifically, we evaluated the effect of a single dose of vepdegestrant on the PK of a single dose of dabigatran etexilate (probe substrate for P‐gp) or rosuvastatin (probe substrate for BCRP). 23 , 24 In ongoing clinical trials of vepdegestrant in patients with breast cancer, caution is recommended when using vepdegestrant with sensitive substrates of P‐gp, and no dose reductions of BCRP substrates are required.

Dabigatran etexilate is a small‐molecule prodrug that is a known substrate of intestinal P‐gp and is recommended as a probe substrate by regulatory agencies. 24 , 25 , 26 Dabigatran etexilate has low systemic exposure and is rapidly absorbed and converted to dabigatran (active form) in the intestine and liver upon administration. 18 , 24 , 27 , 28 Although dabigatran etexilate is a substrate of P‐gp, the active form (dabigatran) is not. Modulation of intestinal P‐gp activity affects dabigatran etexilate absorption, which is reflected in dabigatran plasma exposure. 18 , 29 In the current dabigatran study, a single dose of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg was chosen. A single dose was selected to focus on DDIs during drug absorption in the gut, as per the ICH M12 guideline for DDI studies. 30 Previous DDI studies utilized various dose levels, such as a single 75 mg or 150 mg dose, when dabigatran etexilate was used as the probe substrate for P‐gp. 31 , 32 , 33 , 34 A greater magnitude of DDI effect, if present, may be anticipated following a low dose of the substrate. This is because transporter saturation is more likely to occur at higher doses than at lower doses. 35 Additionally, a single dose of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg was expected to be well tolerated by healthy adult participants.

Our study demonstrated that concomitant administration of dabigatran etexilate with vepdegestrant increased dabigatran plasma concentrations. Coadministration of dabigatran etexilate 75 mg with vepdegestrant 200 mg increased the dabigatran plasma AUCinf by 98% and C max by 92%. This finding is consistent with preclinical data from in vitro studies, indicating that vepdegestrant is a P‐gp inhibitor. The increase in dabigatran exposure can be attributed to the inhibition of the P‐gp transporter (an efflux transporter), which decreases the efflux of dabigatran etexilate from the enterocyte to the lumen of the intestines. 36 , 37 The recommendations from local product labels on the concomitant use of P‐gp inhibitors and P‐gp substrates may vary due to differing populations, indications and countries. Therefore, in ongoing clinical studies of vepdegestrant, caution is recommended for coadministration with sensitive substrates of P‐gp.

Rosuvastatin is a known substrate of BCRP and is recommended by regulatory agencies as a probe for BCRP inhibition in DDI studies. 23 , 30 The therapeutic dose level of rosuvastatin ranges from 5 mg to 40 mg, and most DDI studies involving rosuvastatin as a substrate were conducted at the 10 mg or 20 mg dose level. 23 , 38 , 39 , 40 , 41 In this study, rosuvastatin 10 mg was expected to lead to a sufficient amount in plasma for detection and be generally well tolerated by healthy adult participants according to data from other rosuvastatin DDI studies and data from a single dose escalation study in which rosuvastatin was dosed up to 80 mg. 38 , 39 , 40 , 41 , 42 Similar to dabigatran etexilate, a single dose of rosuvastatin was administered to focus on DDIs during the absorption phase in the gut.

Concomitant administration of rosuvastatin 10 mg with vepdegestrant 200 mg had a minor effect on plasma concentrations of rosuvastatin, increasing AUClast by 11% and C max by 21%. It is important to note that rosuvastatin AUCinf was not reported for four participants in period 1 (rosuvastatin alone) and in period 2 (rosuvastatin coadministration with vepdegestrant); two of these participants did not have reportable AUCinf in both periods. Therefore, AUClast, which was available for all participants, is a more adequate PK parameter than AUCinf to describe the inhibitory effect on BCRP. These findings suggest that vepdegestrant has a minor inhibitory effect on BCRP and that dose reductions of BCRP substrates (e.g., methotrexate, rosuvastatin and sulfasalazine) are not warranted when coadministered with vepdegestrant. 43

One limitation of these two studies is the use of a single vepdegestrant dose. Although the effect of multiple vepdegestrant doses on the PK of dabigatran and rosuvastatin might be interesting, the rationale for using a single dose was to focus on relevant DDIs during drug absorption in the gut, as per the ICH M12 guideline for DDI studies. 30 Another limitation may be that only one staggered dosing schedule (time interval between vepdegestrant administration and dabigatran or rosuvastatin) was used in each study. The staggered dosing was implemented to allow time for vepdegestrant to dissolve in the stomach, thereby maximizing any potential inhibitory effect on gastrointestinal transporters. The staggered dosing time of 1.5 h was empirically chosen, and the effect of varying staggered dosing intervals on DDIs was not evaluated.

Single doses of dabigatran etexilate and rosuvastatin with or without a single dose of vepdegestrant were well tolerated and revealed no new safety signals. All AEs were mild or moderate in severity in the dabigatran study, and all AEs were mild in the rosuvastatin study; no participants discontinued either study due to AEs.

CONCLUSION

Evaluation of the potential for DDIs between vepdegestrant and dabigatran etexilate (a P‐gp substrate) or rosuvastatin (a BCRP substrate) reveals that coadministration with vepdegestrant doubled dabigatran exposure and resulted in a minor increase (11%) in rosuvastatin exposure in humans.

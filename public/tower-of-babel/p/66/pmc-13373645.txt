FLT3 inhibitors in AML: from classic scaffolds to next-generation approaches
Authors: Fatma M. Elmenier, Eman M. E. Dokla, Nermin Samir, Deena S. Lasheen, Khaled A. M. Abouzid, Dalal A. Abou El Ella
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) is a key driver of acute myeloid leukemia (AML); mutations within FLT3, specifically ITD lesions and TKD point mutations, promote proliferation and are associated with poor prognosis. Although FLT3 inhibition is central to AML therapy, resistance, particularly via D835 activation-loop variants and the F691L gatekeeper substitution, limits durability. Among the major therapeutic classes, type I inhibitors bind the active (DFG-in) conformation, whereas type II inhibitors stabilize the inactive (DFG-out) state. In contrast, irreversible covalent inhibitors target reactive cysteine residues within the kinase domain. Collectively, these approaches represent complementary therapeutic strategies with distinct resistance liabilities and structural design considerations. This review integrates structural biology with medicinal-chemistry evidence across type I, type II, irreversible, and dual-modality FLT3 inhibitors, analyzing how hinge contacts, back-pocket occupancy, and warhead placement govern activity across wild-type and mutant FLT3. We map resistance-defining residues (e.g. F691, D835, N676, N701) and design tactics to preserve potency against resistant variants. We also summarize combination therapy that augments selective FLT3 blockade and outline PROTAC approaches that induce FLT3 degradation, positioning these modalities as alternatives when single-molecule polypharmacology is constrained. Finally, we catalogue dual-target FLT3 chemotypes, highlighting examples that retain activity against F691L and D835 in cellular systems and xenografts. Overall, this review provides a section-by-section guide covering FLT3 structure and mutation hotspots, analyses of type I, type II, and irreversible inhibitors, dual-modality designs, combinations, PROTACs, and future perspectives. It links binding mode, covalent engagement, and second-target selection to recurrent resistance biology to guide more resilient FLT3-targeted therapies for high-risk AML.

Introduction

Overview of AML and the role of FLT3 in AML pathogenesis

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is the most common form of adult leukemia and represents a biologically complex, clinically aggressive clonal malignancy originating from hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). It is characterized by uncontrolled proliferation and accumulation of immature myeloblasts in the bone marrow, peripheral blood, and other tissues.1–3 AML accounts for nearly one-third of adult leukemia cases and approximately 1% of all cancers worldwide.4,5 Despite advances in molecular classification and targeted therapies, AML continues to exhibit poor long-term survival and high relapse rates, particularly in genetically defined high-risk groups.6–9 According to the American Cancer Society, an estimated 22 010 new AML cases and 11 090 deaths are projected in the United States in 2025, underscoring its significant clinical burden.4 The disease predominantly affects older adults (median age at diagnosis ∼69 years) and shows a slightly higher incidence in males than females (ratio 5 : 3).4,10 Although the lifetime risk remains relatively low (∼0.5%), AML is associated with rapid progression and poor prognosis, highlighting the urgent need for effective therapeutic strategies.

Among the most recurrent and clinically relevant genetic lesions in AML are mutations in the FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) gene, which have been extensively investigated as therapeutic targets.1,2,10–12 FLT3, a member of the class III receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) family, plays a pivotal role in hematopoietic stem cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation, processes that, when dysregulated, drive AML pathogenesis.13 Structurally, FLT3 comprises an extracellular ligand-binding region with five immunoglobulin-like domains (D1–D5), a transmembrane domain, an intracellular juxtamembrane (JM) domain, and a bipartite tyrosine kinase domain (TKD1 and TKD2), which forms the catalytic core14–16 (Fig. 1).

Schematic representation of FLT3 receptor architecture, showing extracellular domains, JM domain, and TKD subdomains.

Upon ligand binding, FLT3 undergoes dimerization and autophosphorylation within its tyrosine kinase domain (TKD), which contains the highly conserved Asp–Phe–Gly (DFG) motif. This conformational change shifts the DFG motif to the “DFG-in” position, stabilizing the active kinase conformation and enabling ATP binding. Conversely, the “DFG-out” state corresponds to an inactive kinase form.17 Activated FLT3 triggers phosphorylation of downstream signaling cascades, including RAS/MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and STAT5, which regulate cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation (Fig. 2).2,18,19 In healthy individuals, FLT3 activation is transient and ligand-dependent, maintaining hematopoietic homeostasis; however, in AML, FLT3 wild-type (FLT3-WT) receptors are often overexpressed, and activating mutations disrupt this regulation, locking the receptor in a constitutively active state.17,20

FLT3 mutations in AML: clinical and molecular impact

Mutations in the internal tandem duplication (ITD) region and tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) lead to constitutive activation of FLT3, even in the absence of ligand binding, thereby triggering aberrant downstream signaling.10,13 ITD mutations occur within the JM domain and are present in approximately 30% of AML cases, whereas TKD point mutations, most frequently at aspartic acid residue 835 (D835) in the activation loop, account for about 10%.1,3,14 These alterations disrupt hematopoietic homeostasis by promoting uncontrolled proliferation and impairing normal differentiation. Constitutive FLT3 activation also stimulates anti-apoptotic pathways, including PI3K/AKT and RAS/MAPK, conferring survival advantages and resistance to apoptosis.10

Clinically, FLT3-ITD AML correlates with inferior overall survival and higher relapse rates, particularly when ITD insertions are longer or present at high allelic ratios.18,21,22 TKD mutations, such as D835 variants, stabilize the active kinase conformation and contribute to resistance against type II inhibitors.18,23 These strong associations between FLT3 mutations and poor outcomes underscore the need for next-generation FLT3 inhibitors capable of overcoming mutation-driven resistance.24

FLT3 inhibitors: classification and mechanistic diversity

FLT3 inhibitors belong to the broader class of kinase inhibitors, which act by occupying the ATP-binding site and blocking catalytic activity.25 Kinase inhibitors are generally categorized into six types based on their binding mode: type I (active conformation), type II (inactive conformation), type III, IV, V, and type VI (covalent irreversible inhibitors)26 as illustrated in Fig. 3.

Classification of kinase inhibitors based on binding mode and conformational state.

FLT3 inhibitors are typically classified by two criteria: generation (first or second) and binding mode (type I, type II, or irreversible).22,27,28 First-generation inhibitors are multi-kinase agents with broad activity, often associated with off-target toxicities.13,22 Second-generation inhibitors were designed for improved FLT3 selectivity and potency.10,11,13 Type I inhibitors bind to the ATP pocket in the active (DFG-in) conformation and remain active against FLT3 harboring ITD or TKD mutations, whereas type II inhibitors target the inactive (DFG-out) state and are generally ineffective against TKD variants.15,29 Examples include midostaurin and lestaurtinib (first-generation type I), sorafenib (first-generation type II), crenolanib and gilteritinib (second-generation type I), and quizartinib, the only FDA-approved second-generation type II inhibitor.14 In addition, FF-10101 represents a selective irreversible FLT3 inhibitor.30

Despite initial clinical success, FLT3 inhibitors face significant challenges, including resistance and tolerability concerns. Activation loop mutations (e.g., D835) and gatekeeper substitutions (F691L) reduce inhibitor binding, while adaptive signaling further limits efficacy, particularly under monotherapy.2,24,27,31 In addition, first-generation multi-kinase inhibitors and even quizartinib are associated with off-target toxicities such as myelosuppression.11,32 These limitations have driven the development of next-generation strategies, including more selective second-generation agents, irreversible covalent inhibitors, dual-target designs, and degraders, aiming to improve selectivity, overcome resistance, and enhance clinical tolerability.

Design rationale for next-generation FLT3 inhibitors: strategies to overcome resistance and enhance therapeutic durability

Early optimization of reversible FLT3 inhibitors revealed critical limitations, notably resistance mutations and short-lived pharmacodynamic effects, prompting the evolution of multiple complementary strategies. Core approaches include irreversible covalent inhibitors, which ensure prolonged target engagement, and strategies exploiting distinct kinase conformations (type I and type II) as templates for further optimization. Additional innovations involve hybrid scaffolds integrating covalent warheads with structural features that confer conformational selectivity, and dual-modality designs that target FLT3 alongside cooperating oncogenic pathways. Emerging modalities such as proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) and rational combination strategies further expand the therapeutic landscape. Together, these strategies aim to enhance selectivity, overcome resistance, and deliver durable therapeutic responses. A detailed understanding of FLT3 structural dynamics underpins these strategies, and will be discussed in the following section.

Structural and conformational biology for FLT3

FLT3 kinase domain architecture

Understanding the structural organization of FLT3 is essential for elucidating its activation mechanism, mutation susceptibility, and potential for selective inhibition. The FLT3 gene, located on chromosome 13q12, comprises 24 exons spanning over 100 kilobases and encodes a membrane-associated protein of 993 amino acids (∼160 kDa).33,34 Structurally, FLT3 resembles other protein kinases, featuring a smaller N-terminal lobe (N-lobe) composed of a five-stranded β-sheet and a single α-helix (αC), and a larger C-terminal lobe (C-lobe) consisting of seven α-helices and three β-strands. These lobes are connected by a short hinge segment that provides rotational flexibility35 (Fig. 4).

3D structure of FLT3 displaying the ATP-binding site with gilteritinib (PDB ID: 6JQR). Gilteritinib is shown as grey sticks; the DFG-in motif is represented as green sticks. Image was generated using ChimeraX 1.11 software.

The JM domain, positioned between the lobes, maintains an autoinhibitory conformation by interacting with the activation loop.36,37 Activation of FLT3 occurs through JM displacement and structural rearrangements in the phosphate-binding loop (P-loop) and activation loop (A-loop), enabling ATP binding and catalytic activity.27 The TKD mediates phosphorylation of substrates by transferring phosphate groups from ATP within a deep catalytic cleft. TKD1 and TKD2 are separated by a flexible kinase insert, and full receptor activation depends on a hinge region that stabilizes the catalytic architecture.23

DFG-in vs. DFG-out conformations of FLT3

Investigating the conformational flexibility of FLT3, particularly within its activation segment, is essential for understanding how its catalytic function is regulated. The catalytic activity of FLT3 is governed by the position of the Asp–Phe–Gly (DFG) motif within the A-loop.35 In the active state, the DFG motif adopts a “DFG-in” configuration, where Asp829 coordinates magnesium ions and Phe830 faces the back cleft, enabling ATP binding and catalytic activity.23 This conformation is stabilized upon ligand-induced dimerization and autophosphorylation and is selectively targeted by type I inhibitors such as midostaurin and gilteritinib (Fig. 5A).10,27,38

Conversely, the “DFG-out” conformation represents an inactive state in which Asp829 flips outward and Phe830 occupies a hydrophobic pocket, accessible only in this inactive configuration, disrupting magnesium coordination and destabilizing the active site.27,36,38,39 This rearrangement creates a unique allosteric binding site exploited by type II inhibitors, including quizartinib and sorafenib, which lock FLT3 in its inactive form (Fig. 5B).2,18,35 Mutations within the A-loop (e.g., D835 variants) or gatekeeper residue (F691) can shift conformational equilibrium toward the active state, reducing type II inhibitor efficacy.11,17 Understanding these dynamic states is critical for designing conformation-specific inhibitors capable of overcoming resistance.

FLT3 mutation hotspots and structural impact

FLT3 mutations occur at distinct structural hotspots that disrupt normal regulatory mechanisms and promote constitutive kinase activation.10,13,18,23 These alterations include internal tandem duplications within the JM domain, A-loop substitutions such as D835 variants in the tyrosine kinase domain (Fig. 6), and less frequent non-canonical changes involving the gatekeeper residue (F691) and hinge region.11,17,24 Collectively, these mutations impair autoinhibition, stabilize active conformations, and influence inhibitor binding, contributing to therapeutic resistance.22,23,29,39

Internal tandem duplications (ITDs)

In AML, FLT3-ITDs disrupt the autoinhibitory JM domain, rendering the receptor constitutively active in the absence of ligand.40 ITDs vary in length from as few as 3 base pairs to over 400 base pairs41 and longer insertions correlate with worse clinical outcomes.34,41,42 Their incidence is age-dependent41 and co-occurrence with nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1), a nucleolar phosphoprotein involved in ribosomal biogenesis and frequently detected in around 30% of adult AML cases,43,44 is frequent and may attenuate the adverse impact of ITDs, particularly at low mutant-to-wild-type allelic ratios.45 NPM1 contributes to leukemogenesis by promoting uncontrolled centrosome duplication, impairing tumor suppressor pathways, disrupting DNA repair, and activating the Myc oncogene.46,47

Structurally, ITD insertions typically occur within the JM-Z segment of the JM domain, located near the hinge region. The JM domain can be subdivided into three segments for clarity: JM-Z (proximal to the hinge), JM-S (switch motif), and JM-B (binding motif).48 ITD insertions in JM-Z shift the position of JM-S, impairing its ability to guide JM-B to its intended binding site. This disruption destabilizes the interaction between JM-Z and the N-terminal lobe, leaving the JM domain in a detached, non-functional conformation.27,34,48 Additionally, ITDs often duplicate JM tyrosines (Tyr589, Tyr591, Tyr597), which are essential for autoinhibition; their duplication abolishes JM control and promotes persistent activation.15,27 This structural alteration underlies the aggressive biology of FLT3-ITD AML, correlating with inferior survival and higher relapse rates.21,22

Functionally, these changes facilitate ligand-independent receptor dimerization, favor an open conformation that exposes key phosphorylation sites, and drive constitutive autophosphorylation with activation of downstream signaling, supporting cytokine-independent cell survival and proliferation in FLT3-ITD+ leukemic cells.40

From a medicinal chemistry perspective, ITD-driven constitutive activation often remains targetable by type II inhibitors that bind the DFG-out state (e.g., quizartinib, sorafenib), because the kinase retains transient DFG-out occupancy, allowing these inhibitors to bind and inhibit enzyme activity effectively.49,50 However, durability can be limited by secondary TKD mutations (notably D835 variants) or gatekeeper substitutions (F691), which further stabilize the DFG-in conformation and reduce inhibitor binding, along with adaptive pathway upregulation.17,24,51,52

Tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations

FLT3-TKD mutations are point mutations within the A-loop that stabilize the active kinase conformation, overriding JM autoinhibition and sustaining downstream signaling.22 Among these, substitutions at aspartate 835 (D835) are the most frequent and clinically relevant.53 Molecular dynamics and docking studies show that D835 variants (e.g., D835V, D835Y, D835F, D835H) disrupt critical hydrogen bonds, increase structural flexibility, and shift equilibrium toward the DFG-in state, explaining persistent activation and resistance to inhibitors that require the inactive DFG-out conformation.53,54 Besides D835, substitutions at isoleucine 836 (I836M/N) and less frequent changes such as D839G also contribute to autoactivation.34,55,56

Functionally, TKD mutations promote constitutive phosphorylation and activation of downstream pathways similar to ITD, driving cytokine-independent proliferation and therapy resistance. Clinically, they often emerge as secondary lesions following FLT3 inhibitor therapy, correlating with relapse and poor outcomes.23,57,58

From a therapeutic perspective, this DFG-in bias compromises type II inhibitors such as quizartinib, while type I inhibitors (e.g., gilteritinib, crenolanib) and irreversible covalent inhibitors (e.g., FF-10101 targeting Cys695/Cys828) retain activity regardless of A-loop status.30 Understanding these conformational shifts is critical for designing next-generation inhibitors capable of bypassing A-loop mutations.

Non-canonical FLT3 mutations

Non-canonical FLT3 mutations refer to less common or atypical alterations outside the classical ITD and D835 hotspots.22,59–61 These secondary mutations typically arise after FLT3 inhibitor therapy as resistance mechanisms and may co-occur with ITD lesions.22,62 Depending on their structural location, they can alter protein folding, ATP-binding geometry, or conformational plasticity, thereby influencing the efficacy of both type I and type II FLT3 inhibitors.24 Reported variants include single amino acid substitutions at the gatekeeper residue (F691),24 hinge region (N676, A627, N710),63 A-loop (Y842), cysteine residue Cys695 (critical for covalent inhibitor binding),64 other TKD positions such as Y693C, D698N, G697R,24 and even extracellular domain changes (e.g., S451F, V491L).22 Although less frequent, in-frame deletions and frameshift mutations, primarily in the JM domain, have also been documented and may retain kinase activity and inhibitor sensitivity.65,66 A comprehensive summary table (Table 1) is provided at the end of this subsection, consolidating major and non-canonical FLT3 mutations with details on location, frequency, structural and functional impact, resistance profile, current TKIs or drug discovery efforts, and therapeutic relevance/design strategies.

Comparative summary of major and non-canonical FLT3 mutations and their implications for inhibitor design

Gatekeeper (F691) mutation

The gatekeeper residue phenylalanine (F691) lies adjacent to the ATP-binding pocket and regulates access to the hydrophobic back pocket exploited by type II inhibitors.67 In its native form, F691 provides π–π stacking and hydrophobic packing that stabilize inhibitor binding.68 Substitution by leucine (F691L) removes the aromatic ring and reduces steric bulk, weakening back-pocket π–π interactions and destabilizing DFG-out binding, which underpins resistance to quizartinib and related type II agents. Furthermore, reduced affinity to some type I scaffolds has also been described.22,24,27,68–70 By contrast, substitution by isoleucine (F691I) is less disruptive, as its branched aliphatic side chain better preserves the hydrophobic fit, yielding lower resistance compared with F691L.54

F691L is among the most frequent on-target relapse mutations, often co-occurring with ITD/TKD lesions.22,24,42,68–70 From a therapeutic perspective, ponatinib and pexidartinib/PLX3397 can retain activity through alternative back-pocket engagement,71,72 while irreversible covalent inhibitors (e.g., FF-10101) bypass F691L via Cys695 covalent capture.30,73

Hinge region (N676, A627, N701K) mutations

Mutations within the hinge region, which plays a critical role in anchoring inhibitors through hydrogen bonding at the ATP-binding site, represent clinically relevant secondary alterations that often emerge after FLT3 inhibitor therapy and contribute to drug resistance.35,63,74 Variants at N676, such as N676K, N676D, and N676S, occur in TKD1 near the ATP-binding site. Among these, N676K is the most frequently reported and functionally characterized, while evidence for N676D and N676S remains limited and primarily inferred from sequencing data rather than detailed structural or functional studies. These substitutions disrupt the autoinhibitory function of the JM domain and reinforce kinase domain stability, promoting constitutive activation.75 Structural modeling suggests that N676K enhances interactions between the JM domain and the hydrophobic pocket targeted by FLT3 inhibitors, reducing inhibitor affinity without abolishing ATP-site binding.63,75 Resistance is therefore driven by allosteric effects rather than direct steric hindrance, as the DFG-in/DFG-out transition remains possible.63 This subtle conformational reinforcement explains why N676K mutations impair inhibitor binding indirectly, destabilizing hinge flexibility and favoring persistent signaling.76

A627 mutations, including A627E, A627P, and A627T, also cluster in TKD1 but exhibit distinct structural consequences. A627E introduces a negatively charged residue that disrupts local folding and sustains ERK1/2 activation despite FLT3 inhibition, partly through MCL-1 upregulation.15 A627P imposes rigidity, enabling substrate binding and downstream activation even with reduced FLT3 autophosphorylation. Both variants strongly activates MAPK and AKT pathways while inducing only limited STAT5 phosphorylation.77 A627T, a rare substitution, is predicted to exert minimal steric impact but may subtly alter ATP-pocket geometry.78

Another important hinge-related alteration is N701K, which involves substitution of asparagine with lysine adjacent to the hinge region. Structural modeling shows that Lys701 projects toward the inhibitor-binding cavity but fails to replicate the stabilizing interaction with Asp698 seen in the wild type. Its unneutralized positive charge introduces unfavorable electrostatic interactions with positively charged inhibitors, creating enthalpic penalties and reducing binding affinity.64 Functionally, FLT3-ITD/N701K confers broad resistance to multiple FLT3 inhibitors,79,80 driven by these subtle electrostatic and steric effects rather than complete ATP-pocket obstruction.64

Collectively, hinge-region mutations undermine FLT3 inhibitor efficacy by preserving ATP-pocket accessibility while subtly altering conformational dynamics, thereby destabilizing binding for both type I and type II inhibitors.14,27 Among these, A627E is the most clinically significant, driving strong resistance with sustained ERK/MCL-1 signaling, whereas N676K is functionally validated but less frequent, with N676D/S rare and poorly characterized. The A627P and A627T variants are uncommon and described mainly in preclinical modeling studies. Notably, the N701K mutation, positioned adjacent to the hinge region, has been shown to block gilteritinib binding via electrostatic and steric effects and confers broad resistance to several FLT3 inhibitors in vitro and in patients (e.g., FLT3-ITD/N701K).79 Overcoming hinge-driven resistance, including N701K, thus requires next-generation inhibitors designed for hinge adaptability as well as rational combination approaches, such as pairing FLT3 inhibitors with MAPK or MCL-1 pathway blockers.81,82 Irreversible covalent agents like FF-10101 and dual-target molecules may also offer partial solutions, but hinge flexibility remains a key challenge for sustained therapeutic effect.

Activation loop (Y842) mutation

The A-loop is a critical regulatory segment of FLT3 and frequently influences kinase activity.37,68 Within this loop, Y842 plays a pivotal role in maintaining conformational stability and downstream signaling.37 Mutations at this residue, Y842C, Y842F, and Y842H, have distinct functional outcomes.37,54,83,84 Y842C is the most disruptive, as loss of the aromatic ring and hydroxyl group increases loop flexibility, destabilizing the inactive conformation and reducing inhibitor efficacy. In contrast, Y842H retains partial polar interactions, leading to milder structural perturbation and less pronounced resistance.54 Y842F preserves aromaticity but abolishes hydrogen bonding, selectively disrupting RAS/ERK signaling by weakening FLT3–SHP2 interaction without affecting AKT activation.21 Functional studies confirm that FLT3-ITD/Y842 mutations reduce cell viability and colony formation, with Y842F notably causing delayed tumor growth in vivo, underscoring the pivotal role of Y842 in RAS/ERK-driven leukemogenesis.21,85

Cysteine-targeting (Cys695) mutation

Cys695 is a cytoplasmic cysteine residue within the FLT3 kinase domain, located adjacent to Cys694, the hinge-binding residue that anchors ATP-competitive inhibitors via hydrogen bonding86,87 (Fig. 7A and B). This proximity places Cys695 near the ATP-binding site, making it a critical nucleophilic target for irreversible FLT3 inhibitors such as FF-10101.64 Its thiol group enables covalent bond formation, ensuring prolonged target engagement and sustained kinase inhibition.73 Resistance frequently arises through C695F substitution, which eliminates the thiol moiety and abolishes covalent binding.64,88 Other reported variants include C695Y, C695S, and C695R, all replacing cysteine with residues lacking nucleophilic functionality.30,73 These mutations compromise the design of covalent inhibitors by removing the reactive site required for irreversible inhibition.

Although Cys695 is essential for covalent inhibition, its side chain projects away from the ATP-binding cavity; therefore, mutations at this position do not directly impair reversible inhibitor binding.64 Moreover, oxidation of Cys695 may also modulate FLT3 signaling by inducing redox-dependent conformational changes, adding an additional regulatory layer that could be exploited for designing inhibitors targeting oxidative states of FLT3.86 Understanding these structural nuances and the role of Cys695 in resistance is critical for designing next-generation covalent FLT3 inhibitors capable of overcoming mutation-driven resistance.73

Miscellaneous rare mutations

Although FLT3-ITD and D835 mutations are the most prevalent FLT3 alterations in AML,89 several less frequent but clinically relevant variants have been reported. One example is N841I in the A-loop, which induces conformational changes that promote ligand-independent constitutive activation and signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (STAT5) phosphorylation, a key downstream mediator of FLT3 signaling involved in hematopoietic regulation, cell proliferation and survival and its elevated expression is closely associated with therapeutic resistance,90 without affecting receptor trafficking to the plasma membrane.23,91 Beyond ITDs, point mutations within the JM domain, including V592A, V579A, F594L, and F590/GY591D,23,33 enhance FLT3 autophosphorylation and activation, although their effects are generally weaker than those observed with classical ITD or TKD mutations.23 Clinical analyses indicate that V592 variants may co-occur with ITD lesions at relapse. Additionally, about 9% of patients with FLT3-ITD acquire new mutations (in TKD, ITD, or as point mutations) during disease progression.74,92

Other rare alterations include a combined 6-bp ITD in the JM domain and a 10-bp deletion in the TKD, which together introduce a valine–isoleucine–proline–threonine (VIPT) sequence and result in substitutions at D835 and, less commonly, I836.22,23,31 D324N, located in the immunoglobulin-like domain, occurs in around 1.5% of AML cases but shows minimal phenotypic impact in vitro; interestingly, it is more frequent in CML and ALL than AML.23 D839, positioned within the A-loop, participates in a hydrogen-bond network stabilizing the inactive conformation; thus, D839G mutation disrupts these interactions, destabilizing the inactive state and reducing inhibitor binding affinity.54 Additional secondary TKD substitutions, G697R/S, Y693C/N, and D698N, have been associated with acquired resistance to FLT3 inhibitors.16,22,29,56,75 Notably, G697R does not alter FLT3 phosphorylation levels, but may still contribute to clinical resistance through subtle structural effects.93

Implications for mutation-driven selectivity and next-generation design

The structural dynamics of FLT3, particularly within the kinase domain and activation loop, critically dictate inhibitor binding affinity and selectivity.103 Activation loop mutations such as D835 variants shift the equilibrium toward the active DFG-in state, compromising type II inhibitor binding,24,104 whereas gatekeeper substitutions like F691L primarily disrupt hydrophobic and π–π interactions within the back pocket without altering the DFG-in/out balance.38,69 Consequently, type I inhibitors, which target the active form, generally retain activity against both FLT3-ITD and TKD mutations, while type II inhibitors remain largely restricted to ITD-driven AML due to their dependence on the inactive state.51 Irreversible covalent inhibitors circumvent these limitations by forming permanent bonds with nucleophilic residues (e.g., Cys695), ensuring sustained inhibition regardless of conformational bias.84 Beyond canonical hotspots, rare non-canonical mutations in the hinge region (e.g., N676K, A627E) and activation loop (e.g., Y842C) further complicate inhibitor design by introducing steric and electrostatic perturbations that destabilize binding.24 Collectively, these insights underscore the necessity of mutation-tailored strategies, whether through dual-binding scaffolds, hinge-adaptive chemotypes, or covalent warheads, to overcome resistance and achieve durable clinical responses.

Type I FLT3 inhibitors: structural features and mechanistic insights

The type I FLT3 inhibitors, also known as DFG-in inhibitors, can interact with both active and inactive conformations of FLT3. These inhibitors typically occupy the ATP-binding site and act as ATP-competitive inhibitors,28 which enables them to effectively target FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutated receptors.11,105 In addition to binding within the ATP pocket, they may also engage residues near the gatekeeper region adjacent to the activation loop.106 Notably, most type I inhibitors display broad kinase activity, which limits their selectivity.51,89 Common examples include midostaurin, lestaurtinib, gilteritinib, crenolanib, sunitinib, and pacritinib.16

First-generation inhibitors

Midostaurin

Midostaurin was the first type I FLT3 inhibitor approved by the FDA in 2017 for adult patients with FLT3-mutated AML.17,29,107 It has shown clinical efficacy in patients harboring FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations.3,107Midostaurin is a multi-kinase agent, inhibiting FLT3 along with c-KIT, PKC, SYK, FLK-1, AKT, PKA, FGR, SRC, PDGFR, and VEGFR.12,105In vitro, it induces G1 arrest and apoptosis in Ba/F3 cells harboring FLT3 mutations, with IC50 values below 10 nM.16 Clinically, it is administered in combination with cytarabine and daunorubicin during induction, followed by high-dose cytarabine consolidation.14,34

Midostaurin binds to the ATP pocket in the active (DFG-in) conformation of FLT3 and can also interact with the inactive (DFG-out) state. Docking studies indicate that its binding involves hydrogen bonds with hinge residues (Cys694 and Glu692) and additional polar contacts that stabilize the complex (Fig. 8).20,50 In FLT3-ITD mutants, midostaurin shows stronger affinity compared to wild-type, particularly in the DFG-in state, due to an increased number of polar interactions.50 These features explain its ability to maintain activity across different FLT3 conformations.

2D interactions of type I first-generation FLT3 inhibitors within the ATP-binding site, as reported for midostaurin, lestaurtinib, and sunitinib.16 Hydrogen bonds are depicted as red dashed lines, and hydrophobic contacts are shown as brown dashed blocks.

Resistance to midostaurin can arise from both on-target and off-target mechanisms. Among FLT3 mutations, hinge-region substitutions such as N676K/D/S and G697R/S, as well as gatekeeper mutations F691I/L, significantly reduce drug binding.14,18,22–24,46,51,91,98 A rare non-JMD-inserting ITD variant (A627E) has also been reported in cases of primary resistance.15 In contrast, activation loop mutations (e.g., D835X, Y842C) generally do not impair midostaurin activity.27 Off-target resistance involves JAK pathway alterations, including V617F, S703I, R724H, and A573V, which were detected in approximately 46% of resistant patients.14

Lestaurtinib

Lestaurtinib was the first FLT3 inhibitor investigated for AML and was granted orphan drug status by the FDA in 2006.34 In addition to FLT3, it potently inhibits JAK2 and neurotrophic receptors TrkA, TrkB, and TrkC.31,106,107Lestaurtinib exhibits strong in vitro activity against FLT3, with an IC50 of 3 nM,16,27 and inhibits both FLT3-WT and mutant FLT3 (ITD and TKD), contributing to prolonged survival in preclinical models.16,105,108

Similar to midostaurin, lestaurtinib primarily binds to the ATP-binding site of FLT3 in its active conformation, while retaining the ability to interact with the inactive state. Its binding involves hydrogen bonds with hinge residues Glu692 and Cys694 (Fig. 8).16

Resistance to lestaurtinib involves multiple mechanisms. FLT3 mutations such as F691 (gatekeeper) and A627P reduce drug binding.18,20 Secondary activation loop mutations (D835 and I836) were reported in approximately 20% of AML patients receiving lestaurtinib.14 Off-target mechanisms include Pim-1 overexpression, which activates STAT5 signaling, and SHP1 suppression, both contributing to resistance.14,62 Elevated FLT3 ligand levels have also been associated with reduced sensitivity to lestaurtinib.14

Sunitinib

Sunitinib exhibits broad kinase activity, targeting FLT3 along with KIT, PDGFR, VEGFR, RAS/RAF, and JAK2.16,33,89 It is active against both FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations.34 Clinically, sunitinib is approved for the treatment of gastrointestinal stromal tumors, advanced renal cell carcinoma, and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors.16

Docking studies suggest that sunitinib binds to the ATP-binding site of FLT3 in its active (DFG-in) conformation. Its binding involves hydrogen bonds with hinge residue Cys694 and Asp829 at the DFG motif, stabilizing the interaction within the ATP pocket (Fig. 8).16,18

Sunitinib retains activity against the F691L gatekeeper mutation, as modeling studies indicate it does not rely on direct interaction with this residue.16 However, other mutations such as A848P, A627P, and Y842C have been reported in AML patients treated with sunitinib.18,84

Second-generation inhibitors

Gilteritinib

Gilteritinib was FDA-approved in 2018 for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory FLT3-mutated AML.34,109 It offers greater specificity, longer half-life, and higher potency compared to first-generation inhibitors, with reduced off-target toxicity.89,110Gilteritinib is active against both FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations,34,89 and also inhibits AXL, a kinase implicated in resistance to FLT3 inhibitors.34 Its IC50 against FLT3 is approximately 0.29 nM.16,51

Gilteritinib primarily binds to the ATP-binding site of FLT3 in its active conformation. It forms two hydrogen bonds with hinge residues Cys694 and Glu692 and engages in hydrophobic interactions with the gatekeeper residue F691 (Fig. 9).84,111 These interactions stabilize its binding within the ATP pocket. Although designed for the active state, gilteritinib can also interact with the inactive (DFG-out) conformation, as shown by docking studies.50

2D interactions of type I second-generation FLT3 inhibitors within the ATP-binding site, as reported for gilteritinib,84,111crenolanib,16,112 and MRX-2843.100 Hydrogen bonds are depicted as red dashed lines, and hydrophobic contacts are shown as brown dashed blocks.

Resistance to gilteritinib is frequently associated with gatekeeper mutation F691L, which disrupts hydrophobic interactions critical for binding.3,24,27 Additional mutations such as Y693C, G697S, and D698N also reduce drug affinity.24,27

Crenolanib

Crenolanib was initially developed as a PDGFR inhibitor but later demonstrated high affinity for FLT3 16,35 with an IC50 of approximately 2 nM.20 It exhibits potent activity against both FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations.31,110 As a second-generation FLT3 inhibitor, crenolanib offers improved specificity, potency, and pharmacokinetic properties compared to first-generation agents.33,89,110

Docking studies indicate that crenolanib binds to the ATP-binding site of FLT3 in its active conformation, forming a key hydrogen bond with hinge residue Cys694.16,35 Additional hydrophobic interactions involve residues such as Leu616, Ala642, Phe691, and Cys693, while aromatic contacts with Phe830 and a hydrogen bond between its terminal amino group and Asp698 further stabilize the complex (Fig. 9).35,112

Resistance to crenolanib can occur through multiple mechanisms. On-target mutations such as F691L (gatekeeper), N701K, and K429E reduce drug binding.16,24,27,105,113 Off-target resistance may involve activation of the RAS/MAPK pathway or co-occurring IDH1 mutations, both of which diminish crenolanib's efficacy.59,114

MRX-2843

MRX-2843 is a dual inhibitor targeting FLT3 and MERTK, a tyrosine kinase frequently overexpressed in AML.34,98,100 It demonstrates potent activity with enzymatic IC50 values of 0.64 nM for FLT3 and 1.3 nM for MERTK.34,100,115MRX-2843 retains inhibitory activity against quizartinib resistance-associated mutations, including F691L and D835 variants,34 and shows comparable potency to gilteritinib in FLT3-ITD AML cell lines.115

Structural modeling based on the crystal structure of its analogue UNC569 suggests that MRX-2843 occupies the ATP-binding site and can engage both active and inactive conformations of FLT3.116,117 This dual conformational compatibility contributes to its robust activity against gatekeeper F691L mutation. A distinctive feature of MRX-2843 is its flexible cyclopropylethyl moiety, which forms favorable van der Waals contacts with either leucine or phenylalanine at position 691, supporting strong binding regardless of mutation status (Fig. 9).100

Preclinical studies confirm that MRX-2843 maintains activity against common resistance-conferring mutations such as F691L and D835, making it a promising candidate for overcoming limitations of other FLT3 inhibitors.34,100 To date, no additional resistance-conferring mutations or bypass signaling pathways have been reported for MRX-2843.

Type II FLT3 inhibitors: structural features and mechanistic insights

Type II inhibitors share a similar hinge-binding interaction with type I inhibitors but differ by extending into an allosteric pocket adjacent to the ATP-binding site, stabilizing the inactive DFG-out conformation.20 This structural requirement explains their selectivity for FLT3-ITD AML cells and lack of activity against TKD mutations, which favor the active state.17,52 Representative type II inhibitors include sorafenib, ponatinib, and quizartinib.114

First-generation inhibitors

Sorafenib

Sorafenib is one of the most widely used first-generation type II inhibitors.110,118 It was originally developed as a Raf-1 inhibitor to block the RAS–MEK–ERK survival signaling pathway,16,113 but it also exhibits multi-kinase activity, targeting PDGFR, VEGFR2/3, and c-kit, in addition to FLT3.35,52Sorafenib fully suppresses FLT3/ITD activity with an IC50 of 69.3 ng mL−1107,119 and shows greater efficacy against FLT3-ITD and D835G mutations than D835Y or FLT3-WT in Ba/F3 cells.16

Molecular dynamics simulations revealed that sorafenib adopts the canonical type II binding pose within the FLT3 kinase domain.35 Its N-methylpicolinamide “head” anchors the hinge region through two hydrogen bonds with Cys694, while the pyridine moiety interacts with hydrophobic residues Ala642 and Leu818. The central phenyl ring forms edge-to-face π–π interactions with Phe691 (gatekeeper) and Phe830 (DFG motif). The urea linker establishes favorable hydrogen bonds with Glu661 and Asp829, and the hydrophobic 1-chloro-2-(trifluoromethyl)benzene tail occupies the allosteric back pocket, forming hydrophobic contacts with Met665. These interactions stabilize the inactive DFG-out conformation (Fig. 10).16,35 Notably, the D835V mutation alters Phe830 dynamics, disrupting sorafenib's binding pose.35

2D interactions of type II first-generation FLT3 inhibitors within the ATP-binding site, as reported for sorafenib,16,35ponatinib,20,121pexidartinib,18 and tandutinib.20 Hydrogen bonds are depicted as red dashed lines, and hydrophobic contacts are shown as brown dashed blocks.

Resistance to sorafenib can occur through multiple mechanisms. On-target mutations such as F691L, D835F/V/Y, Y842C/H, and A848P significantly reduce drug binding.24,27,51,113,118 Off-target resistance involves activation of alternative pathways, including PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling,18,62 JAK1 V658F mutation,14 and PIM-1 overexpression, which promotes proliferative and anti-apoptotic signaling through increased MCL-1 expression.14

Ponatinib

Ponatinib emerged as another inhibitor designed to exploit the inactive conformation of FLT3 by extending into the allosteric pocket adjacent to the ATP-binding site.15,89,120 This structural orientation underpins its activity against FLT3-ITD mutations, while its efficacy against TKD variants remains limited.120 Notably, ponatinib retains inhibitory activity against the gatekeeper F691L mutation in preclinical models106,107 and has demonstrated potency against additional FLT3 mutants, including Y693C and D698N.112 Beyond FLT3, ponatinib is a multikinase inhibitor with activity against Abl, c-KIT, c-Src, FGFR1, PDGFRα, VEGFR2, and LYN.106

Molecular modeling and SAR analyses revealed that ponatinib achieves its inhibitory effect through a well-optimized scaffold that anchors the hinge region and extends into the allosteric pocket, stabilizing the DFG-out conformation.20,121 Its imidazopyridazine core forms key hydrogen bonds at the hinge, while the phenyl moiety (Ring A) occupies the hydrophobic pocket located behind the gatekeeper residue. The ethylene linker maintains rigidity, promoting the extended conformation, and two aromatic rings (Ring A and B) establish van der Waals interactions within the adenine-binding site (Fig. 10).121

Resistance to ponatinib can arise from activation loop mutations such as Y842C/H or D839G, which disrupt key molecular interactions and destabilize the inactive FLT3 conformer, thereby reducing binding affinity.54

Pexidartinib

Pexidartinib was introduced as a first-generation inhibitor with selective activity against FLT3-ITD, exhibiting an IC50 of 11 nM.18,34 It was specifically developed to address resistance driven by the gatekeeper F691 mutations, particularly F691L.34 While pexidartinib effectively targets FLT3/F691L, it remains vulnerable to several other mutations.27 Beyond FLT3, it also inhibits KIT and CSF1R, reflecting its multikinase profile.106

Compared with other inhibitors in this class, pexidartinib features a shorter linker between its central and tail pyridine rings, which enhances interactions within the activation loop and helps compensate for the reduced binding affinity caused by the F691L mutation.24 The molecule retains key hydrogen bonds with Asp829 and hinge residues Cys694 and Glu692, thereby maintaining potency despite structural changes in the gatekeeper region (Fig. 10).18 Unlike most inhibitors that employ urea or amide linkers, pexidartinib incorporates a methylamine linker, a distinctive structural feature that supports this binding mode.20

Resistance to pexidartinib has been associated with activating mutations such as M664I, which disrupt its binding and reduce inhibitory activity.27,110

Tandutinib

Tandutinib exhibits inhibitory activity against FLT3 by stabilizing its inactive conformation and occupying an additional hydrophobic pocket adjacent to the ATP-binding site.84 In addition to FLT3, it also targets PDGFR and KIT, reflecting its multikinase profile.16,106 Functionally, tandutinib promotes apoptosis and suppresses FLT3/ITD phosphorylation, cellular proliferation, and downstream signaling through the MAPK and PI3K pathways.119

Molecular docking studies indicate that tandutinib fits into the adenine-binding pocket of FLT3, with its 4-isopropoxyphenyl urea moiety extending into the allosteric back pocket, consistent with the binding mode of other inhibitors in this class (Fig. 10).20

Resistance to tandutinib primarily arises through on-target secondary mutations at the activation loop residue D835, such as D835V and D835Y, which hinder binding by stabilizing the FLT3 kinase in the active (DFG-in) conformation.122 Additionally, cancer cells can bypass FLT3 inhibition via upregulation of alternative signaling pathways, including PI3K/AKT/mTOR, RAS/MEK/ERK, and JAK/STAT.14 Another mechanism involves the overexpression of drug efflux pumps, notably MRP7/ABCC10, which reduces intracellular drug concentration and contributes to resistance.123

Second-generation inhibitors

Quizartinib

Quizartinib (Vanflyta®), approved in July 2023 for newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD AML in combination with standard chemotherapy, stabilizes FLT3 in its inactive DFG-out conformation by occupying the ATP-binding site and extending into the adjacent allosteric pocket.84,124 This structural mechanism accounts for its potent activity against FLT3-ITD mutations, with an IC50 of 0.56 nM in MV4-11 cells,16 while TKD mutants remain resistant.84 This drug demonstrates high selectivity, nanomolar potency, and a prolonged half-life.89,110 In addition to FLT3, quizartinib also inhibits c-KIT and PDGFRα.27,120

Structural analysis of FLT3 bound to quizartinib reveals a well-defined network of interactions that stabilize the inactive DFG-out conformation. The urea linker forms two hydrogen bonds with Glu661 and Asp829, anchoring the inhibitor within the catalytic cleft. The central phenyl ring is positioned between Phe691 (gatekeeper) and Phe830 of the DFG motif, creating strong π–π stacking interactions. The imidazobenzothiazole moiety contributes an additional weak hydrogen bond with Cys694 and engages in π–π interactions with Tyr693 in the hinge region. The isoxazole ring extends into a hydrophobic pocket comprising Met664, Met665, Ile674, Leu802, and Ile827, while the morpholinoethoxy group improves solubility without compromising binding (Fig. 11).23,35,49

2D interactions of type II second-generation FLT3 inhibitors within the ATP-binding site, as reported for quizartinib23,35,49 and MZH29.125 Hydrogen bonds are depicted as red dashed lines, and hydrophobic contacts are shown as brown dashed blocks.

Resistance arises from on-target mutations, including F691L, D835F/V/Y, Y842H/C, E608, N676D/K, and G697R,22,27,34,49,110 which disrupt key contacts and destabilize the inactive conformation. Off-target mechanisms include activation of AXL, PIM, ERK, MAPK via FGF2/FGFR1, JAK/STAT5, and PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathways.14,18,24,34,105 Bortezomib has shown efficacy in overcoming quizartinib resistance in AML cell lines,51 while FF-10101, an irreversible covalent FLT3 inhibitor, retains activity against a broad spectrum of quizartinib-resistant mutations (including F691L, D835, and Y842 variants) in preclinical models and primary AML samples.73

Bortezomib has shown efficacy in overcoming quizartinib resistance in cell lines, while irreversible inhibitors like FF-10101 retain activity against F691L and other resistant mutations.107

MZH29

MZH29 is a novel FLT3 inhibitor that demonstrates potent activity against WT-FLT3 and clinically relevant resistance-associated mutations, including FLT3-ITD, FLT3-ITD/F691L, FLT3-D835H/Y/V, and FLT3-K663Q, as confirmed in engineered BaF3 cell assays.125 This broad-spectrum activity positions MZH29 as a promising candidate for the treatment of AML driven by either FLT3-WT or drug-resistant FLT3 variants.125 In AML cell lines, MZH29 exhibits remarkable potency, with an IC50 of 0.14 nM in MV4-11 cells (FLT3-ITD) and comparable sub-nanomolar activity in MOLM-13 and MOLM-14 cells.125

Docking studies using FLT3 in its inactive conformation (PDB ID: 4RT7) revealed that MZH29 occupies the ATP-binding site.125 Consistent with other type II inhibitors, the urea linker between two phenyl rings forms two hydrogen bonds with Glu661 of the αC helix. The quinoline moiety maintains a critical hydrogen bond with Cys694 at the hinge region, while the morpholine group forms an additional hydrogen bond with Leu616. A cation–π interaction between Lys644 and the phenyl ring further stabilizes the complex (Fig. 11).125

Unlike quizartinib, which loses efficacy against the F691L gatekeeper mutation, MZH29 retains strong inhibitory activity, achieving an IC50 of approximately 1.1 nM in BaF3 cells expressing FLT3-ITD/F691L.125 Docking studies using a modeled FLT3/F691L structure revealed that MZH29 forms a robust interaction network, including four hydrogen bonds with Lys614, Glu661, Cys694, and Asp829, as well as a π–π stacking interaction with Phe830. These interactions are thought to underpin its retained potency against the gatekeeper mutation, with Glu661 and Asp829 identified as critical residues for effective inhibition of FLT3-F691L.125

To provide a clear comparison between the two major classes of FLT3 inhibitors, Fig. 12 summarizes the common structural features that distinguish type I from type II inhibitors. While both classes share certain pharmacophoric elements essential for kinase binding, they differ in their interaction patterns with the ATP-binding site and adjacent regions, which influences their activity against FLT3-WT and mutant forms.

Irreversible FLT3 inhibitors: covalent warheads and cysteine targeting

Irreversible FLT3 inhibitors represent a next-generation strategy to overcome resistance associated with reversible agents (Table 2). Unlike type I and type II inhibitors, these molecules form covalent bonds with nucleophilic residues, primarily cysteine, within the FLT3 kinase domain, ensuring prolonged target engagement, reduced dissociation rates, and sustained inhibition of FLT3 signaling.30,51,87,126 This approach confers potent activity against FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations, including D835 variants that compromise type II inhibitors.84,98,114 Recent efforts have expanded covalent design to target alternative cysteine sites, such as Cys828, thereby broadening therapeutic options.18 By integrating conformational selectivity with covalent chemistry, irreversible FLT3 inhibitors hold promise for durable clinical responses in AML.

Comparative overview of the reported irreversible inhibitors

FF-10101

FF-10101 is the only selective irreversible FLT3 inhibitor under clinical investigation.23,73 It exhibits potent activity against FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations, including quizartinib-resistant variants, and effectively inhibits phosphorylation of FLT3 and downstream signaling pathways such as ERK, STAT5, and AKT.73,87 This broad activity extends to gatekeeper and noncanonical mutations, as well as extracellular activating variants that confer resistance to other FLT3 inhibitors.87

Structurally, FF-10101 incorporates an acrylamide warhead that forms a covalent bond with the thiol group of Cys695 adjacent to the ATP-binding site.18,73,87 In addition to covalent anchoring, FF-10101 establishes two hydrogen bonds via its pyrimidinamine moiety with hinge residues Glu692 and Cys694, and an additional hydrogen bond between its central carbonyl group and Cys695.18 Further stabilization occurs through hydrogen bonding with Lys644 (Fig. 13).16 These interactions enable FF-10101 to engage both active and inactive FLT3 conformations, underpinning its strong selectivity and potency.73

2D interaction diagrams of the irreversible FLT3 inhibitors, FF 10101, Liu 4K, and FLC 8, within the ATP binding site. For FF-10101, the figure highlights its covalent engagement with Cys695.18,73,87 For Liu-4K, the covalent bond to Cys828 is illustrated.127 For FLC-8 covalent engagement with Cys807 is shown.126 Hydrogen bonds are depicted as red dashed lines, hydrophobic contacts as brown dashed blocks, and Cys–inhibitor covalent bonds as bold magenta lines.

Resistance to FF-10101 primarily arises from mutations at Cys695 (C695F/R/S/Y), which abolish covalent binding. Secondary mutations such as N701K, associated with gilteritinib resistance, also reduce FF-10101 sensitivity. Additional resistance mechanisms include MYCN/D31P and NRAS/G12C mutations acquired during treatment, which further compromise efficacy.88

From a clinical perspective, FF-10101 represents the most advanced irreversible FLT3 inhibitor currently undergoing clinical development. It was evaluated in a first-in-human Phase 1 study where the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) was established at 75 mg twice daily and the recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D) was established based on consistent and tolerable suppression of FLT3 signaling. Early clinical data indicates activity in relapsed or refractory AML, including cases previously exposed to FLT3 inhibitors, although definitive efficacy conclusions, especially after gilteritinib, remains preliminary and requires validation in larger studies.87 Pharmacologically, FF-10101 achieves sustained FLT3 inhibition despite a relatively short terminal half-life, reflecting its covalent mechanism in which prolonged target engagement is eliminated from systemic exposure, thereby enabling flexible integration into combination myelosuppressive regimens. Clinically, it demonstrates an overall favorable tolerability profile, with diarrhea being the most consistent dose-related adverse event above the RP2D. However, its irreversible engagement of the relatively conserved Cys695 residue raises potential concerns regarding selectivity and the likelihood of resistance emergence through cysteine mutation, as already suggested by preclinical and early clinical observations. Compared to preclinical covalent FLT3 inhibitors targeting alternative cysteine residues, these features highlight both the therapeutic advantage of robust target occupancy and the inherent limitations of reliance on a single covalent anchoring site, underscoring the need for continued optimization of covalent FLT3 inhibitor design strategies.87

8-Phenylquinazolin-2-amine derivative (Liu-4k)

Wei et al. reported Liu-4k as a highly potent and selective irreversible FLT3 inhibitor (IC50 = 8.3 nM). It demonstrated strong antiproliferative activity against FLT3-ITD+ AML cells and resistant variants such as D835V and D835I. Mechanistically, Liu-4k covalently targets Cys828 within the ATP-binding site, suppressing FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signaling cascades, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.127 These findings position it as a promising covalent FLT3 inhibitor targeting Cys828 for AML therapy. No resistance data have been reported for this compound, and it remains at the preclinical stage.

Structurally, Liu-4k adopts a type I binding mode. Its quinazoline core anchors the hinge region via hydrogen bonds with Glu692 and Cys694, while the acrylamide warhead, positioned ortho on the phenyl ring, enables covalent engagement with Cys828. The methylpiperazine moiety extends toward the solvent, forming an additional hydrogen bond with Leu616 and enhancing arene–hydrogen interactions with its side chain (Fig. 13).127

FLC-8 (Shi-18)

FLC-8 is a selective irreversible FLT3 inhibitor with IC50 value of 10.2 nM, and low-nanomolar antiproliferative activity in FLT3-ITD+ AML cells (MOLM-13 IC50 = 2.45 nM; MV4-11 IC50 = 0.28 nM).126 In Ba/F3 panels, it retains potency against FLT3-ITD (IC50 = 3.10 nM) and resistance-associated variants including FLT3-ITD/G697R (IC50 = 11.60 nM) and FLT3-ITD/N676D (IC50 = 24.10 nM), while showing reduced activity on F691L and D835V/Y mutants.126 Functionally, FLC-8 inhibits FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream STAT5/AKT/ERK signaling, induces G0/G1 arrest and apoptosis, and demonstrates dose-dependent antitumor efficacy in MV4-11 xenografts with favorable tolerability.126 Structurally, FLC-8 adopts a type II (DFG-out) binding mode that orients its para-acrylamide warhead toward a novel covalent site, Cys807, within the ATP-binding cleft; mass spectrometry peptide mapping confirms covalent modification at Cys807.126 The 6-methylisoxazolo[5,4-b]pyridin-3-amine core anchors the hinge, while the aryl-urea segment aligns the Michael acceptor for irreversible capture of Cys807 and contributes to Glu661/Asp829 hydrogen-bonding and π–π stabilization along the phenyl linker126 (Fig. 13). Consistent with this type II, back-pocket–dependent geometry, activity is attenuated by F691L and D835 substitutions, which restrict back-pocket formation or cysteine accessibility and thereby disfavor covalent capture at Cys807.126

Comparative analysis of covalent FLT3 inhibitors and cysteine targeting landscape

Irreversible FLT3 inhibitors utilize distinct covalent targeting strategies by engaging different cysteine residues, most notably Cys695, Cys828, and Cys807. These residues occupy structurally distinct positions within the ATP-binding cleft, leading to differences in accessibility, selectivity, and resistance susceptibility. FF-10101 targets the relatively conserved Cys695 near the hinge region, enabling strong and durable target engagement. In contrast, Liu-4k covalently binds to Cys828, a residue located deeper within the ATP-binding pocket, which may confer improved selectivity due to its less conserved local environment. Meanwhile, FLC-8 targets Cys807 within a BP-II region, rendering its accessibility highly dependent on conformation. These structural distinctions are mirrored in their binding behaviors, with Liu-4k functioning as a type I inhibitor, FLC-8 operating through a type II binding mode, and FF-10101 displaying conformational flexibility that enables engagement of both active and inactive kinase states. Collectively, the identification of multiple ligandable cysteine residues broadens the covalent design landscape for FLT3 inhibition, while simultaneously increasing the complexity of achieving optimal selectivity and minimizing off-target covalent interactions.

Synergistic approaches: dual-modality FLT3 inhibitors and beyond

Resistance to single-target FLT3 inhibitors remains a major clinical challenge, often driven by secondary mutations and adaptive signaling. Dual-modality strategies aim to overcome these limitations by simultaneously inhibiting FLT3 and cooperating oncogenic pathways within a single molecule. This approach offers two key advantages: comprehensive blockade of parallel survival mechanisms, and reduced likelihood of resistance compared to monotherapy.122 From a medicinal chemistry perspective, dual inhibitors integrate structural features for both targets into one scaffold, balancing potency, selectivity, and drug-like properties.128 This section highlights representative dual FLT3 inhibitors and their role in addressing resistance through multi-target strategies.

Dual FLT3/ACVR-1 inhibitors

Activin receptor-like kinase 2 (ACVR1), also known as ALK2, is a serine/threonine kinase within the TGF-β superfamily that mediates phosphorylation of SMAD1/5 transcription factors.129,130 Once phosphorylated, SMAD1/5 forms a complex with SMAD4 and translocates to the nucleus, regulating transcription of genes involved in cell growth and differentiation. Beyond canonical SMAD signaling, ACVR1 also interacts with non-SMAD pathways, including MAPK and PI3K/AKT, contributing to leukemic cell survival and therapy resistance.129 Importantly, ACVR1 is overexpressed in FLT3-mutated AML, and inhibition of ACVR1 expression sensitizes AML cells to FLT3 inhibitors, highlighting its role as a critical biomarker and therapeutic target.129 In the context of FLT3-driven signaling, dual inhibition of FLT3 and ACVR1 offers a rational strategy to overcome resistance to single-target FLT3 inhibitors.

TP-0184 (itacnosertib)

TP-0184 is a potent dual FLT3/ACVR1 inhibitor designed to overcome resistance to FLT3 inhibitors and suppress AML progression synergistically with venetoclax.129TP-0184 effectively and selectively inhibits both kinases, with Kd values of 0.247 nM for ACVR1 and 0.16–19 nM for FLT3 mutants, enabling strong activity against resistant FLT3 variants comparable to gilteritinib while simultaneously targeting ACVR1.129

Binding mode analysis reveals that TP-0184 occupies the ATP-binding pocket of FLT3 in a manner similar to gilteritinib but with distinct interaction patterns. The pyridinyl-pyrimidine nitrogens of TP-0184 form hydrogen bonds with Leu616 and Asp829 of the DFG motif, while one pyridine ring engages in hydrophobic contact with Phe691 (Fig. 14). Unlike gilteritinib's primarily hydrophilic binding, TP-0184 exhibits a more intricate pattern involving both hydrophilic (Asp) and hydrophobic (Leu, Phe) residues, indicating a more complex binding mode.129

TP-0184 has completed Phase 1 first-in-human study in patients with advanced solid tumors131 and was evaluated in a Phase 1/2 clinical trial for the treatment anemia in adults with low- or intermediate-risk myelodysplastic syndromes,132 indicating its progression into early clinical development across multiple disease settings.

Dual FLT3/AKT inhibitors

In refractory AML, treatment with FLT3 inhibitors can lead to elevated FLT3 ligand levels, which sustain activation of downstream mediators such as AKT and contribute to resistance.2,133,134 AKT, also known as protein kinase B (PKB), is a serine/threonine kinase that plays a central role in the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, regulating cell growth, proliferation, and survival. Persistent activation of this pathway promotes leukemic cell survival and limits the efficacy of FLT3-targeted therapy.135 Therefore, simultaneous inhibition of FLT3 and AKT represents a rational strategy to overcome resistance and enhance therapeutic outcomes.

A674563

A674563 is a dual FLT3/AKT inhibitor with IC50 values of 134 nM and 180 nM, respectively.2 It exhibits potent activity against FLT3-ITD mutations and can overcome resistance driven by FLT3 ligand-induced signaling by concurrently blocking FLT3 and AKT pathways.136 This dual mechanism positions A674563 as a promising candidate for the treatment of FLT3-ITD+ AML.2

Modeling studies indicate that A674563 binds within the ATP-binding pocket of FLT3 through two key hydrogen bonds: one between its indazole NH and Cys694 in the hinge region, and another between its pyridine nitrogen and Lys644. Additionally, its terminal phenyl group extends toward the solvent-exposed area, forming a π–π interaction with Tyr842, which helps stabilize the complex (Fig. 14).136

Preclinical data indicate that A674563 retains strong activity against FLT3-ITD and effectively counteracts ligand-driven resistance.136 However, it remains at the preclinical stage, and no clinical trials have been reported to date. Further studies are needed to confirm its efficacy against additional resistance-associated mutations and to evaluate its therapeutic potential in AML.

Dual FLT3/AMPKα inhibitors

AMPK acts as a central metabolic sensor in AML, regulating energy homeostasis and stress responses. It supports leukemia stem cell fitness through mitophagy and mitochondrial quality control and modulates the DNA-damage response in blast populations.137 Although AMPK has been reported to exhibit both tumor-suppressive and tumor-supportive roles, its activity is constitutively high in AML stem cells, suggesting a survival advantage for leukemic cells.138 This duality makes AMPK an attractive secondary target alongside FLT3 to overcome resistance and disrupt metabolic adaptation in AML.

Wu-5

Wu-5 (Fig. 14) is a novel USP10 inhibitor that simultaneously targets FLT3 and AMPKα signaling pathways. It promotes apoptosis and restores sensitivity to FLT3 inhibitors in FLT3-ITD+ leukemia cell lines and primary AML samples.139Wu-5 demonstrates an IC50 of 8.3 µM against USP10 and effectively suppresses FLT3-driven signaling while inhibiting AMPK-mediated survival pathways, offering a synergistic activity with crenolanib against FLT3-ITD and AMPKα, supporting its potential utility in FLT3-ITD-positive AML.139

Dual FLT3/Aurora inhibitors

Aurora kinases are serine/threonine protein kinases that play a critical role in cell cycle regulation. There are three isoforms, Aurora A, B, and C, with Aurora A and B frequently overexpressed in various solid tumors and hematologic malignancies.2,140 Among these, Aurora B is particularly important for sustaining AML cell survival. Recent studies suggest that dual FLT3/Aurora inhibitors provide greater therapeutic benefits compared to selective FLT3 inhibitors in FLT3-mutant AML by simultaneously targeting cell cycle progression and FLT3-driven signaling.2

Dual FLT3/Aurora A inhibitors

CCT245718

CCT245718 is a dual FLT3/Aurora A inhibitor that demonstrates promising activity against FLT3 mutations associated with resistance. It exhibits higher affinity for the FLT3-D835Y mutant (Kd = 39 nM) compared to FLT3-ITD (Kd = 170 nM), indicating its potential to overcome activation loop–driven resistance.141 In addition, CCT245718 shows strong selectivity for Aurora A over Aurora B, with IC50 values of 0.015 µM and 3.045 µM, respectively.141

Modeling studies suggest that CCT245718 binds within the ATP-binding pocket of FLT3, maintaining a key hydrogen bond with Cys694 in the hinge region and forming an additional hydrogen bond with Asp694, which stabilizes the complex (Fig. 14).141

Hsieh-39

Hsieh-39 is a dual FLT3/Aurora A inhibitor originally identified through high-throughput screening and structure-based design. It demonstrates potent inhibitory activity against FLT3 and Aurora A, with IC50 values of 7 nM in FLT3-driven AML cell lines (MOLM-13 and MV4-11) and 15 nM for Aurora A.142 This strong activity suggests its potential to overcome resistance and disrupt cell cycle progression in FLT3-mutated AML.

Modeling studies indicate that Hsieh-39 binds within the ATP-binding pocket of FLT3 through multiple stabilizing interactions. The pyrimidine nitrogen forms a hydrogen bond with Cys694 in the hinge region, while the NH of the pyrrole establishes an additional hydrogen bond with the carbonyl group of Cys694. The urea moiety contributes further stability by forming hydrogen bonds with Lys644 and Asp829, and the isoxazole substitution extends toward a hydrophobic pocket adjacent to Phe621, engaging in CH–π interactions (Fig. 14).142

Dual FLT3/Aurora B inhibitors

Chern-54

Chern-54 is an acylureidoindolin-2-one derivative designed as a dual FLT3/Aurora B inhibitor. It exhibits remarkable potency, with IC50 values of 0.5 nM for FLT3 and 0.4 nM for Aurora B, and demonstrates strong antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects in AML models.143 Structure–activity relationship studies highlight the importance of the 6-acylureido and 3-pyrrolemethylidene groups on the indolin-2-one scaffold for maintaining kinase inhibition and cytotoxicity.2

Modeling studies suggest that Chern-54 binds within the ATP-binding pocket of FLT3 through two key hydrogen bonds: one between the NH of the indolinone ring and Glu692, and another between its carbonyl group and Cys694 in the hinge region. Additional stabilization may occur through interactions with Leu616 and Asp829, as well as hydrophobic contacts near Val624 (Fig. 14).144

Dual FLT3/Aurora A and B inhibitors

Mobinitinib (CCT241736)

Mobinitinib is a dual FLT3/Aurora kinase inhibitor developed for AML treatment.145 It exhibits strong activity against FLT3, with Kd values of 6.2 nM for FLT3-WT and 38 nM for FLT3-ITD, and also potently inhibits Aurora A and Aurora B with Kd values of 7.5 nM and 48 nM, respectively.146,147 This profile suggests its potential to address resistance and disrupt cell cycle progression in FLT3-mutated AML.

Modeling studies indicate that mobinitinib occupies the ATP-binding site of FLT3, maintaining interactions typical of type I kinase inhibitors. Key hydrogen bonds involve hinge residues such as Cys694, while hydrophobic substituents stabilize the complex within the catalytic pocket (Fig. 14).146

Dual FLT3/AXL inhibitors

AXL, a TAM-family receptor tyrosine kinase, is frequently overexpressed in AML and has been implicated in disease progression and therapy resistance.2 In FLT3-driven leukemogenesis, AXL signaling cooperates with FLT3 to enhance leukemic blast proliferation in both FLT3-mutated and wild-type AML, contributing to reduced responsiveness to selective FLT3 inhibitors.2,148,149 Accordingly, dual inhibition of FLT3 and AXL has emerged as a rational strategy for AML treatment, as exemplified by gilteritinib.148,149

JRF104

JRF104 is a potent dual FLT3/AXL inhibitor, with IC50 values of 0.42 nM and 1.22 nM, respectively.150,151 In AML cell models harboring FLT3-ITD, JRF104 markedly suppresses proliferation and retains antiproliferative activity across clinically relevant variants, including the gatekeeper F691L.150,151 Beyond FLT3, JRF104 inhibits AXL-regulated migration and exhibits robust antitumor activity in vivo, with strong tumor growth inhibition in xenograft models.151 These findings position JRF104 as a promising dual-target candidate capable of addressing resistance arising from FLT3-TKD lesions and AXL upregulation. To date, no chemical structure or detailed binding-mode studies have been published for JRF104; available data are limited to abstract-level reports describing its potency and preclinical efficacy.

CTS2016

CTS2016 has been reported as a selective, orally bioavailable small-molecule that dually targets AXL and FLT3 and shows single-digit nanomolar enzymatic potency.152,153CTS2016 elicits potent growth inhibition and apoptosis across AML cell lines bearing FLT3-ITD and/or TKD mutations and demonstrates pronounced efficacy against the refractory FLT3-ITD/F691L model. Notably, selectivity over other kinases, including c-KIT, may mitigate off-target hematologic toxicity compared with multikinase FLT3 inhibitors.153CTS2016 has entered Phase I clinical evaluation in relapsed/refractory AML or intermediate/high-risk MDS, with additional preclinical reports confirming activity across AML/MDS and selected solid-tumor models.153,154 To date, no chemical structure or binding-mode data have been publicly disclosed for CTS2016.

Dubermatinib (TP-0903)

Dubermatinib (Fig. 15) is a dual AXL/FLT3 inhibitor with demonstrated potency against both targets and a clear therapeutic rationale in AML.152,155,156 It exhibits sub- to low-nanomolar inhibition of AXL and FLT3-clinically relevant mutants, including FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y (IC50 = 27 nM, 3.9 nM, and 0.12 nM, respectively), and resensitizes FLT3-ITD MOLM-13 cells to midostaurin and quizartinib, supporting its capacity to counter acquired resistance to selective FLT3 inhibitors.152,155 Taken together with its activity on AXL, this profile underscores dubermatinib's dual-target potential in AML, where concurrent blockade of FLT3 signaling and AXL-mediated adaptive pathways may deliver deeper and more durable responses.152,155 From a clinical standpoint, dubermatinib has shown acceptable tolerability in ongoing clinical studies that inform dose and safety expectations relevant to hematologic applications.155,157–159

Dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 inhibitors

Although BCR-ABL1 is classically associated with CML and Ph+ ALL, its signaling architecture (STAT5, PI3K/AKT, MAPK) overlaps with FLT3 pathways that drive leukemic proliferation and resistance in AML; consequently, ABL1-directed TKIs with FLT3 inhibitory activity (e.g., ponatinib) have demonstrated efficacy in FLT3-mutated AML, supporting the therapeutic plausibility of dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 inhibition.121,160,161 Moreover, in disease settings where FLT3 is co-activated alongside BCR-ABL1 (e.g., blast-phase CML with FLT3 pathway activation), preclinical studies demonstrate that simultaneous blockade of both nodes can overcome resistance and suppress proliferation, further motivating this dual-target approach.162

KF1601

KF1601 (Fig. 15) is a dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 inhibitor reported to suppress FLT3 signaling and cell proliferation in BCR-ABL1+ models.162 Although originally explored in CML contexts, its dual activity suggests potential utility in AML settings where FLT3 and BCR-ABL1 pathways converge. Biochemical and cellular assays confirm FLT3 inhibition, supporting its inclusion among emerging dual-target strategies for resistant leukemias.162

Rebastinib (DCC-2036)

Rebastinib (Fig. 15) exhibits dual inhibition of FLT3 and BCR-ABL1, with biochemical IC50 values around 2 nM and 0.8 nM, respectively.152,163 Initially developed to address ABL1 resistance, its low-nanomolar FLT3 potency underpins inclusion among dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 inhibitors with relevance to AML and CML applications.148,156

Li-1

Li-1 (Fig. 15) emerged from a focused imidazo[1,2-a]pyridine library screen as a dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 inhibitor with potent activity against resistance-conferring FLT3 variants, notably FLT3-ITD/D835Y and FLT3-ITD/F691L.164 In cellular assays, Li-1 demonstrated potent antiproliferative activity against MOLM-14 with IC50 value of 3.65 nM, and activity was retained in MOLM-14-D835Y (IC50 = 2.08 nM) and MOLM-14-F691L (IC50 = 4.23 nM).164 These findings nominate Li-1 as a potential lead for further optimization toward balanced dual FLT3/BCR-ABL1 coverage encompassing D835 and F691L resistance settings.

Dual FLT3/BCL-2 inhibitors

BCL-2 is a central anti-apoptotic regulator in AML, where it neutralizes pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins (e.g., BIM) and multidomain effectors (BAX, BAK), thereby blocking mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and apoptosis. This anti-apoptotic mechanism enables leukemic cells to evade programmed cell death and contributes to treatment resistance.165–167 Consequently, simultaneous inhibition of FLT3 and BCL-2 has demonstrated synergistic antileukemic effects in preclinical models and is considered a promising therapeutic strategy in AML.165,168,169 To date, no single small-molecule inhibitor capable of targeting both FLT3 and BCL-2 has been reported. An alternative approach involves the design of hybrid molecules that link two pharmacophores, one for FLT3 and one for BCL-2, within a single scaffold; however, these remain experimental and have not surpassed the efficacy of conventional combination regimens.165 Current evidence indicates that polypharmacy, such as combining a selective FLT3 inhibitor (e.g., gilteritinib) with a BCL-2 antagonist (e.g., venetoclax), achieves superior potency and pharmacological feasibility compared to polypharmacology strategies, underscoring the present clinical preference for combination therapy over single multitarget molecules in this context.165,170

Dual FLT3/BET inhibitors

BET proteins (bromodomain and extra-terminal family) play a pivotal role in leukemogenesis by regulating transcription of oncogenic drivers such as MYC and anti-apoptotic factors including BCL-2, thereby sustaining leukemic cell survival and proliferation.81,171 The BET family comprises BRD2, BRD3, BRD4, and BRDT, which function as epigenetic readers that bind acetylated lysine residues on histones, promoting transcriptional activation and cell cycle progression.171 Although BET inhibitors have shown promise in disrupting these pathways, their efficacy as single agents in AML is limited due to low selectivity and hematologic toxicity.81,171 Consequently, combining BET inhibitors with FLT3 inhibitors has demonstrated synergistic antileukemic activity in FLT3-ITD AML models, enhancing apoptosis and overcoming resistance mechanisms.81,171 Among reported compounds, fedratinib exhibits dual activity against FLT3 and BET targets, although its primary mechanism involves JAK2 inhibition and its BET activity remains secondary.172 To our knowledge, no clinically approved dual FLT3/BET inhibitors currently exist, underscoring the need for rational design strategies that integrate kinase inhibition with epigenetic modulation within a single scaffold.

Schönbrunn-3

Schönbrunn-3 (Fig. 15) is the most potent dual FLT3/BET inhibitor reported to date. Developed from the dianilinopyrimidine scaffold of fedratinib, it demonstrates strong activity with IC50 values of 1.1 nM for FLT3 and 34 nM for BRD4.173 Functionally, it induces G1 cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis, recapitulating the pharmacological effect observed when BET and FLT3 inhibitors are combined.173

Dual FLT3/BTK inhibitors

BTK (a Tec-family kinase) is frequently overexpressed/activated in AML and contributes to survival and proliferation signaling in myeloid and B-lineage contexts.175 While BTK inhibition alone shows antileukemic effects, dual FLT3/BTK targeting is proposed to deliver greater efficacy and help reduce toxicity and resistance compared with single-target agents.175

Ling-14m

Ling-14m is a potent aminopyrimidine derivative that inhibits BTK (IC50 = 40 nM), FLT3 (IC50 = 2.4 nM), and the FLT3-D835Y mutant (IC50 = 5.9 nM).175 It demonstrates superior activity compared to sorafenib, and gilteritinib in AML cell lines (MV4-11 and MOLM13), highlighting its potential as a lead compound.175 Docking studies indicate that the aminopyrimidine nucleus forms hydrogen bonds with Val615, Leu616, and Cys694 at the FLT3 hinge region, while the hydroxamic acid moiety interacts with polar residues to strengthen binding. Fluorine substitution on the phenyl ring enhances electronic effects, and the N-methylpiperazinyl group forms an additional hydrogen bond with Asp829, stabilizing the complex (Fig. 16).175

Ling-RSH-7

Ling-RSH-7 exhibits dual inhibition of FLT3 (IC50 = 12 nM) and BTK (IC50 = 47 nM) and shows strong cytotoxicity in MV4-11 cells (IC50 = 3 nM), with retained activity against the FLT3-D835Y mutant.176 Its FLT3 binding mode involves hydrogen-π interactions between the aminopyrimidine core and Val624, Leu616, Gly697, and Asp698, while the N-methylpiperazine group occupies the back pocket and forms hydrogen bonds with Cys828. The hydrazide moiety also interacts with Leu616 and Asp698, reinforcing FLT3 affinity (Fig. 16).176

Luxeptinib (CG-806)

Luxeptinib (Fig. 16) is a next-generation multitarget inhibitor that potently blocks FLT3, BTK, and Aurora kinases, with additional suppression of c-MYC signaling.177 It retains activity against resistant FLT3 variants, including F691L and D835 mutations, and inhibits FLT3 phosphorylation along with downstream AKT/mTOR/S6K and MAPK pathways, leading to G1 cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis.177 In Ba/F3 cells harboring F691L, luxeptinib achieves an IC50 of 10.0 nM, outperforming quizartinib, gilteritinib, and crenolanib under matched conditions.177Luxeptinib accommodates both FLT3-ITD and TKD mutants. Thus, it is currently under Phase I clinical evaluation for relapsed/refractory AML.178

Dual FLT3/CBP inhibitors

CREB-binding protein (CBP, encoded by CREBBP) and its paralog p300 (encoded by EP300) are lysine acetyltransferase coactivators that regulate chromatin accessibility and transcription by acetylating histone tails (e.g., H3K27ac) and recruiting transcriptional machinery to oncogenic programs.179 These epigenetic regulators facilitate leukemogenesis and represent validated therapeutic targets in AML.82,180 In FLT3-mutant AML, CBP/p300 activity sustains FLT3 expression and downstream signaling, providing a complementary axis to kinase inhibition.82 Pharmacologically, CBP/p300 blockade reduces H3K27ac at the FLT3 promoter, suppresses FLT3 transcription, and, when combined with FLT3 inhibitors, enhances suppression of STAT5, AKT, and ERK pathways, yielding synergistic antileukemic effects across cell lines and patient-derived samples.82 Notably, CBP/p300 inhibition restores quizartinib sensitivity in FLT3-TKD–resistant models, and high EP300 expression correlates with poor prognosis and FLT3 overexpression.82 To date, no single small-molecule agent has been reported to inhibit both FLT3 and CBP/p300; current evidence for this strategy derives from combination studies and the established efficacy of CBP/p300 inhibitors in AML, supporting dual pathway blockade as a promising approach to overcome resistance and improve outcomes.

Dual FLT3/CDK inhibitors

Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) cooperate with FLT3-driven signaling to sustain leukemic proliferation and survival in AML.64,181 CDK4 and CDK6, which phosphorylate RB to drive G1–S transition, are frequently upregulated in FLT3-ITD AML; their inhibition enhances apoptosis and reverses resistance to FLT3 inhibitors such as quizartinib.182,183 CDK2, essential for S-phase entry and DNA replication, has been implicated in AML cell-cycle progression; selective CDK2 blockade or degradation induces differentiation and synergizes with FLT3 inhibition in preclinical models.184 CDK9, a transcriptional elongation kinase, maintains expression of survival genes such as MCL1; its inhibition downregulates anti-apoptotic programs and potentiates FLT3 inhibitor-induced cytotoxicity.185,186 Collectively, these findings underscore the therapeutic rationale for dual FLT3/CDK inhibitors to achieve deeper pathway suppression and overcome resistance mechanisms in AML.2

AMG925 (FLX925)

AMG925 is a type I dual inhibitor of FLT3 and CDK4 (Fig. 16), exhibiting sub-nanomolar potency with IC50 values of 1 nM and 3 nM, respectively, and retained activity against activation-loop mutants such as D835Y/V.14,148,182,183 By binding the active DFG-in conformation, AMG925 effectively suppresses FLT3 signaling while constraining CDK4-dependent cell-cycle transit, resulting in synergistic antileukemic effects in preclinical models.187In vivo studies demonstrated significant tumor growth inhibition in FLT3-mutated AML xenografts, supporting its translational potential.187

ETH-155008

ETH-155008 demonstrates balanced dual inhibition across FLT3 and CDK4/6,22 with IC50 values of 0.5 nM, 0.6 nM, 0.8 nM, and 1.4 nM against FLT3, FLT3-ITD, CDK4/D1, and CDK6/D1.188 Early clinical evaluation was initiated to assess safety, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy (Phase 1a/1b; AML and B-cell malignancies).189,190 Its chemical structure is not publicly provided in the search results.

FN-1501

FN-1501 is a multi-CDK dual design that inhibits FLT3 and CDK2/4/6 with IC50 values of 0.28 nM, 2.47 nM, 0.85 nM, and 1.96 nM, respectively, and has progressed to clinical testing.64,184,191,192 Molecular modeling indicates that FN-1501 adopts a type I binding mode within the FLT3 ATP pocket. The pyrazole-3-carboxamide scaffold forms two hydrogen bonds with hinge residues Glu692 and Cys694, while the 7H-pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine moiety occupies the hydrophobic pocket. The N-methylpiperazine group extends toward the solvent-accessible region, contributing to complex stabilization (Fig. 16). This interaction pattern supports activity against clinically relevant FLT3 variants and complements CDK inhibition to reinforce cell-cycle arrest.184,192

Ali-5l

Ali-5l, an oxindole-based derivative, exhibits dual activity against FLT3 and CDK2 with IC50 values of 36.21 nM and 8.17 nM, respectively.193 Molecular docking indicates a type I FLT3 binding mode in which the oxindole–pyridyl framework forms hydrogen bonds with Cys694 and Asp829, accompanied by hydrophobic contacts within the adenine pocket (Leu616, Val624, Ala642, Leu818, Asp829) and π-stacking of the terminal pyridyl against Phe691; these interactions collectively stabilize the complex (Fig. 16).193

CDDD11-8

CDDD11-8 is a selective dual inhibitor of FLT3 and CDK9 (Fig. 16), exhibiting Ki values of 13 nM and 8 nM, respectively.185 In addition to CDK9, it shows weaker inhibitory activity against other CDK isoforms, including CDK1, CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, and CDK7, but with markedly lower potency compared to CDK9.185,186 Importantly, CDDD11-8 retains activity against the clinically relevant FLT3-D835Y mutation, supporting its potential to overcome resistance associated with activation-loop variants.185 The combined inhibition of FLT3 signaling and CDK9-mediated transcriptional elongation (e.g., MCL-1 expression) provides a dual mechanism aimed at disrupting proliferative and survival pathways in AML.185,186

Dual FLT3/CHK-1 inhibitors

Checkpoint kinases CHK1 and CHK2 orchestrate DNA damage responses by phosphorylating and inhibiting CDC25 phosphatases, thereby enforcing cell-cycle arrest and allowing repair before mitotic entry.194 Although selective CHK1 inhibitors such as prexasertib showed preclinical synergy with DNA-damaging agents, clinical trials in AML, including a Phase I study (NCT02649764), demonstrated only modest activity, underscoring that monotherapy checkpoint inhibition is insufficient.194 Preclinical studies have shown that dual inhibition of FLT3 and CHK1 enhances cytotoxicity, restores p53-mediated apoptosis, suppresses c-MYC, and overcomes both adaptive and acquired resistance to FLT3 inhibitors in FLT3-ITD AML models.195 By integrating FLT3 and checkpoint blockade into a single molecule, this strategy reduces the complexity of combination therapy and maximizes pathway inhibition, reinforcing the case for designed dual FLT3/CHK1 inhibitors.

Zhou-30

Zhou-30 is a dual FLT3/CHK1 inhibitor based on a 2-aminopyrimidine scaffold. It exhibits strong nanomolar potency against FLT3-WT, FLT3-D835Y, and CHK1, with IC50 values of 5.61 nM, 9.17 nM, and 26.78 nM, respectively, and effectively inhibits FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signaling (STAT5, AKT, ERK).195 Structural analysis indicates a type I FLT3 binding mode, engaging the hinge via hydrogen bonds with Cys694 and Glu692, an ionic interaction with Asp698, and an additional hydrogen bond with Lys644 (Fig. 17).195In vivo, Zhou-30 produced marked tumor growth inhibition and prolonged survival in FLT3-ITD and FLT3-ITD/D835Y xenograft models, with favorable oral pharmacokinetics, supporting preclinical status. Zhou-30 retains activity against major resistance-conferring FLT3 mutations, including D835 variants, F691L gatekeeper, and non-canonical lesions (e.g., Y842H, N676D), supporting its potential utility in overcoming acquired and adaptive resistance in AML based on both in vitro and in vivo investigations.195

Liu-18

Developed within the same 2-aminopyrimidine series that also includes Zhou-30, Liu-18 was advanced as a potent dual FLT3/CHK1 inhibitor, exhibiting IC50 values of 1.6 nM for FLT3-WT and 3.2 nM for CHK1, while effectively suppressing FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signaling (STAT5, ERK) in cellular assays.196 Docking studies indicate a type I FLT3 binding mode (Fig. 17), characterized by bidentate hydrogen bonding of the aminopyrimidine core to hinge residue Cys694, complemented by solvent–region interactions involving the cis-cyclohexanediamine (hydrogen bond/salt-bridge network with Asp759 and Arg745) and an additional hydrogen bond between the hydroxyl group of the tetrahydroisoquinoline and Tyr696.196Liu-18 maintains strong antiproliferative activity against BaF3 cells harboring clinically relevant resistance mutations, including FLT3-TKD variants (D835V/F, F691L) and compound lesions (FLT3-ITD/D835Y, FLT3-ITD/F691L), as well as non-canonical mutations such as FLT3-ITD/N701K, supporting its potential to overcome acquired resistance.196In vivo, Liu-18 achieved dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition in MV-4-11 xenografts and extended survival in a disseminated MOLM-13 model, with favorable oral pharmacokinetics and low acute toxicity, supporting its preclinical status. Additionally, Liu-18 demonstrates >1700-fold selectivity over c-KIT and markedly reduced hERG affinity (IC50 = 58.4 µM), indicating a lower risk of myelosuppression and QT prolongation compared with some FLT3 inhibitors.196

Liu-30

Developed within the same 5-trifluoromethyl-2-aminopyrimidine series reported by the group, Liu-30 was developed as a potent dual FLT3/CHK1 inhibitor, displaying IC50 values of 16.4 nM for FLT3-WT, 22.8 nM for FLT3-D835Y, and 25.6 nM for CHK1, while effectively suppressing FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signaling (STAT5, ERK, AKT) in AML cells.197 Docking studies indicate a type I FLT3 binding mode, with the aminopyrimidine core forming two key hydrogen bonds to hinge residue Cys694, and the primary amine engaging Arg745 and Asp759 in the solvent region; an additional interaction between the methylsulfonyl group and Lys614 further stabilizes the complex (Fig. 17).197Liu-30 exhibits strong antiproliferative activity against MV4-11 cells with IC50 value of 2.96 nM and retains potency across BaF3 panels harboring clinically relevant resistance mutations, including FLT3-F691L, FLT3-D835F/V, and compound lesions (FLT3-ITD/D835Y), with IC50 values in the range of 26–76 nM.197In vivo, Liu-30 demonstrated favorable oral pharmacokinetics in mice and achieved high kinase selectivity over 12 tested kinases, while showing >460-fold selectivity over c-KIT and markedly reduced hERG inhibition (36% at 10 µM), supporting its safety profile.197

Dual FLT3/EGFR inhibitors

Aberrant activation of EGFR is a hallmark in many solid tumors, and limited evidence suggests EGFR signaling may be active in a subset of AML cases.198,199 Although EGFR inhibitors alone have shown minimal benefit in AML, dual inhibition of FLT3 and EGFR offers a rational approach to suppress convergent survival pathways (RAS/MAPK, STAT5) and potentially overcome resistance.200,201 This strategy may provide additive or synergistic effects in EGFR+ AML subsets, supporting the design of multi-kinase scaffolds incorporating both targets.

Ruserontinib (SKLB-1028)

Ruserontinib (Fig. 17) is an orally bioavailable multikinase inhibitor that has advanced to Phase III clinical trials, marking its most mature stage of development.202–204Ruserontinib potently inhibits EGFR, FLT3, and ABL kinases, with IC50 values of 31 nM and 4 nM against EGFR (wild-type and L858R mutant, respectively), and 55 nM against FLT3.205 In cellular assays, ruserontinib exhibits remarkable antiproliferative activity against FLT3-ITD+ MV4-11 cells (IC50 = 2 nM), while inducing G1-phase arrest and apoptosis, as evidenced by caspase-3 activation. Mechanistically, ruserontinib suppresses FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream signaling, including STAT5 and ERK1/2, in a dose-dependent manner.202,205In vivo, oral administration of ruserontinib achieved complete tumor regression in MV4-11 xenografts at 10–20 mg per kg per day, with favorable pharmacokinetics and low acute toxicity.205 Dual targeting of FLT3 and EGFR addresses pathway convergence implicated in resistance, positioning ruserontinib as a promising option for AML subsets with EGFR-driven signaling.

Dual FLT3/Fes inhibitors

Feline sarcoma (Fes; also known as Fps) is a non-receptor cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase predominantly expressed in myeloid hematopoietic cells, where it regulates proliferation, survival, differentiation, and innate immune signaling.2,206 In FLT3-ITD AML models, Fes has been implicated as a cooperating downstream effector; it is overexpressed and constitutively activated in FLT3-ITD+ cell lines, and contributes to propagation of FLT3-driven signaling.2 Collectively, genetic and pharmacological studies confirm that Fes acts as a cooperating effector in FLT3-ITD AML, and simultaneous inhibition of FLT3 and Fes, whether through dual inhibitors or combined strategies, can effectively suppress leukemic signaling and enhance cytotoxicity, reinforcing the rationale for dual-target design.207

TAE-684

TAE-684 is an aminopyrimidine-based ATP-competitive inhibitor (Fig. 17) originally developed targeting ALK, but it also exhibits activity against FLT3 and Fes kinases.207,208 In enzymatic assays, TAE-684 inhibits Fes, FLT3-WT, FLT3-ITD, and FLT3-D835Y with IC50 values of 569 nM, 536 nM, 120 nM, and 218 nM, respectively.148,207 In FLT3-ITD AML models, TAE-684 suppresses proliferation and induces apoptosis to a degree comparable or superior to FLT3-selective inhibitors, consistent with dual pathway engagement; this includes robust effects in MV4-11, MOLM-13, and MOLM-14 lines at sub-µM concentrations.207 Despite its initial ALK-focused profile and development as a CNS-penetrant ALK inhibitor, there are no publicly reported in vivo PK or xenograft data for the FLT3/Fes context.

HG7-92-01

HG7-92-01 is a pyrrolopyridine-based inhibitor (Fig. 17) showing dual activity against Fes and FLT3-ITD.207,208 Biochemically, it inhibits Fes, FLT3-WT, FLT3-ITD, and FLT3-D835Y with IC50 values of 527 nM, 536 nM, 149 nM, and 3646 nM respectively.148,207 In cell-based assays, HG7-92-01 demonstrates potent apoptosis induction in MV4-11, MOLM-13, and MOLM-14 lines at 1 µM, with GI50 values of 19 nM, 93 nM, and 34 nM, respectively.207 This cellular potency, despite less potent biochemical activity on Fes, suggests effective target engagement. However, similar to TAE-684, no in vivo efficacy or pharmacokinetics data have been disclosed.

TL02-59

TL02-59, a phenoxyquinazoline derivative (Fig. 17), is reported as a dual FLT3/Fes inhibitor, with IC50 values of 633 nM and 290 nM, respectively.209,210 In cellular studies, TL02-59 suppressed proliferation of FLT3-ITD+ models, MV4-11 and MOLM-14, with IC50 values of 0.78 nM and 6.6 nM, respectively.209 Given the strong cellular potency versus modest FLT3/Fes enzymatic inhibition, kinome profiling and enzyme assays identified the myeloid Src-family kinase, FGR, as the primary driver target with picomolar potency, with Lyn/Hck also engaged, while FLT3/Fes likely contribute contextually in FLT3-ITD models.209 Binding mode analyses suggest a type II-like interaction with FLT3.211In vivo, TL02-59 demonstrated significant antileukemic activity in AML xenograft models, supporting favorable exposure and translational activity.209

Dual FLT3/FGFR inhibitors

Fibroblast growth factor receptor-1 (FGFR1) is a receptor tyrosine kinase essential for hematopoietic development, and FGFR1 rearrangements define an aggressive spectrum of myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms that frequently evolve to AML.214,215 Mechanistically, aberrant FGFR1 signaling can upregulate FLT3 (and KIT) transcription thereby reinforcing leukemogenic circuits and providing a rationale to target both axes in tandem.214 Consistent with this, combination strategies that inhibit FGFR1 together with FLT3 (or other cooperating kinases) show synergistic growth suppression in FGFR1-driven models and support the broader concept that simultaneous blockade of FLT3 and parallel survival pathways improves efficacy and mitigates resistance compared with single-agent therapy.214

Nefextinib (MAX-40279)

Nefextinib (Fig. 18) is a dual FLT3/FGFR inhibitor currently in Phase I clinical evaluation (NCT04187495) for relapsed/refractory FLT3-mutated AML, designed to block FGF2/FGFR-driven adaptive signaling implicated in FLT3 inhibitor resistance.22,216,217 In preclinical studies, nefextinib retained activity against FLT3-WT and FLT3-D835Y.218 It achieved 58–106% tumor growth inhibition in AML xenograft models without significant body weight loss, supporting its favorable efficacy profile.194 Although detailed biochemical, its development underscores the therapeutic potential of targeting FGFR-mediated bypass pathways alongside FLT3.

Olverembatinib (GZD824)

Olverembatinib is a multi-kinase inhibitor with potent activity against FLT3, FGFR1, and PDGFRα, supporting its application in AML and other hematologic malignancies.219,220 Enzymatic assays report IC50 values of 1.33 nM (FLT3), 4.14 nM (FGFR1), and 2.08 nM (PDGFRα).220Olverembatinib demonstrates strong activity against FLT3-ITD, including resistant variants such as F691I and G697R, and effectively suppresses FGFR1- and PDGFRα-dependent leukemia cells in vitro and in vivo.220 In MV4-11, Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD-F691I, and FGFR1-driven models, oral dosing at 10–20 mg kg−1 every other day achieved near-complete tumor regression.220 Structural modeling suggests that its pyrazole[3,4-b]pyridine scaffold anchors the hinge region within the ATP pocket (Fig. 18), while the amidic moiety forms two hydrogen bonds; the trifluoromethyl phenyl group occupies a hydrophobic pocket, and the alkyne substituent contributes favorable van der Waals interactions without steric clash. A methylpiperazinyl group extends toward the solvent region, potentially enhancing hydrogen bonding and solubility.221 This interaction network supports its potency against gatekeeper and P-loop hinge mutations, reinforcing its potential utility in overcoming resistance in AML, alongside its established activity in CML, where it has completed Phase III clinical evaluation.222

Dual FLT3/haspin inhibitors

Haspin (GSG2) is a mitotic kinase responsible for histone H3 threonine-3 phosphorylation, a modification essential for chromosome segregation.223,224 In AML, genetic depletion or pharmacological inhibition of haspin suppresses leukemic cell growth, highlighting its therapeutic relevance.225 Concurrent inhibition of FLT3 and haspin offers a rational approach to overcome resistance to FLT3 inhibitors by combining disruption of FLT3-driven survival signaling with mitotic stress, thereby enhancing cytotoxicity and reducing relapse risk.198

HSK205

HSK205 is the only dual FLT3/haspin inhibitor currently reported. In biochemical assays, it inhibited FLT3-ITD with IC50 value of 0.199 nM and produced 94% FLT3 inhibition at 50 nM; in the same campaign, the compound bound haspin with Kd value of 0.55 nM and showed 97% haspin inhibition at 50 nM.226 In FLT3-driven cellular models, HSK205 retained potency against clinically relevant resistance mutations, achieving GI50 values of 2.2 nM and 4.3 nM in FLT3-ITD/F691L and FLT3-ITD/D835Y cell lines, respectively, supporting its potential to address on-target resistance to current FLT3 inhibitors.226

Docking studies indicate that HSK205 adopts a type I FLT3 binding mode (Fig. 18), forming two hydrogen bonds with hinge residue Cys694 and an additional interaction between the benzamide carbonyl and Arg764. The phenyl ring at C7 extends toward the solvent-accessible region, supporting optimal orientation within the ATP pocket. This interaction network explains the compound's high potency and mutation resilience.227 Collectively, these findings support dual FLT3/haspin inhibition as a fruitful therapeutic strategy for AML and justify further development of HSK205.226

Dual FLT3/hedgehog inhibitors

The hedgehog (Hh) pathway regulates stem-cell self-renewal and survival programs that are co-opted in leukemogenesis, contributing to proliferation, therapy resistance, and maintenance of leukemic stem cells in AML.228–230 In FLT3-mutant settings, this axis provides a complementary survival route that can blunt the cytotoxic effects of selective FLT3 blockade, establishing a clear mechanistic rationale for concurrent targeting of both pathways.228,229

Consistent with this rationale, co-inhibition of FLT3 and Hh enhances antileukemic activity compared with single-agent therapy. For example, crenolanib combined with the Hh inhibitor HPI-1 produces synergistic growth suppression in MOLM-14 and MV4-11 cells and translates into reduced tumor burden and improved survival in mouse models.231 Moreover, because FLT3-ITD can drive non-canonical PI3K-mediated GLI activation, simultaneous blockade of FLT3, GLI, and PI3K augments downstream pathway suppression, decreases proliferation and colony formation, induces apoptosis, and prolongs survival in xenograft models, collectively reinforcing the validity of dual targeting in this axis.229 To date, no small-molecule inhibitor has been reported that dually targets FLT3 and the Hh pathway within a single scaffold; the available evidence for this strategy is derived from rational combination regimens rather than true dual chemotypes.228–231

Dual FLT3/HDAC inhibitors

Histone deacetylases (HDACs) are Zn2+-dependent deacylases that regulate chromatin and non-histone proteins; their dysregulation contributes to leukemic survival and stress adaptation in AML.232,233 In FLT3-mutant AML, FLT3 blockade can trigger an adaptive epigenetic escape in which FOXO-driven HDAC8 upregulation deacetylates and functionally suppresses p53, sustaining leukemic persistence under TKI therapy. Pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of HDAC8 restores p53 activity and amplifies FLT3-TKI efficacy in FLT3-ITD models, providing a strong biological premise for concomitant FLT3 and HDAC targeting in a single chemotype.234,235 Beyond HDAC8, class-I HDACs (HDAC1/2/3) and HDAC6 are also implicated in AML biology, supporting the rationale for isoform- or class-selective HDAC targeting alongside FLT3.232

In FLT3-mutant models, adding an HDAC inhibitor to FLT3 blockade improved survival in xenografts, and directly inspired a pharmacophore-fusion campaign that delivered dual FLT3/HDAC chemotypes.236 Consistently, class-I-selective HDAC inhibition shows synergy with FLT3 inhibition and reverses quizartinib resistance, achieving tumor regression in FLT3-ITD/TKD xenografts and near-eradication in patient-derived xenograft models.237 Taken together, these data show that concomitant inhibition of FLT3 and HDACs is a reported and biologically coherent strategy in AML; therefore, pursuing dual FLT3/HDAC inhibition is a valid medicinal-chemistry approach for this indication.

From a design perspective, dual FLT3/HDAC chemotypes couple a kinase-recognition core that engages the FLT3 ATP site with an HDAC zinc-binding group (ZBG) via a flexible aliphatic linker (∼5–7 atoms).238 Within this model, hydroxamates remain the most efficient ZBG, while benzamides and hydrazides are explored to enhance selectivity and pharmacokinetics.239

Lu-63

Lu-63 was the first reported FLT3/HDAC dual inhibitor, showing sub-nanomolar potency on FLT3-ITD and HDAC1 with IC50 values of 0.844 nM and 30.0 nM, respectively, in addition to potent anti-proliferative activity in MV4-11 and MOLM-13 (IC50 = 92 nM and 142 nM, respectively).234 Mechanistic assays confirmed dual pathway inhibition, apoptosis induction, and cell-cycle arrest in MV4-11.234

Molecular docking indicated a type I FLT3 binding mode in which Lu-63's pyrazole NH and the adjacent carbonyl together tether the hinge, forming two hydrogen bonds to Glu692 and Cys694; the N-benzyl benzimidazole occupies a hydrophobic pocket spanning the canonical sub-pockets, the dichloro substitution contributes halogen bonding, and the hydroxamate side chain projects toward solvent, consistent with an ATP-site-anchored recognition core (Fig. 18).234 For HDAC engagement, Lu-63 uses a hydroxamate ZBG delivered by a 6-carbon linker into the catalytic tunnel, while the FLT3-binding pharmacophore serves as the cap at the enzyme surface, matching the three-part HDAC pharmacophore (ZBG-linker-cap).234,239

Hou-25h

Hou-25h is a dual FLT3/HDAC1 chemotype with IC50 values of 0.6 nM and 144.8 nM, respectively, and shows strong anti-proliferative activity in FLT3-mutant models; in comparative studies it surpassed gilteritinib, vorinostat, and their combination in MOLM-13 assays and xenografts while showing lower normal-cell cytotoxicity and reduced in vivo toxicity.236

A pharmacophore-fusion design was applied for Hou-25h, where the gilteritinib-derived FLT3 recognition core was retained to ensure type I anchoring (Fig. 18), and vorinostat's hydroxamate ZBG delivered by a long alkyl linker was appended at the solvent-accessible tail to reach the HDAC catalytic tunnel, while the FLT3-binding scaffold served as the HDAC cap. This layout preserves ATP-site engagement on FLT3 and installs the canonical HDAC module (ZBG-linker-cap) within one chemotype, accounting for the dual potency.236,239

Liu-9e

Liu-9e is a dual FLT3/HDAC1/3 inhibitor with IC50 values of 30.4 nM (FLT3), 52.4 nM (HDAC1), and 14.7 nM (HDAC3), and demonstrates potent antiproliferative activity in MV-4-11 cells (IC50 = 30 nM).240 It also induces robust apoptosis in MV-4-11 and shows activity against FLT3-ITD and TKD variants (including D835V and F691L) in Ba/F3 panels, supporting its potential to overcome resistance.240

Docking indicates a type I FLT3 pose (Fig. 18), where the aminopyrimidine core and adjacent carbonyl jointly tether the hinge via two hydrogen bonds with Cys694, while hydrophobic substituents occupy the back pocket. For HDAC engagement, Liu-9e employs a benzohydrazide ZBG (non-hydroxamate) delivered by a flexible linker into the catalytic tunnel to chelate the Zn2+ and form multiple hydrogen bonds.240

Ling-6s

Ling-6s is an aminopyrimidine–hydroxamate dual FLT3/HDAC chemotype that shows low-nanomolar activity against FLT3 and class-I HDACs, with IC50 values of 14 nM (FLT3), 27 nM (HDAC1), and 20 nM (HDAC6).241 It retains enzymatic potency against the resistance-relevant FLT3-D835Y mutant (IC50 = 55 nM) and displays strong antiproliferative effects in FLT3-driven cellular models (MV4-11, IC50 = 29 nM).241 Mechanistically, Ling-6s suppresses p-FLT3 and downstream p-STAT5 while increasing histone H3 acetylation in MV4-11, consistent with concomitant pathway blockade.241 Docking supports a type I anchoring mode in FLT3 (Fig. 18), in which the aminopyrimidine core engages the hinge through two hydrogen bonds to Cys694 and Leu616, with additional stabilization near Leu616. The hydroxamate-bearing tail projects toward solvent and engages Arg834 and Asp839 via polar contacts. For HDAC engagement, the hydroxamate zinc-binding group extends down the catalytic tunnel to coordinate the catalytic Zn2+ and forms a small hydrogen-bond network at the rim of the pocket, while the FLT3-recognition headgroup acts as the enzyme-surface cap, an arrangement consistent with ZBG-linker-cap topology.241In vivo, Ling-6s produced dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition in a Jeko-1 xenograft with no evident toxicity; rat pharmacokinetics indicated acceptable exposure with oral dosing and improved systemic availability after i.p. administration, supporting its preclinical developability.241

Dual FLT3/IDH1/2 inhibitors

Isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutations represent an important, though less frequent, genetic alteration in AML compared to FLT3 lesions. IDH1 and IDH2 mutations occur in approximately 15–20% of AML cases and are associated with aberrant production of the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate, which disrupts epigenetic regulation and impairs differentiation.27,242,243 Importantly, these mutations can co-occur with FLT3-ITD, creating a high-risk molecular profile that drives leukemogenesis through complementary mechanisms.27,58 Clinical data indicate that nearly 30% of patients harboring IDH1/2 mutations also carry FLT3 alterations,244 supporting the rationale for simultaneous inhibition of both targets to achieve deeper pathway suppression and overcome resistance.

Currently, selective inhibitors of IDH1 (ivosidenib) and IDH2 (enasidenib) have gained FDA approval for AML.27,245 However, no small-molecule agent has been reported that dually targets FLT3 and IDH1/2 within a single scaffold. Therapeutic strategies therefore rely on combination regimens, such as pairing gilteritinib with ivosidenib or enasidenib, which are under evaluation in a Phase 1b trial for relapsed/refractory AML harboring co-mutations.244 Preliminary results suggest that these combinations are generally well tolerated and exhibit encouraging antileukemic activity.246 The absence of true dual FLT3/IDH inhibitors underscores a significant medicinal chemistry opportunity, as a single chemotype capable of concurrent FLT3 and IDH blockade could streamline therapy, reduce drug–drug interaction risks, and potentially enhance clinical durability.

Dual FLT3/IKK2 inhibitors

Aberrant NF-κB signaling is a major survival pathway in AML, promoting proliferation, anti-apoptosis, and therapy resistance.2 NF-κB activation occurs through phosphorylation of IκB by the IKK complex, which includes IKK1 (α), IKK2 (β), and the regulatory subunit IKKγ.247,248 Among these, IKK2 is a critical kinase for classical NF-κB signaling and has been implicated in AML pathogenesis, partly through cross-talk with FLT3-driven pathways.2 FLT3 activation (wild-type or ITD/TKD mutants) can stimulate NF-κB, reinforcing leukemic survival.2 Therefore, simultaneous inhibition of FLT3 and IKK2 offers a rational strategy to block both oncogenic and survival signaling. Preclinical studies confirm that dual targeting enhances apoptosis and reduces AML cell viability compared with single-target approaches, supporting its therapeutic potential in FLT3-mutated AML.2,249

AS602868

AS602868 is an anilino-pyrimidine ATP-competitive inhibitor (Fig. 19) that dually targets IKK2 and FLT3, with IC50 values of 70 nM (IKK2), 618 nM (FLT3), and 1076 nM (FLT3-D835Y).2,249 Beyond kinase inhibition, AS602868 suppresses NF-κB signaling by blocking IκBα phosphorylation and directly interferes with FLT3 phosphorylation in both wild-type and mutant forms (ITD, D835V, D835Y).213 In Ba/F3 FLT-WT and mutant models, AS602868 inhibited proliferation with IC50 values in the micromolar range (1.46–5.86 µM).2,249 Mechanistically, AS602868 induces apoptosis through mitochondrial depolarization, caspase-3 activation, and PARP cleavage, accompanied by downregulation of Bcl-xL, an anti-apoptotic protein regulated by NF-κB.2,249 Importantly, in vivo studies indicate that AS602868 does not significantly impair normal hematopoiesis, suggesting a favorable safety profile.213 However, these dual effects highlight the activity of AS602868 in overcoming resistance and improving therapeutic outcomes in FLT3-mutated AML, the clinical trails were discontinued.

Dual FLT3/IRAK inhibitors

Adaptive resistance to FLT3 inhibitors in AML often arises through activation of innate immune signaling, particularly via IRAK1/4, which sustains NF-κB activity and promotes leukemic survival under FLT3 blockade.250 IRAK4 is a key mediator in these pathways, and its dysregulation, especially through spliceosome-driven IRAK4-L isoform expression, has been linked to aggressive disease biology.251 This interplay provides a strong rationale for dual inhibition of FLT3 and IRAK kinases to suppress both the primary oncogenic driver and adaptive escape mechanisms. Preclinical studies demonstrate that combining FLT3 and IRAK inhibitors achieves superior cytotoxicity and reduces relapse compared with single-agent therapy, highlighting the therapeutic potential of this strategy in FLT3-ITD AML.250,252

NCGC1481

NCGC1481 represents a rationally optimized imidazo[1,2-a]pyridine derivative (Fig. 19) designed for dual inhibition of FLT3 and IRAK kinases to counteract both primary and adaptive resistance mechanisms in FLT3-mutated AML.252,253 Biochemical profiling confirmed potent inhibition of IRAK1 (IC50 = 26 nM), IRAK4 (IC50 = 1 nM), and FLT3 (IC50 < 0.5 nM).254 Importantly, NCGC1481 maintains sub-nanomolar binding across clinically relevant FLT3 variants, including ITD, D835Y/V, F691L, and compound mutations such as ITD/D835V, as well as less frequent substitutions (K663Q, N841I, R834Q).254

Structural studies reveal a type I FLT3 binding mode, with the imidazo[1,2-a]pyridine scaffold anchoring the hinge region; notably, proximity to Phe691 supports a modest edge interaction that is preserved upon the F691L substitution, while the absence of critical contacts with K663, N841, and R834 explains maintained affinity across these variants, explaining its resilience to resistance-conferring mutations.252

In cellular assays, NCGC1481 demonstrated robust antiproliferative activity in FLT3-driven AML models, achieving IC50 values of 30 nM in MOLM-14(D835Y) and 667 nM in MOLM-14(F691L) variants.254In vivo, NCGC1481 produced significant tumor regression and prolonged survival in FLT3-ITD xenograft models, outperforming approved single-target FLT3 inhibitors such as quizartinib and gilteritinib.252–254 Collectively, these findings underscore NCGC1481 as a dual FLT3/IRAK inhibitor with broad mutation coverage and superior preclinical efficacy, supporting its further development in relapsed/refractory AML.252,254

Thomas-31

Thomas-31 is an imidazo[1,2-a]pyridine derivative (Fig. 19) that continues the NCGC1481 program toward dual FLT3/IRAK inhibition in AML, aiming to sustain FLT3 blockade while suppressing IRAK-driven adaptive signaling.255 Biochemically, it binds FLT3 with very high affinity (Kd = 0.074 nM) and retains dual potency on IRAK1 and IRAK4 (Kd = 0.53 nM and 0.096 nM, respectively), while maintaining inhibitory activity across clinically relevant FLT3 mutants, including ITD, D835 variants, and the gatekeeper F691L.255 In cellular models, Thomas-31 produced robust antiproliferative activity in FLT3-driven lines, MOLM-14(D835Y) and MOLM-14(F691L) with IC50 value of 7 nM and 44 nM, respectively.255

In vivo, Thomas-31 prolonged survival comparable to gilteritinib in AML xenografts and showed good in vitro ADME and favorable pharmacokinetics, supporting preclinical developability.255 Although no clinical evaluation has been disclosed, the compound is positioned as an advanced preclinical tool validating IRAK-mediated adaptive resistance and the value of dual targeting in FLT3-mutant AML. Consistent with the series design and prior structural work on its predecessor, Thomas-31 is assumed to share the same type I, hinge-anchored binding mode as NCGC1481.255

Thomas-27

Thomas-27 advances the same imidazo[1,2-a]pyridine series (Fig. 19) with a design emphasis on lower hERG liability while retaining balanced dual inhibition of FLT3 and IRAK1/IRAK4.256 In enzymatic assays, it remained potent and selective, with IC50 values of 10 nM (IRAK1), 0.7 nM (IRAK4), and <0.5 nM (FLT3), alongside markedly reduced hERG block (IC50 = 6.7 µM).256 In cellular assays, Thomas-27 demonstrated strong antiproliferative activity in FLT3-driven AML models, achieving IC50 values of 15 nM in MOLM-14 (D835Y) and 139 nM in MOLM-14 (F691L), consistent with sustained activity against activation-loop and gatekeeper mutations and effective suppression of NF-κB signaling.256 Consistent with NCGC1481, Thomas-27 is assumed to preserve the type I, hinge-anchored binding mode and front-pocket occupancy that underpin its broad mutation coverage.256In vivo, Thomas-27 prolonged survival superior to gilteritinib in AML xenografts, underscoring that mitigating hERG while maintaining dual potency can improve the overall preclinical therapeutic window.256

Emavusertib (CA-4948)

Emavusertib (Fig. 19) is an oral and selective IRAK4 inhibitor that also inhibits FLT3, supporting dual suppression of innate-immune and FLT3-driven signaling in AML.251,257 In biochemical assays, emavusertib inhibits IRAK4 with IC50 value of 30 nM 258 and binds FLT3-WT and mutants including ITD, ITD/D835V, ITD/F691L with Kd values range of 8–31 nM.259 In AML cellular models, it exhibits cytotoxic activity across FLT3-mutant cell lines with IC50 values range of 58–200 nM.259Emavusertib favourable ADME characteristics, good oral bioavailability, and robust tumour growth inhibition in both FLT3-WT and FLT3-mutant AML mouse models, with toxicity studies confirming good tolerability at efficacious exposures.258–260

Emavusertib is currently being evaluated in a Phase 1/2 clinical trial in relapsed/refractory AML as monotherapy and in combination with azacitidine or venetoclax (NCT04278768).251,261–263

KME-0584

KME-0584 (chemical structure not disclosed) is a highly potent IRAK1/IRAK4/pan-FLT3 inhibitor (IC50 = 23 nM, <1.29 nM, and <0.5 nM, respectively), with superior potency and therapeutic efficacy against IRAK1/4, FLT3-WT, and FLT3-mutant AML models.264KME-0584 effectively inhibits TLR- and IL-1R-mediated NF-κB signaling, achieving complete pathway blockade in THP-1 NF-κB reporter assays.264 In primary AML colony-forming assays, it suppresses leukemic progenitor function, including models resistant to hypomethylating agents and venetoclax, and shows superior efficacy to gilteritinib in FLT3-ITD xenograft models, with PK properties supportive of oral dosing.264 Clinically, KME-0584 has received FDA IND clearance for evaluation in relapsed/refractory AML and high-risk MDS, enabling initiation of a Phase 1 clinical trial.265

HPB-092

HPB-092 (chemical structure not disclosed) is a dual FLT3/IRAK4 inhibitor advanced for AML, exhibiting potent biochemical activity against FLT3-ITD (IC50 = 0.24 nM), FLT3-D835Y (IC50 = 0.18 nM), and IRAK4 (IC50 = 3.1 nM), with cellular antiproliferative activity in FLT3-mutant models (MV4-11 IC50 = 6.5 nM).266In vivo, HPB-092 produced dose-dependent tumor regression in MV4-11 and MOLM-13 xenografts and showed high kinase selectivity, no CYP3A4 inhibition up to 50 µM, and no hERG inhibition up to 10 µM; additionally, 28-day GLP toxicology studies demonstrated a favourable safety profile.266 Clinically, HPB-092 is being evaluated in a Phase 1 monotherapy study in relapsed/refractory AML (NCT07137637).267 The structure HPB-092 has not yet been publicly reported.268

Liu-HB-29

Liu-HB-29 is a pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine analogue (Fig. 19) developed as a potent dual FLT3/IRAK4 inhibitor within a structure-guided optimization campaign.268 It exhibits strong biochemical potency against FLT3-WT and IRAK4, with IC50 values of 1.95 nM and 53.72 nM, respectively, and maintains activity across major FLT3 resistance mutations.268 In BaF3 cells harbouring variable FLT3 mutations, Liu-HB-29 inhibited FLT3-ITD, F691L, D835F, D835V, and ITD/D835Y variants with IC50 values of 21.84 nM, 24.48 nM, 23.24 nM, 20.11 nM, and 2.86 nM, respectively, demonstrating broad coverage of activation-loop, gatekeeper, and compound mutations.268 In AML cellular models, it achieved single-digit nanomolar potency in MV4-11 and MOLM-13 (IC50 = 1.8 nM and 7.86 nM, respectively), confirming effective dual-pathway engagement.268 Mechanistically, Liu-HB-29 suppressed FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream STAT5, AKT, and ERK activation while simultaneously inhibiting IRAK4 phosphorylation and NF-κB (p65), confirming on-target pathway blockade.268Liu-HB-29 demonstrated acceptable safety profile, moderate oral bioavailability supporting preclinical developability.268 Collectively, Liu-HB-29 displays potent dual FLT3/IRAK4 inhibition, broad mutation coverage, and activity against both acquired and adaptive resistance, positioning it as a competitive advanced lead within the FLT3/IRAK inhibitor series.

Structural modeling supports a type I FLT3 binding mode, where the pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine core forms bidentate hinge hydrogen bonds with Cys694, supplemented by additional interactions involving Tyr693, Cys695, and Tyr696.268

Dual FLT3/JAK2 inhibitors

Aberrant activation of Janus kinase 2 (JAK2) is a hallmark of dysregulated cytokine-driven JAK–STAT signaling, contributing to malignant proliferation and survival in myeloproliferative neoplasms and subsets of AML.269,270 In FLT3-mutated AML, compensatory activation of JAK2-STAT pathways can maintain STAT5 phosphorylation despite FLT3 inhibition, enabling leukemic persistence and limiting the durability of FLT3-targeted therapies.269 Multiple studies have demonstrated that FLT3 inhibitor resistance can emerge through activating JAK mutations, and that co-treatment with JAK inhibitors resensitizes FLT3-ITD AML cells to FLT3 inhibitors, directly validating the therapeutic value of simultaneously suppressing both pathways.271 Further mechanistic evidence shows that adaptive JAK2 signaling is a recurrent escape route during FLT3-TKI therapy, and that combinatorial inhibition of FLT3 and JAK2 more effectively suppresses downstream STAT5 and MAPK signaling than FLT3 inhibition alone.272 Collectively, these findings establish a strong rationale for designing dual FLT3/JAK2 inhibitors that consolidate the proven therapeutic synergy observed with FLT3 and JAK inhibitor combinations.

Fedratinib (TG101348)

Fedratinib is an orally bioavailable potent dual FLT3/JAK2 inhibitor (Fig. 20) with IC50 values of 15 nM and 3 nM, respectively.273 In FLT3-ITD AML cellular models, it suppresses proliferation in MV4-11 and MOLM-13 with EC50 values of 57 nM and 69 nM, respectively, consistent with effective inhibition of FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream STAT5/ERK/AKT signaling.274 Clinically, fedratinib is approved for myelofibrosis, and despite its preclinical FLT3/JAK2 activity, it has not been advanced into AML clinical trials; its characterized safety/pharmacology and dual-target profile nonetheless support its inclusion among FLT3/JAK2 chemotypes in AML-oriented medicinal chemistry.275,276

Momelotinib (CYT387)

Momelotinib is a type I dual JAK2/FLT3 inhibitor (Fig. 20) with potent activity against FLT3-mutated AML. It inhibits JAK2 with an IC50 of 18 nM and suppresses FLT3-WT, FLT3-ITD, and Ba/F3 FLT3-dependent proliferation with IC50 values of 320–401 nM, while inhibiting primary FLT3-ITD AML cells at 165–203 nM.277,278 It effectively inhibits activation-loop mutants D835, D839, and Y842, consistent with a type I binding preference for the active/DFG-in FLT3 conformation and also overcomes cytokine-mediated resistance by inhibiting JAK2-dependent survival signals induced by GM-CSF and IL-3, thereby deepening FLT3 pathway suppression.277 Docking studies predict that momelotinib anchors via a hinge hydrogen bond to Cys694 and two additional hydrogen bonds to Glu817 and Ser618 within the P-loop.277

Pacritinib (SB1518)

Pacritinib is a potent type I dual FLT3/JAK2 inhibitor (Fig. 20) with IC50 values of 22 nM (FLT3-WT) and 23 nM (JAK2).279,280 It suppresses FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream STAT5/ERK/AKT signaling in FLT3-ITD AML cell lines (MV4-11, MOLM-13) and in FLT3-WT RS4; 11 cells, with proliferation IC50 values of 47 nM, 67 nM, and 930 nM, respectively.280,281Pacritinib shows high potency against FLT3-TKD resistance variants, including FLT3-D835Y (IC50 = 3.1 nM) and FLT3-ITD (IC50 = 9 nM).282In vivo, pacritinib induces complete regressions in MV4-11 xenografts and achieves 83% tumor-growth inhibition in MOLM-13 models in a dose-dependent manner.280 Clinically, pacritinib has undergone early AML exploration, including a pilot Phase I in FLT3-mutated AML and a terminated Phase II trial (NCT02532010), but has not progressed into a dedicated AML clinical program.282

Chen-18e

Chen-18e is a 4-(1H-pyrazol-4-yl)pyrimidin-2-amine chemotype advanced as a dual JAK2/FLT3 lead, inhibiting JAK2 (IC50 = 0.7 nM) and FLT3-WT/FLT3-D835Y (IC50 = 4 nM each), thereby consolidating JAK–STAT and FLT3 pathway suppression in AML.283 In FLT3-ITD models, it suppresses proliferation with IC50 values of 5.3 nM (MV4-11) and 72 nM (MOLM-13), accompanied by inhibition of FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream STAT5/ERK/AKT signaling.283 Modeling in FLT3 supports an ATP-site binding mode featuring hydrogen bonds to Met665 and Asp829 and a cation–π contact with Phe830 (Fig. 20), consistent with potency against D835 variants.283 Orally, Chen-18e produced marked tumor-growth inhibition activity in MV4-11 xenografts, with favorable pharmacokinetics and a promising safety profile characterized by weak hERG inhibition (IC50 = 28 µM) and minimal CYP3A4 liability, thus supporting a preclinical status.283

Zhao-14j

Zhao-14j is a 4-piperazinyl-2-aminopyrimidine advanced as a dual JAK2/FLT3 inhibitor with IC50 values of 27 nM and 30 nM, respectively.269 In AML settings, it shows antiproliferative activity in FLT3-ITD MV4-11 cells with GI50 value of 11.0 µM and induces G1/S arrest and apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner.269 Molecular docking into FLT3 in the DFG-out conformation, showed a quizartinib-like binding mode, with a hinge hydrogen bond between the 2-aminopyrimidine and Cys695, π–π stacking of the “B” phenyl ring with Tyr696, an additional hydrogen bond between the acetyl carbonyl and Asp829, and occupation of the back pocket with edge-to-face contacts to Phe691/Phe830, while the morpholine projects to solvent accessible region (Fig. 20).269

As a continuation of the 4-piperazinyl-2-aminopyrimidine series exemplified by Zhao-14j discussed previously,269Zhao-11r emerges as a selective dual FLT3/JAK2 inhibitor with potent IC50 values of 0.51 nM and 2.01 nM, respectively.284 In AML models, it shows potent antiproliferative activity in MV4-11 cell line with IC50 value of 9.43 nM and induces dose-dependent apoptosis.284 It effectively inhibits mutant FLT3 including, FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y with IC50 values of 0.36 nM and 2.95 nM, respectively. Dual FLT3/MER (MERTK) inhibitors

MERTK is a TAM-family receptor tyrosine kinase aberrantly overexpressed in about 80–100% of AML patient samples, while exhibiting minimal expression in normal hematopoietic progenitors, establishing it as a leukemia-selective vulnerability.2,286 MERTK activation propagates pro-survival signaling through ERK1/2, AKT, and STAT6, thereby sustaining leukemic proliferation and apoptosis resistance.2,286 Genetic depletion of MERTK using RNA interference markedly increases apoptosis in AML cells with limited effects on normal hematopoietic cells, reinforcing its therapeutic window.286 These observations are further supported by independent preclinical studies showing that selective MERTK inhibition suppresses AML cell growth and down-modulates AKT/ERK signaling, with similar effects observed upon MERTK knockdown.287 FLT3 and MERTK converge on overlapping downstream nodes (notably STAT5/AKT/ERK); hence, concomitant inhibition provides a mechanistic rationale to deepen pathway blockade, mitigate adaptive escape under FLT3 monotherapy, and enhance cytotoxicity in FLT3-mutant AML.2 This dual-target concept has been validated preclinically with chemotypes optimized to engage both kinases and to retain activity against resistance-relevant FLT3 variants.100,288,289

UNC2025 (MRX-6313)

UNC2025 is a potent, ATP-competitive type I dual inhibitor of MERTK and FLT3, displaying sub-nanomolar enzymatic potency (IC50 = 0.74 nM and 0.80 nM, respectively) and favorable kinase-selectivity over related TAM members.117 In cellular settings relevant to AML, UNC2025 suppresses pFLT3 in MOLM-14 (FLT3-ITD) cells with IC50 value of 14 nM, accompanied by marked reductions in colony formation.117 Pharmacodynamic studies further demonstrate that a single oral dose in leukemia-bearing mice achieves >90% inhibition of phospho-MERTK in bone-marrow blasts, corroborating its oral bioavailability and on-target activity.117,289UNC2025 binding mode is consistent with a canonical type I inhibitor based on its cocrystallographic data with MERTK kinase domain (Fig. 21).290 Collectively, these biochemical, structural, cellular, and in vivo data support UNC2025 as a rigorously profiled FLT3/MERTK dual inhibitor with robust target engagement and anti-leukemic activity.

Dual FLT3/MEK inhibitors

Constitutive FLT3 signaling engages the canonical RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK/MAPK cascade, a pathway that governs proliferation, survival, and differentiation decisions in hematopoietic cells and is recurrently deregulated in leukemia.2 In FLT3-mutant AML, persistent MEK/ERK activation has been observed even when FLT3 phosphorylation is suppressed, implicating the MAPK arm as a key bypass node for adaptive and microenvironment-mediated resistance to FLT3-directed therapy; accordingly, concomitant blockade of FLT3 and MEK has been proposed to deepen target pathway shutdown and restore apoptotic responses.2,292 This dual-target rationale is further reinforced by studies showing that bone-marrow niche factors (including hypoxia and stromal coculture) sustain MAPK signaling and blunt FLT3 inhibitors efficacy, whereas dual FLT3/MEK targeting retains cytotoxicity under these conditions and reduces leukemia burden in vivo.292

E6201

E6201 is an ATP-competitive dual MEK1/FLT3 inhibitor (Fig. 21) (MEK1 IC50 = 5.2 nM).292,293 In AML cellular assays, E6201 shows low-nanomolar activity in FLT3-ITD models, with IC50 values of 1.9 nM (MV4-11) and 4.9 nM (MOLM-13), and it retains nanomolar potency in FLT3-TKD mutant settings, whereas activity at Phe691 substitution is reduced relative to ITD/TKD yet remains consistent with cellular target engagement.292 Functionally, dual FLT3/MAPK blockade by E6201 sustains apoptosis and maintains cytotoxicity under hypoxia and stromal co-culture, with in vivo studies demonstrating reduced leukemia burden and prolonged survival, supporting convergent targeting of FLT3 and MEK in resistant AML.292 A Phase 1/2a study in hematologic malignancies with FLT3 and/or RAS mutations (NCT02418000) was terminated during dose-escalation for insufficient efficacy.

Dual FLT3/Mnk2 inhibitors

Mitogen-activated protein kinase–interacting kinases Mnk1 and Mnk2 lie downstream of RAS/RAF/ERK and p38-MAPK and uniquely phosphorylate eIF4E, a translational switch that supports leukemic proliferation and stress adaptation in AML.294 In FLT3-mutated settings, persistent MAPK signaling sustains Mnk-driven eIF4E phosphorylation under FLT3 blockade, enabling cap-dependent translation and attenuating FLT3-inhibitor cytotoxicity.294–296 Consistent with this, pharmacologic data in AML models and dual-design studies indicate that concurrent FLT3 and Mnk2 inhibition deepens pathway suppression, restores apoptosis, and can overcome resistance, including FLT3-ITD models with acquired TKI tolerance.294,297,298 Collectively, these findings validate Mnk2 as a cooperating survival node in FLT3-mutant AML and provide a clear rationale for designed dual FLT3/Mnk2 inhibitors or combination strategies that recapitulate this dual blockade.

Wang-16a

Wang-16a is an indolo-pyrimidine derivative (Fig. 21) identified as a dual FLT3/Mnk2 inhibitor (Ki = 0.34 µM and 0.11 µM, respectively) with anti-proliferative activity in MV4-11 cells with GI50 value of 0.60 µM.299 In MV4-11 mechanistic assays, it reduced phosphorylation of both eIF4E and STAT5, consistent with concurrent suppression of the Mnk/eIF4E axis and FLT3 signaling; functionally, it increased apoptosis, induced G1/sub-G1 accumulation, and down-regulated Mcl-1 compared to a single-target inhibitor.299

K783-0308

K783-0308, discovered by structure-based virtual screening, is a dual FLT3/Mnk2 inhibitor with IC50 values of 680 nM and 406 nM, respectively.294 In AML cells, it demonstrated micromolar potency with IC50 values of 10.4 µM (MV4-11) and 10.5 µM (MOLM-13) and increased sub-G1 in a concentration- and time-dependent manner.294

Molecular docking of K783-0308 places the benzimidazole as the hinge binder in FLT3 (Fig. 21), forming a hydrogen bond to Cys694, while the adjacent aryl system projects into the DFG-adjacent hydrophobic tunnel and engages a cluster of hydrophobic contacts (notably Val624, Ala642, Lys644, Phe691, Leu818, Cys828), consistent with ATP-site occupancy; the N-cyclopentylamide extends toward the solvent front where its amide NH can form an additional polar contact, and the cyclopentyl ring packs against Leu616, collectively stabilizing the pose (Fig. 21).294

H104

H104 (chemical structure not disclosed) was generated by optimizing a dual pyrido[3,2-d]pyrimidine MNK/PIM lead300 to selectively target Mnk1/2 and FLT3 while losing PIM activity.298 It inhibited Mnk1, Mnk2, and FLT3-ITD with IC50 values of 19 nM, 8 nM, and 46 nM, respectively, and showed antiproliferative activity in FLT3-ITD AML cells (MOLM-13 GI50 = 1.07 µM).298 Notably, H104 retained superior activity in sorafenib-resistant MOLM-13 cells (GI50 = 0.44 µM), accompanied by decreases in p-eIF4E, c-Myc, and MCL-1, consistent with dual suppression of FLT3 signaling and the Mnk/eIF4E axis.298

Dual FLT3/MDM2 inhibitors

MDM2 is an oncogenic E3 ligase that negatively regulates p53 by targeting it for ubiquitin-mediated degradation, thereby suppressing p53-dependent apoptosis and cell-cycle arrest.304 Accordingly, MDM2 inhibition is effective only in TP53-wild-type AML, as TP53-mutant or TP53-deleted leukemias cannot benefit from p53 reactivation.305 In FLT3-mutated AML, p53 activity modulates responsiveness to FLT3 inhibitors, and MDM2 blockade can restore apoptotic sensitivity in FLT3-ITD cells.306 Although no single dual FLT3/MDM2 inhibitor has been reported to date, combination strategies effectively mimic dual-pathway suppression. Notably, the MDM2 inhibitor NVP-HDM201 significantly enhanced FLT3-inhibitor sensitivity when combined with midostaurin in FLT3-ITD/TP53-WT models, supporting its translational potential.307

Dual FLT3/menin inhibitors

Menin, encoded by MEN1, is an essential scaffold protein that anchors the menin–KMT2A (MLL1) transcriptional complex, maintaining expression of leukemogenic HOX genes, including HOXA9 and MEIS1, that are central to the survival of KMT2A-rearranged and NPM1-mutated AML subsets.242,308 Clinically, menin inhibitors such as revumenib and ziftomenib have shown potent clinical activity in these genetically defined AML groups.43,309 Mechanistically, menin inhibition suppresses MEIS1, leading to downregulation of FLT3 transcription and attenuation of STAT5A-driven survival pathways. In parallel, FLT3 inhibitors directly block the kinase activity of FLT3-ITD/TKD, providing a complementary mechanism of suppression.43 This convergence creates a strong rationale for combined menin and FLT3 targeting, because the two pathways converge functionally and cooperatively sustain leukemic fitness. Although no dual FLT3/menin inhibitor exists, the combination pharmacologically mimics dual inhibition, producing deeper suppression of HOX–MEIS1–FLT3 signaling and enhanced apoptosis in FLT3-ITD/NPM1-mutated or KMT2A-rearranged AML models.310,311 Importantly, ziftomenib shows strong activity in KMT2A-rearranged AML harboring FLT3-ITD, supporting the translational relevance of this dual-pathway strategy.43,309,312

Dual FLT3/PDGFR inhibitors

Platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFRα/β) are class-III RTKs that are structurally homologous to FLT3, sharing conserved hinge contacts and ATP-site topology.313 Aberrant PDGFR signaling is recurrently detected in myeloid neoplasms and contributes to leukemic proliferation and survival through activation of MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and STAT pathways that extensively overlap with FLT3-driven signaling.314 In FLT3-mutated AML, PDGFR activation can support compensatory survival signaling when FLT3 is pharmacologically inhibited, thereby diminishing the depth and durability of FLT3 blockade.2,315 Given the high structural similarity between FLT3 and PDGFR, many FLT3 inhibitors inherently retain PDGFR activity, including midostaurin, crenolanib, sorafenib, sunitinib, and tandutinib, as a result, concurrent inhibition of FLT3 and PDGFR suppresses parallel RTK-driven survival circuits, enhances cytotoxicity, and can limit adaptive resistance that emerges under selective FLT3 targeting alone.2 Multi-target inhibitors such as olverembatinib further support this concept by demonstrating potent FLT3 inhibition alongside PDGFR suppression, achieving robust antitumor activity in FLT3-ITD and kinase-fusion-driven AML models.220,221 Collectively, the overlapping signaling architecture of FLT3 and PDGFR provides a clear mechanistic rationale for dual-target FLT3/PDGFR inhibitors.

Linifanib (ABT-869)

Linifanib is a diaryl-urea type II multi-kinase inhibitor with IC50 values of 4 nM against FLT3 and 66 nM against PDGFR-β.84,316 In AML models, it demonstrate potent antiproliferative activity with GI50 values of 4 nM (MV4-11) and 6 nM (MOLM-13).317 Activity is partially retained against D835H and D835Q activation-loop variants, whereas the D835Y and D835V substitutions confer marked resistance.317In vivo, linifanib produces marked tumor regression in MV4-11 xenografts, consistent with complete suppression of FLT3 phosphorylation.317 Pharmacokinetic studies indicate good oral exposure, low systemic clearance, and moderate half-lives across preclinical species.316 Structural modeling in VEGFR-2 supports a DFG-out type II binding mode (Fig. 22), a configuration expected to extend to FLT3 owing to the conserved architecture of the kinase fold.316 Clinically, linifanib was evaluated in a Phase 1 study in relapsed/refractory AML, demonstrating on-target FLT3 pathway inhibition as monotherapy and in combination with cytarabine, however, the program did not progress to later-phase AML development.318

AGL 2043

AGL-2043 is a tricyclic quinoxaline inhibitor (Fig. 22) that potently targets class III receptor tyrosine kinases, displaying an IC50 of 90 nM against PDGFR-β and effectively suppressing FLT3 phosphorylation in Ba/F3 cells.319 Although it inhibits FLT3 signaling in cellular models, no in vivo data or clinical studies have demonstrated therapeutic activity in AML, and its efficacy in AML remains unsubstantiated.

Dove-102

Dove-102 is a bis(indol-2-yl)methanone that exhibits dual FLT3/PDGFR activity, with IC50 values of 33 nM and 171 nM, respectively.320 In AML-relevant models, it inhibits proliferation of 32D cells driven by FLT3-WT or FLT3-ITD at around 0.1 µM and induces apoptosis in primary AML blasts, consistent with on-target FLT3 pathway suppression.320 Molecular docking to the FLT3 crystal structure supports a hinge-anchored, bidentate interaction (indole-NH and methanone carbonyl with the backbone of Cys694) combined with hydrophobic engagement near Phe691 (Fig. 22), providing a structural rationale for FLT3-over-PDGFR selectivity within this chemotype.320 No AML in vivo efficacy or clinical evaluation has been reported for this compound.

Lu-S21

Lu-s21 is a diaryl-urea based isoxazolo[3,4-b]pyridin-3-amine that exhibits balanced dual FLT3/PDGFR activity, with enzymatic IC50 values of 4 nM (FLT3) and 8 nM (PDGFRβ), and antiproliferative effects in MV4-11 (IC50 = 0.12 µM).321 Docking to FLT3 supports a type II-like pose anchored at the hinge through a bidentate interaction in which the isoxazole nitrogen and oxygen interact with Cys694, complemented by an additional hydrogen bond to Glu692 via the anilide amino group; the urea linker further contributes hydrogen bonds to Asp829 and Glu661, consistent with an extended DFG-out engagement321 (Fig. 22).

Dual FLT3/PIM inhibitors

PIM kinases (PIM1, PIM2, PIM3) are constitutively active serine/threonine kinases that promote leukemic cell survival by stabilizing MYC, phosphorylating pro-apoptotic proteins such as BAD, and enhancing mTOR-mediated metabolic programs.322,323 In AML, PIM1 and PIM2 are frequently overexpressed, particularly in FLT3-ITD contexts, where persistent STAT5 activation directly induces their transcription.324,325 This creates a cooperative signaling circuit in which FLT3-ITD activates STAT5, STAT5 upregulates PIM, and PIM reinforces STAT5-dependent survival pathways, creating a self-sustaining proliferative loop.325

PIM overexpression contributes to adaptive and acquired resistance to selective FLT3 inhibitors, as PIM kinases preserve STAT5 phosphorylation, maintain MCL-1 levels, and prevent apoptosis even when FLT3 kinase activity is pharmacologically suppressed.324,326–328 Conversely, genetic or pharmacologic PIM inhibition restores FLT3-TKI sensitivity in resistant FLT3-ITD models and enhances cytotoxicity in primary AML samples, highlighting the therapeutic relevance of targeting this axis.324,328 This biological rationale has motivated the design of dedicated dual FLT3/PIM chemotypes, several of which have advanced into preclinical and clinical development.

Dapolsertib (SEL24-B489)

Dapolsertib is a first-in-class type I dual FLT3/pan-PIM inhibitor (Fig. 22) distinguished by balanced nanomolar potency across all PIM isoforms and strong activity against oncogenic FLT3 variants.329 It potently inhibits PIM1, PIM2, and PIM3 with Kd values of 2 nM, 2 nM, and 3 nM, respectively. Parallel FLT3 binding studies demonstrate preferential activity toward FLT3-ITD (Kd = 16 nM) compared with FLT3-WT (Kd= 160 nM).329 Across FLT3 resistance-associated mutations, dapolsertib retains potent activity against activation-loop substitutions (D835H/V/Y) and gatekeeper variants such as ITD/F691L, while having reduced susceptibility toward a subset of non-canonical hinge and activation-segment mutations (K663Q, N841I, R834Q).329 In AML cellular systems, dapolsertib shows broad, mutation-resilient cytotoxicity. It markedly reduces viability in FLT3-ITD-driven MOLM-13 and MV4-11 (IC50 = 93.8 nM), induces S-phase depletion, suppresses STAT5 signaling, downregulates MCL-1, and triggers PARP cleavage, consistent with dual FLT3/PIM pathway inhibition.329,330

Dapolsertib completed AML-directed clinical evaluation in the phase 1/2 DIAMOND-01 trial (NCT03008187), which established its safety profile and modest single-agent activity in relapsed or refractory AML.

SGI-1776

SGI-1776 is a first-generation pan-PIM inhibitor (Fig. 22) that also exhibits notable activity against FLT3 in FLT3-mutant contexts. Biochemically, it inhibits PIM1, PIM2, and PIM3 with IC50 values of 7 nM, 363 nM, and 79 nM, respectively, and additionally suppresses FLT3 with an IC50 value of 44 nM.331 In AML systems, SGI-1776 shows preferential cytotoxicity in FLT3-ITD cell lines and inhibits FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream AKT/ERK/STAT5, inducing annexin-V positivity and caspase-3 cleavage at low micromolar concentrations.332,333 Antitumor effects were also documented in MV4-11 xenografts under tolerable dosing, consistent with dual inhibition of PIM substrates (e.g., c-MYC Ser62, BAD Ser112) and FLT3-driven survival signaling.333 Despite this activity, clinical development was halted in Phase I for cardiotoxicity consistent with hERG liability.331

RF-1302

RF-1302 (detailed chemical structure not disclosed) is a dual PIM1/FLT3 inhibitor with IC50 values of 1–2.3 nM and 0.8–2.2 nM, respectively, demonstrating markedly higher potency than the benchmark FLT3 inhibitors gilteritinib, quizartinib, and midostaurin.334 In AML cellular models, RF-1302 displays strong cytotoxicity toward MV4-11 and MOLM-13, with IC50 values of 5.7 nM and 9.5 nM, respectively, confirming effective suppression of FLT3-ITD-driven survival signaling.334In vivo, RF-1302 achieved 95% tumor growth inhibition in MOLM-13 xenograft models and induced complete tumor regression in MV4-11 xenografts, with the treatment being well tolerated and associated with minimal body-weight loss.334

EC-70124

EC-70124 is a synthetic indolocarbazole multi-kinase inhibitor structurally related to midostaurin (Fig. 22)335 and exerts potent inhibition of both FLT3 and PIM kinases.336 It potently inhibits FLT3 and PIM1/2/3 with Kd values of 3.2 nM, 2.9 nM, 3.1 nM, and 0.4 nM, respectively.336EC-70124 retains nanomolar activity across FLT3-ITD, FLT3-TKD, gatekeeper, and representative non-canonical mutations, and consistently suppress FLT3–STAT5, Akt–mTOR–S6, and PIM–BAD signaling pathways.336,337 In AML cellular models, it displays strong cytotoxicity toward MV4-11 and MOLM-13, with IC50 values of 12.27 nM and 13.0 nM, respectively.336In vivo, EC-70124 demonstrates potent antitumor efficacy in FLT3-ITD AML xenograft models, including complete tumor regression and inhibition of FLT3–STAT5 signaling; it also shows improved metabolic stability and lower plasma-protein binding versusmidostaurin.336EC-70124 remains in preclinical development, with no active AML clinical trials reported.337

Dual FLT3/PI3K inhibitors

Phosphoinositide 3-kinases (PI3Ks) are central mediators of prosurvival signaling downstream of growth factor receptors and are constitutively active in AML, where they maintain AKT and ERK phosphorylation and support leukemic proliferation and apoptosis resistance.339,340 In FLT3-mutated AML, FLT3-ITD drives strong activation of PI3K/AKT signaling, and persistent PI3K activity has been shown to blunt the cytotoxic effects of FLT3 inhibitors.216,341 This provides a mechanistic basis for combining FLT3 and PI3K inhibition to achieve deeper pathway suppression and prevent adaptive survival signaling.

Although no dual FLT3/PI3K small-molecule inhibitor has been reported, several studies demonstrate that concurrent inhibition of FLT3 and PI3K produces synergistic antileukemic activity. Quizartinib combination with PF-04691502 (PI3K/mTOR inhibitor) markedly reduced the viability of FLT3-ITD AML cells, induced robust G1 arrest and apoptosis, and achieved up to 90% cell death within 48 hours, outperforming either agent alone.134 This synergy reflects coordinated suppression of FLT3-driven STAT5/MAPK signaling together with PI3K-dependent AKT survival pathways, supporting the therapeutic validity of co-targeting FLT3 and PI3K/mTOR in AML.134,342 A similar benefit was observed with isoform-selective PI3Kδ inhibition. Co-treatment with CAL-101 (PI3Kδ inhibitor) and quizartinib produced a synergistic antitumor effect in FLT3-activated AML models, mediated by enhanced inhibition of AKT and ERK phosphorylation, key downstream nodes shared between FLT3 and PI3Kδ.343In vivo, the combination led to superior tumor regression compared with monotherapy, and importantly, it overcame acquired resistance to FLT3 inhibitors in mutant FLT3-ITD AML cells.343 Collectively, these studies validate PI3K signaling as a cooperating survival axis in FLT3-mutated AML and demonstrate that dual targeting of FLT3 and PI3K, whether through combination or dual-target design, represents a therapeutically promising strategy, particularly for drug-resistant FLT3-ITD AML.

Dual FLT3/RET inhibitors

RET is a receptor tyrosine kinase activated by GDNF-family ligands to drive prosurvival and metabolic programs in responsive tissues.344 In AML, RET signaling activates mTORC1 and suppresses autophagy, creating a permissive metabolic state; accordingly, RET blockade reactivates autophagy and limits leukemic growth.345–347 Functional studies show that distinct AML subsets depend on RET, and that RET–mTORC1 activity stabilizes leukemogenic drivers, including mutant FLT3, in FLT3-ITD contexts.348 Consistently, genetic or pharmacologic RET inhibition impairs FLT3-dependent AML, is accompanied by autophagy induction and FLT3 depletion, and thereby provides experimental support for combining RET and FLT3 inhibition to deepen pathway shutdown and limit persistence under FLT3-directed therapy.348 In line with this, danusertib and crenolanib, used together to co-inhibit RET and FLT3, significantly reduced viability and proliferation and increased apoptosis in FLT3-mutant AML cell lines and primary samples versus either agent alone, supporting co-targeting of these pathways to enhance antileukemic efficacy in AML.346 Despite this biological rationale, RET-directed agents in AML remain limited, and reports of compounds that address both axes are only beginning to emerge.

PLM-102

PLM-102 (chemical structure not disclosed) is a dual FLT3/RET inhibitor developed to overcome resistance to current FLT3 inhibitors and evaluated as a therapeutic candidate for AML.349,350 In biochemical assays, PLM-102 exhibited sub-nanomolar IC50 values against FLT3-WT, FLT3-ITD, FLT3-TKD, and FLT3 ITD/TKD compound mutants, demonstrating stronger inhibitory activity than the FDA-approved FLT3 inhibitor gilteritinib.349,350 In FLT3-mutated AML cell lines, PLM-102 displayed 18 to 25-fold higher antiproliferative potency than gilteritinib, consistent with profound inhibition of FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream STAT5/ERK/AKT signaling.349 These effects were accompanied by strong apoptotic responses, including PARP cleavage and caspase-3 activation, in alignment with its on-target suppression of FLT3 signaling.349,350In vivo, it achieved marked tumor growth suppression and complete tumor regression in multiple AML xenograft models, including FLT3-ITD and drug-resistant FLT3-ITD-TKD variants, following once-daily oral dosing.349,350 The compound also retained activity in gilteritinib-resistant models, demonstrating effective pathway inhibition in settings driven by TKD, Ras-associated, and ligand-dependent resistance mechanisms.351

PLM-101

PLM-101 (Fig. 23) is an indirubin-derived dual FLT3/RET inhibitor with potent IC50 values of 0.565 nM and 0.849 nM, respectively.352 In Ba/F3 cells, it retains activity against resistant mutants, inhibiting FLT3-ITD-D835Y (GI50 = 0.7 nM) and FLT3-ITD-F691L (GI50 = 10.5 nM).352 In FLT3-ITD+ AML cell lines, PLM-101 reduces the viability of MV4-11, MOLM-13, and MOLM-14 with IC50 values of 3.26 nM, 10.47 nM, and 9.46 nM, respectively, suppresses FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream PI3K/AKT and Ras/ERK signaling, and induces apoptosis.352 In addition, RET inhibition by PLM-101 promotes autophagic degradation of FLT3, which adds to its antileukemic effect compared with FLT3-selective agents.352 Pharmacokinetically, PLM-101 is orally bioavailable with pH-dependent, gastric-favored absorption, and once-daily oral dosing reduces tumor growth in flank xenografts (MV4-11, MOLM-14).352

Taha-30

Taha-30 is an isoindigo-based dual FLT3/RET lead emerging from a docking-guided series; it inhibits FLT3-D835Y with an IC50 of 88 nM and RET with an IC50 of 57 nM, and shows selective cytotoxicity in HL-60 leukemia cells (IC50 = 0.31 µM).353 Molecular docking of Taha-30 into the inactive FLT3-WT binding site indicates a pose in which the sulfonamide engages the hinge-proximal region near Cys828/Asp829, while the indolone amide orients toward the Glu692/Cys694 hinge, and the fused alicyclic flank packs into the hydrophobic cavity delineated by Leu616/Leu818/Val675/Cys694 (Fig. 23), stabilizing the complex and rationalizing its potency.353

Dual FLT3/SYK inhibitors

Spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase that plays a central role in hematopoietic signaling, regulating immunoreceptor-driven activation, differentiation, and survival pathways through phosphorylation of downstream adaptors and engagement of STAT5, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK cascades.354,355 In AML, SYK functions as a critical cooperative partner of FLT3, where its overexpression amplifies FLT3-ITD-driven signaling, enhances proliferation, and promotes leukemic stem-cell maintenance.2,355,356 Importantly, SYK activation persists even when FLT3 is pharmacologically suppressed, therefore concomitant inhibition of SYK and FLT3 produces deeper pathway shutdown, increased apoptosis, and superior antileukemic effects compared with FLT3 inhibition alone, as shown by the quizartinib and PRT062607 (SYK inhibitor) synergy.356 As noted previously, midostaurin exhibits multikinase activity that includes inhibition of both FLT3 and SYK,357 illustrating the therapeutic validity of concurrently targeting these two cooperating kinases.

Mivavotinib (TAK-659)

Mivavotinib is a dual type I FLT3/SYK inhibitor (Fig. 23), identified from a SYK-focused program, with IC50 values of 4.6 nM and 3.2 nM, respectively.358 In cellular systems, it suppresses the viability of FLT3-ITD+ AML lines with EC50 = 18 nM (MV4-11) and 110 nM (MOLM-13), whereas FLT3-WT RS4-11 and non-FLT3-driven RA1 cells show minimal sensitivity, indicating FLT3-context dependence of its antileukemic activity.358In vivo, mivavotinib produces tumor regression in MV4-11 xenografts at 60 mg per kg per day and shows marked tumor-growth inhibition in SYK-driven DLBCL models, supporting dual-axis efficacy in preclinical settings.358 Clinically, mivavotinib has advanced into Phase I/Ib–II studies for hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, including relapsed/refractory AML and lymphoma.359

Tamatinib (R406)

Tamatinib (Fig. 23), the active metabolite of fostamatinib (Fig. 23), exhibits dual SYK/FLT3 inhibition with an IC50 value of 41 nM on SYK and about five-fold weaker potency against FLT3.360 In FLT3-ITD systems, R406 retains clear FLT3-relevant activity, inhibiting Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD proliferation (IC50 = 36.7 nM) and remaining active under dual-node activation (IC50 = 223.7 nM in FLT3-ITD + SYK-TEL; 27.0 nM in FLT3-ITD + TEL-SYK), with concordant suppression of FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream STAT5/AKT/S6 and restoration of apoptosis.357 In the same dual-activated models, highly selective FLT3 inhibitors such as quizartinib and crenolanib lose potency, whereas tamatinib preserves functional suppression, highlighting the benefit of concurrent FLT3 and SYK blockade in dual-driven AML.357 Clinically, fostamatinib, the prodrug of tamatinib, is FDA-approved for chronic immune thrombocytopenia, providing an established pharmacokinetic and safety framework for potential repurposing.361 While not approved for AML, tamatinib has entered early exploratory investigation in hematologic malignancies, supported by strong mechanistic evidence that dual FLT3/SYK inhibition can overcome resistance that limits the efficacy of FLT3-selective agents.362

Dual FLT3/TAF1 inhibitors

TAF1 is the largest part of the TFIID complex and uses its kinase function and two acetyl-lysine-binding bromodomains that regulate chromatin accessibility and transcription initiation.365 In leukemia, TAF1 depletion markedly reduces proliferation and self-renewal, confirming its essential role in sustaining oncogenic transcriptional programs.366 In addition, the TAF1(2) bromodomain binds acetylated p53 and promotes its phosphorylation-dependent degradation, thereby weakening p53-mediated apoptosis in leukemic cells.367,368 In FLT3-mutated AML, constitutively active FLT3-ITD and TKD variants sustain leukemic survival through strong STAT5 and MAPK signaling, independent of external regulatory signals.367 In contrast, TAF1 maintains the chromatin-dependent transcriptional output required for leukemic proliferation and self-renewal.367 Because FLT3-driven kinase signaling and TAF1-driven transcription operate as parallel survival pathways, inhibiting either target alone is unlikely to achieve complete suppression of leukemic fitness.367

Välimäki-3i-1246

Välimäki-3i-1246 (Fig. 24) is a dual FLT3/TAF1(2) inhibitor exhibiting a balanced activity profile, with IC50 values of 18 nM (FLT3) and 280 nM (TAF1(2) bromodomain).367 Docking studies indicate that the compound adopts a type II binding pose in FLT3 while also engaging the acetyl-lysine-recognition pocket of TAF1(2), supporting productive dual-target accommodation.367 In cellular assays, Välimäki-3i-1246 retained antiproliferative activity in the FLT3-dependent MOLM-13 AML model, achieving an IC50 of 292.7 nM.367 This profile demonstrates that the compound remains active at relevant cellular concentrations and supports the feasibility of concurrent inhibition of FLT3 signaling and TAF1-mediated transcriptional regulation within a single scaffold.

Dual FLT3/TrKA inhibitors

TrkA is a neurotrophin receptor that drives neuronal survival and differentiation and also contributes to early hematopoietic development.369 In AML, TrkA activation, via gene fusions or overexpression, supports leukemic proliferation and survival, and TRK fusions in hematologic malignancies are targetable with TRK-directed agents.370 However, selective TrkA inhibition alone is not lethal in AML models, indicating that TrkA-driven signaling cooperates with other oncogenic nodes and that combined pathway blockade is required for effective cytotoxicity.370 From a medicinal-chemistry perspective, there is pharmacologic precedent for FLT3 scaffolds that also inhibit TrkA with low-nanomolar activity, including gilteritinib, midostaurin, lestaurtinib, and crenolanib.371 Together with evidence that TrkA single-agent blockade is insufficient in AML, these cross-target activities support the rationale for designed dual FLT3/TrkA inhibition to deepen pathway shutdown, reduce compensatory signaling, and address heterogeneity and resistance under FLT3-selective therapy.371

4ACP

4ACP (Fig. 24) is a benzimidazole-based dual FLT3/TrkA inhibitor developed through scaffold modification of quizartinib and identified as a potent lead for AML treatment.371 It shows nanomolar inhibitory activity against FLT3-WT as well as the clinically relevant FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y mutant forms, with IC50 values of 43.8 nM, 97.2 nM, and 92.5 nM, respectively, in addition to potent TrkA inhibition (IC50 = 23.6 nM).371 In cellular assays, 4ACP shows preferential activity in FLT3-ITD+ AML, MOLM-13 and MV4-11, with IC50 values of 38.8 nM and 54.9 nM, respectively, while lacking cytotoxicity in FLT3-ITD− AML lines (THP-1, GDM-1), consistent with on-target pathway engagement.371 Mechanistically, 4ACP suppresses ERK1/2 and mTOR signaling downstream of FLT3-ITD, induces G0/G1 arrest, and triggers apoptotic/necrotic cell death.371

Docking to FLT3 supports a type I, DFG-in pose, with the benzimidazole anchoring the hinge Cys694, the acetamide forming a hydrogen bond with Asp829, and the piperidinylethoxy substituent oriented toward Cys695.371

Dual FLT3/TOPK inhibitors

TOPK is a serine/threonine kinase of the MAPKK family that regulates key processes in cell-cycle progression, mitotic fidelity, apoptosis, and tumor growth.372 In AML, TOPK is markedly overexpressed across multiple cell lines, and its inhibition leads to reduced proliferation and robust induction of apoptosis, supporting its role as a cooperating survival driver.373 Preclinical studies have shown that TOPK blockade can impair leukemic viability and prolong survival, establishing TOPK as a rational therapeutic target in hematologic malignancies.373 Given that FLT3-mutated AML frequently develops resistance through activation of parallel signaling pathways,374 dual inhibition strategies that suppress both FLT3-driven survival signals and TOPK-mediated proliferative mechanisms may achieve deeper pathway shutdown than selective FLT3 inhibition alone.

HSD-1169

HSD-1169 (Fig. 24) is a pyrazolo[4,3-a]phenanthridine-based dual FLT3/TOPK inhibitor that shows potent enzymatic inhibition, with IC50 values of 5 nM (FLT3-WT), 2 nM (FLT3-ITD), and 21 nM (TOPK).375 It retains high affinity against clinically relevant resistance mutations including, FLT3(D835Y) (Kd = 1.1 nM), FLT3(D835H) (Kd = 4.4 nM), FLT3(D835V) (Kd = 1 nM), and FLT3(ITD-F691L) (Kd = 1.2 nM) supporting its utility in overcoming FLT3-TKD-mediated therapeutic escape.375 In cellular assays, HSD-1169 selectively suppresses FLT3-dependent AML proliferation, inhibiting MV4-11, MOLM-14, and sorafenib-resistant MOLM-13-res (FLT3-ITD/D835Y) subline with IC50 values of 5.4 nM, 4.9 nM, 5.1 nM, respectively.375 Mechanistically, HSD-1169 downregulates FLT3 autophosphorylation and suppresses downstream STAT5 signaling, confirming on-target FLT3 pathway inhibition, while concurrently reducing TOPK mRNA and protein levels, consistent with dual-node blockade.375 These effects are accompanied by apoptotic responses, including PARP cleavage and Annexin-positive staining, across FLT3-ITD AML cells.375 Together, the biochemical potency, selective antiproliferative profile, and dual FLT3/TOPK mechanistic effects support the utility of HSD1169 in targeting FLT3-ITD AML, including disease harboring secondary FLT3-TKD mutations associated with resistance to current FLT3 inhibitors.

Dual FLT3/tubulin inhibitors

Cytoplasmic microtubules, composed of α/β-tubulin heterodimers, are essential for mitosis, intracellular trafficking, and cellular architecture; accordingly, microtubule-targeting agents (MTAs) remain among the most potent cytotoxic strategies in oncology and have demonstrated robust activity in AML models.376–378 In FLT3-mutant AML, the efficacy of selective tyrosine-kinase inhibitors is often curtailed by on-target TKD mutations (e.g., D835 variants, F691L) and adaptive persistence.2,24,27 Accordingly, concomitant inhibition of FLT3 signaling and microtubule dynamics is expected to deepen pathway suppression by coupling withdrawal of FLT3-dependent survival cues with mitotic catastrophe, as preclinically validated by distinct colchicine-site–binding chemotypes that also directly inhibit FLT3 (e.g.tirbanibulin and chalcone hybrids), each demonstrating reversal of TKD-mediated resistance in FLT3-mutant AML.379,380

For dual FLT3/tubulin designs, the colchicine site on β-tubulin is the practical choice because it accommodates compact, drug-like ligands; by contrast, the vinca and taxane sites typically require bulkier frameworks, which are less amenable to fusion with a kinase-recognition head and risk inflating size and lipophilicity.381 Within the colchicine-site pocket, a general pharmacophore (two hydrophobic/aromatic domains in a non-coplanar arrangement connected by a short linker capable of hydrogen bonding) has been widely validated across diverse scaffolds.381–383 While a 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl ring is frequently used, electron-rich bioisosteres (e.g., other substituted aryl/heteroaryl rings) can substitute without loss of affinity, providing latitude to merge a colchicine-site fragment with an FLT3 hinge-binding head when needed.384,385

Tirbanibulin (KX2-391)

Tirbanibulin is a dual FLT3/tubulin inhibitor (Fig. 24) that shows potency across FLT3-ITD and TKD-mutant contexts (including D835 variants and F691L), inhibits FLT3 autophosphorylation with downstream STAT5/MAPK suppression, and induces anti-proliferative/apoptotic effects in FLT3-mutant AML models.380 In AML cell panels, tirbanibulin exhibits low-nanomolar activity against engineered FLT3-mutant Ba/F3 cells including, Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD (IC50 = 19.5 nM), Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD-D835Y/V/F (IC50 = 27.3 nM, 10.6 nM, and 9.6 nM, respectively), Ba/F3-FLT3-ITD-F691L (IC50 = 37.1 nM), and FLT3-ITD lines including, MV4-11 and MOLM-13 (IC50 = 14.2 nM and 15.4 nM, respectively).380In vivo, oral tirbanibulin prolonged survival in an FLT3-ITD/F691L murine leukemia model, supporting the translational relevance of dual FLT3/tubulin targeting.380 A phase Ib, open-label, oral monotherapy study in elderly AML established 120 mg once daily as the maximum tolerated dose for tirbanibulin.386

Xu-8g

Xu-8g is a multitarget small molecule that combines potent FLT3 inhibition with antitubulin activity and displays pronounced cytotoxicity in AML models.387 In biochemical assays, it inhibited FLT3 (IC50 = 11.6 nM) and showed tight binding in KINOMEscan to FLT3 (Kd = 26 nM) with preserved affinity for FLT3-ITD (Kd = 34 nM) and FLT3-ITD/D835V (Kd = 33 nM), supporting engagement of clinically relevant variants.387 In parallel, Xu-8g inhibited tubulin polymerization with IC50 value of 1.94 µM.387 In MV-4-11 cells, it achieved an IC50 of 0.144 nM, and suppressed p-FLT3 and p-STAT5.387In vivo, Xu-8g produced antitumor activity in a leukemia xenograft at tolerated doses, but its exposure was limited by suboptimal pharmacokinetics, characterized by rapid clearance and poor oral performance, indicating that pharmacokinetics remains the primary liability at this stage.387

Docking into the FLT3 ATP-binding site placed the pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine core in the hinge region, forming two hydrogen bonds with Cys694 (Fig. 24), an additional hydrogen bond from the side-chain hydroxyl to Val675, and an NH–π contact with Phe691.387 The chemotype design supports dual activity, where the 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl motif supports colchicine-site antitubulin activity, while a hinge-addressing pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine core, functionalized at C-5 with heteroatom-containing substituents, provides kinase recognition. Consistently, SAR showed that attenuating either element (e.g., trimming the trimethoxyphenyl or altering the C-5 substituent/side-chain hydroxyl) diminishes cellular potency, in line with concurrent antitubulin and FLT3 pathway suppression.387

Faisal-4 (chalcone 4)

Faisal-4 is a chalcone-based dual FLT3/tubulin inhibitor (Fig. 24) identified by differential screening in FLT3-ITD-dependent leukemia models.379 It directly inhibited FLT3 in vitro with a weak IC50 value of 5.7 µM, blocked microtubule repolymerization in cells (complete at 5 µM), displaced colchicine (75–87% at 12.5–50 µM), and induced mitotic arrest and apoptosis.379 In AML panels, it showed selective sub-micromolar growth inhibition in MV-4-11 and MOLM-13 (GI50 = 0.24 µM and 0.22 µM, respectively), retained equipotent activity in MOLM-13-RES (FLT3-ITD/D835Y) (GI50 = 0.22 µM), and suppressed FLT3 signaling in MV-4-11 (reduced p-STAT5 and p-ERK), consistent with dual pathway engagement.379

Docking to FLT3 supports back-pocket hydrophobic engagement rather than hinge-Cys capture, with a π–σ contact to Val624, π–π stacking with Phe691 and Phe830, and a carbonyl–Lys644 hydrogen bond, indicating a non-hinge pharmacophore that addresses the hydrophobic cleft adjacent to the ATP site.379 For tubulin, the compound occupies the colchicine-binding site with one aromatic ring superposing the trimethoxyphenyl subpocket of colchicine.379

Dual FLT3/topoisomerase II inhibitors

Topoisomerase II (topo II) is an ATP-dependent nuclear enzyme required for DNA decatenation and chromosome segregation, with topo IIα being the proliferative isoform highly expressed in dividing hematopoietic blasts.388,389 In AML, topo II poisons (anthracyclines) remain foundational components of induction therapy and generate cytotoxic DNA double-strand breaks that drive apoptotic clearance of leukemic cells.388,389 Clinically, the contemporary standard for newly diagnosed, fit AML patients includes anthracycline–cytarabine (7 + 3) induction combined with a type I FLT3 inhibitor (e.g., midostaurin in the RATIFY regimen).390 This establishes routine, guideline-supported co-administration of topo II-directed DNA-damage pressure with FLT3 blockade, providing a clinically validated rationale for mechanistically integrating both activities into a single dual-target chemotype.

From a medicinal-chemistry perspective, topo II shares conserved ATP-binding architecture and structural features with other ATP-utilizing enzymes, including kinases.391 This structural homology enables the fusion of FLT3-recognition motifs (hinge/back-pocket binders) with topo II-addressing pharmacophores (planar intercalative/stacking systems or catalytic-site binders) within a single scaffold.389 Reported multitarget frameworks situate FLT3/topo II hybrids as a mechanistically coherent extension of the established clinical pairing of anthracyclines with FLT3 inhibition in AML.389

Symadex (C-1311)

Symadex (Fig. 24) is an imidazoacridinone with dual activity on FLT3 and topo II. In enzyme assays, it inhibits FLT3-WT and FLT3-D835Y with IC50 values of 8–15 nM, and shows direct but modest topo II inhibition (decatenation IC50 = 44 µM).392–394 In AML cells, it is cytotoxic to MV4-11 and MOLM-13 with IC50 values of 60 nM and 80 nM (24 h treatment), respectively; FLT3 knockdown in MV4-11 attenuates both cytotoxicity and apoptosis, supporting a direct FLT3-ITD contribution to activity.395

Kandeel-19

Kandeel-19 (Fig. 24) is a pseudo-pyrido-acridone that exhibits dual inhibition of FLT3 and topoisomerase II with comparable low-micromolar potencies (FLT3 IC50 = 3.22 µM, topo II IC50 = 6.26 µM) and shows antiproliferative activity in HL-60 leukemia cells (IC50 = 3.23 µM).396 Docking studies indicate a type I FLT3 pose in which the acridone carbonyl engages the hinge and hydrophobic contacts around Leu616 stabilize occupancy of the ATP site; the carbohydrazide spacer orients the terminal aryl toward a proximal pocket, supporting FLT3 recognition in this scaffold.396

Dual FLT3/VEGFR inhibitors

Vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR2) is a key mediator of angiogenic signaling within the bone-marrow microenvironment, where VEGF secretion by AML blasts and stromal cells establishes autocrine and paracrine loops that promote leukemic survival, chemoresistance, and niche-dependent protection.398,399 In FLT3-mutated AML, VEGF/VEGFR2 signaling promotes leukemic proliferation, survival, and chemotherapy resistance, and its inhibition suppresses AML cell growth in vitro and in xenograft models.399,400 These findings provide a biologically coherent rationale for the development of dual FLT3/VEGFR2 inhibitors, designed to simultaneously target leukemic proliferation and the angiogenic cues that sustain FLT3-mutant AML.

Semaxanib (SU5416)

Semaxanib is an indolinone multi-kinase inhibitor (Fig. 25) originally developed as an anti-VEGFR agent and subsequently shown to inhibit FLT3 (wild-type and mutant) and KIT, providing a pharmacologic basis to target leukemic signaling together with the angiogenic microenvironment in AML.401,402 In FLT3-driven systems, semaxanib inhibited FLT3 autophosphorylation in cellular assays with an IC50 value of around 100 nM, suppressed downstream MAPK and STAT5 signaling, reduced proliferation of Ba/F3 ITD-mutant FLT3 cell line (IC50 ≈ 250 nM), and induced apoptosis.403 Clinically, single-agent activity was modest in refractory AML, with few objective responses, and development was discontinued owing to limited efficacy.404,405

SU14813

SU14813 (Fig. 25) is a multitargeted indolinone inhibitor that inhibits VEGFR2- and FLT3-dependant phosphorylation in cellular and in vivo models, with cellular IC50 of 40 nM and 50 nM, respectively.406 In multiple xenograft models, driven by FLT3, VEGFR, PDGFR, or KIT, SU14813 induced marked tumor growth inhibition, ranging from near-complete growth arrest to overt regression, together with substantial anti-angiogenic effects.406

Yang-33

Yang-33 (Fig. 25) is a pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine-based dual FLT3/VEGFR2 inhibitor with IC50 values of 39 nM and 12 nM, respectively.407 It showed selective nanomolar antiproliferative activity in FLT3-ITD MV4-11 cells (GI50 = 4 nM), suppressed phosphorylation of FLT3, STAT5, and ERK1/2 and induced concentration-dependent apoptosis.407 In FLT3-ITD xenografts, daily oral administration at 10 mg kg−1 induced complete tumor regression, while 3 mg kg−1 stabilized growth with no observed toxicity.407 Preliminary pharmacokinetics indicated good oral exposure, plasma half-life of 12.6 h, and 67% bioavailability.407

He-17b

Qi-4f

Qi-4f is a 1,3-thiazinan-4-one urea derivative identified as a dual FLT3/VEGFR2 inhibitor with IC50 values of 6.9 nM and 14.6 nM, respectively.409 In MV4-11 cells, it showed potent antiproliferative activity with IC50 value 12.1 nM.409

Dual FLT3/Wnt/β-catenin inhibitors

Wnt/β-catenin signaling is a conserved pathway that regulates development, stem-cell homeostasis, and tissue maintenance.410 In AML, β-catenin is required for the self-renewal and maintenance of leukemia stem cells (LSCs) while being dispensable for adult hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), providing a selective therapeutic vulnerability.411 Aberrant β-catenin activity contributes to chemoresistance, survival of stem-like leukemia populations, and relapse, and is implicated across multiple leukemias, including AML, ALL, CML, and CLL.412,413

In FLT3-mutant AML models, pharmacologic disruption of β-catenin (C-82) combined with FLT3 inhibitors (sorafenib or quizartinib) produced synergistic antileukemic activity in primary samples and xenografts, decreased nuclear β-catenin and c-MYC, abrogated stromal protection, and improved survival versus either agent alone.414 Collectively, these data support a dual FLT3/Wnt strategy that reduces FLT3-dependent blast burden and eliminates β-catenin-dependent LSCs, thereby limiting resistance and relapse.414

SKLB-677

SKLB-677 (Fig. 25) is a pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine-based dual-pathway agent that combines potent FLT3 inhibition with suppression of Wnt/β-catenin signaling.415 In biochemical assays, SKLB-677 exhibits strong affinity for FLT3, with Kd values of 0.74 nM for FLT3-WT and FLT3-D835H, and 1.3 nM for FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y, demonstrating preserved target engagement across clinically relevant activation-loop mutations that often compromise type II FLT3 inhibitors.415 In FLT3-driven AML cell lines, SKLB-677 displays exceptional potency, inhibiting MV4-11 and MOLM-13 with IC50 values of 0.079 nM and 0.116 nM, respectively, reflecting strong FLT3 pathway suppression together with reduced β-catenin activity and marked depletion of LSC and leukemia-initiating cell (LIC) populations.415

Although SKLB-677 clearly downregulates Wnt/β-catenin signaling, no specific molecular target within the pathway has been identified, and its Wnt-related effects likely reflect broader pathway inhibition rather than engagement of a defined Wnt component.415In vivo, SKLB-677 exhibits robust oral antitumor activity, inducing marked tumor growth inhibition in FLT3-driven xenograft models and demonstrating favorable pharmacokinetic properties, supporting its progression as a preclinical lead.415

Multi-kinases inhibitors

Foretinib (XL880)

Foretinib is a multikinase inhibitor targeting MET, AXL, and VEGFR2, yet its therapeutic activity in AML is predominantly driven by direct inhibition of FLT3.152 It binds FLT3 and suppresses receptor autophosphorylation and downstream signaling, demonstrating lower IC50 values in FLT3-ITD patient samples than several clinically approved FLT3 inhibitors, underscoring its strong potency in primary AML contexts.418 In FLT3-ITD AML cell lines, foretinib displays sub-nanomolar activity, inhibiting MV4-11 and MOLM-13 with IC50 values of 0.16 nM and 0.89 nM, respectively, and it induces apoptosis in both cell lines and primary FLT3-ITD AML blasts.418Foretinib also preserves activity against secondary FLT3-ITD mutations, as indicated by its activity against Ba/F3 FLT3-ITD-D835Y/V (IC50 = 4 nM and 3.59 nM, respectively), Ba/F3 FLT3-ITD-N767K (IC50 = 7.92 nM), and Ba/F3 FLT3-ITD-Y842C (IC50 = 3.17 nM); importantly, it also remains effective against FLT3-ITD-F691L, which is frequently associated with clinical resistance to quizartinib and gilteritinib.418

Cabozantinib (XL184)

Cabozantinib (Fig. 26), a structural analogue of foretinib, is a multikinase inhibitor with potent activity against MET, VEGFR2, KIT, RET, AXL, ROS1, and FLT3.152,419 It inhibits FLT3 with an IC50 of 11.3 nM, while maintaining sub-nanomolar potency on VEGFR2 and low-nanomolar activity on other oncogenic kinases.420 In FLT3-ITD AML cell lines, it exhibits strong cytotoxicity, suppressing proliferation of MV4-11 and MOLM-13 cells with IC50 values of 2.4 nM and 2.0 nM, respectively, whereas FLT3-WT leukemia cell lines remain resistant (IC50 > 2 µM), indicating selective dependence on FLT3-ITD signaling.421 Mechanistic studies show that cabozantinib effectively blocks FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream STAT5, AKT, and ERK signaling cascades, leading to G0/G1 arrest with reduced cyclin E and survivin, and restoration of pro-apoptotic markers such as p27 and Bak.421In vivo, daily oral treatment results in significant tumor growth inhibition in MV4-11 and MOLM-13 xenografts and prolongs survival, supporting its preclinical activity against FLT3-ITD AML.421Cabozantinib is FDA-approved for several solid tumours, and a Phase I clinical trial for relapsed/refractory AML (NCT01961765) has been completed, with no subsequent AML-directed clinical expansion reported to date.421

Sitravatinib (MGCD516)

Sitravatinib is a multikinase inhibitor with nanomolar activity against several oncogenic RTKs, including AXL, MET, KIT, PDGFR, and VEGFR family of kinases as identified in its initial biochemical characterization.422 Beyond these targets, sitravatinib was shown to potently inhibit FLT3 with IC50 value of 8 nM.422 In FLT3-ITD AML models, it exhibits strong cellular potency, inhibiting MV4-11 and MOLM-13 proliferation with IC50 values of 0.556 nM and 1.511 nM, respectively, retains activity against the F691L mutation (BaF3-FLT3-ITD-F691L IC50 = 4.75 nM), whereas its potency is substantially diminished against FLT3-TKD mutations such as D835Y/V/F.423Sitravatinib suppresses FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream ERK/AKT signaling, including under cytokine-induced protective conditions, supporting its ability to overcome both adaptive and mutation-driven resistance.423

Docking studies indicate an ATP-site binding mode involving interactions with Cys695, Asp698, and Asp829, along with a halogen contact with Val615, consistent with high-affinity engagement of the active FLT3 conformation (Fig. 26).423In vivo, sitravatinib outperforms gilteritinib in FLT3-ITD and FLT3-F691L xenograft models, producing superior pathway suppression and antitumor effects, reinforcing its preclinical potential as a multikinase-derived agent with strong FLT3 inhibitory activity.423

Merestinib (LY2801653)

Merestinib (Fig. 26) is a type II ATP-competitive multikinase inhibitor originally developed as a potent MET inhibitor and later shown to target RON/MST1R, FLT3, AXL, MERTK, TEK, ROS1, DDR1/2, and MKNK1/2.159,424 In FLT3-ITD+ AML models, merestinib potently inhibits MV4-11 cells with an IC50 value of 4 nM.424 Mechanistic studies show that merestinib suppresses Mnk1/2-mediated eIF4E phosphorylation and inhibits leukemic progenitor growth, leading to significant antileukemic effects in AML xenografts.159 A Phase I study evaluating merestinib in combination with the FGFR inhibitor LY2874455 in relapsed/refractory AML has been completed, with no subsequent AML-directed development reported (NCT03125239).

Chern-36

Chern-36 (Fig. 26) is a multitargeted inhibitor displaying low-nanomolar activity against FLT3, KDR, VEGFR3, and c-KIT, with IC50 values of 2.3 nM, <1 nM, 22.4 nM, and 5.1 nM, respectively.425 In AML models, it shows potent antiproliferative effects, inhibiting MV4-11 cells with an IC50 value of 28 nM and maintaining selective cytotoxicity toward FLT3-driven leukemia lines compared with solid tumor cells.425 It also suppresses cellular kinase phosphorylation, inhibiting FLT3, c-KIT, and KDR autophosphorylation with IC50 values below 10 nM.425In vivo, once-daily oral dosing of Chern-36 (20 mg kg−1) produced significant antileukemic activity in MV4-11 xenografts, halting tumor growth without treatment-related mortality.425 Pharmacokinetic evaluation showed low clearance, a half-life of 1.1 h, and low oral bioavailability (2.2%) following a single administration in rats.425

Dual type I/II inhibitors

FLT3 inhibitor performance is tightly linked to the conformational state of the kinase, and recurrent TKD mutations frequently shift the equilibrium between the active DFG-in and inactive DFG-out states.252 As outlined earlier in this review, D835 activation-loop substitutions stabilize the active conformation and restrict access to the DFG-out pocket required for type II inhibitors, while the gatekeeper F691L remodels the hydrophobic back pocket and weakens type I binding.429,430 These conformational shifts narrow the structural window in which single-mode agents can maintain potent FLT3 inhibition, contributing to loss of activity and incomplete pathway suppression in AML.252

Across the broader kinase field (including FLT3 structural modeling and mutational contexts), analyses show that ligands able to accommodate both DFG-in and DFG-out conformations display greater resilience to activation-loop rearrangement, back-pocket remodeling, and mutation-driven conformational drift.104,431 Applied to FLT3, the dual type I/II approach aims to sustain target engagement regardless of DFG orientation or activation-loop position, broadening coverage across ITD and TKD variants (including D835 and F691L) and improving the likelihood of continuous suppression of FLT3-driven signaling in AML.104

Marbotinib

Marbotinib is a next-generation FLT3 inhibitor derived from a hybrid scaffold that merges structural features of the type II inhibitor quizartinib with type I bisarylmethanone derivatives.432 This strategy yielded a compound with comparable potency to quizartinib against FLT3-ITD, achieving an IC50 of 2 nM in MV4-11 cells.432 In biochemical assays, marbotinib inhibits FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y with IC50 values of 2.3 nM and 4.84 nM, respectively.104 Functionally, marbotinib blocks FLT3-ITD autophosphorylation and suppresses downstream STAT5 and ERK signaling, leading to cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis consistent with on-target FLT3 inhibition.432 Across leukemic panels, it shows high functional selectivity for FLT3-dependent cells, where it robustly inhibits proliferation in FLT3-ITD+ MV4-11 and MOLM-13 cells, while sparing FLT3-WT lines, FLT3-null controls, primary PBMCs, and c-KIT-dependent mast cells.432

A key advantage is the preserved activity against therapy-emergent TKD mutations, including FLT3-ITD/D835Y and the compound mutant FLT3-D835Y/N676K, both of which confers strong resistance to approved FLT3 inhibitors.432 Its ability to maintain potency across these variants is attributed to the structural flexibility of its hybrid scaffold, which enables productive binding orientations compatible with both the DFG-in (active) and DFG-out (inactive) conformations of FLT3.432In vivo, a water-soluble marbotinib-carbamate derivative demonstrates significant antileukemic efficacy and prolongs survival in FLT3-ITD MV4-11 xenograft models without apparent toxicity.432 Overall, marbotinib provides a potent, selective, and mutation-resilient FLT3 profile, representing a promising preclinical example of a hybrid chemotype with emergent dual type I/II characteristics.

Molecular modeling indicates that marbotinib can accommodate both active and inactive FLT3 conformations through two opposing poses that arise from its nearly symmetrical bisarylmethanone architecture (Fig. 27A and B). In the inactive (DFG-out) state, marbotinib engages the ATP-binding site and extends into the adjacent allosteric back pocket in a type II-like orientation. The methanone carbonyl forms a hydrogen bond with the hinge Cys694, the urea establishes a second hydrogen bond with Glu661, and the tert-butyl-isoxazole tail occupies the allosteric back pocket in a mode characteristic of type II FLT3 inhibitors. Favorable van der Waals contacts are formed by the bisarylmethanone core within the adenine pocket.432 In the active (DFG-in) state, marbotinib rotates by a 180° to maintain hinge anchoring while reconfiguring its peripheral contacts, where the NH engages Cys694, the indole-OH forms a hydrogen bond with Asp829 in the catalytic loop, and the tert-butyl–isoxazole repositions toward Asn701 near the pocket entrance rather than penetrating the back pocket.432

Mahboobi-29 and its carbamate derivative, Mahboobi-42

Mahboobi-29 and its carbamate analogue, Mahboobi-42, belong to a bisarylmethanone-based series of FLT3 inhibitors developed to enhance potency, solubility, and conformational adaptability.104 Their TFA salts (29b, 42b) exhibit potent biochemical inhibition of FLT3-ITD and FLT3-D835Y, with IC50 values of 2.4 nM and 3.83 nM for Mahboobi-29b and 0.81 nM and 0.62 nM for Mahboobi-42b, respectively.104 In cellular assays, both display sub-nanomolar antiproliferative activity in MV4-11 andMOLM-13 cells (Mahboobi-29b: IC50 = 0.07 nM and 0.20 nM, respectively; Mahboobi-42b: IC50 = 0.48 nM and 0.67 nM, respectively).104 Selectivity testing in non-FLT3-driven cell lines demonstrates that the HCl salt (Mahboobi-29c) produces no cytotoxicity up to 100 nM in hepatocellular, lung cancer, and endothelial cells, indicating preservation of functional selectivity toward FLT3-dependent signaling.104 The HCl salts (Mahboobi-29c and Mahboobi-42c) maintain activity across FLT3-ITD, FLT3-D835Y, FLT3-ITD/D835Y, and FLT3-ITD/N676K mutations in Ba/F3 models, with Mahboobi-29c exhibiting IC50 values in the range of 0.29–11.69 nM and Mahboobi-42c exhibiting IC50 values in the range of 3.34–87.39 nM.104

Mechanistic studies confirm that Mahboobi-29c results in concentration-dependent suppression of phosphorylated FLT3 along with inhibition of downstream STAT5, MAPK, and AKT signaling in FLT3-ITD Ba/F3 cells, consistent with on-target blockade of mutant FLT3.104 For in vivo studies, only the HCl salts were advanced due to superior aqueous solubility.104Mahboobi-29c demonstrates good tolerability in mice up to 75 mg kg−1 and produces marked antileukemic efficacy in MV4-11 xenografts, with strong tumor-growth suppression and extended survival without treatment-related mortality.104 Its carbamate derivative, Mahboobi-42c, while potent in vitro, showed lower tolerability and was therefore not evaluated in efficacy studies.104 Overall, Mahboobi-29 and its carbamate derivative represent potent, mutation-resilient, and selectively optimized FLT3 inhibitors whose salt-dependent solubility and conformational flexibility support their performance across biochemical, cellular, and in vivo systems.

Molecular modeling supports dual type I/II binding. In the inactive, DFG-out state, the ligands adopt a quizartinib-like type II pose in which the methanone carbonyl anchors to the hunge Cys694, the urea moiety interacts with Glu661, and the tert-butyl-isoxazole group occupies the hydrophobic back pocket. π–π stacking with Phe691 and extensive van der Waals contacts stabilize this orientation (Fig. 27C and E).104 In the active, DFG-in conformation, the ligands rotate into an inverse pose (Fig. 27D and E), with the benzofuran core aligning at the hinge region and the terminal amine or carbamate substituents repositioning toward solvent-exposed regions. This reorientation accommodates the altered activation loop geometry and explains the preserved activity of both Mahboobi-29 and Mahboobi-42 against D835Y and N676K.104 The carbamate derivative exhibits slightly deeper complementarity in the back pocket, imparting a modest type II bias.104

Comparative analysis of dual target FLT3 inhibitors: binding modes, mutation coverage, and developmental status

The comparative analysis presented in (Table 3) reveals that managing resistance in FLT3-mutated AML cannot be effectively solved with a single universal strategy, due to limitations within FLT3 signaling and acquired mutations. Current data suggests that dual-target FLT3 inhibitors represent the most clinically advanced successful strategy compared with dual type I/II binders or highly selective, single-mode FLT3 inhibitors. The majority of compounds which have successfully progressed into clinical trials belong to the dual FLT3/kinase or multikinase categories, likely because simultaneous blockade of FLT3 and compensatory survival pathways (such as AXL, JAK2, PIM, SYK, MEK, IRAK, or Aurora kinases) provide broader suppression of adaptive resistance. In contrast, many preclinical compounds remain limited to potent biochemical inhibition without demonstrating durable in vivo efficacy, pharmacokinetic stability, acceptable selectivity, or safety profiles sufficient for clinical translation. Clinically advanced compounds (such as dubermatinib, luxeptinib, dapolsertib, ETH-155008, cabozantinib, fedratinib, and emavusertib) generally exhibit broad mutation coverage together with pathway-level suppression, whereas numerous preclinical candidates mainly demonstrate enzymatic potency against isolated FLT3 mutants without comprehensive resistance control or toxicity characterization.

Comparative analysis of dual-target FLT3 inhibitors: binding modes, mutation coverage, and developmental status

This preferential enrichment of certain dual-target combinations, particularly FLT3/AXL, FLT3/JAK2, FLT3/PIM, FLT3/SYK, and FLT3/MEK inhibitors, likely reflects the central role of these signaling nodes in mediating adaptive resistance, cell survival, and microenvironment-driven escape pathways. Interestingly, the majority of dual-target inhibitors appear to behave mechanistically as type I-like binders rather than type II inhibitors because of the superior ability of type I inhibitors to retain activity against activation-loop TKD mutations such as D835 variants with targeting FLT3-ITD as well. Conversely, selective type II inhibitors often maintain strong potency against FLT3-ITD over TKD resistance, particularly D835, thereby limiting their long-term therapeutic durability. Nevertheless, selective type II inhibitors may still be advantageous in patients with isolated FLT3-ITD disease because of their high selectivity and potentially lower off-target toxicity. Pure type I inhibitors provide broader TKD mutants coverage but may suffer from insufficient suppression of bypass signaling and emergence of compound mutations such as ITD/F691L. Accordingly, dual type I/II conformational strategies theoretically offer an attractive compromise by targeting both active and inactive kinase states simultaneously, although this approach remains largely preclinical and has not yet demonstrated clear clinical superiority. The limited clinical emergence of true dual type I/II inhibitors may result from the considerable structural challenge of achieving balanced affinity for both conformations while maintaining favorable pharmacokinetics and kinase selectivity.

Moreover, the mutation coverage pattern across the table indicates that FLT3-ITD remains the most consistently targeted alteration across nearly all therapeutic strategies because it represents the dominant oncogenic driver and the most frequent FLT3 abnormality in AML. However, secondary TKD mutations, particularly D835Y/V/H and the gatekeeper mutation F691L, emerge as the major resistance-associated mutations during treatment and therefore constitute the principal challenge for next-generation inhibitor design. Notably, type II inhibitors generally show reduced activity against D835 substitutions due to disruption of the inactive DFG-out conformation, whereas many type I and dual-target inhibitors retain partial or substantial activity against these mutants. In contrast, F691L remains one of the most difficult resistance mutations to suppress effectively because it induces steric and conformational alterations that impair inhibitor binding across multiple inhibitor classes, explaining why compounds capable of targeting both D835 and F691L mutations simultaneously are considered particularly valuable in resistant AML settings.

Importantly, the overall partial failure of dual-target strategies can be attributed to several factors, including excessive polypharmacology toxicity, inadequate selectivity, insufficient activity against compound resistance mutations, poor pharmacokinetic exposure, and the biological heterogeneity of AML clones that allows rapid activation of alternative survival pathways. Consequently, no single strategy appears universally optimal. Selective type I inhibitors may be preferable in TKD-dominant resistance, whereas selective type II inhibitors may remain useful for highly FLT3-dependent ITD disease. Dual-target inhibitors are likely more appropriate in relapsed or molecularly heterogeneous AML characterized by adaptive bypass signaling. Meanwhile, PROTAC-based FLT3 degraders may offer a future advantage by eliminating both catalytic and scaffold functions of FLT3 and potentially overcoming kinase-domain mutation dependence altogether, although clinical evidence remains immature. Finally, duality therapy probably represents the biologically rational long-term strategy, because AML resistance is increasingly recognized as a multifactorial and dynamic evolutionary process rather than a single kinase-binding problem.

Combination therapy

Despite major advances in FLT3-targeted therapy, resistance remains the principal barrier to durable remission in both monotherapy and combination settings.216 Primary resistance, although relatively uncommon in FLT3-mutated AML, can attenuate therapeutic response from the outset,216,437 whereas secondary resistance, emerging during treatment, is far more prevalent and often contributes to relapsed or refractory disease.216,437 These mechanisms encompass both FLT3-dependent and FLT3-independent pathways, each capable of undermining clinical efficacy.216 Recent translational and clinical studies reinforce this distinction and highlight the need to complement FLT3-directed therapy with agents that target additional survival pathways contributing to resistance.

Approaches to overcome resistance broadly include the development of next-generation FLT3 inhibitors and dual-target agents, as well as rational combination strategies integrating FLT3 blockade with inhibitors of dysregulated signaling pathways, anti-apoptotic proteins, epigenetic regulators, or other oncogenic drivers.51,216 Although the clinical utility of agents such as quizartinib and gilteritinib can be limited by resistance and toxicity, combining FLT3 inhibitors with other targeted therapies has demonstrated the ability to maintain efficacy while improving safety profiles and lower-dose combinations can further enhance tolerability without compromising therapeutic activity.438 While multi-agent combinations allow simultaneous targeting of several oncogenic pathways, they also introduce challenges related to pharmacokinetic interactions, safety, and treatment complexity.439 Dual-target inhibitors can mitigate some of these challenges by integrating multiple mechanisms into a single molecule, thereby simplifying administration while preserving multi-pathway engagement.439

Examples include synergy between the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib and the PI3K inhibitor copanlisib, which strengthens growth suppression in AML models and illustrates how convergent pathway targeting can counteract resistance while allowing dose rationalization.438 Parallel strategies have explored pairing FLT3 inhibitors with BET inhibitors, yielding enhanced suppression of FLT3-ITD AML and supporting the concept that co-targeting transcriptional and kinase programs can deepen responses.171 In FLT3-mutated models with gilteritinib resistance, the multi-targeted FLT3 inhibitor sitravatinib shows improved activity when combined with the BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax, producing greater reductions in cell viability and increased apoptosis relative to either monotherapy.440 Similarly, combinations of midostaurin or gilteritinib with the BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax promote concurrent suppression of MCL-1 and BCL-2, thereby augmenting apoptosis and mitigating pro-survival signaling.24 Co-targeting the nuclear export protein (XPO1) alongside FLT3 has also produced synergistic anti-leukemic activity, enhancing apoptosis and differentiation and contributing to the reversal of resistance in FLT3-mutated AML.441 Clinically, triplet regimens combining hypomethylating agents, venetoclax, and FLT3 inhibitors have shown high rates of measurable residual disease-negative remission in both newly diagnosed and relapsed FLT3-mutant AML, offering promising outcomes for patients who respond suboptimally to HMA/venetoclax alone.442 These triplet combinations are increasingly viewed as rational therapeutic options for older or unfit AML patients. Ongoing clinical trials evaluating gilteritinib with venetoclax and azacitidine (NCT05520567) further demonstrate the therapeutic potential of multi-agent regimens for newly diagnosed FLT3-mutant AML.443

Within current practice, combination therapy has become central to AML management, particularly for younger patients and in induction settings. The most widely adopted approach remains conventional chemotherapy or hypomethylating agents paired with midostaurin, while multiple trials continue to evaluate selective FLT3 inhibitors in combination with chemotherapy, HMAs, or additional targeted agents acting on non-FLT3 pathways.27 The emerging evidence base suggests that future treatment paradigms will increasingly integrate rational doublet or triplet strategies tailored to disease biology, with the dual aims of prolonging response and deferring resistance while maintaining a manageable safety profile.

PROTACs as an emerging strategy for FLT3 degradation

The sustained challenge associated with current FLT3 inhibitors, particularly the emergence of secondary resistance driven by on-target point mutations, kinase overexpression, or adaptive signaling, has accelerated the exploration of strategies that operate independently of classical occupancy-driven inhibition. Among these, proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) have emerged as a promising next-generation modality, designed not to transiently inhibit FLT3 catalytic activity but to eliminate the protein altogether through catalytic, event-driven degradation.444,445

PROTACs are heterobifunctional molecules comprising a ligand that binds the protein of interest (e.g., FLT3), a recruiter that engages an E3 ubiquitin ligase such as CRBN, VHL, MDM2, or cIAP, and a linker that spatially orients both moieties to favor productive ternary complex formation (Fig. 28).444,446 FLT3 inhibitors including quizartinib, dovitinib, and gilteritinib have been repurposed as warheads in FLT3-directed PROTAC designs, capitalizing on their well-characterized binding modes.394 The linker (its length, flexibility, polarity, and topology) critically dictates ternary complex stability, cellular uptake, and metabolic fate.444,446

Diagrammatic representation of PROTAC molecules with illustration of its mechanism of action.

Mechanistically, the PROTAC molecule simultaneously binds FLT3 and the E3 ligase, enabling ubiquitination of the kinase. The ubiquitinated FLT3 is subsequently recognized by the 26S proteasome and degraded, resulting in sustained depletion of both wild-type and mutant FLT3 from the cell.444 This catalytic mechanism allows PROTACs to exert potent effects at low (often sub-stoichiometric) concentrations and can circumvent resistance mechanisms associated with reversible inhibitors, such as increased FLT3 expression or mutations that reduce inhibitor residence time.444,445

Recent reports demonstrate the feasibility of FLT3-directed PROTACs as an emerging AML therapeutic class. Wang et al. designed PROTACs capable of degrading FLT3 and suppressing downstream STAT5 activation, with efficacy retained against FLT3-ITD and several clinically relevant TKD mutants.445 Hesham et al. provided further analysis, summarizing multiple FLT3-PROTAC chemotypes and highlighting their ability to overcome resistance to conventional inhibitors in preclinical AML models.444 Together, these studies support the translational promise of targeted FLT3 degradation as a strategy to address resistance that cannot be overcome by warhead re-engineering alone.

Despite their potential, several challenges continue to limit clinical translation. PROTACs typically possess high molecular weights and substantial polar surface area, complicating oral bioavailability, intracellular delivery, and metabolic stability.447,448 The selection of an appropriate E3 ligase recruiter is often constrained by tissue distribution, endogenous substrate load, or unexpected toxicities arising from degradation of native E3 targets.447 Another limitation is that, although PROTACs can bind certain tubulin-related targets, no PROTAC capable of degrading tubulin itself has been reported, and only limited degradation of associated proteins has been achieved.447 Continued optimization of linker architecture, E3 ligase choice, and physicochemical properties will be essential for clinical advancement.

Nevertheless, PROTACs offer several advantages over classical inhibitors, including durability of response, activity at sub-stoichiometric concentrations, the ability to target proteins previously considered “undruggable”, and potentially reduced susceptibility to common resistance mutations.447,449 Their modularity enables rapid development once a suitable warhead and E3 ligand pair is established. As rational design strategies advance and FLT3 degrader scaffolds improve, PROTACs may ultimately complement or surpass current generations of FLT3 inhibitors in AML, particularly in resistant or relapsed disease.

Comparative medicinal chemistry analysis of reported FLT3-targeting PROTACs

Analysis of the reported FLT3-PROTACs highlights the critical contribution of the three fundamental PROTAC components, including the warhead, linker, and E3 ligase anchor (Fig. 29), where their precise combination plays a decisive role in determining efficiency, while even minor structural modifications may markedly diminish or abolish activity.444

Representative examples for common anchors, linkers and warheads used in the reported FLT3-PROTACs design. Anchors are represented in red; linkers are represented in black, while warheads are represented in blue in addition to purple arrows which illustrate the position of attaching.

Based on different anchor analysis, FLT3-PROTACs predominantly utilize CRBN ligands (thalidomide, lenalidomide, pomalidomide), VHL ligand (VH032), or cIAP ligand (LCL161) as E3 recruiters. Overall, CRBN-based degraders generally exhibit higher potency than VHL or cIAP-based counterparts, which may be attributed to higher CRBN expression in AML cells and its dominant use in clinically advanced PROTACs. In some cases, VHL-based PROTACs can provide favorable activity depending on the overall PROTAC design context. Within CRBN ligands, lenalidomide and pomalidomide typically confer stronger antiproliferative and degradation effects than thalidomide, consistent with their higher binding affinity and more efficient CRBN-mediated complex formation.444

Regarding linker analysis, FLT3-PROTACs exhibit substantial diversity in linker design, with optimization generally relying on iterative evaluation of linker type, length, and tethering position. Overall, rigid heterocyclic linkers demonstrated superior potency. Piperazine–piperidine linker showed enhanced pharmacokinetic properties and improved bioavailability compared with other flexible linear linkers. Additionally, highly potent degraders containing polyethylene glycol (PEG) linkers have also been reported. Linker length critically influences ternary complex formation, where excessively long linkers may induce a hook effect that reduces FLT3 degradation efficiency. However, no universal preference for shorter or longer linkers has been established, as activity depends on the overall PROTAC architecture. Additionally, the linker attachment site on both the E3 ligase recruiter and FLT3 warhead markedly affects degradation potency, highlighting the importance of proper tethering point selection and binding mode optimization.444

Concerning the warhead-based analysis, selection of an appropriate FLT3 inhibitor warhead is critical for the design of effective FLT3-targeting PROTACs. The chosen warhead should possess strong anti-AML activity, favorable pharmacokinetic properties, and high selectivity toward FLT3 to minimize off-target toxicity and bone marrow suppression associated with multikinase inhibition. Nevertheless, multikinase inhibitor-based warheads may provide advantages in overcoming drug resistance in AML. Among the reported designs, gilteritinib-based warheads combined with CRBN recruiters, particularly lenalidomide, produced some of the most potent FLT3 degraders.444

Future perspectives

The need for effective AML therapies that combine high efficacy with minimal cytotoxicity remains urgent.2 Dual-target inhibitors have gained attention as a rational strategy to address resistance associated with single-agent therapies. By modulating two or more interconnected signaling nodes, dual inhibitors can blunt compensatory activation and improve response durability in FLT3-driven disease.2 In preclinical models, many dual-target designs outperform single-kinase agents, often achieving additive or synergistic effects.2,450 Nevertheless, despite promising signals in early clinical development, hurdles remain, primarily toxicity management, selectivity, pharmacokinetic constraints, and incomplete characterization of long-term adverse effects.2 As clinical datasets expand, dual-target agents will require more rigorous, mechanism-driven evaluation to identify target pairs that deliver genuine therapeutic advantage in AML.

To guide that evaluation, a broader trend across targeted therapy is the shift from incidental multikinase activity to rational, structure-guided polypharmacology, in which target pairs are chosen for mechanistic complementarity rather than breadth alone.451 This design philosophy is increasingly supported by network-pharmacology perspectives and contemporary surveys of multi-target agents, which emphasize principled selection and property discipline over promiscuity.452,453 In parallel, predictive frameworks, including deep-learning target prediction, network-level synergy modeling, and morphology/gene-expression surrogates, are beginning to prioritize synergistic pairs before synthesis, reducing reliance on empirical screening.452,454,455

Looking beyond inhibition, modalities are converging. Reviews across targeted protein degradation and covalent chemistry anticipate hybrid strategies, dual-target degraders, degraders aimed at bypass nodes, and covalent–multitarget hybrids, extending durability where inhibition alone falls short.456–458 For medicinal chemists, these directions motivate design spaces that integrate orthogonal mechanisms within a single scaffold while maintaining tractable physicochemical profiles.

Finally, while traditional drug combinations remain a benchmark strategy, they can introduce divergent PK, metabolism, and drug–drug interactions.450 In contrast, a well-engineered single entity that coordinates multiple targets offers integrated pharmacology and simplifies regimens, provided its design addresses the liabilities above. Meta-analyses and clinical-trial mapping across oncology consistently show that combinations improve response and survival but at the cost of increased toxicity, underscoring why mechanism-driven multitarget designs are attractive when they can deliver coordinated pathway control without accumulating off-target burden.459,460

Conclusion

Despite advances with first- and second-generation FLT3 inhibitors, acute myeloid leukemia remains difficult to control because on-target mutations and pathway adaptation erode response durability. Gatekeeper F691 substitutions disrupt back-pocket interactions, D835 activation-loop variants bias FLT3 toward the active state, and hinge-adjacent changes such as N676 and N701 penalize classical hinge geometries; together they explain much of the loss of efficacy seen with established scaffolds. Within this context, type I inhibitors preserve activity when the kinase favors the DFG-in state, type II inhibitors are effective where an inactive pocket remains accessible, and irreversible covalent designs can restore engagement when local cysteines are suitably positioned.

The accumulated evidence also supports dual-modality strategies when resistance is driven less by binding failure than by compensatory signaling. Chemotypes that combine FLT3 inhibition with a second, biologically validated target, such as AXL or MERTK at the receptor level, IRAK or JAK2 for STAT5 rescue, or Aurora and HDAC for proliferative and epigenetic adaptation, show preclinical activity, including against F691L and D835. Translation will require careful management of selectivity, pharmacokinetics, and tolerability, but these data indicate that well-chosen dual targets can delay resistance relative to single-target agents.

The developments summarized in this review illustrate how FLT3-directed therapy has broadened beyond classical type I and type II inhibitors to include irreversible covalent inhibitors, rational combination strategies, PROTAC-based degradation, and a wide range of dual-modality approaches that address recurrent resistance pathways. These complementary modalities demonstrate how different mechanisms can compensate for one another, whether by restoring kinase engagement, suppressing adaptive signaling, or promoting FLT3 depletion. As these strategies continue to evolve, integrating structural insight with careful pharmacological evaluation will be critical to translating them into durable clinical benefit. Together, they outline a realistic and evolving path toward more robust FLT3-targeted therapies capable of addressing the persistent challenges of high-risk AML.

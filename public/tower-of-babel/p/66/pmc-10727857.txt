Tuberculous Lymphadenitis in a Patient Treated with Dupilumab: A Case Report
Authors: Dong Heon Lee, Narang Hong, Hyung Don Kook, Hye Jung Jung, Mi Yeon Park, Jiyoung Ahn
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Tuberculous lymphadenitis is among the most frequent presentations of extrapulmonary tuberculosis; the most common presentation is isolated chronic non-tender lymphadenopathy in young adults without systemic symptoms. Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody directed against interleukin-4 receptor-α that blocks the synergistic effects of interleukin-4 and interleukin-13 on allergic inflammation. Its well-known adverse events are allergic conjunctivitis, injection site reaction, and dupilumab facial redness. A 32-year-old female with severe atopic dermatitis was treated with dupilumab for 2 months at our clinic. She complained of multiple enlarged palpable lymph nodes on the right side of the neck and inguinal area for 2 months. Laboratory tests showed an increased total eosinophil count and immunoglobulin E level, as well as positive interferon-γ release assays. Radiological examination showed multiple low echoic and heterogeneous well-enhancing lymph nodes in level II, III, IV, and V of the neck. Histological examination revealed caseous necrosis and tuberculoid granuloma. The lymph node enlargements were completely relieved after antituberculosis treatment. The mechanism for the development of tuberculous lymphadenitis in a patient receiving dupilumab is not fully understood yet. In some previous studies, treatment with dupilumab suppressed the expression of genes related not only to T helper 2 and eosinophil response but also to proinflammatory responses. It could not inhibit the intracellular growth of Mycobacterium tuberculosis in macrophages, predisposing them to the development of tuberculous infection. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report on the development of tuberculosis lymphadenitis in a patient treated with dupilumab.

INTRODUCTION

Tuberculous lymphadenitis is among the most frequent presentations of extrapulmonary tuberculosis, and its most common presentation is isolated chronic non-tender lymphadenopathy in a young adult without systemic symptoms. Dupilumab, an interleukin (IL) 4 receptor α-antagonist that inhibits IL-4 and IL-13 signaling by blocking the shared IL-4α subunit, is the first biologic for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD). Conjunctivitis, herpes infections, and injection site reactions were the most frequent adverse events of dupilumab described in phase 3 clinical trials123. However, various real-world adverse events have also been reported4. Herein, we present the case of a patient with severe AD who developed tuberculous lymphadenitis after dupilumab treatment.

CASE REPORT

A 32-year-old Korean female with severe AD was treated with biweekly dupilumab for 3 months at our clinic. She had no specific previous medical history including tuberculosis, other than severe AD. She complained of multiple enlarged palpable lymph nodes on the right side of the neck and inguinal area after the fifth injection of dupilumab (Fig. 1). She had no other symptoms such as fever, night sweats, or weight loss. Laboratory tests showed an increased total eosinophil count of 949/µl, a total immunoglobulin E level of 6,925 IU/ml, and positive interferon-γ release assays. She had a normal chest X-ray and a negative sputum test. Additional radiological examinations (ultrasound and computerized tomography) showed multiple low echoic and heterogeneous well-enhancing lymph nodes in level II, III, IV, and V of the neck (Fig. 2). A biopsy showed lymphohistiocytic infiltrates around tuberculoid granuloma and epithelioid cell granulomas with caseous necrosis (Fig. 3). Although acid-fast bacilli staining and a polymerase chain reaction (PCR) test for Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) and non-tuberculous mycobacteria were negative, the histologic findings were sufficient to diagnose as tuberculous lymphadenitis. The lymph node enlargements were completely relieved after antituberculosis treatment (seven months of rifampicin, isoniazid, ethambutol, and pyrazinamide).

DISCUSSION

Various cytokines and chemokines are involved in the immune pathway of MTB infection5. Cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor-α, interferon-γ, IL-1β, IL-12, IL-17, IL-22, and IL-23 are essential for survival following MTB infection. Chemokines, such as CCL-2, -7, -12, -17, -19, -21, and -22 and CXCL-1, -2, -3, -5, -6, -7, -8, -9, -10, -11, and -13 mediate efficient migration of dendritic cells and MTB-specific T cell activation6.

In some previous studies on transcriptomic analyses in patients treated with dupilumab78, it was revealed that dupilumab suppresses the expression of genes related not only to T helper (Th) 2 and eosinophil response but also to proinflammatory responses, including IL-17/22 signaling and the Th1 pathway. Hamilton et al.7 performed quantitative reverse transcription-PCR mRNA expression analyses of post-treatment skin biopsy specimens from moderate-to-severe AD patients with dupilumab. The authors found decreased changes in the mRNA expression of IL-23/p19, which is required for IL-17 and IL-22 expression during MTB infection, as well as of CCL-17 and -22, which mediate optimal granuloma formation in reaction to mycobacterial antigen. Moreover, a study by Guttman-Yassky et al.8 found decreased changes in mRNA expression of IL-22 (which might inhibit the intracellular growth of MTB in macrophages) and other chemokines, such as CCL-19, CXCL-1, CXCL-2, and CXCL-3 (which might mediate efficient immune cell activation) in patients treated with dupilumab.

Through this process, changes in the expression of cytokines and chemokines caused by dupilumab can lead to immune imbalance. A decreased expression of the cytokines and chemokines against tuberculous infection after treatment with dupilumab could not inhibit the intracellular growth of MTB in macrophages, predisposing to the development of tuberculous infection.

Although various adverse events of dupilumab have been reported to date, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first report on the development of tuberculosis lymphadenitis in a patient treated with dupilumab. Future research is required to clarify the mechanism underlying the development of tuberculous lymphadenitis in patients with AD treated with dupilumab.

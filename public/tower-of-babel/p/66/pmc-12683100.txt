Coccidiosis in chickens: From temporal patterns of Lesion development and Oocyst shedding to predictive modeling
Authors: C Bortoluzzi, CA Phillips, AJ Cowieson, SM Ramirez, B Lumpkins, G Mathis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoccidiosis continues to be an impactful parasitic disease in the poultry industry, and the increasing resistance to conventional anticoccidials such as ionophores and chemicals underscores the necessity of a deeper understanding in the behavior of this parasite. These experiments aimed to evaluate the distribution of lesion scores, oocysts shedding, growth performance, and to develop coccidiosis prediction models in broiler chickens undergoing a coccidiosis challenge and supplemented or not with ionophores or chemical/ionophore blend. Three experiments were conducted with 1,200 (Exp. 1) and 800 (Exp. 2 and 3) Cobb 500 male chickens in a completely randomized block design. Treatments included: (1) Control, (2) Challenged, (3) Challenged supplemented with ionophore (Salinomycin, SAL) at 66 ppm, and (4) Challenged supplemented with a blend of chemical/ionophore (Maxiban®, MAX) at 72 ppm. Birds were raised on new litter with 12 (Exp. 1) or 8 (Exp. 2 and 3) replicate pens/treatment. Coccidia challenge involved E. acervulina (150,000), E. maxima (50,000), and E. tenella (150,000) oocysts (Exp. 1 and 2), and E. acervulina (150,000), E. maxima (75,000), and E. tenella (150,000) oocysts (exp. 3), given via oral gavage at d14. The studies lasted 35d. Birds were necropsied at d19, 20, and 21 for lesion score evaluation, and excreta samples collected for oocyst per gram (OPG) analysis during two cycles of the coccidia (d19-22 and d26-28). Growth performance (body weight gain, BWG; feed intake, FI; and feed conversion ratio, FCR) was assessed weekly and from 0 to 35d. Overall, from d1-35, both SAL and MAX improved FI, BWG, and FCR (P < 0.01) while partially reducing the mortality rate (P < 0.05). Regardless of anticoccidial treatment, the peak of lesions of E. acervulina was on d19, and E. maxima and E. tenella on d20 with a reduction in severity of lesions afterwards. The peak of oocysts shedding was at d20 (1st cycle) and at d27 (2nd cycle). Both treatments (SAL and MAX) attenuated the coccidiosis lesions of the three Eimeria species and OPG (P < 0.01) during the three timepoints evaluated. The prediction models identified total OPG as key predictor for lesion severity. BWG at d28 was associated with E. acervulina and E. tenella lesions, and total OPG at d20 and d28, and BWG at d35 with E. acervulina and E. maxima lesions, and total OPG at d19, d20, and d28. In conclusion, this study demonstrates the value of frequent, time-sensitive measurements and the potential of data-driven approaches. Hence, the integration of classical statistics with machine learning techniques may be essential for improving the understanding of coccidiosis in broilers.Keywords: Broilers, Challenge models, Coccidiosis, Lesions, Oocyst per gram

Introduction

Coccidiosis continues to be the most important parasitic disease in the commercial broiler production industry due to its negative effects on the health and welfare of birds as well as on economic losses (Blake et al., 2020; USAHA, 2019). Eimeria, the causative agent of coccidiosis, can be found widely distributed around the world. After ingestion, sporulated oocysts release the sporocysts that invade epithelial cells of the intestine of broilers. Briefly, the species-specific protozoa, an obligatory intracellular parasite, undergoes several stages of multiplication in the intestine until a new batch of non-sporulated oocysts are released into the environment that will sporulate and once again become infective (Allen and Fetterer, 2002; Tonda et al., 2018; Mesa-Pineda et al., 2021). This process leads to subclinical and clinical damage and inflammation of the intestine, impairs feed and water consumption, is responsible for dehydration, loss of performance, and potentially death of the infected animals (Mesa-Pineda et al., 2021). A 2022 survey of U.S. veterinarians identified coccidiosis as the leading disease-related challenge in broiler production (USAHA, 2019). The intestinal malabsorption caused by Eimeria species, such as E. acervulina and E. maxima, can reduce body weight gain (BWG) by over 100 g, resulting in an estimated global financial loss of approximately US$ 14 billion. The average cost per chicken produced due to this condition is around US$ 0.22 (Blake et al., 2020). In addition to the direct economic losses, coccidiosis is a key predisposing factor for necrotic enteritis (NE), caused by Clostridium perfringens, as well as the increased proliferation of Campylobacter and Salmonella in the caeca, further exacerbating the economic impact (Blake et al., 2020).

The evaluation of macroscopic lesions characteristic of each Eimeria species in different intestinal regions and oocysts shape, size and shedding have traditionally been used as the gold standard methods for diagnosis of coccidiosis (Conway and McKenzie, 2007). As a subjective analysis, the recognition and identification of lesions requires a certain degree of knowledge and experience (Shirley et al., 2005), is ranked according to the severity of lesions, and follows a widely used scoring system (Johnson and Reid, 1970). When coccidiosis challenge studies are conducted, the gross evaluation of the intestine and oocysts shedding is traditionally performed once at 6- or 7-day post-infection (dpi) (Cardenas et al., 2017; Tonda et al., 2018; Levine et al., 2018; Chasser et al., 2020; El-Shall et al., 2024). However, it's important to note that the length of the life cycle of different Eimeria species can vary (Allen and Fetterer, 2002), especially when a mixed-species challenge is used. As a result, the distribution and peak of lesions, along with oocyst shedding, may vary across species, resulting in different responses depending on the day of evaluation. Hence, it is essential to understand the temporal variation of lesions in the intestine and the patterns of oocyst shedding, and how these factors are associated with performance parameters.

Coccidiosis in poultry has led to the adoption of various control methods, such as biosecurity measures and in feed use of ionophores and synthetic drugs like salinomycin, narasin, nicarbazin, and others, that target different developmental stages of the parasite (Fatoba and Adeleke, 2018). Resistance against these drugs is a growing issue, particularly in E. acervulina, E. maxima, and E. tenella species with resistance to nicarbazin and narasin detected in field isolates (Bafundo et al., 2008). Additionally, there is growing interest in alternative control methods due to public pressure to reduce drug use in the food chain, and therefore, the need for the successful development of novel approaches. However, there is a gap of knowledge in the research field when it comes to the timing of when target responses are measured as well as the magnitude of the coccidiostat’s effects which may play a critical role in the development of prediction models and decision-making process.

The hypothesis of these experiments was that a challenge with Eimeria acervulina, E. maxima, and E. tenella would result in temporal variation in coccidiosis lesions and oocyst shedding in broiler chickens, and that the feed supplementation with anticoccidials would interfere with this dynamic, impacting the accuracy of prediction models. The goal of the studies was to assess the impact of a mixed coccidiosis challenge model and commercially available anticoccidial products on the temporal distribution of coccidiosis lesions and oocyst shedding in broiler chickens, as well as to establish associations between lesion scores, oocyst shedding, and growth performance.

Material and methods

Housing, birds, and treatments

The animal care and use procedures adhered to the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching (FASS, 2010), under the supervision of a licensed poultry veterinarian. Three studies were conducted with only healthy birds. A total of 1,200 (Exp. 1) or 800 (Exp. 2 and 3) Cobb 500 chicks were obtained from Cobb Vantress hatchery, Cleveland, GA and used in the studies. To ensure consistency across experiments, only male birds were used. The chicks were weighed and assigned to 48 (Exp. 1) or 32 (Exp. 2 and 3) floor pens (25 birds per pen, with 12 (Exp. 1) or 8 (Exp. 2 and 3) replicate pens per treatment. The four treatments were assigned according to a completely randomized block design, and included a non-challenge control, challenged control, and a challenged control supplemented with Salinomycin (SAL) at 66 g/MT of feed, or supplemented with Maxiban® (MAX) (Elanco, Indianapolis, USA) at 72 g/MT of feed.

The feed included non-medicated commercial-type broiler starter, grower, and finisher diets, formulated according to NRC guidelines and consisting of commonly used feed ingredients in the United States (Table 1). The birds were provided feed ad libitum, starting from their arrival. The feeding phases were Starter (0-14 d), Grower (14-28 d), and Finisher (28-35 d). All the feed was provided in mash form. The animals were kept in 1.5 × 1.5-meter pens with about 13 birds per square meter (25 chicks/pen). The pens were located in a barn with litter that was not replaced during the study, but before the initiation of each study the dirt litter was replaced by new litter in its entirety. Each pen was provided with a feeder and nipple drinker. Gas heaters-maintained temperature, and fans provided ventilation. The lighting scheme was 24 hours of light from day 1 to day 14, then 18 hours of light to day 42.

Coccidia challenge

At 14 d of age all the birds (except the control group) received a freshly passed culture of mixed sporulated oocysts of E. acervulina, E. maxima and E. tenella at the following doses: Exp 1 and 2: 150,000, 50,000 and 150,000 oocysts/bird; and Exp 3. 150,000, 75,000 and 150,000 oocysts/bird, respectively. The inoculum, diluted in tap water, was mixed into the feed found in the base of each tube feeder.

Data collection and analysis performed

Birds and feed were weighed by pen on d0, 14, 21, 28, and 35 to determine BWG, feed intake (FI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR).

Evaluation of lesion scores and oocysts shedding

On d19, 20 and 21, three birds from each pen were selected, euthanized by cervical dislocation, weighed, and examined for the degree of presence of coccidia lesions. The Johnson and Reid (1970) method of coccidiosis lesion scoring was used to score the infected region(s) of the intestine. The score was based on a 0 to 4 score, with 0 being normal and 4 being the most severe.

On d19, 20, 21, and 22 and 26, 27, and 28 approximately 10 fresh excreta samples were collected from each pen and pooled to create one composite sample per pen. These representative samples were tested to determine the degree of oocysts shedding/cycling. Oocysts per gram (OPG) were determined for each sample by using the McMaster chamber technique (Hodgson, 1970).

The floor pens were checked daily for mortality and birds were culled only to relieve suffering. When a bird was culled or found dead, the pen, date and removal weight (kg) were recorded. A gross necropsy was performed on all dead or culled birds to determine the sex and probable cause of death.

Statistical analysis and modeling

The data from all three experiments were combined and analyzed as one-way ANOVA using JMP 16.0 version. Pen was considered the experimental unit. Experiment was included in the model as “random” attribute to remove the source of variation associated with each experiment. The means showing significant (P ≤ 0.05) and trending (0.05 < P ≤ 0.10) treatment differences in the ANOVA were compared by Tukey’s test. All data were tested for normality and homogeneity of variances before pooling the three datasets together. Mortality, lesion score, and OPG were analyzed by Kruskal-Wallis’s test, and the means separated by Dunn’s test. Categorical analysis was performed to analyze the frequency of lesion scores by using JMP 16.0 version.

The models herein were trained using the XGBoost python package (Chen and Guestrin, 2016). XGBoost uses the gradient boosting ensemble learning method. This is a supervised learning algorithm, which means the algorithm uses data with known labels to learn an equation to predict unknown labels from data. Like all ensemble learning methods, XGBoost is based around the idea of training many weak learners and combining their predictions to make a strong model. In the case of XGBoost, the base level weak learner is a classification and regression tree (CART). The XGBoost algorithm trains a CART, then adds subsequent CARTs that are trained to fill in weaknesses in the predictions of previous trees (a process called boosting).

Growth performance

The average growth performance data from the three experiments is presented in Table 2. During the first- and second-weeks post-challenge (d14-21 and d21-28), there was a significant decrease in FI, BWG, and FCR due to the challenge. However, the dietary inclusion of SAL and MAX mitigated these effects (P < 0.01). A similar pattern was observed for the cumulative periods between d0-21 and d0-28 (P < 0.01). Over the entire experimental period (days 0-35), the challenge significantly impaired BWG by 17.7 % and FCR by 7.3 % (P < 0.01). Both anticoccidials mitigated the losses in growth performance and mortality caused by the challenge.

Lesion scores – frequency and average

Lesion score results are shown in Figs. 1 and 2, displaying the average across the three experiments. The peak of E. acervulina lesions occurred on d19 (5 days post-infection, dpi), with a subsequent reduction (2.93, 2.54, 2.07 on d19, d20, d21, respectively) and in both the frequency of lesions and the average score (P < 0.05). Over the three evaluated days, the challenged control group exhibited the highest scores, while the anticoccidials significantly (P < 0.05) reduced these scores (approximately 35, 29, and 25 % reduction in the scores of E. acervulina on d19, d20, d21, respectively). For E. maxima and E. tenella, the peak of lesions was observed on d 20 (6 dpi). The use of anticoccidials significantly (P < 0.05) reduced the frequency and average of E. tenella lesions (80, 72, and 71 % reduction in the scores of E. tenella on d19, d20, d21, respectively, with MAX). However, for E. maxima, the anticoccidials were only effective in significantly reducing lesions by 47 % (MAX) on d19 (5 dpi). On d20, only Maxiban reduced the lesions (32 %), and by d21, there was no significant difference between the three challenged groups.

Oocysts excretion

The total oocyst excretion (pooled across the three Eimeria species) results are shown in Fig. 3. Given that the challenge was applied on d14, excreta were collected daily from d19-22 (5-8 dpi) to cover the first cycle of oocyst shedding. During this period, the peak of oocysts shedding in the challenged control group occurred on d20, with a reduction observed thereafter. The oocyst counts were significantly higher (P < 0.05) in the challenged control group compared to the other groups on d19-21 (approximately 70 % reduction in OPG with coccidiostats). To cover the second cycle of oocysts shedding, excreta samples were also collected daily from d26-28. During this phase, the peak of shedding occurred on d26, with significant reduction (P < 0.05) in the groups under either anticoccidial control method (approximately 67 % reduction in OPG with coccidiostats).

Predictive modelling

The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) prediction model plots for lesion scores are presented in Fig. 4. The models with the highest coefficient of determination (R²) are highlighted, specifically those at d19 (5 dpi), with additional models provided in Supplemental Figures 1 and 2. The primary features used to predict the lesions of the three Eimeria species on d19 were total OPG from d19-21 and total OPG from d19-22. Elevated oocyst shedding during these periods was associated with more severe lesions, as indicated by the red dots positioned to the right of zero. For E. acervulina, the prediction model also incorporated FCR at d0-14 (i.e., before the challenge) and d0-35, as well as total OPG at d26. Notably, the model suggested that birds exhibiting poorer FCR at d14 (red dots positioned to the left of zero) were less likely to develop severe E. acervulina lesions on d19. The prediction models for E. maxima and E. tenella predominantly relied on OPG data, with higher OPG values associated with more severe lesions. Additionally, BWG from d14-21 emerged as a significant predictor for E. maxima lesions, with lower BWG indicating increased lesion severity.

Prediction of Eimeria acervulina, Eimeria maxima, and Eimeria tenella lesions on day 19 (5 days post-infection) represented as a SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) plot. SHAP is a summary plot for machine learning models providing the contribution of each input feature to the model’s predictions. The vertical axis lists the features from top to bottom by their mean absolute SHAP value. Features at the top have, on average, have the greatest impact on the model’s output. The horizontal axis is the SHAP value itself. A point positioned to the right of zero indicates that, for that sample, the feature increased the predicted value above the baseline; a point to the left indicates it drove the prediction downward. The further a point lies from zero, the stronger its influence. These SHAP values are expressed in the same units as your model’s output (in this case predicted Eimeria lesions at d 19). Color adds an extra layer of information: blue denotes a low feature value, and red denotes a high feature value. Thus, a red dot far to the right tells you that a high feature measurement strongly increased the prediction for that sample, while a blue dot far to the left shows that a low measurement strongly decreased it.

Models to predict FCR and BWG at d28 and d35 were also developed, although these models exhibited lower R² compared to the lesion score models. These models are presented in Supplemental Figures 3 and 4. The early onset of E. acervulina and E. maxima lesions (d19, or 5 dpi) were the top two features for predicting BWG at d35, followed by total OPG numbers at d19, d20, and d28. Furthermore, the prediction of FCR at d35 utilized total OPG numbers from d19-21, E. acervulina lesions at d21 (7 dpi), and E. tenella lesions at d20 (6 dpi) as top features. This demonstrates the close association among these parameters and FCR, highlighting the importance of taking these measurements across several time points.

Discussion

Over the past four decades, the foundational understanding of the biological characteristics behind the diagnosis and control of avian coccidiosis has remained largely unchanged. Nevertheless, advancements in molecular biology have significantly enhanced our comprehension of Eimeria spp. genetic diversity, mechanisms of prophylaxis, and the emergence of drug resistance (Blake, 2025). A novel dichotomous key has been developed to facilitate the identification of Eimeria species infecting poultry, based on the predominant anatomical site of lesion development, clinical presentation, and oocyst morphometrics (Blake, 2025). Despite these molecular and diagnostic innovations, the accurate, timely, and efficient diagnosis of coccidiosis in both field and experimental settings continues to rely predominantly on classical parasitological techniques established over half a century ago (Johnson and Reid, 1970). The prepatent period, defined as the interval between oocyst ingestion and their initial detection in excreta, varies among the main Eimeria species affecting commercial poultry, i.e., E. acervulina (∼86 h), E. maxima (∼120 h), and E. tenella (∼132 h) (Blake, 2025). Consequently, lesion scoring and quantification of oocyst shedding at multiple post-infection time points are critical, particularly under controlled experimental conditions where the timing of challenge exposure is precisely known. In the current study, the integration of classical coccidiosis diagnostics with machine learning techniques supported our objective in demonstrating the importance of data collection throughout the Eimeria spp. life cycle.

Additionally, we assessed the relationships among the various parameters and developed models to predict lesion scores and growth performance. Overall, the challenge significantly impacted FI, BWG, and FCR, but the inclusion of coccidiostats mitigated these effects. E. acervulina lesions peaked at d19, while E. maxima and E. tenella lesions peaked at d20, with anticoccidials significantly reducing lesion scores. OPG peaked at d20 and d27 with slight shifts in this behavior with the supplementation of anticoccidials which significantly reduced total OPG. The prediction models identified total OPG as key predictors for lesion severity, while BWG at d28 was closely associated with E. acervulina and E. tenella lesions, and total OPG at d20 and d28. At d35, E. acervulina and E. maxima lesions, and total OPG at d19, d20, and d28 were the features used to predict BWG.

Among the three Eimeria species used here, E. acervulina, which has the shortest prepatent period, exhibited the highest lesion scores at d19 (5 dpi), followed by a decline. In contrast, lesions caused by E. maxima and E. tenella peaked at 6 dpi. The biochemical and genetic mechanisms underlying the intracellular development of Eimeria spp. remain largely undefined (Allen and Fetterer, 2002). Nevertheless, investigations into precocious and drug-resistant lines of Eimeria tenella have led to the identification of two linkage groups associated with these phenotypes, which have been mapped to chromosomes 1 and 2. These findings provide a foundation for further exploration of genetic loci that may regulate the parasite’s life cycle(Allen and Fetterer, 2002). While the anticoccidial treatments used in this study effectively reduced lesion severity, they had a minimal influence on the temporal distribution of lesions across the three consecutive days of assessment. Santos et al. (2024) reported that using a relatively low infection dose at d7 to induce subclinical coccidiosis, with OPG analysis conducted every five days starting on d15, resulted in peak oocyst shedding on d15 (8 dpi), as one would expect. Lesion scores were assessed on d13, 22, 28, and 35, with E. acervulina and E. maxima peaking on d13 (6 dpi), and notably, E. tenella peaking later on d22 (15 dpi). Cowieson et al. (2020), in their study on the effects of coccidiosis on the behavior of blood biomarkers throughout the lifespan of chickens, observed not only temporal shifts in several biomarkers but also peak lesion scores for E. acervulina and E. tenella at 13 dpi. Therefore, frequent evaluation of lesion scores and oocyst shedding is a valuable strategy, particularly in subclinical models, as it captures the progressive increase in oocyst load in the litter due to parasite cycling. An important enhancement to future studies, beyond the scope of the current work, would include lesion scoring assessments at 12-14 dpi, aligned with OPG data collected during this period to capture the second cycle of parasite replication. Additionally, the inclusion of blood biomarker analysis, as demonstrated by Cowieson et al. (2020), would provide further valuable insights.

In addition to conducting traditional statistical analyses, we sought to identify meaningful associations among features and to develop predictive models for key outcomes, such as lesion scores and growth performance. In recent years, numerous studies have highlighted the potential of machine learning techniques in livestock research, particularly in the context of image-based monitoring systems for the early detection of poultry diseases (Wang et al., 2019; Okinda et al., 2019; Yadav and Jadhav, 2019; Machuve et al., 2022). In this study, we utilized the XGBoost Python package (Chen and Guestrin, 2016), a powerful and flexible gradient boosting algorithm well-suited for both regression and classification tasks across a wide range of datasets. XGBoost is particularly advantageous for small to medium-sized datasets, offering high performance and efficiency. However, on larger datasets, it may be outperformed by deep learning models such as neural networks.

The prediction of coccidiosis lesions at d19 (5 dpi) yielded the most accurate models (R² > 0.70), outperforming predictions made for d20 and 21 (6 and 7 dpi, respectively). This suggests that early lesion onset could be predicted with greater precision. OPG emerged as the primary feature associated with lesion severity, regardless of Eimeria species, followed by BWG and FCR during the acute infection phase (d14–21). Chasser et al. (2020) reported weak correlations between lesions, BWG, and OPG, and yet emphasized the value of including OPG in assessments to better understand parasite replication. Despite OPG being one of the outcomes with the highest variability in coccidiosis research, our findings highlight the critical role of oocyst shedding as a key indicator in such studies. Growth performance metrics, including FCR and BWG, were also predicted using the available data, although the models resulted in lower R² values compared to those predicting lesion scores. Notably, models forecasting performance at d28 demonstrated better predictive accuracy, particularly for FCR, than those at d35. This discrepancy may be attributed to the diminished impact of the challenge during the d28-35 period, as well as the absence of input features collected beyond d28. Nevertheless, as reported by Santos et al. (2024), birds exhibited detectable levels of E. tenella lesions and oocyst shedding at d35. Therefore, incorporating such data at d35 (21 dpi) in the present study could have potentially enhanced the accuracy of the model.

In summary, this study presents a comprehensive evaluation of the temporal dynamics of coccidiosis in broiler chickens under controlled challenge conditions, both in the presence and absence of anticoccidial interventions. The results demonstrate that, while anticoccidial treatments effectively reduced lesion severity and oocyst shedding, they did not influence the timing of lesion development. Predictive modeling identified total OPG as a key indicator of lesion severity and performance outcomes and validated the value of frequent, time-sensitive measurements and the potential of data-driven approaches. Therefore, integrating classical diagnostic methods with machine learning approaches may be crucial for enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of coccidiosis monitoring and management in poultry production.

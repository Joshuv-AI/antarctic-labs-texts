Poricoic Acid A attenuated TGF-β1-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition in renal tubular epithelial cells through SPRY2/ERK signaling pathway
Authors: Ming Xiang, Haiying Chen, Xiangdong Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The progression of renal fibrosis is difficult to reverse, and Poria cocos, one of the main components of Wenyang Zhenshuai Granules, has been shown to be crucial to the development of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). This study aimed to examine the molecular mechanism by which Poricoic Acid A (PAA) inhibited the advancement of EMT in renal tubular epithelial (RTE) cells. The protein levels of sprouty RTK signaling antagonist 2 (SPRY2) extracellular regulated protein kinases (ERK), and p-ERK were measured. The EMT progression of RTE cells was evaluated by a series of experiments. The regulatory relationship of PAA to SPRY2 was determined by cycloheximide, molecular docking and drug affinity target stability and immunoprecipitation. The overexpression of SPRY2 or PAA intervention suppressed the HK-2 and NRK-52E cell's viability, proliferation and migration ability of TGF-β1-induced while raising the levels of E-cadherin and decreasing those of collagen I, collagen III, Fibronectin1, α-SMA, Vimentin, ZEB1, Twist, Snail and Slug. PAA was able to be combined with SPRY2 protein. Besides, we found that PAA intervention increased the stability of SPRY2 through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, did not affect ERK levels, and reduced the levels of p-ERK. Finally, we found that inhibiting SPRY2 negated the beneficial effect of PAA on TGF-β1-stimulated RTE cells. PAA alleviated the EMT of RTE cells by modulating the SPRY2/ERK pathway.

INTRODUCTION

Renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF) is a permanent organic lesion that is typically characterized by the accumulation of renal parenchymal scarring. One potential treatment strategy for interstitial fibrosis is to slow its progression. To date, there are no specific drugs or therapies that can be used to treat interstitial fibrosis or prevent the progression of the condition to end-stage renal disease [1].

The kidney's epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a multifactorial process. Renal tubular epithelial (RTE) cells lose their marker proteins throughout the EMT process, resulting in the transformation into myofibroblasts, which is one of the features of RIF [2,3]. EMT not only participates in the formation of renal fibrosis but also exacerbates the fibrotic process through paracrine mechanisms [4,5]. Therefore, inhibiting renal tubular cell EMT is a potential therapeutic strategy to help delay or reverse RIF.

Poria cocos is one of the main components of Wenyang Zhenshuai Granules. Poria cocos (Schw.) Wolf, a type of edible fungus, has been widely used in nutritional supplements, dietary supplements, and functional foods [6]. Research has shown that Poria cocos has anti-renal fibrosis effects [7,8]. Its main active ingredient is Poricoic Acid A (PAA). PAA is a triterpenoid compound isolated from Poria cocos, which can significantly alleviate kidney fibrosis and prevent renal function decline [9,10]. Research by Miao et al. [11] has shown that the components 5,6,7,8,3',4'-hexamethoxyflavone and matairesinol in Poria cocos could counteract the aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling pathway to combat renal fibrosis. Other studies have demonstrated that PAA could induce mitochondrial autophagy by downregulating FUN14 Domain Containing 1 to combat kidney fibrosis and alleviate diabetic nephropathy foot cell damage [12]. Poria cocos has become one of the important drugs in combating renal fibrosis.

The Sprouty protein family is a widely distributed negative regulatory factor in cells that is linked to the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) pathway [13]. Sprouty RTK signaling antagonist 2 (SPRY2), a member of the SPRY signaling protein family, is crucial for cellular migration, proliferation, and apoptosis [14,15]. SPRY2 could inhibit tumor cell proliferation and migration by reducing ERK activity, thus exerting an inhibitory effect on lung cancer [16]. Previous studies have demonstrated that inhibiting ERK phosphorylation could suppress EMT in RTE cells [17]. Additionally, research indicated a close association between SPRY2 and EMT, with the downregulation of SPRY2 promoting changes in vascular signaling and EMT phenotypes [18]. Hsa-miR-22-3p indirectly regulated SPRY2 to suppress EMT and cell migration/invasion in liver cancer cells [14]. Cancer upregulated gene 2 downregulated SPRY2 expression through c-Cbl to promote EMT in lung cancer cells [19]. However, whether SPRY2 can regulate EMT through ERK in renal cells is still unknown.

Based on the above research background, we assumed that PAA was crucial to EMT. In this study, we constructed an EMT cell model and conducted relevant studies on the role and molecular mechanism of PAA.

METHODS

Cell culture and establishment of EMT cell model

The human RTE cells HK-2 (AW-CNH063, Abiowell) and Rat NRK-52E cells (AW-CNR084, Abiowell) were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (AW-M006, Abiowell) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (10099141, Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (AWH0529a, Abiowell), and incubated at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

The EMT model was constructed in vitro by treating TGF-β1 (CS-X11258, Shanghai C-reagent Biotechnology) with 5 ng/ml (HK-2) [17] and 10 ng/ml (NRK-52E) [20] for 48 h, respectively. The morphology of the cells was observed through an optical microscope (BA210T, Motic).

Experimental grouping

In this study, the cell experiments were divided into 12 groups: 1. Control Group: Cells were cultured normally. 2. TGF-β1 Group: Cells were intervened with TGF-β1 for 48 h. 3. TGF-β1 + oe-NC Group: Following oe-NC transfection, cells were intervened with TGF-β1 for 48 h. 4. TGF-β1 + oe-SPRY2 Group: Cells were transfected with oe-SPRY2 and then intervened with TGF-β1 for 48 h. 5. TGF-β1 + 1 PAA Group: After receiving TGF-β1 for 24 h, the cells were co-culture with 1 μM PAA for 24 h. 6. TGF-β1 + 10 PAA Group: After receiving TGF-β1 for 24 h, the cells were co-culture with 10 μM PAA for 24 h. 7. TGF-β1 + 50 PAA Group: After receiving TGF-β1 for 24 h, the cells were co-culture with 50 μM PAA for 24 h. 8. TGF-β1 + 100 PAA Group: After receiving TGF-β1 for 24 h, the cells were co-cultured with 100 μM PAA for 24 h. 9. TGF-β1 + PPA + si-NC Group: Following transfection with si-NC, the cells were intervened for 24 h with TGF-β1 and co-cultured with 10 μM PAA for 24 h. 10. TGF-β1 + PAA + si-SPRY2 Group: Following transfection with si-SPRY2, the cells were intervened for 24 h with TGF-β1, and then co-cultured with 10 μM PAA for 24 h. 11. TGF-β1 + Cycloheximide (CHX) Group: After being intervened for 24 h with TGF-β1, the cells were co-cultured for 6 h with 100 μM CHX (Abx076513, Abbexa). 12. TGF-β1 + PAA + CHX Group: Following a 24-h intervention with TGF-β1, the cells were co-cultured with 10 μM PAA for 24 h and with 100 μM CHX for 6 h. Lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Invitrogen) was used for transfection. The sequence of the si-NC plasmid is TTGTCGTAGTGGCCATT. The sequence of the si-SPRY2 plasmid (HG-Hi005842, Honorgene) is GCTCAAGCCAGGTGAGCTTAA.

Western blot

Cells were gathered and lysed using RIPA lysate (AWB0136, Abiowell). Protein concentration was determined using the Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) kit (AWB0156, Abiowell). After electrophoresis using SDS-PAGE and transferring the proteins to the membrane, an then they were blocked. Primary antibodies (SPRY2 (ab85670, 1:1,000, Abcam), collagen I (4695-1-AP, 1:2,000, Proteintech), collagen III (ab7778, 1:5,000, Abcam), Fibronectin1 (15613-1-AP, 1:1,000, Proteintech), zinc finger E-box binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1) (21544-1-AP, 1:1,000, Proteintech), Twist family bHLH transcription factor 1 (Twist) (ab50887, 1:1,000, Abcam), Snail (13099-1-AP0, 1:500, Proteintech) and Slug (12129-1-AP, 1:1,000, Proteintech), α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) (AWA46841, 1:1,000, Abiowell), Vimentin (AWA01208, 1:1,000, Abiowell), E-cadherin (AWA43562, 1:1,000, Abiowell), p-ERK (28733-1-AP, 1:3,000, Proteintech), ERK (16443-1-AP, 1:2,000, Proteintech), TGF-β1 (AWA00083, 1:1,000, Abiowell), β-actin (AWA80002, 1:5,000, Abiowell)) were added to the membrane, respectively. After incubation, the membrane was incubated with the diluted secondary antibodies (Goat anti-Rabbit IgG (H+L) (AWS0002, 1:5,000, Abiowell), Goat anti-mouse IgG (H+L) (AWS0001, 1:5,000, Abiowell)). A chemiluminescence imaging equipment (ChemiScope6100, Clinx science instruments) was used to observe the membrane after it had been incubated for 1 min with ECL chemiluminescence solution (AWB0005, Abiowell). The protein band was analyzed using Image J software.

Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Using Trizol (15596026, Thermo) isolated total RNA. After the concentration and purity of the extracted RNA were then measured using UV spectrophotometry cDNA was created using a reverse transcription kit (CW2569, Cwbio) RT-qPCR reaction using an UltraSYBR Mixture (CW2601, Cwbio). β-actin allowed as internal control. Differences in gene expression were computed with the 2-ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences were designed as follows. H-β-actin: forward 5’-ACCCTGAAGTACCCCATCGAG-3’, reverse 5’-AGCACAGCCTGGATAGCAAC-3’; H-SPRY2: forward 5’-CTCCGTGTGGACAGTCTAGC-3’, reverse 5’-GAAGTGTGGTCACTCCAGCA-3’.

CHX method

The previous method was used to assess the stability of the SPRY2 protein [21]. After treating cells with 100 μM CHX to prevent protein synthesis, the cells were collected at the specified time (0, 2, 4, 6 h), and the level of SPRY2 was analyzed using Western blot.

Drug affinity responsiveness target stability (DARTS)

Cells were divided using M-PER cleavage buffer (78501, Thermo Fisher Scientific). After, the TNC buffer was added to the cracking buffer. The lysates were divided into 1.5 ml tubes and incubated with 1 μM and 10 μM PAA at room temperature for 1 h. Then, the lysate was digested with Pronase. Further analysis s was performed by Western blot.

Immunoprecipitation (IP)

TGF-β1-stimulated RTE cells were lysed with IP lysis buffer. The ant-SPRY2 antibody was added to the sample lysate and incubated at 4°C overnight. Pre-treated protein A/G agarose beads were added into the lysate, incubated with the antibody, and incubated at 4°C for 2 h. After washing the protein complex with buffer solution, Western blot analysis was performed with Ubiquitin (10201-2-AP, Proteintech).

5-ethynyl-2-deoxyuridine (EdU) detection

After being cultivated in 100 μl of 50 μM EdU (C0075L, Beyotime) media, the cells were fixed in 50 μl of 4% paraformaldehyde (AWI0056b, Abiowell). Following, glycine, penetrant and 1×Apollo dye solution were added, followed by incubation, away from light (room temperature). Following the incubation, 100 μl 1×Hoechst 33342 reaction solution was added to each well and incubated away from light at room temperature for 30 min. Photographs were taken under a fluorescent microscope (DSZ2000X, Beijing cnmicro instrument) as soon as the samples were stained.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

A 96-well plate was filled with 5 × 104 cells/well in 300 µl of medium and incubated. Cell viability was measured using a CCK8 working solution (AWC0114b, Abiowell). The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (MB-530, Heales) after incubating for 4 h.

Transwell

The lower chamber of the Transwell was filled with 500 μl of 10% FBS (10099141, Gibco) complete medium. Following trypsin digestion, the cells were resuspended in serum-free medium at a density of 2 × 106 cells/ml. Following the addition of 100 μl of cells to each well on Transwell coated with or without Matrigel. The cells were incubated at 37°C for 48 h. Next, cells were preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde (N1012, NCM Biotech) and then 5 times cleaned with water after being dyed for 5 min with 0.1% crystal violet. Subsequently, an inverted microscope was used for examination and imaging (DSZ2000X, Beijing cnmicro apparatus).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software, with data expressed as mean ± standard deviation. For normally distributed data, unpaired t-tests evaluated two-group comparisons, while one-way/two-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc tests assessed multiple-group differences. Significance was defined as p < 0.05.

RESULTS

SPRY2 inhibited EMT in RTE cells

To study the influence of SPRY2 on the progression of EMT, we created an EMT model by inducing HK-2 cells by TGF-β1, and then we constructed SPRY2 overexpression. The findings of the Western blot and RT-qPCR analysis, displayed in Fig. 1A, demonstrated a significantly lower level of SPRY2 expression in the TGF-β1 group in contrast to the Control group. When compared to the TGF-β1 + oe-NC group, the level of SPRY2 in the TGF-β1 + oe-SPRY2 group was much higher, suggesting that SPRY2 was successfully overexpressed.

Optical microscopy showed that after SPRY2 overexpression, the fibroblast-like appearance obtained by TGF-β1 was transformed into a polygonal or pebble-like epithelial morphology (Fig. 1B). The successful development of the EMT model was demonstrated by Western blot analysis, which revealed that the TGF-β1 group had significantly higher levels of α-SMA, Vimentin, collagen I, collagen III, Fibronectin1 and significantly lower levels of E-cadherin when compared to the Control group. This suggests that TGF-β1 induces EMT. While compared with the TGF-β1 + oe-NC group, the EMT levels of HK-2 cells in the TGF-β1 + oe-SPRY2 group were significantly decreased (Fig. 1C). In addition, protein levels of key EMT transcription factors (ZEB-1, Twist, Snail and Slug) were significantly higher in the TGF-β1 group compared to the control group. Compared with the TGF-β1 + oe-NC group, the protein levels of EMT transcription factors were significantly reduced in the TGF-β1 + oe-SPRY2 group (Fig. 1D).

The results of the EdU and CCK-8 assays (Fig. 1E–G) revealed that overexpression of SPRY2 reversed TGF-β1-induced cell viability and proliferation. As shown in Fig. 1H, I, overexpression of SPRY2 reduced TGF-β1-induced cell migration and capacity. In summary, the experimental results confirm that EMT of RTE cells can be inhibited. Taken together, the experimental results confirmed that SPRY2 could inhibit EMT in RTE cells.

PAA inhibited EMT in RTE cells

To investigate how PAA affected the progression of EMT, we intervened in the EMT model groups with 0, 1, 10, 50, and 100 μM of PAA and conducted relevant indicator tests. As shown in Fig. 2A, PAA transformed the fibroblast-like appearance induced by TGF-β1 into a polygonal or pebble-like epithelial form, and the inhibitory effect of PAA became more pronounced with increasing concentration. Fig. 2B–D indicated a noteworthy rise in EMT levels in the TGF-β1 group than the Control group. The EMT levels in the TGF-β1 + 1 PAA group did not significantly differ from those of the TGF-β1 group. On the other hand incubation with higher concentrations of PAA (10, 50, and 100 μM) noticeably elevated E-cadherin levels, whereas decreased α-SMA, Vimentin, collagen I, collagen III, Fibronectin1, ZEB-1, Twist, Snail and Slug levels.

The EdU and CCK-8 assay findings revealed that the TGF-β1 group's cell viability and proliferative capability were significantly higher than the Control group. Interestingly, treatment with 1 PAA did not alter these parameters relative to TGF-β1 alone. However, increasing PAA concentrations (10, 50, and 100 PAA) produced a dose-dependent suppression of cell viability and proliferation in the presence of TGF-β1 (Fig. 2E, F).

Fig. 2G, H demonstrated that TGF-β1 stimulation significantly increased HK-2 cell migration. Co-treatment with 1 μM PAA did not significantly affect these TGF-β1-induced migratory and invasive properties. However, higher concentrations of PAA (10, 50, and 100 μM) markedly attenuated both migration and invasion capacities in TGF-β1-treated cells. In summary, the above results indicated that 10, 50 and 100 μM PAA could inhibit EMT in RTE cells.

PAA inhibited ERK phosphorylation by promoting SPRY2 expression

The previous experimental findings of this investigation showed that both SPRY2 and PAA could inhibit the EMT of RTE cells. To elucidate the mechanism of inhibiting the EMT process further, molecular docking was performed between the molecular structure of PAA and SPRY2 protein (The chemical formula of PAA is shown in Fig. 3A). The binding energy of molecular docking was –7.2 kcal/mol, which was less than –5 kcal/mol. The molecular docking results, as displayed in Fig. 3B, suggested that PAA could attach to the SPRY2 protein well on its own. PAA interacted with the amino acids and was stably bound to the SPRY2 protein's cavity, according to visual analysis performed with PYMOL. Further, we verified the combination of PAA and SPRY2 by the DARTS method (Fig. 3C).

Western blot analysis showed that the TGF-β1 stimulation inhibited the expression of SPRY2. However, ERK expression remained unchanged and p-ERK expression increased dramatically. The expression of p-ERK dramatically decreased, ERK expression did not change significantly, and SPRY2 expression significantly increased in the TGF-β1 + PAA group compared to the TGF-β1 group. TGF-β1 + PAA + si-SPRY2 expression dramatically dropped, ERK expression did not change significantly, and p-ERK expression significantly increased when compared to the TGF-β1 + PAA + si-NC group (Fig. 3D).

The stability analysis of SPRY2 protein showed that at 2, 4, and 6 h, the stability of SPRY2 protein in the TGF-β1 + PAA + CHX group was better than in the TGF-β1 + CHX group, indicating that PAA intervention could increase the stability of the SPRY2 protein (Fig. 3E).

MG132 treatment of TGF-β1-stimulated cells significantly elevated SPRY2 levels compared to TGF-β1 alone, while addition of PAA did not further alter SPRY2 expression (TGF-β1 + MG132 vs. TGF-β1 + MG132+PAA: no significant difference) (Fig. 3F). In addition, we detected the ubiquitination of SPRY2 through IP experiments. We found that PAA can inhibit SPRY2 ubiquitination (Fig. 3G). These results suggest that PAA inhibits ubiquitination and protein degradation of SPRY2 through the proteasome pathway, thereby increasing the expression of SPRY2 and leading to inhibition of ERK phosphorylation.

PAA alleviated EMT of RTE cells through SPRY2/ERK pathway

To further study the mechanism by which PAA alleviated EMT in RTE cells, we conducted SPRY2 knockdown. As Fig. 4A–C showed, after PAA treatment, the EMT induced by TGF-β1 was inhibited, while the forced expression of SPRY2 partially nullizes the role of PAA. The EdU and CCK-8 assay findings revealed that the TGF-β1 + PAA group exhibited noticeably lower cell viability and proliferation capacity than the TGF-β1 group. In contrast, the TGF-β1 + PAA + si-SPRY2 group exhibited a notable increase in cell viability and proliferation capacity than the TGF-β1 + PAA + si-NC group (Fig. 4D–F). As demonstrated in Fig. 4G–I, after PAA treatment, the migration and invasion capacity of HK-2 and NRK-52E cells induced by TGF-β1were decreased. However, overexpression of SPRY2 eliminated PAA's inhibition of TGF-β1-induced cell migration and invasion. Overall, the experimental results confirmed that PAA alleviated EMT in RTE cells through the SPRY2/ERK pathway.

DISCUSSION

In the progression of RIF, EMT in RTE cells is a crucial stage, characterized by decreased adhesion ability of epithelial cells, increased α-SMA expression, basement membrane disruption, and enhanced cell invasion and migration capabilities [22,23]. Many cytokines, such as TGF-β1, regulate the EMT. Research has indicated that TGF-β1, a profibrotic factor, is essential for RTE cells to change from an epithelial to a mesenchymal phenotype [24,25]. Therefore, it has become crucial to block or reduce TGF-β1 activity in order to prevent and treat RIF.

As a member of the SPRY family, SPRY2 has been demonstrated to express itself in a number of cancers and is intimately linked to the initiation and spread of tumors [26,27]. It functions as a regulator of the Ras/Raf-1 signaling pathway, negatively regulated growth factors and cellular activators to inhibit tumor growth [28]. It could also induce autophagy in tumor cells by regulating the expression of LC3, thus promoting tumor autophagy-dependent cell death [29]. Additionally, SPRY2 could inhibit Ras pathways and Rho protein activity, affecting the movement and metastasis capabilities of tumor cells [30]. Research has also indicated that upregulation of SPRY2 expression promoted the cancer phenotype and EMT in colorectal cancer [31]. So, does SPRY2 have an impact on EMT in RTE cells? We conducted a study by overexpressing SPRY2 to investigate this. The research results showed that TGF-β1-induced HK-2 cells EMT were reversed by overexpressing SPRY2. These results revealed that SPRY2 was crucial in inhibiting EMT in RTE cells.

One of Poria cocos' primary active ingredients, PAA, has been demonstrated to have pharmacological effects, including anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and anticancer qualities [32]. In a study by Wu et al. [33], PAA was shown to regulate the Sirtuin 6/NF-κB signaling axis to inhibit inflammatory responses in chondrocytes, thereby relieving the progression of osteoarthritis. Furthermore, research has indicated that PAA modulated the expression of genes related to metastasis and EMT indicators, thereby greatly inhibiting the invasion, migration, and metastatic capacity of liver cancer cells [34]. This study investigated how EMT in RTE cells is affected by PAA, and the results showed that intervention with different concentrations (10, 50, and 100 μM) of PAA could inhibit EMT in RTE cells.

Both SPRY2 and PAA could inhibit EMT in RTE cells, so was there an interaction between SPRY2 and PAA? In the present study, molecular docking analysis showed that PAA spontaneously binds in the cavity of SPRY2 protein and interacts with amino acids. We then tested the stability of SPRY2 protein by adding MG132, and we found that MG132 treatment reversed the inhibition of TGF-β1 on SPRY2 protein. Finally, we confirmed that PAA inhibited the ubiquitination of SPRY2 through IP experiments. These findings are innovative and suggest that PAA may inhibit EMT in RTE cells by regulating SPRY2 protein expression through the proteasome pathway.

ERK is a serine/threonine protein kinase widely existing in mammalian cells [35]. Previous studies have shown that inhibiting ERK phosphorylation could inhibit TGF-β1-induced fibrosis [36] and EMT [17,37] in RTE cells. This is reflected in the inhibitory effect of ERK pathway blocking on fibrotic markers such as collagen I, fiber junction, α-SMA, and EMT markers such as Vimentin, Twist, and Snail [38-40]. What's more, research shows that SPRY2 exerts anti-tumor and anti-inflammatory effects by blocking the activation of the ERK pathway [41]. In TGF-β1-induced EMT models, we observed that treatment with PAA enhanced the SPRY2 expression and blocked the ERK phosphorylation without affecting total ERK levels. However, when we transfected SPRY2 siRNA to reduce SPRY2 expression, we noticed a significant increase in ERK phosphorylation while total ERK levels remained unchanged. This is consistent with the previously reported negative regulatory effect of SPRY2 on ERK phosphorylation [42]. Building on these findings, we suggest that PAA suppresses the activation of the ERK pathway by upregulating SPRY2 expression, thereby potentially alleviating TGF-β1-induced EMT in RTE cells. This mechanism suggests that PAA could be a promising intervention for addressing RIF.

The study further conducted SPRY2 knockdown, followed by intervention with PAA for relevant assessments. The results showed that PAA alleviated EMT in RTE cells and mitigated the progression of renal fibrosis by modulating the SPRY2/ERK pathway.

Our study provided evidence that PAA could alleviate EMT in RTE cells by modulating the SPRY2/ERK pathway. This result implied that PAA may be a promising approach for treating renal fibrosis, offering new therapeutic insights for advancing treatment strategies for patients with renal fibrosis.

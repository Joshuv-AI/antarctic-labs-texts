Screening of putative inhibitors for cytotoxin VacA from Helicobacter pylori
Authors: Chao Liu, Chongxing Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCytotoxin VacA, a major virulence factor of Helicobacter pylori—the principal etiological agent of gastric cancer—represents a compelling therapeutic target. Pharmacological inhibition of VacA through small-molecule agents offers a putative strategy to substantially mitigate gastric carcinogenesis risk. Notably, contemporary medical practice lacks a single “specific drug” capable of independently eradicating H. pylori, with current management limited to anti-inflammatory regimens. In this context, drug repurposing has gained prominence as a cost-efficient paradigm in pharmaceutical innovation, optimizing resource allocation during the discovery phase. The present investigation thus focuses on identifying novel small-molecule inhibitors specifically targeting the VacA protein. Our multi-tiered screening methodology integrates computational and biophysical approaches: molecular docking, ADMET, Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations and MM/PBSA calculations with the ZINC15 database constituting our primary compound source.Following rigorous virtual screening, seven lead candidates emerged based on AutoDock Vina scoring metrics. These were subjected to extended molecular dynamics simulations to validate binding affinity and conformational resilience under physiological conditions. Subsequent pharmacokinetic profiling benchmarked key parameters against established drug-likeness criteria, while energy decomposition analysis precisely mapped critical protein-ligand interaction hotspots within the VacA binding pocket. This systematic investigation not only identifies putative small-molecule inhibitors of cytotoxin VacA but also delineates a rational framework for virulence factor-targeted therapy against H. pylori infection. Introduction

Helicobacter pylori, a bacterium infecting nearly half the global population, stands as the foremost risk factor for gastric cancer development1. Familial cohorts undergoing H. pylori eradication therapy exhibit significantly reduced gastric cancer incidence2. While antibiotics such as Amoxicillin and Clarithromycin remain standard treatments, escalating antibiotic resistance has rendered current therapeutic approaches suboptimal. Recognizing this urgent public health challenge, the World Health Organization has designated H. pylori as a high-priority pathogen requiring accelerated research, as antibiotic-resistant strains pose the most critical threat to human health globally3. Enhanced understanding and innovative management strategies for H. pylori infections hold transformative potential for public health, underscoring the imperative for continued scientific investigation and therapeutic development in this vital domain.

Our analysis of large cohorts representing diverse populations revealed that VacA serological responses to Helicobacter pylori correlate with an elevated risk of colorectal cancer4, underscoring VacA’s pivotal role in H. pylori pathogenesis. Mechanistically, VacA induces epithelial cell vacuolation, modulates mitochondrial membrane rupture to trigger cytochrome c release, and alters apoptotic pathways and cellular signaling cascades5. Beyond these cellular effects, VacA transverses epithelial barriers to suppress T-cell proliferation, thereby dysregulating immune responses6. At the genetic level, VacA stimulates transient increases in mitochondrial translocase activity while promoting accumulation of transcription/DNA maintenance factors (TFAM) and mitochondrial DNA polymerase (POLG)7—phenomena associated with severe gastric lesions. This genetic impact is further evidenced by VacA’s inhibition of store-operated calcium entry (SOCE), which blocks NFAT nuclear translocation and downregulates T-cell proliferation8. The bacterium’s morphological plasticity—spiral forms enabling motility and spherical forms facilitating adherence to gastric mucus—enhances its invasiveness9. Critically, VacA promotes intracellular reservoir formation, enabling H. pylori survival in gastric environments even post-eradication therapy10. Additionally, VacA triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress, inducing autophagic cell death in gastric epithelial cells11. Recent structural studies provide compelling validation through cryo-EM reconstruction of VacA’s low-temperature architecture, solidifying the mechanistic basis for these multifaceted pathogenic roles12. Collectively, these findings establish VacA as a central virulence factor with implications spanning cellular, immunological, and genetic dimensions of H. pylori pathogenesis (Fig. 1A).

Robust rationale for selecting VacA over other targets has been found:1 Pathogenic Centrality: As a key virulence factor driving gastric carcinogenesis, VacA directly contributes to epithelial damage, immune evasion (e.g., T-cell suppression), and mitochondrial dysfunction. Urease primarily aids acid adaptation, while CagA modulates cytoskeletal dynamics—VacA’s multifaceted pathogenic profile offers broader intervention potential.2 Structural & Functional Uniqueness: Cryo-EM structures reveal oligomerization-dependent activation, enabling rational inhibitor design targeting protomer interfaces. Molecular dynamics simulations confirm stable drug-binding pockets in oligomeric states. 3 Clinical Relevance: Families undergoing H. pylori eradication show reduced gastric cancer incidence, underscoring the therapeutic value of targeting virulence factors like VacA rather than basal metabolic enzymes. Moreover, VacA is drug-accessible during infection mainly through these three aspects: 1 Secretory Pathways: VacA is secreted via type III secretion systems, localizing to gastric epithelial surfaces where inhibitors can access extracellular domains. 2 Oligomerization Dynamics: Functional assays confirm oligomerization is critical for vacuolation and immune modulation. Computational screening prioritized compounds that disrupt oligomeric interfaces, validated through MM/PBSA binding energy calculations. 3 Conformational Stability: Simulations demonstrate VacA maintains active conformations for under gastric pH, providing a therapeutic window for small-molecule engagement.

The ZINC15 database has emerged as a pivotal resource in the discovery of small-molecule therapeutics for major diseases including H. pylori infection and gastric cancer13. Researchers have utilized this platform to identify eight antibacterial compounds, with two demonstrating potent broad-spectrum activity against antibiotic-resistant determinants in Escherichia coli14—a compelling demonstration of its utility in combating drug-resistant pathogens. Beyond antimicrobial discovery, the ZINC15 database has been instrumental in advancing computational drug design methodologies. For instance, training on its extensive repository of 13.6 billion small molecules enabled the development of a rapid, high-precision deep docking framework capable of processing billions of molecular structures15. This innovation has become a cornerstone in modern drug development pipelines, significantly enhancing efficiency and accuracy. A notable application occurred during the 2019 COVID-19 pandemic, where researchers screened 30,000 compounds from the ZINC15 library and identified two promising inhibitors targeting the Mpro enzyme, offering critical insights for antiviral therapy16. Such examples underscore the database’s transformative impact on accelerating therapeutic innovation. Consequently, the ZINC15 library has evolved into an indispensable tool in drug design, substantially reducing development costs while enabling breakthroughs in treating complex diseases. Its continued utilization will remain crucial in the search for effective small-molecule therapeutics against H. pylori-associated pathologies and gastric cancer, cementing its role as a foundation for next-generation pharmaceutical research.

Virtual screening assumes paramount importance in contemporary drug development. As modern pharmaceutical discovery enters the big data era, candidate drug datasets have expanded exponentially17. The emergence of COVID-19 has notably accelerated advancements in computer-aided drug design (CADD), cementing virtual screening as a cornerstone methodology in therapeutic innovation18. This approach enables rapid screening, prediction, and optimization of vast compound libraries, significantly reducing both time and financial investments. Research institutions have successfully identified novel COVID-19 therapeutics through virtual screening, demonstrating superior efficacy compared to existing treatments19. For instance, a research team of University of Cambridge repurposed an established liver disease medication as a potential prophylactic against SARS-CoV-2 infection, exemplifying the strategic repurposing of pharmaceuticals for novel applications. With continuous advancements in computational technology and the proliferation of big data, virtual screening is poised to dominate drug development pipelines, offering more precise and efficient methodologies that further reduce development costs. Consequently, its utilization will remain critical in addressing challenges such as H. pylori infections or gastric cancer, where targeted small-molecule therapeutics are urgently needed.

MD simulation analysis

To investigate the interaction and binding conformation between the screened small molecule drugs and VacA protein, we performed a 100 ns all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulation of the protein-ligand complex using gromacs/2020.420. The average RMSD results of the 3 MD runs are presented in Fig. 2. To establish a control for comparison, we performed the same equilibration setup for the protein without any small molecules. It can be observed that the shaded area of the green curve (ZINC000012348428) becomes extremely broad in the later stages of the simulation. This intuitively illustrates that, although the average RMSD is 1.5 nm, across different independent runs(Fig. A3), the molecule might merely drift by 1.0 nm in some cases, while undergoing severe dissociation of up to 2.0 nm in others. This strongly corroborates our assessment that this molecule is an unstable false positive. In contrast, the red (ZINC000013923892) and brown (ZINC00005731542) curves exhibit excellent performance. Not only are their error bands exceptionally narrow (indicating high reproducibility), but their absolute RMSD values are also consistently suppressed below the black reference line. Given the premise of binding to a highly flexible protein, this demonstrates that these two ligands are not only stable themselves, but they also serve to stabilize the entire protein complex, making them highly probable putative candidate molecules. By comparing initial (0 ns) and final (100 ns) positions of trajectories using PyMol visualization, we confirmed that the regions occupied by small molecules exhibited remarkable positional stability, indicating robust drug-protein interaction stability—results are visually summarized in Fig. 3. Further quantitative analysis included root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) calculations, with the top-performing small molecule serving as a reference control. Findings revealed that all compounds except ZINC000012348428 maintained structural stability throughout the simulation period. Consistently stable radius of gyration (Rg) values demonstrated that the target protein preserved its compact folded conformation under physiological conditions (300 K, 100 ns). Complementary root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) analysis provided detailed insights into residue flexibility, particularly focusing on amino acids within the small molecule binding pocket. This multi-parameter approach—integrating trajectory comparison, RMSD/Rg/RMSF metrics, and visual validation—offers a rigorous framework for assessing both ligand stability and protein dynamics, while precisely mapping critical interaction sites for therapeutic optimization.

Discussion

The rapid advancement of computer-aided drug design (CADD) has increasingly gained traction in drug discovery due to two compelling advantages. First, CADD’s accelerated development enables the identification of compounds with enhanced therapeutic potential, as validated by subsequent experimental studies demonstrating robust efficacy22. Second, conventional drug development is hindered by lengthy timelines and exorbitant costs, which inflate medication prices and restrict access for patients, thereby exacerbating preventable morbidity and mortality23. These dual drivers have propelled CADD’s exponential growth.Through virtual screening, researchers rapidly pinpoint small molecules with high-affinity binding to target proteins. Molecular dynamics simulations then rigorously verify these interactions under physiological conditions24. Complementary MM/PBSA analysis dissects amino acid contributions throughout simulations, providing molecular insights to guide rational drug modification and next-generation compound optimization25.This integrated computational approach has yielded tangible breakthroughs, exemplified by the identification of a putative small-molecule inhibitor targeting the VacA protein from Helicobacter pylori—a key virulence factor implicated in gastric carcinogenesis. Structurally, adopting the established nomenclature by Zhang et al.12, the VacA protomer derived from the 6NYF cryo-EM map consists of the N-terminal p33 domain (amino acids 27–299), which primarily drives pore formation, and the C-terminal p55 domain (amino acids 335–811), which dictates receptor binding. Our computational evaluations reveal that the putative inhibitors do not bind arbitrarily; rather, their binding pockets are strategically located at the interdomain interface. By simultaneously interacting with the p33 domain and the structurally stable proximal part of the p55 domain (e.g., residues ASP346 and ASN382), these small molecules act as cross-domain bridges. This specific spatial orientation suggests a mechanism whereby the ligands stabilize the hexameric core architecture, thereby potentially disrupting the conformational dynamics required for VacA’s pathogenic oligomerization and cellular internalization.Such advancements not only enhance therapeutic precision but also offer clinicians novel strategies to combat gastric cancer and related gastrointestinal diseases, underscoring CADD’s transformative potential in modern pharmacology.

Furthermore, it is important to note that the cryo-EM structure 6NYF lacks the coordinates for the highly flexible interdomain linker spanning residues 300–334, which connects K299 of the p33 domain and Q335 of the p55 domain. Our molecular docking and dynamic analyses confirm that the identified putative ligands exclusively interact with the well-resolved, stable secondary structures at the interdomain interface. Therefore, they are lodged well away from this missing, highly flexible central region, ensuring that the absence of these coordinates in the initial model does not compromise the reliability of our binding affinity and stability predictions. Moreover, this interdomain targeting strategy represents a significant paradigm shift from previous screening efforts. Earlier studies primarily utilized the crystal structure of the isolated p55 domain (PDB ID: 2QV3), which limited the discovery of inhibitors to p55-specific intra-domain pockets. By leveraging the near-atomic hexameric 6NYF structure, our study successfully identifies ligands that exploit the previously inaccessible p33-p55 interdomain interface. This fundamental structural distinction explains the differential binding patterns between prior knowledge-based inhibitors and our current candidates, highlighting the advantage of using oligomeric templates for discovering cross-domain stabilizing agents.

As artificial intelligence and machine learning continue to advance, the future of Computer-Aided Drug Design (CADD) appears increasingly promising. This methodology has already demonstrated notable success in treating conditions such as cancer, HIV, and COVID-1926,27. The expanding volume of available data and computational capacity is poised to significantly enhance the precision of virtual screening processes, thereby facilitating the identification of more efficacious therapeutic compounds. Moreover, the integration of CADD with emerging technologies—including high-throughput screening and CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing—has the potential to revolutionize the landscape of drug discovery and development. Furthermore, the convergence of CADD with patient-specific omics data (e.g., genomics, proteomics) could substantially advance personalized medicine by enabling the design of individualized treatment regimens. In summary, CADD has already exhibited significant potential in drug discovery, and its trajectory remains highly promising as technological advancements continue to unfold.

Materials and methods

Protein preparation

The 3D structure of VacA (PDB ID: 6NYF) was downloaded from PDB database and then processed using AutodockTools-1.5.7. This involves removing water from the structure, and then adding polar hydrogen atoms since the structure does not contain hydrogen atoms. The VacA protein is then set as the receptor and Gasteiger charges are added to generate a pdbqt file, which completes the protein preprocessing.Notably, the generated hexameric VacA model was constructed without the missing interdomain region. Because residues 300–334 are highly flexible and are entirely absent from the 6NYF cryo-EM structural template (located between K299 of the p33 domain and Q335 of the p55 domain), we intentionally excluded them. We did not arbitrarily model this disordered loop to prevent introducing structural artifacts into our ligand screening protocol.

Compound retrieval and preparation

The ZINC database, which is one of the largest libraries of organic small molecules, originates from the labs of Irwin and Shoichet at the Department of Pharmaceutical Chemistry, University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). Neutral molecules that are commercially available and Lead-like drugs are selected in this study. Approximately 180,000 small molecules were selected after a rigorous screening process. The 180,000 small molecules were pre-processed using POAP software with GAFF force fields, followed by hydrogenation of the small molecules, detection of torsional bonds, and set as ligands to generate pdbqt files, thus completing the pre-processing of the small molecules.

Molecular docking

Oligomerization is critical for the normal function of VacA. By reviewing relevant literature and the high-resolution 6NYF model, we defined the interface bridging the N-terminal p33 domain (residues 27–299) and the C-terminal p55 domain (residues 335–811) as the primary targeted docking region. Although the central coordinates of docking may not be highly precise, it is only necessary to determine the docking box that encloses the maximum region where the ligand could potentially bind. This process results in the determination of the dimensions and coordinates of the enclosing box. The specific binding site is determined using the open-source virtual screening tool Autodock Vina for predicting the binding affinity between the protein and small molecules28. The binding affinities of the 18,000 small molecules with 6NYF protein are predicted and sorted, and the top 7 small molecules are selected for subsequent screening.

The prediction of ADMET properties

ADME refers to the process of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of drugs after they enter the human body, and is used to predict drug efficacy, while toxicity (T) is used to predict drug toxicity. The SwissADME server is an online, no-cost tool that can forecast the ADME characteristics of small molecules29. The extent of toxicity can be determined by the degree of which a chemical substance is toxic to humans or animals and may damage the quality of organs. ADMETlab 2.0 (https://admetmesh.scbdd.com/) is an enhanced version of the widely used ADMETlab, which is used for systematic evaluation of ADMET properties, as well as some physicochemical properties and drug chemistry friendliness30. These two web servers can be used for predicting ADMET properties.

Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation

The system has achieved thermodynamic equilibrium at the specified temperature and pressure following completion of the two-phase equilibration protocol. With equilibrium established, positional restraints can now be systematically released to initiate molecular dynamics (MD) simulations for data acquisition. Key structural metrics—including root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) for conformational stability assessment, radius of gyration (Rg) for compactness evaluation, and root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) for residue flexibility analysis—will be visualized using xmgrace software as per established protocols31. This methodological framework ensures rigorous quantification of protein dynamics and ligand-binding characteristics under physiologically relevant conditions, providing essential data for subsequent mechanistic interpretation and therapeutic optimization.

Our system incorporates the eukaryotic gastric epithelial membrane model to replicate the physiological lipid environment. This aligns with experimental evidence showing VacA’s α5-helix hexamer inserts into host membranes to form ion channels, disrupting cellular ion balance—a mechanism validated through cryo-EM and electrophysiological studies. We constructed a heterogeneous membrane model with VacA’s hexameric pore pre-inserted, using CHARMM-GUI to add DPPC lipid bilayer membrane to the system and meanwhile proper number of sodium counter ions was added to neutralize this system.

Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) quantifies the average spatial displacement of atoms across different structural states, serving as a critical metric to assess conformational stability. For proteins, RMSD calculations reveal the mean positional deviation between simulated conformations and the initial reference structure, averaged over all atoms. The trajectory of RMSD values—both for the protein and its bound ligand—acts as a key indicator of simulation stability, with convergent trends signifying equilibrium attainment.

Radius of gyration (Rg) provides a quantitative measure of protein compactness. A stable Rg profile throughout the simulation indicates sustained folding integrity, as unfolded states typically exhibit temporal fluctuations in Rg.

Root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) captures the temporal average of atomic positional variations, directly mapping residue flexibility and dynamic behavior across the simulation. While RMSD trajectories diagnose global equilibrium status, RMSF profiles offer granular insights into localized flexibility patterns—particularly at critical sites such as ligand-binding pockets. This tripartite analysis (RMSD for stability, Rg for compactness, RMSF for flexibility) constitutes a robust framework for evaluating protein dynamics and optimizing ligand-binding interactions in molecular modeling studies.

MM/PBSA energy decomposition

To quantify the contribution of individual amino acids, we compute the difference in binding free energy between the bound and unbound states of solvated molecular pairs, or alternatively, compare the free energies of a single molecule across distinct solvated conformations. However, the dominant energy contribution stems from solvent-solvent interactions, where the total energy exhibits significantly greater fluctuation amplitudes compared to binding energy. This disparity necessitates extended simulation times for convergence. Consequently, we decompose the total solution-phase binding free energy into molecular mechanical components (binding free energy in vacuum) and solvation energy contributions, which are calculated independently32.

Conclusions

Helicobacter pylori serves as the principal etiological agent in gastric carcinogenesis, with nearly half of the global population harboring this pathogen. Despite Nobel laureate Barry Marshall’s seminal identification of H. pylori in the human stomach in 2005, there remain no FDA-approved therapeutics specifically targeting the VacA virulence factor. This study addresses this unmet need through systematic in silico drug screening of the ZINC15 compound library, focusing on the VacA protein as a key therapeutic target.The virtual screening protocol was rigorously validated using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, with comprehensive evaluation of ADMET (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity) properties. Through multi-faceted validation incorporating pharmacokinetic profiling and structural stability analysis, five lead compounds were identified as putative candidates due to one false positive ligand. These molecules underwent MM/PBSA energy decomposition analysis to elucidate key amino acid interactions, providing critical insights for subsequent drug development.The identified small molecules represent highly putative drug candidates for further experimental validation in gastric cancer treatment paradigms. This computational approach establishes a robust framework for targeting VacA-mediated pathogenesis, offering a pathway toward precision therapeutics against H. pylori-associated malignancies.

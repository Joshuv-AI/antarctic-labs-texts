Prognostic and immunological characterization of elderly acute myeloid leukemia evaluated by butyrate metabolism-related genes
Authors: Yiwei Li, Liming Shan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe diagnostic and therapeutic value of butyrate metabolism-related genes (BMRGs) in cancer has been widely applied, however, their prognosis and potential therapeutic value in elderly acute myeloid leukemia (EAML) have not been fully elucidated.MethodsThe clinical information and gene expression data of the EAML patients were obtained from the GEO database and TCGA database. Firstly, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and univariate Cox regression were used to screen for BMRGs significantly associated with the prognosis of EAML patients. A prognostic risk stratification model for EAML patients was then constructed based on multivariate COX regression. Prognostic value of the prognostic risk stratification model was confirmed by Kaplan-Meier curves and validated on an independent external queue dataset. Meanwhile, the biological pathways, immune microenvironment and drug sensitivity of EAML patients with different prognostic risk stratification were further analyzed.Results4 BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2 and SERINC5) were ultimately identified as genes associated with the prognosis of EAML. A prognostic risk model was constructed based on multivariate COX regression and 4 BMRGs. The Kaplan-Meier curves and independent external queue dataset confirm the prognostic model’s excellent diagnostic value. Immune infiltration analysis’ results confirm 4 BMRGs are correlated with various immune cells significantly.ConclusionThis was the first comprehensive analysis highlighting the prognostic significance of BMRGs in EAML. The findings not only reveal novel gene biomarkers with potential clinical value but also offer a theoretical foundation for advancing personalized treatment strategies in EAML.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03778-4.Keywords: Elderly acute myeloid leukemia, Butyrate-metabolism, Prognosis, Immune

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is a hematological malignancy characterized by impaired differentiation and clonal proliferation of myeloid progenitor cells, resulting in their accumulation in the peripheral blood and bone marrow. AML predominantly affects the elderly, with a median age at diagnosis of 68 years [1]. In clinical practice, drug therapy is an important treatment method for cancer [2]. The development of hypomethylating agents (HMAs), such as decitabine and azacitidine [3], as well as combination therapies involving HMAs with venetoclax or ivosidenib for IDH1-mutated AML [4, 5], has led to notable therapeutic advances in elderly acute myeloid leukemia (EAML). Nevertheless, the prognosis of EAML remains poor, primarily due to the toxicity of intensive chemotherapy and the high prevalence of comorbidities in this population [6]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying EAML and to identify novel therapeutic targets to improve patient outcomes.

Butyrate, a short-chain fatty acid (SCFA), is produced by gut microbiota through the fermentation of dietary fiber and serves as a primary energy source for colonocytes [7]. Butyrate metabolism has been shown to play a critical role in tumor progression and immune regulation [8]. It also inhibits cancer cell migration by inducing autophagy and apoptosis [9]. In hematological malignancies, including AML, butyrate appears to exert important biological effects. Compared to healthy individuals, AML patients exhibit significant alterations in gut microbiota diversity and composition, along with notable changes in butyrate-related metabolic pathways [10, 11]. However, the role of butyrate metabolism in EAML remains poorly understood. Investigating butyrate metabolic pathways in EAML may offer valuable insights into its molecular pathogenesis and uncover novel therapeutic strategies [12]. To date, no systematic studies have explored the involvement of butyrate metabolism-related genes in EAML.

In this study, we systematically investigated the prognostic significance of butyrate metabolism-related genes (BMRGs) in patients with EAML. We performed consensus clustering, constructed a prognostic gene signature, conducted functional enrichment analyses, and explored the association between BMRGs and the immune microenvironment in EAML. These findings enhance the current prognostic evaluation framework and offer novel insights into potential therapeutic strategies for EAML.

Materials and methods

EAML dataset preprocessing

Two independent datasets, GSE37642 and TCGA_LAML, were included in this study. The GSE37642 dataset was obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), and the TCGA_LAML dataset was downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Patients aged ≥ 60 years were selected for prognostic analysis. GSE37642 was used as the training cohort, while TCGA_LAML served as the validation cohort. Raw microarray data were processed, and gene expression levels were assessed using R software (version 4.4.1) [13]. A total of 329 butyrate metabolism-related genes (BMRGs) were identified from the Molecular Signature Database (MsigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp) using “butyric acid” as the keyword.

Identification of BMRGs with potential prognostic value in EAML patients

Firstly, the surv_cutpoint function in R package “surviminer” was used to calculate the optional cutoff value of per gene in all samples and the samples were classified into high- and low-expression groups accordingly. Univariate Cox regression analysis was then performed to identified genes linked to survival in GSE37642 dataset, validated using TCGA_LAML dataset. Overlapping molecules of genes associated with survival and BMRGs were considered candidates. The prognosis of the selected candidates was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier curves followed by log-rank tests through the “survminer” package. Afterwards, the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) of the “glmnet” software was used to analyze and determine the most valuable prognostic genes for EAML patients, and ten-fold crossover was used to validate the ideal values for determining penalty parameters [14].

Consensus clustering analysis of survival related BMRGs

EAML patients’ Consensus clustering analysis is based on all EAML patient samples in the GSE37642 dataset. The R’ ConsensusClusterPlus package was used to determine the reliability and number of clusters obtained from consensus clustering analysis (k = 10 and 1000 repeats). The consensus matrix and relative changes in the area under the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curve are used to determine the optimal number of clusters obtained from consensus clustering analysis. Then the “ggplot2” package was used for principal component analysis (PCA) to confirm the number of clusters, and Kaplan Meier curves were plotted to confirm the different subtypes’ prognostic value in EAML patients after clustering classification [15].

A BMRGs prognostic model constructed and validated

Based on GSE37642 dataset, multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression was used by us to analyze the coefficients of BMRGs, and the risk scores for prognostic signature of patients were constructed based on the coefficients. The detailed formula for risk score is as follows: ∑Exp_i.β_i = Exp1* β1 + Exp2 *β2 + Expi * βi, where Exp and β represent expression levels and coefficients of selected BMRGs, respectively. Patients were classified into high- and low-risk subtypes according to the median risk scores. Kaplan-Meier analysis followed by log-rank tests was employed for prognostic analysis using the “survminer” package [16].

Pathway enrichment analysis

Based on GSE37642 dataset, genes with correlation coefficients | R |>0.3 obtained from Spearman correlation analysis were considered to be significantly correlated with risk scores. The clusterProfiler package was used to perform Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional analysis and Gene Ontology (GO) functional analysis on these genes significantly correlated with risk scores. The c2.cp.v7.2.symbols.gtm file is used for gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). Normalized P-value < 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.2 and were the thresholds for selecting differential pathways [17].

Differences in tumor immune microenvironment among different risk groups

The CIBERSORT software package was used to analyze the infiltration of 22 immune cells in EAML in the GSE37642 dataset. We analyzed the Spearman relationship between immune cells and BMRGs and plotted a network diagram. By the ggplot2 of the R software, the results were visualized [18].

Estimation of therapeutic drugs

Based on GSE37642 dataset, the oncoPredict software package was used to predict EAML drug activity and evaluate the differences in drug activity among low-risk score group and high-risk score group [19].

Statistical analyses

To compare the two variables, we used t-tests or Wilcoxon tests. To evaluate survival differences, Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests were employed. BMRGs were evaluated for their prognostic value using univariate and multivariate Cox regression models. The R program was used for all of the methods involved in this investigation. The threshold for statistical significance was P < 0.05 [20].

Results

Identification of BMRG with potential prognostic value in EAML patients

Based on GSE37642 dataset and TCGA_LAML dataset, 234 genes were associated with prognosis significantly in EAML (P < 0.05), after taking the intersection of 234 genes and 329 BMRGs, a total of 6 BMRGs were associated with prognosis significantly in EAML (P < 0.05). LASSO analysis was used to screen 6 BMRGs (Based on GSE37642), resulting in 4 BMRGs (CCT5 [hazard ratio (HR): 1.93, 95% confidence interval (95% CI): 1.13–3.30, P = 0.0161], CYP19A1 [HR: 2.68, 95% CI 1.33–5.42, P = 0.00598], SECISBP2 [HR: 0.52, 95% CI 0.34–0.78, P = 0.00185] and SERINC5 [HR: 0.57, 95% CI 0.37–0.90, P = 0.015]) that was associated with prognosis (Table S1, Figure S1). The Kaplan-Meier curves confirmed 4 gene’s prognostic value (Fig. 1A, B). In EAML, high expression of CCT5 and CYP19A1 were linked with poorer prognosis and high expression of SECISBP2 and SERINC5 were linked with better prognosis.

Identification of BMRGs with potential prognostic value in EAML patients. The Kaplan-Meier curves confirmed each gene’s prognostic value (A, B)

Consensus clustering analysis of prognostic survival related 4 BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2, SERINC5)

By analyzing the cumulative distribution function (CDF) and CDF difference curve, the consensus clustering analysis results indicate that k = 2 seems to be more stable than k = 3–6 (Figure S2A-B). PCA was further used to verify the reliability of the clustering analysis results, the results showed that compared to k = 3–5, the samples at k = 2 exhibited clustering together and high similarity (Figure S3). Thus, we stratified the EAML patients into 2 clusters. The Kaplan-Meier curves confirmed EAML patients in cluster 2 had a better prognosis than cluster 1 (Figure S2C).

A BMRGs prognostic model constructed and validated

A prognostic risk score for EAML patients was established based on the expression levels and corresponding coefficients of four selected BMRGs. The risk score was calculated using the following formula: Risk score = (0.97 × CYP19A1) + (0.49 × CCT5) + (− 0.62 × SECISBP2) + (− 0.47 × SERINC5). Patients were stratified into low-risk and high-risk groups according to the median risk score. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high-risk group had significantly poorer overall survival compared to those in the low-risk group (Fig. 2A). The prognostic characteristics were then validated in TCGA_LAML dataset, showing the similar result of GSE37642 dataset (Figure S4). These analysis results demonstrate the stability and reliability of the prognostic features of EAML. We also examined the risk score predictions for 1-year, 2-year, and 4-year mortality rates, in the GSE37642 dataset, the area under the curve (AUC) values of the risk model were 0.77, 0.80, and 0.77 at 1, 2, and 4 years, respectively. In the TCGA_LAML dataset, the AUC values of the risk model were 0.83, 0.83, and 0.91 at 1, 2, and 4 years, respectively (Figure S5). The risk score was identified as an independent risk factor for the prognosis of EAML patients by multivariate Cox regression analysis (Fig. 2B).

These analysis results demonstrate the stability and reliability of the prognostic features of EAML, and the risk score was identified as an independent risk factor for the prognosis of EAML patients by multivariate Cox regression analysis (P < 0.01 represents significance) (B)

Identification of the prognostic model-related biological pathways

616 significantly negatively correlated genes and 893 significantly positively correlated genes were selected (Table S2). The results of the KEGG pathways and GO are shown in Fig. 3A, B. The functional analysis results were mainly enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, mitochondrial ATP synthesis coupled electron transport.

Differences in tumor immune microenvironment among different risk groups

The four prognostic BMRGs were also significantly associated with immune cell infiltration in EAML (Fig. 4). Specifically, CCT5 and CYP19A1 exhibited significant negative correlations with the infiltration levels of CD8⁺ T cells and B cells, whereas SECISBP2 and SERINC5 showed significant positive correlations with the infiltration of these immune cell types.

Estimation of therapeutic drugs

Using the oncoPredict software package in R, we evaluated and calculated 198 drugs’ drug sensitivity in EAML. We identified 4 drugs with significant differences in IC50 between the low-risk and high-risk groups (Entinostat, Epirubicin, Trametinib, Docetaxel) (Fig. 5A). Among them, the SERINC5 gene was associated with Epirubicin, Trametinib and Docetaxel was positively correlated, the CCT5 gene was associated with Docetaxel is negatively correlated (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

EAML refers to AML diagnosed in patients over 60 years of age. The prognosis of EAML remains poor, with a median survival of approximately 2–3 years. Currently, there is a lack of effective predictive biomarkers in clinical practice to assess treatment outcomes in EAML. As a result, some patients with unfavorable prognoses fail to receive timely and appropriate interventions, adversely affecting their survival [21]. Butyrate has been reported to play an important role in the prognosis of cancer patients [8]. In addition, butyrate plays various important roles in the immune system and maintaining intestinal homeostasis [22]. Butyrate is considered crucial in cellular energy metabolism and the digestive system, providing energy to the colonic mucosal layer and regulating inflammation and gene expression [23]. Butyrate can inhibit the growth of harmful bacteria, promote the proliferation of beneficial bacteria and optimize the intestinal microbiota environment [24]. According to reports, there have been significant changes in the diversity and composition of the gut microbiota in AML patients, as well as significant changes in the butyrate related metabolic pathway [10, 11]. Therefore, studying genes related to the butyrate metabolic pathway may provide valuable insights into its molecular pathogenesis. In this study, we identified four BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2 and SERINC5) that are significantly associated with EAML prognosis. These genes may provide valuable insights into the molecular pathogenesis of EAML and reveal new therapeutic strategies.

Based on 4 BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2 and SERINC5), we constructed a prognostic signature based on these 4 BMRGs, and the patients can be divided into high- and low-risk groups according to the risk scores for prognostic signature. The risk scores for 1-year, 2-year, and 4-year mortality rates were further predicted, in the GSE37642 dataset, the AUC values of the risk model were 0.77, 0.80, and 0.77 at 1, 2, and 4 years, respectively. In the TCGA_LAML dataset, the AUC values of the risk model were 0.83, 0.83, and 0.91 at 1, 2, and 4 years, respectively, exhibiting superior performance than some other signatures in predicting the prognosis. In previous studies, a necroptosis-related risk score model was constructed in AML, as for the training cohort, the AUCs of 1-, 3- and 5 years were 0.724, 0.745, and 0.745, respectively. And the AUCs of the testing cohort was 0.654, 0.674, and 0.655, respectively [25]. A butyrate metabolism-related risk score model was constructed in colorectal cancer, and AUC was 0.739, 0.753, and 0.727 for the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS [8]. As well, the risk score can be act as an independent risk factor for prognosis of EAML patients. Our prognostic signature may be severed as a valuable complement for the prognosis evaluation of EAML. However, an increased data is needed to analyze the interaction between our prognostic signature and existing risk stratification.

Our study therefore found that 4 BMRGs play an important role in EAML’s immune regulation, providing EAML’s immunotherapy a theoretical basis. We found that CCT5 and CYP19A1 were significantly negatively correlated with immune infiltration of CD8 + T cells and B cells, while SECISBP2 and SERINC5 were significantly positively correlated with immune infiltration of CD8 + T cells and B cells. In EAML, high expression of CCT5 and CYP19A1 were linked with poorer prognosis and high expression of SECISBP2 and SERINC5 were linked with better prognosis B cells maintain health by producing mature antibody responses and innate-antibody like responses, as well as presenting antigens and secreting cytokines as immune modulators [26]. In different types of cancer, such as non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the number and activity status of B cells have been proven to be directly related to the prognosis of patients. In some studies, an increase in the number of B cells is associated with longer survival in patients, which may be related to the role of B cells in promoting immune responses [27]. There is a close relationship between immune cells and the prognosis of diseases, especially in cancer treatment [28]. Multiple studies have shown that the abundance and functional status of immune cells have a significant impact on the prognosis of patients [29]. In the study of advanced plasma ovarian cancer, the abundance of activated memory CD4 + T cells and other immune cells is closely related to the prognosis of patients. Patients with higher abundance usually have better prognosis and longer survival [30]. When CD8 + T cells recognize their antigens and are activated, they have three main mechanisms for killing infected malignant cells. activated CD8 + T cells express FasL on the cell surface, which binds to the receptor Fas on the target cell surface. This binding leads to the trimerization of Fas molecules on the surface of target cells, thereby aggregating signaling molecules together. These signaling molecules activate the caspase cascade, leading to apoptosis of target cells [31]. Meanwhile, immune infiltration of CD8 + T cells and B cells had been reported to be associated with AML disease [32, 33], T cells and B cells were both lymphocytes and important components of the body’s immune response function [34]. The immune response mediated by T cells was called “cellular immunity”, which refers to the differentiation, proliferation and transformation of T cells into sensitized T cells after being stimulated by antigens. T cells had direct killing effects on antigens and synergistic killing effects on cytokines released by sensitized T cells. Some cytokines released by T cells can promote the activation of B cells [35]. Therefore, the high expression of CCT5 and CYP19A1 may inhibits the activation of B cells and enhance the immune response mediated by T cells and B cells in patients by activating T cells and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, the high expression of SECISBP2 and SERINC5 may promote the activation of B cells and enhance the immune response mediated by T cells and B cells in patients by activating T cells and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Further in-depth research is needed on the specific immune mechanisms.

Targeted therapy for genes and related signaling pathways with oncogene alterations will continue to be an important model for cancer treatment in the foreseeable future, as cancer is a genetic disease driven by oncogene alterations [2]. In addition, the focus of precision oncology is increasingly shifting towards liquid biopsy, as it is non-invasive and can be repeated at different time points to help monitor disease progression [36]. Our study identified four BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2 and SERINC5), which may be potential therapeutic targets for EAML. In addition, the next step is to validate the expression of four BMRGs in peripheral blood samples based on a prospective cohort, and incorporate them into liquid biopsy panels for cancer detection, thereby translating our mechanistic insights into clinically actionable dynamic patient management assays.

This study has several inevitable limitations. First, the analysis was primarily based on retrospective data from the public database, which may be subject to potential confounding factors; thus, prospective clinical studies combining immunohistochemistry and PCR analysis can further validate the prognostic value of these BMRGs. Secondly, the gene selection in this study is based on traditional machine learning methods, which may limit the overall prognostic accuracy of the proposed features. Therefore, it is necessary to combine multiple bioinformatics methods to further study the impact of genes on cancer (For example, using Generative Adversarial Networks to model and enhance gene expression data of BMRGs. This method provides a powerful tool for generating realistic synthetic datasets, improving feature extraction, and identifying subtle expression patterns that traditional methods may miss). Third, data on BMRG expression in EAML patients undergoing immunotherapy were not available, making our assessment of the relationship between the risk signature and immunotherapy response indirect. Therefore, future prospective studies involving EAML patients receiving immunotherapy are essential to confirm the clinical applicability and predictive value of our prognostic model. In addition, further in vitro and animal experiments are needed to validate the functions of the four BMRGs (CCT5, CYP19A1, SECISBP2 and SERINC5) in EAML, as well as the roles of the four BMRGs in supplements/butyrate and gut microbiota.

Conclusion

In summary, this study is the first to comprehensively evaluate the prognostic significance of BMRGs in EAML. Four prognostic BMRGs were identified, and a risk model based on these genes was constructed and validated using an independent external dataset. The prognostic signature was shown to be an independent risk factor in EAML patients. Additionally, the four BMRGs were found to be closely associated with the immune microenvironment of EAML. This was the first comprehensive analysis highlighting the prognostic significance of BMRGs in EAML, revealing potential prognostic gene markers in EAML, which will help develop new prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in clinical practice. And it provides a theoretical basis for promoting personalized treatment strategies in EAML.

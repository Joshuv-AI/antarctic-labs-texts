Efficacy and safety of transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation in the treatment of moderate-to-severe itch after burns on limbs (TE-ITCH): protocol for a multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled clinical trial
Authors: Xue Heng, Lei Cao, Wei Zhu, Ling Zhou, Linjun Li, Ning Li, Gaoxing Luo, Haisheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionItching is a common and distressing clinical manifestation following burns, which severely impairs patient quality of life. Current pharmacotherapy for post-burn pruritus is limited by low efficacy and frequent side effects. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), a non-invasive neuromodulation technique, is widely used for analgesia and exhibits a favourable safety and tolerability profile. Although itch and pain are both nociceptive sensations, they constitute distinct sensory modalities. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of TENS for treating moderate to severe post-burn itching.Methods and analysisThis multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled clinical trial intends to recruit 382 patients with moderate-to-severe itching (the Worst Itch Numeric Rating Scale (WINRS) score of ≥4) after burns from multiple burn centres across China for TENS treatment. The recruitment process began in April 2025 and is expected to be concluded in October 2027. Eligible patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either TENS stimulation (experimental group) or placebo stimulation (control group). The intervention will be administered once daily for 30 min across seven consecutive days. The primary outcome is the 7-day itch improvement rate, defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a reduction of at least 50% in the WINRS score on day 7 of the intervention, assessed on day 8, compared with their prerandomisation baseline. Secondary outcomes include the severity of itching, neuropathic symptoms and signs, sleep disturbance, quality of life, scar severity and safety. Exploratory endpoints consist of neuroimaging markers and scar function parameters. A physician and the patient will complete all assessments during the intervention phase and at the follow-up visit on day 14.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval for this multicentre study was granted by the leading ethics committee, the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University (KY2024217). All authors are affiliated with the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500099822.

Introduction

Itching is one of the most common clinical manifestations after burns, significantly affecting patients’ quality of life. According to reports, the incidence of itching among severe adult burn patients at discharge and 6 months, 1 year and 2 years postdischarge is 93%, 86%, 83% and 73%, respectively.1 Another report indicates a 37.3% incidence of itching among paediatric patients with smaller burn areas.2

Currently, antihistamines constitute the most common first-line treatment for post-burn itching in clinical settings, including topical antihistamine creams (such as doxepin cream) or oral antihistamines (like cetirizine and loratadine). However, a study evaluating the efficacy of antihistamines in burn patients with itching revealed that the effective rate of this treatment approach is only 20%,3 possibly due to the predominance of neuropathic factors in post-burn itching. Although antiepileptic drugs (gabapentin and pregabalin) could partially alleviate post-burn itching, they are associated with a higher incidence of adverse reactions such as ataxia and dizziness and are not recommended for children. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a non-pharmacological approach to treat post-burn itching.

Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), also known as peripheral nerve coarse fibre electrical stimulation, is a neuromodulation therapy that delivers specific low-frequency pulsed currents through the skin. It is commonly used for the management of neuropathic pain. Itch and pain are common types of nociceptive sensations, but they represent distinct sensory modalities.4 Currently, there is only one clinical study on TENS5 and one on scrambler therapy for post-burn itching.6 These studies support the effectiveness of TENS in alleviating post-burn itching. However, they have limitations such as small sample sizes and the absence of placebo controls. Due to the scarcity of reports on TENS in burn patients, we retrospectively analysed 11 patients with moderate to severe post-burn itching who received TENS treatment. Results indicated that 60% of patients (n=7) experienced varying degrees of itching relief, with four achieving a relief rate exceeding 50%. No adverse events occurred during stimulation, which preliminarily supports the efficacy and safety of TENS for moderate to severe post-burn itching.

This multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled clinical trial will evaluate the efficacy and safety of TENS for treating moderate to severe post-burn pruritus. The study aims to improve quality of life for patients with burn-related itching and to establish a novel therapeutic approach for this condition.

Methods and design

Study design and patient enrolment

This study is a multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled trial, which is scheduled to commence patient enrolment in April 2025 and conclude in October 2027. The protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the Army Medical University (No. KY2024217) and seven other burn centres. All ethical approvals have been uploaded as online supplemental file 1. All participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrolment.

Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to either the TENS group or the placebo group in a 1:1 ratio. The study flowchart is presented in figure 1, and the schedule of enrolment, interventions, and assessments is detailed in online supplemental table S1. Adequate enrolment of participants is ensured through online and offline recruitment across all participating centres. This protocol adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines, and the trial is prospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500099822). Randomisation and blinding

Patients who meet the inclusion criteria will be randomly assigned to the sham group or the TENS group. Randomisation is centrally performed by one independent investigator according to a web-based randomisation procedure on an online Electronic Data Capture system. Randomisation will be stratified by sites with randomised permuted blocks of 4, 6, 8. The allocation ratio of the placebo and TENS group will be 1:1. The result of randomisation will be informed to the intervention administrator who enrolled this participant in the way of 'Ppogramme 1 or programme 2’. The intervention administrators and the outcome evaluators are two different individuals. The participant and outcome evaluators are concealed from the result of randomisation but are required to record the judgement of stimulation (TENS or placebo).

Interventions

TENS Group: Itching severity is assessed 1 day prior to enrolment. TENS treatment is started on the first post-enrolment day. Electrodes are positioned on normal skin surrounding the scar, with the anode proximal and the cathode distal. Stimulation parameters are 100 Hz frequency and 150 µs pulse width for 3 s, alternating with a 2 Hz frequency and 200 µs pulse width for 3 s. Treatment intensity, set between 15 and 20 mA, is adjusted to the maximum tolerable level that will not induce obvious muscle contraction. Each daily session lasts 30 min and is repeated for seven consecutive days.

Placebo group: Itching severity is assessed 1 day prior to enrolment. From the first day after enrolment, participants receive placebo stimulation with the same TENS device. The electrode placement matches that of the TENS group. The treatment intensity is initially set to 5 mA to produce a perceptible sensation and is then immediately reduced to 0 mA. All other stimulation parameters are identical to those used in the experimental group. Each placebo treatment session lasts 30 min and is administered once daily for seven consecutive days.

Basic treatment: Silicone, pressure, onion extract and laser treatment for scar are used according to the expert consensus.6 Other medications for itch, including antihistamines, opioid receptor agonists or antagonists, ondansetron, gabapentin, pregabalin and antidepressants, were not administered during the study period.

Outcome measurement

Primary outcomes

7-day itch improvement rate

The itch severity is evaluated by Itch Numerical Rating Scale (INRS) (range 0–10, NRS score 0 (None) or score 1–3 (Mild) or score 4–6 (Moderate) or score 7–8 (Severe) or score 9–10 (Very severe)). The WINRS which is provided in online supplemental table S2 on every day is recorded from baseline (day 0) to day 8. If the WINRS score on day 7 (assessed on day 8) achieved a reduction of at least 50% compared with the baseline before randomisation, the outcome is defined as the itch improvement. Otherwise, it is considered as no improvement. The reduction rate is calculated by the following formula: (WINRS score on day 0−WINRS score on day 8)/WINRS score on day 0×100%.

Secondary outcomes

Changes of the ISS and WINRS score

The pruritus severity was assessed by the Itch Severity Scale (ISS)7 is available in online supplemental table S3 and WINRS score on the day before enrolment, at the time of randomisation, and on days 1–8 and day 14 after intervention. The highest, lowest and median value of ISS and WINRS score were compared and analysed between the two groups.

Change of the Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms and Signs score

The neurogenic symptoms and signs were regularly assessed by the Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms and Signs8 at the fixed time on the day before enrolment, at the time of randomisation, and on days 1–8 and day 14 after intervention.

Improvement of sleep disorder

The sleep disorder was assessed by Sleep Disorder Numerical Rating Scale and is provided as online supplemental table S4 and smart wearable devices on the day before enrolment, at the time of randomisation, and on days 1–8 and day 14 after intervention.

Quality of life

The ItchyQoL9 scale and EuroQol 5-Dimension 5-Level Questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L)10 scale were used to evaluate the quality of life before enrolment and on day 8 and day 14 after randomisation.

Scar severity

The Patient-Oriented Scar Assessment Scale11 was used to evaluate the scar severity at day 1 before enrolment, day 8 and day 14 after randomisation.

Incidence of adverse events

All possible adverse reactions were recorded, including but not limited to pain at the site of stimulation, allergies. And the correlation with interventions was determined.

Exploratory outcomes

Neuroimaging changes

3T functional MRI scans were performed within 1 week before enrolment and on the 7±1 day after enrolment. The changes in signal intensity ratio, functional connectivity and anatomical connectivity were compared and analysed within possible itch regulation regions, including the spinal cord, anterior cingulate cortex, insular cortex, prefrontal cortex, primary/secondary somatosensory cortices and periaqueductal grey matter at 1 week before enrolment, day 7±1 after randomisation.

Skin physiological parameters

DermaLab Combo Skin analyser was used to determine skin parameters such as high-resolution ultrasound, transepidermal water loss, elasticity, hydration, sebum, pH value, temperature, dermatoscope and skin colour on the 1st day before enrolment and the 8th day after enrolment.

Sample size estimation

The sample size was calculated for the primary endpoint of the 7-day itching improvement rate. In a randomised controlled trial,5 TENS significantly reduced the VAS score compared with conventional anti-scar treatment in 20 patients with severe pruritus (6±2.62 vs 3±2.62, p<0.05). Another self-controlled study12 of 16 patients with moderate-to-severe itch demonstrated that scrambler therapy significantly lowered NRS scores (6.75±1.13 vs 4.13±1.45). Preliminary unpublished retrospective data indicate an approximate effective rate of 40% for TENS, vs 15% in a control group. Based on these data, the 7-day itching improvement rate was conservatively set at 35% for the experimental group and 20% for the control group. A superiority margin of 0% was specified for a one-sided test with an α of 0.025, a power of 0.9 and a 1:1 allocation ratio. Applying the Superiority by a Margin test for two proportions in PASS software (V.21.0.3) yielded an estimated total sample size of 362 participants. Accounting for a 5% loss to follow-up, the required sample size is 382 participants, with 191 per group. This sample size may be adjusted during the trial based on the observed treatment effect difference. Recruitment began in June 2025, and the study will conclude following the final patient follow-up in October 2027.

Statistical analysis

Data processing

Clinical study data are privacy-oriented, all data are entered in pairs using an electronic case report form, and the database is locked at the end of the trial. The last observation carryover method is used for randomised missing data.

Data analysis

An independent statistical analysis team was established for independent data monitoring and statistical analysis. The intention-to-treat analysis was selected for the statistical analysis of trial efficacy, with the per-protocol dataset (ie, statistical analysis based on data from all patients meeting the trial protocol criteria) serving as a reference. The χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare event rates, while the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was employed for comparing continuous variables. The treatment response (response vs non-response) will be analysed as a dichotomous variable, and the primary outcome measure of this study is a ratio variable. Statistical tests were conducted using superiority evaluation indicators, with a one-sided significance level of 0.025. Additionally, prespecified subgroup analyses were performed. A p<0.05 was considered statistically significant for the differences tested. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 or SAS 9.4 software.

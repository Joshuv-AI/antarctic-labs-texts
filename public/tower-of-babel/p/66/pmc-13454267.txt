Machine learning-driven image encryption using SVM for enhanced security and computational efficiency
Authors: Saba Inam, Shamsa Kanwal, Sumaira Mushtaq, Shaha Al-Otaibi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A balance between security and computational efficiency is a key goal in image encryption. Traditional methods involve a number of calculations to achieve satisfactory resilience and make it hard to use in real-time environments. This research offers a new paradigm through the combination of Machine Learning (ML) methods with the improvement of the encryption’s space efficiency but preserving the security at the same time. A support vector machine (SVM) is used in the suggested method to divide blocks of image pixels into three types: low information, moderate information, and high information constituents. The encoding process just involves high as well moderate information blocks; low information blocks are left unaltered. There is a considerable reduction in processing overhead when using this selective encryption technique. The correlation, PSNR, MSE, entropy, energy, and contrast are the measures that are employed for evaluating the security of the proposed technique. These evaluation metrics prove exceptional results of accuracy 97.4%, entropy of 7.999, correlation of 0.0001, and energy of 0.0153. According to this work, there is clear progression in the field of image encryption since computational issues for machine learning driven selective encryption are elaborated. The approach is unique, reproducible and efficient to propose a solution to secure communications through encryption within today’s complex data transfer processes.

Introduction

Sensitive digital images that are used commonly are gradually getting popular in medical, military and defense areas in IoT1. This has due to result in a rise in the volume of data transmitted in the online platform. Due to the fact that internet is always associated with insecure transmission, such a surge increases vulnerability of sensitive and classified information to interception and exploitation by the bad actors. Lack of secured communication systems adds to this by showing that communication data needs to be protected from unauthorized use. To this challenge cryptography2,3 becomes very crucial since it uses enriched mathematical formula to encrypt the data before sending across in order to prevent distortion.

In the IoT ecosystem where the integrity of the transmitted data has to be protected encryption forms one of the key layers of protection. Encryption transforms the written content into forms that cannot be understood by your foes thus it becomes extremely difficult, almost computationally infeasible for the opponent to understand the source information. A number of encryption standards have already been put into use, including DES4, AES5, ADEAD6, and RSA7. Regardless of how efficient these algorithms are, they still can’t handle massive data, particularly in constrained IoT usage scenarios where real-time rate priority is paramount.

Likewise, chaos-based encryption techniques8,9 that use the mathematical formulas have good security context to it. While the chaotic systems are effective if used for digital data protection their computation requirement is a big issue for real time IoT. However, given the volume of data that would be transmitted in IoT, the balance between optimization and security turns out to be a critical issue. Efforts have been made to enhance encryption algorithms to satisfy the real-time requirements of the IoT appliances while still maintaining security as a principal research interest.

Inherent in the creation of digital images is the data requirement and so for such images the challenge increases. Often excess or invariant value of pixel information encumbers computation during encryption process. For example, as depicted in Fig. 1, it may turn out that some regions of an image, for example, Area C, contains very small changes in the pixels. Encrypting such regions also incurred more processes and time since it consumes extra computational power for no added security advantage. This is a typical case where it is necessary to apply the method of differential encryption targeting regions where valuable information is stored instead of performing unnecessary computations.

To overcome these challenges incorporating machine learning10 into encryption processes can become the effective solution. While other forms of learning such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning among others, have a useful application in the handling of IoT data due to their ability to process large volumes of data in near real-time. Since IoT devices are both producing and utilizing data at a prodigious rate; often faster than tens of billions of bytes daily; information security mechanisms based on the application of ML-powered algorithms can facilitate exponentially superior rates of encryption and decryption. This means that IoT algorithms and analytical models need to learn from data and cannot rely on initial programming; this is where Machine Learning comes into play. This way integrating the use of ML in encryption strategies11, can enable automated determination of the relevant information and call for encryption. This integration not only increases computational effectiveness, but also provides security that is solid in real-time conditions.

Motivation

While in the digital age data is transmitted at exponential rates through the Internet of Things, enhanced encryption algorithms are needed that ensure optimal security with optimal computationally resources. The conventional techniques of encryption are quite proficient for providing a challenging security platform but they fall short in their computational interactions while applied on large volumes of data or real time processing. This limitations become especially crucial in IoT settings where things constantly produce massive volumes of data and run under protracted limitations of resources.

Chaotic systems that are recognized for their helpful security constraints are hidden by implementations because of their elevated computational density. To manage these challenges the effective incorporation of ML techniques into the encryption process presents a revolutionary solution12. ML not only acknowledge critical data regions but also minimize computational needs that makes encryption processes far more effective and suitable for immediate IoT uses.

The research underlying this contribution was consequently called to address the need for constraint-based encryption methodologies tailored to prevent unauthorized access to regions of interest (ROI) in digital images13. By doing so the computational burden is minimized while still maintaining the security. The combination of chaotic encryption has all the potential to be wholeness answer for current complex data oriented society’s security and unachieved speed.

Contributions

To overcome the contradiction between high computation and security of images this work presents a new scheme of image encryption that uses both chaotic logistic maps and machine learning algorithms. The main contributions of this work are as follows:

Enhanced image encryption approach

A new approach to the improvement of the encryption efficiency with the help of machine learning algorithms, in particular, Support Vector Machine (SVM) is introduced. As for the computational overhead constituent of the traditional encryption methods, this approach lends a significant solution to this problem.

Selective encryption strategy

Therefore, a selective encryption framework is created and only blocks of information of a high and medium level are selected for encryption in an image. The low information blocks are left unencrypted, reducing considerably the computational time, thus making the described approach ideal for applications in real time.

A mathematical model for bit-plane extraction is presented

The mathematical representation of the process of bit-plane extraction of plaintext images is shown in detail in the paper. What this model also shows us in a digital world is that, by encrypting the bit-plane, it will be possible to drastically decrease the computational load without ever compromising the highest level of security of our data.

Machine Learning Metrics: The performance of the classification model developed using the ML algorithms is calculated using the following metrics: F1 Score, accuracy, recall, and precision.

Cryptographic Metrics: Security and efficiency of encryption algorithm is measured by computing the correlation coefficient, entropy analysis, energy and contrast.

The proposed method is then compared to the conventional cryptography techniques to demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method for performance and reliability.

The contributions show that the concept of chaotic encryption combined with ML could lead to the powerful and powerful method of ciphering data. Related work

Recent studies on other encryption techniques such as bit-plane processing14,40, chaos-based cryptography15 and machine learning-based algorithms16 have been pursued as a key objective in digital information protection. Chaos, with its unique properties that generate random numbers, has been found to be a practical method for encrypting digital images17.

A novel method of picture encryption utilising a chaotic S-box was suggested by Lu et al.18. Brantley included cryptanalysis of the means of encryption and examined a method described in19. They made a number of improvements recommendations based on their research: An enhanced encryption system, a new chaotic map, and a substitute box (S-box) are all part of the suggested solution. However, there were instances of insecurity and computational inefficiencies due to the use of a single S-box in their system. An adversary may more easily infer the plain text based on the encrypted communication as the images created by scrambling the plaintext contained identifiable signs of the plaintext. The inefficiency was glaringly obvious for a 256 × 256 image when a single Substitution operation took nearly fifteen seconds. A more in-depth discussion of the issues with single creative S-box systems may be found in20, which also introduced the usage of multiple S-boxes in conjunction with a chaotic dynamical map21. Since this provided better security and numerical outcomes than an independent S-box structure, it is reasonable to assume that the encrypted images could still be deciphered using the original image data.

As a solution, Ahmed and coworkers22 improved the system by adding more S-boxes to the safeguarding process, which allowed them to disguise picture information better. Unfortunately, their technique significantly raised processing complexity, which rendered it unfit for use in real-time scenarios. To tackle these computational issues, Shafique and collaborators23 sought to optimise the method even further.

In an effort to reduce computing costs and storage needs, Leng et al.24 investigated cancelable biometric systems, more especially frameworks for coding palmprints. Orthogonal orientation transposition & multi-directional score-level fusion were two of the approaches they used to take one-dimensional frameworks to two dimensions. Extending this idea, Leng et al.25 used 2DDCT to normalise discriminative coefficients and extract features from left and right palmprints, leading to improved accuracy. Subsequently, they created the Conjugate 2DPlamHash Creative Code (CTDPHC), a multiple-model biometric system that enhanced the reliability of verifications and the capacity to combat counterfeiting without introducing additional computational burden.

A lot of people working on encryption have also used chaos theory. By converting pixel values to frequencies using a discrete wavelet (DWT), Pourased and coworkers26 developed a chaos-driven method for picture encryption. A three-step method including pre-processing, encryption, and post-processing was suggested by Li et al.27 as a picture encryption scheme based on chaos theory. Although this technology successfully improved security, it was not suited for use in real-time applications due to the high computing expenses it entailed. Image encryption using fractional-order chaotic systems connected with neural networks was also explored in28, although the computational complexity of these methods was higher. Multiple assaults, including entropy attacks and cypher text only attacks, might compromise encryption systems that depend on permutation or diffusion, as highlighted by Shannon’s cryptographic concepts of disarray and propagation29–32.

Researchers have suggested tweaks to current algorithms to get around these restrictions. Incorporating random key creation, Lin et al.33 presented a chaos-based form of AES. To achieve diffusion over chaotic sequences, Kumar in collaboration with colleagues34 used a zigzag scan approach to shuffle picture pixels. While in principle these were approved by Shannon, the methods were too slow when they are applied in turn, so they are not suitable for real-time applications. The study demonstrates that existing encryption techniques offer a good level of security and efficiency. Based on this, this study proposes a time-saving image encryption technology that fuses the machine learning algorithm, random coding algorithm, and bit plane extraction algorithm, to solve these problems. The goal of this method is to minimise computational expenses while yet achieving robust security.

Efficient and semantically-supported encryption methods have been further investigated recently. For example, in the case of encryption methods, it is possible to enhance processing efficiency for large-scale datasets by using cross-image permutation and diffusion35 and to optimize security and computing performance by using semantically enhanced selective encryption schemes36 based on parallel computing. These methods emphasize shifting and effective methods of data encryption. In comparison, the proposed method focuses on machine learning–driven block classification combined with chaos-based encryption to achieve a balance between computational efficiency and security.

The methodology is presented in Sect. 4 and the experimental validation is included in Sect. 5. In the final section (Sect. 6), the study is finally completed.

Bit-plane extraction

The bit-plane extraction approach39,40 involves dividing an image into its constituent bit-planes, each of which represents a particular shade of pixel intensity. An 8- bit grayscale image is composed of 8 bit-planes, where the 8-bit plane is called the most significant plane, because it contains lot of information as compared to the 1 st least significant plane.

In the proposed work, only bit-plane 8, 7, 6 and 5, which contain more than 90% of the image information, are encrypted. This selective approach eliminates lower bit-planes BP4 to BP1, so as to minimize complexity in the calculation without compromising security.

Proposed methodology

Image encryption is the method of converting the confidential image data so that they cannot be understood and accessed by unauthorized person. This involves distortion in the pixel positions (confusion) and the pixel intensity (diffusion) in order to provide a firm encryption method. Many approaches available are like, chaotic maps and selective encryption but major idea is to optimize the computation complexity for data security.

The herein presented cryptographic system utilizes machine learning with enhanced encryption efficiencies and enhances the security of image encryption. The system is divided into two main modules: an ML module and an encryption module in which the two work together to simplify the process of encrypting information.

Machine learning module

The machine learning portion of the system uses SVM, for classifying image blocks as to their informational content. The plaintext image is then segmented into block size as 8 × 8 pixels (64 pixels per block) and features are then extracted from each block. These features includes:

Encryption module

The encryption module aims at securely encrypting the identified high and moderate information blocks, but at the same time keeping the computation efficient. The steps are as follows:

Bit-Plane Extraction:

The binary bit-planes of each of the pixels selected for encryption are broken down. If the image is grayscale and 8 bits wide, there are 8 bit-planes (BP1 - BP8), the most significant being BP8. Only the most important bit-planes (BP8, BP7, BP6 and BP5) are encrypted – they carry more than 90% of the information in the image – to optimize processing. This selective approach reduces computation without compromising security.

Confusion

Bit-planes are chaotic permuted by the pixel positions and the chaotic sequences acquired from LSC chaotic map. It disarranges the spatial relationship of nearby pixels, hence; there is considerable difficulty that an attacker would experience in trying to identify the encrypted data pattern. The chaotic sequences are then employed to mix the image blocks and randomize the pixels in every block such that even minor alteration to the input image yield difficult transform in the output. This approach greatly improves the security of the encryption as it brings unpredictability both at a block and pixel level which makes it immune to cryptography. Hence the blocks formed in the original image are shifted to new positions and also pixels are rearranged within the particular blocks it becomes more difficult to decrypt by unauthorized scanner.

Diffusion

In the diffusion stage, a Random Image (RI) having the same dimensions as the plaintext image is generated, and is then Exclusive OR (XOR) with the pre-ciphered segments. This always means that any slight variation of the plaintext will be greatly reflected in the encrypted image thereby making it difficult to decipher any pattern. The image having a pixel value is through pixel substitution based on a chaotic sequence from the logistic map and form S-Box for mapping pixel values. The region of the image HIB, MIB and LIB passes through different surfaces of chaotic sequence and XOR encryption. Algorithm 3: Chaotic logistic map-based s-box generation and pixel substitution

Following the information classification of the picture block using supported vector machine, the last stage in encrypting the images is a logistic network chaotic map S-Box42,43. This S-Box is used to apply pixel substitution to every pixel in the encrypted image, making it more secure. S-Box makes use of a logistic pseudo-random generating sequence generated by a single efficient and straightforward equation, which characterises the chaotic logistic map. Because the logistic map incorporates randomness, the encrypted image is highly resistant to attacks.

The produced S-box is not only tested against the Strict Avalanche Criterion (SAC), but also against nonlinearity other differential uniformity measures. These features are critical for the proposed S-box to be resistant to linear and differential cryptography, and the findings show that it does.

This is where the Iterated Chaotic Stochastic Map function, along with the logistic maps parameter and initial condition, is used to construct S-Box for a particular message block. The aforementioned map produces a disorderly sequence that is subsequently transformed into matrices after being sorted according to uniqueness. The S-Box, which is this matrix, is where the encrypted image’s pixel values are mapped. The value from S-Box is substituted for every pixel in the jumbled image. Because of this, the encrypted version of the image differs greatly from the original. In order to increase computing speed and security, the substitution procedure ensures that only the data blocks classified inside the critical grouping in the SVM classification algorithm will be encrypted.

In algorithm 3, we outline the given steps of implementing this methods of pixel substitution and the encryption. Implementation of the logistic map in the generation of the S-Box brings in complexity and non-linearity in the encryption process and thus makes computation for reverse in search of the parameters of the chaotic map difficult. This method dramatically enhances the level of protection over the encryption to which it is infeasible for the image to be decrypted by an adversary who does not have the appropriate logistic map parameters.

Algorithm 4: Region-specific chaotic encryption

Algorithm 4 uses logistic map based chaotic sequences to encrypt each region individually. The HIB + MIB region is subjected to XOR based chaotic encryption and a row permutation to further increase randomness and the ability to stand up to cryptographic attacks. At the same time, the LIB region itself is encrypted without extra permutations in order to remain computationally balanced. Last but not the least, the two encrypted domains are reconstructed and resulted in the fully encrypted image, which ensures the trade-off between security and speed (Fig. 4).

Entropy is one of the basic measurements occurring and has a large role to play in most cryptography algorithms in understanding how secure an encryption algorithm is. Entropy quantifies the pixel distribution patterns of an image in terms of randomness or chaos. An encrypted image is supposed to have a maximum entropy since maximum entropy would suggest the surface of the image is well scattered and cannot be easily recognized. The entropy value is high, which means that encrypted image does not contain quite distinguishable patterns, and therefore the encryption is more secure. As such, entropy proves to be a good measure of how effectively the originals image structure has been concealed the underlying encryption; entropy therefore increases as the level of encryption increases. Sensitivity analysis

Number of pixels change rate (NCPR) and unified average fluctuating intensity (UACI) are two other useful indices for evaluating encryption efficiency. They all evaluate how encryption impacts an image’s pixel format. NPCR is a direct indication of the encryption’s capacity to alter the value of the pixels of the image since it measures the pixel that has transformed between the normal and encrypted images. More values of pixels have been changed when the NPCR value is higher. This is a good thing for encryption systems since it shows that the algorithm distorted the picture just as much as the data. If the NPCR value is high, it means that the encrypted image will vary greatly in response to even a tiny change to the input image—in this case, a little variation in the initial picture is calculated. This is an indication of a strong image encryption system.

UACI, on the other side, measures the mean intensity disparity between comparable original and encrypted picture details. A higher UACI value indicates that the encryption technique has effectively adapted multiple pixel intensities to separate the encrypted image from the original image, which quantifies the real execution of the algorithm. The amount of cryptographic safety can be increased by combining NCPR and UACI to determine if an encryption system can disguise the initial image and if it is impossible to discover sequences or connections in the encrypted picture. Chaotic map randomness analysis

A detailed randomness analysis is carried out to validate the suitability of the chaotic map used for cryptographic applications. The sequences generated by chaos-based encryption systems are crucial for the security of the encryption system, with its unpredictability and statistical randomness being key.

The sensitivity to initial conditions is explored by adding a small change to the initial value. The sequences thus obtained are very divergent, verifying the strong chaotic behavior and high sensitivity that is desirable for secure key generation.

Statistics randomness is measured by histogram distribution analysis of the chaotic sequence generated. This appears to be a uniform distribution, meaning there is no pattern to the distribution of the sequence. In addition, the autocorrelation analysis shows that the correlation between the elements of the sequence is quickly close to zero, which proves the independence of the generated values.

The results show that the chaotic map that is used in this study produces highly random and unpredictable sequences, which are suitable for secure image encryption applications.

Robustness analysis

Apart from security evaluation, the robustness of the proposed encryption scheme is evaluated when common signal degradations and attacks like the addition of noise, cropping and data loss are considered.

For the purpose of assessing the noise robustness, Gaussian noises with different noise variance levels are added to the encrypted image. The decryption results show that the original image can still be recovered with good visual quality, a result that demonstrates the resistance to interference of noise.

The Cropping attacks are simulated by cropping parts of the encrypted image. Even though there are partial data losses, the image that was decrypted still has a lot of structural information, which proves that it is robust against occlusion attacks.

The results illustrate that the proposed encryption scheme remains secure and functional even under challenging scenarios, making it suitable for practical applications, especially in IoT settings.

Differential attacks

Differential attacks are very important as are the abilities to resist such attacks; all friendly encryption systems would need to have such abilities as part of their functions. Differential attacks take small changes in the input data to observe the corresponding changes on the out, as encrypted. In any good encryption system, no matter how small modifications may be made to the original plain image, changes should occur in the encrypted image that prevent an attacker from making relevant conclusions regarding the original data. A good encryption algorithm should reflect high sensitivity to the input differences, and therefore, differential attacks should not be able to reveal much information from the encrypted data. Measuring system resistance in such attacks allows one to estimate the effectiveness of the encryption process against cryptographic analysis tools intended to leverage deterministic patterns between the input and encrypted output. A secure encryption system will withstand differential attacking by guaranteeing that modification on the original image does not impair the security of the encrypted image or aid the recovery of the original image. The robust performance of the suggested encryption scheme against the application of differential attacks is presented in Fig. 6 by the high value of NPCR.

While reconstruction improves robustness against data loss, it may introduce redundancy. However, this redundancy does not expose plaintext information directly, as reconstruction operates on encrypted domains. The trade-off between recoverability and security is acknowledged and discussed.

The chaotic logistic maps yields a high random value making the system crude and difficult to reverse. Pixel substitution new S-Boxes which are created dynamically are another new feature meaning the process of encryption is more complex. XOR operations and row permutations additional steps to the security in that it destroys any patterns and makes a random/complete change to the encrypted data. This system is relatively very resistant to most crypt-analytic attacks. Differential and statistical attacks also show that NPCR, UACI, entropy and SSIM make a model more robust. It makes affair that small changes in the input image lead to dramatic changes in the encrypted image making it even more difficult for attackers to decipher the encryption. Machine learning brings improved flexibility and performance into the system. Real time parameter turning enhances performance while automated block classification enhances that the concentration is directed towards the sensitive areas of a block. Trained models also identify other attack patterns making a system more secure. The potential of the cryptosystem in the future is closely prefixed with growth. Which machine learning can improve the effectiveness of encryption methods and how federated learning increases security in clouds? Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves high entropy, low correlation, and strong resistance against differential and statistical attacks while maintaining computational efficiency. Compared to existing approaches, the framework provides an improved trade-off between security strength and processing cost, making it suitable for real-time and resource-constrained environments.

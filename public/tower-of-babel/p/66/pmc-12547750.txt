Computational Evaluation of Azinane–Amide-Coupled Triazoles Enabled by an Alkali Metal Reagent as Anti-Enzymatic Agents
Authors: Zeeshan Munir, Aziz-ur Rehman, Muhammad Athar Abbasi, Sabahat Zahra Siddiqui, Ali Imran Mallhi, Javed Iqbal, Hira Khalid, Shahid Rasool, Muhammad Ashraf, Syed Adnan Ali Shah, Syahrul Imran
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A new electrophile was synthesized and merged with 1,2,4-triazole and piperidine to avail the aimed library of compounds, 10a i‑v and 10b i‑v . Various 4-(substituted)­benzenesulfonyl chlorides (1 i‑v ) were treated with ethyl piperidin-4-carboxylate (2a) and ethyl piperidin-3-carboxylate (2b) to acquire 3a i‑v and 3b i‑v esters bearing a sulfamoyl moiety. These esters were converted to different 1,2,4-triazoles 6a i‑v and 6b i‑v through the formation of carbohydrazides and thiosemicarbazides as intermediates. A new electrophile, compound 9, was synthesized by reaction of bromoacetyl bromide (7) with an aromatic amine (8) under basic conditions. This electrophile was treated with 6a i‑v and 6b i‑v to avail the aimed library of compounds, 10a i‑v and 10b i‑v . The structural confirmation of all of the synthesized compounds was performed through 1D NMR data. The whole library of synthesized compounds was evaluated for their pharmacological potential against acetylcholinesterase (AChE), butyrylcholinesterase (BChE), alpha glucosidase, and urease enzymes. Compound 10a iii showed better % inhibition (63.37 ± 1.25) against the AChE enzyme, while 10b i showed better % inhibition (72.38 ± 1.21) against the BChE enzyme as compared to the reference standards, Eserine and Pyridostigmine. 10b v showed best activity against the alpha-glucosidase enzyme with 64.7 ± 1.84% inhibition and the best activity against the urease enzyme was presented by compound 10b iii with the present inhibition potential of 49.3 ± 1.5. The best biofilm inhibition activity was shown by compound 10b ii with a % inhibition of 84.03141361 against bacterium, Escherichia coli. Molecular docking was performed to check the active sites of enzymes giving different values of binding affinity. The physicochemical properties and bioactivity values of synthesized compounds rendered them as active drugs. DFT was performed to study the electronic characteristics including molecular electrostatic potential (MEP), structural optimization, and HOMO–LUMO energy gap to evaluate reactivity or stability of the synthesized compounds. Furthermore, full insight evaluation may lead us to find the best antienzymatic drug candidates in the future.

Introduction

The heterocyclic compounds have numerous applications in different fields of medicines, nanochemistry, agriculture, and pharmacy. From a medicinal point of view, heterocyclic compounds having nitrogen are more important in treating different diseases. , Triazoles are heterocyclic compounds having three nitrogen atoms; specifically, 1,2,4-triazole has a wide range of applications as anti-inflammatory, antiallergic, antioxidant, antifungal, anticancer, antimicrobial, and antidepression. Drugs having 1,2,4-triazole moiety are alprazolam, itraconazole, fluconazole, voriconazole, ribavirin, anastrozole, and letrozole, which are used as anticonvulsant, antifungal, antiviral, and antineoplastic. In addition to 1,2,4-triazole, some other scaffolds are also used in target molecules such as sulfamoyl and piperidine, which are also useful in combating different diseases. , Sulfamoyl moieties are present in sulfonamides which is used to treat different activities such as antiviral and antifungal actions. Medicines having sulfamoyl , as the main ingredient are sulfadiazine and bactrim. Other medicines are Diamox and glucotrol used for glaucoma and diabetes. On the other side, piperidine is also present in minoxidil, which is used to treat hair loss or baldness. It is also used in evista, risperdal, and imodium to treat osteoporosis and diarrhea and also acts as antipsychotic. ,

Two enzymes, AChE and BChE, are produced in the body. Their overproduction and accumulation in the body cause neural dysfunction and several degenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease. Actually, a neurotransmitter called acetylcholine is hydrolyzed into acetic acid by AChE and BChE. Their excess production causes brain disorders. They affect the post synaptic membrane of the neuromuscular junction of various cells and so their inhibitors are required to stop their activity. Bacterium, Helicobacter pylori, produces urease which converts urea into carbonic acid and ammonia. The produced ammonia having alkaline pH acts as a blanket on bacteria which survive in an acidic medium. These bacteria cause gastric cancer, and if it is untreated, it causes ulcer also. So, antiulcer agents are needed to be developed to stop or inhibit urease to kill or reduce H. pylori. The metabolism of carbohydrates of the body is disrupted by Diabetes mellitus (DM). This disruption causes disorders related to hyperglycemia. A postprandial increase of glucose in blood sugar resulted from the hydrolysis of higher carbohydrates by an alpha-glucosidase enzyme. The blood sugar levels can be controlled through the inhibition of this enzyme. Different molecules including acarbose and miglitol have been employed as inhibitors of this enzyme to cure DM.

To understand the effectiveness and resistivity of these compounds against bacteria, different bacteria in the form of a thick biofilm were used against synthesized compounds or antibiotics. Biofilm inhibition can be achieved through various methods containing antimicrobial agents such as antibiotics, surface modification such as coating, enzyme-based treatment, and natural compound such as essential oils. , To predict the properties, activities, and characters of synthesized molecules, researchers also use computers before lab experiments. For example, density fraction techniques are used to check the molecular electrostatic potential, NMR, vibrational frequencies, structure, and dipole moment. Highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) studies were observed to get location of each atom within the synthesized molecule along with minimum energy space.

The purpose of current research work was to combine the antienzymatic and antibacterial effectiveness of numerous moieties such as piperidine, sulfamoyl, and 1,2,4-triazole in a single compound. The prepared compounds were evaluated biologically by acetylcholinesterase (AChE), butyrylcholinesterase (BChE), and urease inhibition, along with biofilm inhibition. Their activity relation and physicochemical properties were investigated through computational studies, including molecular docking and DFT.

Chemistry

We have decided to choose one compound of 10a ii for the understanding of readers. We will discuss its structure, chemistry, and characterization. This compound was obtained as white with 80% yield. Functional groups were characterized by IR spectroscopy. The C–H and CC bonds of the aromatic ring were indicated by peaks at 3077 and 1598 cm–1, respectively. The CN and C–N bonds of the triazole ring appeared at 1643 and 1170 cm–1, respectively. The SO2 peak appeared at 1367 cm–1 and the C–Cl peak at 711 cm–1. The number of protons, their environment, and position were indicated by the 1HNMR spectrum working at 600 MHz (Figures , ). The protons of the aromatic ring attached to sulfonyl presented two peaks at 7.72 (d, J = 7.5 Hz, 2H, H-2″ and H-6″) and 7.52 (d, J = 7.8 Hz, 2H, H-3″ and H-5″). The five protons of the aromatic ring attached to nitrogen of the carbamoyl group resonated at 7.45 (t, J = 7.18 Hz, 2H, H-3‴′ and H-5‴′), 7.38 (t, J = 6.9 Hz, 1H, H-4‴′), and 7.28 (d, J = 7.7 Hz, 2H, H-2‴′ and H-6‴′). The protons of S-substituted CH2 appeared as a singlet at 3.96 (s, 2H, H-2‴). Piperidine has nine protons which were justified by following peaks appearing at 3.80–3.76 (m, 2H, He-2′ and He-6′), 2.68–2.62 (m, 3H, Ha-2′, Ha-6′, H-4′), 2.10–2.04 (m, 2H, He-3′ and He-5′), and 2.00–1.97 (m, 2H, Ha-3′ and Ha-5′). The two signals of the ethyl group attached to a nitrogen of the triazole ring appeared at 3.89 (q, J = 7.2 Hz, 1H, H-6) and 1.30 (t, J = 8.0 Hz, 3H, H-7) as a quartet and triplet. The methyl group attached to the nitrogen of the carbamoyl group presented one singlet at 3.29 (s, 3H, H-3‴). 13CNMR spectral data explained the carbon skeleton of the selected compound at 150 MHz (Figure ). The two quaternary carbons of the triazole ring appeared at 156.6 (C-5) and 149.8 (C-3). The four peaks for six carbon atoms (two quaternary and four methine carbons) of the aromatic ring having the sulfonyl group appeared at 139.4 (C-1″), 134.9 (C-4″), 130.1 (C-3″ and C-5″), and 129.4 (C-2″ and C-6″). The ethyl group attached to the triazole ring appeared at peaks 29.7 (C-6) and 15.6 (C-7). The carbon skeleton of the aromatic ring attached to the nitrogen of the carbamoyl group showed four peaks at 142.8 (C-1‴′), 129.0 (C-3‴′ and C-5‴′), 128.5 (C-4‴′), and 127.1 (C-2‴′ and C-6‴′) for one quaternary and five methine carbons. The methylene carbon atom at S-substitution was justified by a peak of 37.9 (C-2‴). Carbonyl carbon of amidic functionality presented a peak at 166.8 (C-1‴). The methyl carbon attached to the nitrogen of the carbamoyl group appeared at 38.7 (C-3‴). The four methylene and one methine carbons of the piperidine ring resonated at 45.3 (C-3′ and C-5′), 37.5 (C-2′ and C-6′), and 31.5 (C-4′). This detailed spectroscopic study corroborated the structure of the selected compound as 2-((5-(1-((4-chlorophenyl)­sulfonyl)­piperidin-4-yl)-4-ethyl-4H-1,2,4-triazol-3-yl)­thio)-N-methyl-N-phenyl acetamide.

AChE and BChE Inhibition

We synthesized compounds, 10a i‑v and 10b i‑v , which were analyzed for biological potential against enzymes such as acetylcholinesterase (AChE) and butyryl cholinesterase (BChE) using serine and pyridostigmine as reference standards. All of the synthesized compounds were found active against these enzymes with variable inhibition potential (Table ). The most active compound against AChE was 10a iii having an inhibition potential of 63.4 ± 1.25% at 0.5 mM, whereas the enzyme inhibition of eserine was 92.5 ± 1.34% and that of pyridostigmine was 89.6 ± 1.41% at 0.5 mM. The high activity of this compound may be associated with the presence of 4-methylphenyl sulfonyl piperidin-4-yl group in the target molecule. Compounds 10b ii , 10b iii , 10b v , and 10a v had moderate values of inhibition potential such as 43.6 ± 1.29%, 41.6 ± 1.27%, 45.8 ± 1.31%, and 42.6 ± 1.27%, respectively, which are about 50% in comparison of standards. The synthesized compound, 10b i , was found to be the most active one against BChE with an inhibition potential of 72.4 ± 1.21% at 0.5 mM. This compound contains a phenyl sulfonyl piperidin-3-yl group which may be attributed to high activity of this compound against this enzyme. The structure and configuration of this compound was suitable to fit and inhibit the active site of this enzyme. In comparison to it, eserine presented an inhibition potential of 85.8 ± 1.27% and pyridostigmine of 90.6 ± 1.18% at 0.5 mM. The remaining compounds showed variable inhibition potential ranging from low to 60% with respect to the reference.

Urease Inhibition

The list of synthesized compounds, 10a i‑v and 10b i‑v , was also subjected to enzyme inhibition against urease enzyme with thiourea as the reference standard (Table ). Three compounds were found to be about 50% active against this enzyme in comparison to the reference. Compound 10b iii had the highest inhibition potential of 49.3 ± 1.5 at 0.5 mM in comparison to that of thiourea as 98.2 ± 0.18% at 0.5 mM. 4-Methylphenyl sulfonyl piperidin-3-yl group may be responsible for the highest activity of this compound among this series.

α-Glucosidase Inhibition

All of the synthesized compounds were evaluated for α-glucosidase inhibition potential and are presented as percent inhibition with reference to acarbose as a standard drug (Table ). The whole series of compounds were found active against this enzyme with varying inhibition potential. Compound 10b v , bearing a 4-fluorophenyl sulfonyl piperidin-3-yl group, had the best inhibition potential as 64.7 ± 1.84 at 0.5 mM. Five compounds, 10a iii , 10a iv , 10a v , 10b ii , and 10b v , presented potential comparable to the reference standard, 61.5 ± 1.87, 57.8 ± 1.62, 58.6 ± 1.53, 63.8 ± 1.54, and 64.7 ± 1.84% at 0.5 mM, respectively. Standard drug acarbose had inhibition potential as 65.7 ± 1.53% at 0.5 mM. Excellent inhibitory potential of these compounds may be attributed to the presence of different varying groups including 4-methylphenyl sulfonyl piperidin-4-yl, 4-methoxyphenyl sulfonyl piperidin-4-yl, 4-fluorophenyl sulfonyl piperidin-4-yl, 4-chlorophenyl sulfonyl piperidin-3-yl, and 4-fluorophenyl sulfonyl piperidin-3-yl, respectively. The remaining compounds also presented considerable potential against this enzyme, except 10b iv which was the least active one.

Biofilm Inhibition

Antibacterial potential of synthesized compounds was assessed through a biofilm inhibition assay. The results demonstrated that the synthesized compounds possess broader antibiofilm activity, but further testing against other pathogens is necessary to confirm this (Table ). All the synthesized compounds develop biofilm composed of polysaccharide, nucleic acid, and protein against the said bacterium. Bacteria grow in the form of biofilm, which forms a hard surface at the infection site to minimize action of antibiotics. The thicker is the biofilm, the minimum is the action of antibiotic. In biofilm development against Gram-negative bacteria, ciprofloxacin is used as a standard. Percentage biofilm inhibition of ciprofloxacin is 67.53926702. Among the synthesized compounds, five compounds, 10a i , 10a v , 10b i , 10b ii , and 10b iii , presented better biofilm inhibition than standard as 80.10471204, 78.27225131, 70.05759162, 84.03141361, and 78.0104712, respectively.

DFT Analysis

In search of electronic properties, biological potential, and factors affecting biological behavior of synthesized compounds, their structures were optimized (Table ) by Gauss View 6.0.16 by FMO analysis and MEP studies. HOMO (π donor) and LUMO (π acceptor) are energy variables called Frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs). Energy gaps of orbitals are related to reactivity and stability of compounds bearing electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups. If the energy gap is high, then the compound will be harder, less reactive, and more stable. Low energy gap means soft molecules and more reactive ones, because they give electrons to acceptors. Synthesized compounds, 10a iii , 10b i , and 10a i , were stable and less reactive with low activity against the enzyme because of no covalent bond formation (Table ). These have an energy gap of 0.19 eV. The remaining compounds presented an energy gap of 0.16 or 0.17 eV. Compound 10a iv showed the least reactivity due to a higher energy gap of 0.2 eV, as shown in Table , which is why this compound remains least reactive against AChE, BChE, and urease. The higher gap in HOMO–LUMO energy might make it unreactive with the enzyme, showing least potential. But, on the other hand, this stabilization might facilitate the enzyme to remain intact with active posture and might show good potential against α-glucosidase.

Conclusion

In medicinal chemistry, heterocyclic compounds, especially triazole analogues, are of keen interest to researchers because of their biological potential as new drug candidates against enzymes and microbes. In this study, a multistep synthesis was performed to acquire 1,2,4-triazole analogues 10a i‑v and 10b i‑v , bearing piperidine. Their biological potential was assessed against AChE, BChE, urease, and alpha-glucosidase enzymes along with Escherichia coli. Compound 10a iii was the most active against AChE with an inhibition potential of about 70% in comparison to reference standards. Furthermore, four compounds presented with about 50% inhibition potential relative to the references. Compound 10b i was found to be the most active against BChE with an inhibition potential of about 85% relative to references. Urease enzyme was moderately inhibited by this series of compounds except three compounds which presented about 50% inhibition potential in comparison of thiourea as a reference. Five compounds were found to possess inhibition potential comparable to that of acarbose as a reference against the alpha-glucosidase enzyme. The antibacterial potential evaluation through biofilm inhibition also revealed five compounds which were better than the reference standard, ciprofloxacin. The computational study including molecular docking, DFT, structural optimization, FMO, and MEP was also performed to infer about the binding modes of these compounds in the active site of enzymes. The results of the computational study characterized the synthesized compounds as considerable new drug candidates, and their physicochemical properties confirmed their usage as new drug candidates. The most active molecules among the whole series of compounds may be studied for further analysis.

For the current research work, chemicals from Sigma-Aldrich were purchased and utilized. Thin layer chromatography was employed for the confirmation of synthesis of target molecules. Methanol, ethyl acetate, and n-hexane in different ratios made the mobile phase and silica on an aluminum plate acted as the stationary phase. TLC plates were visualized under an ultraviolet light of 254 nm. The melting points of synthesized compounds were noted through a Griffin–George melting point instrument. Different characterization techniques were applied such as infrared spectroscopy using an FTIR spectrometer to identify different functional groups and 1HNMR and 13CNMR by using a Bruker spectrometer operating at 600 and 150 MHz.

Synthesis of 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-4-(ethoxycarbonyl)­piperidine (3a i‑v ) and 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-3-(ethoxycarbonyl)­piperidine (3b i‑v )

4-Substituted-benzenesulfonyl chloride (1 i‑v , 0.05 mol) was mixed with 4-(ethoxycarbonyl) piperidine (2a, 0.05 mol) and 3-(ethoxycarbonyl) piperidine (2b, 0.05 mol) in distilled water separately. The pH of the reaction mixture became acidic due to the release of HCl, and it was maintained at 9–10 by adding 5% Na2CO3 solution. This mixture was kept on stirring for 4–5 h. TLC was performed to check the purity of the product and reaction completion. Cold distilled water was added to achieve precipitation. The title compounds were filtered, washed with distilled water, and dried.

Synthesis of 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-4-(1-hydrazinocarbonyl)­piperidine (4a i‑v ) and 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-3-(1-hydrazinocarbonyl)­piperidine (4b i‑v )

Compounds 3a i‑v and 3b i‑v (0.04 mol) were mixed with hydrazine (10–15 mL) in methanol and refluxed for 3 h. TLC was performed to monitor the reaction completion and the formation of desired products. Excess methanol was evaporated, and the reaction mixture was cooled to room temperature to acquire the precipitates. The title compounds were separated through filtration, washed with n-hexane, and dried.

Synthesis of N-Ethyl-2-(1-((4-(un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl)­piperidin-4-carbonyl)-1-hydrazinecarbothioamide (5a i‑v ) and N-Ethyl-2-(1-((4-(un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl)­piperidin-3-carbonyl)-1-hydrazinecarbothioamide (5b i‑v )

Compounds 4a i‑v and 4b i‑v (0.04 mol) were mixed with ethyl isothiocyanate in ethanol separately and refluxed for 4–5 h. TLC was performed to confirm the reaction completion. The precipitates of title compounds were washed with chilled distilled water, filtered, and dried.

Synthesis of 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-4-(4-ethyl-4H-1,2,4-triazol-3-thio-5-yl)­piperidine (6a i‑v ) and 1-[(4-(Un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl]-3-(4-ethyl-4H-1,2,4-triazol-3-thio-5-yl)­piperidine (6b i‑v )

Compounds 5a i‑v and 5b i‑v (0.03 mol) were treated with 20% KOH and refluxed for 3–4 h. TLC was performed to confirm cyclized triazole products. The resulting product was acidified with HCl to adjust pH at 5–6. The product was washed with distilled water, filtered, and dried.

Synthesis of N-Methyl-N-phenylacetamide (9)

The titled electrophile, 9, was prepared by stirring N-methylaniline (7) with bromo acetyl bromide (8) in an alkaline medium. Compound 7 (0.03 mol) was taken in an iodine flask followed by the addition of 10% aqueous Na2CO3 (10–12 mL). The equimolar compound 8 was added to the mixture and subjected to stirring for one hour. Precipitates were removed by filtration and drying.

Synthesis of 2-((4-Ethyl-5-(1-((4-(un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl)­piperidin-4-yl)-4H-1,2,4-triazole-3-yl)­thio)-N-methyl-N-phenylacetamide (10a i‑v ) and 2-((4-Ethyl-5-(1-((4-(un)­substituted-phenyl)­sulfonyl)­piperidin-3-yl)-4H-1,2,4-triazole-3-yl)­thio)-N-methyl-N-phenylacetamide (10b i‑v )

Compounds, 6a i‑v and 6b i‑v (0.02 mol), were dissolved in DMF (10 mL) by stirring for 10 min. 1–2 pinches of LiH were poured, and the reaction mixture was stirred again for 15–25 min. The electrophile, 9, was added to the above reaction mixture and kept on stirring for 10–12 h. TLC was performed to monitor the reaction. After completion, a few drops of concentrated aqueous KOH were added. Ice cold distilled water was added to get the precipitates which were filtered, washed, and dried.

Computational Studies

Computational studies include molecular docking, physicochemical properties, determination of bioactivities, and density functional theory (DFT).

Molecular Docking

The binding modes of the synthesized compound with an active site of the enzyme were determined by molecular docking studies. Crystal structures of four proteins of four enzymes were downloaded from protein data bank (PDB). The four enzymes were AChE, urease, alpha-glucosidase, and BChE with the ID of proteins as 1C20, 3LA4, 5NN6, and 6ESJ, respectively. , In the downloaded structures, water molecules and ligands were removed by cleaning in Biovia Discovery Studio 2021. The structures of synthesized compounds were drawn in ChemDraw. The files were cleaned with root center and grid designing, which were performed with spacing to target the whole proteins and their active sites. Protein files were saved as quotient files (pdbqt file) and continued docking using Autodock and Autodock Vina under standard protocols. ,

Density Functional Techniques

Newly synthesized compounds were subjected to density functional techniques. Gauss View 6.0.16 software was downloaded to draw the molecular structure. Its optimization was performed by the 6-311++G (d, p) basis set. Gaussian 06W was studied to analyze MEP. It includes the drug-receptor and enzyme substrate interaction with the B3LYP method and 6-311++G (d, p) basis set. MEPs were used to check reactive sites of electrophile and nucleophile attacks on synthesized compounds. To find the energy gap between HOMO (highest occupied molecular orbital) and LUMO (lowest unoccupied molecular orbital), molecular orbital analysis was utilized. The energy gaps provide information about chemical behavior, reactivity, and stability. Different groups attached to compounds affect the HOMO–LUMO energy gaps. The small gap shows a high reactivity and low stability. Similarly, large gaps make molecules less reactive because LUMO cannot add electrons. Electron-withdrawing groups decrease the gap of HOMO–LUMO of compounds.

Physicochemical Properties of Compounds

Physicochemical properties included surface area, weight, Lipinski violation, volume, enzyme inhibitor, protease inhibitor, and kinase inhibitor. These properties analyze the ability of compounds to act as a drug. All the above parameters were determined computationally by PreADMET and DataWarrior software. ,

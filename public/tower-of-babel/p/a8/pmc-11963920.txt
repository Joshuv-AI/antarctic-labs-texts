Associations of 24‐Hour Central Systolic Blood Pressure With Multiorgan Damage in Nondialysis Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease
Authors: Cheng Chen, Ye Zhu, Lingling Liu, Jianting Ke, Wenjuan Yu, Qirong Song, Man Li, Ying Tang, Cheng Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMultiple target‐organ damages (TODs) in the same patient are common and further increase the risk of cardiovascular disease. However, the relationship between ambulatory central systolic blood pressure (SBP) and multiple TODs has yet to be explored.Methods and ResultsMobilO‐Graph PWA was used to monitor the participants' ambulatory blood pressure, and the presence of left ventricular hypertrophy, carotid hypertrophy, and kidney injury were used to define TOD. Logistic regression analyses and receiver operating characteristic analyses were used to explore the correlation between SBP and TOD. Overall, 2018 nondialysis patients with chronic kidney disease were included and 580 (28.74%) had multiple TODs. Twenty‐four‐hour central SBP with c2 calibration exhibited a stronger correlation with the increasing number of TOD compared with 24‐hour brachial SBP in ordinal logistic regression analyses. In the multivariable analyses with the presence of multiple TODs, the odds ratios were 1.786 (95% CI, 1.474–2.165; P<0.001) for 24‐hour brachial SBP and 1.949 (95% CI, 1.605–2.366; P<0.001) for 24‐hour central SBP with c2 calibration. The receiver operating characteristic analyses also showed that 24‐hour central SBP with c2 calibration had higher discrimination than 24‐hour brachial SBP regarding multiple TODs (P<0.001). In addition, using 130/135 mm Hg as the threshold for 24‐hour brachial SBP/central SBP with c2 calibration to cross‐classify, the prevalence of multiple TODs was greater in cases of concordant hypertension compared with cases of isolated brachial hypertension and concordant normotension, with no difference between the latter 2 conditions.ConclusionsTwenty‐four‐hour central SBP with c2 calibration was more associated with the presence of multiple TODs compared with 24‐hour brachial SBP and was helpful in risk classification of multiple TODs among nondialysis patients with chronic kidney disease.

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is increasingly recognized as a significant worldwide health issue, 1 , 2 while hypertension is a major modifiable cause of early death, 3 which is highly common in the condition of CKD. 4 Appropriate management of hypertension improves the adverse prognosis of patients with CKD, 5 and the method of blood pressure (BP) measurement is crucial for hypertension management. Central BP represents the true load imposed on the heart, brain, kidney, and large arteries, and is different from the BP that is measured at the arm. 6 , 7 Due to the physiopathological advantage, amplification phenomenon, and the different efficacy of antihypertensive drugs on central versus brachial BP, 8 , 9 the measurement of central BP may be promising.

As is well known, target‐organ damage (TOD) is not only a response to hypertension but also a marker of preclinical or asymptomatic cardiovascular disease (CVD). The presence of TOD increases the CVD risk, and the risk is even higher in cases of damage affecting multiple organs. 10 , 11 In addition, the reversion of TODs with antihypertensive treatment improves the outcomes associated with hypertension. Ambulatory brachial BP is typically considered superior to clinic brachial BP regarding TOD, 12 while only a few relatively small studies explored the relationship between 24‐hour central pressure and 24‐hour brachial pressure concerning TOD, 13 , 14 , 15 leading to inconsistent findings.

Little is known about the relationship between ambulatory central BP and TOD in the population of CKD, which is characterized by special pathophysiologic mechanisms of hypertension, unusual BP rhythm, and a high incidence of CVD. 4 , 16 Because multiple TODs in the same patient are common and further increase CVD risk 17 , 18 and investigation of multiple TODs can help in recognizing high‐risk or very high‐risk patients at an early stage, the correlation between ambulatory central BP and multiple TODs needs to be studied. To address these knowledge gaps, we explored the association between ambulatory central BP and the damage affecting multiple organs in a large nondialysis population with CKD.

Methods

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Design and Participants

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee and the Institutional Review Board of the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐Sen University. All patients enrolled in this study provided written informed consent before data collection.

This multicenter, cross‐sectional study included patients admitted to the Department of Nephrology at the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐Sen University and the Third Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University between April 2018 and June 2023. The inclusion criteria were (1) patients diagnosed with CKD according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes guidelines, 19 specific diagnostic criteria presented in Data S1, (2) age >18 years, (3) completed ambulatory blood pressure monitoring of valid quality, and (4) completed 3 kinds of TOD assessment. Exclusion criteria were (1) transplant or dialysis; (2) arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, sick sinus syndrome, II or III degree atrioventricular block); (3) ongoing treatment with moderate to high dose glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants; (4) known nonhypertensive causes of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH); (5) breastfeeding or pregnancy; (6) estimated glomerular filtration rate fluctuations exceeding 30% within the previous 3 months; (7) cardiocerebrovascular disease within the previous 3 months; and (8) an unstable clinical condition, including recent serious infections or aggressive cancers. We re‐reviewed the patient's diagnosis, and no patient in this study was diagnosed with secondary hypertension such as primary aldosteronism, Cushing syndrome, pheochromocytoma, or paraganglioma.

BP Measurements

Clinic brachial BP was assessed before the ambulatory blood pressure monitoring device was placed at the doctor's office. Three readings were obtained at 1‐ to 2‐minute intervals in a seated position using a standard oscillometric device after a 5‐minute rest. The mean of the 3 measurements was used for analyses.

MobilO‐Graph PWA (IEM, Stolberg, Germany) was used to measure central and brachial BP in all participants, which has been verified for the measurement of brachial BP by the criteria of the British Hypertension Society and the European Society of Hypertension. 20 , 21 The accuracy of the automated oscillometric instrument in measuring central BP was validated against invasive gold‐standard high‐fidelity microtip catheters and the noninvasive, validated SphygmoCor device. 22 , 23 In addition, the feasibility and reproducibility of assessing central hemodynamics at rest and during daytime ambulatory monitoring have been proved. 24 , 25 The monitor was usually fitted between 8:00 and 9:00 am for 24 hours, and the automated measurements were taken at 15‐minute intervals during the day (7:00 am to 10:00 pm) and 30‐minute intervals during the night (10:00 pm to 7:00 am). Proper cuff size was selected in accordance with the arm circumference, and the device was placed on the nondominant arm. To allow for a direct comparison of brachial and central BP, measurements with device failure to estimate central parameters along with the brachial BP were excluded. Those with >20 daytime and 7 nighttime readings were regarded as valid. 26 The brachial systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP calibration method (c1 calibration), and the mean arterial pressure and diastolic BP calibration method (c2 calibration) were both used in the current research since there is no agreement on the best calibration method.

Assessment of TOD

The presence of LVH, carotid hypertrophy, and kidney injury were used to define TOD.

Echocardiography

Linear measurements of the left ventricular internal diameter, interventricular septum, and posterior wall thickness were assessed at the end of diastole by experienced echocardiographers. Left ventricular mass is calculated by the formula, left ventricular mass (g)=0.8×(1.04×[(left ventricular internal diameter+interventricular septum+posterior wall thickness)3–left ventricular internal diameter3]+0.6). 27 LVH was defined as >115 g/m2 for men and >95 g/m2 for women after left ventricular mass was standardized to body surface area. 28

Carotid Ultrasonography

B‐mode ultrasonography was used by experienced operators to measure the carotid intima‐media thickness. The distance between the typical echoes from the lumen–intima and media–adventitia interfaces was used to define intima‐media thickness. 29 This was accomplished by averaging 3 measurements made on both the left and right common carotid artery, starting ≈15 mm proximal to the carotid artery bulb at the end of the diastole. Carotid intima‐media thickness >0.9 mm was considered as carotid hypertrophy. 30

Kidney Assessment

Serum creatinine was measured using an isotope dilution mass spectrometry‐traceable approach, and estimated glomerular filtration rate was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration creatinine equation. 31 In accordance with the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes guidelines, kidney injury was defined with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of <60 mL/min per 1.73 m2. 19

Other Data

Additional information, such as sociodemographic details and medical history, was gathered during the initial research visit and from clinical documents. The baseline laboratory values were defined within 3 days of conducting ambulatory blood pressure monitoring. SBP≥140 mm Hg, or diastolic BP≥90 mm Hg, or the usage of antihypertensive medications for the treatment of hypertension were regarded to be hypertension. Fasting blood glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, or random blood glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L, or usage of glucose‐lowering medications were all considered to be diabetes.

Statistical Analysis

Data analyses were performed with SPSS 25.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL) and R version 4.3.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as P<0.05. Continuous variables were presented as the mean±SD or median (interquartile range) on the basis of the normality of distribution, which was performed by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Frequencies and percentages were used to present categorical variables. P trend was calculated by the χ2 trend test for categorical variables or linear regression trend test for continuous variables among the 4 groups. To compare the association between BP indices and the growing number of TOD (0 TOD, 1 TOD, 2 TOD, 3 TOD), univariable and multivariable ordinal logistic regression analyses were used. In addition, univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to further subdivide the association between BP indices and the presence of TOD (≥1 TOD) and multiple TODs (≥2 TOD). Pseudo R 2 was used to assess the fit of the model. Due to the collinearity between central and brachial BP, these parameters were added separately into each model. The regression model was expanded to include an interaction term among the subgroups of age, sex, CVD history, antihypertensive medication, and BP indices regarding multiple TODs.

Receiver operator characteristic analyses were established to evaluate the discrimination of BP indices in relation to the presence of TOD and multiple TODs, with area under the curve (AUC) values and their 95% CIs calculated, and the Delong method was used to compare AUCs. Based on the results of the prior part of this study, and that central SBP with c2 calibration (c2SBP) was closer to central pressure measured invasively than central SBP with c1 calibration (c1SBP), 22 participants were divided into 4 groups based on the cross‐classification of ambulatory brachial SBP (bSBP) 30 (130 mm Hg) and central c2 SBP thresholds 8 (135 mm Hg). Analyses of multivariable logistic regression were used to compare the incidence of TOD and multiple TODs in each of these various groups. Variables that were considered clinically relevant or that showed a univariable association (P<0.05) with outcome were included in multivariable analyses, age, sex, body mass index, alcohol intake, smoking, diabetes, cardiovascular disease history, antihypertensive medications, lipid‐lowering drugs, serum phosphate, intact parathyroid hormone, 24‐hour heart rate, and clinic SBP were included in fully multivariable analyses.

Association of BP Indices With the Increasing Number of TODs

All BP indices were significantly related to the growing number of TODs, while 24‐hour c2SBP consistently showed a stronger association with the increasing number of TODs than other BP indices in univariable analyses and partially adjusted other covariates, which was evident through the largest odds ratio (OR) and the highest R 2 values inclusion of 24‐hour c2SBP in the models (Table 2). After fully adjusted other covariates including clinic SBP, 24‐hour bSBP with an OR of 1.947 (95% CI, 1.672–2.268; P<0.001), 24‐hour c1SBP with an OR of 1.916 (95% CI, 1.629–2.253; P<0.001), and 24‐hour c2SBP with an OR of 2.098 (95% CI, 1.800–2.447; P<0.001) respectively, and the model involving 24‐hour c2SBP provided the highest R 2 (Table 2, model 4).

Association of BP Indices With TOD

All BP indices were significantly correlated with the presence of TOD, while 24‐hour c2SBP provided the largest OR in univariable and multivariable analyses, and the same models with 24‐hour c2SBP produced the highest R 2 (Table S1; Table 3). Notably, with the presence of multiple TODs, 24‐hour c2SBP consistently showed an advantage over other BP indices, reflected in the largest OR and the highest R 2 value inclusion of 24‐hour c2SBP in univariable analyses and partially adjusted other covariates (Table S1). After fully adjusting other covariates, the ORs were 1.786 (95% CI, 1.474–2.165; P<0.001) for 24‐hour bSBP, 1.740 (95% CI, 1.417–2.137; P<0.001) for 24‐hour c1SBP and 1.949 (95% CI, 1.605–2.366; P<0.001) for 24‐hour c2SBP respectively, and the model including 24‐hour c2SBP provided the highest R 2 (Table 3). In addition, subgroups of age (<45 years and >45 years), sex (female and male), CVD history (with or without), antihypertensive medication usage (with or without) yielded consistent results. No interaction was identified between BP indices and sex, CVD history, and antihypertensive medication usage, whereas there was a significant interaction between BP indices and age regarding multiple TODs (Table S2).

Receiver Operator Characteristic Analyses of TOD

All BP indices significantly discriminated the presence of TOD; AUCs were 0.693, 0.714, 0.708, and 0.724 for clinic SBP, 24‐hour bSBP, 24‐hour c1SBP, and 24‐hour c2SBP respectively, according to the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis (Figure 2A). The Delong method showed that 24‐hour c2SBP had greater power of discrimination in comparison with clinic SBP, 24‐hour bSBP, and 24‐hour c1SBP (Table S3). Furthermore, the AUCs were 0.675, 0.705, 0.690, and 0.716 for clinic SBP, 24‐hour bSBP, 24‐hour c1SBP, and 24‐hour c2SBP regarding the presence of multiple TODs (Figure 2B). Likewise, the comparison of AUCs demonstrated that clinic SBP and 24‐hour c1SBP had comparable predictive discrimination (P=0.245), 24‐hour bSBP had a higher discriminatory ability than clinic SBP (P=0.018), and 24‐hour c1SBP (P<0.001), while 24‐hour c2SBP had a stronger discriminatory ability versus 24‐hour bSBP (P<0.001; Table S3).

Cross‐Classification of Central and Brachial Systolic Hypertension

Applying a cross‐classification approach between ambulatory brachial hypertension (threshold, 130 mm Hg) and ambulatory central hypertension by c2 calibration (threshold, 135 mm Hg), the population was divided into 4 groups. A total of 1438 patients with concordant normotension (71.26%), 148 patients with isolated brachial hypertension (7.33%), 10 patients with isolated central hypertension (0.50%), and 442 patients with concordant hypertension (20.91%; Figure S2), the characteristics of participants according to cross‐classification is shown in Table S4. The multivariable logistic regression analysis included the other 3 groups since the size of the isolated central hypertension group was so limited, and the concordant normotension was set as the reference. Table 4 shows the adjusted ORs of the presence of TOD and multiple TODs associated with the hypertension subgroups. Regarding the presence of TOD, the multivariable‐adjusted ORs were 1.391 (95% CI, 0.843–2.337; P=0.204) for isolated brachial hypertension and 2.044 (95% CI, 1.363–3.109; P<0.001) for concordant hypertension. Moreover, the odds of the presence of multiple TODs for isolated brachial hypertension was not significantly different from concordant normotension (OR, 1.216 [95% CI, 0.784–1.863]; P=0.375), while concordant hypertension was associated with higher odds (OR, 2.417 [95% CI, 1.799–3.249]; P<0.001).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, the present study first explored the association of 24‐hour central SBP and multiple TODs in a large nondialysis population with CKD. We found that 24‐hour c2SBP exhibited a stronger correlation with the increasing number of TODs and was more related to the presence of TOD and multiple TODs compared with 24‐hour bSBP. In addition, a greater prevalence of TOD and multiple TODs were observed in cases of concordant hypertension compared with cases of isolated brachial hypertension and concordant normotension, with no difference between the latter 2 conditions.

Assessment of TOD is recommended by hypertension guidelines, and LVH, carotid hypertrophy, and kidney injury are predictors of adverse clinical events, which are easily accessible in clinical practice. 30 , 32 Moreover, a number of studies reported that the risk of adverse clinical outcomes increased as the number of damaged organs grew. 33 , 34 , 35 , 36 The presence of multiple TODs in the same individual is not rare, which was confirmed by our findings. Although only 3 kinds of TODs were assessed, the presence of multiple TODs was up to 28.74%. Central BP is a direct measurement of the pressure to which vital organs are exposed, 7 and it may have the potential to improve the assessment of TOD. In this study, we found that ambulatory central SBP increased as the number of TODs rose, implying a close link between ambulatory central SBP and TODs. A study by Protogeroua et al showed that classifying individuals according to ambulatory bSBP levels led to a significant overlap in ambulatory central SBP, 37 such as a part of the individuals having 24‐hour bSBP between 130 and 139 mm Hg had similar 24‐hour central SBP with those having 24‐hour bSBP between 120–129 mm Hg. Furthermore, this phenomenon was not a random effect, as it was observed in clinic central SBP. 38 Therapeutic strategies are often made on the basis of bSBP, and perhaps we lowered the originally modest central SBP.

In the present study, it was observed that 24‐hour c2SBP was superior to 24‐hour bSBP regarding the presence of TOD. Particularly, our findings first suggested that 24‐hour c2SBP demonstrated a greater association with the increasing number of TODs, and was more related to multiple TODs compared with 24‐hour bSBP. A study by Kusunoki et al 13 was partially in accordance with our findings, which suggested that 24‐hour brachial as well as 24‐hour central SBP were all significantly associated with the presence of TOD, and both 24‐hour bSBP (OR, 1.03 for 1 mm Hg increase) and central SBP (OR, 1.12 for 1 mm Hg increase) maintained statistical significance when each pair of BP values was included in the same model. However, 2 cross‐sectional studies demonstrated that both 24‐hour brachial and central SBP were correlated with the presence of TOD, but 24‐hour central SBP is not superior to 24‐hour bSBP. 14 , 15 Perhaps the differences in study populations, relatively small sample sizes, and different approaches to target‐organ assessment account for the inconsistent results. Furthermore, we think the more critical point is the calibration of central BP.

c2SBP was reported to be closer to invasively measured central pressure than c1SBP. 22 , 23 Furthermore, according to Weber et al, there was a weaker association between 24‐hour bSBP and 24‐hour c2SBP than that between 24‐hour bSBP and 24‐hour c1SBP. 8 A prospective multicenter study suggested that 24‐hour c2SBP had better statistical predictive power than 24‐hour bSBP for LVH. 39 Besides, another recent study demonstrated that 24‐hour c2SBP but not 24‐hour c1SBP showed superiority over 24‐hour bSBP in terms of carotid arterial damage. 40 In the current study, it was 24‐hour c2SBP rather than 24‐hour c1SBP that revealed an advantage compared with 24‐hour bSBP. Based on the existing studies and our findings, 24‐hour c2SBP was used for cross‐classification. Using 130/135 mm Hg as the threshold for 24‐hour bSBP/c2SBP to cross‐classify, our results indicated that the prevalence of TOD and multiple TODs was higher in cases of concordant hypertension compared with cases of isolated brachial hypertension as well as concordant normotension, and there was no statistical difference between concordant normotension as the reference and isolated brachial hypertension. Although the proportion of isolated brachial hypertension in this study was only 7.33%, individuals with this condition may not be the minority, given the high prevalence of hypertension. 41 , 42 The management of isolated brachial hypertension deserves consideration for the already modest value of central SBP, the high economic burden of hypertension, 43 and the side effects of antihypertensive drugs. 44

There are some strengths in this study. All participants included in the current study had comprehensive evaluations, and the sample size was large. Moreover, the value of ambulatory central SBP in the present study was obtained using 2 calibrated methods. Additionally, we considered the patient as a whole and examined the relationship between ambulatory central SBP and the number of target organs affected in the patient rather than studying each type of TOD since multiple TODs have a worse prognostic implication than a single one. Investigating a specific type of TOD may not be able to recognize the real risk to which patients are exposed. However, some limitations in this study should be acknowledged as well. First, this is a cross‐sectional, observation study from which no relationship of cause and effect can be drawn. Second, the association between clinic central SBP and TOD could not be obtained because clinic central SBP was not measured in this study. Third, extrapolation of the findings to other populations requires caution, as the population in this study was Chinese inpatients with CKD. Fourth, the ambulatory central SBP threshold has not been recommended by any guideline, and studies on this topic are rare. The ambulatory central SBP threshold used for cross‐classification in this study was derived from a recently published article by a worldwide research consortium. 8 Hence, the ambulatory central SBP threshold used in this study requires further validation in prospective studies. Fifth, a high‐salt diet is well known to influence the presence and severity of TOD. However, this study may not be able to properly address the effect on the results due to the lack of dietary surveys. Finally, 148 patients with isolated brachial hypertension (7.33%) were found in this study on the basis of the cross‐classification of ambulatory bSBP and central c2SBP, and the relatively small sample size may have an impact on the results. A study reported that the prevalence of isolated brachial hypertension was 0.9% to 3.4% according to the cross‐classification of clinic brachial and central SBP. 45 Perhaps the prevalence of isolated brachial hypertension is relatively low and there is a need to expand the sample size for further validation.

In conclusion, this study revealed that 24‐hour c2SBP was more associated with the growing number of TODs and was more related to the multiple TODs compared with 24‐hour bSBP. Thus, ambulatory central SBP may be more closely linked to the formation of TODs and more associated with the risk of adverse outcomes. In addition, the prevalence of multiple TODs in cases of isolated brachial hypertension was not different from the cases of concordant normotension, which also highlighted the role of ambulatory central SBP in the risk classification. Future large‐scale prospective studies on cardiovascular events and renal outcomes are needed to validate the clinical value of ambulatory central SBP.

Correction: Divergent effects of environmental concern and risk perception on pro-environmental intention: an international study across 17 countries
Authors: Yuzuki Kikko, Tsukasa Ishigaki
Published: 2026

Correction to: Scientific Reports 10.1038/s41598-025-91677-7, published online 05 March 2025

The original version of this Article contained errors in the Results, Discussion, Conclusion and in Tables 6 and 7.

In the Results, under the subheading ‘Relationship between constructive concepts’,

“The results showed that all statistically significant path coefficients were positive, and that the path coefficient value varied between countries, supporting H1 and H2. This indicated that EC and risk perception were positively associated with pro-environmental intentions. Slovakia (SK) and Lithuania (LT) have EC-to-intention path coefficients higher than 0.9. These two countries have relatively small populations compared to the others; ERP to Intention path coefficients are higher in the United States (US), Finland (FL), and Norway (NO) at 0.8, 0.72, and 0.71, respectively. In all countries, the value of the path coefficient from EC to Intention is greater than the value of the path coefficient from ERP to Intention. This suggests that EC is more likely to affect Intention than ERP. All covariance values are positively significant, with values ranging from 0.38 to 0.68, indicating that the relationship between EC and ERP is moderately correlated.”

now reads:

“The results of EC-to-intention showed that all statistically significant path coefficients were positive, and that the path coefficient value varied between countries, supporting H1. This indicated that EC was positively associated with pro-environmental intentions. Finland (FL) and Norway (NO) have EC-to-intention path coefficients higher than 1.2. The ERP-to-intention path coefficients were statistically significant in five countries: Switzerland (CH), Italy (IT), South Korea (KR), Slovakia (SK), and the United States (US). Although statistically significant effects were not observed in all 17 countries, four of the five countries in which statistical significance was detected exhibited positive estimated coefficients. Therefore, hypothesis H2 received limited support. In the subsequent analyses of ERP, we focus on the five countries that exhibited statistically significant differences. All covariance values are positively significant, with values ranging from 0.22 to 0.83, indicating that the relationship between EC and ERP is moderately or highly correlated.”

Under the subheading ‘Relationship between estimated path coefficients and cultural indicators across countries’,

“Table 7 shows that individualism and indulgence have negatively significant partial correlation coefficients with respect to the EC path coefficients. In relation to the ERP path coefficient, individualism and indulgence have positively significant partial correlation coefficients, while power distance and uncertainty avoidance have negatively significant correlation coefficients. No significant cultural trends were found in the covariance between EC and ERP. As a result, our SEM measures extracted statistically significant relationships between the four cultural tendencies and each country’s environmental intention formation. We were also able to extract differences in the relationship between EC and ERP regarding individualism and indulgence.”

now reads:

“Table 7 shows that individualism and indulgence have positive significant partial correlation coefficients with respect to the EC path coefficients, while power distance and uncertainty avoidance have negatively significant correlation coefficients. Power distance, individualism and indulgence were significant in the covariance between EC and ERP.”

In the Discussion, under the subheading ‘Differences of influence from ECs and risk perception to pro-environmental intention across countries’,

“For instance, in Croatia, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Lithuania, Slovenia, and Slovakia, increasing risk perception appeared to be significantly more effective in enhancing pro-environmental intention than increasing EC. In Finland, Norway, and the United States, both EC and risk perception exerted a similar influence on environmental intention, signifying that both were significant factors. Specifically, variations in the magnitudes of the path coefficients were observed within each country. For instance, in Croatia, the path coefficient value from EC to intention was 0.15, whereas that from ERP to intention was 0.89. This indicated that in Croatia, the influence of increased EC and risk perception on pro-environmental behavior differed.

The estimated path coefficients exhibited considerable variations. Among the countries with statistically significant results for the relationship between EC and intention, Japan had a minimum value of 0.73, while Slovakia had a maximum value of 0.94. Regarding the relationship between ERP and intentions, South Korea had a minimum value of 0.13, while the United States had a maximum value of 0.80.”

now reads:

“For instance, in Switzerland, South Korea, Slovakia, and United States, increasing EC appeared to be significantly more effective in enhancing pro-environmental intention than increasing ERP. In Italy, both EC and ERP exerted a similar influence on environmental intention, signifying that both were significant factors. Specifically, variations in the magnitudes of the path coefficients were observed within each country. The estimated path coefficients exhibited considerable variations. Among the countries with statistically significant results for the relationship between EC and intention, Italy had a minimum value of 0.14, while Norway had a maximum value of 1.29. Regarding the relationship between ERP and intentions, Switzerland had a negative value of − 0.10, while Slovakia and the United States had a maximum value of 0.14.”

In the Conclusion,

“The results supported all the hypotheses.”

now reads:

“The results supported two hypotheses: the EC-to-intention relationship and the covariance between EC and ERP.”

The original Article has been corrected.

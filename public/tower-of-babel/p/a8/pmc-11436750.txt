Mineralization characteristics of soil organic carbon under different herbaceous plant mosaics in semi-arid grasslands
Authors: Bo Cheng, Hai-yan Dai, Tie-jun Liu, Hu Liu, Jin-rong Li, Rui-jun Qiao, Yang Shao, Sheng-li Wang, Jian Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe aimed to explore the mineralization characteristics of soil organic carbon(SOC) under different plant species in semi-arid grassland and provide basic soil carbon cycling data. Leymus chinensis, Stipa krylovii Roshev, Artemisia frigida, and Agrophorn cristam (L.) Gaertn were selected as the plant species. Incubation experiment were conducted on SOC mineralization in soil aggregates with particle sizes of > 2, 1–2, 0.25–1, and < 0.25 mm. The cumulative SOC mineralization amount in L. chinensis with a particle size > 2 mm was the highest, exceeding that of A. cristam (L.) Gaertn by approximately 136.14%. S. krylovii Roshev (70.73%), L. chinensis (58.05%), and A. frigida (33.73%) exhibited pronounced promotion effects on mineralization. The potential SOC mineralization of S. krylovii Roshev was the greatest among all species at the same soil particle size. The potential SOC mineralization was highest at a particle size of > 2 mm for all plant types. All plant types increased the SOC mineralization rate and cumulative mineralization in soils with large particle sizes, the mineralization reaction occurred more strongly. Organic carbon cumulative SOC mineralization rapidly increased in all tests during the first 20 days and gradually slowed thereafter.Keywords: Semi-arid grassland, Organic carbon mineralization, Plant community, Soil aggregate, Potential mineralization, Promotion effectSubject terms: Ecology, Environmental sciences

Introduction

Scattered patches of herbaceous plants are a common morphological feature of grasslands. This patchy distribution is caused by several factors, including varying grazing management methods, differences in soil hydrological conditions caused by diverse grassland microtopographies, disturbance by mound-building animals, and inherent species characteristics1. The differences in plant communities affect the stability of organic carbon sequestration and the efficiency of carbon pool dynamics2. The canopy closure and ground cover of different plant communities vary, leading to different abilities to intercept rainfall and reduce surface soil erosion3. At the same time, differences in soil humus, plant root activity range, and root exudates in different plant communities can directly change the physical structure of soil and indirectly change its internal biological and chemical factors, leading to changes in the protection mechanism of soil organic carbon, thereby affecting the fixation and dynamic transformation of soil organic carbon4.

Soil aggregates are heterogeneous independent units. Based on their diameter, aggregates are typically categorized as microaggregates (< 0.25 mm) or macroaggregates (> 0.25 mm), and their content and quality can affect air permeability, water retention, and soil erosion resistance5,6. The formation of soil aggregates is mainly related to the use of soil organic carbon as a binder7. The formation of aggregates inevitably involves the participation of soil organic carbon, and there is a close relationship between aggregates and soil organic carbon8. Usually, organic carbon exists in different ways, one is encapsulated in voids during the formation of aggregates, and the other is related to some mineral substances that form aggregates9. Therefore, the degree of protection of organic carbon by aggregates can be described by the processes of “isolation” and “adsorption”. The properties and content of organic carbon are influenced by the particle size of aggregates, mainly due to the different protective mechanisms of aggregates with different particle sizes on organic carbon, which in turn affects its integrity, stability, and the decomposition and accumulation of organic carbon10. Therefore, studying the dynamic changes in the composition characteristics of soil aggregates is of great help for the in-depth study of carbon cycling.

SOC mineralization refers to the decomposition of SOC into simple, small molecules that are converted into CO2, H2O, and inorganic salts11. This process promoted soil nutrient cycling, while simultaneously producing and releasing a large amount of CO2 into the atmosphere. The SOC mineralization rate depends on external conditions, such as microbial activity, supply of organic substrate, temperature, and precipitation12–15. This process is linked to soil carbon storage, which reflects soil carbon cycle dynamics, serves as an important indicator of SOC stability and the global carbon cycle, and is an important part of the terrestrial carbon cycle.

Grassland is an important part of the terrestrial carbon reservoir, research on grassland SOC mineralization has focused on the influence of soil conditions, climate conditions, and exogenous additives on SOC mineralization on a relatively large scale16–18. There are currently few studies on the mineralization of SOC by different grassland plants at a small scale. However, due to the differences in plant morphology of different vegetation types in grasslands, the types of litter and root exudates, it is inevitable that the impact of different plants on SOC mineralization in grasslands varies. To explore this difference, this study took common herbaceous plants in desert grasslands as research objects, collected soil samples in the field, and conducted indoor simulated SOC mineralization experiments to study the SOC mineralization rate, SOC mineralization amount, and the promotion effect of different plants on SOC. Our results are important for protecting regional ecological environments.

Among all treatments, only the stimulation effect of Leymus chinensis on SOC in the 1–2 mm particle size fraction was − 3.93%. All other treatments had a positive effect on SOC, among which the promotion effects of S and L were significantly higher than those of A and C in particle sizes a and c, and the promotion effect of S was significantly higher than that of L, A, and C in particle sizes b and d.

Discussion

Due to the differences in developmental morphology, litter quality, and secretion composition of different plants during their growth process, there are significant differences in the impact of plants on their growth soil19–21. Studies have shown that there is a correlation between aboveground vegetation and soil moisture content and soil composition22,23. There is a positive correlation between soil aggregates and SOC content, which is because SOC is mainly distributed in large water stable aggregates, so SOC content increases with the increase of aggregate particle size24.The reduction in soil aggregate structure directly causes a reduction in SOC content, further supporting the theory of aggregate hierarchy)25–27. This is consistent with the research results of this study. The experimental results show that under the same conditions of above-ground plant conditions, both the SOC mineralization rate and the cumulative amount of SOC mineralization increase with the increase of soil particle size. Moreover, soil disturbance and vegetation can affect SOC distribution in soil aggregates. Wei et al. (2017) indicated that vegetation restoration could increase the SOC content in macroaggregates and decrease it in microaggregates, clay, and silt particles28. The long-term enclosure of grasslands changes vegetation types, microbial community characteristics, and stability of soil aggregates, thus affecting the mineralization characteristics of SOC29,30. The results of this study also indicate that aboveground vegetation can effectively increase the rate and cumulative amount of SOC mineralization. Among them, S. krylovii Roshev and L. chinensis have a more significant promoting effect on SOC mineralization, while the effect of A. cristam (L.) Gaertn is relatively weaker.

The promotion effect refers to the phenomenon in which the addition of exogenous organic matter can alter the SOC decomposition rate. This effect was discovered by Löhnis (1926) when the soil of leguminous plants decomposed, revealing that the addition of fresh organic residues to the soil promoted the mineralization of organic matter, and named by Bingeman et al. (1953)31,32. Moreover, Kuzyakov and Cheng (2001) found that root exudates alter the physical and chemical properties of soil, including soil pH, soil structure, and soil water content33. The active substances in root exudates also affect the activity of microorganisms, which alters the work efficiency of soil microorganisms, thereby affecting SOC processes. Many studies have shown that active substances secreted by plant roots positively affect promotion effects in SOC34–36. In this experiment, no aboveground plants, roots, related active enzymes, and microorganisms that can affect SOC mineralization were added to the soil in the SOC mineralization test, because the respiration of these substances may affect the amount of CO2 produced, thereby affecting the experimental results. However, because the experimental soil has been in a plant growth environment for a long time, the soil already contains a certain amount of secretions and other substances, which can effectively show the impact of aboveground plants on SOC mineralization. The results showed that S. krylovii Roshev, L. chinensis and A. frigida had obvious stimulating effect on SOC, which could effectively improve SOC mineralization and accelerate the process of soil C cycle. Some plant roots have a negative promotion effects on SOC, which may arise from competition for exogenous substances between the root system and microorganisms. Microorganisms that use SOC preferentially utilize root exudates, which decompose more readily than SOC, resulting in reduced SOC decomposition and negative promotion effect37. The results of this experiment indicate that the stimulating effect of L. chinensis on SOC was significantly weaker than that of the other three plants, and even had a negative stimulating effect when the soil particle size was 1–2 mm.The reason may be that there are substances in the secretions of L. chinensis that inhibit the mineralization of SOC, or that L. chinensis changes the physical structure or water conditions of the soil during its growth process. This requires further research in subsequent studies.

Conclusion

Soil particle size and aboveground vegetation have a significant impact on SOC mineralization. The SOC mineralization rate and cumulative SOC mineralization amount are positively correlated with soil particle size. Under five different aboveground vegetation conditions, the test group with soil particle size > 2 mm has the highest SOC mineralization rate and cumulative SOC mineralization amount. When the size of soil aggregates is the same, the SOC mineralization rate and cumulative SOC mineralization amount of S. krylovii Roshev and L. chinensis are higher than those of A. frigida and A. cristam (L.) Gaertn, and the SOC mineralization rate and cumulative SOC mineralization amount of all aboveground vegetation are higher than that of the control (no aboveground vegetation). The four plants all promote the organic carbon mineralization process of soil, among which S. krylovii Roshev and L. chinensis have a stronger promoting effect on SOC, while A. cristam (L.) Gaertn has a weaker one.

Materials and methods

Overview of the study area

The research area (Fig. 5) is located at the National Field Scientific Observation and Research Station of Grassland Ecohydrology at the northern foot of the Yinshan Mountains in the Darhan Muminggan Joint Banner, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China (41°21′9″N, 111°12′55″E). The climate is a semi-arid monsoon continental in the mid-temperate zone, with an average annual precipitation of 246 mm and average annual temperature of 3.4 °C. The soil type was characterized as loam. Grassland plants in this area are dominated by L. chinensis, S. krylovii Roshev, A. frigida, A. cristatum (L.) Gaertn, Cleistogenes squarrosa, and Stipa capillata.

Location of the study area, design of the sampling sites, and dominant grassland.

Experimental design

Soil sample collection

Relatively flat terrain was selected for the study area. In September 2021, vegetation surveys were performed on sample plots (150 × 150 m) comprising the same soil type, and the vegetation in the sample plots was distributed in a mosaic pattern. L. chinensis, A. cristam (L.) Gaertn, A. frigida, and S. krylovii Roshev were dominant and interspersed with various plants, such as C. squarrosa, Aster altaicus, and Neopallasia pectinata. Soil samples were collected from typical sample plots where L. chinensis (L), A. cristam (L.) Gaertn (C), A. frigida (A), and S. krylovii Roshev (S) were the dominant species, as well as from plots without plants (CK) (Fig. 1). An 8-cm diameter soil auger was used to collect soil samples, In each plant quadrat (L. chinensis (L); A. Cristam (L.) Gaertn (c), A. FRIGIDA (a); S. Krylovii Roshev (s), Without above ground plants (CK)) collect three soil samples (0–30 cm).Then mix three soil samples, rocks and insects were removed, and put the sample in 4 ℃ incubator and take it back to the laboratory. One portion of the soil samples was air-dried in a cool place for five days and passed through a 2-mm sieve to determine the physical and chemical properties of the soil. The remaining portion was used for aggregate classification and mineralization incubation experiments.

Aggregate classification

Soil sample aggregates were classified using the dry-sieving method under optimal humidity conditions38. The collected soil samples (L, C, A, S, and CK) were evenly spread in an incubator (LHS-150SC, Shanghai Jiecheng Experimental Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and dried at a constant temperature of 25 °C to remove impurities. Then 300 g of soil was selected and passed through 2 mm soil sieve, 1 mm soil sieve and 0.25 mm soil sieve in order to screen the soil samples with the first four particle sizes (> 2 mm, 1–2 mm, 0.25–1 mm and < 0.25 mm) for the incubation experiments of organic carbon mineralization.

Mineralization culture experiments

SOC mineralization culture experiments on the aggregates were conducted using a constant-temperature culture with alkaline solution absorption Three replicates were set up for each treatment, and an empty bottle with no soil (SCK) was prepared as a control, yielding 63 total culture bottles. Mix 20 g of soil sample evenly with quartz sand(The use of quartz sand helps to maintain soil aeration and moisture, making the test conditions closer to the natural state)(Meihong Environmental Protection Technology Co., Ltd. Chengdu, China) and then distribute it uniformly in a sealed culture bottle(Anhui Benyue Experimental Equipment Co., Ltd. Hefei. China) with a diameter of 8 cm and a height of 10 cm. Soil sample moisture content was adjusted with distilled water up to 60% of the field moisture capacity, and pre-incubation was performed at 25 °C in the dark. After seven days of pre-cultivation, place a small beaker containing 10mL of 0.5 mol/L NaOH in the culture bottle to absorb the CO2 released by organic carbon mineralization, set up three culture bottles without soil as blank controls. During the cultivation process, the NaOH solution that had absorbed CO2 was periodically take out at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 19, 24, 28, 36, 44, 54, 66, and 80 days. A standard solution of 0.5 mol/L HCl is added to titrate the solution in a small beaker. Finally, the amount of CO2 released is calculated based on the amount of HCl standard used. Each time the CO2 release is measured, the opening of the culture bottle is kept open for 2 h to ensure sufficient oxygen for soil microbial respiration39.

Determination of soil indicators

Soil pH was measured with a pH meter; SOC was assessed using potassium dichromate with external heating; alkaline-hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN) was evaluated using the alkaline hydrolysis diffusion method; available phosphorus (AP) was quantified by sodium bicarbonate leaching and by the molybdenum–antimony anti-colorimetric method; and available potassium (AK) was evaluated by NH4OAc leaching and flame photometry40.

A multitask deep learning framework for the automated quantification of abdominal tissue morphology on ultrasound and the therapeutic monitoring of diastasis recti abdominis
Authors: Xiaohua Zhao, Youliang Zhang, Yitao Jiang, Xinchun Li, Wanglin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDiastasis recti abdominis (DRA) requires accurate ultrasound-based quantification of abdominal tissue morphology, but manual measurement is operator-dependent and inefficient for longitudinal monitoring. This study developed and evaluated a multitask deep learning model for automated abdominal ultrasound analysis and the monitoring of magnetic stimulation therapy.MethodsA retrospective dataset of 2,750 abdominal ultrasound images from 632 women was used to train a ResNet-50-encoded U-Net with dual branches for tissue segmentation (linea alba, rectus abdominis, and subcutaneous fat) and anatomical region classification. Model performance was evaluated on an independent set of 550 images. The trained model was then applied prospectively to 21 patients undergoing six sessions of targeted abdominal magnetic stimulation therapy, with ultrasound measurements obtained before treatment, 1 day after the first session, and 1 day after the sixth session.ResultsThe model achieved Dice coefficients of 0.979±0.013 for subcutaneous fat, 0.951±0.066 for rectus abdominis muscle, and 0.828±0.074 for the linea alba. Automated measurements showed strong agreement with expert measurements, with intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) of 0.988 for subcutaneous fat thickness (SET), 0.955 for the interrectus distance (IRD), and 0.917 for rectus abdominis muscle thickness (RAMT). After six therapy sessions, the IRD decreased from 20.17±7.81 to 15.22±6.58 mm (P=0.04). Left and right RAMT increased from 8.27±1.04 to 10.26±1.23 mm and from 8.22±1.11 to 10.19±1.45 mm, respectively (both P values <0.001). There was a nonsignificant (P=0.05) decrease in SET from 16.56±3.56 to 14.30±3.55 mm.ConclusionsThe proposed multitask model enabled automated multitissue quantification on abdominal ultrasound images with strong agreement with expert measurements. It may support reproducible diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring of patients with DRA. Meanwhile, its ability to monitor treatment-related changes, particularly increases in short-term muscle thickness and reductions in subcutaneous fat, should be interpreted cautiously and validated in larger controlled studies.

Introduction

Diastasis recti abdominis (DRA), characterized by the pathological separation of the rectus abdominis muscles due to the widening of the linea alba (1,2), is a prevalent condition affecting up to 30% of postpartum women and individuals with obesity or chronic abdominal strain (3,4). It is associated with functional impairments, chronic low back pain, and negative impacts on quality of life. Accurate diagnosis and quantitative monitoring of DRA rely heavily on ultrasound imaging, which allows for the noninvasive measurement of key anatomical parameters: interrectus distance (IRD) (5,6), rectus abdominis muscle thickness (RAMT) (7), and subcutaneous fat thickness (SFT) (8). However, conventional manual ultrasound assessment is operator-dependent, time-consuming, and subject to interobserver variability, limiting its utility in large-scale screening and longitudinal therapeutic monitoring.

Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have shown promise in automating medical image analysis (9). The majority of preliminary studies have primarily focused on deep learning applications for abdominal tissue segmentation on computed tomography or magnetic resonance images (10-12). The existing approaches based on ultrasound images (13) also entail several limitations: (I) they often focus on single tasks (e.g., segmentation only), neglecting the importance of anatomical-aware modeling, which captures contextual information about the acquisition region and is essential for accurate multitissue quantification; (II) models are typically trained and validated on retrospective datasets lacking rigorous evaluation in clinical scenarios, particularly for tracking therapeutic responses.

To address these deficiencies, we developed and validated a novel multitask deep learning model for automated abdominal ultrasound analysis. In this study, we designed a unified AI system capable of simultaneous multitissue segmentation and anatomical region classification. Second, we applied this anatomical-aware framework to quantify longitudinal changes in IRD, RAMT, and SFT in patients with DRA undergoing magnetic stimulation therapy in order to assess its utility in clinical treatment monitoring.

We devised a robust U-Net-based multitask architecture trained on a large retrospective dataset of images (N=2,750) and rigorously validated it against expert annotations. Furthermore, we applied this model to analyze a clinical DRA treatment cohort (N=21), providing the AI-quantified abdominal tissue outcomes following magnetic stimulation therapy for DRA. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2025-aw-2188/rc).

Methods

Cohorts and data collection

As illustrated in Figure 1, abdominal ultrasound images were retrospectively collected from 632 participants (mean age 29.2±3.5 years) treated at a local hospital between January 2023 and December 2024, yielding a total of 2,750 images for model construction. This dataset comprised 930 images acquired from the upper abdominal region at the umbilical level and 1,820 images acquired from the lateral abdominal regions along the rectus abdominis muscle (including both the left and right sides), all of which were used for developing and training the AI model for automated analysis of DRA.

Flowchart of study design and data collection. In model construction, the training dataset included 2,750 retrospectively collected abdominal ultrasound images (930 from the upper abdominal region and 1,820 from the lateral abdominal region) from 632 participants (from January 2023 to December 2024). The DRA treatment dataset included images from 21 patients with DRA who were administered magnetic stimulation therapy (from January 2025 to June 2025), with ultrasound images being acquired at three treatment time points. DRA, diastasis recti abdominis.

Additionally, a separate cohort of 21 postpartum participants (mean age 28.2±4.1 years) undergoing DRA repair therapy was prospectively enrolled between January 2025 and June 2025 to form the DRA treatment cohort. These participants received targeted abdominal magnetic stimulation therapy via the MagGraver R170 device (Vishee Medical Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China), with treatment sessions administered every 3 days. Ultrasound images for this cohort were systematically acquired at three key time points: (I) pretreatment (Pre-tx)—the baseline assessment prior to the initiation of therapy; (II) posttreatment day 1 (Post-tx-d1)—24 hours following the completion of the first therapy session; and (III) posttreatment day 1 after six sessions [Post-tx-d1(6s)]—24 hours following the completion of the sixth therapy session. This dataset was exclusively reserved for evaluating the clinical measurement performance of the trained AI model.

The inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (I) female individuals aged ≥18 years and (II) referred for diagnostic assessment of suspected DRA (for the model construction dataset) or scheduled for magnetic stimulation therapy for confirmed DRA (for the clinical measurement dataset). Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) ultrasound images of insufficient quality and (II) incomplete or inaccessible clinical documentation. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Research Committee of Huadu District People’s Hospital (No. 2025137). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the model construction data. Written informed consent to participate was obtained from all participants included in the prospective therapeutic monitoring cohort prior to any study-related data collection.

Data acquisition

Ultrasound acquisition was performed under a standardized protocol to improve consistency across examinations.

For the upper abdominal region, images were acquired at approximately 3 cm above the umbilicus via a horizontal scan, with emphasis on clear visualization of the linea alba. For the lateral abdominal regions, images were obtained approximately 5 cm lateral to the umbilicus on both sides, also via a horizontal scan, with the aim of positioning the thickest portion of the rectus abdominis near the center of the image.

To minimize the influence of probe pressure, the transducer was initially applied with gentle compression to achieve adequate image quality, which was followed by a gradual release of pressure. The final frame acquired just before loss of image clarity was selected as the representative image, approximating a near-zero pressure condition.

Data processing

In the model construction dataset, the areas of the linea alba, rectus abdominis muscle, and subcutaneous fat were annotated by two experienced ultrasound physicians (both with over 5 years of experience in annotation) using LabelMe v. 5.8.2. To ensure high-quality annotations, when the interannotator agreement was insufficient (Dice coefficient <0.900), the images were forwarded to a senior physician with 10 years of experience in annotation for further review and consensus annotation. When agreement was high (Dice ≥0.900), one of the annotations was randomly selected as the final ground truth.

For model development, the dataset was partitioned into training (1,925 images), validation (275 images), and independent test (550 images) sets at a 7:1:2 ratio via a stratified random sampling strategy at the participant level. Subject-level constraints were enforced to ensure that images from the same participant were not distributed across different subsets, thereby preventing data leakage. In the test set, the IRD, RAMT, and SFT were measured by the experienced physicians. There were no missing data in this study. Only ultrasound images with adequate image quality and complete annotations were included in the analysis. Images with poor visualization, artifacts, or incomplete anatomical structures were excluded during the data-preprocessing stage.

Model construction

Our abdominal multitask ultrasound measurement model consists of a composite U-Net-like architecture (Figure 2). The image encoder includes a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on the ImageNet dataset. To evaluate the impact of encoder architecture, we additionally explored alternative backbones, including VGG16 and ResNet34. The segmentation decoder features a symmetric structure to the encoder and outputs four distinct channels corresponding to background, the linea alba, subcutaneous fat, and the rectus abdominis muscle. Concurrently, a dedicated anatomical region classification branch is implemented. This classifier is a binary model constructed with three fully connected layers. It takes the deepest, highest-level feature map (512-dimensional) extracted by the encoder as its input and is responsible for distinguishing between upper abdominal and lateral abdominal images.

Architecture of the multitask deep learning model. The composite U-Net framework features include (I) a ResNet-50 encoder (ImageNet-pretrained) for feature extraction; (II) a tissue segmentation branch (outputs the background, linea alba, subcutaneous fat, and rectus abdominis); and (III) an anatomical region classification branch (three fully connected layers processing the encoder’s 512-dimensional latent features). The anatomical region classification branch provides upper abdominal or lateral abdominal region information to the calculation module, which uses segmentation outputs to compute the interrectus distance, rectus abdominis muscle thickness, and subcutaneous fat thickness.

During training, all input images were bilinearly resized to 256×256 pixels. Frame-synchronous data augmentation was applied; specifically, with a one-third probability, one or more transforms (rotation, translation, scaling, cropping, affine warp, brightness, contrast jitter, additive noise, or blur) were randomly sampled and executed at a maximum 20% intensity. Optimization was performed using the Adam optimizer, with an initial learning rate of 1×10−3 and cosine annealing scheduling. The multitask loss function combined (I) the classification branch (cross-entropy loss for anatomical region classification) and (II) the segmentation branch (weighted sum of pixel-wise cross-entropy loss and Dice loss for improved boundary detection). Training proceeded for 1,000 epochs, with the checkpoint that achieved the highest validation area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) and Dice coefficient retained as the final model.

Quantitative measurement

The classification branch outputs the predicted anatomical region (upper abdominal or lateral abdominal), while the segmentation branch generates pixel-wise masks for four anatomical structures: background, linea alba, subcutaneous fat, and rectus abdominis. Quantitative measurements were derived from these masks as follows: the IRD was determined by computing the minimal bounding box that enclosed the segmented linea alba region, with the width of this bounding box recorded as the IRD measurement. For the RAMT and SFT, thickness measurements were sampled at 10-pixel intervals along the longitudinal axis of the segmented regions of the rectus abdominis and subcutaneous fat, respectively. The median value of these sampled measurements was then adopted as the representative thickness for RAMT and SFT.

Statistical analysis

All experiments and statistical analyses were performed with Python v. 3.12.4 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA) and R v. 4.4.1 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The classification accuracy of the anatomical regions was assessed with receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and AUROCs. Segmentation accuracy was quantified via the Dice coefficient, which represents the overlap between the model’s segmentation output and the ground truth annotations. The agreement between the model’s measurements and those obtained by ultrasound physicians was evaluated according to the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). For longitudinal analysis of therapeutic effects on the IRD, RAMT, and SFT measurements across the Pre-tx, Post-tx-d1, and Post-tx-d1(6s) time points, paired Student t tests were employed. Statistical significance was defined as P<0.05. Uncertainty values were statistically analyzed within a 95% confidence interval (CI).

Results

Quantitative analysis of automated abdominal tissue measurements

Our model demonstrated good tissue segmentation and anatomical region classification performance on the independent test set, comprising 167 images acquired from the upper abdominal region and 383 images from the lateral abdominal regions. Quantitative analysis demonstrated strong segmentation and measurement performance of the proposed model. Dice coefficients (mean ± standard deviation) reached 0.979±0.013 for subcutaneous fat, 0.828±0.074 for the linea alba, and 0.951±0.066 for the rectus abdominis (Table 1). Measurement consistency with ultrasound physicians was high, with ICC values of 0.988 (95% CI: 0.986–0.989) for fat thickness, 0.955 (95% CI: 0.939–0.966) for linea alba width, and 0.917 (95% CI: 0.899–0.932) for rectus abdominis thickness; the corresponding mean absolute errors were 0.223, 1.736, and 0.309 mm, respectively (Figure 4). Although linea alba segmentation exhibited relatively lower Dice scores, all ICC values exceeded 0.900, indicating robust and clinically reliable measurement agreement across tissue types.

Therapeutic changes in abdominal anatomy following magnetic stimulation

Images acquired from the upper abdominal and lateral abdominal regions at Pre-tx, Post-tx-d1, and Post-tx-d1(6s) (≈18 days) were analyzed with our validated model to quantify (I) the IRD (upper abdominal region); (II) SFT (lateral abdominal region); and (III) RAMT (lateral abdominal region).

A reduction in IRD was observed from 20.17±7.81 mm (Pre-tx) to 17.55±7.47 mm (Post-tx-d1; nonsignificant change) and further to 15.22±6.58 mm at Post-tx-d1(6s) (P=0.04; Figure 5A). SET decreased nonsignificantly (P=0.05) from 16.56±3.56 to 14.30±3.55 mm after six sessions (Figure 5B).

In addition, bilateral increases in RAMT were observed. Baseline thickness measurements were comparable between sides (left: 8.27±1.04 mm; right: 8.22±1.11 mm). After the first treatment session, thickness increased on both sides (left: 9.10±1.22 mm, P=0.02; right: 8.96±1.22 mm, P=0.05). Further increases were observed after six sessions (left: 10.26±1.23 mm, P<0.001; right: 10.19±1.45 mm, P<0.001), corresponding to an approximate 24% increase from baseline (Figure 5C,5D).

Discussion

Patients with DRA often present with abdominal discomfort, pain, distension and concomitant pelvic-floor dysfunction. When severe, the widened linea alba can predispose individuals to ventral herniation, markedly impairing daily activities and quality of life (1). Accurate quantification of IRD and RAMT is essential for diagnosis and monitoring, yet conventional manual ultrasound is operator-dependent and time-consuming. Previous studies have evaluated the utility of automation in this context; for instance, one study used a substantial dataset of 675 upper abdominal ultrasound images to segment the rectus abdominis muscles for subsequent calculation of linea alba width (13). However, these approaches focused exclusively on a single segmentation task. To address the broader limitations of current methods, we developed a multitask deep learning framework that integrates anatomical region classification with multitissue segmentation. In practical use, ultrasound images with poor quality, artifacts, or incomplete anatomical structures should be excluded or reacquired to ensure reliable model predictions. The model is intended to be used by trained clinicians or sonographers, and basic expertise in ultrasound acquisition is required to ensure appropriate input data.

In the independent test set, our multitask deep learning model achieved high Dice coefficients for subcutaneous fat (0.979±0.013) and the rectus abdominis (0.951±0.066) (Table 1), translating to excellent measurement agreement with expert sonographers (ICC >0.900 for SFT and RAMT) (Figure 4C). This high consistency for fat and muscle tissues underscores the model’s reliability for automated quantification in clinical practice. Although the linea alba segmentation exhibited a relatively lower Dice coefficient (0.828±0.074) (Table 1), this is likely attributable not only to its inherently less distinct boundaries compared to those of more homogeneous fat and muscle tissues but also to its thin, elongated morphology and relatively small target size. These characteristics make the segmentation task more challenging and can lead to lower overlap-based metrics. However, this did not compromise the clinical utility of IRD measurement. The ICC for IRD remained high (0.955), and the mean absolute error was 1.736 mm. This robustness in measurement, despite segmentation challenges at the linea alba boundary, highlights the effectiveness of our bounding box width approach for deriving IRD from the segmented region. Therefore, the model provides a clinically viable, automated solution that mitigates the issues of operator dependency and time cost in conventional manual ultrasound assessment.

Leveraging this validated model, we quantified the longitudinal changes in abdominal anatomy within a cohort of patients who underwent magnetic stimulation therapy for DRA. The analysis revealed significant therapeutic effects, most notably a progressive reduction in IRD (Figure 5A) and notable bilateral increases in RAMT (~24% from baseline to after 6 sessions) (Figure 5C,5D), suggesting a positive impact on muscle structure and function. However, the short-term increases in muscle thickness should be interpreted with caution. Previous studies have reported that high-intensity electromagnetic stimulation can induce rapid increases in abdominal muscle thickness, consistent with our findings (14). Yet, experimental evidence also suggests that magnetic stimulation may lead to acute muscle swelling immediately after treatment, potentially driven by increased muscle tone, hyperemia, and transient fluid shifts within the tissue (15). These physiological responses can temporarily elevate measured muscle thickness and thus not necessarily reflect true structural hypertrophy. Therefore, while the observed increase in RAMT in this study aligns with the literature, it may partially reflect acute effects rather than sustained muscle growth. Future studies with longer follow-up periods are needed to differentiate transient swelling from long-term hypertrophic adaptations.

Although SET did not reduce significantly following the 16-day treatment period (P=0.05; Figure 5B), we were unable to establish a causal relationship between the observed changes in fat thickness and magnetic stimulation therapy. Potential confounding factors, such as concomitant lifestyle modifications (e.g., diet and physical activity), were not systematically controlled for.

This study involved several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, training and validation were confined to a single center. Consequently, external multicenter datasets are required to confirm the model’s applicability across different ultrasound vendors, transducer frequencies, and population types. Second, the implementation of the proposed method depends on static images, and integration into real-time ultrasound acquisition is under development and currently in the testing phase. Third, although SET decreased after magnetic stimulation (P=0.05), the absence of a control group and lack of dietary or physical activity monitoring preclude attribution of this change solely to the intervention. Finally, the therapeutic cohort comprised 21 participants, and thus larger trials are needed to validate the observed changes in muscle hypertrophy and fat thickness.

Conclusions

We developed a multitask deep-learning model that automatically segments and quantifies the subcutaneous fat, rectus abdominis, and linea alba simultaneously from abdominal ultrasound images, with high accuracy and strong agreement with expert measurements. Longitudinal analysis indicated reductions in IRD and increases in RAMT over the treatment period. These findings highlight the potential utility of the proposed framework for automated, reproducible assessment and longitudinal monitoring in patients with DRA.

Knowledge, attitudes and practices towards gestational diabetes mellitus among obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers: a cross-sectional study of 68 hospitals in Hubei Province, China
Authors: Lina Wang, Yuerong Wang, Huajun Chen, Lijuan Liu, Xiaofang Chen, Quan Gan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesTo assess the knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) related to the management of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) among obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers in Hubei Province, China, and to identify factors associated with these domains.DesignCross-sectional study.SettingOnline questionnaire administered in December 2023.ParticipantsA total of 394 healthcare workers from 68 maternal and child health institutions across Hubei Province.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe questionnaire comprised 29 items covering four domains: demographic characteristics, knowledge, attitudes and practices.InterventionsNone.ResultsThe overall awareness rate of GDM knowledge was 58.88%. The mean knowledge score was 5.60±2.23 (range: 0–12). The mean attitude score was 18.66±1.97 (range: 0–20), with 55.08% of participants reporting a positive attitude. The mean practice score was 19.26±2.40 (range: 0–21), with 58.63% reporting good practices. Knowledge scores were positively correlated with practice scores (rs=0.130, p=0.009), and a stronger correlation was observed between attitudes and practices (rs=0.393, p<0.001). Logistic regression showed that higher knowledge scores (OR=1.13; 95% CI 1.03 to 1.24; p=0.009) and higher attitude scores (OR=1.46; 95% CI 1.29 to 1.65; p<0.001) were significantly associated with good practices. Multivariable regression further demonstrated that positive attitudes were independently associated with good practices (adjusted OR=1.44; 95% CI 1.27 to 1.64; p<0.001).ConclusionObstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers in Hubei Province exhibited limited knowledge but generally positive attitudes and good practices regarding GDM management. Targeted educational strategies are warranted to address knowledge gaps, particularly concerning the role of early pregnancy serum ferritin in GDM risk, and to strengthen support for younger and less experienced healthcare workers.Keywords: Diabetes in pregnancy, Knowledge, Attitude, Surveys and Questionnaires

Introduction

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is defined as hyperglycaemia first identified during pregnancy.1 Globally, the prevalence of GDM continues to rise, largely attributable to epidemiological factors such as increasing obesity among women of reproductive age and advanced maternal age.2 According to the International Diabetes Federation, the global prevalence of GDM was approximately 14.0% in 2021.3 Rates vary substantially across regions, including Europe (10.9%),4 Canada and the USA (13.7%),5 and China (14.8%).6 GDM is associated with a higher risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, such as spontaneous abortion, congenital malformations, pre-eclampsia, stillbirth, macrosomia, neonatal hypoglycaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia and respiratory distress syndrome.1 7 Beyond perinatal complications, it also confers long-term health consequences for both mother and child.8 Women with a history of GDM are at increased risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, and their offspring face elevated risks of obesity, impaired glucose metabolism and cardiovascular disorders.9 10

Given the large number of pregnant women affected by GDM in China,6 effective management and intervention are of critical importance. Comprehensive GDM management includes early prevention, timely diagnosis and treatment, and postpartum follow-up. Early preventive measures involve screening high-risk populations, providing dietary and lifestyle guidance, and monitoring gestational weight gain. Robust evidence indicates that such strategies reduce both perinatal and long-term health risks.6 Lifestyle modifications, including dietary adjustment and physical activity, represent the first-line treatment for GDM and significantly lower the risk of disease progression among high-risk women.6 11 However, 15–30% of women with GDM do not achieve adequate glycaemic control with lifestyle measures alone, necessitating pharmacological treatment.12 13

Emerging evidence suggests that maternal iron overload may contribute to GDM pathogenesis through pro-oxidant mechanisms. Excess iron catalyses the generation of reactive oxygen species, leading to oxidative damage of pancreatic β-cells and impairing insulin signalling in the liver and skeletal muscle.14 Serum ferritin (SF), a biomarker of body iron stores, is the most frequently studied indicator in relation to GDM risk.14 Prospective studies have shown that elevated SF concentrations in early pregnancy are associated with a higher risk of GDM.15 16 Moreover, evidence indicates that routine iron supplementation for iron-replete pregnant women may be inadvisable because of the potential increased risk of GDM.17 Therefore, assessing iron status in early pregnancy is crucial for guiding individualised supplementation strategies that prevent maternal iron deficiency while minimising GDM risk. However, current GDM guidelines do not explicitly address the relationship between high iron stores and GDM risk.

Obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers play a pivotal role in the prevention and management of GDM. According to Kelman’s knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) model, knowledge provides the foundation for behavioural change, while attitudes act as the driving force. Therefore, assessing the KAP of obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers is crucial for optimising GDM management. Although prior surveys have assessed the KAP of general practitioners, nurses and midwives in primary healthcare settings with respect to GDM screening and management,18 key risk factors remain insufficiently explored. In particular, previous KAP surveys among obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers have not addressed issues related to GDM and iron overload. This study therefore aimed to comprehensively evaluate their KAP regarding GDM, including aspects related to iron metabolism, in order to inform the development of more precise and effective management strategies.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study surveyed obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers (physicians, nurses and midwives) from 68 maternal and child health (MCH) institutions across Hubei Province, China. The institutions represented tertiary, secondary and primary care levels. Data collection was conducted in December 2023. Inclusion criteria were (1) current employment as a physician, nurse or midwife within an MCH institution in Hubei Province; (2) possession of a professional qualification certificate and (3) voluntary participation. Exclusion criteria were absence from work during the survey period (eg, maternity or sick leave) or employment in a non-clinical role. The survey was anonymous, and no personally identifiable information was collected. Assuming a 50% awareness rate (p=0.5) for the knowledge section, the minimum sample size was calculated as n=Z²×p(1−p)/d², with Z=1.96 and d=0.05, yielding 384 participants.

Questionnaire design

The questionnaire was developed with reference to validated instruments,19 20 the 2022 Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Gestational Hyperglycemia,21 22 and the Queensland Clinical Guidelines (2021).23 The draft was reviewed and revised by six specialists in GDM management. The final instrument comprised 23 items, demonstrating strong content validity: the scale-level content validity index (CVI) was 0.97, and item-level CVI values ranged from 0.83 to 1.00. The questionnaire was pre-tested among 41 obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers, and item wording was refined based on feedback to generate the final version. Pre-test analysis showed good-to-excellent internal consistency (Cronbach’s α=0.72 for attitude; Cronbach’s α=0.80 for practice). Subsequent analysis of the full dataset confirmed reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.72 for attitude; Cronbach’s α=0.84 for practice).

The final questionnaire contained 29 items across four dimensions: (1) demographic characteristics, (2) knowledge, (3) attitudes and (4) practices. Any blank in the options or the fact that all options were chosen for each question in the knowledge section is considered an invalid answer. It included demographic items alongside questions addressing GDM screening, risk factors, preventive measures, treatment approaches and postpartum follow-up (see online supplemental materials). Demographic section recorded: age, education, years of work experience, occupation, professional title and department. The knowledge section included 12 multiple-choice items (K1–K12). A correct response required selecting all accurate options; each correct item scored 1 point, while incorrect or partial responses scored 0. Total scores ranged from 0 to 12. A total score of ≥6 (50% cut-off) on the knowledge section was defined as awareness, a threshold established in line with several KAP studies.24 25 The attitude section contained four items (A1–A4) rated on a 5-point Likert scale (1=‘Strongly Disagree/Not Necessary at All’ to 5=‘Strongly Agree/Very Necessary’). Responses were dichotomised as positive (‘Agree/Strongly Agree’) or negative (‘Neutral’, ‘Disagree’, ‘Strongly Disagree’). Total scores ranged from 4 to 20, with a score ≥20 (median) defined as a positive attitude. The practice section included seven items (P1–P7) rated on a 3-point frequency scale (1=‘Never’, 2=‘Occasionally’, 3=‘Frequently’). Total scores ranged from 7 to 21, with a score ≥20 (median) defined as good practice.

Data collection

The Obstetrics and Gynecology Branch of the Hubei Province Maternal and Child Health Alliance maintains WeChat groups (a widely used social media platform in China) with members from 105 MCH institutions across the province. Convenience sampling was used to recruit research sites by distributing invitations through these groups. Healthcare workers from 68 hospitals agreed to participate, and these hospitals were enrolled as study sites. The survey was hosted on the Survey-Star platform (https://www.wjx.cn/) and accessed through a Quick Response (QR) code distributed to healthcare workers in the 68 hospitals via WeChat. A total of 450 questionnaires were distributed. The platform blocked responses from Internet Protocol (IP) addresses outside the target cities, restricted participation to one submission per IP address and device, and automatically removed duplicates. A total of 399 questionnaires were returned, of which 5 were excluded because all options were selected in the knowledge section. Thus, 394 valid questionnaires were included in the analysis, yielding a response rate of 88.67% (399/450).

Statistical analysis

Normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables are presented as mean±SD, and categorical variables as frequencies (percentages). Between-group comparisons were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Associations among knowledge, attitude and practice scores were assessed with Spearman’s rank correlation. Factors associated with good practice were examined using univariate and multivariable logistic regression, with results expressed as adjusted odds ratios (aORs) and 95% CIs. Statistical significance was set at two-sided p<0.05. All analyses were performed using SAS V.9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.).

Patient and public involvement

No patient is involved.

Results

Demographic characteristics of study participants

A total of 394 valid questionnaires were analysed. Participant demographics are summarised in table 1. Most respondents worked in obstetrics departments (331/394, 84.01%). Physicians constituted the largest group (214/394, 54.31%), followed by nurses (119/394, 30.20%) and midwives (61/394, 15.48%). Over half held intermediate professional titles (211/394, 53.55%), and 79.19% (321/394) had undergraduate degrees. Regarding work experience, 28.68% (113/394) reported 11–15 years of service. The 31–40 years age group represented the largest cohort (176/394, 44.67%), followed by those aged 41–50 years (111/394, 28.17%). Participants aged >50 years accounted for 12.69% (50/394).

Knowledge dimension

The mean knowledge score was 5.60±2.23, and the overall awareness rate was 58.88% (table 3). Among the 12 knowledge items, correct response rates ranged from 4.56% to 79.24% (figure 1). Item K7 had the highest accuracy (79.24%), while five items (K4, K5, K6, K9, K10) exceeded 50%. However, in contrast, K11 had an accuracy of 13.42% and K3 had the lowest at 4.56%.

Physicians had significantly higher knowledge scores (6.13±2.08) than nurses and midwives (p<0.001). Healthcare workers with vice-senior and above titles (6.09±2.05, p=0.002), those with more than 30 years of work experience (6.29±2.18, p=0.030) and those aged between 41 and 50 years (6.12±1.83, p=0.015) also demonstrated significantly higher knowledge scores compared with other groups, with statistical significance observed for these comparisons respectively.

Attitude dimension

The mean attitude score was 18.66±1.97 (range: 4–20), with 55.08% demonstrating positive attitudes (table 3). Response distributions for the four items are shown in figure 2. Most healthcare workers expressed agreement regarding GDM management: 97.21% endorsed appropriate diet and exercise for glycaemic control; 96.45% viewed postpartum management as essential for long-term outcomes; 86.55% supported assessing iron metabolism markers before supplementation in high-risk women; and 86.04% favoured including iron metabolism in GDM guidelines. No significant differences in attitude scores were observed across demographic groups (table 2).

Practice dimension

The mean practice score was 19.26±2.40 (range: 7–21). Significant differences were observed across demographic groups: participants aged 41–50 years scored highest (20.05±1.39; p=0.001); those with postgraduate degrees scored higher (20.00±1.69; p=0.033); and professionals with vice-senior or higher titles also scored higher (19.75±1.46; p=0.004) (table 2). Good practice was demonstrated by 58.63% of participants (n=231; table 3). More than 90% frequently educated high-risk pregnant women on lifestyle modification during early pregnancy and encouraged breastfeeding among GDM mothers. In addition, 73.10% recommended postpartum oral glucose tolerance testing (OGTT) 4–12 weeks after delivery. Over 60% recommended iron metabolism testing for GDM patients, monitored ferritin levels in high-risk women and adhered to supplementation guidelines (figure 3).

Logistic regression analysis of factors associated with good practice

Table 5 presents the univariate and multivariable regression analyses. Higher knowledge scores (OR=1.13; 95% CI 1.03 to 1.24; p=0.009) and higher attitude scores (OR=1.46; 95% CI 1.29 to 1.65; p<0.001) were significantly associated with good practice. Compared with the 21–30 age group, participants aged 31–40 years (OR=1.90; 95% CI 1.04 to 3.47; p=0.038) and 41–50 years (OR=2.99; 95% CI 1.54 to 5.78; p=0.001) had better practice. Similarly, those with intermediate professional titles showed higher odds of good practice compared with junior titles (OR=2.15; 95% CI 1.31 to 3.53; p=0.002). In the multivariable model, higher attitude scores remained independently associated with good practice (aOR=1.44; 95% CI 1.27 to 1.64; p<0.001).

Discussion

This study revealed limited knowledge of GDM diagnosis and management among obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers in Hubei Province, China, despite positive attitudes and relatively good practices. Specific knowledge deficiencies were identified. Participants aged 30–50 years and those with intermediate professional titles demonstrated significantly better practices than younger or junior-title groups. These findings provide insights to inform the design and implementation of targeted education and training programmes to strengthen GDM awareness among this workforce.

Overall knowledge levels were low. Most participants were familiar with the adverse outcomes of poor glycaemic control, glycaemic targets and lifestyle advice. However, major gaps persisted, particularly concerning GDM risk factors, maternal and neonatal complications, and recommended gestational weight gain. Correct response rates varied widely, likely reflecting differences in item difficulty, roles of physicians versus nurses, and limitations of existing training programmes in Hubei Province. Notably, only 4.56% of respondents correctly identified elevated ferritin as an early-pregnancy risk indicator for GDM (K3), possibly due to the omission of iron overload–GDM links in the 2022 Chinese Guidelines.21 22 Moreover, continuing medical education, even in the USA, rarely emphasises this interaction.26 This suggests that lack of awareness regarding iron overload and GDM risk may be widespread.

Most prior KAP studies on GDM have focused on patients or high-risk pregnant women,19 20 27 with few assessing healthcare workers, limiting direct comparisons. Nonetheless, our results align with international findings. For example, Riaz et al28 surveyed 210 physicians in Pakistani cities in 2018, reporting inconsistent diagnostic and screening practices. Utz et al29 examined 259 gynaecologists and endocrinologists across 26 low- and middle-income countries in 2016, finding substantial variability in GDM management. Bimson et al30 similarly observed significant variation in diagnostic and management practices among Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine members in the USA. Collectively, these findings highlight the need for healthcare workers to actively engage with updated consensus guidelines to strengthen knowledge and standardise GDM care.

Attitudes were largely positive: nearly all respondents acknowledged the importance of diet, exercise and postpartum management. Practices were also generally good, including routine GDM screening, health education for high-risk women, individualised lifestyle advice for patients, promotion of breastfeeding and recommendation of postpartum OGTT at 4–12 weeks. Over 60% reported advising iron metabolism testing, monitoring ferritin in high-risk women and restricting supplementation to indicated cases. These results differ from Utz et al,18 who in 2016 reported an 88.50% referral rate in Morocco, likely reflecting differences in professional composition, as their survey included general practitioners, nurses and midwives, whereas our study focused on obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers.

Recent evidence indicates that elevated first-trimester serum ferritin concentrations increase GDM risk.15,1731 Proposed mechanisms include iron overload exacerbating insulin resistance in peripheral tissues and the liver, and directly damaging pancreatic β-cells via oxidative stress and inflammatory responses.32 Thus, excessive iron supplementation may raise GDM risk—a relationship not addressed in current guidelines. In our survey, only 4.56% correctly identified this link. In contrast, attitudes and practices were more favourable: most supported checking iron markers before supplementation, reported monitoring ferritin, and prescribing supplementation only when indicated. These practices likely reflect general principles of anaemia management rather than specific strategies to reduce GDM risk. Nonetheless, most participants expressed a desire for guidelines to incorporate iron metabolism to improve patient care.

The coexistence of limited knowledge with positive attitudes and practices may be explained by several factors. Some actions may be driven by experience or habit rather than knowledge. Social desirability bias may also have influenced responses, with participants reporting behaviours consistent with perceived norms rather than actual practice.33 34 Such bias could lead to overestimation of competence. These findings highlight the complexity of KAP relationships in clinical settings and underscore the need for further research. Addressing these discrepancies is essential for developing effective training curricula that target specific knowledge gaps.

Physicians demonstrated significantly higher knowledge than nurses and midwives, consistent with previous KAP studies in other fields, such as neonatal pain management.35 Knowledge was also higher among participants aged 41–50 years, those with senior titles and those with >30 years of practice. These results suggest that younger professionals, junior-title staff, nurses and midwives may require more targeted training. Similarly, practice scores were higher among participants aged 41–50 years, those with postgraduate degrees and those with senior titles, consistent with findings from a 2024 KAP study on rehabilitation of nerve injury.36

Knowledge was positively correlated with practice, and attitudes with practice, consistent with other KAP studies.37 38 Participants primarily learnt about GDM through guidelines, departmental sessions, lectures and training. These findings suggest that interventions improving knowledge and attitudes may also enhance practice. Further research should explore more effective approaches to strengthen healthcare workers’ KAP on GDM.

Limitations

This study has limitations. First, although multicentre, it was limited to hospitals in Hubei Province; thus, the findings may not be generalisable to other regions. Second, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and does not capture temporal changes. Third, although based on guidelines and literature, the questionnaire may not comprehensively assess GDM knowledge. Finally, social desirability bias may have influenced responses, leading to possible overestimation of practices.

Conclusion

In summary, this study outlines the current KAP regarding GDM management among obstetric and gynaecological healthcare workers in Hubei Province, China. The findings offer evidence to guide targeted education and training programmes, with the goal of improving the quality of GDM clinical care.

Integrated transcriptome and single-cell RNA sequencing analysis revealed the prognostic significance of GBP4 in pancreatic adenocarcinoma
Authors: Qiyu Chi, Feihong Liang, Yaxin Zhang, Changgan Chen, Xuling Chen, Yu Pan, Shangeng Weng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•GBP4 promotes proliferation and invasion in pancreatic cancer cells•Five GBP4-related genes predict prognosis in pancreatic adenocarcinoma•Cell cycle pathways are enriched in high-risk pancreatic cancer patients•Immune infiltration differs significantly between high- and low-risk groups•Ductal cells show dynamic expression of GBP4-related prognostic genesKeywords: Pancreatic adenocarcinoma, GBP4, Prognostic genes, Ductal cells

Introduction

Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) represents a highly malignant neoplasm originating from the epithelial cells of the pancreatic ducta. It has an insidious onset and most patients are diagnosed in the middle or late stages of the disease. Its tumor cells have strong abilities to proliferate, invade and metastasise, seriously threatening the life and health of patients, with an extremely low 5-year survival rate [1]. Currently, comprehensive treatment methods such as chemotherapy, radiotherap, and surgery have limited effects, a high recurrence rate, and significant side effects, resulting in a poor prognosis for patients [2,3]. Against this background, the search for new prognostic genes is of great significance for the diagnosis and treatment of PAAD.

The GBP4 gene, as a member of the guanylate - binding protein family, plays a key role in cellular immune defence [4]. In cancer research, members of the GBP family exhibit context - dependent effects and have different functions in different cancers [5]. For PAAD, although the GBP4 gene has been found to be associated with certain clinical characteristics of patients, its expression pattern and functions remain unknown, and its diagnostic value also awaits further in-depth study [6]. Considering the significant involvement of GBP4 in oncogenic processes, further investigation of its biological functions and molecular mechanisms in PAAD will offer novel perspectives for advancing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in PAAD management.

In the journey of exploring PAAD-related genes and pathogenesis, the choice of research methods is extremely important, and compared with traditional transcriptome analysis, single-cell analysis has unique and significant advantages [7,8]. Traditional transcriptome analysis measures the average gene expression levels of a large number of cells, which can obscure the information about heterogeneity between cells [9]. In contrast, single-cell RNA sequencing technology enables in-depth research at the single-cell level. It can accurately detect tumor heterogeneity, comprehensively and meticulously examine the gene expression patterns in tissues, and enable researchers to identify new biomarkers that were difficult to discover in the past [[10], [11], [12], [13]]. This will further clarify the complex heterogeneity of tumors and provide more precise targets and directions for tumor diagnosis and treatment. For PAAD, this advantage can help identify key cell subsets in disease progression, help elucidate the mechanism of action of the GBP4 gene in different cells, and help develop personalized treatment plans for PAAD, thereby improving the survival rate and quality of life for patients. In addition, single-cell analysis can also provide information about rare cell populations. These cells may play a crucial role in the disease development process but are easily overlooked in traditional analyses [14].

Based on transcriptomics and single-cell RNA sequencing data obtained from public databases, this study investigated the prognostic characteristics and mechanism of action of the GBP4 gene in PAAD patients. The study identified candidate genes related to the GBP4 gene in PAAD by differential expression analysis. Then, prognostic genes were acquired through univariate Cox regression and LASSO regression analyses. After that,we constructed and validated a prognostic model. Meanwhile, pathway enrichment and immune microenvironment analyses were performed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of PAAD. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis was also performed to obtain key cells and explore the dynamic changes in the expression of prognostic genes. To validate these computational findings, we conducted in vitro experiments using pancreatic cancer cell lines and in vivo experiments using mouse models to assess the functional role of GBP4 in pancreatic cancer progression. Through the joint analysis of transcriptome and single-cell RNA sequencing data, combined with multiple bioinformatics methods and validated by functional experiments, this study aims to provide new insights for the prognostic prediction and targeted treatment of PAAD and help improve the diagnosis and treatment status of PAAD patients.

Materials and methods

Data source

From the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds), transcriptome and scRNA-seq datasets pertaining to PAAD were obtained (October 29, 2024). The GSE183795 (GPL6244 platform) included 139 PAAD tumour tissue samples (PAAD group) and 102 paraneoplastic tissue samples (control group) [15]. The GSE57495 (GPL15048 platform) included 63 PAAD tumour tissue samples with survival information [15]. TCGA-PAAD dataset was gotten from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) (October 29, 2024), which included 181 PAAD tumour tissue samples with survival information [16]. The scRNA-seq dataset, GSE155698 (GPL24676 platform) contained 17 PAAD tumour tissue samples (PAAD group) and 3 paraneoplastic tissue samples (control group) [16].

Differential expression analysis

The limma package (v 3.54.1) [17] was used to perform differential expression analysis on PAAD and control groups in GSE183795 dataset (|log2Fold Change (FC)| > 0.5, p < 0.05). Subsequently, ggplot2 package (v 3.4.1) [18] was applied to draw a volcano plot. The 10 DEGs1 (5 up- and 5 down-regulated genes in PAAD group) with the highest |log2FC| were labeled in the volcano plot. In the GSE183795 dataset, the difference in GBP4 gene expression across groups was compared by Wilcoxon test (p < 0.05).

Based on the median value of GBP4 gene expression, PAAD samples with survival information in the GSE57495 dataset were divided into high-expression group and low-expression group. The survival package (v 3.5.3) [19] was used to perform a Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis to determine the differences in overall survival (OS) between the 2 expression groups in PAAD (p < 0.05).

Afterwards, limma package (v 3.54.1) was utilized to find DEGs2 between GBP4 gene high and low expression groups (|log2FC| > 0.5, p < 0.05). Similarly, ggplot2 package (v 3.4.1) was applied to draw a volcano plot and the 10 DEGs2 (5 up- and 5 down-regulated genes) were labeled.

Functional pathways and protein-protein interaction (PPI) analyses of candidate genes

Initially, the ggvenn package (v 0.1.9) [20] was applied to identify candidate genes from the intersection of DEGs1 and DEGs2. Subsequently, to explore the biological roles of candidate genes, Gene Ontology (GO), which involves cellular components (CC), biological processes (BP), and molecular functions (MF), and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), enrichment analyses were carried out using the clusterProfiler package (v 4.2.2) [21] (p.adjust < 0.05, the Benjamini-Hochberg method was used for correction). Pathways were ranked by count numbers, and the top 5 CC,BP, MF pathways, and top 10 KEGG pathways were visualized. With the aim of exploring the interactions of the candidate genes at the protein level, the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins database (https://cn.string-db.org/) was employed to establish a PPI network (confidence interaction > 0.4). Cytoscape software (v 3.7.2) [22] was employed to visualize the proteins interactions.

Identification of prognostic genes for PAAD

To assess the potential value of candidate genes for predicting OS of PAAD patients, within PAAD samples with survival information in the GSE57495 dataset, survival package (v 3.5.3) was applied to conduct univariate Cox analysis (hazard ratio (HR) ≠ 1, p < 0.01) on candidate genes. The results of the analysis were then visualised using forestplot package (v 3.1.1) [19]. In the forestplot, HR < 1 indicated a protective factor and HR > 1 indicated a risk factor. Genes that passed univariate Cox were further subjected to proportional hypothesis (PH) assumption test (p > 0.05) by cox.zph function. The genes that passed the PH assumption test were defined as candidate prognostic genes. Subsequently, utilizing glmnet package (v 4.1.4) [23], the candidate prognostic genes were analyzed by LASSO regression analysis. The lambda value with the minimum average error obtained from the 10-fold cross-validation was fitted into the LASSO analysis to obtain prognostic genes.

Localization analyses of prognostic genes

The location of prognostic genes on chromosomes was analyzed by the OmicCircos package (v 1.36.0) [24]. The mRNALocater database (http://bio-bigdata.cn/mRNALocater) was used to get the projected scores for the subcellular localization of prognostic genes. The highest score was then used to determine the final, accurate localization.

Risk model construction and validation

Within PAAD samples with survival information in the GSE57495 dataset, the following formula was used to determine each PAAD patient's risk score (the expr and Coef represented expression and LASSO coefficient of each prognostic gene, respectively):

Based on the optimal cut-off value of risk score, the PAAD samples with survival information were stratified into high-risk group (HRG) and low-risk group (LRG). The ggplot2 package (v 3.4.1) was employed to visualize the risk score distribution and survival state distribution of these 2 groups. The KM survival curves of 2 groups were generated via survminer package (v 0.4.9) [25], and the OS difference was subjected to log-rank test (p < 0.05). Moreover, survival receiver operating characteristic (ROC) package (v 1.0.3.1) [26] was employed to plot ROC curves (area under curve (AUC) > 0.6). Additionally, in an attempt to further evaluate the accuracy and generalizability of the risk model, we made use of the TCGA - PAAD dataset to conduct the validation.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

The GSEA was conducted to define the signalling pathways in HRG and LRG. Initially, the "c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1.symbols.gmt" from MSigDB (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/downloads.sp) was utilized as the reference gene set. Within PAAD samples with survival information in the GSE57495 dataset, differentially expressed genes across HRG and LRG were analyzed by the limma package (v 3.54.1). The log2FC was calculated and sorted in ascending order. Subsequently, GSEA was performed utilizing the clusterProfiler package (v 4.2.2) to identify significantly enriched pathways (p < 0.05, |Normalized Enrichment Score (NES)| > 1). The top 5 pathways were shown according to the p-value ranked from low to high.

The GSVA was conducted to explore biological functional differences between risk groups. The GSVA score for samples in the training cohort was calculated using the GSVA package (v 1.46.0) [27]. Following this, differential analysis of the GSVA score between HRG and LRG was conducted with limma package (v 3.54.0) (p < 0.05, |t| > 0), demonstrating top 20 differential pathways.

Assessment of immune microenvironment

Studying immune infiltration in PAAD could uncover the role of immune cells in its onset and progression. In the GSE57495 dataset, infiltration abundance of 28 immune cells [28] in HRG and LRG was assessed by ssGSEA algorithm in GSVA package (v 1.46.0) (samples with p < 0.05 were removed). Differential immune cells across risk groups were then obtained by Wilcoxon test (p < 0.05). Moreover, the psych package (v 2.2.9) [29] was applied to analyze Spearman correlation of differential immune cells with prognostic genes (|correlation coefficient (cor)| > 0.3, p < 0.05), and corrplot package (v 0.92) [29] was used to draw a correlation heatmap.

Drug sensitivity analysis

In the GSE57495 dataset, based on 198 chemotherapy drugs provided by Cancer Genome Project (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/), pRRophetic package (v 0.5) [30] was applied to calculate 50% inhibitory concentration (IC50) of them for PAAD samples in HRG and LRG to evaluate the drugs sensitivity. Besides, the drug's IC50 differences were assessed by Wilcoxon test (p < 0.05). The 5 drugs with the most significant difference in IC50 across groups were shown in a box line diagram. Finally, the 3D conformations of the above 5 drugs were visualised using the Pubchem website (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/).

The pan-cancer analysis

Using data from tumor and normal samples from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) database and TCGA, the Sangerbox website (http://sangerbox.com/) was used to analyze the expression differences of the prognostic genes in pan-cancer and their adjacent normal tissues (p < 0.05).

Single-cell analysis

The Seurat package (v 5.0.1) [31] was utilized to conduct the single-cell analysis. In order to improve the reliability of subsequent scRNA-seq data analysis, quality control (QC) of GSE155698 dataset was performed. The QC was performed according to the following specific criteria: 1) deleting cells with gene numbers lower than 300 or higher than 8000; 2) deleting cells with the sum of gene expression numbers lower than 500 or higher than 50,000; and 3) deleting cells with the proportion of mitochondrial genes greater than 20%. After filtering out the cells that did not meet the criteria, the data was normalized. Then the top 2000 highly variable genes (HVGs) was extracted (the 10 HVGs with the largest variation were labeled). Furthermore, HVGs were subjected to PCA. The JackStrawPlot and JackStraw functions were used to choose the proper principal components (PCs).

Next, the filtered cells were then subjected to unsupervised clustering (resolution = 0.25). Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) was thereafter utilized for the demonstration of the results. Subsequently, marker genes for each cell cluster were recognised using the FindAllMarkers.Then,cell clusters were annotated with CellMarker database (http://yikedaxue.slwshop.cn/). The annotation result was visualized in the UMAP plot, and the expression of marker genes in different cell types was illustrated. The Wilcoxon test was used to assess the differential expression of prognostic genes in cells from the PAAD and control groups (p < 0.05), and the result was presented using the ggpubr package (v 0.6.0) [32]. Cells in which all prognostic genes were differentially expressed were defined as key cells.

Pseudo-time and cellular communication analyses

To investigate the heterogeneity of key cells, dimensionality reduction and subsequent clustering of key cellular populations were conducted employing the UMAP algorithm. Afterwards, Monocle package (v 2.26.0) [33] was employed to conduct cells pseudo-time analysis. The plot_pseudo-time_heatmap function was further utilized to explore the trend of prognostic genes expression with key cells differentiation. Finally, cell-type interactions were probed using CellChat package (v 1.6.1) [34], followed by visualisation of cell-cell ligand-receptor pair interactions.

Cell culture

Human pancreatic cancer cell lines (PaTu8988T, BxPC-3, CFPAC-1, SW1990, PANC-1, AsPC-1) were purchased from Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). All cells were cultured in the medium recommended by the manufacturer (Procell) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (HyClone) in a 37°C, 5% CO₂ incubator (Thermo Scientific). The medium was replaced every 48 hours. When cell confluence reached 80%–90%, cells were passaged using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Sigma) and subcultured at a 1:3 ratio into new flasks.

Western blot analysis

Pancreatic cancer cells (PaTu8988T, BxPC-3, CFPAC-1, SW1990, PANC-1, AsPC-1) in the logarithmic growth phase were collected, washed three times with ice-cold PBS, and lysed on ice for 30 minutes using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) containing protease inhibitors (Roche) and phosphatase inhibitors (Thermo Scientific). After centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4°C, the supernatant was collected, and protein concentration was quantified using a BCA kit (Pierce). Equal amounts of protein (30 μg) were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk (BD Biosciences) for 1 hour at room temperature, then incubated overnight at 4°C with anti-GBP4 primary antibody (1:1000, Proteintech) and GAPDH internal control antibody (1:5000, Proteintech). After three washes with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (1:5000, Cell Signaling Technology) for 1 hour at room temperature. Signals were developed using ECL substrate (Millipore) for 5 minutes and captured using a ChemiDoc imaging system (Bio-Rad). Protein expression levels were quantified using Image Lab software (Bio-Rad).

Plasmid construction

A small interfering RNA (siRNA) sequence targeting the GBP4 gene (GenBank accession number NM_052941), with the target sequence 5’-GCTCTTAAAGATCCTTGACAT-3’, was designed and synthesized by as designed and synthesized by APExBIO (Shanghai, China). The siRNA specificity was validated via BLAST alignment to exclude off-target effects. This sequence was cloned into the lentiviral vector GV493 (APExBIO, Shanghai, China). A nonsilencing control siRNA with the sequence 5’- TTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGT-3’ was similarly cloned into GV493 to serve as a negative control. For overexpression (OE), the coding sequence of GBP4 (GenBank accession number NM_052941), spanning nucleotides 108 to 2031,was cloned into the lentiviral vector GV146 (APExBIO, Shanghai, China). A control vector without the GBP4 insert was used as a negative control for overexpression experiments.

Lentivirus production and transduction

This experiment was performed following the guidelines of the Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital. BxPC-3 cells were maintained in DMEM (Hyclone) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Hyclone) at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2/95% air atmosphere. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁵ cells per well. Upon reaching 70% confluency, BxPC-3 cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors GV493 encoding the GBP4-targeting siRNA (siGBP4) or the nonsilencing control, and CFPAC-1 cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors GV146 encoding the GBP4 OE sequence or the empty control vector, both at a multiplicity of infection of 10 in the presence of 8 µg/mL polybrene (MCE). After 24 hours, the medium was replaced with fresh DMEM containing 10% FBS. Stable cell lines were selected with puromycin (2 µg/mL) for 7 days, resulting in BxPC-3(siGBP4) and BxPC-3(Con), CFPAC-1(Con), and CFPAC-1(GBP4-OE) cells [35].

Cell proliferation assay

BxPC-3 (Con), BxPC-3(siGBP4), CFPAC-1(Con), and CFPAC-1(GBP4-OE) cells were seeded into 96-well plates (Corning) at a density of 5 × 10³ cells per well. At 0, 24, 48 and 72 hours, 10 µL of cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (Servicebio) was added per well, followed by incubation at 37°C for 2 hours. Absorbance at 450 nm (OD value) was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy H1) as previously described [35].

Wound healing assay

BxPC-3 (Con), BxPC-3(siGBP4), CFPAC-1(Con), and CFPAC-1(GBP4-OE) cells were seeded into 6-well plates (Corning) at 1 × 10⁶ cells/well. When cell confluence reached 80%, a sterile 200 μL pipette tip was used to create a linear scratch. Detached cells were removed by gentle PBS washing, and the medium was replaced with serum-free medium. Images of the scratch area were captured at 0 and 24 hours using an inverted microscope (Olympus IX73). Scratch width was measured using ImageJ software. Migration index [36] = (Initial scratch width – 24-hour scratch width) / Initial scratch width.

Transwell invasion assay

Transwell chambers (8 μm pore size, Corning) were pre-coated with 50 μL diluted Matrigel (BD Biosciences, 1:8 dilution) and solidified at 37°C for 4 hours. BxPC-3 (Con), BxPC-3(siGBP4), CFPAC-1(Con), and CFPAC-1(GBP4-OE) cells (5 × 10⁴ cells/well) were resuspended in 200 μL serum-free medium and added to the upper chamber. The lower chamber contained medium with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 hours, cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 minutes. Pictures were took by the inverted microscope (Olympus IX73). Then, crystal violet was completely eluted, and the eluate was read at 570nm for OD value on a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy H1). Invasion index [36] = OD570 of invaded cells in experimental group / OD570 of invaded cells in control group.

Subcutaneous tumorigenesis assay

BALB/c nude female mice (GemPharmatech, China) were randomly divided into groups of 5 for further in vivo study. Cells in logarithmic growth phase were adjusted to 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL, and 0.1 mL cell suspension was injected subcutaneously into the right flank of each mouse. The humane endpoint was defined as either tumor diameter exceeding 15 mm or >20% body weight loss. For mice not reaching these endpoints, all remaining animals were euthanized on day 30 to ensure consistent assessment of therapeutic efficacy at this predetermined experimental timepoint. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and graphical representations were performed using SPSS 17.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0 software.The R language (v 4.1.0) was utilized to perform bioinformatic analyses. For experimental data, statistical significance was assessed using unpaired Student’s t-test, one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test, and Wilcoxon rank sum test, with significance defined as p < 0.05. Survival curves were evaluated using the KaplanMeier method, and differences between the survival curves were tested by the log-rank test. A Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to examine the univariate hazard ratios for the study variables.Western Blot and immunohistochemical data were analyzed using ImageJ, with three independent replicates.

Results

Related functional pathways and complex PPI network of candidate genes

In the GSE183795 dataset, 1,289 DEGs1 between PAAD and control groups were acquired, consisting of 901 up- and 388 down-regulated genes in PAAD group (|log2FC| > 0.5, p < 0.05) (Fig. 1a). Specifically, GBP4 had up-regulated expression in the PAAD group (Fig. 1b). Besides, in the GSE57495 dataset, a significant survival difference was observed in PAAD patients between the high and low GBP4 expression groups. (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1c). There were 649 DEGs2 between 2 expression groups, including 335 up- and 314 down-regulated genes in the high expression group (|log2FC| > 0.5, p < 0.05) (Fig. 1d). Afterwards, 141 candidate genes were identified from the intersection of DEGs1 and DEGs2 (Fig. 1e).

Notably, a total of 221 GO pathways showed substantial enrichment of the candidate genes including 183 BP pathways like "nuclear division", 22 CC pathways like "spindle", and 16 MF pathways like "microtubule binding" (p.adjust < 0.05) (Fig. 1f, Table S1). Moreover, candidate genes were significantly enriched in 11 KEGG pathways, such as "cell cycle", "cellular senescence" and "staphylococcus aureus infection" (p.adjust < 0.05) (Fig. 1g, Table S2). The candidate genes' enrichment in GO and KEGG pathways suggested their potential involvement in critical biological processes that contribute to the progression of PAAD. Specifically, these genes might regulate cell proliferation, a hallmark of cancer development. Additionally, their involvement in pathways like "cellular senescence" and "staphylococcus aureus infection" suggested that they could influence the tumor microenvironment and immune responses, which were crucial for the initiation and progression of PAAD.

The PPI network included 115 proteins with 867 distinct protein-protein interactions (26 isolated proteins were eliminated), emphasizing the sophisticated network of interactions exhibited by the protein products derived from the candidate genes. (Fig. 1h).

Identification of 5 prognostic genes

Within GSE57495 dataset, 10 candidate genes associated with OS of PAAD patients remained through univariate Cox analysis (HR ≠ 1, p < 0.01) (Fig. 2a). Specifically, these 10 candidate genes were linked to unfavorable prognosis (HR > 1), implying a possible role in promoting PAAD progression. Besides, PH assumption was confirmed to be met for these 10 candidate genes based on the statistical validation(p > 0.05), including GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, KRT23, BCAT1, KLK6, ACSL5, MET, IGF2BP3, and SPRR1A (Fig. S1). Consequently, they were designated as candidate prognostic genes for PAAD. Subsequent refinement using the LASSO algorithm identified 5 prognostic genes at the optimal lambda value (lambda = 0.0843), which included GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, BCAT1, and SPRR1A (Fig. 2b-c).

Chromosomal and subcellular localisation of prognostic genes

GBP2 and SPRR1A were located on chromosome 1, MMP7 on chromosome 11, NEDD4L and KRT6A on chromosome 12 (Fig. 2d). In addition, BCAT1 was localized to the nucleus; while GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, and SPRR1A were all localized to the cytoplasm (Fig. 2e).

Development of a prognostic risk model demonstrating robust predictive performance and clinical applicability

The formula for calculating the model, risk score = 0.3536 × GBP2 + 0.0823 × KRT6A - 0.2733 × MMP7 + 0.0842 × BCAT1 + 0.0718 × SPRR1A. Based on the optimal statistical value (cutpoint = 9.051726), the PAAD patients with survival data in GSE57495 dataset were split into HRG and LRG (33:30). Distribution of risk score and survival state within risk groups illustrated that mortality of PAAD patients increased with increasing risk score (Fig. 3a-b). Moreover, KM survival curves uncovered PAAD patients in LRG had markedly higher survival probability (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3c). The good efficacy of the risk model was then demonstrated by the AUC at 1, 3, and 5 years, which were 0.815, 0.849, and 0.895, respectively (Fig. 3d).

The risk model was validated within TCGA-PAAD dataset. The PAAD patients in this dataset were classified into HRG and LRG (136:45) based on optimal cut-off value (cutpoint = 3.848038). The stratification of risk scores and corresponding survival status distribution (Fig. 3e-f), KM survival curves (p < 0.00014) (Fig. 3g), and ROC curves (AUC all exceeded 0.6) (Fig. 3h) were found to be largely congruent with the corresponding results within the GSE57495 dataset. This observation suggested that the risk model exhibited effective and robust predictive capacity across various contexts.

Key functional pathways of HRG and LRG

Employing GSEA, the biological pathways associated with the HRG and LRG within the GSE57495 dataset were investigated. The HRG and LRG were significantly enriched in 33 KEGG pathways (Table S3). Notably, among the top 5 pathways, "cell cycle" was significantly enriched in the HRG, whereas these pathways, "neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction", and "glycine, serine and threonine metabolism" were enriched in the LRG (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4a). The enrichment of the "cell cycle" pathway in the HRG suggested that this pathway was essential for proliferation and development of PAAD. Conversely, the enrichment of pathways in the LRG indicated that these pathways might modulate the tumor microenvironment and metabolic processes.

The GSVA results showed that the up-regulated pathways in HRG were "O-glycan biosynthesis", "p53 signaling pathway", and "pathogenic Escherichia coli infection", among others (Fig. 4b). The p53 pathway was crucial for regulating cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and DNA repair, and its dysregulation could lead to uncontrolled cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, which are hallmarks of cancer. Conversely, pathways such as "fatty acid metabolism" were down-regulated in HRG (Fig. 4b), indicating this metabolic reprogramming could affect the tumor's ability to generate energy and synthesize essential biomolecules, potentially influencing its growth rate and response to therapeutic interventions.

Correlation of prognostic genes with immune cells

In the GSE57495 dataset, the relative proportions of 28 distinct immune infiltrating cell types were examined within both the HRG and LRG (Fig. 4c). The 5 immune cells displayed significant infiltration differences between the HRG and LRG (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4d). For example, central memory CD8 T cells exhibited markedly higher infiltration level in HRG, while activated B cells exhibited markedly higher infiltration level in LRG (p < 0.01). The prognostic gene BCAT1 showed no significant correlations with any of differential immune cells (Fig. 4e). Central memory CD8 T cells showed a substantial positive correlation with GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, and SPRR1A, but activated B cells showed a significant negative correlation with KRT6A. Understanding these immune dynamics was crucial for developing targeted immunotherapies that could modulate the tumor microenvironment and enhance anti-tumor immunity in PAAD.

Differential drugs sensitivity

Significant variations in IC50 values for 140 therapeutic drugs were observed across risk groups (p < 0.05) (Table S4). The top 5 drugs exhibiting the most pronounced differences in IC50 values were elephantin, I.BRD9, MK.2206, sorafenib, and uprosertib, which demonstrated elevated IC50 values in the HRG (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4f), implying that lower doses could be efficacious in LRG. The drug sensitivity might be influenced by the risk stratification of PAAD patients, with potential implications for personalized treatment strategies. Lastly, the 3D conformations of the above 5 drugs were shown in Fig. S2.

Prognostic gene expression in various cancer tissues

The analysis revealed significant differential expression patterns of prognostic genes across multiple cancer types when compared with their respective normal tissue counterparts (Fig. S3), for example, in cancers of the digestive system such as stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD). The expression levels of these genes could predict patient prognosis and guide personalized treatment strategies.

Ductal cells: key cells

Prior to QC of the scRNA-seq dataset GSE155698, 49,868 cells and 24,732 genes were included. After QC, 41,984 cells were retained, with the gene count remaining unchanged at 24,732 (Fig. S4a-b). The top 2000 HVGs and 50 PCs were selected for cells' clustering (Fig. S4c-e). The UMAP clustering classified the cell into 21 cell clusters (resolution = 0.25) (Fig. 5a), which were annotated by marker genes into 10 cells types, namely T cells, neutrophils, ductal cells, macrophages, acinar cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, stellate cells, B cells, and endothelial cells (Fig. 5b, Table S5). Moreover, all prognostic genes were differentially expressed in ductal cells from both PAAD and control groups (Fig. 5c). Therefore, ductal cells were recorded as the key cells.

Dynamic expression of prognostic genes in ductal cells

The ductal cells were divided into 22 cells subclusters (Fig. 5d). The ductal cells differentiated over time from top to bottom (Fig. 5e), and underwent 7 different states of differentiation (Fig. 5f). The expression of BCAT1 and KRT6A rose in the later stages of ductal cells differentiation. The expression of GBP2 rose in the pre-differentiation stage and declined in the later stages. The expression of MMP7 continued to rise throughout differentiation, whereas SPRR1A was unchanged (Fig. 5g).

In the PAAD and control groups, ductal cells demonstrated strong interactions with other annotated cells types and played a pivotal role in the overall cellular communication network. There were more ductal cell interactions with acinar, stellate, and endothelial cells in the PAAD group than in the control group, but fewer interactions with B cells (Fig. 5h-i). This was evident not only in their impact on the network's structure but also in their receptor-ligand interactions with other cells types. In the control group, ductal cells binding to the CD74+CD44 receptor of macrophages via the ligand MIF had the highest communication probability (Fig. S5a). In the PAAD group, ductal cells had the highest probability of communication via ligand MIF binding to the CD74+CXCR4 receptor of B cells (Fig. S5b). This shift in cellular interactions might reflect alterations in the tumor microenvironment that contribute to PAAD progression. This enhanced interaction could facilitate tumor growth and invasion by promoting angiogenesis and fibrosis, which were critical for providing the tumor with nutrients and structural support. Conversely, the decreased interaction with B cells might indicate a suppressed immune response, potentially allowing the tumor to evade immune surveillance and further progress.

Functional validation of GBP4 in pancreatic cancer

Building on our bioinformatic analyses suggesting GBP4’s involvement in pancreatic cancer progression, we experimentally investigated its functional role using pancreatic cancer cell lines and mouse models. To explore the functional role of GBP4 in pancreatic cancer, we analyzed its protein expression in six pancreatic cancer cell lines (PaTu8988T, BxPC-3, CFPAC-1, SW1990, PANC-1, and AsPC-1) using Western Blot techniques. This initial step revealed that BxPC-3 cells displayed the highest GBP4 expression, while CFPAC-1 cells had the lowest (Fig. 6a). Capitalizing on this difference, we selected BxPC-3 for GBP4 knockdown experiments and CFPAC-1 for GBP4 overexpression studies. Subsequent Western Blot analysis confirmed the success of these genetic manipulations, showing a significant reduction of GBP4 in BxPC-3 cells (Fig. 6b) and a significant increase in CFPAC-1 cells (Fig. 6c), setting the stage for functional assessments.

Our investigation into the effect of GBP4 on cancer cell behavior proceeded with a series of in vitro assays. Using CCK8 assays to measure proliferation, we found that knocking down GBP4 in BxPC-3 cells significantly decreased cell growth at 72 hours (p = 0.004) (Fig. 6d), whereas overexpressing GBP4 in CFPAC-1 cells significantly increased proliferation (p = 0.025) (Fig. 6e). These results indicated that GBP4 promotes pancreatic cancer cell proliferation.

We then turned to wound healing assays to evaluate cell migration. In BxPC-3 cells, GBP4 knockdown significantly reduced scratch closure (p =0.026) (Fig. 6f), indicating impaired migratory capacity. Conversely, in CFPAC-1 cells, GBP4 overexpression significantly enhanced scratch closure (p = 0.003) (Fig. 6g), suggesting heightened mobility. To further probe metastatic potential, Transwell assays assessed cell invasion. GBP4 knockdown in BxPC-3 cells significantly reduced the number of invaded cells (p = 0.003) (Fig. 6h), while overexpression in CFPAC-1 cells significantly increased invasion (p = 0.003) (Fig. 6i). Together, these findings underscored the role of GBP4 in enhancing both cancer cell migration and invasion.

To determine whether these cellular effects translated to tumor behavior, we conducted in vivo experiments using subcutaneous tumor models in mice. Tumors derived from GBP4-knockdown BxPC-3 group were significantly reduced in tumor volume and weight compared to the control group (Fig. 6j). In contrast, tumor volume and weight were significantly increased in GBP4-overexpressing CFPAC-1 group compared to the control group (Fig. 6k). These findings underscored GBP4 actively drives pancreatic cancer progression by promoting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro, and enhancing tumor growth in vivo, positioning it as a potential target for therapeutic intervention.

Discussion

This study investigated the mechanism of action and prognostic value of GBP4-related prognostic genes in PAAD. Five prognostic genes, namely GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, BCAT1, and SPRR1A, were screened out in this study. Based on the screened prognostic genes, A prognostic risk model for PAAD was developed and subsequently validated through Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and ROC curve evaluation. The results showed that this model could effectively distinguish low-risk and high-risk patients and was of great value for the prognostic assessment of PAAD patients. Moreover, the biological pathways, tumor microenvironment, and drug sensitivity of the HRG and LRG were further explored, as well as the chromosomal and subcellular localization of the prognostic gene. In the single-cell data analysis part, we discovered the important role of ductal cells in PAAD by using the scRNA-sequencing data set. Pseudo-time analysis revealed the changes in gene expression during cell differentiation, and cell-cell communication analysis explored the interactions between ductal cells and other cells. This investigation seeks to establish new insights for the targeted treatment and prognostic diagnosis of PAAD.

In PAAD research, GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, BCAT1, and SPRR1A are key genes closely related to prognosis. They are interrelated and together influence the development of PAAD. The GBP2 gene is located on human chromosome 1, and the protein it encodes is involved in the host defence. It is regulated by the type I interferon signaling pathway [37]. This study speculates that it acts on PAAD by affecting the functions of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, which is consistent with its immunomodulatory role in other studies and supports the research findings [38]. The effect of GBP2 on the tumor microenvironment led us to focus on the KRT6A gene related to cell structure. KRT6A is a member of the keratin gene family, and the protein it encodes maintains the structure of epithelial cells [39]. Although there is no direct research on its relationship with PAAD, in oral squamous cell carcinoma, it is related to tumor invasion and metastasis. Cell structure affects the extracellular environment, and the MMP7 gene plays an important role. The enzyme encoded by MMP7 is involved in the degradation of the extracellular matrix and is closely related to various physiological and pathological processes as well as the development and metastasis of digestive tract tumors [[40], [41], [42]]. For instance, in colorectal cancer [43], MMP7 has been shown to promote cancer cell invasion. In our study of PAAD, MMP7 was identified as a prognostic gene. Given its established role in colorectal cancer and other related research, it is reasonable to infer that MMP7 may also contribute to the invasion and metastasis of PAAD cells. This finding is consistent with previous studies, thus confirming the reliability of our research results [44]. The invasion and metastasis of tumor cells affected by MMP7 involves metabolic changes, which are related to the BCAT1 gene [45].

BCAT1 encodes an enzyme involved in the metabolism of branched-chain amino acids and is important for the maintenance of physiological functions and the proliferation and survival of tumor cells. In breast cancer, elevated expression levels were significantly correlated with adverse clinical outcomes [[46], [47], [48]]. Cell metabolism affects the cell barrier function, and the SPRR1A gene is related to this. The protein encoded by SPRR1A is involved in cell keratinization and maintains the physical barrier of tissues [[49], [50], [51]]. Although there is no direct research on its relationship with PAAD, in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, it is related to tumor progression. However, due to the molecular heterogeneity of tumors, the specific mechanisms of action of these five genes’ actions in PAAD and other tumors still need to be further investigated.

The prognostic model constructed in this study based on five genes demonstrated that the LRG exhibited superior survival rates. The model achieved excellent performance in PAAD prognosis prediction, with 1-year, 3-year and 5-year AUC values of 0.815, 0.849 and 0.895, respectively.Compared with similar studies,Peng et al. [52] also identified a low-risk group with better survival using a prognostic model based on five lactylation-related genes. However, their reported 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year AUC values (0.722, 0.793, 0.782) were lower than those of the present model, with the differences being more pronounced at the 1-year and 5-year time points.Ma et al. [53] reported that a model based on seven cancer-associated fibroblast-related genes achieved 1-5 year AUC values ranging only from 0.680 to 0.857.Ba et al. [54] developed a prognostic model based on five mitochondrial metabolism-related genes, reporting 1-year, 3-year and 5-year AUC values of 0.73, 0.85 and 0.85, respectively. While their 3-year and 5-year AUC values were close to those of the present model, the 1-year AUC value remained lower.In summary, the model developed in this study demonstrates certain advantages, suggesting greater clinical utility, particularly for long-term prognostic assessment in PAAD.

GSEA reveals significant differences between the HRG and LRG of PAAD at the biological pathway level. In the HRG, the "cell cycle" pathway is significantly enriched, and its overactivation accelerates cancer cell proliferation and worsens the prognosis [55] In the LRG, the "neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction" pathway, the "glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism" pathway and the "proximal tubule bicarbonate reclamation" pathway are enriched. They inhibit tumor invasion and metastasis and improve the prognosis by regulating cell communication, limiting tumor cell energy production and biosynthesis, and neutralizing acidic substances, respectively [[56], [57], [58]]. GSVA reveals further differences. In the HRG, the "O-glycan biosynthesis" pathway and "p53 signaling pathway" are upregulated. The former alters the glycosylation pattern of glycoproteins on the surface of cancer cells, facilitating their immune escape, while the latter, due to mutations in the TP53 gene, leads to continuous proliferation of cancer cells and enhanced invasive and metastatic capabilities [59,60]. In the LRG, the "oxidative phosphorylation" pathway is active, stabilizing tumor cell metabolism and limiting invasiveness [61]. These pathway differences not only reveal the regulatory network of gene function, but also demonstrate significant associations with immunological characteristics within the tumor microenvironment. Therefore, the analysis of immune infiltration is particularly important.

These pathway differences reveal the regulatory network of gene function and suggest potential mechanisms by which GBP4 and related genes influence pancreatic cancer progression. To confirm these computational predictions, we conducted functional experiments to directly assess the role of GBP4 in cancer cell behavior and tumor growth. Our in vitro assays demonstrated that GBP4 knockdown in BxPC-3 cells significantly reduced proliferation, migration, and invasion, while its overexpression in CFPAC-1 cells enhanced these malignant behaviors. Complementary in vivo studies using mouse subcutaneous tumor models further confirmed that GBP4 promotes tumor growth, as knockdown led to tumor regression and overexpression resulted in more aggressive tumor growth. These results not only corroborate the prognostic significance of GBP4 but also position it as a potential therapeutic target, warranting further investigation into its molecular mechanisms.

In this study, immune infiltration analysis was performed based on the complexity of the tumor microenvironment and revealed the differences in immune cell infiltration between the HRG and LRG, as well as the associations with prognostic genes. For example, in the HRG, the infiltration of central memory CD8+ cells is increased, but their activity is inhibited, leading to a poor prognosis. In the LRG, the infiltration of activated B cells is increased, which is beneficial for the prognosis. KRT6A is negatively correlated with activated B cells. In the HRG, increased in the expression of KRT6A reduces the infiltration of activated B cells. BCAT1 has no significant correlation with differential immune cells. GBP2, KRT6A, MMP7, and SPRR1A are positively correlated with central memory CD8 T cells, but their functions are affected by the tumor microenvironment. The differences in immune infiltration imply complex interactions between cells. Single-cell analysis technology allows this relationship to be analysed at a more microscopic level.

Drug sensitivity analysis in this study revealed differential IC50 values for 140 drugs, including MK-2206 and Sorafenib, between the HRG and LRG groups. MK-2206 is a potent AKT inhibitor effective against all AKT isoforms [62]. The AKT signaling pathway plays a critical role in cell proliferation, survival, and angiogenesis, and is frequently dysregulated in various human cancers [63]. By inhibiting AKT kinase activity, MK-2206 suppresses tumor growth and induces cell death [[64], [65], [66]]. In PAAD, MK-2206 reduces cancer cell viability and enhances the efficacy of gemcitabine [67]. Sorafenib is a multikinase inhibitor that acts against multiple cancers by targeting both tumor cells and tumor vasculature. For example, Sorafenib is a first-line targeted therapy for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), significantly improving patient survival [68]. Furthermore, combining MEK inhibitors with Sorafenib enhances its efficacy against HCC cells by attenuating p-ERK rebound [69].These findings suggest that drugs such as MK-2206 and Sorafenib may offer potential directions for personalized PAAD treatment based on HRG/LRG stratification. However, their specific roles in PAAD and their connection to GBP4-related pathways require further experimental investigation.

Single cell analysis has identified ductal cells as key cells in PAAD. As ductal cells play an important role in pancreatic physiology and share similar characteristics with cancer cells, it is speculated that their abnormalities will disrupt the microenvironment and promote tumor development. However, more research is still needed to explore their functions and therapeutic targets. The study of key cells provides a new perspective for understanding the pathogenesis of PAAD, and also lays the groundwork for evaluating the research results and looking ahead to future research directions.

Conclusion

This study uncovers prognostic genes related to GBP4 and clarifies the critical role of GBP4 in promoting pancreatic cancer progression. Through validation of these findings with functional assay, our research enhances the rationale for targeting GBP4 to improve prognosis in PAAD.

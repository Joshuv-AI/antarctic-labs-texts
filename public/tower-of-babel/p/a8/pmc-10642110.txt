The Impact of the COVID-19 Pandemic Restrictions on the Provision of Adult Congenital Heart Disease Care Across Canada: A National Survey
Authors: Ruochen Tony Mao, Luc Beauchesne, Ariane Marelli, Candice Silversides, Annie Dore, Javier Ganame, Michelle Keir, Rafael Alonso-Gonzalez, Isabelle Vonder Muhll, Jasmine Grewal, Anne Williams, Payam Dehghani, Samuel Siu, Amer Johri, Elisabeth Bedard, Judith Therrien, Doug Hayami, Catherine Kells, Robin A Ducas
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe COVID-19 pandemic significantly impacted health care access across Canada with the reduction in in-person evaluations. The aim of the study was to examine the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on access to health care services among the Canadian population with adult congenital heart disease (ACHD).MethodsAll Canadian adult congenital heart affiliated centres were contacted and asked to collect data on outpatient clinic and procedural volumes for the 2019 and 2020 calendar years. A survey was sent detailing questions on clinic and procedural volumes and wait times before and after pandemic restrictions. Descriptive statistics were used with the Student t-test to compare groups.ResultsIn 2019, there were 19,326 ACHD clinic visits across Canada and only 296 (1.5%) virtual clinic visits. However, during the first year of the pandemic, there were 20,532 clinic visits and 11,412 (56%) virtual visits (P < 0.0001). There were no differences in procedural volumes (electrophysiology, cardiac surgery, and percutaneous intervention) between 2019 and 2020. The mean estimated wait times (months) before the pandemic vs the pandemic were as follows: nonurgent consult 5.4 ± 2.6 vs 6.6 ± 4.2 (P = 0.65), ACHD surgery 6.0 ± 3.5 vs 7.0 ± 4.6 (P = 0.47), electrophysiology procedures 6.3 ± 3.3 vs 5.7 ± 3.3 (P = 0.72), and percutaneous intervention 4.6 ± 3.9 vs 4.4 ± 2.3 (P = 0.74).ConclusionsDuring the pandemic and restrictions of social distancing, the use of virtual clinic visits helped to maintain continuity in ACHD clinical care, with 56% of ACHD visits being virtual. The procedural volumes and wait times for consultation and percutaneous and surgical interventions were not delayed.

Because of advancements in surgical technique and percutaneous intervention, roughly 90% of children born with congenital heart disease (CHD) live into adulthood.1 Clinical practice guidelines have been created and published to help direct and improve the quality of care delivered to patients with adult CHD (ACHD). The 32nd Bethesda conference stressed the importance of ongoing follow-up as these patients are at increased risk for poor outcomes compared with the non-CHD population.2, 3, 4

At the start of 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic took the world by storm, resulting in major obstacles in provision and utilization of health care services. Patients were afraid of acquiring COVID-19 at health care facilities; they were unable to access care due to lockdown restrictions and suspension of scheduled health care services. This led to delays in cancer diagnosis, missed vaccinations, and decreased visits to the emergency department.5, 6, 7 One systematic review documented a median reduction in health care visits by 42.3%, a median reduction in diagnostic imaging by 31.4%, and a median reduction in therapeutics by 29.6%.8 The impact of COVID-19 on the delivery of cardiac services has also been documented. It was shown that in the United Kingdom there was a 50% reduction in the volume of patients presenting to the cardiology department and fewer cases of myocardial infarction were being diagnosed. There was also a decrease in other services; there was an 80% reduction in outpatient clinic referrals, the number of electrocardiograms and echocardiograms performed decreased by 50%, and the use of cardiac rehabilitation decreased by 45%.9 The same phenomenon was also described in other countries around the world.7, 8, 9 In Ontario, Canada, there was a decrease in the number of referrals for percutaneous and surgical procedures and a decrease in procedural volume, but the wait times for these procedures did not increase.10

In this study, we sought to determine if the COVID-19 restrictions delayed access to health care services among the Canadian population with ACHD. The specific services measured in our study include clinic evaluation, procedural volumes, and wait times in Canada.

Methods

Data sources

An electronic survey was distributed to all Canadian Adult Congenital Heart Network affiliated ACHD centres in 2021. The centres were contacted via email, and all 15 centres across Canada agreed to participate and contribute data for this study. The survey included retrospective data from 2019 (prepandemic) and 2020 (pandemic) on all patients ≥18 years of age followed at these centres. The survey included 3 categories: clinic visits, procedures, and wait times.

Under the category of clinic visits, data were collected on the total number of in-person visits and the total number of virtual visits. Regarding procedures, programme level data were collected on the total number of cardiac surgeries performed, the total number of electrophysiology (EP) procedures performed (devices and ablations), and the total number of percutaneous procedures performed. Lastly, for wait times, data were collected for each procedure listed above and for new consultations in the ACHD clinic.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used for the number of responses collected. The data are expressed as mean with standard deviation. The t-test was used for statistical analysis; P values of <0.05 were considered statistically significant. The normality of the data set was analysed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. All analyses were performed using the Prism 9 software.

Results

Survey response: study setting

All 15 Canadian Adult Congenital Heart centres across Canada responded and filled out the survey collecting data from 2019-2020. The total number of patients living with ACHD actively followed with booked clinic visits across Canada was 36,708, with the majority being followed in large centres such as Ontario, Québec, British Columbia, and Alberta. There were 95 practicing ACHD cardiologists in Canada, with a mean of 6 ± 3 affiliated with each centre. There were 19 specialized ACHD surgeons in Canada, primarily centred around 8 regional surgical centres (no ACHD surgical centre in Manitoba or Saskatchewan, and only 1 servicing Atlantic, Canada). Across Canada, there were 22 nurses who provided care in the ACHD clinics/programmes.

Survey response: Clinic visits

In 2019, the total number of clinic visits was 19,326; the overwhelming majority (98.5%) were in-person (n = 19,030). Comparatively, in the first year of the pandemic in 2020, the total number of clinic visits was 20,532, and 9120 (44%) visits were in-person, whereas 11,412 visits were completed virtually. In comparison with the prepandemic year, the proportion of virtual visits increased from 1.5% to 55.6% of the total clinic visits (P < 0.0001). Survey response: Procedure volume

Total procedural volumes for ACHD care are presented in Figure 2, with most of the procedures performed occurring in large ACHD centres capable of complex surgery and intervention. The Université de Montréal programme performed 89 congenital diagnostic catheterizations in 2020, which was a 75% increase from the previous year. In addition, they also performed more congenital cardiac surgeries and EP procedures in 2020 compared with 2019 (101 vs 58, a 74% increase, and 162 vs 116, a 40% increase). In 2020, the Toronto programme performed 163 congenital percutaneous interventions vs 119 in 2019, which was a 37% increase. On the converse, there was a decrease in centres like Alberta (38% fewer diagnostic catheterizations, 20% fewer surgeries and percutaneous interventions, and 60% fewer EP procedures), Dalhousie (57% fewer diagnostic catheterizations and 40% fewer surgeries), and Manitoba (66% fewer percutaneous interventions and 30% fewer surgeries). In 2019, across Canada there were 1385 total diagnostics catheterizations performed compared with 1408 in 2020 (data from British Columbia were not available). In 2019, there were 405 percutaneous interventions vs 457 in 2020, and there were 417 cardiac surgeries compared with 443 in 2020. Lastly, there were 268 EP procedures in 2019 compared with 302 in 2020. Total procedural volumes by provincial region are outlined in Figure 3.

Discussion

The results from this study highlight the change in health care delivery and utilization for patients with ACHD in Canada during the COVID-19 pandemic. Overall, clinic and procedural volumes were maintained though a shift in care delivery. Before the pandemic, the majority of the clinic visits were done in the traditional in-person fashion. However, despite the social distancing restrictions implemented in Canada, patients were still able to maintain their clinic follow-up through virtual care, which has helped maintain or, in some cases, exceed the clinic volume at prepandemic levels. This trend was also seen in California, USA.9 Based on prior prepandemic literature, videoconferencing sessions have been used to facilitate case discussions between institutions in Canada and were found to be a feasible medium to facilitate ACHD patient care.14 The pandemic was a catalyst for widespread adoption of virtual conference sessions, which expanded into patient care. We now know that virtual visits are feasible and acceptable to patients. Benefits of virtual visits include easier access to health care services as they can help reduce the need for transportation and also the costs for virtual visits are lower. Disadvantages include the lack of a physical examination and breakdown in the relationship between patients and their health care providers especially for patients who are in a new therapeutic relationship and have not met their physician face to face. Virtual care will likely have an increased role in health care delivery to patients with ACHD going forward, especially in Canada, where geographical distances make transportation an issue especially for patients living further distances away from regional specialist centres. If virtual care is used properly, it will allow care providers to connect with their patients more easily, which has the potential to reduce the volume of patients lost to follow-up.

Despite the documented decrease in utilization of health care resources among other areas of medicine, the volume of procedures performed in the population with ACHD did not change significantly between 2019 and 2020. This was contrary to the trend seen around the world where there was an overall reduction in the volume of cardiac procedures provided to the general population. In Canada, at the beginning of the pandemic, there was memorandum to halt nonurgent cardiac procedures. In the study by Tam et al.,10 it was seen that the overall volume of cardiac procedures was decreased in Ontario. The precise explanation for the lack of decrease in ACHD-related cardiac procedures is unclear as this information was not captured by the initial survey. However, based on our observations, multiple reasons could be responsible for this finding. During the pandemic, resources and capacity for procedures and operating rooms were reduced for all patients. When scheduled general procedures were cancelled, more resources may have become available to congenital patients. This could be due to the maintenance of ACHD clinic visit numbers throughout the pandemic. It is possible that other patient populations did not have the same degree of routine care as patients with ACHD, and thus were not referred on for as many procedures during the study time frame. Also, this finding could be due in part to the urgency with which the procedures were required for patients with ACHD, prioritizing them over other elective cases. During the pandemic in 2020, 2 larger ACHD referral sites in Canada (Montreal and Toronto) saw an increase in procedure volume, which likely balanced the decline in volume seen in other centres because the overall volume across Canada did not change. In Canada, there are 15 centres that provide specialized care for patients with ACHD. Due to the geography and distribution of the population, there is heterogeneity in the resources available at each centre. Larger centres in Toronto and Montreal have greater resources and personnel able to provide advanced congenital cardiac surgery and percutaneous intervention, thus making them ideal choices to act in the capacity of hub centres for ACHD care.

There was no significant difference in the wait times for congenital cardiac procedures before and during the first year of the pandemic. The wait time for congenital cardiac procedures was heterogeneous across Canada, but overall, there was a trend towards increased estimated wait times, which is likely the result of multiple factors.11 Furthermore, compared with the recommended maximum acceptable weight times defined by the Canadian Cardiovascular Society, the current national average far exceeds the recommended time in Table 1 (1.5 months for nonurgent consults, ACHD surgery, and percutaneous procedure, and 3 months for the EP procedure).12,13,15

The long wait time is similar to the general population and is reflective of the difficulties accessing care in Canada, which is a publicly funded health care system. There are substantial delays in the referral of patients from a primary care practice to accessing specialty services. Factors that have contributed to the excess wait times include limitations in specialty care resources and higher demands on the health care system given the growing and aging population. In 2007, there were 20 congenital cardiac surgeons and 80 ACHD physicians. In 2020, there were 19 congenital cardiac surgeons and 95 physicians involved in the care of patients with ACHD (no major change in surgeons and a 19% increase for ACHD cardiologists), whereas the population with ACHD doubled from 96,000 to 183,000.16 In order to address the care needs of the growing population with ACHD, a multifaceted approach will likely be required. This may include primary care and general cardiology physician education, increased training of specialists in ACHD, tailored remuneration models for ACHD care, and shared and extended care through nursing and allied health.

Study limitations

As the methodology of this study was survey data, there are several important limitations to consider. Data were self-reported, and independent verification was not possible. Another limitation includes the timeline of data collection as the COVID-19 pandemic restrictions did not officially start until March 2020 in many Canadian centres. Therefore, data from January and February may affect the results of the 2020 pandemic data. Due to site-specific factors, the collection of data was limited to the calendar year for many centres. Despite this, the authors feel that the findings of this work demonstrate an important shift in patient care that occurred across Canada in 2020. Another limitation of this work is that we were not able to assess for a correlation between the use of virtual visits and patient outcomes. It is well recognized that follow-up impacts mortality in patients with ACHD;17,18 however, at this time, we are unable to determine the role of virtual care in long-term patient outcomes. Future studies will be required to assess the impact of virtual visits on patient outcomes and satisfaction.

Conclusions

In this study, we have shown that despite strict social distancing restrictions, the use of virtual clinic visits has helped to maintain continuity in ACHD clinical care. This suggests that virtual clinics may become an important tool in the future to help maintain close follow-up as patients are widely dispersed across Canada, especially for patients located further away from their ACHD institution. We have also shown that waiting times for ACHD care are still longer than recommended Canadian targets. The total procedural volume across Canada remained unchanged between the first year of the pandemic and the prepandemic year; however, the long-term impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the health of patients with ACHD remains to be determined.

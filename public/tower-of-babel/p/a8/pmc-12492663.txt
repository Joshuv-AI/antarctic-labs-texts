Differential impact of COVID-19 prevention and control measures on physical activity and mental health of junior high school students at different learning stages
Authors: Kai-yun Dou, Ru-bao DONG, Chunlong Gan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to examine the differential associations of COVID-19 prevention and control measures on the physical activity levels (PALs) and mental health (MH) of middle school students at different learning stages. Additionally, it sought to provide evidence for designing interventions to promote adolescent physical activity (PA) and MH.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted over three years using the Physical Activity Questionnaire for Children and Adolescence (PAQ-CN) and the Mental Health Inventory of middle school students (MMHI-60) to assess PALs and MH. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) and cross-tabulation were employed to examine changes in PALs and MH over time.ResultsThe PALs of adolescents were recorded as 2.51 ± 0.69 (95% CI: 2.47, 2.55) before the outbreak, 2.35 ± 0.74 (95% CI: 2.32, 2.39) during the outbreak, and 2.43 ± 0.71 (95% CI: 2.40, 2.47) after the easing of COVID-19 restrictions. Corresponding MH scores were 1.74 ± 0.63 (95% CI: 1.70, 1.77), 1.86 ± 0.73 (95% CI: 1.82, 1.90), and 1.77 ± 0.68 (95% CI: 1.73, 1.80), respectively. Significant changes were observed in both PALs and MH across the different periods (p < 0.01). The odds ratios for changes in these parameters were 28.60%, 35.30%, and 31.30%;The PALs of junior (7–9 grade) and senior (10–12 grade) high school students 2.66 ± 0.67, 2.57 ± 0.69, 2.49 ± 0.73 and 2.27 ± 0.65, 2.19 ± 0.73, 2.35 ± 0.69; the detection rates of psychological problems are 25.9%, 22.2%, 25.6% and 32.8%, 45.5%, 40.7%, respectively.ConclusionThe impact of COVID-19 on the MH of middle school students varied across learning stages, the adverse effects of epidemic prevention and control on PAL and MH in senior high school students are greater than those in junior high school students, with younger age being a protective factor for both MH and PA. These findings highlight the importance of addressing the combined effects of learning pressures and the COVID-19 outbreak on the MH of Chinese adolescents.Keywords: SARS-CoV-2, Physical activity, Mental health, Middle school students

Introduction

Since the COVID-19 pandemic at the end of 2019, it has had multiple impacts on individual behavior [1]. Most studies suggest a decline in PA among children and adolescents during the COVID-19 period [2]. Individuals affected by COVID-19 may experience various psychological issues [3]. China successfully contained the COVID-19 virus largely due to the swift implementation of isolation measures, which have been emulated by other countries [4]. However, the Chinese government’s strict epidemic prevention and control measures have had negative effects on the PA of adolescents, as these measures restrict personal mobility, daily activities, and social interactions [5]. The PA and MH of children and adolescents have been seriously affected by this type of lockdown/silence management, which has had a more severe impact on global MH due to the social distancing measures imposed by COVID-19 [6, 7]. Children and adolescents are particularly affected by these restrictive measures [2]. Until 08 January 2023, when the Chinese government officially lifted the social control measures against COVID-19, different regions in China had implemented two types of social management models based on the severity of the COVID-19 outbreak: normalized prevention and control (allowing people to move freely with a “travel code” but maintaining a safe social distance) and silence management/lockdown (except for personnel involved in the fight against the epidemic, all others were prohibited from going out). After the epidemic prevention and control measures were lifted, people’s social lives basically returned to the state before the outbreak.

Currently, research on the impact of COVID-19 on individuals mainly focuses on the outbreak period, such as the reasons and determinants of restricted PA among children and adolescents during COVID-19 [2]. PA as an effective intervention against COVID-19 [8], the relationship between PA, screen time, and MH of children during the COVID-19 pandemic [9], and the MH of children during school closures and in the context of COVID-19 [10, 11]. The regular epidemic prevention and control measures had significant adverse effects on the PAL of girls and senior high school adolescents [12]. However, the lifting of societal control measures does not imply the elimination of COVID-19 and its effects. The global COVID-19 is still ongoing, and understanding its long-term impact on PA is crucial [13]. However, there is a scarcity of reports or studies on the relationship between PA, MH, and COVID-19 among adolescents during different phases of control measures, including normalization, outbreak, and post-outbreak. The main purpose of this study is to provide reference for PA and MH interventions for middle school students of different genders and learning stages after the implementation of different prevention and control management models during major epidemics, such as how to promote PA among middle school students at home and carry out psychological health education and intervention in schools based on their learning stage and gender characteristics, etc.

Materials and methods

Study design and survey participants

This study was approved by the Academic Ethics Committee of the School of Physical Education at Guizhou Normal University.

In September 2021, the study aimed to explore the relationship between PA and MH among adolescents, while also assessing the current status and changes in PA and MH in this population, particularly considering the potential impact of COVID-19. A convenience sampling method was employed to select three senior high schools(the students enrolled in such schools are senior high school students, namely the 10–12 grade students mentioned in this article) and six junior high schools (the students enrolled in such schools are junior high school students, namely the 7–9 grade students mentioned in this article) in Guiyang City. Adolescents were verbally recruited for participation, and those who expressed interest were provided with a “Parental Informed Consent Form.” Only those students who returned the signed consent forms within one week were included in the study. The same schools were surveyed again in 2022 and 2023, although participants were recruited afresh each year.

The surveys were conducted in three phases: late October 2021, mid-to-late October 2022 (following the containment of the COVID-19 outbreak and the resumption of offline teaching, with the survey conducted approximately 10 days earlier to gather more timely data), and late October 2023. Due to school regulations prohibiting the use of mobile phones on campus, online surveys were not feasible despite most students owning mobile devices. Consequently, a paper-based questionnaire was administered. During periods of inclement weather, when outdoor physical education classes were canceled, surveyors gathered participants in classrooms where the questionnaires were distributed, completed, and collected on-site.

Survey tool

The Physical Activity Questionnaire for Children and Adolescents (PAQ-CN) [14] was utilized to assess the PALs of middle school students, which was modified from the Physical Activity Questionnaire for Children (PAQ-C) [Kowalski KC, et al., 2004]. This questionnaire was selected due to its design specifically for children and its validation in previous studies. It also takes into account the “long time recess for physical activity " commonly practiced in Chinese elementary and middle schools. The scoring method for PAQ-CN is exactly the same as PAQ-C, with higher scores indicating higher PAL. The PAQ-CN is sensitive enough to differentiate between genders and age groups without requiring detailed recollection of exercise duration and intensity. Its clear and straightforward language ensures ease of understanding and completion, minimizing potential recall bias. Furthermore, the PAQ-C has demonstrated good reliability and validity [14, 15]. The internal consistency reliability coefficient was Cronbach’s alpha = 0.802, and the structural validity coefficient was KMO score = 0.872(p < 0.05).

For the MH assessment, the study employed the Mental Health Inventory of Middle School Students (MMHI-60). This inventory consists of 60 items, addressing symptoms related to Obsessive-compulsive symptoms (OCS), paranoia, hostility, interpersonal sensitivity, depression, anxiety, academic pressure, maladaptation, emotional instability, and psychological imbalance. Responses were recorded using a 5-point Likert scale, the lower the average score, the healthier the MH. The overall average score of the scale represents the MH status, while the average score of the subscales represents the health status of each psychological factor. An average score ≧ 2 indicates the presence of health problems, and the higher the average score, the more severe the health problems. The MMHI-60 has shown satisfactory reliability and validity [16]. The internal consistency reliability coefficient (Cronbach’s alpha) in this study was found to be 0.970, and its KMO was 0.980(p < 0.05).

Additionally, a self-designed questionnaire was used to collect basic demographic information such as gender, grade level, and age. After the questionnaires were collected, data entry was conducted by two groups of four individuals working independently. Once data entry was completed, a cross-check between the groups was performed, and any discrepancies were resolved by reviewing the original questionnaires for verification.

Sampling basis

Junior high school students and senior high school students were treated as two distinct groups in this study. The sample size was calculated using the formula: N = Z2 × (P × (1 − P))/E2 [17], where N represents the sample size, Z is the statistical value for a 95% confidence level (Z = 1.96), E is the margin of error (0.05), and P is the estimated proportion (P = 0.5). Based on this formula, the estimated sample size was approximately N ≈ 385. Classes of students were randomly selected from each grade to complete the survey each year. In each survey conducted for this study, the actual sample size exceeded the minimum requirement. The distribution of the sample sizes is presented in Table 1.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 26.0. The primary method of analysis was a one-way ANOVA to compare mean values. Specifically, comparisons were made between the periods before the COVID-19 outbreak (2021), during the outbreak (2022), and one year after the outbreak (2023), focusing on the changes of PALs and MH. Additionally, cross-tabulation was used to compare the detection rates of psychological issues among students. The significance level for all tests was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Changes in adolescent PA and MH levels

An analysis of PAL and MH trends among senior high school and junior high school students from 2021 to 2023 revealed distinct patterns. For the overall sample and senior high school students, PAL scores followed a V-shaped trend, while junior high school students showed a consistent decline. Regarding MH scores and their various subfactors, the overall sample and senior high school students exhibited an inverted V-shaped pattern, with the scores in 2023 being slightly higher than those in 2021, suggesting a recovery. In contrast, junior high school students mostly displayed a V-shaped pattern (except for the hostility and psychological imbalance subfactors), with most psychological factors in 2023 either equaling or surpassing their 2021 levels (Table 2).

Comparison of detection rates of adolescent MH issues

The detection rates of psychological problems in adolescents follow an inverted V-shaped pattern. For junior high school students, the detection rates generally show a consistent downward trend, with the exception of hostility, which also follows a decreasing trajectory. In contrast, among senior high school students, the detection rates of psychological problems exhibit an inverted V-shaped pattern, with the exception of hostility, interpersonal relationships, and psychological imbalance, which show a consistent upward trend. Notably, in all years, the detection rates of psychological problems among junior high school students were lower than those among senior high school students (Table 5).

Discussion

During the COVID-19 outbreak, PAL significantly decreased compared to pre-outbreak levels. Senior high school students experienced a notable decline in PAL during the outbreak, followed by a significant improvement one year later. On the other hand, junior high school students also saw a significant decrease in PAL during the outbreak, and one year later, their PAL not only failed to improve but continued to decline, although the change was not statistically significant. Although there was a slight improvement one year later, PAL remained lower than it was before the pandemic [18]. Throughout the pandemic, PAL in junior high school students was higher than that of senior high school students, which is consistent with the online survey results of a city in southwestern China that younger students tend to be more physically active than older students during non-pandemic periods [19]. One year after the COVID-19 outbreak, PAL significantly increased among senior high school students; however, it continued to decline among junior high school students. Although the annual decline was not statistically significant, the cumulative effect over time was significant. This substantial decrease in PAL among adolescents is consistent with previous survey results in Shanghai that PA time sharply declined for children and adolescents during the pandemic [20].

Before the outbreak, the surveyed individuals were living under routine epidemic control measures in which they could move freely, using “travel codes” while maintaining social distance. Teenagers’ activities within their communities were largely unrestricted. However, after the outbreak, the Chinese government implemented stringent epidemic control measures to contain the virus, which negatively impacted adolescents’ PA by limiting personal mobility, daily activities, and social interactions [5, 6]. In early January 2023, the Chinese government reclassified COVID-19 as a moderately contagious infectious disease, lifting most restrictions on social activities. Consequently, it is understandable that while PAL initially decreased for both adolescents and middle school students, it later increased, whereas the PAL of junior high school students continued to decline. This persistent decline could be attributed to a lack of awareness about the importance of physical exercise, the main factors leading to a decline in the physical health level of middle school students were school, family, community, and lack of lifelong physical exercise awareness among students [21].

The COVID-19 outbreak had a significant adverse impact on the MH of middle school students, particularly older adolescents. In contrast, while the outbreak appeared to have a more favorable effect on the MH of junior high school students, the effect was not statistically significant. The overall prevalence of psychological problems before the COVID-19 outbreak was lower than the survey data from a related study in 2019 [22]. However, the prevalence rate in the year prior to the COVID-19 outbreak was higher than 27.9% [12], which is within the normal range reported in similar studies [23], two surveys using MMHI-60. On one hand, this suggests that the MH of adolescents is generally poor. On the other hand, it indicates that the impact of COVID-19 and its associated control measures on adolescent MH may be limited. During the outbreak, MH levels declined further, though there was a slight improvement one year later. However, most MH indicators did not fully recover to pre-outbreak levels, except for the factors of psychological imbalance and OCS. For senior high school students, overall MH levels declined during the COVID-19 outbreak and had not fully recovered one year later. In contrast, junior high school students showed an improvement in MH during the outbreak, though this was followed by a declining trend one year later. While mental status, OCS, paranoid thoughts, interpersonal relationships, and psychological imbalance all showed declines, they remained higher than pre-outbreak levels. However, other psychological factors worsened compared to pre-pandemic levels. The decline in MH among senior high school students during the outbreak can be attributed to several factors, such as “changes in the lifestyle of children and adolescents combined with the threat of infection during the COVID-19 pandemic” [24], “lockdowns or stay-at-home orders that disrupted normal life” [25, 26], and high levels of academic pressure. It has been observed in survey studies that “the pandemic negatively affected PA, and adolescents under academic pressure are more prone to experiencing anxiety and depression” [27, 28]. In contrast, the improvement in MH among junior high school students during the outbreak may be linked to factors such as “individuals with lower educational levels being more likely to experience psychological distress” [29, 30] and “being younger as a risk factor for MH problems” [31, 32]. This improvement may also be related to the reduced academic pressure following the implementation of the Chinese government’s “Double Reduction” policy [33], as well as the closure of extracurricular training institutions during the outbreak, giving junior high school students more free time. While senior high school students may have been concerned about missing out on additional tutoring, junior high school students likely enjoyed a more relaxed mood due to the suspension of unwanted training programs. One year after the COVID-19 outbreak, the Chinese government lifted social control measures and reopened extracurricular training institutions. Senior high school students were now able to catch up on their studies, while junior high school students faced increased pressure to participate in various training programs arranged by their parents.

The detection rate of adolescent psychological problems ranged from 28.60 to 35.30%. The detection rate for anxiety was between 33.30% and 40.90%, while for depression, it was between 32.60% and 37.10%. Compared with the results of the same cross-sectional survey research, the rate of depression was lower than findings in related studies [34], whereas anxiety symptoms were higher than those reported in similar studies [35], but still below the global general population rates of 50.9% for anxiety and 48.3% for depression in a review [36]. Even during the COVID-19 outbreak, senior high school students exhibited lower depression rates but higher anxiety rates compared to junior high school students, aligning with the results from Chen et al. [27]. The fluctuation in adolescent PAL and MH during different periods of COVID-19 may be attributed to the negative impact of lockdowns on both MH and PA [37], further compounded by concerns about academic performance. The lower detection rate of MH issues in junior high school students, compared to senior high school students, may be related to higher levels of PA, which helps reduce the incidence of depression and anxiety symptoms [38, 39]. One year after the outbreak, the MH levels of junior high school students had returned to pre-COVID-19 levels, while their PAL continued to decline. In contrast, senior high school students showed improvement in both MH and PAL. The significant increase in the detection rate of academic pressure among junior high school students, combined with the resumption of social remedial and training institutions, suggests that the decline in PAL and MH of junior high school students may be linked to the compulsory extracurricular training imposed by parents. Studies have indicated that “extracurricular tutoring jeopardizes children’s normal learning and healthy growth” [40] and that the “double reduction” policies implemented two years later did not alleviate this burden for junior high school students [41]. Even after social restrictions were lifted, junior high school students’ PAL continued to decline, and their MH remained poor. This suggests that families, schools, and society have not sufficiently addressed the adverse effects of COVID-19 on adolescent MH following the removal of epidemic prevention and control measures. The impact of extracurricular training on junior high school students’ PAL and MH may be more significant than previously thought. In summary, academic pressure appears to be a major factor contributing to the low PAL and high detection rates of psychological issues among Chinese adolescents. The effects of COVID-19 on adolescent MH are likely to be long-term.

The low PAL and high detection rate of psychological problems among junior high school students still have long-term negative effects on adulthood; Low PAL is the main factor leading to the spread of various chronic non communicable diseases, and high detection rates of psychological issues among adolescents may be related to their MH in adulthood. Therefore, we should follow the Healthy China strategy, establish and implement health courses in the secondary school system, regularly screen for MH issues among junior high school students, and provide assistance.

Research limitations

The data in this study were collected solely from cross-sectional surveys, which may introduce recall bias. Additionally, due to policy and environmental constraints, although the experiences and social environment of the survey subjects are almost identical, they are not completely the same group, which to some extent reduces the reliability of inferring causal relationships between variables. Therefore, caution should be exercised when interpreting the results. Finally, although we believe that the various social environments formed by epidemic prevention and control measures have the same impact on everyone involved, we did not consider the possible influence of covariates in the statistical analysis. However, the influence of covariates maybe exists.

Conclusion and recommendations

The PAL and mental health of Chinese middle school students are generally low. Under the compounded stress of academic pressures, the COVID-19 outbreak had a severe impact on the PAL of middle school students, while its effect on the mental health of junior and senior high school students differed. Less academic pressure and younger age appeared to be protective factors for mental health during the COVID-19 period. However, the severe academic pressure experienced by middle school students negatively impacted both their mental health and PAL. The long-term effects of COVID-19 on the mental health of middle school students are evident. Therefore, after the easing of COVID-19 restrictions, sustainable interventions for PA and mental health should be implemented, particularly for middle school students.

Prolonged quarantine periods may lead to increased anxiety, fear of contagion, and post-traumatic stress disorder [42]. PA is recognized as an essential measure for reducing infection rates and maintaining overall health in both outdoor and indoor settings [43]. It is recommended that PA be used as a treatment or mitigation strategy for symptoms of mental health conditions [44, 45]. We urge parents, schools, and society in China to prioritize the physical and mental health of adolescents. By following an integrated model for promoting both physical and mental health [38], alongside the slightly adjusted PA guidelines based on the recommended PAL, effective mental health promotion among adolescents can be achieved [12]. The first step toward promoting adolescent physical and mental well-being should begin with encouraging regular participation in PA. Based on PAL and MH screening for middle school students, they would be divided into different groups, using After-school Delayed Services or Sunshine Sports time to systematically promote the health of middle school students.

Perspective

The COVID-19 pandemic and its associated prevention and control measures have had a profound adverse impact on the PA and MH of adolescents. During the outbreak, younger age was a protective factor for MH, while higher educational attainment became protective after the pandemic. The stricter the epidemic prevention and control measures, the greater and more lasting the negative impact on adolescents’ PA and MH, resulting in a longer recovery process. However, the adverse effects of academic pressure on adolescents, particularly younger ones, are even more significant than the effects of COVID-19 and its prevention measures. Following the easing of COVID-19 restrictions, it remains essential for families, schools, and all sectors of society to provide targeted support to promote or restore adolescents’ PA and MH. Reducing academic pressure and the overall burden on adolescents is critical to fundamentally addressing the PA and MH challenges faced by Chinese adolescents.

Comparison of neural and traditional techniques for soliton structures and dynamical behavior in a double-chain DNA model
Authors: Sheikh Zain Majid, Fatma Nur Kaya Sağlam, Mohammad Safi Ullah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, the nonlinear wave dynamics of the Double-Chain DNA model, a molecular system that captures the longitudinal and transverse interactions between two complementary nucleotide chains, are investigated. To construct explicit analytical structures, the generalized Riccati equation mapping method and the modified generalized Riccati mapping neural network method are applied in a complementary manner. Through these approaches, several classes of exact solutions, including hyperbolic, trigonometric, rational, and hybrid waveforms, are systematically derived. Consequently, the spectrum of admissible bright, dark, kink, and anti-kink wave structures supported by the model is significantly expanded. In addition to the derivation of exact solutions, the governing system is reduced to a planar dynamical framework to examine its qualitative behavior. Lyapunov exponent analysis is performed to quantify the long-term stability characteristics of the system and to identify parameter regimes exhibiting strong sensitivity to initial conditions. The analytical findings are further illustrated through 3D surface plots, contour diagrams, and 2D profiles, confirming the geometric and physical consistency of the obtained wave structures.Keywords: Double-Chain DNA model, Soliton solutions, Generalized Riccati equation mapping method, Modified generalized Riccati mapping neural network methodSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NPDEs) play a fundamental role in the mathematical modeling of complex dynamical phenomena arising in physics, engineering, and the biological sciences. Their nonlinear nature enables the description of essential mechanisms such as dispersion, dissipation, wave interaction, pattern formation, and energy localization, which cannot be captured by linear theories. Among the wide class of NPDE solutions, solitons have attracted particular attention due to their localized, shape-preserving, and stable propagation characteristics, making them indispensable in plasma physics1, nonlinear optics2, and biomedical applications3.

In recent decades, nonlinear models have also become essential tools for understanding biomolecular structures, particularly DNA. The double-helix architecture of DNA exhibits rich nonlinear behavior that can be effectively represented using lattice or continuum models governed by NPDEs. A foundational contribution in this field is the Peyrard–Bishop (PB) model, which describes base-pair stretching dynamics using a Morse potential combined with nonlinear stacking interactions; this model provides an effective framework for studying DNA denaturation and energy localization4. The Peyrard–Bishop–Dauxois (PBD) model later refined this formulation by incorporating nonlinear coupling effects and discreteness corrections, thereby improving the thermodynamic accuracy of denaturation predictions5. Parallel to these developments, Yakushevich introduced a rotational model describing torsional excitations in the DNA double helix, capturing the formation and stability of transcription bubbles and topological solitons6. Subsequent improvements to Yakushevich-type models demonstrated that such nonlinear excitations can possess biologically realistic spatial widths and stability characteristics7.

Further studies have explored kink-antikink solutions, bubble formation, and localized excitations using continuum approximations or combined nonlinear mechanisms8. Moreover, recent extensions of PB and PBD models have incorporated fractional derivatives, helicoidal geometry, environmental influence, and stochasticity, yielding new classes of soliton, breather, and rogue-wave-type solutions9. Modified PB models have been used to analyze DNA dynamics in thermostatted or bio-fluid environments, revealing the effects of temperature, viscosity, and external forces on nonlinear wave propagation10. Comprehensive reviews of nonlinear DNA dynamics emphasize that localized excitations, nonlinear stacking interactions, and soliton-like structures play a significant role in gene regulation, transcription, and replication processes11.

Alongside these modeling efforts, a wide range of analytical and computational methods have been developed to obtain exact or approximate solutions for DNA-related NPDEs. In recent years, Riccati-type mappings and auxiliary equation techniques have emerged as powerful tools for constructing exact traveling-wave solutions. Zayed and Arnous applied the generalized Riccati equation mapping method to DNA dynamical systems and obtained hyperbolic, trigonometric, and rational solution families12. A comprehensive qualitative investigation of a (2+1)-dimensional double-chain DNA system was conducted by Kumar in his study using planar dynamical system techniques, Lyapunov exponents, phase portraits, and numerical simulations. The study covered bifurcation analysis, stability classification, chaotic dynamics, sensitivity behaviour and multiple stability13. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of Riccati-inspired frameworks in producing rich solution structures for biomolecular models.

Numerous methods have been developed in the literature to obtain analytical solutions to NPDEs. These methods are effectively used in the investigation of soliton, breather, and periodic wave structures arising in different physical models. In this context, the most commonly used approaches include the new extended direct algebraic method15, the ()-expansion neural network analytical method16, the unified method17, the polynomial expansion neural networks method18, the multivariate generalized exponential differential function approach19, the Levenberg-Marquardt artificial neural network technique20, the generalized Arnous method21, the Kumar-Malik method22,23, the enhanced modified extended tanh expansion method24, the modified Sardar sub equation method25–27, the Khater method28, the multiple exp-function technique29, the extended tanh-coth expansion method30, the sine(cosine)-function method31–33, the ()-expansion method34, the multivariate generalized exponential rational integral function method35, the generalized logistic equation approach36, the -Expansion method37,38, the Hopf bifurcation39, the Hirota bilinear method40, the generalized projective Riccati equation method41,42, the improved Kudryashov method43,44, and neural network methods45–49. However, some of these techniques may require specific balancing conditions, parameter constraints, or reduction transformations, which can limit the classes of solutions that can be obtained. Therefore, the investigation of hybrid and expandable approaches aimed at revealing wider solution spaces for NPDEs remains relevant.

A careful assessment of the current literature on nonlinear DNA dynamical models indicates that several analytical and qualitative aspects of the double-chain formulation remain insufficiently addressed. Notably, previous investigations have neither adopted the generalized Riccati mapping neural network (GRMNN) method for constructing analytical approximations nor employed the generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM) technique to derive explicit families of traveling-wave solutions for this system. Likewise, studies to date have not utilized the planar dynamical system framework, which is crucial for identifying equilibrium configurations, classifying their stability, and analyzing the qualitative geometry of the phase space. An additional limitation concerns the absence of comprehensive dynamical diagnostics; essential tools such as Lyapunov exponents, sensitivity metrics, and phase-portrait structures have not been examined for the double-chain DNA equations. Collectively, these omissions highlight notable methodological and theoretical gaps in the existing body of research. Motivated by these shortcomings, the present study undertakes a rigorous analytical and dynamical investigation of the coupled DNA model. First, the GREMM technique is applied to obtain exact wave solutions and to reveal additional structural properties inherent in the model. Subsequently, the GRMNN technique is employed to produce accurate nonlinear mappings and analytical representations of the governing system. The reduced system is then reformulated within the planar dynamical framework, enabling a systematic examination of equilibrium points, their stability characteristics, and the global behavior of the corresponding trajectories. Furthermore, the stability of the derived solutions is scrutinized through Lyapunov exponents, and sensitivity analyses. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, no previous work has combined Riccati-based neural computational techniques, generalized Riccati formulations, and planar dynamical system theory within a unified analytical framework for the double-chain DNA model. By addressing the aforementioned deficiencies, the present study introduces new solution structures and dynamical insights that substantially enhance the theoretical understanding of nonlinear excitations in DNA molecules.

The GRMNNs integrate the NN model with the GREMM. The key idea is that the activation functions of the first hidden layer are chosen as the solutions of the GREMM. Figure 2 presents the NN framework used for constructing approximate trial functions for GRMNN Procedure.

Determine the Riccati equation by applying the GREMM, and assign its solutions as the activation functions of the first hidden layer.

Solve this algebraic system, and substitute the obtained constants back into the trial function to produce explicit solutions.

Thus, exact solutions of Eqs.(1.1) and (1.2) are obtained using the GRMNN structure, where the first hidden layer consists of GREMM solutions.

The GRMNN framework adopted in this study should not be interpreted as a conventional data-trained neural network model. Rather than involving a learning process based on datasets, loss functions, or gradient-based optimization, the network structure is utilized here as an analytical construction tool for generating suitable trial solution forms. The network parameters are not obtained through backpropagation; instead, they are determined explicitly by inserting the neural-inspired ansatz into the original NPDE and solving the resulting algebraic conditions. In this sense, GRMNN functions as a symbolic neural mapping methodology aimed at producing exact solutions rather than numerical approximations. A key advantage of this approach is that it allows the derivation of solution families directly from the governing equation without requiring intermediate reductions, while offering a more systematic and adaptable functional framework compared with classical expansion-based techniques.

Results and discussion

The analytical wave structures obtained in this study indicate that the Double-Chain DNA model supports a broad spectrum of nonlinear excitations with distinct physical behaviors. While both GREMM and GRMNN are constructed within a Riccati-type analytical framework, their contributions differ in a fundamental way. The classical GREMM requires an intermediate reduction of the governing NPDE into an ODE, which restricts the admissible wave transformations and limits the form of trial functions. In contrast, the GRMNN approach embeds the analytical structure directly into the neural network layers, allowing the construction of trial solution spaces without dimensional reduction. This flexibility enables the derivation of solution families that are not easily accessible through the classical balancing procedure alone. Therefore, GRMNN is not merely a re-parametrization of standard Riccati expansions but provides an extended functional framework guided by the network architecture. In terms of methodology, the principal advantage of the proposed Riccati-based framework is its ability to systematically generate diverse classes of closed-form analytical solutions without relying solely on numerical simulations. However, the applicability of the method depends on algebraic balance conditions and nonlinear parameter constraints, which may limit solution construction for certain parameter regimes. In particular, highly complex nonlinear couplings or strongly non-polynomial terms may reduce the effectiveness of the Riccati-based ansatz and require alternative or hybrid analytical-numerical strategies. Therefore, while the approach is powerful and flexible, its implementation requires careful structural consistency and parameter selection. The presence of kink-anti-kink transitions reflects the possibility of topological energy transport along the molecular chain, whereas bright and dark soliton profiles suggest localized energy concentration and depletion mechanisms within DNA dynamics. Beyond the explicit solution forms, the dynamical assessment provides further physical insight into the model. In particular, the sensitivity analysis demonstrates that even small variations in initial conditions can significantly influence the evolution of the nonlinear waves, highlighting the delicate dependence of soliton propagation on system parameters. Moreover, the Lyapunov exponent computations reveal parameter regimes associated with stable motion as well as transitions toward chaotic behavior, offering a quantitative measure of long-term predictability. Together, these findings strengthen the reliability of the proposed hybrid analytical-neural framework and contribute to a deeper understanding of the nonlinear wave dynamics in biomolecular systems.

Comparisons

This section clarifies the specific methodological advances and dynamical contributions of the present study in relation to the existing literature. In Ref. 55, comparing with our model, authors investigated the new polynomial and soliton solutions by two- and three-dimensional breakdowns. While this contribution enriches the catalogue of analytical wave structures, the scope of that analysis remains primarily within the construction of explicit solution forms. In particular, the investigation does not extend to a unified dynamical interpretation of the reduced system, nor does it include quantitative stability diagnostics. By contrast, the present study introduces a hybrid analytical-neural framework that integrates the GREMM with a GRMNN. This combined formulation not only generates exact traveling-wave solutions but also provides a structurally adaptable trial-function space beyond standard balancing techniques. As a result, hyperbolic, trigonometric, rational, and hybrid solution families are derived within a single coherent framework, leading to a broader and more systematically organized classification of bright, dark, kink, anti-kink, and singular wave profiles. More importantly, the contribution of this work extends beyond solution derivation. The reduced governing equation is reformulated as a planar dynamical system, allowing explicit identification of equilibrium points and qualitative phase-plane behavior. Furthermore, the long-term evolution of the system is quantitatively characterized through Lyapunov exponent analysis, enabling the detection of stable regimes and parameter intervals associated with sensitive dependence on initial conditions. While previous analytical studies have primarily concentrated on deriving exact wave solutions, the present work additionally incorporates Lyapunov exponent analysis and parameter sensitivity evaluation to quantitatively characterize the stability behavior of the system. Therefore, the novelty of the present research lies in establishing an integrated analytical-neural and dynamical analysis framework, which deepens the theoretical understanding of nonlinear excitations in the double-chain DNA model. Conclusions

In this study, a variety of new exact analytical soliton solutions for the Double-Chain DNA model are obtained by employing two neural network-supported analytical approaches, namely the GRMNN and the GREMM. The derived wave structures include bright, dark, kink, and anti-kink solitons, which broaden the existing solution space of the model and provide deeper insight into nonlinear excitations arising in DNA dynamics. To the best of our knowledge, the combined use of these two hybrid methodologies for the Double-Chain DNA system has not been reported previously, and thus the present results offer a novel perspective on the analytical treatment of nonlinear biomolecular wave models. The GREMM approach provides solutions through a classical reduction of the governing NPDE into an ODE framework, whereas the GRMNN technique constructs analytical trial functions directly from the original equation without requiring intermediate transformations. The extensive 3D, contour, and 2D visualizations presented in this work play a key role in confirming the validity and physical relevance of the derived soliton solutions. Moreover, the sensitivity and Lyapunov exponent figures provide further dynamical insight into the stability and parameter-dependent behavior of the Double-Chain DNA model. Overall, these findings confirm that hybrid analytical-neural frameworks can serve as efficient and reliable tools for generating exact solutions in complex nonlinear systems. Future research can extend the proposed methodologies to more generalized DNA models involving variable coefficients, external forcing terms, damping effects, or stochastic perturbations. In addition, investigating higher-dimensional versions of the model could reveal richer nonlinear wave interactions and more complex dynamical patterns. Another promising direction is the incorporation of fractional-order derivatives and memory effects, which can provide a more realistic description of DNA molecular processes. Finally, the present analytical results can also serve as a foundation for numerical simulations and experimental comparisons in nonlinear biophysics.

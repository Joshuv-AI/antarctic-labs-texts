From rough to refined: Applying diamond standards to CAR Treg development
Authors: Francesco W Hamilton, Leonardo MR Ferreira
Published: 2025

Main text

Just as diamonds are forged under pressure and refined through precision, chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) therapies are undergoing their own transformation. Shaping the ability of CAR therapies to become more powerful and refined has been a field of frequent development, with altering their design to increase potency, enhance persistence, and improve safety being the primary focus. CAR T cell therapies have been Food and Drug Administration approved for conditions where inflammation and a robust immune response are key for effective treatment, namely liquid cancers such as leukemia, lymphoma, and multiple myeloma.1 However, not all problems are solved by force. In conditions where overactive immunity causes harm, such as autoimmune diseases or graft-versus-host disease (GvHD), the immune system must be carefully assuaged not provoked. To address this gap, a new type of CAR therapy has emerged that uses the immunosuppressive regulatory T cell (Treg), anti-inflammatory CAR Treg therapy.2

Ever since the 1940s, precious gems, such as diamonds, have been characterized by a grading scale of four C’s: color, clarity, cut, and carat weight. To create a uniform metric of CAR Treg evaluation, we grade CAR Treg immunotherapies on an analogous metric, the four S’s: stability, specificity, survival, and suppression. In this issue, Tripathi and colleagues3 address these metrics toward the creation of a diamond standard CAR Treg therapy (Figure 1).

Diamond standards for chimeric antigen receptor regulatory T cells

The diamond standards to consider when designing chimeric antigen receptor regulatory T cell (CAR Treg) therapies, or 4S’s, are: stability, addressed here by knocking in a constitutive strong murine leukemia virus-derived promoter (MNDp) upstream of the FOXP3 gene; specificity, achieved here by utilizing an anti-HLA-A2 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR); survival, improved here by engineering Tregs with a chemically inducible signaling complex (CISC) that transduces IL-2 signaling intracellularly upon binding and heterodimerization by rapamycin or a rapamycin analog extracellularly; suppression, not directly modulated here but potentially augmented also via IL-2 CISC signaling.

A distinct feature of Treg identity is constitutive expression of FOXP3, a transcription factor key to the Treg lineage whose loss in extreme inflammatory environments leads to loss of Treg stability and function.4 To address this liability, the study uses homology-directed repair (HDR) to insert a constitutive MND promoter in the FOXP3 locus of bulk CD4+ T cells, effectively bypassing gene regulation by the Treg-specific demethylated region and enforcing FOXP3 expression. This creates engineered Tregs (“Eng Tregs”) with stable FOXP3 expression, protected against Treg instability. An additional advantage of this methodology resides in the fact that, while Tregs are rare, representing only 1 out of every 20 CD4+ T cells, here all CD4+ T cells can be utilized and converted into stable Eng Tregs. Much like the debate on manufactured versus natural diamonds, the authors show that, upon T cell receptor (TCR) activation, Eng Tregs express Treg markers (FOXP3, CTLA4, CD25, and ICOS) and suppress T cell proliferation at levels equivalent to those of polyclonal natural Tregs.

To address specificity, the authors introduced an HLA-2 CAR (“A2CAR”) using lentiviral transduction. Antigen specificity is key for Treg function; polyclonal Tregs have been shown in phase 2 clinical trials to be ineffective in ameliorating type 1 diabetes irrespective of dose.5 An A2CAR provides enhanced targeting of HLA-A2-positive organ transplants and GvHD-driving HLA-A2-positive blood cells transplanted into HLA-A2-negative patients.6,7 The authors demonstrate that A2CAR Eng Tregs are activated by HLA-A2-expressing cells (upregulation of CD69, CD71, and CD137), suppress T cell proliferation, and do not upregulate granzyme B or display cytotoxic activity.

Interleukin-2 (IL-2) is a precious stimulatory and survival signal for Tregs, as they do not produce it upon activation, unlike conventional T cells, forcing them to depend on exogenous IL-2 for survival. While many groups have experimented with low-dose IL-2 infusion to boost Tregs, they have had limited success partly due to the unwanted expansion of activated natural killer and T cell populations as a side effect.8 This study achieves a solution to this issue by knocking into the FOXP3 locus the components of what the authors call a “chemically inducible signaling complex” or CISC.9 The components are FKBP-IL2RG, FRB-IL2RB, and FRB. FKBP (FK506-binding protein) and FRB (FKBP-rapamycin binding) are subunits that dimerize upon binding to rapamycin and its analogs. IL-2RB and IL-2RG are the subunits of the IL-2 receptor (IL-2R). FRB serves as a scaffold so that the rapamycin analog can bring FKBP-IL2RG and FRB-IL2RB together in the correct orientation. CISC effectively mimics IL-2R activation upon infusion with rapamycin analogs, leading to STAT5 phosphorylation and ultimately Treg survival, proliferation, and stability. This strategy ensures A2CAR Eng Treg survival through infusion of rapamycin analogs rather than potentially inflammatory IL-2, maximizing safety and Treg longevity.

CISC utilization shines in vivo in a xenogeneic GvHD model where human HLA-A2-positive peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) are used to induce disease in NSG immunodeficient mice and co-infusion of CISC A2 CAR Eng Tregs is used at different doses to prevent disease. At the high 1:1 ratio of CISC A2CAR Eng Tregs to PBMCs, 100% of mice survive GvHD induction by 60 days. Going down to 1:20 CISC A2CAR Eng Tregs to PBMCs, suppression still occurs but only 40% of mice survive. This number goes up to 90% when mice are infused with 1:20 CISC A2CAR Eng Tregs to PBMCs and a rapamycin analog (AP21967), an almost 20-fold increase in CAR Treg potency. These results beg the question if the CISC creates a more persistent cell population (survival), adds additional immunosuppressive ability through IL-2 signaling10 (suppression), or a mixture of both.

While Treg stability, specificity, and survival are specifically addressed in this study, there is no strategy implemented to specifically augment Treg suppression. Evaluating in vitro suppression of T cell proliferation, polyclonal Eng Tregs, and A2CAR Eng Tregs is highly suppressive across Treg:T cell ratios when stimulated by their endogenous TCR, but CAR-activated A2CAR Eng Tregs are only marginally suppressive at low ratios (1:8 Treg:T cell). In addition, there is visible production of interferon-gamma by A2CAR Eng Tregs (though significantly lower than effector T cell controls), echoing recent reports.11 Here lies potential for future research to further engineer CAR Tregs to increase the therapy’s window of suppression. This could be accomplished by tailoring the CAR to Treg function via modifications in affinity or the signaling domain11 or by making “armored” CAR Tregs that secrete high levels of immunosuppressive IL-10 upon CAR activation12 (Table 1).

Enhancing the stability, specificity, survival, and suppression of Treg therapies

This study presents two potential mechanisms for enabling A2CAR expression: lentiviral transduction, which achieves easy yet random genomic integration, and HDR-based A2CAR knockin in the TRAC locus, disrupting endogenous TCR expression. Yet, in vivo Treg function is only assessed for lentiviral A2CAR Eng Tregs. Given that continuous TCR signaling is reported to be necessary for FOXP3 stability and Treg maintenance,20 TCR-positive A2CAR Eng Tregs may demonstrate superior persistence and longevity compared to TCR-null variants in vivo. Future work should provide side-by-side comparisons of A2CAR function in vivo when introduced in Tregs via lentiviral vs. HDR-based approaches to confirm that therapeutic performance is not compromised.

Much like a diamond is needed to cut a new diamond, a key tool in Tregs’ long-term success is infectious tolerance, wherein Tregs teach other cells to become Tregs, extending impact beyond their own lifespan. Recent work suggests that A2CAR Tregs can induce infectious tolerance,21 raising the question of whether the A2CAR Eng Tregs described in this study similarly transmit regulatory function to surrounding naive T cells. Moreover, given that GvHD is a systemic disease and polyclonal Tregs have shown efficacy in the treatment of GvHD in phase 2 clinical trials,22 future work evaluating A2CAR Eng Tregs should focus on solid organ transplantation, where antigen specificity and CAR Treg trafficking may be more significant.7,15,23

“Diamonds are forever” has been a phrase etched into history ever since the 1971 James Bond film of the same name. While Tregs are not international spies safeguarding state secrets, equally important is the idea of a persistent CAR Treg therapy that allows for life-long protection of patients suffering from GvHD, organ transplant rejection, or autoimmune disease. The work of Tripathi and colleagues3 is an important step forward in the creation of long-lasting potent CAR Tregs that could protect patients indefinitely.

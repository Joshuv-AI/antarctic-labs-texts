ARHGAP11A, a member of Rho GTPase activating protein family, as a prognostic biomarker linked to DNA damage response across pan-cancer
Authors: Ke Tan, Yun Wu, Jiaqi Zhang, Yuqiong Ding, Chen Cheng, Zhenyu Yan, Xuetong Wang, Liyuan Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Rho GTPase-activating protein (RhoGAP) family represents a large and diverse group of proteins that act as key regulators of Rho GTPases, small GTP-binding proteins involved in cellular signaling. Tight regulation of Rho GTPase activity is essential for fundamental biological processes, including cell motility, contractility, growth, differentiation, and development. Despite their biological importance, the roles of RhoGAPs in cancer remain largely undefined.MethodsBioinformatics analysis was performed using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), covering over 10,000 samples across 33 cancer types. The role of ARHGAP11A in the DNA damage response was evaluated through single-cell sequencing, western blotting, and colony formation assays.ResultsPan-cancer analysis of RhoGAP family genes identified ARHGAP11A as the most significantly overexpressed member in tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues. ARHGAP11A expression was found to be elevated in most cancer types and was associated with DNA repair activity. Furthermore, its expression positively correlated with tumor mutational burden (TMB), a recognized predictive biomarker for immunotherapy. Intriguingly, it also correlated with increased infiltration of regulatory T (Treg) cells, which are known to suppress anti-tumor immunity. Consistent with these observations, high ARHGAP11A expression was concurrently linked to poor patient survival outcomes across multiple cancers. We also observed a positive correlation between ARHGAP11A and CHK1, suggesting a functional collaboration. In vitro experiments further demonstrated that ARHGAP11A confers resistance to DNA damage induced by CHK1 inhibitors.ConclusionARHGAP11A is a promising prognostic marker in cancer, with potential therapeutic implications through targeting DNA repair pathways and modulating the tumor immune microenvironment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15106-8.Keywords: ARHGAP11A, DNA damge, Tumor mutational burden, Tumor immune microenvironment

Introduction

Rho GTPases are crucial regulators of cellular processes such as cytoskeletal dynamics, cell adhesion, migration, polarity, and cell cycle progression [1]. The Rho GTPase Activating Protein (GAP) family is a large, diverse group of proteins that serves as crucial negative regulators of Rho GTPases (small G-proteins) in cellular signal transduction [2, 3]. While the overall importance of RhoGAPs is recognized, their specific roles in cancer remain largely unexplored. Our investigation into RHOGAP family genes revealed that ARHGAP11A is consistently up-regulated across nearly all cancer types, indicating its potential significant involvement in tumorigenesis. ARHGAP11A was originally studied in the context of neuronal function and brain development. Recent study revealed ARHGAP11A plays a crucial role in radial glial cells (RGCs) by regulating their morphology and ensuring proper neuronal positioning during brain development [4]. Furthermore, ARHGAP11A has been implicated in Alzheimer’s disease, where it promotes Aβ production and neurotoxicity; reducing its levels has been shown to decrease plaque formation, neuronal damage, and improve cognitive function, positioning it as a potential therapeutic target [5].

Growing evidence also suggest that ARHGAP11A may act as a regulator of tumorigenesis. For instance, in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), high ARHGAP11A promote cell proliferation, invasion, and migration [6]. Similarly, in basal-like breast cancer (BLBC), ARHGAP11A and RacGAP1 are highly expressed, with their knockdown significantly impairing cell proliferation [7]. In clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), elevated ARHGAP11A levels correlate with shorter overall survival and enhance ccRCC cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration [8]. Despite these findings, the comprehensive clinical significance of ARHGAP11A, including its expression across diverse tumors, its impact on cancer survival rates, and tumor microenvironment remains to be fully elucidated. Moreover, its precise underlying mechanisms in tumorigenesis and potential therapeutic strategies require further understanding.

To address this question, we analyzed cancer databases to investigate the impact of ARHGAP11A on tumor mutation burden (TMB), the tumor immune microenvironment, and patient survival. Through analysis of data from the TCGA cancer database, we discovered that ARHGAP11A mRNA expression was consistently higher in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues across nearly all cancer types, with elevated ARHGAP11A levels associated with poor prognosis. Additionally, ARHGAP11A expression showed a positive correlation with TMB in several cancer types. To explore the role of ARHGAP11A in the tumor immune microenvironment, we examined its association with immune infiltration, finding that ARHGAP11A upregulation was positively correlated with immunosuppressive regulatory T cell (Treg) infiltration in almost all cancer types and negatively correlated with tumor immune scores in certain cancers. Further analysis of single-cell sequencing data revealed that high ARHGAP11A expression was linked to enrichment in DNA repair processes. ARHGAP11A expression also correlated with Chk1, a critical DNA repair kinase in pan-cancer. In vitro studies showed that ARHGAP11A involved in DNA damage induced by Chk1 inhibitors. Our findings provide evidence for ARHGAP11A’s role in modulating DNA repair response, influencing the tumor immune microenvironment, and affecting patient survival.

Materials and methods

mRNA expression and patient survival

mRNA expression data of 33 cancer types in tumor and normal samples from the TCGA pan-cancer atlas downloaded from UCSC Xena (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages) were used for analysis. Survival data were downloaded from TCGA Pan-Cancer (PANCAN) from UCSC Xena. Differential mRNA expression of ARHGAP11A was analyzed in normal and tumor tissues by Wilcox test and drawn by R package “ggpubr”. Patient survival analysis was performed by using GSCA database [9](https://guolab.wchscu.cn/GSCA/#/). ARHGAP11A mRNA expression data was merged with clinical survival data by sample barcode. Tumor samples were divided into high and low expression groups based on the median mRNA value. Using the R package survival, survival time and status were fitted for both groups. Cox Proportional-Hazards model and Logrank tests were conducted for each gene across all cancer types.

Correlation between gene expression and tumor mutational burden

The relationship between ARHGAP11A mRNA expression and Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient for each sample in the pan-cancer atlas. This analysis was conducted using the Assistant for Clinical Bioinformatics (ACLBI) database.

Correlation between ARHGAP11A GSVA score and immune infiltration

The GSVA score reflects ARHGAP11A expression levels and was calculated using the GSVA R package. The correlation between immune cell infiltration and gene set expression was assessed using Spearman correlation, with P-values adjusted by FDR. Correlation between ARHGAP11A GSVA score and immune infiltration was performed by GSCA database.

Gene expression analysis of scRNA-seq data

The correlation between ARHGAP11A expression and functional status across various cancers was analyzed using the CancerSEA database [10]. In cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC), ARHGAP11A expression was examined via CHARTS [11] (https://charts.morgridge.org/) using scRNA-seq data from GSE70630 and GSE144236. Gene set enrichment analysis was performed following the CancerSEA method.

Cell culture, transfection

HeLa and U2OS cells were cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. siRNA transfection was done using RNAi Max transfection reagent (Thermofish).siRNA ARHGAP11A#1:CUGGUGUCAAUAGAUAUGATT, #2:GGAGCCUGAUAUUAUGGUATT. ARHGAP11A antibody (Bethyl Laboratories, A303-97 A-T).

Clonogenic assay for measuring cell viability

HeLa cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 500 cells/well. Sixteen hours later, cells were treated with Chk1 inhibitor for 24 h. After a 10-day culture in fresh medium for colony formation, colonies were stained with 0.2% Crystal Violet in 50% methanol and counted.

Results

Upregulation of ARHGAP11A mRNA expression in cancers

We first examined the mRNA expression of 58 RhoGAP family genes [2] in cancers using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Our analysis revealed that, among the RhoGAP family, ARHGAP11A exhibited the most significant overexpression in tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1a). By comparing the mRNA levels of ARHGAP11A in tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues across pan-cancer, we found that ARHGAP11A was up-regulated in multiple types, including Bladder Urothelial Carcinoma (BLCA), Breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), Cervical squamous cell carcinoma (CESC), Cholangio carcinoma (CHOL), Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), Esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), Head and Neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), Kidney Chromophobe (KICH), Kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), Kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), Liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), Prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), Rectum adenocarcinoma (READ), Sarcoma (SARC), Stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), Thyroid carcinoma (THCA), and Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma (UCEC) (Fig. 1b).

Prognostic value of ARHGAP11A in cancer patients

ARHGAP11A is highly expressed in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues, suggesting its expression levels may hold prognostic value for cancer patients. Our results support this, showing that ARHGAP11A upregulation is linked to poorer survival rates in ACC, CHOL, KICH, KIRC, KIRP, LIHC, LUAD, Mesothelioma (MESO), and Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) (Fig. 2a and Fig. 2b).

Correlation between the mRNA expression of ARHGAP11A and the infiltrates of immune cells

ARHGAP11A is associated with poor prognosis in multiple cancers, potentially due to its impact on the immune environment. Using the “ImmuCellAI” method to analyze 24 immune cells across various cancers, we found a negative correlation between ARHGAP11A expression and immune infiltration scores in HNSC, CHOL, LUAD, SKCM, TGCT, ESCA, UVM, CESC, THYM, KIRP, PAAD, ACC, Pheochromocytoma and Paraganglioma (PCPG), LUSC, GBM, SARC, and KICH (Fig. 3a). Given that tumor mutational burden (TMB) is predictive for immunotherapy, we further explored its correlation with ARHGAP11A. ARHGAP11A showed a positive correlation with TMB in STAD, ACC, KICH, Brain Lower Grade Glioma (LGG), CHOL, BRCA, LUAD, PRAD, UCS, PAAD, SARC, SKCM, COAD, Acute Myeloid Leukemia (LAML), BLCA, UCEC, KIRC, DLBC, and PCPG (Fig. 3b).

ARHGAP11A highly expressed in cells with DNA repair pathway enrichment

To further explore the impact of ARHGAP11A overexpression in cancer cells and the mechanism through which ARHGAP11A remodels the immune microenvironment, we examined its function using scRNA-seq data from the CancerSEA and CHARTS databases. The CancerSEA database revealed that ARHGAP11A was particularly enriched in cells involved in cell cycle regulation, DNA damage, and DNA repair pathways, with notable expression in LUAD and HNSCC (Fig. 4a-c). We also analyzed scRNA-seq data from glioblastoma (GBM) (GSE70630) and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC) (GSE144236) samples in the CHARTS database to assess whether ARHGAP11A upregulation occurred in the same cell populations enriched in these pathways. As shown in Fig. 4d-g, ARHGAP11A was highly expressed in cells with DNA repair pathway enrichment in both GBM and cSCC samples.

CHK1 (Checkpoint Kinase 1), a key protein kinase in the DNA repair pathway, plays a critical role in maintaining genome integrity. We next investigated whether ARHGAP11A correlates with Chk1 expression. Notably, ARHGAP11A showed a strong correlation with Chk1 in nearly all cancer types, suggesting a functional collaboration between ARHGAP11A and Chk1 in sustaining genome instability (Fig. 5).

Knocking down ARHGAP11A increases sensitivity to Chk1 inhibitor

Cancer database analysis suggests that ARHGAP11A may play a role in the DNA damage response. To investigate this, we used Chk1 inhibitors to induce DNA damage. In cells with ARHGAP11A knocked down, inhibition of Chk1 by Prexasertib and AZD7762 led to increased DNA damage, as indicated by elevated ATR-mediated Chk1 phosphorylation at S345 (Fig. 6a-c). Additionally, the colony formation assay showed that ARHGAP11A knockdown cells were sensitive to Chk1 inhibitor (Fig. 6d). These findings suggest that ARHGAP11A can be targeted by Chk1 inhibitor to enhance DNA damage response.

Discussion

Our study demonstrated that ARHGAP11A was overexpressed in the majority of tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues across pan-cancer (Fig. 1). Additionally, high expression of ARHGAP11A was associated with poor prognosis (Fig. 2). Notably, we observed a positive correlation between the upregulation of ARHGAP11A and tumor infiltration of immunosuppressive Tregs, as well as tumor mutational burden (TMB) across pan-cancer (Fig. 3a and b). Furthermore, single-cell RNA sequencing results showed that ARHGAP11A was enriched in cells with a DNA repair response (Figs. 4a and b and 6). ARHGAP11A expression also correlated with Chk1, a critical DNA repair kinase in pan-cancer (Fig. 5). Additionally, Knocking down ARHGAP11A was found to sensitive to Chk1 inhibitor (Fig. 6). Based on these findings, we propose that ARHGAP11A hyperactivity promotes cancer progression by inducing an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and maintaining genomic stability, making it a potential target for therapeutic interventions.

ARHGAP11A’s correlation with Treg cells positions it as a potential target for tumor immunotherapy. Recent advances in tumor immunotherapy have identified Treg-specific molecules, such as CTLA-4, PD-1, GITR, and others, as promising candidates for depletion or functional modulation [12].

TMB has recently emerged as predictive markers for immunotherapy, including anti-PD-1/CTLA-4 therapies [13–15]. Pembrolizumab, targeting PD-1, is FDA-approved for treating adults and pediatric patients with a tumor mutational burden (TMB) ≥ 10 [16, 17]. Additionally, TMB is positively associated with clinical response to anti-PD(L)1, anti–CTLA-4, and combination ICIs [18]. Notably, ARHGAP11A expression has been found to be positively correlated with TMB in a variety of tumors, suggesting that ARHGAP11A may influence mutational burden and contribute to tumor behavior, potentially affecting the response to immunotherapy.

ARHGAP11A also protects tumor cells from DNA damage. Tumors with high ARHGAP11A expression are associated with an immunosuppressive environment, potentially due to the DNA damage within the tumor cells themselves. This DNA damage leads to genomic instability, triggering the cGAS/STING pathway [19], which results in chronic cytokines production and further reinforces the immunosuppressive microenvironment [20]. In such cases, the application of chemotherapy, which induces severe DNA damage, could strongly activate the cGAS/STING pathway, leading to the release of high levels of type I interferons and the activation of antitumor immune responses [21].

CHK1 plays key roles in cell cycle checkpoints and DNA repair, is also increasingly recognized as a multifunctional protein involved in cancer growth and therapy resistance. Understanding CHK1’s diverse functions is essential for validating it as an anti-cancer target and developing effective combination strategies [22]. CHK1 inhibitors, such as prexasertib, induce excessive DNA replication and metabolic stress, helping to overcome chemoresistance in ovarian cancer [23, 24]. Clinical trials have evaluated prexasertib in combination with the PARP inhibitor olaparib in patients with high-grade serous ovarian cancer and other solid tumors [23], as well as in a Phase 2 trial for BRCA wild-type, platinum-resistant recurrent high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma [25]. Phase 1/2 trials of SRA737 (another CHK1 inhibitor) have shown promising results both as a monotherapy and in combination with low-dose gemcitabine in advanced cancer [25, 26]. Moreover, BLM overexpression has been identified as a potential predictive biomarker for CHK1 inhibitor response in PARP inhibitor–resistant BRCA-mutant ovarian cancer [27]. Combining PARP and CHK1 inhibitors has also proven effective in MYCN-driven tumors [28]. Given ARHGAP11A’s positive correlation with Chk1 and its ability to protect cancer cells from Chk1 inhibitors, ARHGAP11A may serve as a promising biomarker for predicting responses to CHK1 inhibition.

Therefore, strategies targeting the DNA repair response, TMB and Tregs could be effective in treating cancers with expression of ARHGAP11A.

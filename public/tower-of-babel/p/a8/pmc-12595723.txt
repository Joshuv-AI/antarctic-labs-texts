Correlation between 25-hydroxyvitamin D, N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide, inflammatory factors and myocardial injury in children with mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia
Authors: Yingjie Liu, Ping Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe purpose of this study was to examine the relationship between myocardial injury in children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) and changes in serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25-(OH)-D), N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and related inflammatory factors, including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP).MethodsThe study included 300 children with MPP who were hospitalized to Zhangye People’s Hospital, affiliated with Hexi College, between January 2023 and December 2023. Children with myocardial injury were assigned to Group A, while those without were assigned to Group B. The two groups’ levels of NT-proBNP, 25-(OH)-D, and associated inflammatory markers were measured and compared.ResultsPearson correlation analysis confirmed the association between myocardial damage and alterations in 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and associated inflammatory markers in children with MPP. Group A children had lower 25-(OH)-D levels than Group B children (p < 0.05), while Group B children had significantly greater levels of NT-proBNP, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and hs-CRP (p < 0.05). The results of Pearson correlation analysis revealed that the occurrence of myocardial injury in MPP patients could be associated with the levels of 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and hs-CRP.ConclusionIt can be concluded that MPP patients may experience myocardial injury when 25-(OH)-D levels decrease and NT-proBNP and related inflammatory factors increase. Therefore, changes in the levels of these indicators should be taken into account when carrying out clinical treatment.

Background

Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is an acute inflammatory disease of the lungs caused by Mycoplasma pneumonia (MP) infection. It is highly contagious and can be transmitted by droplet and direct contact [1]. Especially in children, the probability of developing the disease is generally higher than in adults due to autoimmune deficiencies. Statistically [2], MPP accounts for about 10% to 33% of all pneumonias, and 10% to 60% of them belong to the pediatric population. The disease gradually manifests itself with symptoms such as fatigue, fever, and cough after 1 ~ 3 weeks of onset [3]. Moreover, as the disease worsens, the inflammatory response of MP may spread to the heart, inducing inflammation of the heart muscle. This can lead to cardiac damage and serious consequences such as cardiac arrhythmias, circulatory disturbances, and heart failure, which can threaten the life of the child [4]. Therefore, investigating the mechanism of myocardial damage and early warning signs in children with MPP is crucial from a therapeutic standpoint in order to prevent and treat the condition.

Immunomodulation, physiologic stress, and inflammatory stimuli all play important roles during MPP disease [5]. Among them, one of the main circulating forms of vitamin D (VD) and a crucial indication for immune modulation and anti-infection processes is 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25-(OH)-D] [6]. It is typically low in children with MPP, and low levels of 25-(OH)-D impair immunological function, which raises the risk of MPP [7]. Whereas N-terminal pro brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) is a typical cardiac stress marker, it is often used as a measurement for the assessment of cardiac function and severity of heart failure [8]. In conjunction with previous clinical cases, it can be found [9] that MPP myocardial injury may lead to a decrease in cardiac function due to damage to cardiomyocytes, which in turn causes an increase in NT-proBNP levels.

In addition, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) are typical markers of inflammatory factors, which contribute significantly to the inflammatory process [10]. Their expression levels are usually elevated in children with MPP. This is a biological defense mechanism of the body against foreign pathogens, and also reflects the intensity of inflammation in children [11]. Among them, elevated expression of TNF-α reflects a strong immune system response to infection. High levels of TNF-α may be associated with the severity and prognosis of the disease course [12], while elevated IL-1β may contribute to the development of systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) [13]. IL-6 is a cytokine with many uses. The powerful immunological response brought on by infection is shown in the significant rise in IL-6, which is correlated with the kind and duration of infection [14]. In addition, hs-CRP is an acute phase protein, which shows rapid elevation in inflammatory response [15].

However, few studies have demonstrated the clear mechanism of serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and related inflammatory factors in inducing myocardial injury in children with MPP. In light of this, this study combines the pertinent pathology findings with a retrospective analysis of the clinical case data of 300 children with MPP who were hospitalized between January 2023 and December 2023. Combined with the relevant pathological findings, the correlation between the changes in the levels of serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and related inflammatory factors and myocardial injury in children with MPP is analyzed in depth. The correlation between the above indicators and myocardial injury in children with MPP should be explored to provide new ideas for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

Methods

General information

The 300 children with MPP admitted to Zhangye People’s Hospital affiliated to Hexi College were included from January 2023 to December 2023 as the study subjects. All diagnoses of the children met the MPP criteria provided in the 2020 SATS Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Community Acquired Pneumonia in Children. The diagnostic process involved combining various types of information, such as clinical manifestations, imaging examinations, serological testing, and molecular testing, including PCR technology for detecting Mycoplasma pneumoniae nucleic acid. After the preliminary screening, two cases were excluded due to serum sample failure. The final sample size included in the analysis was 298. In addition, 300 children with MPP were included in Group A (GA, n = 106) if they showed myocardial injury through electrocardiogram (ECG), serum biochemistry, and cardiac ultrasound. Those who did not show myocardial injury were included in Group B (GB, n = 192).

Inclusion criteria

(a) Children in both groups met the criteria for MPP determination provided in the 2020 SATS Guidelines: Diagnosis and Management Of Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Children [16]. (b) Children in GA were diagnosed with MPP with myocardial injury by ST-segment changes, T-wave inversion, or QT interval prolongation on ECG, cardiac troponin >0.2 µg/L, and creatine kinase isoenzyme (CK-MB) >25.0 U/L. (c) The age of the subject children ranged from 1 to 6 years. (d) Medical records were complete. (e) The family of the subject child could actively cooperate.

Exclusion criteria

(a) Combination of respiratory diseases. (b) Combination of other immune dysfunctions. (c) History of congenital mental illness. (d) Combination of congenital heart disease.

Removal criteria

(a) Invalid serum sample. (b) Incorrect information collection.

Diagnostic criteria for myocardial injury

(a) Two independent cardiovascular physicians performed blind interpretation. The criteria were ST segment depression or elevation of at least 0.1 mV, T wave inversion in at least two leads, or a QTc interval exceeding 440 ms. (b) Left ventricular ejection fraction was below 55%, or there was abnormal wall motion. (c) The cardiac troponin I level was higher than 0.2 µg/L (using the ARCHITECT i2000SR detector), and the creatine kinase isoenzyme level was higher than 25.0 U/L (using the rate method).

Research methods

Survey tools

Children’s serum biochemical indicators collection form developed by the hospital, including 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and hs-CRP.

Measurement of serum biochemical indicators

Five milliliters of blood drawn from children’s blood vessels during routine blood draws served as the test sample. Sample processing method: 3200r/min, 10 cm centrifugal radius, and the processing time was 10 min to obtain the serum. Storage method: −20℃. 25-(OH)-D was detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using a kit (E-EL-H0109c, Elabscience) with a detection sensitivity of 0.5 nmol/L. The intra-batch coefficient of variation (CV) was less than 5%, and the inter-batch CV was less than 8%. NT-proBNP was detected using an enzyme immunoassay (EIA) kit (Roche, 1177767322) with a sensitivity of 5 ng/L. The intra-batch CV was less than 4%, and the inter-batch CV was less than 6%. Inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and hs-CRP) were detected using an immunoassay on the Siemens ADVIA Centaur XP platform. The sensitivity of hs CRP detection was 0.1 mg/L, and the sensitivity of cytokine detection was less than 1 pg/mL. The intra - and inter assay CV were both less than 7%.

The threshold setting was based on: (a) According to the guideline consensus, NT-proBNP levels above 300 ng/L were diagnostic criteria for pediatric heart failure (refer to the 2020 AHA guidelines for more information). (b) A pre-experimental ROC analysis was conducted to determine the optimal cutoff values for 25-(OH)-D (not exceeding 58 nmol/L, with an AUC of 0.76) and hs-CRP (not less than 5.5 mg/L, with an AUC of 0.82), based on 50 samples in the early stage.

Methodology of the survey

This study used a self-designed “Collection Table for Serum Biochemical Indicators in Children” to systematically collect serum biochemical indicators from children. The study also validated the use of techniques such as the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), the enzyme immunoassay (EIA), and the immunoassay to measure the indicators. The investigation team consisted of 2 pediatricians with more than 5 years of experience and 3 nurses who received unified professional training. Together, they completed serum biochemical index determinations, filled out collection forms, and performed related testing operations. The completed collection forms were collected on the spot, checked for details, and signed by the person responsible for completing the form. The survey had a 100% response rate (298 out of 298), and all of the samples included in the analysis provided complete and valid data.

Statistical methods

SPSS 26.0 was utilized to handle and analyze the data. To check if the measurement data in this investigation followed a normal distribution, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was employed. Correlation analysis of serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and related inflammatory factor levels with myocardial injury in children with MPP

Multivariate analysis results

Binary logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the independent associations of each serum marker with myocardial injury, after adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, BMI, and MPP duration. Logistic regression showed that after adjusting for age, BMI, and disease duration, a decrease in 25-(OH)-D (OR = 0.82, 95% CI: 0.75–0.90), an increase in NT-proBNP (OR = 1.03, 95% CI: 1.01–1.05), and an increase in IL-6 (OR = 1.48, 95% CI: 1.22–1.79) were independently associated with myocardial injury (p < 0.001). Discussion

In recent years, the number of children with MPP has increased, as has the risk of MPP-related myocardial injury. To analyze the pathophysiology of MPP and to develop more precise and efficient diagnostic and treatment methods, it is crucial to investigate the relationship between serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and associated inflammatory variables and MPP myocardial injury in children. 25-(OH)-D, the primary circulating form of VD in the human body, is crucial for preserving healthy bones and appropriate calcium and phosphorus metabolism [17]. However, with the increasing research in China, several studies have confirmed that VD is not only involved in bone health, but also may be involved in the formation and development of some non-skeletal diseases [18], such as cardiovascular diseases, autoimmune diseases, and infectious diseases [19]. In addition, NT-proBNP is a regular cardiac hormone whose levels tend to increase when the heart is stressed or injured [20]. Moreover, inflammatory mediators have mechanisms that promote inflammation, modulate immune responses, and trigger tissue damage during MPP disease [21]. Therefore, examining the relationship between myocardial injury and alterations in serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and associated inflammatory factor levels in children with MPP is crucial for determining the degree of cardiac damage, tracking the efficacy of treatment, and learning more about the immunological mechanisms underlying the disease process. For clinical management and treatment, it can offer more precise therapeutic and diagnostic data. Additionally, it lowers the risk of cardiac damage, advances the understanding of pediatric MPP, and offers a solid scientific foundation for the creation of new treatment approaches.

Through this study, it was found that the occurrence of myocardial injury in MPP patients may be associated with 25-(OH)-D levels. When 25-(OH)-D levels reduced in children with MPP, the risk of myocardial injury was higher. Conversely, when myocardial injury occurred in children with MPP, their 25-(OH)-D levels were generally low. This was attributed to the fact that, under normal conditions, VD had immunomodulatory effects and it could influence the differentiation and function of immune cells [22]. Whereas, lower levels of 25-(OH)-D could lead to an imbalance in immunomodulation and abnormal calcium and phosphorus metabolism [23], making the inflammatory response in children with MPP increased at the same time. This could affect the normal function and microenvironmental structure of cardiomyocytes, further damaging them [24]. Whereas NT-proBNP was the precursor of B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) [25], it was synthesized and secreted mainly by ventricular myocytes. In other cardiovascular conditions such heart failure and myocardial damage, NT-proBNP levels were noticeably higher [26]. As for when children with MPP were at risk of myocardial injury, the synthesis and release of NT-proBNP was increased as a way to increase the secretion of natriuretic peptides and reduce the cardiac burden [27]. In addition, in the state of myocardial injury, activation of the sympathetic nervous system, and activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) further drove NT-proBNP synthesis and release [28]. It also promoted physiological stress responses such as vasoconstriction and sodium retention, which further aggravated the cardiac burden [29]. Therefore, when NT-proBNP levels were elevated, it could signal the creation of a myocardial injury situation.

In addition, this study once more discovered aNC between TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and hs-CRP levels and whether or not cardiac damage occurred in children with MPP. This laterally indicated that when serum inflammatory mediators were elevated in children with MPP, the formation of myocardial injury could be accelerated. The immune system would be highly activated in the condition of MPP, and the immune cells would release a lot of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and other chemicals [30]. This strong inflammatory response could lead to an imbalance in immune regulation [31], which in turn could trigger damage and apoptosis of cardiomyocytes [32]. In addition, TNF-α in it could activate neutrophils as well as alter the properties of vascular endothelial cells [33], further exacerbating myocardial injury. Whereas IL-1β was a cytokine belonging to the interleukin-1 family [34]. In children with MPP, elevated levels of IL-1β would directly act on cardiomyocytes while driving the stimulation of the production and release of other inflammatory factors [35]. This could exacerbate the degree of myocardial damage and trigger cardiac dysfunction. Second, IL-6 was a chemokine. High levels of IL-6 would stimulate the proliferation of activated B cells [36] and secretion of large amounts of antibodies that stimulate hepatocytes to synthesize acute phase proteins [37]. This could induce an increased inflammatory response in cardiomyocytes and promote the development of myocardial injury. In addition, hs-CRP was an acute-phase response protein synthesized by hepatocytes when the organism was subjected to severe stimuli such as microbial invasion or tissue injury [38]. Elevated hs-CRP levels in children with MPP were a clear indicator of tissue injury and a significant inflammatory response [39]. High levels of hs-CRP could indirectly exacerbate myocardial injury by affecting the expression and secretion of other biomarkers [40].

Conclusion

This study found that a decrease in 25-(OH)-D levels and an increase in NT-proBNP and related inflammatory cytokine levels could be associated with myocardial injury in children with MPP. However, there are still some limitations in this study, such as the selection of samples may have some bias, resulting in the results of the study are not representative enough. Second, a static study might not be able to capture the dynamic changes in the aforementioned variables at different stages of MPP. Time-series studies are needed to more fully understand the changes in the levels of 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and related inflammatory factors in children with MPP. Therefore, the biological relevance of these relationships, including the modulation of other immune cells and secreted immunomodulatory substances, requires further experimental and clinical research. Comprehensive analysis of the relevant biomarkers will help to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the interaction of changes in serum 25-(OH)-D, NT-proBNP, and related inflammatory factor levels with myocardial injury in children with MPP.

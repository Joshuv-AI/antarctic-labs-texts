AI is not replacing engineers: It is empowering them
Authors: Tsu-Jae Liu
Published: 2026

Engineering has always been about improving lives and shaping a better future. Today, artificial intelligence (AI) is augmenting the ability of engineers to create new solutions and to advance technology for the benefit of humanity. At the National Academy of Engineering (NAE), we see AI as an intelligent assistant that empowers engineers to be more creative, more efficient, and more impactful than ever before.

Tsu-Jae Liu. Image credit: Christopher Michel (photographer).

Across industries, AI is accelerating engineering progress by allowing engineers to focus less on routine tasks and more on creative problem solving and innovation. Intelligent design tools enable engineers to test their ideas in hours instead of months. AI-driven simulations help ensure infrastructure safety, optimize energy systems, reduce waste in manufacturing, and advance medical discoveries and technologies that improve quality of life.

AI is also making the engineering profession more accessible and inclusive. Students and early-career professionals can learn more quickly with personalized feedback and by iteratively ideating, building, and evaluating their designs virtually.

As AI empowers engineers, it also increases their responsibility. To safeguard humanity, engineers must follow human-centered principles in developing and using AI. At the NAE, we emphasize that technology advancement must go hand in hand with ethical and responsible leadership. Engineers are uniquely positioned to ensure AI systems are reliable, transparent, and aligned with human values.

Preparing future engineers for success in the era of AI begins with rethinking how we educate students. Engineering education must become more student centered and multidisciplinary. In addition to building a strong foundation of knowledge in math, science, and core engineering concepts, students should learn how to use AI as a tool to strengthen design thinking, analysis, and problem solving. Equally important, they must understand the limitations of AI and the legal and ethical implications of their work. Tomorrow's engineers should have not only technical skills, but also societal awareness and responsibility.

Collaboration across work sectors is essential to meeting the nation's growing need for engineering talent. As shown in Fig. 1, demand for new engineers has consistently outpaced supply for more than two decades, and projections indicate that the gap will continue to widen through 2030. According to analysis by Boston Consulting Group and SAE International, the United States will need roughly 400,000 new engineers each year, with shortages expected to persist well into the next decade. This imbalance is especially pronounced in rapidly evolving areas such as AI and machine learning. Addressing it will require employers to take a more active role in workforce development by partnering with educators and creating flexible pathways for early-career professionals to build new skills as technologies change. A strong engineering workforce is built through sustained investment in training, opportunity, and inclusion, ensuring that innovation keeps pace with demand and continues to serve the best interests of society.

By empowering engineers to work more creatively and efficiently, AI expands their capacity to build a safer, healthier, and more sustainable future for our nation and the world. That promise, and the responsibility that comes with it, is central to the NAE’s mission.

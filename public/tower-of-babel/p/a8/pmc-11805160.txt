Implementation of Case-Based Learning in 1st-Year MBBS Students -Future of Medical Professionals Learning Methods in Indian Medical Colleges?
Authors: Mandar Vilas Ambike, Jayshree Sachin Kharche, Shraddha Shripad Kulkarni, T Vijaya Sagar, K Vijayakumar, Suresh Babu Kondaveeti
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Case Based Learning (CBL) is the best method for correlation of knowledge with clinical scenarios. The present study is planned to implement case-based learning in 1st-Year MBBS students and find out the perception of the students as a guideline to incorporate this activity in the curriculum.Material and Methods:The study was conducted among 1st-Year MBBS students. The students who consented to participate were divided into 23 groups each containing 5-6 participants. The case along with the reference material was allotted to the groups of students sequentially. Faculty was allotted to each group of students for their help and discussion on the given case. The completed activity was uploaded on Moodle for all the students to go through. After 25 weeks, students’ perception of the activity were obtained with the help of validated questionnaire through Google Forms. Data was analyzed in SPSS software.Results:85% of students felt that CBL helped them understand clinical applications, enhanced problem-solving skills and retention of knowledge. More than 85% of students were comfortable with CBL in terms of relevance to the curriculum, mode of conduction, and development of interest. Over 90% of students mentioned that provided resources and faculty were helpful.Conclusion:The study concludes that successful implementation of case-based learning is possible for 1st-Year MBBS medical students. Students perceived CBL as a motivating and effective teaching-learning method.

INTRODUCTION

With the evolution of the medical curriculum and the emergence of new technologies, it has become increasingly difficult for instructors to pique undergraduate medical students’ interest in completing the learning objectives successfully.[1] In addition, there is an immense need to teach problem-solving, critical thinking, and reasoning, and other skills, which have become a must attribute for a successful doctor.[12]

CBL focuses on teaching subject content and at the same time engages the students in real-life case scenarios.[12] CBL is the best method for the correlation of knowledge with clinical scenarios. CBL can offer a better understanding of the students and may offer more readiness for the clinical postings. In the new Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) curriculum, Early Clinical Exposure, AETCOM modules are added, which can be complemented by the inclusion of CBL into the curriculum. Therefore, the present study aimed to implement case-based learning in 1st-Year MBBS students and find out the students’ perception of a guideline to incorporate this activity in the curriculum.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

The study was conducted in a medical college in western Maharashtra. Institutional ethics committee was obtained for the study. The study population was 150 students studying in 1st-Year MBBS batch 2020-21. Inclusion Criteria were 1st-Year MBBS students who had given informed consent and those students not willing to participate were excluded from the study. Out of 150 students, 133 students were included in the study.

A detailed case was prepared by all the faculties from clinical subjects each Thursday of the week on current topics or the latest topics covered in the syllabus. The topic for the case allotted to the student was aligned with the theory classes covered in 1st-Year MBBS subjects.

A total of 23 groups of students, each 5 to 6 students were formed according to roll number. Each group was given the case sequentially. Students were provided with resources to be refereed like e-books, e-contents and books from the institutional library. Also, depending on the topic, students were provided with resource persons for the case. (e.g., Case of Fracture – resource person – Orthopedic surgeon and Radiologist). They were given a week’s time to work on it.

Students were instructed to contact the allocated resource person if they need any help.

Once students completed the task faculty had one-to-one discussions with the students till they were satisfied. Once the activity was complete it was uploaded on Moodle for all the students to go through. In this manner a total of 23 clinical cases were allotted to the group of students sequentially. After 25 weeks students’ perception of the activity was obtained with the help of validated questionnaire. Through a Google form, a questionnaire was disseminated to participants. Systematically collected and documented data was subsequently analyzed using version 20 of Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS).

OBSERVATION AND RESULTS

A total of 133 first MBBS students participated in the study. The questions were analyzed for Perceptions of the students about the benefits of CBL, feedback about the CBL method, and feedback about support from faculty and resources.

As mentioned in Table 1, most students in the study gave positive feedback about case-based learning. More than 90% Students mentioned that CBL helped them to better understand basic sciences, knowledge retention and problem-solving skills. More than 95% Students were comfortable with CBL in terms of relevance to the curriculum, mode of conduction and development of interest. Also, students didn’t feel it as extra burden and enjoyed the feel of doctor as were introduced to case.

DISCUSSION

Majority of students mentioned that CBL helped them better understand basic sciences, knowledge retention, and problem-solving skills. Similar results were revealed by research by Annadani and Undi,[3] who found that case-based learning stimulated student interest in learning and promoted teamwork and group learning. CBL promoted self-directed learning and aided in applying information to clinical practice.

Students were comfortable with CBL in terms of relevance to the curriculum, mode of conduction, and development of interest. According to research by Nair et al.,[4] CBL encouraged students to learn and made the learning process more fun when compared to other teaching strategies.

85% of the students were satisfied with the role played by the faculty, like clear instructions, helpful and approachable. Resources were useful and the Learning Management System (LMS) was easy to operate for implementation. Ma et al.[5] had similar findings. Features of CBL appreciated by students like interesting ways, practical application of knowledge, enhancement, and exploration of core aspects of the topic, learning by doing which helps to develop problem-solving skills, integration with clinical subjects, interaction with patients and healthcare team. Seshan et al.,[6] mentioned that CBL helps to enhance critical thinking ability along with better communication skills, enhanced team building and better presentation skills. CBL contributed to deeper understanding of content and reduced the gap between theory and practice. Emphasis on applicative teaching rather than the old way of didactic teaching will be the future of medical education[7] and it was observed that most of the universities emphasized case-based learning.[8]

CONCLUSION

The study concludes that the implementation of case-based learning for 1st-Year MBBS medical students was successful. Students perceived CBL as a motivating and effective teaching learning method. Students perceived that the use of LMS for CBL was user-friendly.

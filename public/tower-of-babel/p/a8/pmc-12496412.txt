Commentary on “Real-world use of an artificial intelligence–powered clinical decision support tool for ovarian stimulation”
Authors: Rishita Ojha
Published: 2025

I read with interest the recent article published in F&S Reports and was encouraged by the work of Bixby and Miller (1), who explored the integration of an artificial intelligence (AI)–powered clinical decision support tool (CDST) for guiding ovarian stimulation in a real-world in vitro fertilization treatment setting. The implementation of such tools in live clinical environments represents a critical transition from research to impact, and this study presents an important precedent. However, I wish to highlight several areas where AI design, evaluation, and future deployment could be strengthened or more clearly articulated, particularly when this tool is to serve as a scalable and trustworthy model for reproductive healthcare.

Although the article briefly describes that the CDST is based on a proprietary, trained neural network model, no technical details regarding its architecture, feature selection, training-validation split, regularization, or performance metrics (e.g., mean squared error, overfitting prevention strategies) are shared. In the context of high-stakes medical applications, model transparency is critical not only to assess validity but also to allow external auditing, replication, and refinement (2). Publishing at least the skeleton of the algorithmic framework (e.g., feedforward network vs. ensemble, number of layers, data distribution) is essential to bridge the gap between reproducibility and clinical adoption.

The investigators mentioned the integration of the CDST into clinical workflow but do not describe how the tool communicates its predictions or recommendations to physicians. Explainable AI is a vital component for clinician trust, especially in dose-related decisions. Studies have shown that clinicians are more likely to trust AI when the model can offer interpretable justifications, such as feature importance, local attribution scores (e.g., SHapley Additive exPlanations values), or counterfactual reasoning (3). For future iterations, embedding interpretability alongside predictions would enhance both clinical uptake and medicolegal defensibility.

The investigators rightfully acknowledged the limitations of single-center deployment. However, from an AI perspective, the more pressing concern is the absence of validation across external populations, particularly with demographic variability. Artificial intelligence models are susceptible to data drift when deployed outside their training environment, and performance can degrade silently over time (4). Future work should include ongoing monitoring mechanisms and validation in geographically and ethnically diverse populations to ensure model robustness under real-world heterogeneity.

One of the most exciting aspects of clinical AI is its potential to augment, not replace, human expertise. However, the study does not report how often clinicians overrode or modified the CDST recommendations, nor does it analyze whether such modifications improved or worsened outcomes. This feedback loop between human and AI decision-making is central to the evolution of human-in-the-loop learning systems (5). Future studies might consider embedding clinician feedback to allow dynamic learning and iterative refinement of the model.

Finally, given the data sensitivity in reproductive medicine, it would be valuable to explore federated learning frameworks, allowing multiple centers to collaboratively train AI models without sharing raw patient data. Such architectures support both privacy preservation and scalability, core requirements for global clinical AI applications.

Conclusion

In conclusion, Bixby and Miller’s (1) study marks a meaningful advancement in applied reproductive AI. However, the long-term viability and trust in such systems will depend on transparent architecture, explainability, ongoing validation, and human-AI synergy. I look forward to future reports from this group or others that extend this foundational work with these considerations in mind.

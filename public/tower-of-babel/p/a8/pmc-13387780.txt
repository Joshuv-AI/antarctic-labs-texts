Causal effects among hypertension, osteoporosis, and metabolites: A Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Yiguo Chen, Wei Wang, Xidong Xu, Xiaohua Huang, Bing Wei, Hongliang Jin, Qiyuan Pan, Yang Yang, Zhangyu Wang, Chao Feng, Li Chen, Zhijun Zhao, Yao Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Studies have examined the connection between blood metabolites, osteoporosis (OP), and hypertension; however, there is not enough data to pinpoint their precise cause.Methods:OP and associated traits served as outcomes, with hypertension serving as the exposure variable. The main methodology was the random-effects inverse variance weighted (IVW) technique. Horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity of outcomes were assessed by sensitivity testing. Finally, to find putative mediating metabolites, a two-step Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was carried out.Results:IVW analysis revealed a substantial negative connection between hypertension and lumbar spine bone mineral density (odds ratio [OR] = 0.961, 95% CI: 0.927-0.997; p < 0.05) and heel bone mineral density (OR = 0.982, 95% CI: 0.971-0.992; p < 0.001), as well as a strong positive correlation with OP (OR = 1.165, 95% CI: 1.067-1.273, p < 0.001). Sensitivity analysis validated the robustness of the results. The blood metabolites (R)-3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine, 1-(1-enyl-palmitoyl)-GPE (p-16:0), hydroxyasparagine, adenosine 5’-diphosphate (ADP) to 5-oxoproline ratio, sphinganine-1-phosphate levels, and sphingosine-1-phosphate levels showed preliminary suggestive mediating effects on the association between hypertension and decreased bone mineral density in statistics.Conclusion:Our study revealed a positive correlation between hypertension and OP and suggested that blood metabolites may play a mediating role in it. These findings provide clues for a deeper understanding of the potential mechanisms between hypertension and bone health and lay a scientific foundation for exploring targeted intervention strategies in the future.

1. INTRODUCTION

The prevalent systemic, chronic metabolic bone disease known as osteoporosis (OP) is characterized by a decline in bone mineral density (BMD) and degradation of the microstructure of the bone, which increases the risk of fractures and causes the bone to become more fragile.1 Nowadays, it has become a significant global public health concern. Over the last three decades, 41.5 million instances of OP have been reported worldwide. It is predicted that in the upcoming 10 years, there will be 263.2 million cases.2 As a result, it is critical to determine the cause of OP and implement early preventative measures for tube insertion.

According to available data, age, sex hormones, and vitamin D consumption are just a few of the numerous risk factors for OP.3,4 A growing body of epidemiological research points to a possible link between OP and hypertension.5,6 A meta-analysis showed that hypertensive patients have a significantly higher risk of low BMD compared with nonhypertensive patients, and this association is more evident in patients receiving antihypertensive treatment.7 However, it is worth noting that another research discovered no link with femoral neck BMD but a favorable correlation with hypertension and lumbar spine BMD.8 To pinpoint the precise causal association among hypertension, OP, and BMD, more investigations are necessary. The relationships are still contentious.

OP is characterized by improper metabolism of bone tissue. The gold standard for diagnosing the condition is the identification of changes in BMD using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry9; however, its clinical use is limited because it is unable to assess the pathological process. Nonetheless, recently developed blood metabolomics, which is utilized to look for early diagnostic biomarkers of OP, can detect the end products of organism metabolism.10,11 Additionally, studies have demonstrated the critical roles that pathways, including fatty acid metabolism, glycerol phospholipid metabolism, and amino acid metabolism, play in the initiation and development of hypertension.12,13 OP and hypertension may share metabolically oriented pathogenic mechanisms.

By allocating genetic alleles at random, the Mendelian randomization (MR) approach removed the impact of reverse causation and unknown confounding factors and reduced measurement errors. Of these benefits, genetic variations were used as instrumental variables (IVs). The purpose of this study was to first examine the causative association between OP and hypertension using a two-sample MR (SMR). Using mediation analysis, the study analyzed the function of metabolites in moderating this relationship. This might offer novel perspectives on the etiology and mitigation of hypertension in combination with OP.

2. METHODS

2.1. Research design

Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) linked to exposures and outcomes are used as IVs in MR. To avoid bias due to population heterogeneity, this study selected genome-wide association study (GWAS) data from European-ancestry individuals with mutual sample independence and used a two-sample MR approach to evaluate the causal relationship between hypertension and OP and its related traits, while also assessing potential reverse causality. Sensitivity analysis was performed. To determine whether blood metabolites mediate this causal link, mediation analysis was used, and the coefficient product approach was applied to estimate indirect effects and mediation proportions. The analysis of this study follows a predetermined causal inference path. First, the causal effect of hypertension on OP was validated through MR, followed by independent screening of metabolites significantly associated with OP. Finally, the causal effect of hypertension on these metabolites was examined to evaluate the mediating role in the hypertension-metabolite-OP pathway. All analyses were based on independent genetic IVs and GWAS summary data, and the causal effects of each step were separately estimated using MR methods. This process aims to explore mechanism pathways based on clear causal assumptions, without iterative or result-driven variable screening. Three fundamental assumptions guided all MR analyses in this study: (1) IVs must have a significant correlation with the exposure variable; (2) IVs must not have a correlation with confounding variables; and (3) IVs can only influence the outcome through the exposure variable. The institutional review boards have given their approval for the use of GWAS data, which come from publicly accessible datasets (Fig. 1).

2.2. Data sources

The GWAS data on hypertension, which includes 316 345 control samples and 137 312 case samples, is sourced from the FinnGen R11 database (https://www.finngen.fi/en).14 The research by Marta et al,15 which included 56 637 people with European ancestry, provided the GWAS data for OP. A total of 426 824 heel BMD samples,16 56 284 total body BMD samples,17 53 184 fracture cases,16 and 32 735, 28 498, and 8143 samples for femoral neck BMD, lumbar spine BMD, and forearm BMD, respectively,18 were also included in the analysis along with GWAS data on OP-related characteristics. The Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging (CLSA), a cohort study with 8299 participants,19 provided the GWAS blood metabolite data. The GWAS Catalog (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/) has the GWAS summary statistics under catalog numbers GCST90199621-GCST90201020. Supplementary Table 1, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A398, has information on 1400 metabolites. All GWAS data used in this study were obtained from independent sources. 2.3. Screening for IVs

IVs pertaining to exposure should fulfill the subsequent requirements: (1) Increase the threshold from p < 1 × 10−8 to p < 1 × 10−5 as the screening criterion, as only a few loci are significant for metabolites; (2) Parameters were set as follows: r2 = 0.001 and kb = 10 000 to exclude linkage disequilibrium, ensuring the independence of the selected IVs; (3) To guarantee strong associations between the IVs and the exposure factor, the genetic variance explained (R2) and F statistics were calculated for each SNP. The strength of the IVs was assessed using F statistics, and only SNPs with an F statistic >10 were included. F < 10 was considered to indicate significant bias in the causal estimates. The F statistic of this study is detailed in Supplementary Table 2, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A399; (4) To remove bias, get all screened SNPs20 using the LDtrait tool (https://ldlink.nih.gov/?tab=ldtrait), removing SNPs linked to confounding variables (such as aging-related features, weight, alcohol usage, smoking), and OP; (5) The SNPs for both the exposure and outcome were harmonized to ensure allele alignment. Palindromic and incompatible SNPs were excluded. The MR-PRESSO outlier test was applied to detect and remove anomalous SNPs in the IVs, thereby reducing bias, correcting for horizontal pleiotropy, and ensuring the suitability of the final IVs for subsequent analyses.

2.4. Statistical analysis

The major technique to establish the causal association between OP and hypertension is the random-effects inverse variance weighted (IVW). Auxiliary analysis techniques included MR-Egger regression, the weighted median method, simple model methods, and weighted model methods. Based on this, two SMR study was carried out using the same procedure as a reverse MR analysis to determine whether OP and associated characteristics as exposure variables impact hypertension.

To minimize the risk of type II errors (false negatives) and ensure comprehensive capture of potential biological signals in exploratory contexts, the study did not apply multiple test corrections (Benjamini-Hochberg correction) to the entire dataset. Instead, it prioritized sensitivity analysis of multiple complementary MR methods (IVW, MR Egger, weighted median) to support the consistency of results, which enhanced the reliability of the discovery. To make sure the SNPs were free of heterogeneity and pleiotropy, sensitivity studies included the Leave-one-out technique, Cochran’s Q test, MR-Egger’s test, Mendelian Randomization Pleiotropy RESidual Sum and Outlier (MR-PRESSO) test, and Robust Adjusted Profile Score (MR-RAPS). Horizontal pleiotropy was assessed using the intercept term from MR-Egger regression. A statistically significant intercept (p < 0.05) was interpreted as evidence of horizontal pleiotropy, while a nonsignificant intercept (p > 0.05) indicated the absence of detectable horizontal pleiotropy. It should be emphasized that, considering the potential coinfluencing factors of aging between hypertension and OP, we conducted conservative screening in the analysis. Referring to the previous GWAS summary data on lifespan, parental lifespan, and epigenetic aging,21,22 we identified potential aging-related loci and excluded variants overlapping with the candidate tool SNP in this study for sensitivity analysis. It should be pointed out that this step aimed to reduce the potential level of pleiotropy risk associated with aging, rather than correcting for age as a nongenetic confounding factor. The heterogeneity of SNPs was assessed using Cochran’s Q test, and the random-effects IVW model was used to conclude that heterogeneity was present when p < 0.05.

Additionally, we used MR-PRESSO to identify and eliminate outliers from the IVs. The MR-RAPS method was used to mitigate the impact of weak IV and residual level pleiotropy on causal estimation. The study also used MR Steiger analysis to evaluate the directionality of the causal association, thereby ensuring that the results were not biased by reverse causality.23 To be sure that no single SNP unduly influences the findings, each SNP was systematically deleted using the Leave-one-out approach.

Finally, a two-step MR mediation analysis was performed (Fig. 1B). The total effect (Beta(total)) represents the overall effect of hypertension (exposure) on OP (outcome). The effect of hypertension on the metabolite is denoted as Beta1, and the effect of the metabolite on OP and its related phenotypes as Beta2. Potential mediators were retained only when the direction of effect estimates was logically consistent. The mediation effect was calculated using the product method (Beta1 × Beta2). The direct effect was obtained by subtracting the mediation effect from the total effect, that is, Beta(total) − Beta1 × Beta2. The proportion mediated was calculated as (Beta1 × Beta2)/Beta(total). The delta method was used to calculate the 95% CI.

We also used multivariate MR analysis (MVMR) to explore the independent effects of metabolites on OP and its related features. MVMR used MVMR-IVW and MVMR Egger methods, with MVMR-IVW being the main approach. Under the MVMR framework, if metabolites maintain significant effects after adjusting for hypertension, it supports their causal role as a potential mediator. If the effect weakens or is no longer significant, it suggests that the association observed in univariate two-step MR may partially reflect the direct genetic effect of SNPs rather than the true mediating pathway.

R software (version 4.3.1) and the R packages “TwoSampleMR” and “MR-PRESSO” (1.0) were used to perform the aforementioned analysis. The findings were shown as 95% CI and odds ratios (OR), with p < 0.05 denoting a significant causal association between the exposure and the outcome.

3. RESULTS

3.1. The causal relationship between hypertension and OP

The IVs included in this investigation are listed in Supplementary Table 3, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A400, after being chosen based on screening criteria, with weak IVs and linkage disequilibrium being eliminated. The intensity of all IVs ranged from 29 to 283 (Supplementary Table 2, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A399). (The threshold underwent no multiple detection corrections and may result in false positive results.) An increasing prevalence of hypertension and an increased risk of OP were shown to be significantly correlated by the IVW findings (Fig. 2A) (OR = 1.165, 95% CI: 1.067-1.273, p < 0.001). Furthermore, negative causal relationships were found between hypertension and heel BMD with lumbar spine BMD (OR = 0.982, 95% CI: 0.971-0.992, p < 0.001; OR = 0.961, 95% CI: 0.927-0.997, p < 0.05). (The detailed results are shown in Supplementary Table 4, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A401). The direction of the causal association was consistently shown by the MR-Egger regression, weighted median method, weighted model method, and simple model method (Fig. 2B). In addition, MR-PRESSO and MR-RAPS also showed consistent and robust correlation results (Supplementary Table 5, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A402).

Results of IVW MR of hypertension and OP and their related characteristics. A, IVW results between hypertension and OP and its associated characteristics. B, Scatter plots. C, Inverse IVW results between hypertension and OP and its associated characteristics. IVW = inverse variance weighted; MR = Mendelian randomization; OP = osteoporosis; OR = odds ratio; SNP = single-nucleotide polymorphism.

No statistically significant differences (p > 0.05) were found between the findings of the MR-Egger regression and Cochran’s Q test, suggesting no horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity between SNPs. Heel BMD data, however, showed signs of heterogeneity (p < 0.05). Since this study used the random-effects IVW method, the presence of heterogeneity did not compromise the robustness of the results. Moreover, all results passed Steiger filtering (Supplementary Table 6, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A403). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, which iteratively removed each SNP associated with hypertension and reanalyzed the data using the remaining SNPs, demonstrated that none of the identified causal associations were driven by any single IV (Supplementary Figures S1-S3, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A413). The sensitivity analyses confirmed that the MR results were robust and reliable.

3.2. Reverse MR results

In the reverse MR analysis, OP, lumbar spine BMD, and heel BMD were treated as exposures, while hypertension was the outcome (multiple tests should be noted). The results indicated no reverse causal relationship between heel BMD or OP and hypertension (p > 0.05), whereas lumbar spine BMD showed a negative association with hypertension (OR = 0.943, 95% CI: 0.904-0.984, p = 0.007) (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Table 7, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A404). Sensitivity analyses of the reverse MR indicated no heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy (Supplementary Table 6, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A403 and Supplementary Figure S4, htttp://links.lww.com/JCMA/A414).

3.3. Mediating role of metabolites in the relationship between hypertension and OP and other phenotypes

We first evaluated the potential associations between 1400 metabolites and OP, lumbar spine BMD, and heel BMD (full significant results with p < 0.05 are provided in Supplementary Table 8, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A405) (based on uncorrected p values). To enhance the reliability of the findings, metabolites exhibiting horizontal pleiotropy or inconsistent directionality across the other four analytical methods were excluded. According to the IVW results, 57 metabolites showed significant causal associations with OP, 103 with lumbar spine BMD, and 213 with heel BMD (Supplementary Table 9, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A406). The IV intensity of all metabolites was between 19 and 21 (Supplementary Table 2, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A399). Steiger filtering confirmed the directionality of the causal associations between the metabolites and OP and related phenotypes (p < 0.05), and sensitivity analyses demonstrated the robustness of these results (p > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 11, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A408). Both MR-PRESSO and MR-RAPS exhibited consistent and robust causal association results (Supplementary Table 10, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A407).

There are significant metabolites shared among different skeletal phenotypes. To avoid repeated counting, the causal relationships between hypertension and the 329 independent metabolites significantly associated with OP, lumbar spine BMD, or heel BMD were assessed. After excluding metabolites with horizontal pleiotropy, IVW results indicated that hypertension had significant causal associations with 46 metabolites (p < 0.05) (Fig. 3; detailed results are shown in Supplementary Table 12, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A409). The intensity of IVs was between 29 and 283 (Supplementary Table 2, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A399). Sensitivity analysis revealed horizontal pleiotropy for 2-hydroxystearate, X-22834, deoxycholic acid glucuronide levels, and the inosine 5’-monophosphate (IMP) to urate ratio, which were therefore excluded from subsequent mediation analyses. The remaining results were robust (Supplementary Table 13, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A410). In MR-PRESSO and MR-RAPS, these results also exhibited consistent and robust causal association (Supplementary Table 14, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A411).

MR IVW results of hypertension and metabolites. IVW = inverse variance weighted; MR = Mendelian randomization; OR = odds ratio; SNP = single-nucleotide polymorphism.

Preliminary mediation analysis identified multiple potential metabolic pathways: (R)-3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine, 1-(1-enylpalmitoyl)-GPE(p-16:0), and hydroxyasparagine may mediate the effect of hypertension on OP, while 5α-androstan-3α, 17α-diol monosulfate, sphinganine-1-phosphate, and sphingosine-1-phosphate may mediate their reducing effect on heel bone density. However, the proportion of mediating effects of each metabolite was relatively low (6.68%-12.1%), indicating that these associations were only exploratory findings (Table 2). Multivariate MR analysis showed significant associations between 1-(1-enylpalmitoyl)-GPE (p-16:0), hydroxyasparagine, and OP, as well as between 5α-androstan-3α, 17α-diol monosulfate and heel BMD after adjusting for hypertension, supporting their potential causal mediating role (Supplementary Table S15, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A412). However, caution should still be exercised when interpreting results.

4. DISCUSSION

Given that hypertension is one of the main illnesses that affects the elderly, there is reason to be curious about the connection between OP and hypertension. Thus, using bidirectional twoSMR analysis, our work investigated the risk of OP that was markedly elevated by hypertension. The pathogenic process may be accomplished through metabolic pathways. Moreover, a negative causal relationship was observed between hypertension and both lumbar spine BMD and heel BMD.

Our study confirmed a positive causal relationship between hypertension and OP, which is consistent with previous research. A population-based cross-sectional study found that the prevalence of OP was significantly higher in subjects with hypertension than in those without (10.8% vs 4.3%).24 Another study also showed that hypertensive women were more likely to have OP compared with nonhypertensive women (33.2% vs 23.3%).25 These associations may be related to dysregulation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), insulin resistance, and enhanced inflammatory responses. Hypertension is often accompanied by RAAS dysregulation,26 and angiotensin, a key regulator within RAAS, plays an important role in modulating osteoclast-mediated bone resorption.27,28 It can stimulate osteoclast activity by activating nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) receptors, thereby accelerating bone resorption and contributing to OP progression.29 The use of RAAS inhibitors significantly reduces the incidence of osteoporotic fractures.30 Additionally, hypertensive patients often exhibit insulin resistance,31 which can influence bone metabolism through multiple mechanisms and increase the risk of OP.32 Meanwhile, levels of inflammatory cytokines are significantly elevated in hypertensive patients.33 Increased secretion of these cytokines exacerbates inflammatory responses, disrupts the balance between osteoblasts and osteoclasts, enhances osteoclast activity, and ultimately leads to OP.34,35

In reverse MR analysis, we found a causal relationship between OP and hypertension, but lumbar spine BMD was negatively correlated with hypertension. It should be noted that the OP-related GWAS features we included are heterogeneous, including fracture patients and BMD in different parts of the body. These indicators vary in anatomical location, bone tissue type, metabolic regulation, and systemic factors, and may therefore affect blood pressure–related changes through different biological or metabolic pathways. The lumbar spine BMD mainly reflects the axial bone density rich in trabecular bone, which is more significantly affected by endocrine, inflammatory, and metabolic factors,36,37 and is therefore more likely to be related to cardiovascular or metabolic status. When combining multiple sites or different types of indicators into OP, the effect of site specificity may be diluted, resulting in an overall association that is not significant. However, further validation of the specific association between the two is needed in more independent cohorts and finer phenotypes in the future.

Our study also revealed that hypertension differentially affects BMD at various skeletal sites, showing a negative effect on both lumbar spine and heel BMD. A meta-analysis indicated that hypertensive patients have a significantly higher risk of low BMD than nonhypertensive individuals (OR: 1.33, 95% CI: 1.17-1.53, p < 0.01).7 Two additional cross-sectional studies reported lower BMD in hypertensive groups compared with healthy controls,38,39 which is consistent with our findings. Based on existing evidence, RAAS dysregulation may be a key underlying mechanism. As mentioned, RAAS dysfunction in hypertension can lead to abnormal aldosterone secretion, and excessive aldosterone may disrupt bone metabolism, reduce bone mass, and decrease BMD.40 Interventions such as aldosterone antagonists (eg, spironolactone) have been shown to moderately regulate bone metabolism and improve skeletal health.41,42

We further identified that the blood metabolites (R)-3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine, 1-(1-enyl-palmitoyl)-GPE (p-16:0), and hydroxyasparagine may be involved in the metabolic pathways linking hypertension and OP. (R)-3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine is closely associated with fatty acid β-oxidation, and previous studies have suggested its dysregulation in both hypertension and bone metabolism.43,44 The discovery supports the conceptual hypothesis that hypertension may impair skeletal health by disturbing metabolite and fatty acid oxidation processes. However, further validation is required. As for 1-(1-enyl-palmitoyl)-GPE (p-16:0) and hydroxyasparagine, limited existing evidence means our findings only provide hypothetical directions for future exploration regarding their potential roles in the association between hypertension and OP. In addition, the ADP-to-5-oxoproline ratio showed a mediating trend in the relationship between hypertension and lumbar spine BMD. This ratio may reflect the systemic redox status: hypertension-associated oxidative stress may lead to the accumulation of 5-oxoproline,45 which is known to be linked to low bone mass.46 Meanwhile, ADP can promote bone resorption via the P2Y12 receptor.47–49 We therefore speculate that this ratio may mediate the effects of hypertension on bone through an interactive network involving oxidative stress and purinergic signaling (the ADP-P2Y12 axis). Similarly, sphinganine-1-phosphate and sphingosine-1-phosphate exhibited mediating trends between hypertension and heel BMD. As key intermediates in sphingolipid metabolism, both are widely involved in cell signal transduction and proliferation regulation.50,51 Metabolic disturbances induced by hypertension may disrupt their homeostasis,52 thereby impairing osteocyte function and reducing BMD.53 Notably, given the small effect estimates and wide CIs, these quantitative proportions should not be interpreted as effect sizes with definitive biological significance. The core value of this study lies in revealing potential metabolic pathway directions and providing hypothetical clues for subsequent mechanistic research, rather than identifying precise effects.

Of note, the statistical robustness observed in this study (ie, consistency in the direction of results across different sensitivity analyses) is not directly equivalent to biological validity. Although sensitivity analyses excluded apparent horizontal pleiotropy, metabolites sharing biosynthetic pathways in the metabolic network often exhibit strong collinearity. Statistically significant associations may therefore reflect the true causal effect of key nodes within the pathway, whereas other correlated metabolites may be detected merely as passive concomitant associations. In other words, the robustness of statistical results ensures the reliability of the observed associations, whereas establishing a specific causal biological role still requires direct validation through subsequent functional experiments.

There are several limitations in our study. (1) Limitations in population and samples: First, the GWAS data we used come from individuals of European descent. Although using single population data helps reduce confounding bias introduced by differences in population structure and improves the internal validity of the analysis results, it also limits the universal applicability of research conclusions. Therefore, to extrapolate our findings to other ethnic groups, further study is required. Second, the sample size of metabolite GWAS used for identifying metabolite-related genetic tools (n = 8299) is much smaller than that of GWAS for hypertension and OP. This difference may lead to lower variance and insufficient statistical power in the genetic IV of metabolites, thereby limiting the detection ability of mediation analysis and increasing false negative results. (2) Limitations in methodology and statistical analysis: First, the mediation analysis in this study involves multiple hypothesis testing, which increases the risk of false positives. Due to the lack of strict correction, such as false discovery rate (FDR), the results mediated by relevant metabolites should be considered as suggestive potential signals and be interpreted with caution. Second, MR analysis based on publicly available aggregated data cannot completely exclude the effects of pleiotropy bias and phenotype heterogeneity. Due to limitations in data format, sex- or age-specific effects cannot be explored. Although some bias has been corrected through sensitivity analysis, residual pleiotropy risks may still exist, and future research needs to be further validated based on individual-level data or stratified GWAS summary results. Third, the impact of hypertension on OP and BMD is characterized by multiple factors and pathways, and aging is a common pathogenic factor for both. Although this study attempted to reduce potential confounding by excluding age-related SNPs, the effectiveness of this method is limited by the coverage of existing GWAS databases. More importantly, the reliability of MR analysis results relies entirely on the core assumption that IVs satisfy the exclusivity constraint, and it is difficult to completely exclude the influence of residual confounding factors in practical analysis. Finally, this study relies on publicly available GWAS aggregated data for secondary analysis, which limits our exploration of potential nonlinear relationships between exposure and outcomes. The potential interactions between genes and important environmental factors cannot be evaluated. In future research, it is necessary to rely on individual-level data and apply nonlinear MR methods for further validation. (3) Limitations in biological mechanism: First, even after adjusting for possible confounding variables, residual confounding factors cannot be completely excluded in this magnetic resonance investigation. Second, although blood metabolites were identified as mediators in the relationship between hypertension and OP, lumbar spine BMD, and heel BMD, the underlying pathway mechanisms require further investigation.

In conclusion, this study confirmed a positive causal relationship between hypertension and OP, as well as negative causal associations with both lumbar spine BMD and heel BMD. However, given that this study was mainly based on data from individuals of European ancestry, without detailed sex- or age-stratified analyses, and considering the heterogeneity in phenotype definitions, these findings should be regarded as preliminary epidemiological clues rather than definitive causal clinical conclusions. Nevertheless, our results still provide novel insights into the potential metabolic mechanisms underlying the impact of hypertension on bone health and lay a scientific foundation for future in-depth functional validation and stratified studies in diverse populations.

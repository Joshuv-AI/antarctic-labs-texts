Neoadjuvant anthracycline-free versus anthracycline-containing chemotherapy combined with dual HER2 blockade in HER2-positive breast cancer: a real-world comparative study
Authors: Asli Buyukkuscu, Zehra Sucuoglu Isleyen, Omer Burak Ekinci, Tugay Atasever, Kubra Akkaya, Okan Aydin, Gamze Kulduk, Emir Celik, Kayhan Erturk, M. Mustafa Atci
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAnthracycline-free neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with dual HER2 blockade represents an alternative to anthracycline-containing regimens in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer; however, real-world evidence remains limited.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 162 patients with stage I–III HER2-positive breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant trastuzumab and pertuzumab between 2022 and 2025. Patients received anthracycline-free (TCHP/TCH; n = 51) or anthracycline-containing (AC-THP/AC-TH; n = 111) chemotherapy. The primary endpoint was pathological complete response (pCR; ypT0/is ypN0). Secondary endpoints included treatment-related toxicity and cardiac safety.ResultsThe overall pCR rate was 56.8%. pCR was achieved in 64.7% of patients in the TCHP/TCH group and 53.2% in the AC-THP/AC-TH group (p = 0.168). In multivariable analysis, age > 65 years was associated with a lower probability of pCR (OR 0.08; 95% CI, 0.01–0.57), while hormone receptor–negative status was associated with higher pCR rates (OR 2.21; 95% CI, 1.02–4.76). Grade 3–4 hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities differed between regimens. Cardiac toxicity was rare and similar between groups.ConclusionsIn a real-world setting, anthracycline-free neoadjuvant chemotherapy with dual HER2 blockade achieved pCR rates comparable to anthracycline-containing regimens with acceptable toxicity and preserved cardiac safety, suggesting that it may be a reasonable treatment option for selected patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-026-02312-w.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy in women worldwide. Approximately 15–20% of cases are HER2-positive, a biologically aggressive subtype historically associated with poor prognosis prior to the advent of HER2-targeted therapies [1, 2].

The incorporation of trastuzumab, and subsequently dual HER2 blockade with trastuzumab and pertuzumab, has substantially improved outcomes in HER2-positive breast cancer [3]. In the neoadjuvant setting, dual HER2-targeted therapy combined with chemotherapy has become the standard of care for patients with stage II–III disease, achieving high pathological complete response (pCR) rates in pivotal randomized trials such as NeoSphere, BERENICE and TRYPHAENA [4–7]. Achievement of pCR is a robust prognostic marker and is strongly associated with improved long-term survival outcomes in this subtype [4, 8].

Anthracyclines have traditionally been a core component of breast cancer chemotherapy; however, their use is limited by dose-dependent cardiotoxicity, which is of particular concern when combined with HER2-targeted agents [9, 10]. As a result, anthracycline-free regimens incorporating taxanes and carboplatin have been developed to reduce treatment-related toxicity without compromising efficacy [7].

The phase III TRAIN-2 trial demonstrated comparable pCR, event-free survival, and overall survival between anthracycline-containing and anthracycline-free chemotherapy regimens combined with dual HER2 blockade, with significantly lower rates of cardiotoxicity and hematologic toxicity in the anthracycline-free group [11, 12]. These findings were supported by the TRYPHAENA study and subsequent meta-analyses, which confirmed preserved efficacy alongside improved cardiac safety.[7, 13].

Despite strong evidence from randomized trials, these studies often include highly selected patient populations, limiting generalizability to routine clinical practice. Therefore, real-world data are needed to better define the optimal chemotherapy backbone for dual HER2 blockade in unselected patients.

In this single-center real-world study, we compared the efficacy and safety of anthracycline-containing versus anthracycline-free neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens combined with dual HER2-targeted therapy in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer, aiming to provide clinically relevant evidence to guide treatment selection in routine practice.

Methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective, single-center study included 162 female and male patients with stage I–III HER2-positive invasive breast cancer who received neoadjuvant systemic therapy followed by surgery between January 2022 and January 2025 at the Medical Oncology Clinic of Prof. Dr. Cemil Taşçıoğlu City Hospital, Istanbul, Türkiye. HER2 positivity was defined according to the 2018 ASCO/CAP guidelines as immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3 + or IHC 2 + with confirmed fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) amplification [14]. Tumor staging was performed using the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM classification system [15].

Eligible patients were aged ≥ 18 years, had histologically confirmed HER2-positive early-stage breast cancer, a baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥ 50%, no prior systemic therapy for malignancy, and clinical stage T1c–4 N0–3 M0 disease. All patients received neoadjuvant systemic therapy (TCHP/TCH or AC-THP/AC-TH) and subsequently underwent definitive breast and axillary surgery. Patients were excluded if they had T1a–b tumors, metastatic disease, a history of prior or concurrent malignancy, incomplete clinical or follow-up data, or missing cardiac monitoring records.

Baseline clinicopathologic data, including ECOG performance status, clinical tumor (T) and nodal (N) stage, hormone receptor (ER/PR) status, and histologic grade (G1–3), were extracted from medical records. Patients were categorized into anthracycline-containing and anthracycline-free treatment groups.

Treatment regimens

Anthracycline-free regimens included TCHP (n = 44), consisting of docetaxel (75–100 mg/m²), carboplatin (AUC 5–6), trastuzumab (8 mg/kg loading dose followed by 6 mg/kg maintenance), and pertuzumab (840 mg loading dose followed by 420 mg maintenance), administered every 21 days for six cycles. A subset of patients received TCH (n = 7), in which pertuzumab was omitted.

Anthracycline-containing regimens comprised AC-THP (n = 100), with doxorubicin (60 mg/m²) and cyclophosphamide (600 mg/m²) administered every 21 days for four cycles, followed by a taxane (docetaxel 75 mg/m² or paclitaxel 70–90 mg/m² on days 1, 8, and 15) in combination with trastuzumab and pertuzumab every 21 days for four cycles. The AC-TH regimen (n = 11) was administered without pertuzumab.

All treatment protocols were delivered in accordance with NCCN (v3.2024) and ESMO (2019) guidelines [16, 17]. Dose modifications or treatment delays were implemented in cases of grade ≥ 3 toxicity or clinically significant declines in LVEF. Patients with residual invasive disease after surgery received adjuvant trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) for up to one year when clinically indicated and available; otherwise, trastuzumab monotherapy was continued to complete one year of anti-HER2 therapy. Patients achieving pathological complete response (pCR) also continued trastuzumab to complete one year. Adjuvant endocrine therapy was administered to hormone receptor–positive patients, and radiotherapy was delivered according to standard indications.

Endpoints and follow-up

Pathological complete response was defined as ypT0/is ypN0, indicating the absence of invasive carcinoma in both the breast and axillary lymph nodes [18]. Due to limited follow-up, survival outcomes were not analyzed.

Treatment-related adverse events were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0 [19]. Cardiotoxicity was defined as an absolute decrease in LVEF of ≥ 10% from baseline or an absolute LVEF < 50%.6 Patients were followed every 3 months during the first 2 years, every 6 months until year 5, and annually thereafter.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient demographics and clinical characteristics. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples t-test, and categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Variables with clinical relevance and/or a p value < 0.20 in univariate analyses were entered into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of pCR, and results were reported as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Prof. Dr. Cemil Taşçıoğlu City Hospital (Approval No: E-48670771-514.99-286278766).

Following ethics committee approval, patient records were retrospectively reviewed and analyzed. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee.

Results

Patient characteristics

Patients treated with TCHP/TCH were significantly older than those receiving AC-THP/AC-TH (mean age, 55.9 ± 12.7 vs. 47.7 ± 9.5 years; p < 0.001), with a higher proportion aged > 65 years (25.5% vs. 4.5%; p < 0.001). ECOG performance status 0 was more common in the AC-THP/AC-TH group (82.9% vs. 49.0%; p < 0.001), as was premenopausal status (57.3% vs. 26.5%; p = 0.001).

Histologic subtype, tumor grade, clinical T stage, nodal stage, overall stage distribution (stage I–II vs. III), hormone receptor status, and Ki-67 expression were comparable between the two groups (all p > 0.05). Although a higher proportion of node-negative patients was observed in the anthracycline-free group, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.063). Baseline clinicopathologic characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

In the comparison of baseline clinicopathological characteristics according to pathological complete response (pCR) status, most variables were well balanced between patients with residual disease and those achieving pCR (Supplementary Table S1).

Pathological response and surgical outcomes

The overall pathological complete response (pCR; ypT0/is ypN0) rate was 56.8%. The pCR rate was achieved in 64.7% of patients treated with TCHP/TCH and in 53.2% of those treated with AC-THP/AC-TH, with no statistically significant difference between groups (p = 0.168).

Breast-conserving surgery was performed more frequently in the AC-THP/AC-TH group than in the TCHP/TCH group (54.1% vs. 36.0%; p = 0.034). Axillary surgical management (sentinel lymph node biopsy vs. axillary lymph node dissection) did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.628).

Rates of residual tumor ≤ 1 cm (31.4% vs. 28.8%; p = 0.129), axillary complete response (ypN0; 80.4% vs. 70.3%; p = 0.339), and Miller–Payne grade 5 response were similar between the TCHP/TCH and AC-THP/AC-TH groups. Use of adjuvant trastuzumab and trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) did not differ between treatment arms. Surgical and pathological outcomes are detailed in Table 2.

Predictors of pathological complete response

In multivariable logistic regression analysis, age > 65 years was independently associated with a significantly lower likelihood of achieving pCR (odds ratio [OR], 0.08; 95% CI, 0.01–0.57; p = 0.011). Hormone receptor–negative disease was independently associated with higher pCR rates (OR, 2.21; 95% CI, 1.02–4.76; p = 0.043).

Treatment with TCHP/TCH showed a nonsignificant trend toward increased pCR compared with anthracycline-containing regimens (OR, 2.03; p = 0.073). Clinical T stage, nodal stage, overall stage, and Ki-67 expression were not significantly associated with pCR. Model calibration was excellent (Hosmer–Lemeshow p = 0.964). Treatment-related toxicity

Treatment-related adverse events are summarized in Tables 4 and 5. Low-grade gastrointestinal toxicities were more frequent in the TCHP/TCH group, including diarrhea (60.8% vs. 24.3%; p < 0.001), nausea (94.1% vs. 77.5%; p = 0.009), and vomiting (37.3% vs. 16.2%; p = 0.003). Fatigue (88.2% vs. 65.8%; p = 0.003) and pain (64.7% vs. 45.9%; p = 0.026) were also more commonly reported with TCHP/TCH.

Cardiac safety

Cardiac toxicity was infrequent in both treatment groups. Left ventricular systolic dysfunction was observed in 2.0% of patients receiving TCHP/TCH and 1.8% of those receiving AC-THP/AC-TH. All detected declines in left ventricular ejection fraction were within the 0–10% range. No patient experienced an ejection fraction reduction ≥ 11%. Cardiac safety outcomes were comparable between regimens.

Discussion

In this real-world, single-center study, we compared the efficacy and safety of anthracycline-free (TCHP/TCH) and anthracycline-containing (AC-THP/AC-TH) neoadjuvant chemotherapy backbones combined with dual HER2 blockade in patients with early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer. The overall pathological complete response (pCR) rate of 56.8% observed in our cohort is consistent with those reported in contemporary neoadjuvant trials of dual HER2-targeted therapy, supporting the external validity of our findings despite the older and clinically more heterogeneous population treated in routine practice [5–8].

Although no statistically significant difference in pCR was observed between treatment groups, pCR rates were numerically higher with the anthracycline-free TCHP/TCH regimen compared with anthracycline-containing therapy (64.7% vs. 53.2%). In multivariable analysis, treatment with TCHP/TCH showed a borderline association with higher pCR likelihood, suggesting that anthracycline-free regimens are at least non-inferior—and potentially favorable—with respect to neoadjuvant efficacy. Given the limited sample size and number of events, this numerical advantage may not have reached statistical significance but remains clinically meaningful.

These findings are consistent with emerging real-world evidence. Large observational cohorts from Samsung Medical Center and the Polish National Oncology Institute have reported pCR rates ranging from approximately 53% to 64% with neoadjuvant TCHP or TCbH-P regimens, closely mirroring the outcomes observed in our study. Collectively, these data reinforce the notion that anthracycline-free dual HER2 blockade delivers robust neoadjuvant efficacy beyond the highly selected populations typically enrolled in randomized clinical trials [20, 21].

Importantly, the clinical characteristics of patients receiving TCHP/TCH in our cohort underscore the relevance of these findings. This group was significantly older, with a substantial proportion aged ≥ 65 years, and had poorer baseline performance status—features often underrepresented in pivotal trials. Despite this unfavorable baseline profile, pCR rates remained high, highlighting the preserved biological efficacy of anthracycline-free regimens in more vulnerable patient populations. While age ≥ 65 years was independently associated with a lower probability of achieving pCR, the overall effectiveness of TCHP/TCH in this subgroup remains notable and aligns with international real-world experience [21].

Consistent with prior studies, hormone receptor negativity emerged as an independent predictor of pCR. This observation aligns with pooled analyses such as CTNeoBC, randomized trial data, and real-world cohorts, supporting the concept that HR-negative/HER2-positive tumors are particularly sensitive to dual HER2 blockade [8, 18, 21]. In contrast, the lack of significant associations between pCR and clinical stage, nodal status, or Ki-67 expression suggests that traditional clinicopathologic variables may have limited incremental predictive value in the context of modern HER2-targeted neoadjuvant therapy.

The safety profiles observed in this study were consistent with the known toxicity patterns of the respective regimens. Low-grade gastrointestinal toxicities, fatigue, and pain were more common in the TCHP/TCH group, reflecting the expected adverse event spectrum of carboplatin- and pertuzumab-containing regimens [7, 11, 12]. Importantly, these events were predominantly low grade and manageable with standard supportive measures, allowing all patients to complete planned neoadjuvant therapy.

Hematologic toxicity differed by treatment backbone. Grade 3–4 thrombocytopenia and anemia occurred exclusively in the TCHP/TCH group, consistent with the myelosuppressive effects of carboplatin. Conversely, grade 3–4 neutropenia was more frequent with anthracycline-containing regimens, reflecting the known toxicity of anthracyclines and alkylating agents [7, 11, 12]. Importantly, toxicity outcomes did not consistently favor anthracycline-free regimens, as several adverse events were more frequently observed in this group; however, the majority of these events were grade 1–2, manageable with standard supportive care, and did not lead to treatment discontinuation, suggesting that this approach is feasible even in older and less fit patients in routine clinical practice.

Cardiac safety outcomes were reassuring and aligned with prior reports from TRYPHAENA and BERENICE [6, 7]. Clinically relevant declines in left ventricular ejection fraction were rare, mild, and comparable between treatment groups, with no patient experiencing a reduction ≥ 11%. These findings are particularly relevant given the older age and higher comorbidity burden of patients receiving TCHP/TCH and support the favorable cardiac safety profile of anthracycline-free regimens. Considering the cumulative and late-onset cardiotoxicity associated with anthracyclines, our results provide additional real-world evidence supporting the use of anthracycline-sparing strategies [9, 10].

This study has several limitations inherent to its retrospective, single-center design, including potential selection bias and residual confounding. Treatment allocation was not randomized and was influenced by clinical factors such as age and comorbidity burden. Although a higher proportion of node-negative patients was observed in the anthracycline-free group, this difference did not reach statistical significance and should therefore be interpreted with caution. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, this may limit direct comparability between groups, while still reflecting real-world clinical practice.

In addition, the increasing use of anthracycline-free regimens in recent years in line with guideline recommendations may have contributed to the observed patient distribution.

Furthermore, the relatively small sample size and limited follow-up preclude definitive conclusions regarding long-term survival outcomes. Therefore, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger, prospective real-world cohorts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, anthracycline-free neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with dual HER2 blockade achieved pCR rates comparable to anthracycline-containing regimens in this real-world cohort.

However, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the retrospective study design and baseline differences between treatment groups, including age and ECOG performance status. Although a higher proportion of node-negative patients was observed in the anthracycline-free group, this difference did not reach statistical significance; nevertheless, it may still have influenced treatment allocation.

In terms of safety, toxicity profiles differed between regimens and did not consistently favor anthracycline-free therapy; higher rates of gastrointestinal and certain hematologic adverse events were observed in this group, whereas neutropenia was more frequent with anthracycline-containing regimens. Notably, although toxicities were more frequent in the TCHP-based group, the majority were grade 1–2, manageable with standard supportive care, and did not lead to treatment discontinuation.

Despite these differences, adverse events were generally manageable and did not lead to treatment discontinuation, and clinically significant cardiac toxicity was infrequent and comparable between groups.

Overall, these findings suggest that anthracycline-free regimens represent a reasonable neoadjuvant option for selected patients with early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer in routine clinical practice, while prospective studies are needed to confirm these results.

Research Note: The infectious bursal disease (Gumboro disease) vaccination scheme affects the quantitative and qualitative carcass characteristics and the immune response of Ross 308 broiler chickens
Authors: Marcin Wegner, Michał Gesek, Mirosław Banaszak, Marek Adamski, Sebastian Wlaźlak, Jakub Biesek
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAfter different vaccinations, the study aimed to assess carcass composition, meat quality, and the level of the infectious bursal disease antibody titers in broiler chickens. In the experiment, 4 (30 chicks per group, 120 total) groups of Ross 308 broiler chickens (males and females) were included. Then, they were vaccinated at the hatchery. Group I was immunized against infectious avian bronchitis (IB) and Newcastle disease (ND) using vaccine A (20 mL/100 birds) and vaccine B (20 mL/100 birds), respectively, with injectable vaccine C (0.2 mL/each) targeting Infectious bursal’ and Marek's diseases (MD). Group II received the same vaccinations for IB and ND, with separate injectable D vaccines for MD (0.2 mL/unit) and E for infectious bursal’ disease (0.2 mL/each). Group III was similarly vaccinated for IB and ND; however, they received only vaccine D (0.2 mL/each) for MD, and on d 23, vaccine F was administered via drinking water against Gumboro disease (0.05 mL/each). Group IV served as the control and was not vaccinated. Ten carcasses from each group on d 42 were taken for carcass composition analysis and meat quality, including pH, color, and water-holding capacity (WHC). During rearing, on d 14, 28, and 42, blood was collected to determine the level of antibodies against infectious bursal’ disease. Additionally, hematocrit (HCT), hemoglobin (HGB), and red blood cells were analyzed. In group I, a higher bursa of Fabricius weight than in the other groups was found (P < 0.001), and the liver weight was lower compared to group II (P = 0.020). In breast muscle, the pH24 was higher in the control group than in group I (P = 0.036). Lightness (L*) in breast and leg muscles from group I was higher than in group II (P < 0.05). WHC was higher in group I than in group II (P = 0.025). The antibody titers of infectious bursal’ disease was higher in group I on d 28 than in other groups (P < 0.05). Vaccine C (1 dose 0.2 mL/1 bird) of the reconstituted vaccine with the live recombinant virus strain vHVT013-69, not less than 3.6 - 5.0 log10 PFU) had a beneficial effect.Key words: broiler, Gumboro antibody titer, immunization, meat quality, vaccine

INTRODUCTION

The production of broiler chickens requires producers to maintain the birds' safety and obtain good quality meat for consumers. In a short period of rearing, there are many risks, including diseases, which often include Marek's disease, Gumboro, Newcastle disease, and infectious bronchitis of birds (Wegner et al., 2023). As described by the previous authors, selecting an appropriate vaccination program and effective vaccines may affect the immune response and the composition of carcasses or meat quality.

The vaccination schedule may vary depending on the country or even the farm, as studied by Chung et al. (2021). The effect of vaccination against Newcastle disease (ND), infectious bronchitis (IB), and infectious bursal disease (IBD) was investigated. The impact on slaughter yield, feed conversion ratio (FCR), and carcass characteristics, including carcass weight, percentage of leg muscles, and meat quality, including drip loss, cooking loss, pH, and L*, a*, b* was demonstrated.

Wegner et al. (2023) investigated 2 different vaccination programs against infection bronchitis virus (IBV) and avian metapneumovirus (aMPV) in broiler chicken production. The impact of vaccination on body weight and the percentage of wings, heart, liver, and spleen was demonstrated, and there was no negative impact on the immune response. Previously, different vaccination methods (in ovo or spray) for diseases (Marek's disease, IBD, turkey rhinotracheitis, or IBV) influenced carcass weight, meat quality, and chemical composition.

Gumboro disease (IBD) is the reason for inefficient production, especially of young broiler chickens with a high mortality rate. Therefore, combating IBD is very important and can be done through immunization with various vaccines, including live, inactivated, or recombinant ones (Isihak et al., 2021). In the previous study, the authors showed that the immune response was improved depending on the vaccines used (level of antibodies titer against IBD) and simultaneously impacted the body weight of chickens.

Therefore, the appropriate vaccination program scheme must have a beneficial (or neutral) effect on the immune response and the quality of the chicken meat.

Based on the above, a research hypothesis was formulated that the use of various vaccination programs has an impact on the tissue composition of carcasses, physicochemical characteristics of breast muscles and legs, as well as on the level of blood antibody titers of the Gumboro disease in Ross 308 broiler chickens, which was verified by the research objective: Different vaccination schedules affect carcass composition, meat quality and the level of Gumboro disease antibody titers in broiler chickens.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The experiment complied with Directive No. 2010/63/EU and the Act of 15 January 2015, protecting animals used for scientific or educational purposes. The research material and blood samples were obtained after the slaughter and routine veterinary work. It follows the ARRIVE guidelines.

Experimental Setup and the Vaccination

The experimental material consisted of 120, 1-day-old broiler chickens Ross 308 divided into 4 groups of 30 each (groups I, II, III, and IV). The chicks were obtained from a parent broiler flock with the same preventive program. Then, they were vaccinated at the hatchery by spraying and injection on the first day and drinking water on the 23rd day of life.

Group I was vaccinated with vaccine A by spraying on infectious avian bronchitis (IB, dose 20 mL/100 birds) and vaccination B on Newcastle disease (ND, dose 20 mL/100 birds). One injectable vaccination with C vaccine for Gumboro and Marek's disease (MD, dose 0.2 mL/each) was also performed. Group II was vaccinated with vaccine A by spraying on infectious bronchitis (IB, dose 20 mL/100 birds) and B vaccine on Newcastle disease (ND, dose 20 mL/100 birds). Injectable vaccination was also conducted with the D vaccine for Marek's disease (MD, 0.2 mL/unit) and the E vaccine for Gumboro disease (dose 0.2 mL/each). Group III was vaccinated with vaccine A by spraying on infectious avian bronchitis (IB, dose and 20 mL/100 birds) and B vaccine on Newcastle disease (ND, dose 20 mL/100 birds). Injectable vaccination was also conducted with the D vaccine for Marek's disease (MD, dose 0.2 mL/bird). On the 23rd day of life, birds from group III were vaccinated with vaccine F, administered in drinking water against the Gumboro disease (dose 0.05 mL/each). Group IV (control) was not vaccinated with any vaccine.

Characteristics of the veterinary medicinal product were as follows:

✓A - 1 dose (20 mL/100 birds) of a reconstituted vaccine containing live virus of infectious bronchitis, strain H120, not less than 3.7 - 4.7 log10 EID50;

A - 1 dose (20 mL/100 birds) of a reconstituted vaccine containing live virus of infectious bronchitis, strain H120, not less than 3.7 - 4.7 log10 EID50;

✓B - 1 dose (20 mL/100 birds) of reconstituted vaccine, containing live virus of bird swine virus, strain VG / GA, not less than 5.5 - 6.7 log10 EID50;

B - 1 dose (20 mL/100 birds) of reconstituted vaccine, containing live virus of bird swine virus, strain VG / GA, not less than 5.5 - 6.7 log10 EID50;

✓C - 1 dose (0.2 mL/1 bird) of the reconstituted vaccine, containing live recombinant virus strain vHVT013-69, not less than 3.6 - 5.0 log10 PFU;

C - 1 dose (0.2 mL/1 bird) of the reconstituted vaccine, containing live recombinant virus strain vHVT013-69, not less than 3.6 - 5.0 log10 PFU;

✓D - 1 dose (0.2 mL/1 bird) of a reconstituted vaccine containing a live virus strain Rispens CVI 988, not less than 3.0-4.0 log10 PFU,

Chicken Housing

In regulated environmental conditions, birds were reared in the broiler house on sawdust litter. Initially, the chicks were kept at 32°C, gradually reducing to 20-21°C on d 28. Other environmental parameters (air humidity, ventilation) were adjusted following the recommendations by conducting daily observations and adapting to the prevailing conditions. Artificial light was used, with a color similar to natural light and an intensity of 20 lux. Harmful gas concentrations do not exceed the permissible standards, i.e., CO2 – 3000 ppm, NH3 – 20 ppm, H2S – 5 ppm. The rearing was performed until d 42. During the rearing, 4 feeding periods were used, in which granulated commercial diets were used: up to d 10 (starter: 22.7% of crude protein and 12.3 MJ metabolizable energy (ME) in 1 kg of feed mixture), from d 11 to d 23 and from d 24 to d 35 (grower: 20.5% of crude protein and 12.9 MJ ME) and from d 36 to d 42 (finisher: 18.4% of crude protein and 13.3 MJ ME). Throughout the rearing period, the birds were fed ad libitum with access to fresh water at 14°C.

Carcass Characteristics

On d 42 of rearing, 10 birds from each group were selected for slaughter. The birds were stunned and then slaughtered by severing the spinal cord between the first cervical vertebra and the occipital condyle, according to the standards of the slaughterhouse. Carcasses from each group, after d 42, were prepared for dissection. The liver, gizzard, heart, spleen, and bursa of Fabricius were collected. Offal and immune organs were weighed. The carcasses were cooled at 4°C for 24 h (Hendi, Poznań, Poland). The pH of the breast muscles was measured (pH24) using a CP-401 pH meter with an OSH 1205 type dagger electrode for meat testing (Elmetron, Zabrze, Poland), and the carcasses were weighed. The slaughter yield was calculated based on the live body weight. Leg muscles (deboned), breast muscles, skin with subcutaneous fat, and abdominal fat were dissected from the carcasses.

The breast and leg muscles were collected to determine their ability to maintain water and evaluated for color. The color measurement of muscles was conducted using the CR 400 colorimeter (Konica Minolta, Tokyo, Japan) in the L*, a*, b* color scale, where L* - lightness, a* - redness, and b* - yellowness. For water-holding capacity (WHC), muscle samples weighing 280-320 mg were placed on Whatman paper between 2 glass plates loaded with a 2 kg weight for 5 minutes. After 5 minutes, the samples were weighed again. Water holding capacity, expressed as a percentage, was calculated from the ratio of the sample weight after pressing to its initial weight pressing (mg). These practices were described by Biesek et al. (2022) with appropriate sources of methods.

Serological Tests for Antibody Titers of Gumboro Disease

On d 14, 28, and 42, blood was collected for serological tests to determine the titers of the Gumboro virus antibodies (n = 23 birds per treatment). Blood was collected from the brachial wing vein into 1.5 ml tubes with anticoagulant (heparin). The veterinarian collected the samples. Samples were then taken for the experiment. The tests were conducted using the IBD + profloc test in an accredited laboratory.

Additionally, hematocrit (HCT), hemoglobin (HGB), and red blood cells (RBC) were determined in the blood of 42-day-old chickens.

Statistical Calculation

Using the STATISTICA PL 10.0 (2010) software, the mean values and pooled standard error of the mean (SEM) of the studied traits were calculated. The analysis of variance was used. The significance of differences between the chickens from the 4 groups was checked using the Tukey test. The significance of differences in blood parameters and antibodies was assessed using the nonparametric analysis of ANOVA and the Wilcoxon test. The P-value for the significant differences was assuming < 0.05.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Broiler chicken carcasses for postmortem analysis was selected from groups whose average body weight was 2,998.9 g ± 342.2 in Group I, 2,973.7 g in Group II, 2,921.3 g in Group III, and 2,830.0 g in Group IV. The average FCR was 1.70, 1.71, 1.69, and 1.76 kg/kg sequentially.

In Table 1, the carcass composition, immune organ weight, and meat quality features are shown. Significantly greater liver weight was found in group II compared to group I (P = 0.020). Groups II, III, and IV showed a significantly lower weight of the bursa of Fabricius than in group I (P < 0.001). The breast muscles of chickens from group IV were characterized by a significantly higher pH value 24 h after slaughter than the I group. Additionally, breast muscle from group I had higher lightness than group II (P < 0.05). The leg muscles of birds from group II showed significantly lower lightness and WHC compared to group I (P < 0.05) (Table 1). According to Wegner et al. (2023), vaccination against infectious bronchitis (IB) in rearing Ross 308 broiler chickens (d 1 or 7) significantly influenced liver weight.

Contrary to our research, Chung et al. (2021) found no changes in the liver weight of Cobb 500 chickens after the administration of different types of vaccines. The authors used a different vaccination protocol that included ND+IB and IBD vaccines administered at various times. In the study by Al-Mayah and Tabeekh (2010), an intermediate vaccine and an intermediate-plus type of vaccine were used on d 14 of rearing Hubbard chickens. The weight of the bursa of Fabricius was significantly lower in the group receiving the intermediate-plus vaccine. As the authors point out, changes in the weight of this organ (after using IBD vaccine intermediate-plus) may result from the degree of attenuation of a given vaccine and the level of maternal antibodies in the chicks.

The pH value of meat results mainly from postslaughter changes and is an essential indicator of meat quality. Wegner et al. (2024) showed significant changes in the pH of the breast muscles of Ross 308 chickens depending on the vaccination program. In the group where the Bioral H -120 (C) + Gallivac IB88 (D) vaccine was administered, on d 7 of the birds' life, a significantly higher pH of the breast muscles was found, associated with higher muscle yellowness.

However, in our research, the significantly lowest pH of the breast muscles could be associated with the significantly highest lightness of the muscles of birds in group I. This parameter also determines changes in the meat's ability to retain water. Low pH increases moisture and meat softness due to more intense water release processes (Göçmen et al., 2023). The presented study did not analyze the pH of the leg muscles. However, the significantly higher WHC of the chicken leg muscles in group I results from increased WHC from the muscle tissue.

Also, the hematocrit and blood morphology of Ross 308 broiler chickens were additionally analyzed, but no graphical presentation was shown. The average RBC in Group I was 2.74 mln/µL, 2.87 mln/µL in Group II, 2.72 mln/µL in Group III, and 3.03 mln/µL in Group IV. At the same time, HGB was at levels 14.22 g/dl, 15.05 g/dl, 14.07 g/dl, and 15.90 g/dl, respectively, and HCT was 36.06%, 38.12%, 34.35%, and 39.48%, respectively.

In the analyzed periods, only birds from group I were protected during the entire rearing period (i.e., from d 14 to d 42). On d 28, the highest level of antibody titers in group I was found, compared to the other groups. Simultaneously, in group III, the level was the lowest than in the other groups (P = 0.021) (Table 2). It could indicate that in the remaining groups, regardless of the vaccine used, its method of administration, or the date of vaccination, there is a so-called immunological gap in wk 4 of bird life. The analysis of the level of antibody titers of the Gumboro disease in our study showed the so-called immunological gap and insufficient protection of all birds around 4 wk of age, except for chickens vaccinated with vaccine C (group I).

Creating such an immunological gap may affect the deterioration of the flock's health status in production. According to Prandini et al. (2008) and Rashid et al. (2013), 3- or 6-wk-old broiler chicken flocks are the most susceptible to Gumboro disease, directly affecting production efficiency. In a study by Prandini et al. (2008), it was observed that the administration of Vaxxitek in ovo vaccine or injectable on the first day of broiler chickens' life resulted in antibody levels in serum on d 28 ranging from 7,000 to 8,000. Our study similarly found comparable levels of Gumboro antibody titers in group I, which was vaccinated with the C vaccine.

In contrast, Sawsan et al. (2013) found the level of antibodies in unvaccinated birds on d 28 - ranging from 713 to 868, which was lower than in our research (Group IV - 1952). The antibody level shown by the authors in the group of unvaccinated birds on d 28 was the level of maternal antibody titers, which decreases with the age of the birds. However, in broiler chickens vaccinated with the Vaxxitek in ovo, the authors found significantly higher titers on d 28 than in our study. In the Ross 308 chickens evaluated in this study, at 42 d of age, the antibody levels in the serum, regardless of the given vaccine, ranged from 12,213 (group IV) to 13,372 (group I). The findings from Prandini et al. (2008) and Rashid et al. (2013) were remarkably consistent. They demonstrated that birds, irrespective of their group affiliations, displayed heightened Gumboro disease antibody levels at the end of the rearing period.

In conclusion, the administration of a single injection vaccination with Vaccine C, 1 dose (0.2 mL per bird) of the reconstituted vaccine, containing the live recombinant virus strain vHVT013-69 (not less than 3.6 - 5.0 log10 PFU), demonstrates a significant positive impact on the Bursa of Fabricius weight for Ross 308 broiler chickens. Exclusive immunization against Gumboro disease (infectious bursal disease) throughout the rearing period was observed only in chickens inoculated with Vaccine C, as evidenced by the antibody titers. Additionally, vaccination programs have the potential to indirectly influence meat quality, leading to observed positive outcomes such as a higher muscle lightness due to the lower pH of breast muscles. However, adverse changes in the water-holding capacity of leg muscles were noted, which may impact the suitability of the meat for processing. Thus, the use of Vaccine C, 1 dose (0.2 mL per bird) of the reconstituted vaccine containing the live recombinant virus strain vHVT013-69 (not less than 3.6 - 5.0 log10 PFU), is shown to have a beneficial effect on the obtained results.

Latent profile analysis of quality of life in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery and its perioperative risk factors
Authors: Xiao Gu, Ping Wang, Xin He, Feng Guo, Yuchen Wang, Ying Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCardiac surgery that uses cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is a complex and important procedure, but few studies have examined the quality of life in patients by population stratification.ObjectiveTo explore the heterogeneity in quality of life and examine its perioperative risk factors in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery, thereby providing a basis for formulating personalized interventions.MethodsThe clinical data of patients who underwent on-pump cardiac surgery between January 2019 and December 2023 were retrospectively collected. From July 2024 to December 2024, postoperative telephone follow-up was conducted and the health-related quality of life (HRQoL) was assessed using the 12-Item Short Form Health Survey Version 2 (SF-12v2) scores. Then, latent profile analysis (LPA) identified characteristic categories of HRQoL. Univariate analysis and multivariate logistic regression were employed to determine perioperative factors associated with HRQoL.ResultsA total of 427 patients were categorized into low (L group, 19.9%), moderate (M group, 54.1%) and high (H group, 26.0%) HRQoL subgroups, with significant intergroup differences (P < 0.01). Logistic regression analysis revealed that perioperative hypertension, ejection fraction (EF) and duration of mechanical ventilation were significantly associated with HRQoL (P < 0.01) among groups. Compared to H group, preoperative atrial fibrillation was significant in M group (P < 0.01), while postoperative myocardial infarction was significant in L group (P < 0.01).ConclusionThe quality of life is heterogeneous among patients after on-pump cardiac surgery. Perioperative cardiovascular and respiratory factors are closely associated with unfavourable outcomes. Targeted monitoring and management of these modifiable factors may therefore improve HRQoL in high-risk populations.Clinical trial registryThe study has been registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400093824).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05827-4.

Introduction

After more than half a century of development, Cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) has become a widely adopted technique in cardiac surgery. With the advancement of surgical technique, clinical practitioners, patients undergoing cardiac surgery and their families are no longer solely concerned with surgical outcomes, such as mortality and survival rates [1, 2]. Instead, there is growing emphasis on the impact of surgical treatment on physical and mental health, as well as social functioning-commonly termed quality of life. Consequently, the improvement in postoperative long-term health-related quality of life (HRQoL) has become a key criterion in evaluating the efficacy of cardiac surgical interventions.

Patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery often present with complex and severe perioperative conditions. Besides, the procedure itself is technically challenging and carries significant risks. The studies have indicated that CPB can lead to significant alterations in physical activity, cognitive function, and emotional well-being after surgery [3, 4]. Some studies have identified numerous perioperative factors that influence the postoperative outcomes of extracorporeal circulation surgery. Nevertheless, the existing studies often frequently neglect to consider population stratification and overlook individual heterogeneity.

Latent profile analysis (LPA) is a person-centered statistical approach that identifies and delineates heterogeneous subgroups within a population based on distinct response patterns to observed variables [5]. By classifying individuals into different latent profiles, LPA enables an in-depth investigation into the characteristics of different subpopulations. The studies have shown that the health status of patients with heart disease could be divided into different models using LPA, which highlights the multidimensionality of health in these patients and supports the development of targeted interventions for patients with different health needs [6, 7]. It is essential that personalized intervention strategies are developed for these patients.

The aim of this study was to explore the association between distinct profiles of HRQoL identified by LPA and perioperative factors in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery. The findings are intended to inform and enhance perioperative clinical management strategies, with the ultimate goal of optimizing both surgical outcomes and quality of life in this patient population.

Methods

Research design

All original clinical results of patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery were manually collected from the clinical information system. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Lianyungang First People's Hospital and had been registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400093824). This study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Inclusion criteria: (1) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade II-IV; (2) Patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery; (3) Age ≥ 18 years; (4) Complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with secondary on-pump cardiac surgery; (2) Patients with neurological or cognitive impairment; (3) Patients with a history of severe allergic reactions; (4) Pregnancy; (5) Patients with chronic renal insufficiency or impaired liver function before operation. Removal criteria: (1) Patients who could not complete the assessment of HRQoL; (2) Other special cases that may potentially affect the statistical results.

HRQoL

12-Item Short Form Health Survey version 2 (SF-12v2) scale was adopted for assessing HRQoL [8, 9]. The questionnaire comprises 12 items, each with 3 to 5 response options, collectively measuring eight health domains: physical functioning, role physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, role emotional, and mental health. The scale is applicable to both general and clinical populations, and is particularly valuable in chronic disease management and rehabilitation evaluation. Scores on the SF-12v2 were collected via telephone follow-up. Given the retrospective nature of this study and the relatively long follow-up period, telephone interviews were chosen to maximize the response rate. Patients who could not be contacted after three attempted calls on separate dates were considered lost to follow-up and excluded from the analysis. To ensure consistency and minimize interviewer bias, we used a standardized interviewer training manual that unified the tone, reinforced the interview rules, and adopted uniform questioning techniques. All interviews were conducted by trained research assistants who were blinded to the patients’ clinical information.

Demographic data and preoperative factors

Demographic data included sex, age and body mass index. Preoperative factors included hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, smoking history, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, ASA physical status classification, New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class, preoperative pulmonary infection and preoperative left ventricular ejection fraction (EF, %).

Intraoperative factors

Intraoperative factors included type of surgery, resuscitation method, use of vasoactive drugs, fluid intake, blood loss, red blood cells transfusion, autologous blood, machine blood, operation time, CPB duration and maximum lactic acid (24 h). Machine blood was defined as the volume of residual blood remaining in the cardiopulmonary bypass circuit after weaning from bypass, which was routinely collected and reinfused into the patient. Maximum lactic acid (24 h) was defined as the highest arterial lactate level recorded within 24 h after surgery.

Postoperative factors

Postoperative short-term prognostic indicators included acute kidney injury requiring continuous renal replacement therapy, maximum vasoactive-inotropic score within 48 h, ventilation time (min), length of stay, intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay, re-intubation, re-sternotomy, in-hospital mortality and postoperative complications.

Statistical analysis

Excel was used to establish the database. Latent profile analysis (LPA) was performed using Mplus 8.3 to classify patients into distinct latent profiles based on their SF-12v2 scores. IBM SPSS 27.0 was used for descriptive statistics, univariate analysis, and multivariate logistic regression.

Continuous variables that followed to the normal distribution were presented as the mean and standard deviation, while those that did not conform to a normal distribution were described as the median and quartile. Categorical variables were expressed in terms of frequency and percentage. For continuous variables, comparison was made using one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Univariate analysis was performed for each variable across the derived HRQoL profiles. Subsequently, the impacts of perioperative factors on the HRQoL of patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery were examined. All variables with a significance level of P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were evaluated for multicollinearity. Using the latent profiles of HRQoL as the dependent variable, variables that showed statistical significance (P < 0.05) in the univariate analysis were incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent risk factors influencing HRQoL after on-pump cardiac surgery.

Results

Description of the patients

The medical records of 513 patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery between January 2019 and December 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. Among the patients, 20 patients did not meet the inclusion criteria. Subsequently, the patients were observed through telephone follow-up from July 2024 to December 2024. Thirty-four patients were lost to follow-up, and 32 patients recorded deaths, including 10 patients in the postoperative period and 22 patients during the follow-up period. Causes of death during follow-up period included cardiac events (n = 14), malignant tumor (n = 1), cerebral hemorrhage (n = 4), septic shock (n = 1), liver failure (n = 1), and accident (n = 1). Latent profile analysis of HRQoL

To identify distinct profiles of HRQoL, LPA was conducted using the eight domains of the SF-12v2 as indicator variables in maples version 8.3. Models specifying one to five latent profiles were estimated and compared. Model fit was evaluated using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and adjusted BIC, with lower values indicating better fit. Classification accuracy was assessed using entropy, which ranges from 0 to 1, and values above 0.80 indicate that more than 90% of individuals are accurately classified into their respective profiles. Comparisons between nested models (e.g., k-profile vs. k-1-profile models) were performed using likelihood ratio tests, specifically the Lo-Mindell-Rubin likelihood ratio test (LMRT) and the bootstrap likelihood ratio test (BLRT). A significant p-value (< 0.05) suggests that the k-profile model provides a better fit to the data than the k-1-profile model.

The results showed that the AIC, BIC and albic values for profiles 1 to 4 gradually decreased as the number of model classifications increased. The 4-profile model had the highest entropy value of 0.906 with all LMRT and BLRT p-values statistically significant, but one of its profiles accounted for only 1.6% of the total sample (Table 1). Generally, each profile should comprise at least 5% of the total sample [10, 11]. Such a small class size raises concerns about statistical stability and limits meaningful clinical interpretation and subsequent analyses. In contrast, the 3-profile model yielded well-balanced class proportions of 19.9%, 54.1%, and 26.0%, with clear gradient patterns across all HRQoL dimensions, offering strong clinical interpretability. Based on a comprehensive evaluation, this study selected the 3-profile model as optimal. The three profiles were labeled as the low HRQoL group (L group), moderate HRQoL group (M group), and high HRQoL group (H group). Multivariate logistic regression of perioperative related factors

In the univariate analysis, collinearity diagnostics were performed for all variables with a significance level of P < 0.05. The results indicated no multicollinearity, as tolerance values exceeded 0.5 and variance inflation factors (VIF) ranged from 1.036 to 3.857. Therefore, all variables with P < 0.05 were retained for further analysis. However, given the violation of the parallel lines test (P < 0.001), ordinal logistic regression was deemed inappropriate. Consequently, multinomial logistic regression was used as the final analytical method.

Multivariate logistic regression with L group vs H group

For the L group, EF was identified as a protective factor (β = −0.187, OR = 0.831, 95% CI: 0.774–0.892). For each 1% increase in EF, the odds of L group classification declined by 16.9%. EF was positively correlated with a greater propensity for assignment to the H group compared with the L group.

In our cohort, a longer ventilation time was correlated with an increased probability of being in the L group (β = 0.036, OR = 1.036, 95% CI: 1.012–1.061). For every 1-min increase in ventilation time, the odds of L group membership rose by 3.6%.

Hypertension was identified as an independent factor associated with diminished odds of experiencing a favorable HRQoL (β = −0.936, OR = 0.392, 95% CI: 0.191–0.803).

Postoperative myocardial infarction demonstrated a strong negative association with HRQoL (β = −14.900, OR = 3.38E-7, 95% CI:3.28E-8–3.49E-6). Discussion

In existing research, the lack of a unified quality of life assessment standard has led to the use of diverse tools, which affects comparability across studies [12, 13]. Sanders et al. [14] primarily used the 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) series of quality of life tools and identified 103 independent predictive factors after cardiac surgery. Nevertheless, there are significant differences across studies in terms of tool selection, timing of assessment, and outcome definitions. In the present study, SF-12v2 was used to assess HRQoL in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery. As a shortened version of the SF-36 [15], SF-12v2 retains conceptual consistency while improving practicality. The previous study reported that lower SF-12 scores in cardiac patients was associated with a 5% increased risk of mortality in the first year after discharge [16]. The validity and reliability of the SF-12v2 has also been well established in Chinese populations [17, 18].

Most previous studies have assessed the quality of life in patients using the total score of the SF-12v2, without accounting for population heterogeneity [19]. To our knowledge, this study is the first to apply LPA to examine HRQoL in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery. Model fitting results supported the presence of heterogeneity in HRQoL in patients, with a 3-profile solution best capturing distinct profiles. Although this study employed a retrospective design and did not measure baseline HRQoL prior to surgery, the results indicate that patients who undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery had lower scores on all dimensions of the SF-12v2 scale, which is consistent with previous findings. Muthukrishnan et al. [20] reported that patients experience improvements in HRQoL after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) compared to their preoperative state, but their outcomes remain lower than those of the general healthy population.

On-pump cardiac surgery serves as a primary intervention for heart disease. While many patients experience relief from preoperative symptoms, improved cardiac function, and a renewed ability to engage in physical activities after surgery, this outcome is not universal [21]. This study identified several factors associated with HRQoL outcomes in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery, including low preoperative EF, hypertension, and prolonged mechanical ventilation time. These findings highlight the need for healthcare providers to recognize and actively manage these risk factors in clinical practice.

Low EF is a well-established predictor of adverse outcomes and is incorporated into all major prognostic scoring systems. The patients with low EF present with more severe baseline characteristics than those with higher EF and are associated with higher rates of postoperative complications and mortality [22]. The results of this study show that patients with a low preoperative EF may be classified into a subgroup with lower HRQoL. The previous study explored the impact of CABG on HRQoL and identified reduced left ventricular function (EF 30–50%) as an independent risk factor for poorer mental HRQoL [23]. There is no doubt that favorable EF is essential to minimize the risk of postoperative mortality and improve the quality of life. Kadric et al. [24] reported that improving the systolic function of the left ventricle benefits quality and length of life.

In this study, preoperative hypertension was associated with classification into a subgroup exhibiting lower HRQoL. This finding is further supported by the research of Sjöland et al. [25], who reported that hypertensive patients demonstrate less improvement in physical component summary scores after CABG compared to non-hypertensive individuals. In contrast, Hermits et al. [26] reported that a history of hypertension seems to have a minor impact on improved well-being five years after coronary surgery. The differing outcomes across various experiments may be related to the baseline characteristics of patients, including age, gender, and the severity of pre-existing conditions. It is important to note that the predictive factors of HRQoL among hypertensive patients are modifiable [27]. The impact of hypertension on HRQoL warrants further investigation, and there is a need for clinicians to integrate its management into comprehensive perioperative practices.

Prolonged mechanical ventilation was identified as an independent risk factor for reduced HRQoL in patients, particularly in the L group. This finding is consistent with previous reports indicating that prolonged mechanical ventilation is strongly associated with adverse postoperative outcomes [28]. It has been demonstrated that CPB itself can induce systemic inflammatory response syndrome, and mechanical ventilation (especially long-term positive pressure ventilation) may aggravate alveolar damage, leading to ventilator-associated lung injury [29]. In addition, the release of inflammatory factors such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α may further impair systemic organ function and delay the rehabilitation process [30]. Prolonged mechanical ventilation is also associated with muscle atrophy and diaphragm dysfunction, which can result in decreased mobility and prolonged hospital stays [31]. Minimizing ventilation duration through optimized perioperative respiratory management may therefore represent a crucial strategy to reduce CPB-induced lung injury and enhance HRQoL.

In addition to the perioperative modifiable indicators mentioned above, this study also identified two factors with event-related characteristics: preoperative atrial fibrillation and postoperative myocardial infarction. The mechanisms by which these two factors impact HRQoL are more complex and warrant further investigation.

Multiple studies involving CABG have indicated that postoperative new-onset atrial fibrillation is associated with increased in-hospital mortality and prolonged length of stay [32]. However, its long-term impact on HRQoL remains less thoroughly investigated. In this study, preoperative atrial fibrillation was found to be associated with an increased likelihood of classification into the M group, while its effect in the L group was not statistically significant. This phenomenon may be attributable to heterogeneity among patients with atrial fibrillation. Those with persistent atrial fibrillation may have adapted to chronic symptoms, whereas individuals with new-onset atrial fibrillation might experience a more pronounced decline in HRQoL due to an acute symptomatic burden.

The majority of patients who experienced a postoperative myocardial infarction in this study were classified into the L group. The large absolute coefficient and the extremely low odds ratio suggest a strong negative association between postoperative myocardial infarction and HRQoL. However, only five patients (1.1%) in this cohort experienced this complication. With such a small number of events, the estimate is statistically unstable and unreliable. Therefore, this finding should be interpreted with caution, and further validation through larger sample sizes or prospective studies is warranted to confirm this relationship.

In this study, factors such as type of cardiac surgery, postoperative complications beyond myocardial infarction, and length of ICU stay were significant in univariate analysis and were entered into the multivariate regression model, but they did not remain statistically significant after adjustment. A possible explanation lies in the nature of the study design. Unmeasured or inadequately controlled confounding factors, such as preoperative functional status and the severity of comorbidities, may be associated with multiple independent variables, thereby masking their independent effects in the multivariate analysis. Additionally, the relatively limited sample size inherent to a single‑center study may have reduced the statistical power to detect independent effects after adjusting for multiple covariates.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, HRQoL was assessed using telephone-administered questionnaires, which may introduce reporting bias. This concern is particularly relevant for the psychological domain of the SF-12v2, as these scores are more susceptible to patients' momentary emotional states and subjective interpretation. It is noted that the direction of bias (likely under-reporting of emotional problems) would bias our estimates toward the null, suggesting that the associations we observed may be conservative. Second, due to the retrospective design, preoperative baseline HRQoL data were unavailable. This prevents distinguishing between postoperative changes and pre-existing patient status. Consequently, the findings represent cross-sectional associations rather than causal inferences. In the future, these findings should be interpreted with caution and validated through larger, multi-center prospective studies that incorporate preoperative baseline HRQoL assessments.

Conclusion

The modifiable factors identified in this study may serve as a basis for developing personalized interventions to improve quality of life. Specifically, patients with low preoperative EF or hypertension should receive increased attention through enhanced perioperative monitoring and personalized care. In addition, efforts to minimize the duration of mechanical ventilation should also be prioritized. These approaches could contribute to improved quality of life in patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery.

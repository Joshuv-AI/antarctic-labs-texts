Intragastric Balloon as Part of a Multidisciplinary Treatment Approach to Obesity-Related Heart Failure
Authors: Dylan Vainer, Juliana Simonetti, Tuan Pham, Lillian Khor
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObesity is a prevalent and under-recognized contributor to heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. We present a unique case of a patient with severe, diuretic-refractory obesity-related heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and cognitive delay who experienced complete resolution of heart failure symptoms following endoscopic gastric balloon placement. This intervention, combined with supervised group living and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) therapy, resulted in a 48.9% total body weight loss and discontinuation of oxygen and loop diuretics. This case underscores the therapeutic potential of endobariatric procedures in patients with significant weight-related comorbidities who are nonsurgical candidates.

INTRODUCTION

It is well known that obesity is one of the most common and clinically relevant phenotypes of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), with specific pathophysiological mechanisms. Over 80% of patients with HFpEF have either overweight or obesity, highlighting the crucial role that obesity plays in the pathogenesis of HFpEF.1 Compared with nonobese patients with HFpEF, patients with obesity demonstrate increased plasma volumes, greater elevation in filling pressures with exercise, and increased pericardial restraint with increased ventricular interdependence.2 Despite increasing recognition, HFpEF remains often neglected as a cardiovascular complication among patients with excessive adiposity, which leads to the underdiagnosis and undertreatment of obesity-related HFpEF3.

Weight loss, whether through lifestyle modifications, pharmacotherapy, or bariatric surgery, should be the target of treatment in patients with obesity-related HFpEF. There are data to suggest that bariatric surgery in patients with obesity and HFpEF improves symptoms and New York Heart Association class, as well as reduces heart failure admissions and reverses left ventricle remodeling.4 In addition, a nationwide analysis has shown that mortality was lower among those with obesity and HFpEF who had undergone bariatric surgery compared to those with obesity and HFpEF who had not undergone surgery.5 The SUMMIT trial was a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial that showed that treatment with tirzepatide, a glucagon-like peptide-1/glucose dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GLP-1/GIP) agonist, led to a lower risk of death from cardiovascular causes or worsening heart failure than placebo in patients with HFpEF and obesity (hazard ratio, 0.62; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.41 to 0.95, P = 0.026).6

Roux-en-Y gastric bypass and laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy are the 2 most common bariatric surgical procedures performed in the United States. While these surgical procedures are considered the most effective and durable treatments of obesity, only 1%–2% of eligible patients undergo surgical management for obesity despite its effectiveness regarding weight loss and obesity-related comorbidities.7 Endobariatric procedures, which include the gastric balloon and endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, have recently emerged as minimally invasive procedural options to fill the gap between medical and surgical treatments of obesity and may be an alternative when there are medication intolerances or contraindications to traditional bariatric surgery. To the best of the author's knowledge, there are no published data in the current literature that analyzes the safety or efficacy of endobariatric procedures on the natural history of obesity-HFpEF. In this study, we present a case of a 39-year-old male patient with a history of cognitive delay and diuretic-refractory obesity-related HFpEF who underwent endoscopic gastric balloon placement, which along with addressing his social determinants of health and medical treatment, led to a significant amount of weight loss and resolution of his clinical heart failure syndrome and recurrent heart failure admissions.

In addition to medical, surgical, and endoscopic treatments, understanding the context of a patient's socioeconomic background is crucial to adequately addressing the health consequences of obesity. The obesity epidemic has the greatest prevalence among socially disadvantaged groups and underrepresented persons, including ethnic/racial minorities and individuals with cognitive delay and lower socioeconomic backgrounds.8 Interventions to address the socioeconomic challenges of patients with obesity include culinary medicine classes, which have been shown to increase knowledge of healthy food choices, improve food preparation self-efficacy, and enhance healthy dietary behaviors.9 This case highlights the importance of addressing a patient's social background to optimize their chance for sustainable weight loss success.

CASE REPORT

A 39-year-old man with a medical history of significant cognitive delay, class III obesity (body mass index [BMI] 70), obesity-related HFpEF, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and obstructive sleep apnea had been admitted 9 times to the hospital for heart failure exacerbation and volume overload over a period of 16 months. The diagnosis of HFpEF was based on both clinical and echocardiographic/hemodynamic criteria. The patient experienced symptoms of chronic dyspnea on exertion, lower extremity edema, and orthopnea. An echocardiogram was notable for an ejection fraction of 71%, mild left ventricular hypertrophy, and a severely enlarged right ventricle with normal systolic function. A right heart catheterization performed during the ninth hospital admission showed elevated right atrial pressure (14 mm Hg) and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (20 mm Hg) with normal resting cardiac output (6.7 L/min). At home, the patient was on 6 liters of oxygen chronically while taking very significant doses of diuretics including metolazone 10 mg, spironolactone 200 mg, canagliflozin 100 mg, and bumetanide 4 mg TID to maintain euvolemia. Over that period, the patient's baseline weight fluctuated between 460 and 490 pounds. Of note, the patient had completed a neurocognitive evaluation during this time which concluded that he had a broad independence score of 68, which is equivalent to the developmental age of a 14-year-old. The patient's cognitive delay led to poor dietary compliance and medication adherence, which predisposed him to repeated episodes of volume overload. He had been living alone in his own apartment despite a limited ability to care for himself, and his baseline cognitive status predisposed him to binge eating episodes of very high caloric, low nutritious foods. In addition, his poor functional status and impaired mobility due to severe obesity limited his ability to take loop diuretics or attend cardiac rehab.

The patient was not considered to be a bariatric surgical candidate due to his decompensated heart failure. Therefore, the decision was made to pursue endoscopic gastric balloon placement to facilitate weight loss and hopefully reduce the patient's high diuretic and oxygen requirements. The patient underwent gastric balloon placement with an Orbera Intragastric Balloon System (Boston Scientific). The balloon was initially filled with 600 mL of saline and 10 mL of methylene blue. Shortly thereafter, the patient started losing weight rapidly and his weight loss continued at a steady rate for approximately 2 years, until his weight plateaued at approximately 280 pounds. One month after the balloon placement, the patient was also made a legal ward of the state (due to an inability to care for self in the setting of cognitive delay and multiple medical issues) and placed in a supervised group home where his medications, sodium, and caloric intake were administered and supervised by nursing staff. The group home also led to lifestyle coaching including cooking, supervised exercise, and occupational training. Importantly, the patient no longer had easy access to large amounts of highly processed and caloric foods that he previously had access to in his apartment. The culinary medicine program at the group home taught him how to prepare meals at home and limit salt through “medically tailored” meals that gave him the skills to make sustainable behavioral changes.

The patient was started on weekly semaglutide injections approximately 20 months after balloon placement to help maintain his ongoing weight loss, which was then transitioned transiently to tirzepatide following a lapse in insurance coverage. The patient achieved his weight loss nadir of 251 pounds 2 and a half years after the initial procedure while off all weight loss medications, which represented a 48.9% total body weight loss compared to his peak weight of 490 pounds just prior to the gastric balloon placement. His lowest BMI after all interventions was 36, down from a peak BMI of 70. The patient was admitted for heart failure exacerbation only one time after his gastric balloon procedure, which was a vast improvement from the previous 16 months, when he had been admitted 9 times (Figure 1). An echocardiogram obtained near the time of his lowest weight was notable for a significant decrease in estimated pulmonary arterial pressure and improvement in right ventricular dilation compared with his echocardiogram 5 years prior. In addition, the patient has since been able to wean off oxygen completely and discontinue all loop diuretics while maintaining euvolemia by only taking a low dose of spironolactone (Table 1).

DISCUSSION

We present the case of a patient with a history of long-standing, severe obesity-related HFpEF whose heart failure syndrome and significant diuretic and oxygen requirements completely resolved after losing nearly 50% of his total body weight with a combination of gastric balloon placement, GLP-1/GIP therapy, and improvement in living situation. This study highlights the importance of weight loss as the central target of therapy in patients with obesity and HFpEF. Until the recent SUMMIT trial, there had been no specific medical therapy that had been shown to reduce cardiovascular mortality in patients with obesity-related HFpEF. Despite this, obesity remains an under-recognized and undertreated comorbidity in HFpEF.

The most recent American College of Cardiology guidelines on the management of HFpEF recommend that patients with HFpEF and a BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 be referred to a multidisciplinary team of medical, surgical, and nutritional experts in obesity.10 In patients who are not surgical candidates, consideration of a GLP-1/GIP agonist such as tirzepatide is strongly recommended given the beneficial results of the SUMMIT trial. While bariatric surgery is still the most effective treatment modality for patients with obesity, the development of endobariatrics may offer patients a less invasive approach than traditional surgery. These procedures, which include space-occupying devices and gastric remodeling procedures, may be an option for patients who are reluctant to undergo surgery or who are poor surgical candidates. In the case of this patient, he was declined for bariatric surgery because of his decompensated heart failure but was able to safely undergo gastric balloon placement.

The Orbera Intragastric Balloon is the most frequently used intragastric balloon approved for the treatment of obesity. It is indicated for use as an adjunct to weight reduction for adults with obesity with BMI ≥ 30 and < 40 kg/m2, specifically in those who have previously failed lifestyle interventions including supervised diet, exercise, and behavior modification programs.11 In the case of this patient, the procedure was performed off-label given that his BMI was far higher than the recommended indication of BMI < 40. In addition, there is a relative contraindication of serious psychiatric illness that can compromise patient understanding of or compliance with follow-up visits and removal of device after 6 months. There was concern that this patient's developmental delay could inhibit his ability to comply with recommended postprocedural dietary modifications. However, given the severity of his illness and lack of response to high-dose diuretics and inability to undergo bariatric surgery, the decision was made to offer an endobariatric procedure that had a more favorable safety profile.

This case study is unique in that it is the first in the literature to highlight the potential safety and efficacy of the gastric balloon procedure in patients with obesity-related HFpEF. While the gastric balloon should not receive all the credit for this patient's dramatic weight loss and subsequent improvement in cardiovascular health, it was the initial intervention in the patient's course to change the trajectory of his heart failure history. The mechanisms by which the balloon initiated improvement likely include restrictive, neurohormonal, and alteration of gastric emptying. The balloon promotes the feeling of satiation and uses restriction as a mechanism for weight loss and possibly decreases the release of ghrelin, which is an orexigenic hormone.12 In addition, delayed gastric emptying leads to an increase in the length of satiation.13 Furthermore, the patient's change in environment to a supervised group home in which he had access to and education regarding healthy food and meal preparation further sustained his weight loss and thus facilitated his recovery from heart failure. Supervised lifestyle coaching that focused on heart failure-specific dietary restrictions, healthy nutrition, and assistance with medication regimen were crucial to this patient's stabilization and recovery. Long-term therapy with semaglutide and then tirzepatide were also very helpful to reduce the patient's binge eating tendencies and thus restrict his caloric intake. This patient's success was in part due to the unique collaborative nature of a comprehensive weight loss program, which included specialists in obesity medicine, cardiology, cardiac rehab, and gastroenterology.

There is tremendous potential in the use of endobariatric procedures to treat patients with obesity-related comorbidities, including HFpEF, who are poor surgical candidates or unwilling to undergo traditional bariatric surgery. Unfortunately, the availability and uptake of bariatric endoscopy procedures as a tool for weight loss by physicians remains suboptimal. Lack of familiarity, concern about the relative novelty of the field when compared with bariatric surgery and antiobesity pharmacotherapy, and high costs combined with poor insurance coverage are significant barriers that have affected the growth of bariatric endoscopy procedures as a primary option for weight loss.14 These factors limit the overall generalizability of the approach that was taken in this patient's unique case.

Overall, this case highlights the complex, multifactorial nature of obesity compounded by socioeconomic limitation and cognitive impairment and emphasizes the need for a multidisciplinary comprehensive program to optimally address the cardiometabolic consequences of obesity. The gastric balloon served as the initiating step in a comprehensive and multidisciplinary approach that involved endoscopic, medical, nutritional, and social interventions which ultimately helped the patient achieve a healthier weight and improve his cardiometabolic health. Moving forward, there is a need for randomized controlled trials that evaluate the use of endoscopic balloons and other endobariatric procedures as a potential treatment for obesity-related HFpEF and other obesity-related comorbidities in patients who are high-risk surgical candidates.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide.1 In patients who underwent radical surgery, about 50% were diagnosed as stage I disease.2 Surgery including lobectomy and systematic lymph node dissection (LND) is the standard initial therapy even in early-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).3 Systematic LND could remove occult metastasis, thus provide accurate staging and improve survival.4 However, radical LND also leads to more surgical trauma and prolonged operation time. An alternative approach is selective LND, which is derived from the lobe-specific lymphatic drainage pattern.5 The ground glass nodules (GGNs) account for a large part of stage IA NSCLC and the lymph node metastasis rate is as low as 0.2%–1.3%.6 7 Studies demonstrate that there is rare lymph node metastasis in NSCLC manifesting as GGNs when the consolidation-to-tumour (C/T) ratio is ≤0.5.6–8 Previous studies have explored the LND strategy in part of these patients. JCOG1211 is a non-randomised trial to confirm the efficacy of a segmentectomy for clinical T1N0 lung cancer with dominant ground glass opacity (2–3 cm, C/T 0.25–0.5), in which mediastinal LND is not mandatory.9 Though there could be no necessity of mediastinal LND in these surgeries, there is no relative guideline or evidence from randomised trials.

Therefore, we have conducted a phase III trial to evaluate the non-inferiority in overall survival (OS) of spared mediastinal LND compared with systematic mediastinal LND in patients with stage IA NSCLC manifesting as C/T ≤ 0.5 GGNs. The study is a non-inferior statistical comparison, with 681 patients in each group. The anticipated enrol period is 2–3 years, followed by an observation period of 5 years.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

The aim of this study is to evaluate the non-inferiority in OS of non-mediastinal LND compared with mediastinal LND in patients with stage IA NSCLC manifesting as C/T ≤ 0.5 GGNs. A schematic of the study flow is provided in figure 1. The study is being conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013). The study was approved by the Ethic Review Board of Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital (approval number E2020306 and E20220838), and informed consent will be obtained from all individual participants. The study was started in May 2022 and the planned end date is May 2029. The current protocol version is 1.1, version date is May 2023.

Patients and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

Inclusion criteria

A two-step registration strategy is applied because the histological type of the GGNs is to be confirmed intraoperatively in most patients. The inclusion criteria is: (1) Thin-slice HRCT (High-resolution computed tomography) shows single GGN with C/T ≤ 0.5 and no lymph node metastasis; (2) Clinical stage IA NSCLC (TNM eighth classification) diagnosed prior or in surgery; (3) No history of malignancies within past 5 years or lung surgery and (4) No anticancer treatment prior to surgery. Definition of ground glass and solid components on thin-slice CT images were as follows: a window level of 600–700 Hounsfield units and a window width of 1500–2000 Hounsfield units. Solid components were defined as areas of increased opacification that completely obscured the underlying vascular structures on thin-slice CT. Ground glass components were defined as areas of increased hazy density that did not obscure the underlying vascular structure.

Exclusion criteria

Patients are excluded if they meet any of the following criteria: (1) simultaneous or metachronous (within the past 5 years) double cancers; (2) active bacterial or fungous infection; (3) interstitial pneumonitis, pulmonary fibrosis or severe pulmonary emphysema; (4) systemic steroidal medication; (5) uncontrollable diabetes mellitus and (6) uncontrollable hypertension or history of severe heart disease, heart failure.

Randomisation and intervention

After the confirmation of NSCLC and negative hilar lymph node by intraoperative frozen section, patients are randomised in each group using a computer-generated randomisation scheme with permuted block size maintained by a centralised randomisation system. Researcher of the study centre will generate the allocation sequence with enrolled sequential number. The mediastinal LND group is defined as the experimental group and the spared mediastinal LND group is defined as the control group. In the experimental group, lobectomy or sublobectomy with systematic hilar and mediastinal LND is performed. The area of mediastinal LND includes stations 2R, 4R,7, 8 and 9 for right lung tumours, 4L, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 for left lung tumours according to the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer node map. In the control group, lobectomy or sublobectomy with hilar LND is performed. Dissection of interlobar, peribronchial and segmental lymph nodes is not mandatory in both arms.

Safety assessment

Any unfavourable medical occurrence in a participant that was judged as related to a trial procedure was defined as an adverse event (AE), irrespective of whether the events were related to the study intervention. All potential AEs were monitored under the supervision of the Institutional Ethical Review Committee during the entire trial. Subjects could withdraw from the trial at any time if they could not tolerate treatment. AEs were recorded in all the subjects.

Outcomes

The primary outcome is OS time defined as days from randomisation to death for any patient. The censored data will be labelled as days from randomisation to the end of follow-up if the patient is alive at the end of follow-up. The secondary outcomes include relapse-free survival (RFS) survival, proportion of local recurrence, duration of hospitalisation, duration of chest drainage tube placement, operation time, blood loss. RFS time is defined as days from randomisation to relapse or death for any patient.

Data collection and management

Research data were collected, recorded and managed by four researchers based on good clinical practice guidelines. Clinical data of participants from four time points were collected: first, baseline data, including age, sex, height, weight and so on, were extracted before surgery; second, intraoperative data, including the timing and duration of general anaesthesia, operating time, surgical approach, pattern of lymph nodes dissection and intraoperative complications were collected during the operation; third, postoperative data, including pathological stages, lymph node metastasis, duration of hospitalisation, duration of chest tube placement, operation time, blood loss and early postoperative complications, were gathered 2 weeks after surgery, these data will be provided in the interim analysis; and forth, follow-up data, including the condition of recurrence or metastasis, survival status were recorded every 6 months postsurgery. Research data will be audited yearly by researchers of each study centre.

Follow-up

All randomised patients are followed up for at least 5 years. Tumour markers and chest CT are evaluated at least every 6 months during the first 2 years and at least every 12 months for the duration of follow-up.

Sample size

According to previous studies on stage IA subsolid and pure GGNs, the 5-year survival rate is 87.6%–100%.10 11 We assumed an expected 5-year OS rate of 90% in the mediastinal LND group and an anticipated accrual time of 2 years and a follow-up time of 5 years. Based on a one-sided significance level of 5%, a power of 80%, a non-inferiority margin of 5% for 5-year OS rate and a drop-out rate of 5%, the total sample size was set at 1362 patients (681 per arm) by PASS V.11.0 software.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data will be described with means and SDs when they follow normal distribution, otherwise, they will be described with median and quartile. Qualitative data will be described with incidence and percentage. The difference of OS and RFS between non-mediastinal LND group and mediastinal LND group will be assessed by the log-rank test. HR associated with OS and RFS will be estimated by the multivariate Cox regression model. The difference of proportion of local recurrence between two groups will be tested by the χ2 test. The duration of hospitalisation, duration of chest drainage tube placement, operation time and blood loss between two groups will be compared by t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. A p<0.05 indicates statistically a significant difference.

Study centres

The study centres and anticipated number of enrolment are: Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital (712), Tangdu Hospital (300), Liaoning Cancer Hospital and Institute (200), Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (150). Primary investigators of the study centres will have access to the final trial dataset.

Ethics and dissemination

The Protocol Review Committee of the Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital approved this original and revised version of the protocol in October 2020 and August 2022, and signed informed consent is collected from all participants before enrolment. The data set used during the study will be available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. The results will be disseminated in peer-reviewed journals and will be presented in academic conferences as well.

Limitation and potential problems of the study

The present study is a multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial. The primary outcome is OS, if the non-inferiority of primary outcome is observed, the control group would be more favourable considering reduced operation trauma. A limitation is the long follow-up period to observe 5-year OS, which may lead to some drop-out of patients. Another problem we may encounter is that more and more therapeutic options are available for NSCLC, including small molecular target drugs, immunotherapy. Though 5-year OS is still the gold standard to assess prognosis, 5 years may not be enough for early-stage NSCLC in the future.

Targeted risk assessment of maximum residue levels for nicotine in spices
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA received a mandate from the European Commission to perform a targeted risk assessment of maximum residue levels (MRLs) for nicotine in spices. EFSA performed the acute (short‐term) and chronic (long‐term) dietary risk assessment considering the nicotine exposure via residues in spices at the level equal to the temporary maximum residue level (tMRL) of 0.3 mg/kg as proposed by the European Commission based on the new monitoring data provided. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the tMRL for the group of spices of 0.3 mg/kg is unlikely to pose a risk to consumer health.Keywords: nicotine, spices, pesticide, MRL, consumer risk assessment

Background

Nicotine is the main alkaloid in tobacco and other tobacco species and is found in low concentrations also in other crops belonging to the family of Solanaceae. Due to its action as an agonist for acetylcholine receptors, nicotine exhibits insecticidal activity and the compound was used as an active substance in plant protection products in the past.

The use of nicotine as an insecticide was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 1 . Based on the Draft Assessment Report (DAR) prepared by the Rapporteur Member State, the European Commission concluded that the existing evidence is not sufficient to demonstrate a safe use of nicotine as a plant protection product with respect to operators, workers, bystanders and consumers. Thus, the evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC resulted in a decision not to include nicotine in Annex I to that Directive (Decision 2009/9/EC 2 ) and all plant protection products containing nicotine as an active substance had to be withdrawn from the market by 8 June 2009. At that time, no specific maximum residue levels (MRLs) for nicotine were set, nor was that substance included in Annex IV to Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 3 , and therefore, the default MRL of 0.01 mg/kg applied to all products.

In 2009 and 2011, the European Commission asked EFSA to provide advice on the setting of temporary MRLs (tMRLs) for nicotine for a number of commodities, in which residue levels higher than the default MRL of 0.01 mg/kg were repeatedly identified in the framework of controls performed by food business operators and/or national competent authorities (EFSA, 2009, 2011).

Based on the assessment of EFSA, specific tMRLs for nicotine were set in Annex IIIA of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 for wild fungi (Commission Regulation (EU) No 765/2010 4 ) and for rose hips, herbs and edible flowers, teas, herbal infusions and spices (Commission Regulation (EU) No 812/2011 5 ).

The source of these residues in the commodities concerned has not been elucidated. Contaminations during harvest, drying, storage or transport were considered as possible sources of nicotine residues in the concerned products. Other explanations for the contamination were suggested, e.g. cultivation of the crops in fields previously used for tobacco cultivation or the natural occurrence of nicotine in certain plant products.

However, as scientific evidence was not conclusive to demonstrate whether nicotine occurs naturally in the concerned crops, risk managers decided to review the tMRLs after 10 years, taking into account additional information that became available in this period.

Those tMRLs set in 2010 and 2011 were revised based on data provided by EFSA and food business operators by October 2021 (EFSA, 2022, 2023), which showed that while residues of nicotine still occurred in those products at levels higher than the limits of determination (LODs), the residue levels had decreased. Taking into account EFSA's conclusions and based on monitoring data, Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/377 6 established new tMRLs for nicotine. That Regulation also provided for the established tMRLs for nicotine to be reviewed based on monitoring data.

New information was provided to the European Commission by the European Union Reference Laboratories (EURLs), who noted that an LOD of 0.05 mg/kg may be more appropriate for seed spices and fruit spices, and that provided new data on cinnamon showing that higher residues may be expected in this product. Based on the above, Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1536 7 established new MRLs at 0.05 mg/kg for fruit and seed spices (LOD) and at 0.2 mg/kg for cinnamon. Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1536 shall apply from 15 September 2023. This regulation also provided for the established tMRLs for nicotine to be reviewed based on monitoring data.

Recently, new monitoring data were provided by Third Countries and by some food business operators, indicating that the new levels for nicotine in spices may be inadequate and that higher levels may be necessary. Based on those data, the setting of an MRL at the level of 0.3 mg/kg for nicotine in spices might be appropriate. Before proposing the raising of the tMRLs for spices established by Regulation (EU) 2023/1536, the European Commission requests EFSA to assess the potential risk for EU consumers deriving from this (higher) MRL.

Terms of Reference

EFSA is requested, according to Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005:

to assess the acute (short‐term) risk for European consumers related to nicotine exposure via residues in spices (0800000) at the level derived by the European Commission based on the new monitoring data received (i.e., 0.3 mg/kg for spices). In addition, the contribution of nicotine residues in spices at the level of 0.3 mg/kg to the overall chronic (long‐term) exposure of European consumers should be assessed. The exposure/risk assessment shall be performed with the newest version of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo), based on the currently applicable residue definition which comprises only parent nicotine, using the acute reference dose and acceptable daily intake derived by EFSA (2009);

Assessment

EFSA performed the targeted short‐ and long‐term dietary risk assessment related to nicotine exposure considering the proposed tMRL of 0.3 mg/kg for all spices listed in Annex I to Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 under the food code 800000, except horseradish (dry) (commodity code 840040) and ginger (dry) (commodity code 840020), as for these two commodities, the MRLs established for fresh horseradish (commodity code 213040) are applicable, considering a dehydration factor which takes into account that drying is expected to lead to an increase of the residue levels in the processed (dried) products. In addition, other MRLs set at a level above the limit of quantification (LOQ) were taken into account in the chronic risk assessment. A no residues situation is assumed for the remaining commodities.

The PRIMo calculation spreadsheet is a key supporting document to this statement, which is made publicly available as a background document.

Residue levels and residue definition

The enforcement residue definition established in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 comprises the parent compound nicotine only. As no specific residue definition for risk assessment has been established in the EU, the risk assessment is performed for parent nicotine only, assuming that spices contain no nicotine‐related metabolites or degradation products.

In 2011, tMRLs for nicotine in spices were established by Commission Regulation (EU) No 812/2011, based on a request of food business operators who provided evidence that spices often contain residues greater than the default MRL of 0.01 mg/kg. EFSA assessed the available monitoring data and gave a reasoned opinion on the setting of tMRLs for nicotine in spices and the other products concerned (EFSA, 2011). EFSA concluded that the modifications to the MRLs requested by the food business operators (i.e. 0.3 mg/kg for seed and fruit spices and 4 mg/kg for the remaining spice groups) were acceptable with regard to consumer safety. As the origin of these residues was not clear, the MRLs were set on temporary basis, requesting new data/information or any scientific evidence on the natural occurrence or formation of nicotine in the concerned crops.

In 2023, based on monitoring data available from pesticide controls, risk managers decided to lower the MRLs for spices by Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/377. For seed spices and fruit spices, tMRL were set at the level of 0.02 mg/kg; for bark spices, root and rhizome spices, bud spices, flower pistil spices and aril spices tMRLs were lowered to 0.07 mg/kg.

These tMRLs for spices were revised again in 2023 by Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1536, considering information on the appropriate LOQ that can be achieved in routing pesticide monitoring; in addition, new monitoring data on cinnamon were provided to the European Commission which demonstrated that for this commodity a tMRL of 0.2 mg/kg is appropriate.

Consumer risk assessment

The chronic and acute exposure calculations were performed using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the Internationally Estimated Short‐Term Intake (IESTI) methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

As requested in the Terms of Reference, the assessment was performed for the residue definition which covers nicotine only and the ADI of 0.0008 mg/kg bw per day and the ARfD of 0.0008 mg/kg bw (EFSA, 2009).

Chronic (long‐term) dietary risk assessment

EFSA performed a chronic consumer risk assessment, considering the proposed tMRLs for spices (i.e. 0.3 mg/kg) and all other MRLs set at a level above the LOQ, assuming that all these commodities consumed by European consumers contain residues at the level of the tMRL/proposed tMRL. A no residues situation is assumed for the remaining commodities.

For ginger and horseradish (commodity code 840020 and 840040), the exposure calculations were performed with the tMRL established from fresh horseradish (commodity code 213040) (i.e. 0.01* mg/kg), applying a default dehydration factor of 7 to recalculate the tMRL from the fresh to the dried product. For capers, EFSA made an adjustment and re‐calculated the MRL proposal of 0.3 mg/kg which refers to dried capers to fresh capers, as the consumption data for capers implemented in EFSA PRIMo rev. 3.1 most likely refer to fresh capers (EFSA, 2022). Hence, the equivalent residue concentration for fresh capers was derived by applying the drying factor of 4.5. Hence, the calculations are performed with an input value of 0.067 mg/kg.

The chronic exposure related to the consumption of spices accounts for up to 10% of the ADI, with the highest exposure identified for German children. The highest individual long‐term exposure was calculated for vanilla pods (0.75% for German Children); for the remaining spices, the chronic exposure accounted for less than 0.5% of the ADI, respectively.

Acute (short‐term) dietary risk assessment

The acute consumer risk assessment was performed for spices, assuming that all spices except ginger, horseradish and capers contain residues at the level of 0.3 mg/kg. For ginger, horseradish and capers the same assumptions as described for chronic exposure assessment were applied.

Among all spices, the highest acute exposure was calculated for fennel seed representing 29% of the ARfD. The second highest result was obtained for vanilla pods (23% of the ARfD); for the remaining spices, the exposure was below 10% of the ARfD. Among the processed commodities, the highest acute exposure was identified for ginger jam (26% of the ARfD).

The detailed results of the chronic and acute risk calculations derived with PRIMo rev. 3.1 are presented in Appendix A.

Conclusions and recommendations

Overall, the exposure calculations performed by EFSA ascertained that the proposed tMRL for nicotine for spices at level of 0.3 mg/kg are unlikely to pose an unacceptable risk to consumer health.

There was no need to advise risk managers on alternative MRLs for spices, as outlined in the second bullet point of the Terms of Reference, as EFSA did not identify an unacceptable risk for the proposed tMRL of 0.3 mg/kg for spices.

However, EFSA recommends investigating the root cause for the occurrence of nicotine in the commodities concerned, and if possible, develop strategies to avoid or reduce nicotine residues in spices, in view of a possible lowering of the tMRLs in future.

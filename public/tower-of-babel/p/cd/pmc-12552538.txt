Development of a modified QuEChERS-UPLC-MS/MS method based on multi-walled carbon nanotubes for 27 pesticide residues followed by determination of the residue levels and dietary intake risk assessment in Chrysanthemum morifolium Ramat☆
Authors: Jiazhao Ruan, Gaotian Li, Yang Wang, Aoxiang Li, Jiasheng Wang, Hongxia Niu, Yuan Zhou, Xiangjun Yin, Xuming Ji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA modified QuEChERS purification liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) method was designed to identify 27 pesticide residues in Chrysanthemum morifolium Ramat (C. morifolium). The preparation process was optimized, using carboxylated multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) as the single-category sorbents. Matrix-matched calibration curves were adopted for identifying the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 1.62–4.68 μg·kg−1. This method showed high pesticide recoveries at three spiked levels, from 71.6 % to 116.7 %, with the relative standard deviations less than 10 %. By analyzing 40C. morifolium samples, omethoate and epoxiconazole exceeded the maximum residue levels, and their transfer rates to the infusion were estimated to range from 9.4 % to 25.4 %. The two detected pesticides pose a negligible risk to consumer health, with the hazard quotients less than 0.85 %. Moreover, this study establishes an optimized QuEChERS-UPLC-MS/MS method to identify multiclass residues, enhancing the understanding of the potential exposure risk of omethoate and epoxiconazole in C. morifolium.Keywords: Chrysanthemum morifolium Ramat, QuEChERS, Carboxylated multi-walled carbon nanotubes, UPLC-MS/MS, Pesticide residues, Risk assessment

Introduction

Pesticides refer to a category of chemical substances in agriculture and are used in various stages of crop cultivation to safeguard against pest infestations (Riedo et al., 2022). Nevertheless, the extensive and improper application of pesticides has resulted in their entry into the human body based on the food chain, which can potentially threaten the health profiles of developing societies (Schleiffer & Speiser, 2022).

Chrysanthemum morifolium Ramat (C. morifolium), belonging to “Zhebawei”, is widely cultivated in Zhejiang province, China. As a traditional “drug homologous food” plant, C. morifolium has a long tradition of consumption and is included in both the “List of Items that Are Both Food and Medicine” issued by the Ministry of Health of the People's Republic of China and the Chinese Pharmacopoeia (National Pharmacopoeia Commission, 2020). Due to the unique geographical environment and distinctive flavor, C. morifolium has an extensive range of uses, including beverages, herbal teas, and seasonings (Liu et al., 2024). As beverages containing C. morifolium extracts have been the popular drinks, related pharmacological studies primarily focus on C. morifolium, indicating that C. morifolium may possess various therapeutic activities such as anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties (Ma et al., 2021; Ojha et al., 2024). In recent years, the significantly growing demand for C. morifolium has greatly increased the planting and breeding of C. morifolium (Lu et al., 2021).

Nevertheless, the recurrent detection of pesticide residues in chrysanthemum products has drawn great concern (Gomes et al., 2020; Leong et al., 2020). Because of the extended flowering period between September and November, chrysanthemums have a high sensitivity to pest and fungal diseases, which include leaf folding insects, spider mites, and gray mold (Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022). During the growth of C. morifolium prior to harvest, fungicides and insecticides are extensively used to minimize crop losses (Safdar et al., 2022). Currently, the USA and industrialized countries in the European Union have enacted relevant regulations to establish maximum residue levels (MRLs) in chrysanthemums. MRLs of just five pesticides are determined in dry chrysanthemums in China (The State Health Commission et al., 2021). China and other chrysanthemum-cultivating countries are still confronted with significant gaps and insufficient regulations when compared with developed countries. Therefore, it is essential to formulate accurate protocols to determine several pesticide residues, which can maximally minimize health risks.

The analytical methods for detecting multi-pesticide residues predominantly include gas chromatography (GC), gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS), gas chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (GC–MS/MS), gas chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (GC-HRMS), liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), and liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS) (Syrgabek & Alimzhanova, 2022). Nevertheless, methods that depend on gas chromatographic separation are not appropriate for the analysis of compounds that are known to be challenging to vaporize. Although LC-HRMS is a robust technique, it is often relatively costly and mainly used for analyte screening purposes. By contrast, LC-MS/MS provides exceptional sensitivity, low detection limits, and accurate qualitative and quantitative analyses, rendering it the preferred method for determining a wide array of pesticide residues (Greer et al., 2021). Recently, numerous sample pretreatment methods for pesticide residues in “drug homologous food” plants have been proposed, which include accelerated solvent/micro-wave assisted/solid-phase extraction, and Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged, and Safe (QuEChERS) extraction (Fu et al., 2019; Jin et al., 2019; Tsaltaki et al., 2019). Among these methods, QuEChERS extraction is proposed and used in a lot of plant parts including leaves and flowers because it is efficient, simple, and cost-effective. Octadecylsilane (C18), secondary amine (PSA) and graphitized carbon black (GCB) are most frequently utilized purification agents for classical QuEChERS (Jawad et al., 2020; Li et al., 2016). However, due to the physicochemical restrictions of these agents and the increasing spectrum of sample matrices and analytical targets, the pretreatment with classical QuEChERS fails to always satisfy the analytical needs (Majed et al., 2024). Therefore, it is vital to introduce new materials that improve the applicability of classical QuEChERS to enhance its performance (Shahsavani & Fakhari, 2024). Multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), which have high surface area, great affinity to polycyclic compounds, generation of favorable π–π stacking interactions, and great porosity, arouse considerable concern from researchers (Musarurwa et al., 2019). Owing to the special characteristics, MWCNTs are beneficial for the construction of biosensors or electrochemical sensors, and can serve as sorbents or supports to investigate various analytes in the fields of food, biology and environment (Zhou et al., 2023).

As reported in some studies, pesticides are transported in the process of tea brewing (Tascone et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2019). Therefore, it is essential to consider the varying transfer rates of pesticides during C. morifolium brewing. Factors influencing transfer behavior are categorized into two types: pesticide physicochemical qualities and brewing conditions (Devriendt-Renault et al., 2024). With constant physicochemical quality of pesticides, brewing conditions are the determining factor for the transfer rates. In addition, it is well known that brewing conditions are closely associated with dietary consumption, mainly including brewing temperature and brewing time. An increase in brewing duration and brewing temperature significantly enhances pesticide residue transfer in black and green tea (Szternfeld et al., 2023). However, it remains unclear concerning whether different pesticide transfer behaviors are determined by brewing time or temperature in C. morifolium infusion.

This study was aimed at establishing a novel QuEChERS method featuring high-effectiveness and sensitivity for C. morifolium samples, using MWCNTs as the single-category sorbent (a departure from conventional multi-sorbent combinations) and integrating UPLC-MS/MS for detecting 27 pesticide residues. The core scientific novelty lies in: 1) demonstrating that MWCNTs alone can achieve comparable or superior adsorption efficiency to traditional mixed sorbents (PSA, GCB, C18) for different pesticide classes (organochlorines, organophosphorus, etc.); and 2) introducing structure-modified MWCNTs to simultaneously enhance selectivity toward polar and non-polar pesticides. In addition, the pesticides were quantitatively determined and the health risks in its dietary consumption were evaluated. By quantifying residues and evaluating dietary health risks, our findings offer vital data support for pesticide dietary exposure and risk assessment in the commercialization of C. morifolium.

Materials and methods

Reagents and chemicals

Abamectin, acetamiprid, bifenthrin, carbendazim, 3-hydroxy carbofuran, carbofuran, clothianidin, diniconazole, emamectin benzoate, epoxiconazole, fenthion, fenthion sulfoxide, fenthion sulfone, fipronil, fipronil sulfide, fipronil desulfinyl, fipronil sulfone, imidacloprid, iprodione, isofenphos-methyl, myclobutanil, omethoate, propamocarb, propargite, pyraclostrobin, thiamethoxam, and thiophanate-methyl mixed standard stock solutions (100 μg·mL−1) were of high purity (≥98 %) and offered by Agricultural Environmental Protection Institution (Agricultural Environmental Protection Institution, Tianjin, China). Stock solutions were firstly prepared in methanol and then stored at −20 °C. Calibration curves were generated via serial dilution using solvent or matrix-matched standards.

Acetonitrile, acetic acid (AA), ammonium formate, formic acid (FA), methanol, and n-hexane (LC-MS grade) were purchased from Thermo Fischer Scientific (Thermo Fischer Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Ultrapure water was acquired from a local laboratory with a Milli-Q purification system (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). QuEChERS extraction salts, containing sodium acetate (NaOAc) and anhydrous magnesium sulfate (MgSO4), were offered by Sigma-Aldrich (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA). Dispersive solid-phase extraction (d-SPE), including PSA, GCB, and C18, were offered by Agilent Technologies (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). MWCNTs, carboxylated MWCNTs, amino-modified MWCNTs, and hydroxylated MWCNTs were provided by XFNANO (XFNANO, Nanjing, China).

Instruments and apparatus

MS analysis was conducted with the Agilent 1290 liquid chromatography with an Agilent 6495 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Ultrapure water was prepared by using the Millipore water purification system (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). Samples were shaken using the multi-site vortex oscillator (Heidolph, Nürnberg, Germany) and centrifuged using Eppendorf 5810R refrigerated centrifuge (Eppendorf, Hamburgo, Germany). In addition, samples were homogenized using the knife grinder (Retsch, Haan, Germany).

Sample collection

Totally 40C. morifolium samples were obtained from local plantation base or market in Zhejiang, China. Each flower sample was stored at 4 °C immediately in dark prior to residue testing.

In C. morifolium infusion, a standard brewing method was used following the organoleptic evaluation methodology requirement in China (Standardization Administration of China, 2018). At first, samples (3.00 g) were introduced into boiled water (150 mL). Following 3, 5, 10, 20 and 30 min of fusions, tea infusions were filtered and then cooled to the room temperature prior to subsequent detection.

Sample pretreatment

The homogenized sample (2.00 g ± 0.01 g) was dissolved in 10 mL of ultrapure water to extract pesticides from C. morifolium samples. (During optimization of QuEChERS procedure, 0.05 mg·kg−1 of target analytes were spiked into detection-confirmed blank samples; for accuracy and precision validation, low, medium, and high spiking levels of 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 mg·kg−1 were applied). Next, the mixture was transferred into the 50-mL PTFE tubes. Afterwards, acetonitrile-2.5 % FA (10 mL), NaOAc (1.5 g) and anhydrous MgSO4 (6.0 g) were supplemented in this mixed solution in succession after vortex for 1 min. Following centrifugation at 189g for 5 min, the acetonitrile layer (1.6 mL) was collected for purification.

For C. morifolium infusion, infusion (100 mL) was evaporated to 10 mL, followed by transfer to PTFE centrifugation tubes (50 mL). By adding the acetonitrile-2.5 % FA solution (10 mL) and salt mixture (1.5 g of NaOAc and 6.0 g of anhydrous MgSO4), the mixture was subjected to vortex for 1 min. Finally, the supernatant (1.6 mL) was collected by centrifugation for 5 min at 189g. The above extraction procedures were selected based on our preliminary experiments (Ruan et al., 2023).

For purification, the supernatant (1.6 mL) was introduced in the EP tube prefilled with 10 mg of carboxylated MWCNTs. Next, the mixed solution was exposed to vortex for 1 min, and subsequently subject to centrifugation at 748g for 5 min. The supernatant was filtered with the 0.22-μm organic filter membrane for sample injection.

Pesticide analyses with UPLC–MS/MS

An Agilent Poroshell Aq-C18 column (3.0 mm × 150 mm, 2.7 μm) was employed to separate chromatography in the 2-μL injection volume at the column temperature of 30 °C. The mobile phase included A (0.1 % FA aqueous solution) and B (acetonitrile) at a flow rate of 0.3 mL·min−1. Using a linear gradient elution program, the eluent B was maintained at 5 % in 0–3.0 min, increased from 5 % to 40 % in 3.0–8.0 min, and subsequently to 80 % in 8.0–10.0 min. The gradient was kept at 3 min following an increase to 95 % in 10.0–16.0 min, and the rebalance at 5 % in 19.0–20.0 min.

MS analysis was performed with scheduled multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) model, followed by ionization with electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative ion modes. In addition, 3.0 and 10.0 L·min−1 high-purity nitrogen (99.99 %) served as the nebulizer gas and drying gas, respectively, while high-purity argon (99.99 %) with a pressure of 270 kPa was used as the collision gas. Other parameters included the desolvation temperature of 300 °C, and the electrospray voltage of 4.0 kV. Optimal retention time, parent/daughter ions, and collision energy (CE) of each target compound are presented in Table S1.

Method verification and quality control

The analysis method was confirmed in accordance with the criteria in SANTE 11312/2021 (European Commission, 2021). Therefore, the method was demonstrated using the blank C. morifolium sample for the criterion factors including linearity, matrix effects (MEs), LOQ, limit of detection (LOD), accuracy, precision, and recovery. The calibration curve linearity was analyzed based on blank matrix standards at six concentrations of the chosen analytes. MEs were determined as the matrix-to-solvent standard curve slope ratio. LOQ represented the lowest concentration in the linear range, while LOD was 1/3 of LOQ. Reproducibility and recovery tests were performed in six duplicates at three different levels (0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 mg·kg−1), aiming to ensure the high accuracy and precision. Similarly, based on recovery experiments, totally six copies were used for guaranteeing the accuracy of different pesticides. To prevent potential interference and contamination, reagent controls were randomly injected with sample series to check the interference.

Results and discussion

Instrument condition optimization

MS condition optimization

Optimizing MS parameters plays a vital role in achieving the highest sensitivity (Chen et al., 2021). The standard was prepared as a 1.00 mg·L−1 methanol solution. Positive and negative ion scanning modes were constructed, and the first-level full scan was performed to obtain the highly and stably abundant parent ions in each individual analyte standard solution. Subsequently, the compound fragmentor was modified in the single ion monitoring (SIM) mode, where parent ions entered the secondary mass spectrum for reactions including rearrangement and breakage to yield various ion fragments. In the product ion mode, CE at a certain level was used in parent ions in the compound, thus obtaining relevant ion fragmentation. Finally, the optimal CE of target ion fragment was modified in the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode to acquire optimal MS parameters. Table S1 presents the optimized MRM detection parameters.

Optimization of the chromatographic conditions

Following the optimization of MRM parameters, the chromatographic protocol was further modified to enable rapid and creditable peak separation of target analytes. The addition of FA in the mobile phase during UPLC-MS analysis improved the intensity of [M+H]+ peak in the compound, therefore enhancing the sensitivity (Su et al., 2024). However, a certain amount of added ammonium formate enhanced the peak shape of basic compounds (Hu et al., 2015). Four inorganic acetate (water, 0.1 % aqueous solution of FA, 5 mM aqueous solution of ammonium formate and 0.1 % aqueous solution of FA including 5 mM ammonium formate) combinations with organic acetate (acetonitrile) were compared. Therefore, compounds appropriate for the positive-ion mode had decreased half-peak widths, and increased peak areas and peak height values with the 0.1 % aqueous solution consisting of acetonitrile and FA. On the contrary, compounds applicable to negative-ion mode showed superior peak shapes, and increased peak areas and peak height values through water combined with acetonitrile. In brief, 23 compounds (occupying 85.2 % of all analytes) were applicable for the detection of the positive-ion mode, while only 4 flourine-containing insecticides (fipronil, fipronil desulfinyl, fipronil sulfide, and fipronil sulfone) were applicable for the detection of the negative-ion mode (Table S1). Finally, the mobile phase which was consisted of 0.1 % FA and acetonitrile was chosen to improve the efficiency of this proposed analysis method. Meanwhile, an analysis method was constructed on the basis of one-time sample injection under positive and negative ion modes.

Then, injection volumes were compared. Larger injection volumes led to higher sensitivity, whereas superior peak shapes could be obtained by lower injection volumes (Fan et al., 2022). In addition, peak shapes and sensitivities of different analytes at 1, 2, and 5 μL were also compared. Certain target analytes exhibited pre-delayed and flat-topped peaks at the 5-μL injection volume. Moreover, the analytes had similar and good peak shapes at the injection volumes of 1 and 2 μL. The injection volume of 2 μL was used for balancing the requirements of best peak shape and high sensitivity. Fig. 1 displays the role of injection volume in the peak shape of four representative pesticides at 2 and 5 μL. Through the optimization of the elution process, the best separation efficiency for target pesticides was achieved using a gradient multistep elution program within 20 min. Optimization of the QuEChERS procedure

To explore the pesticide residues in C. morifolium, the matrix effect, such as linalool, essential oils, octadecanoic acids, and hexadecanoic acid, need to be considered as it poses significant challenges (He et al., 2021). Although acetonitrile-2.5 % FA was selected as the solvent for extraction in our preliminary experiments (Ruan et al., 2023), the choice of adsorbents during the clean-up step remains critical as the QuEChERS method progresses. Therefore, the roles of conventional adsorbent (PSA, C18 and GCB) (Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2019) and novel adsorbent materials (MWCNTs and the structure modified MWCNTs) in sample purification and extraction rate were also explored. Throughout the experiment, samples fortified with target analytes at 0.05 mg·kg−1 were treated using an optimal protocol with various adsorbents used either individually or in combination (Table. S2). Fig. 3A presents experimental recoveries for those 27 target analytes with the 8 groups of adsorbents. The results indicated that there were 18, 21 and 20 compounds whose recovery rates were 70–120 % in three unspoiled MWCNTs groups, respectively (Fig. 3B). This result suggested that the medium lengths of 10–30 μm MWCNTs exhibited better purification and higher yields than the shorter and longer lengths MWCNTs with the same outer diameter (10–20 nm). Regarding the four different derivative MWCNTs, almost all of them had a higher recovery than the three unspoiled MWCTNs. Among them, the carboxylated MWCNTs group obtained the best mean recovery rate (91.8 %), with all pesticides receiving recoveries within the range of 70–120 % (Fig. 4A). These results demonstrated that our optimized preliminary experiments were advantageous over the combination of conventional adsorbent (PSA, C18 and GCB) (Ruan et al., 2023). The amino-modified MWCNTs group ranked the second with a mean recovery rate of 90.3 %. Nevertheless, five pesticides failed to achieve satisfactory recoveries, especially iprodione, which had a recovery rate of only 59.6 % (Fig. 4A). In summary, carboxylated MWCNTs exhibited relatively higher recovery and better adsorption capacity for interfering substances, making it the most suitable adsorbent.

Finally, the carboxylated MWCNTs dose was also modified to compare the recoveries of target analytes. The purification efficacy increased with the escalating doses based on the color of the injection solution under test (Fig. 4C). However, at 20 mg, carboxylated MWCNTs exhibited higher potential for pesticide adsorption, while only 24 analytes were recovered at >70 %, with a mean recovery rate of 85.7 % (Fig. 4B). Conversely, at the 5 mg level, the mean recovery rate of all pesticides was 92.1 %, which may be attributed to the insufficient low dose for purifying interfering substances in extraction supernatants. Furthermore, this insufficiency caused the enhanced matrix interference, consequently elevating the mean recovery rate. Thus, carboxylated MWCNTs (10 mg) was chosen as the optimal sorbent dose.

Calibration curve linearity, LOD and LOQ

Calibration curve linearity is considered an essential factor used for assessing the quality of an analysis method. Therefore, solvent and matrix-matched standard solutions were prepared for the 27 pesticides at 0.5, 1, 5, 25, 50 and 100 μg·L−1, with the correlation coefficient (R2) being >0.999 (Table 1 and Fig. S1). Based on the minimum concentration in the linear range, mixed standard intermediate solutions were serially diluted with blank matrix extracts to determine LODs and LOQs. According to the dilution factor of 5 existing in the pretreatment of C. morifolium samples, the ultimate LODs and LOQs of different analytes are shown in Table 1. For target analytes, their LOQs were extremely low, from 1.62 to 4.68 μg·kg−1, enabling the sensitive detection of analytes below maximum residue limits (MRLs) (GB 2763-2021) (The State Health Commission et al., 2021). This confirms the high quality of the proposed method when determining pesticide residues in C. morifolium samples.

Matrix effect assessment

ME is identified as a vital UPLC-MS/MS parameter, and it may impact the accuracy and veracity when determining target analytes (Gu et al., 2023). Standard curve slope in the extracted commodity matrix was compared with standard curve slope in the solvent to determine ME (Carvalho Guedes et al., 2016). While ME value of 0.8–1.2 was determined as the weak matrix effect of simple inhibition or enhancement, the value <0.8 presented strong matrix inhibition, and that >1.2 as strong matrix enhancement (Lu et al., 2021). This analysis method was demonstrated using calibration curves for 27 pesticides in C. morifolium matrices. In addition, based on the matrix and solvent standard curve slopes, the ME values of 27 pesticides were also investigated. As shown in Table 1, the varieties with weak MEs occupied 44.4 % (12 pesticides) and almost all of the remaining compounds exhibited strong matrix inhibition in this test. In C. morifolium matrix, joint ionization and analysis of these co-eluting components with the target compound exert a matrix effect on the compound (Loos et al., 2016). Thus, standard matrix-matching calibration was employed to improve the accuracy of quantification.

Accuracy and precision

In accordance with recovery data, the accuracy of the method was assessed. Three concentrations (0.01, 0.05, 0.10 mg·kg−1) were spiked at low, medium, and high levels, and the recoveries were evaluated at each level. Then, both recovery and its RSD were assessed to evaluate the accuracy and the precision of the method. In Table S3, the recoveries of 27 pesticides at those 3 fortified doses were 71.6 %–116.7 %, with RSDs not exceeding 10 % (intra- and inter-day RSD). In accordance with European Union (EU) guidelines (Food Safety, 2009), the recovery rates within 70–120 % and RSDs <20 % represent good accuracy and precision, confirming favorable results for analyzing pesticide residues in C. morifolium.

Comparison of this proposed method with others

Currently, studies on analyzing and identifying pesticide residues in chrysanthemums are limited, and some of them can be observed on Table S4. Regarding recovery, the QuEChERS method achieved a recovery of 71.6–116.7 % for 27 pesticides after the pretreatment. The average recovery was much closer to 100 % than other reported methods (Fan et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2021; Qin et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022). The LODs and LOQs were 0.54–1.55 and 1.62–4.68 μg·kg−1, respectively, conforming to previously reported values (Wang et al., 2022). The results underscore the sensitivity and efficiency of the proposed approach, making it appropriate for investigating pesticide residues in C. morifolium samples. Wang et al. (2023) analyzed pesticide residues in the spiked chrysanthemum samples by UPLC-MS/MS and GC–MS/MS and obtained the recoveries of 75.7–118.2 %, while the LOQs of fresh flowers, dry chrysanthemum sample, and infusions were 0.01–0.05, 0.05, and 0.001–0.005 mg·L−1, respectively, suggesting that SPE effectively purified samples. Different novel adsorption materials were compared, including MWCTNs and structure modified MWCNTs, with conventional adsorbents for purification. Based on experimental analysis, the purification procedure was optimized using carboxylated MWCNTs. According to the findings, the proposed method exhibited high effectiveness on sample purification.

Pesticide residue level analysis

The method was applied in 40 C. morifolium samples collected from the local plantation base or market to show the effectiveness of the established method and the feasibility for routine analysis. Due to the absence of related regulations in chrysanthemums (Table 1), MRLs of pesticides in conventional tea or medicinal plants were temporarily referenced (The State Health Commission et al., 2021). Table S5 summarizes that the pesticides containing omethoate, imidacloprid, epoxiconazole and bifenthrin were monitored, with the detected frequencies from 2.5 % to 15.0 %. Particularly, epoxiconazole was identified at 0.46 mg·kg−1, which far outweighed the temporary MRL in one sample (The State Health Commission et al., 2021). Therefore, it is urgently necessary to formulate MRL criteria for pesticide residues in chrysanthemums, aiming to comply to and guide suitable pesticide use, as well as guarantee food safety.

Omethoate and epoxiconazole transfer rates from C. morifolium samples to infusion and corresponding risk evaluation

Since drinking tea is considered the most direct consumption for C. morifolium, it becomes essential to explore the transfer of pesticide from contaminated C. morifolium to its infusion (Jiang et al., 2020). The transfer rates of omethoate and epoxiconazole from C. morifolium to the corresponding infusion were 0.06 and 0.46 mg·kg−1 for the two detected samples, respectively, both exceeding MRLs. Omethoate and epoxiconazole might have varying transfer rates based on different brewing processes. As shown in Table 2, transfer rates were relatively low with the brewing time of 3 min. When extending the brewing time, these two pesticides were transported, and the rising trend remained stable at 20 min. This might be associated with adsorption-desorption equilibrium of omethoate and epoxiconazole between C. morifolium and water. EDIs after taking C. morifolium infusion were calculated with 1.37 × 10−6–2.51 × 10−6 and 7.21 × 10−6–1.56 × 10−5 mg·person−1·day−1 when 10.0 g C. morifolium was consumed every day. With ADI of 0.0003 and 0.02 mg·kg−1·bw·day−1, hazard quotients (HQs) for omethoate and epoxiconazole were 0.036 %–0.85 %. Since HQs below 10 % are generally shown to have no significant impact on human health because of pesticide exposure (Kuang et al., 2023), the transfer of pesticide residues from C. morifolium to its infusion poses minimal risk to consumers. With the constant consumption of the infusion, these residues would exert a certain cumulative impact on human beings. However, the inherent metabolism of the body could sufficiently lower the associated potential risks to a negligible level and would exert no adverse impacts on human health even with high daily consumption of C. morifolium infusion.

Conclusion

To conclude, this study develops a novel analytical framework for food safety monitoring by integrating carboxylated MWCNTs into the QuEChERS-UPLC-MS/MS methodology. Systematic optimization of sorbent parameters can simultaneously detect of 27 pesticide residues in C. morifolium with high sensitivity (LOQ: 1.62–4.68 μg·kg−1) and accuracy (recoveries: 71.6–116.7 %; RSD <10 %). Due to the reduced chemical usage and enhanced sample cleaning efficiency, this method is successfully used to monitor pesticide residues in C. morifolium and conduct dietary intake risk assessments. Omethoate and epoxiconazole are detected at levels exceeding their respective MRLs. The transfer rates of these pesticides from contaminated C. morifolium to its infusion are accurately estimated, along with a comprehensive dietary intake risk assessment. In addition, the low hazard quotients of omethoate and epoxiconazole indicate negligible health risks to humans. By bridging the critical gap between analytical method efficiency and public health risk evaluation, this study not only provides a validated technical solution for food chemistry laboratories but also establishes a science-based foundation for minimizing pesticide-related health hazards associated with herbal tea consumption.

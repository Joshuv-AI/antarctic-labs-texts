Pediatric Orthopaedic Surgeon Coding Trends After Changes to the Current Procedural Terminology Evaluation and Management Coding Guidelines in 2021
Authors: Dylan S. Koolmees, Alec Kadrie, Clayton C. Bettin, Derek M. Kelly, Benjamin W. Sheffer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStarting in 2021, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services and the American Medical Association revised the Evaluation and Management (E&M) criteria of the Current Procedural Terminology coding selection. This retrospective study determined the changes in E&M coding level for pediatric orthopaedic patients and examined the effect of the changes on percentage of work relative value units (wRVUs) from clinic and surgery.MethodsCoding levels for all outpatient visits at our institution were analyzed from 2020, January 2021, December 2021, and January 2023. The total number of pediatric orthopaedic visits were collected, tabulated under the corresponding E&M code, and expressed as a percentage of total E&M codes within the corresponding time frame. The total change in wRVU generated from the E&M coding changes was compared between the clinic and operating room by examining the total average coding and percentage of total average for each group and the overall total and total percentage change in wRVUs.ResultsThere was a statistically significant difference in level of coding for both established and new patients from 2020 compared with January 2021, December 2021, and January 2023. Established patients showed a large decrease in level-2 visits and a complimentary increase in level-3 visits. New patients demonstrated a statistically significant increase in level-4 visits, which was sustained at the 2-year mark. From 2020 to 2021, there was a 29% increase in percentage of wRVUs from clinic, and a 6-point increase in wRVUs from clinic compared with surgery.ConclusionsOur study found an increase in level of coding after the E&M guideline changes, especially for new patients. This finding does not indicate that there were more complex pediatric orthopaedic cases but rather the new E&M guidelines accurately captured the complexity of each visit. There was a substantial increase in wRVUs from clinics, comparing immediately before and after the guideline change, which was largely sustained at the 2-year mark.Key Concepts(1)This study is important because it highlights specific specialty information for pediatric orthopaedic surgeons before and after the evaluation and management (E&M) guideline changes.(2)It is vital for pediatric orthopaedic surgeons to understand and appropriately apply the guidelines to each patient visit to ensure accurate coding.(3)Our study was the first to demonstrate the changes in level of coding after the E&M guidelines changed, and the effects of percent of work relative value units originating from clinic encounters.Level of EvidenceLevel III, retrospective case control

Introduction

Starting January 1, 2021, the American Medical Association (AMA) and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services completed a series of notable revisions towards the evaluation and management (E&M) of office visit coding and documentation [1]. The changes to the E&M criteria of the Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) coding selection sought to address concerns surrounding administrative burden for physicians in various specialties [[1], [2], [3]]. Key revisions in CPT codes ranging from 99201 to 99215 were designed to remove the history, physical exam, and various bullet points as code selection elements, allow physicians to determine coding level based on medical decision-making (MDM) or total time, and promote payer consistency with increased detail to CPT code descriptions [[2], [3], [4]]. These modifications were designed to add simplicity and flexibility to documentation while allowing physicians to focus coding and documentation around patient care rather than time-wasting mandatory standards [4]. Despite the various 2021 guideline modifications, the structure of E&M codes, coded from levels 1 to 5 with increasingly complexity for both new and established patients, remained unchanged. Various institutions have examined the implications of the new guidelines, with one study examining 303,547 providers across 389 organizations and identifying a decrease in level-3 visits to 38.5% of all E&M visits, a 5.9% relative decrease from the fall of 2020, prior to the new guidelines [5]. On the other hand, level-4 and level-5 visits increased to 40.9% of E&M visits (a 2.2% relative increase) and 10.1% of E&M visits (a 22.6% relative increase), respectively, with variable changes by specialty [5]. Despite these shifts, the authors found no meaningful changes in note length or time spent in the electronic health record (EHR) [5]. In one dermatology study, authors found that coding changes were associated with a 13% increase in level-4 visits and a 20% decrease in level-2 visits, with level-3 visits remaining the most common [6]. Similar trends toward higher code-level selection were observed in both hand surgery and hip/knee osteoarthritis patients [4,8]. Other measured outcomes that authors included were work relative value units (wRVUs), where both patient populations and modeled simulations in orthopaedic patients demonstrated an increase [4,8].

To the best of our knowledge, no current literature has examined the 2021 E&M guideline revisions on coding changes in a pediatric orthopaedic population. Establishing the effects of these changes is important so that we understand the intended benefits and financial implications moving forward.

The primary objective of this retrospective study was to determine the effect of the 2021 E&M guideline changes on the level of coding for established and new patients of pediatric orthopaedic surgeons. Results were also evaluated for the effect of the new E&M guideline changes on wRVU production from clinic compared with surgery. Lastly, we evaluated if any changes in coding were sustained over time. The hypothesis was that there would be an overall increase in the level of coding for both new and established patients as well as an overall increase in the total percentage of wRVUs coming from the clinic.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study was exempt from institutional review board approval since no patient identifiers were used. E&M coding was reviewed for all in-person outpatient visits with physicians in 2020 and January 2021, December 2021, and January 2023. We chose to use a year of baseline information for comparison with immediate changes in coding after the rollout, and to see if there were changes in patterns at 12 months and 2 years.

All locations of a large tertiary orthopaedic private practice in the Mid-South United States were reviewed; six pediatric surgeons (18.31 years average duration of practice, range 3.33–39.00) were followed.

The codes examined for established patients included 99211, 99212, 99213, 99214, and 99215, arranged in order of low (straightforward) to high (complex) medical decision-making. The codes examined for new patients included 99201, 99202, 99203, 99204, and 99205, also arranged from low to high complexity.

The wRVU generated from E&M coding was reviewed for all in-person outpatient visits with the six pediatric surgeons in 2020, 2021, and 2023. That percentage was compared with the percentage of surgical cases performed during the same time.

Statistical analysis

For each time frame (2020, January 2021, December 2021, and January 2023), the total number of pediatric orthopaedic visits was collected, tabulated under corresponding CPT code, and expressed as a percentage of total CPT code. Established patient data and new patient data were tabulated and compared separately. The change in level of codes for each time frame was evaluated using a Chi-square test of association, where a P-value <0.05 indicates that observed frequencies in each coding level are different by more than what would be expected due to random chance.

Discussion

The primary objective of this study was to determine the effect of the 2021 E&M guideline changes on pediatric orthopaedic surgery coding levels, with a hypothesis that there would be a statistically significant increase in the level of coding for new and established patients. We found that this hypothesis was confirmed. For established patients, this increase was seen primarily in level-3 visits, whereas for new patients, the increase was primarily in level-4 visits. These findings were found to be sustained up to the 2-year mark.

Our secondary objective was to evaluate the effect of the new E&M guideline changes on wRVU production from the clinic compared with surgery, with a hypothesized overall increase in the total percentage of wRVUs coming from the clinic, while surgical wRVUs would remain similar. Indeed, we found a substantial increase in the total percentage of wRVUs from clinic, when comparing 2020 with 2021. Our study showed a 40.6% increase in wRVUs from the clinic compared with a relatively stable 7.6% increase in the amount of wRVUs from surgery. This increase was largely sustained at the 2-year mark, with only a slight 3.9% decrease in wRVUs from clinic from 2021 to 2023. However, the increase in wRVUs from 2020 to 2021 could also have been affected by the overall decrease in volume in 2020, due to the COVID19 pandemic.

Despite its considerable impact on a wide range of specialties, the new E&M guideline changes have not been examined in depth, particularly among pediatric orthopaedic patients. In previous studies by Graham et al. and Plusch et al., significant trends toward higher code-level selection were observed in orthopaedics [4,7]. However, these studies were specific to hand surgery and hip/knee osteoarthritis patients, without specific examination of pediatric patients. Skeehan et al. demonstrated an increase in wRVUs with the new guidelines in orthopaedic populations using modeled simulations [8]. The current study builds upon this previous literature by demonstrating a significant increase toward both higher-level coding selection and increased wRVUs in a real cohort of pediatric orthopaedic surgery patients. An additional key strength of this study is the 2 years of follow-up data, allowing for a continuance of these trends to be demonstrated, with associated statistical significance.

One factor that could have assisted with the immediate implementation of the new guidelines was that all providers underwent a 3-h surgeon-led E&M training session in December 2020. One orthopaedic surgeon, a certified professional coder, and the lead business department administrator at the study institution comprised the multidisciplinary team responsible for developing the training materials based on AMA resources [3]. Definitions of terms provided by the AMA were presented, and case examples applying the medical decision-making requirements for each subspecialty were developed and discussed. After this training session, quarterly audits of charges and documentation were performed by the multidisciplinary team, and feedback was provided to the providers on code concordance with AMA guidelines.

This study demonstrates important findings that are broadly applicable to pediatric orthopaedic practices throughout the United States. The new E&M guidelines have removed requirements for excessive history and physical examination bullet points and replaced them with levels of medical decision-making that have allowed for a higher, more appropriate level of coding for pediatric orthopaedic visits. Additionally, it is important to note that patient visits after the 2021 guideline change potentially became more complex, due to delays in care from the COVID-19 pandemic. While there has been an increase in wRVUs due to the new E&M guideline changes, over the same time period, there has been a 9.47% decrease in the Medicare conversion factor, which determines reimbursement in dollars per wRVU, resulting in lower reimbursement for providers. This is especially an issue when considering 2023, when the wRVU from clinic was slightly lower than in 2021 and compounded with the 2.00% decrease in the Medicare conversion factor for that year. As a result, the rate of reimbursement for clinic was decreased, in part due to the cuts in the conversion factor. Therefore, it is vital for pediatric orthopaedic surgeons to properly understand and apply the new E&M guidelines to counter the continuing decrease in reimbursement for services provided.

Limitations of this study include its localization to one geographic region at a single institution in the Mid-South. Further multicenter studies should be done to validate the generalizability of our results outside of this geographic region. Additionally, our study had a limited number of pediatric orthopaedic surgeons. Another limitation is that it is hard to differentiate retrospectively whether there was an increased complexity of patients due to delays in care from a global pandemic or if changes to the E&M guidelines are responsible for the increased average level of coding. Since the changes in coding pattern over time have been sustained even as the effects of the pandemic wane, we believe the coding changes are largely due to provider education about the updated E&M guidelines. Lastly, the effect of training sessions for the orthopaedic providers, as well as regular audits, could have had an effect on the coding level.

Conclusions

The changes in E&M coding, for pediatric orthopaedic surgeons, have not been examined in depth due to the new guidelines. Overall, there was a significant increase in level of coding after the E&M guideline changes, especially for new patients, and this change was maintained until the 2-year mark. This finding does not necessarily indicate that there were more complex pediatric cases but rather that the new E&M guidelines accurately captured the complexity of each visit. Additionally, there was a substantial increase in wRVUs coming from the clinic, compared with the OR. Further multicenter studies should be performed to validate that our conclusions are generalizable outside of the geographic region of study.

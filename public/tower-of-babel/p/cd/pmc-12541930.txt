Minimizing acute kidney injury in cytoreductive surgery with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy: institutional insights into goal-directed fluid therapy and epidural analgesia
Authors: Kuan-Lin Wang, Ching-Wei Chuang, Kung-Yen Chen, An-Chih Hsu, Chun-Jen Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe read with interest the meta-analysis by Chen et al. on postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) following cytoreductive surgery with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (CRS + HIPEC) published in World Journal of Surgical Oncology. In response, we share our institutional experience using a standardized perioperative protocol incorporating goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) and epidural analgesia. This approach was intended to support hemodynamic stability and provide effective analgesia. Among 92 patients undergoing CRS + HIPEC, the incidence of postoperative AKI was 2.2%, which was lower than the rates reported in prior studies. However, our cohort had fewer comorbidities and lower perioperative risk profiles than those included in the meta-analysis, representing an important limitation when interpreting these findings. Taken together, our experience suggests that perioperative strategies such as GDFT and epidural analgesia, combined with favorable baseline characteristics, may contribute to reduced AKI risk in this high-risk population. Larger prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate these observations.

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the meta-analysis by Chen et al. [1] on risk factors for postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) following cytoreductive surgery with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (CRS + HIPEC). This study provides valuable insights into perioperative risk optimization. We commend the authors for their work and would like to share our institutional experience, which may further inform and complement their findings.

Chen et al. identified prolonged intraoperative hypotension—defined as systolic blood pressure (SBP) < 100 mmHg—as a significant predictor of AKI [1], consistent with the established role of renal hypoperfusion in AKI pathogenesis [2]. Inadequate analgesia can further contribute to renal hypoperfusion by triggering sympathetic activation and vasoconstriction, while also amplifying metabolic stress and inflammatory responses that increase AKI risk [3]. Evidence suggests that intraoperative epidural analgesia (EA) may help mitigate these effects by improving renal perfusion and reducing metabolic and inflammatory stress [4, 5].

At our tertiary center, CRS + HIPEC patients under general anesthesia are managed with goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) and EA, aligned with the Society of Onco-Anaesthesia and Perioperative Care consensus guidelines [6]. Hemodynamics are guided using stroke volume variation (< 15%), with additional targets of SBP >90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure >65 mmHg. If hypotension persisted >5 min, norepinephrine was administered, ensuring that no patient experienced prolonged hypotension. Epidural catheters were placed at T9–L1, with continuous intraoperative infusion of local anesthetics, followed by patient-controlled EA postoperatively.

Approved by the Joint Institutional Review Board of Taipei Medical University (No. N202507026), we retrospectively reviewed 92 consecutive CRS + HIPEC patients (54 women, 38 men; mean age 55.3 ± 11.7 years; mean BMI 22.9 ± 3.7; ASA class II/III: 82/10). Cancer types included ovarian (n = 32), colorectal (n = 24), gastric (n = 12), peritoneal mesothelioma (n = 10), appendiceal (n = 8), pseudomyxoma peritonei (n = 4), and others (n = 2). Comorbidities included chronic kidney disease (8.7%), hypertension (19.6%), diabetes mellitus (5.4%), and heart disease (5.4%) (Table 1). The average operative time was 8.7 ± 2.7 h, mean blood loss 350 ± 500 mL, and red blood cell transfusion rate 43% (Table 1). Intraoperative SBP < 100 mmHg occurred in 73 patients (79%) and SBP < 90 mmHg in 36 (39%), but all episodes were transient and promptly corrected. Postoperative day 1–3 visual analogue scale scores remained < 4.

In our cohort, 41 patients received cisplatin, either as monotherapy at 60–80 mg/m² (100–150 mg) or in combination with other agents at 30 mg/m² (30–60 mg). These dosages were comparable to those reported in the cohorts included in Chen et al.’s meta-analysis [1, 7–12]. Other agents administered included mitomycin C (n = 38), docetaxel (n = 7), oxaliplatin (n = 3), epirubicin plus 5-FU (n = 1), gemcitabine (n = 1), and liposomal doxorubicin (n = 1).

Using KDIGO criteria [13], only 2 patients (2.2%) in our cohort developed stage 1 AKI, both after receiving cisplatin 100 mg as a single agent, yielding an incidence of 4.9% among cisplatin recipients. No patient developed chronic kidney disease at 6 months. In contrast, prior studies in Chen et al.’s meta-analysis reported postoperative AKI rates of 13–32% [1, 7–12, 14], with cisplatin-associated incidences as high as 15–48% [1, 7–12]. Compared with those cohorts, our patients had fewer comorbidities and lower blood loss, which likely contributed substantially to the lower AKI incidence, in addition to perioperative strategies. Importantly, both patients who developed AKI had no comorbidities and experienced only transient SBP < 100 mmHg, similar to many patients without renal complications.

We acknowledge that this comparison is limited by important baseline differences between our cohort and those included in the meta-analysis, particularly with respect to comorbidities such as chronic kidney disease, hypertension, and diabetes. These factors are well-established predictors of AKI and were less prevalent in our population, representing a major limitation in interpreting our findings. Therefore, while our data suggest that timely intervention with GDFT and the use of EA may have supported hemodynamic stability and analgesia, the relatively favorable baseline characteristics of our cohort may be an equally important explanation for the low AKI incidence.

In conclusion, the study by Chen et al. [1] highlights the importance of hemodynamic optimization in CRS + HIPEC. Our institutional experience suggests that perioperative strategies such as GDFT and EA, in combination with favorable patient characteristics, may help preserve renal function even in cisplatin-treated patients. However, given the differences in comorbidities and perioperative risk profiles, our findings should be interpreted with caution. Larger multicenter studies are needed to clarify the relative contribution of these strategies versus baseline factors in mitigating AKI risk.

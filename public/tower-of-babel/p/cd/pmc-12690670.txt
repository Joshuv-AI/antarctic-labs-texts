SVSC-05 ROLE TRADITIONAL MEDICAL PRACTITIONERS PLAY IN THE BRAIN CANCER CARE CONTINUUM
Authors: Michelle Chishamiso Madzudzo, Lemuel Mapaike
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTraditional medical practitioners (TMPs) are integral to Zimbabwe’s informal health sector, catering to 80% of the population’s primary healthcare needs. Individuals seek traditional medicine due to cultural beliefs, high cancer care costs, lengthy hospital wait times, and perceived safety and effectiveness. However, consulting TMPs often results in delayed diagnosis and treatment of childhood cancers, particularly brain cancers in Zimbabwe.MethodologyThis qualitative study employed repeat focus group discussions with 64 traditional healers. Semi-structured interviews were conducted, audio-recorded, and transcribed verbatim. Thematic analysis identified emerging themes: risk factors, diagnostic approaches, symptom recognition and interpretation, treatment strategies, and practices.ResultsTMPs reported being spiritually called to heal, using plant, animal, and mineral substances. Most attributed childhood cancer to spiritual or supernatural forces (98%), with witchcraft being the most cited cause (71%). Symptom identification varied, with some recognizing biomedical symptoms and others attributing them to spiritual forces. Nearly all TMPs relied on hospital results for diagnosis, while 2% claimed spiritual diagnosis. Only 5 TMPs reported treating brain childhood cancers, with most combining spiritual and herbal remedies and no standardised treatment protocols were highlighted.ConclusionTraditional medical practitioners play a multifaceted role in Zimbabwe’s healthcare landscape however their practices can lead to delays in effective brain cancer care in the national health care system using the western modalities such as surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Culturally sensitive interventions targeting TMPs are essential to promote early detection, reduce delayed presentations, and enhance timely access to care. By engaging TMPs and clearing defining their role we can foster collaborative relationships that can improve childhood brain cancer outcomes in Zimbabwe.

Special issue on “Emerging applications of ultrasound technology in colloids, polymers and interfaces”☆
Authors: Patrick Tang Siah Ying, Sivakumar Manickam, Tan Khang Wei, Asgar Ali
Published: 2025

Ultrasound has become a transformative and versatile tool in colloidal science, polymer engineering, and interfacial chemistry. Its unique mechanism of acoustic cavitation generates localized hot spots of extreme temperatures, pressures, and shear forces, creating conditions that accelerate chemical reactions, enhance mass transfer, and modulate both molecular and supramolecular structures. These effects enable precise control over emulsification, nanoparticle synthesis, molecular self-assembly, and chemical transformations. Importantly, ultrasound technology also embodies the principles of green chemistry by reducing solvent composition, lowering energy demands, and shortening reaction times.

This special issue of Ultrasonics Sonochemistry highlights contributions that examine the role of ultrasound in designing, controlling, and functionalising colloidal systems, polymers, and interfaces. Collectively, these studies emphasise ultrasound’s dual identity: as a fundamental research tool to probe soft matter dynamics and as a scalable intensification technology for sustainable manufacturing, advanced materials, and biomedical innovation. The papers demonstrate ultrasound’s capability to fine-tune droplet size, nanoparticle morphology, and macromolecular conformation, resulting in improved functionality in emulsions, nanomaterials, and biopolymers. Several studies further highlight its potential in biomass and by-product valorization, improving protein solubility, bioactive extraction, enzymatic digestibility, and overall process efficiency. Others demonstrate ultrasound’s utility in industrial upscaling, membrane development, and biomedical applications, including imaging, drug delivery, and therapy.

Despite these advances, key challenges remain. Energy-efficient scale-up demands improved reactor design, while predictive models linking acoustic parameters to processing outcomes are still underdeveloped. For biomedical applications, biosafety of ultrasound-processed nanomaterials and precise energy delivery protocols remain critical. This collection reflects ongoing efforts to address these limitations, offering new insights into cavitation mechanisms and innovative strategies for coupling ultrasound with complementary technologies.

The papers in this special issue cover a broad range of systems and applications, reflecting the interdisciplinary scope and impact of ultrasound research. Several contributions focus on protein modification and food applications. For example, ultrasound pretreatment combined with glycation improved the stability of low-salt myofibrillar protein emulsions by reducing droplet size, enhancing interfacial coverage, and increasing thermal resistance [1]. Ultrasound-treated fish myofibrillar protein enabled shrimp oil-in-water emulsions with enhanced uniformity, viscoelasticity, and stability [2]. Tao et al. demonstrated that ultrasound applied post-genipin crosslinking optimized protein solubility, dispersion, and emulsifying stability [3]. López-Mártir et al. showed that high-intensity ultrasound enhanced the solubility, digestibility, and flexibility of soursop seeds protein isolates [4]. Application of ultrasound in dry-curing duck egg yolks accelerated salt diffusion and improved microstructure, oil yield, and texture [5]. Together, these studies demonstrate how ultrasound alters protein conformation and interfacial behavior, paving the way for functional foods and nutritionally enriched products.

Ultrasound is also central to nanomaterial synthesis and polymer engineering. Udepurkar et al. reported a continuous ultrasonic microreactor for scalable production of crosslinked polymer nanoparticles with high conversion efficiency and droplet-to-particle fidelity [6]. Fe3O4-decorated graphene nanosheets, prepared under ultrasonic processing and magnetic orientation, enabled polymer liners with superior gas and plasticizer barrier properties [7]. A sonochemical method for synthesising monodisperse silica nanoparticles directly from silicon metal at room temperature demonstrated frequency-dependent size control [8]. Synnatschke et al. developed a sonication-based method for exfoliating sensitive layered materials such as CrTe3, providing new insights into two-dimensional materials [9]. Ultrasound has also been key in synthesising and functionalizing mesoporous silica nanoparticles for nanocomposite membranes with high heavy-metal rejection [10]. Recent advances in ultrasound-assisted colloidal nanocrystal synthesis, imaging, drug delivery, and sonodynamic therapy, emphasizing cavitation-driven nucleation and transformation, were also reviewed [11]. These studies emphasise ultrasound’s ability to accelerate kinetics, simplify synthetic routes, and produce nanomaterials with tailored structures and advanced functionalities.

Another group of contributions explored process intensification and biomass valorization. Sabaruddin et al. reviewed ultrasound-assisted approaches for converting agricultural residues into nanocellulose, lignin nanoparticles, and bioactive compounds, highlighting hybrid methods that combine ultrasonication with enzymatic, microwave, or hydrothermal processes [12]. Ultrasound-assisted acid and alkali pretreatments of durian peel enhanced enzymatic hydrolysis, reduced chemical inputs, and improved fermentability for hydrogen production, offering a sustainable route to waste utilisation [13]. Reviews of ultrasonic microfluidic systems emphasized their potential in droplet manipulation, nanoparticle synthesis, and biomedical encapsulation [14]. These contributions collectively confirm ultrasound’s potential to minimise chemical use, improve efficiency, and enable bioresource valorisation.

Colloid and interfacial science is another area of focus. Wu et al. investigated anodic aluminum oxide formation, showing that ultrasound applied at the anode accelerated pore growth, while application in the electrolyte inhibited ion diffusion, resolving conflicting findings in anodization studies [15]. Studies on biomolecules explored the effects of critical-zone-intensity ultrasound on casein–dextran colloids, demonstrating selective exposure of hydrophilic or hydrophobic groups without altering secondary protein structure, offering strategies to control membrane fouling precursors [16]. Viturat et al. examined ultrasound-assisted Maillard reactions between chitosan and glucose, producing nanoparticles with enhanced antioxidant properties [17].

This special issue demonstrates ultrasound’s remarkable versatility as both a precision tool for probing soft matter and a scalable platform for sustainable processing. Across all contributions, ultrasound is shown to enhance kinetics, control morphology, and improve functional properties of biomaterials, nanomaterials, and polymer systems. Its role as a process intensification technology in food processing, bioresource valorization, water treatment, and nanomedicine is poised to expand further, driven by advances in reactor design and hybridisation strategies.

Protocol for the acquisition and maturation of oligodendrocytes from neonatal rodent brains
Authors: Hanki Kim, Bum Jun Kim, Seungyon Koh, Hyo Jin Cho, Xuelian Jin, Byung Gon Kim, Jun Young Choi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe generation of an oligodendrocyte primary culture model encompassing the diverse stages of the lineage is essential for the in vitro research of oligodendrocyte physiology and pathophysiology. Here, we provide a protocol for generating oligodendrocytes from the neonatal rodent brain. We describe steps for isolating oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) through differential centrifugation, their subsequent expansion, passaging, and differentiation.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Kim et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, Model Organisms, Neuroscience

Before you begin

The protocol describes the specific steps for generating oligodendrocytes (OLs) from postnatal day 1 Sprague-Dawley (SD) rat brains. However, the protocol can also be applied to brains from neonatal C57BL/6 mice, with minor modifications to adjust for different brain volumes. This protocol allows for a high yield of highly pure OPCs, with cost-effective materials and a relatively simpler procedure compared to conventional methods of OL culture, such as the shaking method or antibody-based methods such as immunopanning.1,2,3

Coating cultureware with poly-D-lysine

Timing: 80 min

Primary OLs cannot adhere to culture surfaces without adequate surface coating. Poly-D-lysine (PDL) coating alone is sufficient for the culture of neonatal rodent OLs.

Note: For the convenience of handling and passaging, we recommend preparing large cultureware, such as T75 flasks, for seeding OPCs after initial isolation from the brain.

Note: All steps should be performed within a laminar flow hood.

1.Dilute poly-D-lysine (PDL) to 10 μg/mL in distilled water (DW).a.Coat cell culture surfaces in 10 μg/mL PDL for 1 h.b.Aspirate the PDL solution and wash three times with DW.c.Let the surfaces dry for 10 min at 20°C–25°C and store the cultureware at 20°C–25°C, protected from light, until use.

Step-by-step method details

Below, we provide a step-by-step protocol to isolate OPCs from the neonatal rat cerebral cortex, expand OPC colonies using OPC proliferation-promoting growth factors, subculture the OPC culture for increased purity and spatial redistribution, and finally induce robust differentiation into mature OLs.

Dissection of the P1 rat brain

Timing: 20–30 min

OPCs are isolated from the cerebral cortices of postnatal day 1 (P1) rat pups. The protocol’s first step is to dissect and acquire P1 rat cerebral cortices (Figure 1).

Note: One brain will yield approximately 5∗106 OPCs after five days of proliferation, requiring two T75 flasks and 20 mL proliferation media. Determine the number of pups to use accordingly.

Differential centrifugation with Optiprep

Timing: 40–50 min

Previous studies have demonstrated using density gradient centrifugation with the density agent iodixanol (Optiprep) to separate OPCs from the adult rodent brain.5 In the present protocol, we have adapted the density gradient centrifugation into a two-step differential centrifugation step (Figure 3). It is better suited for mass production and easier to perform as it does not require the layering of gradients.

Note: All steps after dissection should be performed in a laminar flow hood, except for incubation in water baths or centrifugation steps.

Note: Media solutions should be pre-warmed to 37°C before use.

CRITICAL: The concentration of Optiprep, the volume of the solution, and the centrifugal force and time have been designed so that one column (15 mL conical tube) be used for one brain. Processing multiple brains in one column or changing the caliber of the column will change the centrifugation results.

4.Centrifuge the dissociated cell suspension(s) at 200 g centrifugal force for 5 min, 20°C–25°C.a.Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 1 mL of 12% Optiprep medium.Note: Papain inactivation can be performed but is unnecessary. Animal serum should not be used as it may affect the fate determination of OPCs in the cell suspension.b.Triturate 10–20 times with a 10 μL pipette tip. Attach the 10 μL tip to a 1 mL pipette tip to process the whole volume of the cell suspension.c.Add 3 mL of 12% Optiprep medium to obtain a total of 4 mL cell suspension in 12% Optiprep medium.CRITICAL: Avoid introducing air bubbles into the cell suspension, which may damage the cells and lower their viability.

Culture maintenance and OPC proliferation

Timing: 5–6 days

In the previous step, the cells resulting from the differential centrifugation were seeded at a low density of 1∗104/cm2. Maintaining the cultures for 5–6 days in a medium that selectively promotes OPC proliferation results in a culture enriched with OPC colonies with a small number of background neurons (Figure 4).

Note: A neuron-dominant culture can be obtained with a high seeding density and the use of a Neurobasal-based media, supplemented with B27 and without the OPC proliferation-promoting growth factors.

7.Maintain the cultures in OPC medium. Monitor the status of the OPCs with a bright-field microscope.Note: OPC colonies start to become evident around day 3. OPCs can be identified by their bipolar morphology.a.On day three, perform a half-change of OPC media or supplement 30 ng/mL platelet-derived growth factor-AA (PDGF-AA) for convenience.b.The OPC colonies become confluent around days 5-6. Once they become confluent, proceed to the passaging step.Note: Due to the low seeding density, the whole culture surface cannot become confluent without the colonies becoming overcrowded. Proceed to subculture the OPCs before the colonies become too dense.

Passaging of cultured OPCs

Timing: 2 days

After 5–6 days of proliferation, OPC colonies in the culture will reach confluency. To increase the purity of OPCs and acquire more space between cells for successful differentiation, the OPCs should be passaged and reseeded (Figure 5).

8.Briefly equilibrate Accutase to 20°C–25°C. For T75 flasks, 5 mL per flask will suffice.Note: Accumax, used for tissue dissociation, has the same components as Accutase (except for phenol red), only at a three-fold higher concentration. Accumax diluted in PBS will produce the same results as using Accutase.a.Aspirate the media in the flasks and add 5 mLs of Accutase per flask.b.Incubate the flasks in a cell culture incubator for 5 min.c.Examine the cells with a bright-field microscope. The cells should now appear round and bright.Note: The small proportion of neurons will remain attached to the culture surface.d.Gently tap the flasks on their sides to detach the OPCs.Note: Tapping and hitting detaches the OPCs more efficiently than swirling motions.e.Transfer the solution(s) to a 15 mL tube.f.Centrifuge the tubes at 200 g, 3 min, 20°C–25°C.g.Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in OPC medium minus EGF.CRITICAL: EGF should be excluded from the media after passaging. The presence of EGF will cause OPCs to aggregate toward each other, which may hinder the successful differentiation of OPCs into mature OLs due to the lack of space to accommodate the increase in cell size during OL differentiation.h.Count the OPCs and seed them onto the desired culture surfaces at a density of 1∗104/cm2 in OPC medium minus EGF.Note: Seed the OPCs onto inserts with nanofibers at this step for in vitro myelination.i.Maintain the culture for two days for stabilization.

Expected outcomes

We obtained high-purity, high-yield cultures of OPCs and mature myelinating OLs using the above protocol without requiring extensive equipment or reagents. After initial isolation and five days of proliferation, we obtained colonies of OPCs, identified by their expression of the OPC marker NG2, and a small number of neurons (Figure 7A). Microglia were not present, and astrocytes were present at a negligible level (<1%).1 We passaged and redistributed the OPCs to acquire space for differentiation (Figure 7B). After passaging, the purity of OPCs was increased to 98–99%.1 Finally, after four days of differentiation with a Neurobasal-based OL differentiation media, the OPCs were differentiated into mature OLs with complex processes, positive for myelin basic protein and adenomatous polyposis coli protein. Upon seeding onto cultured cortical neurons or aligned nanofibers, the cultured OLs enwrapped the structures (Figure 7C).

Limitations

The mature OLs in culture cannot be maintained indefinitely and will start to deteriorate around day 7. Considering that OLs in mixed glial cultures or OL-neuron co-cultures can be maintained for a much more extended period, we speculate that the serum-free OL differentiation media used in this protocol is not sufficient for long-term support of pure OL cultures, where OLs are not enwrapping neurons and cannot benefit from factors secreted by different cell types.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Low yield of cells after differential centrifugation (step 6b).

Potential solution

A decrease in cell yield is mainly due to insufficient tissue dissociation. Potential solutions are as follows.

•Thorough trituration: A more thorough trituration of the cortices, especially before dissociation (as it helps increase surface area to allow better contact with the dissociation enzyme) may help increase cell yield.

Problem 2

Debris in the OPC cultures during proliferation (step 7).

Potential solution

Due to the presence of a density gradient agent during differential centrifugation and a low seeding density afterward, tissue debris in the culture is negligible. If the debris must be removed, resuspending the cells in 1 mL of PBS and centrifuging at 200 g, 3 min will serve the purpose at the expense of a decrease in total cell yield.

Problem 3

Emergence of microglia in OPC cultures during proliferation (step 7).

Potential solution

During differential centrifugation, microglia should be sequestered in the pellet of the first centrifuge in 12% Optiprep. In theory, the centrifuge results should be reproducible, given that the density of the solution, the centrifugal force, and time are the same. However, if the microglia cannot be wholly excluded through the steps described in the protocol, increasing the time of the first centrifuge may solve the problem.

Problem 4

Failure to detach OPCs during passaging (step 8).

Potential solution

The main reason OPCs become undetachable is their differentiation. After differentiation, OPCs grow in tightly packed colonies, which rapidly proliferate, especially around days 4-6. This may cause them to spontaneously differentiate if not promptly passaged and redistributed. Upon reaching confluency, the OPCs should be promptly detached and passaged. Passaging on day five will, as a general rule, also prevent the undesired differentiation of OPCs, although there will be cases in which the OPCs could have been maintained until day 6 for a higher cell yield.

Problem 5

Low viability of cells upon counting or cell death during culture (step 6a-step 9).

Potential solution

The decrease in cell viability during the initial isolation of OPCs may be due to rough handling, such as an overly vigorous trituration or a prolonged period. The time taken from the decapitation of the pups (step 1a) to the incubation of cells (step 6c) should be kept within 2 h. Media and solutions should be prepared in advance or during waiting periods, such as the 30-min dissociation or the two differential centrifugation periods, to save time.

Cell death during OPC proliferation, passaging, and differentiation may occur for a few reasons. OPCs and OLs are sensitive to the surrounding environment, especially the change in pH. The time taken to examine the cells should be kept short, and the humidity and CO2 levels in the cell incubator should be well-maintained. OL lineage cells are especially vulnerable to basic conditions, and there are protocols for OL culture that advise using incubator CO2 settings higher than the standard 5%.3 Also, the over-proliferation of OPCs after passaging will cause them to die during differentiation due to a lack of space. Seeding densities may be lowered to 0.7∗104 if over-proliferation is problematic.

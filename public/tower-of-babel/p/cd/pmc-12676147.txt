Despite robust evidence demonstrating that cardiac rehabilitation (CR) improves recovery, reduces hospital readmissions, enhances quality of life, and decreases morbidity and mortality,1 participation among patients undergoing transcatheter heart valve (THV) procedures remains suboptimal. Discrepancies in referral practices remain a significant barrier to consistent implementation of evidence-based recommendations in clinical settings.2 At a tertiary hospital in Northern California, patients undergoing THV procedures were not consistently provided with CR education or had documentation of referral services completed prior to discharge, highlighting a gap between evidence-based recommendations and actual practice. A focus on patient education and referral documentation is twofold in meeting institutional quality metrics—documentation of referral is emphasized by the Society of Thoracic Surgeons/American College of Cardiology (STS/ACC) Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry, while provision of structured education is emphasized by the American Association of Cardiovascular and Pulmonary Rehabilitation (AACVPR).

Project Rationale

CR improves functional capacity, exercise tolerance, and outcomes following transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and other structural heart procedures.3,4 However, substantial variation in referral practices persists and evidence suggests CR participation is closely tied to hospital-specific practice patterns.2 During a quarterly Multidisciplinary Valve Clinic Committee meeting focused on the TVT Registry metrics and performance review, variable rates for documentation to CR referral were identified leading to a gap in consistent CR referral for patients undergoing TAVR, transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR), and mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (mTEER). In quarter 4 (Q4) of 2022, a focused quality-improvement initiative was launched to standardize documentation of CR referrals and education prior to discharge for THV patients, aiming to support recovery and align with evidence-based recommendations and quality metrics.

Project Description

The primary aim of this project was to increase the rate of CR referral documentation for patients undergoing THV procedures to 100% prior to hospital discharge. The project used a Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) model to implement structured changes in clinical workflow. This approach allowed for iterative testing, stakeholder engagement, and sustainable integration of CR referral documentation into discharge planning for all THV patients. Over the course of 7 quarters, the PDSA cycle provided a structured framework to identify gaps, implement targeted workflow changes, and foster multidisciplinary collaboration.

Plan

The planned standardization aligned with the STS/ACC TVT registry data definition for documentation to CR referral and the standards established by Joint Commission Comprehensive Cardiac Center certification. The cardiac program manager, TVT data manager, and cardiac catheterization lab director reviewed existing discharge workflows and order sets. They developed an action plan to improve compliance and standardization of the referral process. The STS/ACC TVT Registry defines CR referral as documented provider–patient communication recommending CR and either an official referral order to a CR program or documented patient refusal, completed before hospital discharge.5 The planning phase also included developing a process for documenting patient education. This is a requirement for Joint Commission Comprehensive Cardiac Center certification, which follows the AACVPR Phase I definition. The AACVPR defines Phase I of CR as the provision of comprehensive long-term services involving medical evaluation, prescriptive exercise, cardiac risk factor modification, education, counseling, and behavioral intervention.6

Do

The cardiac program manager met with the THV interventional physicians to formalize updated CR orders in the electronic health record and partnered with the nurse manager of cardiac rehab to coordinate operational changes. The TVT data manager reviewed CR referral criteria with the cardiology nurse practitioner (NP) service. The NP created a smart phrase for use in all THV discharge summaries, emphasizing CR education and referral. Within the discharge summary, the CR smart phrase states: CR has been recommended. At the time of hospital discharge, education was provided on rehab activities/exercises to be completed at home and for attending a CR program in the patient's area of residence when deemed appropriate per the patient's primary cardiology team. The outpatient CR nurse manager modified bedside consult workflows and implemented a system to track referral completion, supported by an increase in registered nurse staffing to ensure weekday coverage. In accordance with the AACVPR Phase I definitions,6 CR registered nurses provided patient education, assessed eligibility, and placed referrals when criteria were met. To maintain quality and consistency, missed consults were reviewed monthly with identified gaps addressed through targeted audience, workflow adjustments, and ongoing team feedback.

Study

The nurse manager for cardiac rehab conducted monthly audits and evaluated missed consults, which informed targeted follow-up. The missed consults were reviewed with quality improvement stakeholders, and opportunities to improve compliance were discussed and implemented. This ongoing review process supported feedback loops and reinforced adherence to the workflow. As a result, quarterly performance steadily improved, ultimately achieving and sustaining 100% documentation of CR referral rates following full implementation.

Act

The standardized referral documentation and education process was successfully expanded to include additional cardiac programs beyond the THV population. This initiative supported broader process alignment and standardization across the hospital cardiac service line.

Project Outcome, Impact, and Future Direction

Prior to project implementation, baseline CR referral documentation practices at this institution were low and highly variable across TVTs. For TAVR, referral documentation rates were 8.3% in Q1 2021. After targeted education and communication with discharging NP providers, rates rose to 97% by Q2 2022 and reached a sustained 100% beginning in Q2 2023, resulting in all 456 TAVR patients receiving CR referral education and documentation prior to discharge. Similar improvements were observed in other valve therapies: mTEER referral rates increased from 0% in Q1 2021 to 100% from Q2 2023 onward (n = 59), TMVR referrals rose from a 7.7% rate at the beginning of 2022 to a sustained 100% by Q2 2023 (n = 8), and transcatheter tricuspid procedures achieved 100% referral from Q2 through Q4 2024 (n = 7). These results demonstrate that a standardized discharge workflow can eliminate variation and achieve sustained adherence to CR referral documentation across the spectrum of THV interventions.

The project utilized a standard quality-improvement methodology by application of the PDSA model.

Overall, the implemented process change resulted in 100% documentation of CR referrals prior to discharge across TAVR, TMVR, mTEER, and transcatheter tricuspid procedures over a 7-quarter period (nearly 2 years), representing a total of 530 patients. In addition, compliance with CR consult orders and education documentation significantly improved as it was integrated into the standardized CR referral process. Utilizing a standardized quality-improvement framework and a multidisciplinary approach, measurable improvements in the documentation of CR referral and education practices were achieved for both the TVT Registry metric and Joint Commission Comprehensive Cardiac Center certification requirements.

Discussion

This initiative aimed to improve the process of CR referral documentation for all THV patients prior to discharge, aligning with evidence-based recommendations. While patient outcomes were not measured, research shows that CR participation is associated with reduced mortality and hospitalizations, with the potential to prevent up to 180,000 admissions annually if national participation rates reached 70%.1 The standardization of patient education and documentation of provider–patient discussions regarding CR prior to hospital discharge is an essential first step toward improving CR enrollment and participation.

Conclusions

Effective communication among multidisciplinary stakeholders is fundamental for driving sustainable process improvement. By applying the PDSA model, the multidisciplinary team identified opportunities to enhance CR referral practices prior to discharge and implemented collaborative, team-based interventions. This initiative led to improved documentation of CR referrals and education within our program and offers a replicable framework that other institutions can adapt to optimize the CR referral process and achieve STS/ACC TVT quality benchmarks and compliance with AACVPR recommendations.

Circ_MACF1 targets miR‐421 to upregulate FMO2 to suppress paclitaxel resistance and malignant cellular behaviors in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Xiaoting Qian, Chunhua Chen, Sanxiang Tong, Jun Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChemoresistance remains an enormous challenge in the treatment of lung adenocarcinoma (LADC). Circular RNAs (circRNAs) exhibit important regulation in tumor progression and chemoresistance. This research focused on exploring the regulatory function and mechanism of circ_MACF1 (has_circ_0011780) in paclitaxel (PTX) resistance in LADC.MethodsCirc_MACF1, miR‐421 and flavin‐containing monooxygenase 2 (FMO2) were determined by RT‐qPCR. MTT was applied to detect IC50 of PTX. The proliferation analysis was performed using EdU and colony formation assay. Cell apoptosis and motility were examined using flow cytometry and transwell assay, respectively. Western blot was administered for protein detection. A dual‐luciferase reporter assay was performed for confirming target interaction. PTX sensitivity in vivo was researched via xenograft tumor assay.ResultsExpression of circ_MACF1 was decreased in PTX‐resistant LADC tissues and cells. Circ_MACF1 overexpression reduced chemoresistance, proliferation, motility and accelerated apoptosis in PTX‐resistant LADC cells. Circ_MACF1 targeted miR‐421 and miR‐421 upregulation reverted circ_MACF1‐evoked effects. FMO2 served as a downstream target of miR‐421 and circ_MACF1 sponged miR‐421 to elevate the expression of FMO2. MiR‐421 enhanced PTX resistance and LADC progression via targeting FMO2. FMO2 knockdown enhanced IC50 of PTX and cell proliferation. In vivo, circ_MACF1 elevated PTX sensitivity of LADC by mediating miR‐421/FMO2 axis.ConclusionThese findings elucidated that circ_MACF1 inhibited PTX resistance by absorbing miR‐421 to upregulate FMO2 in LADC.Keywords: Circ_MACF1, FMO2, lung adenocarcinoma, miR‐421, paclitaxel resistance

INTRODUCTION

Lung adenocarcinoma (LADC) is a common histological type of non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) which is usually diagnosed at an advanced stage. 1 , 2 The modern treatments for LADC include chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy and targeted therapy. 3 Paclitaxel (PTX) is a well‐known chemotherapeutic drug used for many cancers, including LADC. 4 However, the drug resistance has limited the antitumor effectiveness of PTX. Therefore, it is pivotal to seek novel biomarkers to elevate the sensitivity of PTX in LADC.

CircRNAs are specific noncoding RNAs with important gene regulation by serving as molecular sponges of miRNAs. 5 , 6 CircRNAs have been affirmed as crucial biomolecules in tumor development and chemoresistance. 7 , 8 Yu et al. have shown that circ_0003998 contributed to docetaxel resistance by sponging miR‐326 in LADC cells. 9 Zheng et al. purported that circPVT1 targeted miR‐145‐5p/ABCC1 axis to promote cisplatin and pemetrexed resistance in LADC. 10 Nevertheless, circRNA research in PTX resistance of LADC is unreported. Circ_MACF1 (has_circ_0011780) was reported to inhibit NSCLC cell progression via the miRNA/mRNA axis. 11 It is likely that circ_MACF1 may regulate the chemoresistance (such as PTX) in LADC.

MiRNAs can govern gene levels by binding to 3′untranslated regions (3′UTRs) of mRNAs. MiR‐421 has been found to affect chemoresistance in glioma and gastric cancer via reducing ZFPC5 and ATM. 12 , 13 Duan et al. also found that miR‐421 conferred PTX resistance in NSCLC by inhibiting the KEAP1 level. 14 FMO2 is a member of the flavin‐containing monooxygenases (FMOs) family and is associated with drug metabolites or chemoselection. 15 FMO2 functions as a tumor inhibitor in LADC. 16 It remains to be explored whether FMO2 is related to PTX resistance and whether miR‐421 could target FMO2 in LADC.

In this study, circ_MACF1 function in regulating PTX resistance was first disclosed in LADC. More importantly, the potential of circ_MACF1 as a miR‐421 sponge to induce FMO2 level change was researched.

METHODS

Tissues source and cell culture

Tissues were acquired from 59 LADC patients at Chun'an First People's Hospital, Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital (Chun'an Branch), including 27 tumor‐responsive samples and 32 tumor‐resistant samples. These specimens were immediately stored in liquid nitrogen. Our study was authorized by Ethics Committee of Chun'an First People's Hospital, Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital (Chun'an Branch). All patients signed the written informed consent forms.

A549 and HCC827 were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). A549 and HCC827 cells were respectively cultivated in F‐12K and RPMI‐1640 with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in a 37°C humid incubator containing 5% CO2. Cell reagents were provided by Gibco. A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells were established by exposing to increasing doses of PTX (Invitrogen). 17 Subsequently, A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells were incubated with 50 nM PTX to maintain the resistant phenotype.

Cell transfection

Circ_MACF1 was cloned into an empty vector (pCE‐RB‐Mam; Ribobio) to construct pCE‐RB‐Mam‐circ_MACF1 (circ_MACF1). MiR‐421 mimic (miR‐421), miR‐421 inhibitor (anti‐miR‐421), siRNA against FMO2 (si‐FMO2) and negative controls were also synthesized by Ribobio. Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen) was utilized for cell transfection.

Reverse transcription‐quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR)

After RNA extraction by TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), cDNA was synthesized using PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara) and PCR was reacted with TB Green Premix Ex Taq II kit (Takara) following the users' guidelines. The 2−∆∆Ct approach was employed for data analysis. In addition, RNA was isolated after cells were incubated with actinomycin D (2 mg/mL; Sigma‐Aldrich). Circ_MACF1 and MACF1 were tested as described above. GAPDH and U6 were employed for expression normalization of circ_MACF1/FMO2 and miR‐421, respectively. The sequences of primers are listed below: circ_MACF1, F: 5′‐CCAGACAGAACAAAAGGAAGTGG‐3′ and R: 5′‐TTGTATGCTGTTTGATCCAGGG‐3′; MACF1, F: 5′‐GATCTTACAGGAGCGAGCGG‐3′ and R: 5′‐CATCAGCGACTCTGACCACA‐3′; miR‐421, F: 5′‐CTCACTCACATCAACAGACATTAATT‐3′ and R: 5′‐TATGGTTGTTCTGCTCTCTGTGTC‐3′; FMO2, F: 5′‐TTGACGCAGTTATGGTTTGC‐3′ and R: 5′‐AATCACCAGGATGCGTTTTC‐3′; GAPDH, F: 5′‐ACAACTTTGGTATCGTGGAAGG‐3′ and R: 5′‐GCCATCACGCCACAGTTTC‐3′; U6, F: 5′‐ GGAACGCTTCACGAATTTG‐3′ and R: 5′‐ATTGGAACGATA CAGAGAAGATT‐3′.

3‐(4,5‐dimethyl‐2‐thiazolyl)‐2,5‐diphenyl‐2H‐tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay

Cells were treated with different concentrations of PTX for 48 h and 10 μL MTT (Invitrogen) added for 3 h, followed by incubation with dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) (Invitrogen) and the determination of OD value at 570 nm through a microplate reader. IC50 of PTX was calculated using PTX concentration at 50% cell viability. Cell proliferation was analyzed after transfection in A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells.

5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine (EdU) assay

The EdU kit was provided by Ribobio. 1 × 105 cells were plated overnight and 100 μL EdU medium was added for 2 h. Then, fastened cells using 4% paraformaldehyde (Sangon Biotech) were incubated with 100 μL 1 × Apollo staining solution. Hoechst3342 solution was used for staining the cell nucleus and cell observation was performed under a fluorescent microscope (Olympus).

Colony formation assay

A total of 200 cells in 12‐well plates were cultured in the incubator for 14 days. The white colonies were fixated and stained with 4% paraformaldehyde and 0.1% crystal violet (Sangon Biotech), followed by counting the number of colonies.

Flow cytometry

Annexin V‐FITC/PI kit (Sigma‐Aldrich) was utilized for apoptosis determination. Briefly, cells were washed in precooled phosphate buffered saline (PBS) (Invitrogen). Then the cells were resuspended in annexin V‐FITC and propidium idodide (PI) according to the manufacturer's guidelines, and apoptotic cells (FITC+/PI+, FITC−/PI+) were analyzed under a flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

Transwell assays

The assessment of cell motility was performed via transwell chamber (Corning Life Sciences). First, cell suspension was prepared in serum‐free medium and seeded into the upper chamber. Then cell medium with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. Cell fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde and cell staining in 0.1% crystal violet were conducted after 24 h. Then the migrated and invaded cells were numbered, and cell images under 100× magnification were acquired. Specially, the upper chamber was coated with Matrigel (Corning Life Sciences) before cell seeding in the invasion analysis.

Western blot assay

The extraction of protein was carried out via radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (Sigma‐Aldrich). The separated proteins by SDS‐PAGE were promptly transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Sigma‐Aldrich) and blocked in 5% skim milk (Sangon Biotech). Antibodies were purchased from Abcam. The primary antibodies (1:1000) anti‐Snail (ab31787), anti‐E‐cadherin (ab40772) and anti‐FMO2 (ab95977) were incubated at 4°C overnight using anti‐glyceraldehyde 3‐phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH: ab181602, 1:3000) as a control. Then, goat anti‐rabbit secondary antibody (ab205718) was incubated at 1:5000. Finally, enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (Sigma‐Aldrich) was employed for the detection of bands and expression analysis was performed using Image J software (NIH).

Dual‐luciferase reporter assay

Circ_MACF1 or FMO2 WT and MUT sequences were respectively constructed into pmirGLO vector (Promega). Luciferase construct (circ_MACF1‐WT, circ_MACF1‐MUT; FMO2‐WT, FMO2‐MUT) and miR‐421 or miR‐NC were cotransfected into cells, followed by the detection of luciferase activity using a dual‐luciferase reporter system (Promega) as per the manufacturer's specification.

In vivo experiment

The stable cell line was constructed in A549/PTX cells by lentivirus transfection of lenti‐circ_MACF1 or lenti‐NC (Geneseed). Subsequently, the stable cells (3 × 106/0.2 mL PBS) were subcutaneously injected into the flank of six‐week‐old male BALB/c nude mice (Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology) and mice were treated with 2 mg/kg PTX (Invitrogen) once every two days. After cell inoculation for 10 days, tumor length (L) and width (W) were determined every 5 days and volume (L × W2 × 0.5) was recorded. Then, 30 days later, the mice were sacrificed using the CO2 asphyxia method in accordance with the current AVMA guidelines. Tumors were dissected from mice and weighed, followed by RT‐qPCR and western blot analysis. FMO2 and Ki67 in tumor tissues were detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC). The antibodies of FMO2 (ab95977) and Ki67 (ab15580) were purchased from Abcam. All procedures were approved by the Animal Ethical Committee of Chun'an First People's Hospital, Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital (Chun'an Branch).

Statistical analysis

Three independent experiments were performed. The data are expressed as mean ± SD, and were analyzed employing SPSS 19.0. The linear analysis was implemented via Pearson's correlation coefficient. Differences were analyzed through a student's t‐test and one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's test. The difference was statistically significant when p < 0.05.

RESULTS

Circ_MACF1 was downregulated in PTX‐resistant LADC tissue and cells

RT‐qPCR was conducted for circ_MACF1 detection. As shown in Figure 1a, circ_MACF1 was signally decreased in tumor‐resistant tissues in contrast to tumor‐responsive tissues. Relative to parental cells, the downregulation of circ_MACF1 was also observed in A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells (Figure 1b). MTT assay revealed that IC50 of PTX was obviously increased in A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells, which suggested the successful establishment of PTX‐resistant cell lines (Figure 1c, d). MACF1 mRNA level was markedly reduced by actinomycin D treatment while circ_MACF1 was unaffected, showing that circ_MACF1 was more stable than linear transcript (Figure 1e, f). Circ_MACF1 might be related to the resistance of PTX in LADC.

Circ_MACF1 was downregulated in paclitaxel (PTX)‐resistant lung adenocarcinoma (LADC) tissue and cells. (a, b) Circ_MACF1 level in tumor‐responsive samples (n = 27)/tumor‐resistant samples (n = 32) (a), and PTX‐resistant/parental LADC cells (b). (c, d) IC50 of PTX was examined by 3‐(4,5‐dimethyl‐2‐thiazolyl)‐2,5‐diphenyl‐2H‐tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay in A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells. (e, f) Circ_MACF1 and MACF1 were measured after actinomycin treatment. *p < 0.05.

Overexpression of circ_MACF1 repressed PTX resistance and malignant behaviors in PTX‐resistant LADC cells

The regulatory role of circ_MACF1 in PTX resistance was explored in A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells. RT‐qPCR showed that circ_MACF1 expression was markedly upregulated but MACF1 level was unchanged in the circ_MAF1 group compared with the vector group (Figure 2a, b). IC50 of PTX was significantly lower by circ_MACF1 transfection, implying that the circ_MACF1 overexpression inhibited PTX resistance (Figure 2c, d). By performing EdU (Figure 2e) and colony formation assays (Figure 2f), cell proliferation was found to be suppressed by upregulation of circ_MACF1. MTT assay also affirmed the inhibitory regulation of circ_MACF1 in proliferation (Figure 2g, h). The apoptotic rate was notably increased by circ_MACF1 (Figure 2i). Cell migratory and invasive abilities were repressed after circ_MACF1 expression was elevated (Figure 2j, k). Western blot results exhibited that Snail was downregulated and E‐cadherin was upregulated in circ_MACF1‐transfected cells (Figure 2l, m). Circ_MACF1 could inhibit the PTX resistance and impede LADC cell progression.

Overexpression of circ_MACF1 repressed paclitaxel (PTX) resistance and malignant behaviors in PTX‐resistant lung adenocarcinoma (LADC) cells. A549/PTX and HCC827/PTX cells were performed with vector or circ_MACF1 transfection. (a, b) Circ_MACF1 and MACF1 detection by RT‐qPCR. (c, d) IC50 analysis of PTX by 3‐(4,5‐dimethyl‐2‐thiazolyl)‐2,5‐diphenyl‐2H‐tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. (e–h) Proliferation analysis by 5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine (EdU) assay (e), colony formation assay (f) and MTT (g, h). (i) Apoptosis assessment using flow cytometry. (j, k) Cell motility analysis by transwell assy. (l, m) Snail and E‐cadherin protein determination using western blot. *p < 0.05.

Circ_MACF1 targeted miR‐421

Through the prediction of Circinteractome website (https://circinteractome.nia.nih.gov/), circ_MACF1 was found to contain an miR‐421 binding site (Figure 3a). The relative luciferase activity in the circ_MACF1‐WT group was suppressed after overexpression of miR‐421, with no apparent influence in the circ_MACF1‐MUT group (Figure 3b, c). The miR‐421 level was repressed after transfection of circ_MACF1 in comparison to the vector group (Figure 3d). Contrasted with tumor‐responsive tissues, miR‐421 was upregulated in tumor‐resistant tissues (Figure 3e). Circ_MACF1 was found to be negatively correlated to miR‐421 (r = −0.8591, p < 0.0001) (Figure 3f). High miR‐421 level was noticed in PTX‐resistant cells compared to A549 and HCC827 cells (Figure 3g). Circ_MACF1 could evoke negative regulation of miR‐421.

Circ_MACF1 targeted miR‐421. (a) Circ_MACF1 and miR‐421 site prediction in Circinteractome. (b, c) Dual‐luciferase reporter assay for validating circ_MACF1 and miR‐421 interaction. (d) The miR‐421 quantification through reverse transcription‐quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR) after transfection with circ_MACF1 or vector. (e) RT‐qPCR for miR‐421 detection in paclitaxel (PTX)‐resistant and responsive lung adenocarcinoma (LADC) tissues. (f) Pearson's correlation coefficient for linear relation analysis of circ_MACF1 and miR‐421. (g) Expression detection for miR‐421 in PTX‐resistant cells. *p < 0.05.

Upregulation of miR‐421 reversed the circ_MACF1‐induced inhibitory effects on PTX resistance and LADC progression

The circ_MACF1‐mediated miR‐421 inhibition was obviously reversed by miR‐421 mimic (Figure 4a). IC50 analysis by MTT assay indicated that miR‐421 mimic alleviated the inhibition of PTX resistance caused by circ_MACF1 (b). Meanwhile, we found that proliferation suppression (Figure 4c–f) and apoptosis enhancement (Figure 4g) by circ_MACF1 were counterbalanced after the overexpression of miR‐421. The introduction of miR‐421 neutralized the suppressive regulation of circ_MACF1 in migration/invasion (Figure 4h, i) and the protein changes of Snail/E‐cadherin (Figure 4j, k). All in all, circ_MACF1 absorbed miR‐421 to regulate PTX resistance.

(j, k) Western blot for protein detection. *p < 0.05.

Circ_MACF1 induced FMO2 upregulation through sponging miR‐421

Targetscan (http://www.targetscan.org) predicted the site binding between FMO2 and miR‐421 (Figure 5a). Then, miR‐421 mimic was presented to decrease luciferase activity of FMO2‐WT plasmid, while this effect was prevented in FMO2‐MUT group (Figure 5b, c). Anti‐miR‐421‐mediated inhibitory effect on the miR‐421 expression was conspicuous (Figure 5d). The miR‐421 inhibitor evoked the promoting regulation of FMO2 (Figure 5e, f). Compared with tumor‐responsive tissues, FMO2 mRNA and protein levels were declined in tumor‐resistant tissues (Figure 5g, h). A negative relationship (r = −0.8424, p < 0.0001) was found between miR‐421 and FMO2 in tumor‐resistant tissues (Figure 5i). FMO2 was also downregulated in LADC cells with PTX resistance (Figure 5j, k). Circ_MACF1 overexpression triggered the upregulation of FMO2, whereas this effect was relieved with miR‐421 transfection (Figure 5l, m). FMO2 could be regulated by the circ_MACF1/miR‐421 axis.

(e, f) FMO2 examination using reverse transcription‐quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR) and western blot after anti‐miR‐NC or anti‐miR‐421 transfection. Downregulation of miR‐421 suppressed PTX resistance in LADC cells by promoting the FMO2 expression

Anti‐miR‐421‐induced FMO2 upregulation was abated by si‐FMO2 (Figure 6a,b). IC50 of PTX (Figure 6c) and cell proliferation (Figure 6d–g) were restrained but cell apoptosis was enhanced (Figure 6h) after anti‐miR‐421 transfection, while knockdown of FMO2 abrogated these influences. Additionally, the introduction of si‐FMO2 eliminated these repressive effects of anti‐miR‐421 on cell motility (Figure 6i, j). MiR‐421 inhibitor downregulated Snail level and promoted E‐cadherin expression by upregulating FMO2 in western blot (Figure 6k, l). FMO2 knockdown increased IC50 of PTX and cell proliferation (Figure S1). Altogether, miR‐421 regulated PTX resistance and LADC development by targeting FMO2.

(d–g) The analysis of cell proliferation. (h) Cell apoptosis analysis. (i, j) The evaluation of cell motility. (k, l) The measurement of metastatic proteins. *p < 0.05.

Circ_MACF1 contributed to PTX sensitivity in LADC by modulating miR‐421 and FMO2 in vivo

Tumor volume and weight in the xenograft model were shown to be reduced in the PTX + lenti‐circ_MACF1 group compared with the PTX + lenti‐NC group (Figure 7a, b). Circ_MACF1 expression in the PTX + lenti‐circ_MACF1 group was overtly higher than the PTX + lenti‐NC group, suggesting that circ_MACF1 was successfully overexpressed in mice (Figure 7c). Circ_MACF1 upregulation resulted in obvious miR‐421 level inhibition (Figure 7d) and promotion of FMO2 mRNA or protein level (Figure 7e, f). IHC analysis indicted the elevation of FMO2 protein and inhibition of pro‐proliferative Ki67 level by circ_MACF1 upregulation (Figure 7g). The above evidence clarified that circ_MACF1 facilitated PTX sensitivity in vivo via interacting with miR‐421 to regulate FMO2.

(e, f) FMO2 level analysis in tumor tissues. (g) FMO2 and Ki67 protein detection via immunohistochemistry (IHC) assay in tumor tissues. *p < 0.05.

DISCUSSION

In our current study, circ_MACF1 was manifested to repress PTX resistance and retard malignant development through acting on the miR‐421/FMO2 axis. Circ_MACF1 shows promise to become an excellent therapeutic biomarker in LADC with PTX treatment.

CircRNAs are single‐stranded and highly stable RNA molecules in human genomes. The circRNA expression is usually abnormal in multiple cancers, and circRNAs are implicated in the various pathophysiological processes, including drug resistance. 18 , 19 Circ‐SORE could enhance the resistance of sorafenib in hepatocellular carcinoma and circ_0004507 induced the resistance of cisplatin in laryngeal cancer. 20 , 21 CircEXOC6B inhibited cell chemoresistance for PTX in ovarian cancer, 22 and circ_0011292 sensitized NSCLC cells to PTX. 17 Herein, circ_MACF1 was downregulated in PTX‐resistant LADC samples and cells. Expression dysregulation implied a potential relationship between circ_MACF1 and PTX resistance in LADC. As expected, PTX resistance was decreased after circ_MACF1 level was upregulated. More interestingly, overexpression of circ_MACF1 decreased proliferation, motility but accelerated apoptosis in PTX‐resistant LADC cells. A series of results demonstrated that circ_MACF1 elevated the susceptibility of PTX to further impede the cancer progression in PTX‐resistant LADC cells.

CircRNAs exert biological functions by acting as the gene regulators through miRNA sponge roles. 23 For example, hsa_circRNA_002178 expedited the developing process of breast cancer by increasing the level of COL1A1 via sponging miR‐328‐3p. 24 Zhu et al. elucidated that circRNA_103809 elevated cisplatin sensitivity by regulating COT1 as a miR‐377‐3p sponge in NSCLC cells. 25 Huang et al. have discovered that circRNA_103809 enhanced chemoresistance and accelerated bladder cancer progression by activating the miR‐516a‐5p/FBXL18 network. 26 During this study, circ_MACF1 upregulated FMO2 via acting as a miR‐421 molecular sponge. The circ_MACF1/miR‐421/FMO2 axis was presented in the PTX‐resistant LADC cells.

The recovered cellular assays were performed to further affirm circ_MACF1/miR‐421/FMO2 axis in regulating PTX resistance. The suppressive influences of circ_MACF1 on PTX resistance and LADC progression were counterbalanced by miR‐421 overexpression. The same reversal of si‐FMO2 for the function of anti‐miR‐421 was also noticed in PTX‐resistant cells. Thus, circ_MACF1 was considered to reduce PTX resistance by affecting the FMO2 level through targeting miR‐421. Animal assay also showed that PTX sensitivity in vivo was increased by circ_MACF1 via regulating miR‐421 and FMO2.

In conclusion, circ_MACF1 suppressed PTX resistance and LADC progression through depending on the miR‐421/FMO2 axis. To our knowledge, this is the first time that the PTX resistance mechanism in LADC has been elucidated. Circ_MACF1 can be used to enhance the therapeutic effect of PTX on LADC in the clinic.

A three-level risk stratification algorithm for arrhythmia based on CNN-LSTM palindromic structure
Authors: Hua Zhang, Jing Li, Mingjie Wang, Xuexi Xuan, Kai Xia, Yuanyuan Tan, Li Li, Jianfeng Chen, Haiyan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCardiovascular diseases are characterized by sudden onset, high mortality rates, and high recurrence rates, making early screening and timely intervention essential. However, due to insufficient medical resources, real-time and refined electrocardiogram monitoring of patients is difficult to implement. Meanwhile, the complex spatiotemporal features of electrocardiogram (ECG) signals bring challenges to multidimensional feature mining and accurate recognition. Therefore, in this study, we propose a three-level risk stratification algorithm for arrhythmia, which not only ensures precise intervention for different types of arrhythmias to achieve optimized allocation of medical resources but also comprehensively captures the spatiotemporal features of ECG signals for high-accuracy risk stratification detection. The algorithm first proposes a three-level arrhythmia risk stratification scheme, classifying cases as Normal, Not Life-threatening, and Life-threatening, with corresponding intervention measures of no need for monitoring, close attention, and immediate intervention, respectively. The core model of this algorithm is a deep learning fusion network based on the palindromic structure of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory network (LSTM), which enables precise three-level risk detection for all types of arrhythmias. Experimental results demonstrate that the algorithm exhibits favorable generalization performance across multiple datasets. It not only outperforms standalone CNN and LSTM models but also surpasses classical classifiers. Notably, on the 2-second ECG segments from the gold-standard dataset, it achieves an accuracy of 99.68% and a Specificity of 99.65%.The 10-fold cross-validation yields an accuracy of 99.62% ± 0.09% for 3-second segments, and patient-level validation on 10-second segments achieves an accuracy of 96.5%. The satisfactory results across varied segment lengths verify the reliability and practicality of the proposed method. In both arrhythmia risk stratification and detection tasks, the algorithm presents moderate competitive advantages.Keywords: Arrhythmias risk detection, Risk stratification algorithm, Convolutional Neural Network, Long Short-Term Memory network, Palindromic structure

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases occur with randomness and suddenness, making them the leading life-threatening killer, characterized by acute onset, high mortality, and high recurrence rates, thus requiring early detection, diagnosis, and treatment [1]. In clinical practice, electrocardiograms (ECG) are a vital tool for diagnosing and monitoring cardiovascular diseases [2]. However, due to limited medical resources, ECG backlogs have become a widespread issue, placing a heavy ECG interpretation burden on physicians and delaying timely responses to critical cases. Addressing the significant challenge of ensuring timely treatment of cardiovascular emergencies within healthcare systems, the most effective strategy involves establishing standardized ECG grading criteria to optimize risk stratification, introducing artificial intelligence-assisted diagnosis, and developing an ECG early warning system. Artificial intelligence, machine learning and deep learning possess powerful nonlinear fitting capabilities. They can automatically extract high-dimensional features and model time-series signals, assisting physicians in rapid ECG waveform interpretation, arrhythmia screening and disease risk assessment, and effectively improving the efficiency and accuracy of early screening and clinical diagnosis of cardiovascular diseases.

In the field of AI-assisted diagnosis, the majority of research focuses on detecting specific arrhythmia types and lack grading [3, 4]. A minority of studies target the detection of critical arrhythmias, employing a two-level classification into critical and non-critical categories [5–7], or focus on detecting specific critical types [8, 9], However, arrhythmias are complex and vary widely in severity, ranging from mild rhythm abnormalities to life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias, all of which require clinical attention. Existing methods fail to meet the practical needs of clinical risk assessment and hinder the refined allocation and rational scheduling of medical resources. Therefore, a comprehensive grading system is urgently needed to accurately identify normal, non-life-threatening, and life-threatening arrhythmias. This can effectively address the limitations of single-level and incomplete coverage in current research, while providing reliable technical support for early clinical warning, tiered intervention, and optimized allocation of medical resources.

Currently, the most widely used ECG auxiliary diagnosis models are predominantly based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. CNN employ convolutional kernels for local receptive fields, effectively capturing local features and detailed information. Through the use of multiple convolutional and pooling layers, they hierarchically extract abstract features from low-level to high-level representations. Consequently, Alin et al. [10] utilized 2D CNN for arrhythmia detection based on ECG signals, while Bollepalli et al. [11] proposed a CNN-based algorithm for real-time arrhythmia detection. This hierarchical feature extraction approach of CNN, characterized by spatial invariance, enables learning spatial features across different frequencies. However, CNNs exhibit a limited capacity for capturing long-term dependencies. LSTM networks, in contrast, leverage internal memory cells and gating mechanisms (including input, forget, and output gates) to effectively learn temporal correlations and trends within sequences. This makes them particularly adept at handling long time-series data and tasks requiring modeling of long-term dependencies. For instance, Boda et al. [12] proposed and demonstrated a novel framework for patient-specific ECG beat classification using LSTM-based RNN, and Wang [13] proposed a modified bidirectional LSTM (MB-LSTM) network for recognizing atrial fibrillation (AF) and atrial flutter (AFL) signals. Although LSTM excel at extracting contextual features from ECG data, they suffer from higher computational costs, longer training times, and inferior spatial feature extraction capabilities compared to CNN [14].

ECG signals are inherently time-dependent bioelectrical signals characterized by specific waveform patterns. A comprehensive and multi-level extraction of both spatial (i.e., waveform morphology) and temporal (i.e., dynamic variations over time) features from ECG signals is crucial for enhancing model performance in diagnostic and predictive applications. Such an integrated feature extraction strategy enables more accurate capture of ECG signal information, thereby improving model generalizability and accuracy. Consequently, many studies have explored the fusion of CNN and LSTM architectures. The prevalent fusion architectures typically employ single-layer integration, primarily following either a CNN-LSTM approach (where CNN precedes LSTM). Examples include Venkatesh et al.‘s CNN-CNN-CNN-BiLSTM model for atrial arrhythmia detection [15], Petmezas et al.‘s CNN-CNN-CNN-LSTM framework for atrial fibrillation identification [16], and Shi et al.‘s CNN-LSTM architecture for 15-class arrhythmia classification [17]. Alternatively, some adopt an LSTM-CNN structure, as demonstrated by Liu et al.‘s attention-based hybrid LSTM-CNN model for multi-class arrhythmia detection [18]. While effective, such single-layer fusion may compromise fine-grained feature representation when processing complex spatiotemporal patterns. In contrast, the multi-layer CNN-LSTM fusion structure gradually expands the spatiotemporal receptive field and steadily captures complex spatiotemporal correlations and dynamic variations [19]. CNN-LSTM has been widely applied in ECG detection in existing studies, yet most models lack multi-layer alternating stacking design. Accordingly, this paper establishes a multi-layer fusion diagnostic prediction model. Adopting the CNN-LSTM-CNN-LSTM-CNN palindrome hierarchical structure, it excavates ECG spatiotemporal features comprehensively and multidimensionally.

In summary, to ensure timely and appropriate medical attention for all patients, this study proposes a three-level risk stratification algorithm for arrhythmia that effectively addresses healthcare resource constraints and allocation optimization. Furthermore, we introduce a CNN-LSTM palindrome network architecture for comprehensive multi-level spatiotemporal feature extraction, resolving the accuracy limitations in existing deep learning-based arrhythmia diagnostic methods caused by incomplete feature representation.

The contributions of this study: (1) A three-level cardiac arrhythmia risk classification system was constructed by considering the severity of the arrhythmia condition, the impact on hemodynamics, and the demand for medical resource allocation.

(2) A three-level cardiac arrhythmia risk detection algorithm PaliConvLSTM based on the fusion layer of palindrome structure CNN-LSTM was proposed, seamlessly integrating the morphological features extracted by CNN into the LSTM time-series processing framework.

Capture the complete rhythm segment

The physiological cardiac rhythm originates from the sinoatrial node and manifests as sinus rhythm, which is considered the standard normal electrocardiographic pattern in healthy individuals. Deviation from normal electrical conduction patterns disrupts cardiac periodicity and rhythmicity, thereby inducing arrhythmias characterized by aberrant electrophysiological activity. To facilitate model learning and feature recognition, it is essential to extract specific rhythm segments individually. Therefore, the first step in data preprocessing involves isolating complete segments of specific rhythm types based on annotations from publicly available ECG databases. Malignant ventricular arrhythmias, namely “ventricular flutter”, “ventricular tachycardia” and “ventricular fibrillation”, can cause severe hemodynamic changes, easily lead to sudden cardiac death, and are of extremely high urgency and require immediate response. Common arrhythmias such as atrial bigeminy, ventricular bigeminy, and 2° heart block do not endanger life in a short time, but also need to be focused on. Therefore, considering factors such as urgency and medical resource allocation, this paper proposes a three-level classification scheme for arrhythmia risks. This risk classification scheme can not only take into account all arrhythmia diseases of different levels of criticality, but also enable the optimal allocation of medical resources.

PaliConvLSTM: Palindrome-structured CNN-LSTM classification algorithm for arrhythmia three level risk detection

As a complex physiological signal, the ECG signal demonstrates the continuous evolution of cardiac electrical activity, including the sequential and morphological changes of its sub - waves (such as the P - wave, QRS complex, T - wave, etc.). These changes are a direct reflection of cardiac electrophysiological activities and contain abundant physiological and pathological information. Thus, an effective risk - detection model not only needs to precisely capture the temporal dynamics of the signal but also has to thoroughly understand the spatial morphological characteristics of the signal. CNN can successfully extract rich morphological features from the data, while LSTM networks are adept at capturing and analyzing the temporal patterns of these features over time [22, 23]. Accordingly, this paper adopts the CNN-LSTM-CNN-LSTM-CNN palindrome hierarchical architecture and establishes an arrhythmia risk detection algorithm based on CNN-LSTM fusion layers, named PaliConvLSTM. With multi-dimensional and multi-level feature extraction capability, it can conduct temporal analysis and spatial morphology recognition simultaneously. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the model employs two CNN-LSTM blocks to extract multi-scale spatiotemporal features from the training set. A subsequent CNN captures high-level information, generating the ultimate feature map, which is fed into a fully connected layer for feature integration and classification. The specific workflow is as follows. The dataset is first input into a convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract key spatial features. The resulting features are then passed to a long short-term memory network (LSTM) to extract key temporal features. This feature information subsequently undergoes further processing through additional CNN and LSTM layers to extract spatiotemporal features at distinct scales. The extracted spatiotemporal features are then input into a final CNN layer to capture high level information and generate the ultimate feature maps. Finally, these features are fed into the fully connected layer for consolidation and classification. Given that this architecture follows the sequence “CNN-LSTM-CNN-LSTM-CNN”, it is designated as a palindromic structure. This structural design enables the spatial morphological features of electrocardiogram waveforms extracted by CNN to be seamlessly integrated into the LSTM temporal modeling framework. Through the alternating nested and progressively progressive structural pattern of “CNN-LSTM-CNN-LSTM-CNN”, multi-scale spatial features and temporal rhythm information can be hierarchically coupled and iteratively optimized.

ECG databases

This study utilizes three databases for experimentation, the MIT-BIH Malignant Ventricular Arrhythmia Database (VFDB) [28, 29], the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (MITDB) [30, 29], and the Creighton University Ventricular Tachyarrhythmia Database (CUDB) [31, 29]. VFDB: The MIT-BIH Malignant Ventricular Arrhythmia Database, is provided by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The data were digitized at a sampling rate of 360 samples per second, with 11-bit resolution within a ± 10 mV range.

Data were digitized at 360 samples per second with 11-bit resolution within a ± 10 mV range. It encompasses a wide spectrum of clinically significant arrhythmias, including Atrial bigeminy, Ventricular bigeminy, 2° heart block, Idioventricular rhythm, Nodal (A-V junctional) rhythm, Paced rhythm, and Ventricular trigeminy. Data preprocessing

For the three experimental databases, data preprocessing is carried out strictly in accordance with the procedure described in section “Preprocessing”. First, based on the rhythm annotation information, complete rhythm segments corresponding to various arrhythmia types are extracted from the first lead of each database listed in section “ECG databases” in a unified manner. For analytical and experimental purposes, these segments are then segmented into fixed durations of 2s, 3s, 5s, 8s and 10s. Subsequently, these fixed segments are labeled according to the three-level arrhythmia risk classification scheme normal, Not Life-threatening, and Life-threatening. The normal class exclusively comprises Normal Sinus rhythm (labeled as “(N”), while the Life-threatening class includes Ventricular Flutter (labeled as “(VFL”), Ventricular Tachycardia (labeled as “(VT”), and Ventricular Fibrillation (labeled as “(VF”). Eventually, after processing, the VFDB formed the VFDBs dataset, the MITDB formed the MITDBs dataset, and the CUDB formed the CUDBs dataset. Each dataset was cut according to the fixed length of the rhythm segments, and datasets with lengths of 2s, 3s, 5s, 8s and 10s were formed again. Meanwhile, in order to verify the generalization performance of the proposed algorithm, the VFDB and CUDB were specifically merged to form the CU - VFDBs dataset. Since the sampling rate of the VFDB is 360 and that of the CUDB is 250, in order to enable them to be trained at the same sampling rate, the VFDB was subjected to equidistant down - sampling processing.The data distribution of the four datasets is shown in Table 2. For instance, the VFDBs 2s subset comprises 22,776 segments, including 8,614 normal, 10,140 Not Life-threatening, and 4,022 Life-threatening rhythms (further categorized as 1,151 (VF, 299 (VFL, and 2,572 (VT instances). For all datasets, one-third of the data was randomly partitioned as the test set, with the remainder constituting the training set.

Model preparation and validation

To validate the performance and generalization capability of the proposed PaliConvLSTM algorithm, its performance was benchmarked against both deep learning and traditional machine learning approaches. Consequently, it was first compared against standalone CNN and standalone LSTM models, followed by comparisons with classical machine learning algorithms derived from diverse classification principles. To ensure comprehensive and complete validation, both comparative experiments were conducted across all segments of all datasets. Here, the standalone CNN and standalone LSTM models refer to single-type network models constructed solely from the CNN layers and LSTM layers, respectively, of the PaliConvLSTM model. The selected machine learning algorithms are the rule-based hierarchical decision algorithm Decision Trees [32], the ensemble learning-based Random Forest (RandomForestClassifier) [33], the connectionism-based Multilayer Perceptron (MLPClassifier) [34], the instance-based lazy learning algorithm K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) [35], and the maximum-margin geometric classifier Support Vector Machine (SVM) [36].

The evaluation metrics employed in this study are Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity and Confusion Matrix. Accuracy represents the overall correct prediction probability of the model, as shown in Eq. (7). Sensitivity refers to the probability of correctly identifying normal heartbeat samples, as defined in Eq. (8). Specificity denotes the probability of accurately detecting all abnormal arrhythmia samples covering Not Life-threatening and Life-threatening cases, as defined in Eq. (9). In the confusion matrix, it represents the proportion of actual negative samples that are correctly identified, characterizing the model’s performance in detecting abnormal heartbeats. From a clinical perspective, the detection of abnormal arrhythmias carries greater early warning value than identifying normal cardiac signals. Therefore, Accuracy is used to evaluate the comprehensive performance of the model, Sensitivity is adopted to assess its recognition ability for normal rhythm samples, and Specificity is applied to reflect the model’s capacity to screen out various arrhythmia abnormalities. In this study, the normal class is designated as the Positive class (P), and both Not Life-threatening and Life-threatening arrhythmia classes are collectively defined as the Negative class (N).

Experiment setup

Experiments are implemented based on Python and TensorFlow 2.6, with hardware configured as an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU and 16 GB RAM. The Adam optimizer (learning rate set to 0.001) and sparse categorical cross-entropy loss are used for training, with a maximum of 100 epochs. Early stopping, learning rate decay, and best model saving strategies are employed to prevent overfitting, with patience values set to 10 and 3, respectively. Model performance is comprehensively evaluated using multiple metrics including accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity.

As clearly shown, the PaliConvLSTM model achieves Accuracy ranges of [89.95%, 99.69%] and Specificity ranges of [93.06%, 99.65%]. Traditional machine learning algorithms such as SVM and KNN suffer drastic performance fluctuations, fundamentally due to heavy reliance on manually designed features. Depending on fixed statistical features, these algorithms fail to adapt to data distribution variations caused by high noise and inconsistent segment lengths across databases, resulting in unstable evaluation indicators [38].

These results demonstrate that PaliConvLSTM possesses superior generalization capability across multiple databases, as well as enhanced robustness when processing ECG segments of varying durations, compared to the other algorithms.

Discussion

Addressing the shortage of medical resources in ECG diagnostics, this paper proposes a three-tier arrhythmia risk classification scheme to ensure timely and appropriate care for all patients while optimizing resource allocation. Given the transient, abrupt nature of arrhythmias and rapid morphological evolution of their waveforms, intelligent detection necessitates integrating both temporal and morphological features. Consequently, we introduce a classification model based on a CNN-LSTM palindromic network architecture. This framework classifies arrhythmias into three clinical urgency levels, normal, requiring close monitoring, and requiring immediate intervention. The hybrid CU-VFDB dataset yielded 95.07% accuracy and 95.01% Specificity, further confirming the robustness of our work across multiple databases.

Conclusion and further research

This study develops a three-level arrhythmia risk stratification system driven by clinical severity, which overcomes the limitations of oversimplified and incomplete grading in existing research. It enables refined classification of arrhythmias across different severity levels and provides a systematic standard for clinical risk assessment. The proposed PaliConvLSTM model leverages a CNN-LSTM palindromic structure to effectively capture spatiotemporal correlations and dynamic features of ECG signals. It achieves an accuracy of 99.68% and a specificity of 99.65% on 2-second MITDB ECG segments, demonstrating favorable performance in arrhythmia risk detection. This study offers an efficient and reliable technical solution for intelligent ECG diagnosis, with important implications for improving early screening capabilities and optimizing medical resource allocation.

Meanwhile, this study has several limitations. The experimental dataset suffers from class imbalance, and no data augmentation strategies were adopted to optimize the sample distribution, which to a certain extent affects the model’s learning performance for minority arrhythmia samples. The proposed three-level risk stratification system and PaliConvLSTM model still possess favorable clinical translation potential. They can be integrated into portable ECG devices, dynamic monitoring systems or hospital ECG analysis platforms for real-time arrhythmia screening, risk early warning and auxiliary clinical decision-making. In future studies, we will focus on optimizing the data imbalance problem by introducing data augmentation, sample balancing strategies and improved loss functions to further enhance the classification stability and generalization ability of the model. In addition, multimodal medical data such as ambulatory blood pressure and blood oxygen saturation will be incorporated in subsequent research. The current network architecture is inherently compatible with multimodal inputs, requiring only minor adjustments to the input layers without major structural modifications. A cross-modal feature fusion layer will be designed, and transfer learning will be adopted to improve the model’s adaptability to complex clinical scenarios and realize the dynamic calibration of risk classification thresholds.

The effects of exercise intervention on complications and pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with overweight or obesity: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jun Kuang, Suwen Sun, Fengmei Ke
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Being overweight or obese is closely related to adverse pregnancy outcomes and harms both pregnant women and neonates, increasing complications during pregnancy and leading to adverse pregnancy outcomes. Consequently, a meta-analysis was done to investigate exercise therapy impact on the complications and outcomes of pregnant women who are obese or overweight.Materials and Methods:The computer system searched PubMed, Cochrane Library, Wanfang, EMBASE, and CNKI databases. The search duration extended from the database establishment until October 2022. A thorough search was carried out for pertinent studies investigating exercise therapy impact on pregnancy complications and outcomes in obese or overweight pregnant women. The heterogeneity test was carried out using Cochrane Q and Chi-square tests. The combined findings from the meta-analysis were examined for reliability and robustness using sensitivity and publication bias analyses. All statistics used RevMan 5.3 software.Results:This meta-analysis included data from 8026 patients from 22 randomized controlled trials. The results demonstrated that exercise therapy might successfully decrease the incidence of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) (odds ratio [OR] = 0.85, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.74–0.96, P = .01) and macrosomia (OR = 0.78, 95% CI: 0.68–0.89, P < .001), and control weight gain during pregnancy (SMD = −0.2, 95% CI: −0.31 to −0.08, P < .001).Conclusions:Exercise therapy can control weight gain during pregnancy, lower the incidence of GDM and the macrosomia rate, and improve adverse pregnancy outcomes.

1. Introduction

The World Health Organization defines obesity as a body mass index (BMI) of ≥ 30 kg/m2, and overweight as a BMI of ≥25 kg/m2.[1] Pregnancy complications such as preeclampsia, pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH) syndrome, and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)—all of which harm both the mother and the unborn child health—may be more common in obese or overweight women than in normal-weight women.[2,3] The problem of overweight and obesity caused by excessive nutrition and poor lifestyle is widespread worldwide, and no country seems to be able to avoid or reverse this situation successfully. This trend is also reflected in the pregnant women group. In Europe, the proportion of overweight or obese pregnant women is 33% to 50%, and the proportion has reached 40% in the UK.[4,5] With the development of the times, the structure of the childbearing population in China has gradually changed after the 2-child policy was opened. More and more women of childbearing age are overweight or obese. Prenatal weight gain is typical in pregnant women, and the population of obese or overweight pregnant women is rising. Increasingly attention has been paid to overweight or obese pregnant women.[6]

GDM means abnormal glucose metabolism during pregnancy and is one of the most common complications of overweight or obese pregnant women. Most patients will recover to normal blood glucose after delivery, but later diabetes mellitus type 2 risk will increase greatly.[7] GDM is extremely harmful to both maternal and child and can affect the health of mother and offspring, possibly causing severe adverse consequences such as PIH, preeclampsia, and fetal anomaly.[8] Obese or overweight pregnant women are at increased risk of giving birth to macrosomia (babies with a birth weight of more than 4000 g) and have a higher risk of PIH syndrome[9] which is also a specific disease during pregnancy, indicating that pregnant women have increased blood pressure (BP) (systolic BP ≥ 140 mm Hg and/or diastolic BP ≥ 90 mm Hg) during pregnancy. Some patients experience abdominal discomfort or thrombocytopenia, which has seriously affected the health of the mother and child and is among the leading reasons for death among pregnant women and infants.[10,11] Therefore, enough attention must be paid to the management of gestational weight to avoid unnecessary risks to pregnant women and the fetus.

Several studies have reported that many adverse outcomes in pregnant women and neonates are linear with BMI.[12] The increased BMI of pregnant women after pregnancy significantly increases the risk of GDM, premature birth, macrosomia, and stillbirth.[13–16] Therefore, the problem of overweight or obese pregnant women has currently attracted widespread attention. The main intervention method at this stage is to develop a detailed diet and exercise plan for pregnant women to control their weight of pregnant women. In particular, prenatal exercise intervention reduces weight gain in pregnant obese or overweight women and alleviates the risk of complications such as GDM and PIH syndrome. This study used meta-analysis to explore how exercise therapy influences pregnancy complications and outcomes in obese or overweight women, to provide relevant data support for evidence-based practice, and improve the adverse pregnancy outcomes caused by overweight or obesity.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Search strategy

A computer system searched PubMed, Cochrane Library, Wanfang, EMBASE, and CNKI databases. From its establishment until October 2022, the database was searched. The following search terms: (exercise OR “physical activit*”) AND (pregnancy OR “pregnant women”) AND (obesity OR overweight). The retrieval language for literature was restricted to English or Chinese.

2.2. Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: The research type: randomized controlled trial of the effect of exercise therapy on pregnancy complications and pregnancy outcomes of overweight or obese pregnant women. Subjects: Pregnant women aged ≥18 years and with BMI ≥ 25 kg/m2 at the first pregnancy test before or after pregnancy, and pregnant women without kidney diseases, heart diseases, asthma, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, and mental system diseases. Intervention measures: For pregnant women weight, nutritional status, personal preferences, etc, a professional sports therapist provided exercise guidance in line with their situation. Outcome indicators: Pregnancy complications and pregnancy outcomes were the main outcome indicators. Complications during pregnancy include GDM, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (hypertension and preeclampsia). Pregnancy outcomes included maternal outcomes (cesarean section and postpartum hemorrhage) and neonatal outcomes (premature birth, macrosomia, and neonatal asphyxia). A secondary outcome measure was weight gain during pregnancy. Exclusion criteria: Reviews, conference abstracts, research protocols, letters, animal studies, and studies with insufficient data or duplicate publications (if there are duplicate publications, the most recent published study was selected).

2.3. Data extraction

Independently, 2 investigators screened articles and retrieved data using exclusion and inclusion criteria. If there is a disagreement, the decision should be discussed with a third person. Data were extracted using a pre-designed data extraction table, which included the publication year, the first author name, research design, study country, sample size, intervention measures, and outcome indicators.

2.4. Quality assessment of the included studies

The Cochrane Handbook 5.0 “Risk of Bias Assessment” tool assessed the included literature methodological quality, including: the random allocation method, that is, the method for generating random sequences; the implementation of the allocation of hidden; whether to use blind method; the integrity of the result data; whether research results are reported selectively; and whether there are other sources of bias.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 from Cochrane Cooperative Network. The effect quantity for measurement data (body weight gain during pregnancy) was weighted mean difference, the effect quantity for enumeration data was odd ratio (OR), and the 95% confidence interval for each effect quantity was provided. Heterogeneity was quantified by I2 statistic and Chi-square test. A fixed-effects model was used if P > .1 and I2 < 50%; otherwise, a random-effects model was used (P ≤ .1, I2 ≥ 50%).[17,18] The reliability and robustness of the combined meta-analysis findings were analyzed using a sensitivity analysis by excluding each study one at a time. Using a funnel plot, publication bias was assessed. P < .05 was deemed statistically significant.

2.6. Ethical considerations

As the meta-analysis is based on published studies, ethical committee approval is not required.

3.2. Meta-analysis results

3.2.1. Meta-analysis results of complications during pregnancy.

3.2.1.1. GDM

Twenty literature reports on the impact of exercise therapy on the incidence of GDM had 7791 cases. The experimental group included 3935 cases, with 546 cases experiencing GDM. The control group included 3856 cases, of which 619 had GDM. As there was no statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .010, I2 = 48%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The findings revealed that the experimental group had a significantly lower incidence of GDM (13.88% vs 16.05%) than the control group (OR = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.74–0.96, P = .01) (Fig. 2A). Meanwhile, we also further verified this result with the random-effect model (OR = 0.43, 95% CI: 0.58–0.91, P = .006).

Forest plot of meta-analysis results of complications during pregnancy. (A) Gestational diabetes mellitus. (B) Pregnancy-induced hypertension. (C) Pre-eclampsia.

3.2.1.2. PIH

Nine articles reported the influence of exercise therapy on the occurrence of PIH, and 4567 research subjects were analyzed. The experimental group included 2278 cases, with 167 cases having gestational hypertension, and the control group included 2289 cases, of which 197 cases had gestational hypertension. As there was statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .01, I2 = 59%), using a random-effect model the meta-analysis was performed. The experimental group had 7.33% gestational hypertension compared to 8.61% in the control group, although statistically, the difference was insignificant (OR = 0.65, 95% CI: 0.41–1.03, P = .02) (Fig. 2B).

3.2.1.3. Pre-eclampsia

Seven articles reported the influence of exercise therapy on the occurrence of preeclampsia, and 3810 research subjects were analyzed. The experimental group included 1903 cases, of which 75 cases had preeclampsia, and the control group included 1907 cases, of which 74 cases had preeclampsia. As there was no statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .50, I2 = 0%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. Preeclampsia was slightly greater in the experimental group (3.94% vs 3.88%) than in the control group; however, the difference was statistically insignificant (OR = 1.01, 95% CI: 0.73–1.41, P = .94) (Fig. 2C).

3.2.2. Meta-analysis results of pregnancy outcomes.

3.2.2.1. Cesarean section

20 articles reported the influence of exercise therapy on the occurrence of cesarean sections, and 7752 cases were analyzed. The experimental group included 3909 cases, with 1185 cesarean section cases, and the control group included 3843 cases, with 1243 cesarean section cases. As there was no statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .45, I2 = 1%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The experimental group had a lower cesarean section incidence than the control group (30.31% vs 32.34%); however, the difference was statistically insignificant (OR = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.83–1.01, P = .09) (Fig. 3A).

Forest plot of meta-analysis results of pregnancy outcomes. (A) Cesarean section. (B) Premature birth. (C) Postpartum hemorrhage. (D) Macrosomia. (E) Neonatal asphyxia.

3.2.2.2. Premature birth

17 articles reported the effect of exercise therapy on the incidence of preterm birth, and 7308 cases were analyzed. The experimental group included 3689 cases with 253 cases of premature birth, and the control group included 3619 cases with 276 cases of premature birth. Since there was no statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .85, I2 = 0%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The results show no significant difference between the experimental and control groups in the incidence of premature birth (OR = 0.89, 95% CI: 0.74–1.06, P = .18) (Fig. 3B).

3.2.2.3. Postpartum hemorrhage

Four articles reported the influence of exercise therapy on the occurrence of postpartum hemorrhage, and 4004 research subjects were analyzed. The experimental group included 2011 cases, of which 302 cases had a postpartum hemorrhage, and the control group included 1993 cases, of which 286 cases had a postpartum hemorrhage. As there was no statistically significant heterogeneity across studies (P = .23, I2 = 31%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The results showed that the postpartum hemorrhage incidence in the experimental group (15.02% vs 14.35%) was slightly higher than that in the control group; however, the difference was statistically insignificant (OR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.88–1.26, P = .56) (Fig. 3C).

3.2.2.4. Macrosomia

15 articles on the effect of exercise treatment on the incidence of macrosomia, including 7168 subjects were, reviewed. The experimental group had 3588 cases, with 439 cases of macrosomia, while the control group had 3580 cases, with 543 cases of macrosomia. As there was no significant statistical heterogeneity across studies (P = .07, I2 = 38%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The incidence of macrosomia was significantly lower in the experimental group (12.24% vs 15.17%) than in the control group (OR = 0.78, 95% CI: 0.68–0.89, P < .001) (Fig. 3D).

3.2.2.5. Neonatal asphyxia

Three articles reported the influence of exercise therapy on the occurrence of neonatal asphyxia, and 1790 subjects were analyzed. The experimental group included 897 cases, with 6 cases of neonatal asphyxia, and the control group included 893 cases, with 9 cases of neonatal asphyxia. As there was no statistical heterogeneity across studies (P = .82, I2 = 0%), using a fixed-effect model, a meta-analysis was performed. The results exhibited that the incidence of neonatal asphyxia in the experimental group (0.67% vs 1.01%) was lower than that in the control group; however, statistically, the difference was insignificant (OR = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.24–1.93, P = .47) (Fig. 3E).

3.3. Meta-analysis results of body weight gain during pregnancy

15 articles covering 6812 subjects were reported on the effects of exercise therapy on the gain in body weight during pregnancy. The experimental group included 3448 cases, and the control group included 3364 cases. There was significant statistical heterogeneity across studies (P < .001, I2 = 75%), using the random effect model for the meta-analysis was performed. The results showed that the combined effects of exercise therapy on the weight gain during pregnancy of overweight or obese pregnant women were statistically significant (SMD = −0.20, 95% CI: −0.31 to −0.08, P < .001) (Fig. 4).

Forest plot of meta-analysis results of body weight gain during pregnancy.

3.4. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

During the sensitivity analysis, 1 study was excluded at a time. It was discovered that this had a minimal influence on the overall meta-analysis findings, and the results were rather stable. 4. Discussion

In this meta-analysis of pregnancy complications and outcomes in women who are overweight or obese, we identified that exercise intervention could reduce the incidence of GDM and the macrosomia rate and control body weight gain during pregnancy. Previous studies have shown that excessive gestational weight gain (GWG) is associated with GDM, gestational hypertension, and preeclampsia.[41] To ensure the health of mothers and their offspring, the Institute of Medicine has developed GWG guidelines based on maternal pregnancy BMI.[42] However, this does not inhibit the development trend of gestational diabetes. Although current studies have indicated that GDM is associated with many risk factors such as genetic predisposition, age, and family history, overweight or pre-pregnancy obesity is still the primary and controllable risk factor for GDM.[43,44] Proper exercise can increase the sensitivity of insulin receptors, improve insulin resistance, and promote peripheral glucose uptake and utilization and insulin secretion by pancreatic cells, thereby lowering blood glucose overall.[45] This is also in agreement with the results of this study. The relationship between excessive gain in body weight during pregnancy and GDM has been confirmed because excessive body weight gain leads to the increase of adipose tissues in the body, while the content of insulin receptors in adipose tissues is relatively low, the sensitivity of tissues in obese individuals to insulin is decreased, and the islet beta cell reserve function is also decreased.[46,47]

Studies have shown that the incidence of macrosomia in pregnant women with GDM is 2.09 times that of normal pregnant women.[48] Macrosomia is one of the complications of overweight or obese pregnant women, as well as one of the complications of GDM. If pregnant women have excessive nutrition during pregnancy, the intrauterine development of the fetus is easy to accelerate, and the weight is overweight at birth. Macrosomia is not only closely related to diabetes, obesity, and hypertension in adulthood but has also been found by some scholars to be at risk for breast cancer and other cancers in adulthood.[49,50]

In summary, the core purpose of exercise intervention is to control the weight of pregnant women during pregnancy. Few studies have examined exercise interventions for overweight or obese pregnant women. This study aimed to explore the effects of exercise interventions on complications and outcomes of pregnancy in overweight or obese pregnant women to provide the basis for prevention work in hospitals and relevant institutions. It also makes pregnant women attach importance to their weight control.

Heterogeneity is generally referred to as an indicator of the degree of heterogeneity in a study, and is divided into 3 main types, namely clinical, methodological, and statistical, based on their different sources. Clinical heterogeneity comes from subjects, interventions, and outcome indicators; methodological heterogeneity comes from statistical methods and their effects vary; and statistical heterogeneity comes from a combination of the 2 previous types of heterogeneity. The accuracy of meta-analysis is significantly influenced by the heterogeneity of the data. The heterogeneity found in meta-analysis varies from study to study. By using random effects models, the heterogeneity is still not negligible and the results should therefore be treated with caution.

There were still the following shortcomings in this study. First, in the included literature, medium-intensity exercise was adopted in all experimental groups, but due to the differences in measures and frequency, it might affect the results to a certain extent. Second, the sample size included in the literature regarding the impact on pregnancy outcomes was small, and there was a high heterogeneity regarding the impact on the rise in body weight throughout pregnancy, which might affect meta-analysis results. Economic costs are not evaluated.

5. Conclusion

In general, exercise therapy benefits overweight or obese pregnant women in terms of pregnancy complications and outcomes, effectively reducing the incidence of GDM and the macrosomia rate and controlling pregnancy-related body weight gain. However, due to the included studies’ heterogeneity, more multicenter, large sample, and high-quality trials with more rigorous and standardized designs are needed in the future to improve the quality of the study and provide a more scientific basis for exercise therapy effects on pregnancy complications and outcomes for women who are overweight or obese during pregnancy.

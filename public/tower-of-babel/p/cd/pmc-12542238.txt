Decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate following substitution of Nevirapine-based regimen with Tenofovir-based regimen in Indonesia
Authors: Evy Yunihastuti, Pringgodigdo Nugroho, Muhammad Yusuf, Salfia Dian Lastari, Fadlika Harinda, Tiara Kumala Putri, Meivina Ramadhani Pane, Metra Adi Pratama, Mira Yulianti, Alvina Widhani, Suzy Maria, Anshari Saifuddin Hasibuan, Maruhum Bonar Hasiholan Marbun, Sukamto Koesnoe, Teguh Harjono Karjadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIndonesia started transitioning people living with HIV (PLHIV) from Nevirapine (NVP)-based antiretroviral therapy to tenofovir (TDF)-lamivudine-dolutegravir fixed-dose combination (TLD) in 2023. We aimed to evaluate changes in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and predictors of rapid eGFR decline within 6 months during this programmatic dolutegravir (DTG) transition.MethodsThis prospective observational study included all adult PLHIV (≥ 18 years old) who received an NVP-based regimen initially, had baseline eGFR of more than 60 mL/min/1.73m2 before the transition, and underwent the DTG transition program in Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital from March 2023 to January 2024. Participants who did not have at least two eGFR measurements were not included in the study. The decline in the eGFR during the study period was defined by a minimum 25% eGFR decline from baseline, and multivariate regression was used for analysis.ResultsA total of 329 participants were transitioned to TLD. Of these patients, 242 (73.6%) transitioned from the zidovudine-lamivudine fixed-dose combination and NVP (AZT/3TC+NVP), and 87 (26.4%) transitioned from TDF+3TC+NVP. Within six months of observation, 87.2% of participants had eGFR decline. Sixty-one participants (18.5%) experienced rapid eGFR decline: 19.8% and 14.9% in those who had previously used AZT and TDF, respectively. Factors related to rapid eGFR decline were hypertension (aRR 1.87; 95% CI: 1.15 – 3.05) and longer usage of previous ART (aRR 1.05; 95% CI: 1.01 – 1.10).ConclusionRapid eGFR decline after transitioning from an NVP-based regimen to TLD warrants close monitoring, especially in patients with hypertension and longer use of ART.Keywords: Dolutegravir, Drug substitution, Kidney function, Nevirapine, Tenofovir, ART, HIV

Background

The use of antiretroviral therapy (ART) has decreased morbidity and mortality among people living with HIV (PLHIV). In 1987, zidovudine (AZT) became the first approved ART and nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). Nevirapine (NVP) was approved nine years later as the first non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) [1]. Since then, NVP has been used and was one of the longest-running ARTs before its drug resistance emerged [2].

In 2018, WHO recommended dolutegravir (DTG), an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI), as DTG can be taken once daily and has a high barrier to resistance. Since 2022, Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)/lamivudine/dolutegravir (TLD) has been recommended as the first line treatment for ART-naive PLHIV in Indonesian guidelines, except for those who had tuberculosis who still uses tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/lamivudine/efavirenz (TLE) [3, 4]. In 2023, Indonesia started phasing out NVP following the WHO recommendation. All PLHIV who underwent an NVP-based regimen were programmed to transition to TLD, whether they had been using AZT/3TC + NVP or TDF + 3TC + NVP. TDF is one of substrates that interact with creatine transporters in kidney tubules, such as organic anion transporters 1 & 3, and multidrug resistance protein transporter 4 [5]. The use of TDF is associated with reduced kidney function marked by a decline of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Close monitoring of kidney function and other side effects by a physician is needed when using TDF [6].

The reported incidence of TDF impaired kidney function differs widely due to different definitions of impaired kidney function. The pooled incidence rate of impaired kidney function due to TDF was 12.50 (95%CI: 9.00–17.36) per 1000 person-years (PYs) while using the definition of declining eGFR to less than 60 ml/min/1.73m2. A range of cumulative incidence from 3.1 to 34.0% was reported in studies using more than 25% decrease in eGFR as the outcome [7]. In some countries, tenofovir alafenamide fumarate (TAF) is the preferred option because the effects on the kidneys are milder. A meta-analysis from randomized control trials of ART-naive patients using a TDF-based regimen indicated that the mean eGFR decline was − 5.26 mL/min/1.73m2, which was significantly higher than TAF-based regimen at 4.98 mL/min/1.73m2 in week 48 [8]. However, TAF is not available in Indonesia. In several studies, the worsening of eGFR was irreversible, unless TDF was stopped immediately. Early or rapid changes in eGFR should be a concern because they can predict kidney disease, and people experiencing this are 5 times more at risk. Early change is defined by a ≥ 25% decrease in eGFR [9]. In addition, people with short-term changes in eGFR are also two times more at risk for cardiovascular events [10].

Our study aimed to evaluate changes in eGFR after transitioning to FDC TLD from NVP-based ART in PLHIV in Indonesia. We also evaluated predicting factors associated with rapid kidney function decline within 6 months of the programmatic DTG transition in Indonesia.

Methods

Study population

This prospective observational study was conducted in Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital, a tertiary hospital and center for HIV in Jakarta, from March 2023 to January 2024. All PLHIV who underwent the government programmatic transition from nevirapine-based ART were included in the study. Nevirapine-based treatment included AZT/3TC + NVP or TDF + 3TC + NVP. All participants received TLD after transitioning. The inclusion criteria were an age of over 18 years and having a baseline eGFR of more than 60 mL/min/1.73m2 before transitioning. Creatinine clearance was estimated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) which provides more accurate eGFR estimation [11]. Serum creatinine and eGFR were routinely measured at 2 months and 6 months after transitioning to DTG. Participants who did not have eGFR measurements in at least two different months were not included in the study. Rapid eGFR decline during the study period was defined by a minimum 25% eGFR decline from baseline based on previous studies [12–15]. Discontinuation of TLD was based on intolerable side effects (i.e. drug hypersensitivity, neuropsychiatric symptoms) and shared decision-making between the patient and physician. The were two groups being observed in this study, those who transitioned from AZT/3TC + NVP and those who transitioned from TDF + 3TC + NVP.

Data collection

Patients’ age, sex, body mass index (BMI), duration of previous ART regimen, HIV risk factor, serum creatinine, eGFR at baseline, opportunistic infection, comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, hypertension), and coinfections (hepatitis B or hepatitis C) were recorded. BMI was measured and classified according to WHO criteria for the Asia Pacific which are underweight (< 18.5 kg/m2), normoweight (18.5–22.9 kg/m2), overweight (23–24.9 kg/m2), and obese (≥ 25 kg/m2) [16].

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies with percentages while continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile ranges (IQR). Bivariate and multivariate analysis were done with binomial regression for factors associated with 25% eGFR decline (age, sex, history of TDF, BMI, previous ART regiment duration, eGFR before starting TLD, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and hepatitis B or C coinfection). The outcome was presented with risk ratio (RR) and the variables with p-values less than 0.25 were included in the multivariate analysis with adjusted RR. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered significant, and all statistical analyses were done in STATA® 14.2 (StataCorp LLC).

Results

Demography

A total of 426 PLHIV participated in the government transition program. However, 18 participants did not have baseline eGFR and 79 did not have eGFR measurements within 6 months after transitioning to TLD. A total of 329 participants were included in the study, of whom 242 (73.6%) were transitioned from AZT/3TC + NVP and 87 (26.4%) were transitioned from TDF + 3TC + NVP. The median age was 43 years (IQR 40–48) and 63.2% of participants were male. 93% of participants who had previously used AZT had baseline eGFR of more than 90 mL/min/1.73m2, but only 65.5% who had previously used TDF had baseline eGFR of more than 90 mL/min/1.73m2. Of all the participants, 17.3% had hypertension, 3.6% had diabetes mellitus, and 18.2% had hepatitis B or C co-infection (Table 1). None of the participants used other concomitant nephrotoxic drugs.

Participant characteristics by TLD status

Egfr declined after transitioning to TLD

Most participants (87.2%) experienced eGFR decline within 6 months after transitioning to TLD. Sixty-one participants (18.5%) experienced rapid eGFR decline (more than 25% from baseline) (Fig. 1). Ten of them (16.4%) even reached eGFR of below 60 mL/min/1.73m2. Of those who had previously used TDF, 14.9% had a > 25% eGFR decline. Higher percentage of those who transitioned from AZT/3TC + NVP was observed compared to TDF + 3TC + NVP (88% to 85.1%). Hypertension (aRR 1.85; 95% CI: 1.15–3.05; p-value = 0.012) and longer usage of previous ART (aRR 1.05; 95% CI: 1.01–1.10; p-value = 0.047) were predictive factors 25% decline of eGFR from baseline (Table 2).

Predictive factors associated with 25% eGFR decline from baseline among participants who transitioned to TLD

Following the transition to TLD, 25 participants (10.3%) then substituted to AZT/3TC + DTG (23) and 3TC + DTG (2) due to significant eGFR decline. One participant substituted to TDF/3TC/EFV due to drug allergy. Reversibility of eGFR decline (increase of eGFR > 20%) was found in 13 PLHIV: 10 from AZT/3TC + NVP group and 3 from TDF + 3TC + NVP group. Among those who discontinued TLD when eGFR was < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 (8 PLHIV), the median eGFR decreased from 88 (min-max: 66.5–110.7) to 63.1 (min-max: 60.1–66.1) mL/min/1.73m2, a 28.3% decrease. However, among those who discontinued TLD when eGFR was > 60 mL/min/1.73m2 (16 PLHIV), the median eGFR decreased from 97.3 (min-max 72.1–113.2) to 90.5 (min-max 87–94) mL/min/1.73m2, only a 7% decrease. Discontinuing TLD when eGFR was still > 60 mL/min/1.73m2 showed better eGFR recovery.

Discussion

In this study, most of the PLHIV were relatively healthy, having been on NVP-based therapy for a median of 11 years and were without recent opportunistic infection. The reason for ART transitioning was the phasing out of NVP as the majority (88.8%) had undetectable viral loads. Most TLD transition studies published describe change from TLE to TLD [17]. Here, we describe the change from AZT or TDF in combination with NVP to TLD, which has never been reported before.

The majority of Indonesian PLHIV who transitioned from the NVP-based regimen to TLD experienced eGFR decline (87.2%). A remarkable eGFR decline (more than 25%) was observed in 18.5% of participants. Although this number is still within the range of other studies done in Thailand, Japan, Malaysia, and South Korea (3.1–34%) [12, 13, 18–22], we only carried out observations for 6 months. Other studies may have recorded observations for up to years.

Another interesting finding was that the decline was observed in those who had used TDF for quite a long time (14.9%). This suggests that DTG alone may be associated with kidney impairment. A study of 34 healthy patients on DTG alone showed that serum creatinine levels may increase by 10–14%. However, the authors concluded that it was non-pathological and reversible [23]. The rise of serum creatinine after DTG usage is due to the decreased secretion of tubular creatinine. DTG inhibits kidney transporter OCT2 at the basolateral membrane of proximal renal tubular cells, multidrug and toxin extrusion transporter 1 (MATE1), and multidrug and toxin extrusion transporter 2-K (MATE2-K) [24, 25]. On the other hand, TDF inhibits organic anion transporter 1 & 3 (OAT1 & OAT3) and multidrug resistance protein transporter 4 (MRP4) [5]. Secondary analysis of the ADVANCE study on South African PLHIV initiating DTG-based therapy showed that the mean increase in serum creatinine was 12.25 µmol/L after 48 weeks [26]. Mpofu et al. [5], reported that increased DTG concentration and TDF usage were associated with increased serum creatinine levels. Both drugs inhibit creatinine elimination by inhibiting kidney transporters. The study showed that PLHIV who use TDF and DTG simultaneously experience a higher increase in serum creatinine than those who use DTG only. The possibility of synergism between TDF and DTG should be considered. Previous studies suggest that while TDF and DTG can affect kidney function through different mechanisms, the changes are usually mild and stabilize over time in most patients [27–29]. However, careful monitoring is recommended, especially in patients with risk factors for kidney disease.

Our study showed that hypertension and longer usage of previous ART were associated with eGFR decline. These results aligned with several other studies that used the same definition for eGFR decline [30–32]. Low et al. [30], found that hypertension is associated with eGFR decline of more than 25% from baseline (aOR 3.49; 95% CI 1.28–9.46). A study by Tan et al. [31], showed that, compared to patients who had received ART for 12 weeks, PLHIV who had received ART for 144 weeks (aOR 4.1; 95% CI 2.0–8.4) and 168 weeks (aOR 8.4; 95% CI 1.2–4.3) were more at risk of eGFR decline of more than 25%. Furthermore, a study by Srisopa et al. [32], indicated that longer use of TDF, specifically more than 3 years, is associated with eGFR decline of more than 25% (aOR 1.93; 95% CI 1.53–2.43). Other studies also found that hypertension [33–39] and longer ART use duration [40–44] are associated with eGFR decline to less than 60 mL/min/1.73m2. Hypertension cause progressive arteriosclerosis/nephrosclerosis and higher intraglomerular pressure, leading to lower eGFR [45, 46]. Longer use of ART also means longer exposure of ART mechanism in increasing serum creatinine levels as explained previously, also leading to lower eGFR [5, 24].

Twenty-five participants (10.3%) discontinued TDF due to significant eGFR decline. Sixteen participants discontinued TDF when their eGFR was >60 mL/min/1.73m2. Discontinuing TDF when eGFR is >60 mL/min/1.73m2 increases the likelihood of complete kidney function recovery with adjusted OR 4.075 (95% CI 1.977–6.783) [32]. This is in line with our finding, which showed TLD discontinuation when eGFR is >60 mL/min/1.73m2 showed better recovery compared to discontinuing when eGFR is < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 (7% decrease and 28.3% decrease). After TDF discontinuation, most participants transitioned to AZT/3TC + DTG. Only one patient transitioned to 3TC + DTG dual treatment. However, a single tablet combining 3TC and DTG is not yet available in Indonesia, and TAF is not easily accessible.

This study highlights the need for kidney function assessment before ART initiation and regular monitoring of kidney function for PLHIV who have been transitioned to TLD as well as PLHIV receiving TLD as a first-line ART regimen in Indonesia. Close monitoring will provide the best support to prevent ART-related ESRD and other serious adverse events. In settings with limited access to laboratory testing, monitoring guidelines should consider focusing on higher-risk patients, which, in our situation, were patients with hypertension and longer use of ART. A previous study showed that their patients were afraid when they were about to change medication; therefore, doctors must provide adequate counseling and monitoring to help ease their uncertainty regarding the regimen transition [47].

The limitations of this study were the single-center and relatively small sample size. However, because our center had the highest number of nevirapine users in Indonesia before transitioning to TLD, this study can be representative of Indonesia. There might be different findings if the study was done in different populations or with longer observation. This observational study evaluated data from routine clinical care of participants who had creatinine measurements at baseline and at least one measurement during follow-up. Data on other possible contributing factors to kidney function decline, such as other nephrotoxic drugs as well as urinalysis, were not available and thus could not be investigated.

In conclusion, eGFR decline was frequently observed in PLHIV transitioning from an NVP-based regimen to TLD. The significant number of rapid eGFR decline incidents after this programmatic transition warrants regular monitoring to prevent kidney disease and possible adverse events, especially those with hypertension and longer use of ART. Those planning on transitioning to TLD should be screened for any risk factors regarding eGFR decline.

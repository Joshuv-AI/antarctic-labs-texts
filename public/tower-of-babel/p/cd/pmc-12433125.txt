The impact of NF-κB2 mutations on Tfh cell differentiation and their critical role in immune function
Authors: Yao Chen, Weitao Zhou, Leying Li, Tong Chen, Qi Wu, Qifan Li, Yufeng Zhou, Liling Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe non-canonical NF-κB2 pathway is crucial for immune regulation, and pathogenic mutations in NF-κB2 are linked to common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), recurrent infections, and autoimmune diseases. T follicular helper (Tfh) cells play a key role in B cell differentiation and antibody production, but the effects of NF-κB2 mutations on Tfh cell differentiation remain unclear. This study investigates the clinical and functional consequences of two NF-κB2 mutations: c.1714G>A (p.A572T) and c.2540dupT (p.R848Efs*38).MethodsWe analyzed clinical features, immunophenotypes, and endocrine profiles of three patients carrying NF-κB2 mutations. Transcriptome sequencing of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from Patient 3 (P3) and five healthy donors was performed to examine gene expression changes. Flow cytometry quantified Tfh cell populations, and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) validated the expression of genes involved in Tfh differentiation. The impact of NF-κB2 mutations on p100 processing and nuclear translocation was assessed via western blot and immunofluorescence in HEK293T cells.ResultsPatient 1 (P1) exhibited mild clinical features, primarily asthma, while Patient 3 (P3) presented with severe immunodeficiency, recurrent pulmonary infections, and hormonal deficiencies. Transcriptome sequencing revealed significant downregulation of T cell differentiation pathways in P3, particularly Tfh-related genes such as ASCL2, IRF4, and BHLHE40. Flow cytometry confirmed a marked reduction in circulating Tfh cells in P3. Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated that the R848Efs*38 mutation impaired the conversion of p100 into p52 and disrupted nuclear translocation.ConclusionsThis study identifies novel mechanisms by which NF-κB2 mutations impair immune function. The R848Efs*38 mutation disrupts Tfh cell differentiation by interfering with p100 processing and reducing key Tfh-related transcription factors. These findings enhance our understanding of NF-κB2-related immunodeficiencies and their molecular underpinnings, contributing to the broader knowledge of immune regulation and potential therapeutic targets.Keywords: NF-κB2, T follicular helper cells (Tfh cells), common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), immunodeficiency, gene mutation

Highlight box

Key findings

• Two NF-κB2 mutations, c.1714G>A (p.A572T) and c.2540dupT (p.R848Efs*38), showed variable clinical effects—A572T caused mild asthma, while R848Efs*38 led to severe immunodeficiency, recurrent infections, and endocrine dysfunction. Transcriptome analysis of R848Efs*38 revealed downregulation of T follicular helper (Tfh) differentiation genes (ASCL2, IRF4, BHLHE40), with flow cytometry confirming reduced Tfh cells. Functional assays showed defective p100 processing and impaired p52 nuclear translocation in R848Efs*38.

What is known and what is new?

• NF-κB2 mutations cause common variable immunodeficiency, hypogammaglobulinemia, and recurrent infections. NF-κB2 regulates immune cell development, but its role in Tfh differentiation is unclear.

• NF-κB2 mutations impair Tfh differentiation, reducing key transcription factors and cytokines. The R848Efs*38 mutation disrupts p100 processing, providing a molecular mechanism for its immunodeficiency effects.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The findings highlight the critical role of Tfh cell deficiency in individuals with NF-κB2 mutations and its contribution to immune dysregulation. Dysregulated Tfh-related cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-6 and IL-21 may represent actionable therapeutic targets. Further studies are needed to investigate NF-κB2’s role in adaptive immunity.

Introduction

The NF-κB family contains a conserved N-terminal Rel homology domain (RHD), which plays a key role in inflammation and immune response. It includes five inducible transcription factors: RelA, RelB, NF-κB1-p105/p50, NF-κB2-p100/p52, and c-Rel (1). Full-length NF-κB2/p100 interacts with other NF-κB family members to inhibit their transcription activity (2-4). Upon activation of the NF-κB2 pathway, NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK) works with IκB kinase α (IKKα) to phosphorylate p100 at Ser866 and Ser870, promoting its ubiquitination at Lys855. The proteasome then degrades the C terminal portion of p100, generating the active form of p52 (5,6).

Pathogenic mutations of NF-κB2 give rise to a range of clinical phenotypes, including hypogammaglobulinemia, recurrent lung infections, lymphadenopathy, and autoimmune diseases, with varying severity. This condition is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner (7,8). In 2013, Chen’s team first reported that individuals with NF-κB2 mutations manifested features of common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and corticotropin deficiency. Flow cytometric analysis of peripheral blood lymphocyte showed a significant increase in naïve cells and a decrease or even loss of memory B cells (9). T follicular helper (Tfh) cells play an important role in regulating B cell development and differentiation within the germinal center (GC), facilitating antibody production and the establishment of memory B cells following viral infections (10). While studies have shown that pathogenic mutations in NF-κB2 disrupt the maturation and differentiation of B cells in the GC, it remains unclear whether they also affect the differentiation and function of Tfh cells in the GC (8,11,12).

The principal structural domains of the NF-κB2 gene encompass the RHD, ankyrin repeat domain (ARD), processing-inhibitory domain (PID), and NIK-responsive sequence (NRS) (13,14). Mutations within these distinct domains result in different functional impairments of NF-κB2, which in turn lead to a broad spectrum of clinical phenotypes (7,8). In patients with autosomal dominant NF-κB2 deficiency, who also exhibit ectodermal dysplasia and anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies, mutations in NFKB2 impair p100 processing, resulting in the generation of p52 (7). Consequently, the clinical characterization of each novel mutation enriches the global database of human gene mutations.

In this study, the functional alterations and clinical characteristics associated with two novel NF-κB2 mutations located in distinct domains were examined: c.1714G>A (a novel mutation) and c.2540dupT. The c.1714G>A (p.A572T) variant is in the ARD of the NFKB2 protein, a region critical for mediating protein-protein interactions. In contrast, the c.2540dupT (p.R848Efs*38) mutation results in a premature truncation of the C-terminal death domain—normally terminating at amino acid 900—which is essential for the proteasomal processing of p100. Notably, these two variants are associated with distinct clinical phenotypes. The mechanism by which NF-κB2 influences T cell differentiation was also investigated through transcriptome sequencing of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Furthermore, the proportion of Tfh cells in the patient’s PBMCs was evaluated using flow cytometry, and messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) expression levels of genes involved in Tfh differentiation were quantified. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2025-122/rc).

Methods

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments, with approval from the Ethics Committee of the Children’s Hospital of Fudan University (No. 2023-117). Informed consent was obtained from all patients’ legal guardians.

Plasmids and mutagenesis

Total RNA was isolated from HEK 293T cells using TRIzol reagent (ThermoFisher, 15596026) and subsequently reverse transcribed (Vazyme, R323-01) to generate the complementary DNA (cDNA) template. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification of the target genes NF-κB2 and MAP3K14, plasmid vector linearization, and homologous recombination were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Vazyme, C112). The NF-κB2 mutant was engineered using a site-directed mutagenesis kit, followed by digestion with DpnI (TOYOBO, SMK-101) to eliminate the template.

Cell culture

HEK293T cells were maintained in DMEM (Gibco, 11995065) as the basal medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, 10099158) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Beyotime, C0222). HEK293T cells were transiently transfected with the PEI reagent according to the manufacturer’s protocol. PBMCs were isolated using Ficoll density gradient centrifugation (Cytiva, 17144002). PBMCs were cultured in RPMI 1640 (Gibco, 11875119) as the basal medium, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, 10099158) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Beyotime, C0222) to generate complete culture medium. Each in vitro functional experiment was performed in three independent biological replicates.

Western blots

HEK293T cells were incubated for 30 minutes in RIPA lysis and extraction buffer (Thermo Scientific, 89901), supplemented with Halt Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (Thermo Scientific, 78440). Protein concentrations were measured using a BCA Assay Kit (Vazyme, E112). The protein samples were mixed with 5X SDS-PAGE Loading Buffer (Beyotime, P0015L), boiled, and separated on 10% Tris-Glycine gels (Epizyme, PG212). The proteins were then transferred to a PVDF membrane and detected by Western blot using antibodies against NF-κB2 (CST, #3017), p-NF-κB2 (CST, #4810), and GAPDH (Proteintch, 1E6D9). All antibodies were used at a 1:1000 dilution for the Western blot experiments.

Gene expression

RNA from transfected HEK293T cells was extracted using Trizol and reverse transcribed to cDNA following the manufacturer’s instructions (Vazyme, R323-01). Gene expression was measured using SYBR Green (Vazyme, Q711) in a real-time PCR system, and relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−△△Ct method. The primers used for qPCR are displayed in the Table S1.

Immunofluorescence

Cells were plated in glass-bottom dishes and transfected with NF-κB2-WT and two mutants. After fixation, permeabilization, and blocking, the cells were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with primary antibodies (1:400 dilution): anti-NF-κB2 (CST, #3017) and anti-p-NF-κB2 (CST, #4810). After washing, the secondary antibody (AF488-conjugated anti-rabbit, Abcom, ab150077) was applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Cells were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, C1002), quenched, and imaged using a Leica TCS SP8 confocal microscope.

Luciferase reporter assay

Luciferase reporter constructs were generated by cloning the promoter regions of each candidate gene into the pGL3-basic vector, which drives the expression of Firefly luciferase upon promoter activation. In addition, a commercially available κB-Luc reporter plasmid (Beyotime, RG027) was used to evaluate NF-κB transcriptional activity. Wild-type HEK293T cells were seeded into 24-well plates and transfected using PEI 40K transfection reagent (Servicebio, G1802) with the following plasmids per well: 300 ng of reporter plasmid (either promoter-driven pGL3 or κB-Luc), 30 ng of pRL-TK Renilla luciferase control plasmid (internal control), MAP3K14 expression plasmid, and either wild-type or mutant NF-κB2 expression constructs. After 24–48 hours of incubation, cells were harvested, and luciferase activity was measured using the Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Thermo Fisher Scientific), following the manufacturer’s instructions. Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity to account for transfection efficiency. Transcription factor activity was assessed using Relative Luciferase Activity (RLA), calculated as the ratio of Firefly luciferase to Renilla luciferase luminescence.

Flow cytometry

Centrifuge 2×105 cells, discard the medium, and resuspend in Flow Cytometry Staining Buffer. Incubate with diluted primary antibody (1:100) at 4 ℃ for 20 minutes. Wash, permeabilize with Fixation/Permeabilization Buffer at 4 ℃ for 20 minutes, then wash again. Resuspend in PBS and transfer to flow cytometry tubes. Apply antibodies: CD3-Percpcy5.5 (BD, 757705), CD4-BV421 (BD, 566703), CD8-BV510 (BD, 742391), CD45RA-PEcy7 (BD, 568557), CXCR5-APC (BioLegend, 356907).

Transcriptome sequencing

PBMCs were isolated, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol. RNA purity and concentration were measured using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (ThermoScientific, Waltham, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, USA). Transcriptome libraries were prepared using the VAHTS Universal V5 RNA sequencing Library Prep Kit, and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis was performed by OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed and graphed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 and ImageJ software. Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired t-test. A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Clinical symptoms observed in the patients

In family A, the proband, Patient1, is a 5-year-old girl (Figure 1A) who, developed recurrent cough, frequent expectoration, and wheezing over a 6-month period. She was diagnosed with bronchopneumonia at a local hospital, despite the causative pathogen being unidentified, and later diagnosed with asthma at age 6 years. She had no history of chronic abdominal pain, diarrhea, headaches, dizziness, or developmental delay, and her immunoglobulin levels and B cell counts were normal (Table 1).

Exome sequencing identified a heterozygous NF-κB2 mutation (c.1714G>A, p.A572T) in the proband (Figure 1B), which was also present in her mother, P2, with no significant history of infections. The mother of the P1 patient did not present any clinical symptoms. As our facility is a children’s hospital, a clinical examination of the mother was not performed. The mutation was absent in her father and twin sister, confirming maternal inheritance.

Family B, proband P3, an 11-year-old male, was diagnosed with CVID at age 6 years (Figure 1A). He was born at full term via normal delivery but has had recurrent infections since birth. At age 6 years, he was hospitalized for unexplained fever, with laboratory results revealing Mucor infections in blood and bone marrow, along with adenoviral and Mycoplasma pneumoniae infections in the lungs. At age 10 years, he developed severe pneumonia following a COVID-19 infection. His immunological profile during non-infectious periods (Table 1) showed low levels immunoglobulin A (IgA) and immunoglobulin M (IgM), reduced B cells, increased T cells, and low ACTH and cortisol levels (Table 1). We conducted follow-up immunophenotyping analyses for the P3 patient from ages 6 to 11 years (Figure S1).

Whole exome sequencing identified a heterozygous mutation in the NF-κB2 gene (c.2540dup T, p. R848Efs*38) (Figure 1B). The mutation was absent in his parents and his 16-year-old sister, confirming it as a de novo mutation.

Decreased nuclear translocation of R848Efs*38 mutants

A572T and R848Efs*38 mutants were then transfected into HEK 293T cells. Western blot analysis showed that the R848Efs*38 mutant exhibited a reduced molecular weight of p100 and abnormal generation of p52 (Figure 2A,2B). The non-canonical NF-κB pathway is activated by NIK (MAP3K14). To examine the expression of the two mutants after NIK activation, MAP3K14 plasmids were co-transfected with wild-type (WT), A572T, and R848Efs*38. After activation, WT-p100 and A572T-p100 were phosphorylated and processed into p52, whereas R848Efs*38 exhibited abnormal p100 phosphorylation and failed to process into p52 (Figure 2A,2B). We have quantified the relative levels of p100, p52, and phosphorylated NFKB2 across different experimental groups, with and without MAP3K14 (Figure S2). We performed dual-luciferase reporter assays to investigate the functional differences between the WT and the two mutants. The results showed that the A572T variant exhibited no significant change in function, while the R848Efs*38 mutant demonstrated reduced transcriptional activity (Figure S2F).

Immunofluorescence showed that A572T translocated to the nucleus normally, whereas R848Efs*38 did not (Figure 2C). Thus, A572T processing was unaffected, while R848Efs*38 exhibited impaired p52 activity and enhanced IκBδ regulation.

Tfh cell differentiation pathways are downregulated in R848Efs*38 patient

Tfh differentiation is regulated by several transcription factors, such as BCL6, ASCL2, IRF4, PRDM1, FOXO1, and FOXP1 (15,16). To investigate the effect of R848Efs*38 mutation on Tfh differentiation, transcriptomic analysis was performed on PBMCs from individuals with the R848Efs38 mutation and five healthy volunteers. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis showed downregulation of T cell differentiation pathways (Figure 3A). Heatmap analysis revealed a decrease in several positive regulators of Tfh differentiation (Figure 3B). Volcano plot analysis further showed a significant decrease in the expression levels of positive regulators of Tfh differentiation, such as ASCL2, BCL6, IRF4, and BHLHE40, while the expression of certain negative regulators was increased (Figure 3C).

Additionally, GO enrichment analysis of interleukins associated with T cell subset development showed downregulation of the interleukin (IL)-6-related signaling pathway (Figure 3D). Despite performing GO enrichment analysis, no significant differences were found in the biological processes related to IL-21 production. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis of pathways associated with IL-6 indicated that STAT3, a key transcription factor downstream of IL-6 signaling (17,18), showed no significant variations (Figure 3E).

Decreased Tfh cell count in individuals with R848Efs*38 mutation

Flow cytometric analysis of PBMCs from P1, P2, and P3 revealed significant findings. Compared to healthy donors, P3 exhibited a notable decrease in the proportion of memory T cells (Figure 4A,4B). Further analysis of Tfh subsets showed a marked reduction in Tfh cells in P3 (Figure 4C). In contrast, no such changes were observed in P1 and P2. Discussion

The NF-κB2 transcription factor regulates a broad range of immune responses and immune cell differentiation processes. This study summarizes the clinical features associated with the A572T and R848Efs38 mutations and evaluates their functional impacts. We examined the peripheral blood Tfh cell count and cytokine levels related to Tfh differentiation in patients with the R848Efs38 mutation and analyzed RNA-seq data from PBMCs to explore the mechanisms underlying the reduction in Tfh cells caused by NF-κB2 mutations.

Previous reports have shown that NF-κB2 mutations are primarily located in the C-terminal domain, with clinical features predominantly characterized by hypogammaglobulinemia. Some patients also present with autoimmune symptoms, such as alopecia, and endocrine abnormalities, such as ACTH deficiency (8,19). In this study, we identified that the A572T mutation is located within the ARD, with carrier P1 exhibiting asthma as the primary clinical feature, but without significant changes in immunological parameters. In contrast, R848Efs*38 was characterized by recurrent pulmonary infections. However, his immunoglobulin G (IgG) levels were not significantly reduced, while IgA and IgM levels showed a marked decrease, which differs from previous findings (9). Additionally, R848Efs*38 exhibited reduced ACTH and cortisol levels, which may, to some extent, affect immune responses.

Tfh cells are recognized as essential for B cell proliferation and differentiation, particularly in the development of long-lived GC B cells, plasma cells, and high-affinity antibodies, all of which require Tfh cell support (10,16,20). The NF-κB2 pathway promotes Tfh cell development by NIK-mediated induction of inducible T-cell co-stimulator ligand (ICOSL) expression in B cells (21). The differentiation of Tfh cells is driven by multiple cytokines, with IL-6 and IL-21 activating key transcription factors such as STAT3 and BCL6 to promote this process (17,22). In our analysis, flow cytometry of peripheral blood from R848Efs*38 carriers revealed a significant decrease in both memory B cells and Tfh cells, supporting previous findings of reduced Tfh generation associated with NF-κB2 mutations (12). Furthermore, cytokine assays indicated a marked reduction in IL-6 and IL-21 levels in these carriers. RNA-seq enrichment analysis also showed a decline in the expression of genes involved in IL-6 production. These findings suggest that impaired Tfh development in NF-κB2 mutation carriers may be related to altered cytokine signaling, although the precise mechanisms require further investigation.

A distinct gene expression profile is exhibited by Tfh cells in comparison to other T cell subsets, with BCL6 being identified as a critical transcriptional regulator of their differentiation (23,24). In addition, the differentiation of Tfh cells is positively regulated by various other transcription factors, including ASCL2, TCF1, MAF, BATF, STAT3, BHLHE40, and IRF4 (15,25-29). In contrast, regulatory factors such as PRDM1, FOXO1, FOXP1, KLF2, BACH2, and TBX21 facilitate the differentiation of non-Tfh subsets by antagonizing genes that promote Tfh differentiation (15,30). Recent studies have shown that in peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL), NF-κB2 acts as a transcriptional activator of IRF4 expression and cell proliferation (31). RNA-seq analysis revealed a significant reduction in several positive regulators of Tfh differentiation in R848Efs*38 carriers, including IRF4, BCL6, ASCL2, BHLHE40, MAF, and TET2. Similarly, mRNA expression trends in P3 PBMCs were consistent with the RNA-seq results, supporting the role of NF-κB2 in Tfh differentiation.

Undoubtedly, there are some limitations in this study. Although NFKB2 is related to Tfh differentiation, its specific role in Tfh generation and differentiation remains to be further validated due to the lack of animal models. In conclusion, we found that the NFKB2 mutation leads to the downregulation of several positive regulatory factors involved in Tfh differentiation, and the expression levels of these factors show statistically significant differences when comparing P3 with HC. We measured the expression levels of regulatory factors only at the mRNA level, so further investigation is needed to clarify how NFKB2 regulates these genes. Additionally, due to the low detectability and poor reproducibility (with significant intra-group variability) of some regulatory factors, our results may be biased.

In summary, we describe two cases of NF-κB2 mutations: the A572T variant, which showed no significant immunological abnormalities, and the R848Efs38 variant, which was associated with a pronounced depletion of B cells and a marked reduction in the Tfh subset. RNA-seq analysis of peripheral blood from the R848Efs*38 case revealed significant downregulation of T cell differentiation pathways and a notable reduction in the expression of genes that drive Tfh differentiation.

Conclusions

This study identifies two NF-κB2 mutations (A572T and R848Efs*38) with distinct clinical effects. While A572T caused mild symptoms, R848Efs*38 led to severe immunodeficiency, recurrent infections, and endocrine dysfunction. Functional analysis showed that R848Efs*38 disrupts p100 processing, reduces p52 levels, and impairs NF-κB2 signaling. Transcriptome and flow cytometry analyses confirmed that NF-κB2 mutations impair Tfh differentiation, reducing key transcription factors and circulating Tfh cells. These findings highlight NF-κB2’s role in adaptive immunity and suggest genetic screening and targeted therapies for affected patients.

Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis that improved after a COVID-19 infection: a case report
Authors: Chunyu Xu, Li Gao, Xi Wang, Zhanwei Hu, Chengli Que
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP) is a rare disease caused by the accumulation of abnormal surfactant in the alveoli, leading to impaired gas exchange. A 54-year-old woman presented with intermittent wheezing who developed increasing infiltrates gradually on chest CT, was diagnosed with PAP in our department in December 2017. She then underwent granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) inhalation therapy for three months without improvement. Regular follow-up imaging and pulmonary function tests suggested disease progression. However, the patient declined whole-lung lavage (WLL) due to her unrestricted daily activities. Two months after a coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection in February 2023, the patient’s chest CT became clear, accompanied by improved pulmonary function. Repeated HRCT of the chest in March 2024 confirmed no signs of recurrence, nor in pulmonary function tests. In this case, COVID-19 infection may have contributed to the improvement of this patient’s PAP, raising the hypothesis of an immunomodulatory effect.

Background

Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP) is a rare disorder characterized by the accumulation of abnormal surfactant proteins and lipids in the alveoli, resulting in impaired gas exchange. Over 90% of PAP cases are autoimmune pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (APAP), caused by macrophage dysfunction resulting from neutralizing antibodies against granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) [1]. Clinically, PAP can present as incidental abnormalities in imaging, dyspnea on exertion, pulmonary fibrosis, hypoxic respiratory failure, and even death as the disease advances. However, spontaneous remission has been observed in some cases [2].

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) during the pandemic, has been associated with poor prognosis in those with pre-existing lung diseases and may also pose an additional risk factor for severe pneumonia in PAP patients [3]. In December 2022, China experienced a widespread outbreak of the Omicron variant of SARS-CoV-2. Duan et al. reported a single-center study indicating that APAP patients with poor baseline respiratory function were more likely to develop hypoxia after a COVID-19 infection, though fatal events remained rare [4].

Herein, we report a case of a patient with PAP who improved after COVID-19 infection, suggesting possible immunological interactions between PAP and COVID-19.

Case presentation

A 54-year-old woman presented to our hospital with intermittent wheezing and patchy ground-glass opacities on chest CT in March 2017. She was allergic to cephalosporins, mangoes, paints and century eggs, along with a diagnosis of allergic rhinitis. She was a non-smoker but worked in a restaurant with exposure to oil fumes. The patient underwent two bronchoscopies at the outpatient clinic, and transbronchial lung biopsy indicated interstitial pneumonia. The patient was initially diagnosed with interstitial lung disease and she resumed oral prednisone acetate (40 mg/d). During tapering of prednisone, the patient was hospitalized for severe wheezing following a cold in December 2017.

After admission, the patient’s complete blood count, liver and renal function tests, coagulation profile, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, immunoglobulin levels, lymphocyte subpopulations were all within normal limits, except the serum total IgE level was elevated at 1252 kU/L. Tests for cryptococcal antigen, CMV-DNA and EBV-DNA were negative. Additionally, previous immunological screening performed in the outpatient department showed a positive antinuclear antibody (ANA) titer of 1:100, with negative results for rheumatoid factor (RF) and antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA). Chest CT revealed multiple patchy ground-glass opacities in the subpleural regions of both lungs, accompanied by interlobular septal thickening, forming a characteristic “crazy-paving” pattern (Fig. 1A). She underwent a third bronchoscopy, which revealed white and cloudy BAL fluid. Transbronchial lung biopsy showed essentially normal alveolar structures, with homogeneous pink staining in the alveolar lumen, which was positive for PAS and PAS-D staining (Fig. 2). As GM-CSF antibody testing was unavailable in our hospital, she was diagnosed with PAP, with a possible diagnosis of APAP.

The patient underwent three months of GM-CSF inhalation therapy with no improvement in symptoms. She declined whole-lung lavage (WLL) and received no other treatments. Since then, regular pulmonary function evaluations have shown a progressive decline, with FVC%predicted decreasing from 117.1% to 112.8% and DLco%predicted from 111.5% to 80.9% over four years (Fig. 3). Concurrently, chest CT scans revealed a gradual expansion of abnormal shadows in both lungs (Fig. 1B).

Five years after the PAP diagnosis, in December 2022, a week before her planned follow-up, the patient developed fever, cough, and chest pain. A nasal swab test for SARS-CoV-2 antigen was positive, and converted to negative within eight days with only symptomatic therapy. Two months later, to our surprise, chest CT showed a significant reduction in pulmonary opacities, as well as an improvement in pulmonary function (Figs. 1C and 3). Approximately one year after the COVID-19 infection, the patient underwent another chest CT, which showed complete resolution of pulmonary infiltrates, stable pulmonary function, and overall better than before COVID-19 (Figs. 1D and 3).

In addition, we monitored the patient’s allergic rhinitis during long-term follow-up. Before the COVID-19 infection, the patient’s allergic rhinitis symptoms showed no significant change despite the progression of PAP. Initially, the proportion of peripheral blood eosinophils was 7.7%, which decreased to 0% during treatment with prednisone acetate. During follow-up prior to the COVID-19 infection, it gradually increased in a fluctuating pattern to 12.7%. The total serum IgE level fluctuated below 1200 kU/L. After the COVID-19 infection, the patient’s symptoms of allergic rhinitis worsened significantly. The proportion of peripheral blood eosinophils significantly rose to 29.6%, while the total serum IgE concentration increased to 1785 kU/L. It seemed to be a process that was completely opposite to the clinical course of PAP (Fig. 4).

Discussion and conclusion

In this case, we observed the whole process of a gradual accumulation of abnormal surfactant in the lung almost from the de novo stage to the resolution after COVID-19 infection. Anti-GM-CSF antibody testing was unavailable in our hospital, which limits the diagnostic certainty of APAP. However, regular follow-up allows us to obtain more detailed information about the patient’s clinical progress.

The progression of PAP is unpredictable. Spontaneous remission may occur in 17-25% of cases, but no predictive factors have been identified so far, and secondary deterioration may occur [5]. The exact mechanism underlying spontaneous remission remains unclear, though potential factors include infection-induced immune reconstitution, natural repair of the lung, and recovery of airway clearance. Additionally, lifestyle and environmental changes, such as the elimination of harmful substances, may also contribute to spontaneous remission. In previous clinical practice, we have observed spontaneous remission in PAP patients following removal from dust exposure or smoking cessation. In this case, although the patient worked in a restaurant, she was not a chef, and therefore had minimal exposure to oil fumes. Moreover, she has had no changes in her occupational environment throughout the disease course, suggesting that this factor had little or no impact on the improvement of PAP. In previous reports of improvement in PAP after infection, it has not been possible to determine whether they were due to infection or were treatment-related [6]. Our patient, however, did not receive antiviral therapy at the time of COVID-19, making it more probable that the improvement in PAP was attributed to infection, although other factors cannot be excluded with certainty.

COVID-19 is a clinical syndrome caused by SARS-CoV-2 infection, characterized by symptoms ranging from mild to severe pneumonia, which can progress to respiratory failure, mechanical ventilation and death. According to literature, new-onset PAP and exacerbation of pre-existing PAP have been reported relatively common after COVID-19, while cases of PAP remission are rare. In addition to the progressive elevation of multiple inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, including IL-6, TNF-α, CXCL10, etc [7], SARS-CoV-2 infection also stimulates GM-CSF signaling in alveolar macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils [8]. Moreover, a feedback loop between SARS-CoV-2 infected alveolar macrophages and T cells promotes ongoing alveolar inflammation and macrophage activation [9]. Since impaired macrophage function is central to PAP pathogenesis, such immune modulation may transiently restore macrophage activity and enhance surfactant clearance. Therefore, activation of GM-CSF signaling and alveolar macrophages may contribute to the remission of PAP observed in this case.

In this case, the patient exhibited not only significant remission of PAP following COVID-19, but also marked increases in peripheral blood eosinophils and total serum IgE levels, with worsening symptoms attributable to allergic rhinitis. This phenomenon suggests that SARS-CoV-2 infection may have triggered dynamic changes in Th1/Th2 immune responses. Previous studies indicate that COVID-19 may trigger both Th1 and Th2 immune responses during the acute phase, with Th2 polarization becoming increasingly pronounced as the disease progresses [10]. Th1 activity might contribute to enhanced macrophage function and clearance of surfactant in PAP, while Th2 dominance may underlie the increased eosinophils and worsening of allergic symptoms observed in this patient. However, since no cytokine or lymphocyte subset testing was performed in this case, direct immunological evidence is lacking. Future studies with detailed immune profiling are essential to better understand the immunomodulatory effects of SARS-CoV-2 in PAP patients with allergic conditions.

In addition to the present case, several other cases of PAP remission following infections have been reported (Table 1) [6, 11, 12]. Especially, Atsushi Yanagisawa et al. reported a 77-year-old male patient with PAP who showed significant improvement after COVID-19 treated with remdesivir [12]. Unlike previously reported cases, our patient not only exhibited remission of PAP after COVID-19, but also showed worsening of allergic rhinitis, reflecting dynamic changes in Th1/Th2 immune responses. This further indicates the changes in immune status associated with SARS-CoV-2. In addition, our patient did not receive antiviral therapy during the COVID-19, which partially excludes the influence of antiviral treatment on the outcome of PAP. Moreover, differences in dominant strain of SARS-CoV-2 at different time points might not affect patients to the same degree. Further research is needed to explore the association between SARS-CoV-2 strain types and the clinical course of PAP.

This case has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First of all, at the time of initial diagnosis, anti-GM-CSF antibody testing was not available in our hospital, which led to a presumptive diagnosis of APAP. Although the complex immune interactions observed during the disease course support the likelihood of APAP to some extent, a definitive diagnosis of APAP could not be established. Additionally, the patient’s last chest CT prior to SARS-CoV-2 infection was performed one year before infection. The patient unfortunately contracted COVID-19 when follow-up imaging was originally planned, which prevented us from obtaining radiological data closer to the pre-infection state. This made it difficult to fully exclude other unrecognized contributing factors, which may have introduced bias or reduced the precision of our conclusions. We will continue to follow up this patient and observe her subsequent clinical course in the future.

Although unresponsive to inhaled GM-CSF, the patient’s remission after COVID-19 suggests the association between PAP and infections, especially viral disease. This finding may offer insights into novel therapeutic strategies. More data are needed to validate this hypothesis and to facilitate the prediction of clinical remission, which might be helpful in the development of novel therapeutic modalities for PAP patients.

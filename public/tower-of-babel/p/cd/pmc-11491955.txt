Text mining approach in chicken meat welfare
Authors: Valentina Cesari, Giulia Gislon, Luciana Bava, Ivan Toschi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The aim of the study was to analyze the aspects affecting broiler welfare with the use of Text Mining technique. This approach converts text into numerical data and analyzes word frequency distributions, enabling the extraction of useful information and the identification of relationships between elements. Text mining has limitations, i.e. ambiguity and context sensitivity, making it difficult to capture nuanced meanings. The search was carried out with Scopus using the term “Welfare” with the keywords “Chicken”, “Broiler”, “Broiler chicken”, and “Chicken meat”, to create a database of abstracts. Text Mining and Topic Analysis were performed on the abstracts (1228 documents) using the Software R 4.3.1., analyzing also the weight of bigram and trigram. Publications on broiler welfare are present in the bibliography since 1990′s, but in the last 10 years, for the interest of public opinion, the numbers of publications significantly increased (76.5% of all documents published). USA, Brazil, and Europe-27 published 60% of the documents found. The works were published in a high number of journals, but 37% of them are published in only 4 journals (Poultry Science, Animals, Applied Animal Behavior Science and Animal Welfare). Text Mining analysis identified key terms related to the slaughter phase, housing management, and environmental conditions such as light quality and quantity. Moreover, a high correlation was found between some terms, underlying the importance of the effects of rearing, slaughter phases and litter management on broiler welfare. Most of the countries focused their research on some specific topics identified by Topic Analysis, mainly genetic selection, feeding, stocking density, slaughter, and consumer perceptions. Poultry Science published the highest number of papers (18%) and the topics more investigated were growing performance, transport and slaughter, and litter management. In conclusion, the high number of publications on chicken welfare underlines the importance of broiler welfare both in Europe and in other countries, even if it is difficult to identify common research topics among the geographic areas and the evolution over the time.

INTRODUCTION

Post-World War II, broiler chicken production in America and Europe transitioned from rural to intensive systems. In the past 3 decades, there has been a growing focus on animal welfare in these systems. Today, 90% of the global broiler production is organized in intensive breeding systems (Augère-Granier, 2019), characterized by high productive efficiency and stocking density, greater growing rates of hybrids, short production cycles, excellent feed conversion ratio, and high carcass yield and meat-bone ratio.

At the present, chicken meat is undoubtedly among the most requested products of animal origin worldwide, with a production of 123 million tons of meat, corresponding to approximately 75 billion of animals slaughtered per year (FAO, 2023).

The main producers of chicken meat are the United States, with 21.0 million tons of product per year, followed by China (about 14.3 million tons), Brazil (14.2 million tons), and EU-27 (11.4 million tons). Other important producers of broiler meat are Russia (4.7 million tons), Indonesia, Mexico, and India (about 3.8 million tons), Thailand (3.2 million tons), and Argentina with a production of 2.3 million tons. Among the European countries, Poland is the largest producer with almost 2.5 million tons of carcass weight, more than double the production of Italy each year; in fact, in Italy, over 550 million heads are slaughtered, yielding 1.02 million tons of chicken meat. Following Poland, the main producers are Germany, France and Spain with 1,39, 1,16 and 1,15 million tons of broiler meat, respectively (AVEC, 2023).

Animal welfare is crucial for several reasons, including ethical, environmental, social, and economic dimensions. These factors drive the scientific community to investigate and find the best possible rearing solutions to ensure optimal living conditions for animals. When considering broiler rearing under intensive conditions, many factors must be considered, as they can directly impact their welfare. Housing conditions (ventilation, temperature, humidity, and lighting programs) and the environmental enrichment of broiler farms are the most important aspects of chicken rearing, because these aspects directly affect animal welfare, growth performances and meat quality (El-Deek and El-Sabrout 2019; Marchewka et al., 2023; Riber et al., 2018; Tainika et al., 2023). Management of the animals, mostly in terms of stocking density and the use of cage, frequent in some countries, could affect animal behavior and welfare (Chen et al., 2020; El-Deek and El-Sabrout 2019). In addition, behaviour and growth may be affected by feeding programs, diet composition and water supply (FSA et al., 2023). Other aspects of the productive cycle, such as capture, transport, and slaughtering (Benincasa et al., 2020; Jacobs et al., 2017), could influence broiler welfare, affecting carcass and meat quality of the animals (i.e., bone lesions, and myopathies).

Recently, sensitivity of the consumers to the ethical aspects of broiler production has increased considerably, and several states have issued laws on the regulation of poultry welfare. Generally speaking, the most important producers of broiler meat have limited or no legislations on animal welfare and protection. In the United States there is no confederation law on the subject of breeding poultry for meat production, even if the National Chicken Council (NCC, the national trade association representing vertically integrated broiler producer-processors) has developed welfare guidelines to assure the humane treatment of broilers and to promote the production of quality products (NCC, 2022). On the contrary, in Europe, regulations and directives have been implemented in the last 20 years. In 2007 the Eu Directive, 2007 (concerning the laying down minimum rules for the protection of chickens reared for meat production) was adopted with the aim of regulating the main aspects of poultry production system, discussing in particular the rearing system (mostly in terms of stocking density) and the management of the housing environment and animals. In 2023, moreover, following a request from the European Commission, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) gave an independent view on the protection of broilers during the different phases of the production cycle (day-old chicks, broiler breeders and broilers for meat production) considering the different factors affecting broiler welfare (FSA et al., 2023).

As suggested by Sahadevan et al. (2012) in biological research, establishing the state of the art by searching and collecting information present in the domain has equal importance as the experimental phase. Scientific literature is increasingly vast and, therefore, obtaining structured and relevant information is very time consuming. Text analysis tools, like Text Mining (TM) technique, could be an interesting solution, because it permits to have an automatic extraction of unknown and potentially useful information from the texts. This method supports the identification of relationships between various elements of the collected information, in order to discover knowledge (Garcia, 2013) useful for the researchers, but also for livestock sector stakeholders. The technique of TM can be used to summarise information into charts or maps, to identify hidden structures between concepts or groups or concepts, to extract hidden associations between textual elements, to provide an overview of the contents of a large collection of documents and to categorise texts by discovering relevant groupings (Abutridy, 2000).

Considering the sector of poultry science and, in particular, chicken meat welfare, many reviews have been presented in scientific literature (in our research 59 papers, till December 2023) but none of these with a so structured approach as TM.

For this reason, the aim of this study was to analyse the international scientific literature on chicken meat welfare, with the use of TM approach, through the analysis of the most important aspects affecting broiler welfare highlighting the differences among the various geographic areas and the evolution over the time.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Database Preparation

The database of abstracts that covered the topic of the present study was created by searching documents in Scopus scientific database in the fields “article title, abstract, keywords”. The term “Welfare” was employed alongside the keywords “Chicken”, “Broiler”, “Broiler chicken”, and “Chicken meat”. The logical operator was “and”.

Although the keyword ‘poultry’ was initially included, it was excluded to focus specifically on broiler farming, thus omitting other species like ducks, turkeys, and geese. For Scopus it was adopted the restriction criterion of Subject Area, limited to Agricultural and Biological Sciences, Veterinary and Multidisciplinary. We included documents published between 1970 (even if the first document was found in 1991) and December 2023. Documents selected were original articles, reviews, book chapter and conference proceedings. A total of 6333 documents were extracted. Reasons for subsequent exclusion from the database were: no author available, no abstract available and no keywords available. In addition, a screening of all the articles was performed to delete the duplicated ones. Additionally, the authors manually screened the database to eliminate abstracts deemed off-topic, specifically excluding articles that do not examine the relationship between broilers and welfare, based on their scientific expertise.

The final dataset consisted of 1,228 documents.

Descriptive Statistics

Some descriptive statistics of the literature selected were performed to observe how different geographic regions around the world approach the topic of chicken meat welfare. All the descriptive statistics were conducted with the Software SAS 9.4 and Software R 4.3.1.

Text Mining

A Text Mining (TM) analysis was performed on the abstracts of the selected publications to find the most important words of the data corpus and their associations. The TM approach converts the text into a numeric information and analyses the word frequency distributions (Nalon et al., 2021; Zuliani et al., 2021). For TM the Software R 4.3.1. was used. To preprocess the text data, words were converted to lowercase and any unusual characters were removed. Punctuation, common English stopwords, numbers, and extra white spaces were also eliminated. In addition, keywords used in the literature search were removed to avoid low discriminative information due to their presence in almost all retrieved abstracts (Nalon et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2016). Finally, stemming was performed to reduce all words to their root form using the R library “SnowballC.”

A Document Term Matrix (DTM) was created to organise the terms (both terms and roots) into a matrix with the documents in the rows and the terms in the columns. A Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TFIDF) technique was used to assign a relative weight to each word (Salton and Buckley, 1988). This represents the importance of a term in the whole collection of documents.

Only terms with a length ≥4 were kept for the DTM. Terms that were scattered were removed from the matrix, reducing the DTM from 8173 to 1275 terms. A graphical representation of the most important terms (by keeping only those with a weight ≥10) was created with R Software, and all the terms were represented as a histogram. The association between terms with frequencies ≥10 was evaluated by considering a correlation limit of 0.4. Bigrams and trigrams analysis were also performed, by using R library “RWeka”, and the first 10 bigrams and trigrams were plotted on the weight basis. The thresholds for term weight (≥10) and correlation (0.4) were chosen after several tests as the best compromise to capture the most significant results from the text analysis, while still retaining important information.

Using this Text Mining approach for literature analysis overcomes the issue of open access policies regarding the dissemination and accessibility of broiler welfare research. This is because, within this technique, only the abstract is used. This work is highly reproducible because it relies on data that are accessible to everyone, without being limited by journal subscriptions.

Topic Analysis

Topic modelling analysis was performed using the Software R 4.3.1. for clustering terms and documents in the corpus into homogeneous groups based on the research topics. This analysis, indeed, is useful to uncover the structure of meaningful themes among collections of documents as well as to discover hidden textual patterns (Zuliani et al., 2021).

The approach of Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA; Blei et al., 2003) was used for topic modelling.

The LDA approach was used because of its popularity in the international literature for performing topic modeling analysis. The approach of LDA is often preferred for text mining analysis due to its interpretability, as it provides clear insights into the topics present in the texts by modeling each document as a mixture of topics and each topic as a mixture of words. It is a generative model that assumes documents are generated from a distribution of topics, which helps in understanding latent relationships between documents and topics. Additionally, LDA is flexible and performs well with large text corpora, allowing for the discovery of a variable number of topics. It also offers a view of how topics are distributed across documents, which helps in understanding the relationships between different themes within a corpus.

Typical values of alpha and beta were used, that is 50/k (with k being the number of topics) and 200/W (with W being the number of words in vocabulary), respectively (Griffiths et al., 2004).

Each term retained by the text mining was scored inside each topic based on the probability of belonging to that specific topic (beta). Terms are typically sorted in descending order of probability. The first 10 terms for each topic were chosen as those that most characterise the topic.

The number of topics (k) was selected to provide a comprehensive description of the corpus and a percentage of documents per topic ≥5%. Different trials were performed, namely 5, 8, 10, 15, 20 topics; 10 topics were eventually selected.

Each topic was labelled based on the most important terms and documents that belonged to it.

Statistical analysis for the topic trend over the years and over the different geographical areas was performed using the Software SAS 9.4, by PROC FREQ model. The charts were made using the Software R 4.3.1.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Documents related to chicken meat welfare showed varying distribution across the World: until 2023, European continent had published 48% of the documents, followed by North America (17%), Asia (15%), and South America (14%). The contribution to the international bibliography of Africa and Oceania was small, namely 4% and 3%, respectively.

Publications in the Europe-27 were fairly evenly distributed, with a total of 420 documents; in particular, Germany published 6% of the international bibliography, Italy 4%, Spain 3%, France 2%, and Poland 2%.

China emerged on the other Asian countries with 59 documents, representing 5% of the international bibliography on chicken welfare, while other countries, such as Russia (2 documents), Indonesia (1 documents), Mexico (13 documents), India (19 documents), Thailand (4 documents), and Argentina (7 documents) had an extremely low number of published documents (Figure 1).

Even if the largest broiler producers worldwide such as USA, Brazil, and Europe-27 are sensitive to the animal welfare and contributed most to the international bibliography, a correlation analysis between country-level production and the numbers of publications is not always possible because other large broiler producers (e.g., Russia and Indonesia) published less documents probably because the culture and the perception of people in animal welfare is different.

Scientific publications on broiler welfare are present in the international bibliography since 1990′s, and the majority of documents (76.5%) were published in the last 10 years, probably due to the increasing concern for animal welfare expressed by the citizens, with the highest number of publications occurring in 2022 (145 publications). The interest of public opinion in the animal welfare, the need of legislators to acquire correct information from the world of science, useful to promote regulation, and the law in welfare matter had probably prompted scientist to increase research in this topic.

Until a few years ago, the discussion on poultry welfare was limited to the European countries, however, quite recently, the condition of broilers and laying hens have become the subject of public debate in several non-European countries, such as USA, Australia, Japan and Korea (Bessei, 2018). The number of documents from each country suggests a relationship between the level of research on this topic and the presence of broiler welfare regulations. Public opinion has also influenced research to explore various aspects of broiler welfare.

As reported by Van Horne and Achterbosch (2008), animal welfare is now considered an important topic in Europe-27, where welfare is regulated by the Eu Directive, 2007 issued, due to the attention of the consumers to welfare problems in intensive chicken farming (namely with high stocking density), as well as in many non-European countries, due to the export of poultry meat. Brazil, USA, and Europe-27 that (with Thailand) are the major poultry exporters worldwide accounted for 70% of poultry meat exchange (USDA, 2024), and also accounted for a similar percentage of publications about poultry welfare (Figure 2). The first year of publication on chicken welfare in the USA was 1992 (Figure 2). In the USA, which traditionally had focused researches in the areas of transport and slaughter (Mench, 2008), the National Chicken Council (NCC) developed broiler welfare guidelines (2022), while in Brazil private certification schemes (GLOBALG.A.P.® and Certified Humane®) for welfare-friendly poultry production have become widespread, even if Brazil started to publish 10 year later than USA, namely in 2002 (Figure 2).

The first year of publication in Europe-27 was 1996, in Belgium, and, towards the end of the last century, most European countries initiated the publication of documents on chicken meat welfare: France and Germany started to publish in 1997, while in Italy animal welfare has become an important topic since 2006 (Figure 2). This trend of increasing publications about chicken welfare in the European Community is probably due to the growing attention of the member countries to the animal welfare in intensive farming, demonstrated by the recommendations concerning avian species adopted by the European Community (Eu Directive, 2007 and European Commission, 2009). Greece was the most recent country of European Union and worldwide to publish about broiler welfare, in 2014, and the number of documents published till now is low (14 documents), probably due to the low chicken production (Figure 2).

United Kingdom was one of the first countries that started to publish in 1993, and the country is also one of those with the highest number of publications up to date, namely 133 documents (Figure 2).

The first country ever to publish on chicken welfare was Canada, publishing for the first time in 1991 a paper of Leeson about the use of growth-promoters on poultry meat. However, Canada had produced only 35 documents until 2023 (Figure 2).

The concept of animal welfare is still relatively new in China, but its importance is growing, due to the improvement of socio-economic conditions. Recently, some studies focused on the relationship between broiler production system and animal welfare (Chen et al., 2020), even if Sinclair et al. (2020) underlined that some scientific publications are still written in Chinese and hardly accessed by the world scientific community. According to the results obtained, China started to publish on broiler welfare in 2009, even if, until last year it published almost 60 documents (Figure 2).

However, in some areas of the World, such as Indonesia, Mexico and India, the perception of benefits achieving from introducing animal welfare improvements in the intensive farming is still low, even if they are important producers of broiler meat. In these areas, indeed, there is a widespread belief that welfare is a benefit for developing countries, not for less developed countries, where people need to buy food as cheap as possible and can't choose and discern food (Sinclair et al., 2019; Wilcox et al., 2024). For this reason, the number of publications in those underdeveloped countries is still low, despite the high production of chicken meat.

Only 62% of the scientific papers, that is, 758 out of 1,228 documents, were published in the journals depicted in this figure. The highest number of papers was published on Poultry Science (i.e. 215 out of 1,228 scientific documents, i.e. 18%), representing the most specialized and prestigious Journal in the sector (with an Impact Factor of 4.4 in 2024), which commenced the publication of articles on chicken welfare in 1996.Figure 3Distribution of published papers by journal, considering only journals with number of publications ≥10.Figure 3

Following Poultry Science, the second journal per number of publications was Animals (114 articles), an emerging European journal, the third journal was Applied Animal Behaviour Science (65 articles), and the fourth was Animal Welfare (56 articles), which published studies on animal welfare covering various species.Those 3 journals mentioned above, together published the 19% of all the 1,228 scientific publications considered.

The main chicken meat producers, United States, China, and Europe-27, published mainly on Poultry Science (162 papers) and Animals (71 papers), even if Brazil had published the most on its national journals, namely “Revista Brasileira de Ciencia Avicola” (29 papers) and “Engenharia Agricola” (12 papers). Europe-27 was the one with highest number of publications on Poultry Science (i.e. 90 papers), followed by USA, with 60 papers. Europe-27 was also the one with the highest number of publications on Animals (i.e. 41 papers), followed by USA with 23 papers. China published almost equally on Poultry Science, and Animals.

Considering the other big chicken meat producers, the few papers published by Mexico (13 documents) were presented mainly on Animals, while Thailand published only 4 papers on 4 different journals, not including Poultry Science either Animals. As for Brazil, Argentina and India had published on chicken welfare the most on their national journals, namely “Revista Veterinaria” (5 documents) for Argentina and “Indian Journal of Animal Research” and “Indian Journal of Animal Sciences” (8 documents) for India.

Text Mining

The text mining analysis, performed to analyse the relationship between welfare and poultry production, revealed the most important terms (both terms and roots) according to the weight, based on a Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TFIDF) technique (Figure 4).Figure 4Graphical representation of the most relevant words (by keeping only those with a weighting ≥10). Darker color means heavier words, based on a term frequency-inverse document frequency (TFIDF) technique.Figure 4

Graphical representation of the most relevant words (by keeping only those with a weighting ≥10). Darker color means heavier words, based on a term frequency-inverse document frequency (TFIDF) technique.

The ten most important terms extracted from the documents considered were “light” (TFIDF=17.4), “group” (TFIDF=15.4), “bird” (TFIDF=14.2), “density” (TFIDF=14.2), “system” (TFIDF=13.6), “anim” (TFIDF=13.5), “stress” (TFIDF=12.4), “score” (TFIDF=12.3), “treatment” (TFIDF=12.2), and “poultry” (TFIDF=12.1).

Some terms, such as “group”, “anim” and “bird” are common words used in all the publications, regardless of the research purpose. On the contrary, other terms were referred to specific scientific issues: “stress”, “stun”, and “transport” are words related to slaughter phase, while “density”, “system”, and “litter” were related to the housing management.

The word “light” was associated with the brightness of the environment, understood as an environmental parameter, rather than the live weight of broilers, as demonstrated by the correlation analysis. A high correlation (r = 0.54), indeed, was found between “light” and “intens” confirmed also by the results of bigram analysis where the TFIDF of the bigram “light intens” was high (TFIDF = 162). High correlations between “light” and the term “blue” (r = 0.44) and the term “diod” (r = 0.41) were also found, underlying the importance of quality and quantity of light in poultry farm or at slaughterhouse, attributable to the effect of the color, and to the recent interest of light-emitting diode (LED) lamps. Even if breeding companies usually suggest specific lighting programs and some countries adopted specific directives related to the light management, indeed, in the scientific literature there are numerous publications (Arowolo et al., 2019; Gomes de Oliveira and Camargos Lara, 2016) that investigated the relationships between the lighting programs, the growth performances and animal welfare.

As expected, the term “temperature” was associated with “humid” (r = 0.47) and “ambient” (r = 0.42) and the term “stress” resulted to be correlated with “heat” (r = 0.59). Heat stress (which occurs when high temperature and humidity prevent a broiler from regulating its body temperature) is one of the most important environmental stressors in intensive production systems (Oluwagbenga and Fraley, 2023), and the relationship between temperature and air humidity (expressed by the Temperature Humidity Index - THI) is an important aspect. The THI index significantly affects animal welfare and in intensive housing systems the control of temperature and air humidity could be made only by cooling and ventilation systems. With the bigram analysis, the weight of the bigram “heat stress” was high (TFIDF = 239), underlying that in the papers analysed the 2 terms appeared together most of the time. Another of the most important aspects affecting animal welfare is the slaughtering, because of the high number of animals slaughtered per hour. The good correlation found between the term “stun” and “electr” (r = 0.72), “bath” (r = 0.55) and “stunner” (r = 0.41) underlined that the most critical phase of the slaughter process is the stunning, which induces loss of consciousness in the animals (Berg and Raj, 2015).

In our research, the high weight (TFIDF = 414) of the bigram “stock densiti” evidenced the importance effect of stocking density on broiler welfare. The European legislation and most of the regulations of the other non-European countries, in fact, focus on this aspect. This consideration was also supported by the weight (TFIDF = 48) of the trigram “high stock densiti”, which underlined the relationship between the number of birds reared per unit of area, the nonoptimal environment conditions and the possible negative effects on the animal welfare and behaviour (Gül et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2024).

One of the negative consequences of inadequate farming density is podal lesions. The high weight of the bigram “gait score” (TFIDF = 243), of the trigram “footpad dermat” (TFIDF = 56) and of the trigram “dermat hock burn” (TFIDF = 35) underlined the high correlation between broiler welfare and foot pad and hock burn lesions, that appear when high stocking density and inadequate environmental management determine poor quality of the litter (Tainika et al., 2023; Kwon et al., 2024).

Among all the aspects that particularly influence chicken welfare, we must undoubtedly mention the issue of the use of cages, which in many countries is a topic of debate, both for public opinion and at political level (see for example in 2018 the European Citizens’, 2018 asking to prohibit the use of cages for EU farmed animals). In the majority of global broiler supply chain, broilers are kept on litter-bedded floor and not in cages, in order to avoid skin and leg lesions, which can affect meat quality and animal welfare, even if in some areas of the world (such as Africa, India, Russia, and some Asian countries) cages are commonly used. In the text mining the term “cage” was not among the top 10 most important word, while it appeared at 35 place, out of 1,275 terms. This is due to the fact that for the countries mentioned above the use of cage is not considered a problem. Moreover, these countries probably published in their original language, therefore English scientific literature regarding this aspect is low, determining an underestimate importance of the effect of cage on chicken welfare, as emerged also from the descriptive statistics.

Most of the countries focused on few topics, mainly related to productive factors such as genetic selection, feeding, and stocking density, slaughtering, and consumer perceptions.

USA, the World's leading producer of chicken meat and the first country in terms of scientific publications, published more on Topic 10 - Growing performance and Topic 4 - Genetic selection, attributing to these themes an important effect on broiler welfare, because welfare problems could be reduced through genetic selection and management of animals. China, instead, second in terms of production, published more on Topics 8 - Animal feeding and Topic 6 - Control of slaughter phases (Table 1). This is probably because China only began to deal with chicken welfare in 2009, and the sector is still very focused on increasing production, which in the last 15 years has grown by over 30%. Brazil, third largest producer of chicken meat in the world and second in terms of publications, published, above all in the Topic 9 - Heat stress and environmental housing conditions (Table 1), probably because broiler chicken production in Brazil is significantly affected by heat stress. In addition, Brazil, together with Spain, is the countries that published the most on the Topic 1- Transport and slaughter.

The topics that emerged from this analysis for the first 3 countries listed in the Table 1 emphasize that their scientific interest had been closely tied to purely productive aspects, in line with their type of production system.

At European level (27 Members States), where the legislation about broiler welfare has been applicated from 2007, all the topics were equally represented. The high percentage of publications in some topics, such as Topics 2, 3 and 4 underlined the importance of management choices and the effect of selection on animal health, even if some European countries focused their research on specific themes: animal feeding for Poland (40%), litter management for Germany (21.7%), and genetic selection for Spain (24.2%) (Table 1).

Italy published above all on Topic 3- Meat production system and consumer perception, and Topic 4- Genetic selection, even if other topics had been investigated, highlighting the importance given to several aspects of chicken meat production that may affect animal welfare (Table 1).

United Kingdom, that is one of the countries with the highest number of publications, paid particular attention to themes about rearing management and welfare evaluation in slaughter phases, publishing mainly on Topic 2- Stocking density, and Topic 6 - Control of slaughter phases, namely 20.3% and 22.6%, respectively.

Since 1998, the approach to the topic of animal welfare has become complex, as demonstrated by the fact that an increasing number of topics are recorded every year. Since 2007, all the topics identified in this work are present, although the percentage of each topic changes from year to year. This could be attributable to the legislation for the protection of broiler chickens adopted in Europe, which undoubtedly influenced the main exporting countries.

Topic 2 – Stocking density, Topic 4 - Genetic selection, and Topic 6 – Control of slaughter phases are those with the greater variability over the time: these topics were investigated mostly at the end of the ‘90s and in recent years. Probably this is due to increased attention of the public opinion and of legislators to management and genetic aspects, as demonstrated by the EFSA document of 2023, in which the welfare of the chick and the breeders are also considered. Starting from 2007, the attention of the scientific community has been focused on the aspects related to the meat production and consumer perception (Topic 3), and to the litter management (Topic 7), even if the topics related to productive factors (Topic 9 e 10) have remained at high level, because consumer perception towards animal production has changed over the time and because breeding companies have selected chickens more efficient, but with more welfare problems.

The number of publications on Topic 1, instead, has decreased probably because in the vertical integrated production chains the transport and slaughter are standardized phases hardly modified by technique and equipment innovation.

The first publication on Poultry Science regarding broiler welfare was in 1996 (Figure 7), when in the scientific literature there were very few works on this topic. Starting from 2007, the papers on broiler welfare published each year on Poultry Science significantly increased and started to deal with an increasing number of topics. Topics related to transport and slaughter (Topic 1), to the litter management (Topic 7), and to the different productive factors (Topics 8 and 10) have been the most investigated; in particular, the number of studies about growing performance are always very high. Topics 2, and 5, instead, are less represented in this journal, even if in 2023 works about Topics 2 are significantly increased. Moreover, the countries that have published the most on this journal over the years were the United States (60 papers) and Europe-27 (90 papers); in particular, in Europe-27 the Northern European countries (mainly the Netherlands) published more on broiler welfare. Even if China started to publish later on Poultry Science the number of papers (12 papers) is already comparable to that of the other countries such as UK (13), and Denmark (11).

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the high number of publications extracted on chicken welfare show that animal welfare is an important topic, both in Europe and in other countries, due to the increasing interest of researchers and consumers. However, results obtained from the present bibliographic analysis highlight a certain level of difficulty in identifying common lines of research on the effect of different topics on the broiler welfare, both globally and at European level. In this sense, the different aspects discussed are the result of both the context of the country and the research skills of the groups working on it. Regardless of the need to give regulatory guidance on broiler welfare, all the big producers have felt in the recent years the need to enact specific legislation on animal welfare or private certification schemes. Even if USA is the country where industrial poultry farming has developed and established, Europe has given a huge contribution in terms of scientific production, even more considering the 133 works of United Kingdom.

The theme of chicken welfare has been much investigated with a scientific approach so far, but, in the future, further study is needed as there are still problematic welfare situations in many countries.

In particular, the advancement of new research and techniques (capable of early evaluation of disease development, stress, and pain), the ability to provide health treatments to individual animals, and the proper management of capture, transport, and slaughter phases will contribute to reducing animal welfare problems and help formulate adequate answers to questions relating to ethical issues in broiler chicken production.

In addition, the new technologies implemented in the chicken production chain, based on precision techniques, pave the way for new insights.

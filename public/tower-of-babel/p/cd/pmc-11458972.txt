Active balancing strategy for AUV power battery pack based on PSO-PID algorithm
Authors: Shaowei Zhang, Yuli Hu, Silun Luo, Yuhan Li, Hairui Liang, Juchen Li, Liteng Zeng, Yu Pei, Lu Liu, Xuefei Wang, Chengyi Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, the battery inconsistency equalisation strategy is investigated and a novel fusion model based on equivalent circuit models is proposed. The three equivalent circuit models, 1RC, 2RC and PNGV, are weighted and fused by BP neuron network, which realizes the complementary advantages of the three equivalent circuit models. Even though the estimated values of all three models are lower than the true value, they can still be close to the true value after reasonable weight allocation. With reference to the open-source DST dynamic operating test data from the Advanced Battery Association of America, a comparison is made with the three common equivalent circuit models of 1RC, 2RC and PNGV. It is found that the proposed novel fusion model has the highest estimation accuracy with a maximum error of only 0.00947; the RMSE is 0.00217. The 1RC equivalent circuit model has the worst accuracy with a maximum error of 0.10145 and an RMSE of 0.02153. The 2RC and PNGV models have accuracies between the two. Then an active equalisation system is constructed to realise the charge equalisation of multiple unbalanced single batteries by distributed power supply. The PSO-PID strategy is used to control the topology circuit for adaptive equalisation. The equalisation effect of different sized battery packs is verified by simulation. Compared with the traditional logic control strategy, the method is simple and effective, and the equalisation effect is better as the number of inconsistent battery cells increases. In a battery pack with five initial SOC inconsistencies, the inter-cell variability is quickly equalised. When dynamic disturbances are introduced into the system, the algorithm also keeps the battery packs within the equalisation range with an average variance of 0.0016.Keywords: Lithium ion battery, Balanced management, Battery model, Optimize control, AUV

Introduction

Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUV) are currently widely used in geological surveys, water quality monitoring, and underwater cluster operations. Due to the high energy density and excellent discharge characteristics of lithium-ion batteries, they meet the future complex and changing mission requirements of the vehicle. Therefore, the traditional thermal power system is gradually replaced by the lithium-ion battery as the main energy source of the electric power system. Vehicles equipped with electric power system in the underwater operation of small vibration noise, stealthy, and not subject to the influence of the depth of navigation, can be closer to the target to implement effective combat. As the research hotspot of each country's underwater equipment, in recent years, for the speed of the vehicle, endurance, safety and reliability of the demand for more and more high indicators, which has become the core competitiveness of the underwater vehicle. At the same time, this is a big challenge for the energy system. Developing more energy-dense battery materials determines the lower limit of power system performance, while improving energy utilization through energy management (BMS) determines the upper limit of the power system. Among them, battery inconsistency equalisation, as one of the functions of BMS, directly affects the range and reliability of the vehicle. Because during the manufacturing process of batteries, it is inevitably to cause the small physical or chemical differences between the individual cells. When the vehicle is undergoing rapid manoeuvres, the high rate of current discharge exacerbates this phenomenon of imbalance. When there is inconsistency between the battery cells during charging, the higher capacity battery will reach the cut-off voltage earlier, but the other battery cells have not completed charging, if the charging continues at this time, the battery will be overcharged, there is a risk of thermal runaway(see Fig. 1Fig. 1). When discharging, the lower capacity battery will reach the cut-off voltage earlier, so that the battery pack stops discharging, and if it continues to discharge, it will cause irreversible damage to the battery.

Auv energy system.

The inconsistency study for batteries mainly includes the following aspects. Build an accurate battery model to characterise the operating state of the battery and estimate the state parameters such as SOC of each single cell. Build a balancing topology circuit to determine the imbalance of the battery pack based on the state parameters of the battery, and then use the balancing strategy to control the topology circuit for balancing.

Research on battery modelling

Establishing a battery model is the first and most fundamental step of any energy management system. Since the battery is an independent monomer, except for the voltage and current which can be measured directly by the instrument, other parameters such as the state of charge (SOC) and the state of health (SOH) of the battery can not be obtained by direct measurements, and they need to be obtained by many times of charging and discharging tests after the static state, which can not be used in practice. Therefore, a battery model is used to describe the internal working process of the battery, so that the battery parameters can be obtained through the battery model to provide a reference basis for the balancing strategy. Battery models are divided into three main categories according to the modelling approach: electrochemical models, equivalent circuit models and data-driven based models.

Electrochemical modelling

Electrochemical modelling describes the chemical reaction process inside the battery by building a complex mathematical model. Therefore, the accuracy of the model is relatively high. Commonly used electrochemical models are single-particle models, quasi two-dimensional models and simplified two-dimensional models. Due to the complexity of the electrochemical model, it is currently not suitable for engineering practice, only for research.

Equivalent circuit modelling

Equivalent circuits are used to characterise the operating process of a battery through the properties of electronic components such as capacitors, resistors, and inductors. It includes internal resistance model, RC model, PNGV model, GNL model. Different equivalent circuit model models have been analysed in literature [[1], [2], [3]]. Among them, the internal resistance model only consists of an ideal voltage source and internal resistance in series, which is a simple structure. But the accuracy is too low. The RC equivalent circuit model is used in most of the current national and international studies [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. A pair of RC networks is connected in series with the internal resistance model to characterise the polarisation of the battery, which has a high accuracy and a relatively simple structure. The PNGV model is based on the RC model and a capacitor is connected in series with the RC model to express the change of the open-circuit voltage with the time integral of the current. The GNL model has the characteristics of the PNGV and the RC models, but the structure is more complicated and it is difficult to identify the parameters.

Data-driven model

Data-driven models are black-box models, which do not need to construct mathematical relational equations for specific batteries, but rather construct non-linear mapping relationships between input variables and output variables from experimental data of batteries. Algorithms such as machine learning, neuron networks, and vector products are usually used. The process of building the model is done autonomously by the algorithm by learning from the data. However, a large amount of data is required for training to achieve high accuracy.

Literature [8] developed a fast multi-fault diagnosis method for lithium-ion battery packs by quantifying the charging voltage variation curve based on the curve Manhattan distance technique.Zhang C et al. [9] used an LSTM architecture to estimate the battery SOH based on the incremental energy per SOC, which improves the effectiveness and robustness of the estimation method.

Study of the equalisation topology circuit

The equalisation strategy applies equalisation to the battery by controlling the equalisation topology circuit. According to the equalisation method it can be divided into active and passive equalisation. The passive equalisation circuit is to release the excess energy in the form of heat through energy consuming elements such as resistors, achieving balance within the battery pack. Therefore, the circuit structure is simple and easy to control, only need to control the on-off of the switch and incorporate energy consuming components into the higher energy single to eliminate inconsistency. However, passive equalisation leads to energy loss and reduces energy utilization. If the heat released is not handled in time, it will also lead to thermal runaway of the battery pack. Therefore, passive equalisation circuits are suitable for small-capacity battery packs.

Active equalisation is the main method being studied. Active balancing is achieved by transferring energy from the battery cells, so the method has almost no energy loss. According to the different energy transfer elements used in active equalisation topology circuits can be classified as switched inductive, switched capacitive, switched converter, and switched transformer. The inductive and capacitive types temporarily store the excess energy of a single battery through the energy storage characteristics of its own capacitive-inductive elements, and then transfer the stored electrical energy to other single batteries through switching control. The circuit structure is simple and easy to control.

The switching transformer type transfers the excess power of the battery to the secondary side winding through mutual inductance by means of the primary side windings at both ends of the battery, and then transfers it to other battery cells.

Switching converter on the other hand, through the DC/DC converter can realise the energy conversion between single cell to single cell, single cell to battery pack and battery pack to single cell.DC/DC converter includes various components such as capacitors, inductors, windings and so on. Equalisation is good and fast, but at the same time the control is more complex and costly.

Wang Y G et al. [10] estimated the battery SOC using EKF algorithm based on an equivalent circuit model, combined with a small active equalisation circuit to achieve the battery pack equalisation control.Liu R et al. [11] also used EKF algorithm for estimating the battery SOC, and balanced the inconsistency of the battery pack by using capacitive circuit. And Kai Z et al. further used the RLS-EKF algorithm to improve the SOC estimation accuracy and realized the energy transfer between batteries through inductors [12].

Research on balanced control strategies

The control strategy is mainly divided into two types: logic control and optimisation control. Logic control is to consider all possible inconsistencies in the battery pack, and artificially formulate the direction of energy flow in the equalisation circuit. For example, the widely used “peak shaving and valley filling” strategy. When the inconsistency of the battery is detected, according to the data of each single cell returned by the BMS, the average value of the battery pack power is searched for, and then the power of the single cell higher than the average value is transferred to the battery lower than the average value. Finally, the whole battery pack is balanced. This control strategy is simple and effective, but with the increase in the number of single cells in the battery pack, the possible inconsistencies also increase, and the need to develop a more complex and variable strategy.

Optimal control is a strategy that has emerged in recent years. Continuously iteratively optimize control strategies through the particle swarm algorithm, genetic algorithm, ant colony algorithm and other optimisation algorithms. Until an optimal control strategy is found that minimises the set objective function value, i.e. the inconsistency of the battery.

In this paper, a new model based on the weighted fusion of neuronal networks is proposed based on the characteristics of three equivalent circuit models. The model brings together the advantages of the three equivalent circuit models and makes the results of the model closer to the experimental values through the optimal allocation of weights. A set of distributed active equalisation topology circuits is also designed. A PID controller is used to automatically control the switching of the topology circuit, and the three parameters in the PID controller are optimised by a particle swarm optimisation algorithm. The equalisation control of multiple battery cells at the same time is achieved, and the consistency of the battery pack can be ensured in dynamic working conditions. BPNN network fusion based models

There has been a lot of research and application of these three equivalent circuit models. All three equivalent circuit models have their own accuracy and characteristics. In this paper, the three equivalent circuit models are fused using BP neuron network. The advantageous features of the three models are gathered and their disadvantages are avoided. At a certain point in time, the model with lower accuracy is assigned less weight, and the model with higher accuracy is assigned more weight, so as to realise the complementary advantages between the equivalent circuit models. At the same time, the three equivalent circuit models are used to jointly characterise the operating process of the battery, so that the model estimation results are closer to the real value. The ampere-time integration method is the most commonly used method to estimate the SOC of a battery. The relative value of the charge can be obtained by integrating the current. It is simple to operate and has high accuracy. However, in practice, the integration error increases due to the current drift phenomenon of the Hall sensor that measures the current. Since the battery equalisation process is carried out in a simulation environment, there is no influence of current drift. The initial state of charge of the battery is set artificially, so the SOC value of the battery during operation can be accurately measured using the ampere-time integration method.

In this paper, a distributed active equalisation topology is designed. Each battery cell is connected in parallel with an equalisation circuit. When the BMS detects the inconsistency between the batteries, it sends a control signal to the equalisation circuit of the corresponding battery, and the equalisation power supply is connected to the circuit to equalise the batteries through the switching of the controllable switch, and the equalisation power supply is disconnected after the equalisation is completed. This circuit structure can ensure that the normal charging of the battery pack at the same time for a number of battery monomer equalisation. Before the completion of the battery pack charging can be achieved before the battery single unit equalisation. As shown in Fig. 14.

Distributed active equalisation circuits.

It is assumed that when the SOC of battery 1 is higher than that of batteries 2,3, the control switches corresponding to batteries 2,3 are closed for equalisation, and equalisation is stopped when the variance of the SOC of the three battery monomers is within the desired range. The circuit structure is simple and easy to operate, and the control of the switches can be quickly achieved using logic control. There are 12 imbalance states in the equalisation system for the three battery cells and the corresponding 6 control strategies. As shown in Fig. 15.

Control strategies.

The specific control method is as follows: through the battery state fed back by the BMS, the strategy corresponding to the imbalance state of the battery pack at the current moment is judged according to the if conditions, and then control signals are sent to the switches according to the strategy. However, as the size of the battery pack increases, the imbalance state of the battery pack and the corresponding control strategy will multiply with each increase in the number of battery cells and control switches. When the number of battery cells is four, there are 52 cases in which only one cell has the largest SOC, 12 cases in which two cells have the largest SOC, and 4 cases in which all three cells have the largest SOC, i.e., there is only one cell imbalance. So adding up there are a total of 68 unbalanced states. And the corresponding controls include: there are 4 types of controls that actuate only one switch. There are 6 types of strategies to actuate two switches. There are 4 types of controls that actuate three switches. There are 14 control strategies in total. It can be seen that there are so many states and corresponding control strategies when only one battery cell and control switch are added. When the number of batteries continues to increase, the situation will become more complex and variable, and if the logic control strategy is still used, it is inevitable that some of the unbalanced states and control strategies will be missed, resulting in the battery pack not being able to obtain adequate balance in the dynamic working conditions.

In order to solve this problem, this paper proposes an intelligent optimisation control strategy. The control strategy is dynamically adjusted according to the state parameters of the battery pack, and the control strategy is optimised by the particle swarm optimisation algorithm, so that the balancing circuit can manage the balancing of multiple single batteries even when facing complex dynamic working conditions. Since the control strategy is decided by the algorithm independently without human intervention, the design cost and error probability are greatly reduced compared with the traditional logic control strategy.

Equalisation control strategy based on PSO-PID

PID automatic control

PID controller due to the implementation of simple, robust and other advantages are currently widely used in a variety of fields. PID controller does not need to model the controlled object, only according to the output of the controlled object and the expected value of the error, after the proportional link, the integral link, the differential link of the three parts can be achieved to the controlled object of the feedback control. The control principle is shown in Fig. 16. In this paper, PID control is used to achieve automatic on-off switching of the equalisation circuit. The SOC deviation between battery cells is taken as the output of the controlled object of the PID system. Then a minimum error is given as a reference quantity for PID control. The PID control of the switching signal of the equalisation circuit is carried out through the deviation of the minimum error and the system output error, so that the inconsistency between the battery monomers reaches the minimum error, or is kept within a small range.

Where Kp, Ki, Kd are the proportional, integral, and differential coefficients of the PID, respectively.

The role of the proportional control link is to expand the error between the controlled object and the desired value, improve the response speed of the system so that the controlled object quickly approach the desired value. If the proportionality coefficient is too large, the system will produce a large amount of overshoot, too small and will reduce the accuracy of control.

Integral control link is to reduce the static error of the system. If it is too large, it will produce integral saturation and lead to overshooting, and if it is too small, it will not be able to eliminate the static deviation completely.

The differential control link is to predict future deviations based on the trend of the deviation e. If the differential coefficient is too large it will reduce the robustness of the system, and if it is too small it will not be able to predict the trend of the error may reduce the stability of the system. So the PID controller feedback control of the system will make the output of the system overshoot and oscillation.

For the equalisation circuit, the controlled object is a Boolean variable with only two state quantities “0″ and “1". If overshooting occurs in the system, it means that the switch has made an incorrect action, resulting in the equalisation circuit not being able to equalise the inconsistency of the batteries in time, and may even exacerbate the inconsistency of the battery pack. In order to reduce the overshoot and oscillation of the system and improve the dynamic response and stability of the equalisation circuit, an optimisation algorithm is used to find the most suitable PID control parameters.

Summary

In order to improve the range and reliability of electrically powered underwater vehicles, this paper investigates the internal inconsistency of their power packs. Firstly, three commonly used equivalent circuit models of 1RC, 2RC and PNGV are established respectively. Each of these three equivalent circuit models has its own characteristics in application. So in order to be able to combine their advantages and improve the accuracy and robustness of the model, this paper proposes a fusion model based on BPNN network. The estimates of the three models are weighted and fused through the weight calculation process of the neuron network to achieve the complementary advantages between the models. The accuracy of the model is then verified using the DST dynamic charge/discharge cycle test. The model also has a fast dynamic response ability, and can achieve accurate estimation at sudden voltage changes. Even if the estimated values of the three models are smaller than the true value, the final estimation result can be made close to the true value through reasonable weight fusion. Since the study is carried out in a simulation environment, the SOC of the battery can be accurately calculated using the ampere-time integration method.

Then a distributed active equalisation topology circuit was designed. The equalisation power supply is connected in parallel with a single battery that needs to be equalised, and the equalisation circuit is controlled by the on-off switch. The structure is simple and ensures that the battery can be charged while managing the equalisation of multiple inconsistent batteries. Fewer components are used and the reliability is high. However, as the size of the battery pack increases, the number of cells to be equalised increases. The combination of control switch actions also becomes more complex, and conventional logic control is not applicable. So the PSO-PID optimised control strategy is used. The equalisation circuit is automatically controlled with a PID controller and the control parameters are optimised with an optimisation algorithm. Finally, the equalisation process of 3 series, 4 series and 5 series battery packs is simulated. The PSO-PID control strategy can quickly and effectively achieve the equalisation of inconsistent batteries and keep the inconsistency of the battery packs within a small range. As the number of cells increases, the advantages of the PSO-PID control strategy become more obvious. Compared with the passive equalisation and switched inductive equalisation strategies commonly used today, the equalisation strategy proposed in this paper has high energy utilization, while reducing the complexity of the topology and improving the control efficiency when equalizing multiple batteries.

The internal inconsistency of the battery pack then leads to one of the main reasons for the reduction of battery life. Current research on balancing strategies is increasingly moving towards simple and reliable topologies, intelligent control strategies, and so on. For machine learning, deep learning, intelligent AI algorithms field will also be more closely linked.

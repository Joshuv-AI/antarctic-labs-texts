Differences in survival outcomes between HER2-low and HER2-zero breast cancer across heterogeneous HR expression patterns: a real-world study
Authors: Yi Zeng, Pengfei Qian, Guanhong Li, Yu Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHER2-negative breast cancers can be further subclassified into HER2-low and HER2-zero subtypes. The DESTINY-Breast04 trial has established HER2-low as a research hotspot, with recent studies indicating superior survival rates in HER2-low patients than HER2-zero patients. The impact of heterogeneous hormone receptor (HR) expression patterns on HER2-negative breast cancer has not been comprehensively investigated. This study explored how varying ER and PR expression patterns affect survival rates in patients with HER2-negative breast cancer.MethodsThis retrospective study comprised 648 HER2-negative breast cancer patients treated at our hospital between 2010 and 2023. Six subgroups were compared, including ER+/PR+/HER2-low, ER+/PR+/HER2-zero, single HR+/HER2-low, single HR/HER2-zero, ER-/PR-/HER2-low, and ER-/PR-/HER2-zero.ResultWith disease-free survival and overall survival as primary endpoints, the cohort included 648 HER2-negative breast cancer patients. Compared to HER2-zero, HER2-low patients showed a higher proportion of ER positivity (q = 0.016). The ER+/PR + group exhibited a higher proportion of HER2-low, lower N stage, lower histological grade, and lower Ki-67% (p < 0.001), and were also more likely to achieve pCR after neoadjuvant therapy. In the overall population, HER2-low exhibited better survival outcomes. Compared to single HR+/HER2-low, ER+/PR+/HER2-low had longer DFS (p < 0.001). HER2-low is an independent prognostic factor for survival outcomes in HER2-negative patients.ConclusionOur findings suggest that HER2-low breast cancer is associated with improved survival outcomes compared to HER2-zero breast cancer. HER2-low breast cancer exhibits different survival outcomes across different HR subgroups. These findings may guide future biomarker-driven treatment strategies in HER2-negative breast cancer.Keywords: Breast cancer, HER2-low, Estrogen receptor, Progesterone receptor

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most prevalent malignancy among women globally [1]. Molecular subtyping based on immunohistochemical (IHC) profiles of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) stratifies breast carcinomas into Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and triple-negative subtypes [2]. These subtypes exhibit significant therapeutic and prognostic heterogeneity, with HER2 expression serving as a critical determinant for subtyping and treatment decisions.

The HER2 classification paradigm has evolved from a binary system to a continuum model, facilitated by advanced detection techniques (e.g. quantitative immunofluorescence [3], morphological imaging [4]). Accoding to the 2018 ASCO/CAP guidelines [5], HER2-negative status encompasses IHC scores of 0, 1+, or 2 + with negative in situ hybridization (ISH). Driven by antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) advances, HER2-negative disease is now refined into two subtypes: HER2-low (IHC 1 + or IHC 2+/ISH-negative) and HER2-zero (IHC 0). This reclassification is underscored by the DESTINY-Breast04 trial, which demonstrated significant clinical benefit of trastuzumab deruxtecan in metastatic HER2-low breast cancer [6], despite no therapeutic benefit from trastuzumab in this population [7].

​ This reclassification raises a pivotal question: Does HER2-low status define a distinct biological subtype with unique clinical outcomes in early breast cancer [8]? Current evidence suggests HER2-low tumors exhibit higher hormone receptor positivity and lower proliferative activity than HER2-zero tumors [9, 10], potentially conferring a survival advantage. A meta-analysis confirmed improved overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in HER2-low early breast cancer [11]. However, the survival disparity appears modulated by hormone receptor (HR) status. While the OS advantage is maintained in HR + subgroups, it is attenuated or absent in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) [12]. Notably, emerging data suggest HER2-low status may even correlate with inferior prognosis in early-stage disease [13].

​ Despite the DB04 trial establishing a metastatic treatment paradigm [6], a critical knowledge gap remains regarding the prognostic significance of HER2-low status in early-stage breast cancer. Prospective studies systematically comparing HER2-low and HER2-zero subgroups or clarifying heterogeneous molecular subtype impacts (e.g., HR+/TNBC) are lacking [8, 13], hindering precision treatment development.

​Therefore, this study employed a large-scale retrospective analysis, defining HER2-negative breast cancer as HER2-zero or low expression (IHC 1 + or IHC 2+/ISH-negative). Combined with distinct HR expression patterns, we systematically compared survival differences among these subgroups and investigated the influence of molecular subtypes and treatment modalities on prognosis, aiming to provide high-level evidence for clinical decision-making.​.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This monocentric retrospective cohort study, conducted in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [14] and the Declaration of Helsinki, utilized institutional electronic medical records (EMR) for data abstraction. Ethical approval (No.2025-KY-047-01) was obtained from the Institutional Review Board at The Affiliated Huizhou Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University.

This retrospective cohort included stage I-III breast cancer patients who underwent surgical intervention between November 2010 and December 2023 at The Affiliated Huizhou Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University, encompassing both neoadjuvant-treated and upfront surgery subgroups. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Pathologically confirmed primary invasive ductal carcinoma; (2) HER2-negative tumors, including HER2-low and HER2-zero subtypes; (3) Complete pathological data. The exclusion criteria comprised: (1) Exclusive neoadjuvant endocrine therapy; (2) No surgical treatment; (3) Stage IV breast cancer; (4) Loss of follow-up data; (5) Concurrent malignancies. This study focused on operable stage I-III patients, excluding stage IV cases based on: (1) Standard surgery + adjuvant therapy reduces heterogeneity from palliative care; (2) DFS requires radical treatment evaluation; (3) Pathological specimen integrity (advanced cases often lack surgical tissue). Of the 760 initially eligible patients, 648 comprised the analytical cohort after rigorous screening (Fig. 1) and were stratified according to HER2 status in HER2-low (n = 428) and HER2-zero (n = 220) cases.

Flowchart depicting the inclusion of study cases

Clinical-pathological data were abstracted from validated dual-source electronic medical records (EMR) and digital pathology archives. Variables encompassed: demographic characteristics (age, menopausal status), tumor biology (histological grade, lymphovascular invasion), treatment details (surgical procedure, systemic therapy regimens), and biomarker profiles (ER/PR/HER2/Ki-67) following standardized protocols. Centralized pathological review was conducted by dedicated breast pathologists using whole slide imaging. Tumor, Node, Metastasis (TNM) staging adhered to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition guidelines. All immunohistochemistry (IHC) slides were subjected to a double-blinded review by three dedicated breast pathologists. The biomarkers assessed included estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), and Ki-67 [15]. To ensure the robustness of HER2 classification, in accordance with the 2024 CSCO guidelines, IHC 0 was defined as no staining or ≤ 10% of invasive tumor cells with incomplete, faint membrane staining; IHC 1 + as > 10% of invasive tumor cells with incomplete, faint membrane staining; IHC 2 + as > 10% of invasive tumor cells with weak to moderate intensity, complete membrane staining or ≤ 10% of invasive tumor cells with strong, complete membrane staining [16]. Discordant cases (n = 35, 5.1%) were resolved by consensus review using a multiheaded microscope. Cases with equivocal HER2 status (IHC 2+, n = 78, 11.4%) underwent reflex fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing, with all cases confirmed as lacking HER2 amplification (FISH-negative). Though ASCO recommends a 1% cutoffImportantly [5], several studies indicate that breast cancers with low estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) expression (1–9% staining) share biological behavior akin to classical triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and derive limited benefit from endocrine therapy [17]. Consequently, HR staining proportion < 10% is utilized as the threshold for defining negative receptor expression in certain regions [18]. Therefore, in this study, ER and PR expression of 1–9% and 0 expression were defined as HR-, while ER or PR expression ≥ 10% was defined as HR+. Following the China Anti-Cancer Association (2022 CACA) guideline, a Ki-67 index of 20% was applied as the cutoff value to distinguish between high and low proliferative activity [19]. A total of 760 patients met the inclusion criteria. Following the screening process (detailed in Table 1), 648 patients were finally included in the present study. Based on HER2 status, the cohort was stratified into a HER2-low group (n = 428) and a HER2-zero group (n = 220). And were further divided into six subgroups: ER+/PR+/HER2-low, ER+/PR+/HER2-zero, single HR+/HER2-low, single HR/HER2-zero, ER-/PR-/HER2-low, ER-/PR-/HER2-zero. This categorization is justified based on two factors: (1) the limited sample size of ER-/PR + cases in this cohort (n = 7), and (2) evidence demonstrating that both ER-/PR + and ER+/PR- tumors exhibit survival outcomes intermediate between ER+/PR + and ER-/PR- breast cancer, coupled with limited benefit from endocrine therapy [20, 21]. Hence, these two groups (ER-/PR + and ER+/PR-) were combined into a single category termed “single hormone receptor-positive” (single HR+).​.

Study endpoints and definitions

The co-primary endpoints encompassed DFS and OS stratified by HR/HER2 molecular subtypes. Secondary analyses evaluated survival disparities within each biomarker-defined subgroup. DFS was calculated from definitive surgery to the earliest occurrence of: (1) histologically/radiologically confirmed locoregional recurrence or distant metastasis; (2) biopsy-proven contralateral primary breast carcinoma; (3) all-cause mortality.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 29.0 software. Characteristics between groups were compared using the chi-square test. The Benjamini-Hochberg procedure was applied to control the false discovery rate (FDR < 0.05). The Bonferroni correction was applied to assess intergroup differences.Survival rates were calculated and survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method, with differences in survival between groups compared using the Log-rank test. The Cox proportional risk regression model was used to analyze the significant independent risk factors associated with DFS and OS in patients. Variables significantly associated with outcomes (univariate Cox p < 0.05) were retained in the multivariable Cox model to mitigate overfitting. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinicopathological factors associated with HER2-low and HER2-zero breast cancer

A comparison of the clinicopathologic characteristics of patients grouped according to HER2 status is presented in Table 1. Compared to HER2-zero patients, HER2-low patients exhibited a lower percentage of grade 2–3 breast cancer (p = 0.036), as well as a higher percentage of ER+ (p < 0.001) and PR+ (p = 0.036). In addition, the use of chemotherapy regimens was significantly different between the two groups (p = 0.001). After Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment, only estrogen receptor (ER) status retained statistical significance (q = 0.016).

Clinicopathological factors associated with distinct ER and PR expression patterns

This study divided the 648 patients into three subgroups: ER+/PR+, Single HR+, and ER-/PR-. The comparison of clinicopathological characteristics among these three groups is displayed in Table 2. After Bonferroni correction (α = 0.0167), the following significant differences were found. The single HR⁺ group is more common among older patients, while the ER-/PR- group is relatively younger. The ER-/PR- group displayed significantly aggressive features: 73.2% were high-grade (G3) tumors (vs. ER+/PR + group 16.2%, p < 0.001), and 90.7% had Ki-67 ≥ 20% (vs. ER+/PR + group 52.8%, p < 0.001). Surgical modality, adjuvant chemotherapy acceptance rate, T stage, and lymphovascular invasion status showed no statistically significant differences among the groups (all p > 0.0167).

Survival outcomes of HER2-low and HER2-zero patients

The median follow-up was 52 months (range 9-169 months). All OS events occurred after a preceding DFS event. During the follow-up period, a total of 80 patients (12.3%) experienced a DFS event, and 37 patients died. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed significant prognostic differences among the molecular subgroups, demonstrating that HER2-low status was associated with improved survival outcomes in both the overall cohort (DFS: p = 0.001; OS: p < 0.001) and the ER+/PR + subgroup. Furthermore, co-expression of ER and PR conferred an enhanced survival advantage, which was particularly pronounced in tumors with HER2-low expression. KM survival curves demonstrated that in the entire HER2-negative cohort of patients, DFS (p = 0.001) and OS (p < 0.001) were significantly longer in HER2-low patients than in HER2-zero patients (Fig. 2A, B), consistent with the results observed in the ER+/PR + subgroup (Fig. 2C, D). In contrast, there was no significant difference in DFS (p = 0.353) and OS (p = 0.246) between HER2-low and HER2-zero status in the ER-/PR- group (Fig. 1G, H). Notably, the DFS remained comparable in the single HR + group (p = 0.089), while a significant difference in OS was observed between the two (p = 0.033) (Fig. 2E, F).

Survival outcomes of patients with distinct ER and PR expression patterns

The impact of distinct ER and PR expression patterns on HER2-negative patients was further analyzed. In the overall HER2-zero population, ER+/PR + status was associated with superior DFS and OS (all p < 0.05) compared to the single HR + and ER-/PR- groups (Fig. 3C, D, G, H). However, DFS (p = 0.507) and OS (p = 0.539) remained comparable between the single HR + and ER-/PR- groups (Fig. 3K, L). DFS was significantly longer in the ER+/PR+/HER2-low group compared to the Single HR+/HER2-low (p = 0.012) and ER-/PR-/HER2-low (p < 0.001) groups (Fig. 3A, E), while DFS was significantly longer in the ER+/PR+/HER2-low group than in the ER-/PR-/HER2-low group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 3F), but not substantially different from the OS of single HR+/HER2-low (p = 0.616) (Fig. 3B). A significant difference in OS (p = 0.022) was observed between the single HR+/HER2-low and ER-/PR-/HER2-low groups, while no significant difference was found in DFS (p = 0.507)(Fig. 3J, I).

Subgroup analysis of HER2-Low postmenopausal patients

Given the superior survival outcomes observed in the HR+/HER2-low subgroup, we sought to assess the consistency of these differences across premenopausal and postmenopausal patient groups. Accordingly, we analyzed survival outcomes in each menopausal subgroup for ER+/PR+/HER2-low and single HR+/HER2-low status. In the premenopausal subgroup, there was no significant difference in DFS (p = 0.067) and OS (p = 0.409) between ER+/PR+/HER2-low and single HR+/HER2-low groups (Fig. 4A, B). However, in the postmenopausal subgroup, ER+/PR+/HER2-low showed better OS (p = 0.014), although there was no significant difference in DFS (p = 0.082) (Fig. 4D, C). In addition, survival outcomes were compared according to the use of postmenopausal endocrine therapy regimens. No significant differences in DFS (p = 0.404) and OS (p = 0.457) were observed in the HR+/HER2-low group treated with aromatase inhibitors (AI) or tamoxifen (TAM) (Fig. 5).

In the adjuvant group, univariable analysis showed HER2 status, T stage, histologic grade, ER status, PR status, pCR, breast surgery, and vascular invasion were all significantly associated with DFS (all p < 0.05). Compared with N2 (HR = 2.814, 95%CI 1.432–5.526, p = 0.003) and N3 (HR = 6.151, 95%CI 3.222–11.742, p < 0.001), N0 was associated with significantly better DFS. Variables significant during univariable analysis were incorporated in multivariable analysis. Results showed HER2 status (HR = 2.235, 95%CI 1.331–3.754, p = 0.002) remained an independent prognostic factor for DFS. Besides, patients with pT1 tumors exhibited improved DFS compared to those with pT4 tumors (HR = 8.247, 95%CI 2.59-25.581, p < 0.001). Besides, patients with pN3 nodal stage exhibited worse DFS than those with pN0 stage (HR = 3.287, 95%CI 1.496–7.219, p = 0.003).

Univariable analysis showed ER status, PR status, HER2 status, vascular invasion and neural invasion were significantly associated with OS (all p < 0.05). Compared with pT1, pT3 (HR = 10.295, 95%CI 2.720-38.965, p < 0.001) and pT4 (HR = 42.703, 95%CI 11.967-152.382, p < 0.001) showed worse DFS. Multivariable analysis of OS significant variables showed HER2 status (HR = 2.8612, 95%CI 1.048–6.509, p = 0.039), neural invasion (HR = 2.885, 95%CI 1.231–6.760, p = 0.015), and ER status (HR = 0.301, 95%CI 0.115–0.789, p = 0.015) remained independent prognostic factors. Compared with pT1, pT3 (HR = 8.757, 95%CI 1.829–35.700, p = 0.015) and pT4 (HR = 32.477, 95%CI 7.316-144.169, p < 0.001) showed worse OS.

In the neoadjuvant therapy group, all patients received the EC (epirubicin + cyclophosphamide) *4 followed by T (docetaxel) *4 regimen. Preoperative core needle biopsies did not provide evaluable data for neural invasion, Vascular invasion, or histologic grade. Additionally, accurate assessment of T-stage and N-stage was unattainable from biopsy specimens. Consequently, these parameters were excluded from the analysis. In the univariable analysis of DFS and OS (Tables 5 and 6), only pCR demonstrated statistical significance for DFS (HR = 0.263, 95%CI 0.090–0.772, p < 0.001). Although HER2-zero status showed trends towards increased risk for DFS (HR = 2.920, 95%CI 1.770–4.816) and OS (HR = 1.176, 95%CI 0.227–6.091), differences were not statistically significant (all p > 0.05). These findings suggest a potential adverse prognostic effect of HER2-zero status, though constrained by: (1) limited cohort size (n = 74); (2) low event rates (15 DFS/7 OS events). Formal validation requires larger prospective cohorts. Additionally, caution is warranted in interpreting the elevated hazard ratio (HR) observed for T4-stage disease, as this finding is substantially limited by the small cohort size (T4, n = 14), compromising the reliability of the analysis.

Discussion

Through retrospective analysis, this study further stratified early-stage HER2-low and HER2-zero breast cancer patients into six subgroups to explore the clinicopathological characteristics and heterogeneity in survival outcomes. Our research not only revealed significant differences in biological behavior between HER2-low and HER2-zero populations but also emphasized the interactive effects of hormone receptor (HR) expression patterns and menopausal status on prognosis, providing new evidence to support clinical decision-making in this field.

This study confirmed that HER2-low patients have lower histologic grades (e.g., a higher proportion of G1/G2) and higher hormone receptor positivity rates compared to HER2-zero patients. After the Benjamini-Hochberg correction, they still showed a higher proportion of ER expression. As demonstrated by Almstedt et al. [22] and Khalil et al. [23], HER2-low tumors exhibited significantly improved histological differentiation and lower Ki-67 proliferation indices versus HER2-zero counterparts (p = 0.001 and p = 0.013, respectively), coupled with higher ER positivity rates (p = 0.002), supporting the hypothesis that HER2-low tumors may display a relatively indolent biological behavior. These findings hold significant clinical implications and provide actionable insights. First, the HER2-low pathological profile—characterized by lower histological grade and HR positivity—may be directly implicated in prognosis. For instance, HER2-low patients demonstrate prolonged DFS and OS [22], particularly within the HR + subset where this advantage is most pronounced [24, 25]. This survival benefit is partially attributable to its ​more indolent tumor biology, evidenced by reduced proliferative activity and lower tumor mutational burden [26]. Therapeutically, the recognition of HER2-low as a biologically distinct subtype opens avenues for novel targeted strategies [27]. Specifically, high ER positivity indicates enhanced endocrine sensitivity, while the HER2-low status may confer susceptibility to ADCs such as trastuzumab deruxtecan [22]. These agents utilize HER2 receptors to deliver cytotoxic payloads, offering transformative potential for patients historically classified as HER2-negative and overlooked for targeted therapies. Furthermore, accurate HER2-low identification demands refined immunohistochemical interpretation criteria. Crucially, the interplay between HER2 status and HR expression (e.g., lower histological grade in HR+/HER2-low tumors) should inform personalized treatment algorithms [28], thereby mitigating unnecessary treatment escalation. Further corroborating this, Shi et al. demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of HER2-low tumors in HR + cohorts compared to HR- groups, with HER2-low constituting 68.1% of ER+/PR + subgroups [29]. Mechanistically, ER exerts dual regulatory control by transcriptionally repressing HER2 amplification while concomitantly activating MAPK/ERK and PI3K-AKT signaling cascades, establishing an autoregulatory circuit that maintains HER2 at subthreshold levels through dysregulated post-translational modifications [30, 31]. This biphasic regulation induces oncogenic rewiring via ER-mediated MYC amplification and Wnt/β-catenin pathway attenuation, diverting dependency from HER2-driven pathways [32, 33]. Reciprocal phosphorylation crosstalk further triggers non-canonical EGFR signaling that promotes HER2 endocytosis and membrane downregulation [34, 35] comprehensively elucidating the ER-HER2-low association in HR + breast cancer.

Given the potential prognostic advantage associated with HER2-low status, we stratified patients by HR expression patterns. This analysis revealed that the ER+/PR+/HER2- subgroup​(both HER2-low and HER2-zero) demonstrated the most favorable survival outcomes, a finding corroborated by external studies [36]. Breast cancer with ER+/PR + exhibits distinctive molecular features, including a higher frequency of PI3K/AKT pathway activation. This provides a protective effect by promoting tumor biological stability and potentially superior response to endocrine therapy [37], thereby resulting in enhanced survival outcomes relative to other HR subtypes. The survival benefit observed in the ER+/PR+/HER2-low breast cancer subtype in this study potentially stems from a sophisticated network of multiple signaling pathways, including inhibiting CDK4/6 activity [38, 39] and moderately activating mTOR [40] to mitigate treatment resistance [41]. Nevertheless, the prognostic significance of HER2-low in HR- cases, particularly triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), is contentious. No significant survival differences were observed in the HR- subgroup in this study, aligning with findings from Xia [12] and Xu [42]. However, Ergun et al. documented substantial improvements in DFS (HR = 0.88, 95% CI 0.83–0.94) and OS (HR = 0.87, 95% CI 0.78–0.96) in the HR-/HER2-low group [43]. Such contradictions could arise from internal heterogeneity within TNBC: HER2-low TNBC might encompass subsets such as basal-like subtypes (chemotherapy-sensitive) or immunoinflammatory types (elevated PD-L1 expression) [44, 45], whereas HER2-zero TNBC has not shown a distinct biological phenotype in some investigations, potentially linked to mesenchymal-like or hyperproliferative subtypes [46, 47]. Future studies are required to investigate in conjunction with molecular subtyping. Additionally, this study found no statistically significant differences in DFS and OS between ER-/PR- and Single HR+. One study comparing DFS across different HR expression patterns found that ER+/PR + patients were significantly superior to those with ER or PR negativity within molecular classification groups, and HR expression status significantly impacted DFS [48]. Moreover, in HR+/HER2-negative breast cancer, low HR expression or single-receptor positivity was associated with poorer prognosis, and ER-/PR + status was even linked to high recurrence risk [49], whereas high co-expression of ER/PR was associated with better treatment response [50]. This echoes the perspective that Single HR + and ER-/PR- share similar biological characteristics, further highlighting the central role of ER+/PR + within the HER2-negative framework: the synergistic reinforcement of intrinsic tumor stability renders the subtle variations in HER2- unlikely to substantially reduce survival [51].

This study revealed distinct OS benefits exclusively in postmenopausal ER+/PR+/HER2-low patients. Real-world evidence reveals that DFS and OS benefitting postmenopausal patients in the HER2-low group (p = 0.004, p = 0.009, respectively) [25]. Among postmenopausal pN0 patients, ER+/PR+/HER2-low tumors exhibit significantly superior survival outcomes (e.g., DFS and BCSS) relative to ER+/PR-/HER2-low counterparts [36]. Major cohort studies demonstrate that single hormone receptor-positive subtypes (e.g., ER + PR- or ER-PR+) display increased incidence of adverse clinical characteristics and elevated mortality risk versus ER+/PR + tumors (reflected in survival rates of 90.6% vs. 83.8% and 78.1%, respectively) [52]. Limited by the small sample size, the study failed to identify significant survival differences in postmenopausal patients receiving distinct therapeutic regimens. Notably, we observed comparable pCR rates between HER2-low and HER2-zero subgroups following neoadjuvant therapy, contrasting with existing literature [53]. Multiple studies have consistently reported significantly lower pCR rates in HER2-low tumors compared to HER2-zero tumors. This difference may reflect intrinsic biological characteristics of HER2-low disease, notably an enriched hormone receptor (HR)-positive population [54] and reduced proliferative activity [55]. Notably, this disparity is most pronounced within the HR- subset, where HER2-zero tumors demonstrate significantly higher pCR rates [56]. The apparent contradiction between our findings and prior reports may stem from the limited neoadjuvant cohort size in the present study, which reduces statistical power. This underscores the imperative to standardize diagnostic thresholds​for HER2-low classification in clinical practice and incorporate HR status into therapeutic decision-making. To definitively resolve this discrepancy and optimize treatment strategies, future prospective investigations arewarranted to delineate the specific role of HER2-low status in the neoadjuvant setting.

Furthermore, HER2-low breast cancer’s prognostic advantage may derive from its distinct metabolic stability (attenuated LDH activity, predominant LDH-M isoenzyme) [57, 58] and radiomic divergence: HR+/HER2-low tumors predominantly exhibit focal calcifications while TNBC/HER2-low show hybrid circumscribed-invasive morphology [59]. Subsequent investigations should employ comprehensive multi-omics approaches to decipher the fundamental biology underlying these metabolic-imaging correlations.

Multivariable Cox proportional hazards modeling demonstrated HER2-low status as an independent prognostic factor associated with improved DFS and OS. Pooled meta-analysis revealed HER2-low status conferred 14% and 10% relative risk reductions for DFS and OS, respectively (p < 0.001), with enhanced protective effects observed in hormone receptor-positive cohorts (DFS: HR = 0.88, 95% CI 0.85–0.91; OS: HR = 0.87, 95% CI 0.83–0.91) [60]. These findings have important clinical implications: (1) NCCN guidelines do not incorporate HER2-low status, potentially underestimating prognosis in some low-risk patients; (2)HER2-low status may correlate with endocrine therapy sensitivity; however, these single-center findings are hypothesis-generating and require validation in multicenter cohorts; (3) Clinical trials of novel ADCs (e.g., T-DXd) for HER2-low should consider HR status interactions.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis, it is inherently susceptible to selection bias, with the 10% immunohistochemistry (IHC) cutoff used to define HR- status differing from the 1% threshold recommended in ASCO guidelines [5]. However, several studies indicate that breast cancers with low ER and PR expression (1–9% staining) share biological behavior akin to classical triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Thus, we adopted a 10% threshold based on these evidences [18]. Larger prospective studies are needed to validate the clinical relevance of this 10% cutoff. Second, the follow-up period was relatively limited, necessitating extended follow-up to assess how distinct HR expression patterns influence long-term survival in both HER2-low and HER2-zero subtypes. Owing to limited sample size, ER-/PR + and ER+/PR- tumors were combined into a single HR + category, precluding independent analysis; future studies should analyze these subgroups separately to clarify their distinct biological behaviors. Third, the limited inclusion of patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy weakened the statistical power for this subgroup. Furthermore, as the cohort included chemotherapy-naïve patients, comparative analysis of different chemotherapy regimens was not feasible. Last, the exclusion of stage IV patients warrants future multicenter investigations to examine the biological continuum of HER2-low across disease stages. Due to the inability to ascertain cause-specific deaths during follow-up, this study utilized OS as the primary endpoint instead of BCSS, which may limit the reliability of findings. Nevertheless, according to the STROBE statement, OS remains a robust endpoint. Further investigation is warranted to determine the association between BCSS and HER2 status.

Conclusion

This study confirms HER2-low as an independent prognostic factor in early breast cancer, whose co-expression with ER/PR synergistically improves survival, suggesting it should be incorporated into risk stratification and detection guidelines to optimize precision treatment. Routine differentiation of HER2-low vs. HER2-zero in early breast cancer may enhance prognostic precision and guide treatment individualization.

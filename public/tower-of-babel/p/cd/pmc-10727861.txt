Missense Variant c.3301C>T (p.R1101W) in von Willebrand Factor A Sequence in a Patient with Recessive Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa Pruriginosa with Compound Heterozygous COL7A1 Variants
Authors: Hyeok-Jin Kwon, Dong-Wha Yoo, Jung-Ho Yoon, Namhee Kim, Ki-Ho Kim
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB) pruriginosa is a rare subtype of DEB characterized by multiple, violaceous, and severe pruritic lichenified nodules along with blisters. Here, we report the case of a Korean male who, since the age of 3 years, had multiple pruritic nodules with blisters on both lower extremities. Genetic testing is required to diagnose DEB pruriginosa because its clinical and histologic features are inconclusive. We identified compound heterozygous COL7A1 variants of c.5797C>T (p.R1933*) and c.3301C>T (p.R1101W) in the patient, leading to a diagnosis of recessive DEB pruriginosa. Among the variants identified, c.3301C>T is a novel missense variant that has not been reported previously. This variant is in exon 26, which encodes von Willebrand factor A (vWFA) in collagen type VII. vWFA is known to preserve normal dermal structures by interacting with dermal collagens and basement membranes. Considering that this variant contradicts the general concept that autosomal dominant inheritance is more common and that variants typically occur in the triple helical collagenous domain of COL7A1 in DEB pruriginosa, we focus on the rarity of this case and the possible pathogenic role of the c.3301C>T (p.R1101W) variant.

INTRODUCTION

Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB) is a group of inherited bullous skin diseases involving skin fragility, characterized by blisters, erosions, and scars that occur in response to minimal trauma and can be accompanied by nail dystrophy1234. This fragility results from defects in anchoring fibrils dominantly composed of collagen type VII, which maintain the integrity between the epidermis and underlying dermis1234. COL7A1, which encodes collagen type VII, is the only gene identified to be associated with DEB.

Pathologic findings are equally important to subepidermal blisters originating from collagen type VII for diagnosing DEB. More precisely, immunofluorescence mapping and transmission electron microscopy are considered useful diagnostic tools as they can speculate candidate proteins and identify not only dermal detachment below the lamina densa, but also evident reduction of anchoring fibrils, respectively5. However, if pathologic findings are inconclusive because of various factors, such as specimen selection, genetic testing should be performed as a next step to ensure accurate diagnosis5. Furthermore, as DEB has both dominant and recessive forms, it is important to perform genetic testing in the aspect of family counselling56.

DEB pruriginosa is a rare DEB phenotype characterized by severe pruritic, lichenified, and violaceous nodules with blisters that are typically found in the extremities along with nail dystrophy278. Here, we describe a case of recessive DEB (RDEB) pruriginosa in a male patient presenting mild cutaneous lesions caused by compound heterozygous variants c.3301C>T and c.5797C>T in COL7A1. To the best of our knowledge, c.3301C>T is a novel missense variant in exon 26, which encodes von Willebrand factor A (vWFA) in the non-collagenous (NC) 1 domain of COL7A1. This is the first report of the c.3301C>T variant in COL7A1, highlighting its possible pathogenicity.

CASE REPORT

A 17-year-old Korean male complained of pruritic violaceous blisters and nodules on both legs since the age of 3 years (Fig. 1A). Blisters developed after minor mechanical trauma and changed the lichenified nodules during repetitive scratching (Fig. 1B). Mild toenail dystrophy was also observed (Fig. 1C). Except for his legs and toenails, no cutaneous or mucosal lesions were observed. Neither the maternal nor paternal lines showed the same clinical history as the patient. Pathologically, several dermal vacuoles with an intact dermo-epidermal junction were observed, with no subepidermal blisters detected (Fig. 2A). Transmission electron microscopy revealed prominent and diffuse vacuolar changes in the upper dermis; however, it was unclear whether the anchoring fibrils had meaningfully diminished (Fig. 2B, C). Because the initial histologic findings were not clear enough to diagnose DEB, we performed two additional skin biopsies on the leg. Nevertheless, subepidermal blisters were not observed, and pathologic findings did not differ considerably from those of the initial sample.

Clinical and histologic findings were inconclusive; thus, targeted next generation sequencing was performed in both the patient and his parents using a panel of 26 genes related to ichthyosis and epidermolysis bullosa (ABCA12, ALOX12B, ALOXE3, COL17A1, COL7A1, CYP4F22, DSP, DST, EXPH5, FERMT1, ITGA3, ITGA6, ITGB4, JUP, KRT14, KRT5, LAMA3, LAMB3, LAMC2, LIPN, NIPAL4, PKP1, PLEC, PNPLA1, TGM1, and TGM5). The patient harbored two variants of COL7A1: the novel missense variant c.3301C>T (p.R1101W) (NM_000094.4), derived from his father, and a known premature termination codon (PTC) variant, c.5797C>T (p.R1933*) (NM_000094.4), derived from the mother (Fig. 3A~C). Therefore, a diagnosis of RDEB pruriginosa was made owing to compound heterozygous COL7A1 variants, with one variant obtained from each parent (Fig. 3D).

Considering the favorable clinical course based on the patient’s mild cutaneous manifestations, we prescribed only topical corticosteroid (2.5% hydrocortisone lotion) and oral antihistamine (levocetirizine 5 mg once per day and bepotastine 10 mg twice per day) to control his pruritus. As an outpatient, his itching has been well-controlled and his daily activities have been unaffected, despite the partially remaining cutaneous nodules.

DISCUSSION

In general, patients with RDEB possessing variants that introduce PTCs display more lethal clinical courses because total transcripts are reduced in number and thus cannot assemble to form functional proteins of anchoring fibrils2349. In connection with this, a previous case containing the c.5797C>T (p.R1933*) variant, which induces a PTC, presented with the “Hallopeau-Siemens type” known as the most severe clinical form of RDEB displaying generalized blisters; severe nail dystrophy; mucosal involvement; contracture of extremities including, hands and feet; and multiple organ involvement1011.

Interestingly, although our patient had a c.5797C>T variant, pruritic nodules were found in localized areas, with minimal nail dystrophy. This favorable clinical course may be attributable to the compound heterozygous variants composed of a PTC and a missense mutation. This notion is in agreement with previous studies that focused on the genotype-phenotype correlation of DEB and showed that heterozygote carriers with a PTC variant on one side of the allele were clinically unaffected239. Hence, we supposed that together with the PTC variant c.5797C>T, another non-PTC pathologic variant contributing to the milder clinical features may exist.

In addition to c.5797C>T (p.R1933*) in exon 71, a PTC variant known to be pathologic in RDEB121314, we identified c.3301C>T (p.R1101W), a missense variant in exon 26 encoding vWFA. Although missense variants are commonly found in RDEB, this variant has not been reported, and vWFA is an uncommon mutated site in RDEB234. Based on the 2015 American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology guidelines, this variant is likely pathogenic15. First, this variant was not found in the Korean Reference Genome Database and showed an allele frequency of 0.00086% in gnomAD Exomes (PM2). Second, this variant was detected in trans with another well-known pathologic variant (c.5797C>T) (PM3). Third, numerous missense variants are known to be pathogenic in RDEB (PP2). Finally, in various computational in silico predictive programs, nine pathogenic predictions were reported from BayesDel addAF, EIGEN, FATHMM-MKL, LISTS2, MVP, VariantAssessor, VariantTaster, Polyphen2, and SIFT (PP3). Thus, the variant is likely pathogenic (2 moderate [PM1–PM6] and ≥2 supporting [PP1–PP5])15.

vWFA is in the NC1 domain of collagen type VII (Fig. 4A), which plays an important role in stabilizing dermal structures by binding to the lamina densa and to collagen types I and IV (Fig. 4B)161718. In the dermis, collagen type VII consists of anchoring fibrils that maintain stability between the epidermis and the underlying dermo-epidermal junction; collagen types I and IV comprise a large portion of the dermal materials, such as collagen fibers and anchoring plaques, respectively (Fig. 4B)161718. Therefore, vWFA contributes to the reinforcement of the dermal architecture by interacting with the basement membrane and collagen types I and IV. In the case of the vWFA defect, dermal structures can be destroyed by dermal vacuolar changes or formation of subepidermal blisters (Fig. 2B, C, 4B). Furthermore, several studies have noted that the NC1 domain in collagen type VII is a major epitope for autoantibodies in patients with epidermolysis bullosa acquisita, which also clinically results in skin blisters16181920. More specifically, it is reported that autoantibodies against vWFA are commonly detected in these patients161819. In connection with this, we supposed that missense mutation c.3301C>T affecting vWFA might contribute to the pathologic findings observed in this case. Therefore, though subepidermal detachment below the lamina densa was not found, we concluded that dermal vacuolar changes originated from the pathogenesis of DEB.

Although both dominant and recessive inheritance can occur, dominant inheritance appears to be more common. The pathogenesis of DEB pruriginosa is often derived from glycine substitution or in-frame skipping variants in the triple-helical collagenous domain (THC) of COL7A178. However, our patient was diagnosed with RDEB pruriginosa and possessed heterogeneous variants composed of a PTC variant in the THC and a missense variant in vWFA, which is unusual compared with other cases78.

In conclusion, we identified the novel missense variant c.3301C>T (p.R1101W) in exon 26, which encodes vWFA in the NC1 domain of COL7A1, in a patient diagnosed with RDEB pruriginosa. Because of the physiologic importance of preserving the dermal architecture, this case highlights that genetic defects in vWFA may induce progression of DEB and contribute to its characteristic clinical and pathologic features in combination with another variant. Further in-depth studies are needed to clarify the molecular pathophysiology of DEB. This case, thus, provides additional genetic variant information for inclusion in databases, as this new variant may be pathogenic.

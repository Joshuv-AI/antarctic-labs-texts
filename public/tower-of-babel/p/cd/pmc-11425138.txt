A hybrid approach for power quality event identification in power systems: Elasticnet Regression decomposition and optimized probabilistic neural networks
Authors: Indu Sekhar Samanta, Pravat Kumar Rout, Kunjabihari Swain, Murthy Cherukuri, Subhasis Panda, Mohit Bajaj, Vojtech Blazek, Lukas Prokop, Stanislav Misak
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The transformation of traditional grid networks towards smart-grid and microgrid concepts raises many critical issues, and quality in the power supply is one of the prominent ones that needs further research. Developing and applying power quality (PQ) recognition methods with efficient and reliable analysis are essential to the fast-growing issues related to modern smart power distribution systems. In this regard, a hybrid algorithm is proposed for PQ events detection and classification using Elasticnet Regression-based Variational Mode Decomposition (ER-VMD) and Salp Swarm Algorithm optimized Probabilistic Neural Network (SSA-PNN). The Elasticnet Regression (ER) process is suggested to modify the conventional VMD approach instead of the Tikhonov Regularization (TR) method to enhance performance and obtain better band-limited intrinsic mode functions. This idea results in robust and effective reconstruction features and helps to obtain accurate classification using the classifier. In the classification stage, a Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) based PNN is used for the PQ event, considering the relevant features obtained from ER-VMD. The system parameters often influence PNN performance, and SSA is used to determine the ideal values to improve the PNN's capacity for more accurate classification. The numerical values of the accuracy percentage, percentage of sensitivity, and percentage of specificity in the case of real-time data are found as 98.58, 100, and 98.46, respectively. The acquired comparison findings demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed technique in terms of rapid learning speed, smaller computational complexity, robust performance for anti-noise conditions, and accurate identification and categorization.

Introduction

In recent times, power systems have transitioned towards smart and microgrid concepts. The integration of power electronics-based interfacing devices, filters, digital controllers, switching devices, and nonlinear loads influences the system more due to harmonic injection, and remedial measures are required to handle the unacceptable issues [1]. PQ event detection and classification are required research areas in this direction. The quality power supply in terms of less variation in voltage, current, and frequency of power signals as per the desired standards within a specified limit is necessary to restrict the disturbances due to harmonic contamination [2]. As this raises many issues, monitoring assessment, reliability, and stability of the system performance, parameter estimation of filtering, harmonic control strategy formulation, and detection need to be addressed [3]. The primary step for all the remedial measures to reduce the PQ problems is to monitor for accurate detection and make correct decisions for further fruitful efforts. However, as the major PQEs are dynamic and complex with diversified variations in characteristics, it isn't easy to formulate an approach to handle all the real-time conditions [4]. Looking at the above, this study attempts to design and develop a novel approach with higher accuracy, robustness, and reliability for PQE detection and classification.

The PQE detection and classification plays a critical role in power system operation to provide a quality power supply, to design the overall system to reduce the harmonic impacts, to avoid adverse impacts on other components, and overall to make the supply reliable and secure with quality power. In addition, it is generally believed that high-quality power is more efficient, allowing the equipment to run on optimal energy consumption, avoiding breakdowns, overheating, maintenance, etc. Proper design needs to be implemented to improve power quality according to PQE diagnosis because it may lead to other ways towards short or extensive power interruptions, noisy electrical equipment, voltage sags, voltage surges, harmonic distortion, and high-speed transients. The process of formulating the PQE diagnosis generally followed four steps as adopted in this study. The first step is to collect the non-stationary power signal data to analyse further for feature extraction. The second step is to find out, from these data, the relevant features to characterize the signal properly. The third step is to classify the signal according to the features extracted in Step 2. In step 4, the decisions are made accordingly to signal the need to take adequate actions for the detected disturbance. The study also adopts similar steps for the proposed methodology for the PQ analysis.

The PQEs put their signature differently on the signal characteristics change and, subsequently, the feature values. PQ events may be accurately detected and classified by analysing the feature sets using the right classifier. Signal processing tools have been used extensively in the past three decades to retrieve dominant and effective features and characterize these changes appropriately [5,13]. The PQEs are detected by analyzing these features. The short-time Fourier Transform (STFT) technique fails to be applied extensively for PQ analysis because it has a limited range of time and frequency resolution [6,7]. Later, Wavelet Transform (WT), although it has a better time-frequency resolution, fails to attack to apply for the PQ analysis due to its major limitation to handle noisy and complex signals and the need for a higher level of signal decomposition [8,9]. An advanced technique known as S-transform has emerged with accurate phasor estimation and improved time-frequency resolution and is comprised of WT and STFT for PQ analysis [11,12]. Considerable processing time and inappropriate harmonics measurement are the major limitations of ST in PQ analysis [10,14]. Because of its capacity to analyse non-stationary and nonlinear power signal time-series data, another strong approach, depending on the combination of Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) with Hilbert Transform (HT), has been used for signal analysis [15,16]. It was found later that in PQ applications, this method cannot distinguish different components in narrow-band signals and cannot decide the best IMF selection [17]. Beyond the mentioned techniques, many other processes have played a significant role in the PQ analysis, like Wavelet Packet Transform (WPT), Kalman Filter (KF), and Mathematical Morphology (MM) [4]. Still, it is a challenging field of research to arrive at an improved method to formulate for PQE detection and analysis. This study attempts to develop an approach based on VMD for better PQ detection through computing good features. The VMD technique is chosen in this study because many of its capabilities have not yet been explored, and the results are very promising for VMD applications in signal processing analyses and feature extraction [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. A modified form is presented in this study for better and more effective feature extraction to characterize the PQE signals.

Intelligent techniques are necessary for decision-making and accurate classification of PQ analysis. Several methods have been suggested in recent times, like Fuzzy Logic (FL), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Expert Systems (ES) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) [22,23]. FL has many advantages in PQE classification, like rule-based FL, which reduces the challenges of modelling and analyzing and the capability of handling complex problems with uncertainty and ambiguity [24,25]. However, the major limitations are that it is impossible to handle any variation in the disturbance during the procedure and the high convergence rate. ES is a very effective tool for classifying missing or insufficient data sets [26]. Highly dependent on rules framed, high convergence time, and complex design for diversified data sets are the limitations that make this tool-less attractive for PQ analysis in the later period. SVM is another powerful technique widely used even for PQ analysis because of its better learning process and potential to handle very large solution spaces [27]. However, the accuracy decreases in the case of low training samples, and robustness fails. Among other techniques applied for PQ analysis, various authors suggest ANN-based approaches in many modelling and forms. These techniques are mathematically flexible to handle the changes and achieve high accuracy in practical applications [28,29]. This tool has several limitations, including increased computational complexity [30]. It also shows performance sensitivity to network architecture [31] and is affected by signal noise levels [33]. To explore potential improvements, this study incorporates Probabilistic Neural Networks (PNN) [32], a straightforward ANN-based tool. The study aims to enhance the PNN parameters by optimally selecting values using the Simulated Annealing (SSA) technique [34]. The SSA technique is further detailed in Refs. [35,36].

Deep learning (DL) has recently been applied extensively for fault detection and classification, voltage and frequency regulation, power quality event analysis, harmonic analysis, signal decomposition and feature extraction, and grid stability and control. The inherent characteristics, like the ability to handle complex, high-dimensional data and learn from it, make it attractive to apply to various power engineering problems. Normalization, feature extraction, data segmentation, and classification are the major ways DL has been applied in PQ analysis [[40], [41], [42]]. High accuracy and adaptability are the two major factors in the DL approach that outperform conventional PQ analysis and detection methods. However, the major challenges to applying the DL effectively are data quality and quantity requirements for training effective models, computationally intensive processes, parameter and learning dependency, and availability of powerful hardware for training and deployment [40]. DL can be a better method for PQ disturbance detection and classification. It can be significantly enhanced, leading to better monitoring, analysis, and management of power systems. As in this study, low-dimensional data was handled, and the authors prefer to research and propose a simple algorithm based on a hybrid approach using ER-VMD and SSA-optimized PNN, avoiding the complexity in DL.

Variational-mode-decomposition (VMD)

VMD is a signal processing technique primarily used to decompose a signal into a finite number of intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) or modes. Due to its adaptive, non-recursive approach, it is extensively used for signal decomposition. It is particularly effective in handling non-stationary power signals. VMD decomposes power signals into various modes representing a specific frequency band. Each mode is comprised of an amplitude-modulated and frequency-modulated signal component. The decomposition is achieved by adapting a variational approach. In this case, an optimization problem is solved to minimize the sum of the bandwidths of the modes. The bandwidth of each mode is assessed using the Hilbert transform. This helps compute the analytic signal, and instantaneous frequency is calculated from it. Although the respective modes extracted by VMD are similar to IMFs obtained by Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), VMD has certain pros concerning robustness, mode mixing and noise resilience. This study generally uses VMD for feature extraction specific to power-quality applications. The reason for taking VMD in this proposed approach is its robustness and precise decomposition capability to extract the dominant features needed for analysing PQ signals.

The VMD was successfully applied to decompose non-stationary PQ signals. It decomposes the PQE signals adaptively into a fixed number of band-limited modes. The signal at the input can be recovered using the number of modes and the sparsity properties. The bandwidth of each mode is determined as follows: The real signal is converted to an analytic signal with the help of the HT. The shifting of the frequency spectrum to the base-band region is done by adding an exponential term with the approximated mode centre frequency. Every demodulated signal's Gaussian smoothness is used to calculate the bandwidth of each mode.

Three parameters in the VMD algorithm play a major role in signal decomposition. These parameters are named Data Fidelity Constraint (DFC), Lagrangian-Multiplier (LM), and Mode Number (MN). The value of LM (λ) regulates the quality of reconstruction. The bandwidth parameter DFC (α) decides the series of centre frequencies confined by diverse modes of VMD. For clear disturbance recognition, the value of α may vary from a lower value to a higher value. The value of α should be small for a broad range of frequency alterations, and the value should be large for lesser frequency alterations. The MN (K) value should be predefined. Concurrent updates of all the modes in VMD avoid the problem of mode mixing to a large extent.

Elasticnet Regression-based Variational Mode Decomposition (ER-VMD)

As mentioned earlier, VMD is a prominent signal decomposition technique that decomposes non-stationary PQ signals into band-limited IMFs. On the other hand, ER is a prominent linear regression technique that effectively combines the penalties of Lasso (L1) and Ridge (L2) regressions. It is generally useful for handling PQ signal datasets with multiple features, few of which are highly correlated with others. This study uses ER to optimize feature selection and reduce possible overfitting. The features extracted from the IMFs using the VMD are used in an ER model in this proposed algorithm to predict an outcome variable that helps to select the most relevant and optimal IMFs or features among large numbers and highly correlated features extracted. The combination of L1 and L2 regularization in ER helps to manage multicollinearity and prevents overfitting. This, in turn, helps in selection when the number of features (IMFs) is large compared to the number of observations and when complex, multi-component signals need to be analyzed and interpreted, as in this study. This idea has been implemented in the proposed approach. For more details, the readers are advised to read reference [21,43].

The Tikhonov Regularization process is used in the original VMD to reconstruct the input signal. In the proposed ER-VMD, the Elasticnet-Regression (ER) process is used for the same purpose [21]. The ER is the convex grouping of Ridge and Lasso, where the consequences of the regression techniques are linearly combined. This regression type is helpful when the properties of the signal are correlated. A quadratic penalty term is utilized to conquer the limitations of the Lasso regression carrying, l1 terms. The minimization problem can be rewritten as [21]:(17)Vmin{‖V−V0‖22+α‖∂tV‖22+β‖∂tV‖1}

The result of the above problem of minimization is given as follows [21]:(18)Vˆ(ω)=V0ˆ1+αω2+βjω

A single global minimum is obtained due to a quadratic penalty term. This, in turn, makes the cost function strictly convex. The augmented Lagrangian equation is denoted as [21]:(19)L({vk},{ωk},λ)=α∑k=1K‖∂t[(δt+jπt)∗vk(t)]e−jωkt‖22+β∑k=1K‖∂t[(δt+jπt)∗vk(t)]e−jωkt‖1+‖V(t)−∑k=1Kvk(t)‖22+⟨λ(t),V(t)−vk(t)⟩

The ER-VMD improves the IMF separation in components having correlation property, but updating the centre frequency remains the same as the VMD.

Useful and dominant features are computed to characterize the PQ signals from the extracted data sets. In this study, ER-VMD is used along with signal statistics for feature extraction. The selection of suitable PQIs among all possible choices is a very important task since these factors directly influence the efficiency of the classifier used. Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA)

The SSA is one of the newly formulated meta-heuristic algorithms based on swarm and was developed by Mirjalili et al., in 2017. A close observation of the Salp chain's food-searching behaviour and their coordination motion in the deep ocean inspires the development of SSA. The transparent and barrel-shaped Salps come under the family of Salpidae. The food source available in the salty water of the deep sea is the target of the Salp swarms, which is the mathematical representation of search space [35]. In the Salp chain, there are two classes of population: the first is the leader, while the remaining are the followers. At the beginning of the chain, the leader controls it. The others pursue one another and are linked to the chain. The leader, Salp, updates its stance on food sources throughout the duration of the food quest. Following one another, the Salps position for the follower is updated. According to the plan, as stated in Eq. The SSA outperforms other meta-heuristic optimization techniques in many ways. The best solution in SSA is preserved and stored in the food source variable to enable quick convergence during subsequent exploration and the achievement of an ideal solution during the exploration and exploitation stages. Secondly, the leader and followers act coordinatedly and move according to their responsibilities to find the optimum value. The leader continuously updates its position concerning the food source. However, the followers adopted a different strategy and updated their position coherently concerning other followers and the leader. Thirdly, the deliberate movement towards the best value influences the searching strategy to have less probability of being trapped in the local minima. At last, in SSA, only one controlling parameter C1 decremented linearly. So, SSA is simple and easy to implement due to its lack of complexity, unlike other optimization techniques.

SSA in PNN-SSA classifier

The spread and the weight parameters have a big impact on a PNN's classification accuracy. This study employs the SSA (Salp Swarm Algorithm) to determine the ideal spread parameter and weights. The SSA algorithm initiates with the arbitrary initialization of a set of σ values. Then, optimization is carried out on a particular sample taken from the dataset. The final value produced through this technique is regarded as the best for that specific sample. Every sample data point undergoes this process many times to guarantee that the best and most appropriate value is found, resulting in high classification accuracy.

The SSA-PNN combinational algorithm uses the Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) to obtain the optimal parameters for a Probabilistic Neural Network (PNN). Conclusion

This detailed study on PQ events' automatic recognition concludes with an advanced approach based on the ER-VMD-SSA-PNN method. A three-step process comprised of segmentation, feature extraction, and decision through classification is followed in this proposed technique. Covering all single and multiple events with noise signal occurrence possibilities in real-time conditions is simulated to retrieve the data. Additionally, the experimental setup's real-time PQE signals are employed. In the second stage, the modified VMD is proposed to retrieve the features with better accuracy and computational speed successfully. In the third stage, an optimally tuned PNN through the SSA optimization technique is suggested as a classifier to make an accurate decision on detection. The results and discussions as presented reveal the enhanced performance of the proposed ER-VMD-SSA-PNN technique compared to other methods.

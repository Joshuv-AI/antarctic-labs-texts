Novel approach to early prediction of alzheimer’s disease progression using integrated deep regulatory genetic neural network and optimized deep belief networks
Authors: S. Roobini, M.S Kavitha, S. Karthik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To enhance the prediction progression of Alzheimer’s Disease is very excavating process. Problem statement: It is often very difficult to implement in the chronic neurodegenerative disease-related preprocessed gene expression data. Especially, Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) prediction is a very crucial process in AD metadata diagnosis. Novelty: To explore this challenging prediction process in brain disease prediction, this research presents a proposed deep learning model, namely the Integrated Deep Regulatory Genetic Neural Network and Optimised Deep Belief Networks (IDRODN). This integration increases the affluence of prediction progression from genomic data. These prediction systems help identify early AD. Method: This research utilizes the IDRODN, which can predict and confine each network’s neurons and hidden layers against the benchmark dataset of Alzheimer’s gene expression and uncertainty to predict Alzheimer’s Disease. Key results: The comparative analysis on data from the Alzheimer’s disease gene expression data Initiative database has achieved an accuracy of 98.3%. In addition, it has achieved a high F1 score of 0.986 for predicting different stages from Gene expression data. Implications: This shows the most accurate technique for predicting Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) using the prognostic IDRODN model.

Introduction

According to medical science, memory and cognitive abilities are often affected by the deterioration of brain cells. This cognitive and memory loss is classified as Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) in the medical field. According to the World Health Organisation (WHO), public health issues, including Alzheimer’s Disease, require research, data and policy initiatives. WHO says that current research is needed to understand, prevent and manage this specific medical condition. Generally, as people age, the prevalence of brain-related diseases such as Neurodegenerative Diseases, Cerebrovascular Diseases, Seizure and Electrical Activity Disorders, Structural or Traumatic Disorders, Mental and Behavioural Brain Disorders and Infectious Brain Diseases increases, causing many problems for humanity. To control this, early detection of disease progression is very important for future care planning and treatment interventions1. Among the above-mentioned diseases, the most important are the Neurodegenerative Diseases such as Alzheimer’s Disease, Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS / Lou Gehrig’s Disease), Frontotemporal dementia (FTD), Huntington’s Disease, Multiple system atrophy (MSA), Parkinson’s Disease, and Spinocerebellar ataxia. Among these, genes play a key role in understanding the underlying causes of Alzheimer’s Disease and predicting its progression. Considering the causes of the above-mentioned Diseases, recent advances in genetic research in the field of Gene expression2, data taken in the medical field and in prediction3, especially the use of microarray technology, it is imperative to investigate the processes of Alzheimer’s-like disorders at the onset and the gene expression variations that result from them4,5. Analyzing gene expression patterns alone cannot yield conclusive results on the molecular processes that drive development6. Using these, we can provide researchers with significant insights with minimal output by turning genetic data into a robust predictive tool. However, in the case of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), prediction is crucial for predicting how the disease will progress and for implementing prompt treatments to minimize cognitive loss7. However, predicting the range of possible outcomes a patient will achieve will greatly assist healthcare professionals in tailoring their treatment to meet their needs. More broadly, early and accurate prediction improves clinical trial8,8 design and allows for the development of more specific AD drugs. Microarray studies offer interesting options for understanding Alzheimer’s development, often outperforming established approaches such as gene expression data in early detection 10. Genetic data from microarrays is a key component in predicting disease progression11. The combination of gene expression data with advanced machine learning algorithms enables highly accurate and personalized predictions12, which can be used to guide specific treatments and management plans. This research validates the predictive accuracy of the proposed method in two groups2,6. This research focuses on two representative simulations using the proposed method:(i)The effective Prognosis of Alzheimer’s Disease associated with optimized deep belief networks for Alzheimer’s Disease prediction(ii)Integrated Deep Regulatory Genetic Neural Network with Optimized Deep Belief Networks (IDRODN) with the help of gene expression data for the prognosis of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD).

The importance of APOE-ɛ4 in Alzheimer’s disease

The apolipoprotein E gene is a gene that provides instructions for the formation of lipids in the brain. There are three types of this gene, commonly known as APOE-ɛ2, APOE-ɛ3, and APOE-ɛ4. Of these, APOE-ɛ4 is considered the leading genetic risk factor for Alzheimer’s Disease. Because the APOE gene is located on chromosome 19, it is this variant that is responsible for the apolipoprotein E protein, which is essential for metabolism and transport in the brain. These variants are significantly associated with an increased risk of developing Alzheimer’s Disease. Step 4: Unsupervised Pre-training: Initially, the Optimised Deep Belief Network (ODBN) goes through unsupervised pre-training. The Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) techniques allow the model to discover and set up the network’s weights. Also, the risk of overfitting and enhancing generalisation is fixed with the help of RBMs, which are energy-based models. An RBM has a visible layer v and a hidden layer h. These layers are connected by weights W, biases b for the visible layer and biases c for the hidden layer. Conclusion

This research aimed to predict the future course of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) using modern machine learning algorithms and gene expression data. By extracting transcription factor binding sites (TFBS) and confidence ratings using DRGNN and incorporating Optimised Deep Belief Networks (IDRODN) for classification, we tried to improve the forecasting of AD development. The Integrated Deep Regulatory Genetic Neural Network and Optimised Deep Belief Networks (IDRODN) model had an amazing 0.986 accuracy, indicating its potential for precise future forecasting. The examination of the findings demonstrates the model’s capacity to uncover important genetic markers associated with AD development. It has enhanced the prediction progression of chronic neurodegenerative disease-related pre-processed genomic data, especially Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) prediction, effectively. Also explored many challenges in predicting brain disease. The proposed deep learning model, namely Integrated Deep Regulatory Genetic Neural Network and Optimised Deep Belief Networks (IDRODN), identified early prediction in Alzheimer’s Disease 29. Finally, analysis of data from the Alzheimer’s Disease gene expression data database achieved 98.3% accuracy. In addition, it has achieved a high F1 score of 0.986 for predicting different stages from Gene expression data. Future research should focus on applying these approaches to large-scale datasets to confirm findings and improve predictive capability for Alzheimer’s Disease.

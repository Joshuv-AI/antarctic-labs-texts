Improved thermal stability and flame retardancy of soybean oil-based polyol rigid polyurethane foams modified with magnesium borate hydroxide and ammonium polyphosphate
Authors: Xu Zhang, Qihong Guan, Yueqi Wen, Zhi Wang, Hua Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe concept of sustainable development has led to a growing research interest in bio-based flame-retardant polyurethane foams. These foams offer environmentally friendly, sustainable, and flame-retardant raw materials for the construction, automotive, and furniture industries. The 15 wt% soybean oil-based polyol rigid polyurethane foams (RPUF-S3A20B system) were modified with 20 wt% ammonium polyphosphate (APP) and homemade magnesium borate hydroxide (MgBO2(OH)). Thermogravimetric analysis, pyrolysis kinetic analysis, limiting oxygen index (LOI) test, cone calorimetry (CONE), morphological analysis, and smoke density (Ds) were employed to investigate the impact of MgBO2(OH) on the thermal stability and flame-retardant properties of the RPUF-S3A20B system. The results indicated that RPUF-S3A20B12.5 with 12.5 wt% MgBO2(OH) had better thermal stability and higher activation energy. In addition, its LOI was increased by 3.1% compared to RPUF-S3A20 without MgBO2(OH). The peak heat release rate (PHRR) and total heat release rate (THR) of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 at a radiant flux of 25 kW/m2 were reduced by 45.8% and 35.0% compared with RPUF-S3A20. RPUF-S3A20B12.5 demonstrated the lowest smoke density (17.4 and 17.5) and highest light transmission (73.9% and 73.7%) in both flameless and flame conditions, indicating superior flame-retardant and smoke-suppression properties. These findings offered valuable insights for further research on synergistic flame-retardant systems in bio-based polyurethane foams.Keywords: Polyurethane foam, Ammonium polyphosphate, Magnesium borate hydroxide, Thermal stability, Flame retardancySubject terms: Polymers, Characterization and analytical techniques

Introduction

With the growing global market, rigid polyurethane foam (RPUF) has rapidly evolved into one of the most diverse and widely used plastics below1. It is commonly used in a variety of industries including automotive, furniture, construction, packaging and manufacturing2. RPUFs are generally made through the reaction of polyols and isocyanates. With the concept of sustainable development, research on the utilization of bio-based materials instead of petrochemical feedstock has received widespread attention3. Most of the studies conducted on bio-based RPUFs have been directed at bio-based polyol materials. Commonly used polyols include the synthesis of bio-polyols from vegetable oils such as rapeseed oil, cashew nutshell oil, and soybean oil. Their advantages are that the waste is easier to handle and less harmful to the environment. In addition, the process of using vegetable oil as a raw material is simpler than that of petroleum production, which requires the epoxidation reaction in the early stage and the hydroxylation reaction in the later stage.

Among the many polyols synthesized from vegetable oils, the production process using soybean oil as the main raw material for the production of polyols is already quite mature. It has been extensively incorporated into substrates to study its effects on materials. Veronese et al. modified soybean oil using peracid method and synthesized formic acid soybean polyols with formic acid and hydrogen peroxide and used the synthesized formic acid soybean oil-based polyols to manufacture RPUF. The results indicated that the pyrolysis stage of the RPUF made with modified soybean oil was in accordance with the normal polyurethane pyrolysis pattern4. Tang et al. synthesized phosphorylated soybean oil-based polyol (PESO), which was added in different ratios to the PUF. The SEM test indicated that the vesicle pores of the PUF with the addition of PESO had the characteristics of pore size reduction. The cone calorimetry (CONE) test showed that the flame retardancy was improved when the addition of PESO was 12.3 wt%, and the specimen residue after combustion was increased5. Qi et al. prepared soybean oil-based polyester polyol using epoxidized soybean oil as raw material. Soybean oil-based polyurethane was synthesized and characterized by reacting soybean oil-based polyol with isophosphodone diisocyanate. The results showed that its thermal stability was enhanced6. Dhaliwal et al. used soybean oil-based polyol as the substrate of PUF and added nano-clay (Cloisin) in the concentration range of 2–10% clay (Cloisite Na +). The test results indicated that the modified foam leaded to a denser char layer formed during combustion and the burning rate of PUF was reduced by 38%7.

With exploring the modification of RPUF substrates by adding biomass polyols, it has been found that the properties such as flame retardancy of the modified materials have been improved. In addition, some inorganic flame retardants are often introduced into RPUF8,9. Ammonium polyphosphate (APP) is a high-quality phosphorus-based flame retardant, which has been extensively utilized in composite flame retardancy. Chen et al. prepared a new type of expansive alumina-silica hydrogel bivalve microencapsulated APP flame retardant and filled it into polypropylene as a flame retardant. The results showed that the alumina-silica hydrogel bivalve microencapsulated APP improved the thermal stability and charring residue at high temperatures10. Liu et al. added both APP and aluminum hydroxide as flame retardants to polyisocyanate-PUF and the flame retardancy of the foam were shown to be significantly improved11. Zhang et al. modified flexible polyurethane foam with talcum powder, APP and homemade strontium stannate and demonstrated that the flame retardancy of flexible polyurethane foam was improved by CONE and thermogravimetry12.

However, the inclusion of APP in composites also poses problems. In order to achieve a significant flame-retardant effect, a large amount of APP needs to be added, which can lead to the deterioration of the morphology of the composite. Therefore, APP is often used in combination with other flame retardants. In recent years, researchers have explored the use of magnesium and boron as additional flame-retardant elements. Studies have shown that these elements can enhance the flame-retardant properties of materials. Tang et al. synthesized halogen-free and environmentally friendly magnesium hydroxide (Mg (OH)2). The TPE-Mg (OH)2 composites exhibited the best flame-retardant properties when the Mg (OH)2 content was optimized up to 35 wt%13. Zhang et al. used disodium octaborate tetrahydrate, boric acid/borax and ZnBO4 nanoparticles as active flame-retardant components and introduced them into APP. The results showed that the flame retardant, heat release and smoke release properties of bamboo yarns with different boron-based components and APP were significantly improved14.

Magnesium borate hydroxide (MgBO2(OH)) is a novel halogen-free flame-retardant additive that offers environmental benefits such as reduced smoke emissions and lower risk of producing corrosive or toxic fumes compared to traditional halogenated flame retardants. Its comprehensive performance makes it a valuable research subject with significant application potential. Current research on MgBO2(OH) was mainly focused on material preparation, with limited research on incorporating MgBO2(OH) into polyurethane materials for enhanced flame-retardant properties. Therefore, with a green and sustainable philosophy, MgBO2(OH) was prepared and applied to the RPUF. MgBO2(OH) and APP formed a synergistic flame-retardant system to modify soybean oil-based polyol RPUF. Compared with RPUF modified by APP alone, the thermal stability, flame retardancy and smoke suppression properties of the modified RPUF in this system were further improved, which provided a good reference for the research on the construction of a new green flame-retardant system for the RPUF.

Experiments

Materials

Soybean oil polyol (SO, hydroxyl value: 450 ± 30 KOH/g) was provided by Baichuan Chemical Co., Ltd. (Jining, China). Polyether polyol (330N), triethanolamine (AR), silicone oil (L-580) and stannous octanoate (T9) were purchased from Zhuolian Zhichuang Polymer Co., Ltd. (Changzhou, China). Polyisocyanate methylene (PM200) was purchased from Wanhua Polyurethane Co., Ltd. (Yantai, China), ammonium polyphosphate (AR) was purchased from Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Guangdong, China). Sodium hydroxide standard solution was provided by Anda Experimental reagents Co., Ltd. (Xiamen China), and the deionized water was prepared by this laboratory.

Preparation of MgBO2(OH) flame retardants

Magnesium chloride hexahydrate (24.12 g) and sodium tetraborate decahydrate (12 g) were dissolved in 200 mL the deionized water, respectively. And then the two solutions were mixed. A certain amount of polyethylene glycol was added and stirred using a glass rod to fully mix it in the solution. Sodium hydroxide at a concentration of 10% was added to the antecedent solution with constant stirring to produce a white suspension. Preparation of MgBO2(OH) and APP modified soybean oil-based polyol RPUF

The RPUF was prepared by one-step free-foaming method. First, the materials were weighed accurately according to the proportion. The reaction ingredients soybean oil-based polyol and polyether polyol 330N, stannous octoate T9 (catalyst), silicone oil L-580 (stabilizer), triethanolamine (cross-linking agent) and deionized water (blowing agent) were poured into the mixing vessel in a specific order. The mixture was then mechanically stirred at 800 r/min for 30–60 s to ensure uniform mixing. Then the weighed isocyanate quickly was breathed into the above mixing container and mechanically stirred at 800 r/min for 10 s. Finally, when the semi-finished product was seen to expand and felt slightly hot inside the mixing container, the obtained mixture was quickly poured into the mold to make it free foaming. The uncooked RPUF was placed in the experimental chamber at room temperature to keep it for more than 24 h. And the RPUF was obtained to be used for the experimental cutting. The preparation flowchart was shown in Fig. 3. In the preliminary work16, RPUFs modified with 15 wt% soybean oil-based polyol and 20 wt% APP presented the best thermal stability and flame retardancy. Therefore, on the basis of that, the samples prepared with 15 wt% soybean oil-based polyol, 20 wt% APP, MgBO2(OH) of 2.5 wt%, 5 wt%, 7.5 wt%, 10 wt% and 12.5wt% were named RPUF-S3A20B2.5, RPUF-S3A20B5, RPUF-S3A20B7.5, RPUF-S3A20B10 and RPUF-S3A20B12.5, respectively.

Schematic diagram of the modified RPUF preparation process.

Measurement and characterization

The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of different RPUFs under air atmosphere was performed using a DTU-2A differential thermogravimetric analyzer (Beijing Boyuan Precision Technology Co., Ltd., China). About 10–12 mg of the samples were heated from 40 to 800 °C at 10 °C/min, 20 °C/min, 30 °C/min and 40 °C/min. The temperature index and activation energy (E) can be calculated from the TG curve. The combustion characteristics of the RPUFs were tested by a cone calorimeter (CONE, FTT, 0242, UK) under three radiant fluxes of 25 kW/m2, 35 kW/m2 and 50 kW/m2. The flame-retardant property of RPUFs was evaluated by using limiting oxygen index tester (LOI, FTT, 1402072, UK). The residue obtained from the cone calorimeter was observed using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, S4800, Hitachi High-Tech Corporation, Japan). Smoke density chamber (FTT-NBS, UK) was used to measure the smoke density (Ds) and transmittance in both flame and flameless states of five samples.

Results and discussion

Thermogravimetric analysis

TGA is a common technique for evaluating thermal degradation of polymeric materials. The TG and differential thermal analysis (DTG) curves of five RPUFs in the air atmosphere were shown in Fig. 4. The thermal decomposition of the RPUFs depends on the number of polyurethane bonds and the content of aromatic groups17. The DTG curve under the air atmosphere exhibited three distinct stages, as depicted in Fig. 4b. The materials belonging to the RPUF-S3A20B series displayed the highest degradation rates in the hard (215.5–430.0 °C) and soft (430.0–690.0 °C) zones when subjected to a heating rate of 20 °C/min. The initial stage involved the breakdown of carbamate bonds in the hard section, resulting in the formation of isocyanates, alcohols, CO2 and water vapor. The inclusion of soybean oil-based polyol enhanced the cross-linking degree of the system, making it more challenging for the hard chain segments to decompose. Flame retardant APP decomposed to produce NH3, N2 and other gases upon heating, while the addition of MgBO2(OH) led to the generation of water vapor, diluting the combustible gases. The dispersion of MgBO2(OH) within the system effectively isolated some of the heat, and gradually increased the decomposition temperature of the hard chain segment. The release of these gases caused a reduction in the material's mass during the thermal decomposition. The second stage of decomposition primarily involved the degradation of the soft segments, linked to ester bonds and long chains of aliphatic hydrocarbons in the foam. Despite the disintegration of relatively stable chains of small molecules, mass loss persisted. The final decomposition stage was associated with the breakdown of residual organic matter present in the foam.

TG and DTG curves of and five kinds of the modified RPUFs at different heating rates. (a) 10 °C/min, (b) 20 °C/min, (c) 30 °C/min and (d) 40 °C/min.

The DTG curve can determine the pyrolysis temperature parameters and weight loss percentage of the five samples at different heating rates. (as shown in Fig. 4 and Table 1). At 20 °C/min heating rate, the initial decomposition temperatures of five RPUFs were progressively increased. The initial decomposition temperatures were 11.3 °C, 11.4 °C, 14.2 °C, 14.7 °C and 18.6 °C higher, respectively, compared to RPUF-S3A20 (204.2 °C). Compared to RPUF-S3A20 (91.8%), the weight loss of the five RPUFs was reduced to 7.8%, 1.7%, 7.9%, 10.4% and 14.6%, respectively. It can be seen that the initial decomposition temperature of the samples increased with the addition of MgBO2(OH) and the weight loss percentage decreased with the increase of MgBO2(OH). RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had the highest initial decomposition temperature and the smallest weight loss percentage. In addition, RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had a high residual rate of 22.8% at 800 °C. This was due to the fact that the flame retardant was dispersed in the system and the thermal decomposition of MgBO2(OH) produced water vapor. And APP decomposed thermally to produce gases that do not participate in the combustion reaction, such as NO, NH3 and N2, etc. At the same time, these gases insulated and diluted the combustible gases in the vicinity of the RPUF pyrolysis zone, thus delaying the occurrence of thermal decomposition of the material. Additionally, the magnesium borate (MgB2O5) produced by the MgBO2(OH) dehydration and the polyphosphoric acid produced by the APP dehydration were both incorporated into the carbon layer, which increased the density of the carbon layer and reduced the heat transfer during the combustion. The continuous addition of MgBO2(OH) also promoted the formation of carbon after the RPUF combustion.

Pyrolysis kinetic analysis

The pyrolysis kinetic analysis was carried out on the basis of the analysis of the thermal decomposition process of five RPUFs. The thermal stability of the five materials was analyzed by calculating their activation energies (E) using four different methods. The larger values of E indicated better thermal stability. Four methods were used for the calculation of activation energy, Flynn–Wall–Ozawa method (Eq. (1)), Kissinger method (Eq. (2)), Starink method (Eq. (3)), and Coats-Redfern method (Eq. (4))18–21.

Limiting oxygen index test

The LOI is used to determine the level of flame retardancy of a polymer material from the difference in oxygen and nitrogen concentrations. The LOI of the RPUF without flame retardant modification usually exhibited around 19%. Thus, it can be designated as a highly flammable polymer material. A higher LOI indicated that the material was less prone to combustion reactions. As can be seen in Fig. 5, the LOI of five RPUFs increased compared to that of RPUF-S3A20 (21.5%) and the LOI of the samples increased with the increase of MgBO2(OH) addition. The LOI of RPUF-S3A20B2.5 to RPUF-S3A20B7.5 increased from 22.3 to 24.4%, an increase of 2.1%. When the addition of MgBO2(OH) was continued, the LOI also increased significantly, but at a slower rate until the maximum LOI of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 reached 24.6%, which was an enhancement of 3.1% compared to RPUF-S3A20 (21.5%). It can be proved that MgBO2(OH) had the ability to prevent the combustion of the RPUF. This was because when the sample was heated, MgBO2(OH) decomposed into MgB2O5 and water vapor, which absorbed part of the heat and prevented oxygen and flammable gases in the air from coming into contact with the specimen. Thus, RPUF-S3A20B12.5 showed the best flame-retardant effect.

CONE calorimetry test

The flame-retardant properties of the modified RPUFs were analyzed by the CONE test. Figures 6, 7, 8 illustrated the heat release rate (HRR) and total heat release (THR) curves of the five RPUFs at various radiation intensities and the CONE date was displayed in Table 6. Flame intensity was correlated with the release of low molecular weight compounds such as isocyanates, and was measured by the peak HRR (PHRR)22. As depicted in Figs. 6, 7, 8, all the analyzed samples exhibited a peak value for this metric. The modified RPUFs with MgBO2(OH) reached PHRR approximately 50 s after the start of combustion, and then tended to decrease as the combustion weakened, finally reaching a steady state around 100 s. At 25 kW/m2, the PHRR value of sample decreases from 602.7 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20) to 326.6 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20B12.5), which was a reduction of 45.8%. And at a radiation intensity of 35 kW/m2, the peak value decreases from 506.8 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20) to 331.6 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20B12.5), which was a reduction of 34.6%. Similarly, at 50 kW/m2, the peak value decreases from 847.3 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20) to 551.8 kW/m2 (RPUF-S3A20B12.5), which was a reduction of 34.9%. These results showed that the addition of MgBO2(OH) reduced the PHRR of the modified RPUF, and the PHRR of the material decreased with the increase of MgBO2(OH) addition, which confirmed that a certain amount of MgBO2(OH) enhanced the flame-retardant effect of the modified RPUF. The PHRR values of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 showed the lowest PHRR at different radiation intensities.

A similar relationship was observed when analyzing the effect of adding MgBO2(OH) flame retardant on THR. At 25 kW/m2, the THR value decreased from 18.3 MJ/m2 (RPUF-S3A20) to 11.9 MJ/m2 (RPUF-S3A20B12.5), which was a reduction of 35.0%. The results showed that the THR of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 presented the lowest value, and PHRR and THR results indicated that RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had the best flame-retardant properties.

The improved flame retardancy of the modified RPUF was due to the synergistic effect of MgBO2(OH) and APP. The water vapor produced by MgBO2(OH) and the N2 and NH3 produced by APP effectively diluted the air, and the MgB2O5 and polyphosphoric acid produced by their decomposition adhered to the interior of the carbon layer to improve the quality of the carbon layer. The flame retardancy of the modified RPUF was also improved by the attachment of MgB2O5 and polyphosphoric acid to the carbon layer.

Morphological analysis

After conducting the CONE test, the residual carbon was analyzed both macroscopically and microscopically to examine its morphology. The macro combustion situations of the samples were analyzed by observing the residual char, as depicted in Fig. 9. It can be observed that the surface of the char layer exhibited noticeable holes and slight fractures after combustion. As the content of MgBO2(OH) increased, the presence of holes in the carbon layer decreased and no fracture phenomenon was observed. The residual carbon of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 exhibited the most complete and dense structure. The enhanced whitening of the carbon layer surface in the flame-retarded samples containing MgBO2(OH) was attributed to the formation of fully decomposed MgB2O5 crystals. It provided some flame-retardant effect by encapsulating the surface of the carbon layer. The results of the SEM experiment were presented in Fig. 10. The pores of the carbon residue after the combustion were larger when MgBO2(OH) was added at 2.5 wt%, 5 wt% and 7.5 wt%, respectively. The residual carbon had obvious cracks, and these samples could not produce a better insulation layer during combustion and could not prevent oxygen and flame from entering the material well. When the addition of MgBO2(OH) was 10 wt%, the residual carbon pores were reduced. When the MgBO2(OH) additive amount reached 12.5 wt%, the highest density of carbon layer can be observed, and there was no obvious pores or cracks. The dense carbon layer hindered the diffusion of free radicals, thereby halting the chain reaction. In addition, a significant amount of flame-retardant powders were found on the surface of the char layer, which helped to delay combustion in the condensed phase of the modified RPUF and achieve the expected flame-retardant effect. This was consistent with the results obtained from the macroanalysis, which further validated the superior flame-retardant properties of RPUF-S3A20B12.5.

Smoke density

Smoke concentration in a real fire was a critical factor that can be fatal. It can lead to asphyxiation of people in the environment and impede rescue efforts due to high smoke density. Therefore, smoke suppression and flame retardation were given equal importance in the study of combustion properties of flame retardant polymeric materials. As shown in Fig. 11, the RPUFs undergoing flameless combustion exhibited a peak in smoke volume around 600 s, followed by a plateau. The addition of more MgBO2(OH) resulted in a decrease in the Ds of the sample and an increase in transmission. At 1200 s, the Ds of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 (17.4) was the lowest, while the transmittance of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 (73.9%) was the highest. In comparison to RPUF-S3A20, the Ds of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 decreased by 37.9% and the transmittance increased by 16.9%. This phenomenon can be attributed to the addition of MgBO2(OH), which enhanced the formation of a carbon layer, which effectively suppressed combustion and smoke diffusion.

Under the flame condition, the RPUFs were subjected to both the thermal radiation effects of the radiating cone and the effects of the burning flame. The sample burned faster when exposed to the flame, with Ds peaking at 200 s and transmittance being minimized. Figure 12 revealed that RPUF-S3A20B12.5 (17.5) had the smallest Ds, while RPUF-S3A20B12.5 (73.7%) exhibited the highest transmission. In comparison to RPUF-S3A20, RPUF-S3A20B12.5 showed a 36.0% reduction in Ds and an 18.8% increase in transmittance. These findings aligned with previous results in flameless conditions, demonstrating the superior smoke suppression effect of RPUF-S3A20B12.5.

Flame retardant mechanism

Figure 13 showed a schematic diagram of the flame retardant mechanism. In this flame-retardant system, MgBO2(OH) and APP had a synergistic flame-retardant effect in both the gas and condensed phases. In the gas phase, when MgBO2(OH) was heated as a flame retardant in a composite material, it generated water vapor. The presence of water vapor not only absorbed heat in the air but also diluted the combustible gases surrounding the material. APP decomposed gases like N2, NO and NH3 to dilute the air and separated combustible gases from coming into contact with combustion materials. Additionally, at high temperatures, the P element contained in APP itself produced PO⋅ and HPO⋅ free radicals, which captured active free radicals like H⋅, HO⋅ and O⋅ that were generated during the combustion chain reaction, ultimately terminating the combustion process16. Flame retardation in the condensed phase was due to the conversion of MgBO2(OH) to MgB2O5 and the dehydration of APP to generate polyphosphoric acid, which were attached to the carbon network framework, played a reinforcing role, increased the density of the carbon layer, and impeded the transfer of heat, thus acting as a condensed phase flame retardant. In addition, the hydroxyl group in soybean oil-based polyol can react with eNCO of isocyanate to increase the cross-linking density of the modified RPUF, which also improved its thermal stability.

Conclusion

Improved thermal stability and flame retardancy of soybean oil-based polyol RPUF modified with MgBO2(OH) and APP had been investigated using thermogravimetric analysis, pyrolysis kinetic analysis, LOI, CONE and Ds. The TG results demonstrated that the inclusion of MgBO2(OH) raised the initial decomposition temperature of the modified RPUFs. And as the amount of MgBO2(OH) increased, the weight loss percentage of the modified RPUFs also decreased. The highest initial decomposition temperature and lowest weight loss percentage of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 was observed at four heating rates. RPUF-S3A20B12.5 exhibited the highest E value among the five RPUFs, aligning with the TG analysis and indicating superior thermal stability. Additionally, it also exhibited the highest LOI of 24.6%. Compared with RPUF-S3A20, the PHRR of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 was reduced by 45.8%, 34.6% and 34.9%, and the THR was reduced by 35.0%, 41.6% and 56.4%, under 25, 35 and 50 kW/m2, which showed that RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had better flame retardancy. The SEM results showed that the char layer of RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had the highest density and the smallest pores. The Ds results demonstrated that RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had the lowest Ds and the highest transmittance. This was due to the fact that MgBO2(OH) and APP in the system could play an active role in both the gas and condensed phases, which improved the flame retardant and smoke suppression properties of the material.

In conclusion, the simultaneous addition of MgBO2(OH) and APP can significantly enhance the thermal stability and flame retardancy of the modified RPUF and RPUF-S3A20B12.5 had the highest thermal stability and flame retardancy. The current results showed that the modification of soybean oil-based polyol RPUF with MgBO2(OH) and APP had a promising application prospect in flame retardant. Synergistically modified RPUF had the potential to provide environmentally friendly, durable, flame-retardant and smoke-resistant materials for a variety of industries including construction, automotive, furniture and aerospace, which could provide a valuable reference for future research on the environmental and flame-retardant modification of the RPUF.

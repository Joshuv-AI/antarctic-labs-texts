Changes in the congenital heart disease spectrum at China national children's medical center: a review of 10-year data
Authors: Jia Zheng, Zhiqiang Li, Song Bai, Jian Guo, Zhenjiang Song, Qilin Li, Nan Ding, Yaobin Zhu, Fan Fan, Zhangke Guo, Hanlu Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the decline in the birth rate and simultaneous advancements in medical technology in China, research on how birth population changes have affected congenital heart disease (CHD) surgery in the last 10 years has increased.MethodWe investigated the medical data of all patients diagnosed with CHD treated at the surgical department at our hospital from January 2013 to December 2022, including their age and the type of surgery. We classified patients into two groups based on their age: less than 1 year (younger) and greater than 1 year (older). We defined patent ductus arteriosus, atrial septal defect, and ventricular septal defect as simple CHDs and others as complex diseases. Vascular ring diseases, including double aortic arch, pulmonary artery sling, and aberrant left subclavian artery with diverticulum, were considered special conditions.ResultWe found that 6440 patients underwent surgery at our hospital in the period mentioned. The number of surgeries conducted annually increased significantly from 609 in 2016 to 941 in 2019 but gradually declined since 2020. The proportion of simple CHD cases increased annually, whereas the proportion of complex CHD cases declined. The proportion of patients with vascular ring disease gradually increased. The proportion of older children increased, whereas that of younger children decreased each year. All changes were statistically significant.ConclusionsOwing to advancements in medical technology, economic factors, change of birth rate and abortion rate, the number of surgeries for CHDs has decreased significantly. It is challenging to predict future trends. Nevertheless, this phenomenon is specific to China. Policymakers, hospitals, and surgeons must introduce appropriate adjustments in response to this change.

Introduction

Changes in the disease spectrum are influenced by increased investment in healthcare, economic development, and change of birth rate and abortion rate. Some scholars emphasize that the social determinants of health can be conceptualized as influencing health at multiple levels throughout one's life [1]. Congenital heart disease (CHD) results from a combination of genetic and environmental factors. Its incidence rate, which is 0.8 % according to reports [2], does not vary significantly among different races, geographic regions, and countries with varying levels of economic development. Some scholars suggest that a greater prevalence of left heart system anomalies, such as aortic coarctation and hypoplastic left heart syndrome, is observed among European and American populations with complex CHD. In Asian populations, particularly East Asians, tetralogy of Fallot is more prevalent [[3], [4]]. In our hospital, the China National Children's Medical Center, we have previously found evidence to support this perspective. However, as China's economic development slows, reforms in the health sector—particularly the growing emphasis on prenatal malformation screening—and shifts in reproductive concepts among the Chinese population have led to a distinct CHD spectrum in China compared to that in other global regions, characterized by significant changes in both year number and disease spectrum. Here, we analyze and summarize data collected over the past 10 years from a single center and report the changes observed.

Materials and methods

We collected the medical records of patients diagnosed with CHD admitted to our surgical department from January 2013 to December 2022, including their age and the type of surgery they underwent. We classified patients into two groups based on their age: under 1 year (younger) and above 1 year (older). With respect to the types of CHD, we defined patent ductus arteriosus, atrial septal defect, and ventricular septal defect as simple CHDs, consistent with findings from our research. Aortic coarctation, endocardial cushion defect, tetralogy of Fallot, Williams syndrome, anomalous pulmonary venous connection, complete transposition of the great arteries, and pulmonary atresia were considered complex CHDs. Our cardiology department maintains long-term collaborations with internal medicine and imaging centers. With advancements in medical examination technology, an increasing number of patients with vascular ring diseases are undergoing surgical interventions. These conditions include a double aortic arch, pulmonary artery sling, and aberrant left subclavian artery with diverticulum. We excluded patients who received interventional treatment at the cardiology department. The number of patients who underwent intervention increased from 120 in 2013 to 254 in 2022. The primary disease were patent ductus arteriosus and atrial septal defect. In the past 4 years, the increase in the number of patients with ventricular septal defect has offset the previous decline in the number of patients undergoing complex CHD angiography. These patients were predominantly classified as having simple CHD or as children older than 1 year.

Statistical analysis

We used SAS software for statistical analysis. The count data of the constituent ratios were used to characterize the dataset. The Cochran-Armitage trend test was used for statistical evaluation. A P-value <0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Discussion

Our results indicate that the CHD spectrum in China has changed significantly during the period mentioned. The proportion of surgical procedures for simple CHDs and vascular ring diseases has increased significantly. This trend may be partially attributed to the year-on-year decline in China's newborn population. Data from the National Bureau of Statistics of China illustrate that the annual birth population in China from 2013 to 2022 was 16.4, 16.87, 16.55, 18.83, 17.75, 15.23, 14.65, 12.00, 10.62, and 9.56 million, respectively (Fig. 7) [5].Fig. 7Data of newborn children in China from 2013 to 2022 and abortions in China from 2013 to 2021.Fig. 7

Data of newborn children in China from 2013 to 2022 and abortions in China from 2013 to 2021.

Concurrently, data from China's Ministry of Health indicate that the proportion of artificial abortions has remained high. The annual number of artificial abortions from 2013 to 2021 was 6.23, 9.62, 9.85, 9.64, 9.62, 9.74, 9.76, 8.62, and 9.50 million, respectively [6] (Fig. 7). Owing to specific circumstances, we could not obtain the 2022 abortion data from public government reports.

Experts estimate that the actual number of artificial abortions in China is approximately 13 million per year, a number widely reported in the media. This number is significantly higher than the reported statistics, as it does not include the abortions conducted at private hospitals and unregistered clinics [7].

According to existing reports, before 2005, cleft lip was the most commonly reported birth defect, whereas, nowadays CHD ranks first [8]. In China, from 2005 to 2010 the incidence of congenital heart anomalies, neural tube defects, congenital musculoskeletal and limb anomalies, and Down syndrome in children under 5 years decreased by 14.18 %, 72.03 %, 11.24 %, and 4.01 %, respectively. Meanwhile, the incidence of congenital digestive system malformations increased by 187.83 %. The incidence of congenital heart anomalies initially increased and subsequently decreased, with the largest increase occurring between 2010 and 2016 (the annual percentage change was 2.05 %) and the largest decrease occurring between 2016 and 2019 (the annual percentage change was −10.67 %) [9]. Neural tube defects can be reduced through folic acid supplementation, and Down syndrome can be detected via blood and exfoliated cell tests. However, currently, there is no medical evidence to suggest the prevention of CHD. CHD prevention is largely reliant on prenatal examinations and timely detection.

By 2010, China had established a maternal and child health monitoring network covering 140 million people [10], and it has continued to expand over the past 10 years. Since 2012, the Chinese government initiated a newborn disease screening subsidy program in certain regions, which led to a significant advancement in screening technologies and methodologies. With the advancement of prenatal screening, cardiac ultrasound can now be used readily to detect most complex CHDs [11,12]. This is significant because ventricular septal defect is not a component of fetal circulation, yet it is one of the key components of several complex CHDs [13]. Given the economic burden and psychological pressure associated with such conditions, in China, pregnant women and their families are more inclined to terminate pregnancies when presented with these diagnoses. According to the data of our center, the number of patients with single ventricle and PA/VSD has dropped to less than 5 cases per year since 2013. This situation is significantly different from that in Western countries. Additionally, in clinical settings, we encounter cases of in vitro fertilization, where parents invest significantly in conception. Although such children may potentially experience various challenges, including poor nutrition, their families are often more willing to pursue treatment.

Among the cases reported, the proportion of vascular ring disease has increased gradually. We considered two factors. First, as a comprehensive pediatric hospital, patients with vascular ring diseases are more likely to present with symptoms treatable using internal medicine. The most common cause is pneumonia. Few children will present with dysphagia [14]. Second, significant improvements have been made in inspection technology and the expertise of personnel working in imaging centers in China.

We analyzed the data of patients with CHD at Fuwai Hospital [15], which is recognized as the world's largest cardiovascular hospital, and observed a similar trend in the number of surgeries. Notably, in 2021, Fuwai Hospital established a branch in collaboration with the Henan Provincial People's Hospital. Since Henan is the most populous province in China, the data from this branch have slowed the decline (Fig. 8).Fig. 8Surgeries conducted for CHD in Fuwai Hospital.Fig. 8

Data from the Extracorporeal Branch of the Chinese Society of Biological Engineering were also analyzed [16]. The number of pediatric congenital heart surgeries reduced from 61,825 in 2017 to 38,174 in 2022, a decline of 38.25 %, with an average yearly decrease of 6.38 %. The trend is similar to the one in our report (Fig. 9).Fig. 9Number of pediatric cardiac surgeries conducted in China from 2017 to 2022.Fig. 9

A study based on data from the Global Burden of Disease database showed that the annual percentage rate change for congenital heart disease in China decreased significantly from 2015 to 2019, with a value of −2.4 % (−3.7 % to −1.1 %) [17]. This decline cannot be explained simply by the improvement of citizens' living standards. It is more due to social behavior intervention.

We speculate that these factors will continue to drive changes in the CHD spectrum in China and will place increasing pressure on all china cardiac surgery departments to adapt.

Conclusion

Owing to advancements in medical technology, economic factors, change of birth rate and abor-tion rate, the number of surgeries for CHD in recnet years in China has decreased significantly. It is challenging to predict future trends. Nevertheless, this phenomenon is specific to China. Policymakers, hospitals, and surgeons may introduce appropriate adjustments in response to this change.

Limitation

Owing to the lack of a unified CHD database in China, data from various hospitals are neither shared nor standardized. The changes observed in the data from our center may not be applicable at a national scale.

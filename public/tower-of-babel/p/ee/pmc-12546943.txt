Research on the similarity calculation of short text in the terminology domain based on siamese BERT model
Authors: Fei Chen, Zhenling Zhang, Yangli Jia, Ruchao Jia, Haitao Wang, Xinyu Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study focuses on the task of term-domain short-text similarity computation. It addresses two main challenges: dataset scarcity and insufficient deep semantic extraction. To solve these issues, we first construct the multi-domain Terminology Definition Similarity (TDS) dataset using an automated pipeline. This pipeline combines data generation (based on the GPT-4 model) with a manual verification process involving expert quality control. The design ensures the production of high-quality data. We then present an innovative model named SDQKC (Sbert + dynamic QK + contrast). The model optimizes the Siamese BERT network through a dynamic QK co-attention mechanism and enhances its deep-level semantic understanding by incorporating contrastive learning. Experimental results show that the SDQKC model achieves Pearson correlation coefficients of 0.69384 on the BQ Corpus (Bank Question Corpus) and 0.69511 on the TDS dataset. These results significantly outperform other baseline models, demonstrating the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed methodology.Keywords: Terminology definition, Similarity calculation, Pre-trained model, Contrastive learning, Attention mechanismSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Terminology standardization is the process of using standardized methods to study domain-specific terms. It involves examining conceptual characteristics, relationships between concepts, conceptual descriptions, and classification systems1. A key challenge in this field is efficiently computing text similarity between term definitions. Addressing this challenge is essential for both terminology standardization and knowledge management.

In recent years, the rapid expansion of terminological resources across different domains has created valuable data for terminology research and knowledge management2. Computing text similarity between term definitions is a key step in this process. It helps identify semantically related definitions from large collections of unstructured texts and supports tasks such as terminology standardization, classification, and knowledge base construction. Despite its importance, current studies on definition similarity face several challenges. High-quality, domain-specific datasets remain limited, while general-purpose datasets often fail to capture the subtle semantic distinctions. The diversity of expression patterns and the inherent semantic complexity of definitions further complicate similarity measurement. Addressing these challenges requires methods that can effectively extract semantic information from definition texts and measure their similarity with high accuracy. Such methods are essential not only for building terminology knowledge graphs but also for advancing terminology research and improving information retrieval.

Text similarity computation is a crucial research topic in natural language processing. It supports applications such as information retrieval, machine translation, and question-answering systems by measuring semantic similarity between texts3. Traditional text representation models, including Word2Vec, Skip-Gram, and Glove4, map texts into low-dimensional vector spaces. These models achieved notable success in early studies. However, they have limited representational capacity. They also struggle to handle word polysemy. With recent advancements in deep learning, Transformer-based pre-trained models have become a major research focus. Models such as BERT, Sentence-BERT5, and GPT6 learn rich linguistic representations through pre-training on large-scale textual corpora. They can then be fine-tuned for specific tasks, which significantly improves performance in NLP applications7,8. These models use context-aware semantic representation mechanisms. This allows them to better capture the meaning of texts and partially mitigate polysemy issues that challenge traditional approaches. Despite their strong performance in general text similarity tasks, pre-trained models still face limitations in processing terminological definitions. Terminological definitions are usually short and precisely formulated. In them, subtle semantic distinctions are critical. Building on these insights, the main contributions of this work are outlined below:

1) This study proposes and constructs the Terminology Definition Similarity (TDS) dataset. It addresses the limitations of conventional dataset construction methods. To achieve this, we integrate large language models (LLMs) and develop an automated data generation pipeline based on GPT-4. The pipeline produces large-scale pairs of semantically similar and dissimilar sentences. In this way, it removes the need for labor-intensive manual annotation and ensures high-quality dataset construction. Our approach reduces annotation costs and improves scalability.

2) This study introduces a dynamic Query-Key (dynamic QK) co-attention mechanism to improve the Siamese BERT network. In this approach, the dynamic QK parameters are generated based on the input at each layer, ensuring that different inputs produce different parameters. These dynamic QK parameters interact with the attention scores to perform a second-stage correction of the attention distribution, thereby helping the model focus more effectively on semantically critical regions and enabling more precise semantic discrimination.

3) This study introduces contrastive learning to enhance the model’s semantic representation by constructing positive and negative sample pairs. In each training iteration, a positive sample is first selected from the batch as an anchor. The anchor is then paired with other unselected samples in the batch to form negative pairs. By dynamically constructing positive and negative pairs within the batch, the model’s ability to distinguish semantic differences is strengthened. This mechanism forces the model to learn more discriminative semantic representations. It effectively improves the model’s accuracy in handling term definitions that are lexically similar but semantically different (i.e., hard negative samples).

Related work

In the domain of short-text similarity computation, existing methodologies have evolved along three primary trajectories: Shallow matching approaches based on traditional feature engineering, semantic representation methods leveraging deep learning and hybrid characterization techniques incorporating pre-trained models. Early research predominantly employed conventional methods such as TF-IDF weighted term frequency matching and edit distance computation. While offering computational efficiency, these methods struggled to capture deep semantic relationships in texts. With the advancement of deep learning technologies, Siamese networks have gained widespread adoption due to their dual-tower architecture and parameter-sharing mechanism. Examples like Siamese LSTM and Siamese CNN have significantly enhanced the ability to capture semantic differences by learning distributed representations of texts in an end-to-end manner. However, they still face limitations in addressing the sparse features of short texts and inadequate modeling of long-range contextual dependencies. More recently, pre-trained language models like BERT and Sentence-BERT have achieved deep contextual representations of short texts through dynamic word embeddings and multi-layer self-attention mechanisms. Meanwhile, researchers have introduced strategies such as knowledge distillation to optimize the balance between semantic space alignment and computational efficiency. These advancements have driven a paradigm shift in the field, moving from local feature matching to global semantic comprehension.

Yang9 proposed a pyramid matching kernel-based approach for short-text matching, combining BERT encoding, attention mechanisms, and LSTM networks to enhance contextual information capture. Meng et al.10 addressed the limited feature extraction capability of Siamese-LSTM models by introducing an improved architecture integrating attention mechanisms and pre-trained BERT, thereby strengthening synonym recognition and inter-word interaction. Zhao et al.11 developed the Re-LSTM model, which fused weighted word embeddings with LSTM networks to balance computational efficiency while preserving both term frequency and semantic representations, offering a lightweight solution for text similarity analysis. Gao et al.12 introduced SimCSE, a contrastive learning-based self-supervised framework that dynamically generated text augmentation samples via dropout techniques. By constructing positive pairs and optimizing semantic space distributions, this method effectively mitigated semantic representation biases in unlabeled data scenarios. Li et al.13 presented a semantic similarity model combining Siamese BERT architecture with attention mechanisms, reducing computational complexity through parameter-shared twin networks while dynamically assigning word-level weights via attention, significantly improving short-text contextual representation and semantic discrimination accuracy. Liu et al.14 proposed SpeBERT, a syntax-enhanced BERT variant that dynamically extracted task-critical sentence components through dependency parsing. They integrated mean pooling and multi-strategy fusion methods with [CLS] embeddings, demonstrating performance gains in sentiment classification and semantic similarity tasks. Yu et al.15 designed a patent document matching framework integrating multiple BERT variants (DeBERTa-v3-large, ELECTRA) with specialized preprocessing strategies (V3). This approach fused weighted predictions from heterogeneous models and introduced structural input markers (e.g., [TAR]) alongside dynamic token scoring mechanisms, providing an optimized solution for patent classification systems. Zhao et al.16 developed an Enhanced Inter-sentence Attention (EIA) architecture using multi-head self-attention, implementing universal enhancement (amplifying global inter-sentence attention) and specific enhancement (WordNet-based semantic relation weighting). A gated fusion module dynamically combined original and enhanced attention features, validating improved similarity modeling through strengthened semantic associations. Chen et al.17 introduced CNN-ATT-SBert, a Siamese network-based model that fused multi-scale CNN with attention mechanisms to extract multi-granularity text features and amplify critical semantic information.

Although significant progress has been made in short-text semantic representation and similarity computation, two key challenges remain to be addressed. First, at the data level, the field of term definition similarity computation lacks high-quality, high-difficulty specialized datasets. Existing general-purpose similarity datasets are insufficient to support the nuanced semantic differentiation required in the domain of term definitions, thereby limiting the advancement of critical tasks such as terminology standardization and knowledge graph construction. Moreover, the construction methods of existing text similarity datasets are relatively homogeneous, heavily relying on manual annotation, which demands substantial human resources. Second, at the model architecture level, the standard attention mechanism is limited by its reliance on shallow statistical features and static weight assignment, leading to insufficient modeling of deep semantic associations between terms and susceptibility to interference from high-frequency noise words, ultimately affecting semantic discrimination accuracy. To address these issues, this paper proposes the following innovations: on one hand, we leverage the GPT-4 model to build an automated data generation pipeline, eliminating the need for manual instance-by-instance annotation and enabling the high-quality construction of term definition datasets. On the other hand, this study adopts dynamic QK co-attention to improve the Siamese BERT network. Unlike most existing attention-enhanced Siamese BERT methods, which usually strengthen sentence representations by adding extra attention layers or introducing structured features, our dynamic QK mechanism directly re-parameterizes the core computation of attention. In this mechanism, Q and K are no longer based on fixed projection matrices. Instead, they are dynamically generated from the input with correction terms to refine the original attention scores. This design represents a modification of the attention computation itself, rather than a simple addition to the existing architecture.

Dataset

Currently, the domain of terminological definitions lacks systematic datasets for text similarity computation, which constrains research and applications in terminology standardization, knowledge integration, and related natural language processing tasks. To address this gap, we have constructed a specialized high-quality dataset for text similarity computation in terminological definitions. This resource aims to support the unification and standardization of terminological definitions, facilitate multi-source knowledge fusion and retrieval, and provide robust data foundations for machine translation, term retrieval and text generation tasks. Concurrently, it advances research in automated processing and quality control within terminology management systems.

In constructing the dataset, we employ the GPT-4 model as the core tool for generating similar and dissimilar sentence pairs, guided by the following considerations: First, the state-of-the-art GPT-4 model exhibits exceptional semantic comprehension and generation capabilities. This enables the efficient production of high-quality, semantically rich sentences and ensures that the dataset achieves high standards in diversity and accuracy. Second, the GPT-4 model can generate similar and dissimilar sentences based on input term definitions. These sentences closely align with the contextual usage, accurately simulating variations in term definitions in real-world scenarios. This provides reliable and practical data support for subsequent text similarity calculations. Furthermore, leveraging GPT-4’s advanced capabilities significantly enhances data construction efficiency, substantially reducing the temporal and economic costs associated with manual annotation while ensuring the dataset’s scale and quality meet research requirements. By integrating GPT-4, we establish an automated pipeline for generating high-quality, diversified TDS datasets, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The pipeline takes original term definitions as input. It uses the GPT-4 model with different prompts and parameters (e.g., Temperature) to generate semantically similar and dissimilar sentences. Labels (1 or 0) are automatically assigned to these sentences. Finally, the TDS dataset is constructed. The detailed procedure is illustrated in Sect. 3.1.2.

Dataset generation

Data preparation

Ensuring high-quality input is critical to generating a high-quality dataset. Prior to feeding the term definitions into the data generation pipeline, we performed strict preparation and normalization on term definitions sourced from diverse domains.

1) Deduplication and uniqueness assurance: We first employ the MinHash LSH algorithm to efficiently remove identical or highly redundant term definition entries (similarity ≥ 90%) from the terminological corpus. This process ensures the uniqueness of raw terminological definitions, serving as a prerequisite for generating diversified similar and dissimilar sentence pairs in subsequent stages.

2)Punctuation and format sanitization: We systematically detect and remove redundant, erroneous, or non-standard punctuation marks (e.g., consecutive periods, commas) in terminological definitions. Text formats are standardized, and special characters that may interfere with semantic interpretation are eliminated.

3) Null and invalid data filtering: Entries containing empty definitions, excessively short texts (length < 5 tokens, insufficient to convey complete definitions), or excessively long texts (potentially containing redundant information or multiple merged definitions) are removed. Overly brief sentences lack contextual coherence for generating meaningful variants, while lengthy texts risk introducing noise.

4) Stripping non-core information: Remove common additional notes at the end of definition texts, such as “See also,” “Source,” etc. While these pieces of information, although valuable, are generally not considered part of the definition itself in core semantic similarity calculation tasks. Stripping them helps models focus on the core explanations of term definitions.

After data cleaning, we selected 3,700 standardized term definitions from the definitions of terms across multiple domains.

Design of the TDS dataset generation pipeline

We constructed a TDS dataset generation pipeline based on OpenAI’s GPT-4 model, as illustrated in Fig. 1. To ensure high-quality output of the dataset generation pipeline, we designed prompts tailored for generating similar and dissimilar sentences and adjusted related parameters such as temperature. During each GPT-4 invocation, terminological definitions were input to the model to generate corresponding similar sentences, dissimilar sentences, and their labels.

First, we input the 3,700 selected terminology definitions into the data generation pipeline and use the initial prompt as follows:

Prompt

Generate one semantically similar sentence and one semantically dissimilar sentence for each provided sentence, while maintaining the format of a definition.

The original text in Chinese is:“为提供的每一个句子生成一个语义相同的相似句和一个语义不同的不相似句, 并保持定义的形式。”.

However, the similar and dissimilar sentence pairs generated through this prompt exhibit a significant limitation. Even the simplest string matching methods can effectively distinguish between them. As a result, the dataset lacks sufficient challenge, limiting its ability to support complex text similarity calculation tasks. Specifically, similar sentence pairs predominantly achieve high similarity through superficial transformations like synonym substitution or word order modification, while dissimilar pairs demonstrate only surface-level semantic divergences that fail to reflect deeper semantic changes.

To address this issues, we conducted multiple experimental iterations optimizing both the prompt design and critical generation parameters. This optimization process involved not only refining the instructions themselves, but also exploring critical parameters of the generation model, particularly the configuration of temperature and penalty parameters18.

Under default parameters, even with optimized prompts, the model exhibits conservatism in generating high-difficulty dissimilar sentences, struggling to achieve the objective of “minimal lexical modification with substantial semantic divergence.” Concurrently, similar sentence generation demonstrates lexical homogeneity with insufficient diversity. To enhance lexical variation, we systematically evaluated gradient-increasing temperature parameters (0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9). At temperatures ≤ 0.6, generated sentences maintain strong semantic consistency but suffer from limited lexical substitutions and oversimplified syntax, with partial outputs overly proximate to original definitions. When the temperature is increased to 0.8, the model-generated content demonstrates significant diversity in lexical choices and syntactic structures. However, some sentences exhibit semantic deviations that fail to strictly preserve the original meaning. At a temperature of 0.7, the generated similar sentences strike an optimal balance between semantic consistency and lexical diversity, largely fulfilling the requirement to “maximize character-level modifications while preserving identical semantics.”

For dissimilar sentence generation, temperature parameters were tested starting at 0.1 using a gradient-increasing approach, aiming to induce minimal edits with abrupt semantic shifts. At 0.1 and 0.2, generated sentences showed only minor lexical adjustments and insufficient semantic differentiation. Subsequent parameter elevation to 0.3 and 0.4, combined with reinforced prompts, yielded critical improvements. At 0.3, the model achieved substantial semantic transformations while preserving syntactic structures, effectively balancing the dual requirements of “minimal character-level modifications” and “substantial semantic transformation.”

In addition, we attempted to control vocabulary repetition and promote expression diversity by adjusting the frequency penalty and presence penalty parameters. After multiple rounds of evaluation, we found that setting both parameters to 1.1 effectively reduced repetitive vocabulary. This adjustment also enhanced lexical diversity and semantic coverage, leading to an overall improvement in generation quality.

By combining the refined design of prompts with careful adjustments to temperature and penalty parameters, we finalized an improved set of instructions. This configuration aims to more effectively control the generation process and enhance the quality of the dataset.

The prompt for generating similar sentences is as follows:

Prompt

For each given sentence, generate a semantically equivalent sentence by altering the maximum number of characters while preserving the original meaning.

The original text in Chinese is: “为提供的每一个句子生成一个语义相似句, 在生成相似句时改变最多的字但保持语义不变。”.

The prompt for generating dissimilar sentences is as follows:

Prompt

For each given sentence, generate a semantically dissimilar sentence by altering the minimum number of characters while changing the original meaning.

The original text in Chinese is:“为提供的每一个句子生成一个语义不相似句, 在生成不相似句时改变最少的字但使其语义改变。[不相似句]”.

The optimized prompt requires the model to preserve semantic invariance through maxmal alterations when generating similar sentences, while demanding minimal alterations that induce significant semantic divergence for dissimilar sentence generation. This prompt optimization significantly improves the diversity and semantic discrimination of data sets, and increases the difficulty of data sets to a certain extent.

During the dataset construction process utilizing the generation pipeline, the curated terminology definitions (3,700 entries) were fed into the system. For each definition, the pipeline generated a corresponding similar sentence and a dissimilar sentence pair, with similar sentences labeled as 1 and dissimilar counterparts as 0. The labeling mechanism is directly governed by the prompt’s design logic, thereby eliminating the need for manual per-entry annotation and ensuring fully automated and consistent data construction. The generated text pairs were systematically organized into a standardized JSON format (as illustrated below) to streamline downstream analytical tasks and data processing.

The TDS dataset generation pipeline employs GPT-4’s API implementation, strategically adjusting temperature parameters, penalty configurations, and prompt engineering to optimize textual diversity and semantic relevance. This framework further integrates automated annotation mechanisms during data production, effectively eliminating labor-intensive manual labeling processes. The resultant output demonstrates high efficiency in generating a standardized terminology definition dataset characterized by semantic richness and consistent quality metrics.

Manual verification

Using the 3,700 sentences filtered through data cleansing as input texts, we employed the TDS dataset generation pipeline (Sect. 2.1.3) to produce one semantically positive example and one semantically negative example per sentence, yielding 7,400 sentence pairs. To ensure data quality, we performed manual sampling inspection by randomly selecting 30% of the 7,400 pairs for expert review by specialists with terminological and NLP expertise. Approximately 3% of the sampled data exhibited anomalies, which were subsequently corrected. Through rigorous quality control, we finalized a high-quality TDS dataset containing 7,400 short-text pairs.

Evaluation using BERT-base achieved an F1 score of 80.77% on the TDS dataset, underperforming compared to mainstream public benchmarks (BQ: 83.01%, LCQMC: 86.25%). This performance gap does not arise from data quality deficiencies, which have been validated through human evaluation. Instead, it reflects the dataset’s intentionally challenging design. The TDS dataset compels models to move beyond shallow statistical patterns by emphasizing deep semantic equivalence relations and minimizing surface-feature correlations. This requires a comprehensive understanding of terminological logic and domain knowledge. Experimental results demonstrate the dataset’s effectiveness in evaluating semantic reasoning capabilities in short-text similarity models.

Dataset information

The TDS dataset comprises 7,400 high-quality short-text pairs, with similar sentence pairs labeled as 1 and dissimilar pairs labeled as 0. Spanning multiple domains, the dataset ensures diversity and broad applicability. To support model training and evaluation, the dataset is divided into three subsets. 70% is used as the training set to learn patterns and features. 15% is reserved for the validation set, which is used to optimize hyperparameters, select the best models, and prevent overfitting. The remaining 15% constitutes the test set, which rigorously assesses the model’s generalization capability on fully independent data.

Models and methods

To address the limitations of Siamese BERT networks in terminological definition short-text similarity computation-specifically, insufficient semantic understanding precision and inadequate diversity in data samples-this study proposes the SDQKC model. Building on the Siamese BERT architecture, we introduce dynamic QK interactions to refine the attention mechanism and incorporate contrastive learning. The structure of the proposed model is illustrated in Fig. 2.

SDQKC Model.

The model processes two input sentences through a shared-parameter BERT encoder, generating sentence embeddings via self-attention mechanisms and positional encoding. Within the Transformer layers, dynamic QK parameters (Wgq, Wgk) are generated dynamically based on the input to each layer, meaning the parameters vary depending on the specific input received. These dynamic QK parameters interact with attention scores to perform secondary corrections, enabling more precise capture of inter-sentence semantic relationships. The detailed calculation process is presented in Sect. 4.2.

Subsequently, the model concatenates the representations of both sentences and computes the average of valid tokens within each sentence as the sentence-level representation. It then models pairwise similarity by calculating differences (e.g., absolute differences) between sentence pairs. A fully connected layer (MLP) maps these representations to the label space for classification tasks, with dropout applied to mitigate overfitting. Finally, similarity scores are computed via Softmax, yielding predicted labels.

Furthermore, the model incorporates a contrastive learning strategy to optimize the distribution of sentence pairs in the semantic space during training. This strategy pulls semantically similar sentences closer and pushes dissimilar ones farther apart in the vector space, thereby enhancing the model’s discriminative ability for textual semantics and enabling more precise similarity computation.

Sbert

The Siamese BERT architecture is a dual-branch neural network based on parameter sharing. The structure is illustrated in Fig. 3. Its core principle involves processing input sentence pairs sentence1 and sentence2 through weight-shared BERT encoders to extract context-sensitive semantic representations. After obtaining the BERT outputs, mean pooling is applied by averaging the embeddings of all valid tokens, resulting in holistic sentence representations denoted as vectors U and V respectively.

Sbert Structure.

To fully integrate features from both sentences, the architecture first concatenates the vectors [U, V] to preserve global sentence information, then calculates the element-wise absolute difference |U − V| to emphasize fine-grained distinctions between the sentences. Finally, the fused vector [U, V, |U − V|] is fed into a multilayer perceptron (MLP) to compute the similarity score.

Contrastive learning

Contrastive learning, a pivotal paradigm in unsupervised representation learning, has demonstrated significant performance advantages in natural language processing tasks such as semantic matching and text classification. Its core mechanism lies in constructing a discriminative semantic metric space: By designing positive-negative sample pairs, the framework drives encoders to differentiate inter-instance semantic similarities, thereby optimizing the geometric structure of feature distributions. Specifically, through optimization constraints imposed by contrastive loss functions, the model compels anchor samples to approximate cosine similarity extremes with positive samples in vector space while establishing distinct separation boundaries from negative counterparts. This discriminative contrastive training strategy not only mitigates the heavy reliance on annotated data inherent to traditional supervised learning but also enhances fine-grained text representation capabilities via implicit semantic alignment.

The model incorporates contrastive learning by assuming a batch contains N sample pairs {(a1, b1), (a2, b2) …. (an, bn)}, from these N pairs of samples, one positive pair is selected as the positive example (assuming (a1, b1) is the positive pair). Then, the selected positive sample a1 is combined with the other samples in the same batch, excluding b1, to construct negative examples.

We assume that a and b represent any constructed sentence pair. Specifically, they can be a positive sentence pair (e.g., a selected positive examples) or a negative sentence pair (e.g., a constructed negative examples). To further optimize this discriminative separation, the model employs contrastive loss to quantify differences between positive and negative samples. Experiment

Dataset

In addition to the TDS dataset, this chapter also uses a public dataset, BQ Corpus (Bank Question Corpus)20, to validate the effectiveness of our model. The BQ dataset contains Chinese question pairs designed for question matching tasks in the banking and finance domain. It is divided into training, validation, and test sets. Furthermore, we introduce the LCQMC dataset to evaluate the generalization capability of the proposed model by calculating its ACC and F1 scores.

Evaluation metrics

When selecting evaluation metrics, we referenced practices established in prior research. For instance, Ayetiran et al.21 proposed metrics such as AvgSimS and MaxSimS in their EDS-MEMBED model to evaluate the effectiveness of polysemous word representations. However, since our study focuses on sentence similarity tasks rather than word sense disambiguation, these metrics are not fully compatible with our objectives. Considering the nature of our task and following common practices in related studies17, we ultimately adopted five types of evaluation metrics widely used in similarity research: loss curves, F1-score, accuracy (ACC), Pearson correlation coefficient, and Spearman rank correlation coefficient.

The F1-score serves as a comprehensive metric combining precision (P) and recall (R), where P = number of correct predictions/total number of predictions, and R = number of correct predictions/total number of ground truth labels. Its calculation formula is as follows22:

The correlation coefficient is a statistical metric that measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables, with values ranging from − 1 to 1. A coefficient greater than 0 indicates a positive correlation, while a value less than 0 signifies a negative correlation. A coefficient of 0 implies no linear relationship. The closer the absolute value approaches 1, the stronger the correlation (either positive or negative). Among various correlation measures, the Pearson correlation coefficient and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient are two widely adopted methods. For model evaluation, we first generate vector representations of sentences using each candidate model. Next, cosine similarity is computed between sentence pairs based on these vectors. Finally, the computed similarity scores are compared against ground-truth labels to derive correlation coefficients, thereby quantifying alignment between model predictions and human annotations.

Experimental results on the TDS dataset demonstrate that traditional word embedding models such as Word2Vec, Glove, and FastText achieve Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients below 0.27, indicating that shallow semantic representations relying on word-frequency co-occurrence fail to capture sentence-level semantic relationships, particularly in complex semantic tasks.

In contrast, the BERT baseline model BERT-wwm average shows moderate improvements over traditional models but still underperforms, while BERT-wwm with direct [CLS] token usage exhibits notably poor results, revealing the inadequacy of [CLS]-based semantic representation. Sbert and its variants demonstrate superior sentence semantic representation capabilities. The Sentence-BERT model achieves significant performance gains through its Siamese network architecture, validating the effectiveness of sentence-level supervised training. The CNN-ATT-SBert model further enhances performance by integrating convolutional neural networks with attention mechanisms to optimize local-global feature fusion.

The SDQK model shows significant significant improvements in both Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients compared to Sentence-BERT and CNN-ATT-SBert. This indicates that dynamic QK scoring effectively enhances semantic discriminability and produces higher-quality sentence embeddings. It is worth noting that on the TDS dataset, the SDQKC model shows a slight decrease in the Spearman coefficient, while the Pearson coefficient increases. This is not a sign of model instability but results from the different focuses of the two metrics. Contrastive learning pulls positive pairs closer and pushes negative pairs farther apart, which strengthens the linear relation between predictions and true labels. As a result, the Pearson coefficient improves. However, during this process, the internal ranking of some pairs with similar similarity scores may change slightly, causing minor fluctuations in the Spearman coefficient, which measures the monotonic relationship. Overall, the core discriminative ability of the model is steadily enhanced.

At the same time, we also introduced the advanced general-purpose embedding model Qwen-Embedding for comparison. The TDS experiment results show that these large-scale pretrained general-purpose models perform well in short-text semantic discrimination tasks. They significantly outperform traditional baseline models, demonstrating the generalizability and strong capability of large-scale pretraining with knowledge augmentation strategies in semantic modeling. However, despite their overall strong performance, our proposed SDQKC model achieves the highest Pearson coefficients on both datasets. This is achieved through domain-adaptive fine-tuning and the integration of dynamic attention and contrastive learning for fine-grained semantic modeling. These results indicate that when dealing with domain texts containing specialized terminology, our model shows unique adaptability and higher semantic precision.

The BQ and TDS datasets show similar trends in model performance. Traditional word embedding models exhibit clear limitations in short-text semantic discrimination tasks. In contrast, sentence-level semantic representation models, such as Sentence-BERT, demonstrate significant advantages, confirming the effectiveness of the Siamese network architecture in structuring the semantic space. Our proposed model further improves semantic understanding capabilities.

On the domain-specific TDS dataset, Sbert achieves an F1 of 79.05% and ACC of 80.56%. Introducing dynamic QK in SDQK raises the F1 to 82.45% and ACC to 83.33%, an increase of about 3.4 and 2.8% points over Sbert. Adding contrastive learning in SDQKC further improves the F1 to 84.06% and ACC to 85.05%, increasing by about 1.6 and 1.7 points over SDQK and about 5 points overall compared to Sbert. These changes indicate that dynamic QK drives the main performance jump, while contrastive learning steadily enhances discriminative ability, especially for recognizing fine-grained differences in term definitions.

A similar trend is observed on the general corpora BQ and LCQMC, although the magnitude differs. On BQ, Sbert achieves an F1 of 79.36%, SDQK reaches 83.56%, and SDQKC attains 84.82%, giving SDQKC an improvement of about 5.5 points over Sbert. Both precision and recall increase clearly from Sbert to SDQKC. For example, on BQ, P rises from 79.37% to 84.94% and R from 79.36% to 84.71%. This indicates that the improvements enhance both discriminative accuracy and coverage of true positives.

The experiments show that the dynamic QK mechanism provides the foundation for the performance jump. Contrastive learning acts as an amplifier and further improves the discriminative ability of the representation space. They enable SDQKC to achieve stable leading performance across all metrics on both domain-specific and general datasets.

Conclusion and future work

Text semantic similarity computation is an important task in natural language processing. In this paper, we propose an innovative model, SDQKC, built on the Sbert architecture. By introducing a dynamic QK mechanism to adjust attention scores, the model improves semantic representation and achieves more accurate semantic understanding. Contrastive Learning enhances the diversity of samples by introducing a large number of hard negative examples, thereby further improving the model’s semantic understanding capability. Importantly, the contribution of SDQKC lies not only in performance improvements but also in rethinking the parameterization process of attention. Compared with traditional Siamese BERT methods that enhance attention by stacking additional modules, the dynamic QK mechanism reconstructs the interaction between Q and K, enabling context-adaptive attention. This mechanism-level innovation ensures the model’s unique advantage in fine-grained semantic discrimination for short texts. In addition, we have created a high-quality TDS dataset, filling the current gap in the domain-specific dataset landscape, and conducted experiments on the TDS, BQ, and LCQMC datasets. Based on the experimental results from these datasets, our proposed model outperforms several traditional and state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating its effectiveness and generalizability. However, the current loss function used still has certain limitations in modeling semantic distances.

In future work, we plan to explore the following directions. First, we will integrate the SDQKC model with domain-specific pre-trained models such as Clinical BERT for Chinese and Patent BERT26 to enhance performance in vertical domains. Second, we will extend the task from monolingual to multilingual term alignment to evaluate the model’s cross-lingual semantic understanding capability. Finally, we will incorporate multimodal information27 beyond text to provide richer contextual signals for term similarity computation, further improving the model’s accuracy and robustness.

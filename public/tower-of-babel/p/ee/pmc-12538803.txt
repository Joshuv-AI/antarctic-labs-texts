Association between the Zhejiang University index and chronic diarrhea: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Dongzhi Hong, Bin Xi, Nannan Li, Xiaoxiao Shao, Beilei Fu, Xinyu He, Tingting Yan, Chenxing Wang, Hao Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic diarrhea is a widespread digestive symptom with unclear etiology. The association between metabolic disorders and chronic diarrhea remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the association between the Zhejiang University (ZJU) index, a recently developed metabolic index that provides a more comprehensive assessment of metabolic disorders, and chronic diarrhea.MethodsThe study included 6,224 participants who completed the chronic diarrhea questionnaire in the 2005–2010 cycles of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. The ZJU index was sex-specific and was calculated based on fasting plasma glucose, triglycerides, body mass index, alanine aminotransferase, and aspartate transaminase. Chronic diarrhea was assessed based on the Bristol Stool Form Scale. We used multivariable weighted logistic regression to estimate the odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea. Non-linear associations were evaluated using restricted cubic splines. Moreover, subgroup and sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of the findings.ResultsThe prevalence of chronic diarrhea was 6.72% (95% CI: 5.93%; 7.50%). Each 10-unit increase in the ZJU index was associated with higher odds of chronic diarrhea (OR: 1.37, 95% CI: 1.20; 1.55, P < 0.001). Participants in the highest quartile of the ZJU index had higher odds of chronic diarrhea than those in the lowest quartile of the ZJU index (OR: 2.02, 95% CI: 1.37; 2.96, P = 0.002). Subgroup analysis revealed a higher odds of chronic diarrhea among non-Hispanic whites.ConclusionsHigher ZJU index is associated with increased odds of chronic diarrhea, underscoring the importance of maintaining normal blood glucose, lipid levels, and body mass index to reduce the risk.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03287-8.Keywords: ZJU index, Chronic diarrhea, Metabolism, NHANES, Public Health

Introduction

Chronic diarrhea is a common gastrointestinal symptom with a complex etiology, affecting an estimated 3–20% of the global population [1–4]. The prevalence of chronic diarrhea is increasing, posing a significant public health challenge. In the United States, approximately 6.6% of the population suffers from this condition [5, 6], making it the second most common gastrointestinal symptom for which patients seek medical care [7]. In addition, chronic diarrhea adversely affects physical and mental health and imposes a substantial financial burden on society and healthcare systems [4, 7].

Although frequently overlooked, chronic diarrhea is associated with numerous risk factors [8]. For example, it is closely associated with metabolic diseases [5, 9–14]. Moreover, two studies reported that patients with obesity are at an increased risk of developing diarrhea [5, 10]. Evidence also exists linking non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) to chronic diarrhea [11, 12]. In addition, two observational studies have indicated that patients with diabetes are more likely to experience chronic diarrhea [13, 14]. However, a study from South Korea found that this association was not significant [15]. Given the evident connection between chronic diarrhea and metabolic diseases, identifying a robust indicator to reflect metabolic disorders and disease risk is crucial.

The Zhejiang University (ZJU) index is a new sex-specific metabolic index based on fasting plasma glucose (FPG), triglycerides (TG), body mass index (BMI), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate transaminase (AST) [16]. This novel index is a sensitive measurement, outperforming individual markers such as TG and BMI in identifying metabolic disorders [17]. The ZJU index was initially developed to predict the risk of NAFLD, and more recently, it has been reported as a useful tool for predicting diabetes and sarcopenia [16, 18, 19]. Notably, chronic diarrhea has been suggested to be closely related to blood sugar level, BMI, and NAFLD [5, 10–14, 20]. However, the combined effects of blood glucose, obesity, and liver function on chronic diarrhea remain unknown.

Therefore, using the data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), we aimed to examine the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea.

Methods

Data and study participants

This research utilized a cross-sectional approach, drawing on data from the NHANES, which is administered by the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS). These data are a valuable resource for understanding the health and nutritional status of the US population. They are widely used in health policy development, disease prevention strategies, and public health evaluations. Further details can be found in previous studies [21]. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations, and all participants provided written informed consent. To ensure participant confidentiality, the NHANES database anonymizes all participant information.

The data used in this study were obtained from the 2005 to 2010 NHANES cross-sectional cycles, which included 31,034 participants aged ≥ 20 years. After excluding individuals with missing data on BMI, FPG, TG, ALT, and AST (n = 22,418) and those lacking the Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) or weekly bowel movement frequency (n = 2,392), a final sample of 6,224 eligible participants was included in the analysis (Fig. 1).

Assessment of the ZJU index

The ZJU index was calculated using the following formula: ZJUindex=FPGmmol/L+TGmmol/L+BMIkg/m2+3∗ALTU/LASTU/L+2(iffemale). The laboratory testing methods used in this study, including blood specimen collection and biochemical assays, are consistent with the standard NHANES protocols and can be found in the NHANES Laboratory Procedures Manual [22, 23].

Ascertainment of chronic diarrhea

Consistent with the previous studies, chronic diarrhea in our study was defined using the BSFS [5, 24]. Participants were presented with the BSFS, a widely used tool for assessing chronic diarrhea, and asked to identify the number corresponding to their typical stool type. Those who responded with type 6 (fluffy pieces with ragged edges, mushy stool) or type 7 (watery, no solid pieces) were classified as having chronic diarrhea [25].

Covariates

Sociodemographic characteristics, health-related behaviors, and major chronic diseases were included as covariates. Sociodemographic variables comprised age, sex, race, marital status, and educational level. Health-related behaviors encompassed drinking and smoking status, nutritional intake (fiber, carbohydrates, fats, proteins, and sugars), and total calorie intake from the initial day of the 24-h dietary recall. Major chronic diseases encompassed hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Supplementary Table 1 presents the specific categorization methods.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized according to chronic diarrhea and ZJU index groups. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the Student’s t test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages) and were analyzed using chi-square tests. We used the Wald method to calculate the weighted prevalence and corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI).

Our analyses accounted for the complex sampling design and weights, in accordance with NHANES analytic guidelines. Multiple imputations were performed for missing covariate values (Supplementary Table 2 provides the proportion of missing data and details of the multiple imputation process). Weighted multivariable logistic regression with four models was used to estimate the odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs for the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea. Model 1 accounted for sociodemographic characteristics. Model 2 was built on Model 1 by adjusting for drinking and smoking status. Model 3 was further adjusted for major chronic diseases, while Model 4 extended these adjustments to include nutrient and total calorie intake. A trend test was conducted by categorizing the ZJU index into quartiles. Moreover, restricted cubic spline (RCS) models with three knots were used to evaluate non-linear associations between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea. To facilitate the interpretation of the strength of associations reported in this study, we included a standard classification of ORs based on the previous literature[26] (Supplementary Table 3).

Subgroup analyses based on sex, race, marital status, education, smoking status, drinking status, hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease were performed to explore whether the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea differed across subpopulations. Interaction effects were assessed using analysis of variance to compare regression models with and without interaction terms [27]. Three sensitivity analyses were performed to examine the robustness of the results, as follows: (1) individuals with over 21 bowel movements per week or a BSFS type of 6 or 7 were classified as having diarrhea; (2) participants with constipation who responded as type 1 (separate hard lumps such as nuts) or type 2 (sausage-like but lumpy) on the BSFS scale [28] were excluded; and (3) participants with missing covariates were excluded to assess the potential impact of multiple imputations on the results.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.2; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) and Zstats v1.0 (www.zstats.net), with a two-sided P value < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among the 6,224 participants included in this study, 48.94% were men, with a mean age of 49.38 ± 17.85 years and an average ZJU index of 40.47 ± 8.02. Table 1 provides the baseline characteristics of participants by chronic diarrhea. Among them, the prevalence of chronic diarrhea was 6.72% (95% CI: 5.93%; 7.50%, weighted). Participants with chronic diarrhea had higher ZJU index levels, were older, more likely to be female, of Mexican American or other Hispanic descent, had a high school education or below, abstained from alcohol, and exhibited higher rates of diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease than those without chronic diarrhea. Participants with chronic diarrhea also reported lower fat intake than those without chronic diarrhea. Supplementary Table 4 presents the basic characteristics of the ZJU index quartiles.

Association between ZJU index and chronic diarrhea

Table 2 presents the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea. After full adjustment for covariates, each 10-unit increase in the ZJU index was associated with a 37% increase in the odds of chronic diarrhea (OR: 1.37, 95% CI: 1.20; 1.55). When divided into quartiles, participants in the highest quartile had significantly higher odds of chronic diarrhea compared to those in the lowest quartile (OR: 2.02, 95% CI: 1.37; 2.96, P-trend < 0.001). As illustrated in Fig. 2, an RCS analysis showed that the odds of chronic diarrhea increased linearly with higher ZJU index values. However, no evidence of a non-linear association was observed.

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analyses were conducted based on sex, race, marital status, educational level, drinking status, smoking status, diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease to investigate the associations between ZJU index and chronic diarrhea in different sub-populations (Table 3). The interaction analysis revealed that the ZJU index was associated with higher odds of chronic diarrhea among non-Hispanic White participants (OR: 1.53, 95% CI: 1.29; 1.81, P-interaction = 0.011). Subgroup analyses revealed similar associations across other sub-populations, with no significant interactions observed.

Sensitivity analyses

When participants with more than 21 bowel movements per week or a BSFS type of 6 or 7 were classified as having diarrhea (n = 522), a significant association was found between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea (OR: 1.41, 95% CI: 1.25; 1.58; Supplementary Table 5). Moreover, participants in the high ZJU index group had higher odds of chronic diarrhea than those in the low ZJU index group (OR: 2.10, 95% CI: 1.46; 3.00). Similar results were observed after excluding patients with constipation, as presented in Supplementary Table 6. After excluding participants with constipation, defined as those reporting Type 1 or Type 2 on the BSFS (n = 464), the significant association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea persisted (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.23; 1.56). Participants with a high ZJU index had higher odds of chronic diarrhea than those with a low ZJU index (OR: 2.01, 95% CI: 1.41; 2.86). Finally, after excluding participants with missing covariates to assess the potential impact of multiple imputations on the results (n = 853), the previously observed associations between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea were reaffirmed (OR: 1.31, 95% CI: 1.15; 1.50; OR: 1.74, 95% CI: 1.17; 2.59; Supplementary Table 7).

Discussion

This study involving 6,224 participants found a significant association between a higher ZJU index and increased odds of chronic diarrhea. A positive linear association was observed, with individuals in the highest quartile of the ZJU index having a 102% greater odds of chronic diarrhea. In addition, the interaction effect revealed that an increase in the ZJU index was associated with an elevated risk of chronic diarrhea among non-Hispanic Whites.

Previous epidemiological studies conducted in the Japanese population have examined numerous risk factors associated with chronic diarrhea [8]. A recent US cross-sectional study indicated a significantly increased prevalence of chronic diarrhea in patients with diabetes [13]. This aligns with our findings, which may be attributed to long-term high blood glucose levels impairing intestinal nerve function, potentially causing intestinal dysfunction and flora imbalance [20]. Moreover, high blood glucose levels reportedly increase the risk of intestinal infections by damaging the intestinal barrier. High blood sugar levels are also believed to act on intestinal epithelial cells through the bidirectional glucose transporter GLU2. This, in turn, induces changes in the expression levels of metabolism-related genes [29]. In addition, an NHANES-based study found that a high BMI is associated with chronic diarrhea [30]. In individuals with a high BMI, intestinal compliance decreases while the pain threshold increases, potentially leading to the rapid passage of intestinal contents and, ultimately, diarrhea [31–33]. Three recently published cross-sectional studies based on the US population have also indicated that obesity is an important factor in chronic diarrhea [5, 34, 35]. Furthermore, a retrospective study demonstrated an association between NAFLD and chronic diarrhea, with patients with NAFLD being more prone to chronic diarrhea [36]. This may be attributed to the close relationship between NAFLD and bile acid diarrhea and the fact that this process involves the functional regulation of fibroblast growth factor 19 (FGF19). FGF19, secreted by the ileum, inhibits bile acid production in the liver and regulates its reabsorption [37]. Low FGF19 levels reduce this inhibitory effect, leading to excessive bile acid synthesis. Excess bile acids in the colon promote water and electrolyte secretion, resulting in diarrhea [38]. FGF19 also regulates bile acid metabolism and influences carbohydrate and lipid metabolism. Increased bile acid synthesis caused by low levels of FGF19 may affect metabolic balance in the liver, thereby promoting NAFLD [37, 39]. In our study, a higher prevalence of chronic diarrhea was observed among female participants. Although the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea did not significantly differ by sex, the higher prevalence observed in females may reflect sex-specific physiological factors. Estrogen has been reported to affect gastrointestinal motility and visceral sensitivity through both direct receptor-mediated pathways and indirect modulation of other signaling systems, which may contribute to altered bowel function in women and partially explain the observed disparity [40]. Previous study on insulin resistance and its impact on chronic gastrointestinal health has compared various metabolic indices, including the triglyceride-glucose index and its derivatives [41]. The study found that triglyceride-glucose adjusted for body mass index was the most effective predictor of early-onset diarrhea, suggesting a closer association between BMI and diarrhea. However, the ZJU index provides a more comprehensive approach by integrating BMI, FPG, TG, ALT, and AST, while also accounting for sex-specific differences [16]. Notably, the ZJU index was originally developed to predict NAFLD in Chinese adults and has since been used to predict the occurrence of diabetes [18]. This multidimensional approach allows the ZJU index to offer a more holistic measure of the metabolic factors associated with chronic diarrhea. In contrast to the triglyceride-glucose index, which primarily focuses on insulin resistance and BMI, the ZJU index encompasses a broader range of metabolic indicators, potentially providing a more robust and clinically relevant tool for predicting chronic diarrhea. The studies and mechanisms described above suggest that chronic diarrhea is strongly associated with blood glucose levels, BMI, and NAFLD. The strength of the association observed in our study was weak to moderate, with participants in the highest quartile of the ZJU index exhibiting higher odds of chronic diarrhea as compared to those in the lowest quartile (OR: 2.02, 95% CI: 1.37; 2.96). The relatively narrow confidence intervals suggest that this finding is both statistically significant, highlighting a robust association between elevated ZJU index levels and chronic diarrhea. However, some subgroups presented with wider confidence intervals, reflecting potential variability and reduced certainty. These findings underscore the need for cautious interpretation in subgroup analyses and highlight the importance of conducting more comprehensive studies in the future to explore different subgroups.

Although previous studies have examined various risk factors for chronic diarrhea [4, 8], few have assessed the relationship between disorders of glucose and lipid metabolism and chronic diarrhea. Our study is unique in its comprehensive analysis of metabolic indicators, including blood glucose, lipids, obesity, and liver injury. This approach offers valuable insights into preventing and treating chronic diarrhea from a metabolic perspective. Our findings highlight the ZJU index as a risk factor for chronic diarrhea. Our study offers a theoretical basis for improving the treatment of chronic diarrhea caused by disorders of glucose and lipid metabolism, as well as a means of identifying disease burden in patients with diabetes mellitus and NAFLD with comorbid diarrhea. From a public health perspective, these findings have significant implications, particularly for the U.S. healthcare system. Over recent decades, gastrointestinal disorders have constituted a substantial proportion of healthcare utilization and expenditure, primarily owing to the increasing prevalence of unhealthy dietary habits. The incidence and associated costs of chronic diarrhea have also increased over time [7, 42, 43]. Our findings support the utilization of the ZJU index by health workers for the early identification of individuals who are prone to developing chronic diarrhea. Patients should be advised to maintain optimal levels of blood glucose, lipids, and body weight through a healthy lifestyle to address glucose and lipid metabolism disorders. While disorders of glycolipid metabolism are crucial in the pathogenesis of chronic diarrhea, the underlying mechanisms of chronic diarrhea remain unclear and require further investigation.

This study has several strengths. First, national-level data were employed to investigate the relationship between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea using a large sample size. Concurrently, we employed a sophisticated multistage probability sampling methodology to ensure that our findings are both representative and generalizable. We employed a series of sensitivity analyses to guarantee the robustness of our findings. Nevertheless, this study had some limitations. First, chronic diarrhea was defined based on self-reported questionnaires, potentially introducing reporting bias. Second, the NHANES database does not include information on the specific causes of chronic diarrhea, which limits our ability to perform further analysis on these potential underlying factors. The future research should explore the association between the ZJU index and specific causes of chronic diarrhea, including IBD and functional bowel disorders. Finally, the cross-sectional design of this study limits the ability to infer causality. Future longitudinal research is required to explore the association between the ZJU index and chronic diarrhea, focusing on elucidating whether chronic diarrhea is a consequence of obesity, diabetes, or NAFLD. Clinical studies are also essential to gain insights into the predictive value and utility of the ZJU index.

Conclusions

Our study underscores that a higher ZJU index is associated with higher odds of chronic diarrhea. Maintaining healthy blood glucose and lipid levels, along with a healthy body weight, may reduce the risk of chronic diarrhea, highlighting the importance of metabolic regulation in preventing this condition.

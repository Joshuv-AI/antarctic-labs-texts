P116 A retrospective review of polysomnography results in children referred for suspected obstructive sleep apnoea
Authors: C Riach, R Willink, L Teoh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPaediatric obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), if left untreated, can lead to significant morbidity. Adenotonsillar hypertrophy is the most common risk factor, but obese children are also at risk of OSA. Many children undergo adenotonsillectomy without having a prior diagnostic polysomnogram (PSG). We aim to identify risk factors which may predispose to OSA in our referred paediatric cohort.MethodsThis was a retrospective review of children who underwent PSG between Feb 2022-Jan 2024. For categorical variables [sex, ethnicity, co-morbidities (atopy/neuromuscular disease/syndromes)], the primary outcome was OSA (AHI≥1). For continuous variables (age, BMI, OSA-18 score, tonsillar grade), the outcome was Apnoea-Hypopnoea Index (AHI). Five patient groups were considered: full cohort, surgery/no surgery, diagnostic/titration PSG. We obtained p-values for two-sided tests of association between 10 variables and corresponding outcomes, giving 50 tests with critical p-values of 0.05/50=0.0010.ResultsNinety-six studies were conducted in patients with a median age 7.6 years, range 0.3-16.4. (Diagnostic:Titration, 72:24; Male:Female, 65:31; European:Māori/Pasifika, 55:36). Median BMI was 18.6, range 11.7-57.1. For diagnostic studies (median AHI 2.8/hour, range 0-132.3), a positive association between BMI and AHI is demonstrated (p=0.0001, Kendall τ=0.32, 95% CI 0.16-0.46). The association is stronger when patients with neuromuscular disease/syndromes (28/96) are excluded (p=0.00002, Kendall τ=0.41, 95% CI 0.24-0.56).Discussion/ConclusionIn children referred for a diagnostic PSG, AHI tends to increase with BMI. Screening for OSA symptoms should be considered in children with a high BMI and a diagnostic PSG performed.

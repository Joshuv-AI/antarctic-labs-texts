Magnesium-containing implants enhance bone healing: A mechanobiological perspective
Authors: Zhenkang Wen, Lei Lei, Haozhi Zhang, Zheyu Jin, Zhengming Shan, Weiyang Liu, Wenxue Tong, Jiankun Xu, Ling Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Unlike traditional implants primarily composed of bioinert materials, magnesium (Mg) -a degradable biomaterial - offers significant promise for next-generation bone healing implants, whether utilized as a primary structural component or a supporting material. While most research focuses on Mg's bioactive and osteoimmunological effect, this review highlights its mechanobiological role, summarizing the merits of Mg-containing implants in facilitating mechanotransduction and associated cellular events during the bone healing. Beyond introducing Mg's biomechanical benefits in preventing stress shielding, this review synthesizes its unique attributes: exceptional bone-implant integration and synergistic effects with physical stimuli to amplify new bone formation. Crucially, we also summarize the activation of mechanotransduction signaling pathways, providing a mechanistic basis for Mg's positive mechanobiological influence. Finally, we discuss challenges arising from the interaction between physical loading and Mg degradation, alongside future perspectives and potential solutions to bridge the gap between theory and clinical application, thereby accelerating translation applications of Mg-containing implants.

Introduction

Bone, a connective tissue comprising trabecular and cortical regions, features a robust mineralized matrix deposition.1 Beyond providing exceptional mechanical strength functions for locomotion and support, bone requires exposure to mechanical stimuli including fluid shear stress, compression, and tensile strain, for its regeneration, homeostasis, and intrinsic development.2 Skeletal cells (osteoblasts, osteoclasts osteocytes, and progenitor cells) and adjacent tissue cells (e.g. endothelial cells, myocytes, fibroblasts) sense these stimuli. Mechanical cues activate channels via membrane deformation or cytoskeletal transduction, triggering signaling cascades that converting physical stimuli into biological signals. This process, termed mechanotransduction,3, 4, 5, 6, 7 modulates cellular behavior to adapt to the local mechanical environment. While bone possesses regenerative capacity, functional and structural restoration of fractures necessitates appropriate physical stimulation and fixation.8,9 The intrinsic healing process involving hematoma formation, inflammatory phase, callus formation, and remodeling phase, each exhibits distinct biomechanics and mechanosensitivities.10 Consequently, researchers are developing personalized treatments that leverage the biological effects of dynamic mechanical stimuli (varying in intensities and duration) to enhance bone regeneration.

Fractures involving instability, displacement, or severe soft tissue injury, require transient or prolonged implantation of plates, screws, nails, or pins for anatomical alignment and stabilization. Traditional implants, typically composed of non-degradable materials like stainless steel, titanium (Ti), cobalt-chromium (Co-Cr) alloys, are designed for excellent biocompatibility, mechanical strength, fatigue resistance, corrosion resistance, and surface properties to integrate with native bone and facilitate mechanotransduction.11 However, their non-degradability and bioinertness lead to significant limitations: stress shielding, inadequate osseointegration, and the frequent necessity for secondary removal surgery.12 Furthermore, their limited bioactivity poses challenges in pathological conditions such as osteoporotic fracture, osteonecrosis, bisphosphonates-related atypical femoral fracture (AFF), and diabetic settings. In these cases, deteriorated skeletal cell activity and impaired neurovascularization increase the risk of surgical failure.

In relation to the mechanical properties, implants are expected to match the surrounding bone tissue while maintain their mechanical support, which not only facilitates the mechanotransduction for new bone formation but also ensures the smooth transfer of physical loading.13 While the stiffness of implants (such as the traditional Ti and Co-Cr alloys) is far excessive, the loading stress is mainly concentrated on implants, depriving surrounding bone of appropriate mechanical stimulation.14 This phenomenon is known as “stress shielding”, attributing to enhanced bone resorption and reduced new bone formation for poor osseointegration and compromise healing outcomes. Similarly, inferior mechanical properties of implants (such as polylactic acid screws) lead to maximum stress distribution on adjacent bone, which risks to hinder bone growth and leads to microfracture.15 Therefore, innovative implants with mechanical properties comparable to bone tissue is crucial to optimize their mechanobiological performance and achieve better healing outcomes.

Magnesium (Mg), as a biodegradable metal with excellent biocompatibility and a Young's modulus akin to the cortical bone, has emerged as a transformative material for implant engineering in recent years. Its appeal lies in the ability to reprogram the local microenvironment to potentiate bone formation. Clinical evidence underscores it promise: a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that Mg screws in patients with femoral head osteonecrosis provided superior blood supply and firmer fixation with a lower risk of loosening compared to titanium (Ti) screws.16 Furthermore, a case report documented successful elbow fracture healing within three months using Mg screws, with no surgical failures orcomplications.17

Beyond obviating the need for surgical removal surgery, the degradation products of Mg-containing implants (Mg ions (Mg2+), hydroxide ions and hydrogen gas) exert direct biological effects that promote new bone formation and the coupled neovascularization.18,19 For instance, following the insertion of the Mg intramedullary nails, robust new bone formation was observed alongside increased neuronal production of calcitonin gene-related polypeptide-α (CGRP) in both the femoral cortex and the ipsilateral dorsal root ganglia (DRG). This CGRP release was shown to reinforce the osteogenesis of the periosteum-derived stem cells.20 Similarly, An et al. discovered that the peri-implant hydrogen gas and alkaline environment synergistically enhanced the proliferation of periosteum-derived stem cells via the sensory neuronal pathway.21

Consequently, engineering efforts for next-generation implants are increasingly focused on the degradable materials, with Mg as a leading candidate. Mg-containing implants reduce stress shielding

Traditional non-degradable materials, such as Ti and cobalt-chromium (Cd-Co) alloys, are favored for skeletal implants due to their excellent mechanical properties, biocompatibility, corrosion resistance and long-term stability. These bioinert materials provide reliable mechanical support and structural fixation to facilitate new bone formation. However, they exhibit a significant drawback: high stiffness. The elastic moduli of the Ti alloys, stainless steel, and cobalt alloys (∼110 ​GPa, 200 ​GPa, and 230 ​GPa, respectively) are substantially higher than that of cortical bone.22,23 Cortical bone, with a porosity of <30 ​% and a density close to that of aluminum (Al) or Mg (approx. 1.85 ​g/cm3), is far less rigid.24,25 This mechanical mismatch leads to a phenomenon known as stress shielding. The excessively stiff implant bears the majority of the mechanical load, creating a concentrated stress distribution within the implant itself. Consequently, the surrounding bone tissue is deprived of the physiological loading essential for mechanotransduction. This deficiency in mechanical stimuli hinders the normal healing process, resulting in impaired callus formation, reduced ossification, and bone non-union. The mechanobiology underlying this failure involves an upregulation of bone resorption. Stress shielding has been shown to nearly double osteoclast numbers and RANKL expression while reducing OPG production.26, 27, 28 This imbalance exacerbates cortical bone osteoporosis and lowers bone mineral density. The resulting implant-induced bone loss affects not only the fracture site but the entire bone structure, increasing the risk of implant loosening and secondary fracture following removal.29

To prevent stress concentration on implants, the mechanical properties must closely match those of the adjacent bone to avoid a load-bearing mismatch. Mg-based metals are considered innovative implant materials due to their high tensile strength (200 ​MPa) and an excellent elastic modulus (∼45 ​GPa) that is remarkably similar to cortical bone (15–30 Gpa).22,30 This compatibility is demonstrated by finite element analyses and clinical observations. For instance, Luo et al. reported that Mg screws transfer approximately 50 ​% more stress to the surrounding bone tissue than Ti screws.31 Similarly, the stress shielding ratio for bone fixed by Mg plate and screws was calculated to be 1.52, lower than titanium (1.98) or stainless steel (3.82).32 In an ankle fracture model, Mg screw fixation resulted in a total deformation of 0.1275 ​mm and the maximum von Mises stress of 68.351 ​MPa. Crucially, the maximum stress on the Mg screws and themselves was 59.5 ​MPa, below both the stress on Ti screws and the failure threshold for the implant. These favorable mechanical conditions correlated with improved biological outcomes, including a reduced inflammation index at day 3 post-surgery and excellent wound healing and functional recovery in all cases.33 While the degradable polylactic acid (PLA) screws are clinically approved for bone fixation such as anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction, their insufficient mechanical strength (Young's modulus about 2–4 Gpa) leads to stress concentration around bone tunnel, which can be prevented by the replacement of high-purity Mg screws for significant peri-implant bone growth and lower risks of trabecular microfractures.15,34,35

The beneficial effect of eliminating stress shielding also extends to Mg-coated alloys. In a proximal femur fracture model, a biodegradable Mg alloy bionic Gamma nail (Young's modulus ∼45 ​GPa) reduced the maximum stress (18.7 ​MPa versus 32 Mpa), the minimum stress (−12.6 ​MPa versus −23.5 Mpa), and the von Mises stress (14.0 ​MPa versus 31.3 ​MPa) compared to the traditional implants.36 Furthermore, commercial alloys like MgYREZr, Mg-Zn-Y-Al, and LAE442 exhibit a similarly Young's modulus, underscoring their broad potential to mitigate stress shielding and promote bone formation.37, 38, 39 Therefore, whether used as a supportive core or an additive coating, Mg efficiently attenuates stress shielding to create an optimal mechanotransduction for bone healing (Fig. 2. Mg-containing implants enhance osseointegration

The significance of osseointegration

In addition to stress shielding, another challenge presented by traditional implants is the unsatisfying osseointegration owing to their bioinert nature. “Osseointegration”, a term first described by Branemark, describes the structural and functional connection between an implant and native bone, a concept that extends beyond simple bone growth.40,41 Its defining criteria include implant stability under functional loading without micromotion, and new bone or marrow deposition directly on the implant surface without the intervention of fibrotic tissue or connective tissue.42,43 Failure to achieve osseointegration - a well-documented cause of peri-prosthetic aseptic loosening in arthroplasty - increases the risk of fracture, post-surgical pain and infection.44, 45, 46, 47, 48 This failure induces significant micromotion at the bone-implant interface under loading. The excessive micromotion at the first week post-surgery is a primary inhibitor of bone ingrowth,49, 50, 51 while persistent micromotion (typically 50–150 ​μm) can provoke chronic inflammation and bone resorption, leading to osteolysis and early symptomatic loosening.51, 52, 53, 54, 55, 56

Mechanisms of Mg implants in promoting osseointegration

Establishing stable bone-implant contact hinges on osteoinduction and osteoconduction, processes heavily influenced by implant composition.57,58 Traditional bioinert implants achieves only a physical connection, lacking biochemical binding to the surrounding bone tissue.59 In contrast, Mg-containing implants, actively promote osseointegration through multiple mechanisms: they accelerate osteogenic differentiation, mineralization and neurovascularization; inhibit bone resorption, and provide immunomodulation.20,60, 61, 62 Evidence for enhanced osseointegration with Mg is substantial. During the corrosion of Mg-Ca-Zn alloys, a biomimetic mineralized layer formed on the degrading outer surface, fostering integration.63 In physiological solutions, Mg pins developed a calcium phosphate-rich corrosion layer. The expansion of this layer, coupled with new hard tissue generation, mechanically locked the implant in place, increasing its pull-out force.64 Surfaces modified with Mg ions (such as vacuum arc implantation) or Mg-doped coatings promote initial cell adhesion, osteoblast differentiation, ALP activity, and calcium (Ca) accumulation.65,66 Notably, even on the Ti implants with identical surface morphology, the addition of Ca-Mg enhances bone organization on the implant surface.67

The pro-osseointegration effects of Mg are also mediated by other biological interactions. For instance, increased innervation featuring local accumulation of neuronal CGRP can be detected after Mg alloys implantation, which activates the Protein Kinase A (PKA)/FAK signaling pathway in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) to drive osteogenesis.20,68 Furthermore, Mg alloys exhibit potent immunomodulatory effects by promoting M2 macrophage polarization and the secretion of anti-inflammatory cytokines.69,70 This modulates the local immune environment, curbing excessive inflammation and orchestrating a regenerative microenvironment conductive to the subperiosteal bone formation and neovascularization, thereby significantly strengthening the bone-implant interface71,72 (Fig. 2. C, D).

Furthermore, the features of the implant surface (e.g., roughness, wettability and hydrophilicity) and the bioactivity surface modifications are critical for directing cell migration, adhesion, and differentiation.49,73,74 Consequently, strategic modification of the Mg-containing alloys can optimize both their degradation kinesis and bone-implant integration. For example, micro-arc oxidation (MAO), also known as plasma electrolytic oxidation (PEO), can be applied to create a micro/nano-porous coating on the Mg alloys. This layer enhances structural integrity, improves corrosion resistance, and promotes cell adhesion, proliferation, osteogenic differentiation, and angiogenesis.75, 76, 77 Studies by Rendenbach and Berger, demonstrated that PEO-treated WE43 Mg screws and plates exhibit superior osteoconductivity, increased osseointegration, and a more controlled degradation rate over the first six months compared to their unmodified counterparts.78,79 Nanospiked modification of Mg or the surface coating of tantalum/Poly(ether imide) exhibits superior osseointegration as well as controlled degradability and corrosion resistance, showing promising orthopedic implant applications.80, 81, 82, 83

The importance of controlled degradation and initial fixation

A firm initial fixation is a prerequisite for successful osseointegration and eventually functional restoration. While the degradable nature of Mg eliminates the need for surgical removal surgery, its degradation must be carefully controlled. In the critical early post-operative phase, characterized by an inflammatory environment and low pH that can accelerate corrosion, the implant must retain sufficient mechanical strength to facilitate callus formation and maturation.84,85 Subsequently, as hard callus maturation and remodeling commence (around 1.5–2 months), the implant's corrosion should progress in tandem with new bone formation, creating complementary trajectories where Mg erosion gradually makes space for regenerating new bone without inducing a shielding effect.12,86,87 Importantly, Mg-containing implants maintain their osteointegrative efficacy even in challenging pathological conditions like osteoporosis and diabetes (Fig. 3).88 In osteoporotic rats, Mg-loaded Ti screws stimulated robust new bone formation on their surfaces within the first week post-implantation, a process associated with BMP6 upregulation driven by the release of Mg2+.89 Similarly, MgO-coated implants have been shown to counteract diabetes-induced endothelial dysfunction and promoted osseointegration during alveolar bone healing.90 Atypical femoral fracture (AFF), one of the potential complications of long-term bisphosphonates (BPs) therapy, refers to a type of a low-energy fracture specifically in the subtrochanteric region and the diaphyseal region.91 On account of prolonged BPs administration, the impaired angiogenesis and inhibited osteoclasts activities damage bone remodeling and induce microcracks, compromising the mechanical properties of cortical bones for low-stress induced fractures. Consequently, nearly half of the patients suffered from persistent pain, limited functional recovery, delayed healing and even non-union.92,93 Compared to the traditional implants, the fixation of Mg-containing intramedullary nails promoted the healing of BPs-induced AFF and fibrosis through the synthesis and release of CGRP, showcasing their translational potential in AFF healing.94Fig. 3The effects of Mg-modified intramedullary nails on bone healing. In conclusion, Mg-containing implants with the controlled degradation profiles offer a unique dual advantage: they provide crucial mechanical support in the early stages for healing while simultaneously fostering a biochemical environment conductive to strong and stable osseointegration.

Mg-containing implants and physical stimuli synergistically enhance bone healing

The skeleton requires mechanical signals for its development, homeostasis maintenance and regeneration, which involve the dynamic regulation of multiple cell type. The appropriate mechanical stimuli were critical for the activation of bone formation and bone-implant contact during the bone healing progression.58,95 Under the unloading conditions or the diminishment of gravity, aberrant behaviors of skeletal cells can be detected including increasing number and activity of osteoclasts, impaired function of osteoblasts, compromised osteogenic potential of progenitor cells, and the induction of osteocyte apoptosis. Concomitant with decreased serum 1,25(OH)2-vitamin D parathyroid hormone (PTH) but escalated N-telopeptide, the loss of bone mass and mineralization can further induce osteoporosis and secondary fracture.96, 97, 98, 99, 100, 101 In this part, the influence of Mg-containing implant treatment and physical stimuli on the bone healing process is discussed.

Micromotion

During the bone healing, although a rigid fixation and transient immobilization in the early stage produce low strains to endow the functional or structure alignment for the primary formation of callus, a flexible fixation converts high strains and initiates the generation of cartilaginous callus or secondary bone healing through the controlled micromotion.14,102,103 By offering a moderately stressful environment, the osteogenic activity of stem cells and bone formation-associated proteins including Osterix and type I collagen are boosted, which is termed elastic osteosynthesis.104,105 Therefore, unlocked fixation systems including dynamic locking screws, biodegradable screws, active intramedullary nails and active locking plates emerge to harness the merits of controlled micromotion for bone fracture healing.106,107 In this case, the incorporation of the degradable Mg is preferrable for the unlocked fixation. Compared to the axial micromotion locking plates, the Mg-containing variable fixation plates could achieve a dynamic balance featuring the stable fixation in the early stage, and the axial micromotion fixation in the late stage, which facilitated the new bone formation and the healing strength in the fracture gap.108 Similarly, the engagement of bioresorbable Mg in elastic stable intramedullary nails enhanced callus formation in the early stage and accelerated the late-stage mineralization, increasing the total bone-tissue volume and high-density bone volume.109,110 Rössig et al. had underscored that the application of LAE442 Mg-based intramedullary interlocked nailing system in the sheep model oriented the direct bone-to-implant interface. In particular, the formation of mature cortical and cancellous bone with moderate gas formation and minor inflammatory infiltration were detected.111 Notably, a wide range of micromotion is inappropriate for bone healing as mentioned. Therefore, the application of Mg-containing implants ought to avoid the sole pursuit of degradation-associated benefits but prioritize the mechanical support in case of excessive instability and micromotion.

Physical exercise

The early exertion of physical exercise or mechanical loading is encouraged to reinforce the bone healing kinetics and stimulates the new bone formation evidenced by the clinical studies and animal studies.112, 113, 114 In specific, increased osteocyte numbers, serum alkaline phosphatase levels, and the volume of new bone in the defect area were observed after the commencement of early treadmill training in rat.115 The employment of Mg-containing implants has been confirmed to improve the biological effects of exercise. With the early running exercise (starting from the second day after implantation for 6 weeks), rat underwent Mg-pin fixation could strengthen the orientation of mineralized collagen fibers and the alteration of bone nanostructure around the implants. The degradation of Mg also led to the temporal change of bone-implant integration and mechanical loading pattern.116 Without obvious effect on the degradation of high-purity Mg screws, Gao et al. discovered that treadmill exercise at a speed of 1.0 ​km/h for 30 ​min (5 days/week) remarkably accelerated the peri-implant new bone formation with increased elastic moduli, underlining the synergistical role of dynamic loading and Mg during postoperative bone healing.117

Other physical therapies

By gradual distraction of the bone segments, distraction osteogenesis (DO), as a mechanobiological technique, exerts tension stress to stimulate bone regeneration-associated biological events such as angiogenesis and osteogenesis for bone lengthening, critical-sized bone defect healing, and fracture healing.118, 119, 120 Li et al. reported that the application of Mg nails could synergistically foster the DO-induced bone regeneration. Mechanistically, the CGRP/FAK/VEGF signaling pathway was activated to strengthen the angiogenesis-innervation coupling during the new bone formation.121 By binding to Ptch protein, the Mg2+ released from the high-purity Mg pins activated hedgehog/Wnt signaling pathway, which promoted the consolidated stage of DO for osteogenesis.122

While the low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) is universally adopted for bone formation as a harmless and non-invasively technique, and its application facilitates the peri-implant bone formation and mineralization after the biodegradable porous Mg alloy implantation. For example, enhanced osteocalcin and type I collagen with attenuated osteolysis were found under the combination treatment of LIPUS and Mg alloys, and hardness value of the new bone was upregulated by 15 ​%, which was comparable to the bone mass from healthy bone.123,124 In vitro assays, the supplementation of Mg sulfate elevated the LIPUS-induced osteoblast mineralization and migration. According to the transcriptome sequencing analysis, TGF-β and MAPK signaling pathways were activated, and mineralization-related genes including bone morphogenetic protein 6, noggin, bone morphogenetic protein receptor (BMPR)1A, BMPR2 and SMAD 5/8 were predominately upregulated, confirming the osteoinduction of Mg and LIPUS co-treatment.125 Thus, the application of Mg-containing implants can boost the therapeutic effects of various physical therapies on bone healing (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Synergistical effect of Mg-containing implants and physical stimuli. Overloading affects the performance of Mg-based implants

Localized supraphysiological loading-characterized by inappropriate fixation, excessive intensity of exercise, and premature exercise-will disrupt the callus formation and remodeling, leading to delayed healing and even non-union. While the moderate simulation could lead to advanced callus bridging and ossification by the fourth week, a 10- to 20-fold increase of loading strain induced robust apoptosis in osteoblasts and osteocytes, resulting in delayed healing and non-union.126 Compared to the physiological stimulation, a supraphysiological level elevated the number of osteocytes-promoted osteoclasts by 1.9-fold, the production of membrane-bound RANKL by 3.3-fold, and the generation of nitric oxide by 3.2-fold.27,127 In addition to the detrimental effects on local skeletal cells, high strain of mechanical stimuli can accelerate the degradation of Mg, which may lead to the early diminish of bone-Mg-containing implant integration, the occurrence of bone loss and threat of secondary fracture due to the loss of mechanical support.12 Furthermore, high concentrations of Mg2+ and robust gas bubble released from the implants may disrupt the bone biomechanics, lead to deteriorated inflammation, and counteract the Ca influx to break down the stem cells osteogenesis and local mineralization.128,129 Therefore, the application of high-purity Mg-containing implants in weight-bearing sites or under the excessive loading elicits a wide range of concerns. However, the controlled degradation of Mg alloys may partially counteract the adverse effects of overloading due to its excellent bioactive effects on osseointegration and osteogenesis, allowing early exercise and rehabilitation. Alternatively, the anti-fibrotic effect of Mg materials, as long as the degradative speed is reasonable, underlies their potential in preventing the delayed healing or non-healing situation which are the common consequence of overloading. For instance, the local injection of Mg-containing hydrogel in osteoporotic fracture sites could intervene the SOST/TGF-β signaling pathway. As a consequence, the flexural fracture strength was improved by 27.8 ​% and the fibrotic evolution was hindered manifested in impaired fibroblast activities and aberrant collagen deposition.130 In contrast to the Ti implants, the Mg2+ released from high-purity Mg-containing implants reinforced the expression of immunomodulatory proteins and vascularization while reducing fibrotic encapsulation on the implant surface.131 To balance the bioactive effects and detrimental influence of degradation, modified techniques have been suggested to extend the application of Mg-containing implants. For example, compared to the Ti, high-purity Mg and WE43 plates, the ZK60 plates exhibited a lower fracture risk and higher biomechanical stability.132 The WE43 Mg alloys processed at temperatures between 350 ​°C and 450 ​°C demonstrated optimized formability, balancing thermal softening, and strain hardening.133 In addition, the surface modification via micro-arc oxidation could simultaneously achieve excellent corrosion resistance property while preserving the original mechanical properties, cytocompatibility, and biocompatibility of Mg alloys.134 Therefore, advanced techniques for Mg-containing implants modifications are potential to attenuate degradation while maintaining the original mechanical properties and the releases of Mg2+ to reduce the adverse effects of excessive loading. A well-designed Mg-containing implant with tailored degradation behaviors is worthy for exploit to fit in dynamic loading paradigm.

Mg degradative products directly guide mechanosensitivities

Once exerting, mechanical stimuli are preliminarily sensed by the mechanosensors. The anchor of cell in the extracellular matrix offers the perception of physical stimuli through the deformation of cell membrane or the transduction of cytoskeleton.3,4 As followed, the activation of mechanosensitive ion channels (such as Piezo family and transient receptor potential (TRP) family) and signaling pathways (such as Wnt signaling pathway and Hippo signaling pathway) triggers a cascade of biological reactions, completing the mechanotransduction to transfer a mechanical signal into the biological signal for cellular behavior regulation.5, 6, 7 Regarding the promotive role of Mg-containing implants in physical stimuli-accelerated bone formation, underlying mechanotransductive channels and signaling pathways that may contribute to mechanobiological effects of Mg-containing implants are discussed in this part (Fig. 6).Fig. 6The interplay between degradation products from Mg-containing implants and mechanotransduction signaling pathways. Wnt/β-catenin pathway

Wnt proteins, encoded by 19 genes in human body, functions in various biological processes through binding to the transmembrane receptors (such as Frizzled receptors and lipoprotein receptor-related proteins (LRPs)), and other co-receptors (including tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptors (RORs) and receptor tyrosine kinase (Ryk)).135,136 The signal transmission of Wnt can be driven by the canonical signaling pathway or the non-canonical signaling pathway, among which the β-catenin-regulated canonical signaling pathway is well-studied.137 After the binding between Wnt proteins and receptors or co-receptors, glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β) and casein kinase 1 (CK1) are recruited to the cellular membrane for LRP phosphorylation, which releases β-catenin from the destruction complex for nucleic translocation and target gene transcription.138,139

The activation of Wnt/β-catenin pathway in skeletal cells can be triggered by multiple types of mechanical loading including stretch and fluid shear stress, playing crucial roles in fracture healing, bone development, and bone volume maintenance.137,140 During the bone healing process, emerging evidence demonstrates the function of Mg2+ on Wnt signaling pathway activation. For example, Xu et al. elaborated that the Mg2+ could be captured by osteocytes to elevate the cAMP levels for the activation of ATF4-dependent Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. This mechanism explains enhanced bone formation and improved fracture healing in the CFTR-deficiency mice by either the oral Mg supplementation or the implantation of Mg alloys.141 In the osteoporotic mice, the adaptation of biomimetic porous Mg alloys repaired the bone defect accompanied with increased expression of Wnt5a, β-catenin, and bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2).142 In vitro, the supplementation of Mg (10 ​mM) promoted the bone formation and mineralization by enhancing the migration, proliferation, and osteogenesis of the BMSCs on account of active β-catenin expression.143,144 Additionally, the Mg activated Wnt/β-catenin could also promote the expression of chondrogenesis factors (Sox9, Aggrecan, Col2α1) of stem cells and optimize the metabolic activity of chondrocytes for cartilage repairment, suggesting their potential for fibrocartilaginous callus formation.145,146

The coupling of neurovascularization is indispensable during the bone healing process to provide innervation, paracrine stimuli, nutrients, oxygen, and systemic hormones for new bone generation, endochondral ossification, intramembranous bone formation, and remodeling.147,148 Intriguingly, the oral Mg gluconate (MgG) administration or the implantation of MgG-incorporated scaffolds increased the production of Wnt-related angiogenic and osteogenic factors for bone defect repair.149 In the steroid-induced osteonecrosis model, Mg-coated Sc alloys enhanced the coupling of angiogenesis and osteogenesis to reconstruct the femoral head through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling activation.150,151 In specific, the Mg2+ treatment induced cytoskeleton remodeling and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition of human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), promoting the HUVECs migration and invasion.152 In addition, evidence indicated that the consecutive release of Mg2+ from the Mg-encapsulated scaffolds promoted the proliferation, migration, and repair phenotype transition of the Schwann cell in a Wnt/β-catenin-dependent manner, restoring the axonal regeneration, remyelination, and reinnervation of the muscle tissue.153,154 Therefore, in addition to the direct effects on osteogenesis, the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway may also contribute to Mg-containing implants -increased innervation and angiogenesis during bone healing process.

Integrin pathway

At the cell adhesive sites, mechanical cues are transduced through a “molecular clutch”-a cluster composed of extracellular matrix, actin cytoskeleton and mechanosensitive proteins, among which integrin serves as the bridge for cell-matrix connection.155 By triggering the downstream events, such as the activation of focal adhesion kinase (FAK), Src family kinase, and the nucleic translocation of YAP, the mechanical signals are converted into biochemical signals for the regulation of cell mechanics and metabolism.156,157 The initial of integrin-mediated adhesion is critical for the progenitor cells proliferation, osteogenesis, and the subsequent deposition of fibronectin and collagen to realize the osseointegration for bone-implant bridging.158,159

The integrin pathways are established regulators of the bone healing promoted by Mg-containing composite biomaterials. Nie et al. indicated that extensive spreading mineralized collagen and firm adhesive pseudopodia were generated on the surface of Ca-Mg alloys, among which the activated integrin α2β1-FAK-ERK1/2 signaling pathway and inactivated RANK signaling pathway were responsible for the osteogenic differentiation of progenitor cells and the M2 polarization of macrophages.160 Compared to the Mg-free Al2O3 bioceramics, the modification of Mg substantially facilitated the expression of β1-, α5β1-, and α3β1-integrins receptors for the human bone-derived cell adhesion accompanied by enhanced expression of Shc isoforms and FAK.161 Similarly, in vivo and in vitro assays have revealed that the coating of Mg in phosphate cements oriented the adsorbed fibronectin with higher receptor binding affinity and enhanced the focal adhesion, actin cytoskeleton assembly and osteogenesis of BMSCs.162, 163, 164, 165 Taken together, these findings established the integrin-based adhesion and the downstream signaling as key mediators of the mechanobiological effect of Mg-containing implants, especially the superior osseointegration efficacy.

Calcium ion channel

The signal transduction of mechanical stimuli commonly induces calcium ion (Ca2+) influx through opening the gap junction of corresponding channels (such as Piezo family and transient receptor potential vanilloid (TRPV) family). This Ca ion influx transmits extracellular mechanical signals into intracellular biochemical signals via the regulation of calmodulin-calcineurin pathway, ERK/MAPK pathway, YAP/TAZ pathway and other various kinases pathway for gene expression mediation.166 Therefore, the adequate Ca availability is necessary for bone health, remodeling, and regeneration as the main component of extracellular matrix as well as the cofactors for cellular responses.167

However, Mg2+ acts as the natural antagonists of Ca2+ by competitively binding to various biological system such as enzymes and nucleic acid.168,169 For example, the competitive binding of Mg2+ on dihydropyridine-sensitive, voltage-dependent channels mostly impaired the Ca2+ influx for the airway smooth muscle relaxation.170 Elucidated by the whole-cell configuration of the patch-clamp technique, the activity of calcium channels was inhibited by either excessive intracellular and extracellular Mg2+. While the increasing intracellular Mg2+ reduced the peak of calcium current and inactivated curve to left in time and voltage dependent manners, the increasing extracellular Mg2+ rapidly shifted the current-voltage and the inactivation curves to the right with little effect on the inactivation kinetics.171 Under the elevation of extracellular Mg2+ concentrations of 4.8 ​mM and 9.6 ​mM, Ca2+ current was suppressed by nearly 40 ​% and 46 ​%, respectively.172 During the musculoskeletal development, while appropriate Mg levels promoted the mineralization of BMSCs, overload of Mg2+ was inadequate for osteoblast differentiation, mineralizing activity, and hydroxyapatite crystals deposition due to the impaired Ca2+ influx.173, 174, 175 Herein, renal failure with excessive concentration of serum Mg might be subject to osteodystrophy and adynamic bone disease.176 Since the Mg is a cofactor for adenylate cyclase activation, the Mg-Ca interaction is also mediated by parathyroid hormone (PTH) and 1,25(OH)2-vitamin D, accounting for the hypocalcemia in the condition of Mg deficiency and the high risk of fracture due to the excessive Mg intake.177,178

Intriguingly, Mg-induced new bone formation can benefit from the Ca2+ influx-associated signals. For example, decreased Ca2+ influx due to the addition of Mg inhibited the production of inflammatory cytokines and NF-κB signaling pathway in microphages, and exerted antioxidant effect for the maturation of dendritic cells and T lymphocytes activation.179,180 Hou et al. revealed that the proliferation of osteoblasts and osteopontin (OPN) production were enhanced in a dose dependent manner of Mg via the increased ratio of phosphorylated-calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase IV (p-CaMKIV) and phosphorylated-cyclic-AMP response binding protein 1 (p-CREB).181 And the incorporation of Ca2+ and Mg2+ in biomaterials scaffold accelerated various cellular events for osteogenic differentiation.182 Nevertheless, the application of Mg-containing implants should prevent the overload and rapid release of the Mg2+. The gating mediation and dual function of Mg2+ on L-type Ca2+ channels had been indicated characterized by enhanced basal activity of Ca2+ channels towards low Mg2+ levels and inhibited channel activities towards high Mg2+ levels.183 The interaction between Mg and Ca channels in bone healing process should be considered as a intricated crosstalk with spatiotemporal dynamics, underscoring the demand of flexible and thoughtful implant design.

Other mechanotransductive signaling pathways

Yes-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ) are well-known as the instrumental mediators during the mechanical signal interpretation.184 Under the conditions of the large adhesive area, rigid substrate, or mechanical stimuli, the YAP/TAZ can be translocated into nucleus to dominate various behaviors including proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. The activity of YAP/TAZ is commonly regulated by phosphorylation, and switch-on Hippo signal is the best understood upstream factor for the retention of the phosphorylated YAP/TAZ in the cytoplasm.184, 185, 186, 187 The activation of YAP/TAZ is associated with bone formation events such as osteoblast lineage differentiation, osteocytes anabolism, and impaired osteoclasts activity. Tts dysfunction contributes to developmental deformities, bone loss, the development of osteoarthritis, and spontaneous bone fractures.188, 189, 190 The involvement of YAP/TAZ in Mg alloys was introduced by Costa et al. through secretome analysis to promote the osteoblast differentiation of human mesenchymal stem cells.191 During the formation of endothelial cells spheroids, the administration of Mg2+ fostered the proliferation of stalk cells and the activity of tip cells via nuclear localization of YAP protein, explaining the enhanced vascularization during bone healing process after Mg biomaterials treatment.192

The Ras homolog gene family member A (RhoA), a small GTPase, is documented to orchestrate the Rho-associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase (ROCK) for cytoskeleton reorganization, which is critical for the transduction of biophysical cues, including ECM mechanical properties, tensile stress, compressive stress, shear stress, and hydrostatic pressure.193,194 With the stimulation of BMP2, the RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway directs the cell spreading and the generation of cytoskeletal tension for the osteogenesis of human mesenchymal stem cells.195 In addition, RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway is crucial for the assembly of actin cytoskeleton and stress fibers, which regulate the metabolic activities of osteoblasts, osteocytes, and osteoclasts for bone volume maintenance.196, 197, 198 Of note, the Mg2+ serves as a cofactor during the binding of GTP-bound RhoA to ROCK protein. With the addition of Mg2+, the GTP hydrolysis rates was lit up by 4-10-fold concomitant with the increased k(cat) values for higher catalytic efficiency and specificity.199 Although the interplay between of Mg2+ and RhoA/ROCK pathway in bone healing is yet to be determined, evidence has showcased that nerve conduit, which encompassed glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), Gelatin methacryloyl (Gel), hydroxylapatite (HA), and Mg, promoted the regeneration of peripheral nerves axons and the thickness of the myelin sheath due to the regulation of PPAR-γ/RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway.200 Considering the necessity of innervation during new bone formation, the activation of RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway may enrich the understanding of increased peripheral nerves spouting during the application of Mg-containing implants.

Degradable byproducts and mechanotransduction

Beyond the release of Mg2+, the priority of Mg-containing implants is embodied in the generation of alkaline condition and hydrogen gas as the byproducts, which can synergistically enlarge the bone healing effects of implants.21 Some scientists have also specified their engagement for mechanotransduction pathways activation during the bone healing process. After the Mg alloy implantation, absorbable hydrogen gas was accumulated around the implants, leading to the recruitment of cells (especially macrophages) and the increasing expression of mechanosensitive Piezo1 channel for the initiation of bone formation-associated events.201,202 In addition, spatial transcriptomics depicted the enrichment of cytoskeletal reorganization-responsible genes, revealing the cellular response to the mechanical cues and the activation of mechanical pathways due to the generation of bubbles.203 Due to the initial of inflammation after implantation or infection, the microenvironment with a low pH value can stimulate the osteoclasts-mediated bone resorption and disrupt the number or normal function of osteoblasts.204, 205, 206, 207 In this case, the alkaline environment created by Mg-containing implant degradation can neutralized the acidic microenvironment to enhance the bone-implant connection. In addition, the YAP activity was pH-dependent featuring that compared to a neutralized environment, an extracellular pH value of 7.0 could interfere its dephosphorylation for osteogenesis, proliferation, and apoptosis.208,209 In conclusion, beyond the Mg2+ release, the generation of alkaline environment and hydrogen gas cooperatively amplify the mechanobiological effect of Mg-containing implants.

Discussion and future perspective

Due to their remarkable bioactive properties, Mg implants have attracted significant clinical interest, with most of the investigations indicating comparable or superior efficacy in bone healing compared to traditional implants (Table 1). From the mechanobiological perspective, this review further underscores the exceptional capacity of Mg implants to eliminate stress shielding, promote osseointegration, and enhance physical stimuli-induced osteogenesis. However, the degradable nature of Mg necessitates careful consideration of the intensity, strain, and duration of physical stimuli. While the degradation products may enhance the mechanosensitivity of skeletal cells and activate key mechanical signaling pathways to foster bone formation, excessive physical loading can conversely accelerate implant degradation. Consequently, innovative strategies are being employed to improve the corrosive resistance of Mg-containing implants. Although impurities like iron or copper can accelerate degradation through galvanic coupling, high-purity (>99.99 ​%) Mg-containing implants demonstrate superior corrosion resistance.210, 211, 212 Alternatively, the addition of micro-elements, such as Al and zinc, can slow the cathodic reaction rate for local corrosion and generate a compact passivating or oxide film on the surface to protect the Mg from anode activity.213 In addition, grain refinement-a technique to optimize the grain size, content, and second phases of Mg, and the surface modification to generate a corrosive coating (such as MAO technique and the plasma spray of bioactive ceramics) are also effective in developing corrosion-resistant Mg-containing implants.214Biosensors represent a promising technology for monitoring the local electrochemical environment around the biomedical implants. Based on the mechanism of sensing, orthopedic biosensors are classified into optical sensors, resonant sensors, magnetic sensors, acoustic sensors, piezoelectric sensors, and capacitive/resistive sensors.215 By tracking the spatiotemporal degradation profile and measuring the local loading pressure or the chemical conditions, the real-time inspection of biosensors can comprehensively depict the status of Mg-containing implants and the response of surrounding tissue. Rich et al. have synthesized a sensor complex to monitor the loading strain, temperature, pH value, and motion. Compared to the Ti plates, a higher pH value (7.4 ​± ​0.8 versus 6.6 ​± ​0.4) in the surrounding tissue and a higher loading strain in the Mg plate implantation group were recorded by the biosensor complex, and once case subjected to excessive loading was well-predicted plate failure within 31 ​h.216 Catalytic biosensors equipped with microdialysis probe to examinate the peri-implant Mg2+ levels, and the real-time visual microsensors with capability to visualize the hydrogen gas permeation were developed with great potential to employ on the Mg-containing implants for corrosive inspection.217, 218, 219 Significantly, a fully bioresorbable, wireless, passive and battery-free biosensor is promising to meet clinical demands for setting time points observation without extraction surgeries and adverse effects.220 With the energy transfer and sensor interrogation by the electromagnetic, ultrasound or other non-invasive techniques, the smart biosensors adhered on implants or surrounding bone tissue comprehensively provide adequate information of exerted mechanical strain, the real-time alteration of biochemical environment, and the corrosion rate of implants, directing the clinical instructions on the intensity, frequency and duration of exercise, locomotion, or physical therapies.221,222 Such biosensors complex can also fully harness the bioactive and mechanobiological effects of Mg-containing implants with less adverse effects. Additionally, the in vivo monitoring of Mg-containing implant behavior through the biosensor can help identify the limitations, thereby guiding the production of more clinically feasible Mg-containing implants.

Computational analysis and modeling are equally critical for predicting the Mg-containing implants behaviors. Based on customized subroutine, a continuum damage mechanism finite element model can predict the mass loss of Mg-based alloys in vitro with potential applications for in vivo prediction.223 Quinn et al. proposed a phenomenological model for Mg performance prediction, which could capture multi-directional pitting and pit morphologies, and avoid the independent values and effects.224 Currently, the rapid development of artificial intelligence and big data modeling drastically empowers the computational analysis of high-throughput data and precise prediction of the implantation outcomes. For dental patients, the AI algorithms and deep learning-based methods were applied to maximize the predictive potential in prognosis of implantation based on radiographic imaging, bone quality, implant location, and other factors.225, 226, 227 Therefore, the comprehensive integration of these cutting-edge techniques, such as the combination of the efference of biosensors and deep learning-guided algorithms with the input of radiographic data, will prove invaluable for guiding surgical operations and prognostic assessments. Nevertheless, significant challenges persist for the clinical application of orthopedic biosensors. A primary concern is the biosafety, which necessitates the validation of biocompatibility, non-cytotoxicity, and non-immunoreactivity, especially during long-term implantation. Even when the biocompatible materials are used, inflammation control remains a major issue, potentially leading to implant failure or aseptic loosening. The introduction of an exogenous biosensor carries a substantial risk of infection as the potential adhesion of bacteria or biofilm.228 Second, the accuracy, stability and signal-to-noise ratio are challenging along with the intervene of metal implants, the growth of fibrotic tissue, the mechanical stimulation during locomotion, and the physiochemical changes of microenvironment. While the self-powered piezoelectric and trioeletric biosensors enable the feasibility of battery-free strategies, low power output necessitates complex power management circuits.215 Therefore, an ideal biosensor with excellent biocompatibility, long-term stability, a reliable energy source, high accuracy, and long-range wireless signal transmission within a compact size still requests a lot of technological breakthroughs. Nevertheless, despite numerous obstacles, it is believed that the great achievements in biosensor technology and the incorporation of other intelligent advancements will effectively guide the bone healing of patients treated with well-designed Mg implants. This is an interesting field worthy to explore.

Conclusion

Mg, as a degradable material, is a promising candidate for the next-generation othopaedic implants whether used as high-purity Mg constructs or as coating. While most research emphasizes its bioactive effects, this review addresses a critical gap by focusing on its mechanobiological influence. The preliminary advantage of Mg-containing implants, with similar mechanical properties to cortical bone, lies in preventing stress shielding, which allows physiological mechanical loading to stimulate bone healing. Furthermore, Mg promotes superior osseointegration, enhancing the feasibility for challenging skeletal conditions like osteoporotic bone and femoral head necrosis while reducing risks associated with excessive micromotion and other complications. Specifically, Mg2+ release, alkaline microenvironment generation, and hydrogen gas evolution can activate mechanosensitive signaling pathways. However, excessive physical loading facilitates Mg degradation, potentially compromising mechanical integrity and exacerbating inflammation. To fully exploit the superior merits of Mg implants in bone healing while mitigating their limitations, we underscore the significance of a small size of biosensor, which is wireless and energy-reliable with exceptional accuracy and stability to provide real-time and accurate in vivo monitoring. Integrated with cutting-edge interdisciplinary technologies such as big data analytics and artificial intelligence, it can offer invaluable guidance for the design and modification of Mg implants, the implementation of personalized therapeutic strategies, and the dynamics of rehabilitation protocols, thereby fostering optimal healing of bone defects.

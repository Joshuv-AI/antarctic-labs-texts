Long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab with or without background csDMARDs in rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Gerd R Burmester, Vibeke Strand, Alan J Kivitz, Chih-Chi Hu, Sheldon Wang, Hubert van Hoogstraten, Gabriella L Klier, Roy Fleischmann
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab with/without conventional synthetic (cs)DMARDs in RA.MethodsThe analyses evaluated two open-label extensions (OLEs): EXTEND and MONARCH OLE, which included patients from six randomized trials. Patients received sarilumab 200 mg once every 2 weeks (q2w) for at least 264 weeks up to 516 weeks (EXTEND: Sarilumab Monotherapy and Sarilumab + csDMARD groups) or for 276 weeks (MONARCH OLE: Continuation and Switch groups). Primary endpoints included safety, immunogenicity and changes in laboratory parameters. Secondary endpoints included clinical signs and symptoms along with health-related quality-of-life (HRQOL) questionnaires.ResultsThe Sarilumab Monotherapy (n = 111), Continuation (n = 165) and Switch (n = 155) groups received sarilumab monotherapy, while the Sarilumab + csDMARD group (n = 1910) received sarilumab in combination with csDMARDs. Incidence of one or more treatment-emergent adverse events was 126 (Sarilumab Monotherapy group), 169 (Sarilumab + csDMARD group), 159 (Continuation group) and 159 (Switch group) events/100 patient-years. Neutropenia was the most common adverse event. Neutropenia was not associated with an increased incidence of infections. Most neutropenia cases normalized on-treatment. Adverse events of special interests, such as malignancies, major adverse cardiovascular events, venous thromboembolism and gastrointestinal perforations, were rare. Immunogenicity was low and not associated with hypersensitivity reactions or discontinuations due to lack or loss of efficacy. Improvements in clinical signs and symptoms and HRQOL, observed during the initial blinded trials, were maintained throughout the OLE assessment period.ConclusionsLong-term sarilumab treatment with/without csDMARDs in patients with RA revealed no new safety findings. Introduction

RA is a chronic systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease that primarily affects the synovial membrane of the diarthrodial joints and is characterized by persistent synovitis and progressive destruction of cartilage and bone [1, 2]. IL-6 is a key pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathogenesis of RA, causing synovial and systemic inflammation [3–5] produced by a variety of cell types including synovial and endothelial cells leading to local IL-6 production in inflamed joints, as in RA [5].

Sarilumab is a human recombinant IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to both soluble and membrane-bound IL-6 receptors (IL-6R) to inhibit IL-6-mediated signalling. Sarilumab 200 mg once every 2 weeks (q2w) s.c. administration is approved for the treatment of adults with moderately to severely active RA, who are inadequate responders (IR) or intolerant to one or more DMARDs, as monotherapy or in combination with MTX or other conventional synthetic (cs)DMARDs [6].

Although the efficacy and safety of sarilumab as monotherapy or in combination with csDMARDs have been demonstrated in phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that are active comparator- and placebo-controlled in adults with active RA, its use in a chronic disease such as RA necessitates long-term evaluation of its safety and efficacy [7]. The current analyses evaluated the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab with or without csDMARDs in patients with active RA using full data sets from two long-term open-label extensions (OLE): EXTEND (NCT01146652) and OLE of MONARCH (NCT02332590; MONARCH OLE).

Methods

Trial design

The phase II/III randomized trials included in the EXTEND [8–12] and the phase III double-blind RCT MONARCH [13] have been described previously.

Refer to Supplementary Figs S1 and S2, available at Rheumatology online, for trial design. As shown in Supplementary Fig. S1, the EXTEND trial was a multicentre OLE that assessed the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab monotherapy (Sarilumab Monotherapy group) and sarilumab in combination with csDMARDs (Sarilumab + csDMARD group) in patients enrolled from five phase II/III randomized trials: NCT01217814, ASCERTAIN (NCT01768572), TARGET (NCT01709578), MOBILITY (NCT01061736) and ONE (NCT02121210). MONARCH OLE was a multicentre OLE following the MONARCH RCT that assessed the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab monotherapy in the Continuation group (patients who remained on sarilumab monotherapy during both double-blind period and OLE) and the Switch group (patients who switched from adalimumab monotherapy in the double-blind period to sarilumab monotherapy in the OLE).

Patients

The EXTEND trial included adult patients with RA (age ≥18 years) who were either TNF-IR who had failed two or fewer TNF antagonists (NCT01217814); TNF-IR or -intolerant (ASCERTAIN and TARGET); MTX-IR (MOBILITY); non-biologic DMARD-IR or -intolerant (ONE). The MONARCH OLE trial included adult patients with RA who were deemed inappropriate candidates for MTX treatment, intolerant of MTX or MTX-IR after ≥12 weeks of treatment.

Treatments

Patients were enrolled to receive sarilumab 200 mg s.c. q2w for a maximum of 516 weeks (minimum 264 weeks) in EXTEND and 276 weeks in MONARCH OLE trials. A small group of patients in the EXTEND trial who initially received 150 mg once every week for a short duration switched to 200 mg q2w upon final selection of the doses for the phase III randomized trials. Patients continued to receive sarilumab until it became commercially available in their respective country or end of the trial in 2020. Patients receiving background csDMARDs in the double-blind (DB) period continued it throughout the OLE, which could be reduced in dose, discontinued or switched to an alternative approved csDMARD for safety or tolerability reasons. Background csDMARD therapies received are provided in Supplementary Table S1, available at Rheumatology online. Sarilumab dose reductions to 150 mg s.c. q2w were allowed in the event of neutropenia, thrombocytopenia or elevated liver enzymes; patients were allowed to switch back to 200 mg q2w at the investigator’s discretion.

Objectives and assessments

Patient discontinuation over time was assessed using Kaplan–Meier method. The primary objective of these analyses was to evaluate the long-term safety of sarilumab with or without background csDMARDs by assessing the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), TEAEs of special interest (AESIs) based on the standardized Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Affairs (MedDRA) queries (SMQ), presence of anti-drug antibodies to sarilumab and changes in specific laboratory parameters. Investigator-reported TEAEs of diverticulitis/gastrointestinal (GI) perforation were identified using a MedDRA high-level term, diverticulum inflammations, and an SMQ, GI perforation, respectively. The MedDRA SMQ GI perforation includes events such as fistula or rectal abscess. Therefore, suspected cases of GI perforation were reviewed medically. Major adverse cardiovascular (CV) events were reviewed by an independent CV adjudication committee and defined as CV death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina or transient ischaemic attack. Venous thromboembolism (VTE), a composite of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and/or pulmonary embolism (PE), was analysed post hoc based on the MedDRA high-level group terms Embolism and Thrombosis (DVT) and Pulmonary vascular disorders (PE). Leukopoenia was included as an AESI to capture investigator-reported AEs of neutropenia.

Secondary objectives were to analyse the long-term effectiveness of sarilumab and health-related quality of life (HRQOL) assessed by patient-reported outcomes. Efficacy for Sarilumab Monotherapy and Sarilumab + csDMARD groups of the EXTEND trial are presented up to 504 weeks (Supplementary Table S2, available at Rheumatology online). However, ACR components were collected only up to 264 weeks. After week 264, tender and swollen joint counts (based on 28 joints), patient’s global assessment of disease activity and high-sensitivity CRP were assessed to calculate DAS28-CRP. Efficacy for the Continuation and Switch groups in the MONARCH OLE trial are presented up to 252 weeks.

The Medical Outcomes Survey Short Form 36 (SF-36), Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy—Fatigue (FACIT-F) and Rheumatoid Arthritis Specific Work Productivity (WPS-RA) were evaluated in the Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, although assessment periods differed (Supplementary Table S2, available at Rheumatology online). Further, Work Productivity and Activity Impairment (WPAI) and sleep quality visual analogue scale (VAS) scores were assessed in the Sarilumab + csDMARD group. Patient-reported outcomes were not assessed in the Sarilumab Monotherapy group.

Statistical analysis

Safety and efficacy analyses were descriptive and as observed. Categorical variables were summarized as numbers with percentages. Continuous variables are summarized as means, s.d.and s.e. Adverse events were summarized as exposure-adjusted incidence rates [number of events and number of events/100 patient-years (PY)]. Baseline values were defined as the last available value prior to the first dose of the investigational drug during the DB period. Open-label extension populations are defined in Supplementary Table S3, available at Rheumatology online. All summaries and statistical analyses were generated using SAS Version 9.4.

Results

Patient population and treatment exposure

Overall, 2021 and 320 patients received open-label treatment in EXTEND (Sarilumab Monotherapy, n = 111; Sarilumab + csDMARD, n = 1910) and MONARCH OLE (Continuation, n = 165; Switch, n = 155), respectively, shown in Supplementary Fig. S2, available at Rheumatology online. Two patients enrolled in the Sarilumab + csDMARD group did not receive treatment. TEAEs were the most common reason for treatment discontinuation. Although patients in the Sarilumab Monotherapy group had a shorter follow-up period, permanent treatment discontinuation rates were similar between groups in the respective studies (Fig. 1). As all patients in the Sarilumab Monotherapy group were enrolled from the ONE randomized trial, which started at a much later date, a shorter follow-up period was anticipated. Overall, patients were predominantly female and Caucasian with a mean age of ∼50 years; patient demographics and disease characteristics were similar between groups in the respective studies at baseline (Table 1).

Cumulative treatment exposures in the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups were 453.8 PY, 8552.7 PY, 594.6 PY and 537.3 PY, respectively, and the mean (minimum–maximum) durations of treatment exposure were 4.1 (0.03–5.8), 4.5 (0.02–9.9), 3.6 (0.03–5.1) and 3.5 (0.03–5.1) years, respectively.

Primary outcomes

Treatment-emergent adverse events

Overall, 88.3%, 92.1%, 86.7% and 87.1% of patients in the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups experienced one or more TEAE with an exposure-adjusted rate of 125.8, 169.4, 159.1 and 159.0 events/100 PY, respectively (Table 2). Deaths due to TEAEs were reported in 1.8% (0.6 death/100 PY), 2.1% (0.6 death/100 PY), 3.0% (1.0 death/100 PY) and 1.9% (0.7 death/100 PY) patients in Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups (Supplementary Table S4, available at Rheumatology online). Analyses revealed no new safety signals. The most commonly reported TEAE was neutropenia (Sarilumab Monotherapy, 13.8 events/100 PY; Sarilumab + csDMARD, 9.1 events/100 PY; Continuation, 11.1 events/100 PY; and Switch, 14.8 events/100 PY). Other frequently reported TEAEs (≥5 events/100 PY in either group) were injection-site erythema, nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, accidental administration of two or more sarilumab doses within 11 days, bronchitis and RA. Serious adverse events (SAEs) were reported in 24.3%, 32.3%, 15.2% and 20.0% patients in the Sarilumab Monotherapy (8.8 events/100 PY), Sarilumab + csDMARD (12.3 events/100 PY), Continuation (6.0 events/100 PY) and Switch groups (10.3 events/100 PY), respectively. Treatment discontinuations were most frequently due to herpes zoster infections, increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT), neutropenia, injection site erythema and increased hepatic enzyme levels.

Infections

Overall, infections were the most common class of TEAEs: 36.2, 45.4, 39.7 and 46.1 events/100 PY in the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, respectively (Table 2). Serious infections were observed at 1.7, 3.3, 0.7 and 2.2 events/100 PY, respectively. Although a numerically higher numbers of serious infections occurred in Sarilumab + csDMARD than Sarilumab Monotherapy groups and in Switch than in Continuation groups; no unexpected SAEs or changes in the overall safety profile were reported during both the studies. Rates of opportunistic infections ranged from none to low (Sarilumab Monotherapy: n = 0; Sarilumab + csDMARD: n = 74, 0.9 events/100 PY; Continuation: n = 3, 0.5 events/100 PY; and Switch: n = 7, 1.4 events/100 PY). Herpes zoster was the most frequently reported opportunistic infection (Sarilumab Monotherapy: n = 0; Sarilumab + csDMARD: n (number of events) = 44 (46), 0.5 events/100 PY; Continuation: n (number of events) = 2 (2), 0.3 events/100 PY; and Switch: n (number of events) = 5 (5), 0.9 events/100 PY).

Adverse events of special interest

Treatment-emergent adverse events of diverticulitis/potential GI perforations were infrequent: Sarilumab Monotherapy (n = 2, 0.4 events/100 PY; lower GI perforations: n = 0), Sarilumab + csDMARD (n = 28, 0.4 events/100 PY; lower GI perforations: n = 4, <0.1 events/100 PY), Continuation (n = 1, 0.2 events/100 PY; lower GI perforations: n = 0) and Switch (n = 0) (Table 2).

Malignancies were reported in 1.8% to 3.9% of patients between the two studies with an exposure-adjusted incidence rate of 0.6, 0.6, 0.5 and 1.3 events/100 PY in the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, respectively. No more than one patient in each of the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Continuation and Switch groups had the same malignancy type. Basal cell carcinoma (n = 8, 0.4%), prostate cancer (n = 3, 0.2%), breast cancer (n = 2, 0.1%), colorectal adenocarcinoma (n = 2, 0.1%), papillary thyroid cancer (n = 2, 0.1%) and squamous cell carcinoma of the skin (n = 2, 0.1%) occurred in more than one patient in the Sarilumab + csDMARD group.

Other AESIs, such as major adverse CV events and VTE events, were not frequent. The CV adjudication committee adjudicated 0.6, 0.6, 0.2 and 0.4 CV TEAEs/100 PY as major adverse CV events in Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, respectively. The incidence of DVT and PE events was none to low across the two studies. Exposure-adjusted incidence rates of DVT were 0, 0.2, 0 and 0.2 events/100 PY, and PE were 0.6, 0.1, 0.2 and 0 events/100 PY in Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, respectively.

Moreover, the rate of AEs analysed by 6-month interval remained stable or decreased over time (Supplementary Figs S3–S6, available at Rheumatology online). Data at the end of the study should be interpreted with caution because of the small number of patients under follow-up.

Immunogenicity

Anti-drug antibody positivity was 5.7% to 7.3% across all treatment groups and not associated with hypersensitivity reactions or permanent treatment discontinuations due to lack or loss of efficacy (Supplementary Table S5, available at Rheumatology online).

Laboratory abnormalities

Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) <1000 cells/mm3, ALT >3 upper limit of normal and platelet count <100 × 109 cells/l occurred in 10 (9.1%), 3 (2.7%) and 1 (0.9%) patients in Sarilumab Monotherapy and 244 (12.8%), 190 (10.0%) and 74 (3.9%) patients in Sarilumab + csDMARD groups, respectively. Similarly, 24 (15.2%), 19 (11.5%) and 4 (2.4%) patients in Continuation and 24 (15.6%), 13 (8.4) and 4 (2.6%) patients in Switch groups had ANC <1000 cells/mm3, ALT >3 upper limit of normal and platelet count <100 × 109 cells/l, respectively (Supplementary Table S6, available at Rheumatology online). In the majority of patients, a second assessment was within normal limits without interruption of treatment. Overall, patients with ANC below the lower limit of normal had similar incidences of infections compared with those with normal ANC counts (Supplementary Table S7, available at Rheumatology online). Thus, incidence of infections was generally similar between patient groups regardless of reported neutropenia.

Efficacy outcomes

Improvements in the signs and symptoms of RA achieved during the DB period were maintained throughout the OLE assessment period. Mean DAS28-CRP, mean Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI) and proportions of patients with ACR 20/50/70 responses, DAS28-CRP <2.6 and CDAI ≤2.8 (CDAI remission) were maintained across all treatment groups (Figs 2 and 3), and this was similarly true for individual ACR components (Supplementary Figs S7 and S8, available at Rheumatology online). Both Continuation and Switch groups demonstrated stable mean DAS28-ESR levels over time and proportions of patients with DAS28-ESR <2.6 (remission) and DAS28-ESR <3.2 (low disease activity) were maintained (Fig. 3).

Improvements in patient-reported outcomes including HRQOL (SF-36; Fig. 4); fatigue (FACIT-F); work within and outside the home, as well as participation in family, social and leisure activities by WPS-RA (Supplementary Fig. S9 and Table S8, available at Rheumatology online) during the DB period were further improved or maintained in the Sarilumab + csDMARD group as well as in the Continuation and Switch groups. Discussion

These analyses confirm the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab monotherapy or combination treatment with csDMARDs in RA. No new TEAEs occurred in the OLE; commonly reported TEAEs included neutropenia, injection site erythema, nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, accidental administration of two or more sarilumab doses within 11 days, bronchitis and RA, consistent with those reported in the primary sarilumab phase III trials [9–13] and known AE profile of IL-6 signalling inhibition [15]. These long-term analyses of EXTEND and MONARCH OLE trials revealed an incidence of 8.8, 12.3, 6.0 and 10.3 SAEs/100 PY in the Sarilumab Monotherapy, Sarilumab + csDMARD, Continuation and Switch groups, respectively. Although no direct comparisons can be made, it is noteworthy that an integrated long-term safety analysis of tocilizumab, another IL-6R inhibitor in RA, reported 14.4 SAEs/100 PY [16].

Neutropenia was the most common TEAE across all treatment groups, consistent with the published sarilumab phase III data [9–13]. Rates of infection were similar in patients with and without neutropenia with no association between infections and maximum grades of neutropenia. Further, in most patients with ANC <1000 cells/mm3, neutrophil counts normalized on treatment. One explanation could be neutrophil margination, a consequence of IL-6 pathway blockade, resulting in migration of neutrophils in extravascular pools without impairing neutrophil function [17, 18]. Additional evidence that neutrophil function is not impaired after IL-6 inhibition is provided by in vitro and ex vivo studies, which have demonstrated that apoptosis, priming of respiratory burst, expression of adhesion molecules and chemotaxis remain unaffected after IL-6 signalling inhibition [19]. Furthermore, a post hoc analysis demonstrated that the majority of patients in the RCTs (77%) and OLEs (90%) who temporarily discontinued sarilumab treatment due to low ANC were able to continue or reinitiate treatment without apparent clinical impact on long-term efficacy or safety [20].

The current analyses demonstrated a limited number of opportunistic infections, most commonly, herpes zoster. Patients with RA are at an increased risk of herpes zoster infection due to increasing age and glucocorticoid use [21]. Many studies have concluded that there is no increased risk of herpes zoster infections with the use of biological DMARDs vs csDMARDs, yet a few have claimed an increased risk with biological DMARDs [22]. In the current analyses, 53 of 91 opportunistic infections were due to herpes zoster, similar to the incidence reported in the phase III RCTs. Occurrences of herpes zoster infections with sarilumab is not new and observed in the primary phase III RCTs as well [10, 11]. Low vaccination rates for herpes zoster infection were reported in the current analyses. Promotion of awareness of the need as well as the safety of administration of the virus vaccine must be emphasized [22].

Lower GI perforations, which may be associated with IL-6R blockade therapy [23] were rarely observed. However, the protocols excluded patients with a history of diverticulitis [24], which might have alleviated the risk of GI perforations in this population.

A controversial meta-analysis by Simon et al. [25] demonstrated that patients with RA are at an increased risk of malignancies, particularly lung cancer and lymphomas, compared with the general population. All malignancies (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer) in the current analyses were lower than in the general RA population in the USA (1.3 malignancies/100 PY) including lung cancers (0.2 events/100 PY) and lymphomas (0.09 events/100 PY) [26]. These current analyses indicated that patients treated with sarilumab had a low incidence of malignancies compared with a population of 13 869 patients with RA who had no medical history of receiving IL-6 inhibitors [26].

Patients with RA have a higher risk of CV diseases [27], postulated to be due to accelerated atherosclerosis resulting from underlying chronic systemic inflammation [16]. Many studies have linked CV events with RA disease activity [28–30]. IL-6 inhibitors may help reduce the inflammatory response implicated in development of CV disease [31, 32]. These current analyses demonstrate that long-term treatment with sarilumab did not elevate the risk of major adverse CV events; the incidence rates across all treatment groups were lower than the general population with RA [33] despite elevated lipid levels (the lipid paradox) [34] and appear similar to those with another IL-6R inhibitor (tocilizumab) [35].

It has become well recognized that patients with RA have a two- to three-fold increased risk of VTE events compared with the general population [27]. In the current analyses, the incidence of VTE events was generally lower than the general RA population who received one or more csDMARDs (DVT, 0.5 events/100 PY; PE, 0.3 events/100 PY) [36].

Moreover, after long-term treatment with sarilumab, improvements in efficacy [10, 11, 13] and patient-reported outcomes [14, 37–40], achieved during the initial RCTs of sarilumab, were maintained or further increased.

Over the long term, if a patient achieves clinical remission or very low disease activity, it is recommended to consider tapering and even discontinue csDMARDs in case the patient continues to do well. In the event of an AE thought to be due to csDMARDs, one should consider tapering or discontinuation of the csDMARD as long as the patient does not worsen.

Together, these analyses confirm the long-term safety and efficacy of sarilumab in RA. Although both EXTEND and MONARCH studies were OLEs, the long treatment periods, high enrolments and treatment completion rates, and inclusion of patients with diverse medication histories which mimic the real-life setting of RA treatment, make the findings of these analyses robust. Key limitations are that the data are derived from OLE and include only ‘successful completer’ patients. Additionally, the MONARCH OLE trial enrolled a relatively smaller number of patients with RA compared with the EXTEND trial.

Conclusions

Long-term treatment with sarilumab with or without background csDMARDs in patients with RA identified no new safety signals. The results are consistent with IL-6 signalling blockade and confirm the safety profile of sarilumab from the phase III RCTs. Improvements in clinical efficacy and patient-reported outcomes including HRQOL, fatigue, participation and work productivity reported during the initial phase II/III randomized trials were maintained or further increased.

Research note: Identification and characterization of SLC11A1 gene promoter of chicken
Authors: Mengjie Wang, Jingjing Wang, Xiaoya Wang, Lijie Zhang, Xinran Liu, Qianmei Gao, Qinghe Li, Guiping Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSLC11A1 is a key gene involved in the regulation of innate immunity, and its expression level is closely associated with disease resistance in livestock and poultry, making it an important candidate gene for disease-resistant breeding. The functional characteristics of the promoter region are central to deciphering the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of a gene. In this study, we focused on the chicken SLC11A1 gene and retrieved sequences flanking the transcription start site (TSS) from the NCBI database. By integrating bioinformatic analysis with a dual-luciferase reporter assay system, we systematically identified the core promoter region and functionally characterized the regulatory roles of cis-regulatory element and their associated transcription factor binding profiles. Results revealed a canonical TATA-box element located at -31 bp to -28 bp upstream of the TSS of the chicken SLC11A1 gene. Functional analysis using a series of truncated luciferase reporter constructs demonstrated that the region from -172 bp to +19 bp constitutes the core promoter of the chicken SLC11A1 gene, with the segment from -172 bp to -36 bp being essential for basal transcriptional activity, the TATA-box was shown to synergistically enhance transcriptional efficiency when combined with this region. Prediction of transcription factor binding sites indicated that Sp1 exhibited the highest binding frequency within the core promoter, followed by C/EBPalpha and other factors. These findings define the fundamental regulatory architecture of the chicken SLC11A1 promoter, providing essential insights for future studies on the transcriptional regulation and biological functions of SLC11A1.Keywords: Chicken, SLC11A1 gene, Promoter, Transcriptional regulation

Introduction

SLC11A1 (NRAMP1) is a critical component of the innate immune network (Mudasir Ahmad, et al., 2018), encoding a transmembrane transporter localized to phagosomal and lysosomal membranes of macrophages. It regulates intracellular homeostasis of metal ions such as Fe²⁺ and Mn²⁺, thereby shaping the microbial survival niche and modulating reactive oxygen and nitrogen species production, which collectively influence host defense against intracellular pathogens such as Salmonella and Mycobacterium (Canonne-Hergaux, et al., 1999; Cunrath and Bumann, 2019). In livestock and poultry, SLC11A1 expression correlates strongly with immune competence and disease resistance, making it a prime candidate for molecular breeding (Chong, et al., 2023; Hu, et al., 2015). Chickens, a major agricultural species, are highly susceptible to intracellular pathogens such as Salmonella, resulting in significant economic losses (Mudasir Ahmad, et al., 2018). The SLC11A1 mediated innate immune response in macrophages serves as a critical defense mechanism against these infections. Notably, SLC11A1 is rapidly upregulated during acute Salmonella enteritidis infection in chicks, particularly in heterophils and the spleen, where its enhanced expression strengthens host immunity (Hu, et al., 2015). These findings highlight SLC11A1 as a key player in chicken antimicrobial defense and a promising target for precision breeding.

Despite its critical role in innate immunity, the transcriptional regulation of SLC11A1 remains poorly understood in chickens. The promoter, which controls spatiotemporal gene expression via binding of RNA polymerase II and transcription factors, has not been systematically characterized in chickens. While partial characterization exists in mammals (Chong, et al., 2023), functional conservation of regulatory mechanisms in chickens remains unclear. These knowledge gaps hinder a comprehensive understanding of the gene’s transcriptional regulation and impede its molecular application in disease-resistant breeding programs in chickens.

Here, we integrate bioinformatics analysis with dual-luciferase reporter assays using genomic sequences flanking the transcription start site (TSS) from NCBI. We identify the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene, define the synergistic role of the TATA-box with upstream sequences, and predict key transcription factor binding sites. These findings provide essential molecular insights into the transcriptional regulation of SLC11A1, laying a foundation for future precision breeding strategies in disease-resistant poultry.

Materials and methods

Primer design

Using the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), we retrieved the chicken SLC11A1 gene (GenBank ID: 395811). Based on the TSS of this gene, we selected the genomic region spanning −2090 bp to +486 bp for analysis and designed multiple pairs of primers for promoter truncation flanking this region. An NheI restriction site preceded by the protective base pair “cg” was incorporated at the 5′ end of each upstream primer, while a HindIII restriction site preceded by the protective bases “CCC” was added to the 5′ end of each downstream primer to ensure efficient enzymatic digestion. All primers were synthesized by BGI Co., Ltd., and their sequences are listed in Table 1.

Construction of luciferase vector

Chicken cecal tissue was collected and genomic DNA was extracted as template for amplification of the truncated promoter fragments via polymerase chain reaction (PCR). PCR products were analyzed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, followed by purification using a standard agarose gel DNA recovery kit (Tiangen Biotech, Beijing, China). The purified promoter fragments were then ligated into the NheI and HindIII sites of the pGL3-basic vector (Promega, Beijing, China). The ligation products were transformed into E. coli DH5alpha competent cells, and the resulting transformation mix was spread onto LB solid medium containing ampicillin (100 μg/mL). After incubation at 37 °C overnight, individual colonies were picked for colony PCR screening. Positive clones were selected based on PCR verification, expanded in culture, and subsequently used for plasmid extraction using the EndoFree Midi Plasmid Kit (Tiangen Biotech). The purified recombinant plasmids were stored at −20 °C for subsequent use. All cloned plasmids were confirmed by sequencing.

Cell culture, transfection, and luciferase assays

The chicken macrophage (HD11) cells was used as the experimental model. Cells were seeded into 24-well plates and cultured in complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Waltham, MA), 5% chicken serum (Solarbio, Beijing, China), 1% sodium pyruvate (Gibco), 1% l-glutamine (Gibco), 1% non-essential amino acids (Gibco), and 0.1% β-mercaptoethanol (Gibco). Cultures were maintained at 39 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. When cell confluency reached 60-70%, transient transfection was performed using the TransIT-X2® Dynamic Delivery System (Mirus, MIR6000). Each well received a total of 487.5 ng of the firefly luciferase reporter construct containing the truncated SLC11A1 promoter and 12.5 ng of the Renilla luciferase internal control plasmid. After 40 h of transfection, cells were lysed using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, E1910) to measure luciferase activities. The relative promoter activity was calculated as the ratio of firefly luciferase activity to Renilla luciferase activity. Each experimental group was performed in triplicate biological replicates to ensure reproducibility and statistical reliability.

Bioinformatics analysis of promoter

By analyzing the core cis-regulatory elements in the promoter region using the TSSW online tool (http://www.softberry.com/berry.phtml?topic=tssw&group=programs&subgroup=promoter). Transcription factor binding sites within the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene were predicted using the Alibaba 2 online tool (http://gene-regulation.com/pub/programs/alibaba2/index.html) under default settings.

Statistical analysis

Experimental data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD). Data analysis and visualization were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.2.1 software. Differences among multiple groups were assessed by one-way analysis of variance, followed by Duncan’s multiple range test for pairwise comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Preliminary identification of the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene using a dual-luciferase reporter assay

To preliminarily define the core functional region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene promoter, six truncated promoter fragments of varying lengths (F1 to F6) were constructed into recombinant luciferase reporter constructs. Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that fragments F4 (−672 bp to +486 bp) and F5 (−206 bp to +486 bp) exhibited significantly higher relative promoter activities (P < 0.05). Their activities were significantly greater than those of fragments F1 (−2090 bp to +486 bp), F2 (−1607 bp to +486 bp), F3 (−1176 bp to +486 bp), and F6 (+234 bp to +486 bp) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A). Based on these results, the core candidate promoter region was preliminarily delineated as −672 bp to +233 bp, and this fragment was selected for subsequent fine-scale truncation analysis.

To further refine the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene, a series of 5′-truncated luciferase reporter constructs were generated based on the initially identified candidate region (−672 bp to +233 bp). Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that constructs F7 to F12 (−672 bp to +233 bp, −571 bp to +233 bp, −474 bp to +233 bp, −377 bp to +233 bp, −275 bp to +233 bp, and −172 bp to +233 bp) all exhibited high promoter activity (Fig. 1B). Notably, all these active constructs contained the common fragment −172 bp to −36 bp. Conversely, when this fragment was absent, constructs F13 to F15 (−35 bp to +233 bp, +49 bp to +233 bp, and +138 bp to +233 bp) completely lost promoter activity (Fig. 1B), suggesting that the −172 bp to −36 bp region is essential for transcriptional initiation of SLC11A1. To determine whether the −172 bp to −36 bp region identified above possesses independent promoter activity, two additional constructs were generated: F17 containing only the −172 bp to −36 bp fragment, and F16 containing only the −672 bp to −173 bp fragment (lacking −172 bp to −36 bp). Dual-luciferase assays demonstrated that the fragment F16 lost promoter activity when the −172 bp to −36 bp segment was removed, whereas the −172 bp to −36 bp fragment alone was sufficient to drive robust reporter gene expression (Fig. 1C). These findings collectively demonstrate that the −172 bp to −36 bp region constitutes the indispensable core element required for basal promoter activity of the chicken SLC11A1 gene.

Functional analysis of the TATA-box element

Bioinformatic prediction via TSSW confirmed canonical TATA-box located at −31 bp to −28 bp upstream SLC11A1 TSS (Fig. 1D), which aligns with the classic location of eukaryotic TATA-box elements (Bucher, 1990). However, functional deletion analysis revealed that fragments downstream of −36 bp (e.g., F13-F15), which fully contained the TATA-box element, exhibited no significant promoter activity when the core region −172 bp to −36 bp was absent (Fig. 1B). Conversely, the fragment F12 covering both −172 bp to −36 bp and the TATA-box showed significantly higher promoter activity than the fragment F17 containing only −172 bp to −36 bp (Fig. 1C), suggesting functional synergy between the upstream −172 bp to −36 bp region and the TATA-box.

To precisely delineate the core promoter boundaries, fine truncation constructs covering −172 bp to +19 bp, −172 bp to +79 bp, −172 bp to +131 bp, and −172 bp to +186 bp (F18-F21) were generated. Dual-luciferase assay results indicated that the F18 fragment exhibited the highest promoter activity, approximately 8.6 times that of the F17 fragment. Further extension to F19, F20, and F21 resulted in varying degrees of decreased promoter activity (Fig. 1E).

Based on these findings, the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 gene was ultimately defined as −172 bp to +19 bp. Within this region, the −172 bp to −36 bp segment is essential for maintaining basal transcriptional activity. The TATA-box element located at −31 bp to −28 bp synergizes with this upstream core region to significantly enhance transcription initiation efficiency. Together, these elements constitute a precise and efficient transcriptional initiation regulatory module for this gene.

Prediction of transcription factor binding motif in the SLC11A1 core promoter

Transcription factors play an indispensable role in gene expression regulation. Subsequently, we predicted transcription factors binding sites within the core promoter region of the chicken SLC11A1 and quantified their abundance. The analysis identified Sp1 as the predominant binding transcription factor, with 13 binding sites. C/EBPalpha ranked second, with 3 binding sites detected. All other transcription factors, including NF-kappaB2, RXR-beta, c-Ets-1, T3R-alpha, YY1, T3R, and REB1, were found to have only one binding site each (Fig. 1F). These binding characteristics further illuminate the regulatory mechanisms underlying the SLC11A1 gene. Sp1, a ubiquitously expressed general transcription factor, regulates basal transcription by binding to GC-rich elements in DNA (Singh, et al., 2012). Its high frequency of predicted binding sites in the core promoter suggests that Sp1 may be a key regulator of the basal expression of SLC11A1. C/EBPalpha is a key transcription factor driving macrophage differentiation (Laiosa, et al., 2006), and its presence in the core promoter of chicken SLC11A1 suggests macrophage-specific regulation. Binding sites for NF-kappaB2, activated during pathogen challenge (Gupta, et al., 2010), and c-Ets-1, a lymphoid-enriched factor involved in immune cell development (Russell and Garrett-Sinha, 2010), further indicate integration of innate and adaptive signals. Given SLC11A1's critical role in resistance to intracellular pathogens, this regulatory network may fine-tune its expression during host defense in chickens. However, experimental validation is still required to confirm whether these computationally predicted transcription factors are functionally involved in the transcriptional regulation of the chicken SLC11A1 gene.

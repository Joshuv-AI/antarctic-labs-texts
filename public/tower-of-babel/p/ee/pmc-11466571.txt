Short-term photovoltaic forecasting model with parallel multi-channel optimization based on improved dung beetle algorithm
Authors: Keyong Hu, Chunyuan Lang, Zheyi Fu, Lidong Wang, Yang Feng, Ben Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate prediction of photovoltaic(PV) generation plays a vital role in power dispatching and is one of the effective ways to ensure the safe operation of power grid. In response to this issue, this paper improves the Rhino beetle optimization algorithm (LSDBO) using Logistic chaos mapping and sine function strategies an optimizes the PCL-MHA model (running CNN-MHA and LSTM-MHA models in parallel, PCL; Multi-Head-Attention, MHA) to enhance predictive accuracy. Various experiments were conducted using historical data from the Alice Springs PV system in Australia. PV component technologies comprise monocrystalline silicon and polycrystalline silicon, with array fixed on the ground. Through experiments, the proposed model in this paper achieved the best results in 16 metrics under different weather conditions, with average values of 98.43 % for R2, 2.69 % for MSE, 7.8 % for MAE, and 15.09 % for RMSE. Compared to other models, an average improvement of 2.41 %, 6.6 %, 7.77 %, and 11.21 % in these metrics.Keywords: Prediction, PV, LSDBO algorithm, Parallel multi-channel

Introduction

The development and utilization of renewable energy have become key strategies for countries worldwide to achieve sustainable development. Moreover, the countries globally have established some important goals of peaking carbon emissions and achieving carbon neutrality in order to address climate change and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. PV generation plays a critical role in achieving these goals as a clean and renewable energy source. However, the output of PV is subject to fluctuations due to the factors such as solar radiation, weather conditions, and seasonal variations. Therefore, accurate prediction of PV output is essential for energy system planning and management. Precise forecasting of PV can assist grid operators and energy planners in scheduling and optimizing energy supply, ensuring the stable operation of the power system. By predicting the supply of PV, it is possible to better integrate and coordinate other energy resources, balance supply and demand, and improve energy utilization efficiency, thereby driving the realization of carbon neutrality and peaking the goals of carbon emissions.

At that time, the methods for PV prediction were mainly represented by physical methods, statistical methods, and machine learning. Among them, the physical method was a method of predicting PV generation based on physical models or electronic component models. For example, A model of electronic components in PV semiconductor equipment that takes solar radiation and its distribution as inputs [1]. A simple physical model was used to predict PV generation based on meteorological data and satellite detection data [2]. By considering the electrical characteristics of PV panels and semiconductor physics principles, a complex physical model was established that took into account the structural principles of the PV panels and weather factors [3]. Although the physical prediction method had some advantages, such as strong interpretability and applicability to the different environmental conditions, it also had some drawbacks. For instance, establishing complex physical models required a significant amount of expertise and technical support. There were limitations in dealing with complex weather conditions and dynamic PV systems. The physical method was unable to consider certain complex nonlinear and stochastic factors, such as cloud shading effects and sudden weather changes. On the other hand, statistical methods were based on historical data and statistical principles for modeling and prediction. It typically utilized past observation data to infer future PV generation. Examples included Markov chains [4] and fuzzy theory [5]. Most statistical methods involved linear predictions and required extensive data processing. Statistical methods were sensitive to abnormal weather conditions and were not conducive to accurate predictions.

To address the limitations of physical and statistical methods, machine learning methods have emerged. Although machine learning has addressed some of the issues associated with physical and statistical methods, continuous improvements are still being made to enhance prediction accuracy. The main directions of improvement focus on the analysis and extraction of key features and the construction of neural network models.

The analysis and extraction of key features primarily involved extracting more important information from existing environmental factor features. For example, mutual information entropy correlation analysis was used to extract weather fluctuation features [6]. Similar samples were searched for using K-Means++ and then trained [7]. Convolutional neural network(CNN) were utilized to extract meteorological features [8]. All these studies attempted to extract features from meteorological data and use them for training PV prediction models. Meteorological data generally reflected the overall environmental conditions on a large scale and may not have accurately reflected the specific conditions around the PV panels. In this paper, more accurate environmental data was obtained by utilizing sensors on the PV panels to monitor parameters such as light intensity and temperature in real-time [9].

The construction and optimization of neural network models can be categorized into the following approaches.

(1)Stacking single-channel models: Artificial intelligence methods are currently the mainstream approach for photovoltaic power prediction, possessing advantages such as high accuracy and strong generalization capabilities [10]. Lee et al. [11] employed two types of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) models to predict hourly photovoltaic power generation, with experimental results showing significant improvements over Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Deep Neural Networks (DNN). As development progressed, it was discovered that RNN networks can suffer from exponential decay or growth of gradients when the sequence length is long during training, making it challenging to effectively update network parameters. Ahn et al. [12] utilized Long short-term memory (LSTM) networks based on RNNs for short-term photovoltaic power prediction, addressing issues like gradient vanishing and exploding gradients present in traditional RNNs. However, as network depth increases, LSTM performance tends to decrease. Agga A et al. used CNN to capture spatial features and local details in the data, but the local receptive field of the convolutional kernels limits their ability to perceive global information, leading to suboptimal peak prediction. To address the problems of decreasing LSTM performance with increasing network depth and the weak global information perception of convolutional networks, the CNN-LSTM model was proposed [13]. The emergence of single-channel model stacking methods provided solutions to existing problems in single-model predictions, leading to the development of numerous model stacking approaches such as CNN-GRU [14] and CNN-LSTM-Attention [15]. Currently, these methods have been proven to enhance prediction accuracy. However, with continuous model stacking and increasing network depth, the phenomena of gradient explosion and vanishing gradients become more prevalent. Since the optimization problem of deep learning models is typically non-convex, with multiple local optima and saddle points, increasing the number of models also increases the quantity of local optima and saddle points. This often results in models getting trapped in local optima and saddle points, thus inhibiting the achievement of the best predictive accuracy.

Background theories

Dung Beetle Optimizer

DBO(Dung Beetle Optimizer, DBO) [21] is a swarm intelligence optimization algorithm that is based on the distinct behaviors exhibited by individual beetles in a population of dung beetles. This algorithm categorizes dung beetles into four types based on their behaviors: roller beetles, breeder beetles, forager beetles, and thief beetles.

The most renowned behavior of dung beetles is "rolling the ball." Long Short-Term Memory network

PV generation exhibits significant periodicity, indicating that the data has temporal correlation over time. This temporal correlation is crucial in PV prediction models. As a result, early researchers utilized Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) as the fundamental model for predicting PV generation, as RNNs are capable of effectively capturing dependencies between features when processing time series data. However, RNNs face challenges in learning long-term dependencies during the training process, leading to the vanishing or exploding gradient problem, where early inputs are forgotten. To address these issues, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) was introduced, incorporating specialized memory cells that can better retain and extract long-term dependency information. The introduction of gating mechanisms resolves the problem of vanishing or exploding gradients. The use of a forget gate allows the network to learn whether to forget the previous cell state, addressing the difficulty of retaining information from early inputs. Convolutional neural network

CNN mainly consists of input layer, convolutional layer, pooling layer, fully connected layer, and output layer. The most important parts for feature extraction are the convolutional layer and the pooling layer. In the convolutional layer, convolutional kernels operate on time series data, extracting local features by sliding windows, where the size of the kernel determines the number of time steps covered in the time series data, enabling the capture of feature information at different time scales. Pooling layers are used for downsampling, retaining key features to achieve spatial extraction of data. The structure of the CNN network is illustrated in Fig. 3.

Multi-head-attention mechanism

In traditional neural network models, the encoder-decoder structure faces some issues when processing input sequences. It encodes the input sequence into a fixed-length hidden vector and then decodes it into an output sequence. When dealing with longer sequences, because all input sequences are given the same weight, the model cannot distinguish the importance of different input sequences, and it is difficult to retain key information, which affects the performance of the model.

To address these problems, a multi-head attention mechanism, enhanced and fused by the scaled dot-product attention mechanism, is utilized. It defines multiple parallel attention subspaces to capture complex dependencies between the data. Each attention head can simultaneously learn the relationships of sequence elements across the different time dimensions. In addition, computing multiple attention vectors in parallel enables in-depth detection of dependencies in input content from multiple perspectives. This enhances the model's linearity, helps mitigate overfitting, and improves generalization. The schematic diagram of the multi-head attention mechanism is illustrated in Fig. 4.

Algorithm improvement

Logistic chaos mapping strategy

The DBO algorithm utilizes a random generation method for initializing the population. However, this approach can lead to increased overlap among individuals in the population, making it challenge to maintain diversity. After random distribution, the population tends to cluster in a specific area, limiting its coverage of the entire variable space and restricting the search range. Furthermore, the clustering of the population increases the likelihood of getting trapped in local minima, impacting the convergence accuracy of the algorithm and resulting in local optima. To address these issues, this study introduces the Logistic chaotic mapping strategy. The Logistic chaotic mapping possesses inherent chaotic properties and can simulate the behavior of low-dimensional turbulence. It leverages the sensitive dependence on initial values characteristic of chaotic systems, enabling a more uniform distribution of individuals within the population and better coverage of the feasible domain. Moreover, the Logistic chaotic mapping retains the unpredictability of dynamic systems, partially capturing the stochastic nature of the problem. By incorporating the Logistic mapping for population initialization, the population gains are improved exploration capabilities. The different population initializations are depicted in Fig. 5.

Random initialization population(a), Initialization population of Logistic chaotic map(b).

In Fig. 5(a) and (b), the population size is fixed at 20, with a ratio of 1:2:1:1 for Rolling ball dung beetle, Female dung beetles, Foraging dung beetle, and Thief dung beetle, respectively. In Fig. 5(a), the population is randomly initialized, resulting in a majority of individuals clustering along the diagonal. This leads to a limited exploration of the areas surrounding the diagonal when searching for the global optimum, while the top-left and bottom-right regions remain largely unexplored. However, by incorporating the Logistic chaotic mapping strategy in the original algorithm, as shown in Fig. 5(b), the population becomes uniformly distributed across the entire solution space. During the subsequent iterations, the population can thoroughly explore the solution space. This approach effectively addresses the issues encountered during population initialization in the DWO algorithm. The strategy ensures a uniform distribution of the population and enhances the algorithm's exploration capabilities and convergence accuracy.

Additionally, the Logistic chaotic mapping introduces a random number sequence with uniqueness and repeatability. Given a seed value, the resulting random number sequence is reliable and reproducible, thus ensuring the algorithm's stability.

The "dancing" behavior of sine functions

The Rolling ball dung beetle exhibits both rolling behavior and "dancing" behavior. In the traditional DBO algorithm, the "dancing" behavior of the Rolling ball dung beetle is obtained using the tangent function to determine the new rolling direction. However, the range of the tangent function is (-∞, +∞), which may cause the beetle to move a significant distance away from its current position when iteratively determining the next forward position. As a result, it may fail to thoroughly explore the surrounding space and potentially miss the global optimum.To address this issue, in addition to Equation (1), this study replaces the tangent function with the sine function. The sine function has a range of [−1, 1]. By multiplying it with the sine function, the next iterated position remains in the vicinity of the original position. On this basis, the Logistic chaotic mapping is introduced, which not only slows down the contraction of the search space but also allows for thorough exploration of the search space, avoiding getting stuck in local optima. Model design

This paper proposes the PCL-MHA network architecture, which combines the strengths of CNN-MHA and LSTM-MHA models to improve the accuracy of PV prediction. The CNN model possesses powerful local spatial awareness, effectively capturing local features within input data. Specifically, by sliding convolutional kernels over every possible position in the input data, element-wise multiplication is performed, followed by summation of the results. This summation yields the output value of the convolution operation, also known as a feature map. Each element in the feature map represents the local feature at that position in the data. Each receptive field corresponds to the local features in each space, and as the convolutional kernel slides continuously, spatial features and relationships are constantly extracted. CNN typically employs convolutional kernels of different sizes to capture features of various scales and focus more on local dependencies. In this model, a multi-head attention mechanism is introduced to selectively aggregate these features, strengthening key feature representations. Each attention head can focus on different features, comprehensively capturing global dependencies.

Similarly, the core of the LSTM model is the memory cell. Through control of the forget gate, input gate, and output gate, the memory cell can selectively retain or forget past information and integrate current input with past memory. This enables LSTM to better understand temporal dependencies in time series data. The memory cell achieves long-term states through the forget gate, preserving all past memories to facilitate long-term memory, thereby better capturing long-term dependencies in sequences.In this model, a multi-head attention mechanism is introduced, allowing the model to automatically adjust attention allocation based on the importance and relevance of the data. This resolves issues where important time steps in long sequences may be forgotten or blurred by LSTM. The combined model structure used in this paper is illustrated in Fig. 7.

Datasets description

The experimental data in this paper were provided by Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre (DKASC) [22], and the data near Alice Springs was selected as the case study in this paper. Each data set includes various environmental factors and PV output power data. The environmental factors include power, temperature, humidity, global horizontal irradiance, diffuse horizontal irradiance, and precipitation. The data was collected with a time resolution of 5 min, resulting in 288 data points per day.

To further observe the fitting between different models and actual power, we chose to zoom in on a specific region of the images. As shown in Fig. 9(a) and Table 7, under clear sky weather conditions, the sunlight intensity remains relatively stable without cloud cover obstructing the sun. Consequently, the solar panels receive a stable amount of solar energy, allowing each model to predict the power fluctuation curve accurately, with R2 values all exceeding 95 %. However, individual models like CNN and LSTM struggle to capture the complex nonlinear relationships within the data, resulting in lower predictive accuracy compared to other models. For example, the RMSE values for CNN and LSTM are 36.7 % and 22.3 %, respectively. In contrast, stacked single-channel models like CNN-GRU and CNN-LSTM-Attention show better results. By combining CNN with GRU or LSTM-Attention and leveraging the strengths of each model, these two models enhance the nonlinear capabilities and information extraction, achieving RMSE values of 20.26 % and 21.92 %, respectively, representing an average improvement of 9.24 % and 7.58 % over CNN and LSTM. LSDBO-PCL-MHA and DBO-PCL-MHA, utilizing parallel training structures and algorithmic hyperparameter optimization, achieve RMSE values of 9.85 % and 11.48 %, respectively, resulting in enhancements of 10.41 %–26.85 % and 8.78 %–25.22 %. Additionally, after improvements to DBO, there is a 1.63 % increase in RMSE. These results effectively demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed algorithmic enhancements and the feasibility of the parallel training network structure. Comparing CNN-GRU, CNN-LSTM-Attention, CNN, LSTM, and DBO-PCL-MHA models under clear sky conditions, our model shows an average improvement of 1.4 %, 4.86 %, 10.29 %, and 12.89 % in R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE, respectively.

As shown in Fig. 9 (b), (c), and (d).When facing various weather conditions such as cloudy, partly cloudy to sunny, or rainy days, the continuous fluctuations in irradiance and partial shading of some PV panels directly impact the amount of solar energy received, leading to significant fluctuations in the power output of solar panels. Especially on rainy days, pollutants like rainwater and mud falling on the surface of the panels affect the efficiency of light absorption, causing a significant decrease in power generation. In the above situations, whether using a single model or a stacked single-channel model, it is difficult to achieve satisfactory results in prediction. The rapid changes in irradiance and temperature increase the uncertainty of the data. The overly simplistic structure of a single model makes it challenging to accurately capture the patterns and trends in the data. The average performance metrics R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE for CNN and LSTM models are 95.99 %, 7.22 %, 16.13 %, 24.79 %, and 94.05 %, 11.37 %, 16.22 %, 30.93 %, respectively. Stacked models, being single-channel, pass the output of the previous model directly to the next model, leading to issues such as poor information flow, information decay, and signal interference. For instance, if the first model fails to accurately capture the complex patterns of irradiance and temperature variations, it will transmit inaccurate information to the next model. This inaccurate information transmission can affect the learning ability of subsequent models, increase prediction errors, and consequently reduce the overall accuracy of the model. The average performance metrics R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE for CNN-GRU and CNN-LSTM-Attention models are 95.56 %, 8.96 %, 14.9 %, 27.26 %, and 92.98 %, 14.28 %, 19.61 %, 34.55 %, respectively. From the above metrics, it can be observed that as data uncertainty increases, continuously stacking modules does not effectively improve prediction accuracy. Instead, it can lead to a decrease in prediction accuracy during training due to issues like poor information flow.

The model in this paper optimized parameters through the LSDBO algorithm, changed the model structure from a stacked single-channel model to a parallel multi-channel model, and introduced the MHA mechanism to reduce redundancy and noise in information transmission. Finally, the outputs of the two channels were simultaneously fed into the MHA mechanism for weighted fusion, combining the advantages of both models to improve the efficiency and accuracy of information transmission. As shown in Table 7, the average performance metrics R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE for LSDBO-PCL-MHA and DBO-PCL-MHA are 97.82 %, 3.26 %, 8.44 %, 16.83 % and 97.02 %, 4.65 %, 9.98 %, 19.9 %, respectively, showing different degrees of improvement compared to single and stacked models. By only improving the DBO algorithm, while keeping the model architecture unchanged, the metrics have improved by 0.8 %, 1.39 %, 1.54 %, and 3.07 % respectively.

In summary, under partly cloudy, cloudy, or rainy conditions, the model proposed in this paper has shown an average improvement of 2.75 %, 8.2 %, 10.58 %, and 15.67 % in R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE compared to other models, as well as 3.59 %, 9.4 %, 8.61 %, and 13.98 %, and 1.9 %, 2.75 %, 1.59 %, and 2.31 %. These data demonstrate that by altering the model structure and optimizing algorithm parameters, the model exhibits strong generalization and robustness, delivering high predictive accuracy under complex weather conditions.

It can be seen from Fig. 10 (a) that under all weather conditions, the indicators of the original model are better than those of the post-ablation model. Taking a sunny day as an example, when only MHA module is removed, MSE, MAE and RMSE decrease by 1.93 % on average, because this module can not only improve the model's processing capacity for sequence data, but also reduce the risk of information loss. Therefore, the reason for the decrease is very likely that the model's processing capacity for sequence data has decreased. After removing only LSDBO algorithm, MSE, MAE and RMSE decreased by 0.8 % on average. After the removal of LSDBO algorithm, the hyperparameters of the model need to be updated through experience and continuous experiments. Although this approach can be very close to the global optimal hyperparameters, it will cost a lot of time. When MHA module and LSDBO algorithm are removed and parallel training structure is modified to serial training structure, MSE, MAE and RMSE decrease by 10.55 % on average. It is not difficult to see from the above results that the improvement of the model by a single module is quite limited. When several modules are used in combination, the prediction accuracy of the model can be significantly improved. This also proves the synergistic effect of each module in this model. LSDBO algorithm optimizes the hyperparameters of the model, saves a lot of time and cost, and can accurately optimize the parameters to the global optimal. MHA module can effectively integrate the feature information of different locations, and help CNN and LSTM model better understand and process sequence data. The parallel multi-channel structure can prevent the problem of poor information transmission and information loss in the training process, and can combine the advantages of CNN-MHA and LSTM-MHA. Conclusion

The paper proposed a short-term PV forecasting model based on LSDBO optimization, named PCL-MHA, and conducted multiple experiments using historical data from the Alice Springs PVsystem in Australia. Comparative results show that, through the optimization of the LSDBO algorithm and improvements in the model structure, the proposed model outperformed other models under four weather conditions: sunny, sunny to partly cloudy, cloudy, and rainy, with an average increase of 2.41 %, 6.6 %, 7.77 %, and 11.21 % in R2, MSE, MAE, and RMSE metrics, respectively. Furthermore, verification through ablation experiments demonstrated that removing a single module led to an average decrease of 0.8 %–1.93 % in metrics, while removing multiple modules resulted in an average decrease of 10.55 %. This proves that MHA, LSDBO, and parallel training network structures play varying roles in the model, collectively striving to enhance model performance and prediction accuracy through parameter optimization, feature extraction, dependency capture, and parallel training.

In summary, the experimental results yield the following research findings.

3.Combining MHA with CNN and LSTM enhances the model's understanding of complex data relationships, thereby improving model performance.

In conclusion, the model presented in this paper demonstrates a high level of predictive accuracy under different weather conditions, providing a reliable reference for grid operations, optimized energy supply, and rational scheduling. This approach introduces new ideas and methods to the field of PV prediction, showcasing a certain level of innovation and practical value.

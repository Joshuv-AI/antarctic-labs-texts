Letter to the Editor Regarding the Article “High-Definition Intravascular Ultrasound Versus Optical Coherence Tomography: Lumen Size and Plaque Morphology”
Authors: Hao-Wei Lee, Yoshinobu Onuma, Patrick W Serruys
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the study comparing high-definition intravascular ultrasound (HD IVUS) and optical coherence tomography (OCT) for lumen assessment.1 The authors are to be commended for their comprehensive design integrating both in vitro and clinical data. However, we would like to raise a methodological consideration regarding the interpretation of “accuracy.”

In the study, microcomputed tomography (μCT)-derived measurements from silicone models were used as the reference standard. Although μCT provides high-resolution imaging and enables 3-dimensional reconstruction, μCT-based measurements are not direct physical ground truth but reconstructed representations subject to segmentation variability, threshold selection, voxel resolution limits, and reconstruction artifacts. Thus, μCT should be considered a high-quality reference modality rather than an absolute standard.

In contrast, a prior validation study using precision-drilled plexiglass phantoms with known physical dimensions demonstrated that OCT measurements closely match true lumen size under controlled conditions.2 Phantom geometry was designed using stereolithography-based modeling and manufactured with high precision. Lumen dimensions were verified using optical calibration under 40-fold magnification, achieving a tolerance of approximately 0.003 mm. Ex vivo OCT measurements showed an almost perfect correlation with true phantom dimensions (r = 1.000), supporting the phantom geometry as a reliable ground truth (Figure 1). Accordingly, in such settings, ground truth is defined by the actual physical geometry with minimal uncertainty.

In the present study, HD IVUS was closer to μCT-derived measurements, whereas OCT showed systematic underestimation. However, this may reflect higher agreement between HD IVUS and μCT rather than true superiority in accuracy. If μCT deviates from actual dimensions due to reconstruction-related biases, agreement with μCT does not necessarily indicate accuracy relative to true lumen size.

Therefore, the conclusion that HD IVUS provides more accurate lumen assessment should be interpreted with caution. A more precise interpretation is that HD IVUS demonstrates closer agreement with μCT-derived measurements, whereas OCT exhibits systematic bias relative to this reference. Future studies incorporating physical phantoms or hybrid validation frameworks may better define true accuracy.

In summary, the definition of ground truth is pivotal in comparative imaging studies. Distinguishing between agreement with a reference modality and true measurement accuracy is essential to avoid misinterpretation.

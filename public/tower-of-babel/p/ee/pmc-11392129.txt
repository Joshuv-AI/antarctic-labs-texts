A commentary on ‘Application and challenges of ChatGPT in interventional surgery’
Authors: Zhiwei Song, Yiya Xu, Yingchao He, Yinzhou Wang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The field of artificial intelligence (AI) has undergone a boom in recent years, and the potential of ChatGPT in surgical applications, particularly in interventional procedures, is promising1,2. This topic has recently been summarized and extensively discussed by Wu et al.3. This paper is renowned for its comprehensive explanation of how ChatGPT can assist healthcare professionals in preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative interventional procedures, as well as in the field of medical education and medical image analysis. However, several critical issues remain to be addressed when implementing ChatGPT in interventional procedures.

Firstly, GPT-4, recognized as a language model4, lacks direct physical interaction capabilities, preventing its direct involvement in surgical operations or tasks requiring physical intervention. Moreover, while GPT-4 can process input data such as textual descriptions and numbers, it cannot directly collect data from medical devices like monitors and ultrasound equipment, relying instead on other systems to convert this data into processable textual or numerical formats. Integrating ChatGPT with robotic surgical systems and medical monitoring systems can partially address this limitation: ChatGPT can offer decision support to the robotic system, which then executes the physical operations. Integrating GPT-4 into existing medical monitoring systems aids doctors in monitoring and evaluating patient status through the analysis of real-time data, potentially enabling the timely detection of emergencies.

Secondly, the performance of GPT-4 critically depends on the quality and breadth of its training data, potentially impacting AI outcomes5. Obtaining high-quality medical data is often challenging due to privacy concerns and strict regulatory protections. This necessitates partnerships with hospitals, research organizations, and healthcare providers to access clinical data. Medical data can exhibit biases, for instance, data concerning specific groups might be overrepresented or overlooked. Such biases can result in uneven AI model performance in real-world applications, particularly affecting underrepresented groups. Therefore, it is imperative to amalgamate data from diverse regions and populations to augment the dataset’s breadth and representativeness. Continuous advancements in medical technology and treatments necessitate using data that is not only high-quality but also regularly updated to reflect current medical standards and practices. This entails regular updates to the training dataset to ensure the AI model remains aligned with the latest medical knowledge and techniques. Simultaneously, ongoing validation and testing guarantee the accuracy and reliability of the model’s output.

Finally, data privacy and security are paramount when processing medical data that involves personal health information via GPT-4 or any other AI technology6. The following measures can help safeguard data privacy and security: (1) Implement strong encryption to protect data both in storage and during transit. Ensure that all sensitive data is encrypted prior to leaving the local environment and that access to decrypted data is limited to authorized users. (2) Minimize data exposure: Adhere to the principle of data minimization by processing only the necessary data required for a specific function. For instance, de-identified or anonymized data should be utilized for purposes not requiring direct patient identification. (3) Strict access control: Ensure that only personnel with the necessary authorization have access to sensitive data. (4) Compliance with regional regulations: Ensure that all data processing activities adhere to the relevant laws and regulations of the specific region or country. (5) Ongoing risk assessment and training: Regularly conduct risk assessments for data protection and security to identify and mitigate potential risks and threats. Additionally, provide training for all employees handling sensitive data on data protection and privacy protocols.

In summary, the study by Wu et al. holds significant reference value for the future application of AI in interventional procedures. With ongoing technological advancements, enhanced data management and regulatory compliance, and thorough adaptation and training of healthcare professionals, ChatGPT is poised to have a promising future in surgery.

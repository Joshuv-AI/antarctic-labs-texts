In this issue, 1 case report and 5 original articles have been published.

1. Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity Caused by Non-Anesthesiologists: A Narrative Review of Case Reports.

Sohn JT, et al.

This article analyzed 53 case reports (59 patients) of local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) caused by non-anesthesiologists. Lidocaine was the most frequent agent (59.33%), with overdose being the primary cause (67.8%). Subcutaneous infiltration (38.98%) and topical application (35.59%) were common routes. CNS symptoms predominated. Lipid emulsion therapy yielded 100% recovery versus 73.8% with supportive care only. Education on dose limits, patient risk factors, and ensuring lipid emulsion availability are essential preventive measures.

2. Research Ethics Challenges in Pandemic Korea and Their Implications for the Revised 2024 Declaration of Helsinki.

Kim OJ, et al.

This study investigated ethical challenges in South Korea’s COVID-19 research and their implications for the 2024 revised Declaration of Helsinki. Using a scoping review framework, ten major issues were identified, including participant safety, rapid IRB review, inclusion of vulnerable groups, and consent adaptation. The study demonstrates how the 2024 revision—highlighting public health ethics, community engagement, and scientific rigor—responds to these challenges and provides strategies to strengthen ethical research governance in future pandemics.

3. Gradual Increase in Lung Cancer Risk Due to Particulate Matter Exposure in Patients With Pulmonary Function Impairments: A Nationwide Korean Database Analysis.

Lim JU, et al.

This study investigated the interaction between long-term PM2.5 exposure and lung function impairment on lung cancer risk in 43,973 Koreans from KNHANES (2007–2020). Higher PM2.5 levels were associated with increased lung cancer risk (ORs, 1.41–1.68 across quartiles). The effect was amplified among individuals with impaired lung function—ORs of 2.74 for obstruction and 3.43 for PRISm in the highest quartile. These findings highlight PM2.5’s carcinogenic potential, particularly in those with obstructive respiratory conditions.

4. A Multicenter Study on Symptomatic Neonatal Herpes Simplex Virus Infection in Korea From 2008 to 2017.

Kim YJ, et al.

This study investigated the incidence and clinical features of neonatal herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection in Korea through a multicenter retrospective review (2008–2017) across 12 hospitals. Sixteen neonates were identified, estimating an incidence of 1 per 7,888 hospitalized newborns. HSV-1 accounted for 62.5% of cases, and 68.8% involved CNS disease. Two infants (12.5%) with disseminated infection died. Despite rarity, neonatal HSV infection in Korea shows notable mortality, underscoring the need for early recognition and management.

5. Epidemiological Trends and Clinical Features of Trichophyton rubrum Infections: A 10-Year Retrospective Review of 38,391 Cases (2014–2023).

Lee WJ, et al.

This study analyzed 38,391 cases of Trichophyton rubrum infection in Korea from 2014 to 2023 to assess incidence trends and epidemiologic features. T. rubrum accounted for over 90% of dermatophyte infections, showing a slight annual decline. The highest incidence occurred in men in their 50s, with a marked summer peak. Tinea pedis was the most common presentation, followed by tinea unguium and tinea cruris. Targeted prevention for older males during summer may reduce infection burden.

6. Systemic Vasculopathy Associated With Homozygous RNF213 Mutation Presenting as Peripheral Pulmonary Artery Stenosis and Coronary Vasospasm Without Moyamoya Disease: A Case Report.

Kim DH, et al.

This is a case of a 19-year-old man with exertional dyspnea and chest pain who carried a homozygous RNF213 p.R4810K mutation but no evidence of moyamoya disease. This case expands the phenotype of RNF213-related systemic vasculopathy beyond cerebral involvement and highlights the need for genetic testing in young patients with unexplained vascular disease.

High‐Detectivity MOCVD‐Epitaxial h‐BN‐Based Vacuum UV Photodetectors With Pt Nanoparticle‐Engineered Localized Surface Plasmon Resonance Enhancement
Authors: Zhiwei Gao, Yufan Wei, Weijie Liu, Yongming Zhao, Zhengang Liang, Yuhui Zeng, Zhencheng Li, Yang Peng, Feng Wu, Hao‐Chung Kuo, Jiangnan Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study addresses fabrication challenges in hexagonal boron nitride (h‐BN)‐based vacuum ultraviolet photodetectors (VUV PDs) by implementing platinum nanoparticle (Pt NP)‐induced localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) effects to enhance photoresponse characteristics. Size‐controlled Pt NPs (32 nm, 50 nm, 85 nm) are synthesized on metalorganic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD)‐derived h‐BN surfaces through a cost‐effective dewetting process, with systematic evaluation under 185 nm VUV irradiation. The 32‐nm Pt NP‐enhanced device demonstrates optimal performance, achieving 1.02 mA/W responsivity (143% improvement) and 2.95 × 1011 Jones specific detectivity (117% enhancement) at 20 V bias. Numerical simulations confirm that LSPR mechanisms intensify localized electric fields and photon absorption efficiency. This work presents the first LSPR‐enhanced h‐BN VUV photodetector utilizing Pt nanoparticles (NPs). The proposed design effectively addresses responsivity limitations in two‐dimensional material‐based detectors. Furthermore, it creates new opportunities for advancing extreme‐environment sensing and imaging technologies. The methodology maintains compatibility with scalable MOCVD fabrication processes, with performance improvements directly correlating to precise NP size optimization, suggesting plasmonic engineering as a critical pathway for next‐generation optoelectronic devices.Keywords: boron nitride, localized surface plasmon resonance, MOCVD, ultrawide‐bandgap semiconductors, VUV photodetector

Introduction

Photodetectors operating in the vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) spectrum (10–200 nm wavelength) play significant roles across diverse disciplines such as optics, materials science, information science, biomedicine, environmental science, and space technology, including solar storm monitoring, deep‐space communications, and analysis of planetary atmospheric constituents [1, 2, 3, 4]. In contrast to conventional ultraviolet chromatographic systems, wide‐bandgap semiconductor (WBGS)‐based VUV photodetectors exhibit more extensive application potential owing to their small footprint, low operating voltages, and excellent spectral selectivity [2, 5, 6]. Present investigations of WBGS materials for VUV photodetection are predominantly centered on GaN, diamond, and Ga2O3 [7, 8, 9, 10]. Nevertheless, the constrained bandgaps of these materials hinder the realization of selective response to VUV radiation. AlN and h‐BN have garnered widespread interest due to their outstanding optoelectronic characteristics. AlN has been thoroughly studied for this purpose, as evidenced in prior studies [1, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15]. In contrast, BN demonstrates a significantly larger bandgap (∼5.97 eV) that is particularly advantageous for VUV detection. Its band‐edge absorption coefficient reaches 7 × 105 cm−1, tripling that of AlN, while maintaining robust thermal stability and radiation tolerance. These combined properties position BN as a promising candidate material for constructing high‐performance VUV PDs. Yet, studies concerning BN thin‐film‐based photodetectors are still comparatively scarce [16, 17, 18]. Mechanically exfoliated h‐BN monolayers and few‐layer systems exhibit exceptional crystallinity and remarkable VUV detection performance. However, achieving precise thickness control and dimensional uniformity during fabrication remains a critical challenge [6, 19]. These technical limitations significantly impede their practical implementation in advanced 2D optoelectronic devices. Although scalable techniques including reactive magnetron sputtering, low‐pressure chemical vapor deposition (LPCVD), and metal‐organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) successfully generate large‐area h‐BN films with controlled stacking architecture, critical material limitations persist. The deposited films frequently contain disordered phases—combining amorphous, turbostratic, and polycrystalline domains—that fundamentally compromise detector responsivity. Furthermore, these structural imperfections continue to hinder practical VUV detection performance, necessitating substantial material engineering improvements [20, 21]. Consequently, innovative processing technologies must be developed to improve the performance of photodetectors based on bottom‐up synthesized h‐BN thin films. Recent advances have revealed that metallic nanoparticle (NP)‐activated localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) phenomena exhibit remarkable capabilities for boosting the optoelectronic characteristics of h‐BN‐integrated deep ultraviolet photodetectors (DUV PDs). The LSPR phenomenon enhances optical absorption, leading to increased generation of photogenerated charge carriers that ultimately boost the photocurrent. Zhu and colleagues demonstrated that incorporating Au NPs into a 200‐nm‐thick h‐BN deep UV PD enhanced the responsivity by approximately 7‐fold at a high bias voltage of 35 V, with this enhancement being exclusively observed under positive bias conditions [22]. Kaushik and collaborators [23] employed the LSPR properties of aluminum nanoparticles in the DUV spectral region to construct a boron nitride‐based DUV photodetector, realizing a 5.5‐fold photocurrent enhancement at the wavelength of 205 nm. The device demonstrated approximately 60% enhancement in responsivity at a low bias of 1 V while preserving outstanding UV–visible rejection performance. Nevertheless, the influence of LSPR on the performance of VUV PDs fabricated from h‐BN thin films has not been comprehensively investigated in existing studies.

This work demonstrates the application of LSPR from Pt NPs for substantially improving the performance of VUV PDs fabricated from MOCVD‐derived h‐BN nanosheets. The Pt NPs were synthesized through a cost‐effective dewetting approach. A systematic investigation was conducted on the effect of evaporation‐deposited Pt film thickness on the resulting nanoparticle size, followed by an in‐depth examination of the enhancement mechanisms governing the improved VUV photodetection capabilities in h‐BN films through Pt NP integration. Results and Discussion

Figure 1 presents the characterization results of h‐BN films fabricated by MOCVD. Figure 1a displays a cross‐sectional transmission electron microscope (TEM) micrograph of the h‐BN film, in which the highly crystalline h‐BN layer is distinctly visible as the brightest region, exhibiting a homogeneous thickness of 41.26 nm. The high‐resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) image in Region A clearly reveals the interlayer h‐BN structure, exhibiting atomic‐scale ordering and quasi‐single‐crystalline features. Figure 1b presents the AFM surface morphology of h‐BN, showing uniform ridged protrusions formed by thermal mismatch‐induced wrinkling during cooling. Excessive nitridation arising from prolonged ammonia introduction leads to the formation of white particulates, where suppressed boron adatom mobility facilitates three‐dimensional island growth. Furthermore, Figure 1c shows the in‐plane TEM image of ultrasonically exfoliated h‐BN, where the HRTEM image in Region B reveals in‐plane atomic ordering, confirming superior crystallinity in both interlayer and in‐plane directions for MOCVD‐grown h‐BN. The Raman‐active E2g phonon modes were identified at 1367.8 cm−1 for h‐BN and at 417.2, 577.8, and 753.5 cm−1 for the sapphire substrate, as depicted in Figure 1d. As evidenced in Figure 1e, the h‐BN film exhibits a (0002) diffraction peak at 26.7° in XRD analysis, accompanied by AlN signatures generated during sapphire nitridation pretreatment, whose buffer layer serves to alleviate lattice mismatch with the substrate. The UV–visible transmission spectrum presented in Figure 1f reveals a distinct knee point at approximately 215 nm, followed by a rapid transmission decrease, characterizing the optical behavior of h‐BN. The calculated bandgap energy from the Tauc plot methodology is 5.76 eV, aligning with literature values for multilayer h‐BN structures [27].

XPS analysis was performed on the h‐BN thin film, prior to and after the fabrication of Pt nanoparticles by thermal dewetting, to examine the effect of the Pt nanoparticles on the surface defects of h‐BN. As depicted in Figure 2, the primary spectral features are observed at binding energies of approximately 190.5 eV (B‐N), 398.0 eV (N‐B), 531.5 eV (O‐B), and 532.9 eV (O‐C). The surface states of the h‐BN were modified by the generation of platinum nanoparticles in the thermal dewetting process. Diverse point defects (e.g., vacancies, antisite defects, and substitutional impurities) were generated by the formation of Pt nanoparticles. This is evidenced by minor shifts (∼0.1 eV) observed in the B 1s and N 1s binding energies; the B‐N bond binding energy decreased from 190.5 to 190.4 eV (Figure 2a), whereas the N‐B bond binding energy shifted from 398.1 to 398.0 eV (Figure 2b). Additionally, a notable broadening of the full width at half maximum (FWHM) for the B‐N‐ and N‐B‐related peaks was observed. The FWHM for the B‐N bond broadened from 1.11 to 1.19 eV and for the N‐B bond from 1.11 to 1.14 eV, as illustrated in Figure 2a,b. Specifically, some defects within the h‐BN film are manifested as the substitution of nitrogen lattice sites by oxygen atoms (forming OB defects) or the combination of oxygen atoms with dangling bonds from boron at defect locations. Direct evidence supporting this is provided by the observed increase in O‐B bond content, which is presented in Figure 2c. At the same time, the surface reactivity of the h‐BN was enhanced. The defect sites served as highly reactive centers, by which ambient carbon/oxygen‐containing species were adsorbed. This is evidenced by an increase in the O‐C bond content, as shown in Figure 2c.

XPS spectra of the h‐BN thin film acquired before and after the formation of Pt nanoparticles via the thermal dewetting process: (a) B 1s; (b) N 1s; (c) O 1s.

Pt films with three thicknesses (approximately 2, 3, and 4 nm) were deposited on h‐BN thin films, and the AFM surface morphologies of Pt nanoparticles (NPs) after annealing are shown in Figure 3a–c. The h‐BN thin films exhibit smooth surfaces where Pt NPs with quasi‐circular shapes are randomly distributed in size and position. The Pt NP grain size progressively increases with original Pt film thickness during the dewetting process. The corresponding particle size histograms in Figure 3d–f statistically demonstrate that the mean diameters (based on normal distribution fitting) of Pt NPs derived from ∼2, 3, and 4 nm Pt films through the dewetting process are 32 nm, 50 nm, and 85 nm, respectively. To construct h‐BN‐based PDs with/without NPs, gold electrodes (40 nm thickness) arranged in MSM geometry with 30 μm interdigitated spacing were deposited on both pristine and NP‐modified film surfaces, as schematically shown in Figure 4a. Electrical characterization was performed for all four PD configurations under both dark conditions and 185 nm VUV light irradiation. The voltage‐dependent photocurrent (I ph) responses at 185 nm illumination are presented in Figure 4b. A remarkable I ph enhancement under high bias voltages was clearly observed in NP‐incorporated devices, attributable to the Pt NPs’ presence. The NP‐mediated dark current (I dark) characteristics are displayed in Figure 4c. MSM PDs fabricated on D32, D50, and D85 substrates showed I dark values of 0.10 pA, 0.25 pA, and 6.69 pA, respectively, whereas the reference h‐BN PD without NPs exhibited an ultralow I dark of 0.03 pA. A systematic dark current elevation was detected in NP‐containing devices. Notably, PDs constructed on D85 substrates demonstrated significantly increased dark current levels. This increased dark current likely originates from defect states introduced during NP decoration on the h‐BN matrix. The interface between Pt NPs and h‐BN may create defect states that facilitate charge carrier trapping and introduce additional current transport mechanisms, thereby increasing dark current. Figure 4d demonstrates that under 185 nm irradiation, the photocurrent exhibits substantial enhancement with increasing power density, whereas the dark current maintains consistently low values, indicating a direct proportionality between photogenerated carriers and photon absorption. Through power‐law fitting of the I ph∝P α relationships, the extracted exponents α for MSM photodetectors fabricated on D32, D50, and D85 substrates were determined as 0.98, 0.99, and 1.00, respectively. For MSM devices fabricated from as‐grown h‐BN films, the derived power‐law exponent α reached 1.06. The proximity of α values to 1 indicates minimal photocurrent attenuation caused by defect‐mediated carrier recombination within the films. Beyond current‐voltage measurements, we systematically characterized the temporal current response (I–t curves) of the photodetectors under fixed bias conditions. The temporal response measurements were conducted by periodically modulating the 185‐nm vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) light source (without mechanical chopping) and synchronously recording the photocurrent transients. Figure 4e presents the photodetectors' dynamic response to modulated optical stimuli. The plot demonstrates reliable and effective responses of the PDs to the modulated optical input, highlighting their operational stability. At 20 V applied bias with 0.67 mW cm−2 illumination, Pt‐functionalized MSM PDs on D32, D50, and D85 substrates exhibited photocurrents of 1.48, 1.04, and 0.77 nA, respectively, revealing 111%, 49%, and 10% enhancement compared to the 0.70‐nA baseline current from the pristine h‐BN device, demonstrating the efficacy of Pt nanoparticle modification. After the observation of stable temporal responses, we calculated the rise and fall times for each PD. As the measured response times were limited by the light source switching speed, the actual PD response is expected to be faster than the calculated values. The rise time τ r (decay time τ d) is defined as the time interval required for the detector output to rise from 10% (fall from 90%) to 90% (10%) of its maximum value. Using this criterion, τ r and τ d for the pristine h‐BN‐based MSM PD were calculated as 215.50 and 41.29 ms, respectively, as shown in Figure 4f. For Pt NP‐modified devices, τ r values of 214.15, 210.5, and 207.43 ms with corresponding τ d values of 21.45, 41.78, and 41.46 ms were obtained for D32‐, D50‐, and D85‐based MSM PDs, respectively (Figure 4g–i). This allows us to conclude that the detector speed remains unaffected by Pt NP surface modification on h‐BN, showing comparable performance to PDs fabricated on pristine h‐BN.

Figure 5a reveals null photoresponse in the 254–355‐nm spectral range, whereas the responsivity progressively increases as the wavelength shortens from 254 to 185 nm (vacuum ultraviolet region). At 185 nm illumination, the Pt‐functionalized detectors demonstrated peak responsivities of 1.02 mA/W (D32), 0.56 mA/W (D50), and 0.47 mA/W (D85), significantly exceeding the 0.42‐mA/W baseline value from the unmodified h‐BN device under equivalent measurement conditions. Remarkably, this translates to 143% peak responsivity enhancement through Pt nanoparticle integration, verifying their plasmonic enhancement effects. Conclusion

In summary, we have successfully demonstrated for the first time a plasmon‐enhanced VUV PD based on h‐BN films utilizing LSPR in Pt nanoparticles. The nanoparticles were synthesized via a cost‐effective and efficient thin‐film dewetting method, modifying MOCVD‐grown h‐BN through controlled evaporation of Pt films with varying thicknesses. NPs dewetted from 2, 3, and 4 nm films showed average diameters of 32, 50, and 85 nm, respectively. VUV PDs based on pristine h‐BN and modified versions (D32/D50/D85 with 32/50/85 nm NPs) underwent comprehensive optoelectronic characterization. At 20 V bias with 0.67 mW/cm2 illumination, D32 devices exhibited 1.02 mA/W responsivity (143% enhancement) and 2.95 × 1011 Jones detectivity (117% improvement). Numerical simulations of extinction characteristics and electric field distributions at 185 nm VUV revealed the enhancement mechanism: LSPR in NPs improves light absorption and local field intensity, boosting photocurrent generation. Furthermore, even with Pt‐modified h‐BN surfaces, we believe plasmonic enhancement will play vital roles in addressing the low responsivity of 2DLM‐based VUV devices, and our study represents a significant step in this direction.

Experimental Section

Growth of h‐BN films by MOCVD and subsequent thermal annealing: Approximately 40 nm of h‐BN were grown on 2‐inch double‐side‐polished sapphire substrates by using a commercial MOCVD system at 1215°C under a reactor pressure of 70 mbar with H2 carrier gas. A pulsed source‐injection mode composed of two steps in a single pulse cycle was employed: (1) injection of 20 sccm triethylborane (TEB) for 2 s, followed by (2) injection of 2000 sccm NH3 for 4 s. A total of 1200 pulse cycles resulted in the growth of h‐BN. The as‐grown h‐BN film, deposited via MOCVD, was subjected to thermal annealing at 1650°C for 120 min in an argon (Ar) atmosphere at atmospheric pressure, with an Ar gas flow rate of 200 sccm, as shown in Supporting Information S1: Figure S1a.

Pt nanoparticles (NPs) of varying sizes were prepared on h‐BN films: Pt films with nominal thicknesses of approximately 2, 3, and 4 nm were deposited on the h‐BN surface using electron beam evaporation with a base pressure of 10−6 Torr. Subsequently, the samples were subjected to rapid thermal annealing at an optimized temperature of 850°C for 300 s in a nitrogen atmosphere. This process induced dewetting of the Pt film, resulting in the formation of Pt NPs on the h‐BN films, as shown in Supporting Information S1: Figure S1b,c.

Fabrication of VUV MSM photodetectors (PDs): A 40‐nm Au metal layer was evaporated onto both pristine and Pt NP‐modified samples through a mask to define an interdigitated electrode geometry characteristic of metal–semiconductor–metal (MSM) structures, with an electrode width and interelectrode spacing of 30 μm, as shown in Supporting Information S1: Figure S1d.

Characterization: The surface morphology of the h‐BN films was characterized using a Jupiter XR atomic force microscope (AFM), and the microstructure of the h‐BN/sapphire heterostructures was examined using a Tecnai G2 F30 transmission electron microscope (TEM). Raman spectroscopy was performed using a confocal Raman microscope (alpha300R, WITec) with a 532‐nm laser as the excitation source. Absorption spectra were recorded using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Lambda 35, PerkinElmer). X‐ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were conducted using a SmartLab 9 kW diffractometer (Rigaku) to confirm the composition of the Pt NP/h‐BN/sapphire structure. Photodetection measurements were conducted under illumination from a low‐pressure mercury lamp emitting at 185 nm, whereas spectral selectivity was evaluated using a 222‐nm KrCl excimer lamp and deep‐UV LEDs emitting in the range of 254–360 nm. The optical power density of the illumination sources was measured using a calibrated optical power meter (Thorlabs PM100 USB, S401C, and ES120C). Electrical and optoelectronic measurements (I–t and I–V curves) were obtained using a Keithley 2902 and a Keithley 4200 source meter, respectively. Simulations were conducted via the 3D finite‐difference time‐domain method implemented in Ansys Lumerical 2025 R1, wherein the refractive indices of materials were sourced from published references. The diameters of the hemispherical nanoparticles were designed with three size gradients: 32, 50, and 85 nm. The computational region was configured to fully cover the structural model, whereas boundary conditions were implemented using perfectly matched layers (PML). Electromagnetic simulations captured both the electric field profiles and extinction spectra of platinum nanoparticles under periodic arrangement across varying wavelengths.

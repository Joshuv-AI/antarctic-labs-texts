Anomalous Systemic Arterial Supply to the Left Basal Segment With Arterial Dissection of the Feeding Artery: A Case Report and Literature Review
Authors: Luping Ma, Ruiming Xia, Zhongqiang Yu, Liang Wang, Wenbin Hu, Kelin Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAnomalous systemic arterial supply to the left basal segment is a rare congenital vascular anomaly, particularly when complicated by dissection of the feeding artery. This case report aims to present a unique instance of this condition and to review relevant literature. We present a young patient who was hospitalized with sudden chest pain, leading to the discovery of an anomalous artery originating from the thoracic aorta that supplied blood to the basal segment of the left inferior lobe of the lung, accompanied by dissection of the feeding artery. The patient underwent successful surgical resection of the anomalous vessel via thoracotomy, and postoperative recovery was unremarkable during follow‐up evaluations. This case highlights the importance of recognizing anomalous systemic arterial supply as a potential cause of acute chest pain and underscores the need for timely surgical intervention.

Introduction

Anomalous systemic arterial supply to the left basal segment primarily originates from the descending aorta, supplying the bronchi and lung parenchyma of the left lower lobe. This abnormality had previously been classified as a type of pulmonary sequestration by Pryce [1]. However, this abnormality is currently considered a distinct disease entity and is differentiated from pulmonary sequestration based on the presence or absence of bronchial abnormalities. We present a rare case of anomalous systemic arteries supplying the left lower lung lobe complicated by arterial dissection, which was successfully treated with surgical resection. To improve the understanding of the disease, which can be diagnosed correctly through CTA to find anomalous systemic arteries supply.

Case Presentation

Case History

A 35‐year‐old man presented to our hospital with sudden chest and back pain after running last night, tearing pain accompanied by chest tightness and shortness of breath symptoms. The patient had no remarkable medical history, and a physical examination revealed good health.

Investigations, Diagnosis, and Treatment

Chest computed tomography (CT) displayed tortuous vascular shadows in the lower lobe of the left lung surrounded by patchy ground glass density shadows, and the distribution of the bronchial tree in the lower lobe of the left lung was normal (Figure 1). CT angiography (CTA) displayed a dilated and tortuous aberrant systemic artery originating from the thoracic aorta supplying the left lower lung lobe (Figure 2a); an intimal flap was observed within the artery, creating true and false lumina (Figure 2b,c). Maximum intensity projection (MIP) reconstruction of the pulmonary artery vessels displayed normally distributed pulmonary artery branches in the lower lobe of the left lung (Figure 3). Laboratory test results were as follows: white blood cell count, 9.1 × 109/L; ultrasensitive C‐reactive protein, 30.8 mg/L. Three days after admission for anti‐infective treatment, the patient underwent thoracoscopic resection of the left lower lobe of the lung. During the operation, the abnormal blood‐supplying artery was found to originate from the thoracic aorta, the widest part of which was 1.2 cm in diameter, and the distal part was an aneurysm‐like dilatation; the entire left lower lung exhibited solid atelectasis; therefore, a complete resection of the left lower lobe was performed (Figure 4).

Outcome and Follow‐Up

The patient recovered well postoperatively. A 6‐month follow‐up chest CT scan showed no significant abnormalities.

Discussion

The etiology of anomalous systemic arterial supply to the left basal segment is unclear, and it may be related to the lack of degenerative remnants of the primitive aortic branches supplying the pulmonary blastema during the embryonic period in the preaortic developmental stage [2]. The main clinical symptoms are haemoptysis and shortness of breath after an activity [3]. The acute chest pain in our patient was considered to be related to the formation of arterial dissection of the blood‐supplying artery. Chronic vascular hypertension resulted in progressive wall thickening, calcification, and elasticity loss, culminating in aneurysm formation and subsequent arterial dissection [4, 5].

The imaging features of the abnormal systemic arterial supply to the left basal segment include the following: (1) The abnormally thick systemic artery originates from the thoracic aorta or abdominal aorta. (2) Absence of the pulmonary artery in the affected area (complete type) and normal pulmonary artery in the affected area, with dual blood supply from the body artery and pulmonary artery (incomplete type). (3) Normal lung development with continuous bronchial tubes. (4) Normal pulmonary venous drainage [2, 6]. The imaging findings of this patient are consistent with the literature reports; it is a rare incomplete type and complicated with arterial dissection of the feeding artery.

Clinical misdiagnosis of pulmonary sequestration requires differentiation. Lobectomy is the treatment of choice for pulmonary sequestration [7, 8]; abnormal systemic arteries supplying the left basal segment may be managed via thoracoscopic lobectomy, vascular interventional embolization, or vascular anastomosis, depending on the vessel diameter and clinical symptoms [5]. Timely intervention is crucial for managing vascular anomalies to prevent life‐threatening complications such as massive hemoptysis, arterial rupture, or paradoxical embolism [9]. This is especially critical in high‐risk cohorts like pregnant patients, where gestational hemodynamic changes exacerbate vascular stress. Recent cases exemplify modern management benefits: preoperative embolization effectively controlled hemorrhage in a giant arteriovenous malformation, while successful endovascular repair of anomalous systemic arterial supply at 22 weeks'—gestation demonstrated minimally invasive feasibility in complex obstetric scenarios with favorable maternal‐fetal outcomes [10].

For pulmonary sequestration, surgical intervention is recommended for most patients, particularly in cases of recurrent pulmonary infection or suspected malignancy that cannot be ruled out [8]. Accurate differentiation from pulmonary sequestration is critical to prevent unnecessary invasive interventions and direct appropriate clinical management. Compared to lobectomy, minimally invasive approaches—including transarterial embolization, thoracoscopic anomalous artery ligation with lung preservation, and resection using Dacron‐reinforced aortic stump closure—demonstrate reduced morbidity [3, 8, 11, 12, 13]. But coil embolization of the anomalous arterial supply can cause early postoperative pulmonary infarction [3]. Finally, the patient underwent thoracoscopic resection of the left lower lobe of the lung and recovered well after a 6‐month follow‐up.

Anomalous systemic arterial supply to the left basal segments is a rare congenital anomaly, exceptionally uncommon when complicated by dissection of the feeding artery. CTA reconstruction techniques are essential for diagnosing this condition, precisely delineating aberrant vasculature, and differentiating it from pulmonary sequestration to guide management decisions. Contemporary therapy favors minimally invasive approaches—notably transarterial embolization or video‐assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS)—selected according to vascular anatomy and symptomatology. Multidisciplinary team (MDT) collaboration remains indispensable, particularly in high‐risk contexts such as pregnancy, to optimize clinical outcomes.

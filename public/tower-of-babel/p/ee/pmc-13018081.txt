Evolution of the ilizarov technique: A scoping review of improvement
Authors: Jian Gao, Wei Shi, Haichuan Sun, Feifan He, Chong Gao, Lei Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe Ilizarov technique is a cornerstone of orthopedic reconstruction, yet it faces persistent challenges such as prolonged external fixation and patient discomfort. This scoping review aims to synthesize recent advancements in the Ilizarov method, focusing on innovations in fixation devices, osteotomy techniques, bone transport protocols, and multimodal integrations.MethodsA literature search was conducted in PubMed/MEDLINE from inception to November 2024. Studies reporting innovations in device design, surgical technique, or adjunctive therapies related to the Ilizarov method were included. Key findings were synthesized narratively across four predefined domains. ResultsRecent improvements include: (1) lighter and smarter external fixators (e.g., hexapod systems, computer-aided design); (2) refined osteotomies (e.g., low-energy, bifocal/trifocal strategies) to preserve biology and reduce treatment time; (3) optimized transport protocols (e.g., acute shortening, cable-pulley systems); and (4) multimodal integration with technologies such as 3D printing, internal fixation, induced membrane technique, and biologics (e.g., BMSCs, growth factors), which have shown potential to enhance bone regeneration and reduce complications. ConclusionThe Ilizarov technique continues to evolve through device innovation, biological refinement, and technological integration. These advancements enhance its efficacy and patient outcomes, paving the way for broader clinical applications and further research.Keywords: Ilizarov technique, External fixation, Osteotomy, Bone transport, Distraction osteogenesis

Introduction

Since its inception over 70 years ago, the Ilizarov technique has been a clinical mainstay for stimulating bone and tissue regeneration through the application of continuous stretch stress. It has been effectively used to address a range of conditions, including osteomyelitis, nonunion fractures, bone defects, bone tumors, joint stiffness, and various deformities [1–4]. The foundational “tension-stress rule,” introduced by Gavriil Ilizarov, has propelled significant advancements in bone lengthening and reconstruction. Contemporary surgical practices in these areas are largely predicated on his principles.

Despite ongoing refinements, the Ilizarov technique faces notable limitations, such as aesthetic concerns, extended external fixator wear times, pin site infections, docking site nonunions, risk of refracture, and psychological impacts on patients [5–7]. However, ongoing research in fundamental areas, including signal transduction pathways, bone morphogenetic proteins, angiogenesis, inflammation, and stem cell targeting, may offer future resolutions to these challenges [8]. The technique’s high mechanical stability is achieved through diverse fixation methods, and ongoing developments are focused on enhancing osteotomy procedures to minimize blood supply damage and improve bone transport mechanisms. The incorporation of pharmaceuticals, cellular therapies, and growth factors has demonstrated promising outcomes, suggesting that complications can be mitigated through a multifaceted approach.

The present article is a narrative literature review that aims to synthesize and critically appraise the recent advances in the clinical application of the Ilizarov technique, highlighting innovations that enhance its effectiveness and patient outcomes and outlining future directions for research and practice.

Methods

Approach and scope

This article is a scoping (non-systematic) review aiming to synthesise contemporary advances in the Ilizarov technique. The goal was neither to exhaustively capture all literature nor to perform statistical pooling, but to provide a balanced, critical and up-to-date overview covering four key domains: (1) external-fixator innovations, (2) osteotomy refinements, (3) bone-transport protocols, and (4) integration with adjunct technologies.

Information sources and search strategy

A electronic search was performed in PubMed/MEDLINE from inception to 31 Nov 2024. The search combined MeSH terms and free-text terms related to the Ilizarov technique and its recent advances. The PubMed search string is presented in Box 1; Reference lists of key papers and recent relevant reviews were hand-searched for additional studies.

All records were imported into EndNote and de-duplicated. Titles and abstracts were independently screened by two reviewers (J.G. and W.S.) who each have > 5 years of orthopaedic research experience. Discrepancies were resolved by discussion or by a third reviewer (C.G.) when necessary. Full texts of potentially eligible articles were retrieved and assessed for final inclusion.

Results

Improvement of fixation devices

Gavriil Ilizarov’s invention of the ring external fixator, inspired by the bicycle wheel, revolutionized orthopedics with its versatility in treating a variety of conditions across different body parts [9]. Comprising components such as plates, bolts, rings, rods, and hinges, the device’s adaptability is notable. However, it has faced criticism for its aesthetic bulkiness, portability challenges, complex setup, and susceptibility to pin site infections [10].

In response, enhancements have focused on mechanical stability, a critical factor in fracture treatment. Innovations include the use of half-pins fixators in conjunction with wires, the employment of double rings to bolster the framework, and modifications to the design and connection methods of nuts [4, 10, 11]. Manufacturers have responded to patient needs by offering external fixators in various colors and customized sizes, as well as incorporating advanced materials like carbon fiber for reduced weight and improved X-ray transparency. Surface treatments with hydroxyapatite or growth factors on fixation pins and wires have been introduced to diminish tissue irritation and infection rates, thereby enhancing device stability [11]. This improvement not only enables patients to initiate joint mobilization and weight-bearing exercises at an early stage but also reduces the risk of soft tissue inflammation and infection at the pinhole sites by alleviating the compression exerted by the fixation device on the surrounding soft tissues [10]. Moreover, optimizing the angular configuration and tension control strategy of the ring, pins, wires, and half-pins in the fixation device design allows for better stress distribution, thereby preventing stress concentration and reducing the occurrence of pin-bone interface infection [12].

Advancements in automation, such as the introduction of automatic distraction devices, have alleviated the patient’s burden of routine adjustments and allowed for personalized distraction rates. These innovations may contribute to a reduced risk of nerve damage and nonunion complications [13]. Preoperative planning, computer-aided design, and 3D printing have further streamlined the surgical process, increased precision and minimizing complication rates [14].

Alternatives to the traditional ring fixator, the hexapod external fixators, such as the Taylor frame, have been developed to offer lighter, more comfortable options for patients. These devices provide the same therapeutic benefits with the added advantage of easier installation and multi-angle adjustment capabilities, particularly useful in femoral disease treatment [15–17]. However, THE unilateral rails may not offer the same stability as traditional fixators and could be prone to angular deformities [15].

Intramedullary nail lengthening devices represent another method for addressing bone defects, offering benefits such as reduced external fixation duration and fewer complications in select cases [18]. However, they carry a higher risk of deep infection and are not suitable for defects caused by infection, trauma, or bone tumors. (Fig. 2)

Improvement of osteotomy

Osteotomy is a critical preliminary step in the Ilizarov technique for bone transport or lengthening, designed to initiate the formation of bone or tissue scaffolded by the fracture hematoma. The optimal osteotomy minimizes damage to the periosteum and intramedullary blood supply, reducing heat injury and fostering bone healing. Gavriil Ilizarov’s method, utilizing a continuous tension wire osteotomy, is considered a low-energy, protective approach. However, it presents challenges, including heightened risks of infection and fracture displacement, particularly with small bone segments [19].

The role of intramedullary blood supply in the osteotomy is contested. Some clinicians prefer porous drilling osteotomy, despite its potential to harm blood supply, due to its procedural efficiency. In contrast, the Afghan or Gigli saw osteotomy creates a tunnel under the periosteum, allowing bone cutting while preserving the periosteal blood supply. This method supports bone healing with minimal invasion and without the need to remove the external fixator [20]. Dome and cross-osteotomies have also been employed to enhance stability and mitigate the risk of refracture in specific clinical scenarios [21].

Traditional bone transport employs single-level osteotomy, yet advancements have introduced bifocal or trifocal osteotomies to reduce external fixator time. Although these methods may cause more damage to the intramedullary blood supply, they stimulate sufficient osteogenesis to compensate [22]. Studies have demonstrated that bifocal osteotomy can lead to better outcomes in terms of external fixation index, fracture healing time, and docking site nonunion rates [23]. For bone defects exceeding 6 cm, double-level osteotomy has been proposed to further decrease treatment duration and complications [24]. Notably, bifocal osteotomy has been effective in both bone and soft tissue defect management, offering an alternative to skin flap transplantation [25].

Clinicians must decide between bifocal or trifocal osteotomy based on patient-specific factors, including bone quality, defect size, and surrounding soft tissue conditions. While these advanced osteotomies can lead to hourglass-shaped bone regeneration, they may also require additional fixation elements, potentially increasing the risk of pin site infections. (Fig. 3)

Improvement of bone transport

A pivotal element of the Ilizarov technique is the application of distraction forces that stimulate bone and tissue regeneration. The direction of these forces significantly influences outcomes; for instance, distraction from the proximal to the distal end enhances osteogenesis and diminishes the risk of varus deformity and nerve injury [26]. The standard distraction rate is 1 mm/day, applied in 3–4 increments, which may correlate with nerve tissue regeneration kinetics.

Pre-clinical in vivo models have investigated the impact of distraction rate on growth factor expression at the fracture site: a rate of 1 mm/day in four increments sees significant increases in stem cell factor (SCF) and transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1) mid-distraction, with insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) peaking at the end. Conversely, a faster rate of 3 mm/day in 120 increments results in decreased IGF-1 and TGF-β1 levels mid-distraction, followed by an increase in IGF-1, VEGF, and TGF-β1 at the conclusion [27]. Despite suggestions that increased daily distraction frequency could boost bone formation, research indicates no significant difference in bone healing or complications when the frequency is varied under mechanical distraction [28].

The Ilizarov technique offers three strategies for bone defect management: bone lengthening, bone transport, and acute shortening followed by lengthening. Acute shortening facilitates early wound closure, accelerates stem cell healing and growth, reduces external fixation duration, and can be integrated with bone grafts [28–30]. However, the shortening distance should be limited to 3–4 cm per session to avoid complications such as vascular and nerve injury. Doppler Ultrasound monitoring is recommended during this process [31]. Alternative methods, like accordion traction and tibial transverse bone transport, have been used to address specific clinical challenges, including diabetic foot ulcers [32–33]. (Fig. 4)

Traditional Ilizarov technique, involving cross-wires passing through bone and soft tissue, presents risks such as infection along the nail path. Innovations like Cable pulley transport method have been introduced to mitigate these complications. This method uses a steel cable wrapped around the distal end of the transport segment, managed through a system of rings and pulleys, reducing issues related to the nail path. However, it demands precise bone segment control during distraction and may require additional fixation for effective healing [34]. This technique is not suitable for osteoporotic patients and necessitates careful consideration of patient-specific factors.

Multimodal application

The Ilizarov technique has been increasingly integrated with modern technologies, enhancing its efficacy and patient outcomes. These include preoperative computer-aided planning, 3D printing, intraoperative robot assistance, and the use of adjunctive fixation devices such as plates or intramedullary nails. Additionally, the application of bone substitutes, growth factors, and medications at bone defect sites has shown promise in accelerating the healing process.

Preoperative planning and advanced technologies

Preoperative planning utilizing CT-based three-dimensional imaging and digital reconstructions allows for the creation of precise external fixation models. This technology enables the preparation and assembly of external fixators prior to surgery, optimizing positioning and reducing operative time. Postoperatively, distraction direction and speed can be fine-tuned according to digital designs, enhancing bone transport accuracy, reducing deformity, and promoting healing [14, 35]. The rapid evolution of computer-aided systems and 3D printing in orthopedics has significantly benefited both patients and healthcare providers, making the Ilizarov technique more convenient and effective. This improvement not only enables more precise placement of the device, reducing soft tissue trauma caused by repeated adjustments, but also allows for better planning of pin positions and wire trajectories through three-dimensional imaging, thereby lowering the risk of infection [36].

Combined fixation methods

To curtail external fixation duration and discomfort, some studies have combined external fixators with plate or intramedullary nail fixation. Despite potential risks such as increased infection and surgical complexity, this approach has demonstrated a reduction in fixator time and complications, along with improved anatomical and functional outcomes [37, 38]. For instance, a study by Gupta et al. on infected tibial nonunions treated with a combination of locked plating and external fixation reported high patient satisfaction and functional results [37]. The combined use of external and internal fixation (e.g., bone transport with intramedullary nailing) can effectively reduce the duration of external fixation, thereby decreasing the risk of pin site infection associated with long-term external fixation [38]. Combined fixation effectively enhances the mechanical stability of the distraction zone, provides a more favorable biomechanical environment for bone regeneration, and facilitates early weight-bearing and functional rehabilitation for patients [39].

Bone substitutes and growth factors

Furthermore, adjunct therapies such as bone substitutes, platelet-rich plasma, and medications like Teriparatide have been employed to enhance docking site healing. Rollo’s work combining the Ilizarov technique with platelet-rich plasma or Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy for treating nonunions showed improved functional outcomes and rapid bone regeneration, facilitating earlier removal of external fixators and enhancing quality of life [40, 41]. A randomized cross-over trial demonstrated that Teriparatide administration during the consolidation phase of distraction osteogenesis significantly increased the mineralization rate of the regenerated bone [42].

Various bone grafts substitutes have been explored to enhance the Ilizarov technique. Bone graft materials can be strategically used at the docking site to improve the union rate [43]. The docking site is inherently at risk of nonunion due to insufficient biological stimulation or inadequate callus formation, especially in critical-sized defects [44]. Autogenous bone graft is combined with the Ilizarov bone transport method, it improves union outcomes by addressing the biologically compromised docking area, significantly enhancing the union rate [45]. Bioactive glass and calcium phosphate-based ceramics have been used as adjuncts to Ilizarov techniques for treating infected tibial nonunions [43, 46]. These materials serve not only as structural fillers but also provide antibacterial effects and osteoconductive scaffolds that support bone in growth [47]. Their use in combination with Ilizarov techniques has shown radiographic and clinical outcomes comparable to autografts, while eliminating the need for additional surgery to harvest grafts and the associated complications [43, 48].

Induced membrane technique

The induced membrane technique, also known as the Masquelet technique, is a two-stage procedure. In the first stage, thorough debridement is performed, followed by the placement of a polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) cement spacer, which is typically loaded with antibiotics. Within 4 to 8 weeks, a thick vascularized membrane forms around the cement spacer. This membrane is rich in osteoinductive factors, such as vascular endothelial growth factor, transforming growth factor-β1, and bone morphogenetic proteins [49, 50]. In the second stage, the cement spacer is removed while carefully preserving the integrity of the membrane, and autogenous cancellous bone or other bone substitute materials are implanted into the biochamber. The induced membrane not only prevents the absorption of the graft material but also creates a favorable biochemical microenvironment for osteogenesis and vascular ingrowth [51]. However, the Masquelet technique is limited by the need for a large amount of bone grafting, especially when dealing with extensive bone defects. Its efficacy may be compromised in patients with impaired bone regeneration potential or persistent infections.

Integrating the induced membrane technique with the Ilizarov technique leverages the complementary advantages of both methods. The Masquelet technique establishes a well-vascularized, osteoinductive environment, enhancing the biological potential for bone healing, while the Ilizarov method provides rigid mechanical support and actively stimulates bone regeneration through controlled distraction osteogenesis [52]. By combining these techniques, the induced membrane technique can mitigate some of the inherent limitations of traditional bone transport. For instance, the osteogenic properties of the membrane can improve healing at the docking site, reducing the risk of nonunion and refracture occasionally encountered with Ilizarov surgery alone. Moreover, the mechanical environment provided by the Ilizarov fixator may help maintain the integrity of the induced membrane during bone transport, thereby preserving its biological activity throughout the treatment process [53, 54]. This synergistic effect leads to a more predictable healing trajectory, shortened total treatment time, and ultimately improved functional outcomes for patients with extensive bone defects [55, 56]. Additionally, the use of antibiotic-loaded cement spacers in the first stage not only aids in membrane formation but also provides high local concentrations of antibiotics, thereby controlling infection and reducing the risk of recurrent osteomyelitis [52, 57]. However, during the application of the Ilizarov fixator and subsequent bone transport, it is crucial to preserve the integrity of the induced membrane, as any structural damage may weaken its osteoinductive potential [51]. Further controlled studies are needed to refine the surgical protocol, optimize patient selection, and minimize potential complications associated with prolonged external fixation and bone grafting [58].

Bone mesenchymal stem cells application (BMSCs)

BMSCs have been extensively characterized for their ability to differentiate into osteoblasts, chondrocytes, and adipocytes [59]. Their osteogenic differentiation is mediated by complex interactions of biochemical signals, including growth factors such as bone morphogenetic proteins and basic fibroblast growth factor, which enhance the expression of osteogenic markers and promote matrix mineralization [60]. The combination of BMSCs therapy with the Ilizarov technique is based on the five key components of bone healing: osteogenic cells, osteoconductive scaffolds, osteoinductive signals, mechanical stability, and adequate vascularity. BMSCs directly meet the requirements for osteogenesis and osteoinduction by providing progenitor cell sources and secreting growth factors that stimulate bone formation and angiogenesis [60, 61]. Meanwhile, the Ilizarov method provides mechanical stability and controlled tension, which are crucial for stimulating callus formation and ensuring the proper alignment of the regenerating bone [62].

Firstly, mechanical stimulation applied through distraction osteogenesis has been shown to enhance the osteogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs. The cyclic strain and tension generated by the Ilizarov frame induce upregulation of osteogenic gene expression in mechanotransduction signaling pathways, thereby amplifying the regenerative capacity of BMSCs [63]. Secondly, the local delivery of BM-MSCs during the application of the Ilizarov fixator creates a growth factor-rich local bioenvironment, which further supports neovascularization and provides necessary cues for tissue remodeling [64]. In a clinical trial involving 25 patients with infected tibial nonunions, intrabone injection of autologous BM-MSCs combined with Ilizarov surgery achieved stable fracture healing and significantly reduced the duration of external fixation by approximately 2.5 months compared with conventional treatment [65]. With further advances in stem cell research, scaffold design, and mechanobiology, the combined BM-MSC/Ilizarov approach is expected to significantly transform the management of challenging nonunions and large bone defects, bringing about a paradigm shift in the field of bone regeneration.

While these combination approaches have shown potential in improving bone quality and reducing complications, the limited number of cases studied suggests a need for larger sample sizes to ensure the generalizability of these findings. (Fig. 5)

Discussion

The Ilizarov technique is a minimally invasive approach preserving surrounding tissue and periosteum. By connecting various needle types to bone or soft tissue, the technique generates continuous distraction forces, stimulating bone and tissue regeneration and enhancing local blood supply. This facilitates early functional rehabilitation, making the Ilizarov technique valuable in orthopedic treatments for fractures, osteomyelitis, nonunions, bone defects, bone tumors, joint stiffness, and deformities. Its broad acceptance across orthopedics underscores its versatility and efficacy. (Fig. 5)

Despite its successes, the Ilizarov technique faces challenges that warrant further investigation: enhancing the quality of bone and soft tissue reconstruction, reducing healing and device wear times, and minimizing discomfort associated with external fixations. Unraveling the mechanisms by which distraction stimulates stem cell transformation, understanding the molecular interplay between stress and tissue regeneration, and controlling gene expression related to the technique are areas ripe for exploration (Fig. 6).

The Ilizarov technique has shown remarkable adaptability, with ongoing improvements driven by basic science research. In clinical practice, refinements in external fixation have prioritized stability and patient comfort. Optimal osteotomy selection and the strategic use of multiple osteotomy levels have the potential to shorten the duration of external fixation and expedite bone healing. Careful distraction direction, speed, and mode selection can mitigate complications such as nail path issues.

Furthermore, the integration of advanced technologies—computer-assisted planning, 3D printing, robotic surgery, novel materials, platelet-rich plasma, and pharmacological agents—has the potential to further reduce complications and improve outcomes. As our understanding of the Ilizarov technique deepens, its refinement and application are set to expand, offering even greater benefits to patients and the field of orthopedics.

This review has several limitations. First, our literature search was restricted to articles published in English, which may have introduced a language bias. Second, due to the heterogeneity of the included studies (e.g., variations in patient populations and outcome measures), a formal meta-analysis was not feasible. Finally, the majority of the available evidence consists of retrospective case series, highlighting the need for more high-quality prospective studies in the future.

FDG PET/CT in Duodenal Follicular Dendritic Cell Sarcoma
Authors: Jian Geng, Jie Luo, Fang Huang, Jianjun Qian, Xiaoyuan Chu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Follicular dendritic cell sarcoma (FDCS) of the duodenum is extremely rare. We describe the contrast-enhanced CT and FDG PET/CT findings in a case of duodenal FDCS. Contrast-enhanced CT revealed a large, irregular mass originating from the horizontal portion of the duodenum. The mass demonstrated heterogeneous enhancement, with internal areas of low-attenuation necrosis and coarse calcifications. The mass showed significantly increased FDG uptake. The patient underwent surgical resection, and histopathologic examination revealed a spindle cell tumor positive for CD21 and CD35, confirming the diagnosis of FDCS.

A previously healthy 72-year-old woman was referred for imaging after a right upper abdominal mass was palpated on physical examination. She reported nonspecific abdominal discomfort but no weight loss, fever, or B-symptoms. An initial contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen revealed a large, irregular mass measuring 7.4×5.9 cm and originating from the horizontal portion of the duodenum. The mass demonstrated heterogeneous enhancement, with internal areas of low-attenuation necrosis and coarse calcifications. Duodenal adenocarcinoma, gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST),or lymphoma was suspected.

Subsequently, an 18F-FDG PET/CT was performed for staging and characterization. Maximum intensity projection (MIP) images revealed intense, multifocal FDG avidity within the abdomen (A and B). Axial fused PET/CT images confirmed that the large duodenal mass was highly metabolically active (SUVmax 10.76).

Histopathology revealed a spindle cell neoplasm (A, hematoxylin-eosin stain, original magnification ×40). The definitive diagnosis of FDCS was confirmed immunohistochemically by strong positivity for CD21 (B, original magnification ×40), CD35 (C, original magnification ×40), Ki67 20% (D, original magnification ×40), and negativity for EBER, CD34, CD117, CK-PAN, CK-L, MDM-2, SMA, S100, SOX10. FDCS is a diagnostic mimic, often resembling more common sarcomas, GIST, or lymphoma on imaging.1,2 On CT: the lesion typically manifests as a large, solitary mass with well-defined margins that exhibits invasive characteristics. It often demonstrates heterogeneous enhancement due to necrosis and may contain calcifications.3,4 18F-FDG PET/CT: FDCS is consistently FDG-avid, making PET/CT an invaluable tool for initial staging, identifying occult metastatic disease, and evaluating treatment response.5–7 This case emphasizes that FDCS should be considered a potential diagnosis for hypermetabolic duodenal lesions, particularly when more common possibilities like adenocarcinoma, lymphoma, or GIST are less likely. Definitive diagnosis always requires histopathology and immunohistochemical analysis (CD21/CD35 positivity).1,8

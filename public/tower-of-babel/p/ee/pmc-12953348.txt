Exploring immune-inflammatory cross-talk in atherosclerosis using AI-enhanced multi-omics approaches
Authors: Zhenzhen Zhao, Zhijie Zhao, Hongling Jia, Yuelong Qin, Zhikai Xiahou, Wenwu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtherosclerosis is a complex chronic inflammatory disease characterized by lipid accumulation, vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMCs) proliferation, and immune cell infiltration. However, the heterogeneity and functional dynamics of VSMCs in atherosclerotic lesions have not been systematically elucidated. In this study, we integrated single-cell transcriptomics with spatial transcriptomics analysis to delineate the developmental trajectories and functional states of VSMC subsets. Our analysis identified a distinct LUM + VSMCs subpopulation residing at an early stage of differentiation, marked by elevated cellular plasticity, stemness features, and strong activation of pro-inflammatory signaling pathways. Spatial transcriptomics and in vitro functional assays—including EdU incorporation, apoptosis quantification, and cell viability assays—further validated LUM as a key regulator promoting VSMC proliferation and inflammation. Importantly, the C0 LUM + VSMCs were predicted to interact extensively with endothelial cells and macrophages through CD99, MIF, and CCL-mediated signaling axes, underscoring their role as dedifferentiated, pro-inflammatory effector cells contributing to disease progression. These findings highlight the power of big data-enabled single-cell approaches in identifying cell-type–specific mechanisms and offer new avenues for precision diagnostics and biomarker discovery in inflammatory vascular diseases.Keywords: Single-Cell sequencing, Atherosclerosis, Vascular smooth muscle cells, Gene functionality, Diagnostic biomarker, Inflammation, Spatial transcriptomics

Introduction

So far, atherosclerosis has been identified as the underlying cause of coronary artery disease, carotid artery disease, and peripheral artery disease. Atherosclerosis itself is rarely fatal. However, it leads to life-threatening clinical events such as acute coronary syndrome and stroke when thrombosis occurs on ruptured or eroded atherosclerotic plaques [1–3]. As developing countries undergo an “epidemiological transition,” infectious diseases have declined, and chronic diseases, particularly those caused by atherosclerosis and hypertension, have become significant public health issues [4, 5]. Despite the use of contemporary interventions and optimal pharmacotherapy, recurrent events following acute coronary syndrome remain unacceptable [6–8].

The main pathological feature of atherosclerosis is local lipid deposition in arteries, accompanied by the proliferation of smooth muscle cells (SMCs) and fibrous matrix, resulting in the formation of atherosclerotic plaques [9]. Atherosclerosis [10, 11] is a chronic progressive inflammatory disease in which inflammation serves as the common basis for physiological and pathological changes during its development [9–13].Inflammation plays a crucial role in the occurrence and progression of atherosclerotic plaques. In the early stages of atherosclerosis, endothelial cell injury, abnormal lipid metabolism, and hemodynamic impairment are considered to be the causes of atherosclerosis. The formation process of atherosclerosis is believed to be associated with flow-mediated inflammatory changes in endothelial cells (ECs) [13].Many cells and cytokines are involved in this process, including macrophages, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), adhesion molecules, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) [14]. Abundant low-density lipoprotein (LDL) is modified into oxidized LDL (OxLDL) and accumulates in the vascular wall, leading to the formation of atherosclerotic plaques [15].

The concept of VSMCs in human atherosclerosis during the “transition phase” was first proposed in 1961 [16]. Under conditions of vascular injury, VSMCs can transition from a quiescent “contractile” phenotype to a pro-inflammatory phenotype. Activated VSMCs can effectively proliferate and migrate, promoting vascular wall repair. In human atherosclerosis [17] and cultured VSMCs, the transition from a contractile VSMC state to a macrophage-like lipid state has been identified with the development of molecular probes, recognizing cell-restricted genes or their encoded proteins [18]. Lineage tracing studies in mouse models of atherosclerosis have further supported the concept of VSMC transformation into macrophages [19–21].

Traditional transcriptomic approaches based on bulk RNA sequencing analyze gene expression across large populations of cells, providing only averaged expression profiles. However, this obscures critical cellular heterogeneity, especially in complex diseases like atherosclerosis, where rare or transient cell states may play decisive roles. In contrast, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) offers high-resolution insights into individual cellular behaviors and has been increasingly applied in cardiovascular researc [22].

In this study, we employed an integrative single-cell framework to delineate the heterogeneity, dynamic transitions, and regulatory programs of VSMCs in atherosclerosis, identifying distinct inflammatory and proliferative subpopulations and validating these findings with in vitro and spatial analyses. Nevertheless, although previous studies have described VSMC transdifferentiation toward fibroblast- or macrophage-like states, their functional diversity in inflammation and systematic characterization at the single-cell level remain insufficient, underscoring the central question addressed by our work.

Materials and methods

Single-Cell Suspensions, library Construction, and sequencing

We accessed the Gene Expression Omnibus [23] (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and retrieved the scRNA-seq dataset GSE159677, which was generated from calcified atherosclerotic core (AC) plaques and patient-matched proximal adjacent (PA) carotid artery tissues collected from three individuals undergoing carotid endarterectomy. Since we used publicly available data from the database, ethical approval was not required for this study.

The gene expression data was imported into R software and analyzed using the Seurat R package [24]. We excluded low-quality cells based on the following criteria: (1) 300 < nFeature < 7,500; (2) 500 < nCount < 100,000; (3) mitochondrial gene expression not exceeding 25% of the total gene count per cell; (4) red blood cell gene expression not exceeding 5% of the total gene count per cell.

Determination of cell subtypes

We performed batch effect correction using the harmony R package [25, 26]. The dimensionality reduction parameter (dim value) was set to 30, and the resolution parameter was set to 1.2. We normalized and scaled all gene expression data using the NormalizeData and ScaleData functions from the Seurat R package [27]. The most variable genes in the PCA were selected using the “FindVariableFeatures” function [28], and the top 2,000 genes were retained. FindClusters was used to assign cells into different clusters based on the top 30 principal components, with a resolution of 1.0. The “FindClusters” and “FindNeighbors” functions from the Seurat package were used for cell clustering, and clusters were annotated using the expression of marker genes [27]. The “FindAllMarkers” function was used to identify differentially expressed genes (marker genes) for each cluster.

GOBP enrichment analysis

For Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) [29] enrichment analysis, we performed enrichment analysis on the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of each cell type using the clusterProfiler package. The pathways associated with each cell type were derived from the biological processes of the Gene Ontology.

Cell trajectory analysis

To analyze cell trajectories, we employed three software packages. Firstly, we used the cytoTRACE algorithm package to evaluate the stemness of cells within each subpopulation. Subsequently, we employed the Monocle package to construct cell differentiation trajectories. This allowed us to observe the developmental progression of each subpopulation along the inferred trajectories. Finally, we utilized slingshot to further analyze the cell trajectories during the differentiation process of endothelial cells. Slingshot leveraged the getLineages function to fit a minimum spanning tree (MST) for inferring cell lineages and estimated the cellular expression levels of each lineage over inferred time using the getCurves function.

Single-Cell metabolic analysis

We investigated metabolic pathways at the single-cell level to uncover cellular energy dynamics and biosynthetic activity. By analyzing key metabolic genes and their regulatory networks, we identified variations across cell populations, offering insights into their functional states and metabolic adaptations.

Cell-to-Cell communication

We used the CellChat package in the context of single-cell RNA sequencing data. The CellChatDB.huma database served as a reference for ligand-receptor interactions. By setting a significance threshold of P value < 0.05, we predicted cell-to-cell interactions between different cell types.

Transcription factor (TF) analysis

We first constructed the gene regulatory network (GRN) to infer interactions between transcription factors and target genes. Next, we predicted cell developmental trajectories and states. Finally, we used the AUCell algorithm to evaluate transcription factor activity across different cells. This approach provides a comprehensive insight into transcription factor functions and regulatory mechanisms at the single-cell level [30].

Cell culture

The HA-VSMC cell line was sourced from Procell in Wuhan, China. The cells were cultured in Endothelial Cell Medium (ECM, SCIENCELL) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, penicillin(100U/mL), and streptomycin (0.1 mg/mL). Cultures were maintained in a standard incubator at 37℃,5% CO2, and saturated humidity. Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were harvested for subsequent experiments.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay for the HA-VSMA cell line after cell transfection. Cells were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 5*10^3 cells per well and incubated for 24 h [31]. Subsequently, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm on days 1, 2, 3, and 4 using an enzyme marker, and the cell viability was calculated. The average OD values were plotted on a line graph.

Flow cytometry (FCM)

Flow cytometry (FCM) [32] is a technique used to rapidly analyze cellular characteristics in a fluid stream. It involves the use of specific antibodies to quantify multiple parameters of individual cells, including surface markers, intracellular protein expression, cell cycle, DNA content, etc. FCM has advantages such as high throughput, high sensitivity, and high specificity, and it is widely used in immunology, hematology, oncology, and other fields.

Wound healing

To evaluate wound healing, stable transfected cells were seeded in a 6-well plate and cultured until confluence. A sterile 200µL plastic pipette tip was used to create scratches in each well, and cells were washed with PBS to remove cell debris. Subsequently, cells were incubated in serum-free medium. Images of the scratches were captured at 0 h and 48 h, and the scratch width was measured using Image-J software. The healing rate was calculated using the formula: (0–48 h scratch area / 0 h scratch area) * 100 [33, 34].

Transwell experiment

Transwell assay was performed to assess cell migration ability. The upper chambers of a 24-well plate were coated with a matrix gel solution (BD Biosciences, USA), and cell suspensions were seeded in the upper chamber while medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. The plate was then incubated in a cell culture incubator for 48 h. After removing cells from the upper chamber, the remaining cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Cells were counted in 5 random fields under an optical microscope.

5-Ethyl-2 ' -deoxyuridine proliferation assay

The EdU proliferation assay was performed to assess cell proliferation. Transfected HA-VSMC cells were seeded in a 6-well cell culture plate (5*10^3 cells per well) [35]. After 24 h at room temperature, EdU working solution was added to the cell culture medium and incubated for 2 h. Subsequently, cells were washed twice with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, treated with glycine (2 mg/ml) and 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min. Finally, cells were treated with 1 ml of 1X Apollo and 1 ml of 1X Hoechst staining reaction solution for 30 min. Images were observed using a fluorescence microscope to assess cell proliferation.

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) data analysis

Robust Cell Type Decomposition (RCTD) was a method for deconvoluting ST data based on scRNA-seq reference data. By constructing a reference expression profile for different cell types, RCTD employs optimization algorithms such as Poisson regression to estimate the cellular composition of each spatial spot, providing confidence assessments and visualization (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software. Unless otherwise stated, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Cluster analysis of atherosclerotic cells

In our study on atherosclerosis, scRNA-seq was performed on six tissue samples obtained from three patients (Fig.1). These samples included the core area of atherosclerosis (AC, N = 3) and the adjacent area of atherosclerosis (PA, N = 3). After rigorous quality control and data filtering, a total of 45,690 cells were included in the transcriptome analysis. To visualize the cellular landscape and molecular characteristics, several analyses were conducted. Firstly, the UMAP map was generated to show the distribution of cell aggregation, sample source, tissue type, and cell cycle, and it can be seen that in the AS sample, there are more cells derived from PA (Fig. 2A and B). Based on differential gene expression, six cell types were annotated, including T-NK cells, ECs, VAMCs, macrophages, B-plasma cells, and mast cells (Fig. 2C and D). TAGLN, ACTA2, MYL9, TPM2 and CALD1 were highly expressed in VSMCs. Further analysis focused on the differential expression of specific markers and characteristics in the major cell types of atherosclerosis. Figure 2E shows the differential expression of Cell Stemness AUC, nCount RNA, nFeature RNA, S.score, and G2M.score. It indicates that VSMCs exhibit higher levels of cell stemness and are more active in terms of RNA content and gene features, suggesting their potential for differentiation and proliferation, which may be relevant to disease progression. The enrichment analysis of functional pathways for the six cell types is displayed in Fig. 2F, providing insights into the specific biological processes and pathways associated with each cell type. VSMCs were enriched in leukocyte mediated immunity, regulation of innate immune response, regulation of response to biotic stimulus, positive regulation of cytokine production and immune response-regulating signaling pathway. We presented a comprehensive analysis of the cellular composition and cell cycle dynamics in atherosclerosis. VSMCs were found in both AC and PA, with a higher abundance in PA (Fig. 2G-H). These analyses contribute to understanding the cytological landscape and molecular characteristics of atherosclerosis, shedding light on the cellular processes and potential therapeutic targets associated with the disease.

Cluster analysis of smooth muscle cell subsets in atherosclerosis

After performing quality control and removing batch effects, we obtained 8,436 high-quality VSMCs from atherosclerotic samples. The UMAP plot (Fig. 3A) illustrates the clustering of these high-quality cells and provides information about the tissue sources. Furthermore, it presents the annotation results of subclusters of atherosclerosis VSMCs based on differentially expressed genes, along with the distribution of tissue types (AC, PA) and cell cycle stages (G1, G2M, S) (Fig. 3B-D). The five cell clusters were annotated as follows based on the expression of differentially expressed genes: C0 LUM + VSMCs, C1 RAMP1 + VSMCs, C2 PTP4A3 + VSMCs, C3 CARMN + VSMCs, and C4 DLX5 + VSMCs (Fig. 3E). Differential analysis was conducted to examine the differences in Cells Stemness-AUC, nCount-RNA, nFeature-RNA, S.score, and G2M.score among these subgroups of VSMCs, as well as among different tissue types and cell cycle stages (Fig. 3F-H). The highest score was observed in the stemness gene set of C0 LUM + VSMCs, indicating their propensity for differentiation and their robust capacity for value-added processes. Additionally, the elevated levels of nCount-RNA and nFeature-RNA in C0 LUM + VSMCs reflect their heightened cellular activity. Cells in the G1 cell cycle stage exhibited higher levels of Cell-Stemness-AUC, nCount-RNA, and nFeature-RNA, indicating their active cellular state and their potential for value-added processes across diverse tissue locations. Notably, cells located in the PA site demonstrated greater activity. The expression of marker genes annotated for smooth muscle cells in atherosclerotic vessels is presented in Fig. 3I. HIF1A, CD44, KLF4, TWIST1, CTNNB1 and EPAS1 are highly expressed in VSMCs (Fig. 3J). Finally, the distribution of tissue types among the five VSMC subgroups is displayed using stacked bar charts and box plots. C0 LUM + VSMCs were found in both AC and PA, with a higher prevalence in PA. C1 RAMP1 + VSMCs and C3 CARMN + VSMCs were more abundant in AC, while the distribution of C2 PTP4A3 + VSMCs and C4 DLX5 + VSMCs showed no significant difference between PA and AC (Fig. 3K and L).

CytoTRACE and Monocle2 Quasi-timing analysis of atherosclerotic VSMCs

In order to investigate the impact of different VSMC subsets on the progression of atherosclerosis and infer cell differentiation trajectories, we utilized CytoTRACE and Monocle2. The differentiation potential of VSMCs in atherosclerosis was analyzed by CytoTRACE, and different cell types were indicated by different colors (Fig. 5A-B). The results showed that C0 LUM + VSMCs and C4 DLX5 + VSMCs had higher CytoTRACE scores, indicating a higher level of cell stemness, suggesting that they may be at the initial stage of atherosclerosis progression and play an important role in promoting the progression of the whole atherosclerotic disease. On the other hand, C3 CARMN + VSMCs had the lowest CytoREACE scores, suggesting that they may be at an advanced stage of disease progression. RPL10, RPS12, RPL7A, RPL13 and RPL30 were positively correlated with CytoTRACE score (Fig. 5C). CytoTRACE related stemness genes TWIST1, HIF1A and EPAS1 were highly expressed in C0 LUM + VSMCs (Fig. 5D). Subsequently, we employed Monocle to infer the cell differentiation trajectory in atherosclerotic disease (Fig. 5E-F). C0 LUM + VSMCs exhibited the lowest pseudo-timing level, while C3 CARMN + VSMCs displayed the highest pseudo-timing level. The inferred cell differentiation track was as follows: C0 LUM + VSMCs→C2 PTP4A3 + VSMCs→C4 DLX5 + VSMCs→C3 CARMN + VSMCs→C1 RAMP1 + VSMCs (Fig. 5G). C0 LUM + VSMCs were distributed in the initial phase of the pseudo-timing trajectory, C1 RAMP1 + VSMCs were distributed in the later phase, and the other subsets tended to be distributed in the intermediate phase (Fig. 5H). The differentiation trajectory of VSMCs was visualized through a two-dimensional trajectory diagram. This simulated differentiation trajectory consisted of two branching points, dividing the cells into five states, with state 1 as the starting point. After passing through the first branch, state 3 further differentiated into state 4 and state 5 (Fig. 5I).

Next, we analyzed the distribution of cell subpopulations, tissue sources, and cell cycle stages across the five states. C0 LUM + VSMCs were predominantly found in state 1, while C1 RAMP1 + VSMCs, C2 PTP4A3 + VSMCs, and C4 DLX5 + VSMCs were distributed across state 2, state 3, and state 5. Inflammation related enrichment of VSMCs in atherosclerosis reveals that C0 LUM + VSMCs are closely related to multiple inflammation related pathways

Due to the activation of the inflammatory response during the progression of atherosclerosis and its impact on disease development, we aimed to identify the VSMC subsets closely associated with the inflammatory response. We performed GOBP enrichment analysis on molecular pathways related to inflammation and found a strong correlation with C0 LUM + VSMCs. Firstly, the density distribution of VSMCs subsets under different inflammation-related enriched pathways is shown, and different VSMCs subsets have certain levels of distribution in these pathways (Fig. 7A, C, E, G and I). The pathways acute inflammatory response, cytokine production involved in inflammatory response, inflammatory response, positive regulation of acute inflammatory response, and regulation of neuroinflammatory response were all expressed at higher levels in the C0 LUM + VSMCs subpopulation than in other subsets. Moreover, these enriched pathways primarily originated from cells in the PA region (Fig. 7B, D, F, H and J). In conclusion, C0 LUM + VSMCs may actively participate in related inflammatory reactions and exhibit an increased inflammatory phenotype, which could potentially promote the progression of atherosclerosis.

Cellular crosstalk patterns between C0 LUM + VSMCs and other cells

To investigate the role of C0 LUM + VSMCs in atherosclerosis, we used CellChat to analyze the communication between C0 LUM + VSMCs and other cells. In the initial stage, we examined the intensity and quantity of interactions between C0 LUM + VSMCs and other cells (Fig. 8A). The results revealed a relatively strong interaction between C0 LUM + VSMCs and macrophages. By selecting C0 LUM + VSMCs as the source and macrophages and ECs as the target, we focused on cell-cell interactions mediated through direct contact, where the CD99 signaling pathway plays a pivotal role. Specifically, the key receptor-ligand pairs involved are CD99-CD99 and CD99-PILRA (Fig. 8H-I). To further explore the secretory cell interaction patterns among C0 LUM + VSMCs, macrophages, and ECs, we examined the relevant protein signaling pathways. When considering C0 LUM + VSMCs as the source and macrophages and ECs as the target, with a focus on secretory interaction patterns, we identified significant roles of the MIF and CCL signaling pathways. In vitro experimental verification of LUM+

In our experiment using HA-VSMCs, we compared the negative control group with the knock-down group. Several assays were conducted to evaluate the effects of LUM gene knockdown on cell activity, apoptosis, proliferation, migration, and invasion. In the cell activity assay (Fig. 11A), the CCK-8 assay showed a significant decrease in cell activity upon LUM gene knockdown. This indicates that LUM plays a role in promoting cell proliferation. Figure 11B and C demonstrate that the deletion of the LUM gene led to an increased rate of cellular apoptosis, suggesting that LUM has an inhibitory effect on apoptosis and promotes cell survival. EdU staining (Fig. 11D and E) revealed that LUM gene knockdown inhibited the proliferation of VSMCs. This further supports the role of LUM in promoting cell proliferation. The scratch test (Fig. 11F and G) showed that LUM knockdown significantly slowed down the migration of VSMC cells. This indicates that LUM is involved in promoting cell migration. Additionally, the Transwell test (Fig. 11H-J) demonstrated that LUM knockdown significantly reduced the migration and invasion abilities of VSMC cells. This suggests that LUM plays a role in promoting both cell migration and invasion. Overall, the findings from our experiment indicate that LUM has a positive effect on the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of VSMCs. The knockdown of LUM resulted in decreased cell activity, increased apoptosis, inhibited proliferation, and reduced migration and invasion abilities. These results suggest that LUM promotes the progression of atherosclerosis by influencing the behavior of VSMCs.

Discussion

Atherosclerosis plays a crucial role in cardiovascular diseases and is a chronic arterial condition characterized by endothelial dysfunction, inflammatory response, lipid accumulation, and abnormal vascular remodeling and VSMC proliferation [36, 37]. Previous studies have demonstrated the key role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis [38], and VSMCs can be stimulated into a pro-inflammatory phenotype, significantly promoting atherosclerotic calcification, leading to the formation of calcifying vascular cells (CVCs). scRNA-seq [39, 40] has been widely used in cardiovascular disease research to elucidate cellular subpopulations, transcriptional changes under normal and pathological conditions, and identify phenotypic heterogeneity within single-cell subtypes [41].Through scRNA-seq analysis, we have identified VSMC subpopulations that play a critical role in the progression of atherosclerosis, key pathways involved in the interaction between VSMCs and endothelial cells, and hub genes influencing disease progression, deepening our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying atherosclerosis. VSMCs are the major cell type present in atherosclerotic plaques at all stages [42], and VSMC-related genes play important roles in atherosclerosis [43, 44]. Under vascular injury conditions, VSMCs can transition from a quiescent “contractile” phenotype to a pro-inflammatory phenotype, also known as dedifferentiated or synthetic VSMCs, with enhanced migratory and proliferative capacities. These activated VSMCs can effectively proliferate and migrate, promoting vascular wall repair [45].

VSMC phenotype switching leads to a less differentiated form lacking VSMC markers, including macrophage-like cells, which directly contribute to atherosclerosis progression. Additionally, VSMC proliferation may be beneficial throughout the entire process of atherosclerosis [45]. Our findings confirm that VSMC phenotypic switching contributes to a less differentiated, macrophage-like state, which plays a direct role in atherosclerosis progression. While VSMC proliferation may have protective effects in certain contexts, its dysregulation is closely linked to disease advancement. By analyzing high-quality single-cell data, we identified six major cell types and further delineated five VSMC subpopulations. Notably, the C0 LUM + VSMCs displayed stem-like features and pro-inflammatory potential, suggesting they may represent a key transitional phenotype in disease development [46]. Our analysis revealed that C0 LUM + VSMCs exhibited the highest stemness scores among all VSMC subpopulations, along with elevated levels of nCount RNA and nFeature RNA, reflecting a highly active and undifferentiated cellular state. These features are consistent with a dedifferentiated, stem-like phenotype. The predominance of these cells in the G1 phase further supports their proliferative nature. Notably, CD34 and KLF4, two key stemness-related genes, were significantly upregulated in this subgroup. CD34, in particular, has been recognized as a marker of progenitor-like VSMCs and is known to be induced during phenotypic switching [47]. These findings suggest that C0 LUM + VSMCs may represent a plastic, inflammation-prone subpopulation with a pivotal role in atherosclerosis progression. Furthermore, co-expression of CD31 and/or CD34 in heterogeneous cells has been observed in progressing atherosclerotic lesions [48]. Increased expression of CD68 and KLF4 has been observed in smooth muscle cells (SMCs) isolated from atherosclerotic arteries [49]. HIF1A-induced upregulation of KLF4 plays a crucial role in VSMC migration under hypoxia [50], and KLF4 is involved in VSMC phenotype switching in cardiovascular diseases. In summary, the above findings indicate that C0 LUM + VSMCs exhibit a low level of differentiation, representing a dedifferentiated phenotype.

To further investigate the metabolic features of atherosclerotic VSMCs, we analyzed metabolic pathway activity across the five identified VSMC subpopulations. Oxidative phosphorylation emerged as the most prominently activated pathway. This is particularly relevant, as mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation is a major source of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which play a key role in vascular pathology. Excessive oxidative stress is known to promote VSMC apoptosis, hypertrophy, and fibrosis, thereby contributing to the progression of atherosclerosis and other aging-related cardiovascular diseases, including myocardial infarction and heart failure [51]. MiR-127-3p promotes macrophage proliferation in atherosclerosis by interfering with fatty acid profiles and oxidative phosphorylation [52]. Reduced oxidative phosphorylation, decreased production of nitric oxide and peroxynitrite, can contribute to the pathogenesis of dementia and atherosclerosis [53]. These findings suggest that enhanced oxidative metabolism in specific VSMC subsets may underlie their pathological activation and inflammatory potential in atherosclerotic lesions.

To delineate the differentiation landscape of atherosclerotic VSMCs, we applied CytoTRACE, Monocle2, and Slingshot. All three trajectory inference methods consistently indicated that C0 LUM + VSMCs represent a less differentiated, stem-like phenotype. In CytoTRACE, both C0 and C4 subgroups exhibited high stemness scores, while Monocle analysis revealed that C0 cells occupied the earliest pseudotime states, suggesting a primitive differentiation status. Slingshot further positioned C0 LUM + VSMCs as the origin of all three inferred lineages, and intriguingly, in Lineage 1, they also appeared at the terminal state—implying a possible dedifferentiated, activated state with dynamic phenotypic cycling.

Given the well-established role of inflammation in both the initiation and progression of atherosclerosis [9–13], we further examined the enrichment of inflammatory pathways. C0 LUM + VSMCs showed significant association with multiple inflammation-related terms, including acute inflammatory response, cytokine production, and neuroinflammatory regulation, highlighting their likely involvement in immune modulation within plaques. Taken together, these findings suggest that C0 LUM + VSMCs may represent a proliferative, pro-inflammatory, and dedifferentiated subpopulation that contributes to disease progression in early stages and possibly plaque remodeling or stability in advanced stages.

To elucidate the intercellular communication landscape in atherosclerosis, we employed CellChat to analyze signaling interactions involving VSMC subpopulations. Notably, C0 LUM + VSMCs exhibited strong bidirectional communication with macrophages, suggesting a potentially important role in immune modulation. Input and output signals from C0 cells were predominantly classified under Pattern 1, enriched for signaling pathways such as COLLAGEN, FFN1, CD99, SPP1 (input), and MIF, APP, CXCL, PTN (output).

Among these, MIF (macrophage migration inhibitory factor) [54] is a pro-inflammatory cytokine that can be induced by oxLDL in VSMCs via NF-κB activation, promoting VSMC migration and contributing to plaque progression [33]. CXCL12, another key output signal, is involved in cell chemotaxis, proliferation, and metabolism, and is known to accelerate atherosclerosis through mechanisms including dyslipidemia, inflammatory neovascularization, and insulin resistance [55]. Furthermore, we identified CD99 as the dominant pathway mediating interactions between VSMCs and endothelial cells, especially in the case of C0 LUM + VSMCs. CD99 facilitates monocyte transendothelial migration [56–58], and its blockade has been shown to significantly reduce leukocyte infiltration and plaque burden [59].These findings highlight the potential role of C0 LUM + VSMCs as pro-inflammatory communicators in the atherosclerotic microenvironment, engaging in pathological crosstalk with both macrophages and endothelial cells.

TF regulatory network analysis identified RARG, HLX, KLF4, FOXO1, and KLF3 as the top regulators in C0 LUM + VSMCs. Among them, KLF4 is a well-established driver of SMC phenotypic modulation, with upregulated expression in intermediate and fibroblast-like SMC states. KLF4 also contributes to foam cell formation via enhanced unfolded protein response and lipid uptake, underscoring its key role in VSMC phenotype transitions and atherosclerosis progression [60]. Both LUM and ODC1 are crucial for VSMC migration and proliferation [61, 62].Collectively, these data suggest that LUM acts as a positive regulator of VSMC pathological activation, contributing to disease progression [63]. As a defining marker of the C0 subpopulation, LUM has been implicated in promoting atherosclerosis. Alongside genes such as Cemip, Mfge8, Spp1, and Serpina3, LUM participates in atherosclerosis-associated gene expression programs. Future studies should further investigate whether the intervention of key factors such as LUM, KLF4 or MIF can achieve the regulation of phenotypic transformation of VSMC, and evaluate whether this subpopulation is associated with plaque stability and the risk of bleeding and rupture, so as to provide potential targets for clinical treatment and risk prediction.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study underscores the critical role of C0 LUM + VSMCs in atherosclerosis progression. These cells, identified at an early differentiation stage with high proliferative capacity, exhibit a dedifferentiated and pro-inflammatory phenotype characterized by distinct gene expression programs. Integrating single-cell transcriptomics with experimental validation and leveraging advanced big data analytics and network-based approaches, we demonstrated that C0 LUM + VSMCs are enriched in inflammation-related signaling pathways, including CD99, MIF, and CCL, and actively engage in intercellular communication with endothelial cells and macrophages to drive disease progression. The unique molecular signatures and regulatory networks of C0 LUM + VSMCs reveal promising opportunities for the discovery of novel diagnostic biomarkers and the development of personalized therapeutic strategies. This work highlights the transformative potential of multi-omics integration and AI-powered functional genomics in advancing precision medicine for inflammatory vascular diseases.

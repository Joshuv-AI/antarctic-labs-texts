Validity of a seven-gene signature as a non-invasive biomarker for cirrhosis: a cross-sectional study in Egyptian patients with HBV infection
Authors: Ahlam Abd El-Kader, Mai Abd El-Meguid, Naglaa Zayed, Shereen Abdel Alem, Ahmed Barkat, Reham M Dawood
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatitis B virus (HBV) infection poses a significant public health challenge, leading to liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The clinical course of HBV infection is influenced by viral, environmental, and host genetic variants. Recently, a Cirrhosis Risk Score (CRS) based on seven genetic variants has been developed to identify individuals at risk of developing cirrhosis. This study aimed to assess the predictive value of seven gene signatures in Egyptian patients with HBV infection as molecular biomarkers for cirrhosis.MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, 170 HBV-infected patients exhibiting various degrees of liver fibrosis were recruited. Genotyping for the seven gene SNPs was performed using allelic discrimination assays. Subsequently, the non-invasive scores APRI and FIB-4 were computed and compared with CRS values. Furthermore, an in silico analysis was performed to examine the alterations in gene expression associated with the progression of fibrosis.ResultsThe findings revealed a significantly higher prevalence of high CRS scores in late fibrosis (p = 0.006). The mean of CRS was higher in late than in early fibrosis (0.70 vs. 0.60; P = 0.003). However, APRI and FIB-4 scores did not differ significantly across the groups. The ROC analysis indicated that the CRS score can distinguish between patients with early and late fibrosis, with an AUC of 0.63 (p = 0.0034). The risk genotypes in DEGS1 (rs4290029), STXBP5L (rs17740066), and AQP2 (rs2878771) were associated with late fibrosis. According to the in silico analysis, there is a remarkable upregulation of DEGS1 and a downregulation of STXBP5L in late fibrosis as compared to early fibrosis. While the AQP2 showed no significant variation between early and late fibrosis.ConclusionThe CRS score showed a modest predictive ability in HBV-infected Egyptian patients; it can be used in conjunction with imaging and clinical tools to improve risk stratification. Moreover, DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2 genetic variants demonstrated a significant association with cirrhosis risk. However, only the DEGS1 and STXBP5L genes showed dysregulated expression levels, suggesting their potential relevance as diagnostic biomarkers in liver fibrosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04812-7.Keywords: HBV, CRS, SNPs, Liver, Cirrhosis

Introduction

Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection continues to pose a significant global public health concern, affecting more than 257 million individuals and causing over 850,000 fatalities annually due to liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [1–3]. Despite the availability of preventive vaccines and antiviral therapies, the incidence of the disease continues to rise, with over 1.5 million new infections occurring every year [4]. Approximately one-third of global liver cancer-related deaths are attributed to HBV infection [5]. In Egypt, the burden of chronic hepatitis B infection is particularly severe, with an estimated two to three million individuals affected [6]. The susceptibility and severity of liver disease progression are consequences of the interplay between viral factors and host genetic characteristics, alongside physiological, environmental, and metabolic factors [7]. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have elucidated the impact of single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in regulating immune responses and susceptibility to liver disease, including persistent HBV infection and the development of liver fibrosis [8–10]. However, there is limited research on the role of gene polymorphisms in the development of HBV-related liver cirrhosis; there is a relative paucity of research examining their role in the progression to cirrhosis, particularly within diverse ethnic populations. A seven-single-nucleotide polymorphism-based Cirrhosis Risk Score (CRS) was previously established in Caucasian patients with hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection and has demonstrated its efficacy in predicting fibrosis progression with clinical significance [11]. In our laboratory, the CRS signature has been validated for its capability to distinguish between mild and advanced fibrosis in HCV patients and to detect the HCV recurrence after liver transplantation [12, 13]. Considering the similar fibrogenic pathway present across chronic liver diseases, including hepatitis B and hepatitis C, the CRS may also serve a valuable role in assessing fibrosis risk in HBV-related conditions. The objective of the current study is to evaluate the correlation between cirrhosis-risk SNPs in HBV-infected patients and the probability of fibrosis progression. Additionally, the comparative accuracy of the three scoring systems—CRS, APRI, and FIB-4— has been assessed in predicting advanced fibrosis development.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient recruitment

The current research is a cross-sectional study conducted on HBV-infected patients to assess the relationship between cirrhosis-related genetic variants and fibrosis progression. One hundred seventy HBV-infected patients were recruited from the Tropical Medicine Department, El Kasr El Aini, Faculty of Medicine, Cairo University, Egypt, between January 2023 and March 2024. The main inclusion criteria comprised patients aged 18–65, exhibiting serological evidence of chronic hepatitis B, defined as sustained anti-HBsAg positivity > 6 months, and HBV DNA positivity in serum. The study’s exclusion criteria encompassed coinfection with other chronic liver diseases such as hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), type II diabetes, and severe obesity/metabolic syndrome. This study was designed and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in reporting observational research.

Ethical approval

The research conducted at the National Research Center, Cairo, Egypt, was approved by the research ethics committee under protocol number 12,410,123, adhering to the guidelines of the Helsinki Declaration. Before blood collection, all eligible participants provided their consent by signing an informed consent form.

Hepatic function assessment and diagnosis of HBV infection

Clinical assessments were conducted to evaluate hepatic function, such as alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin, AFP, and bilirubin levels, through routine blood and biochemical tests using an automated biochemical analyzer (Model 7180; Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. Serological markers of HBV infection, such as hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), antibody to HBsAg (anti-HBs), hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg), antibody to HBeAg (anti-HBe), and antibody to hepatitis B core antigen (anti-HBc), were measured using the ELISA technique (Accu-Tell® ELISA kit). HBV infection diagnosis was established through real-time PCR using a Rotor-Gene® Q Instrument for the presence of HBV DNA, following the manufacturer’s protocol with the Artus® HBV RG PCR Quantification Kit (QIAGEN, Germany). All laboratory parameters were performed using standardized protocols and validated instruments.

Liver fibrosis assessment

The severity of liver fibrosis was assessed using the Fibroscan. The results indicated liver stiffness and fibrosis stage, expressed in kilopascals (kPa). The FibroScan score correlates directly with the degree of fibrosis, with certain kPa values corresponding to the METAVIR stages (F0–F4). The widely recognized thresholds for individuals infected with HBV are as follows: Up to 5.9 kPa roughly denotes F0, 6.0-7.1 denotes F1, 7.2–10.4 denotes F2, 10.5–18.1 denotes F3, and ≥ 18.2 kPa denotes F4 [14]. Subsequently, based on the Fibroscan scores, patients were categorized into two groups: early fibrotic (F0-F1, n = 85) and late fibrotic (F2-F4, n = 85) groups.

Association of non-invasive serum markers for fibrosis progression

The fibrosis scoring system (FIB-4) and Aspartate Aminotransferase-to-Platelet Ratio Index (APRI) scores are valuable non-invasive tools for liver fibrosis assessment. The FIB-4 score is computed based on age, serum AST, ALT levels, and platelet counts according to Sterling’s formula [15]: [Age (year) × AST (IU/l)]/ [platelet count (× 109/l) × ALT (IU/l)1/2]. A FIB-4 score less than 1.45 typically indicates minimal to no fibrosis, while a score ranging from 1.45 to 3.25 suggests an indeterminate state, and a score exceeding 3.25 signifies an increased risk for advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis. For this study, we used the 1.45 FIB-4 cutoff for descriptive classification.

On the other hand, the APRI score, calculated using AST and platelet count according to Wai’s formula [16]: APRI = (AST (IU/L)/upper limit of normal of AST)/platelet count (109/L) × 100; upper limit of normal of AST = 40 IU/L. The APRI score below 0.5 indicates moderate fibrosis, while a score exceeding 1.5 signifies significant fibrosis.

Extraction of genomic DNA

Five ml of venous blood was withdrawn from HBV-infected patients on anticoagulant (EDTA) tubes, and genomic DNA was extracted using a QIAamp DNA Blood Mini extraction kit (Qiagen). The concentration and purity of genomic DNA samples were quantified using a Thermo Scientific NanoDrop™ Spectrophotometer.

Genotyping of CRS

The seven single-nucleotide polymorphisms were genotyped using a real-time PCR protocol employing the TaqMan MGB™ probe for allelic discrimination (Applied Biosystems). In brief, 1.25 µL of a 40X primer and probe mix (ABI/Life Technologies, USA), containing two different reporter dyes (one labeled with VIC® dye and the other with 6FAM™ dye) to distinguish between the two alleles, was added to 12.5 µL of 2X TaqMan® genotyping master mix (ABI/Life Technologies, USA) in a 25 µL final volume of DNase/RNase-free water (Invitrogen/Life Technologies, USA) and template DNA.

The program conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 10 min, 95 °C for 15 s, and 60 °C for 1 min. The last two steps were repeated 40 times. The PCR run was conducted on the Rotor-Gene real-time PCR system (Qiagen, Santa Clarita, CA). Based on the CRS values, the patients were stratified into three groups: high risk (CRS > 0.7), intermediate risk (CRS 0.5–0.7), or low risk of cirrhosis (CRS < 0.5).

The expression levels of DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2 in early and late HBV fibrotic patients via in silico analysis

The gene expression data for DEGS1 (transcript ID: 209250_at), AQP2 (transcript ID: 206672_at), and STXBP5L (transcript ID: 1568896_at) were analyzed from a dataset of 124 liver biopsy specimens obtained from chronic HBV patients [25]. The data were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database under the accession number GSE84044. Fibrosis staging was conducted histologically and classified into four stages, ranging from F0 (no fibrosis) to F4 (cirrhosis). Differential expression analysis between early (F0–F1) and late (F2–F4) fibrosis was performed using linear modeling and the Mann-Whitney analysis, with significance defined as adjusted p < 0.05 and with a |log2 fold-change| exceeding 1.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed through the IBM SPSS (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) version 25. Quantitative variables are expressed as the mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range). For the quantitative variables, which are normally distributed, the t-test was used to indicate the significant differences between the CRS value and early and late fibrosis, as well as CRS scores and parenchymal variations. Qualitative variables are expressed as counts and percentages. The chi-square test was used to assess a significant association between two categorical variables, namely CRS score and gender, at p < 0.05. The Mann-Whitney analysis was performed to compare gene expression levels of selected genes between early and late fibrosis. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis has been performed to test the diagnostic utility of the CRS score. All variables were fully reported, and no missing data occurred. Due to the exploratory nature of the study, no multivariable regression analysis was performed to adjust for potential confounders; therefore, results are presented as unadjusted estimates. The sample size (n = 170) was estimated using Cochran’s formula [26] with a 95% confidence level and a margin of error of 5%.

Results

Clinical and laboratory data of early and late fibrotic HBV-infected patients

A total of 170 patients were assessed for eligibility and included in the final analysis. All enrolled participants completed the study and were categorized into early fibrosis (F0–F1, n = 85) and late fibrosis (F2–F4, n = 85). No participants were excluded after enrollment. The clinical and laboratory findings of the 170 HBV-infected patients are depicted in Table 2. The HBV patients were divided into two groups: early fibrosis (F0-F1, n = 85) and late fibrosis (F2–F4, n = 85). According to the data, elderly patients are susceptible to developing late fibrosis. Moreover, a higher incidence of male patients was significantly observed in the late fibrotic stage (F2-F4) than in female patients (P = 0.008), indicating that males may be at a higher risk of developing liver fibrosis. Moreover, the data obtained from the late fibrotic group demonstrated a significant increase in AST levels relative to those in early fibrotic groups (P = 0.05). All the following markers (hemoglobin, total leukocytes, platelets, creatinine, fasting blood sugar, ALT, total and direct bilirubin, albumin, INR, cholesterol, triglycerides, and AFP levels) did not show statistical significance across the groups.

Correlation between the gender and the CRS value

To evaluate the relationship between gender and CRS values, a further analysis was performed. The distribution of CRS scores among males (n = 121) and females (n = 49) is presented in Table 3. The data demonstrated no significant association between CRS scores and gender. Among males, 15.7% were classified as low risk, 33.1% as intermediate risk, and 51.2% as high risk, while females showed 20.4%, 28.6%, and 51.0% in the respective categories (p = 0.714). These findings indicate that gender does not significantly influence CRS distribution in Egyptian patients with HBV infection.

Assessment of the CRS in early and late HBV fibrotic patients

According to the CRS score, the patients were categorized into three risk subgroups (high risk, CRS > 0.7; intermediate risk, CRS 0.5–0.7; low risk, CRS < 0.5). The distribution of CRS scores in the early (F0-F1, n = 85) and late (F2-F4) stages of liver fibrosis is shown in Fig. 1. The distribution of CRS values varied significantly between the early and late grades of fibrosis as follows: 25.9% low risk, 31.8% intermediate risk, and 42.3% high risk; and 8.2% low risk, 31.8% intermediate risk, and 60% high risk, respectively. Interestingly, a low-risk score (< 0.5) was more frequently observed in early fibrosis than in late fibrosis, and a high-risk score (≥ 0.7) was significantly more frequent in late fibrosis than in early fibrosis (P = 0.006).

Assessment of CRS, FIB-4, and APRI scores in HBV-infected patients

The CHB patients were categorized into early and late fibrosis based on fibroscan, and three markers (CRS, FIB-4, and APRI) were assessed in the enrolled patients. The data revealed that the mean of CRS, FIB-4, and APRI scores in the early fibrotic patients were 0.60, 1.1, and 0.38, respectively, while the mean of CRS, FIB-4, and APRI scores in the late fibrotic patients were 0.70, 1.2, and 0.54, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2. The obtained data showed that the CRS mean was 0.6 for early fibrosis and 0.7 for late fibrosis (P = 0.003). Furthermore, APRI and FIB-4 did not show statistical variation between early and late fibrotic patients.

The relationship between CRS, FIB-4, and APRI values with ultrasonography hepatic changes

The study population was grouped based on ultrasonography results into normal and bright liver (n = 125) and coarse liver (n = 33). The data showed that low FIB-4 scores (< 1.45) were more common in patients with normal and bright livers than in those with coarse livers. Conversely, a high FIB-4 score (> 1.45) was more frequently seen in patients with coarse livers compared to those with normal and bright livers (P = 0.04), as shown in Fig. 3. A higher FIB-4 score was linked to coarse parenchymal changes. However, CRS and APRI scores did not show an association with hepatic parenchymal changes.

Assessment of the prediction ability of the CRS score as a potential diagnostic marker for fibrosis progression

To evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of the CRS score as a potential diagnostic biomarker for cirrhosis progression in HBV patients, the ROC curve analysis was used to examine the CRS score’s ability to discriminate between early and late fibrosis groups. The data obtained from the ROC curve showed that the CRS has a modest discriminatory performance with an estimated AUC of 0.63 (P = 0.0034), a sensitivity of 80% and a specificity of 42.35% at a cutoff = 0.6, as shown in Fig. 4.

The expression levels of DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2 in early and late HBV fibrotic patients via in silico analysis

To further elucidate the expression dynamics of DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2 during fibrosis progression, gene expression data were extracted from the GEO database (accession number: GSE84044) and analyzed as illustrated in Fig. 5. The GSE84044 dataset comprised 124 patients infected with HBV and was divided into early fibrosis (F0–F1, n = 63) and late fibrosis (F2–F4, n = 61). The findings indicated that the expression level of DEGS1 was significantly elevated in patients with late fibrosis relative to the early fibrosis group (p = 0.01). Conversely, the expression of STXBP5L was significantly reduced in patients with late fibrosis compared to early fibrosis (p = 0.0007). Additionally, the expression level of AQP2 showed no significant variation between the early and late fibrotic groups. These findings demonstrate that DEGS1 and STXBP5L may play vital roles in fibrosis progression, while AQP2 appears less directly implicated.

Discussion

Despite significant advancements in the management of chronic hepatitis B, liver diseases such as cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma continue to represent the predominant causes of morbidity and mortality in the world [27]. Identifying patients at high risk for the progression of fibrosis is crucial for facilitating timely intervention. In this regard, host genetic profiling has emerged as a viable tool for risk stratification. Several studies have established a correlation between genetic variants and gene expression to liver disease progression [28, 29]. It has been reported that polymorphisms in the IL10 gene and variations in IL-18 are potentially linked to liver damage and HCC [30, 31]. Moreover, Xu et al. [32] identified six predictive genes (CD24, CXCL6, EHF, ITGBL1, LUM, and SOX9) that can diagnose chronic hepatitis B patients at a heightened risk for cirrhosis.

The current study aimed to validate the CRS score in HBV patients of Egyptian ethnicity and to assess the comparative accuracy of three scoring systems—CRS, FIB-4, and APRI—in predicting the progression of advanced fibrosis. This study represents the first evaluation of CRS host genetic variants for predicting fibrosis development among individuals with chronic HBV infection in Egypt.

It was reported that advanced fibrosis is more prevalent in male than female patients with chronic hepatitis B [33]. Similarly, the current study demonstrated a significantly higher incidence of late fibrosis (F2-F4) in male than female patients (P = 0.008), suggesting that males may possess a greater risk for the development of advanced liver fibrosis. This finding aligns with the research conducted by Peng et al. [22], who indicated a higher male-to-female ratio in the HBV cirrhotic group compared with the non-cirrhotic group. However, in the current study, the CRS scores did not exhibit statistically significant differences between genders, although male patients appeared more likely to present with late-stage fibrosis. This observation implies that the CRS may function as a gender independent biomarker, applicable to both genders in predicting HBV-related fibrosis. Moreover, the present study revealed that 60% of late fibrotic patients had a high-risk score, while 8.2% showed a low-risk score. Regarding the early stage of fibrosis, the data revealed that 42.3% of the early fibrotic patients had a high-risk score, while 25.9% showed a low-risk score. The accuracy of CRS should be validated in the late stage of fibrosis since some patients with an early stage of fibrosis can progress to the late stage over time.

Various non-invasive methods, such as the APRI and FIB-4 scores, have been proposed to assess the stage of fibrosis and to differentiate between patients with cirrhosis and those with early-stage fibrosis. However, these tests exhibit limited utility in predicting patients at high risk of developing cirrhosis [34]. Previously, it was reported that the combination of clinical and laboratory markers may improve the selection of patients at high risk of developing cirrhosis. Furthermore, growing evidence suggests that genetic polymorphisms may be implicated in disease development and progression and can serve as predisposition markers [35]. In the current study, the performance of the CRS, FIB-4, and APRI as non-invasive markers was assessed to monitor the progression of liver fibrosis and to distinguish between patients with late and early fibrosis. The data revealed that the CRS scores were significantly higher in patients with late-stage fibrosis than those with early-stage fibrosis. Besides, FIB-4 and APRI scores were higher in patients with late-stage fibrosis than those with early-stage fibrosis, without reaching statistical significance. These results align with the research conducted by Huang et al. [11], who identified the CRS score as a predictor of liver cirrhosis progression in Caucasian patients with chronic hepatitis C. Additionally, Dawood et al. [12] reported that the CRS score effectively differentiates between mild and advanced fibrosis in Egyptian patients infected with HCV.

Chronic hepatitis B infection induces significant liver parenchymal changes, which impact morbidity and mortality rates. The FIB-4 scoring system, in conjunction with liver tissue imaging through ultrasound (US), is an effective and convenient approach for assessing hepatic tissue alterations [36]. The FIB-4 score is a non-invasive biochemical marker and a scoring system utilized to evaluate morphological changes within the liver parenchyma [37]. The present study investigated the association between the three non-invasive scores (CRS, FIB-4, and APRI) and various grades of hepatic parenchymal changes. The data revealed a significant prevalence of low FIB-4 scores among patients with normal or bright livers, and the highest FIB-4 scores were more frequently observed in patients exhibiting coarse liver features. In contrast, neither CRS nor APRI scores demonstrated a significant association with hepatic parenchymal changes. This aligns with the findings of Ballestri et al. [38], who reported that the FIB-4 index demonstrates a significant potential in diagnosing liver fibrosis resulting from viral hepatitis in patients exhibiting advanced fibrosis. ROC curve analysis indicated that the CRS score possesses a modest discriminatory capability for distinguishing between early and late fibrosis (AUC = 0.63, sensitivity = 80%, specificity = 42.35%). Although the CRS score shows potential for fibrosis risk stratification, its diagnostic accuracy is currently limited and requires further evaluation in larger multicentric studies. Additionally, integration of medical imaging, biochemical, and clinical information is required to improve its predictive power.

On the genetic level, the current findings identified three SNPs—rs4290029 in DEGS1, rs17740066 in STXBP5L, and rs2878771 in AQP2 that are significantly associated with advanced fibrosis. The G allele of the DEGS1 gene (rs4290029) is recognized as a significant risk factor for cirrhosis and plays a critical role in the regulation of ceramide biosynthesis, which impacts autophagy and apoptosis, the key processes in the progression of liver disease [17]. Furthermore, the A allele of STXBP5L (rs17740066) may influence fibrogenesis through its role in vesicular transport; however, the precise mechanisms involved in liver pathology require further investigation [39, 40]. Lastly, the G allele variant of the AQP2 gene (rs2878771) is involved in cell viability, migration, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [41, 42]. The genotype frequency of the remaining genes (AP3S2, TRPM5, TLR4, and AZIN1) is equally distributed between early and late fibrosis. This suggests that the variance within those genes is not involved in the development of liver fibrosis in patients with HBV. This finding contrasts with data from Peng et al. [22], which suggested that TRPM5 and AZIN1 are linked to the risk and progression rate of liver cirrhosis in Chinese HBV patients. Such discrepancies are likely attributable to ethnic variations in allele frequencies and differences in genetic susceptibility to liver disease. To gain deeper insights into the functional role of three candidate genes, DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2, in the liver fibrosis induced by HBV, a transcriptomic analysis has been performed. The obtained data demonstrated a notable upregulation of DEGS1 in late-stage fibrosis, as DEGS1 encodes delta [4]-desaturase, sphingolipid 1, an essential enzyme involved in sphingolipid metabolism. The dysregulation of sphingolipid pathways has been linked to the activation of hepatic stellate cells, inflammation, extracellular matrix deposition, and increased ceramide synthesis, contributing to the progression of fibrosis [43]. Previous research has established a linkage between sphingolipid metabolism and the progression of liver disease, as well as hepatocarcinogenesis, indicating that DEGS1 may be involved in the transition from fibrosis to cirrhosis and HCC [44]. Conversely, the downregulation of STXBP5L suggests an impairment in vesicle transport and exocytosis during the progression of fibrosis [45]. The vesicular trafficking is crucial for the secretion of matrix proteins and cytokines; the loss of STXBP5L expression may exacerbate the dysregulation between matrix production and clearance, thereby inducing fibrogenesis. Moreover, an in vitro study has demonstrated that STXBP5L downregulation promotes apoptosis, cellular migration, invasion, and proliferation [46]. In contrast, AQP2, a water channel protein predominantly produced in renal tissue, exhibited no significant differential expression during liver fibrosis progression. Its minimal expression in liver tissue may be attributable to its low relevance or lack of association with fibrogenic pathways, which elucidates the findings observed in this dataset.

The findings presented in this study demonstrate the importance of the CRS as a non-invasive genetic tool for predicting the risk of fibrosis. However, considering the moderate performance metrics observed, it is advisable to utilize the CRS in conjunction with established clinical tools instead of relying on it as a primary diagnostic tool.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations that warrant careful consideration. The study was conducted at a single center with a moderate sample size, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings; therefore, further studies with larger multicenter samples are recommended. Additionally, the absence of a hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) group, which represents the end stage of fibrosis, is noteworthy. The CRS exhibited only modest accuracy, indicating that it should be combined with other clinical and imaging markers. Furthermore, more in-depth mechanistic analyses are necessary to explore the biological pathways associated with the identified genetic associations.

Conclusion

This study validates the efficacy of a seven-SNP-based Cirrhosis Risk Score (CRS) in predicting the progression of fibrosis among Egyptian patients with HBV infection. The findings suggest that the CRS score, initially developed for populations infected with HCV, provides potential as a non-invasive genetic biomarker for identifying HBV patients at high risk of advanced fibrosis. Moreover, specific SNPs in DEGS1, STXBP5L, and AQP2 demonstrated a significant association with cirrhosis risk, underscoring the importance of host genetic variability in the progression of liver disease. However, only DEGS1 and STXBP5L genes showed expression level dysregulations, suggesting their potential relevance as diagnostic biomarkers in liver fibrosis.

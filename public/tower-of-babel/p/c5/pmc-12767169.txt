Generation of induced pluripotent stem cell lines from patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm carrying the ACTA2 gene mutation
Authors: Tian-Ding Liu, Shuai Sun, Lu Ren, Yan Zhuge, Souhrid Mukherjee, David H Liang, Joseph C Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThoracic aortic aneurysms (TAA) are abnormal dilations of the aorta above the diaphragm. Mutations in the ACTA2 gene are among the most common genetic causes of TAA. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) provide a platform for comprehensive biological investigations and the development of tailored medical strategies for this condition. Here, two iPSC lines were generated from TAA patients harboring the ACTA2 mutation, c.773 G > A. Both iPSC lines demonstrated characteristic pluripotent stem cell morphology, expressed core pluripotency markers, maintained normal karyotypes, and possessed the capacity for trilineage differentiation. These cell lines represent a valuable resource for studying the genetic and cellular mechanisms underlying TAA pathogenesis associated with ACTA2 mutations.

Resource utility

The development of human iPSCs represents a significant milestone in precision medicine, offering a powerful tool to advance our understanding of cardiovascular disorders, including TAA. Here, we generated two iPSC lines from patients harboring a pathogenic mutation in the ACTA2 gene, providing a valuable resource for investigating the underlying mechanisms of TAA and evaluating the potential therapeutic strategies.

Resource details

Among all morbidities in the Western population, TAA represents a significant health concern, affecting approximately 1 % to 2 % of individuals. Atherosclerosis, congenital connective tissue disorders, infectious diseases, and genetic factors are recognized risk contributors. TAA is associated with serious complications, including aortic entrapment, compression of surrounding structures, fistula formation, rupture, and thrombosis, which makes it one of the most severe cardiovascular diagnoses (Verstraeten et al., 2017). The ACTA2 gene encodes smooth muscle alpha-actin, a member of the actin protein family essential for cell motility and muscle contraction (Kaw et al., 2022). TAA caused by ACTA2 mutations involves complex pathological processes, including abnormalities in structural proteins, disrupted signaling pathways, inflammation, and depletion of vascular smooth muscle cells (SMCs) (Yagyu et al., 2023). The advent of human iPSC technology has revolutionized TAA research by enabling the generation of patient-specific SMCs (Zhu et al., 2021). These iPSC-derived SMCs (iPSC-SMCs) exhibit key features relevant to the pathological expansion of blood vessel walls. They are essential for investigating TAA at the cellular level, particularly the effect of ACTA2 mutations on smooth muscle function and vascular wall integrity. Moreover, iPSC-SMCs are valuable for evaluating novel therapeutic agents and developing personalized treatment strategies, which underscores their significant role in advancing TAA research and therapy development.

Here, we used two reprogrammed iPSC lines (SCVli138-A and SCVli139-A) derived from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) isolated from patients with TAA. Specifically, SCVli138-A was obtained from a 56-year-old woman harboring the c.773 G > A variant, and SCVli139-A was obtained from a 28-year-old man harboring the same variant (Table 1). Sendai virus containing four Yamanaka factors was used to reprogram PBMCs into iPSCs. The typical morphology was displayed by two iPSC lines (Fig. 1A, scale bar: 300 μm). The iPSC line’s pluripotency was validated by both RT-qPCR and immunofluorescence for pluripotency markers. In iPSCs, both lines exhibited upregulation of NANOG and SOX2, as validated by RT-qPCR analysis (Fig. 1B). The previously established iPSC line served as a positive control, and iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes were used as a negative control. The three pluripotency markers, NANOG, OCT3/4, and SOX2, were validated at the protein level using immunostaining (Fig. 1C, scale bar: 300 μm). Differentiation into endodermal, mesodermal, and ectodermal lineages was successfully achieved for both lines at passages 19 and 17. (Fig. 1D, scale bars: 100, 300, and 100 μm). The presence of the c.773 G > A mutation was confirmed using Sanger sequencing and compared with a control line SCVI15 (Fig. 1E). Both lines demonstrated normal karyotypes (Fig. 1F, both at passage 15). Despite the presence of the Sendai virus in early passages, it was undetectable after passage 20 in both lines (Fig. 1G). Additionally, both lines tested negative for Mycoplasma contamination (Fig. 1H). To confirm their origin from the PBMC donor and verify line identity, short tandem repeat (STR) profiling was performed at passage 20 (supplementary data).

Materials and methods

Reprogramming

PBMCs were isolated from donor blood through Percoll density gradient centrifugation (GE Healthcare, 17089109), rinsed three times with PBS (141190144), and subsequently maintained in StemPro-34 SFM medium (10639011) enriched with 100 ng/mL FLT3 (PHC9414), 20 ng/mL IL-6 (PHC0063), 20 ng/mL EPO (PHC9631; all from ThermoFisher), 100 ng/mL SCF (300–07), and 20 ng/mL IL-3 (200–3; both from Peprotech). Reprogramming of PBMCs into iPSCs was performed using the CytoTune™-iPSC 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit (ThermoFisher, A16517) (Sun et al., 2024). The cells were replated onto dishes coated with Matrigel (1:500 dilution; Corning, 356231) and maintained in StemPro-34 SFM medium with daily medium changes. Starting on day 7, cultures were switched to iPS-Brew XF medium (130–104–368; Miltenyi Biotec) supplemented with the appropriate additive (130–107–087), with media refreshed daily. Colonies characteristic of iPSCs typically appeared between days 10 and 15 and were manually selected, expanded, and cryopreserved. Quality control procedures were conducted before using the iPSC lines in downstream experiments.

Cell culture

The iPSCs were maintained in Essential 8 Medium (A1517001) supplemented with a reagent (ThermoFisher, A15171–01) and passaged using Gentle Cell Dissociation Reagent (Stem Cell Technologies, 100–0485) upon reaching 90 % confluency. To enhance cell survival during passaging, 5 μM Y-27632 (S1049; Selleck Chemicals) was added to the medium and removed after 24 h. Cells were incubated at 37 °C in 5 % CO2 with daily media replacement.

Trilineage differentiation

To evaluate their pluripotent potential, iPSCs were directed toward the three germ layers. To induce differentiation into mesoderm, we use RPMI medium supplemented with B27 (Gibco, 11875–085/A18956–01) without insulin to culture the cells, and we treat the cells with 6 μM CHIR-99021 (S2924; Selleck Chemicals) for 48 h. Differentiation into endoderm and ectoderm lineages was achieved using the StemDiff™ Definitive Endoderm Kit (STEMCELL Technologies, 05110) and the Human PSC Functional Identification Kit (R&D Systems, SC027B), respectively.

Immunofluorescence staining

Following fixation in 4 % paraformaldehyde (ThermoFisher, 50980487) for 10 min, iPSCs were permeabilized for 10 min with 0.25 % Triton X-100 in PBS (Sigma-Aldrich) at room temperature. After blocking with 3 % BSA in 0.1 % PBST for 1 h at room temperature, cells were incubated with primary antibodies under appropriate incubation conditions (diluted in 0.1 % PBST) overnight at 4 °C. After washing, secondary antibodies (Table 2) were applied in 0.1 % PBST for 1 h at room temperature. ProLong™ Gold Antifade Mountant with DAPI (ThermoFisher, P36931) was used for mounting, and fluorescence images were acquired using a Keyence BZ-X800 microscope.

Extraction process of RNA and RT-qPCR

At passage 15, human iPSCs were used to evaluate the expression of NANOG and SOX2 markers and to verify the presence of the Sendai virus genome. RNA was isolated using the Direct-zol RNA Microprep Kit (Zymo Research, R2062), and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad, 1708891). Gene expression was then analyzed by RT-qPCR using TaqMan probes (Table 2).

Karyotyping

The KaryoStat™ assay (Thermo Fisher) was performed at passage 15 to evaluate genome-wide chromosomal integrity of the iPSC line.

STR analysis

DNA from PBMCs and passage 20 iPSCs was purified using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, 69504), followed by STR genotyping via the CLA IdentiFiler™ Direct Kit (Thermo Fisher, A44661). Capillary electrophoresis was conducted on an ABI 3130xl instrument at the Protein and Nucleic Acid Facility of Stanford University.

Sequencing

The genomic region of interest amplification was performed using PCR with Quick-Load Taq 2X Master Mix (BioLabs, M0271S) (Table 2), following validation of the genomic DNA via STR analysis. PCR cycling conditions were initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min; followed by 39 cycles at 95 °C for 30 s, 58 °C for 15 s, 68 °C for 30 s; 68 °C for 5 min. PCR amplicons were subjected to Sanger sequencing at Quintara Biosciences.

Mycoplasma detection

Mycoplasma contamination was assessed using the MycoAlert Detection Kit (Lonza, LT07–318) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

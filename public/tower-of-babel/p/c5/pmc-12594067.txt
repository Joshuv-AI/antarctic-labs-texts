Evaluating cognitive impairment among a geriatric population in India using the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)–multilingual dementia research and assessment (MUDRA) toolbox
Authors: U Venkatesh, Varkey Nadakkavukaran Santhosh, Hari Shanker Joshi, Ashoo Grover, Om Prakash Bera, Palani Selvam Mohanraj, Manoj Prithviraj, R Durga
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Cognitive impairment disproportionately affects the geriatric population in India due to a combination of nutritional, socioeconomic, and environmental factors.Aim:This study employed the culturally validated Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)–Multilingual Dementia Research and Assessment (MUDRA) Toolbox to investigate cognitive impairment among geriatric population in Gorakhpur, India and to assess their performance in the various cognitive domains of the MUDRA toolbox.Methods:This cross-sectional study included 1013 participants aged over 60 years selected through multistage random sampling across seven blocks in Gorakhpur district. Participants who exhibited cognitive impairment on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) test underwent further assessments across multiple cognitive domains in the MUDRA Toolbox, including tests of attention and executive functions, episodic memory, language, and visuospatial functions. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Chi-square test, Mann–Whitney U test, and multinomial logistic regression.Results:Among 1013 participants, 847 screened positive on MoCA (70.4% mild, 13.2% moderate) for cognitive impairment and were assessed further for other domains of ICMR-MUDRA toolbox. Males performed significantly better than females across multiple MUDRA toolbox domains, including attention and executive functions, episodic memory, language, and visuospatial skills (P ≤ 0.05). Females showed higher error rates in Trail Making Tests and higher line bisection deviation (P < 0.001).Conclusion:Significant cognitive impairment exists among geriatric population in Gorakhpur. It could arise from the disparities in educational attainment and occupational engagement. These findings emphasize the need for early public health interventions specific for middle-aged population to delay the onset of cognitive impairment.

INTRODUCTION

The global demographic transition toward aging populations has positioned cognitive health as a critical public health priority, particularly in low- and middle-income countries like India.[1] Cognitive impairment is a key determinant of functional independence and quality of life in geriatric populations.[2] It disproportionately affects women in India, with disparities rooted in lifelong educational and sociocultural disparities.[3] Cognitive impairment is more pronounced in regions like Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh, where sociodemographic factors and limited access to formal education contribute to distinct cognitive aging patterns.[4] This study employs the culturally validated Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)–Multilingual Dementia Research and Assessment (MUDRA) Toolbox to investigate these disparities, offering insights into how regional contexts modulate the relationship between cognitive health and sociodemographic factors.[56]

Cognitive aging research in India reveals a paradox: While women generally outlive men, they experience accelerated cognitive decline and higher dementia prevalence.[7] The Longitudinal Ageing Study in India (LASI) reveals significant disparities in late-life cognitive functioning across diverse socioeconomic groups.[8] Lower educational attainment among the geriatric population in India limits cognitive reserve development, which is a key protective factor against neurodegeneration.[9] Research indicates that higher education levels enhance cognitive resilience, potentially delaying cognitive impairment.

The assessment of cognitive function in linguistically and educationally diverse populations presents unique challenges. Conventional neuropsychological tools, designed for western educated cohorts, often misclassify cognitive impairment in low-literacy contexts due to cultural and linguistic biases.[10] The ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox addresses this gap through validation across five Indian languages, incorporating tasks sensitive to regional educational disparities.[11] Its adaptation for Hindi-speaking populations in northern India enables nuanced evaluation of attention, memory, and visuospatial domains while accounting for variable literacy levels.[6]

Regional studies in India have predominantly focused on rural and semiurban populations using only screening instruments, without conducting detailed cognitive assessments among individuals identified with impairment.[121314] This has limited the understanding of the unique cognitive health challenges faced by these communities. Gorakhpur’s agrarian economy, coupled with rigid gender roles and limited healthcare infrastructure, creates a distinctive ecosystem for cognitive aging.[15] These contextual dynamics necessitate localized investigations to inform targeted interventions. This study includes the comprehensive application of the MUDRA Toolbox in assessing the cognitive function within a north Indian population. By employing a multidimensional assessment framework across Gorakhpur, the research bridges critical gaps in cognitive impairment epidemiology. The study aims to assess the cognitive impairment among the geriatric population in Gorakhpur, India and to study their performance in the various domains of cognitive health using the IMCR-MUDRA toolbox. Assessing the existing cognitive function of elderly is crucial for designing early public health interventions aimed at delaying cognitive impairment and dementia.

METHODOLOGY

Study design, duration, and study setting

This study was of cross-sectional design and was conducted among the geriatric population in Gorakhpur, a north eastern district of Uttar Pradesh, from March to September 2023. Gorakhpur district has a population of 4.44 million, distributed across 20 administrative blocks and 3448 revenue villages. This study followed the STrengthening the Reporting of OBservational studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for observational studies.

Ethical consideration and informed consent

The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee at All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Gorakhpur institute (IRB Log No: IHEC/AIIMS-GKP/BMR/107/2022), dated: 23-04-2022. The purpose of the study was explained to all participants, and written informed consent was obtained. Those who were willing to give consent were included in the study.

Selection criteria

The study included individuals aged over 60 years and who were permanent residents of the selected blocks. The nonresidents of these villages within the geriatric age group and those with individuals with a history of head injury, infections, stroke, and other neurological or psychiatric disorders that can cause cognitive impairment and those with significant hearing or visual impairments that could interfere with the testing were excluded.

Sample size and sampling technique

The sample size was calculated using the OpenEpi sample size calculator (Version 3.0), based on a previous study by Pandit et al.,[13] which reported a cognitive impairment prevalence of 24.9% in a rural Indian setting. With a 5% alpha error and 80% power, the minimum required sample size was estimated to be 288. Given the use of a multistage random sampling technique, a design effect of three was applied, resulting in an adjusted sample size of 864. To ensure sufficient representation and account for potential nonresponse, the final sample size was rounded to 1000 participants. The present study involved 1013 participants.

A multistage random sampling technique was employed to select study sites and participants in Gorakhpur district to prevent any selection bias. In the first stage, seven blocks were randomly selected from a total of 20 blocks, serving as the primary sampling units. In the second stage, one Primary Health Center (PHC) was randomly chosen from each selected block. Participants who met the selection criteria were consecutively enrolled from each selected PHC during the final stage. At all stages, the random number table method was used to ensure unbiased selection. Figure 1 presents the geographical representation of the study area.

Instrument design and pilot testing

The present study utilized the ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox, a validated instrument designed for linguistic and cultural appropriateness in five Indian languages.[56] For the current study, the Hindi version of the tool was employed. The instrument assesses the cognitive abilities of the participants in various domains such as the global cognitive functions, attention and executive functions, episodic memory, language, and visuospatial functions. Global cognitive function was assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), which served as a preliminary screening tool. Participants who scored below 26 on the MoCA test were classified as mild (18–25), moderate (10–17), and severe (<10) forms of cognitive impairment based on the preset criteria.[1617] Participants with cognitive impairments (MoCA score <26) were further evaluated across remaining domains using the MUDRA Toolbox. Other cognitive domains included in the toolbox were assessed using neuropsychological tests. Attention and executive functions were evaluated using the Trail Making Test, Category Fluency Test, and Phonemic Fluency Test. Episodic memory was assessed through the Verbal Learning Test and the Test Des Neuf Images (TNI-93). Language abilities were measured using the Picture Naming Test and the Frenchay Aphasia Screening Test. Visuospatial functions were evaluated with the Modified Taylor Complex Figure Test (MTCF) and the Line Bisection Test.[18] The Trail Making Test evaluates visual attention, processing speed, and cognitive flexibility through a task that requires connecting sequences of numbers and letters. Category Fluency and Phonemic Fluency Tests assess executive function and verbal fluency by prompting participants to generate words within a semantic category or beginning with a specific letter, respectively. Episodic memory is measured using the Verbal Learning Test, which involves the immediate and delayed recall of word lists, and the Test Des Neuf Images (TNI-93), which uses a series of images to evaluate visual memory. Language abilities are assessed through the Picture Naming Test, which involves naming common objects, and the Frenchay Aphasia Screening Test, which screens for expressive and receptive language impairments. Visuospatial functions are evaluated using the MTCF, which involves copying a complex figure, and the Line Bisection Test, which detects spatial neglect by asking participants to identify the midpoint of horizontal lines.[5]

There were five field investigators, who were trained and calibrated under subject matter experts for assessing each domain of the MUDRA tool box. A pilot study was conducted in 50 participants in one of the blocks in Gorakhpur that was not part of the study. It was conducted to assess the adequacy of responses of the participants in various MUDRA toolbox tests, and the feasibility of conducting the investigation. The results of the pilot study were not included for analysis.

Data collection

Participants underwent a 90-minute face-to-face cognitive assessment covering various domains of the MUDRA Toolbox at each selected PHC. Standardized procedures were implemented across all study sites, including regular calibration of the examiners to ensure consistency and reliability. Scoring for the cognitive assessment test was based on the predefined criteria for each domain of the MUDRA Toolbox. Figure 2 illustrates the methodology of the present study.

Data processing and analysis

Data were entered into Microsoft Excel 2019 and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, 2012). The Shapiro–Wilk test was conducted to assess the normality of data distribution, which indicated that the data were not normally distributed. Descriptive statistics were reported as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, while continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation. The Chi-square test was used to assess differences in categorical variables, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was applied to compare differences in various domains of the MUDRA Toolbox among participants. Multinomial logistic regression analysis was performed to obtain adjusted odds ratios, accounting for all independent variables. Statistical significance was set at P ≤ 0.05.

RESULTS

Demographic profile

A total of 1013 participants (58.7% males and 41.3% females) were included in this study. The majority of study participants were in the 60–64 years age group (males: 35.2%, females: 23.4%). Most participants had primary school education (males: 45.8%, females: 38.3%). Significant sex differences were observed in education levels (χ² = 55.63, P < 0.001), occupation (χ² = 630.48, P < 0.001), marital status (χ² = 54.75, P < 0.001), and socioeconomic status (χ² = 49.63, P < 0.001). Male participants were predominantly engaged in clerical/shop/farming occupations (26.8%), while females were primarily homemakers (29.9%). The majority of participants were married and cohabiting (males: 51.9%, females: 30.5%), and most belonged to socioeconomic Class II (males: 39.3%, females: 22.8%) according to the B.G. Cognitive assessment

MoCA test revealed that 70.4% of participants had mild cognitive impairment, 13.2% had moderate cognitive impairment, and 16.4% had normal cognition. Sex differences were statistically significant difference in cognitive impairment (χ² = 15.19, P < 0.001), with a higher proportion of males showing mild cognitive impairment (42.3%) compared to females (28.1%), while females demonstrated a higher prevalence of moderate cognitive impairment (7.4%) than males (5.8%).

Age also showed statistically significant difference in cognitive function (χ² = 13.41, P = 0.037), with increasing age showcasing higher rates of moderate cognitive impairment. Education level (χ² = 123.25, P < 0.001) showed that individuals with lower educational attainment, particularly those who were illiterate or had only primary schooling, exhibited higher levels of cognitive impairment. Occupation (χ² = 41.24, P < 0.001) was also associated, with daily wagers, homemakers, and the unemployed showing higher impairment compared to those in clerical or skilled roles. Marital status (χ² = 15.38, P = 0.017) revealed that widowed or divorced individuals had higher cognitive impairment than those who were married and cohabiting. Socioeconomic status and geographical location did not show any statistically significant association with cognitive function [Table 2].

Cognitive domain performance

Among the 1013 participants, 847 scored below 26 on the MoCA test and subsequently underwent further assessment across various cognitive domains using the MUDRA Toolbox. Analysis of their performance in the remaining cognitive domains revealed significant sex differences.

Attention and executive function

Males performed better than females in several measures of attention and executive function. Although no significant differences were observed in completion times for the Trail Making Test (Parts A and B), females made significantly more errors in both Part A (P < 0.001) and Part B (P = 0.007). Males demonstrated significantly superior performance in category fluency (P = 0.002) and phonemic fluency (P < 0.001).

Episodic memory

Males outperformed females across all episodic memory measures. In the Verbal Learning Test, males achieved higher scores in total learning (P < 0.001), delayed recall (P = 0.005), and delayed recognition (P = 0.011). Males also performed significantly better on the TNI-93 for both total recall (P < 0.001) and spatial recall (P < 0.001).

Language

No significant sex differences were found in the Picture Naming Test (P = 0.282). However, males performed significantly better on the Frenchay Aphasia Screening Test (P < 0.001).

Visuospatial functions

Males demonstrated superior performance in all visuospatial function tests, including the MTCF test (P = 0.003) and the line bisection test (P < 0.001). Females showed significantly greater line bisection deviation (P < 0.001) [Table 3].

Risk of cognitive impairment

Multinomial logistic regression analysis was carried out to assess the risk of mild and moderate cognitive impairment based on sex and age of the individual, with normal cognition as the reference category. After adjusting for confounding variables, no statistically significant association was found between sex of the individual and the risk of mild cognitive impairment (females: aOR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.40-1.45, P = 0.407) or moderate cognitive impairment (females: aOR = 1.28, 95% CI: 0.57-2.88, P = 0.550). However, it was observed that females had higher risk of moderate cognitive impairment compared to males. Similarly, no significant association was observed between age groups and the risk of mild or moderate cognitive impairment, although adults aged >75 years showed a trend toward increased risk of moderate cognitive impairment (aOR = 2.76, 95% CI: 0.82-9.24, P = 0.100) compared to those aged 60–64 years.

DISCUSSION

Cognitive impairment represents a significant neurological challenge affecting memory, reasoning, and daily functioning among older adults, progressively diminishing their independence and quality of life.[19] Like many countries worldwide, India is experiencing a demographic shift with its older population rapidly increasing, which inherently amplifies the potential risk and prevalence of cognitive impairment.[20] In India, with its rapidly growing elderly population and complex socioeconomic landscape, understanding cognitive impairment becomes critically important due to limited geriatric healthcare infrastructure and traditional family care models undergoing transformation.[21] The intersecting factors of increasing life expectancy, genetic predispositions, socioeconomic disparities, and limited awareness make cognitive impairment research in India not just a medical imperative but also a profound social necessity that demands comprehensive, culturally sensitive investigation and intervention strategies. The current study utilizing the ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox offers an unprecedented exploration of cognitive function among the geriatric population in Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh, revealing intricate patterns of cognitive health that intersect with dynamics of the sex of the individual.

The present study identified a high prevalence of cognitive impairment, as assessed by the MoCA test, with 70.4% of participants exhibiting mild impairment. The prevalence observed in the current study was higher compared to a previous study by Pandit et al.,[13] which reported cognitive impairment rates around 24.9% in rural population; however, the assessment was carried out using Hindi mental state examination scale. Similar studies carried out by Gambhir et al.,[14] Menon et al.,[22] Philip et al.,[23] Bhatia et al.,[24] Panghal et al.,[2] Kaur et al.,[25] and Konda et al.[26] in India also revealed lower prevalence of cognitive impairment; however, the scales used in each study varied. The markedly higher prevalence in the present study could be attributed to a combination of factors, including the use of a more sensitive screening tool such as the MoCA, the inclusion of a predominantly older and underprivileged population, and potential differences in educational levels or comorbidities.[27] Methodological differences such as choice of cognitive assessment tool and threshold criteria may significantly influence prevalence estimates. This is supported by Pinto et al., whose systematic review showed that screening with MoCA detects substantially more cases of mild cognitive impairment than Mini-Mental State Examination, driven by its higher sensitivity in picking up subtle deficits.[28]

The present study revealed a higher prevalence of mild cognitive impairment among males, whereas females had a significantly higher prevalence of moderate cognitive impairment. These findings are consistent with a study by Petersen et al.,[29] which reported a higher prevalence of mild cognitive impairment among men. The findings also align with a longitudinal study by Lin et al.,[30] which found that women experience a faster rate of cognitive decline compared to men. The regressing models in the current study also revealed that females and individuals aged above 75 years had higher risk of moderate cognitive impairment. These findings were consistent with the study by Wang et al.,[31] which reported that females who are older had a higher odds of cognitive impairment.

The current study reveals notable sex-based variations in cognitive performance across multiple neuropsychological domains of the MUDRA toolbox. The findings suggest complex and multifaceted differences in cognitive functioning between males and females that extend beyond simplistic binary interpretations. In the domain of attention and executive function, males demonstrated more consistent performance, particularly in fluency tasks. The significant differences in category and phonemic fluency suggest potential underlying neurological variations in cognitive processing and linguistic retrieval. While completion times remained comparable, the higher error rates among females in the Trail Making Test indicate potential differences in task management and cognitive control strategies. However, a recent meta-analysis by Hirnstein et al.[32] revealed that females had better phonemic fluency compared to males, in contrast to the current results.

Episodic memory performance showed pronounced sex-based disparities, with males consistently outperforming females across various memory measures. These differences were evident in verbal learning, recall, and recognition tasks, highlighting potentially distinct neural mechanisms underlying memory encoding and retrieval. Such variations might reflect complex interactions between biological factors, and neurological development. Language capabilities presented a nuanced picture. While the Picture Naming Test showed no significant sex differences, males performed substantially better on the Frenchay Aphasia Screening Test. This suggests that sex-related variations in language processing might be more apparent in complex linguistic tasks that require broader language comprehension and integration.

Visuospatial functions revealed the most pronounced sex differences, with males demonstrating superior performance across multiple assessments. The significant variations in the MTCF test and line bisection test underscore potential neurological differences in spatial perception and cognitive mapping. The greater line bisection deviation among females might indicate distinct perceptual processing strategies. The findings of this study were in contrast with that of Herlitz et al.,[33] which reported that females performed better than males in verbal episodic memory tasks and certain episodic memory tasks involving a visuospatial component.

The observed cognitive disparities between males and females are deeply rooted in the complex sociocultural landscape of the region. The demographic analysis of the study exposed stark differences in both the sexes in education, occupation, and socioeconomic status, providing a nuanced lens through which to understand cognitive variations. Males demonstrated significantly superior performance across multiple cognitive domains, including attention and executive function, episodic memory, and visuospatial capabilities. These findings resonate with and expand upon previous research by Angrisani et al.,[3] which highlighted the unique cognitive challenges faced by older women in India.

The lower educational attainment among females emerges as a critical factor in cognitive reserve capacity. This observation aligns with research by Jansen et al.,[34] emphasizing the protective role of education against cognitive impairment. The occupational distribution revealed a pronounced divide in the sexes, with males predominantly engaged in clerical, shop, and farming roles, while females were primarily homemakers. This differential social and occupational engagement likely creates significant disparities in cognitive stimulation, potentially explaining the observed performance variations.[35] While univariate analyses in the present study revealed apparent sex differences in cognitive performance, the absence of significant sex and age associations in the multinomial logistic regression models after adjusting for education, age, and occupation suggests that these observed differences may be largely attributable to confounding sociodemographic variables rather than inherent biological sex differences. It was consistent with findings from a previous study by Langa et al.,[36] that educational attainment and occupational complexity are primary mediators of apparent sex disparities in cognitive aging. However, a study conducted in China by Zhong et al.,[37] identified education as a more influential factor in predicting cognitive impairment than occupation.

Limitations and future directions

The current study has certain methodological limitations. The use of the MoCA test for classifying participants based on cognitive impairment may be influenced by the educational status of the population, potentially leading to an overestimation of the prevalence of cognitive impairment in this group.[38] The applicability of ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox and MoCA may be limited in participants with very low literacy levels, and future studies should consider incorporating literacy-independent cognitive assessments such as the Literacy Independent Cognitive Assessment (LICA) or the recently developed Simplified Cognitive Assessment (SPCog) that are specifically validated for rural populations with minimal educational backgrounds.[3940] The study was further limited by the absence of functional impairment assessments and informant-based reports on cognitive decline, which are crucial for distinguishing between normal aging and pathological cognitive changes, particularly in differentiating mild cognitive impairment from early dementia.[41] Another limitation could be the lack of age-stratified random sampling and recruitment from PHCs rather than the community, which may limit the generalizability and introduce selection bias. It has a cross-sectional design that precludes temporal analysis of cognitive function trajectories, limiting causal inferences regarding progression patterns and risk factor interactions over time. Another limitation is the lack of longitudinal follow-ups, which makes it difficult to determine whether the observed cognitive differences are stable characteristics or part of a changing decline trajectory. It also cannot elucidate the specific neurobiological mechanisms underlying these relationships. These limitations highlight the need for prospective longitudinal investigations with biomarker analyses to better characterize cognitive impairment trajectories and their neurobiological underpinnings.

Based on the findings, future research should prioritize the development and evaluation of gender-tailored cognitive intervention programs that address the specific vulnerabilities identified in this study. These interventions should incorporate evidence-based cognitive training protocols adapted to educational backgrounds and cultural contexts prevalent in rural Indian settings. Longitudinal clinical trials are needed to assess the effectiveness of multidomain approaches that combine cognitive stimulation with physical activity and nutritional support, with stratification by sex to determine differential response patterns. Integration of these interventions within primary healthcare frameworks could enhance accessibility, while community-based implementation may improve adherence by addressing sociocultural barriers. Additionally, research should incorporate neuroimaging and biomarker analyses to elucidate the neurobiological mechanisms underlying cognitive responses to these interventions. Such comprehensive approaches would contribute to developing targeted prevention and treatment strategies that address the unique cognitive health challenges faced by older adults in Indian populations.

The current study employed the culturally adapted ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox and provides comprehensive epidemiological information on cognitive function patterns among geriatric residents of Gorakhpur district. These results contribute to the growing body of evidence suggesting that cognitive aging follows heterogeneous trajectories influenced by modifiable social determinants of health. Furthermore, the study offers a methodological framework for investigating cognitive health in Indian settings, where assessment has been historically challenging due to linguistic and educational diversity.

CONCLUSION

This study provides critical insights into the cognitive function among the geriatric population of Gorakhpur, India, utilizing the ICMR-MUDRA Toolbox. The findings reveal a high prevalence of cognitive impairment among the participants, with notable differences across cognitive domains. Males demonstrated superior performance in attention, executive function, episodic memory, and visuospatial tasks, whereas females exhibited a higher prevalence of moderate cognitive impairment. These disparities likely stem from differences in educational attainment and occupational engagements among the participants. The study emphasizes the need for early public health interventions that target modifiable dementia risk factors. According to the Lancet Commission, these key risk factors include low education, social isolation, and limited cognitive stimulation. Such interventions are essential for building cognitive resilience in older adults. This is particularly important for women who have lower educational backgrounds as they may be at higher risk. To address these challenges effectively, targeted cognitive training programs should be implemented alongside broader community-based initiatives. Such comprehensive approaches are especially crucial in resource-limited settings where cognitive impairment prevention requires cost-effective and scalable solutions.

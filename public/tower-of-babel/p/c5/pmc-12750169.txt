Preclinical characterization and anti-RSV efficacy of an oral C-nucleoside derivative
Authors: Yuanguang Chen, Yongqing Liu, Guanguan Li, Chao Peng, Dizhen Liang, Sidi Yang, Peisen Zheng, Shuo Li, Deyin Guo, Qifan Zhou, Xumu Zhang
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a major cause of acute lower respiratory tract infections worldwide, posing a significant threat to infants, young children, immunocompromised individuals, and the elderly1. Nearly all children are infected by the age of two, with about half experiencing multiple infections. In 2019, RSV was responsible for approximately 33 million lower respiratory tract infections in children under five, resulting in 3.6 million hospitalizations and over 100,000 deaths2. In older adults, the incidence of RSV reaches 600.7 cases per 100,000 individuals in developed countries, with mortality rates as high as 9.1% in developing regions. Collectively, the global disease burden of RSV has become comparable to that of seasonal influenza.

Currently, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has only approved Ribavirin for severe RSV infection; however, due to its requirement for nebulized administration and significant toxicity, it is used only as a last resort3. Candidate drugs for RSV currently undergoing clinical trials can be primarily categorized into three groups: F protein inhibitors, RdRp inhibitors, and N protein inhibitors (Supporting Information Fig. S1). Neutralizing antibodies such as Palivizumab and Nirsevimab are available for prophylaxis in high-risk infants but require injection and cannot treat active infection4. Given RSV's nature as a single-stranded RNA virus5 (Supporting Information Fig. S2), various RNA polymerase inhibitors have been explored through drug repurposing. The β-d-N4-hydroxycytidine derivative EIDD-2801 exhibits strong preclinical activity against RSV, but its clinical application remains focused on COVID-19, with limited data supporting efficacy in RSV infection6. Another nucleoside analog, lumicitabine (ALS-8176), demonstrated promising antiviral activity and accelerated viral clearance in early trials as an RSV polymerase inhibitor, but ultimately failed to show efficacy in a Phase IIb clinical study and was discontinued7. Remdesivir (RDV), an aminophosphate prodrug of GS-441524, has been widely used for COVID-19 treatment and is currently being evaluated for its potential against RSV8. However, due to extensive hepatic first-pass metabolism, RDV must be administered intravenously, limiting its use to hospitalized patients and severe RSV cases. The deuterated triester derivative of Remdesivir, VV116, is an RdRp inhibitor that overcomes the limitations of oral administration and is currently under clinical evaluation for the treatment of RSV infection. Non-nucleoside RdRp inhibitors target allosteric (non-catalytic) sites on the polymerase, offering a distinct antiviral mechanism along with favorable resistance characteristics9,10. Although they have attracted considerable attention as promising candidates in RSV drug discovery, these compounds are still limited to preclinical investigation. The absence of oral therapies for RSV leaves high-risk patients without effective intervention, highlighting the urgent need for safe and orally available antivirals.

Our research group has been the first to report the potential of GS-441524 in combatting SARS-CoV-2 both in vitro and in vivo11. By optimizing GS-441524 to enhance pharmacokinetic properties, Shen and our team independently developed two highly potent oral antivirals, VV116 and ATV014, both of which have successfully completed Phase III clinical trials for COVID-1912,13. Notably, VV116 (Trisesterified deuterated derivative of GS-441524) exhibited significant antiviral efficacy against RSV in a mouse model14. During the development of oral broad-spectrum antivirals, we observed that different prodrug modifications of GS-441524 markedly influenced in vitro and in vivo antiviral activity. Thus, we screened a nucleoside analog library against RSV to identify candidates with superior efficacy, reduced toxicity, and improved oral bioavailability, aiming to develop a safe and effective oral therapy for RSV infection.

A series of 5′-OH ester prodrugs of GS-441524 (4a–j) were synthesized (Supporting Information Scheme S1) and evaluated for their anti-RSV activity in Hep-2 cells, alongside GS-441524, RDV, VV116, Molnupiravir and Ribavirin (Supporting Information Table S1). Most of the synthesized compounds demonstrate greater efficacy against respiratory syncytial virus and lower cytotoxicity compared to Ribavirin (EC50 = 26.62 ± 12.815 μmol/L) and Molnupiravir (EC50 > 10 μmol/L). While RDV (EC50 = 0.04 ± 0.003 μmol/L, CC50 = 2.95 μmol/L, SI = 73.75) exhibited the highest antiviral activity, it is also highly toxic (Fig. 1A). Among these, compounds 4d (ATV014; EC50 = 0.52 ± 0.088 μmol/L, CC50 = 44.72 μmol/L, SI = 86) and 4e (ATV017; EC50 = 0.45 ± 0.048 μmol/L, CC50 > 100 μmol/L, SI > 222) exhibited superior anti-RSV potency and lower cytotoxicity compared to VV116 and RDV. The bioavailability of ATV017 in SD rats following a single oral administration of 20, 40, and 80 mg/kg was observed to be 32.5%, 48.9%, and 27.9%, respectively (Fig. 1B; Supporting Information Table S2). Given the strong in vitro inhibitory activities of ATV014 and ATV017 against RSV, we further evaluated their efficacy in a mouse model, with Ribavirin used as a control. BALB/c mice (6–8 weeks old) were intranasally infected with 1.65 × 105 FFU of RSV on Day 0 and subsequently treated with ATV014 (50, 100, or 200 mg/kg, orally, BID), ATV017 (50, 100, or 200 mg/kg, orally, BID), or Ribavirin (50 or 100 mg/kg, intraperitoneally, BID) (Fig. 1D). All mice survived with a 100% survival rate. Notably, Ribavirin-treated mice exhibited significant weight loss from Day 3, whereas those receiving ATV014 or ATV017 maintained stable body weights, indicating good tolerability (Fig. 1E). On Day 4 post-infection, lung tissues were collected for viral RNA quantification (RT-PCR) and viral titration (Immunophagocytosis assay). Both ATV014 and ATV017 at 50 mg/kg showed superior antiviral efficacy compared to Ribavirin at 100 mg/kg, significantly reducing viral RNA copies and viral titers (Fig. 1F). Specifically, ATV014 and ATV017 decreased viral RNA levels by 1.18log10 and 1.47log10, respectively, with viral titers falling below detectable limits. Histopathological analysis revealed only mild lung infiltration in ATV017 treated groups, in contrast to the limited protective effect observed with Ribavirin (Fig. 1G).

We subsequently conducted preclinical studies on ATV017 to evaluate its druggability. ATV017 showed excellent target selectivity in a screening of 87 targets, with no target inhibition exceeding 50% at 20 μmol/L (Supporting Information Table S3). It also exhibited no significant activity against the human POLRMT enzyme within the concentration range of 0.137 to 30 μmol/L. In SD rats, doses of 100 and 400 mg/kg did not affect respiratory parameters or the central nervous system, establishing a NOAEL of 400 mg/kg/day. Assessment of hERG potassium channel current revealed less than 50% inhibition at 30 μmol/L. In beagle dogs, doses of 50 and 100 mg/kg did not significantly impact cardiovascular parameters, with a NOAEL of 100 mg/kg/day. Overall, the safety evaluation indicated no concerns, confirming ATV017's favorable safety profile. ATV017 exhibited significantly greater permeability than GS-441524 in Caco-2 cell studies and appeared to be a weak substrate for efflux transporters, indicating that its prodrug formulation may effectively address the efflux challenges faced by GS-441524 (Supporting Information Table S4).

ATV017 demonstrated moderate plasma protein binding across various species, with free fractions ranging from 0.0894 in rats to 0.3 in dogs, while GS-441524 showed low protein binding (Supporting Information Table S5). Incubation of ATV017 in Caco-2 cells produced GS-441524, and efflux rates remained below 10%, but exceeded 50% with an inhibitor, suggesting ATV017 may act as a P-glycoprotein substrate (Supporting Information Tables S6 and S7). We also explored the co-administration of a P-gp inhibitor with ATV017, but this combination did not significantly enhance anti-RSV activity in vitro (Supporting Information Fig. S3). Further in vitro studies in liver cells, plasma, and whole blood from mice, rats, dogs, monkeys, and humans showed rapid metabolism of ATV017 to GS-441524 (Supporting Information Tables S8–S13). In vitro studies indicated rapid metabolism of ATV017 to GS-441524, primarily via CYP2C8, CYP3A4, and CYP3A5 enzymes, with minimal inhibition from specific CYP inhibitors (Supporting Information Table S14). Following administration in SD rats, ATV017 was poorly recovered in urine and feces, with most excreted as GS-441524, totaling 45.2% recovery (Supporting Information Table S15). Bile excretion of ATV017 was negligible, whereas GS-441524 accounted for 0.639% of biliary recovery, closely mirroring the recovery of GS-441524 alone (Supporting Information Table S16).

In a single-dose study, SD rats tolerated ATV017 at doses of 2000 and 3000 mg/kg, establishing a maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of 3000 mg/kg. Beagle dogs tolerated doses of 500, 1000, and 2000 mg/kg, with an MTD of 2000 mg/kg. In a separate 14-day regimen, both SD rats (100 and 400 mg/kg) (Supporting Information Table S17) and Beagle dogs (50 and 100 mg/kg) (Supporting Information Table S18) showed good tolerance and full survival, yielding a no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of 400 mg/kg/day for rats and 100 mg/kg/day for Beagle dogs. In genotoxicity testing, ATV017 demonstrated no mutagenic potential, as indicated by a positive control group showing significantly more reverted mutant colonies than the solvent control, consistent with historical data. ATV017 did not produce aberrant chromosome structures in CHO-WBL cells with S9 activation, although it tested positive without it. Furthermore, it was negative in both the rat bone marrow micronucleus test and the comet assay in stomach and liver cells. All animal study protocols were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee and all procedures used in animal studies complied with the guidelines and policies of the Animal Care and Use Committee of the respective research units.

While several GS-441524-based prodrugs have been explored for oral RSV therapy, the potent antiviral activity, low toxicity, and synthetic simplicity of ATV017 relative to RDV and VV116 position it as a promising next-generation therapeutic candidate (more details are shown in Supporting Information Figs. S4–S43).

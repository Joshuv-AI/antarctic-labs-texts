AIIT-03 3D PRINTED IN RESIDENCY TRAINING AND PRACTICE OF NEUROSURGERY GHANA EXPERIENCE
Authors: Al-hassan Dasana Andani, Samuel Kaba Akoriyea, Mariela Infante Paez, Jennifer Yenuman Berrick, Rosemond Aboagye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis is a single center report highlighting the use of 3D (three dimensional) printed anatomic models for surgical planning and postoperative reviews in variety of complex neurosurgical procedures including separating craniopagus twins, depress skull fractures, brain and spine tumors and gunshot wounds in Ghana. The Objective is to showcase the use of AI in neurosurgery and specifically 3D stereo lithic skull models.MethodologyAdvanced 3D stereolithic skull models, incorporating venous and arterial reconstruction and brain interfacing, were developed from CT, MRI, and DSA imaging studies. These models were crucial for planning the surgical approaches, particularly in marking the precise route of separation. A staged surgical separation was executed over five stages in the case of the craniopagus separation while a one stage was achieved in rest of surgeries. Residents and specialists had the unique opportunity to appreciate the nuances of the anatomy pre and postoperative improving the learning curve of trainees. This technology provided a tactile, real-life visualization of complex anatomy, important for minimizing surgical errors and optimizing the surgical plan. CONCLUSION:3D printed anatomic models impact spans creating patient-specific models for precise planning and training, designing innovative neurosurgical devices, and developing tissue-engineered implants. The integration of 3D modeling and advanced imaging in pre-operative planning and post operative evaluation was instrumental in the successful separation of twins, brain tumor removal, gunshots highlighting a significant advancement in neurosurgery in Ghana.

A Methodological Approach to Alleviate a Cryptic Occupational Hazard Concerning the Impact of Standing Activities for Extended Duration
Authors: Koumi Dutta, Archak Roy, Abhirup Chatterjee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Extensive standing activities among various professions like nurses, teachers, security guards, and so on, can increase predisposition to corns, callus, heel spur, plantar fasciitis, metatarsalgia, and even varicose veins. This study aims to explore the extent and onset of discomfort experienced by individuals exposed to extensive standing; identifying some major contributors to their discomfort and commonly adopted management for this discomfort.Methodology:Healthy women (n = 162) of age group 30–45 years, occupationally exposed to extensive standing for nearly 40% of the shift duration (8 hours), responded to structured questionnaire with the foot map and Likert scale reporting the region and intensity of discomfort experienced during complete work shift at regular time intervals. Pedobarographical investigations were conducted to identify the plantar pressure distribution and magnitude.Results:Almost 60% of respondents started feeling pain after 3 hours of continuous standing. Discomfort in area of hind foot was most prevalent and overlapping areas of discomfort were unveiled. Significant association (r = +0.42; P < 0.05) between generated intensity of discomfort and duration of standing was identified. Pedobarographical investigations revealed significant reduction in pressure in shod condition than in bare foot situation (P = 0.03). Redistribution of maximum pressure-generating zones of plantar pressure was also observed in shod condition.Conclusion:Extensive standing may affect productivity at workplace considerably, necessitating the relevant effective management. Thus, pain management with respect to time followed by identification of plantar pressure evaluations was important to intervene precisely with time management and pressure redistribution strategies to mitigate this encrypted occupational discomfort.

INTRODUCTION

Multiple profession require work to be performed in standing posture and prolonged standing was identified to cause discomfort and fatigue particularly in legs and back. Attempts to quantify the strain by developing an index have been made to quantify the discomfort experienced however validation of the protocol is required before implementation.[1] Prolonged standing has been identified as an invisible occupational epidemic because of paucity of evidence and exploration, while its impact is robust.[2] In a stimulated occupational environment, prolonged duration of standing can produce acute biomechanical responses that includes 40% of the participants to suffer Low back pain after 2 hours of standing.[3] Occupational groups compelled to perform standing activity wide, including nurses, security guards, chefs, teachers, assembly line workers, and many more. Thus, to comprehend the range of discomforts, it is important to glance through the common reports. Among nurses’ discomfort like burning feet, numbness, bunions, callus have been observed.[4] Standing is often being advised to replace sitting, but after experiencing 2 hours of standing reaction time has been observed to increase and affected mental state[5] both these attributes will definitely impact performance, affect productivity and may increases accident proneness. In an exploration on examination performance, while maintaining the standing posture, it was identified the standing posture significantly affected performance in mathematics examination.[6] It can be inferred that extensive duration of standing can deter performance.

It has been observed that job rotation is a commonly adapted method to manage stress resulting from awkward static posture, thorough review identifies a need for optimization of job rotation methods by reduction of ergonomic risks and job rotation scheduling.[7]

Endurance data has been used to make work rest model for static postures. Since remaining endurance capacity (REC) can predict discomfort, and the relationship between REC and discomfort estimated in Borg Scale is established, Work rest scheduling is a popularly opted method to assist in developing approaches to avoid high levels of workplace discomfort.[8]

Symptomatic management of foot discomfort has been generally addressed by orthotic intervention. Metatarsal dome is the most effective among three different foot orthoses in reducing both peak and mean plantar pressure during standing and slow running, under the first metatarsophalangeal joint during standing and slow running.[9]

In the management of calcaneal spur, flexible footwear, and customized insoles were found reduce pain, plantar load and increase function, however, footwear was more effective than footwear in combination with insole.[10]

Occupation specific modification of pertinent footwear components can prevent the development of foot discomfort. In the journey towards developing an occupational aid related to footwear region of major discomfort experienced by people, performing similar physical activity needs to be assessed thoroughly. Precise discomfort management can be performed with work rest scheduling and job rotation. Before that, it is important to explore the relationship between time and intensity of the discomfort, to device an interventional approach for discomfort management. It is important to conduct related pedobarographical investigations for standing posture to identify the location and magnitude of plantar pressure to device an aid to redistribute the plantar pressure and alleviate related discomfort.

METHODOLOGY

A survey was conducted among 162 women without any reported health issue, belonging to age group 30–45 years with average education level of VIII-X standard of the Indian education system (10 years of total education) drawn randomly. The respondents belonged to different occupations like teaching (n = 74), caregiving (n = 12), hospitality services (n = 61), and housekeeping (n = 15). The purpose of the study was explained to them and written informed consent was obtained. The local ethics committee approved the study protocol. Inclusion criteria for all the respondents where they had a shift duration of 8 hours and spend at least or more than 3-hour 12 min standing at a stretch, per shift. It was indicated that 1 hour of continuous standing and standing over 4 hours in an entire shift was associated with reports of musculoskeletal disorder. A combination of phases of continuous and intermittent standing is observed among the participants.[11] Further, for more suitable inclusion, a prior survey was referred. It was revealed that 95th percentile of the women participants (n = 582) reported onset of foot discomfort after 40% of the 8-hour standard work shift was completed. Therefore, respondents experiencing standing activity extending over 3 hours 12 min were considered for this exploration.

The symptoms most noted and their prevalence were documented among the respondents. Hourly reporting of discomfort in a 10 points Likert scale[12] during the entire shift of work that is eight hours was documented. The discomfort level at the beginning of work was also noted. Severity matrix for pain intensity above five in the Likert scale was plotted in the identified time interval revealing intense pain escalation [Supplementary Figure 1].

Responses to Foot health questionnaire was also collected from the participants.[13] Area of feet that is forefeet, mid feet, and hind feet; the intensity of discomfort for each area was also noted down. Further methods adopted by the participants to relief their pain was also documented.

Delving into the present scenario, 15 observations of pedobarographic was performed on participants performing extensive standing for over 4 hours at a stretch at least at the second hour of the work shift. Maximum plantar pressure in the left and right leg in barefoot and shod conditions was located and their magnitude was reported.

Statistical analyses were done using SPSS 22.0 (IBM, USA). Mann Whitney U test was performed to analyze whether any significant differences were obtained between different condition (viz. barefoot/BF vs shod/S) followed by z-scores for the distributions. Following this, Spearman’s Rank Correlation were performed to analyze association of magnitude of pain with ascend of time. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

Primarily, the common discomfort and symptoms among the participants were mapped. Callus, corn, heel spur, and plantar fasciitis were the pathologies observed among the respondents, as presented the order of prevalence [Figure 1].

Report of foot discomfort among participants

These pathologies are mostly associated with persistent foot discomfort as experienced by the exposed group. The discomfort mapping was conducted to comprehend the situation [Figure 2].

Participants reporting foot discomfort after each hour of their work shift

Steep rise in pain reporting began to increase after 2 hours of work and the increasing trend of pain reporting continued until 6th hour of work and from 6th to 8th or final hour of work no decrease in discomfort reporting pattern was observed.

To further, analyze the severity of the reported discomfort hour wise intensity of discomfort was placed in a severity matrix. The purpose of matrix was to identify the severity of incidences of discomfort from scale 5 to 10 and from 2nd to 7th hour. This time of reporting was identified since, rate of change in intensity of discomfort is more, and hence, the time point of intervention should be mapped within this time range [Figure 3].

Severity matrix for intensity of discomfort at respective hour of work among participants

Significant association (r = +0.42; P < 0.05) between generated intensity of discomfort with participants.

The areas of the feet, which is forefoot, mid foot, hind foot, undergoing most discomfort was checked. Seventy-two participants reported pain at more than one region during the duration of standing [Figure 4].

Area of foot discomfort and prevalence among participants

Fifteen explorations to identify pedobarographs of individuals performing extensive standing revealed that average pressure in barefoot and shod condition in both the left and right feet was significantly decreased (P = 0.04). Moreover, maximum pressure in the left foot underwent a significant decrease in shod than in barefoot condition, but maximum pressure in right foot decreased, but not significantly (P = 0.07) [Figure 5].

Magnitude of maximum plantar pressure in both the left and right feet in barefoot and shod conditions

To add to the quantitative comparison where 26.97–25.9% reduction in magnitude of maximum pressure was observed in shod condition than in barefoot standing, it was observed that the maximum pressure points often were redistributed in a different location in shod condition than the barefoot condition. In 81.2%, the side changed that from left maximum pressure point shifted to right, in 42.1% the position changes from heel to toe. This suggests the importance of footwear in redistribution of the plantar pressure.

While, the general practice among the participants were to opt for footbath, ointment, and medicine in order of preference to relief their discomfort [Supplementary Figure 2].

DISCUSSION

It is important to combine subjective responses from the participants belonging to occupational groups along with objective evaluations to explore the scopes of intervention. Postural stress related to standing activity was evaluated in the considered occupational groups. No significant difference (P > 0.05, NS) was observed among the respondents representing different occupations. The time and job study will help us identify the time point for intervention. The foot area with major prevalence of discomfort will help us delineate the aid/component to proceed with modification. The comprehensive evaluation of pedobarography will be suggestive of the nature of intervention and the expected changes accordingly.

Spacio-temporal analysis of incidences works related Musculo-skeletal disorders is an effective approach, which enables in devising appropriate intervention.[14] The relationship between pain prevalence and hours of work has also been explored as a baseline to develop a suitable work-rest schedule. It has been observed that number of work hours exposure to repetitive strenuous physical activity has a positive relationship with low back pain prevalence.[15] Longitudinal studies on different occupational group has been carried out, like in nurses changes were observed in low back pain intensity before joining work and after continuing work for an year.[16] However, there is scarcity of evidences where pain or discomfort reporting of the participants hourly throughout a work shift. Almost 60% of respondents started feeling pain after 3 hours of continuous standing, and the time interval for intervention was delineated. This is a novel approach to identify the steep increasing trend in discomfort reporting among the participants from 2nd to 6th hour of their work shift making this time interval pivotal for work rest scheduling or related intervention.

This exploration documented the common complaints and areas and the prevalence of foot discomfort experienced among participants performing jobs that involve extended hours of standing. While, it has been revealed that prolonged standing activities enhances the risks of chronic venous disorders[17] incidences of numbness, bunions, callus, burning feet, and other structural deformities has been observed in nurses.[4] Plantar fasciitis was reported among sales personals[18] and teachers[19] performing extended hours of standing. In this study it has been identified that callus followed by heel spur, corns, and plantar fasciitis affect occupational groups performing extended standing disorder. Amalgamating the findings, the area of the foot experiencing most discomfort was unveiled to be hind foot. Discomfort in area of hind foot, mid foot, fore foot was experienced among 67.2%, 32.7%, 24% participants, respectively. Interestingly, about 44% participant had overlapping discomfort prevalence symptoms and none of them used any occupational or orthotic relieving aid. This finding suggests the foot areas where interventions require to be administered for mitigation.

Generally adopted methods to reduce and manage discomforts among the groups occupationally exposed to extensive standing were opting for footbath in hot and cold water, ointments, and pain medication in order of preference. However, none of them opted for footwear or related component modifications. This identifies the necessity for development of effective footwear or related component or occupational aid to prevent the exacerbating impact of extended hours of standing activity.

The pedobarographical investigations in static, postural, and dynamic are generally conducted to provide management approaches for different foot discomforts.[20] This evaluation has been used to evaluate several ensuing pathological condition and foot discomfort related and initiating due to improper pressure distribution of the feet, management recommendations and accurate diagnosis or prognosis can be accessed.[21] In this study, pedobarographical studies revealed significant reduction in plantar pressure about 25%–26% with the use of shoes than in bare feet conditions. However, in unveiled a concern about the relocation of the maximum exerted plantar pressure often shifting it from the left to right and even from heel to toes deviating from the natural propensity as reported in bare feet conditions. Thus, effectiveness of the footwear or related component can be evaluated with the help of pedobarographical analysis in redistribution and reduction of plantar pressure exerted in any occupational group.

CONCLUSION

The combination of work-rest scheduling, along with identification of area of foot discomfort, identifying prevalent practices and bolstering investigation of effectiveness of administered occupational aid should be aimed. The methodical approach encompassing the major causes and their intensities can effectively alleviate any workplace related postural anomaly, among different occupational groups experiencing lower limb activity for extended durations.

Comment: Is ChatGPT a Reliable Auxiliary Tool in Basic Life Support Training and Education? A Cross-sectional Study
Authors: Tanmoy Ghatak, Utsav A Mani
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We read with considerable interest the article by Gupta et al.1 The authors have provided valuable insights into ChatGPT's role in basic life support (BLS) training. Their study highlights both the potential benefits and the current shortcomings of large language models (LLMs) in this area. The accessibility of artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots as supplementary learning aids and the ongoing importance of conventional instructor-led mannequin-based training were both underscored. Other specialties also used ChatGPT with modest accuracy in clinical education and decision support.2,3

Nonetheless, there are some important limitations that merit discussion. First, the reliance on open-ended, subjective prompts likely introduced variability. This approach may not fully reflect ChatGPT's true capabilities. Secondly, the strict “perfect-only” scoring system classified partially correct answers as entirely incorrect, potentially underestimating performance. Third, the responses were evaluated mainly by human raters. There was no comparison against a gold-standard reference, such as ILCOR checklists. Using a less robust reference standard may introduce bias. It can also limit reproducibility. Moreover, repeated testing on similar prompts risks a kind of “prompt overfitting.” In such cases, the model may perform well on guideline-like questions but struggle with novel phrasing. As Liu et al. emphasized, proper machine learning evaluation requires careful alignment. The type of input, the reference standard, and the validation approach must all match.4

Another important issue is the tendency of LLMs like ChatGPT to “hallucinations,” producing answers that sound plausible but are factually incorrect.5 This occurs because these models generate text by predicting likely word sequences from training data, rather than verifying information against authoritative sources. While fluent, such responses can lack medical validity, underscoring the need for human oversight in all educational and clinical contexts.

Future studies could extend their approach in several useful directions:

Future studies could compare ChatGPT's performance on multiple-choice questions (MCQs) and structured checklists with its performance on open-ended prompts. This may help clarify whether variability stems from prompt style or from inherent model limitations. Prior studies in ophthalmology suggest higher accuracy with structured input.3

Beyond text exchanges, combining ChatGPT with low-cost simulation or virtual reality platforms could enrich BLS training. During the COVID-19 pandemic, virtual adjuncts were shown to sustain learning and improve knowledge retention when mannequin-based teaching was not possible.6

As highlighted by Liu et al., evaluation should extend beyond accuracy percentages. It should also examine real-world clinical outcomes. For BLS, this might include whether AI-supported training improves compression depth, ventilation quality, or reduces the time-to-intervention in acute care settings.

Future research should move beyond accuracy alone and examine the ways in which learners actually interact with AI. Abdulnour and colleagues describe two distinct patterns of use: The “centaur” approach, in which tasks are delegated to AI but human oversight is retained, and the “cyborg” approach, where human and AI work in close integration.7 For high-stakes clinical decisions, centaur-like engagement is preferable. Cyborg strategies may be more appropriate for low-risk or well-defined tasks.

In summary, Gupta et al. have contributed significantly by exploring ChatGPT's role in resuscitation education. Addressing the limitations noted above and broadening the scope of evaluation will be crucial to determining how AI can best complement traditional methods and improve patient care. We commend the authors for initiating this important discussion and encourage further interdisciplinary research to define AI's optimal role in resuscitation education.

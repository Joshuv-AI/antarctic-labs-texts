Overcoming EGFR-TKI resistance by targeting the tumor microenvironment
Authors: Jinsong Zhang, Natalie Vokes, Man Li, Jiachen Xu, Hua Bai, Jie Wang, Zhijie Wang, Jianjun Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Targeted therapy has ushered in a new era of precision medicine for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Currently, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) stand as the recommended first-line therapy for advanced NSCLC harboring sensitive EGFR mutations. Nevertheless, most patients inevitably confront the challenge of drug resistance. This phenomenon arises not solely from intrinsic alterations within cancer cells but also from the intricate dynamics of the tumor microenvironment and the complex interactions that occur between cancer cells and their immediate surroundings. This review consolidates the current knowledge regarding EGFR-TKI resistance mechanisms, with a specific emphasis on unraveling the role played by the tumor microenvironment. In addition, the review delineates strategic approaches to surmount TKI resistance, thereby enriching the understanding of the interplay between therapeutic agents and the intricate milieu surrounding cancer cells.

Introduction

The identification of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) alterations has resulted in significant changes in the management of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which is a leading cause of cancer-related death.1,2 The development of EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) has led to a dramatic improvement in the progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and quality of life compared with standard chemotherapy in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC.3, 4, 5, 6, 7 However, resistance to EGFR-TKIs inevitably develops, with most patients lacking further druggable targets. The median PFS ranges from 9 months to 13 months for first- and second-generation TKIs3,5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10.1–18.9 months for osimertinib in the second- and first-line settings, respectively.4,10 Notwithstanding the strides made in delineating the mechanisms of resistance to TKIs, in approximately 10 % of patients who show resistance after first- or second-generation TKI treatment, no mechanism is identified. Resistance mechanisms of EGFR-TKIs

EGFR-TKI resistance is thought to arise from pre-existing resistant clones or through the evolution of induced adaptive resistance or tolerance mechanisms.13,14 In clinical practice, resistance is classified by the timing of onset. Primary resistance refers to resistance that emerges within 3 months or less of initiation of EGFR-TKI therapy,15 whereas secondary or acquired resistance emerges later. The resistance mechanisms to EGFR-TKI identified to-date can be classified into on- or off-target mechanisms. On-target mechanisms include mutations within the EGFR gene, whereas off-target mechanisms involve bypass mutations and phenotypic transformation, located “outside” of the EGFR gene.

As an on-target resistance mechanism, T790M mutation is the most prevalent acquired resistant mutation to first- and second-generation TKIs. The third-generation EGFR-TKIs have emerged as the preferred choice for clinical treatment because of their superior efficacy and safety in overcoming T790M resistance. However, progression occurs after osimertinib treatment, with EGFR C797S mutation being the most common on-target resistance mechanism to osimertinib.16 There are also uncommon EGFR-TKI resistant mutations, such as exon 20 insertion,17 mutations after second-generation TKI resistance such as T854A,18 D761Y,19 L747S,20 and mutations after third-generation TKI resistance such as G796X.21,22 Bypass mechanisms of resistance refer to additional receptor tyrosine kinases (RTK) activation besides EGFR, which also activates key signaling pathways downstream of EGFR. The most common ones are MET amplification and human epidermalgrowth factor receptor-2 (HER2) amplification. MET or HER2 amplification can activate signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT), mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), and phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) signaling pathways downstream of EGFR, leading to EGFR-TKI resistance. Phenotypic transformation is another important off-target mechanism of resistance. Both transformation into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and squamous cell lung cancer can happen after TKI resistance. TP53 and Rb1 loss have been found to play crucial roles in initiating SCLC transformation.23 Further research into transformation mechanisms is warranted to provide early prediction markers and effective strategies for overcoming these resistance mechanisms.

Changes of tumor cell immunogenicity after EGFR-TKI treatment

EGFR mutations have been associated with an immunologically “cold” tumor microenvironment (TME). Although the mechanisms by which these tumors achieve tumor immune evasion are complex,24 one proposed contributing feature is that EGFR mutant tumors have low immunogenicity. EGFR mutant tumors have a lower number of somatic mutations or low tumor mutational burden (TMB), which may lead to fewer neoantigens to provoke an anti-tumor immune response. This reduction in immunogenicity makes these tumors less prone to recognition and elimination by the immune system.25,26 However, treatment with an EGFR-TKI is generally associated with an increase in TMB, suggesting this feature may change with treatment.27

EGFR mutations may further affect tumor immunogenicity through the antigen presentation complex. Major histocompatibility complex (MHC), also known as human leukocyte antigen (HLA), plays a crucial role in antigen presentation, thereby activating T-mediated killing. Several studies have reported an association between EGFR and HLA.28,29 Upon analysis of clinical specimens post-TKI treatment, a significant increase in HLA-I expression was observed compared with treatment-naïve samples. The upregulation of MHC-I not only enhances tumor immunogenicity but also facilitates the recognition and elimination of tumor cells by cytotoxic T cells.30 Following the development of resistance to TKI treatment, a comprehensive multi-proteomic characterization of the antigen-presenting machinery revealed global inhibition of HLA peptide processing and presentation upon osimertinib resistance. Proteomic analyses further demonstrated the downregulation of antigen presentation complex proteins and the immunoproteasome in TKI-resistant lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).31

Finally, there are discrepant data on programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression in EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Although Azuma et al32 reported an association between EGFR mutations and increased PD-L1 expression in surgically resected NSCLC specimens, multiple recent studies demonstrated a negative correlation between EGFR mutation and PD-L1 expression in clinical samples.33,34 Experimental evidence has also shown bidirectional effects of EGFR mutations on PD-L1 expression. EGFR mutation was found to upregulate PD-L1 expression through the activation of PI3K/protein kinase B (AKT), RAS/RAF/mitogen-activated extracellular signal-regulated kinase (MEK)/extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK), or Janus tyrosine kinase (JAK)/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) pathways.35, 36, 37, 38 Conversely, EGFR mutation can indirectly suppress PD-L1 expression by inhibiting interferon regulatory factor-1 (IRF-1),39 which can bind to the PD-L1 promoter upon interferon-γ (IFN-γ) treatment and is key for PD-L1 promoter function.40 Discrepancies among studies may be attributed to the heterogeneity of tumor samples and other confounding TME factors in vivo. The exact relationship between PD-L1 and EGFR mutation is yet to be defined.

Relationship between the TME and EGFR-TKI resistance

EGFR-TKIs have the potential to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment. Beyond neoplastic cells, the TME is mainly composed of other cells, including tumor-associated myeloid cells (TAMCs), tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), natural killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). Extracellular components such as the tumor vasculature, extracellular matrix (ECM), cytokines, and exosomes further contribute to this intricate milieu. Cellular components of the TME

TILs

Successful antitumor immunity relies on the collaborative efforts of various lymphoid components, including CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, B cells, and innate lymphoid cells.41 EGFR-mutated NSCLC is characterized by an “immunologically cold” TME, characterized by a low TMB, increased regulatory T cell (Treg) infiltration, and decreased CD8+ T cell infiltration.26,39 These features are associated with poor response to the anti-PD-1/PD-L1 antibody.33,42, 43, 44 EGFR mutations exert regulatory effects on TILs within the TME. Activation of EGFR signaling was demonstrated to upregulate CCL22 expression, recruiting CD4+ Tregs and simultaneously downregulating CD8+ T cell infiltration through PI3K-AKT activation, IRF-1 downregulation, and reduced secretion of CXCL10 and CCL5.39 Another mechanism is increased CD8+ T cell apoptosis in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, as observed in clinical specimens and in vitro experiments with EGFR-mutant NSCLC-derived exosomes.45 Comparative analysis using multiplexed quantitative immunofluorescence revealed a higher proportion of inactive TILs in EGFR mutant NSCLC tumors.46 Notably, EGFR-TKI treatment, such as erlotinib, showed a potential in reversing these adverse changes, decreasing Treg infiltration in EGFR-mutant LUAD tumors, increasing CD8+ T cell infiltration, and reducing Treg infiltration in LUAD cell lines.39 Another mechanism of action is through the upregulation of MHC-I following EGFR-TKI treatment, which was inhibited in EGFR mutant NSCLC. MHC-I is responsible for tumor antigen presentation and T cell activation. Upregulation of MHC-I could potentiate the tumor killing of EGFR-TKI.30 Moreover, a certain subgroup of TILs may contribute to EGFR-TKI resistance. One study using single-cell transcriptional analysis found that an increased number of eomesodermin (EOMES)+ CD8+ T cells are associated with EGFR-TKI resistance and may be used as an indicator for TKI response.47

Tumor-associated myeloid cells (TAMCs)

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs)

TAMs constitute a prevalent but heterogeneous cellular compartment within the TME, comprising up to 50% of certain solid tumors. TAMs play a pivotal role in disease progression and the development of drug resistance by providing nutritional support to tumor cells.48 These cells exhibit a dual nature, capable of both executing tumor-killing functions and also orchestrating resistance to various anti-tumor therapies. Functionally, they can be broadly categorized into two main types: the pro-inflammatory and anti-tumor M1 subtype (CD86+ CD11b+ F4/80+) and the anti-inflammatory and pro-tumor M2 subtype (CD206+ CD11b+ F4/80+).49 In NSCLC, EGFR mutations trigger the upregulation of immunoglobulin-like transcript (ILT) 4 expression through the activation of ERK and AKT pathways. Subsequently, ILT4 recruits TAMs and induces M2 polarization, thereby contributing to the establishment of an immunosuppressive TME.50 Notably, during the different stages of treatment, macrophages undergo discernible functional alterations. In the early phase of EGFR-TKI treatment, M2 macrophage polarization is inhibited.51 However, with prolonged treatment, resistance to EGFR-TKIs emerges, concomitant with an increase in M2 macrophages.

TAMs contribute to the development of EGFR-TKI resistance through diverse mechanisms. First, TAMs can induce resistance to EGFR-TKIs by activating critical signaling pathways. For instance, intra-tumoral epiregulin (EREG) produced by macrophages interacts with Erb-B2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ERBB2), activating AKT and inhibiting TKI-induced apoptosis in cancer cells, ultimately leading to TKI resistance.52 Novel strategies aimed at targeting EREG/EGFR are currently under development or undergoing clinical trials. In addition, STAT3 is a crossroad of multiple tumor-related signaling pathways, playing a crucial role in tumor development and immune suppression. It also plays an important role in the process of TKI resistance. Sun et al53 demonstrated that targeting the EGFR T790M-cis-L792F/STAT3 tyrosine 705 (Tyr705) / ILT4 pathway could be a potential strategy to overcome osimertinib resistance in NSCLC by reverting the antitumor effect of TAMs. STAT3 can regulate the transcription of cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47) by directly binding to the cis-regulatory elements within the intronic region of the CD47 gene. The STAT3 inhibitor W1131 can downregulate the expression of CD47 and enhance the phagocytic ability of macrophages toward tumor cells, thereby increasing the efficacy of EGFR-TKI.54 Furthermore, both preclinical studies in mouse models and clinical datasets have highlighted the role of TAMs in promoting TKI resistance through interactions with T cells. TAM accumulation in advanced NSCLC tumors suppresses CD8+ T cell activation, limiting the efficacy of TKIs. Notably, the systemic stimulator of interferon genes (STING) agonist benzothiophene oxobutanoic acid (MSA-2) exhibited potential in reinvigorating osimertinib's antitumor activity by inducing T cell activation in mice.55 Third, TAM-derived exosomes emerge as crucial mediators of TKI resistance. Research indicates that TAM-derived exosomes contribute to gefitinib resistance by activating AKT, ERK1/2, and STAT3 signaling pathways.56 In addition, at the 2023 WCLC conference, a single-cell sequencing-based study unveiled the unique immune microenvironment of leptomeningeal metastasis in lung cancer. The study identified a distinct subset of macrophages (RNASE1-M), specifically associated with lipid metabolism, shedding light on associated regulatory mechanisms and potential biomarkers related with osimertinib resistance and leptomeningeal metastasis.57 This suggests that with the advancement of single-cell sequencing technology, a deeper insight into the immune microenvironment characteristics post-TKI resistance can be achieved.

Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs): Beyond TAMs and Tregs, MDSCs are crucial immunosuppressive cell types implicated in tumor immune evasion. Derived from the bone marrow, these cells migrate to peripheral lymphoid organs and tumors, actively participating in the shaping of the TME.58 MDSCs are classified into granular and mononuclear lineages by their origin, i.e., the granular/polymorphonuclear lineage MDSCs (PMN-MDSCs) and the mononuclear lineage MDSCs (M-MDSCs).59 In EGFR-positive lung tumors, EGFR-TKIs recruit MDSCs to the tumor site and induce phenotypic alterations. Specifically, the proportion of M-MDSCs has been observed to increase in tumor-bearing lung tissue, while the percentage of PMN-MDSCs remains unaltered during the course of treatment.51 In the initial stages of TKI treatment, immune-activating changes occur, with increased CD8+ T cells and dendritic cells (DCs), decreased Foxp3+ Tregs, and inhibited M2-like macrophage polarization. Prolonged treatment, however, leads to heightened immune suppression, marked by increased MDSCs, elevated interleukin (IL)-10, and upregulated C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2) expression.60 Upon the development of osimertinib resistance, MDSC recruitment to the tumor immune microenvironment is facilitated through the activation of nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) and MAPK signaling pathways.61 Manipulating MDSCs represents a promising strategy for addressing EGFR-TKI resistance.

DCs: DCs are the most important and potent antigen-presenting cells (APCs). They present antigens to T cells, building a connection between innate and adaptive immunity.62 Increased infiltration of DCs has been observed in the TME of EGFR-mutant LUAD,63 but their functionality appears to be attenuated. In an investigation using Lewis lung cancer (LLC) xenograft mouse models carrying EGFR 19del mutation, it was observed that exosomes originating from EGFR 19del tumors impede DC function, consequently restraining the proliferation of CD8+ T cells.51 The CD47-signal-regulatory protein α (SIRPα) axis represents the first identified checkpoint for phagocytosis. Analyses have reported that EGFR-mutated NSCLCs overexpress CD47 to impede phagocytosis by APCs,64 whereas EGFR-TKIs reduce CD47 expression on pre-apoptotic cells, promoting phagocytosis by APCs toward cancer cells.65 Another study identified STAT3 as a transcriptional regulator of CD47 expression, mediating the evasion of phagocytosis.66 Therefore, a combination of EGFR-TKI with a STAT3 inhibitor or anti-CD47 antibody may hold the potential to overcome EGFR-TKI resistance.

Neutrophils: The impact of neutrophils in cancer involves a dynamic interplay of opposing effects influenced by signals from the TME.67 In NSCLC tumors, the percentage of neutrophils exceeds that in distal lung tissues,68 and EGFR-mutant LUAD tumors exhibit a higher neutrophil density than EGFR-wild type (WT) tumors.69 The neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio (NLR) emerges as a potential predictive biomarker for EGFR-TKI therapy outcomes, with a high NLR correlating with poor prognosis.70, 71, 72 The relationship between neutrophils and EGFR-TKI resistance remains an area for further investigation.

NK cells

NK cells, integral to innate immunity, play a crucial role in immunosurveillance by targeting and eliminating cells with low MHC-I expression, including virus-infected and cancer cells.73 NK cells are typically identified as CD3- CD56+ cells and can be further classified into mature cytotoxic subsets, predominantly CD56dim CD16+ NK cells, which constitute the majority of circulating NK cells.74 One study using the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database found that EGFR-mutated LUAD tumors have lower infiltration of CD56dim CD16+ NK cells compared with EGFR-WT LUAD.63 EGFR-TKIs have been shown to augment NK cell-mediated antitumor immunity, as evidenced by increased peripheral NK cell numbers following gefitinib treatment75 and the upregulation of natural killer group 2, member D (NKG2D) ligands in cancer cells, enhancing NK cell cytotoxicity against tumors.76 In TKI-resistant NSCLC tumors, NK cell function is inhibited through IL-6.66 In addition, one clinical trial has corroborated that the use of SNK01 (autologous NK cells) in combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy, notably cetuximab, in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC exhibiting TKI resistance demonstrated safety and exhibited promising antitumor efficacy.77

CAFs

CAFs, the predominant cell type in cancer stroma, play diverse roles within the TME.78 Recent advancements in technologies like single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) have elucidated CAF diversity, identifying three major subtypes, antigen-presenting CAFs, immune regulatory/inflammatory CAFs, and myofibroblastic CAFs, each with distinct characteristics shared across various tissue origins.79 Current research has focused primarily on immune regulatory/inflammatory CAFs, which are potent secretors of factors including cytokines, hepatic growth factor (HGF), and metabolism intermediates that mediate TKI resistance. One study found that CAFs derived from osimertinib-resistant NSCLC secreted more IL-6, IL-8, and HGF.80 In addition, studies have shown that HGF may play an important role in inducing resistance to EGFR-TKI in EGFR-mutated lung cancer cell lines.81 One study found that HGF binds with the MET receptor in cancer cells, then activates PI3K-Akt and STAT3 signaling, contributing to TKI resistance.82,83 Similarly, in vitro experiments showed that CAFs promote EGFR-TKI resistance through the HGF/insulin like growth factor 1 (IGF-1)/Annexin A2 (ANXA2)/epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) signaling axis.84 In addition, CAFs isolated from tumor tissues produce the tryptophan metabolite kynurenine (Kyn), which significantly increased NSCLC cell proliferation and resistance to EGFR TKIs. Kyn produced by CAFs promoted the up-regulation of aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) signaling in NSCLC, which led to downstream AKT and ERK signaling pathway activation.85

Crosstalk interactions with CAF play a key role in lung cancer resistance to EGFR TKI by mediating EMT and may be an ideal therapeutic target. CAFs affect TKI resistance through induction of the EMT, which is mediated by the induction of a smoothened (SMO)-mediated reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton, as well as the activation of hedgehog signaling.86 Moreover, an immunohistochemistry and qRT-PCR analysis of 110 advanced NSCLC cases revealed that in cases with EGFR mutations, gefitinib resistance was significantly associated with elevated tumor and stromal ANXA2, high levels of CAFs, elevated STAT3, and reduced E-cadherin expression. CAFs and ANXA2 emerge as adverse prognostic factors for advanced NSCLC patients, potentially serving as underlying factors in gefitinib treatment resistance via EMT induction.87

In summary, CAFs are closely associated with TKI resistance through multiple mechanisms, which can be potential targets to tackle TKI resistance. Previous research has mainly focused on immune regulatory/inflammatory CAFs, while the role of antigen-presenting CAFs and myofibroblastic CAFs in TKI resistance is yet to be elucidated. In addition, the broad heterogeneity of CAFs suggests that there may be tumor-specific or even patient-specific CAF subpopulations that contribute to TKI resistance. Deciphering these subpopulations may be critical for optimizing TKI efficacy with greater precision.

The extracellular elements within the TME

Angiogenesis

The TME orchestrates the neovascularization and remodeling of existing vessels, crucial for tumor blood supply. Histological evidence suggests that heightened neovascularization in lung tumors correlates with an unfavorable prognosis.88 Angiogenesis involves multiple cellular events and many interactions between multiple cell types. Many molecules have been identified as regulators of tumor angiogenesis, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), angiopoietin (Ang), endogenous antiangiogenic factors, growth factors and cytokines, and cell–cell adhesion molecules.89 Rahma and Hodi90 identified a reciprocal interaction between tumor angiogenesis and immune cells in the TME. Angiogenesis, driven by factors like VEGF and Ang-2, induces an immunosuppressive microenvironment by inhibiting the function of APCs and effector T cells, while promoting the activity of Tregs, MDSCs, and TAMs. Conversely, immunosuppressive cells within the TME can stimulate angiogenesis. Neutrophils and TAMs can secrete pro-angiogenic factors such as VEGF, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and IL-8, whereas activated DCs express vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 1 (VEGFR-1) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), which promote angiogenesis.

Clinically, angiogenesis is closely related to EGFR mutations and EGFR-TKI resistance. EGFR-mutated NSCLC cells overexpress VEGF via upregulation of hypoxia inducible factor 1 subunit α (HIF-1α), independent of hypoxia.91 Elevated VEGF activates the PI3K/AKT pathway and the MAPK pathway, contributing to acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs.92 The combination of EGFR-TKIs and antiangiogenic agents thus represents a rational therapeutic strategy to boost TKI efficacy. However, clinical data have been mixed. A recent meta-analysis of nine major clinical trials of EGFR-TKIs with or without antiangiogenic agents in first-line, advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC showed that the combination of TKI and antiangiogenic agents achieved a significantly longer PFS, but no difference in OS. A higher incidence of adverse events was found in the combination groups, particularly hypertension and proteinuria. However, results varied by the EGFR-TKI. The combination of erlotinib with bevacizumab or ramucirumab exhibited improved PFS compared with TKI monotherapy, whereas the efficacy of osimertinib in combination with anti-angiogenic agents is still under investigation. In WJOG9717L, the combination of osimertinib and bevacizumab did not achieve PFS improvement in first-line advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC.93 Similarly, Yu et al94 did not observe an improvement in PFS with the combination of osimertinib and bevacizumab compared with that in the FLAURA study.

Other studies, such as the RAMOSE study investigating osimertinib with ramucirumab, are ongoing. In addition, there are promising but early data with other agents.95 A recent study conducted by Chen et al96 showed an overall objective response rate (ORR) of 96.15 % and intracranial ORR of 76.47 % to aumolertinib and anlotinib. PFS and OS data are still pending. Thus, the value of adding angiogenesis inhibitors to EGFR-TKIs remains inconclusive, and further research efforts are ongoing. To date, the only Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved combination therapy is ramucirumab plus erlotinib.

Secreted factors

ILs: ILs and associated cytokines serve as crucial mediators of cellular communication, particularly among immune cells, exerting pivotal roles in cancer with pro-tumor and anti-tumor effects. Protumor interleukins, such as IL-4, IL-6, IL-11, IL-20, and IL-22, contribute to tumor growth. Conversely, antitumor interleukins, including IL-2, IL-7, IL-12, IL-15, and IL-21, play roles in suppressing cancer progression. In addition, certain ILs and cytokines, such as transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), IFN-γ, IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-9, and IL-10, exhibit pleiotropic functions with diverse impacts on the TME.97 From the current research findings, we propose a classification of ILs in accordance with their involvement in TKI resistance, categorizing them as “TKI resistance-promoting” and “TKI resistance-preventing” ILs. The former includes IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and IL-22, whereas the latter includes IL-15. TGF-β: TGF-β participates in several cellular activities and serves as a negative growth regulator in normal cells. In lung cancer, however, it plays important roles in promoting tumor progression and dissemination.101 TGF-β is associated with EGFR-TKI resistance. TGF-β stimulation downregulates SRY-box transcription factor 2 (SOX-2), induces EMT, and promotes resistance to EGFR-TKI in lung cancer.102 In another study, TGF-β was found to downregulate phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) and induce acquired resistance to erlotinib in NSCLC.103 Human apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease 1 (APE1), a TGF-β regulator, plays an important role in EMT. An APE1 redox inhibitor (E3330) remarkably reversed the EMT phenotype and sensitized the cells to EGFR-TKIs.104 Moreover, several studies have clarified microRNA (miRNA)-related mechanisms underlying TGF-induced EMT. The TGF-β1-induced EMT leads to miR-625-3p/AXL receptor tyrosine kinase (AXL) axis–mediated gefitinib resistance via the suppressor of mother against decapentaplegic homolog (SMAD) pathway in lung cancer cells.105 Another study found that the miR-134/487b/655 cluster contributed to the TGF-β1-induced EMT phenomenon and affected the resistance to gefitinib.106 These specific miRNAs may serve as new therapeutic targets in patients with advanced NSCLC. Furthermore, the combination of erlotinib and TGF-β1-EMT inhibitors like dasatinib and cilengitide tends to reverse acquiring resistance to EGFR-TKIs.107,108

ECM

In NSCLC, the interaction between EGFR and ECM plays a critical role in modulating ECM stiffness, influencing angiogenesis within the TME, as well as affecting tumor cell survival, adhesion, invasion, and metastasis.109 Multiple components in the ECM participate in EGFR-TKI resistance. Collagen is the main component of the ECM. It promotes cancer progression,110 and collagen I can induce TKI resistance. Collagen can be taken up by cancer cells through macropinocytosis111 and activate mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) in an AKT-independent manner, inducing TKI resistance.112 Therefore, the combination of EGFR-TKI and an mTOR inhibitor is a possible strategy to overcome TKI resistance. Another study found significant collagen upregulation in erlotinib-resistant tumors compared with treatment-naïve ones. Integrin-β1 may play important roles in inducing TKI resistance.113

Proteoglycans are also involved in EGFR signaling pathways. For example, endocan, a small soluble proteoglycan, is upregulated in NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations. Endocan facilitates EGFR signaling, while activated EGFR signaling in turn induces endocan overexpression via the JAK/STAT3 and ERK pathway, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop to activate EGFR signaling. Breaking the interaction between EGFR and endocan using synthetic endocan peptide inhibited EGFR mutation and tumor growth in TKI-resistant cell with C797S mutation, indicating its potential in overcoming osimertinib resistance.114

Fibrinogen-like protein-1 (FGL-1), a type of fibrinogen, is found to be involved in gefitinib resistance. FGL-1 is upregulated in a dose-dependent manner following gefitinib therapy. FGL-1 inhibits cancer cell apoptosis through the poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1)/caspase 3 pathway, thus inducing gefitinib resistance. Inhibition of FGL-1 restores the antitumor effect of gefitinib.115

Summary of strategies to overcome EGFR-TKI resistance by targeting different components of TME

Targeting intracellular signaling pathways involved in TKI resistance

Numerous signaling pathways have been implicated in TKI resistance, including but not limited to the PI3K/AKT, MAPK, STAT3, and STING pathways. The PI3K-AKT pathway is implicated in TKI resistance associated with TAMs, TILs, angiogenesis, and IL-1β.39,52,53,92,98,116 Moreover, targeting the STAT3 pathway presents a promising multifaceted strategy to overcome EGFR-TKI resistance. Inhibiting STAT3 activation through the use of small molecule inhibitors or antibodies can modulate the polarization of M2 macrophages, thereby mitigating their role in resistance mechanisms.53 In addition, targeting STAT3 interferes with exosome-mediated resistance in TAMs.56 Furthermore, inhibiting STAT3 alleviates immune suppression, reducing the expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-L137 and CD4766 and enhancing the immune system's ability to recognize and combat tumors. By regulating the CD47 expression in DCs, STAT3 inhibition aids in overcoming the evasion mechanisms of tumor cells against DC-mediated phagocytosis. In addition, studies indicate that TAMs play a crucial role in promoting TKI resistance by suppressing the activation of CD8+ T cells. Therefore, modulating these interactions through agents such as STING agonists offers the potential to reinvigorate T cell activity, enhancing the anti-tumor effectiveness of TKIs and opening new avenues for addressing TKI resistance.55 Finally, targeting the MAPK pathway may help overcome TKI resistance, as studies have demonstrated in osimertinib resistance, NF-κB and MAPK pathways recruit immunosuppressive myeloid-derived suppressor cells.61 In summary, interventions targeting intercellular PI3K-AKT, MAPK, and STAT3 pathways, hold promise for overcoming EGFR-TKI resistance through their impacts on multiple facets of the TME.

Boosting antitumor immunity by fostering tumor cell–immune cell interactions

T cell engagers, also recognized as T cell directing bispecifics, represent a recently devised therapeutic strategy. These agents possess the ability to concomitantly engage surface molecules on tumor cells, such as EGFR, and surface molecules on T cells, such as CD3. By activating T cells and recruiting them to tumor cells, T cell engagers can enhance the efficiency of T cell identification and cytotoxicity.117 BC3448 stands out as an innovative CD3/EGFR bispecific antibody engineered to activate T cells through CD3 binding, while simultaneously directing its action toward the eradication of EGFR-mutant tumor cells. In vitro experiments have shown prominent inhibition of the growth in osimertinib-resistant cell lines by BC3448, with ongoing in vivo experiments underway to delve deeper into its potential.118 T cell engagers hold the potential as an efficacious strategy for surmounting TKI resistance. In addition, NK cell-based therapy, particularly utilizing NK92-CD16 cells, demonstrates promise in overcoming EGFR-TKI resistance by enhancing cytotoxicity against TKI-resistant NSCLC cells, especially those overexpressing intercellular cell adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1). Combining NK92-CD16 cells with EGFR antibodies presents a potential strategy to overcome TKI resistance.119

Addressing intercellular communication carriers that contribute to TKI resistance

Strategies to overcome TKI resistance encompass interventions targeting two pivotal facets: extracellular vesicles/exosomes and cytokines within the TME.

Firstly, innovative nanotherapeutic approaches, such as the blood-brain barrier (BBB)-permeable bionic co-delivery system and T12 peptide-modified albumin nanoparticles, aim to intervene in the biological effects of exosomes. These methods are designed to modulate immune cells within the TME, particularly prompting TAM repolarization and reversal of EGFR T790M-associated drug resistance.120 In addition, a nanodrug combining simvastatin and gefitinib proved effective in reversing gefitinib resistance, modulating angiogenesis, M2-macrophage polarization, and intrinsic immunity in remodeling the TME in EGFR-mutant NSCLC.121 Similarly, a dual-targeted co-delivery system of gefitinib and vorinostat demonstrated efficacy in overcoming EGFR-T790M-associated drug resistance, acting on TAMs and HER-2-positive NSCLC cells with mannose receptor overexpression.122

Regarding cytokines, a dichotomy arises between those fostering resistance and those preventing it. Addressing cytokines that promote resistance involves targeted interventions in relevant signaling pathways, exemplified by treatments targeting the IL-1β/EHD1/tubulin beta-III (TUBB3) axis.98 IL-6 is another cytokine that may foster resistance. Researchers have found that depletion of IL-6 can enhance NK and T cell functions, decrease Treg subpopulations, and boost anti-PD-1 efficacy.66 As an antiallergic drug, Tranilast can inhibit IL-6 secreted by CAFs, thereby inhibiting CAF-induced upregulation of phosphorylated STAT3. Combining Tranilast and TKI reverses CAF-mediated NSCLC TKI resistance, both in vitro and in vivo.123 SHP2 inhibition can decrease IL-8 secretion, breaking the CXCL8 feedback loop and improving osimertinib efficacy.99 Conversely, for cytokines preventing resistance, the use of synthetic analogs, such as pro-IL-15, emerges as a strategy to synergize with TKIs, enhancing their efficacy and overcoming targeted therapy resistance.124 A comprehensive approach integrating these strategies holds promise in providing a more nuanced and effective framework for conquering TKI resistance.

Targeting intracellular metabolites that contribute to TKI resistance

The modulation of metabolites produced by cellular components in the TME is a promising avenue for overcoming TKI resistance. Precision interventions targeting the metabolism products generated by cells such as CAFs and TAMs hold the potential to influence tumor cell proliferation and responsiveness to treatment. For example, Kyn produced by CAFs promoted the up-regulation of AhR signaling in NSCLC, which led to downstream AKT and ERK signaling pathway activation. Inhibition of AhR signaling effectively prevented tumor growth and development of EGFR TKI resistance.85 In addition, studies have shown that treatment with the glutamate metabolism inhibitor JHU083 reduces immunosuppressive cells, including monocytes and granulocyte myeloid-derived suppressor cells, Tregs, and pre-tumor CD4+ T helper cell 17 (Th17) cells. The combination of JHU083 with EGFR peptide vaccine (EVax) achieved potent tumor inhibition effect.125 This strategy may involve disrupting metabolic pathways, modulating signaling cascades, and leveraging the biological effects of metabolites to enhance the effectiveness of anti-cancer therapies. This forward-looking research direction is poised to offer novel concepts and strategies for overcoming TKI resistance, driving advancements in the field of cancer treatment.

Conclusion

EGFR mutation in NSCLC tumor cells alters the TME in a way that favors tumor growth. This is achieved through multiple mechanisms, including the secretion of cytokines and exosomes from tumor cells and the upregulation of immune checkpoint ligands such as PD-L1, B7-H3, and CD47 on the tumor cell surface. Key characteristics of the TME in EGFR-mutated NSCLC include: (1) decreased level of TILs; (2) M2 polarization of TAMs; (3) increased infiltration of immunosuppressive cells such as MDSCs and Tregs; and (4) decreased function of NK and neutrophils. Treatment with EGFR-TKIs reverses some of the changes of the TME in EGFR-mutated NSCLC, increasing TIL infiltration, suppressing immunosuppressive cell infiltration, and restoring NK cell function. However, with continued treatment, resistance to EGFR-TKIs inevitably occurs, leading to a TME that further promotes TKI resistance. This leads to changes including immune suppressive cytokines and exosomes secreted by CAFs and TAMs, as well as the emergence of certain TIL and CAF subgroups that contribute to EGFR-TKI resistance. The TME becomes even more intractable than before EGFR-TKI treatment, posing a great challenge to understand the microenvironment in a comprehensive way. It is worth noting that current research mainly focuses on one specific component of the TME, and understanding of the interactions between different components is lacking. Establishing a network of interactions between different elements will be crucial for effectively targeting the TME to overcome TKI resistance.

Recent advancements in sequencing technology and artificial intelligence (AI) have further bolstered the research into TKI resistance. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables mechanistic understanding at the cellular level, enabling the detection of key cellular subgroups responsible for TKI resistance. Trajectory analysis and pseudotime analysis could provide new insights into how the TME dynamically changes overtime. Furthermore, deep learning methods are being developed to extract specific cellular interaction information and associate it with clinical outcomes, which could partially address the lack in the understanding from a holistic view.126 The use of AI to decipher cellular interactions in the TME would be an intriguing future direction.

From the current mechanistic understanding, a diverse array of innovative strategies has emerged to combat TKI resistance. These approaches encompass targeting intracellular signaling pathways, employing T cell engagers, disrupting intercellular communication carriers, and neutralizing intracellular metabolites. The application of these novel approaches will deepen our understanding of TKI resistance mechanisms and possibly aid in managing TKI resistance.

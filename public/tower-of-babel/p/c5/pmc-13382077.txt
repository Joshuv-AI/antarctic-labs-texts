Association Between Inferior Vena Cava Location and the Number of Deployments of a Leadless Pacemaker
Authors: Tomoya Kimura, Yusuke Yoshikawa, Mari Nezu, Fumihiro Sakakibara, Takahiro Iseda, Takanari Fujita, Shuhei Tsuji, Satoshi Shizuta, Takeshi Morimoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionWhile some patients faced the difficulty to implant leadless pacemaker, the relationship between inferior vena cava (IVC) location and procedural difficulty was uncertain.MethodsWe conducted a single‐center, cross‐sectional study including 96 consecutive patients who underwent implantation of Micra leadless pacemakers between July 2022 and November 2024. Based on the positional relationship between the IVC and the right ventricular septum assessed by preprocedural computed tomography, patients were classified into right‐sided and left‐sided IVC groups. The primary outcome was multiple deployments defined as the number of device deployments ≥ 2.ResultsA total of 96 patients were analyzed, including 81 in the right‐sided and 15 in the left‐sided IVC group. The mean age was 84, and 55 (57%) were male patients. Multiple deployments were observed in 39 (41%) patients. The risk of the primary outcome was significantly higher in the left‐sided IVC group than in the right‐sided (67% vs. 36%), with an adjusted odds ratio of 9.5 (95% CI: 1.5–58.7, p = 0.02). Procedure time was numerically longer in the left‐sided group (36 vs. 25 min, p = 0.11). Pericardial effusion occurred only in two patients in the right‐sided group, none requiring intervention.ConclusionsThe left‐sided IVC was associated with multiple deployments of a leadless pacemaker and preoperative computed tomography was useful in assessing the procedural difficulty.Keywords: computed tomography, deployments, inferior vena cava, leadless pacemaker, Micra

Background

Leadless pacemakers have emerged an established alternative to conventional transvenous pacemaker systems. Compared to transvenous pacemakers, leadless pacemakers avoid complications associated with acute pocket and subclavian leads, such as pocket infection, hematoma, and pneumothorax, as well as lead fractures, insulation breaches, venous thrombosis and obstruction, tricuspid regurgitation, and pocket infection in the chronic phase [1, 2, 3, 4]. Since receiving approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2016, the number of leadless pacemaker implantations has increased annually. A retrospective cohort study using data extracted from the National Inpatient Sample database from 2016 to 2020 showed that the number of implantations, which was 3230 in 2016–2017, had significantly increased to 11 815 in 2020 [5]. As of 2022, the number of implant cases has exceeded 150 000 in the U.S. [6].

Several studies have evaluated the procedural difficulty of implantation of leadless pacemaker, and the procedural difficulty was primarily represented by the procedure time or fluoroscopy time [6, 7, 8]. Prolonged procedure and fluoroscopy time in technically challenging cases were often attributed to repeated deployment attempts. Minimizing the number of deployments is clinically important, because multiple attempts increase the risk of complications such as pericardial effusion and device dislodgement [9]. The number of device deployments should be a clinically relevant and practical indicator of procedural difficulty; however, the previous studies did not evaluate the number of deployments and the contributing factors for multiple deployment as the measure of procedural difficulty.

Considering the access route to proceed with the leadless pacemaker, cardiac anatomy might be associated with the difficulty of implantation. We hypothesized that the spatial relationship between the inferior vena cava (IVC) and the right ventricular septum might be associated with the procedural difficulty. Therefore, we investigated whether the anatomical relationship of the IVC with the ventricular septum, as assessed by preprocedural computed tomography (CT), affected the difficulty of leadless pacemaker implantation.

Methods

Study Design and Patient Population

The present study was a single‐center cross‐sectional study. Patients who underwent implantation of the Micra leadless pacemakers (Micra TPS, Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN) at Japanese Red Cross Wakayama Medical Center between July 2022 and November 2024 were consecutively included without exclusion criteria. All patients had bradycardia due to sinus node dysfunction, high degree atrioventricular block, complete atrioventricular block, or brady‐atrial fibrillation.

The present study was approved by the institutional ethical committee at Hyogo Medical University (Approval No. 202504–202). Written informed consent was substituted by an opt‐out method because the present study was conducted in a fashion of retrospective study, and patients who refused participation were excluded from the study. The study procedure was concordant with the Ethical Guidelines of the Ministry of Health, Labor, and Welfare.

Imaging Study and Implantation of Leadless Pacemaker

CT scans and transthoracic echocardiography for anatomic evaluation prior to leadless pacemaker implantation were routinely performed at the study institution. CT and transthoracic echocardiography were performed before the implantation operation. Preoperative CT scans were performed from the neck to the pelvis using plain or contrast imaging to evaluate the access route and cardiac anatomy. CT reconstructions with a slice width of 5 mm were used for subsequent data analysis. Patients were divided into two groups according to the position of the IVC relative to the right ventricular septum. The right‐sided IVC group was defined as having the center point of the IVC located on the right side of an imaginary line extending from the long axis of the right ventricular septal surface at the level of the right atrium–IVC confluence in the axial CT plane, while the left‐sided IVC group was defined as having it on the left side (Figure 1).

This study was conducted by seven interventional cardiologists. All leadless pacemaker implantations were performed via the femoral venous approach. Most procedures were conducted under local venous anesthesia. The Micra transcatheter pacing system was advanced under fluoroscopic guidance into the right ventricle. Right and left anterior oblique fluoroscopic projections, along with contrast injections, were utilized to identify an appropriate implantation site. In all procedures, implantation was attempted into the right ventricular septum. The individualized left anterior oblique angle was determined based on the angle of the imaginary line extending from the long axis of the ventricular septum on the preoperative CT (Figure 2). After deployment, the stability of the device's mechanical fixation was confirmed using a “pull‐and‐hold” test, followed by device interrogation. The criteria for successful implantation were defined as a threshold of ≤ 1.5 V/0.24 ms and a sensed R‐wave > 5 mV. The pacemaker was released when both tests yielded satisfactory results. Hemostasis at the femoral access site was achieved using the Perclose ProStyle closure device (Abbott Vascular Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA).

A fluoroscopic image during the procedure is shown, with the fluoroscopy angle set perpendicular to the right ventricular septum as measured in the axial plane of the CT scan. From the left anterior oblique view, the delivery catheter advanced linearly toward the septum from the anterior side in the right‐sided IVC group, whereas in the left‐sided IVC group, it reached the right ventricular septum by circling around from the posterior side. Outcomes

The primary outcome was the number of patients requiring multiple deployments defined as the number of device deployments ≥ 2. The secondary outcomes included the number of deployments, procedure time, pericardial effusion, and procedure success at 30 days. In the present study, procedure time was defined as the time from right ventriculography to the end of the final device deployment. Pericardial effusion was defined as the presence of new pericardial fluid not present before the procedure or an increase in pericardial fluid detected by postoperative transthoracic echocardiography. Successful implantation after 30 days was defined as the absence of complications requiring reimplantation, such as device implantation failure, device dislodgement, or pacing failure at 30 days postoperatively.

Statistical Analysis

Summary statistics were reported using mean (standard deviation) and interquartile range for continuous variables depending on distributions and percentage for categorical variables. For comparisons between two groups, Student's t‐test or Wilcoxon's rank‐sum test were used for continuous variables, and χ2 or Fisher's exact test was used for categorical variables. Logistic regression models were constructed to estimate odds ratio of left‐sided IVC groups relative to right‐sided IVC group for outcomes and its 95% confidence intervals [95% CIs]. Because the difficulty of leadless pacemaker implantation may also be influenced by other patient factors [7, 8, 9], a multivariable model was constructed incorporating the covariates of tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient (TRPG), right atrial major axis length, and right ventricular mid diameter. p‐values were two‐sided and p‐values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using JMP 17.0 (SAS Institute Inc., Cray NC, USA).

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 96 patients were included in the analysis, of whom 81 had the right‐sided IVC and 15 had the left‐sided IVC (Table 1). Baseline characteristics were comparable between the two groups. The mean age was 84 ± 6 years in both groups, and there was no significant difference in body mass index, weight, or height, although the proportion of male patients was numerically higher in the left‐sided than the right‐sided IVC group (80% vs. 53%, p = 0.053). There were no significant differences in comorbidities including atrial fibrillation, congestive heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, or chronic kidney disease requiring dialysis. The prevalence of atrial fibrillation was numerically lower in the left‐sided IVC group than the right‐sided IVC group (13% vs. 37%, p = 0.07). Sinus node dysfunction was observed only in the right‐sided IVC group (20%). Of the 96 cases, 71 were performed by physicians with five or more cases of experience. The physicians' experiences did not significantly differ between the left‐sided and right‐sided IVC groups. Other pacing indications such as high‐grade atrioventricular block, complete atrioventricular block, and brady atrial fibrillation were similarly distributed between groups (p = 0.11). Echocardiographic findings showed no significant differences in left ventricular ejection fraction, right atrial diameter, or right ventricular mid diameter. However, TRPG was significantly higher in the left‐sided IVC group than the right‐sided IVC group (35 ± 12 mmHg vs. 26 ± 9 mmHg, p = 0.04). Preoperative CT scan showed no significant difference in individual left anterior oblique angle (50 ± 10 degrees vs. 46 ± 9 degrees, p = 0.09)(Table 1).

There were no significant differences in acute electrical performance at the final implantation site between two groups (Table 1). The pacing impedance was similar between the left‐sided and right‐sided IVC groups (515 [468–568] vs. 510 [460–580] Ω, p = 0.79). The pacing threshold tended to be slightly higher in the left‐sided group than in the right‐sided group (0.63 [0.38–1.44] V vs. 0.5 [0.38–0.75] V, p = 0.33). Likewise, sensed R‐wave amplitude was numerically higher in the left‐sided IVC group (9.8 [6.0–15.0] mV vs. 6.8 [5.5–11.3] mV, p = 0.13) (Table 1).

Outcomes

Among the 96 patients, multiple deployments were conducted in 39 (41%). The risk of multiple deployments was significantly higher in the left‐sided IVC group compared to the right‐sided IVC group (67% vs. 36%), with an OR of 3.6 (95% CI: 1.12–11.5, p = 0.03). After adjusting for TRPG, right atrial major axis length, and right ventricular mid diameter, the adjusted odds ratio increased to 9.5 (95% CI: 1.5–58.7, p = 0.02), indicating an independent association between left‐sided IVC and increased likelihood of multiple deployments (Table 2).

In the right‐sided IVC group, a single deployment was achieved in 52 patients (64%), while 15 patients (18%) required two deployments, 6 patients (7%) required three, 1 patient (1%) required four, and 7 patients (9%) required five or more deployments (Table 2). In the left‐sided IVC group, 5 patients (33%) had one deployment, 5 (33%) had two, 3 (8%) had three, 1 (1%) had four, and 1 (1%) had five or more deployments. The number of device deployments was higher in the left‐sided IVC group (median: 2 deployments) than in the right‐sided IVC group (median: 1 deployment) (p = 0.042). Procedure time was longer in the left‐sided IVC group compared to the right‐sided IVC group (36 [25–43] vs. 25 [18–36] minutes), although this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.11). Pericardial effusion was observed in 2 patients in the right‐sided IVC group but not in the left‐sided IVC group. Those with pericardial effusion did not require drainage or open surgery and improved only with follow‐up. Successful implantation after 30 days was observed in 14 patients (93%) in the left‐sided IVC group and in 78 patients (96%) in the right‐sided IVC group (Table 2).

Discussion

The present study used the number of deployments as a clinical indicator for evaluating the difficulty of leadless pacemaker implantation procedures. The main finding of this study was that multiple deployments were required in two of three cases in the left‐sided IVC and were more frequent than in the right‐sided group in one of three cases. The result shed light on the importance of considering the anatomy of a procedural pathway from the IVC to the right ventricular septum, which can be assessed by preoperative CT imaging.

Several studies have assessed the difficulty of leadless implantation procedures using procedural time and fluoroscopy time as outcome measures. A past observational study demonstrated that when right atrial diameter exceeds 4.0 cm and right atrial area exceeds 17 cm2, particularly when exceeding 60 min, the procedure time for Micra implantation is significantly more likely to be substantially prolonged [7]. Additionally, another observational study reported that a right ventricular septal diameter of 35 mm or greater and severe tricuspid regurgitation were significantly associated with prolonged procedure times [8]. Our study included the mid‐right ventricular diameter in the multivariable model as a covariates but the effect of left‐sided IVC was more prominent than the mid‐right ventricular diameter. These findings further supported the notion that right heart anatomical features can influence the technical difficulty of leadless pacemaker implantation. A plausible factor contributing to the higher risk of multiple deployments in the left‐sided IVC group was the greater loss of coaxial alignment. In patients with the left‐sided IVC, the pathway from the IVC to the right atrium, through the tricuspid valve, and toward the right ventricular septum is more tortuous, potentially increasing procedural difficulty (Figure 1B). In the right‐sided IVC group, the device advanced more directly, allowing an increase in tip load (Figure 2A), while in the left‐sided IVC group, the device advanced in a more curved path, which likely reduced tip load (Figure 2B). These findings may explain the differences in the technical difficulty of device deployment.

During the implantation procedure, the use of the individualized left anterior oblique, defined as the separation angle derived from the virtual right ventricular septal line on the axial CT plane, improved visualization of the right ventricular septum (Figure 2). An observational study demonstrated that the angle of the right ventricular septum measured by pigtail catheter during fluoroscopy did not differ from that measured by preoperative CT. Furthermore, the use of the individualized left anterior oblique facilitated differentiation between the right ventricular septum and the free wall, enabling optimal leadless pacemaker placement [10]. Although there is a concept that may overlap with the relationship between the position of IVC and the individualized left anterior oblique angle—namely, that a larger left anterior oblique angle might be a contributing factor—the present study found no significant difference in the left anterior oblique angle between the right‐sided IVC group and the left‐sided IVC group. In addition to angle measurement using preoperative CT, intraoperative angle verification using a pigtail catheter may further enhance the safety and success rate of the procedure.

Completing the procedure with fewer deployments is clinically relevant to procedural safety. One of the most concerning periprocedural complications associated with leadless pacemaker implantation is pericardial effusion. It has been reported that the risk of pericardial effusion increases with the number of deployments. In high‐risk patients, the incidence of pericardial effusion was 1.5% with a single deployment, 9.3% with two deployments, 7.3% with three to five deployments, and as high as 22% with six or more deployments [11]. Thus, detecting a patient factor associated with multiple deployments would be of great use to provide a safe implantation procedure.

To date, no studies have investigated the association between the number of deployments and patient anatomical factors as an indicator of procedural difficulty. Post‐marketing surveillance data and short‐term outcomes of Micra implantation in Japan have shown that procedural success with a single deployment is achieved in approximately 60%–65% of the cases, while 35%–40% of them required two or more deployments [2, 12, 13]. These proportions were similar to the rate of multiple deployments observed in the present study population (41%) and in the right‐sided IVC group (36%). In contrast, the left‐sided IVC group demonstrated a higher proportion of cases requiring two or more deployments (67%), indicating that this group represents a more technically challenging patient population.

A possible factor attributable to the higher risk of multiple deployment was tricuspid regurgitation, since preprocedural transthoracic echocardiography revealed that the left‐sided IVC group had significantly higher TRPG than the right‐sided IVC group (mean 35 vs. 26 mmHg) while no significant right heart chamber enlargement was observed in the left‐sided IVC group. It is possible that significant tricuspid regurgitation compromises the stability of the delivery catheter, making it more difficult to maintain firm contact with the target site. However, the association between the IVC location and multiple deployment remained significant even after adjusting for TRPG, indicating the IVC location as an independent predictor of multiple deployments.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective single‐center observational study with a small sample size, especially the left‐sided IVC group (n = 15), with the multivariable model including the limited number of covariates. This inevitably resulted in wide confidence intervals and could cause overfitting and potentially unstable effect estimates. Thus, our findings should be interpreted with caution and regarded as hypothesis‐generating rather than definitive. The study showed no large difference in the risk of pericardial effusion between the two groups, although an incremental number of deployments was reportedly associated with pericardial effusion due to the implantation of a leadless pacemaker [9]. A future study involving a larger number of patients, operators, and institutions may be warranted to improve the generalizability of the findings. Second, cardiac CT would have been preferable for a more precise assessment of the IVC position. However, plain CT (and occasionally contrast‐enhanced CT) is routinely performed to evaluate the entire venous access route; thus, obtaining the information on the IVC location from such imaging tests would be less invasive for patients. Third, while the primary endpoint in the present study is defined as the number of deployments, this measure—which is intended to assess the difficulty of the procedure—may be influenced by various factors. The difficulty may vary depending on the operator's skill and experience, as well as the condition of the myocardium where the device is placed.

Conclusions

The left‐sided IVC was associated with multiple deployments of a leadless pacemaker and preoperative CT was useful in assessing the procedural difficulty.

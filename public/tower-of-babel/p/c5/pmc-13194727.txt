First-principles study of phenol sensing properties on β-arsenic phosphide monolayers
Authors: M. Vijay Balaji, R. Chandiramouli, R. Bhuvaneswari, V. Nagarajan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
For the very first time, we investigate the adsorption properties of β-arsenic phosphide (β-AsP) monolayer towards the phenolic compounds using first-principles calculations. Firstly, the structural and electronic features of the β-AsP monolayer are studied. The band structure, projected density of states, charge transfer, adsorption energy, recovery time, and work function are estimated for the different phenolic compounds, such as phenol, methyl phenol, dimethyl phenol, chlorophenol, and nitrophenol, adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer. All the compounds are physisorbed on the β-AsP monolayer surface. Especially, the phenol and chlorophenol adsorb significantly, as inferred from the variation in the band gap, work function, and recovery time results. Furthermore, the influence of external biaxial strain on phenol adsorption on the β-AsP monolayers is explored. We suggest that the β-AsP monolayer is a potential candidate for the detection of phenol and chlorophenol.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46191-9.

Introduction

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) have become a crucial part of air pollution in recent years owing to their significant impact on human health and the atmosphere1. Emissions of VOCs have grown to be a significant cause of air pollution, particularly in urban and industrialised areas where VOC concentrations are quite high2,3. The release of contaminated wastewater into the environment from home, industrial, and agricultural processes results in the presence of phenolic chemicals in water bodies as well as in air environment. Moreover, industries such as petrochemicals, textile colouring and dyeing, and polymer manufacturing processes frequently produce contaminated water4,5. Phenols are now considered to be a major source of pollution due to their long-lasting effects and toxicity in the environment6. Phenolic molecules are particularly likely to be disregarded among them. Permitting their release might pose a risk to public health as it could have serious effects on aquatic life, soil, and the natural environment. On other hand, the high consumption of phenolic compounds can cause major harm to humans as well as to the environment7,8. For instance, consumption of phenolic compounds may result in severe liver failure, digestive distress, nausea, vomiting, and even death9,10. Hence, it is very important to develop a sensor with high accuracy and high sensitivity for the protection of human health as well as the environment.

The rapid development of nanotechnology over the last ten years has led to the broad use of nanomaterials in a variety of applications. The study of two-dimensional materials such as graphene11, transition metal dichalcogenides12, and hexagonal boron nitride13 has attracted a lot of attention owing to their distinct physical properties and application in nano-electronics and nano-sensors14–16. Researchers are actively looking for new 2D materials of other elements in a variety of forms as a result of the production of graphene. Recently, black phosphorus was mechanically exfoliated to create phosphorene, a monolayer of black phosphorus17. Furthermore, Zhang et al. successfully prepared atomic-layered blue phosphorus on Au (111) by molecular beam epitaxy18. In addition to phosphorene, several other monolayers are theoretically predicted, such as arsenene19, phosphorene20, nitrogene21, antimonene22, and bismuthene23, that could be stable. These reports have encouraged research interest in group VA-VA bilayer and heterostructures. Particularly, the structures of group-V binary compounds that resemble black and blue phosphorene attracted great attention owing to their unique properties. Suyang et al. reported that blue phosphorene is a potential candidate for a VOC sensor24. Kou et al. suggested that black phosphorene is a promising material for the detection of NOx molecules25. Owing to exceptional gas sensitivity, it is possible to investigate more phosphorene relatives. Recently, the α-AsP monolayer was successfully synthesised. It has a distinctive structure that is akin to black phosphorene26,27. Guo et al. studied the adsorption of NO2 gas on the α-AsP monolayer with a non-metal doped system using first-principles calculation28. Previously, our research group reported different allotropes of group VA and group VA-VA monolayers, which are effectively utilized for the detection of various toxic pollutants using the density functional theory (DFT) method29–32. In the present work, the sensing properties of β-AsP monolayer towards the phenolic compounds such as phenol, methyl phenol, dimethyl phenol, chloro phenol, and nitrophenol are explored at ambient conditions.

Computational methods

In this work, the interaction between the various phenol molecules and the β-AsP monolayer is studied by the DFT method. All the calculations are performed with the Quantum-ATK package33 based on the linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) method with norm-conserving pseudopotential34. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional was utilized35 for the structural optimization. The hybrid-GGA with Heyd – Scuseria - Ernzerhof (HSE06)36 exchange correlation functional is used for analysis of band structure and other non-self-consistent field (NSCF) calculations. Besides, van der Waals (vdW) correction was applied using Grimme’s DFT-D337 approach to account for long-range dispersion interactions. The β-AsP monolayer built with a (4 × 4) supercell contains 16 As atoms and 16 P atoms. During the geometry optimisation, the convergence requirements are 0.01 eV/Å for force on each atom and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{10}^{-5}$$\end{document} eV for energy. Furthermore, Monkhorst-Pack scheme38 is used to sample the k-point mesh as 3 × 3 × 1 for self-consistent field (SCF) calculation, such as optimization and finding the total energy. Then, 9 × 9 × 1 k-point sampling is used for the NSCF calculations, like band structure and projected density of states (PDOS). To exclude the field effect of neighbouring layers, the vacuum distance is kept as 20 Å. Additionally, the thermal stability at ambient temperature (300 K) and elevated temperature (500 K) was investigated using ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation with a total simulation time of 11.5 ps with 2 fs time steps.

Results and discussion

Structural and electronic properties of pristine β-AsP monolayer.

The pristine β-AsP monolayer belongs to the hexagonal crystal system, as shown in Fig. 1. The calculated lattice constants of the β-AsP monolayer is a = b = 3.46 Å, and the bond length between As-P is found to be d = 2.409 Å. Moreover, the attained lattice parameter values in the present work are in good agreement with previously reported work39. The confirmation of material stability is a necessity for the practical application of the computationally predicted two-dimensional materials. The structural and thermodynamic stabilities of the β-AsP monolayer are validated by phonon spectra, cohesive-formation energy, and ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation, respectively. The simulation time step was changed to 2 fs, and the total simulation time was increased to 11.5 ps. The plot makes it evident that the system stabilises around 3 ps, and the energy variation between 3 and 11.5 ps is minimal, indicating high thermal stability at 300 K as well as at 500 K. Next, the electronic band structure of β-AsP monolayer is calculated on selected high symmetry k-path of BZ (Γ-M-K-Γ) with HSE06 functional to understand its electronic properties. The computed band structure elucidates that the β-AsP monolayer possesses an indirect band gap with a value of 2.579 eV. The proposed β-AsP monolayer exhibits a semiconducting nature that can be employed as a chemical nano-sensor. In order to verify the electronic states for electrons to occupy in the system, projected density of states (PDOS) maps42–44 are used. Figure 3 shows the band structure and PDOS maps with the predicted band gap and related peaks emerging in those energy levels. The s-orbitals make very little contribution to the total DOS (TDOS). Evidently, the PDOS reveals that the p-orbitals of phosphorus and p- & d-orbitals of arsenic atoms overlap each other and make p-d orbital hybridization, which controls the band gap and other electronic and optical properties. Hence, β-AsP monolayer can be employed as a biosensor, hazardous gas removal medium, nanoelectronic devices, and chemical sensors due to its semiconducting nature, structural, and dynamical stability. In addition, we performed the electron density analysis using the electron density difference (EDD) diagram as shown in Fig. 4. The blue colour hue indicates the electron deficit region, while the red colour hue indicates the surplus of electrons. Further, the red colour gradient (electron excess) is present throughout the monolayer, causing target molecules to get adsorbed easily45,46.

Adsorption studies of phenols on β-AsP monolayer

At first, the target molecules phenol, methyl phenol, dimethyl phenol, nitrophenol, and chlorophenol are permitted to adsorb on different sites and orientations, including hollow, valley, bridge, top, and parallel sites of the β-AsP monolayer. Among these adsorption sites, the parallel site is found to be optimum, and it is named as the global minima (least energy or stable) site. The remaining higher-order (unstable) sites are not considered in the present work. Fig. S1 shows the plot of adsorption energy with respect to the various possible sites. It is evident that the parallel site is found to be more stable site, since it attains the least adsorption energy value. Table 1 contains the corresponding adsorption energy and the distance between target molecules and base material for all possible adsorption sites. Moreover, all the molecules have aromatic rings that can easily adsorb parallel to the surface of the β-AsP monolayer. Figure 5 shows the stable structure with the ground state energy for each complex structure. The interaction of phenol, methyl phenol, dimethylphenol, chlorophenol, and nitrophenol parallel to the β-AsP monolayer is mentioned as complex-p1, complex-p2, complex-p3, complex-p4, and complex-p5, respectively, as displayed in Fig. 5.

The computed values of adsorption energy fall in the range of less than − 1.00 eV, which indicates the adsorption as physisorption. Moreover, the electron localization function (ELF) is used to elucidate the adsorption mechanism of phenols on the β-AsP monolayer. Basically, the electron localization function is employed to depict the electron localization and delocalization between the substrate and gas or vapour molecules50. The ELF values of 0.0, 0.50, and 1.0 correspond to regions of low charge density, completely delocalized electrons, and fully localized electrons, respectively. As shown in Fig. 6, the ELF values in the intermediate region between molecules and substrate of all the studied complexes are less than 0.5, approaching zero. This indicates a weak interaction, primarily governed by van der Waals forces, between the phenol molecules and the β-AsP monolayer. Therefore, the interaction between the molecules and the β-AsP surface can be categorized as physisorption.

Electronic properties of β-AsP monolayer upon phenol adsorption

To further strengthen the adsorption properties of the β-AsP monolayer towards phenolic molecules, the variation in electronic properties of the base material upon the adsorption of phenols is studied with the support of band structure and PDOS spectrum, as illustrated in Fig. 8(a) and 8(b). Obviously, the band gap of the β-AsP monolayer gets transformed due to the interaction of target phenolic compounds on the stable adsorption sites. The corresponding energy gap values of the complex systems p1, p2, p3, p4, and p5 are 2.102 eV, 2.495 eV, 2.567 eV, 2.352 eV, and 2.577 eV, which are modified from the pristine β-AsP monolayer57–59. Interestingly, the new states evolved in the valence band region during the adsorption of phenol and chlorophenol on the base material. It infers that the interaction of phenol and chlorophenol gives significant changes in the band gap of the β-AsP monolayer.

The basic idea behind a gas sensor is to transform the adsorption signal into an electrical signal that can be detected, often a shift in electrical conductivity60. Thus, the significant change in band structure indicates that the β-AsP monolayer is the right candidate for phenol and chlorophenol detection. To authorize the above results, the difference in electronic states in distinct orbitals in PDOS upon the adsorption of phenols on the substrate must be investigated. The PDOS-maps show a notable peak emerged in valence band maximum (VBM) owing to the interaction of phenol and chlorophenol on β-AsP monolayer, as exposed in Fig. 8. The overlapping of orbitals between the closest atoms of phenol or chlorophenol and β-AsP monolayer is clearly visible.

Fig. 8(a) Band structure and PDOS of phenol, methyl phenol, and dimethyl phenol adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer. (b) Band structure and PDOS of chlorophenol and nitrophenol adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer.

(a) Band structure and PDOS of phenol, methyl phenol, and dimethyl phenol adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer. (b) Band structure and PDOS of chlorophenol and nitrophenol adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer.

Work function variation of β-AsP monolayer upon phenol adsorption

The work function (Φ) is a crucial factor that reflects the characteristics of field emission. It is the minimal energy necessary for an electron within a material to escape into the vacuum71. The surface sensitivity of two-dimensional nanosheets is significantly influenced by their surface potential, which can be determined by modifications in the work function (Φ). Furthermore, it is known that the conductivity of the systems, particularly 2D nanosheets is closely correlated with the variations in the work function72. Thus, the work function of these complex systems is calculated by the following relation (8).8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\varPhi\:={E}_{vac}-{E}_{F}$$\end{document}

In this case, Φ, Evac, and EF refer to the work function, electrostatic potential at infinity, and the Fermi level energy, respectively. The work function plot for pristine and phenol adsorbed on the β-AsP monolayer is illustrated in Fig. 9. The calculated value of work function for the pristine and complex p1, p2, p3, p4, and p5 is 4.77 eV, 4.86 eV, 4.69 eV, 4.82 eV, 4.88 eV, and 4.87 eV, respectively. Notably, the work function of the isolated β-AsP monolayer increases by the adsorption of phenol, dimethylphenol, chlorophenol, and nitrophenol. Besides, when the material is exposed to methylphenol, the work function value decreases. Moreover, based on the type of gas molecule to be adsorbed on the surface, these changes (increase or decrease) in work function may occur. Thus, the adsorption of all phenol molecules makes a change in the work function. Among all phenols discussed, the sensitivity of chlorophenol on the β-AsP monolayer is observed to be prominent.

Effect of external strain on β-AsP monolayer on phenol adsorption

In practice, applying external strain is an effective strategy to transform the electronic attributes of 2D materials74. Besides, applying strain effectively modifies the molecular adsorption behaviour as well. Lu et al. recently noticed that biaxial strain ranging from − 5% to 5% effectively regulated the electrical and optical characteristics, and it is inferred that proper strain enhanced fibrous red phosphorene’s capacity towards photocatalytic water splitting75. Motivated by Lu’s research, biaxial strain between − 4% and 4% was applied to the β-AsP monolayer to comprehend the impact on sensing abilities. For the complex p1 system under applied biaxial strain, the adsorption energy decreases to −0.932 eV. Concluding remarks

To sum up, we used first-principles calculations to investigate the structural, electronic, and sensing properties of the β-AsP monolayer towards phenol, methylphenol, dimethylphenol, chlorophenol and nitrophenol. Initially, the stability of the β-AsP monolayer is confirmed by cohesive energy, AIMD, and phonon band spectrum. Furthermore, from the band structure and PDOS spectrum, the semiconducting nature of the β-AsP monolayer is determined with the energy gap of 2.579 eV. From the value of adsorption energy and ELF plots, the adsorption mechanism of all the considered phenols on the β-AsP monolayer is found to be in the physisorption regime. In particular, it is observed that charge transfer falls between 0.1 e and 0.184 e. Notably, the adsorption of phenol and chlorophenol causes a significant change in the energy gap, showing the higher sensitivity, which is also supported by the work function results. Also, the adsorption behaviour of phenol on the β-AsP monolayer can be effectively controlled by compressive strain. Based on the results, it is evident that the β-AsP monolayer acts as a potential candidate for the detection of phenol and chlorophenol molecules.

Evaluating the effectiveness of problem-based learning combined with six thinking hats in oral pathology education
Authors: Reena Das, Pratibha Ramani, Gheena Sukumaran, Abilasha Ramasubramanian, Deepak Nallasamy Veeraiyan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn Oral Pathology, traditional slide-based teaching methods often focus on passive observation which may not completely ignite the pathology resident’s critical thinking and diagnostic capabilities. Hence we designed and evaluated a modified teaching method which was integrating Problem-Based Learning (PBL) with Six Thinking Hats (STH) methodology in enhancing diagnostic skills in the assessment of diagnostically challenging cases.MethodsA cohort of n = 15 Oral Pathology residents were included for the study. Their diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, and knowledge retention were assessed through traditional slide-based teaching and a PBL integrated with STH approach. The PBL-STH framework engaged residents in a multi-perspective analysis of clinical data, histopathological features, and immunohistochemical markers (e.g., CD20, CD138), using structured group discussions based on the six thinking hats to foster collaborative decision-making.ResultsDiagnostic accuracy significantly improved with the PBL-STH method, with 86.6% of residents accurately diagnosing DLBCL compared to 40% in traditional teaching. Critical thinking and risk analysis were markedly higher with PBL-STH (93.3% vs. 30%), and peer collaboration was observed in all PBL-STH sessions. Additionally, knowledge retention after two weeks was better among residents trained with the PBL-STH approach (90% vs. 55%), and overall satisfaction was significantly higher (93% vs. 50%).ConclusionThe PBL integrated with STH approach offers substantial benefits over traditional teaching methods, improving diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, engagement, and knowledge retention in Oral Pathology. This innovative methodology encourages deeper analysis and collaboration, proving to be a highly effective educational tool for complex diagnostic cases especially in oral pathology which is the backbone for treatment, follow up, prognosis and quality of life of patients Also, implementation of PBL-STH in medical and dental curricula could enhance clinical reasoning and diagnostic skills across specialties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-06554-6.Keywords: Problem based learning (PBL), Six thinking hats (STH), Oral pathology, Diagnostic accuracy, Diffuse large B- cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Round cell neoplasms

Background

Dental education has undergone significant transition, evolving from vast focus on departmental instructions and case- based teaching learning to innovative technology driven curricula offering students a practical understanding of patient care [1]. In dentistry, Oral Pathology (OP) teaching methods vary across institutions based on faculty approaches and curricular structures. While some rely on passive slide observation, which may limit critical thinking and problem-solving skills, others incorporate interactive strategies to enhance analytical learning and engagement. Also, poor slide quality can hinder understanding of subtle histopathological features. While such conventional methods provide foundational knowledge of histopathological features, they fail to engage students in the multidimensional problem-solving needed for real-world clinical practice. Over the past few years, teaching in OP has evolved from didactic learning to case based learning and research oriented approach with emphasis on developing AI- digital slide teaching and learning thereby involving a larger audience [2].

While, technological advancement has incorporated use of virtual reality simulations, digital imaging, 3D printing and digital tools like intraoral scanners [3]; curricula advancement, on the other hand, has integrated teaching with strategies such as Problem Based Learning (PBL) emphasizing on student centric group discussion and educational environment that can develop critical thinking skills [4]. PBL doesn’t only enhance technical proficiency but also play a pivotal role in fostering essential cognitive skills such as critical thinking, decision making, and clinical reasoning.

By integrating PBL into Oral Pathology education, students are encouraged to actively participate in group discussions and collaborate on solving complex cases. This not only enhances their diagnostic and reasoning abilities but also allows them to correlate clinical symptoms with histopathological findings, making the learning process much more clinical-oriented. The peer-to-peer learning environment further strengthens their understanding as they critique and refine each other’s perspectives, enhancing their ability to approach cases with a more comprehensive view. STH- PBL has been immensely implemented mode of teaching and discussion in medical and dental education, including the core subject of pathophysiology, connecting basic science curricula with advanced clinical courses [5, 6].

Six thinking hats (STH) developed by Edward De Bono is a problem solving and decision making tool that encourages looking at a case from multiple perspectives. The tool involves using six colour hats illustrating six different perspectives [7]. The six colors of the hats, viz., white hat (facts and information), red hat (emotions and intuitions), black hat (caution and critical thinking), yellow hat (optimism and benefits), green hat (creativity and innovation), and blue hat (process and control) can be used for refining the thought process of dentists into comprehensive and unified framework. In oral pathology teaching, combining the Six Thinking Hats (STH) approach with problem-based learning (PBL) can create a more structured and balanced learning experience, enhancing critical thinking and decision-making. By donning different “hats,” students explore the case from multiple cognitive angles, leading to a more holistic diagnosis [8]. This study hypothesizes that integrating PBL with the STH methodology will significantly improve diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, engagement, and knowledge retention compared to traditional slide-based teaching. To test this, we evaluated cases of round cell neoplasms using traditional methods and Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL) using the PBL-STH approach, assessing their effectiveness through discussions, feedback, and performance metrics [9].

The integration of STH with PBL is supported by constructivist learning theories, which emphasize active engagement, knowledge construction, and experiential learning. Constructivism, as proposed by Piaget and Vygotsky, suggests that learners develop a deeper understanding of concepts through social interaction and reflective thinking [10]. The STH method aligns with Bloom’s Taxonomy, particularly in fostering higher-order cognitive skills such as analysis, evaluation, and synthesis, which are essential for clinical reasoning. Studies have shown that STH promotes structured problem-solving by guiding students to systematically process information from multiple perspectives, reducing cognitive biases and improving diagnostic accuracy [11]. When combined with PBL, this approach enhances metacognitive awareness, encourages self-directed learning, and strengthens decision-making abilities, making it particularly effective in medical and dental education. The use of STH within PBL ensures that students engage in both convergent and divergent thinking, leading to a more comprehensive and clinically relevant learning experience.

In our study, we evaluated cases of round cell neoplasms using the traditional slide based teaching method and Diffuse Large B- Cell lymphoma (DLBCL) using a Problem based learning approach (PBL) integrated with Six Thinking Hats (STH) methodology. The objective was to compare the effectiveness of these methods in classroom discussions, feedback and assessment. The participants of the study were Oral Pathology residents, who have completed basic competency modules. Evaluations were conducted through student surveys, viva examinations, discussions, and written exam scores [9]. All postgraduate residents in Oral Pathology participated in both teaching approaches to assess their impact on learning outcomes. This study represents a novel application of PBL integrated with STH in the histopathological assessment of complex cases, making it the first of its kind in Oral Pathology.

Methods

Resident enrolment and study procedure

The study was conducted with postgraduate residents (PGs) from the Oral Pathology Division at Saveetha Dental College and Hospitals, Chennai, India.

All the 15 postgraduate residents from the Oral Pathology division of the academic Year 2024, at Saveetha Dental College and Hospitals, participated in the newly designed Six Hats Oral Pathology Curriculum as part of their quarterly module. After giving informed consent, the students first discussed the cases using traditional oral pathology teaching methodology, followed by case analysis by PBL integrated with STH approach, receiving innovative guidance from the moderators. This cycle of case discussions was conducted three times to reinforce learning and application. The cases were allocated to the residents along with the clinical details. The allocation of slides was conducted in a blinded manner by consultants, ensuring that residents were unaware of which cases they would be analyzing beforehand. This process allowed for a direct comparison of their diagnostic performance under both methods, ensuring that differences in outcomes were attributable to the teaching approach rather than variability in case complexity. The moderators were faculty members from the Oral Pathology department, each with a minimum of 15 years of experience in teaching and histopathology slide interpretation.

Traditional slide teaching method

For the study, round cell neoplasms, a histopathologically intriguing group of lesions, was selected. Among these, a case of round cell neoplasm - plasmablastic lymphoma was assigned to the residents’ batch. The slides were viewed, discussed, and diagnosed by the residents, using projection microscope, and reported through active discussion with input and guidance by oral pathology consultants acting as moderators—along with valuable additional information from oral surgeons and oral medicine specialists. During the discussions, patients records, clinical photographs and radiographs were also taken into consideration. The interactive sessions were conducted for two weeks, as part of the scheduled academic curriculum, with each session lasting approximately 2 h. The list of questions for assessment involved the characteristic clinical features, immunohistochemical markers employed, helping in categorization of the neoplasm according to the Revised European American Lymphoma (REAL) classification.

PBL integrated STH method

For the PBL integrated with Six Thinking Hats (STH) study, a diagnostically challenging case of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), categorized under the umbrella of round cell neoplasms, was selected.

The assessment questions included the evaluation of characteristic clinical features and the immunohistochemical markers used, aiding in the categorization of the neoplasm according to the REAL (Revised European-American Lymphoma) classification. The interactive sessions were conducted for two weeks, as part of the scheduled academic curriculum, with each session lasting approximately 2 h. The residents were seated together with the moderators to encourage open communication. Before the class began, they were provided with an overview and guidance on the PBL integrated STH method and the evaluation criteria. A table outlining the six thinking hats, along with their respective colors, along with questionnaire, was created by the authors exclusively for the study, and distributed at the start of the session (Supplementary Table 1). During the case students metaphorically “wore” the six thinking hats, in turn, exploring within the stipulated color framework of the STH. They examined the facts and figures, diagnostic data, slide reading, initial findings, medical history and lab results of the patient (white hat); expressed their individual intuitive diagnosis, mapped the provisional diagnosis and their primary histopathological impression (red hat), applied their knowledge rationally, recorded key points in favour of current provisional diagnosis (yellow hat), engaged in critical thinking about the potential risks and pitfalls in the diagnosis and treatment plan, considering alternative diagnosis and complications arising from it (black hat), brainstormed novel and unconventional approaches for diagnosis, additional ancillary tests like Immunohistochemistry, special stains and Flow Cytometry (green hat). Figure 1 demonstrates the comparative teaching methodologies and assessment strategies used for both the study. During the process, the post graduate residents had acquired knowledge on the case, subsequently having an informative peer to peer critique among fellow residents wherein a healthy argument was made for and against their diagnosis, finally inferring to a common consensus narrowing down to the final diagnosis and treatment plan with constructive inputs and guidance from the moderators (blue hat). Thus the blue hat focuses on a step by step approach to the process of diagnosis and arriving at the final definitive diagnosis.

This analysis was aimed to determine the statistical significance of the variations in performance across these two educational approaches. The overall performance of the residents were measured according to aspects such as diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, engagement and peer collaboration, knowledge retention and overall understanding of the respective cases through PBL- STH and traditional slide teaching method.

In order to evaluate the residents’perceptions and learning experiences, a structured questionnaire was administered at the end of the study. The questionnaire was designed to assess their self-reported improvements in diagnostic skills, critical thinking, engagement, and overall satisfaction with the teaching methodology. The questionnaire consisted of Likert-scale questions (ranging from “Strongly Disagree” to “Strongly Agree”) to capture qualitative feedback. The questionnaire underwent content and construct validity testing and pilot testing.

Results

Comparative evaluation of teaching methodologies

A cohort of 15 residents participated in this comparative evaluation between traditional method and PBL integrated STH method, where their diagnostic abilities were assessed using histopathological and immunohistochemical criteria aligned with the REAL classification of lymphomas.

The results of the individual evaluation of the residents based on parameters such as case based assessment, viva voce score, group discussions, written report and OSCE scores indicated a statistically significant difference in diagnostic performance, with residents demonstrating improved assessment skills in DLBCL when using the PBL-STH method compared to the traditional slide teaching approach for round cell neoplasms. Given the p-value of 0.0001, it was found that the PBL-STH method enhanced diagnostic accuracy and critical thinking in the evaluation of lymphomas.

Diagnostic accuracy and critical thinking

During the overall performance of residents, it was found that only 40% of those taught using traditional slide-based methods could accurately categorize the neoplasm based on the provided clinical and immunohistochemical data. In contrast, when the PBL-STH method was employed, diagnostic accuracy significantly improved. A total of 86.6% of the residents successfully identified the neoplasm as DLBCL, correlating histopathological observations with immunohistochemical markers (e.g., CD20, CD138). In traditional method, only 30% of the residents actively engaged in critical discussions regarding differential diagnoses or potential risks during the session. In PBL- STH, at the “Black Hat” phase, 93.3% of the residents critically evaluated potential pitfalls in the diagnosis, considered alternative neoplasms, and identified key risks in the treatment plan.

Peer engagement and group discussions

Engagement of the peers during traditional sessions were found to be limited. Owing to the didactic learning environment with minimal interaction, the peer-to-peer discussions were sparse. Only 35% of the residents contributed actively to group discussions. However, the PBL-STH approach led to significantly higher engagement levels. All 15 residents actively participated in group discussions, contributing to the diagnostic process by wearing metaphorical thinking “hats” in turns. Peer-to-peer critique was observed in 100% of the sessions, and healthy debates during the “Red Hat” and “Black Hat” phases facilitated a dynamic learning environment that encouraged collaborative problem-solving.

Discussion

In this research work, we propose combining six thinking hats in Oral Pathology division of Dentistry into PBL curriculum, to develop the cognitive process of residents to critically evaluate cases from multiple angles, for better diagnosis, treatment and patient care. Teaching oral pathology slide cases can be challenging due to the complexity of diagnosing and interpreting histopathological features [12–14].

PBL encourages critical thinking and problem-solving, allowing students to work through real-life cases in a collaborative environment [8]. It helps students build diagnostic skills by analyzing pathology slides in a step-by-step process.

However, at many instances, using PBL technique alone as a teaching methodology has resulted as an redundant, prejudiced and partial argument to any case, lessening tutorial quality, owing to the lack of holistic thinking framework [5]. STH provides a framework for thinking from different perspectives, making it easier for students to break down complex cases. Each hat represents a distinct way of thinking, such as focusing on facts, emotions, potential solutions, or challenges, helping residents evaluate oral pathology cases holistically. Incorporating this structured methodology into Oral Pathology education could help residents develop a deeper and more critical understanding of tumor diagnosis, such as in complex cases of Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL) and other round cell neoplasms. By fostering diverse thinking patterns, students are not only learning the technical aspects of pathology but also gaining insights into clinical reasoning and patient management, which is critical for preparing them to be future oral pathologists.

The traditional slide- based teaching, with 40% of the residents being able to categorize the lesion contrasted significantly with 86.6% being able to categorize the lesion in the PBL- STH approach. The integrated approach facilitated the synthesis of clinical and laboratory data, resulting in a more comprehensive and accurate diagnostic approach. With respect to critical thinking and problem solving skills, most students relied solely on slide observation and minimal consideration of alternative diagnoses or clinical complications. The conventional teaching method saw 30% of the residents engaging in critical discussions contrasting with 93.3% critically evaluating and applying their knowledge in correctly identifying the lesion eliminating close differential diagnoses in the ‘black hat’ phase.

The traditional sessions saw minimal engagement of peers with only 35% of the residents contributing actively to group discussions contrasting with the PBL-STH approach leading to significantly higher engagement levels. All 15 residents actively participated in group discussions, contributing to the diagnostic process by wearing metaphorical thinking “hats” in turns. Peer-to-peer critique was observed in 100% of the sessions, and healthy debates during the “Red Hat” and “Black Hat” phases facilitated a dynamic learning environment that encouraged collaborative problem-solving.

When comparing the traditional method to the PBL-STH approach, a much better comprehension, critical evaluation and retention were noted in all spheres namely information gathering, diagnosis, treatment plan, critical thinking, collaboration, peer feedback and final outcome. The residents experienced significant differences in the critical thinking, collaboration and peer feedback phases. Engagement and peer collaboration also peaked.

Residents who underwent PBL-STH training demonstrated better retention and recall of key diagnostic principles. In post-session evaluations conducted as written tests after a span of two weeks, 90% of the residents successfully recalled the clinical features and immunohistochemical markers necessary for diagnosing DLBCL, indicating that the interactive and structured nature of the STH method supported long-term knowledge retention. Resident feedback indicated higher satisfaction with the PBL-STH method compared to the traditional approach. Survey results showed that 93% of the residents rated the PBL-STH sessions as “highly effective” in enhancing their diagnostic skills and clinical reasoning abilities, compared to only 50% for traditional slide-based sessions. Residents appreciated the interactive nature, critical thinking stimulation, and peer feedback integrated into the STH framework.

The current study aimed to assess whether integrating Problem-Based Learning (PBL) with Six Thinking Hats (STH) enhances diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, engagement, and knowledge retention compared to traditional slide-based teaching in Oral Pathology. The findings confirm that PBL-STH significantly improved diagnostic precision, critical analysis, and peer collaboration, fostering active learning and deeper clinical reasoning. Residents trained with PBL-STH retained knowledge more effectively and demonstrated higher diagnostic confidence. These results highlight the need for a shift from passive slide-based teaching to interactive, structured methodologies in Oral Pathology education.

Overall, it was summarized that the traditional slide-based method provides a foundational understanding of histopathology but lacks the depth needed for complex diagnostic scenarios and critical thinking. Whereas, PBL integrated with STH is clearly superior for comprehensive learning, fostering critical thinking, peer collaboration, and a deeper understanding of clinical cases. It results in more accurate diagnoses and personalized treatment plans, making it more effective for advanced medical and dental education. Despite the promising outcomes of integrating PBL with the Six Thinking Hats methodology, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The small sample size and single-institution setting may restrict the generalizability of the findings, necessitating larger, multi-center studies for broader validation. Additionally, the study primarily focused on Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL) and round cell neoplasms, limiting its applicability to other complex oral pathology cases. While short-term knowledge retention was assessed, long-term retention and clinical application remain unexplored. Future studies should incorporate standardized assessment tools, longitudinal follow-ups, and integration with AI-driven digital pathology to further enhance learning outcomes. Expanding this methodology to a wider range of pathologies could also provide a more comprehensive understanding of its impact on diagnostic accuracy and critical thinking in oral pathology education.

Conclusions

The integration of Problem-Based Learning (PBL) with the Six Thinking Hats (STH) methodology enhances diagnostic accuracy, critical thinking, engagement, and knowledge retention in Oral Pathology, particularly for complex cases like Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma. Compared to traditional methods, PBL-STH fosters deeper analysis, peer collaboration, and comprehensive diagnosis formulation. Residents trained with this approach demonstrated improved knowledge retention, active participation, and a better understanding of both diagnostic and therapeutic aspects. Given its success, the PBL-STH method is a valuable educational strategy that should be further explored in medical and dental education.

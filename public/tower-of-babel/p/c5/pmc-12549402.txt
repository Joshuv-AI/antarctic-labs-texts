An overview of advanced nanocarrier systems for Ibrutinib delivery: overcoming pharmacokinetic barriers and enabling targeted cancer therapy
Authors: Akshay Shetty, Mahesha Keerikkadu, Pragathi Devanand Bangera, Vamshi Krishna Tippavajhala, Mahalaxmi Rathnanand
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIbrutinib (IBR), a covalent inhibitor of Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK), has transformed the treatment of B-cell malignancies like chronic lymphocytic leukemia, mantle cell lymphoma, and Waldenström's macroglobulinemia. With its clinical success, IBR is faced with enormous challenges like low aqueous solubility, low oral bioavailability, extensive first-pass metabolism, off-target toxicities, and resistance development. Nanotechnology-based drug delivery systems have been reported to be effective solutions for these issues. This review offers a comprehensive and critical examination of new trends in IBR-loaded nanocarriers, including PEGylated liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, dendrimers, solid lipid nanoparticles, nanostructured lipid carriers, and hybrid nanoplatforms. These nanocarriers showed improved drug solubility, prolonged circulation, controlled release, cancer-specific targeting, and reduced systemic toxicity. Emphasis on advanced approaches such as ligand-mediated targeting, stimuli-sensitive release, and co-delivery systems designed to optimize therapeutic effects and avoid resistance mechanisms. Preclinical models demonstrated improved bioavailability, improved tumor accumulation, and improved safety profiles of the IBR nanocarriers. This review covers the translational hurdles, regulatory aspects, and commercial tractability of nanocarrier-mediated inhibition of BTK. In summary, nanotechnology provides a revolutionary pathway for maximizing IBR therapy that could facilitate more efficient, safer, and targeted care for patients with hematologic cancers.Keywords: Ibrutinib, Bruton's tyrosine kinase, Nanocarriers, Chronic lymphocytic leukemia, Ligand-targeted delivery

Introduction

Ibrutinib (IBR) is a first-in-class, oral small molecule inhibitor of the pioneering drug class that selectively and irreversibly blocks Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK), a non-receptor tyrosine kinase implicated in B-cell receptor (BCR) signalling (Kara et al., 2025; Cameron and Sanford, 2014). BTK is critical for B-cell development, differentiation, proliferation, and survival (Tian et al., 2025). Aberrant BCR signalling in malignant B-cells supports uncontrolled proliferation and resistance to apoptosis (Patton and Woyach, 2024). IBR acts through the covalent binding to the cysteine-481 (Cys481) residue of the active site of BTK, inhibiting its kinase activity and downstream signalling pathways, including those mediated by NF-κB, ERK1/2, and PI3K/AKT (Ehiremen, 2021). The targeted inhibition interferes with the survival signals that maintain the cancerous B-cells, resulting in defective cell proliferation, decreased cytokine production, and enhanced apoptosis (Szklener et al., 2022). IBR is used to treat several B-cell malignancies, such as chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), and Waldenstrom's macroglobulinemia (WM), which are all approved by the US FDA. Because of its more widespread immunomodulatory action, IBR has also shown promise in a number of conditions, such as marginal zone lymphoma and even autoimmune disorders such as autoimmune cytopenias (autoimmune hemolytic anaemia and immune thrombocytopenia) (Buske et al., 2022; Cavalloni et al., 2021; Noy et al., 2020). IBR faces several pharmacokinetic and biopharmaceutical problems in addition to its clinical effectiveness (Azizuddin et al., 2024). These include limited oral bioavailability, rapid systemic clearance, extensive CYP3A4-mediated hepatic metabolism, and low aqueous solubility. All of these lead to suboptimal therapeutic concentrations, systemic toxicity, and drug resistance (Ran et al., 2022). Additionally, some patients experience treatment failure due to acquired BTK mutations and off-target activities (Miklos et al., 2025).

To counteract these limitations, nanotechnology-based drug delivery systems have been of particular interest. Advanced nanocarriers can solubilize IBR, protect it from metabolic degradation, and target its delivery to achieve optimal efficacy and lower toxicity (Mucha et al., 2024). These novel delivery systems are anticipated to enhance the drug's bioavailability, therapeutic index, and drug compliance and decrease the systemic toxicity and off-target interactions (Arseni et al., 2025). Encapsulation of IBR in nanoscale vehicles can permit the regulation of the physicochemical properties of the drug and bio-interactions. They include liposomes, polymeric nanospheres, hybrid nanoparticles, Carbon nanotubes, Solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs), and nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs) (Gavas et al., 2021). Due to their small size and modularity of the surface, nanocarriers have better advantages over conventional drug delivery techniques. Nanocarriers are able to encapsulate lipophilic drugs like IBR and improve solubility while avoiding premature enzymatic degradation of the drug (Sen et al., 2025). Nanocarriers can be made to deliver the drug in a controlled or stimulus-sensitive manner, prolonging circulation time and reducing peak-trough drug concentration fluctuations (Bhadran et al., 2025). One of the most powerful features of nanocarriers is their ability for targeted drug delivery. Through surface functionalization by ligands such as antibodies, peptides, or small molecules, nanoparticles can be directed towards cell types or organs, in this case, cancerous B-cells in the case of IBR therapy. Targeted delivery minimizes off-target effects and maximizes therapeutic efficacy (Hong et al., 2023; Patel et al., 2023b). In addition, nanoparticles may also be accumulated in tumor tissue passively by the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, which takes advantage of the leaky vasculature that is characteristic of tumors (Sharifi et al., 2022). The embedding of IBR into nanocarrier systems not only addresses its solubility and bioavailability issues but also opens up new possibilities to reduce systemic toxicity, bypass drug resistance, and enable site-specific delivery (Gvozdeva, 2025). Electrostatic antibody-inhibitor conjugates are a new receptor-mediated drug delivery platform for IBR that presents a method to evade the limitations of traditional therapy through the ability to specifically target tumors, effectively accumulate intracellularly, and significantly improve therapeutic efficacy while reducing first-pass metabolism (Faust et al., 2022). These approaches underlie the principles of precision medicine by facilitating the administration of effective and customized treatment protocols. With further research in nanomedicine, the integration of IBR with advanced nanotechnologies has tremendous potential for improving clinical outcomes and expanding its application to a wider range of diseases (Behzadmehr et al., 2024).

This review emphasises the global research on recent advancements in nanoparticle-based delivery systems for IBR, with a focus on its pharmacokinetic limitations, including low solubility and systemic toxicity. The review focuses on various nanocarrier platforms, including polymeric nanoparticles, lipid-based systems, and hybrid nanostructures, and examines their physicochemical properties and drug release profiles. This article discusses the preclinical and future clinical proof for the use of IBR-loaded nanoparticles in augmenting therapeutic efficacy, improving targeted delivery, and reducing side effects.

Mechanism of Action and Biopharmaceutical challenges of Ibrutinib

IBR is a highly active, covalent BTK inhibitor that has revolutionized the treatment of several B-cell malignancies, including CLL, MCL, and WM (Barragan et al., 2024). Through irreversible inhibition of the BTK active site, IBR interferes with downstream signals mediating cell survival, proliferation, and migration, leading to apoptosis in malignant B-cells (Karimi et al., 2025). Despite its proven therapeutic efficacy, the clinical advantage of IBR is marred by various physicochemical and biopharmaceutical constraints. These are low aqueous solubility, extensive first-pass metabolism, low oral bioavailability, and dose-limiting toxicity, all of which decrease therapeutic efficacy and adversely affect patient compliance (Charalambous et al., 2018). IBR is a Biopharmaceutics Classification System (BCS) Class II drug, meaning that it has low aqueous solubility with high membrane permeability. The juxtaposition tends to lead to dissolution rate-limited absorption, where drug absorption extent and rate are controlled mainly by its capacity to dissolve in gastrointestinal fluids (Pan et al., 2024). The water solubility of IBR has been found to be less than 1 μg/mL, particularly under the condition of physiological pH between 5.0 and 7.5. The low solubility severely impairs dissolution within the gastrointestinal tract, especially under fasted conditions, which is one of the factors towards its poor and variable oral bioavailability. IBR possesses a log P value of around 3.97, indicating its lipophilicity (Schwarzbich and Witzens-Harig, 2014). Though lipophilicity facilitates membrane permeation, it also leads to strong protein binding with plasma and entrapment in the lipophilic compartments of the body, thus lowering free drug availability (Jiang et al., 2023). Also, the crystalline nature of IBR decreases its dissolution rate further, complicating and making formulation development more challenging. IBR is subjected to extensive first-pass metabolism by the cytochrome P450 enzyme system, notably CYP3A4, in the intestines and liver (Woyach et al., 2024). The metabolic process transforms the parent drug into several inactive or less active metabolites. Extensive metabolism results in significant pre-systemic clearance, thus significantly decreasing the amount of unchanged drug entering systemic circulation (Shi et al., 2020). The CYP3A4 involvement also makes IBR prone to drug-drug interactions, particularly with drugs that are strong inducers or inhibitors of the enzyme. Co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ketoconazole has the potential to heavily increase systemic exposure, increasing toxicity risk, whereas inducers such as rifampin have the potential to decrease plasma levels, which can result in failure of therapy (de Jong et al., 2018; Fancher and Pappacena, 2020). High dosing, which is a consequence of the unsatisfactory biopharmaceutical profile of the drug, leads to dose-dependent adverse effects and toxicity. IBR therapy has several adverse effects, some of them severe enough to require dose reduction or interruption. Diarrhoea, bleeding events, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, fatigue, and infection are common toxicities. One of the significant dose-limiting toxicities is bleeding, which is attributed to IBR off-target activity against other kinases, including Tec family kinases that play a role in platelet signalling (Pacoureau et al., 2021; Paydas, 2019). Likewise, cardiac arrhythmias, most notably atrial fibrillation, are also becoming a major concern, particularly in older patients or those with prior cardiovascular disease. IBR immunosuppressive activity, resulting from its inhibition of B-cell function, makes the patient susceptible to opportunistic infections such as fungal and viral pathogens. These are safety concerns that underline the necessity for better delivery routes that can reduce the effective dose without affecting therapeutic effects (Boriani et al., 2023).

IBR forms a covalent and irreversible binding to an individual cysteine residue (Cys481) within the active site of BTK. Through covalent formation, permanent inactivation of BTK's kinase function is achieved, thus inhibiting downstream signalling (Estupiñán et al., 2021). It is very selective in inhibition, although IBR might inhibit other kinases that have similar cysteine residues, including ITK (Interleukin-2-inducible T-cell kinase), EGFR (Epidermal growth factor receptor), and TEC, potentially leading to therapeutic as well as off-target toxicities (Christensen et al., 2022). The BCR signalling pathway starts with antigen recognition and moves to phosphorylation events mediated by spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK), followed by activation of BTK. Activated BTK phosphorylates phospholipase C gamma 2 (PLCγ2), which initiates the formation of secondary messengers that activate signalling pathways such as NF-κB, MAPK, and PI3K/AKT (Zheng et al., 2022). All these pathways play pivotal roles in B-cell proliferation, survival, migration, and cytokine production. Through inhibition of BTK, IBR successfully interferes with such downstream pro-survival signals, leading to apoptosis and decreased proliferation of neoplastic B-cells (Davis et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022). Apart from its direct cytotoxicity, IBR also targets the tumor microenvironment, which is essential in delivering survival cues to cancer B-cells. It blocks chemokine-dependent homing and adhesion of B-cells to stromal cells in lymphoid organs and bone marrow niches. In response, IBR treatment induces transient lymphocytosis because of the mobilization of malignant B-cells out of tissue compartments into peripheral circulation, a pharmacodynamic effect that must not be confused with disease progression (Rey-Barroso et al., 2024). The reversible character of BTK inhibition permits IBR to exert a long-lasting pharmacodynamic effect despite declining plasma drug levels. This supports once-daily dosing and accounts for prolonged suppression of BCR signalling and persistent clinical response with ongoing treatment (Forestieri et al., 2022). Resistance has developed in some patients, most commonly resulting from point mutations within the BTK binding site, which preclude covalent bond formation and reactivate BTK activity (Fig. 1). These involve a gain-of-function mutation in downstream effectors such as PLCγ2, causing BCR pathway reactivation despite BTK inhibition (Chirino et al., 2023).

Nanocarrier systems for ibrutinib delivery

Chitosan-based nanoparticles

Nanocarrier systems are a promising approach for improving solubility, stability, tumor targeting, and controlled release of IBR (Table 1). Nanotechnology provides an opportunity to enhance drug bioavailability and circulation time, with the added benefit that active targeting is available, and release can be stimulus-responsive (Sirvi et al., 2024) (Fig. 2). The chitosan coating imparts several advantageous properties, including enhanced structural stability, prolonged residence time at cellular surfaces, and improved drug uptake, ultimately contributing to increased therapeutic efficacy of the encapsulated agents. These features, combined with their biocompatibility and ease of surface functionalization, make Chitosomes (CS) promising nanodelivery platforms for a broad spectrum of therapeutic agents, including anticancer, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory drugs (Eleraky et al., 2024). To address the limitations of IBR, liposomal and chitosan-coated liposomal (Chitosomes) formulations were developed using the thin-film hydration technique. Chitosan coating was applied to the optimized liposomes to yield CS. Solubility studies revealed pH-dependent solubility of IBR, with the highest solubility at pH 1.2 and the lowest at pH 7.4. The prepared liposomes and CS exhibited acceptable particle size, polydispersity index, and zeta potential suitable for oral delivery. Entrapment efficiency, measured via the supernatant method, was 74.33 ± 2.77 % for liposomes and 70.90 ± 2.21 % for CS. In vitro release studies showed enhanced drug release from both liposomes and CS compared to pure IBR, with liposomes demonstrating the highest cumulative release at 24 h, followed by CS. In vitro release tests indicated that the chitosan coating reduced IBR release at pH 1.2, enhancing oral bioavailability through mucoadhesion, extended gastrointestinal residence time, and absorption-enhancing effects. The chitosan coating also provided protection against gastric degradation, ensuring more intact drug reached the absorption site. In vivo pharmacokinetic analysis revealed a 3.472-fold enhancement of AUC (0-t) and a 2.07-fold enhancement of Cmax in comparison to the free IBR suspension, revealing appreciably enhanced oral bioavailability In vivo pharmacokinetic analysis also illustrated the enhanced oral bioavailability of IBR from CS over the pure drug, which implies their promising potential as efficient nanocarrier systems for oral delivery of IBR (Bangera et al., 2025). Carbon nanofiber-modified chitosan (CNF-CS) composite microspheres were synthesized for the controlled release of IBR. Surface morphology, particle sizes, and functional group contents of microspheres were determined by ATR-FTIR, scanning electron, and optical microscopy. Incorporation of CNF into microspheres enhanced the encapsulation efficiency of IBR and facilitated controlled and sustained drug release. IBR-CS-TPP-CNF microspheres attained an encapsulation efficiency of 83.09. Release profile, determined by the Korsmeyer-Peppas kinetic model, showed an anomalous diffusion mechanism (swelling-controlled behaviour and diffusion), with the release profile finished within 78 h under optimized conditions (Armutcu, 2022).

Types of Engineered Nanocarriers for Improved Bioavailability, Targeting, and Controlled Release of Ibrutinib.

Nanosuspension and self-nanoemulsifying drug delivery system

Nanosized drug delivery systems have been identified as an effective approach to improve the aqueous solubility and bioavailability of poorly water-soluble active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) due to their smaller particle size and characteristic surface properties (Song et al., 2022). Both systems are smart nanocarrier platforms for solubilization and controlled release of poorly water-soluble drugs, leading to enhanced therapeutic effects (Demirtürk et al., 2022). An IBR nanosuspension was effectively formulated using the precipitation–ultrasonication technique, with particle sizes in the nanometric range of 278.6 to 453.2 nm. Solid-state characterization ensured the polymorphic transition of crystalline IBR to its amorphous form, which led to enhanced dissolution behaviour. Interestingly, the freeze-dried nanosuspension was physically and chemically stable for as long as six months at ambient temperature, and the amorphous character was retained. Release of the drug in vitro by using fasted state simulated intestinal fluid (FaSSIF) exhibited much improved drug release from the nanosuspension in contrast to pure IBR. Precipitation studies at pH 6.8 further confirmed reduced drug precipitation and improved solubility, indicating improved performance under physiological intestinal conditions. Pharmacokinetic studies in rats revealed a marked increase in oral bioavailability of IBR from the nanosuspension relative to the plain drug. Importantly, the variability between fasted and fed states, a known challenge for IBR, was minimized in the nanosuspension-treated group, indicating a reduction in food effect and more consistent absorption. These results overall indicate that the nanosuspension method not only increases solubility and bioavailability but also gives a strong approach to counteract fasted/fed state pharmacokinetic variability, which is an important benefit in clinical practice (Rangaraj et al., 2019). A self-nanoemulsifying drug delivery system (SNEDDS) was formulated for co-delivery of IBR and Naringenin (NRG) to enhance their dissolution behaviour and improve oral bioavailability. Solubility screening and emulsification efficiency studies dictated the preparation of a ternary phase diagram, allowing the identification of the best formulation constituents. Linalool oil was selected for its good solubilizing capability and natural anticancer activity. Polyethylene glycol 200 and Tween® 80 were used as the surfactant and co-surfactant, respectively, because they possessed superb solubilization and emulsification capabilities. A pseudo-ternary phase diagram and constrained mixture design determined the self-emulsifying area, and the best formulation contained Linalool: PEG 200: Tween® 80 in a ratio of 59:11:30. This formulation resulted in a mean droplet size of 12.62 ± 0.83 nm and a very high polydispersity index (PDI) of 0.124 ± 0.03, reflecting excellent uniformity and stability. Molecular docking experiments established strong interactions of IBR and NRG with HER2 (Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2) and FOXM1 (Forkhead Box M1), evidencing their synergistic inhibition of breast cancer cells. In vitro cytotoxicity on MCF-7 breast cancer cells indicated a greatly increased anticancer activity of the optimized IBR–NRG SNEDDS with an IC₅₀ value of 3.12 ± 0.12 μM, equivalent to a 4.89-fold potency increase when compared to the IBR–NRG solution. In in vivo pharmacokinetics, the SNEDDS formulation exhibited 1.76-fold improved Cₘₐₓ and 1.41-fold increased oral bioavailability when compared with the IBR-NRG suspension. These findings highlight the potential of SNEDDS as a co-delivery system for IBR with synergistic potential (Jati et al., 2025).

Liposomes

Liposomes are phospholipid bilayer vesicles of lipid structure that are capable of entrapping both hydrophilic and lipophilic drugs. For the treatment of IBR, liposomal encapsulation offers a number of advantages, including enhanced solubility, protective effect against enzymatic degradation, increased circulation half-life, and targeted delivery to cancerous B-cells (Nel et al., 2023). IBR-loaded liposomes (IBR-LIP) have also been prepared primarily by the solvent injection method, followed in certain instances by solvent evaporation and probe sonication for reducing the size to produce nanosized unilamellar vesicles. IBR and lipids such as phosphatidylcholine and cholesterol are dissolved in a volatile organic solvent such as ethanol or methanol. This is injected rapidly into an aqueous environment under controlled stirring (Bragagni et al., 2012). After this, evaporation of the solvent under low pressure leads to the development of vesicles, while high-energy sonication creates a homogenous particle size distribution in the range 100–200 nm, One such study by Jain et al. employed solvent injection method to produce liposomes which was further made into a liposomal gel, The study explores a topical liposomal gel co-loaded with IBR and Curcumin for the treatment of psoriasis. Optimized liposomes (128.53 ± 1.18 nm; PDI 0.174 ± 0.08) possessed excellent entrapment efficiency (83.15 % and 86.69 %) with sustained drug release, indicating a synergistic, sustained, and effective nanocarrier-based therapeutic regimen (Jain et al., 2022). Another study by Ashar et al. employed a Quality by Design (QbD) approach to prepare PEGylated IBR-loaded liposomes through the solvent injection process. The current study employed a Box-Behnken Design to optimize key formulation parameters such as the phosphatidylcholine to cholesterol ratio, drug content, and sonication time. The optimized formulation showed an excellent encapsulation efficiency of approximately 90.8 %, particle size of approximately 208 nm, and zeta potential of −19 mV, indicating satisfactory colloidal stability. PEGylation was incorporated for the purpose of extending systemic circulation and restricting MPS uptake. In vitro drug release studies showed prolonged release profiles within 24 h, which can maintain therapeutic doses of drugs. The formulation was also found to be stable under simulated gastrointestinal fluids, which points to its oral route suitability. This research underscores the significant role of statistical optimization and PEGylation in enhancing the functionality of liposomal IBR formulations (Ashar et al., 2022). Based on conventional liposomes, follow-up work introduced a silica-coated liposomal system as an oral hybrid nanocarrier for the delivery of IBR. Liposomes of 240 nm showed higher structural stability, sustained release of the drug upon simulated intestinal fluid, and stability against degradation under a gastric condition. In rat pharmacokinetic studies, the Liposomes achieved a 3.12-fold improvement in oral bioavailability and a 4.08-fold increase in half-life compared to IBR suspension, demonstrating the advantages of silica-layered stability and controlled release (Ashar et al., 2024). These studies stress that IBR-loaded liposomal systems, particularly those optimized by statistical design, PEGylation, lyophilization, or silica coating, are capable of imparting spectacular improvements in encapsulation efficiency, gastrointestinal stability, systemic circulation half-life, and oral bioavailability. The liposome formulation coated with silica, for example, presents an extremely stable hybrid structure with a longer pharmacokinetic profile. Lyophilization and PEGylation afford long-term storage feasibility and convenience of reconstitution, necessary for practical use. Cumulatively, these advances speak to the significant translational potential of liposomal and lipid-based nanocarriers for maximizing IBR treatment. Future studies can involve ligand-directed active targeting or co-delivery with complementary drugs to further enhance therapeutic efficacy and patient safety (Singh et al., 2020).

Cyclodextrin-based nanoparticles

Cyclodextrins (CDs) are cyclic oligosaccharides that possess a hydrophobic central cavity and a hydrophilic outer surface, allowing them to form inclusion complexes with a broad variety of hydrophobic guest molecules (Shahriari et al., 2023). This ability of molecular encapsulation has been widely utilized in pharmaceutical sciences to enhance aqueous solubility, chemical stability, and the bioavailability of sparingly water-soluble drugs (Swarna and Gupta, 2025). Additionally, cyclodextrin-based nanocarrier systems have demonstrated considerable promise in maximizing pharmacokinetic profiles by improving systemic absorption, controlling drug metabolism, reducing off-target toxicity, and prolonging the biological half-life of the therapeutic agents. These multi-functional delivery vehicles offer a flexible strategy for enhancing the efficacy and safety of different pharmacologically active substances (Crini et al., 2018). Spray drying and lyophilisation were used to prepare IBR-cyclodextrin inclusion complexes, which were found to be an effective process to enhance the solubility of IBR. Loss of the typical endothermic melting peak of crystalline IBR in DSC traces and loss of sharp diffraction peaks in XRPD patterns signalled an extensive conversion of IBR from its crystalline to amorphous form under complexation. The phase solubility diagram (PSD) conformed to an AL-type profile, proposing a 1:1 inclusion complex formation between IBR and βCD. SEM imaging revealed marked morphological alterations consistent with inclusion complex formation. This solid-state transition is particularly advantageous, as the amorphous form is associated with enhanced aqueous solubility and dissolution rate, potentially leading to improved oral bioavailability of IBR. Hence, the solubility of IBR was increased almost 250 times in comparison to IBR solubility at pH 7.4. Thus, cyclodextrin inclusion complexes can improve the oral bioavailability of BCS Class II drugs like IBR (Swarna, 2024). In another study, IBR-loaded cyclodextrin-based nanosponges were developed. This study systematically evaluated the drug release behaviour of IBR-loaded hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HPβCD) nanosponges (NSPs) in comparison with pure IBR and IBR-HPβCD inclusion complexes. The physical mixture of IBR and HPβCD exhibited rapid release, reaching 66.18 ± 8.10 % within 150 min. The enhanced complexation mediated by carbonyldiimidazole (CDI) not only stabilized the inclusion of IBR within the cyclodextrin matrix but also facilitated a controlled and prolonged drug release, highlighting its potential utility in improving the therapeutic index of anticancer agents through sustained delivery (Sampathi et al., 2025). An impressive solubility increase was noted, being 45 times at 13 mM βCD and 25 °C. The apparent stability constant was estimated to be 3.6 × 103 M−1 at 25 °C, reflecting strong interaction between IBR and βCD. These studies proved the effective encapsulation of IBR and the conversion of its crystalline form to amorphous form. Molecular dynamics simulations afforded structural information, showing the stable embedding of the diphenyl ether moiety of IBR in the hydrophobic cavity of βCD. Binding free energy, as calculated with the MM-PBSA method, revealed positive van der Waals and electrostatic interactions that favor the thermodynamic stability of the complex (Ismail et al., 2025).

Hybrid lipid-polymer nanoparticles

Encapsulation of IBR in polymeric and hybrid nanocarriers is a promising solution to overcome its poor solubility, poor bioavailability, and short systemic clearance (Qiu et al., 2018; Sirvi et al., 2025). Some recent studies have employed diverse polymer matrices such as human serum albumin (HSA), Poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid)-poly(ethylene glycol)-folate (PLGA-PEG-folate), and the hybrid lipid-polymer delivery. These are used to improve pharmacokinetics, specificity of targeting, and therapeutic performance. One study in 2023 employed a QbD approach to design IBR-loaded HSA nanoparticles optimized via a Box-Behnken design. Optimized IBR-HSA NPs showed sustained release for 48 h, showed an AUC enhancement of ∼2.31-fold, and ultra-long half-life from ∼0.43 h to ∼2.89 h against the free drug. Functional evaluation in K562 leukemia cells indicated improved drug uptake, ROS-induced cytotoxicity, and G₂/M cell-cycle arrest. Hemocompatibility was excellent, with negligible hemolysis (<1 %) versus >40 % observed with free IBR. The findings highlight HSA's ability to provide extended systemic exposure with high safety and potent in vitro antileukemic activity (Famta et al., 2023). In an innovative dual-agent approach, researchers encapsulated IBR and hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) within HSA nanoparticles to inhibit drug-induced autophagy in glioma. Biodistribution studies in mice showed that IBR & HCQ-HSA NPs increased tumor accumulation by ∼5.6-fold relative to oral IBR. Increased cytotoxicity in C6 glioma cells and significantly prolonged survival in treated mice verified the therapeutic synergy. This dual-delivery platform exemplifies how polymeric carriers are able to bypass resistance mechanisms and enhance targeted drug efficacy in solid tumors (Yang et al., 2023). A study in 2023 formulated PLGA-PEG-folate nanoparticles (IBT-PPF-NPs) for local delivery of IBR to glioblastoma. With ∼97 % encapsulation efficiency, the mean particle size of ∼208 nm, and drug loading of 8.8 %, these particles increased cellular uptake significantly from ∼47 % to ∼90 % in C6 cells and from ∼55 % to ∼97 % in U87 MG cells. IC₅₀ values decreased from ∼10.2 μM (free IBR) to ∼7.6 μM (NPs) in C6, and comparable effects were observed in U87 MG. Intratumoral delivery in mice induced much more tumor regression than free drug, highlighting the advantages of ligand-mediated targeting for therapy of solid tumors (Morshedi et al., 2025). A novel polymeric platform used silk fibroin nanoparticles conjugated with folic acid to take advantage of folate receptor overexpression on cancer cells. These biocompatible NPs delivered IBR to HeLa, BT-474, and SKBR3 cells with selective uptake in tumor cells relative to normal EA. hy926 cells. Facilitated cellular internalization through folate receptors led to targeted drug delivery, making silk fibroin–based polymers a potential carrier for receptor-mediated nanotherapy. Strictly lipid-in nature, yet optimized by Design of Experiments (DoE) and analytical QbD, nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs) represent an instructive hybrid model that marries polymeric character with lipid-based release functionality. Oral bioavailability was found to be increased by ∼1.82-fold relative to free IBR in a study on IBR-NLCs, confirmed by a fluorimetric analytical technique. This hybrid model shows how merging paradigms of lipids and polymers may improve systemically delivered absorption as well as bioavailability in vivo (Prajapati et al., 2024). These recent studies collectively underscore the power of polymeric and hybrid nanoparticles in enhancing IBR delivery. HSA-based formulations deliver sustained release, extended systemic exposure, hematologic compatibility, and active targeting via receptor-mediated uptake. Folate-targeted PLGA-PEG systems significantly improve tumor cell uptake and reduce IC₅₀ values in glioblastoma. Silk fibroin offers a biodegradable platform for folate-mediated uptake in solid tumors, while hybrid lipid–polymer frameworks like NLCs enhance bioavailability through lymphatic absorption.

In another study, IBR-loaded glycyrrhizic acid-conjugated egg ovalbumin nanoparticles (IBR-GA-EA NPs) and IBR-glycyrrhizic acid complex (IBR-GA-COMP) were developed. Lyophilized IBR-GA-COMP was successfully prepared and characterized using DSC and FTIR, which confirmed the amorphization of IBR and the formation of a stable complex. Similarly, IBR-GA-EA NPs exhibited a spherical morphology with favourable physicochemical characteristics, including a particle size of 194.10 nm, polydispersity index of 0.22, and zeta potential of −33.96 mV. The encapsulation efficiency of the nanoparticles was reported to be 82.88 %. In vitro release studies demonstrated a significant improvement in drug release from both formulations when compared with free IBR suspension. At 24 h, cumulative drug release was increased by 7.29-fold for IBR-GA-EA NPs and 8.67-fold for IBR-GA-COMP. Both formulations exhibited a slow, sustained, and pH-dependent release pattern, with higher release observed at intestinal pH. In vivo pharmacokinetic studies revealed 3.21-fold and 3.41-fold enhancements in the oral bioavailability of IBR-GA-EA NPs and IBR-GA-COMP, respectively, relative to pure IBR suspension. These results point towards the potential of both formulations as effective approaches to enhance the solubility, rate of dissolution, and oral bioavailability of IBR, providing scope for dose reduction and enhanced therapeutic effectiveness in the treatment of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (Prakash Kamath et al., 2024).

Stimuli responsive and smart drug delivery systems

Stimuli-responsive drug delivery systems (SR-DDS) enable the controlled drug release upon internal or external stimulus pH, redox potentials, enzymes, temperature, or magnetic fields (Dai et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024a; Zhao et al., 2025). SR-DDS are highly tailored to the intracellular environment or the tumor microenvironment, and possess great promise to enhance delivery of hydrophobic covalent inhibitors like IBR by facilitating increased tumoral accumulation and reduced off-target toxicity (Maboudi et al., 2024). The pH-sensitive polymeric mixed micelle (PMM) drug delivery system of IBR was developed for enhanced intravenous delivery and targeted release in tumors. The system was designed with vitamin E TPGS (D-α-Tocopheryl polyethylene glycol 1000 succinate) and Solutol® HS15, which were chosen based on good in silico binding affinities towards IBR to ensure good drug–carrier compatibility. The optimized PMM system exhibited a nanoscale spherical shape with a mean particle size of 103.19 ± 14.92 nm, a polydispersity index of 0.387 ± 0.03, and a moderately negative zeta potential of −13.97 ± 1.51 mV, which reflected colloidal stability. Interestingly, the system had a remarkable encapsulation efficiency of 97.73 ± 2.40 %, with an initial burst release of 57.86 ± 2.22 % in 2 h at mildly acidic pH (6.5), resembling the tumor microenvironment, thus validating its pH-responsive release profile. The lyophilized product showed 60-day stability at 5 ± 3 °C, indicating real-world shelf-life potential for clinical use. In vitro cytotoxicity against breast cancer (BC) cell lines, MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231, was significantly reduced by about 15-fold and 10-fold compared to the free form of IBT. This superior efficacy was attributed to enhanced cellular uptake, induction of reactive oxygen species (ROS), disruption of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), and activation of apoptotic pathways, as evidenced by elevated Bax/Bcl-2 protein expression ratios via Western blot analysis. In addition, anti-migratory activity was also seen by inhibiting cancer cell migration, and anti-angiogenic activity was proven through decreased neovascularization in ex vivo chick chorioallantoic membrane (CAM) assays. The PMM formulation was also hemocompatible and intravenously tolerable with an estimated LD₅₀ of 25 mg/kg in female BALB/c mice, and had a markedly extended plasma half-life (t₁/₂ = 18.76 ± 3.83 h), signalling continued circulation and lower dosing frequency (Maji et al., 2025). A 2024 study presented a polydopamine-based nanocarrier platform that is conjugated with anti-CD47 antibodies for delivering IBR to atherosclerotic plaques in a targeted manner. The platform employs pH-responsive reversible disassembly to release IBR selectively within the acidic plaque microenvironment. The system facilitated site-specific immunomodulatory activity and regulated drug delivery to inflammatory cells (Wang et al., 2024b).

Surface-modified and targeted drug delivery systems for Ibrutinib

Surface PEGylation is usually used in concert to enhance colloidal stability and extend systemic circulation by avoiding the mononuclear phagocyte system (Tiwari et al., 2025). Moreover, stimuli-responsive coatings, including pH-sensitive polymers or redox-sensitive disulfide bonds, have been incorporated into the surface design to facilitate site-specific, intracellular release of IBR in the acidic and reductive tumor environment (Lu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024). These site-directed delivery systems not only enhance IBR accumulation in cancer tissues but also minimize toxicity on normal cells, thus improving the therapeutic index (Rajana et al., 2024). Surface-modulated nanoparticle systems offer a rationale strategy for the targeted delivery of IBR and are highly promising for clinical application in B-cell malignancies and more (Abdolvahab et al., 2024). PEG-liposomes (IBR-LIP-PEG) exhibited a marginally more sustained release through steric hindrance by the PEG corona, possibly also leading to prolonged systemic circulation. Stability evaluations under different conditions testified to the strength of the formulations. IBR-LIP and IBR-LIP-PEG maintained physical integrity between pH 4–10, aggregating only markedly at pH 2 because of protonation-induced charge neutralization. Ionic strength examinations revealed structural sensitivity at elevated concentrations of NaCl, but solution stability in PBS and 10 % FBS revealed minimal flocculation without overt instability. The lyophilized formulation was particularly noted to exhibit greater storage stability than its colloidal equivalent, with assay values, entrapment efficiency, and particle size remaining unaltered after six months under accelerated conditions (Panchal et al., 2024). These results demonstrate the significant impact of PEGylation and lyophilization on increasing shelf-life, stability, and controlled release characteristics of IBR-loaded liposomes for possible clinical usage. The present research highlights the central role of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), especially the M2 type, in fostering IBR resistance in mantle cell lymphoma (MCL). IBR-refractory patients had a much-increased percentage of immunosuppressive CD68+CD206+CD86− M2 macrophages and an increased M2/M1 ratio, implying a firm link between macrophage polarization and treatment failure. Co-cultures of MCL cell lines (Mino and JEKO-1) showed that macrophages severely blunt IBR effectiveness by raising IC₅₀ levels and inhibiting apoptosis, which suggests a protective tumor microenvironment (TME). CXCL5 release was greatly enhanced following macrophage-MCL co-culture, with consequent persistent CXCR2 expression on the MCL cells. The CXCL5–CXCR2 pathway was responsible for acquired resistance via BTK-independent survival pathways like Akt, MAPK, and STAT3. Therapeutically, axis inhibition was effective. CXCL5 or CXCR2 blockade re-sensitized MCL cells to IBR, increased apoptosis, and favoured M1 polarization. Notably, IBR combined with the CXCR2 inhibitor SB225002 exhibited synergistic cytotoxicity and tumor regression in vivo, providing a promising strategy to avoid resistance. The research shows strong evidence that the disruption of macrophage-derived signalling, especially the CXCL5–CXCR2 pathway, in combination with BTK inhibition, may be an effective therapeutic strategy for the therapy of IBR-resistant MCL (Sun et al., 2025). Functionalized multi-walled carbon nanotube (f-MWCNT)-based nanocomposites are a potential system for the effective delivery of IBR that overcomes the limitations of the free drug, including poor aqueous solubility, fast clearance, and poor bioavailability (Zhang et al., 2022). Structural characterization by XRD indicated decreased intensity of the plane, validating successful drug incorporation, and Raman spectroscopy established a lowered Iᴰ/Iᴳ ratio from 1.080 for pristine MWCNTs to 0.955 for f-MWCNTs-AgNPs, suggesting successful functionalization and increased binding affinity between IBR and the nanocarrier matrix. The drug release profile was sustained and pH-sensitive, in distinct contrast to the immediate and non-specific dissolution of pure IBR. For cumulative release from f-MWCNT nanocomposites at physiological pH (7.4), values varied from 48 ± 0.6 % to 70 ± 0.6 % for 84 h, and for mildly acidic pH (6.3, which simulates the tumor microenvironment), release was up to 54 ± 0.6 % to 75 ± 0.7 %. The controlled and environment-dependent release behaviour is due to the reduction in hydrogen bond between IBR and f-MWCNT surface at acidic pH levels, enabling targeted intracellular delivery. In addition, the functional groups and high surface area of f-MWCNTs enabled effective conjugation with metallic AgNPs and biocompatible polymers, increasing stability, dispersion, and cellular uptake. Such modifications not only reduced premature drug leakage but also enabled tumor-selective accumulation via augmented permeability and retention effects. In contrast to pure IBR, which is fast metabolized with low systemic retention, f-MWCNT-based formulations confer an extended pharmacokinetic benefit by maintaining therapeutic concentrations at the target location while minimizing off-target toxicity. F-MWCNT-based nanocomposites, particularly f-MWCNTs-AgNPs, represent a stable and reliable drug delivery system with enhanced loading capacity, pH-responsive sustained release, and increased delivery efficacy compared to pure IBR, highlighting their potential for efficient and targeted cancer treatment (Murugesan et al., 2021).

Targeted and surface-engineered nanoparticle systems have been highlighted as a hopeful approach to enhance the site-specific delivery and curative potency of IBR (Table 2). Due to IBR shortcomings, such as fast clearance, off-target toxicities, and poor tumor accumulation, surface modification of nanocarriers with targeted ligands allows active targeting of cancer cells overexpressing certain receptors, thereby increasing the selective uptake and reducing the systemic toxicity (Rangaraj et al., 2020). Several ligands like folic acid, transferrin, hyaluronic acid, peptides, and monoclonal antibodies have been attached onto the surface of polymeric nanoparticles, lipid-based vehicles, and lipid–polymer hybrid systems to target receptors like folate receptor, CD44, or integrins that are often overexpressed in B-cell cancers (Fig. 3). Folate-modified PLGA-PEG nanoparticles have also exhibited increased intracellular delivery and extended release of IBR in lymphoma models (Morshedi et al., 2025).

The research proved that folic acid-functionalized silk fibroin nanoparticles (FA-IB-SFNs) greatly improve the targeted delivery and therapeutic activity of IBR in cancer cells, particularly those overexpressing folate receptors like HeLa, BT-474, and SKBR3. Of five functionalization protocols examined, Method 5 demonstrated higher uptake in HeLa cells with low internalization in normal EA-hy926 cells, which points towards selective targeting. Fluorescence and confocal microscopy validated considerable FA-IB-SFNs accumulation in cancer cells, while flow cytometry showed notably enhanced cellular uptake of FA-IB-SFNs over non-functionalized analogs. Hydrodynamic size marginally increased after drug and FA incorporation, while the zeta potential changed from negative to positive, proving successful surface functionalization. FA-IB-SFNs also produced more potent cytotoxic activity and G1 phase cell cycle arrest in cancer cells, much higher than IB-SFNs. Apoptosis assays showed that FA-IB-SFNs elicited a greater apoptotic response (as high as 83 % in BT-474 cells) than IB-SFNs. Notably, normal endothelial cells exhibited low cytotoxicity and apoptosis, establishing the selectivity of the FA-mediated targeting. The findings place FA-IB-SFNs on the map as a potential nanocarrier for targeted cancer treatment, augmenting cellular uptake, cytotoxicity, and apoptosis in cancer cells while being nontoxic to normal cells (Fuster et al., 2023). The research proves that hyaluronic acid-conjugated IBR-loaded liposomes (HA-IBR-LPs) markedly enhance IBR physicochemical, cellular, and pharmacokinetic characteristics. Inverted microscopy and HR-TEM validated the spherical shape and HA coating, improving colloidal stability and targetability. Release studies in vitro revealed prolonged release for HA-IBR-LPs (up to 34 h) relative to IBR-LPs and free IBR, owing to hydrophilic HA that creates a hydration layer that delays drug diffusion. Hemolysis tests validated high hemocompatibility (<1 %), as suitable for intravenous administration. Cytotoxicity tests in HL-60 cells showed that HA-IBR-LPs exhibited the lowest IC₅₀ value (0.3 μM), much lower than IBR-LPs (0.7 μM) and free IBR (1 μM), because of improved internalization through CD44 receptor-mediated endocytosis. Cellular uptake experiments using FITC-labelled formulations supported this, as HA-functionalized liposomes presented enhanced fluorescence and higher uptake. Pharmacokinetically, HA-IBR-LPs showed a significant rise in Cmax (197.47 μg/mL) and AUC (770.40 μg·h/mL) along with extended MRT, as opposed to free IBR, which showed rapid clearance (AUC 99.67 μg·h/mL). HA coating minimizes opsonization and reticuloendothelial system uptake, thereby extending systemic circulation. HA-IBR-LPs represent a favourable approach to targeted delivery of IBR with enhanced therapeutic response and reduced systemic toxicity (Patel et al., 2025). Research by Faust et al. describes an innovative method to attain tumor-cell-targeted delivery of the BTK inhibitor IBR via an electrostatic antibody inhibitor conjugate (AiC) system. To overcome the drawbacks of traditional antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), scientists developed a non-covalent, self-assembling nanocarrier by conjugating the anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody rituximab to a cationic protamine linker that enables electrostatic interaction with a novel, synthesized anionic derivative of IBR (IBR-Cy3.5). This alteration not only provided a strong anionic nature but also allowed for fluorescent tracking in vitro and in vivo. The formed αCD20 mAb protamine/free protamine IBR Cy3.5 complex was a stable nanoparticle (<200 nm) with more than 100 mol of drug per mol antibody, with selective internalization into CD20+ lymphoma cells and effective BTK inhibition. In contrast to free IBR or uncomplexed IBR-Cy3.5, the AiC system exhibited better suppression of colony growth (>50 % inhibition) and increased apoptosis. In DLBCL xenograft mice, at low doses (4–8 mg/kg antibody; 15–30 nmol drug), tumor growth decreased to <20 % of controls, and survival was extended to 36–40 days compared to 10–20 days for controls. Biodistribution studies revealed targeted tumor accumulation without off-target toxicity. This research proves that a cationic protamine–antibody nanocarrier can deliver an anionic IBR derivative (IBR-Cy3.5) efficiently to CD20+ lymphoma cells by stable electrostatically assembled nanoparticles. It demonstrates dual specificity: targeting the tumor through the anti-CD20 antibody and the intracellular kinase through IBR, while evading non-specific uptake. The formulation significantly enhances cellular internalization, BTK inhibition, apoptosis induction, and tumor growth inhibition in vitro and in vivo at drug concentrations two orders of magnitude lower than those of traditional oral IBR, highlighting its high efficiency and tumor-selective pharmacodynamics (Faust et al., 2022).

Clinical relevance and commercial applications

Nanocarriers like lipid nanoparticles, polymeric micelles, dendrimers, liposomes, SLNs, NLCs, PLHNs, and exosomes have been formulated to encapsulate IBR. These systems allow better solubilization, resistance to enzymatic degradation, and controlled release or sustained release. For example, human serum albumin nanoparticles and PEGylated liposomes have exhibited increased circulation, enhanced area under the curve (AUC), and targeted accumulation in tumor tissue via EPR effects (Dain et al., 2022). From a clinical point of view, nanocarriers offer the possibility of tumor-specific delivery, reducing systemic exposure and dose-related side effects like atrial fibrillation, diarrhoea, and bleeding. In addition, ligand-mediated targeting systems enable active targeting of overexpressed receptors on malignant cells to increase cellular uptake and therapeutic selectivity. In preclinical models, IBR-loaded transferrin-modified PLHNs and chitosan-coated liposomes have shown better antitumor efficacy, enhanced cellular uptake, and increased apoptosis in leukemic cells than free drug (Devin et al., 2022; Hidayat et al., 2025). In the case of drug resistance, IBR nanocarriers could circumvent resistance mechanisms linked to BTK mutations or microenvironmental stresses such as M2 macrophage polarization. Co-delivery platforms loading BTK inhibitors along with PI3K inhibitors or epigenetic regulators can synergistically inhibit compensatory survival pathways. Nanocarrier systems may also be designed for pH- or redox-sensitive drug release for selective discharge of the payload in the acidic tumor microenvironment or intracellular space. On the business side, the international nanomedicine market is set to exceed USD 400 billion in 2030, with the oncology segment the biggest. While no IBR nanoformulation has yet made it to clinical trials or the regulatory pathway, the growing pipeline of nanocarrier-based chemotherapy drugs establishes a strong precedent (Fig. 4). Developing IBR in these delivery systems may provide new intellectual property value, lifecycle extension strategies, and injectable, transdermal, or implantable delivery reformulations, making its market potential wider than oral administration. Nanocarrier platforms are potentially retrievable for precision medicine, where patient stratification according to BTK expression, macrophage phenotype, or tumor biomarker profile can be used. This is in line with the ongoing direction of precision oncology and regulatory focus on personalized therapy. Pharmaceutical companies and startups working on BTK-targeting nanomedicines could expect priority review pathways and orphan drug labels for rare hematological cancers. IBR nanocarriers have great potential to maximize therapeutic efficacy, minimize toxicity, overcome resistance, and expand clinical use. Their successful translation from bench to bedside would depend on rigorous preclinical validation, scalable GMP manufacture, regulatory harmonization, and strategic industry collaborations.

Limitations and future directions

Although the increasing promise of IBR nanocarriers to enhance drug delivery and treatment efficiency is evident, several limitations still impede their clinical realization (Chagas et al., 2025). Among these is the formulation complexity, maximizing drug loading, particle size homogeneity, colloidal stability, and reproducibility between batches, which remains challenging, especially when upscaling production for clinical applications (Zheng et al., 2023) (Fig. 5). Most of the nanoparticle preparation methods, like emulsification, solvent evaporation, and nanoprecipitation, are plagued with low encapsulation efficiency and heterogeneity (Keerikkadu et al., 2025). Safety and biocompatibility are also of top concern; while some carriers employ FDA-approved lipids or polymers, their surface modifications, degradation products, or slow release over periods of time may result in unexpected immunogenic or cytotoxic effects (Egbuna et al., 2021). Furthermore, targeted delivery by ligands such as transferrin, folate, or antibodies has exhibited promise in vitro but is potentially compromised in vivo by non-specific interactions, downregulation of receptors, or heterogeneity within tumors to limit therapeutic specificity (Ibrahim et al., 2023; Ramalho et al., 2022). Nearly all investigations on IBR nanocarriers are presently only available in in vitro cell lines or simple xenograft models that fail to fully reflect the tumor microenvironment, immune response, and metabolic complexities of human cancer (Janssen Pharmaceutical, 2018). In addition, the development of nanomedicines is hampered by regulatory issues of unstandardized characterization protocols, ill-defined safety evaluation guidelines, and unpredictability of pharmacokinetics and biodistribution (Ghosh and Kumar, 2024). Commercial hurdles, such as expensive production, limited scalability, and indefinite access to the market relative to traditional oral IBR formulations, are also industrial viability concerns (Hegde et al., 2023; Pedziwiatr-Werbicka et al., 2021). To address the shortcomings of traditional IBR treatment, AiCs represent a new receptor-mediated delivery platform, offering the ability to selectively target CD20+ lymphoma cells, effectively accumulate intracellularly, and exhibit improved therapeutic efficacy with reduced off-target toxicity, as well as avoidance of first-pass metabolism (Faust et al., 2022). However, more research needs to be conducted for a complete evaluation of their long-term safety, optimal dosing, and potential effect against Leukemia tumors. In the future, several paths could propel the clinical suitability of IBR nanocarriers. Design of stimuli-responsive systems that can release the drug in accordance with pH, redox gradients, enzymes, or external stimuli like heat or ultrasound can increase site-specific delivery and reduce off-target toxicity (Abousalman-Rezvani et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). Biohybrid nanocarriers, i.e., exosomes or nanoparticles coated with cell membranes, hold great promise for evading the immune system, increased targeting, and sustained circulation (Aalhate et al., 2025). Moreover, incorporating molecular profiling technologies to design nanocarriers to the unique attributes of an individual's tumor, e.g., mutation status of the BTK gene or surface receptor profile, is consistent with the objectives of personalized medicine (Molica and Mauro, 2024; Stamatopoulos et al., 2024). Nevertheless, it remains critical that future research focuses on comprehensive in vivo validation, toxicology evaluation, and translational studies in suitable animal models. Aligning regulatory standards and enabling synergistic networks among academia, pharmaceutical companies, and regulators will be essential in effectively taking IBR nanocarriers from bench to bedside.

Current Challenges and Emerging Strategies in Ibrutinib Nanocarrier Development.

Translational challenges

The translational applications of IBR Nanocarriers are subject to significant challenges, including scalability, regulatory acceptability, and cost-effectiveness (Maji et al., 2025). Scalability poses challenges in terms of keeping particle size, encapsulation efficiency, and stability during upscaling. Laboratory operations, such as reverse-phase evaporation or sonication, developed for small-scale batches are not necessarily scalable to industrial production without compromising quality or unnecessarily increasing complexity. Regulatory challenges emerge since nanomedicines need to be stringently characterized, biocompatible, safe, and effective to comply with changing FDA and EMA regulations. Extensive documentation and stringent clinical trials are requisites, creating hurdles particularly for intricate Nanocarriers such as IBR nanoliposomes or lipid carriers (Wang et al., 2022). Cost-effectiveness is also a significant problem. Nanocarriers utilise costly materials, high-priced equipment, and rigorous quality control, thereby increasing production costs. Although they have the potential to enhance oral bioavailability and therapeutic effects, these advantages should justify premium prices, which could restrict access in resource-poor settings (Kurillová et al., 2025). These are issues that demand formulation processes for large-scale reproducibility optimisation, a regulatory strategy in accordance with nanomedicine standards, and cost strategies that balance innovation with accessibility for the effective clinical translation of IBR Nanocarriers.

Conclusion

Nanotechnology-delivery systems have proved to be effective tools to reverse the pharmacokinetic and biopharmaceutical hindrances of IBR, such as low aqueous solubility, high metabolism, and low oral bioavailability. By facilitating enhanced solubility, stability, and site-specific targeting, these systems enhance efficacy and minimize off-target toxicity. Several nanocarrier formulations have been devised to maximize IBR delivery. Nanoemulsions, nanosuspensions, and cyclodextrin inclusion complexes have enhanced dissolution rates and gastrointestinal absorption considerably. Chitosan carrier-based systems provide the added advantages of mucoadhesion and sustained drug delivery, enhancing bioavailability and dosing frequency. Hybrid nanoparticles incorporating polymeric and lipoidal components enhance lymphatic uptake, prolong systemic circulation, and enhance drug loading efficiency. In addition, stimuli-sensitive nanocarriers programmed to discharge IBR upon tumor-specific signals such as pH variations, enzymatic action, or redox potential gradients facilitate targeted drug release at the site of interest while limiting systemic exposure and resultant toxicities. These nanotechnology-based strategies offer a holistic solution to optimize IBR treatment, improve patient compliance, and avoid undesired effects. Electrostatic antibody inhibitor conjugates represent a novel receptor-mediated delivery platform for IBR, enabling precise tumor targeting, efficient intracellular accumulation, and enhanced therapeutic efficacy, while minimizing off-target effects. In the future, further research ought to be directed towards clinical translation, high-volume manufacturing, regulatory compliance, and long-term safety evaluation to facilitate successful integration into everyday oncological practice and unlock their full therapeutic potential.

Protocol for Novel Perioperative Optimization of Obese Osteoarthritic Patients pending Total Knee Replacement with glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (NPO-OOPS-TKR)
Authors: Lawrence C M Lau, Tak W D Lui, Chunyi Wen, Lin Zhu, Sien Lin, Thomas K C Leung, Michelle H Luk, Ping Keung Chan, Amy Cheung, Kwong Yuen Chiu, Henry Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsObesity is associated with higher rates of perioperative complications and worse pain and functional outcomes after total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Preoperative optimization through weight management is therefore clinically appealing, but the current evidence base is mixed. No randomized controlled trial (RCT) has evaluated glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) as a perioperative optimization strategy before TKA, and no such trial has focused on an Asian population. This pilot randomized trial therefore aims to determine the feasibility, tolerability, and acceptability of semaglutide-based optimization before and after TKA in older Asian adults with obesity.MethodsThis is a two-arm pilot RCT that will recruit 54 adults aged 40 to 80 years with obesity (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2) listed for primary TKA. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to semaglutide plus standard TKA care or to usual care alone. The intervention group will receive semaglutide for 48 weeks before and 48 weeks after TKA, with a planned four-week washout period before and after surgery. The control group will receive current standard care, including routine orthopaedic management and standardized general advice on diet and physical activity. Primary outcomes are feasibility outcomes (recruitment, adherence, tolerability, and retention), while pain, body weight, patient-reported outcomes, and perioperative complications are exploratory clinical outcomes intended to inform a future definitive trial.ConclusionThis pilot trial will determine whether a definitive randomized trial is feasible in older Asian adults with obesity awaiting TKA. The study is designed to estimate recruitment, adherence, tolerability, and retention, and to generate preliminary effect-size estimates for a future fully powered trial. Introduction

Studies consistently show that obesity is associated with a higher risk of adverse outcomes after total knee arthroplasty (TKA), including wound complications, infection, venous thromboembolism, acute kidney injury, increased resource use, and inferior patient-reported outcomes compared with non-obese patients.1-3 Obesity is also associated with greater pain before TKA and more residual symptoms (lower Knee Society Score)4 after TKA.5-7 These observations have led to increasing interest in preoperative weight optimization strategies that might improve outcomes in patients with obesity.8,9 In a retrospective study, weight loss intervention led to an improvement in physical function after TKA.9 However, the evidence supporting intentional preoperative weight loss before arthroplasty remains uncertain.10,11 A systematic review of weight-loss diets before elective surgery found only three eligible prospective studies involving 173 participants overall and reported low- to very-low-quality evidence, with no clear reduction in postoperative complications or length of stay.12 In arthroplasty-specific observational studies, preoperative weight loss has not consistently reduced perioperative or 30-day adverse events.11,13,14 More recent work in TKA has similarly reported that preoperative weight loss was not associated with lower postoperative risk, while other studies in arthroplasty populations have suggested possible unintended consequences, including higher early complication rates after sleeve gastrectomy before arthroplasty and worse outcomes in some hip arthroplasty cohorts after preoperative weight loss.13,14 On the other hand, a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with bariatric surgery before TKA found reduced complications in the bariatric surgery group (−16% weight loss in 52 weeks), while a RCT using diet modification found similar physical function but a better cardiovascular profile in the diet group.8,15 These findings support genuine clinical equipoise and justify a cautious, balanced rationale for the present pilot trial.

Our recent systematic review of RCTs identified only three trials involving 198 patients undergoing arthroplasty, two using diet modification and one using bariatric surgery.16 Although those trials reported fewer postoperative complications in intervention groups, the evidence base remains small and methodologically heterogeneous. This limited evidence base should be acknowledged when framing the rationale for further trials.16 In our systematic review, we also found that currently: 1) no RCT studying anti-obesity drug effect before TKA; and 2) no RCT studying weight loss intervention before TKA in an Asian population.

Alternative options are needed as bariatric surgery is invasive with high treatment-related complications, and diet control among osteoarthritic patients is limited by the reported barriers of their desire for high-fat/high-calorie foods, and difficulty in managing negative mood, e.g. stress, depression, and boredom.13,15,17,18 Bariatric and dietary approaches may not be suitable for all patients, and recent arthroplasty literature suggests that preoperative weight loss is not uniformly beneficial as aforementioned.11,13,19,20 These findings support genuine clinical equipoise rather than a presumption of benefit. Besides, our Mendelian randomization study noted that osteoarthritis (OA) is more related to BMI than diet.21 Recently, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs), a diabetes medication, was found to promote weight reduction with a good safety profile.22 GLP-1RAs stimulate postprandial insulin secretion, reduce glucagon secretion, and delay gastric emptying. GLP-1RAs induce weight loss through appetite suppression and reduced food intake.22 GLP-1RAs also reduce cardiovascular-related deaths and morbidities and improve physical function in obese subjects.23,24 GLP-1RAs are therefore a plausible weight loss intervention to optimize TKA candidates, and their potential perioperative effects may extend beyond weight reduction alone and may include metabolic and anti-inflammatory mechanisms. The present pilot study is designed to determine whether such a strategy is feasible and acceptable in the target population before a fully powered efficacy trial is attempted.

How does the existing literature support this research question?

A GLP-1RA trial (STEP6) focused on Asians was performed in Japan and Korea and found semaglutide, a GLP-1RA, could achieve −12% weight loss at week 52 (–13·2% at week 68).25 This provides strong evidence of semaglutide as obesity treatment in Asians and highlights its potential to achieve weight loss before TKA and its beneficial role in the perioperative and postoperative outcome. In the STEP9 trial, patients with obesity and knee osteoarthritis with moderate-to-severe pain, treatment with once-weekly injectable semaglutide resulted in significantly greater reductions in body weight and pain related to knee osteoarthritis than placebo.26 However, the STEP9 trial’s participants had a mean age of 56 years (SD 10) and a mean BMI of 40.3 kg/m2 (SD 7.2); only 5.4% participants were Asians and it was not reported whether these participants subsequently had TKA or not and their outcomes.

Need for a pilot and feasibility study

Whether GLP-1RA semaglutide induced weight loss can lead to perioperative pain relief and improved outcome in TKA is unknown currently, despite its plausibility mentioned above. Obesity is defined as BMI exceeding 25 kg/m2 in the Asia-Pacific region.27 Our TKA patients have a median age at TKA operation of 70 years (IQR 59 to 81), a mean BMI of 27.4 kg/m2 (SD 4.7), and current use of GLP-1RA is minimal among them due to its late uptake into the healthcare system.28,29 The tolerability of this group of patients (older Asian adults with OA knee pending TKA, with obesity defined at Asia-Pacific region standard) on semaglutide with its side-effects (such as diarrhoea and gastrointestinal discomfort) is unknown. Therefore, we designed this pilot study to assess the feasibility of a full trial.

Objectives

The primary objective of this pilot study is to: 1) estimate adherence, tolerability, and follow-up rates; and 2) recruitment and management challenges. The secondary objectives are to: 3) assess the effect of semaglutide for perioperative optimization in TKA and gather data to estimate the effect and sample size for a future trial.

Hypothesis

The hypothesis for this pilot trial is to assess the feasibility of conducting a trial of GLP-1RA for perioperative optimization in TKA. The null hypothesis for the full trial is that clinical, functional, and quality of life (QoL) outcomes in obese patients undergoing TKA will not be improved if this is preceded by GLP-1RA-induced weight loss.

Methods

Study design

This is a pilot RCT to assess feasibility of a full trial comparing GLP-1RA with conventional care for optimization of obese TKA patients. The study is explicitly a feasibility study. It is not powered to detect clinically important differences in postoperative outcomes. Instead, the primary purpose is to determine whether recruitment, allocation, treatment delivery, follow-up, and safety monitoring are sufficiently robust to justify a larger trial. The study is constructed following the CONSORT statement and good clinical practice (GCP) and follows the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki.30 The study has two parallel groups: 1) the intervention group on GLP-1RA compared with; 2) the control group on conventional care, with a 1:1 allocation ratio. The checklist (CONSORT 2010 checklist of information to include when reporting a pilot or feasibility trial) is provided in the Supplementary Material. The study is prospectively registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06807970) on 28 January 2025. Ethical approval (UW 24 to 423) was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of the University of Hong Kong/Hospital Authority Hong Kong West Cluster (HKU/HA HKW IRB). If any amendments to the protocol are to be made, the principal investigator will notify the: 1) HKU/HA HKW IRB; 2) the Drug Office, Department of Health, the Government of Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; and 3) the Health and Medical Research Fund, Health Bureau, the Government of Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. Any deviations from the protocol will be fully documented using a formal report form. The protocol of the clinical trial registry will also be updated accordingly.

Predefined outcome indicators that would enable scale-up to a larger project would be the primary outcome of adherence and follow-up rates greater than 80%. The study will be conducted in the Queen Mary Hospital (QMH) and Duchess of Kent Children’s Hospital (DKCH) for subjects’ recruitment, surgery, and follow-up with approval from HKU/HA HKW IRB as the coordinating centre. Signed consent will be arranged with suitable candidates with thorough discussion of benefits and risks of the study. The study design is reviewed and developed with the Biostatistics and Clinical Research Methodology Unit (BCRMU) of the Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine, University of Hong Kong. The trial steering committee is composed of the principal investigator and co-investigators, in coordination with the BCRMU, who will assess the progress of the trial, and will coordinate and conduct the clinical trial.

Study participants

All patients on the waiting list for primary TKA coming back for follow-up will be screened for eligibility, with the following criteria: aged between 40 and 80 years; BMI ≥ 27 kg/m²; on the surgical waiting list for primary TKA; willing and able to cooperate in a weight management programme; and meeting the indication for semaglutid

The exclusion criteria is: revision surgery or surgery for neoplastic disease; inability to provide informed consent; a medical condition which in the opinion of the investigators makes the patient unsuitable for participation in the trial; contraindication to GLP-1RA, such as history of pancreatitis, severe gastrointestinal diseases such as gastroparesis, thyroid cancer, and inflammatory bowel disease; and lack of acceptance of the randomization process.

Importantly, the protocol does not impose an additional one-year surgical delay beyond routine clinical practice. Patients are recruited from an existing waiting list in which the expected waiting time commonly ranges between four and six years. The study therefore recruits patients who are already expected to remain on the waiting list for at least 12 months, and the protocol aligns the intervention period with that existing waiting-list interval. Participation in the trial will not disadvantage patients who decline enrolment, and patients may withdraw at any time without affecting their standard care. If a participant experiences unacceptable clinical deterioration, serious adverse effects, or a change in surgical priority, the treating team may advance surgery or withdraw the participant from trial treatment as clinically indicated. This is in line with current practice.15,31

Sample size calculation

Assessment with Biostatistics and Clinical Research Methodology Unit (BCRMU) of the Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine determined that since this trial is first-in-human pilot trial of GLP-1RA on TKA, the sample size would be based on the feasibility and budget as the effect size and relevant data are unknown. As discussed above the planned treatment of semaglutide would be 24 months (12 months to allow degree of weight loss plateaus and maintain for another 12 months to maintain weight loss and allow observation of TKA outcome at one year as in standard practice).25,32,33 A total of 27 patients in the intervention group were planned according to funding. This sample size is also in agreement with recommended pilot trial sample size based on the estimated clinical effect size.34

Public and patient involvement

The initial protocol was designed by the principal and co-investigators with advice from the BCRMU and patients on the waiting list for TKA who are suffering from concomitant obesity and osteoarthritis. Further amendments may be made according to performance and feedback from the patient group in future trial.

Randomization

Following consent, baseline assessment, and confirmation of eligibility, participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated block randomization sequence prepared by an independent statistician. Allocation concealment will be maintained using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes prepared by staff not involved in recruitment. Envelopes will be opened only after baseline assessment has been completed. This process is intended to minimize selection bias and preserve allocation concealment. Patients will be enrolled by surgeons and assigned to intervention following the sequence of allocation in number envelopes but the patients will not be blinded in this pilot study because semaglutide is administered with a specialized prefilled pen injector and this pilot study also aims to assess feasibility and tolerability among our patient group. However, assessors and statisticians invovled in data analysis will be blinded to the group allocation.

Study intervention, comparator, and study procedures

Age, sex, medical comorbidities, height, weight, medication history, function and quality of life (QoL) scores, dual-energy radiograph absorptiometry (DEXA) scan, body test, and radiographs of the knee will be performed. Participants will be randomized to one of two groups (1:1 ratio):

1) Control group (TKA alone): Participants in the control group will continue to receive current standard orthopaedic care while awaiting TKA. They will receive standardized general advice on diet and physical activity, consistent with usual practice, but no study-specific pharmacological weight-management intervention.

2) Intervention group (GLP-1RA + TKA): Patients in intervention group will have GLP-1RA semaglutide for 48 weeks followed by TKA and continue semaglutide for 48 weeks during the postoperative follow-up period with a four-week break before and after TKA. This is decided based on balancing the treatment effect of semaglutide, waiting time for TKA, and TKA follow-up requirement. In Hong Kong, the waiting time for TKA would beabout four years and so a period of 12 months treatment of semaglutide is reasonable. And in the STEP6 trial, weight loss at week 52 (-12%) is similar to that in the study end at week 68 (–13·2%),25 and the STEP5 trial showed maintaining semaglutide to week 104 could maintain weight loss of similar degree as in week 52.32 And the consensus of assessing TKA patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) is one-year follow-up.33 Therefore, semaglutide will be given one year before and after TKA. No semaglutide four weeks before and after TKA was designed to avoid its gastrointestinal effects affecting the perioperative period management.

The protocol for using semaglutide will reference that in STEP2, 5, and 6 trials by our endocrinologist (TWDL).25,32,35 Semaglutide, administered with a prefilled pen injector, was initiated at a dose of 0.25 mg once weekly, with dose gradually increased to reach maintenance dose of 1 mg weekly (lower maintenance doses were permitted if participants had unacceptable side effects with the 1 mg dose). This dosage is lower than in STEP6 but it is the current approved dosage in Hong Kong and has good safety and tolerability profile in Hong Kong patients. Based on our clinical experience this dosage would be more proper and safer in this pilot study. General advice on diet and physical activity will be provided.

TKA surgery

Surgery and care of patients undergoing primary TKA at Queen Mary Hospital (QMH) and Duchess of Kent Children’s Hospital (DKCH) is standardized using clinical pathway protocols which have been validated in our previous RCTs, with single prosthesis type and standardized surgical technique.31

Participant follow-up

Both the intervention group and control group will have standardized routine postoperative follow-up visits at six weeks, and at three, six, and 12 months post-surgery.

Blinding and outcome ascertainment

Subjects randomized to the treatment arm will then be referred to clinic for GLP-1RA in addition to the routine TKA clinical pathway. Subjects randomized to the control group will follow the routine TKA clinical pathway. Subjects will not be blinded in this pilot study because semaglutide is administered with a specialized prefilled pen injector and this pilot study also aims to assess feasibility and tolerability among our patient group.

Blinding of the surgical team to preoperative interventions may not be fully possible due to medical need for full medical history if needed, but they would not be provided with grouping information intentionally and they will provide standardized surgical care to all patients.

Data will be analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis and outcome ascertainment will be blinded, in that it will be identified by a blinded research assistant through: Attendance at all unit ward rounds, clinics, and radiograph meetings to capture complications and readmissions and review of all medical records on patient discharge and follow-up clinics. To reduce performance bias, both groups will receive standardized advice on diet and physical activity, routine orthopaedic follow-up, and the same amount of clinical contact, and the same perioperative clinical pathway for TKA.

Statistical analysis

Analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, but the emphasis will be on estimation rather than formal efficacy testing. Feasibility outcomes will be summarized as proportions with 95% CIs, and exploratory clinical outcomes will be presented descriptively with between-group estimates and 95% CIs. Formal significance testing will be interpreted cautiously because the study is not powered for definitive efficacy conclusions. Categorical variables will be applied to chi-squared tests (or Fisher’s exact tests for small samples) while continuous variables will be applied to (parametric) t-tests and (non-parametric) Mann-Whitney tests for symmetrically and asymmetrically distributed data, respectively. Paired (parametric) t-tests and (non-parametric) Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test will be used to assess improvement among patients in the same arm for the pain and functional scores. To reduce performance bias, both groups will receive standardized written lifestyle advice and the same perioperative clinical pathway. The impact of unblinded treatment and differential clinical contact on subjective outcomes will be explicitly acknowledged in analysis and interpretation.

Safety monitoring

We have designed our study in terms of aspects of identifying and reporting adverse events by referencing the Food and Drug Administration semaglutide drug approval information, the STEP7 semaglutide RCT which studied use of semaglutide including a Hong Kong population at a higher dose,41 other semaglutide trials,41-45 and the Dowsey RCT using bariatric surgery for optimization for TKA.15 These previous trials were studying interventions with higher dose/more significant weight loss intervention magnitudes/sicker patients and thus the adverse effects they had would likely be more significant than our trial and provide good references for our plan of identifying and reporting adverse events. The following highlights the key features regarding identifying and reporting adverse events.

Identifying and reporting adverse events

The investigators are responsible for detecting, documenting, recording, and following up on events that meet the definition of an adverse event (AE) or severe adverse event (SAE). All AEs will be collected from the first trial-related activity after obtaining informed consent and until ‘end of trial’ visit, at each of the timepoints the investigators and their research assistants met with the patients (i.e. each follow-up). Specifically, the medication semaglutide requires dosage titration in the early phase every two to four weeks until it reaches its maintenance dosage and this period will be in the first three to four months. Then during the maintenance phase, the patients will be seen monthly for obtaining the medication as well as drug titration. After their operation, the patients will be seen every two weeks in the first six weeks for assessing surgery-related parameters/complications and then they will be seen again monthly for obtaining the medication as well as drug titration. It will be conducted until the end of the study.

AEs and SAEs will all be inputted in a designated report form. The processes for identifying AEs and outcome will be conducted by the investigator and the research assistants. Active surveillance for AEs and outcomes will comprise the following:

Discussion

Studies have found obese TKA patients have higher chances of developing perioperative problems, such as increased narcotics consumption, surgical site infections, revision, compared with non-obese patients.1 Obesity is also associated with greater pain before TKA and more residual symptoms after TKA.5,6 Therefore, weight loss interventions before TKA are being explored recently to improve the perioperative care and outcome.8,9 In a recent retrospective study, a weight loss intervention led to improvements in physical function after TKA.9 Currently, there are limited numbers of RCTs studying weight loss intervention before joint arthroplasty, two using diet modification,8,46 and one using bariatric surgery.15 Systematic review identified that existing randomized evidence on weight loss intervention for TKA is limited and should be interpreted cautiously.16 Although some trials reported fewer postoperative complications and more patients declining surgery because of symptom improvement, the overall evidence base remains small, heterogeneous, and insufficient to establish that intentional preoperative weight loss consistently improves arthroplasty outcomes.8,15,16,46 Besides, perioperative pain was not designed as an outcome in these studies. All these studies were also conducted on Caucasians only.

Currently there is no RCT studying weight loss intervention using an anti-obesity drug before TKA, and no RCT studying weight loss intervention before TKA in an Asian population. Bariatric surgery is invasive with high treatment-related complications; for example, infection related to bariatric surgery in a previous RCT and may not be the best option for mildly obese patients.15,47 Diet modification has limited efficacy for weight loss and is reported to be difficult among osteoarthritic patients.13,14 GLP-1RA, an anti-obesity drug, has not been studied in a RCT currently in terms of its effect on knee arthroplasty. Large database studies have shown that GLP-1 RA can lead to lower odds of sepsis, prosthetic joint infections, readmission but higher odds for acute kidney injury, pneumonia, etc.48 However, those on GLP-1 RA are likely to be more obese with more comorbidities and so these studies cannot fairly assess the effect of GLP-1RA. Besides, perioperative outcomes like pain and function are not documented. Moreover, observational arthroplasty studies have reported inconsistent associations between preoperative weight loss and postoperative outcomes, ranging from no clear benefit to potential harm in selected settings. The rationale for this pilot study is therefore grounded in uncertainty rather than in an assumption of benefit. Besides, any effect of semaglutide on perioperative or postoperative outcomes may reflect mechanisms beyond weight loss alone, including changes in glycaemic control, systemic inflammation, and cardiometabolic health. This distinction is important and will be considered when interpreting the findings of any future definitive trial.

The strength of this study includes performing a RCT for study design which can reduce biases and allows study of the effects of GLP-1RA on TKA patients with obesity more accurately compared with cohort studies. There are also multiple timepoints for assessments, which can monitor the change of clinical outcomes during the study period. There are several anticipated limitations in our study. The patients will not be blinded in this pilot study because semaglutide is administered with a specialized prefilled pen injector which is difficult to make for the control group; therefore, the study cannot assess the placebo effect that may occur with the control group having a placebo. However, as semaglutide can induce significant weight loss, blinding may not be able to fully cover up the grouping arrangement from the patients. Performance bias (e.g. subjectively feeling better on treatment) could exist as the participants are aware of their group assignment; however, we standardize every other part of the protocol including clinical contact, follow-up, and assessment to minimize the effect of co-interventions. On the other hand, we will employ the 1 mg dosage instead of the full strength 2.4 mg for obesity as we consider this dosage has higher tolerability while its effect on weight loss is adequate. Therefore, this pilot study shall not provide data regarding the tolerability if the highest dose of semaglutide is used. In other words, the generalizability to higher-dose regimens is limited in this study.

In summary, this study will study the potential optimization effects of GLP-1RA on patients with obesity having TKA and the feasibility for a larger trial. The result will be important given that the majority of the TKA population falls into the category of obesity in Asia.49 For protocol version 3 (27 August, 2025), recruitment of participants for this study started on 30 November 2025, and is expected to end on 31 May 2026.

Take home message

- This is the first randomized controlled trial investigating the use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) as a perioperative optimization strategy before total knee arthroplasty.

- Feasibility and perioperative outcomes will be assessed to inform a larger definitive trial.

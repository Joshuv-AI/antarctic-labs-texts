Once-daily oral ritlecitinib or brepocitinib versus placebo in patients with moderate-to-severely active Crohn's disease (PIZZICATO): an international, randomised, phase 2a trial
Authors: Severine Vermeire, Jessica R. Allegretti, Hyo Jong Kim, Millie D. Long, Jaroslaw Leszczyszyn, Stefan Schreiber, Jaroslaw Kierkus, Paulette D. Chandler, Jyoti Ramakrishna, Elena Peeva, Michael S. Vincent, Negin Shojaee, Ping Mahling, Christopher Banfield, Anindita Banerjee, Jeremy D. Gale, Kenneth E. Hung

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundCrohn's disease (CD) has a high unmet need for treatment. Ritlecitinib (Janus Kinase [JAK]3/tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma [TEC] family kinase inhibitor) or brepocitinib (tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2)/JAK1 inhibitor) are promising therapeutic targets. We aimed to evaluate efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetics, and pharmacodynamics of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib versus placebo in patients with moderate-to-severely active CD.MethodsPIZZICATO was a multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, induction-maintenance, phase 2a study in patients with moderate-to-severely active CD. Eligible patients (18–75 years old) had an established diagnosis (endoscopic and histopathology) of ileal, ileocolonic, or colonic active CD at least 3 months before the study started and had documented treatment failure with at least one conventional CD therapy. This study was double-blind during the 12-week induction phase and was open label for the subsequent 52-week open-label extension (OLE) phase. Study participants were enrolled from 140 sites in 26 countries and were randomly assigned (1:1:1) to receive once-daily oral ritlecitinib (200 mg [8 weeks] followed by 50 mg [4 weeks]) or brepocitinib (60 mg [12 weeks]) or placebo in the induction phase, followed by a reduced dose of 50 mg ritlecitinib or 30 mg brepocitinib during the 52-week OLE phase. The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients who achieved Simple Endoscopic Score (SES) for CD (SES-CD 50; defined as ≥50% reduction from baseline) at Week 12 (ritlecitinib versus placebo; brepocitinib versus placebo), and was assessed in the full analysis set (FAS; all participants who received at least one dose of the study drug or placebo). The primary endpoint was changed from clinically-meaningful endoscopic improvement (CMEI) by protocol amendment during study execution. The safety profile of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib was assessed as a secondary endpoint during the induction phase and as a primary endpoint during the OLE phase. Safety was assessed in the safety analysis set (SAS; same as FAS). This trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT03395184, and EudraCT, 2017-003359-43.FindingsBetween Feb 2, 2018, and Oct 19, 2023, 244 patients (median age: 33.0 [18, 71]) were randomly allocated into study groups (ritlecitinib n = 93; brepocitinib n = 72; placebo n = 79) and included in the FAS and SAS. Of 244 participants dosed in the induction phase, 217 (88.9%) completed the induction phase and entered the OLE phase; 216 were dosed in the OLE phase. Disease activity was generally similar across groups and the majority of participants were tumour necrotizing factor (TNF) inhibitor experienced. For the primary outcome at Week 12, a significantly higher proportion of participants achieved SES-CD 50 with ritlecitinib (difference versus placebo: 14.3% [90% CI 4.0–24.5]; p = 0.012) or brepocitinib (21.4% [10.0–32.9]; p = 0.0012) than did those receiving placebo. Regarding safety, during the induction phase, 146 (59.8%) patients reported 334 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs); for the OLE phase, 169 (78.2%) patients reported 521 TEAEs. Most TEAEs were mild or moderate. Most common being severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) test positive (six patients, 6.5%). In the ritlecitinib 200/50 mg group, headache (five patients, 6.9%) in the brepocitinib group, and worsening of underlying CD (eight patients, 10.1%) in the placebo during the induction phase; and worsening of underlying CD in the ritlecitinib 200/50 mg to 50 mg (12 patients, 14.3%) and brepocitinib 60 mg to 30 mg (11 patients, 17.2%) group, and SARS-CoV-2 infection (six patients, 16.7%) in the placebo to ritlecitinib 50 mg group during the OLE phase. No deaths were reported.InterpretationRitlecitinib and brepocitinib showed significant improvement in the primary outcome versus placebo for treatment of moderate-to-severely active CD, with acceptable safety profiles. This should be considered as preliminary evidence of efficacy. It is not known how effective these treatments are likely to be in patients with milder disease. Further studies are required for both molecules and to better understand responder and non-responder populations.Funding10.13039/100004319Pfizer Inc.

Research in context

Evidence before this study

A role for pro-inflammatory cytokines in the pathophysiology of Crohn's disease (CD) is supported by extensive literature, suggesting that modulation of cytokine signalling through the inhibition of members of the Janus Kinase (JAK) family might have therapeutic benefit. At the inception of the study (May 2017), review of the clinical literature published in the English language using PubMed search for “Crohn's Disease Treatment” as keyword yielded existing therapies and confirmed that no clinical trials had been conducted in CD with inhibitors of JAK or TEC kinases that had selectivity profiles comparable to ritlecitinib or brepocitinib. Prior to the present study, the only clinical investigation of this hypothesis came from inhibition of JAK1, either selectively (with filgotinib) or in combination with JAK3 (with tofacitinib), and this data gap provided the motivation to investigate the potential efficacy of inhibitors with alternative selectivity over JAK kinases in CD.

Added value of this study

This is the first multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel -group study in CD, comparing the effects of ritlecitinib or brepocitinib with placebo as induction and chronic therapy in the treatment of moderate-to-severely active CD. Both ritlecitinib (JAK3/tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma [TEC] family kinase inhibitor) and brepocitinib (tyrosine kinase 2 [TYK2]/JAK1 inhibitor) demonstrated significant efficacy versus placebo across multiple endoscopic and clinical endpoints, together with acceptable safety and tolerability in a population containing many treatment-refractory patients. This study provides the first evaluation of two novel, subtype-selective JAK inhibitors for both induction and long-term treatment in moderate-to-severely active CD, using an innovative umbrella trial design that optimises benefit-risk assessment and minimises placebo exposure.

Implications of all the available evidence

Based on the results of this study, ritlecitinib and brepocitinib might be considered potential new therapeutic options in patients with moderate-to-severely active CD. However, further larger and longer-term studies are warranted.

Introduction

Crohn's disease (CD) is one of the two main subphenotypes of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) with a high unmet need for treatment due to frequent acute exacerbations causing transmural inflammation within the gastrointestinal tract1,2 and complications including intestinal obstruction, fistulas, abscesses, anal fissures, and ulcers.3 Most patients experience bouts of remission and relapse at unpredictable times with functional impact on health-related quality of life, which is directly correlated with CD activity.1,4 The goal of CD treatment is to reduce inflammation and hence induce and maintain remission.5

Dysregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokine-dependent Janus Kinase (JAK) signalling pathways is an underlying mechanism in CD manifestation.6 JAK (with four isoforms: JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 [TYK2]) signalling pathways regulate fundamental biological processes, including apoptosis, proliferation, development, and differentiation of immune cells.6,7 Thus, targeting mediators of JAK signalling pathways could be a therapeutic strategy in CD management, although not all JAK inhibitors have shown efficacy in CD.8 Upadacitinib, the only JAK inhibitor approved for CD, inhibits JAK1, thus inhibiting the signalling of cytokines that utilise JAK1 when paired with either JAK2, JAK3, or TYK2.9, 10, 11 Therefore, based on their selectivity profiles, different JAK inhibitors may impact CD differently.12

Ritlecitinib (PF-06651600) is a highly selective, multi-kinase inhibitor that selectively inhibits JAK3, so blocking the signalling of pro-inflammatory cytokines implicated in CD pathophysiology, such as interleukins (IL)-7, IL-9, IL-15 and IL-21, whilst sparing immunoregulatory cytokines such as IL-10 that are blocked by JAK1 inhibition.9,13 It also irreversibly inhibits the TEC (tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma) kinase family thereby inhibiting inflammatory cytokine release and cytolytic activity of immune cells (CD8+ T and natural killer cells), which play a role in the pathogenesis of CD.9, 10,13,14 This may provide additional efficacy over JAK inhibition alone. Ritlecitinib (50 mg once-daily [QD]) was approved in the USA for severe alopecia areata in individuals aged at least 12 years based on the ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 study.15,16 In phase 2 studies, efficacy and acceptable safety profiles were observed in patients with rheumatoid arthritis,13 ulcerative colitis (UC),10 and vitiligo.17

Brepocitinib (PF-06700841) is a dual JAK inhibitor and inhibits human TYK2 and JAK1, thereby impacting the signalling of type 1 interferons, IL-12, and IL-23,18 in addition to several other cytokines impacted by JAK1 inhibition. Its potential therapeutic utility was observed in patients with psoriatic arthritis,19 alopecia areata,20 hidradenitis suppurativa,21 and plaque psoriasis.22 It has been hypothesised that selective and combined inhibition of TYK2/JAK1 could be an effective treatment for CD.18,23 Both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib demonstrated efficacy in patients with UC in a phase 2b study.10

The aim of the study was to evaluate efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), and pharmacodynamics (PD) of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib versus placebo in patients with moderate-to-severely active CD.

Methods

Study design and ethics

The PIZZICATO study was a phase 2a, multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled, induction-maintenance, parallel-group CD study. It involved a representative population of patients with moderate-to-severely active CD across 140 sites in 26 countries: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Canada, Croatia, Czech Republic, Georgia, Germany, Hungary, Italy, Republic of Korea, Lebanon, Poland, Russian Federation, Saudi Arabia, Serbia, Slovakia, South Africa, Spain, Switzerland, Tunisia, Turkey, Ukraine, United Arab Emirates, and the US. The umbrella design enables the evaluation of two experimental therapies within the same study, across a contemporaneously recruited population and offers a more ethical and efficient design by allowing the sharing of the control/placebo group across the experimental therapies.24 We evaluated the efficacy, safety, PK, and PD of oral ritlecitinib (200 and 50 mg, QD) and brepocitinib (60 and 30 mg, QD) compared with placebo.

The study protocol (B7981007) was approved by an institutional ethics committee or institutional review board at each study site. Further details are given in the Supplementary Materials (Appendix 1). The study protocol is available in the Supplementary Materials (Appendix 2). The study was conducted according to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the CONsolidated Standards Of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines for randomised controlled trials. Prior to enrolment, all patients, or their legally authorised representative(s), provided written informed consent.

Study participants

Key inclusion criteria were loss of response, inadequate response, or intolerance to at least one conventional treatment option including steroids, immunosuppressants, or biologics (such as TNF inhibitors, integrin inhibitors, and IL-12/23 inhibitors). Patients (18–75 years old) with an established diagnosis (endoscopic and histopathology) of ileal, ileocolonic, or colonic active CD ≥ 3 months before the study were included. Additional criteria are presented in the Supplementary Materials.

Patients were excluded if they had diagnosis of indeterminate colitis, microscopic colitis, ischaemic colitis, infectious colitis, radiation colitis, diverticular disease, UC (or clinical findings suggestive of UC), presence of active fistulae or intra-abdominal or perineal abscesses, strictures with obstructive symptoms, short bowel syndrome, colonic adenomas, dysplasia, or neoplasia. Additional criteria are presented in the Supplementary Materials.

Randomisation and masking

This study was double-blind during the 12-week induction phase and open label for the subsequent 52-week extension phase. After screening (up to 6 weeks), a 12-week induction phase, a 52-week open-label extension (OLE) phase, and a 4-week follow-up phase was planned (Fig. 1). Patients were randomly assigned (2:1) to oral QD doses of 1) ritlecitinib (200 mg for 8 weeks followed by 50 mg for 4 weeks) or matched placebo, or 2) brepocitinib (60 mg for 12 weeks) or matched placebo through the sponsor's interactive response technology system (interactive web-based response [IWR]; resulting in an overall randomisation of 1:1:1 to ritlecitinib, brepocitinib, or placebo).Fig. 1Trial design. Induction phase: FAS: n = 244, SAS = 244. OLE phase: FAS: n = 217, SAS: n = 216. AE, adverse event; OLE, open-label extension.

Trial design. Induction phase: FAS: n = 244, SAS = 244. OLE phase: FAS: n = 217, SAS: n = 216. AE, adverse event; OLE, open-label extension.

The allocation ratio for randomisation was stratified by baseline disease activity/extent (absence of isolated ileal disease and baseline simple endoscopic score for CD [SES-CD] either ≤15 or >15, or presence of isolated ileal disease), steroid use at baseline (yes/no), and prior treatment with TNF inhibitors (experienced/naïve). The analysis was conducted by combining the data from both the placebo groups for a more comprehensive evaluation.

The OLE phase followed the induction phase. Patients receiving a placebo during the induction phase had the opportunity to receive active treatment during the OLE phase. Patients receiving ritlecitinib or matched placebo in the induction phase received ritlecitinib 50 mg QD during the OLE phase. Similarly, patients receiving brepocitinib or matched placebo during the induction phase received brepocitinib 30 mg during the OLE phase.

Following the protocol amendment 5 (27 July 2021), a strategic business decision was taken by the study sponsor to discontinue the brepocitinib arm. Consequently, no new patients were randomised to receive brepocitinib or its matching placebo during the induction phase, nor was brepocitinib administered in the OLE phase, which resulted in a higher number of patients ultimately being randomised to ritlecitinib. However, patients who were already randomised to brepocitinib continued with the allocated treatment through to completion. This discontinuation of recruitment into the brepocitinib (or matching placebo) treatment arm has minimal impact on power for the comparison of ritlecitinib with placebo but does reduce the power of the comparison of brepocitinib with placebo. However, the sample size remains sufficient to enable conclusions to be drawn from the results. Specifically, this decision was not based on safety, efficacy, or quality concerns and did not compromise the overall benefit/risk for participants in this or other clinical trials.

Procedures

SES-CD score was used as an endoscopic scoring system to assess CD activity, with evaluations performed at screening, Week 12, Week 64, and early withdrawal visits. Scores were determined by central readers across five bowel segments, and the median SES-CD score was used for eligibility and outcome assessments. Further details on SES-CD score, biopsy collection, and patients’ eDiary entry are provided within the Supplementary Materials (page 4). Clinically-meaningful endoscopic improvement (CMEI) was ≥3 points reduction from baseline in SES-CD score and assessed at Week 12 and Week 64 (assessed among those who achieved response at Week 12).

Crohn's disease activity index (CDAI), a validated tool for assessing treatment response in CD, was calculated using patient-reported outcomes from eDiaries and investigator assessments. Scores were based on data from the 7 days preceding each visit, with adjustments for endoscopy-related visits. Further details are provided within the Supplementary Materials (page 4).

Stool frequency (SF) and abdominal pain (AP) scores were derived from patient eDiary data for the 7 days preceding each visit, with adjustments for endoscopy-related visits to exclude bowel preparation periods. Endoscopic remission (SES-CD ≤2), and proportion of patients achieving mucosal healing (complete absence of ulcers) was assessed at Week 12 and Week 64 (assessed among those who achieved response at Week 12). Further details are provided within the Supplementary Materials (page 5).

Outcomes

The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving SES-CD 50 (defined as ≥50% reduction from baseline in SES-CD score) at Week 12. The primary endpoint was changed by protocol amendment during study execution, from CMEI to SES-CD 50, to enable efficient comparison with contemporary and emerging clinical trials in CD.

Secondary efficacy endpoints were mean change from baseline in SES-CD score and CMEI (≥3 points reduction from baseline in SES-CD score).25 Details of other secondary efficacy endpoints (SES-DC 25, endoscopic remission, and mucosal healing) are provided within the Supplementary Materials (page 6).

The safety profile of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib was assessed as a secondary endpoint during the induction phase and as a primary endpoint during the OLE phase. Adverse events (AEs), serious AEs (SAEs), withdrawals due to AEs, treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), treatment-related AEs, AEs of special interest; and the incidence and severity of laboratory abnormalities were evaluated.

Exploratory efficacy endpoints were the proportion of patients with a CDAI-100 response and proportion of patients with CDAI remission (defined as CDAI <150) among patients with baseline CDAI ≥220, clinical response (≥30% reduction from baseline in AP or SF; neither worse than baseline), and clinical remission (SF ≤1.5 and AP ≤1; neither worse than baseline). All assessed at Week 12 and Week 64 (assessed among those who achieved response at Week 12).

Plasma concentration–time profiles of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib during the induction phase and OLE phase were analysed as exploratory endpoints. Further details are given within the Supplementary Materials (page 5). The changes in the inflammation biomarkers including high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) and faecal calprotectin (FCP) were analysed from the baseline through Week 12 and Week 64 in blood or faecal samples and presented as placebo-adjusted least square mean (LSM) ratio to baseline (90% confidence intervals [CI]). Other PD parameters are provided in the Supplementary Materials (page 5).

Statistical analysis

To detect a greater SES-CD 50 response rate in ritlecitinib compared with placebo at Week 12, a sample size of 70 patients in the ritlecitinib arm and 60 in the placebo arm were planned to have about 80% probability with a 5% one-sided type 1 error. For brepocitinib, a sample size of 50 patients in the brepocitinib arm and 60 in the placebo arm were planned to have about 72% probability with the same type-1 error. Therefore, based on the sample size calculation using a two-sample test of proportions, approximately 230–250 patients were planned to be enrolled in the induction phase. The efficacy analyses were conducted on the FAS, defined as all randomised patients who received at least one dose of the study drug or placebo. For binary data comparing treatment and placebo groups, the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test was used to calculate CIs to provide an estimate of treatment differences (risk differences) and the corresponding two -sided 90% CIs adjusted for baseline disease activity/extent with the minimum risk weights.26 The prespecified values of the 90% CIs and the corresponding threshold of the statistical significance (one-sided p < 0.05) are used in the reporting of results of this early-phase clinical trial and were calculated using Chan and Zhang or Blyth-Still-Castella exact methods suitable for small samples.27, 28, 29 A two-sided type 1 error of 0.1 (equivalent to 0.05 one-sided) was used to balance speed and cost in this exploratory/learning phase, aligning with industry standards for early-phase CD trials. For both SES-CD 50 and CDAI remission at Weeks 12 and 64, within patients' comparisons were conducted using McNemar's test for active treatment sequences, to assess long term effect.

Missing data due to COVID-19 related reasons were excluded. For SES-CD 50 at week 12, non-response imputation was used for all other missing data. For CDAI-related endpoints, missing values were set to be non-responder only for missing due to discontinuation of treatment, and missing data due to other reasons were excluded.

The safety analysis set (SAS) comprised all patients who received at least one dose of the study drug or placebo. Safety outcomes were summarised descriptively. Categorical outcomes (on-treatment AEs, SAEs, and discontinuation due to AEs) were summarised by patient counts and percentages. PK concentrations in the treatment period were summarised with summary statistics using patients of the PK set (defined, separately for each treatment arm, as all enrolled patients who received at least one dose of study drug and in whom at least one plasma concentration value was reported). PD data was summarised descriptively, and any changes from the baseline were reported.

An external data monitoring committee (E-DMC), responsible for reviewing the accumulating safety data and proposing any necessary protocol modifications to ensure participants’ safety, was utilised for this study. The trial was registered at ClincialTrials.gov, NCT03395184 and EU clinical trials register, EudraCT No. 2017-003359-43.

Prespecified sensitivity analysis of participants achieving SES-CD 50 at Week 12 was conducted using inverse-normal method (full analysis set [FAS], non-responder imputation [NRI]).

Prespecified subgroup analysis of primary efficacy endpoint for biologics experience at Week 12 was also conducted.29 Proportion of patients achieving SES-CD 50 (90% CI) was calculated using Blyth-Still-Casella exact method. Percentage difference from placebo (90% CI) and one-sided p-value of difference from Placebo were calculated using Chan and Zhang exact method.

Post-hoc analysis of participants achieving SES-CD 50 at Week 12 was conducted using a logistic regression model adjusting for covariates including all stratification variables and SES-CD baseline score (FAR, NRI). Variance of the risk difference was estimated by delta method. All other baseline covariates were balanced between active treatment arms and placebo hence were not included.

All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Role of the funding source

In conjunction with external authors, the sponsor of the study (Pfizer, Inc.) had a role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, and writing of the report.

Results

Study participants

Between Feb 2, 2018, and Oct 19, 2023, 645 patients were screened; of whom, 244 were randomly assigned to a study group (ritlecitinib n = 93; brepocitinib n = 72; placebo n = 79) and received at least one dose of study drug(s) or placebo and were included in the FAS and SAS (Fig. 1). Among 244 patients dosed (median age: 33.0 [18, 71]), 217 (88.9%) completed the induction phase and entered the OLE phase; and 216 were dosed in the OLE phase. Dropout rates were comparable between treatment groups in both, the induction and OLE phases; and were slightly lower than in the placebo group in the induction phase. Most patients were 18–44 years of age and there was a slight bias towards male sex. Median disease duration was 9.2 (0.3, 48.2) years in placebo, 7.5 (0.3, 35.1) years in ritlecitinib 200/50 mg QD and 7.4 (0.4, 25.0) years in brepocitinib 60 mg QD groups. Disease activity was generally similar across treatment groups and the majority of participants were TNF inhibitor experienced. Demographic and population characteristics were comparable between groups at baseline, during the induction phase (Table 1) and OLE phase (Table 2). In the induction phase, baseline covariates were mostly balanced between active treatment arms and placebo, except for a slight imbalance in baseline SES-CD score between the brepocitinib and placebo groups. Efficacy

The proportion (90% CIs) of patients achieving SES-CD 50 was significantly higher with ritlecitinib 200/50 mg QD (27.2% [19.6%, 35.7%]) and brepocitinib 60 mg QD (33.8% [25.1%, 43.1%]) versus placebo (12.8% [7.1%, 19.9%]) at Week 12. The primary efficacy endpoint (assessed at Week 12) was statistically significant for both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib treatment groups compared with placebo. At Week 64, the proportions (90% CI) of patients achieving SES-CD 50 were: placebo to ritlecitinib 50 mg: 15.6% (7.8%, 28.7%); ritlecitinib 200/50 mg to 50 mg: 16.0% (9.5%, 24.3%); placebo to brepocitinib 30 mg: 36.7% (22.1%, 52.4%); and brepocitinib: 60 mg to 30 mg 20.7% (12.4%, 30.4%) (Table 3).

The proportion (90% CIs) of patients achieving CMEI (reduction of ≥3 points from baseline in SES-CD) at Week 12 were 42.4% (34.2%, 51.5%), 57.7% (47.6%, 67.2%), and 29.5% (21.7%, 38.9%), in the ritlecitinib, brepocitinib, and placebo groups, respectively (Table 3). The differences (90% CIs) from placebo for CMEI were: ritlecitinib, 13.3% (1.0%, 25.7%; p = 0.0390) and brepocitinib, 29.7% (17.2%, 42.2%; p = 0.0001), respectively.

At Week 64, proportions of patients (90% CI) achieving CMEI response (among those who achieved a response at Week 12) were 27.3% (10.5%, 56.4%) for placebo to ritlecitinib 50 mg, 44.1% (30.7%, 59.5%) for ritlecitinib 200/50 mg to 50 mg, 54.5% (30.2%, 80.0%) for placebo to brepocitinib 30 mg, and 42.1% (28.5%, 56.7%) for brepocitinib 60 mg to 30 mg (Table 3).

At Week 12, CDAI-100 response was observed in significantly more patients in the ritlecitinib (66.7% [55.6%, 76.5%]) and brepocitinib (63.0% [50.0%, 74.5%]) groups than placebo (38.6% [26.4%, 51.1%]) (Fig. 2A). The differences from placebo were: ritlecitinib, 27.2% (10.3%, 44.2%; p = 0.0051) and brepocitinib, 22.4% (4.8%, 40.1%; p = 0.019). Similarly, CDAI remission was also observed in significantly more patients in the ritlecitinib (54.9% [42.5%, 66.9%]) and brepocitinib (54.3% [41.9%, 67.0%]) groups than placebo (20.5% [12.0%, 32.5%]) (Fig. 2B). Compared with placebo (64.6%), the proportion of patients with TEAEs was lower in ritlecitinib group (49.5%) and higher in brepocitinib group (68.1%) (Table 4). Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

Dose-normalised concentration profiles for ritlecitinib from patients with CD suggest similar Cmax values but lower AUC values when CD parameters were compared with those previously reported from UC.10 Brepocitinib PK appeared similar between patients with CD and those previously reported from UC10 (Supplementary Table S4).

At baseline, the median hsCRP ranged from 0.66 mg/dL to 1.85 mg/dL and the median FCP ranged from 1106 μg/g to 1457 μg/g in all treatment groups. At Week 12, ritlecitinib and brepocitinib reduced hsCRP and FCP compared with placebo and the reduction was statistically significant. Through Week 64, decreases from baseline in hsCRP and FCP were observed for all treatment groups. Additional parameters are presented in the Supplementary Materials (page 5).

Discussion

In this 64-week phase 2a trial of patients with moderate-to-severely active CD, both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib demonstrated therapeutic efficacy with significant improvement versus placebo across multiple endoscopic and clinical outcomes in this particularly treatment-refractory patient population, with prior treatment failure after more than one conventional CD therapy. These results were observed despite the hybrid dosing regimen used for ritlecitinib during the 12-week induction phase (200 mg for 8 weeks followed by 50 mg for 4 weeks), which was based on contemporary non-clinical long-term toxicology data supporting 200 mg treatment for only up to 8 weeks. Placebo groups were combined for analysis.

Statistically significant endoscopic improvement in the primary efficacy endpoint SES-CD 50 was observed in both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib arms compared with placebo at Week 12. Significant differences versus placebo were consistently observed in the proportion of patients achieving SES-CD scores, CMEI, CDAI remission, CDAI-100 response, clinical response and remission by SF or AP with ritlecitinib and brepocitinib treatment at Week 12. At Week 64, it appeared that the selected maintenance doses for both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib were unable to sustain the Week 12 effect on SES-CD 50 improvement and CDAI remission. The CDAI-100 response rate, along with clinical response rate and clinical remission rate, also exhibited a decline up to Week 64 across all treatment groups, suggesting that for both agents higher doses than those selected for the chronic dosing period will likely be necessary to maintain or even to increase efficacy beyond Week 12. Thus additional, well-designed studies are needed to optimise the dosing regimen for both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib as treatments for both induction and long-term maintenance of remission in CD.

A phase 3 study conducted with the JAK1 inhibitor upadacitinib in a similar patient population provides extended data on maintaining remission over a year, a vital consideration in CD management.11 In this study, upadacitinib induction (12 weeks) and maintenance (52 weeks) treatment was superior to placebo with a higher percentage of patients achieving CDAI remission with 15 mg upadacitinib (37.3%) and 30 mg upadacitinib (47.6%) compared with placebo (15.1%) at Week 52.11 These data on maintenance of disease remission with upadacitinib provides a critical benchmark for early evaluation of the potential for sustained efficacy with ritlecitinib or brepocitinib. Despite differences in study design, the current study not being designed to evaluate efficacy in maintenance of remission, and doses of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib not being optimised for this objective, the level of efficacy observed in the PIZZICATO study is encouraging and deserving of further characterisation in future studies.

The efficacy of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib in a related inflammatory condition, moderate-to-severely UC, in a phase 2b umbrella study underscore their potential as emerging treatment options for IBD. After 8-weeks of induction therapy, both ritlecitinib and brepocitinib were more effective than placebo, with acceptable short-term safety profiles. Specifically, the proportion of patients achieving modified clinical remission at Week 8 were notable across doses of both drugs. Remission rates were up to 36.0% for ritlecitinib and up to 25.5% for brepocitinib.10 The therapeutic benefit of these drugs were sustained over a treatment period of up to 32 weeks, while maintaining an acceptable safety profile.30 These outcomes highlight the importance of continuing to elucidate the therapeutic profile of these drugs in sub-types of IBD.

Ritlecitinib and brepocitinib had acceptable safety and tolerability profiles that were consistent with previous studies in other autoimmune conditions.10,13,18 In the current study, the proportion of patients with TEAEs was lower in the treatment groups compared with the placebo group. AEs were mostly mild or moderate, with the most common being infections and infestations in the induction phase and gastrointestinal disorders in the OLE phase. The incidence of herpes zoster infection across all treatment groups was similar to observations from a previous phase 2 study in UC (one each in ritlecitinib and brepocitinib).10 No dose-related effects on safety were observed either for ritlecitinib or brepocitinib. Also, no clinically significant findings were observed for any laboratory parameters following dosing with either ritlecitinib or brepocitinib.

AEs known to be associated with JAK inhibition,31 including herpes viral infections, serious infections (cytomegaloviral infection), and URTIs were observed in a similar proportion in both treatment groups in this study during the induction phase. These AEs were also reported in patients who received upadacitinib in a phase 3 study of CD.11 JAK inhibitors may be associated with an increased risk of major CV events and venous thromboembolism in certain patient groups.31 In this study, no thromboembolic events or adjudicated CV events occurred in the ritlecitinib arm and three events (pulmonary embolism, acute myocardial infarction, and superficial vein thrombosis; one event each) were observed in the brepocitinib arm. The relatively small sample size and limited duration of follow up prevent conclusions from being drawn regarding the long-term safety profiles of either ritlecitinib or brepocitinib in CD. Study sample was sufficiently powered (80%) for ritlecitinib and placebo comparison, and slightly underpowered (70%) for brepocitinib and placebo.

This study has many strengths. This is the first assessment of these two novel, second generation, subtype-selective JAK inhibitors, ritlecitinib (JAK3/TEC) and brepocitinib (TYK2/JAK1), in adults with moderate-to-severely active CD, both for induction treatment and as longer-term therapy within the same clinical trial. This allows for a characterisation of the benefit-risk of ritlecitinib and brepocitinib in this population. Ritlecitinib is approved for the treatment of alopecia areata and has safety labelling similar to that of other JAK inhibitors approved for the treatment of IBD. Taking into account the EMA regulatory guidance,32 JAK inhibitors should be prescribed to patients where the benefit-risk ratio is most favourable. The innovative and efficient umbrella study design tested two drugs simultaneously, with direct qualitative assessment of the interventions within the same study, and the 2:1 active drug versus placebo randomisation ratio allowed for exposure of fewer patients to placebo treatment, an attractive feature for participants and physicians. The merging separate placebo groups for the final analysis in umbrella trials is novel and appealing given the difficulties encountered in enrolling participants for IBD clinical trials.10,33,34 While umbrella trials are used predominantly in oncology, their application in this context holds considerable promise.24

This study has a few limitations. An accurate assessment of the efficacy of ritlecitinib in the induction phase might not be revealed by the current study design, as contemporary non-clinical toxicology studies limited the induction dosing of 200 mg ritlecitinib to only 8 weeks. In this study, 200 mg ritlecitinib was followed by 50 mg for 4 weeks. The potential for increased efficacy with a prolonged higher dose induction regimen is suggested by the SES-CD 50 data that indicate a failure of the 50 mg QD dose to maintain induction efficacy or to provide any efficacy during the OLE phase to patients who received placebo during induction. This is also supported by the longitudinal CDAI remission and clinical response/remission by SF or AP data that demonstrate numerically higher ritlecitinib efficacy, as compared with placebo, through 8 weeks, which then decreases through Week 12 associated with decreasing the ritlecitinib dose to 50 mg. Additionally, the approach that excludes participants missing CDAI due to reasons other than early treatment discontinuation works best when the data are missing completely at random (MCAR) or the proportion of missing data is small. Other methods, such as MI, may be explored to enhance inference in the future. Whilst the patient population was well balanced between males and females and was geographically diverse, the majority of patients were White, and a more ethnically diverse set of patients, who better represent the global patient population, should be considered in future studies to avoid limiting generalisability of the results. This study was conducted in patients with moderate-to-severely active CD, and it is not known how effective these treatments are likely to be in patients with milder disease. Moreover, an inherent limitation is that it is a phase 2a study, which, despite the endpoints being consistent with pivotal studies, should be considered as preliminary evidence of efficacy.

Further studies are needed to fully optimise the dosing regimen of these subtype-selective JAK inhibitors, for both induction and maintenance of remission, and to understand their precise mechanisms of action in CD. Future clinical trials should investigate whether the populations that respond to treatment are the same for both drugs, or if the two drugs, which have distinct pharmacological profiles, have distinct responder populations, perhaps reflecting differences in the underlying pathophysiology within the broad umbrella of CD phenotype.

In conclusion, ritlecitinib and brepocitinib were effective compared with placebo for the treatment of moderate-to-severely active CD, with acceptable safety and tolerability profiles.

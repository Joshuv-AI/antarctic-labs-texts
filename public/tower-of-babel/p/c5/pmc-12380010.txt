MYCN amplification defines an aggressive phenotype in IDH‐mutant gliomas
Authors: Xujun Xie, Qin Yan, Fang Wang, Jiabin Lu, Yuandong Zhang, Shaoyan Xi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn alignment with the latest WHO classification system, which underscores the integration of molecular alterations in glioma diagnosis and grading, this study investigates the prognostic significance of MYCN amplification in IDH‐mutant gliomas, a relationship that remains poorly characterized despite its established association with adverse outcomes in various malignancies. A cohort of 190 patients with IDH‐mutant gliomas was analyzed for clinical and pathological characteristics. MYCN amplification status was determined using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) with an MYCN‐specific probe. Survival outcomes were assessed via Kaplan–Meier analysis, while independent prognostic factors were identified through multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models. Tumor morphology was systematically evaluated in cases with MYCN amplification. MYCN amplification was identified in 28 of 190 cases (14.7%), demonstrating a significant correlation with advanced tumor grade and elevated Ki‐67 proliferation indices (p < 0.05). Patients harboring MYCN amplification exhibited markedly reduced overall survival compared to non‐amplified cases (112.13 ± 6.58 versus 91.14 ± 14.96 months, p = 0.001), with this association being particularly pronounced in lower‐grade (WHO grades 2 and 3) IDH‐mutant gliomas (122.12 ± 6.81 versus 47.76 ± 6.58 months, p < 0.001). To address limitations in current grading systems, we propose a refined classification approach that upgrades lower‐grade IDH‐mutant astrocytomas with MYCN amplification to high‐grade status. This MYCN‐based grading system demonstrated significant prognostic stratification (112.84 ± 10.40 versus 77.65 ± 11.15 months, p < 0.001). Morphological analysis revealed that 50% of MYCN‐amplified cases (14/28) exhibited distinct epithelioid features, characterized by abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and nuclear displacement. In conclusion, MYCN amplification emerges as a critical prognostic indicator in IDH‐mutant gliomas, particularly in lower‐grade tumors, and is frequently associated with unique epithelioid histological features. These findings highlight the necessity of incorporating MYCN amplification status into grading paradigms for IDH‐mutant gliomas to enhance prognostic accuracy and inform clinical decision‐making.

Introduction

Diffuse gliomas represent the most prevalent malignant primary intracranial tumors, classified into adult‐type and pediatric‐type gliomas according to the latest WHO classification system. Adult‐type diffuse gliomas are further categorized into astrocytomas with isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutations, oligodendrogliomas with IDH mutations and 1p/19q co‐deletions, and glioblastomas with wild‐type IDH [1]. IDH‐mutant gliomas encompass astrocytomas [central nervous system (CNS) tumors, grades 2–4] and oligodendrogliomas (CNS grades 2 and 3). Oligodendrogliomas are characterized by additional genetic alterations, including mutations in capsicum transcriptional repressor (CIC) and/or far upstream element binding protein 1 (FUBP1) and telomerase reverse transcriptase promoter (TERTp). IDH‐mutant astrocytomas frequently exhibit mutations in alpha‐thalassemia/mental retardation syndrome X‐linked (ATRX) and tumor protein 53 (TP53). Recent advances have identified a spectrum of genetic alterations that are characteristic of IDH‐mutant astrocytomas and correlate strongly with histological features and clinical outcomes [2, 3, 4]. Molecular markers such as cyclin‐dependent kinase inhibitor 2A/B (CDKN2A/B) deletion have been integrated into the grading system for IDH‐mutant gliomas [1, 5]. However, the current WHO grading system for IDH‐mutant astrocytomas has demonstrated limited prognostic utility [6, 7]. Although DNA methylation‐based subclassification has emerged as a promising tool to refine the grading of these tumors, its role remains constrained [7, 8, 9]. Furthermore, molecular alterations including platelet‐derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) amplification, CDKN2A homozygous deletion, and cyclin‐dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) amplification have been identified in lower‐grade IDH‐mutant astrocytomas and are associated with adverse prognostic outcomes [10]. These findings underscore the potential for incorporating additional molecular biomarkers to enhance the precision of the current grading system.

The MYC family of proto‐oncogenes, including MYCN, plays a critical role in tumorigenesis by regulating cellular processes such as growth, proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis [11]. MYCN, in particular, functions as a transcription factor critical for nervous system development during embryogenesis [12]. Recent genomic studies have identified MYCN amplification as an independent prognostic factor in IDH‐mutant astrocytomas [13]. In this study, we investigated IDH‐mutant gliomas, including astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas, to evaluate the prevalence and prognostic significance of MYCN amplification using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Additionally, we also analyzed the morphological features associated with MYCN amplification in these tumors. We aimed to elucidate the prognostic significance of MYCN amplification in IDH‐mutant gliomas and to describe the morphological features of IDH‐mutant gliomas with MYCN amplification, thereby contributing to improved risk stratification.

Materials and methods

Cohort

This retrospective, single‐center study included 190 patients diagnosed with IDH‐mutant gliomas at the Sun Yat‐sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Surgical resection, including total and partial resections; (2) Patients with a definitive diagnosis of IDH‐mutant glioma (WHO grade 2, 3, or 4) with IDH mutation and with or without 1p/19q co‐deletion; (3) Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis of MYCN amplification. The exclusion criteria were (1) recurrent cases and (2) incomplete clinical or pathological data.

Pathological and molecular analysis

The following data were retrieved from the electronic medical records: demographics (sex and age), clinical features (tumor location), treatment (surgical resection, radiotherapy, chemotherapy), pathology (histological grade and type), survival status (dead, alive, recurrence), and time to events (overall survival, OS). IDH mutation status was assessed by immunohistochemistry (IHC) using an anti‐IDH1R132H antibody (clone MX031, 1/200, Maxim, Fuzhou, PR China) and Sanger sequencing with the appropriate primers (IDH1: R 5′‐TGTGTTGA‐GATGGACGCCTATTTG‐3′, F 5′‐TGCCACCAACGACCAAGTCA‐3′; IDH2: R 5′‐CTTCCCACTCCTTGACACCA‐3′, F 5′‐GGCAGACTCCAGAGCCCACA‐3′). The 1p/19q co‐deletion was determined based on FISH analysis with probes for 1p and 19q. The expression of Ki‐67 was detected by IHC with antibody MIB (Labvision, Fremont, CA, USA). For the analysis, age was divided into two groups (<40 and ≥40 years) according to the median age. According to the 5th WHO classification, IDH‐mutant gliomas were classified as astrocytomas (CNS WHO grades 2–4) or oligodendrogliomas (CNS WHO grades 2 and 3). MYCN amplification status was determined based on FISH analysis using MYCN probes (2p24, LBP Medicine Science & Technology, Guangzhou, PR China). MYCN amplification was defined as a greater than four‐fold increase in the ratio of MYCN signals to the number of chromosome 2. Signals were counted in 100 tumor cell nuclei, or 10% of the cells were chosen as the cut‐off point. FISH signals for CDKN2A FISH probe (LBP Medicine Science & Technology) were analyzed. Signals were counted in 100 tumor cell nuclei, and 15% of the cells were chosen as the cut‐off point. In control cells, there were 2 green signals for CSP9 and 2 red signals for CDKN2A. Only 2 green signals were defined as a CDKN2A homozygous deletion. Pathological features were centrally reviewed by two pathologists (XX and SX). The public database data from the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center was accessed from https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31263031/.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 23.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Two‐sided χ 2 tests were performed to compare the clinicopathological features between MYCN‐amplified and MYCN‐non‐amplified patients. Overall survival curves were generated by Kaplan–Meier analysis, and curves were compared using a log‐rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to analyze the independent prognostic factors. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Demographics and clinical characteristics of IDH ‐mutant gliomas

The diagnosis and grading of patients were reclassified in accordance with the 5th edition of the WHO Classification of CNS Tumors. The clinical characteristics of 190 patients with IDH‐mutant gliomas in this cohort are summarized in Table 1. The mean patient age was 40.46 years, with a male‐to‐female ratio of 1.04:1. All lesions were supratentorial, with a predilection for the frontal and temporal lobes. Within the cohort, 122 patients were diagnosed with IDH‐mutant astrocytoma, while 68 had IDH‐mutant oligodendroglioma with IDH mutations and concurrent 1p/19q co‐deletion. Follow‐up data were available for all patients, with a median follow‐up duration of 58.09 months (range: 7.46–166.03 months). Univariate analysis revealed that sex, tumor grade, astrocytoma‐to‐oligodendroglioma ratio, and relapse were significantly associated with the prognosis in IDH‐mutant glioma patients (p < 0.05; Table 2). Immunohistochemical analysis of Ki‐67, utilizing a previously established cutoff of 10% [5], revealed that a high Ki‐67 index was significantly correlated with adverse prognosis (p < 0.05; Table 2). In contrast, gross total resection and adjuvant therapy did not exhibit significant prognostic relevance. Multivariate analysis further identified the astrocytoma‐to‐oligodendroglioma ratio, relapse, and Ki‐67 index as independent prognostic factors.

MYCN amplification in IDH ‐mutant gliomas as an independent prognostic factor

In our cohort, MYCN amplification was detected in 28 patients (14.7%), encompassing both astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas (Figure 1A). MYCN‐amplified gliomas exhibited a significant association with higher tumor grades and increased Ki‐67 indices compared to non‐amplified gliomas (p < 0.001 and p = 0.016, respectively; Table 1). Moreover, gross total resection was more commonly performed in patients with non‐amplified gliomas (p = 0.002). No significant differences were observed in age, sex, diagnosis, or adjuvant therapy between patients with MYCN‐amplified and non‐amplified gliomas (Table 1). Univariate and multivariate analyses demonstrated MYCN amplification as a significant predictor of poorer prognosis, with median survival times of 112.13 ± 6.58 months for non‐amplified gliomas versus 91.14 ± 14.96 months for MYCN‐amplified gliomas (p = 0.001; Figure 2A). Stratification analysis further revealed that MYCN amplification was significantly associated with worse prognosis in IDH‐mutant astrocytoma (97.63 ± 9.00 versus 87.30 ± 16.02 months, p = 0.038). In oligodendroglioma, MYCN amplification was linked to shorter survival, although this association did not reach statistical significance (90.90 ± 3.17 versus 59.87 ± 7.89 months, p = 0.184). Additionally, MYCN amplification was established as an independent prognostic factor in IDH‐mutant glioma patients (p = 0.002; Table 2). Analysis of public database data from the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center glioma data corroborated the association of MYCN amplification with poor prognosis in IDH‐mutant gliomas (p < 0.0001; supplementary material, Figure S1). Subgroup analysis indicated that MYCN amplification was particularly predictive of adverse outcomes in lower‐grade gliomas (grades 2 and 3), with median survival time of 122.12 ± 6.81 months for non‐amplified gliomas compared to 47.76 ± 6.58 months for MYCN‐amplified gliomas (p < 0.001; Figure 2B, Table 3).

Reclassification of lower‐grade IDH ‐mutant astrocytoma with MYCN amplification as high‐grade IDH ‐mutant astrocytoma

In light of the established correlation between MYCN amplification and adverse clinical outcomes in patients with lower‐grade IDH‐mutant astrocytomas, we developed a refined grading system by incorporating MYCN amplification status into the existing classification framework. Specifically, lower‐grade astrocytomas demonstrating MYCN amplification were reclassified as high‐grade tumors (designated as MYCN‐grade 4), whereas those without MYCN amplification maintained their original classification (designated as MYCN‐grade 2 and 3). The diagnostic algorithm for grading IDH‐mutant astrocytomas is presented in Figure 3. Both the conventional grading system and the MYCN‐based classification demonstrated significant associations with patient prognosis (107.09 ± 9.95 versus 81.61 ± 12.33 months, p = 0.006; 112.84 ± 10.40 and 77.65 ± 11.15 months, p < 0.001; Figure 4A, B). Importantly, the MYCN‐based grading system exhibited enhanced prognostic stratification capability compared to the current classification criteria.

Diagnostic approach for grading IDH‐mutant astrocytomas. Histologically, CNS WHO grade 4 tumors are characterized by the presence of necrosis and/or microvascular proliferation. Additionally, the homozygous deletion of CDKN2A and/or CDKN2B is warranted for a diagnosis of CNS WHO grade 4. In this study, we propose that MYCN amplification should also be considered a criterion for upgrading IDH‐mutant astrocytomas to CNS WHO grade 4.

Patients with MYCN amplification often exhibit epithelioid differentiation

The histopathological features of MYCN‐amplified cases within our cohort were independently evaluated by two pathologists. Quantitative analysis revealed that 50% of cases (14/28) demonstrated epithelioid differentiation, characterized by the presence of enlarged, eosinophilic cells with glassy cytoplasm. These cellular elements typically exhibited eccentric nuclei containing prominent nucleoli and condensed chromatin patterns (Figure 1B). Furthermore, the cohort displayed diverse architectural patterns, including pseudopapillary formations and vascular‐like structures. Immunohistochemical analysis identified perivascular lymphocytic infiltration in 32.1% of cases (9/28), while calcific deposits were observed with equivalent frequency (9/28). Notably, necrotic foci and microvascular proliferation were identified in 64.3% of specimens (18/28). A comprehensive summary of these histomorphological characteristics is presented in supplementary material, Table S1.

Discussion

The classification and grading of gliomas have emerged as a critical focus in neuro‐oncology research, particularly concerning their implications for therapeutic strategies and prognostic assessment [14]. The integration of IDH1/2 mutation status into glioma classification was first established in the 4th edition of the WHO Classification of CNS tumors [15]. This framework was further refined in the 5th WHO classification, which incorporated CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion as a grading criterion for IDH‐mutant gliomas [1]. Nevertheless, the limitations of the current grading system have been recognized, prompting extensive investigations into the potential utility of additional molecular biomarkers – such as DNA methylation subclassification, aneuploidy, CDK4, and PDGFRA copy number alterations, in conjunction with CDKN2A/B deletion – could enhance the accuracy of grading for IDH‐mutant gliomas [7, 10, 16].

In this study, we characterized IDH‐mutant gliomas and reclassified them according to the latest diagnostic criteria. Our study demonstrates that tumor classification (astrocytoma versus oligodendroglioma), relapse status, and the Ki‐67 index serve as independent prognostic factors, consistent with prior research [5, 17]. Molecular profiling of IDH‐mutant astrocytomas revealed frequent amplifications in CKD4, CCND2, ERBB2, and MYC, although these alterations did not significantly impact survival outcomes [18]. Furthermore, a genomic profile of IDH‐mutant gliomas identified MYCN amplification as a predictor of poor prognosis, with a prevalence of 6.5% (4/95) of IDH‐mutant astrocytomas, while being absent in oligodendrogliomas, IDH‐mutant gliomas, or 1p/19q co‐deleted astrocytomas [13]. Building on these findings, our study utilized a larger cohort to investigate MYCN amplification, detecting its presence not only in oligodendrogliomas (CNS WHO grade 3) but also across astrocytomas (CNS WHO grades 2–4), with a higher prevalence in the latter. MYCN amplification was strongly associated with advanced tumor grades and increased Ki‐67 indices, highlighting its contribution to the aggressive phenotype of IDH‐mutant gliomas. Both univariate and multivariate analyses confirmed that MYCN amplification is a significant prognostic marker, particularly in lower‐grade IDH‐mutant gliomas. Given the limitations of the existing grading system, our findings advocate for its refinement. We propose integrating MYCN amplification, alongside CDKN2A/2B homozygous deletion, into the molecular genetic criteria for CNS WHO grade 4 IDH‐mutant astrocytomas, thereby enhancing prognostic accuracy. The MYC family of proteins, encompassing c‐MYC, MYCN, and MYCL, are essential basic helix–loop–helix leucine zipper transcription factors that regulate proliferation, differentiation, and tumor metabolism [19, 20]. MYC aberrations are frequently documented in various CNS tumors, such as pediatric high‐grade gliomas and spinal cord ependymomas, and are associated with aggressive behavior and poor outcomes despite intensive treatment [21, 22]. This study is the first to highlight the prognostic significance of MYCN amplification in the CNS WHO grading of IDH‐mutant astrocytomas, proposing its consideration in the stratification and management of these tumors.

Morphological analysis of IDH‐mutant gliomas with MYCN amplification demonstrated the presence of epithelioid components in 50% of cases. Epithelioid glioblastoma (eGBM), a distinct glioblastoma subtype characterized by its aggressive clinical course and unique molecular profile [23], has been reclassified independently from IDH‐mutant gliomas in the updated classification system. Notably, epithelioid IDH‐mutant gliomas have not been previously well described, establishing for the first time a potential association between epithelioid morphology and MYCN amplification; though the underlying biological mechanisms remain to be elucidated. Furthermore, perivascular lymphocyte proliferation – a histological feature commonly observed in pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma (PXA), CNS lymphoma, demyelination diseases, and eGBM [23] was also identified in IDH‐mutant gliomas with MYCN amplification. Additionally, calcification, a significant characteristic of oligodendrogliomas, is also commonly observed in IDH‐mutant astrocytomas with MYCN amplification [24].

This study represents the largest cohort to date in which MYCN amplification has been systematically detected in IDH‐mutant gliomas and integrated into tumor grading. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. The single‐center, retrospective design may introduce selection bias, and the use of FISH rather than next‐generation sequencing (NGS) for MYCN detection may limit the comprehensive molecular characterization of these tumors. The mechanisms by which MYCN amplification contributes to aggressive histological and biological behavior remain poorly understood and warrant further investigation. Future multicenter, prospective studies employing advanced sequencing technologies are necessary to validate these findings and further elucidate the role of MYCN amplification in IDH‐mutant gliomas.

Conclusion

MYCN amplification is a critical prognostic marker in IDH‐mutant gliomas, independently associated with advanced tumor grade and reduced survival, particularly in lower‐grade IDH‐mutant gliomas. These MYCN‐amplified IDH‐mutant gliomas exhibited distinct epithelioid histology. Integrating MYCN status into molecular diagnostics enables refined risk stratification, prompting our proposal to upgrade MYCN‐amplified lower‐grade IDH‐mutant astrocytomas to high grade. This paradigm enhances prognostic accuracy and supports tailored clinical management for IDH‐mutant gliomas.

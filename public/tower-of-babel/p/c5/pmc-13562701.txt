A vision-based MobileNet–GRU framework for continuous monitoring of student engagement and emotional states
Authors: Laila Ginedi, Hesham Yousef Mostafa Ali, Shoayee Dlaim Alotaibi, Ameni Filali, Shamiel H Ibrahim, Ahmed I Taloba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudent engagement and emotional state monitoring remains a major challenge in modern education, particularly in online learning environments where direct instructor supervision is limited. Conventional evaluation approaches, such as surveys, quizzes, and feedback forms, rely heavily on subjective judgment and often fail to capture the dynamic temporal patterns of learner attention and behavioral engagement patterns. To address this limitation, this study proposes a vision-based framework that integrates efficient spatial feature extraction with temporal sequence modeling for continuous engagement analysis. This proposed system uses MobileNet to extract visual and behavioral features from classroom images at minimal computation costs. These frame-level features are then processed using a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), a recurrent neural network designed to model sequential dependencies and temporal changes over time. The combined MobileNet–GRU architecture enables accurate recognition of engagement behaviors through variations in student behavioral patterns and classroom activities using standard camera input. Evaluation of the framework is done based on the Classroom Student Engagement dataset provided by Kaggle, where there are 489 classroom pictures labeled to eight classes of student behavior engagement. The experimental results show that the proposed framework has reached 88% in accuracy, 87% in precision, 89% in recall, and 88% in F1-score, making it better than other traditional frame-based or non-temporal models. The findings confirm the importance of temporal modeling for capturing engagement as a continuously evolving process rather than a static state. By combining efficient computer vision techniques with temporal deep learning, this work provides a strong foundation for intelligent educational systems capable of adaptive and personalized learning support.Keywords: Student engagement monitoring, Computer vision, MobileNet, GRU, Temporal modeling, Real-time learning analyticsSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

Traditionally, education systems have used delayed and subjective information to assess student engagement and their emotional attachment in the process of learning activities. Regular activities in the form of post lecture feedback forms, quizzes, attendance and instructor observation only give a partial picture of interaction between students and learning material1. These methods are not usually able to determine real time changes in attention, motivation and emotional conditions which are vital determinants of learning outcomes2. The idea of engagement is dynamic which will be different over time, and in the content of instructional methods and individual learners. Engagement assessment is further complicated in online and hybrid learning conditions where there is no direct interaction between an instructor and a student3. Due to the growing popularity of the digital education platform, the necessity to monitor student engagement in an automated, objective, and continuous manner has become more and more pressing4. Real time detection of the disengagement can be used to guide the timely pedagogical intercession, instructional effectiveness, and individualized learning experiences5. Therefore, to make contemporary, data-driven education, smart systems that will be able to evaluate student behavior and emotional indicators continuously will be needed.

The current methods of student engagement detection relate across several fields, such as survey analysis, behavioral, physiological signal processing, and machine learning. Simple but inappropriate is the use of questionnaire-based and self-report approaches that cannot be used in real-time monitoring6. EEG-based sensor methods, heart rate sensor methods, and eye-tracking sensor methods are objective and yield quantifiable metrics, but demand specialized equipment that makes them difficult to scale and impractical7. As artificial intelligence continues to improve, computer vision-based mechanisms have come out as a promising alternative, as the analysis of facial expressions, the position of the head, and eye movement allow the computer to detect non-intrusive engagement8. Traditional vision-based models are mostly based on convolutional neural networks that are used to detect spatial features on the frames of individual videos. Nevertheless, these approaches typically consider engagement as a fixed characteristic and cannot capture time-dependencies9. Prior research works on Haar wavelets and local binary patterns based facial expression recognition systems have been carried out to provide an effective method for robust and discriminative feature extraction in emotion analysis system10. Likewise, multi-level Haar wavelets based machine learning systems used together with template matching algorithms provide an efficient method for facial feature extraction with low computational cost11. Even though recurrent architectures like LSTM have been investigated to overcome this limitation, they are computationally expensive and therefore are not as applicable in real time educational systems.

The issues by providing a vision-based real-time engagement and emotion monitoring framework designed to combine MobileNet, a spatial feature extraction network, and a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network, a temporal modeling network. MobileNet is chosen based on the lightweight nature and efficiency of the architecture and can be effectively used to cover real-time applications without reducing the quality of feature representations. GRU facilitates the appropriate modelling of chains of dependencies by learning the temporal changes of expressions and behavioral features of the face within video frames. The proposed system will enable the ongoing detection of student engagement levels and emotional conditions on various learning spaces through the classroom behavioral engagement patterns. The methodology creates the balance between precision and computing speed, which offers a scaling solution to online, hybrid, and classroom-based education. The framework provides the basis of adaptive learning systems that can dynamically respond to the patterns of engagement of students.

Problem statement

Although there has been an increasing use of digital and hybrid learning platforms, there is still a high difficulty in gauging student engagement in real-time. The conventional methods of evaluation are not immediate and objective and as such, instructors are unable to react to disengagement in time12. Although EEG, among other physiological sensors, can provide detailed insights into cognition, it is limited in popularity due to its intrusive nature, high cost and complexity of operation. Engagement detectors that use vision provide a non-invasive alternative; nevertheless, most current models concentrate on the frame-by-frame analysis, disregarding the time aspect of engagement. Interaction is not a singular occurrence but a process that is ongoing and is affected by changing mental and emotional conditions13. The predictions that are gained using the models that cannot capture a temporal relationship tend to be unstable or inconsistent. Besides, sophisticated deep learning models can be high accuracy, however, hard and costly to implement in practice. Thus, a light but strong framework that would model spatial and temporal engagement cues effectively and with accuracy in real-life education contexts is necessary.

Motivation of the study

This research can be explained by the growing necessity of intelligent educational technologies that are able to adjust to the needs of learners in real-time. With the increased use of online and hybrid learning environments, the instructor is not as visible to the level of engagement of the student and, as a result, cannot easily intervene to address low engagement levels. Immediate feedback can be given through automated engagement monitoring systems, which allow making pedagogical decisions using data. The development of computer vision and deep learning has rendered such analysis of facial expressions and behavioral cues unobtrusive with the use of ordinary cameras. The choice of accuracy, computational efficiency, and temporal awareness is however a challenge. MobileNet is an efficient architecture that can be used to extract features in real-time, whereas GRU is a lighter architecture that can be used in place of the traditional recurrent networks. The rationale of this work is the necessity to unite these strengths into a single framework that would consider both dynamic spatial and temporal engagement and would set the stage of responsive and personalized learning with minimal exerted computational burden.

Significance of the study

The research will add to the body of knowledge regarding intelligent educational systems; it offers a practical and effective method of real-time engagement and monitoring of the emotions of students in a scale-able manner. The proposed framework combines MobileNet and GRU to overcome the shortages of the standstill and resource-demanding models and provides a balanced solution that can be implemented to the real-world application. It has a methodology that empowers the assessment of continuous engagement, which facilitates timely interventions, instruction and better learning results. The results reveal the significance of the temporal modeling in the engagement detection process and offer the existing empirical evidence to use the lightweight architectures to operate the real-time application. In addition, the suggested framework provides a starting point for future extensions, such as attention-based Transformer models, and adaptive instructional frameworks. This study is important to teachers, developers of learning platforms, and researchers aiming to improve personalized education with data-driven information and be at the same time scalable and efficient.

Related works

Y. Qi, introduce14 a cascaded network of computer vision based analysis, which combines gaze estimation, facial expression recognition, and action recognition in order to assess student engagement in online classrooms. There is also multimodal behavioral data processing, which combines the data to determine the state of attention and categorize the level of engagement more holistically. According to experimental results, the proposed approach is better than benchmark models (LRCN and C3D) and has an increased accuracy in identifying student engagement. The key strength of the approach is that its multimodal fusion approach circumvents the weakness of single feature or single model evaluation strategies and offers a more viable measure of learning engagement. Nevertheless, there could be certain constraints of the method as it is computationally complex, sensitive to video quality, and less effective in generalization as applied to unconstrained reality online learning settings with varying lighting, camera angles, and student behavior.

P. Vanneste et al.15 offer an approach to measure student engagement through computer vision to analyze both the behaviour of individual students and classroom interaction patterns in synchronous hybrid classroom setting. The strategy looks at behaviors that can be observed at the individual level like taking notes and raising hands and the group level like symmetry, response times and intersections of eye-gaze. The findings reveal that personal behaviors are hard to identify with consistency and are not highly related to self-reported engagement in students. Measures of collective behavior can be measured with greater consistency; but the relationship between them and engagement is low. The main strength of the study is its dual-level approach that expands the knowledge beyond individual actions of students and emphasizes the intricacy of the engagement measurement. However, the approach is vulnerable to poor empirical association with perceived engagement and excessive dependence on the behavioral indicators, which highlights the necessity to combine emotional and cognitive aspects in the modeling of engagement in the future.

M. U. Ucar and E. Ozdemir present16 a real-time computer vision-based system to identify students and their engagement through analysis of facial features, head pose, and Eye Aspect ratios based on camera inputs. The technique correlates the attention of students with the direction of the gaze to the teacher or camera and classifies engagement with the help of machine learning. Experimental tests of the UPNA Head Pose Database display high face recognition rates with the Local Binary Patterns technique, whereas the engagement classification with the help of a support vector machine achieves an accuracy of 72.4%. The solution has the benefits of real time monitoring, independent classroom functions and applicability in real life and online learning. The system however is significantly dependent on the use of head pose and gaze direction, which can simplify engagement and its moderate accuracy in classification suggests limited ability to deal with diverse behaviors, lighting, and factors of engagement that affect emotions or cognitive ability.

M. N. Hasnine suggest a17 computer vision method to identify student engagement in online learning through the extraction and analysis of the emotional state of learners in the lecture videos. The approach uses a trained convolutional neural network to identify seven emotional expressions and translates the expressions to three levels of engagement, high engagement, and non-engagement. The system is tested on a short online lecture video with several students and it is able to process both real-time and offline video streams and visualize patterns of engagement in a good way. One of the benefits of the method is its emphasis on emotional measures of engagement that are not based on the data of the learning management system that allows prompt instructional support. The research is however constrained by the evaluation data size, video length and may be subject to the factors of facial visibility, light and cultural differences in the display of emotions.

Z. Li introduces the18 computer vision-based engagement evaluation which is aimed at measuring student engagement in online and asynchronous learning settings via facial expression analysis. The method involves facial expression identification of learners within a web-based learning platform to capture the facial expression changes of the learners during the education sessions and present the patterns to be graphically analyzed instructionally. The findings show that the system can be used successfully to point out the areas of learning material where a decrease in engagement might occur and thus help instructors to determine the areas in which they should improve their pedagogy. One of the major benefits of such a technique is that it can be used in both asynchronous environments, where an immediate response is constrained, and that it can be extended to the case of corporate training, which aims at emotional feedback. Nevertheless, the system mostly depends on cues of facial expression that might not indicate cognitive engagement in totality and its performance might be influenced by the quality of the camera used, the obedience of learners, privacy, and disparities in the expression of emotions among different people.

M. Irfan is a19 proposed hybrid system of real-time engagement detection in virtual classrooms based on facial biometrics, computer vision, and deep learning techniques. The framework presupposes creating a new eye-gaze dataset based on webcam photographs, using HOG and Linear SVM to find and detect faces, convolutional neural network to identify gaze direction, and OpenPose with MobileNetV1 to detect body poses. The engagement is defined through fusion of gaze and posture features into three levels of engagement. The findings show the accuracy of gaze classification is high at 99.50% and the total engagement detection accuracy at 90%. The major strengths of the method are real-time functionality, multimodal integration of features, and educator-performing feedback to optimize the process of learning. Nonetheless, the quality of the webcams, the controlled conditions in which the viewing takes place, and the cooperation of the user may influence the performance of the system, and the factors of emotional and cognitive engagement are not explicitly modelled.

Z. Abdelhadi presents20 TeacherEye, an analytical system based on AI, which is used to track the engagement and behavior of students in online classes and tests by analyzing real-time video and audio streams together. The approach combines cutting-edge face detection and identification with vision-based behavior analysis, such as focus tracking, emotion recognition, sleep detection, phone use detection, and multi-person detection, with speech transcribed and analyzed with the help of a large language model to show potential suspicious behavior. The system also uses a vision-language model to suggest the correct actions of the instructors and logs of behavior. The findings indicate the possibility of a multimodal, holistic monitoring model that can facilitate the assessment of engagement as well as academic honesty. The main benefits are real time operation, multimodal fusion and automated decision support to the educators. Nonetheless, the method can create privacy and ethical issues, relies on high-quality audio-video information and might be a challenge when it comes to scaling and generalizing the method in different learning settings and cultures.

Z. A. Ahmed suggests21 the use of an automated real-time system based on video to identify student interaction in the online learning classroom by monitoring the behavior of eye gaze using webcam input. The procedure trains a MobileNet model using an eye-image dataset to recognize gaze-related engagement states, and subsequently, it is combined with the Viola-Jones face detection algorithm and OpenCV to perform it in real-time. The validation performance is high as shown by the experimental results, with the MobileNet model scoring about 99% accuracy in eye-gaze classification, which allows to detect engaged and disengaged states with high accuracy. One of the main benefits of the method is that it has a lightweight architecture, which can be effectively deployed on any standard laptop camera with little overhead processing and immediate feedback provided by the use of an alert mechanism. The system, however, is relying on eye-gaze information alone, and it can therefore be less engaging than it might have been, as well as its performance can be decreased through the course of head movements, variations in lighting, occlusions, and lack of emotional/cognitive engagement modeling.

M. Haque introduces22 the BrutNet, a deep learning structure to achieve automatic violence detection and classification through video sequence by the use of spatiotemporal feature learning. The procedure makes use of the time-distributed convolutional layers to obtain frame-level features of short video clips that are then encoded and worked out through a gated recurrent unit to obtain the temporal dynamics followed by binary classification with the help of dense layers. Experimental evidence shows that BrutNet has a test accuracy of 90, which is better than a previous version of LSTM-based ResNet50 on a high-resolution violence detecting dataset. The method also has benefits which include successful spatiotemporal modelling, less overfitting due to dropout regularization and better performance with complex video patterns. The model however needs a large amount of computing power to train, uses labeled video data, and only supports binary classification which can limit its applicability to less subtle or realistic violence cases with a wider range of contextual differences.

W. Zhang presents23 a sentiment analysis framework of online public opinion monitoring, which involves using an emotion computing algorithm and a deep learning classification model. The approach initially gives Chinese words an emotional intensity with the help of a binary tree-based organization to capture dependency relations and handle feature sparsity and fine-grained negative emotions. The resultant full text enriched features are subsequently fed into a CNN-LSTM model with the convolutional layers generating local semantic patterns and LSTM layers maintaining long-term contextual dependencies. As the experimental findings show, the given model records lower and more competitive rates in sentiment classification than traditional procedures and classifiers, and works well in the real emergency management situations. Its main strengths are enhanced processing of the scarce short texts, enhanced portrayal of complex negative emotions, and relevance to policy-focused emotion regulation problems. Nevertheless, it is methodologically Chinese-language specific, and needs well-planned linguistic material, and might be prevented by scalability problems in a situation where it has to adapt to multilingual or fast-changing online discourse.

M. S. Abdallah24 suggests a streamlined deep learning-oriented real-time dynamic sign language detection system optimized towards a lean resource challenged mobile phone. The approach combines MediaPipe to extract hand and pose landmarks efficiently and use GRU and one-dimensional CNN models to extract time and space gesture features. A preprocessing plan equalizes frame sequences and balances depth and location deformities in order to enhance recognition strength. A new American sign dataset, DSL-46, and the known LSA64 and LIBRAS-BSL datasets are used in the evaluation of the system. The experimental performance is high with recognition accuracies of 98.8, 99.84, and 88.40 over the respective datasets with the ability to process in real-time. Its main benefits are lightweight architecture, fast inference, and mobile deployment, which are part of accessibility by the hearing-impaired user. Nevertheless, the method might be constrained in dealing with gestures of very high complexity, cross cultural differences in signs and also manage very large vocabulary sets without further training information (see Table 1).

MobileNet–GRU based real-time engagement detection framework

The main aim of the current study is to structure and deploy a computer-vision-based platform that has the potential to monitor student engagement and behavioral status in different learning contexts in real-time. Old methods of measuring student engagement usually utilize post-lesson assessment, surveys, or observation by the teacher. These are subjective and delayed methods thereby limiting the opportunity of undertaking timely interventions in the learning process. Conversely, engagement measurement can be conducted continuously, automatically, objectively through the application of artificial intelligence and computer vision techniques, which it is possible to adjust the instructions, responding to the needs of students in real time.

The approach to be used in this research project is also designed to suit the problem of detecting and analyzing unobtrusive behavioral features in video streams in real time. This is done through a multi-step procedure that entails first acquiring the dataset and then preprocessing it therefore carefully in order to guarantee the quality and consistency of the input data. The consecutive steps aim at isolating pertinent spatial features with the help of the MobileNet architecture that is especially effective and powerful in terms of its applications in real-time. GRUs are then used to model these features in time in order to identify the temporal patterns of engagement and behavioral dynamics.

The methodology is also aimed at constructing a predictive model as well as making it practically applicable in the actual classroom settings are shown in Fig. 1. All the stages of the pipeline, such as data preparation to temporal modeling, have been described with the objective of obtaining a high prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Besides, the system will be designed to be deeply integrated with adaptive learning systems, which will enable a dynamic adjustment of what and how one learns, depending on the level of real time engagement. The organized methodology further offers a base to further extensions, such as superior attention systems, reinforcement learning-related adaptation that will further add to personalization and the effectiveness of learning. The existing system makes extensive use of facial expressions and eye gaze behavior because these have a close link with attention levels and can be implemented easily without being computationally complex. Although other behavioral elements like body postures and interactive elements could also be used, they were omitted from the existing model in order to keep computation simple.

Dataset collection

The dataset used in this study is the Classroom Student Engagement Dataset available on Kaggle25. The dataset was originally developed at Lahore Garrison University for automated classroom engagement analysis and consists of 489 classroom images extracted from real classroom video footage. The images were manually annotated using the Roboflow annotation platform with more than 4,500 object annotations representing different student behavioral activities and classroom postures. The dataset contains eight behavioral categories namely look_forward, turn_head, using_device, read, write, sleep, stand, and handrise, which represent different levels of student attention, participation, and engagement during classroom learning sessions. The dataset includes variations in illumination, pose, occlusion, and classroom background conditions, enabling the proposed framework to learn robust visual engagement patterns under realistic classroom environments. During preprocessing, corrupted samples and annotation inconsistencies were removed to improve data quality and model reliability. Sequential frame groups were constructed from extracted classroom video frames to support temporal modeling using the GRU network. The dataset was divided into training, validation, and testing subsets using a standard 70%:15%:15% split for model training and performance evaluation (see Fig. 2).

Face detection and cropping.

The second step of preprocessing is the isolation of the facial area on each video frame since the facial expressions are the most informative features of student involvement and emotional conditions. The detection of faces is done with the help of strong algorithms like MTCNN (Multi-task Cascaded Convolutional Networks). Face detection for the experimental environment was achieved through the use of MediaPipe Face Detection, which was chosen for its compactness, fast inference times, and reliable performance in real-time classroom settings. The media pipeline can reliably detect faces with minimal motion and changes in lighting, making it useful for tracking user participation in classroom environments on normal hardware. In the case of an image with more than one face, a single face of a student is allowed to be retained according to frame-to-frame consistency in the tracking. Frames whose face cannot be identified or partially obstructed are interpolated between the neighboring frames or removed. This makes the GRU input sequences to have temporal continuity. All the faces that are detected are resized to a standard resolution (e.g., 224 × 224 pixels) to fit the input specifications of MobileNet, and the aspect ratio is preserved to prevent any distortion of facial features. There is also face detection that is accompanied by alignment methods in order to adjust the slight rotations and tilts so that the eyes and mouth can always be on the same position in the frames. This correspondence enhances the stability of features extraction, enabling the model to be able to learn spatial patterns. The output obtained after cropping and aligning faces is the fundamental input of the stage of extracting spatial features.

Frame augmentation and frame normalization

Upon face detection and cropping, normalization and augmentation of each frame is done to improve model generalization are shown in Eq. (4). Normalization converts pixel values within a standardized range, usually 0–1 or −1 to 1, making the gradient changes in training more constant and allowing a pre-trained MobileNet to be used with the new weights. The use of standardization in all the frames minimizes the variability caused by the lighting variations, camera exposure and inconsistency in colors. Data augmentation is used to improve the variety of training set and avoid overfitting. The most popular augmentation methods are horizontal flipping, random cropping, brightness adjustments, and slight rotations. These changes model natural variations in classroom situations, including movement of head, posture of students or even change of lighting in the environment, which enhances the generalization of the model to hidden data. Extractions of Computer Vision Features.

Computer vision feature extraction

MobileNet architecture is chosen in this research because of its efficiency, lightweight architecture and effective operation at real-time image analysis are shown in Eq. (5). MobileNet also employs depth wise separable convolutions (also known as depth wise-separable convolutions) (unlike traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) which can be computationally expensive to execute), a variant of standard convolutions, which consists of decomposing an ordinary convolution into depth wise convolution and pointwise convolution. The method yields highly significant parameter and computation reduction with high feature extraction features hence it is especially appropriate to real-time usage like classroom engagement monitoring. MobileNet is available in several versions such as MobileNetV1, V2, and V3. MobileNetV2 is used in this approach since it also proposes the residual connections and linear bottlenecks that enhance the feature propagation and maintain the spatial information without adding a significant number of computing costs.

Where denotes the i-th input sequence used for temporal modeling, are consecutive feature vectors extracted from successive video frames, and Trepresents the sequence length or number of time steps within the window. Each feature vector is obtained from the MobileNet model and contains spatial information related to facial expressions and engagement cues. These are important features that can be used to examine small movements of the face and micro-expressions that can show the level of engagement. The network is initialized by pre-trained weights of ImageNet dataset. This is the advantage of using pre-trained weights, the network has already trained general-purpose visual features, which can be refined to training in the particular domain of student facial expression. MobileNet architecture is both modular and composed of an input layer, a set of convolutional blocks, each of which has a batch normalization and ReLU6 activation functions, and a global average pooling layer. In this study, the last classification layers are eliminated because the network is used to extract features and not to classify. MobileNet output is a high-dimensional spatial representation of every frame and it helps to capture valuable information in the form of facial contours, facial expression, and micro-expressions, all of which are signs of engagement. MobileNet is also selected, because it is compatible with real-time deployment. The system needs to work with the student classroom behavioral pattern. Therefore, a light and fast model will provide low latency with high accuracy. Moreover, MobileNet can be readily combined with time-varying models like GRU that can keep the time-related variations in engagement. MobileNet is the main building block of the suggested engagement detection system due to its flexibility, performance, and strong ability to extract features.

GRU architecture overview

The Gated Recurrent Unit is one of the recurrent neural networks (RNN) architectures that are particularly created to be efficient models of sequential data, in addition to mitigating the vanishing gradient issue of standard RNNs are shown in Eq. (9). GRUs would be appropriate in the analysis of temporal variations in the student-level engagement as it is capable of modeling dependencies over multiple time steps, and thus can be useful in describing how the emotional state and attention change evolve over time. GRUs, in contrast to regular RNNs, have gating mechanisms that control the information flow and provide the network framework with the ability to remember significant long-term relationships and forget insignificant or outdated data.

Where denotes the updated hidden state at time step t. Here, is the hidden state from the previous time step, is the candidate hidden state, and is the update gate vector. The symbol denotes element-wise multiplication.

GRU architecture comprises of two main gates which are update and reset gates. The update gate regulates the extent to which the hiding state component must be carried forward to the next time stage basically defining the extent to which past information needs to be used in the current prediction. The reset gate instead controls the extent to which the past hidden states are disregarded in computing the candidate activation. A combination of these gates enables the GRU to selectively retain temporal features, the engagement recognition of which include eye movement, facial gesture, and micro-expressions.

In this approach, the GRU is set with several layers to add better ability to capture intricate temporal structures. Nevertheless, every layer operates on the sequential feature vectors generated by MobileNet and sequentially extracts progressively higher-level temporal representations. The result of the last GRU layer gives temporally enriched embedding of the individual sequences that contain both short-term and long-term behavioral patterns. It is the input of the next level of engagement classification. GRUs are selected instead of other recurrent architectures such as LSTM mainly because of their efficiency in computation. They have fewer parameters and similar performance which is important in real-time applications where the processing on a low-latency basis is very important. In addition, GRUs have strong convergence properties during training and they are less likely to overfit when trained with moderate sized datasets like the Student Engagement Dataset. Through GRUs, the methodology makes sure that the model is capable of dynamically modeling the temporal dependence in the behavior of the students and forms the core of real-time engagement monitoring.

Discussion

The results of the present research indicate the ability of merging computer vision and temporal modeling to monitor student engagement behavior in classroom setting. The suggested MobileNet-GRU design is effective to capture the representations of the spatial and temporal aspects of the visual patterns and their behavioral pattern, as opposed to the conventional techniques used in assessments through delayed or subjective feedback. The findings indicate that temporal modeling is a very important tool in predicting engagement stability and accuracy especially when using variations in student behavioral engagement across classroom activities. The heatmaps and graphs of the temporal evolution allow visual analysis which proves the engagement to be extremely dynamic and situational, which supports once again that continuous monitoring should be used instead of static evaluation. The fact that the model has been performing consistently across different cross-validation folds means that it has a high generalization and would be applicable in various learning environments. Although the existing system is successful in providing the short- to medium-term temporal dependency, it might lack the capability of addressing the long-run behavioral patterns. This conclusion inspires the incorporation of attention-based Transformer models, as well as to advance even more the temporal reasoning and individualized instructional adaptation.

Conclusion and future work

The vision-driven student engagement monitoring system in classroom environments that can be used to facilitate adaptive and pedagogically informed learning environments. The suggested methodology effectively combined GRU-based temporal modeling over feature sequences constructed from classroom images. The experimental outcomes showed that spatial-temporal approach is significantly more effective than the traditional frame-based and non-temporal models, with the model being able to stabilize, accurately, and generalizable predictions of engagement. It is also a good system that captures behavioral engagement patterns and student activities hence it is appropriate to be deployed online, hybrid, and face-to-face education. With the aspect of constant engagement checking, the framework will offer actionable information that can assist in providing timely interventions during instruction and evidenced-based teaching practices. The future as work since it will include attention-based Transformer models to improve temporal reasoning with the assistance of self-attention mechanisms. Transformers will allow the model to specialize in the parts of student behavior that are most relevant over time, and enhance long-range dependency modelling to a greater extent compared to GRU. This extension will help adaptive adjustment in real-time adaptation of instructional content, pacing, and interactivity, which will make learning more personal. Intelligent educational systems will be further enhanced in scalability, responsiveness and pedagogical efficacy through the inclusion of sophisticated time-based attention.

Cobalt-catalyzed asymmetric three-component reductive addition of alkynes with alkyl halides and imines
Authors: Liting Hou, Wenwen Wu, Zihan Wang, Xianqing Wu, Jingping Qu, Yifeng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The catalytic dicarbofunctionalization of alkynes is a highly efficient strategy for the rapid assembly of complex, multi-substituted acyclic alkenes from simple feedstocks. Despite significant progress having been made in various racemic transformations, the direct enantioselective construction of multi-substituted acyclic alkenes bearing sterically congested tetrasubstituted stereocenters via multicomponent radical pathways remains a formidable and unsolved challenge in organic chemistry. Herein, we report a cobalt-catalyzed asymmetric three-component radical addition of terminal alkynes, ketimines, and unactivated alkyl iodides, which efficiently delivers polysubstituted allylamine derivatives featuring challenging tetrasubstituted stereocenters. This enantioselective addition protocol proceeds under mild reductive conditions and exhibits broad substrate scope with high functional group tolerance, delivering products with excellent stereoselectivity. Preliminary mechanistic investigations suggest a radical-based cascade process involving stereospecific alkyl radical addition to the alkyne followed by stereoselective addition to the ketimine. This work provides a straightforward and modular strategy to access highly functionalized chiral allylic amines from readily available starting materials.

Introduction

Alkynes are among the most abundant and readily accessible petrochemical feedstocks that are widely employed in organic synthesis1. The introduction of two carbon-based coupling fragments across an alkyne—termed alkyne dicarbofunctionalization—is particularly valuable, as it rapidly enhances molecular complexity and provides efficient access to high-value-added products from simple precursors2–34. Transition metal-catalyzed carbometallation of alkynes constitutes a fundamental organic transformation. In this process, a carbon–metal species undergoes syn addition across the triple bond, generating an organometallic intermediate that can be trapped by diverse carbon electrophiles to afford dicarbofunctionalized products (Fig. 1a)8–10. Despite this approach achieving difunctionalization in one pot, it inherently requires two distinct steps. Over the past few decades, transition metal-catalyzed direct dicarbofunctionalization of alkynes has flourished as a powerful strategy for simultaneously constructing two C–C bonds11–27, encompassing the extensively studied redox-neutral transformation merging an electrophile and a nucleophile that typically is an organometallic reagent (Fig. 1b, i), as well as the less explored reductive variants harnessing two different carbon-based electrophiles as coupling partners in presence of a terminal reductant (Fig. 1b, ii). Reactions proceeding via migratory insertion typically yield syn-selective alkene products, whereas multicomponent transformations involving radical addition predominantly furnish anti-selective products. To date, however, current studies in this field are largely confined to racemic transformations. A landmark contribution was made in 2022 by Zhang and coworkers, who elegantly disclosed a Ni/pyrox-catalyzed enantioselective three-component reductive dicarbofunctionalization of alkynes with tertiary alkyl iodides and aromatic iodides, efficiently achieving construction of diverse axially chiral styrenes with excellent enantioselectivity (Fig. 1c)28. Additionally, the scope of atroposelective three-component alkyne coupling has been further enriched by the groups of Li29, Liu30, Wang31, Song33, Chu34. Chiral allylic amines hold paramount importance in organic chemistry and the pharmaceutical industry due to their unique structural motifs and prevalence in bioactive molecules37. Developing stereoselective synthesis of these useful motifs continues to unlock new pathways for drug discovery, catalysis, and functional materials. While significant progress has been made38–42, directly accessing these valuable scaffolds from simple alkynes via difunctionalization in a single operation represents an efficient and sustainable strategy43–49. Seminal work by the Jamison group achieved nickel-catalyzed redox-neutral asymmetric coupling of alkynes with aldimines and organoboranes via cyclometallation, constructing highly substituted allylic amines bearing tertiary stereocenters47,48. Subsequently, Chen and coworkers elegantly developed a palladium-catalyzed enantioselective difunctionalization of 1,3-enynes with aldimines and boronic reagents to access various α-secondary allylic amines carrying tetrasubstituted olefin functionality49. Despite these notable advancements, the construction of tetrasubstituted stereocenter-decorated allylic amines via multicomponent alkyne dicarbofunctionalization remains unexplored, primarily due to challenges posed by extreme steric congestion. Recently, transition metal-catalyzed reductive additions to carbonyls and imines have emerged as a powerful strategy for the assembly of diverse chiral carbon-carbon bonds50–78. Our group developed the cobalt-catalyzed enantioselective, two-component reaction of imines with various electrophiles, thereby creating a general platform for constructing α-tetrasubstituted amino ester derivatives54–56. Building upon this foundation and our ongoing research interest, we envision that a radical-initiated cascade addition reaction of alkyne and ketimine, with the leverage of alkyl electrophiles as radical precursors under reductive conditions compounded by chiral cobalt catalysis that possesses unique reactivities79–83 may provide a new avenue to tackle this challenge. Herein, we present a Co-catalyzed enantioselective reductive three-component difunctionalization of terminal alkyne with unactivated alkyl iodide and α-imino ketoester, enabling the facile construction of exclusively E-configured polysubstituted alkenes with quaternary stereocenters (Fig. 1d). This transformation features broad functional group tolerance with a high level of stereoselectivity, establishing a new synthetic approach to access these valuable enantioenriched allylic amines.

Results and Discussion

To test the feasibility of the hypothesis, we employed ketimine 1a and terminal arylacetylene 2a as the model substrates with tert-butyl iodide 3a as the radical precursor to investigate this three-component asymmetric reductive addition. Gratifyingly, the reaction could take place smoothly to deliver the desired product 4a in a moderate 57% yield with 97% ee by the utilization of CoI2/NPN ligand84,85 L1 as a catalyst, Mn as a reductant, and iPrOH as an additive in THF at 35 °C (Table 1, entry 1). Switching the tert-butyl group on the chiral oxazoline moiety to less bulky substituents such as iso-propyl (L2), iso-butyl (L3), benzyl (L4), or sec-butyl (L5) groups would largely diminish the reaction yield (31–39%) with varying enantioselectivity (91–98%) (Table 1, entries 2–5). The highly active tert-butyl radical generated in situ from 3a might participate in a homocoupling reaction or form a two-component side product with either ketimine 1a and arylacetylene 2a, which would greatly decrease the concentration of starting material 3a. In view of this circumstance, we tried to investigate the effect of the amount of tert-butyl iodide 3a on the reaction efficiency. Indeed, increasing the equivalent of 3a to 2.0 could dramatically improve the reaction yield to 70% with 98% ee (Table 1, entry 6). Meanwhile, increasing the catalytic loading could also facilitate this three-component reductive addition process (Table 1, entry 7). After identifying the optimal reaction conditions, we started to explore the general applicability of this cobalt-catalyzed enantioselective three-component reductive alkenyl addition towards ketimine (Fig. 2). A broad spectrum of ketimine bearing electronically variable aryl substituents could participate in this reaction smoothly, providing the corresponding products (4a‒4 h) in moderate to good yields (42–83% yields) with exceptional enantioselectivities (96–99% ee). Moreover, introducing a methoxyl group on the ortho-position of the aromatic ring exerted no effect on both reaction yield and enantiomeric excess, affording product 4b in 83% yield with excellent 97% ee, revealing that both the reaction efficiency and enantioselectivity could remain untouched from electronic effect and steric hindrance. Functional groups such as trifluoromethyl (4f) and halogens, including chlorine (4g) and bromine (4h) could be well compatible with this asymmetric reductive addition reaction. Of note was that the medically useful CF3 group decorated ketimine could successfully undergo the three-component reductive addition transformation to give product 4i in 63% yield, albeit with diminished enantioselectivity. Evaluation of the substitution pattern of the aromatic ring on the nitrogen atom moiety revealed that a series of electron-donating and electron-deficient groups could be well tolerated in this reaction, furnishing the corresponding products (4j‒4o) in 59%–84% yields with high enantioselectivities (94–99% ee). Subsequently, we turned our attention to the substrate scope of the other two reaction partners (Fig. 3). With respect to the alkyne fragment, terminal arylacetylene carrying electron-neutral, -donating and -withdrawing substituents could proceed smoothly under the optimal reaction conditions, leading to the corresponding products (4r‒4aa) in moderate to high yields (56%–96% yields) with excellent enantioselectivities (93–99% ee). It was worth mentioning that aryl halides, including bromide (4 s, 4z) and chloride (4 u) attached to the alkyne moiety remained intact, showcasing their suitability for further derivatization transformation. Meanwhile, ortho-substituent on the aromatic ring of alkyne had little effect on reaction efficiency and selectivity, giving product 4 s in considerable yield with high ee. Other functionalities such as piperonyl (4w) as well as carbonyl group (4aa) could also be well tolerated in this reaction. Alkyne such as 2-ethynylthiophene was amenable to this catalytic three-component reaction system, giving product 4ab in 73% yield with 97% ee. Internal alkynes were not compatible with this protocol, probably due to the increased steric hindrance. Next, we continued to evaluate the scope of tertiary alkyl iodides in this protocol. Both cyclic and acyclic tertiary alkyl iodides could proceed smoothly to provide the corresponding products in good yields with excellent enantioselectivity (4ac‒4al). Carbocycle size did not affect the stereoselectivity of this asymmetric three-component addition protocol. Additionally, several simple acyclic tertiary alkyl electrophiles proved to be suitable substrates, delivering products 4ae‒4aj in good yields with admirable enantioselectivity. It was worth mentioning that tertiary alkyl iodides carrying functional groups such as Br (4ag), Cl (4al), ether, and ester (4ak) moieties could be compatible with this reaction, demonstrating potential for late-stage modification of bioactive molecules. Unfortunately, the current conditions proved incompatible with unactivated primary and secondary alkyl electrophiles, due to the rapid competing two-component reaction between the imine and these sterically less hindered alkyl halides. To identify potential reaction intermediates and elucidate the mechanism, we conducted the following experiments (Fig. 5). Reaction of imine 1a and alkyne 2a with alkene-tethered unactivated alkyl halide 3k delivered the cascade cyclization/addition product 4am in 31% yield, indicating that the alkyl radical species might be generated during the reaction (Fig. 5a). When the reaction was terminated after one hour, the alkenyl iodide intermediate (3l) could be observed, implying the existence of alkenyl radical species (Fig. 5b). Reaction of imine with 3l under the standard conditions furnished product 4r in 58% yield with 97% ee, demonstrating that the in situ formed alkenyl iodide might further participate in the following reaction to afford the desired product (Fig. 5c). Omission of the imine under standard conditions afforded trans−1,3-diene 8 in 14% yield (Fig. 5d). This outcome indicated that the alkenyl radical underwent trans-addition across the alkyne, consistent with the product configuration determined via X-ray analysis.Fig. 5Mechanistic studies.a Radical cyclization experiment. b Reaction intermediate probing. c Reaction with alkenyl iodide. d Control experiment. e Plausible mechanism.

Based on the above preliminary results and our previous studies55, a plausible reaction pathway was proposed (Fig. 5e). The [CoI]/L/imine complex int A generated in situ from CoI2, ligand, and substrate was reduced by manganese to afford int B, which then underwent a single electron transfer (SET) process with alkyl iodide to afford int A and alkyl radical species int I. Then, trans-addition of the alkyl radical to alkyne occurred to give an alkenyl radical int II. This intermediate might either undergo XAT process with 3 to form the alkenyl iodide (int III) that could revert to the alkenyl radical statein the presence of cobalt catalyst, or directly participate in asymmetric radical addition to int A to afford int C. Reduction by Mn powder and final protonation with the alcohol additive and ligand exchange with 1 affords product 4 and regenerates int A to finish the catalytic cycle.

In conclusion, we have developed a three-component asymmetric addition reaction of ketimines with unactivated alkyl iodides and terminal alkynes enabled by the cobalt catalysis. This enantioselective multicomponent reductive addition protocol features a broad substrate scope with excellent stereoselectivity, thus providing a facile approach for the construction of polysubstituted allylamine derivatives containing a tetrasubstituted carbon stereogenic center. Preliminary mechanistic studies demonstrate that trans-addition of alkyl radical to the terminal alkyne, followed by asymmetric addition to the ketimine, may be involved during the reaction.

Methods

General procedures of cobalt-catalyzed asymmetric three-component reductive addition of alkyne

To a dried 8-mL vial were added ligand (12 mol%), Mn (2.0 equiv), and imine 1 (1.0 equiv). Then the vial was transferred into the glovebox. CoI2 (10 mol%), THF, alkyne 2 (3.0 equiv), and alkyl iodide 3 (2.0 equiv) were added in sequence, and iPrOH was added finally. The reaction vial was capped and stirred at 15 °C for the required time. After completion, the reaction mixture was filtered through a pad of silica gel, and the filter cake was washed with EtOAc. The resulting filtrate was then concentrated under reduced pressure to give the crude product, which was purified by silica gel flash column chromatography to afford product 4.

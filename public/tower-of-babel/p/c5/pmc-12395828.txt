Activation of M1 macrophages promotes diabetic kidney disease by modulating glycolysis via HIF-1α-HK2 signaling pathway
Authors: Xiaoyan Pei, Lijuan Lu, Ziqiong Huang, Yu Wei, Yu Li, Qiong Wang, Yanli Yang, Langen Zhuang, Guoxi Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveMacrophages and glycolysis play a pivotal role in diabetic kidney disease (DKD). However, the regulation of macrophage glycolysis and its mechanism are still unclear in DKD. Thus, this research intends to investigate the regulatory mechanism of macrophage glycolysis during DKD.MethodsMacrophage polarization in patients with DKD and in mice was assessed using RT-qPCR and immunofluorescence. DKD was induced in the mice through injection of streptozotocin, and a high-fat diet was administered. Hematoxylin and eosin staining and Masson’s trichrome staining were employed to identify pathological alterations and visualize renal collagen fibers in the model mice. Glycolytic metabolite levels were quantified using a commercially available kit. Protein levels of HK2, PFK1, LDH, PK1, and GLUT1 were analyzed via western blotting. Hkb-20 cells exposed to high glucose were utilized as an in vitro experimental model. THP-1 cells were co-cultivated with Hkb-20 cells. The impact of macrophage polarization on the functionality of Hkb-20 cells was assessed through CCK8 assay, flow cytometry, and wound healing assay. Levels of IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α were evaluated using ELISA kits. Immunofluorescence staining was employed to examine the co-localization of hypoxia-inducible factor-1 (HIF-1α) and HK2.ResultsWe found that M1 macrophage polarization and enhanced glycolysis activity emerged in DKD patients and mice. Furthermore, we demonstrated that M1 macrophage polarization promotes glycolysis activity in macrophage and model mice. Moreover, we found that HIF-1α and HK2 were obviously enhanced in the serum of DKD cases and mice. Mechanically, we revealed that HIF-1α participates in M1 macrophage polarization and glycolysis activity in vitro by activating HK2 transcription.ConclusionWe demonstrated that M1 macrophage polarization mediated glycolysis contributed to DKD via regulating HIF-1α-HK2 signaling pathway.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01894-3.

Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a significant factor contributing to chronic renal failure [1]. It has emerged as the primary factor for end-stage renal disease globally, heightening the likelihood of early mortality among patients and posing substantial economic and societal challenges [2]. However, the pathogenic mechanisms underlying DKD are multifactorial and remain poorly understood. Glycolysis, a fundamental cellular metabolic pathway, plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis and progression of DKD. Its metabolic dysregulation not only supplies energy to diseased cells but also contributes to pathological processes such as inflammation and fibrosis [3, 4]. Consequently, targeting the glycolytic process may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for DKD, yet the underlying molecular mechanisms remain elusive.

In the environment of diabetes, chronic hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, and other hemodynamic and metabolic disorders stimulate the intrinsic cells of the kidney and foreign immune cells entering the kidney to continuously secrete inflammatory cytokines, thus triggering a series of inflammatory reactions [5]. At the same time, they also cascade to amplify the intrinsic immune response, promote the infiltration, proliferation, and activation of immune cells in the kidney, and aggravate kidney damage, which is the key pathological mechanism of the occurrence and progress of DKD [6]. Macrophages represent the most prominent immune cell population in renal tissue, with their numbers significantly elevated in both DKD animal models and patients [7]. Studies have confirmed that the extent of macrophage infiltration exhibits a strong positive correlation with elevated proteinuria levels and decreased glomerular filtration rate, while high infiltration levels typically indicate poor patient prognosis [8]. Experimental studies have found that in DKD environments, M1 infiltration is evident in the renal injury area. M1 macrophages promote the inflammatory process by secreting pro-inflammatory factors and cause kidney damage in early DKD [9]. Therefore, reducing M1 polarization of macrophages may confer renal protective effects.

During the progression of DKD, prolonged exposure to hyperglycemia leads to microvascular damage and sustained hypoxia in the renal microenvironment. Under hypoxic conditions, hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) regulates the transcription and expression of various glycolytic genes [4, 10, 11]. This modulation induces metabolic reprogramming in macrophages, shifting their metabolic profile from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis. Notably, the polarization of M1-type macrophages is highly dependent on glycolysis for energy supply [12]. Consequently, inhibition of the glycolytic metabolic process can effectively suppress M1-type macrophage polarization.

This study aimed to investigate whether M1 macrophage activation promotes the progression of DKD by modulating HIF-1α-mediated glycolysis, thereby providing novel theoretical insights and potential therapeutic targets for DKD prevention and treatment.

Materials and methods

Clinical subjects and specimens

Total of twenty serum samples from patients diagnosed with DKD by eGFR and healthy people were collected from Endocrinology outpatient and inpatient. Patients included in the study were aged 18 ~ 70 years and were classified as stage DKD3 (30 ≤ eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²) according to the KDIGO staging criteria. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) presence of other types of renal disease; pregnancy, (2) infections, (3) genetic disorders. All patients received written informed consent.

Cell culture and transfection

ATCC was the source of THP-1, Hkb-20 and 293T cells and cultured in DEME (BL308A, Biosharp) in a humidified chamber at 37°C, 5% CO2 and added 10% FBS (2068801CP, Thermo Fisher), 100U/ml penicillin, streptomycin (BL505A, Biosharp). THP-1 cells were stimulated with 30 mmol/L high glucose (HG) for 24 h induce into M1 macrophages and identified with flow cytometry. The M1 THP-1 cells were transfected with overexpressing and short hairpin RNA (shRNA) of HIF-1α using Lipofectamin 2000 (11668-027, Invitrogen). For coculture between THP-1 cells and Hkb-20 cells, THP-1 and Hkb-20 cells in logarithmic growth period were placed on Transwell’s 6-well plate (3µm,723121, NEST) and adjust density to 1 × 105 cells/mL. And then 1.5 ml of Hkb-20 cells were added into the lower chamber and 500 µL THP-1 cells were added in the upper compartment. The shRNA sequences of HIF-1α as follows: shRNA-1, sense: 5’-GGAAGAAATCAGAATAGAA-3’, antisense: 5’-TTCTATTCTGATTTCTTCC-3’; shRNA-2, sense: 5’-CGATGGAAGCACTAGACAA-3’, antisense: 5’-TTGTCTAGTGCTTCCATCG-3’; shRNA-3, sense: 5’-GAAGAACTATGAACATAAA-3’, antisense: 5’-TTTATGTTCATAGTTCTTC-3’.

Cell-counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Hkb-20 cells with different treatment were collected. And then the cells were added 15 µL CCK-8 (PR645, Dojindo) and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. The microplate reader (VARIOSKAN LUX, Thermo) was applied to detect the OD 450 nm.

Apoptosis assay

The apoptosis index was detected based on Annexin V-FITC/PI Kit (C1062M, Beyotime). Briefly, Hkb-20 cells at different groups were harvested and washed with cold PBS. Next, re-suspended with 300µL Annexin V-FITC binding buffer. And then, 5 µL V-FITC and 10 µL PI were added and incubated without light for 20 min at 23℃. At the end the apoptosis of Hkb-20 cells was utilized to detect using flow cytometry (FACSAriaTMIII, BD).

Wound healing assay

Hkb-20 cells (1 × 105 cells/mL) were inoculated into a 6-well Transwell plate and scratched vertically with a 200 uL pipette tip on 0 h. Thereafter, cells were washed thoroughly via PBS. After 48 h, the migration of the Hkb-20 cells was taken using an invert phase-contrast microscope (DMI8, Leica).

The polarization of THP-1 macrophages detection

The polarization of THP-1 in vitro was measured via flow cytometry. In this process, it was stimulated with 30 mmol/L HG for 24 h. THP-1 cells were collected and washed, subsequently ncubated with 100 µL Staining solution and anti-CD86 (159203, BioLegend) and anti-CD206 (162503, BioLegend) for 30 min at 4℃. Finally, detected using flow cytometry (FACSAria TMIII, BD).

Real time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Trizol reagent (KCD-M1001, Cronda) and Blood RNA Extraction Kit were applied to extract total RNA from cells and blood, respectively. To create complementary DNA (cDNA) following the instructions, reverse transcription kits (KCD-M1003, Cronda) were used to transcribe 800ng RNA in a reverse manner. The relative expression of the CD86, CD206 and HIF-1α was detected by LightCycler® 480II (Roche) with RT-qPCR kit (KCD-M1004, Cronda). The amplification conditions as follows: 95 ℃ for 10 min; 95 ℃ for 10 s, 60 ℃for 30 s with 40 cycles. The β-actin was set as reference. To test the relative level of CD86, CD206 and HIF-1α, 2−ΔΔCt method was used and repeated three times. The primer was synthesized by Shanghai GeneChem Company and the sequences of CD86, CD206, HIF-1α and β-actin as follows: Actin, forward: 5’- AGCGAGCATCCCCCAAAGTT-3’, reverse: 5’-GGGCACGAAGGCTCATCATT-3’; CD86, forward: 5’-CTGCTCATCTATACACGGTTACC-3’, reverse: 5’-GGAAACGTCGTACAGTTCTGTG-3’; CD206, forward: 5’-GGGTTGCTATCACTCTCTATGC-3’, reverse: 5’-TTTCTTGTCTGTTGCCGTAGTT-3’; HIF-1α, forward: 5’- GAACGTCGAAAAGAAAAGTCTCG − 3’, reverse: 5’- CCTTATCAAGATGCGAACTCACA’.

Western blot analysis

Cells or tissues were treated with 100 µL radioimmunoprecipitation assay pyrolysis solution (RIPA, BL504A, Biosharp) to isolate total protein. The Whole Blood Protein Extraction Kit (BB-31406, Bestbio) was used to extract protein from blood samples. Bicinchoninic acid (BCA) kit (BL521A, Biosharp) was applied to test the protein level. The protein was transferred to PVDF( IPVH00010, Millipore) and cultured with primary antibodies, including anti-actin (GB11001, Serivicebio), anti-Transferrin (ab214039, Abcam), anti-HK2 (22029-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-PFK1(55028-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-LDH (66287-1-Ig, Proteintech), anti-PK1 (ab137787, Abcam), anti-GLUT1 (21829-1-AP) and anti-HIF-1α (BF8002, Affinity) overnight at 4 °C after sealing with 5% BSA (4240GR500, BioFROXX) for 1 h at 37 °C. And then incubated with secondary antibody goat anti-rabbit at 37 °C for 1 h. Finally, protein bands were captured using ChemiDocXRS+ (Bio-rad) and analyzed for gray value using Image J software.

Diabetic kidney disease model

36 male C57 BL/6 mice (8 weeks) were purchased from Yangzhou University Comparative Medicine Center and raised in SPF animal laboratory. The SPF animal laboratory must be kept at a temperature of 21–23 ℃ and a humidity level of 60–65% for a duration of one week.

The mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 6 per group): Sham group and Model group. Mice in the Sham group were fed a standard diet. Mice in the Model group were fed a 45% high-fat diet (H10045, hfkbio, Beijing) for one week, followed by a 12-h fast (without water restriction) and subsequent intraperitoneal injection of streptozotocin (STZ, 50 mg/kg) to induce a DKD mouse model. Three days later, the blood glucose was detected via relevant glucose monitor (586, Yuyue). When the blood glucose concentration was >16.7 mmol/L, it met the standard of diabetes. DKD mice were fed with high-fat diet for 6 weeks continually. 24 h urine was taken, and to test the urinary albumin via ELISA kit (JN7140, Jining, Shanghai). If the 24-h urinary albumin excretion of model group was obviously higher, and it was determined that the DKD model was successfully constructed.

To investigate the role of macrophages in DKD, mice were randomly divided into 4 groups (n = 6 per group): Sham group, Model group, Model + macrophage depletion group (Model + CL), and model + macrophage transplantation group (Model + MΦ). In the Model + CL group, after successful establishment of the DKD model, mice were administered clodronate liposomes (20 mg/kg) via slow tail vein injection every 2 days, three times per week, for a total of 6 weeks. For the Model + MΦ group, normal C57BL/6 mice were euthanized, and after disinfection, the abdominal cavity was carefully opened. Peritoneal lavage was performed using 10 mL of DMEM supplemented with 0.1 mL of 0.01% heparin, and the lavage fluid was collected. After centrifugation, the supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in DMEM and seeded in culture flasks. Following 1-hour incubation at 37 °C, the medium was replaced to remove non-adherent cells. After 24 h, adherent cells were gently scraped off, centrifuged, and resuspended in 10 mL of DMEM. A 1 mL aliquot was taken for cell counting using a hemocytometer. The suspension was recentrifuged, and a concentrated cell suspension (100 µL) was prepared for subsequent use. After successful induction of the DKD model, The Model + MΦ group mice were anesthetized and injected with 100 µL of the cell suspension (5 × 10⁶ cells) via tail vein puncture once weekly for 6 consecutive weeks.

At the end of the experiment, the animal serum and kidney tissue were taken for detection of relevant indicators. Serum and part of kidney tissue were stored at -80 ℃ immediately and the rest of the kidney tissue were fixed. The animal experiments adhered to the guidelines set forth by International Committees.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and Masson’s trichrome staining

The 4 μm sections of fixed kidney tissue were embedded in paraffin, and then deparaffinized using xylene and rehydrated with gradient alcohol. H&E staining was carried out by first washing the sections with PBS at 23℃ and then staining with hematoxylin for 5 min. The sections were diluted in 70% ethanol and counterstained with eosin for 2 min at 25 ℃. Follow Masson’s trichrome staining kit (G1345, Servicebio) instructions. The sections were immersed in bouin solution and incubated in an oven at 60 ℃ for 2 h; Then the sections were treated with celestite blue dye solution 3 min, hematoxylin dye solution for 3 min and differentiated with the acid ethanol for 5s. And then stained with ponceau fuchsin dye for 10 min and aniline blue for 5 min after treating with phosphomolybdic acid solution for 10 min. Then the sections were dehydrated using gradient alcohol, mounted based on relevant resinous mounting solution. The images were collected via a light microscope (DMI8, Leica).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The fixed kidney tissue was embedded and cut into 4 μm sections. Then the sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated based on gradient alcohol, subsequently ventilated by Triton X-100 for a duration of 5 min. Antigen repaired was performed by antigen repair solution (pH = 6.0) to incubate for 5 min at 96 ℃. The sections were treated with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide and then incubated at 4 ℃ with anti-HIF-1α (ab179483, Abcam). Thereafter, the sections were added 150 µL the secondary antibody at 25℃ for 1 h. After that, it were incubated with DAB and hematoxylin staining for 1 min. At the end it was dehydrated with ethanol, transparent with xylene, sealed with neutral resin, and observed under the inverted microscope (DMI8, Leica).

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Paraffin was used to embed the kidney tissue that was later cut into 4 μm sections. These sections were deparaffinized and exposed to 0.1% Triton X-100 (1139, BioFROXX) for 5 min to facilitate ventilation. Following this, the sections were blocked with 5% BSA (4240GR500, BioFROXX) in PBS at 25℃ for an hour. Subsequently, the sections were exposed to anti-F4/80 (sc-377009, Santa) and anti-CD206 (18704-1-AP, Proteintech) or anti-CD86 (26903-1-AP) at 4 ℃ overnight. Afterwards, it was obstructed using 2% BSA at 25℃. And then, they were exposed to goat anti-Rabbit IgG-FITC (GB22303, Servicebio) as the secondary antibody.The nuclei were stained with Hoechst33258 (C1017, Beyotime) for 10 min in dark. Images were acquired using an inverted microscope (DMI3000B, Leica).

THP-1 cells were harvested and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 3 min. A 5% BSA blocking solution was then applied, followed by incubation at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2 for 1 h. HIF-1α (66730-1-Ig, Proteintech) and HK-2 (22029-1-AP, Proteintech) primary antibodies were used with overnight incubation at 4 ℃. Post-immunostaining, cells were sealed with 1% BSA for 15 min. FITC-labelled goat anti-rabbit IgG (GB22303, Servicebio) and Cy3-labelled goat anti-mouse IgG (GB21301, Servicebio) secondary antibodies were applied and incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 h. Hoechst33258 staining was carried out at room temperature for 10 min before being placed under a fluorescence microscope for observation.

Inflammatory factors and glycolytic metabolites assay

Inflammatory factors and glycolytic metabolites assay were performed by commercialized kits. The level of TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β were detected by corresponding three kinds of ELISA Kits, respectively. The contents of lactic acid (A019-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering institute), pyruvic acid (E-BC-K130-S, Elabscience), citric acid (A128-1-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering institute), acetyl-CoA (E-EL-0125c, Elabscience), α-Ketoglutarate (MAK054, sigma-aldrich), isocitrate dehydrogenase (MAK062, sigma-aldrich) are detected by kits, respectively. The absorbance was measured via microplate reader (VARIOSKAN LUX, Thermo).

Dual luciferase reporter assay

The HK2 reporter gene vector pGL4.10 with wild type and mutant binding sites and HIF-1α pRL-TK vector were constructed. The HK2 reporter gene vector pGL4.10 and HIF-1α pRL-TK vector were co-transfected into 293T cells by Lipfectamine 2000. After 48 h, the luciferase activity was detected based on microplate reader (SpectraMax i3) according to the manufacturer.

Statistical analysis

The data was analyzed and compared using the SPSS 21.0 software. The variables were found to follow a normal distribution and exhibit homogeneous variance based on the test. The mean ± standard deviation (SD) was reported along with at least three biological replicates. An independent sample t-test was conducted to assess the difference between two groups, with a statistical threshold set at 0.05.

Results

Increased M1-type macrophage polarization in DKD patients and DKD mice

To investigate macrophage polarization in DKD, serum samples were collected from patients diagnosed with DKD and from healthy individuals. The RT-qPCR analysis indicated a significant upregulation of the M1-type monocyte marker CD86 in DKD patients when compared to Normal, while the expression of the M2-type monocyte marker CD206 was notably downregulated (Fig. 1A). ELISA analysis showed a notable increase in IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α levels in the serum of DKD patients compared to healthy individuals (Fig. 1B), indicating a state of microinflammation in patients with DKD. To corroborate the clinical findings, a STZ-induced DKD mouse model was developed. The results from the DKD mouse model revealed a significant elevation in blood glucose levels and 24-h total urine protein compared to the Sham group (Fig. 1C-D). Histological examination through H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining demonstrated features such as increased tubular dilatation, inflammatory cell aggregation in the mesangial zone, glomerular dilatation and sclerosis, and interstitial fibrosis in DKD mice kidneys compared to the Sham group (Fig. 1E). Additionally, paraffin sections of renal tissues from DKD mice were evaluated using IF staining, revealing a high number of infiltrating M1 macrophages (F4/80+/CD68+) in the renal interstitium, with a lower presence of M2 macrophages (F4/80+/CD206+) (Fig. 1F). These findings suggest an M1 polarization of macrophages in DKD.

Enhanced glycolysis activity was observed in DKD patients and DKD mice

Glycolysis is a crucial factor in the progression of diabetic kidney disease. ELISA showed significantly elevated serum levels of lactic acid and pyruvic acid in DKD patients compared to the Normal group. In contrast, the level of citric acid, acetyl-CoA, α-ketoglutaric acid, and isocitrate dehydrogenase in the serum of DKD patients obviously decreased than that of the normal group (Fig. 2A). The levels of key glycolytic enzymes were assessed using western blot analysis. The serum levels of HK2, PFK1, LDH, PK1, and GLUT1 were markedly higher in DKD patients compared to the Normal group, with HK2 showing the most pronounced increase (Fig. 2B). Comparable results were noted when employing ELISA and western blot techniques to evaluate the expression of glycolytic factors and key enzymes in the serum of DKD mice (Fig. 2C-D). These results indicate increased glycolytic activity in the context of DKD.

M1 macrophage polarization promotes glycolysis activity

To investigate the influence of M1 polarization on glycolytic activity, THP-1 was induced to M1 polarization in vitro by 30 mmol/L glucose. After being induced with HG, the M1 macrophages had obviously enhanced while M2 macrophages exhibit a contrary tendency as shown by the flow cytometry analysis when compared to the Control group, indicating that M1 macrophages were successfully induced (Fig. 3A). Sugar metabolites analysis showed that the content of lactic acid and pyruvic acid in the HG group was obviously enhanced than that of the Control group, while the level of citric acid, acetyl-CoA, α-ketoglutaric acid and isocitrate dehydrogenase significantly decreased (Fig. 3B). In addition, we found that the level of HK2 in THP-1 cells obviously enhanced in the HG group (Fig. 3C). Subsequently, THP-1 cells were used to co-culture with HKb-20 cells. CCK-8 analysis showed that Hkb-20 cells viability was significantly inhibited after co-culture with THP-1. And the inhibition for the proliferative capacity of Hkb-20 cells was intensified after co-culture with M1 (Fig. 3D). Flow cytometry analysis showed that compared with Hkb-20 group, THP-1 cells can lead to apoptosis of Hkb-20 cells, and the apoptosis of Hkb-20 cells was further promoted with administration with M1 cells (Fig. 3E). Wound healing test found that compared with the Hkb-20 group, THP-1 cells can prevent the migration of Hkb-20 cells, and that was further inhibited with M1 cells (Fig. 3F). According to the comparison result, the THP-1group exhibits an obviously increase in the expression of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β. The expression level of them were further enhanced after inducing to M1 (Fig. 3G).

To clarify the function of macrophages in DKD glycolysis, DKD model mice were treated with macrophage clearance (Model + CL) and injection (Model + MФ). IF results showed a significant decrease in the levels of CD86 and CD206 after macrophage clearance, whereas macrophage engraftment resulted in a significant increase in the levels of CD86 and a continuous decrease in the levels of CD206 (Fig. 4A). Analysis of pathological tissue testing showed that the kidneys of mice in the model group exhibited glomerular hypertrophy, thickening of the capillary basement membrane, widening of the tethered zone, and renal fibrosis. Macrophage clearance attenuated the degree of renal lesions in DKD mice, whereas the pathological changes in the kidneys were aggravated after macrophage transplantation (Fig. 4B). ELISA results showed that blood glucose and 24-h urinary albumin excretion were significantly reduced in DKD mice after macrophage clearance, and the condition of the mice deteriorated significantly after macrophage transplantation (Fig. 4C). Meanwhile, the levels of IL-6, IL-1 β, and TNF-α were significantly reduced in the model mice after macrophage clearance, whereas the levels of IL-6, IL-1 β, and TNF-α were significantly increased after macrophage transplantation (Fig. 4D). Glycolysis-related metabolite assays showed that lactic acid and pyruvic acid levels were significantly reduced in the model mice after macrophage clearance, whereas the levels of citric acid, acetyl-CoA, α-ketoglutaric acid, and isocitrate dehydrogenase were significantly elevated. However, the serum levels of these metabolites showed opposite changes in mice after macrophage transplantation (Fig. 4E). Furthermore, we found that HK2 protein levels were significantly lower after macrophage clearance compared with the model group. In contrast, HK2 expression was further elevated after macrophage transplantation (Fig. 4F). These results suggest that M1 macrophage polarization promotes glycolytic activity.

HIF-1α participates in M1 macrophage polarization and glycolysis activity in vitro

HIF-1α is a member of the key proteins to regulate glycolysis. Western blot analysis revealed heightened levels of HIF-1α expression in the serum samples obtained from individuals diagnosed with DKD (Fig. 5A). Moreover, increased levels of HIF-1α were distinctly observed in both the serum and kidney tissues of DKD mice (Fig. 5B-C). These findings propose a potential association between HIF-1α expression and the progression of DKD.

Subsequently, the loss- and gain- function of HIF-1α on the glycolysis of THP-1 cells was performed in vitro. RT-qPCR results showed that, sh-HIF-1α-2 showed the best silence effect to HIF-1α (Fig. 5D). Therefore, sh-HIF-1α-2 was selected for the follow-up study. Flow cytometry analysis revealed a significant elevation in the quantity of M1 macrophages in the HG group, accompanied by a reduction in M2 macrophages. Subsequent to the upregulation of HIF-1α, there was a notable rise in the population of M1 macrophages and a simultaneous decline in M2 macrophages. The inhibition of HIF-1α had the potential to counteract the impact of elevated glucose levels on THP-1 cell polarization (Fig. 5E). The western blot results showed that HG could promote the expression of HK2 in THP-1 cells. Overexpression of HIF-1 α further increased the level of HK2 in THP-1 cells. Silence HIF-1 α can reverse the expression changes of that (Fig. 5F). The analysis of carbohydrate metabolites showed that after HG induction, the content of lactic acid and pyruvic acid in THP-1 cells significantly increased, while the content of citric acid, acetyl-CoA, α-ketoglutaric acid and isocitrate dehydrogenase decreased. Overexpression of HIF-1 α aggravated this change while silence of HIF-1 α reversed the changes (Fig. 5G).

To investigate the impact of HIF-1α expression on Hkb-20 cell functionality, we conducted co-cultures of HG-induced THP-1 cells with Hkb-20 cells. Our findings revealed that the upregulation of HIF-1α suppressed Hkb-20 cell activity, elicited apoptotic responses, and inhibited cellular migration. Conversely, the downregulation of HIF-1α reversed the effects of M1 stimulation on Hkb-20 cells (Fig. 5H- J). Through ELISA analysis, we observed a substantial increase in the expression levels of IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α in the M1 + Hkb-20 group. Following the overexpression of HIF-1α, the levels of these cytokines showed further elevation. Conversely, upon HIF-1α silencing, notable reductions in the levels of these cytokines were observed (Fig. 5K). These results indicate the involvement of HIF-1α in the polarization and glycolytic activities of M1 macrophages in an in vitro setting.

HK2 transcription was regulated by HIF-1α

As a speed limiting enzyme in the glycolysis process, HK2 is a key role in glycolysis process. To explore whether HIF-1α can regulate HK2, JASPAR (https://jaspar.genereg.net/) online tool was used to predict the regulation of HIF-1α and HK2 promoter. Predictive analysis shows that HIF-1α can bind the region of HK2 promoter at position 346–355 (GGACGTGCAT) (Fig. 6A). Western blot analysis shows that the expression level of HK-2 was positively regulated by HIF-1α (Fig. 6B). Dual luciferase reporter assay reveal that the relative luciferase activity obviously enhanced under condition of co-transfection with overexpressing HIF-1α vector and pGL3-basic vector containing wild type binding site of HK2. However, that is not changed when co-transfection with overexpressing HIF-1α vector and pGL3-basic vector containing mutant binding site of HK2 than the NC group (Fig. 6C). Moreover, IF analysis showed that HIF-1α and HK2 were co-located in the cytoplasm (Fig. 6D). These finding reflected that HIF-1α involved in the regulation of HK2 transcription.

Discussion

DKD is regarded as one of the most troublesome complications of metabolic diseases [13]. DKD has a multifaceted pathogenesis, whereby macrophage infiltration-induced inflammation within the kidneys is a key role in the onset of the disease [14]. Therefore, it is an effective strategy for the therapy of DKD to clarify the mechanism of macrophage-related renal injury and then intervene in the DKD immune system and chronic inflammatory process, to play the role of renal protection. In this research, we proved that M1 polarization and enhanced glycolysis activity emerged in DKD patients and mice. Subsequently, we demonstrated that M1 macrophage polarization promotes glycolysis activity in both its own and STZ-induced DKD mice. Mechanically, we clarified that M1 macrophage polarization-mediated glycolysis contributed to DKD by regulating HIF-1α-HK2 signaling pathway.

Immune response and inflammatory response is closely associated with the occurrence and progression of DKD [5]. Plenty of research has shown that DKD is a natural immune and chronic low-grade inflammatory disease characterized by immune cell infiltration, local inflammatory reaction, and overexpression of inflammatory factors [15]. Macrophages are the key immune-inflammatory effector cells [16]. Infiltrating macrophages can exist in two activated states - classically activated M1 and alternatively activated M2. M1 primarily contribute to the spread of inflammation and damage to tissues, whereas M2 macrophages possess immune regulatory functions and are anti-inflammatory [17]. A lot of studies have demonstrated that the activated state of infiltrating macrophages under the stimulation of renal tissue microenvironment is mainly M1 macrophages under the condition of diabetes [18]. Such as Yang et al., demonstrated that Tim-3 promoted M1 macrophage activation which will aggravate podocyte injury in DKD via regulating the NF-κB/TNF-α pathway [19]. Feng et al. revealed that PPAR-α agonist fenofibrate is involved in inhibiting M1 macrophages that prevented DKD in dB/dB Mice [20]. Consistent with these results, we found that M1 macrophage polarization emerged in DKD patients and mice. In addition, we demonstrated that M1 macrophage inhibited proliferative capacity and migration of Hkb-20 cells while promoting apoptosis of Hkb-20 cells. These results suggested that M1 macrophage polarization contributed to the progression of DKD.

Metabolic reprogramming is an important mechanism to regulate the activation of different subsets of macrophages. Macrophage-mediated inflammatory diseases have been regarded as appropriate targets for glycolysis-based therapy [21]. It can inhibit macrophage polarization and inflammation by inhibiting the energy metabolism of M1 macrophage glycolysis which will inhibit the progression of the diseases [22]. Several studies also indicated the importance of glycolysis in DKD. Such as Luo et al., indicated that compromised glycolysis contributed to the foot process fusion of podocytes in DKD by enhancing ornithine catabolism [23]. Song et al. indicated that IGFBP5 promoted DKD progression by enhancing endothelial glycolysis mediated by PFKFB3 [3]. However, the energy metabolism mechanism of macrophages and its regulation mechanism in DKD remain unclear. In this research, we verified that enhanced glycolysis activity was observed in DKD patients. In addition, we found that the expression of HK2, PFK1, LDH, PK1, and GLUT1 in the serum of DKD patients significantly increased, of which HK2 was the most significantly upregulated. HK2 is one of the three rate-limiting enzymes in the glycolytic pathway which was found to be closely associated with various kinds of diseases by regulating glycolysis [24, 25]. Macrophages undergo glycometabolism reprogramming from resting state to M1 type classic activated state. M1 macrophages mainly rely on the aerobic glycolysis pathway with a rapid energy supply to obtain energy to play an inflammatory effect [26]. Our current study revealed that the polarization of M1 macrophages augmented glycolysis activity in both in vitro and in vivo settings. These findings revealed that M1 macrophage polarization promoted the progression of DKD via activating glycolysis activity.

HIF-1α was previously considered as a response factor to hypoxia, but abundant studies have verified that it exists in the aerobic environment of tumor and inflammation, and it participates in the induction of inflammation [27]. Meanwhile, HIF-1α also proved that it participated in the regulation of the glycolysis level in various cells [28]. Several studies also showed that HIF-1α mediated glycolysis is a key factor in the development of DKD. Inhibiting the sodium-glucose cotransporter 2, for example, prevented the metabolic switch from lipid oxidation to glycolysis mediated by HIF-1α in kidney tubule cells of diabetic mice [29]. Recently, it showed that extracellular vesicles accelerated macrophage glycolysis via adjusting HIF-1α in DKD [30]. Our study demonstrated that HIF-1α participated in M1 macrophage polarization and glycolysis activity in vitro. Further analysis indicated that HK2 transcription was regulated by HIF-1α. These results indicated that M1 macrophage polarization promoted the progression of DKD via activating glycolysis activity via the HIF-1α-HK2 pathway.

However, our study has several limitations. First, the DKD mouse model cannot fully recapitulate the multifactorial pathogenesis, pathological heterogeneity, and chronic progression of human disease, which may attenuate the long-term regulatory role of chronic microenvironment on cellular evolution and the broader immune cell interaction network involved in human kidneys. Second, the co-culture of THP-1 and Hkb-20 cells used a single DMEM medium, which may slightly amplify the glycolytic flux of THP-1 cells and potentially reduce the half-life and activity of secreted factors under suboptimal culture conditions. Finally, while this study focused on macrophage-renal epithelial cell interactions, it did not comprehensively investigate the synergistic effects of other renal cell types (e.g., podocytes, endothelial cells) and systemic metabolic signaling in DKD pathogenesis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated that M1 macrophage polarization accelerated glycolysis which will promote DKD progression via regulating HIF-1α-HK2 signaling pathway. This study may help to formulate strategies to improve the prevention and treatment of DKD.

Detecting pancreaticobiliary maljunction in pediatric congenital choledochal malformation patients using machine learning methods
Authors: Yifeng Shao, Chengyang Jiang, Runmin Zhang, Kunpeng Yang, Chuanyu Yang, Chengji Dong, Yang Hong, Long Li, Mei Diao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe presence of pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) in pediatric patients with congenital choledochal malformation significantly impacts clinical management and surgical decision-making. Current preoperative evaluation of PBM coexistence remains challenging in children, while intraoperative cholangiography does not consistently provide diagnostic-quality imaging. This study aims to develop machine learning-based algorithm models for detecting pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) in children with congenital choledochal malformation.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study utilizing data from patients with congenital choledochal malformation treated at our center between January 2019 and January 2024. Demographic characteristics, clinical features, and preoperative laboratory parameters were processed through rigorous data curation and feature engineering pipelines. Cases were allocated via random sampling into training (80%) and hold-out test (20%) cohorts, maintaining strict separation between training and test cohorts. Seven machine learning algorithms - Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) - were implemented with five-fold cross-validation. An ensemble voting classifier was specifically constructed using these models. Model performance was quantified through comprehensive metrics including area under the ROC curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative predictive values, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. This study employed the nonparametric bootstrap method to estimate the confidence interval for the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) was employed for model interpretability, with feature importance rankings determined by absolute SHAP value magnitudes.ResultsIn a cohort of 803 pediatric patients with congenital choledochal malformation, 628 (78.2%) demonstrated concurrent pancreaticobiliary maljunction. We developed a detection model incorporating 43 clinical features, with Random Forest showing optimal performance. An ensemble voting classifier integrating seven machine learning algorithms achieved enhanced discriminative performance (AUC: 0.87 (0.81, 0.92); Recall: 0.91 (0.85, 0.95); F1-score: 0.91 (0.87, 0.94)). Key features contributing to PBM detection included: laboratory markers and clinical parameters.ConclusionBy integrating preoperative clinical symptoms and laboratory parameters, machine learning algorithms demonstrated significant detection capability in identifying PBM among pediatric congenital choledochal malformation patients, with the RF model achieving superior performance metrics among all base models. The developed ensemble voting classifier provides valuable preoperative guidance for surgical planning and clinical management, enabling detection of PBM comorbidity before surgery in congenital choledochal malformation cases.Keywords: Machine learning, Congenital choledochal malformation, Pancreaticobiliary maljunction

Introduction

Congenital choledochal malformation is a pathological condition characterized by varying degrees of congenital dilatation of the biliary tract, including the hepatic duct and intrahepatic bile ducts. It is particularly prevalent in Asian populations, especially among infants and females. Previous studies have shown that approximately 72.2% of patients with congenital choledochal malformation are accompanied by pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) [1]. PBM is a congenital disorder caused by the abnormal union of the pancreatic duct and bile duct outside the duodenal wall, forming an abnormally long common channel. PBM can lead to hepatic dysfunction, bile duct perforation, malnutrition, and even malignant transformation. Accurately determining the presence or absence of PBM is critical for minimizing pancreatic duct injury during surgical interventions [2].

For pediatric patients, intraoperative cholangiography (IOC) remains the gold standard for diagnosing PBM. Currently, preoperative diagnostic modalities such as ERCP (Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography), MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography), and CT cholangiography (Computed Tomography Cholangiography) can be employed for diagnosing PBM. However, the accuracy and applicability of these approaches remain challenging in pediatric patients [3]. In cases involving massive congenital choledochal malformation or complete obstruction of the distal cyst, intraoperative cholangiography may also fail to reliably determine the presence of PBM (Fig. 1A–C).

Machine learning (ML) has demonstrated significant potential in disease diagnosis, complication monitoring, prognostic detection, and related medical domains. It has been proven that ML algorithms can interpret the complex and unpredictable characteristics of human physiology with higher accuracy than conventional detection models. Unlike conventional detection models, which rely on manually selected variables for calculations, ML automates all computational processes algorithmically, enabling seamless integration of vast datasets and high-dimensional variables. As a result, ML has found extensive applications in clinical diagnostics, risk stratification, and outcome forecasting [4, 5].

The objective of this study is to develop a user-friendly and non-invasive machine learning model that leverages preoperative clinical indicators to detect pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) in patients with congenital choledochal malformation.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

Subjects

Data were retrospectively reviewed from patients with congenital choledochal malformation who underwent surgical procedures at the Capital Institute of Pediatrics between January 2019 and January 2024. The analysis encompassed baseline characteristics, clinical features, laboratory tests, and imaging examinations. Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) patients with incomplete data (missing over 30% of laboratory items or having ambiguous medical histories), (2) those receiving external drainage prior to radical surgery, and (3) cases demonstrating unsatisfactory outcomes on intraoperative cholangiography. This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (SHELL20240103), with a waiver of informed consent granted due to the retrospective nature of the research (Fig. 2).

Flowchart, the results of patient screening and the protocol for conducting the study

Data collection

The extracted dataset encompassed patients’ baseline characteristics, clinical manifestations, preoperative laboratory investigations, and intraoperative cholangiographic (IOC) findings. All non-ordinal categorical variables underwent one-hot encoding transformation.

Outcome variables

The diagnosis of PBM was established using IOC as the gold standard, defined by the presence of a common pancreaticobiliary channel exceeding 5 mm in length [6]. Two independent experienced surgeons conducted blinded quantitative assessments of cholangiographic images using calibrated measurement tools. Final measurements were determined by averaging values from both observers, with unresolved discrepancies (> 1.5 mm variance) adjudicated through consensus review by a third senior radiologist. All evaluators were strictly blinded to preoperative clinical profiles and imaging findings throughout the analytical process.

Handling of missing data

Variables with missing values exceeding 20% were excluded from the analytical cohort. Missing values in the remaining features were multiply imputed using Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE).

Model development and statistical analysis

Normality of distribution was assessed using the D’Agostino-Pearson test. Continuous variables were presented as mean (standard deviation, SD) for normally distributed data or median (interquartile range, IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Group comparisons were performed using independent Student’s t-tests for parametric data and Mann-Whitney U tests for non-parametric data. Categorical variables were analyzed with Pearson’s chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate based on expected cell frequencies.

The dataset was partitioned with 80% of cases allocated to the training set for model training and 20% reserved as an independent test cohort, which remained completely blinded during the entire modeling process.

Feature selection employed a multi-stage integrated strategy, combining statistical correlation analysis, machine learning-based feature elimination algorithms, and expert clinical judgment to achieve a balance between statistical significance and clinical interpretability. The feature selection process consisted of two stages: first, Spearman rank correlation analysis was performed on the training set to identify and remove highly correlated variables with correlation coefficients greater than 0.7, thereby reducing the impact of multicollinearity on model stability. Subsequently, Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) using a Random Forest regressor as the base estimator was applied to iteratively rank and select the remaining variables, resulting in the final feature subset. Additionally, to enhance the clinical applicability of the model, expert clinical judgment was incorporated during the feature selection process. Based on prior research evidence and medical knowledge, features highly relevant to pathological mechanisms or clinical manifestations were retained. To prevent clinically meaningful key features from being inadvertently removed during automated selection, manual oversight was implemented during the feature elimination phase to ensure that variables with clear medical value were preserved in the final model.

The selected features were employed to construct machine learning models, including logistic regression (linear model) and non-linear algorithms: Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). Hyperparameter optimization was conducted through five-fold stratified cross-validation with grid search, where optimal parameters were determined by maximizing recall. A soft ensemble voting classifier was subsequently constructed by aggregating probabilistic outputs from all models. Prior to model training, Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) was applied exclusively to the training folds during cross-validation to address class imbalance, strictly preventing information leakage into test cohorts.

Model performance was comprehensively evaluated using six key metrics: Accuracy, Sensitivity (Recall), Specificity, Positive Predictive Value (PPV), Negative Predictive Value (NPV), and F1-Score. The F1-Score, defined as the harmonic mean of precision and recall, was calculated as: F1 = 2 × Precision x Recall/(Precision + Recall).

This study used a nonparametric bootstrap method (n_bootstrap = 2000) to estimate the 95% confidence interval for the metrics.

SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis was employed for model interpretability, with feature importance rankings determined by absolute SHAP value magnitudes, and features were selected based on the condition that mean_abs_shap > 0.01. We determined the feature importance ranking based on the absolute values of the Mean Absolute SHAP Value. We used the SHAP Value combined with the proportion of positive and negative impacts to assess each feature’s effect on the outcome.

Result

A retrospective review of 930 consecutive cases undergoing radical surgery for congenital choledochal malformation (complete cyst excision with Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy) was conducted through our institutional electronic medical record system between January 2019 and January 2024. Following rigorous exclusion of 127 cases (14.3% of initial cohort) meeting any of the following criteria: [1] incomplete core clinical documentation (missing > 30% key variables); [2] non-diagnostic intraoperative cholangiograms (failure to visualize pancreatic duct convergence); [3] prior external biliary drainage performed at other hospitals, the final analytical cohort comprised 803 patients (Fig. 2). Within this group, 628 subjects (78.2%) demonstrated radiologically confirmed PBM (Table 1). Preoperative baseline characteristics were comprehensively collected prior to any therapeutic intervention, encompassing 64 variables across seven clinical domains: [1] Demographics: sex and operative age (days); [2] Hematological Profile: white blood cell count (×109/L), red blood cell count (×1012/L), hemoglobin (g/L), platelet count (×109/L), neutrophil count/percentage, lymphocyte count/percentage, monocyte count/percentage, eosinophil count/percentage, basophil count/percentage, and large platelet ratio; [3] Biochemical Markers: C-reactive protein (mg/L), procalcitonin(ng/mL), alanine aminotransferase (U/L), aspartate aminotransferase(U/L), total protein (g/L), albumin (g/L), globulin (g/L), albumin-to-globulin ratio, total/direct bilirubin (µmol/L), alkaline phosphatase (U/L), gamma-glutamyl transferase (U/L), total bile acids (µmol/L), cholinesterase (KU/L), prealbumin (mg/L), blood ammonia (µmol/L), creatinine (µmol/L), urea (mmol/L), and adenosine deaminase (U/L); [4] Metabolic Parameters: total cholesterol (mmol/L), triglycerides (mmol/L), high-/low-density lipoprotein (mmol/L), creatine kinase (U/L), lactate dehydrogenase (U/L), α-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (U/L), amylase (U/L), and creatine kinase-MB isoenzyme (ng/mL); [5] Electrolytes/Iron Homeostasis: potassium/sodium/chloride/calcium/magnesium/phosphorus (mmol/L), serum iron (µmol/L), total/unsaturated iron-binding capacity (µmol/L); [6] Coagulation Profile: D-dimer (mg/LFEU), thrombin time (s), prothrombin time (s), fibrinogen (g/L), fibrin degradation products (µg/mL), and international normalized ratio; [7] Clinical Symptoms: abdominal pain, jaundice, vomiting, and fever documented as binary variables (presence/absence).

Discussion

Our institution has accumulated extensive clinical experience in the diagnosis and management of congenital choledochal malformation, supported by a large patient cohort. In clinical practice, we frequently encounter challenges in obtaining informative intraoperative cholangiograms. Based on our experience, the following factors contribute to these difficulties: Overlapping Opacification: radiographic visualization of the congenital choledochal malformation may overlap with adjacent anatomical structures, impairing the assessment of PBM (Fig. 1C); Distal Obstruction: in cases with complete distal obstruction, contrast agents fail to reflux into the common channel or duodenum, precluding evaluation of PBM; The distal outlet of the cyst is stenotic (3–5 mm): even within this diameter range, the contrast agent sometimes has difficulty flowing into the common channel or the duodenum. We hypothesize that this phenomenon may result from rhythmic contractions of the terminal bile duct or compression of the distal orifice due to surgical manipulation during the procedure [7–9]; Complete Distal Obstruction by Calculi or Protein Plugs: impacted calculi or protein plugs at the cyst’s distal outlet completely block contrast flow into the common channel or duodenum.

In this study, leveraging preoperative data from congenital choledochal malformation patients at our center, we developed multiple detection models to assess the presence of PBM and compared their performance. By constructing a voting classifier based on multiple machine learning algorithms, we further enhanced detection performance and improved the robustness of model application.While machine learning has seen growing applications in pediatric surgery [6, 10–18], no prior models specifically address PBM detection in children with congenital choledochal malformation. Our work represents a pioneering effort with significant clinical applicability.

This study comprehensively evaluated preoperative demographics, symptoms, and lab values. Through rigorous feature engineering screening, 43 clinically relevant features were selected for machine learning modeling. To mitigate bias inherent in single-model approaches, we systematically compared both linear algorithms (LR) and nonlinear algorithms (SVM, RF, XGBoost, AdaBoost, LightGBM, and KNN). Among all base models, the Random Forest (RF) model demonstrated the best performance, achieving the highest recall in the test set. Random Forest (RF) is an ensemble learning algorithm based on decision trees that improves model stability and predictive accuracy by constructing multiple decision trees and combining their outputs through a majority voting mechanism. Its robustness is demonstrated by its ability to effectively handle high-dimensional and complex feature spaces, strong resistance to outliers and noise, and a low tendency to overfit. Additionally, Random Forest provides built-in feature importance evaluation, supporting model interpretability [19]. With these advantages, RF is widely applied in clinical predictive modeling, including disease diagnosis and prognosis assessment [20]. To balance recall and further enhance the overall model performance, we developed a voting classifier.

To interpret the model’s decision-making process, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were employed, quantifying feature contributions.Operative age, abdominal pain, C-reactive protein, and prealbumin serve as positive detection factors for PBM detection. We hypothesize that this may be attributed to the higher detection rate of pancreaticobiliary maljunction in patients with non-stenotic distal choledochal cysts. Due to less severe initial biliary obstruction, smaller cyst size, and more insidious symptoms, these patients receive treatment later compared to those with stenotic-type cysts [21]. Consequently, advanced age emerges as a significant positive factor. To date, no studies have specifically examined PBM across different age groups. In our cohort, among 240 infants under 1 year of age, 143 (60%) had concurrent PBM, while 486 of 563 children over 1 year (86%) exhibited PBM. The median age of the PBM group was 989.7 days, compared to 202.7 days in the non-PBM group (Table 1), demonstrating a statistically significant difference. These findings underscore surgical age as a key factor of PBM. Abdominal pain has been identified as a significant positive factor of PBM. This finding aligns with previous studies demonstrating that abdominal pain is consistently reported as the most prominent clinical symptom in pediatric patients with fusiform or cylindrical congenital choledochal malformation [2, 21]. The underlying mechanism may be attributed to biliary reflux into the pancreatic duct, which can induce pancreatitis and subsequently manifest as abdominal pain in affected children. Persistent pancreatic inflammation activates systemic inflammatory responses, triggering hepatocytes to markedly upregulate synthesis of C-reactive protein (CRP). This pathophysiological mechanism elucidates the validity of CRP as a positive detection factor for PBM [22, 23].

Prealbumin and albumin are generally considered indicators reflecting nutritional status [24]. In our study, these two indicators exhibited opposite detection factors for PBM. Pancreatitis resulting from pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) may lead to persistent consumption of albumin, rendering albumin a negative factor [25–27]. We speculate that the opposing detective effects of albumin and prealbumin may be attributed to their differing biological half-lives [28, 29]. Other negatively associated indicators, such as globulin, urea, and cholesterol [30, 31], may also be related to chronic protein-energy malnutrition (PEM) and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency caused by pancreatitis [32].

Gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), D-dimer, and prothrombin time (PT) were also identified as negative detection factors for pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM). We hypothesize that this association may be due to the fact that patients with distal obstruction often experience more severe biliary obstruction and consequently more significant hepatic injury [21, 33]. Among these patients, the proportion of PBM cases is relatively low, suggesting that their biliary anomalies may be caused by etiologies other than PBM. Previous studies have demonstrated that elevated D-dimer and PT levels are associated with hepatic injury [34–37].

This study has several strengths. First, compared to conventional methods such as endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), and computed tomography cholangiography, our model offers greater cost-effectiveness, convenience, and non-invasiveness in clinical practice for pediatric patients, with broader applicability across diverse settings. Second, preoperative detection of pancreaticobiliary maljunction (PBM) using our model provides an early warning to guide intraoperative decision-making, thereby minimizing inadvertent pancreatic duct injury and reducing surgical complications. Furthermore, leveraging advancements in computer science and clinical informatics, our approach integrates high-dimensional clinical parameters to construct a risk detection model that outperforms single-parameter methods. Local deployment of this model in hospitals with limited expertise could enhance diagnostic and therapeutic capabilities, particularly in resource-constrained clinical environments.

However, we do not currently recommend using this model as the sole diagnostic criterion. Instead, we advocate for employing this model preoperatively as a supplementary tool to assist in interpreting intraoperative cholangiography (IOC) results. For patients with suboptimal IOC performance, the model’s output may be considered as an additional reference. Nonetheless, cautious and meticulous surgical technique remains the more reliable approach.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, although our center is the largest worldwide specializing in the diagnosis and treatment of congenital choledochal malformation, the overall incidence of this condition remains low. The heterogeneous diagnostic and therapeutic capabilities across other medical centers pose challenges for conducting multicenter studies to further validate and refine this detection model. Second, the retrospective nature of this study may introduce selection bias. To address these issues, future efforts should prioritize the standardization of diagnostic and treatment protocols across institutions, followed by prospective multicenter studies with larger sample sizes to enhance the model’s generalizability and robustness. SHAP reveals associations between features and model output but does not imply causal relationships, so clinical inference must be cautious.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the development of a machine learning model based on preoperative data holds promise as a simpler, non-invasive tool for detecting the presence of PBM in patients with congenital choledochal malformation.

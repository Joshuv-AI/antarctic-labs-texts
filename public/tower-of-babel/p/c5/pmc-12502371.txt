Clinical outcomes and prognostic factors in patients undergoing radical nephrectomy with lymphadenectomy for pN1 renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Xianjun Cao, Jiao Jiao, Yanzhen Hao, Guangbin Li, Feng Han, Yu Su
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of radical nephrectomy combined with regional lymphadenectomy in patients with locally advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC) at the pathological N1 stage.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed clinical data from a cohort of 112 patients diagnosed with locally advanced RCC at the pathological N1 stage who underwent radical nephrectomy between June 2012 and June 2024. The indications for LND in this study were preoperative imaging suggesting lymph node enlargement (short diameter ≥1 cm) or intraoperative suspicion of metastasis; the scope of dissection followed the anterior template (including lymph nodes from the renal hilum to the level of the inferior mesenteric artery). The analysis included univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models, as well as Kaplan-Meier survival curves.ResultsThe cohort comprised 112 patients with pT1-4N1M0 RCC, with a mean age of 49 years (±14) and consisted of 74 males and 38 females. Among these patients, 32 had stage pT1-2 cancer, while 80 presented with more advanced stages (pT3 and above). The median follow-up time was 51 months (range: 12–110 months), with 101 (90.2%) patients completing follow-up and 11 (9.8%) lost to follow-up by the end of the study. The median number of lymph nodes dissected during surgery was 8.0 (range 4.0–13.0), and the median number of pathological lymph node metastases was 2.0 (range 1.0–6.0). The postoperative complication rate was 5.4% (6/112), with 5 (4.5%) Grade II and 1 (0.9%) Grade III complications according to the Clavien-Dindo classification. Throughout the follow-up period, tumor progression was observed in 58 patients, including 9 cases of local recurrence and 54 cases of distant metastasis. The median relapse-free survival (RFS) was approximately 0.90 years (range 0.35–2.60 years), while the median overall survival (OS) was about 2.25 years (range 1.15–4.10 years). The 1-, 3-, and 5-year RFS rates were 44.6%, 21.5%, and 12.1%, respectively, and the corresponding OS rates were 78.5%, 36.8%, and 17.2%. Multivariate analysis identified sarcomatoid differentiation (P=0.001) and postoperative tumor progression (P=0.001) as independent risk factors for poor overall survival. Preoperative lymph node enlargement was identified as an independent risk factor for distant metastasis (P=0.038), while multigroup regional lymph node metastasis was linked to an increased risk of local recurrence (P=0.031).ConclusionPatients with lymph node metastatic locally advanced RCC exhibit a high risk of postoperative recurrence and metastasis, leading to a poor prognosis. Consequently, these patients require close monitoring and follow-up. Key adverse factors associated with tumor recurrence or poor survival identified include preoperative regional lymph node enlargement, sarcomatoid differentiation, and postoperative tumor progression in stage N1 RCC.Keywords: Locally advanced renal cell carcinoma; lymph node enlargement, Tumor progression, Prognosis

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors in the urinary system. Despite the increasing detection rate of early-stage RCC due to the widespread availability of health check-ups, approximately 17% of RCC patients are initially diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic disease [1]. Regional lymph node metastasis is one of the common metastatic pathways in RCC. Currently, there is ongoing debate regarding whether radical nephrectomy should be performed concurrently with regional lymph node dissection [2].

The European Association of Urology (EAU) recommends lymph node dissection only during radical nephrectomy [3], while the American Urological Association (AUA) suggests that lymph node dissection should be performed only for definitive diagnosis and tumor staging [4]. The Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of RCC recommend lymph node dissection for patients with enlarged lymph nodes identified by preoperative imaging or palpable regional lymph nodes during surgery. The independent risk factors for tumor recurrence and risk factors for recurrence and metastasis in patients with locally advanced RCC who undergo radical nephrectomy and have positive regional lymph node pathology are currently unclear [5].

This study retrospectively analyzes the data of patients with locally advanced RCC and regional lymph node metastasis at our center to identify factors affecting survival and tumor recurrence, as well as postoperative recurrence and metastasis, providing clinical references for treatment.

Materials and methods

Clinical data

Data were collected from patients with locally advanced RCC who underwent radical nephrectomy combined with regional lymph node dissection between June 2012 and June 2024. Inclusion Criteria: (1) Indications for LND were: ① preoperative CT/MRI suggesting lymph node enlargement (short diameter ≥ 1 cm) (clinically positive); ② intraoperative suspicion of lymph node metastasis (e.g., firm, fixed nodes); ③ routine LND for cT3-T4 stage RCC at our center. (2) Patients without preoperative distant metastasis. (3) Patients undergoing radical nephrectomy and regional lymph node dissection. (4) Pathological stage pT1 ~ 4N1M0. (5) Complete clinical data. Exclusion Criteria: (1) Presence of distant metastasis preoperatively. (2) Preoperative neoadjuvant therapy. (3) No lymph node metastasis in postoperative pathology. (4) History of other malignant tumors. Patients meeting the above criteria were included in the study.

Methods

Basic patient information including gender, age, clinical symptoms, smoking history, diabetes status, hypertension status, and body mass index (BMI) were systematically recorded. Additionally, imaging data such as preoperative lymph node enlargement, surgical details including surgical approach (The surgery was performed via an open transabdominal approach (supraumbilical midline incision) to expose the perirenal and retroperitoneal areas for LND. LND followed an anterior template, with the dissection encompassing para-aortic, paracaval, and hilar lymph nodes from the diaphragmatic crus to the level of the inferior mesenteric artery, preserving the sympathetic chain. Over the 12-year study period, all radical nephrectomies and lymphadenectomies were performed by or under the direct supervision of a consistent team of three senior urological surgeons at our institution, ensuring adherence to the same surgical principles and dissection template to maintain procedural consistency), postoperative complications, and the number of lymph nodes dissected, as well as pathological findings such as the number of metastatic lymph nodes, tumor necrosis, sarcomatoid differentiation, tumor pT stage, and the presence of vascular tumor emboli were meticulously documented. The presence of intravascular tumor thrombus and other key pathological features such as tumor necrosis and sarcomatoid differentiation were initially determined from the formal pathology reports. For this study, all cases with positive findings for these features and a random 10% of negative cases had their slides re-reviewed by a senior uropathologist to confirm the diagnosis and ensure consistency. Furthermore, follow-up data encompassing postoperative local recurrence or distant metastasis, adjuvant therapy administration, survival/death outcomes, survival duration, and time to progression were comprehensively collected. Follow-up was conducted until the patient’s death or until the patient was lost to follow-up, utilizing methods such as telephone consultations, outpatient visits, and inpatient reviews. Our institutional standard follow-up protocol for high-risk RCC consists of clinical evaluation and abdominopelvic contrast-enhanced CT or MRI every 3–6 months for the first two years, every 6–12 months for years three to five, and annually thereafter. Chest imaging was performed at least annually. For patients who did not return for visits, telephone follow-ups were conducted to ascertain their status from local hospitals or family members, and records of imaging performed elsewhere were requested to determine the date and site of progression.

In this study, preoperative lymph node enlargement was defined as the presence of regionally measurable lymph nodes with a maximum short diameter as indicated by imaging [6]. All preoperative imaging (CT/MRI) was independently evaluated by two experienced radiologists (with over 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging), who were blinded to the pathological outcomes. Any discrepancies in lymph node measurement or characterization were resolved by a third senior radiologist to reach a consensus. Multigroup regional lymph node metastasis was defined as the involvement of ≥ 2 lymph node groups within the same anatomical region (e.g., hilar region). Metastasis to multiple anatomical regions was defined as lymph node involvement in different anatomical regions (e.g., hilar region + para-aortic region). Tumor progression was defined as the first imaging evidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis during the follow-up period, assessed using RECIST 1.1 criteria [6].

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.5. For normally distributed quantitative data, the mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s) was reported, with comparisons conducted using an independent t-test. In the case of non-normally distributed quantitative data, the median (Q1, Q3) was utilized, and comparisons were performed using the rank-sum test. Count data were expressed as cases (%) and analyzed using the χ2 test. To identify independent risk factors for tumor recurrence, postoperative recurrence, and metastasis, univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were conducted, along with Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General data

The median follow-up time was 51 months (range: 12–110 months). By the end of the follow-up period, 101 (90.2%) patients had completed follow-up, and 11 (9.8%) were lost to follow-up. A total of 112 patients with pT1 ~ 4N1M0 stage RCC were included in this study. Among them, 74 were male and 38 were female, with a mean age of (49 ± 14) years. All patients underwent radical nephrectomy combined with regional lymph node dissection. The median maximum tumor diameter was 7.6 (5.4, 10.1) cm. Postoperative pathological T stages were as follows: pT1 in 22 patients, pT2 in 10 patients, pT3 in 67 patients, and pT4 in 13 patients. Preoperative lymph node enlargement was observed in 67 patients. The median number of lymph nodes dissected during surgery was 8.0 (4.0, 13.0), and the median number of lymph node metastases in postoperative pathology was 2.0 (1.0, 6.0). The median number of lymph node metastases in patients with pT2 or lower was 2.0 (1.0, 4.0), while in patients with pT3 or higher, it was 3.0 (1.0, 6.8). Pathological types included clear cell carcinoma in 43 cases (38.4%), papillary carcinoma in 26 cases (23.2%), chromophobe cell carcinoma in 4 cases (3.6%), and other types in 39 cases (34.8%). The intraoperative complication rate was 3.6% (4/112), including 4 cases of hemorrhage (transfusion volume 250 ml). The postoperative complication rate was 5.4% (6/112), with 5 (4.5%) Grade II and 1 (0.9%) Grade III complications (Clavien-Dindo classification). Postoperative complications included incisional infection (2 cases, Clavien II), atelectasis (2 cases, Clavien II), other minor complication (1 case, Clavien II), and urinary tract infection (1 case, Clavien III) (Table 1).

The 5-year overall survival (OS) rate was calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method. By the end of follow-up, 11 patients were lost to follow-up and were treated as censored data. The 5-year OS rate was 17.2% (95% CI: 10.5%−24.9%). Postoperatively, a total of 58 patients exhibited tumor progression. This included 9 cases of local recurrence, with 8 of these cases recurring in regional lymph nodes and 1 in the ipsilateral adrenal gland. Additionally, 54 patients developed distant metastasis, comprising 25 with extraregional lymph node metastasis, 17 with lung metastasis, 18 with bone metastasis, and 7 with liver metastasis. Throughout the follow-up period, 59 patients ultimately succumbed to their disease. Of the 58 patients who experienced tumor progression, 21 received systemic therapy: 14 (66.7% of those treated) received PD-1 inhibitor immunotherapy, 6 (28.6%) received tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) targeted therapy, and 1 (4.7%) received supportive care only as their primary post-progression treatment. Among the 54 patients who did not experience tumor progression, 4 (7.4%) received adjuvant therapy, whereas among the 58 patients who did progress, 17 (29.3%) received adjuvant therapy, typically initiated after progression was detected. A direct comparison of recurrence risk based on postoperative adjuvant therapy was challenging due to this selection bias. However, in an exploratory analysis of high-risk patients (pT3/4), those who received adjuvant therapy (n = 6) showed a trend towards longer RFS compared to a matched group who did not (n = 12), although this was not statistically significant (median RFS 15.2 vs. 9.8 months, p = 0.18), highlighting the need for larger, prospective trials.

The median relapse-free survival (RFS) after surgery was 0.90 (0.35, 2.60) years, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year RFS rates of 44.6%, 21.5%, and 12.1%, respectively. The median overall survival (OS) was 2.25 (1.15, 4.10) years, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates of 78.5%, 36.8%, and 17.2%, respectively (Fig. 1).

Risk factors for tumor progression

A total of 58 patients experienced tumor progression (recurrence and metastasis) postoperatively, of which 43 (74.1%) died due to tumor progression. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis showed that patients with preoperative lymph node enlargement had a significantly higher risk of tumor progression (P = 0.021; HR and 95% CI detailed in Table 3). Furthermore, multigroup regional lymph node metastasis was associated with tumor progression in univariate analysis but did not maintain significance in multivariate analysis (detailed in Table 3). Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis demonstrated that patients with preoperative lymph node enlargement had worse RFS (P < 0.0001) (Table 3; Fig. 3).

Subgroup analysis - risk factors for distant metastasis

Among the 54 patients who developed distant metastasis postoperatively, multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis revealed that those with preoperative lymph node enlargement were significantly more likely to experience distant metastasis following surgery. Furthermore, Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis demonstrated that patients with preoperative lymph node enlargement exhibited significantly worse RFS and OS (P < 0.05). Subgroup analysis - factors influencing local tumor recurrence

Nine patients experienced local recurrence postoperatively. Among them, 6 patients had a pT stage of ≥ pT3a, and the pathological types were papillary RCC (7 cases), poorly differentiated RCC (1 case), and renal collecting duct carcinoma (1 case). Six patients died, with pathological diagnoses of papillary RCC (5 cases) and poorly differentiated RCC (1 case). Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis indicated that maximum tumor diameter, pT stage, preoperative lymph node enlargement, number of lymph node metastases, intraluminal tumor thrombus, tumor necrosis, and sarcomatoid differentiation had no significant impact on local recurrence (P > 0.05). Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis indicated that patients with multigroup regional lymph node metastasis exhibited a significantly higher likelihood of experiencing local recurrence postoperatively (P = 0.031; HR and 95% CI detailed in Table 5). This suggests that multigroup regional lymph node metastasis constitute an independent risk factor for local recurrence. Additionally, Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis revealed that patients with multigroup regional lymph node metastasis had notably worse postoperative RFS and OS (P < 0.05). Detailed results are presented in Table 5 and illustrated in Fig. 5.

Discussion

Early-stage RCC often presents with atypical symptoms and is frequently detected during routine medical examinations. Locally advanced RCC patients typically seek medical attention due to symptoms such as hematuria and flank pain caused by tumor invasion of the renal pelvis or renal capsule. Surgery remains the primary treatment modality for non-metastatic RCC. In this study, the 5-year survival rate for patients with locally advanced RCC at pN1 stage was only 17.2%, significantly lower than that of patients with localized RCC, where the 5-year survival rate can reach 80% in previous studies [7]. This discrepancy may be related to the high risk of recurrence and metastasis after surgery in locally advanced RCC. In our study, approximately 51.8% of pN1 patients experienced tumor progression postoperatively, and patients with sarcomatoid differentiation had worse independent risk factors for poor survival (P < 0.05), which is consistent with previous findings [8].

The perinephric lymphatic system is rich in lymphatic drainage and is one of the primary pathways for RCC metastasis [9]. A prospective clinical study published in the European Urology Journal demonstrated that regional lymph node dissection did not improve the prognosis of patients with localized RCC (primarily low-stage pT1 ~ 2N0M0 patients), with a total lymph node metastasis rate of only 4% in postoperative pathology [10]. This may be primarily due to the lower likelihood of metastasis in localized RCC. The high proportion of lymph node infiltration in RCC is associated with higher T stages [11], suggesting that regional lymph node dissection may not be beneficial for lower-stage RCC patients. Additionally, one retrospective study supported that lymph node dissection does not improve the prognosis of RCC patients [12], while some scholars found that lymph node dissection is associated with improved survival outcomes in RCC patients [13], with the effect being positively correlated with the number of positive lymph nodes resected [14]. Therefore, investigating the clinical efficacy of regional lymph node dissection in high-risk locally advanced RCC patients (pT3 stage and above) is of significant importance. In this study, 59.8% (67/112) of patients with positive lymph nodes in postoperative pathology had preoperative lymph node enlargement, with 82.1% (55/67) being pT3 stage or above. Among patients with pT3 stage or above who had no lymph node enlargement on preoperative imaging but positive lymph nodes in postoperative pathology, the proportion was 53.3% (24/45). This indicates that patients with higher pT stages, even if they show no lymph node enlargement preoperatively, are at high risk of lymph node metastasis. In this cohort, patients who underwent regional lymph node dissection had a local recurrence rate of 8.0% (9/112); however, a causal association cannot be definitively established without a control group, and further studies with larger sample sizes and comparative designs are needed to verify this potential benefit. The incidence of postoperative grade 2 to 3 complications was only 5.4% (6/112), and no increase in complications was observed due to regional lymph node dissection. Thus, radical nephrectomy combined with regional lymph node dissection is safe and feasible for locally advanced RCC. It is noteworthy that some studies [12] suggest LND has not shown a survival benefit in RCC, possibly because in cohorts with predominantly early-stage disease, the low rate of lymph node metastasis (< 5%) means the therapeutic value of LND is offset by surgical risks. The patients in our study were largely locally advanced (pT3 and above in 71.4% of cases), which might better highlight the potential local control benefits of LND. It is important to interpret the findings related to local recurrence with caution, as the wide confidence interval in the multivariate analysis (HR 4.911, 95% CI 1.120–21.571) reflects the small number of local recurrence events (n = 9). Recently, sentinel lymph node biopsy has been proposed as a method to assess lymph node metastasis with safety and reliability. However, current studies have small sample sizes, and further large-scale studies are needed to validate its efficacy [15].

Postoperative pathology indicating locally advanced RCC at the pN1 stage carries a high risk of recurrence and distant metastasis. In this study, among 112 patients with pT1 ~ 4N1M0 stage, 54 patients did not experience recurrence or metastasis during follow-up, including 31 patients (57.4%) at pT3 stage or above. This suggests that even among high-risk patients, approximately 48.2% may achieve long-term disease-free status with radical nephrectomy combined with regional lymph node dissection. As previously noted, metastasis to multiple anatomical regions is an independent risk factor for local recurrence. Therefore, patients with such findings should be promptly considered for adjuvant therapy to reduce this risk. Patients with preoperative imaging showing lymph node enlargement are more likely to experience distant metastasis and have shorter survival times. The potential role of neoadjuvant therapy, such as achieving downstaging, for patients with preoperative imaging showing lymph node enlargement requires exploration in future prospective studies, as this was not addressed in our current research [16].

A study on sunitinib for locally advanced RCC with high recurrence risk demonstrated that sunitinib can prolong postoperative recurrence-free survival and reduce the risk of recurrence by 26%. Sunitinib is also recommended by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for postoperative adjuvant therapy in RCC [17]. Another study on pembrolizumab for postoperative adjuvant therapy in locally advanced RCC with high recurrence risk showed that it significantly extended recurrence-free survival and OS, also being recommended by the FDA for RCC postoperative adjuvant therapy [18]. A recent meta-analysis further supported the role of immune checkpoint inhibitors in the adjuvant setting, showing a significant improvement in disease-free survival across multiple trials, particularly in high-risk populations like ours [19]. Additionally, targeted therapy and PD-1 monoclonal antibody immunotherapy provide better treatment options for locally advanced RCC patients with lymph node metastasis. Many studies are currently exploring whether neoadjuvant therapy before surgery for locally advanced RCC can preserve the kidney and reduce the risk of postoperative recurrence and metastasis. A meta-analysis showed that 74.4% of patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy achieved stable disease (SD), with an average tumor shrinkage of 14.4% [20]. Regional lymph node dissection might enhance the efficacy of postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy by reducing tumor antigen burden or mitigating an immunosuppressive microenvironment [21]. In our study, although not a direct comparison due to treatment heterogeneity, observations suggested that patients who underwent LND and received adjuvant immunotherapy tended to have better outcomes than historical cohorts or those not receiving comprehensive treatment, but this requires dedicated investigation.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single-center design may have introduced selection bias (e.g., including only surgical patients and potentially excluding advanced non-surgical cases) and limits the generalizability of our findings. Second, the sample size is relatively small, particularly for subgroup analyses like local recurrence (n = 9), which may affect the statistical power of multivariate analyses and contribute to wide confidence intervals for some hazard ratios. Third, there was no standardized protocol for postoperative adjuvant therapy (e.g., use of targeted therapy or immunotherapy), and variations in its administration could confound the prognostic analysis (e.g., patients receiving immunotherapy might have inherently better survival prospects or tumor biology). Finally, the lack of a non-LND control group means we cannot definitively establish the therapeutic benefit of lymphadenectomy itself, only describe outcomes within a cohort that received it.

To conclude, patients with locally advanced RCC who exhibit positive lymph nodes in postoperative pathology are at a significantly elevated risk of recurrence and metastasis. Consequently, while this study’s findings are observational, they align with some existing literature [22] suggesting that consideration of regional lymph node dissection for individuals with positive preoperative imaging findings, particularly in cases of locally advanced RCC at pT3 stage or higher, may be warranted. This intervention may offer clinical benefits, particularly for local control, but its impact on survival requires validation through prospective, randomized controlled trials.

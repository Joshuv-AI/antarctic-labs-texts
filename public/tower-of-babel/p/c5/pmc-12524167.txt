Leveraging transfer learning and maintenance domain adaptation to improve useful life prediction accuracy of high pressure compressor of aeroengine
Authors: Shaktiyavesh Nandan Pratap Singh, Rohitashwa Shringi, Manish Chaturvedi, Sakshi Shringi, Harish Sharma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPredicting the Critical Useful Life (CUL) of aeroengine components, especially the High-Pressure Compressor (HPC), accurately serves both safety operational needs and maintenance optimisation. Machine learning shows promising results for CUL prediction; however, the missing point is having enough specific model and environment-related failure data to properly train the systems. The research investigates Transfer Learning (TL) and Maintenance Domain Approaches (MDA) to enhance CUL prediction models. The method draws information from aeroengine components that possess abundant failure records, intending to enhance performance in the target HPC despite restricted labelled data availability. The research analyses different MDA strategies that serve to link simulated or historical data to the actual operational conditions and maintenance practices of field operations. Real-world alongside simulated aeroengine data will serve this investigation, which evaluates different TL and MDA techniques with pre-trained model fine-tuning and feature design. Thus, through advanced HPC CUL prediction accuracy, the study predicts significant progress which will improve both predictive maintenance efficiency and aeroengine operational availability. Therefore, this approach employs knowledge in the domain while adopting it to field maintenance properties so that one can derive an accurate, practical CUL prediction for HPC. The proposed methodology provides the following enhanced outcomes:•The trained model has a fitness of 98.82 % and a minimal error index of 7.4926.•The proposed model worked against the test, where the outcome is highly linear with a minimal error index of 11.7181 and 97.02 % fitness.•HPC is found to be a more critical part of the engine.Keywords: Machine learning, Aero engine, Condition monitoring, Useful life, Predictive modelling, Maintenance Engineering and Management, Smart manufacturing

Background

A proper prediction of the Critical Useful Life (CUL) for important aeroengine parts like high-pressure compressors affects both aviation safety and operational expenditure and maintenance procedures. As per the latest trends in diagnosis and maintenance of aeroengines, 22 % research is being focused towards the utilisation of Machine Learning (ML)/ Artificial Intelligence (AI) based solutions [1]. The terminology, like Remaining Useful Life (RUL), which is being explored nowadays, is defined as the time interval between the engine’s maximum cycle of operation and the present cycle of operation. In prediction modelling, the RUL estimation has the potential to reduce aircraft accidents due to engine failures [2]. Whereas, traditional calculation of CUL depends on both physical simulation-based models and statistical dataset analysis from historical breakdowns. CUL prediction constitutes a difficult task because of complex system structures, together with diverse operating environments and limited and unequally distributed failure data. ML models demonstrate strong potential for improving data-based solutions in the prediction of unwanted chemical behaviour. ML models utilise performance and operational parameters to identify complex patterns, which they then use to determine the remaining useful lifetime of components [3]. The application of ML to particular aeroengine models and single units faces major barriers due to restricted access to failed data sets. The process of acquiring enough failure data becomes both expensive and inefficient because it requires a prolonged time, which leads to practical challenges when using new engine models or highly reliable components. The lack of appropriate data requires exploring methods that efficiently use information found across separate yet relevant data sets like the Aeroengine Propulsion System Simulation (APSS) dataset [4]. The TL framework serves as a key solution when addressing this situation. An essential role of TL allows engineers to reuse knowledge acquired from resourceful data domains (different models or similar components or simulation outputs) for domains that possess limited annotated information (specific HPC models operating in certain conditions). TL enables the successful movement of acquired patterns from source domains to enhance the performance of target domain ML models, which lack sufficient data. TL presents itself as an effective framework for this situation. Under the TL framework, learned knowledge from supplier domains which possess ample data (used examples include alternative engine variants and component versions or simulated datasets) is relocated to target domains with minimal labelled data (focused on specific HPC models running in operational environments). The transfer of distinct knowledge patterns through TL boosts the performance capabilities of models which operate in data-limited environments. The mismatch between simulated or generic historical data and actual operational conditions with maintenance practices in the field develops an additional level of difficulty. When applying real-world field data to ML models trained using source data, the degradation in performance becomes a result of domain differences between these datasets. MDA techniques work to solve these challenges through space feature alignment between the domains to create models that demonstrate robustness and generalisation in practical applications.

The development of a methodology which combines transfer learning with maintenance domain adaptation for HPC CUL prediction targets the essential requirement to address challenges from limited available data and changing domains. Strategic predictive maintenance algorithms implemented by maintenance teams achieve greater power through these technological advances to reduce unexpected downtime, improve safety and minimise lifecycle costs of aeroengines. This method utilises domain-related knowledge while adopting models to field maintenance characteristics to create precise, practical CUL prediction for HPC. The method provides a solution to construct a reliable predictive model in situations where failure data exists for restricted HPC models operating in particular field conditions.

Model tuning

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is utilised to extract the feedback of component degradation from the sensor signal and trained model as a data dimension reduction method, which delivered acceptable results for performance optimisation. This research study implements the Component Reduction Criterion at a 95 % variance level. After training, only 2 components were kept out of the 6 PCA coefficients. The identified components exhibit a breakdown of variances equal to 84.6 %, 14.9 %, 0.5 %, 0.0 %, 0.0 % and 0.0 % when no optimiser was utilised. PCA Centres are 51.5025, 109.2468, 1.5905e+03, 553.3677, 47.5413 and 38.8157.

Model training: ensembled bagged-random forest regression

The researchers implemented predictive ML modelling by writing code on the MATLAB platform. The critical analysis aspect of the proposed ML model is carried out through MATLAB software version R2023a, on a high-end computer hardware set, i.e. i7–7500 with GTX1080GPU processor, high bandwidth memory, and solid state drives as available in the University’s Aviation Laboratory. The assessment for selecting the best-fit model depended on the four statistical metrics, including Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Coefficient of Determination (R²), alongside Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The ensemble technique utilises linear grouping of best-fit ML modelling instead of utilising a single fit, and therefore, this method became predominant to improve the prediction performance of any basic ML method [5]. Method validation

The validation and evaluation involved running an ensembled bagged regression-random forest of ML predictive models [7] with 20 % holdout testing for selecting the model which delivered optimal accuracy alongside minimal bias. The established previous research [[4], [5], [6]] provides methodological groundwork for this procedure so that researchers can compare model effectiveness between studies. Testing and validation of the selected model takes place through examination of the test data set. The study uses statistical outcomes of the field dataset to support its validation procedures. The model validation process takes place through a 20 % holdout technique. The RMSE, along with R² values, demonstrate a highly acceptable performance at 7.4926 and 98.82 %. Limitations

This investigation predicts the CUL located in the HPC section of only Turbofan aeroengines to enhance its performance capabilities. The applicability of the Turboprop, Ramjet or other aero engines has not been tested due to the non-availability of the dataset. During the field survey, only 158 respondents could turn out for the subject study.

Ethics statements

We used the Aeroengine Propulsion System Simulation dataset from the NASA prognostics data repository accessible through the provided reference link. The proposed research project maintains clear reporting and follows ethical evaluation procedures through appropriate referencing and functions to enhance long-term predictive abilities. The data from respondents in this study remains fully anonymised, with our research complying with the policies of data redistribution from platform(s).

Telepalliative Care on Quality of Life, Symptom Management, and Patient Satisfaction: A Systematic Review
Authors: Tuti Anggriani Utama, Restuning Widiasih, Kusman Ibrahim, Nita Arisanti, Putri Karisa, Karina Fitria Alhaq
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTelepalliative care has emerged as an innovative approach to expanding access to palliative services through digital health technologies. Despite increasing adoption, evidence regarding its impact on patient and caregiver outcomes remains heterogeneous. This systematic review aimed to synthesize recent evidence on the impact of telepalliative care on quality of life, symptom management, and satisfaction among patients and caregivers receiving palliative care. A systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Literature searches were performed in PubMed, EBSCO, and ScienceDirect to identify studies published between 2021 and 2025. Eligible studies included randomized controlled trials, pilot studies, and qualitative studies evaluating telepalliative care interventions. Data were synthesized narratively, and methodological quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Tools. Twelve studies were included in the review. Telepalliative interventions comprised telephone-based care, mobile applications, messaging platforms, telehealth consultations, remote monitoring systems, and web-based programs. The evidence suggests that telepalliative care may support quality of life, symptom management, and satisfaction in some settings by enhancing communication, symptom monitoring, education, and care coordination. However, findings were heterogeneous across studies, and benefits were not consistently observed across all populations and outcome domains. Improvements in quality of life and symptom management were reported in several studies, whereas psychological outcomes and caregiver-related outcomes, including caregiver burden and self-efficacy, showed less consistent results. Digital literacy, technology access, caregiver support, and disease characteristics were identified as important factors influencing implementation and outcomes. Telepalliative care demonstrates promising potential to enhance palliative care delivery and improve access to services, particularly in resource-limited settings. Nevertheless, intervention benefits vary according to population characteristics, intervention modalities, and implementation contexts. Further high-quality research is needed to identify the most effective telepalliative strategies and optimize implementation across diverse care settings.

Introduction

Palliative care for patients at the end of life consists of continuous care delivered until the final phase of life. This care aims to improve the quality of life of adults, children, and families living with life-threatening illnesses through the identification and management of pain.1 In addition, palliative care encompasses physical, psychological, psychosocial, and spiritual support for terminally ill patients.2 Globally, the World Health Organization (WHO) explicitly highlights the importance of palliative care as part of comprehensive services for non-communicable diseases.3 In Indonesia, palliative care remains less established and less commonly practiced compared to curative and rehabilitative services, and its development poses future challenges.

The demand for palliative services has increased substantially worldwide. It is estimated that by 2060, approximately 87% of individuals with life-threatening illnesses will require palliative care, including 20 million people annually, most of whom are aged over 60 years, with around 6% being children.4 These data underscore the urgency of palliative care in responding to the growing older adult population and the rising burden of complex chronic conditions. Palliative care plays a crucial role in reducing suffering and enhancing the quality of life of patients and families through a holistic approach. Ideally, these interventions are implemented across three main phases of disease trajectory: the early stage of illness, at the time of diagnosis, and when symptoms emerge or deteriorate. With appropriate and sustained implementation, palliative care is expected to offer optimal support throughout the disease process until the end of life.

Rapid advancements in information and communication technology have driven the need for healthcare systems to adapt to digital transformation. This includes the use of digital technologies and health innovations to improve health and well-being.5 The development of telemedicine, the enhancement of digital security, and the strengthening of health information systems contribute to greater acceptance of digital health transformation among stakeholders.6 The use of technology in healthcare serves multiple functions, including improving care outcomes, enhancing public health, improving patient experience, supporting healthcare providers, and reducing health disparities.7

Despite the growing adoption of digital health technologies, important barriers remain in the implementation of telepalliative care.8 Many palliative care recipients are older adults living with advanced chronic illnesses, cognitive decline, functional limitations, fatigue, or multiple symptoms that may hinder their ability to engage with digital platforms.9 In addition, disparities in digital literacy, internet connectivity, access to smartphones or computers, and socioeconomic conditions may influence the effectiveness and accessibility of telepalliative interventions.10 These challenges, often referred to as the digital divide, have become increasingly important considerations in the development and implementation of technology-enabled palliative care services.11

Telepalliative care is an innovative model of remote palliative service delivery that enables communication, monitoring, education, and supportive care through digital technologies. Research findings indicate that telenursing provides valuable support to conventional care, particularly in primary care, rural areas, and low-resource settings.12 Nurses generally perceive telenursing as beneficial, although implementation requires careful adaptation to local contexts and patient needs.13 Telepalliative services have demonstrated promising outcomes when supported by adequate professional competencies, effective communication, and coordinated interdisciplinary care.14 Limited access to healthcare facilities among patients requiring palliative services has further increased interest in telepalliative approaches. Video consultations, mobile applications, web-based platforms, and remote monitoring systems can facilitate home-based palliative care while supporting multidisciplinary collaboration among healthcare professionals.15 However, successful implementation depends not only on the availability of technology but also on patients’ and caregivers’ digital literacy, access to digital resources, caregiver involvement, and healthcare system readiness.

Quality of life is multidimensional, encompassing an individual’s perception of well-being based on physical, psychological, social, and sexual aspects.16 Previous studies have demonstrated that telenursing is effective in improving quality of life among patients diagnosed with chronic illnesses.17 In patients with chronic heart failure, telenursing has been shown to enhance quality of life by guiding patients to maintain daily activities through monitoring their physical condition.18

The presence of multiple symptoms in palliative patients presents a significant challenge for nurses. The use of telehealth or remote interventions has been proven to reduce the severity of symptoms, including pain. However, remote interventions did not produce significant effects in reducing emergency visits or hospital admissions.19 Additional benefits of telenursing include improved self-efficacy and health status, as patients and family caregivers can more effectively manage emerging symptoms.20

Telenursing has also demonstrated positive effects for family caregivers in home-based care, as they perceive remote interventions as providing easier access to information compared to face-to-face visits. Caregivers of palliative patients reported that the use of telenursing is likely to become more widespread in the future, particularly for palliative care. Consistent findings were demonstrated in the study by Abdelwahab et al, which reported increased caregiver satisfaction following a tele-palliative education intervention.17 Caregivers showed improved ability to manage symptoms, direct communication with healthcare professionals, and enhanced emotional support.

Although previous studies have reported potential benefits of telepalliative interventions, the available evidence remains heterogeneous across populations, intervention modalities, and outcome measures. Existing studies have reported inconsistent findings regarding quality of life, symptom management, psychological outcomes, and caregiver-related outcomes. Furthermore, patient-centered outcomes and caregiver-centered outcomes are frequently synthesized together despite representing distinct domains of palliative care. In Indonesia, where telepalliative services remain relatively underdeveloped and geographical barriers continue to limit access to specialist palliative care, a comprehensive synthesis of recent international evidence is needed to inform future service development and policy initiatives. Therefore, this systematic review aimed to synthesize contemporary evidence regarding the impact of telepalliative care on quality of life, symptom management, and patient and caregiver satisfaction.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This systematic review was conducted and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) statement.

Data Source and Search Strategy

This study applied a systematic review design following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Article searches were conducted in PubMed, EBSCO, and ScienceDirect to identify relevant studies published between 2021 and 2025. These databases were selected because they provide broad coverage of health sciences, nursing, medical, and multidisciplinary healthcare literature. The search strategy was developed to capture studies evaluating telepalliative care interventions and their impact on quality of life, symptom management, satisfaction, and related outcomes in palliative care settings.

Eligibility Criteria

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (1) available in full text; (2) published between 2021 and 2025; and (3) employed study designs such as randomized controlled trials, pilot studies, or qualitative studies. Studies were excluded if they were literature reviews, systematic reviews, editorials, conference abstracts, or other publications that did not report original research findings. The publication period was limited to 2021–2025 to capture the most recent evidence reflecting contemporary telehealth technologies, evolving digital health practices, and post-pandemic developments in palliative care delivery.

Study Selection Process

All identified articles were imported into Rayyan, where duplicates were identified and removed using the software’s automated features. Two independent reviewers (TAU and RW) screened titles and abstracts for eligibility, followed by full-text screening of potentially relevant studies. Any disagreements during study selection were resolved through discussion and consensus. When consensus could not be achieved, the corresponding author acted as an independent adjudicator.

Data Extraction

Data extraction was performed by two independent reviewers (KA and KFA) using a standardized data extraction template in Google Sheets (spreadsheet). The extracted data were then organized systematically starting with the extraction of key study characteristics, that includes the names of the authors, year of publication, study design, and population details (including eligibility criteria and sample size). Furthermore, data regarding the exposure (digital health–integrated palliative care intervention), comparison group (usual or standard care), and reported outcomes were recorded. In addition, detailed outcome-related variables were extracted, including measures of caregiver-related outcomes such as caregiver empowerment, burden, psychological well-being, and quality of life. All extracted data were cross-checked between reviewers, and any discrepancies were resolved through discussion and consensus. In cases where consensus could not be reached, the final decision was made by the corresponding author prior to data finalization and manuscript submission to ensure accuracy and consistency of the dataset.

Quality Assessment

The JBI Critical Appraisal Tools were selected because they provide structured methodological assessment across different study designs, including randomized controlled trials, pilot studies, and mixed-methods research. The assessment focused on methodological rigor, risk of bias, validity of outcome measurements, and completeness of follow-up.

Certainty of Evidence

A formal certainty-of-evidence assessment using the GRADE approach was not performed because the review included heterogeneous study designs, intervention modalities, and outcome measures that limited direct comparability across studies. Consequently, the findings should be interpreted with consideration of the methodological diversity of the included evidence.

Data Synthesis

A narrative synthesis approach was employed to summarize and interpret the findings of the included studies. Meta-analysis was not conducted because of substantial heterogeneity across studies in terms of intervention modalities, populations, outcome measures, follow-up periods, and study designs. Consequently, pooling quantitative effect estimates was considered inappropriate. Findings were synthesized according to major outcome domains, including quality of life, symptom management, psychological outcomes, patient and caregiver satisfaction, and factors influencing intervention effectiveness.

Strengthening

To enhance methodological rigor, all stages of article screening, data extraction, and quality appraisal were conducted independently by multiple reviewers using predefined procedures and standardized extraction forms.

The study populations varied, including patients with chronic conditions such as cancer, heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and interstitial lung disease (ILD), as well as family caregivers. Quality Assessment

Effects of Digital Health Interventions in Palliative Care

Quality of Life

Evidence regarding the impact of telepalliative care on quality of life was mixed Studies by Bekelman et al and Mirshahi et al reported improvements in quality-of-life following telehealth-based and nurse-led telepalliative interventions18,19, In addition, Lee et al suggested that mobile-based symptom monitoring may contribute to improved patient engagement and quality of life. However, these findings were not consistently observed across all studies.15 Schuit et al and Dionne-Odom et al reported no significant differences compared with standard care, particularly among caregiver populations. Overall, current evidence suggests potential benefits for quality of life, although findings remain heterogeneous across intervention modalities and target populations.20,21,

Symptom Management

Evidence for symptom management outcomes was generally favorable but remained inconsistent. Studies by Lv et al and Lee et al demonstrated that mobile applications and telehealth systems facilitated symptom monitoring, patient engagement, and early interventions.15,21, Bakitas et al reported clinically meaningful reductions in symptom distress, although statistical significance was not achieved. Similarly, Mayahara et al observed potential benefits in pain management among hospice patients.15,22, In contrast, Ngoma et al found comparable outcomes between intervention and control groups. These findings indicate that telepalliative care may support symptom management in selected settings, although effectiveness is not uniformly demonstrated across studies.22

Psychological Outcomes

Findings related to psychological and emotional outcomes were inconsistent. Akechi et al found no significant improvement in depressive symptoms following a smartphone-based cognitive behavioral therapy intervention.23 Conversely, studies by Mirshahi et al and Schmucker et al reported improvements in engagement, communication, and perceived emotional well-being; however, these effects were not consistently statistically significant. Overall, the available evidence remains insufficient to establish consistent psychological benefits of telepalliative interventions.19,24,

Caregiver Outcomes

Evidence regarding caregiver outcomes was limited and generally inconclusive. Schuit et al found no significant effect of the Oncokompas intervention on caregiver burden, self-efficacy, or health-related quality of life among caregivers of patients with incurable cancer.20 Similarly, Dionne-Odom et al reported no significant improvements in caregiver quality of life, mood, or burden following a nurse-led telehealth intervention.28 These findings suggest that telepalliative interventions may not consistently address caregiver-related outcomes despite potential benefits observed in patient-centered domains.

Satisfaction Outcomes

Satisfaction outcomes were generally positive, although variations were observed across intervention modalities. Ngoma et al and Laranjeira et al reported favorable perceptions of accessibility, communication, and support among users of digital health interventions.22,27 Lee et al also observed enhanced engagement and convenience associated with telehealth-based care. However, Schmucker et al reported no significant differences in satisfaction compared with standard care. Collectively, these findings suggest that telepalliative interventions are generally acceptable to patients and caregivers, although satisfaction outcomes may vary according to intervention characteristics and user context.24

Influencing Factors

Several factors appeared to influence the effectiveness and implementation of telepalliative interventions. Digital literacy, technology access, caregiver support, and healthcare setting were frequently identified as important determinants of successful implementation.27 Variations in disease trajectory, symptom burden, and patient characteristics also contributed to differences in intervention outcomes. These findings highlight the importance of contextual and user-related factors in determining the success of telepalliative care beyond the intervention itself.24

Discussion

Telepalliative care represents a modern information and communication technology–enabled approach that facilitates the remote delivery of palliative care services. Patients and families experience diverse needs throughout the disease trajectory, which may be addressed through the early integration of telepalliative care using flexible care models supported by contemporary digital technologies.29–32 This review found that telepalliative interventions have been implemented across a range of chronic and life-limiting conditions, including heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), interstitial lung disease (ILD), cancer, and end-stage renal disease. The findings suggest that telepalliative care may contribute to improvements in quality of life, symptom management, and satisfaction in some settings; however, the evidence remains heterogeneous, and benefits were not consistently observed across all studies, populations, and intervention modalities. Quality of life remains a critical outcome in palliative care because it reflects patient comfort, well-being, and overall adaptation to the challenges associated with advanced illness. Interventions identified in this review included telephone-based support, mobile applications, messaging platforms such as WhatsApp, and other telehealth approaches designed to support symptom monitoring, communication, emotional support, and care coordination.

Evidence regarding the impact of telepalliative care on quality of life remains mixed. Studies by Bekelman et al and Mirshahi et al reported improvements in quality of life among patients receiving telepalliative interventions, suggesting that remote support, symptom monitoring, and ongoing communication may contribute to enhanced well-being.18,19, However, these benefits were not consistently observed across all studies. For example, Schuit et al found that the Oncokompas eHealth intervention did not significantly improve caregiver burden, self-efficacy, or health-related quality of life among caregivers of patients with incurable cancer.20 The variability in findings may reflect differences in intervention characteristics, participant populations, baseline needs, digital literacy, family support, and levels of engagement with digital technologies. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of telepalliative care may be context-dependent and influenced by both individual and system-level factors.

Evidence from emergency and community-based settings further illustrates the complexity of telepalliative care outcomes. The EMPallA trial included patients with advanced illness presenting to emergency departments and demonstrated substantial variations in quality of life, symptom burden, and loneliness across disease groups.24 These findings suggest that patient needs and responses to palliative interventions may differ according to disease trajectory and clinical context. Consequently, telepalliative services may require flexible and individualized approaches that account for variations in patient characteristics and care settings.

Caregiver-related outcomes appeared less consistent than patient-centered outcomes. Dionne-Odom et al evaluated a nurse-led telehealth intervention for caregivers of individuals with advanced heart failure and reported no significant improvements in caregiver quality of life, mood, or caregiver burden over the study period.28 One possible explanation is that many caregivers reported relatively low levels of distress at baseline, limiting the potential for measurable improvement. These findings suggest that caregiver-focused telepalliative interventions may be more effective when targeted toward caregivers experiencing substantial burden, psychological distress, or unmet support needs. Future research should therefore explore more tailored approaches that address the specific challenges faced by family caregivers throughout the palliative care trajectory.

Evidence regarding the role of telepalliative care in symptom management was generally favorable but remained heterogeneous across studies. Several interventions reported improvements in symptom monitoring, patient engagement, and symptom control. For example, smartphone-based interventions were associated with reductions in pain, fatigue, and sleep disturbances among patients with advanced illness, while digital symptom-monitoring systems appeared to facilitate earlier identification and management of emerging symptoms.15,22, These findings suggest that telepalliative care may enhance symptom management by promoting continuous monitoring, timely communication, and patient participation in care.

However, positive findings were not consistently observed across all studies. Akechi et al found no significant improvement in depressive symptoms following a smartphone-based psychological intervention.23 While Bakitas et al reported clinically meaningful but statistically non-significant reductions in symptom distress among rural patients receiving video-based palliative consultations.25 The variability in outcomes may reflect differences in intervention intensity, symptom severity, follow-up duration, patient engagement, and technological accessibility. Collectively, these findings suggest that telepalliative care has the potential to support symptom management, although its effectiveness may vary according to patient characteristics, intervention design, and care context (Figure 2). Satisfaction outcomes associated with telepalliative care were generally positive across the included studies. Digital interventions were frequently perceived as improving accessibility, communication, and continuity of support, which contributed to favorable user experiences among patients and caregivers.22,27 Application-based interventions offered additional features such as symptom tracking, educational resources, self-management support, and direct communication with healthcare providers, which may enhance user engagement and perceived usefulness. However, the current evidence does not conclusively demonstrate that application-based interventions are consistently superior to telephone-based approaches across all populations and care settings. Differences in satisfaction may be influenced by user preferences, digital literacy, technological accessibility, intervention design, and healthcare context. Therefore, the selection of telepalliative modalities should be guided by patient and caregiver needs rather than assumptions regarding the superiority of a specific technology platform.

Nevertheless, numerous challenges remain in implementation. Telepalliative services must be adjusted to patient disease characteristics and caregiver knowledge, as limited experience with technology can hinder uptake. Despite these challenges, technology offers substantial benefits by improving access, saving time, and strengthening the therapeutic relationship between nurses, patients, and families. Therefore, ongoing development is required to maximize the potential of telepalliative care. Supporting strategies such as user training, provision of user-friendly devices, real-time monitoring tools for objective assessment, and technical assistance are critical for optimizing effectiveness.

Digital Divide and Accessibility Challenges

The findings of this review suggest that the effectiveness of telepalliative care is influenced not only by intervention characteristics but also by patients’ and caregivers’ ability to access and use digital technologies. Many palliative care recipients are older adults with advanced chronic illnesses, functional limitations, cognitive decline, or high symptom burdens that may hinder engagement with digital platforms. In addition, disparities in internet connectivity, device availability, socioeconomic resources, and digital literacy may contribute to variations in participation and outcomes across studies. These findings highlight the importance of providing technical support, digital education, and user-friendly technologies to ensure equitable and effective implementation of telepalliative care.

Implications for Practice and Policy

The findings of this review suggest that telepalliative care may serve as a valuable complement to conventional palliative services by improving access, facilitating communication, and supporting symptom monitoring, particularly for patients facing geographical or resource-related barriers. However, successful implementation requires adequate digital infrastructure, workforce preparedness, technical support, and equitable access to technology. Integrating telepalliative care into existing healthcare pathways may help enhance the accessibility and sustainability of palliative care services while addressing the diverse needs of patients and caregivers.

Limitation

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this review. First, the included studies demonstrated substantial heterogeneity in terms of patient populations, intervention modalities, outcome measures, and follow-up periods, which limited direct comparisons across studies. Second, the literature search was conducted using PubMed, EBSCO, and ScienceDirect and did not include databases such as CINAHL or the Cochrane Library, potentially resulting in the omission of relevant studies. Third, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed because of the methodological and clinical heterogeneity of the included studies. Finally, a formal certainty-of-evidence assessment using the GRADE approach was not conducted. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as a synthesis of the currently available evidence rather than definitive estimates of telepalliative care effectiveness.

Conclusion

This systematic review identified a range of telepalliative care modalities, including telephone-based care, mobile applications, messaging platforms, and telehealth interventions. Overall, the evidence suggests that telepalliative care has the potential to support quality of life, symptom management, and satisfaction among patients and caregivers receiving palliative care. However, the findings remain heterogeneous, and benefits were not consistently observed across all studies, populations, and outcome domains, particularly for psychological and caregiver-related outcomes.

The effectiveness of telepalliative interventions appears to be influenced by factors such as digital literacy, technology access, caregiver support, and patient characteristics. While telepalliative care offers promising opportunities to improve access to palliative services, particularly in resource-limited and geographically dispersed settings, further high-quality research is needed to clarify its long-term effectiveness and identify the most appropriate intervention strategies for different patient and caregiver populations.

Alzheimer's Association research investments break records in 2025
Published: 2025

The Alzheimer's Association invested $112.2 million across its research program in 2025—the largest single‐year investment since the organization's inception.

This translates to new investments in more than 250 scientific investigations through its International Research Grant Program and brings its total active and committed investments to more than $450 million in more than 1200 projects in 56 countries. This investment supports wide‐ranging initiatives, including:

The Alzheimer's Association International Society to Advance Alzheimer's Research and Treatment (ISTAART), which supports a global community of more than 13,000 dementia researchers and clinicians.

The Alzheimer's Association International Society to Advance Alzheimer's Research and Treatment (ISTAART), which supports a global community of more than 13,000 dementia researchers and clinicians.

Alzheimer's Association honors the nearly 12 million Americans who are dementia caregivers

November is National Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month and National Family Caregivers Month, a time when the Alzheimer's Association recognizes the essential role of family caregivers in providing care to those living with dementia and offering comfort, stability, and compassion.

According to the 2025 Alzheimer's Disease Facts and Figures report, in 2024, nearly 12 million caregivers provided more than 19 billion hours of unpaid care to family members living with dementia. 1 This unpaid care is valued at $413.4 billion. More than half of caregivers rate the emotional stress of caregiving as high or very high, and more than a third report physical stress due to caregiving.

“Caregiving is one of the most selfless acts of love there is,” said Elizabeth Edgerly, senior director of community programs and services, Alzheimer's Association. “This month, and every month, we honor those who show up every day to provide care, comfort, and dignity for their loved ones. We're here to make sure they have the resources and support they need.”

To empower more families to find support, connection, and hope and to help families navigate the challenges of dementia, in 2025 the Alzheimer's Association launched several new and expanded resources.

Transitions in Care, a new online education program that helps families navigate the decision to transition a person living with dementia into long‐term care.

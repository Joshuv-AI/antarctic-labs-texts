The predictive value of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] in adverse outcomes for acute kidney injury: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Wenlei Wang, Qing Shen, Xinrui Zhou
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectives: [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] holds much potential as a biomarker for predicting the outcomes of acute kidney injury (AKI). Our meta-analysis pooled their previous research data to obtain a significantly more trustworthy metric.Materials and methodsRelevant articles published up to 17 June 2023 were retrieved from five databases (Cochrane Library/Embase/PubMed/SinoMed/Web of Science). The pre-established inclusion and exclusion criteria determined the selection of publications. Pooled sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), diagnostic odds ratio, likelihood ratio, and summary receiver operating characteristic curve were employed to assess the predictive value. The presence or potential sources of heterogeneity were investigated via subgroup and SEN analyses.ResultsTen published and eligible studies (1559 cases) were included in the evaluation for the capability of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] to predict the poor prognosis of AKI through the random effect model. Pooled SEN, SPE, diagnostic odds ratio, and positive and negative likelihood ratios were 0.82 (95% CI: 0.77–0.86, I2 = 53.4%), 0.64 (95% CI: 0.61–0.67, I2 = 88.3%), 14.06 (95% CI: 7.31–27.05, I2 = 55.0%), 2.859 (95% CI: 2.15–3.77, I2 = 80.7%), and 0.28 (95% CI: 0.20–0.40, I2 = 35.0%), respectively. The estimated area under the curve was 0.8864 (standard error: 0.0306), and the Q* was 0.7970 (standard error: 0.0299). The endpoints and cutoff values were the main causes of heterogeneity.Conclusions[TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] is possible in predicting poor prognosis of AKI, but it is better to be applied along with other indicators or clinical risk factors.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) has replaced the term acute kidney failure given the kidney may be damaged but not necessarily dysfunctional due to the presence of robust glomerular reserve function [1]. It is determined purely on variations in baseline urine output and serum creatinine in present clinical practice. Serum creatinine level reflects renal excretion, and its elevation during 48–72 h after a renal insult denotes the presence of over 50% damaged nephrons [2,3]. The serum level cannot accurately reflect kidney damage due to the various influences on the generation and metabolism of creatinine [4,5]. AKI has been reported to be independently correlated with increased mortality [6,7]. Even its mild type, such as a 50% spike in serum creatinine level, would herald an increased risk of death [8]. AKI-related mortality and morbidity reached 50% in critically ill patients [9]. Thus, it is of great significance to find reliable biomarkers to identify the progression of AKI. Tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase-2 (TIMP-2) and insulin-like growth factor-binding protein (IGFBP7) participate in G1 cell-cycle arrest and prevent the division with damaged DNA in the presence of a definite renal insult from ischemia or other injuries [10,11]. Numerous studies have demonstrated the utilization of the combination of TIMP-2 and IGFBP7 (expressed as [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7]) in diagnosing AKI [12–21]. The combination of the two biomarkers is not affected by non-renal organ dysfunction and is preferable to many biomarkers [22,23]. Both biomarkers have been proposed to enhance the diagnostic precision of AKI and are considered beneficial to AKI management at the 2020 Acute Disease Quality Initiative consensus conference [24]. To evaluate the predictive ability of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] for AKI prognosis, we conducted an up-to-date comprehensive meta-analysis.

Materials and methods

Our study protocol was registered in the PROSPERO database (registration number: CRD4202347412), and our study was reported as per the PRISMA guideline.

Retrieval strategy and study selection

Five online databases (Cochrane Library/PubMed/Embase/SinoMed/Web of Science) were systematically searched for potentially relevant articles published up to 17 June 2023. Search terms were ‘Tissue Inhibitor of Metalloproteinase-2’, ‘Metalloproteinase-2 Tissue Inhibitor’, ‘Tissue Inhibitor of Metalloproteinase 2’, ‘TIMP-2’, ‘insulin like-growth factor binding protein-7’, ‘IGFBP7’, ‘IGFBP7 protein, human’, ‘Acute Kidney Injury’, ‘Acute Kidney Injurie’, ‘Kidney Injuries, Acute’, ‘Kidney Injury, Acute’, ‘Acute Renal Injury’, ‘Acute Renal Injuries’, ‘Renal Injuries, Acute’, ‘Renal Injury, Acute’, ‘Renal Insufficiency, Acute’, ‘Acute Renal Insuffi­ciencies’, ‘Renal Insufficiencies, Acute’, ‘Acute Renal Insufficiency’, ‘Kidney Insufficiency, Acute’, ‘Acute Kidney Insufficiencies’, ‘Kidney Insufficiencies, Acute’, ‘Acute Kidney Insuffi­ciency’, ‘Kidney Failure, Acute’, ‘Acute Kidney Failures’, ‘Kidney Failures, Acute’, ‘Acute Renal Failure’, ‘Acute Renal Failures’, ‘Renal Failures, Acute’, ‘Renal Failure, Acute’, ‘Acute Kidney Failure’. Search syntaxes like ‘OR’ and ‘AND’ were used between those terms. Reference lists of relevant reviews and those retrieved articles were checked to supplement our meta-analysis data. The retrieval was conducted by two researchers (Wenlei Wang and Xinrui Zhou), independently, and their obtained articles were converged for subsequent study collection.

In terms of the eligibility for inclusion, the articles should be (i) original clinical studies and (ii) disease cohort studies/randomized controlled trials; (iii) their AKI patients were older than 18 years old; (iv) their AKI patients were in the condition progressing to severe AKI (stage 3 according to the KDIGO standard) or non-recovery of kidney function (no restriction on definition), or were indicative for renal replacement therapy (RRT) (no restriction on its indications), or were subject to death; (v) urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] was evaluated among these patients; (vi) the true positive (TP), false positive (FP), false negative (FN), and true negative (TN), and sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), area under the curve (AUC), and optimum cutoff value of urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test were reported or could be calculated from published data. The reviews, comments, abstracts (studies with abstracts only), letters, research reports, abstracts from conferences, studies with insufficient or unavailable data, and duplicate publications were excluded. After examining the titles and abstracts, the pertinent articles were re-read and re-screened. Two researchers (Wenlei Wang and Xinrui Zhou) screened the literature strictly as per the pre-established inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Any disagreements during the systematic search were resolved through consultation with the third researcher (Qing Shen).

Data extraction and quality assessment

The required information and data were extracted by two researchers (Wenlei Wang and Xinrui Zhou), independently. The information included the first author, publication year, country, study design type, the definition of AKI, sample size, unfavorable results, sampling time, and measurement method. Data including SEN, SPE, AUC, optimum cutoff value, TP, FP, FN, and TN were collected. The time point with the greatest AUC was selected when AKI was detected at multiple time points during measurement. If the AUC analysis came up with more than one cutoff value, the one with the highest Youden index was chosen. Two researchers (Wenlei Wang and Xinrui Zhou) independently evaluated and cross-checked the quality of finally included studies with the QUADAS-2 tool [25]. Any discrepancies were resolved via consultation with the third researcher (Qing Shen).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted with Meta-DiSc 1.4 software. The pooled SEN, SPE, PLR, NLR, DOR, and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated based on an effects model to evaluate the overall diagnostic accuracy. The five effects were estimated using either a random effects model (the DerSimonian–Laird method) or a fixed effects model (the Mantel–Haenszel method) [26]. The selection of a statistical model was based on the heterogeneity of the pooled effect size. The heterogeneity was assessed by the I2 value and Cochran’s Q index [27]. Heterogeneity existed when the p value of the Q index was less than 0.10. I2 < 25%, 25% < I2 < 50%, and I2 > 50% denote mild, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. A fixed effects model was utilized when I2 < 50%. Otherwise, the random effect model was employed. Sensitivity and subgroup analyses were conducted to dissect the sources of heterogeneity. Deeks’ funnel plot asymmetry test was used to detect the publication bias of included articles via Stata14.0 software (College Station, TX) [28]. p < 0.05 in the Spearman correlation coefficient and the characteristic ‘shoulder-arm’ shape of SROC both demonstrated the heterogeneity brought about by the threshold effects. The area under the SROC curve was used to assess AUC, and a value of ≥0.70 indicated a reliable predictor [29].

Results

Study selection and characteristics

The initial search in the five databases yielded 2057 pieces of related publications, including 285 from Embase, 1431 from PubMed, 284 from Web of Science, 18 from SinoMed, and 39 from Cochrane Library. A total of 71 pieces of literature were collected after screening the title and abstract and eliminating duplicate articles, meta-analyses, reviews, guidelines, letters, conference papers, and animal experiment articles; apart from 42 non-prospective studies, 27 articles might be eligible for final evaluation. Ten eligible studies were eventually included in our meta-analysis. The process of study selection is reported in Figure 1. Through this selection, a total of 10 prospective studies published between 2015 and 2022 were included and 1559 cases were recruited. One study involved multiple centers, and the majority of the remaining articles were conducted in France and Germany. The urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] levels were examined by the NephroCheck® test in nine studies, and by the ELISA kit in one study. The 10 studies measured [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] levels at various times, including admission, 24 h after admission, after cardiac arrest, 8–10 d after vancomycin use, the time after major non-cardiac surgery, 24 h after AKI, enrollment, and discharge. The endpoints of the 10 studies were non-recovery of kidney function in two studies, RRT or mortality in six studies, and stage 3 AKI in two studies. Predictive accuracy

Table 2 details the data collected from the 10 studies. For the AKI prognosis in 10 studies, [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] displayed various sensitivities, specificities, AUC values, and cutoff values. The Spearman correlation coefficient was 0.139 (p = 0.701) in our study, indicating the absence of any threshold effects. The absence of a typical ‘shoulder-arm’ pattern in our symmetric SROC space further demonstrates the lack of threshold effects in our study. Cochrane-Q = 19.99 and p < 0.05 for DOR indicated the heterogeneity in this study is attributable to non-threshold effects. Since the I2 values for SEN, SPE, PLR, and DOR were all greater than 50%, the aforementioned five effects were evaluated by a random effects model. The pooled SEN, SPE, PLR, NLR, and DOR were 0.82 (95% CI: 0.77–0.86, I2 = 53.4%), 0.64 (95% CI: 0.61–0.67, I2 = 88.3%), 2.859 (95% CI: 2.15–3.77, I2 = 80.7%), 0.28 (95% CI: 0.20–0.40, I2 = 35.0%), and 14.06 (95% CI: 7.31–27.05, I2 = 55.0%), respectively. The estimated AUC was 0.8864 (standard error: 0.0306), and the Q* was 0.7970 (standard error: 0.0299). The pooled SEN, SPE, NLR, and PLR are presented in Figure 4.

Sensitivity analysis

As shown in Figure 5, three original studies had high SEN, while other original ones did not affect the computation results. Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis was performed based on the sample size, the outcome endpoint, the cutoff value, and the study quality. As shown in Table 3, the key elements causing heterogeneity were the outcome endpoint and cutoff value. The 10 studies were divided into the ‘RRT/death’ subgroup (six studies) and the ‘non-recovery of kidney function/stage 3 AKI’ subgroup (four studies) based on the outcome endpoints. In the ‘RRT/death’ subgroup, the estimated AUC was 0.8579, and the pooled SEN and SPE were 0.82 (95% CI: 0.75–0.87, I2 = 11.3%) and 0.61 (95% CI: 0.57–0.64, I2 = 85%), respectively. In the latter subgroup, the estimated AUC was 0.9029. The pooled SEN and SPE were 0.82 (95% CI: 0.73–0.89, I2 = 78.1%) and 0.76 (95% CI: 0.70–0.81, I2 = 86.9%), respectively. The urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test revealed an exceptionally superior ability for prognosis prediction among the patients with non-recovery of kidney function/stage 3 AKI. Similarly, 10 included studies were divided into ‘0.3–2 (ng/mL)2/1000’ and ‘>2 (ng/mL)2/1000’ subgroups based on the various cutoff values. Only two studies were included in the latter subgroup. The pooled SPE of urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] > 2 (ng/mL)2/1000 was excellent; the pooled SPE was 0.95 (95% CI: 0.87–0.99, I2 = 12.5%), while the pooled SEN was 0.65 (95% CI: 0.43–0.84, I2 = 51.2%). Six studies were included in the former subgroup. The estimated AUC was 0.8049. The pooled SEN and SPE were 0.83 (95% CI: 0.78–0.88, I2 = 47.2%) and 0.62 (95% CI: 0.59–0.65, I2 = 81.4%), respectively.

Discussion

The last meta-analysis including four investigations was performed in 2017 by Jia et al. for assessing the prognostic value of urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] in AKI patients, the outcome endpoints of which were limited to RRT and death [38]. Jia et al. noted that additional meta-analysis would be required when more trials were available. Our study included the latest studies and covered more adverse outcomes in outcome endpoints (AKI 3/RRT/non-recovery of kidney function/death). Besides, the included subjects in our meta-analysis were adult patients (≥18 years old), the results therefore were not affected by child-related factors. Furthermore, we employed the typical ‘shoulder-arm’ pattern of SROC and the p value of Spearman’s correlation coefficient to reflect the heterogeneity caused by the threshold effects. Subgroup and SEN analyses were conducted regarding heterogeneity caused by non-threshold effects. We again confirmed that urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] is a possible predictor of adverse outcomes for AKI. It would guide the implementation of timely intervention and the modification of treatment strategies to reduce the occurrence of renal adverse events.

AKI is a complicated condition with multiple probable causes, such as sepsis, major surgery, and nephrotoxic drugs. Rapid loss of kidney function is characteristic of AKI [39]. Various cells, inflammatory mediators, and oxidative mechanisms are involved in most AKI cases. Endothelial dysfunction, microcirculation alterations, renal tubular damage, renal venous congestion, and intrarenal inflammation appear sequentially in the pathogenic process of AKI [40]. Stress-induced gene transcription is not responsible for the surge in urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] levels in AKI. The urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] levels might be elevated as a result of enhanced filtration, augmented renal tubular excretion, and decreased renal tubular reabsorption [41]. The urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test has been recently used for determining the prognosis of AKI. Our meta-analysis included 10 studies (1559 patients) and confirmed the role of urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] as a predictive indicator of unfavorable outcomes in AKI patients (AUC = 0.8864, SEN = 0.82, and SPE = 0.64).

The multicenter prospective study undertaken by Titeca-Beauport et al. showed that [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] had great clinical predictive value for severe AKI after cardiac arrest [32]. The AUC, SEN, and SPE were 0.91, 0.97, and 0.72, respectively. Sensitivity analysis, however, revealed the presence of substantial heterogeneity in pooled DOR. Limited sample size and ethnicity-specific study populations could be significant contributors to the heterogeneity. Publication bias could result from a limited sample size. Besides, ‘post-cardiac arrest syndrome’ can also result in early-onset multiple organ dysfunction syndromes. AKI following cardiac arrest is a significant risk factor, which may be associated with low blood pressure and insufficient renal blood flow.

In our meta-analysis, the outcome endpoints (stage 3 AKI, RRT, non-recovery of kidney function, and death) and cutoff values were the primary contributors to the heterogeneity according to subgroup analysis. The estimated AUC of the ‘non-recovery of kidney function/stage 3 AKI’ subgroup was 0.9029, substantially higher than that of the ‘RRT/death’ subgroup (AUC: 0.8579). The duration of AKI correlates with the severity of adverse outcomes [42], but the urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test cannot differentiate transitory from persistent AKI [43]. AKI duration was not taken into account in these included studies in our research and would be a significant source of heterogeneity. Nevertheless, the estimated AUC of urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] in predicting AKI prognosis exceeded 0.7 in both subgroups, demonstrating its superior clinical role in the prognosis prediction of AKI. The cutoff values vary across our included studies and affected the prognostic accuracy as well. The cutoff values of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] were greater than 0.3 (ng/mL)2/1000 in the 10 studies. The SPE of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] > 2 (ng/mL)2/1000 in predicting poor prognosis of AKI was quite significant (SPE = 0.95). The AKI patients with a urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] level > 0.2 (ng/mL)2/1000 have a greater chance of death or dialysis than those with a level < 0.2 (ng/mL)2/1000 [44]. Therefore, additional relevant studies are required to establish the optimum cutoff value for predicting AKI patients’ unfavorable outcomes.

Currently, apart from IGFBP7 and TIMP-2, several biomarkers including liver-type fatty acid binding protein (L-FABP), urinary kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1), calprotectin (S100A8/9), interleukin-18 (IL-18), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), can be used to help judge the occurrence of AKI. [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] was tested for its potential to diagnose AKI early by Jia et al. [45]. Their meta-analysis with nine studies showed that [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] was a significant predictor of developing AKI (SEN = 0.83, SPE = 0.55). Both prerenal and intrinsic AKI could be distinguished by S100A8/9 and NGAL, while S100A8/9 had higher SPE and SEN (SEN = 93.3%, SPE = 94.8%) [46]. MiR-452, TCONS_00016233, and plasma circ_0020339 are interesting candidate biomarkers for the early identification of sepsis-associated AKI [47–49]. Soluble urokinase plasminogen activated receptor (suPAR) is a biomarker used for the prediction of AKI after cardiac surgery [50]. Researchers from the United States assessed the modified renal colic index (mRAI) for AKI risk classification in critically ill adult patients [51].

Mounting studies have demonstrated a correlation between [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] and the adverse outcomes of AKI. When the urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] level was greater than 0.3, AKI patients had a noticeably increased probability of death or receiving higher continuous RRT [52]. The AKI patients with a urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] level of over 0.3 and less than 2.0 (ng/mL)2/1000 had a markedly elevated risk of mortality or probability of receiving dialysis (health benefit: 1.44; 95% CI: 1.00–2.06; p = .05) [44]. In particular, higher risk or probability would be present in the AKI patients with a [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] level over 2.0 (ng/mL)2/1000 (health benefit: 2.16; 95% CI: 1.32–3.53; p = .002) [44]. In addition, Hatton et al. [15] confirmed that [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] level ≥ 0.33 (ng/mL)2/1000 was independently associated with the indication for dialysis and longer ICU stay (odds ratio, 1.71 and 1.06, respectively). Fiorentino et al. [53] discovered that [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test could indeed identify the risk of progression in individuals suffering from AKI. Fiorentino et al. also found that the patients who experienced ‘kidney stress’ and were found and treated within 6 h had a relatively low incidence of adverse outcomes. In contrast, if ‘kidney stress’ persists, there is an increased chance that the patient will progress to stage 3 AKI, death, or a condition requiring dialysis. Gocze et al. [20] and Meersch et al. [54] found that the measurement of the urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] levels allowed for the identification of AKI in high-risk patients. Thus, timely intervention with the KDIGO care bundle, including early optimization of fluid status, discontinuation of nephrotoxic agents, and maintenance of perfusion pressure, can reduce AKI severity in high-risk patients [54]. Likewise, Claisse et al. [55] and Engelman et al. [56] applied [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] to recognize individuals at high risk of AKI and prompt renal protection programs for high-risk patients, which can reduce AKI’s incidence. These findings support the idea that timely intervention strategies for AKI patients can lower the frequency of unfavorable kidney-related outcomes.

The diagnostic and prognostic value in AKI has already been validated, but many other indicators are also connected with AKI. It is tempting to combine other indicators with urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] for diagnosis or prognosis prediction of AKI. Increased urine albumin (up to 100 times) correlates highly with urinary TIMP2 and IGFBP7 excretion [41]. Urinary albumin measurement could be an excellent supplement to [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] in AKI diagnosis, risk classification, and prognostic evaluation. More accurate prediction of the onset and prognosis of sepsis-related AKI could be achieved by combining procalcitonin (PCT) with [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7]. Godi et al. [57] discovered, for instance, that patients with positive tests for both PCT and [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] had a higher probability of having AKI (odds ratio: 26.4; 95% CI: 12.3–56.62; p < .001) and risk of mortality at seven days (health benefit: 4.1; 95% CI: 1.4–11.8; p = .001). Only septic patients had these results validated. Renal damage is also suggested by elevated levels of KIM-1 in the urine. [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] was a better predictive biomarker of AKI caused by sepsis progressing to death when paired with urinary KIM-1 [58]. This study also indicates that the urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test, along with the evaluation of the clinical risk factors (gender, age, APACHE II score, serum creatinine, and albuminuria), could strengthen the capacity to identify AKI development. Similarly, Fiorentino et al. demonstrated that combined application of the urinary [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] test with APACHE II scoring or serum creatinine test enhanced the diagnostic efficacy of AKI [53]. Altogether, it is worthwhile to study the efficacy of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] in conjunction with other indicators in diagnosing AKI and predicting its prognosis.

Our study still has certain limitations. First, our study suffered from heterogeneity, which could lead to overestimating the predictive significance of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] for poor prognosis of AKI. Second, while the collection time of urine samples was different between those included studies, we did not investigate a more precise window of time for sample collection. Third, we failed to establish a high-accuracy cutoff value since it varied among studies. Fourth, we did not analyze the adverse outcomes included separately. Finally, the combination of [TIMP-2]*[IGFBP7] with other indicators or evaluation of clinical risk factors may improve the predictive value for the adverse outcomes of AKI patients. However, only a few investigations have examined such combinations, so further trials are warranted in future research.

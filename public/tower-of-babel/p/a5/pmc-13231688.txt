Prevalence and associated factors of individuals at risk for obstructive sleep apnea in urban, stock-raising, and agricultural Xinjiang, Northwest China, a cross-sectional study
Authors: Dan Yu, Ling Yao, Mei Li, Hui Wang, Wenbo Yang, Shasha Liu, Jing Hong, Xiayire Aierken, Fengyu Pan, Yingchun Wang, Nanfang Li, Mulalibieke Heizhati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe present study aims to investigate the prevalence and related factors of sleep-disordered breathing (SDB), population at risk for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), in urban, stock-raising and agricultural dwellers from Xinjiang, Northwest China.MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, participants aged ≥ 18 years were recruited in Emin, China in 2019. Participants underwent questionnaire assessments, including the No-SAS questionnaire (which incorporates neck circumference, obesity, snoring, age, and sex), and details in medical history and lifestyle.ResultsData for 32,916 participants were analyzed with an average age of (47.3 ± 14.2) years. The overall prevalence of SDB was 24.6%, and was 29.1%, 18.6% and 22.9%, respectively in participants from urban, stock-raising and agricultural regions were (P < 0.001), consistent in different gender and in the young. In logistic regression analysis, common associated factors for SDB presence were cigarette and alcohol consumption, abdominal obesity, dyslipidemia, and presence of hypertension, coronary heart diseases and of stroke for total and region-specific participants.ConclusionsSDB is very common in urban, stock-raising and agricultural local population, who share most of the cardio-metabolic associated factors for SDB.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-026-26820-8.Keywords: Sleep-disordered breathing, Prevalence and associated factors, Urban stock-raising and agricultural areas

Introduction

Sleep breathing disorder (SDB) is a chronic disease, with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) as the most common form, which is characterized by frequent collapse of the upper airway during sleep, or a series of pathophysiological changes [1]. Over the long term, SDBs are associated with increased risk for hypertension, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, cancer, and other chronic diseases and all-cause mortality [2]. Therefore, SDB has been considered a significant public health problem that remains under-recognized, especially in resource-limited settings [2, 3].

According to a recent estimate, 425 million adults aged 30–69 years worldwide have moderate to severe SDB, with the prevalence exceeding 50% in some countries, and still on the rise [4]. Data from the Jackson Heart Sleep study and the Multiracial Atherosclerosis Study conducted in the United States reported that approximately 24% and 15.0% of individuals had moderate (apnea-hypopnea index AHI ≥ 15 event per hour) and severe SDB (AHI ≥ 30 events per hour) assessed by somnography [5, 6], and more prevalent and severe in African Americans. The Benin study reported that the prevalence of SDB (AHI ≥ 5/h) was 43.2% in the general African population assessed using respiratory polygraphy at home utilizing a type-3 device [2]. The ARKH sleep study reported that the prevalence of SDB is 48.9% in adults from Northwest Russia evaluated using the Epworth Sleepiness Scale and body mass index [1].

In China, the prevalence of SDB is 4.1% and 2.1% in middle-aged men and women from Hong Kong [2], 5.7% and 2.4% in men and women from Guangxi [7], and is 4.8% in adults ≥ 20 years in Changchun [8]. In China, most chronic conditions and their associated factors show geographical differences, since the population have different life style, living habits, and geographical environment [5]. Therefore, more setting-specific data on SDB epidemiology are needed in China In addition, some of previous studies were conducted in clinical populations with suspected SDB. Some were limited to some age range and/or a limited number of populations (state agency employees, industrial employees or urban area).

In Xinjiang, located in Northwest China, a province with a population of various lifestyles and dietary habits. Population here may have higher burden for SDB, since local residents have a higher percentage on cigarette and alcohol consumption, and higher prevalence of overweight, obesity, diabetes, and hypertension [3, 9–12], which are associated factors for SDB [10, 11, 13]. Moreover, Xinjiang is one of the four stock-raising areas (accounting for 34.68% of the total land) in China and one-fifth of local residents are leading nomadic or seminomadic lives, making the access of fresh vegetables and fruits difficult, and preferring to consume meat and milk as the principal food and foods high in salt [14, 15]. Therefore, differences in burden and associated factors for SDB may exist among urban, agricultural and stock-raising regions due to differences in lifestyles, behaviors and potential exposures, whereas there is a lack of data on this topic. Thus, the purpose of the current study was to explore the prevalence and associated factors of SDB in total and sex- and age-stratified population from urban, agricultural and stock-raising Emin County Xinjiang using a cross-sectional survey data conducted in 2019 [16, 17].

Methods

Site

Emin County in northern Xinjiang, China, is home to 160,000 multi-ethnic people who live in urban, agricultural and livestock farming environments.

Study population

In this cross-sectional study, we used multi-stage stratified sampling method to enroll participants aged ≥ 18 years [18, 19]. At the first stage, Emin County was divided into 3 settings as urban, agricultural, and stock-raising areas. At the second stage, 2 corresponding streets and villages were randomly selected using the sample random sampling method. At the third stage, subjects aged ≥ 18 years were enrolled from the local residence data, based on inclusion and exclusion criteria [18, 20].

Inclusion criteria: (1) adults who have lived in their current residence for more than 6 months; (2) Those who agree to participate in the survey and sign the informed consent. Those who were unable to cooperate due to mental, physical, and or other reasons, those who were unwilling to participate, and or women with pregnancy at the time of survey were excluded. Those with incomplete NOSAS scale data were further excluded for the sub-study.

This study was approved by the ethic’s committee of People’s Hospital of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region China (ethic’s approval number: KY2026011506).

Data collection

Each participant completed a face-to-face interview questionnaire consisting of the global physical activity questionnaire (GPAQ), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), and NOSAS scale. Data were also collected on participants’ demographic characteristics (age, sex, living circumstances, occupation, education and marital status), medical history (hypertension, diabetes, self-reported coronary heart disease [CHD], and stroke), and lifestyle (smoking and alcohol consumption) [17, 21].

Measurement of BP, height, weight, and waist circumference.

BP was presented as the mean of three measurements using an Omron HEM-1000 electronic sphygmomanometer (20). Three BP measurements were taken, after a rest of at least 5 min, from the unclothed right arm of the person in a sitting position at an interval of at least 1 min. Body weight, height and waist circumference were measured (21). Height and weight were measured to the nearest 0.1 cm and 0.1 kg, respectively, with the participants in lightweight clothing and without shoes. Waist circumference was measured at the midpoint between the lower rib and upper margin of the iliac crest to the nearest 0·1 cm at the end of a normal expiration. BMI was calculated by dividing weight by height-squared (kg/m2).

Laboratory biochemical tests included measurements of serum creatinine, fasting blood glucose (FBG), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), and high- and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL and LDL) applying clinical procedures at local hospitals. All subjects were fasted for ≥ 8 h, and a 5-mL fasting blood sample was collected. Serum creatinine was measured by enzymatic method (sarcosine oxidase enzymatic analysis). Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) was tested using glucose oxidase method. Total cholesterol (TC) and triglycerides (TG) were measured with commercially available reagents.

Diagnostic criteria

NOSAS scale was used for evaluation for SDB assessment. The score of NOSAS is 0–17 points, including 5 items including neck circumference, obesity, snoring, age, and sex. Neck circumference > 40 cm is 4 points, BMI > 25Kg/m2 and < 30 kg/m2 is 3 points, BMI≥30 kg/m2 is 5 points, snoring is 2 points age > 55 years is 4 points, and male is 2 points. A NOSAS score of ≥ 8 is defined as SDB as elsewhere [22].

Variables

Smoking is defined as having smoked more than 20 packs of cigarettes in the past and is currently smoking [17]. Alcohol consumption is defined as drinking alcohol at least once a week during a month at the time of the survey [17]. Physical activity is categorized as high (≥ 3000 MET-minutes per week from any combination of walking, moderate or vigorous-intensity activities, or ≥ 1500 MET-minutes per week from vigorous intensity activity), moderate (not meeting the criteria for the high category but achieving ≥ 600 MET-minutes per week), and low (not meeting any of the aforementioned criteria). Sleep quality was assessed using PSQI, the most common used self-reporting tool for sleep quality in the first 4 weeks [18, 23]; the overall score ranged from 0 to 21, with scores ≥ 6 indicating poor sleep quality [18, 24]. Abdominal obesity is defined if waist circumference is ≥ 90 cm in men and ≥ 85 cm in women [17]. Diabetes is defined as FBG of ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, and/or self-reported diagnosis by physicians, and/or the intake of hypoglycemic agents within the past 2 weeks [16]. Hypertension is defined as a systolic BP of ≥ 140 mmHg and/or a diastolic BP of ≥ 90 mmHg, or the use of anti-hypertensive medication within the preceding 2 weeks [16]. Dyslipidemia is defined as a condition characterized by the presence of TC ≥ 6.2 mmol/L and/or TG ≥ 2.3 mmol/L, HDL-c < 1.0 mmol/L, LDL-c ≥ 4.2 mmol/L, or having received treatment within the past 2 weeks. CHD and stroke were self-reported by the participants. Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is defined as glomerular filtration rate < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 [21, 25].

Statistical methods

Data analysis was performed in total participants, and in participants from urban, stock-raising and agricultural region. Stratification analysis was performed in different age, gender, demographic, life-style and disease status.

Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). One-way analysis of variance was employed to facilitate the comparison of the means between the groups. Nonnormally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range) and compared between groups using Kruskal-Wallis H test. Frequencies (n) and percentages (%) are expressed and compared by chi-squared test or trend chi-squared test.

We conducted univariate logistic regression analysis for the factors related to SDB in the total, urban, stock-raising, and agricultural population, as well as performed the collinearity diagnosis steps (the analysis in total participants was given as sup-Table 1). Factors with P < 0.1 were considered associated with the presence of SDB. The multi-variable model-building process followed a systematic approach outlined below: Variable Entry: All pre-specified independent variables were simultaneously included in the models using the “enter” method to adjust for potential confounding effects. Results are reported as Odds Ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% Confidence Intervals (95% CIs). The statistical significance of individual predictors was determined using the Wald test, with a two-sided p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Software: All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics. Multiple testing correction was applied using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate (FDR) at 0.05 for subgroup analyses across urban, stock-raising, and agricultural areas.

Results

A total of 32,916 people with complete data were included and analyzed, with a mean age of 47.3 ± 14.2 years. Of them, 46.4% were men.

Participants from three region showed significant differences in age, with the youngest from stock-raising region (urban vs. stock-raising vs. agricultural region: 47.8 vs. 43.1 vs. 47.8 years). The proportion of participants engaged in mental occupation in urban, stock-raising and agricultural areas is 52.3%, 13.7% and 16.3% respectively. Within the total population, the prevalence of SDB is the highest in participants from the Han ethnic group followed by those from the Kazakh ethnic group and the lowest in those from others ethnic group (12.6% vs. 6.9% vs. 5.1%, p < 0.001).

In urban areas, the highest prevalence was observed among the Han ethnic group, while the lowest was found among the Kazakh ethnic group (16.5% vs. 5.1%, p < 0.001). In stock-raising areas, the highest prevalence was observed among the Kazakh ethnic group, while the lowest was found among the Han ethnic group (13.8% vs. 2.1%, p < 0.001).

In different ethnic groups, in urban and agricultural areas, the prevalence of SDB is highest among the Han ethnic group than in the Kazakh and other ethnic group (Han vs. Kazakh vs. others: urban: 16.5 vs. 5.1 vs. 7.6%; agricultural: 12.4 vs. 6.5 vs. 4.0%, p < 0.001). In stock-raising areas, the prevalence of SDB is the highest among the Kazakh ethnic group and lowest among the Han ethnic group (13.8%vs 2.1%, p < 0.001).

The overall prevalence of SDB was 24.6%, with a significant difference between gender (men vs. women 39.4%vs 11.8%), and among different age groups (< 45 vs. 45–60 vs. ≥ 60 years: 12.0%vs 22.4%vs 55.0%).

Discussion

SDB remains a common health problem worldwide with several uncertainties [2, 9, 26, 27], such as in status of SDB and the distribution of related factors for agricultural, stock-raising areas and urban residents.

The prevalence of SDB in the urban, stock-raising and agricultural areas were 29.1%, 18.6% and 22.9% respectively, the highest in urban areas and the lowest in stock-raising areas. The reasons standing behind may be as follows: (1) as in other parts of the world, urban residents have more unhealthy lifestyles and eating habits, such as obesity, smoking, drinking, work pressure, air pollution, etc. (2) Different living environments reflect different lifestyles. The prevalence of SDB was lowest among residents living in pastoral areas, which was considered to be related to their relatively young average age. Therefore, an age-stratified analysis was conducted. The prevalence of SDB was lowest among residents in stock-raisers, except for those aged 60 or above. In stock-raisers, the living habits and dietary structure are mainly based on dairy products, beef and mutton and other meat products [28], and possibly therefore muscle function of the oral cavity and mandibular region of specific population may be better, making the muscles of the upper airway not collapse easily, which may be relevant to the lower prevalence of SDB in this specific population [14, 29, 30]. Residents living in stock-raising regions may be more active as in Table 1 due to the life style and production mode, which may be translate into higher metabolic rate and less-fat accumulation.

This study observed that the crude prevalence of SDB was highest among the Kazakh population residing in pastoral areas (13.8%). However, after adjusting for potential confounders, Kazakh ethnicity showed lower odds for the presence of SDB (OR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.41–0.81). This suggests that the initially observed higher prevalence of SDB among Kazakhs in pastoral regions may be largely attributable to greater exposure to specific risk factors—such as smoking, alcohol consumption, and obesity—rather than to ethnic identity per se. In contrast, it is potentially due to protective elements associated with their traditional lifestyle, dietary structure or cultural practices. In total participants, in terms of regional distribution, residents in urban areas and agricultural areas exhibited significantly higher odds of SDB compared with those in pastoral areas. Among the associated factors contributing to the prevalence of SDB, living environment has been identified as an independent and significant factor. These findings highlight the critical importance of employing multivariate-adjusted analyses in multi environmental studies to prevent erroneous conclusions arising from structural confounding.

In contrast, after accounting for these confounding variables, individuals of Kazakh ethnicity may exhibit a reduced association of SDB prevalence, potentially due to protective elements associated with their traditional lifestyle, dietary structure, or cultural practices.

Regression analysis indicated that the related factors for SDB across the three regions under consideration are lifestyle-related. The common related factors across these regions encompass obesity, cigarette and alcohol consumption, presence of dyslipidemia, hypertension, CHD and of stroke. These observations were mainly consistent with previous studies [31, 32]. In addition, these observations suggest that the promotion of healthy lifestyle be the common solution for the population living in different setting.

Current data presented a high prevalence of SDB in the general population of Emin County, which is 24.6%, similar with that of Asian studies [33]. There are some limitations to be considered. First, the NOSAS scale was used to assess SDB, a subjective approach that may introduce some bias to the prevalence of SDB. However, the No-SAS scale has been proven to be useful for the diagnosis of SDB [18, 34, 35], and validated in the Chinese population [35]. In addition, the NOSAS scale had a sensitivity of 79% and a specificity of 69% for SDB assessment [18, 34, 35]. Second, this study did not investigate some information, such as socioeconomic status, household income level, which may be associated with SDB. Despite these limitations, we believe that measuring SDB in population from Xinjiang Northwestern China, fills an important gap in the literature on sleep apnea epidemiology in different residential settings and provides a basis for further research.

Conclusion

SDBs are prevalent in three urban, stock raising and agricultural regions, and share related factors, which may provide disease control and prevention of related consequences.

Patient and Caregiver Involvement With Prior Authorization and Care Delivery and Delays
Authors: Bridgette Thom, Vanessa E. Slater, Jennifer L. Lund, Benyam Muluneh, Brianna Connelly, Fumiko Chino, Michael Anne Kyle, Tess Thompson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This survey study describes outcomes associated with patient and caregiver engagement in the prior authorization process.

Introduction

Payers employ health care utilization–management strategies, including prior authorization (PA), to limit costs and promote evidence-based, guideline-concordant care. However, PA may be inconsistently applied and associated with patient confusion and frustration.1,2,3 Patient engagement in PA processes can add to administrative burden and may be associated with adverse clinical outcomes.4,5,6,7 Limited research has focused on caregivers, who often play important roles managing health care–related tasks, including PA. This study assessed associations between patient and caregiver PA involvement and self-reported outcomes, including receipt of care as prescribed and delayed care.

Methods

In this survey study, an investigator-designed survey was adapted from a prior survey and administered through the Qualtrics Online Panel Platform to US adults from December 2024 to January 2025 (eMethods in Supplement 1); participants provided informed consent.8 The University of North Carolina Institutional Review Board (IRB #20-2338) approved the study, which follows the AAPOR reporting guideline.

Respondents reported any past-year PA experience for their own health care (patient PA) and were asked, “In the past year, have you dealt with prior authorization for a family member or friend’s health care?” Affirmative answers were considered to have caregiver PA experience. The final analytic cohort included respondents who reported any PA experience.

Direct PA involvement was defined as reporting 1 or more listed activity (eg, contacting health care team, insurance, or pharmacy; filing an appeal; or involving an employer or a lawyer). Outcomes included magnitude of problem with PA (moderate or serious vs no or minor problem), self-reported delayed care (vs no delay), and reporting that recommended care was not approved or received (vs care approved or received).

Unadjusted and adjusted log-binomial regressions estimated risk ratios (RRs) and 95% CIs to examine associations between PA involvement and outcomes, with separate models for patients and caregivers (eMethods in Supplement 1). SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute) was used for analysis. A 2-sided P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Among 997 respondents, 286 reported patient PA (28.6%; 52.5% women; 37.1% ages 30-49 years; 2.8% Asian, 26.9% Black, and 65.7% White; 14.3% Hispanic), 182 reported caregiver PA (18.3%; 45.1% women; 50.6% ages 30-49 years; 5.0% Asian, 35.7% Black, and 52.2% White; 19.8% Hispanic), and 11.3% reported both (Table). More than one-half (61.5%) of patients and 81.9% of caregivers reported direct PA involvement (Figure).

Patient PA

Approval and receipt of care as prescribed was reported by 51.8% of respondents; 21.0% noted that although PA was approved, they received different care than originally recommended. More than one-quarter (26.6%) reported a denial. More than one-half (169 respondents [59.1%]) reported delays in care, and nearly one-third (31.8%) rated PA as a moderate or serious problem. In adjusted analyses, direct patient involvement in PA was associated with increased risk of care not being approved or received (adjusted RR [aRR], 2.12; 95% CI, 1.48-3.05), PA being a moderate or serious problem (aRR, 2.20; 95% CI, 1.34-3.60), and delayed care (aRR, 2.15; 95% CI, 1.61-2.86).

Caregiver PA

Among caregivers, self-identified relationships to patients included spouses or partners (31.8%), children (22.0%), or parents (19.2%). Approval and receipt of care as prescribed was reported by 47.8%, with 23.6% noting that although the PA was approved, the patient received different care than originally recommended; 26.9% reported denial of prescribed care. Most with caregiver PA experience (138 respondents [75.8%]) reported delays in care due to PA; more than one-third with caregiver PA experience (37.9%) rated PA as a moderate or serious problem (Table). In adjusted models, caregiver PA involvement was associated with increased risk that recommended care was not approved or received (aRR, 2.33; 95% CI, 1.19-4.56) and PA being a moderate or serious problem (aRR, 2.22; 95% CI, 1.10-4.91). Caregiver PA involvement was associated with increased risk of delayed care in the unadjusted model (RR, 1.48; 95% CI, 1.08-2.03) but not after adjustment.

Discussion

In this survey study, direct patient or caregiver PA engagement was associated with adverse outcomes, including delays, not receiving care as prescribed, and negative perception of PA. This is consistent with prior research describing patient-reported delays and suboptimal care.1,2,8,9 Our findings are among the first, to our knowledge, to examine caregiver PA experiences.

Patient and caregiver PA involvement may be indicative of complicated cases or prior denials. In previous research, patient-reported PA involvement was associated with negative experiences and distrust,8 and qualitative work has highlighted family member effort navigating the health care system to support patients.10 Our findings have implications for clinical practice and policy, highlighting the importance of including caregivers in patient-focused insurance-navigation training. Policy changes, such as limiting types of care requiring PA, increasing transparency, or requiring state-level consumer-assistance programs, may decrease PA-related administrative burdens for patients and caregivers.

Limitations include self-reported data, subject to recall bias or other inaccuracies, and survey data, subject to selection bias. This convenience sample may not be representative of the general population. Because this was a panel survey, we cannot report responses rate or characteristics of nonresponders.

This work highlights negative patient and caregiver experiences of PA. It may be used to shape future research and intervention development to help patients, caregivers, clinicians, and advocates navigate PA-related administrative burden.

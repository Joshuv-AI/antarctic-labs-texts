Towards decarbonizing large-scale industries: A decision support framework for optimizing grid-connected PV-battery energy systems planning – Case study of an OCP mining site, Morocco, North Africa
Authors: Oumaima Mahir, Abdelilah Rochd, Aboubakr Benazzouz, Hicham Ghennioui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Incorporating renewable energy into forthcoming grid-connected or decentralized energy systems assumes an escalating significance and can potentially enhance endeavors toward accomplishing Sustainable Development Goal 7 (SDG7). Nevertheless, deploying sustainable renewable energy-intensive systems may present challenges related to their intermittency and cost instability. This paper proposes the development of a decision support model aimed at planning an optimal on-grid PV battery system. The model builds upon the Open Energy Modeling Framework (OEMOF) and defines asset capacities, energy dispatch, operational strategies, and optimal costs for the designated system. Key factors considered include energy demand profile, photovoltaic potential, electricity tariffs, and the availability of the National Grid. Furthermore, the model evaluation incorporates the computation of pertinent performance, feasibility, and viability indicators, notably the Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE). To validate the framework, the article conducts a case study to determine the optimal sizing and planning of a grid-connected PV battery energy system. The objective is to cater to the electricity needs of an OCP (Office Chérifien des Phosphates) mining site in Morocco. The study considers the characteristics of the national power grid, incorporating time-varying electricity tariffs based on a Demand-Response program taking into account various rates according to the time of use (ToU). The case study includes a sensitivity analysis that examines different factors, namely the proportion of renewable energy, the investment costs of photovoltaic systems and batteries, and the financial parameter known as the weighted average cost of capital (WACC). Through this analysis, the study assesses the impact of these variables on the calculated cost of energy. Experimental results revealed a range of LCOE values from $0.07111/kWh to $0.11847/kWh for high renewable energies integration.

Introduction

As we reach the halfway point to the agenda 2030 deadline, a review of advances on the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) reveals significant challenges. According to the latest SDG progress report [1], more than half of the SDG targets show insufficient headway, while almost 30 % of improvements have stagnated or regressed. These worrying figures particularly concern the targets of reducing poverty, hunger, and global warming. This analysis highlights the urgency of stepping up efforts to ensure that each of the SDGs stays on course and moves towards a sustainable future for all.

Addressing today's key environmental challenges, SDG 7 aims to guarantee that modern society functions in harmony and develops equitably. We need affordable and reliable energy services without depending on inexpensive but energy-intensive fossil fuels, the main producers of GHG emissions that cause climate change [2]. It is therefore essential to redouble our efforts so as not to lose sight of the 2030 target and move towards a just energy transition.

As part of the energy transition, the mining industry contributes to the UN SDGs. This is by facilitating large-scale economic development and providing minerals for technology, infrastructure, energy, and agriculture. In this context, it is imperative to minimize waste production, conduct responsible mining operations, implement safer procedures, integrate sustainable technologies, reduce emissions, and perform effective environmental management [3]. As a result, the mining industry can help address many of the issues raised by the SDGs, notably environmental degradation.

Companies can actively support SDGs by integrating them into their long-term operations and fundamental plans. According to Ref. [4], only a small minority of mining enterprises report on their progress against the indicators or goals they have set. SDG7 stands as a target where the global mining industry still has a long way to go to put in place strategic measures to help achieve it.

Several research studies, including Tiainen et al. [5], Shen et al. [6], Tiainen et al. [7], and Govindan et al. [8], have investigated the impact of the mining factories toward greener production, social responsibility standards, and environmental performance. Norgate and Haque [9] state that over the last 20 years, the mining industry has recognized the significance of promoting sustainability and managing the environment effectively. To this end, Muduli et al. [10] assert that we can put many factors in place, such compliance with legal requirements, appealing to financial institutions, and enhancing eco-efficiency.

Therefore, effective integration of SDG 7 requires support for the early implementation of renewable energy to achieve the goal of a low-carbon economy. In addition, boosting the direct production of renewable electricity by these companies will reduce their dependence on grid power supply, eliminating the need for increased generating capacity and improving system flexibility. Furthermore, the resilience of renewable energy sources (RES) to global crises, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, further underlines their importance. Studies have shown that the renewable energy generation sector is less affected by these disruptions than conventional energy sources [11].

Besides, many articles take sustainability considerations into account when developing mining sites. However, Tsisilile Igogo et al. [12] review the literature to analyze the challenges, possibilities, and enabling strategies for renewable technology integration in mining operations. Anshul Paneri et al. [13] examine the techno-economic viability of RES for switch-on, switch-off mining, a new idea for mobile small-scale mining, in 2021. Eduardo Vyhmeister et al. [14] evaluate the use of photovoltaic and revolutionary wind technologies to supply as much as 10 % current and projected electrical energy needs of the mining industry in Chile. A. Strazzabosco et al. [15] assess the current adoption of clean energy among the mining houses in Australia. Also, literature highlights the integration of renewable energy in the African region. Fabien Muhirwa et al. [16] reviewed research published during the period 2000 to 2022 on substantial extractive industry investment, the associated issues and sustainable development potential of rural communities residing in African mine sites.

In Morocco, renewable energies play a pivotal role in the energy strategy. The country possesses significant renewable potential, letting it meet a substantial proportion of its growing energy needs by replacing fossil fuels. Therefore, Morocco aims to increase the share of renewable energies in installed electricity capacity to over 52 % by 2030 [17]. Morocco will decrease its dependency on fossil fuel sources. The share of coal in installed capacity will drop from 38.8 % in 2020 to 22 % in 2030, while the share of oil-based thermal capacity is reduced to 9.2 % in 2030 from 16.2 % in 2020 [18].

In line with a sustainable growth strategy in the mining sector, the OCP group has implemented a responsible and innovative energy program to reduce its carbon footprint. Thanks to wind, solar, and cogeneration production, OCP has been able to cover 86 % of its needs with green energy, with a vision to reach 100 % coverage by 2030. OCP group guarantees 25 % of Morocco's clean electricity and will make a major contribution to Morocco's goal of producing 52 % of its electricity from renewable and clean sources by 2030 [19]. OCP is preparing for the next stage of energy transformation by creating industry strategies, including supporting the stable integration of the highest possible shares of electricity generation from variable renewable sources.

However, moving to RE technologies at mine sites pose a number of challenges, despite the mining industry's desire to convert and its huge impact on decarbonization efforts. These include the fluctuation and variability of the electricity supply, and the difficult conditions due to the remoteness of the mining sites. At this stage, a growing trend towards electrification systems based on microgrids (MGs) is observed. MGs promote the diffusion of renewable energy sources, and solutions to overcome their intermittency, such as electrical storage. They can operate in grid-connected or off-grid mode.

Optimal microgrid technology selection is a complex subject, mainly concerned with performance in terms of environmental, social, technological, and economic considerations [20]. The optimal combination of renewable energy and energy storage technologies with local energy demand is a major challenge. Hence, it is important to consider many factors, including the high initial investment cost [21], potential capacity limitations based on the inconsistency of the energy sources used [22], infrastructure management, and social impact [23].

In the literature, researchers have developed and discussed various approaches for optimal energy planning. Büyükozkan and Güleryüz [24] select the optimal renewable energy technology from different alternatives as a multi-criteria decision-making problem. Lucas Schmeling et al. [25] presents an overview of relevant publications on the optimal design of various energy systems. Meanwhile, Mohammad Fattahi et al. [26] focuses on sustainable planning of a mining supply chain network using a novel cost-efficient multistage stochastic program in which strategic and tactical planning decisions are integrated. In the other hand, different studies investigate energy systems control, Alejandro Rubio et al. [27] employed an energy system dispatch optimization model including an iterative approach for generating grid constraints, which is decoupled from the linear unit commitment problem.

This is where models and software tools must be developed and implemented to provide a proper frame for the planning of the most efficient energy systems. They allow us to chain the interactions between energy resources, determine their shares, and analyze the risks associated with price evolution. As a result, we can achieve optimal capacity, dispatch, cost, and identify the most useful performance and feasibility indicators, such as LCOE, for valuing projected energy systems. The progress made in the development of these tools and the rigorous examination of their validation forms the subject of an extensive literature. Fernando Martins et al. [28] provide an updated overview of the various modeling tools available to evaluate energy systems in smart European cities. Soner Candas et al. [29] review and compare five open-source frameworks for renewable energy system modeling.

Literature review of some case studies for energy system models relating to Morocco.

The literature review reveals a significant gap in energy systems planning for the Moroccan mining sector, which has received little attention compared to the residential sector. Current literature focuses primarily on the development of household energy systems, neglecting the substantial energy demands and unique challenges of mining operations. This neglect represents a critical omission in current research. Moreover, evaluations of electricity costs from the national grid often rely on simplified billing assumptions, incompatible with Morocco's complex billing model, which includes three tariff levels (peak hours, off-peak hours, and super off-peak hours). This simplification leads to potential inaccuracies in cost estimates, highlighting the need for a more precise approach tailored to Moroccan specifics.

Furthermore, although the use of open-source planning tools is common globally, their application in Moroccan case studies remains limited. This limitation could constrain the depth and scope of research in this area. To advance our understanding of energy systems in Morocco and develop sustainable and effective energy policies, it is crucial to create tailored solutions that consider the particularities of the Moroccan mining sector and its specific billing model.

Our main contributions focus on the development and evaluation of an innovative framework for optimizing energy systems in the Moroccan context. Firstly, we have designed a decision support framework for the optimal sizing of a grid-connected PV-battery energy system, taking into account the unique electricity tariffs in the Moroccan billing model. Based on the Open Energy Modeling Framework, this model accurately assesses system capacities, energy allocation, and optimal costs for different electrification options. To validate our model, we conducted an in-depth techno-economic study of energy technologies, including photovoltaic (PV) systems, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and converters. This study offers a comprehensive analysis of solar demand and resource profiles within the study area, providing an in-depth assessment of the technological and economic viability of these energy solutions.

We applied our framework in a real-life case study to assess its feasibility for supplying energy to a mining site in Morocco, demonstrating the practical applicability and potential benefits of our model. Finally, we carried out a comprehensive sensitivity analysis and assessment of the potential for integrating PV and BESS systems into the Moroccan energy system. This analysis includes key financial and technical parameters such as LCOE, NPV, autonomy factor, and system reliability, providing essential information for decision-making and energy planning. These contributions aim to fill the current gaps in energy system optimization research in Morocco by proposing a rigorous approach tailored to the local context, with significant potential to inform the development of a sustainable and efficient energy policy.

It's then the objective of our contributions to theory and practice. The article is organized as follows: Section 2 presents a literature review. Section 3 introduces the optimization model where the developed software and the applied framework OEMOF are implemented. Section 4 describes the case study and the simulation result. Then, Section 5 presents and discusses simulation results, and Section 6 draws conclusions and gives an outlook on future work.

Literature review

Planning renewable energy systems is a complex undertaking that requires multiple factors to be considered. In addition to the technical challenges of securing a stable and reliable energy supply from intermittent renewable sources due to varying environmental conditions, there are also crucial economic considerations to be taken into account. This is particularly relevant in light of the global economic downturns, which have highlighted the importance of cost effectiveness and financial viability in sustainable energy planning.

Consequently, the drive to design sustainable and cost-effective renewable energy systems is stimulating the development of existing planning frameworks and the emergence of new ones.

It is then conceivable to delve deeper into the literature to undertake a head-to-head of some existing models. The review study in Ref. [47] examine the capabilities and applications of commercial tools that have significantly advanced the planning, modeling, optimization, and performance measurement of photovoltaic systems. The open source Dispatch and Investment Evaluation Tool with Endogenous Renewables (DIETER) is used in Ref. [48]. The authors examine the role of electricity storage in systems with a high variable RES proportion in Germany. Their findings highlight that electricity storage requirements are closely linked to costs and other supply alternatives. According to Ref. [49], the micrOrgridS model was tested to size the components and energy flows of a PV-diesel-battery HMG on the Pacific Island of Lifuka. A detailed analysis of the model was carried out and compared with the results obtained using Homer R Pro 3.11 software. Moreover, the authors in Ref. [50] employed the Calliope framework to build an energy model of the Sardinia region. They validated the model by verifying energy flows between nodes to minimize system costs as the main objective function. Furthermore, the study by Ref. [51], highlights the impact of the increasing integration of renewable energies and storage units in the national electricity grids in Jordan. PLEXOS software is used to analyze this integration and minimize system operating costs. Consequently, the results strongly recommend the use of energy storage to efficiently meet the growing demand resulting from this integration. The literature on [52] utilizes EUCAD (European Unit Commitment And Dispatch) software for developing a long-term energy model incorporating energy storage. This model aims to optimize the distribution of different technologies in a fixed European electricity system. The research conducted by Ref. [53] used STeMES to develop an optimal design and operation model for a hydrogen network. However, this model considers short-term drivers and long-term planning horizons. Likewise, the literature on [54] examines the economic benefits of the main flexibility options in the energy systems of western, northern, and central Europe through BALMOREL. The research project carried out in Ref. [55] explores different scenarios for integrating renewable energies into the energy mix of the Dominican Republic using the OSeMOSYS model. According to the results, a total investment of USD 1405.00 million would be required to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 25 % by 2030. In the context of [56], they study the potential of electricity storage in European energy systems characterized by a high presence of renewables (over 80 %). Therefore, using the REMix optimization model, they endogenously determine electricity generation capacity and distribution, including storage technologies. Researchers at [57] have used the REMod framework to model the German electricity and heat sector in a scenario dominated by renewable energies. The result demonstrates that it is technically possible to meet the entire electricity and heat demand for the building sector with 100 % renewable energy if the heat demand is reduced by at least 60 %. Additionally, the authors in Ref. [58] carried out an analysis of possible energy futures in Egypt using two different approaches, TIMES as an optimization model and the dynamic ELTAP-EGY approach. Similarly, in Ref. [59], the researchers developed and used the oemof-moea model to analyze the Italian energy system for the year 2050 to demonstrate its main characteristics. The results show that investing in flexibility options is required to minimize carbon dioxide emissions. In the literature [60], they present the COMANDO model, illustrated by a case study of a German industrial estate. The EHDO tool is used in Ref. [61], to design and optimize multi-energy systems. Two case studies are carried out, the first on an energy center in Cologne, Germany, and the second on the supply of renewable energy to an island system on Easter Island, Chile. In addition, the authors in Ref. [62], used HOGA software to analyze three proposed renewable energy systems. The results demonstrated that the optimal configurations in terms of cost-effectiveness are PV-wind hybrid systems and stand-alone PV systems.

However, it is clear from these various case studies that optimization models can address complex questions concerning the integration of renewable energies, storage systems and demand-side management. This underlines the growing significance of optimization models within the transition to more sustainable energy systems, while also identifying the challenges for effective implementation of these approaches in practice. Optimization model

Background

The optimal design and dispatching of a microgrid can be achieved using a variety of methods and at different levels, depending on the tool selected. This underlines the crucial importance of choosing the appropriate software according to the specific situation under study, the results expected, and the availability of input parameters.

Researchers have developed numerous decision-aid tools for energy system modeling and optimization. Hans-Kristian Ringkjøb et al. [73] presented a review of different energy system planning and analysis software tools. Kamal Anoune et al. [74] and Sinha.S and Chandel.SS [75] specifically evaluated planning tools as they apply to hybrid energy systems. Connolly et al. [76] investigate in detail more than 37 software tools used to examine renewable energy integration.

various tools discussed in the literature, HOMER is widely regarded as the industry standard, and extensively utilized by project designers and researchers worldwide. It's a user-friendly tool with many features such as system settings, illustrations, and output tables. Nevertheless, its primary limitation lies in its commercial and proprietary nature [77]; Programmers can't modify the tool's code. OEMOF, on the other hand, is an open-source framework for developing models of energy systems and different applications for performing energy system analysis.

Model Description

Open energy modelling framework

OEMOF [78] used extensively in a wide range of research questions such as [79]. This open-source tool written in the Python programming language allows for the definition of high-resolution input data and detailed analysis of the results. In particular, OEMOF enables comprehensive modeling of energy supply systems by representing them in the form of directed graphs. In these graphs, vertices correspond to system components, such as sources, sinks, and technologies, or to buses that regulate the flow of resources between these components. The edges connecting some components and buses are directed and represent the resource flows. Using Pyomo [80], this graph is then converted into a mixed integer linear mathematical optimization problem (MILP) that can be solved numerically by a variety of solvers [81]. The goal is to satisfy all energy demands while minimizing the system costs resulting from the energy flows.

This framework enables the system to meet energy demand at marginal cost-efficiently. Case study

Overview

As part of the OCP strategy, we apply the modeling and optimization framework to the energy system supplying an OCP's mining site to better understand its techno-economic potential.

We define our supply option as a complete set of components (PV, battery storage system, converters, connection to the national grid). Fig. 2 illustrates the architecture of this supply system, which is evaluated based on the reliability and supply cost. Therefore, our work tackles the broader question of efficiently and optimally reducing dependence on the national grid through the implementation of a battery storage system to accommodate the intermittency of PV sources. To analyze this alternative, it is necessary to define optimal overall system capacities and dispatch strategies that can meet the electricity demand currently supplied only by the National Grid.Fig. 2The Energy system model adapted to the simulation tool.Fig. 2

Solar resources

A detailed assessment of the feasibility of the effective use of solar energy in an energy system is of primary importance. The uncertainties associated with the solar resource can make a very important distinction between a benefit and a loss, which underlines the relevance of the solar resource analysis of the chosen site. To optimally size future photovoltaic power plants, it is necessary to evaluate the solar resource using site meteorological data to simulate the yield of the solar station that will be set up at the mine site.

One of the ultimate goals of the assessment is to characterize the solar irradiance available for energy conversion for the region or location of interest over a historical period of interest. Based on the Helioclim, HelioClim3 (HC3V5) [83] database data provided in a TMY (Typical Meteorological Year) format including all geographical and meteorological parameters, a set of evaluation calculations and simulations are made.

We have used three fundamental components of solar radiation at the Earth's surface: global horizontal irradiance (GHI), direct normal irradiance (DNI), and global tilted irradiance (GTI).

Scenario development: sensitivity analyses

To conduct an economic study, we apply the analysis of multiple scenarios in the described case study. This involved performing a sensitivity analysis on some economic parameters. First, we examine the PV and storage system costs sensitivity, along with the sensitivity of renewable energy share within the system under study. Specifically, the analysis takes into account renewable energy shares of 61.18 % and 31.18 %, which have been defined by the OCP group according to its green energy requirements. These shares reflect the OCP's strategic objectives to meet its energy needs while reinforcing its commitment to sustainable development. Second, we evaluate the sensitivity of the weighted average cost of capital (WACC), a widely used financial parameter that determines the required project rate of return. By addressing these factors, we seek to gain a comprehensive understanding of the economic implications and potential outcomes associated with the studied system.

- Scenario 1: PV capex, renewable share (RS)

In this first scenario, we subject the PV investment cost and the share of renewables in the energy system to a sensitivity analysis. Four experiments result from the four combinations introduced by the suggested values of the PV investment cost (present value, present value-25 %) and the share of renewables (61.18 % RS, 31.18 % RS).

- Scenario 2: Battery Capex, RS

The second scenario concerns the investment cost of the batteries and the share of renewable energies in the studied energy system. The investment cost of the batteries takes two values (present value, present value - 25 %), and the share of renewable energies is fixed at two values (61.18 %, 31.18 %).

- Scenario 3: WACC, RS

The third scenario deals with the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) and the share of renewables in the studied energy system. The Wacc takes three values (5 %,7 %), and the share of renewable energy is set to two values (61.18 %, 31.18 %). These combinations generate four sensitivity analysis experiments and thus four simulation results.

It is an indicator of the price of electricity required for a project with revenues equal to costs, including the achievement of a return on investment equal to the discount rate.

Conclusion

This work enabled an economic analysis of the energy system allowing the supply of electrical energy to the studied sites. A sensitivity analysis of a set of economic and financial parameters was also implemented (PV Cost investment, Battery Cost investment, WACC). The sensitivity inputs were presented under a matrix form, which allowed the sensitivity study of two parameters simultaneously generating a multitude of combinations to be simulated, discussed and evaluated in the case study. Two supply options with 61.18 % and 31.18 % renewable energy coverage are presented.

The simulation results are compared economically through the LCOE indicator, for 61.18 % renewable energy coverage, scenario 1 showed that the investment cost of PV improves the economic profitability by reducing the LCOE by 6.34 %, if decreased by 25 %. While scenario 2 showed that the investment cost of the batteries can more strongly improve this profitability if it also decreases by 25 % through a 11.06 % reduction in LCOE. From a financial point of view, the wacc parameter variation also increases its influence on the economic profitability by decreasing LCOE by 8.46 % if we have a wacc of about 5 % instead of the 7 % currently adopted. Although for a 31.18 % renewable energy system, for a 25 % reduction in PV CAPEX, the LCOE decreases by 4.8 % while it decreases by 2.11 % by reducing the battery CAPEX by 25 %. For the same share of renewable energy, if it reaches 5 %, the WACC lowers the LCOE by 4.41 %. Therefore, when tracking the evolution of LCOE for each scenario studied, we infer that the variation in battery CAPEX and WACC affects power generation costs more strongly than the variation in solar CAPEX.

Finally, we can state that the answer to the research question outlined in the case study chapter has been addressed. Based on these financial metrics and in terms of LCOE, installation, operation and maintenance expenses, the reference model with a PV capital cost of about 691 $/kWp and a battery capital cost of about 203 $/kWh and a WACC of 7 % yielded a LCOE of 0.11847 $/kWh, the energy cost can be improved in the near future due to the decreasing cost trend according to the analyses made above and that will be an increasingly cheaper solution to provide electricity, ensuring a constant supply to meet the total expected demand.

Future work will investigate how degradation of battery energy storage systems affects both the cost and efficiency of their use in microgrid applications. This analysis will take into account the constraints associated with the technical specifications of BESS and the particular conditions of the grid. By better understanding how BESS degradation affects these elements, we will be able to develop more resilient strategies for their optimal use in real-world environments.

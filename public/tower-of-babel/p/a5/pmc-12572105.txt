ABSTRACT
We improve upon previous explicit estimates of charm rescattering contributions to the decay \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B^0\rightarrow K^0{\bar{\ell }}\ell $$\end{document}B0→K0ℓ¯ℓ by including contributions from dipole interactions with the intermediate charm-meson states, and by further investigating the structure of the electromagnetic form factors. Using a model of fundamental meson fields inspired by heavy-hadron chiral perturbation theory, augmented by form factors motivated by theoretical considerations as well as experimental data, we provide a thorough investigation of rescattering contributions induced by intermediate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D^{(*)}D^{(*)}_s$$\end{document}D(∗)Ds(∗) states.

Introduction

Flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) transitions in the standard model (SM) provide excellent candidates for the observation of indirect evidence of beyond standard model (BSM) physics due to their heavy suppression in the SM. Of particular interest over the past decade has been the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$b\rightarrow s{\bar{\ell }}\ell $$\end{document}b→sℓ¯ℓ transition: several analyses of exclusive-mode decays indicate significant tensions between data and SM predictions [1–9]. Recent investigations [10–12] accounting for the full-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q^2$$\end{document}q2 range – \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q^2 = (p_\ell + p_{\bar{\ell }})^2$$\end{document}q2=(pℓ+pℓ¯)2 the dilepton invariant mass – and narrow charmonium resonances have shown that these tensions can be well-described by a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim -25\%$$\end{document}∼-25% shift to the short-distance physics encoded in the Wilson coefficient (WC) \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_9$$\end{document}C9, corresponding to the effective operator1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {O}_9 = (\bar{b}_L\gamma ^\mu s_L)(\bar{\ell }\gamma _\mu \ell ). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}O9=(b¯LγμsL)(ℓ¯γμℓ).While the discrepancies between SM predictions and data are clear, the source of the disagreement is challenging to pinpoint. In particular, long-distance contamination arising from the rescattering of intermediate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c\bar{c}$$\end{document}cc¯ states can potentially produce identical effects as those from shifts in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_9$$\end{document}C9 [13–16]. These non-local effects were most recently estimated in Refs. [3, 4], following the approach in Ref. [17]. The method relies on an operator-product expansion combined with a hadronic dispersion relation evaluated in the unphysical negative-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q^2$$\end{document}q2 region. The adopted parameterization satisfies unitarity bounds, and the resulting contribution was found to be small. However, the parameterizations used in Refs. [3, 4] do not account for anomalous branch cuts which extend into the complex-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q^2$$\end{document}q2 plane, which arise from triangle-loop graphs corresponding to the rescattering of intermediate states containing charmed mesons. Several investigations into formally quantifying the magnitude of these effects have been made, e.g. through novel parameterizations for the extrapolation of unphysical results [18] or use of dispersive methods [19]. In Ref. [19], it was found that the anomalous cuts in pion rescattering in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B\rightarrow (\pi ,\rho )\gamma ^*$$\end{document}B→(π,ρ)γ∗ decays could give contributions that are \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(10\%)$$\end{document}O(10%) of the non-anomalous non-local form factors, but this cannot be trivially extended to the charm case due to the qualitatively different analytical structure. In fact, in the charm-loop case, the anomalous branch point always lies in the lower complex half-plane (except for the case of an external pion). Since anomalous effects were found to be the smallest in the light-quark loop under similar conditions, the naive expectation is that their impact is small. Still, an explicit calculation is necessary to confirm this.

In Ref. [20], we took a different approach by performing a direct, albeit model-dependent, estimation of charm rescattering effects in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B^0\rightarrow K^0{\bar{\ell }}\ell $$\end{document}B0→K0ℓ¯ℓ, finding that they were not likely to contribute more than a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10\%$$\end{document}10% effect. This study, however, assumed a high degree of symmetry and neglected several effects which are not necessarily trivially small. The goal of this letter is to extend the work of Ref. [20] to include contributions which can arise from dipole couplings of the photon to intermediate charmed mesons. We further clarify some general conclusions about the sign- and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q^2$$\end{document}q2-dependence of the effect, following from the structure of the electromagnetic form factors.

The paper is organized as follows. The effective model in terms of mesonic fields is introduced in Sect. 2. The form factors needed to modify the point-like interactions introduced in Sect. 2 and to extend our result to the whole kinematic range are discussed in Sect. 3. In particular, the extraction of the dipole form factor from data is described. In Sect. 4, the results of our calculations are shown, and a multiplicity factor is calculated that accounts for additional possible intermediate states in the topologies we considered. We also show the combination of this calculation with the results from the monopole contributions found in [20] and its effect on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_9$$\end{document}C9, accounting for a more general monopole form factor. Section 5 summarizes our results.

Conclusions

Predictive Score for Significant Diastolic Dysfunction in Patients with Morbid Obesity Undergoing Bariatric Surgery
Authors: Aphichat Suphathamwit, Kamheang Vacharaksa, Chaowanan Khamtuikrua, Chanakan Jerdmathawut, Voraboot Taweerutchana, Chutharat Wetchakama, Nipaporn Sangarunakul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMorbid obesity is a well-established risk factor for cardiovascular disease. Diastolic dysfunction, particularly in non-cardiac surgeries, has been associated with increased incidence of adverse cardiovascular events. This study aimed to evaluate the prevalence of diastolic dysfunction in morbidly obese patients undergoing bariatric surgery and to identify associated clinical risk factors using transesophageal echocardiography (TEE).MethodsIn this prospective observational study, 113 morbidly obese patients scheduled for bariatric surgery between April 2019 and January 2023 were enrolled. Diastolic function was assessed after anesthesia induction using TEE and categorized as normal or grade I, II, or III dysfunction based on the E/e’ ratio. The primary study outcome was the prevalence of diastolic dysfunction, while secondary outcomes included identification of risk factors, development of a predictive score and evaluation of perioperative complications.ResultsDiastolic dysfunction was identified in 45.1% of patients (grade I: 21.2%; grade II: 19.5%; and grade III: 4.4%). Age (odds ratio [OR], 1.04; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.01 to 1.08) and continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) use for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) (OR, 4.22; 95% CI, 1.02 to 17.55) were significantly associated with high-grade dysfunction (grades II–III). A predictive score demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy with a cutoff of >120 points. The score was calculated as (age×2)+(coronary artery disease×51)+(chronic kidney disease×102)+(OSA without CPAP×2)+(OSA with CPAP×63). Postoperative outcomes did not differ significantly between groups.ConclusionDiastolic dysfunction is prevalent among morbidly obese patients undergoing bariatric surgery. A predictive score exceeding 120 points may aid in identifying individuals at elevated risk.

INTRODUCTION

Patients with morbid obesity, who are significantly increasing in prevalence in Thailand and worldwide,1,2 are at risk of metabolic disease, obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), and cardiovascular diseases.3-5 Several previous studies reported that patients with morbid obesity have abnormalities in cardiovascular system morphology and physiology, including left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy,4,5 right ventricular dysfunction,6 and LV diastolic dysfunction.7-10 Diastolic dysfunction can cause acute heart failure (HF), which is also called diastolic HF.9,10 Some studies have identified diastolic dysfunction as an independent risk factor for adverse cardiovascular events and longer intensive care unit (ICU) and hospital stays in patients undergoing non-cardiac surgeries.11,12

Preoperative cardiac risk assessment in patients with morbid obesity undergoing non-cardiac surgery continues to present clinical challenges,13 largely due to the absence of standardized guidelines. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is not routinely employed in this population, in part because imaging is technically limited by a suboptimal acoustic window caused by increased chest wall thickness and reduced intercostal space.5,13 Given the elevated risk of coronary artery disease (CAD) in this cohort, LV systolic function and regional wall motion abnormalities are typically prioritized during evaluation.13 However, data concerning the prevalence of diastolic dysfunction in morbidly obese patients are sparse or inadequately reported.7,8 The study investigators hypothesized that determination of a more accurate prevalence of diastolic dysfunction in this population could enhance the effectiveness of preoperative cardiac screening protocols.

Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) provides good image quality14,15 with low complication rates16 and can be safely performed in patients with obesity14. Several studies have reported the usefulness of TEE for preoperative cardiac evaluation in patients with morbid obesity.17,18 However, to our knowledge, the use of TEE for diastolic function evaluation in this patient group remains scarce. Therefore, this study aimed to identify the prevalence of and risk factors for diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery using TEE. Furthermore, this study aimed to develop a predictive score with good sensitivity and specificity for future standard practice of preoperative cardiac assessment.

METHODS

This was a prospective observational single-center study. From April 2019 to January 2023, patients with morbid obesity who were scheduled for bariatric surgery according to the institutional protocol were enrolled in this study. Patients aged 18 to 70 years with a body mass index (BMI) >40 kg/m2 or 35–40 kg/m2 alongside other comorbidities, including hypertension (HT), type 2 diabetes mellitus, OSA, dyslipidemia, and osteoarthritis, were included. Meanwhile, patients with serious illnesses, including significant CAD, active HF, major psychological problems, or a drug abuse history; patients who refused to participate; patients with contraindications for TEE according to the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and the Society of Cardiac Anesthesiologists guidelines;15 or cases in which staff were unavailable were excluded. Patients with procedure cancellations or missing data were withdrawn from the analysis.

All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. The study protocol was approved by the Siriraj Institutional Review Board (Si196/2019) and was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04128735).

Diastolic function assessment

The following five major echocardiographic measurements were used for evaluating LV diastolic function:19 (1) pulse-wave Doppler assessment of transmitral inflow, (2) pulse-wave Doppler assessment of pulmonary vein flow, (3) tissue Doppler assessment of mitral annular velocity, (4) color M-mode flow propagation velocity, and (5) two-dimensional examination of left atrial size and function.

The assessment guidelines of diastolic function from the ASE and the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging20 were developed using a large number of TTE studies on awake, spontaneously breathing participants, and unique intraoperative hemodynamic conditions were noted in anesthetized patients with positive-pressure ventilation that may affect the findings and assignment of diastolic function grade. Therefore, the present investigators used a simplified comprehensive algorithm from a previous study,21 which categorized diastolic dysfunction on the basis of the ratio of transmitral inflow E-wave velocity and tissue Doppler image (TDI) of lateral mitral annulus (e’) velocity (E/e’ ratio) (Supplementary Fig. 1). The lateral annulus e’ velocity is best correlated with LV filling pressure and catheter-derived measurements of LV compliance.22,23 Also, the values of E and e’, measured at the lateral mitral annulus, are proportionate in patients with normal systolic function.22 The simplified algorithm from Swaminathan et al.21 has shown that each grade of increasing diastolic dysfunction is associated with an increased risk of major cardiac adverse event.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was prevalence and grade of diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity using the ratio of transmitral E-wave velocity and TDI of the lateral mitral annulus (e’) velocity. Patients were categorized according to a simplified algorithm from a previous study:21 e’ velocity >10 cm/sec, normal diastolic function; E/e’ <8, grade I (impaired relaxation); E/e’ 9–12, grade II (pseudonormal); and E/e’ >13, grade III (restrictive pattern). Secondary outcomes were risk factors and predictive scores for diastolic dysfunction; cardiac findings (both functional and anatomical abnormalities); prevalence of other perioperative adverse events, including oral injury, pulmonary aspiration, myocardial infarction (MI), HF, and postoperative desaturation; and the respective lengths of ICU and hospital length stays.

Sample size calculation

The sample size calculation was inferred from a previous study8 that reported diastolic dysfunction incidence in patients with morbid obesity at 32.7%. With an allowable error at 0.09 and a confidence level of 0.05, this study required a sample size of 104 participants. Considering a 10% drop-out rate, the sample size was increased to 115 participants.

Study protocol

Following admission, patients were assessed for eligibility and obtained informed consent by the research nurse. All patients received preoperative medications as required. In the operating room, each patient received standard monitoring according to the American Society of Anesthesiologists guidelines, including electrocardiography, non-invasive blood pressure, pulse oximetry, and end-tidal carbon dioxide. Subsequently, general anesthesia was initiated with endotracheal intubation in the ramp position. Usual anesthetic agents were fentanyl (1–2 μg/kg), propofol (1–2 mg/kg), rocuronium (0.6–0.9 mg/kg), or cis-atracurium (0.05–0.1 mg/kg). A rapid sequence induction and intubation technique was used according to the attending anesthesiologist. After the endotracheal tube was secured and the patient’s hemodynamic status was stable, a comprehensive TEE diastolic function evaluation according to the ASE guidelines15,19 was performed by anesthesia staff with a Testamur status of special competence in advanced perioperative TEE, U.S. National Board of Echocardiography. The TEE probe was removed following TEE study completion, and surgery was started. Anesthesia was maintained with sevoflurane or desflurane in a 60% air/oxygen mixture, fentanyl (1–2 μg/kg/hr), and cis-atracurium (0.08–0.1 mg/kg/hr) or rocuronium (0.2–0.3 mg/kg/hr). Patients were extubated at the end of surgery after meeting extubation criteria and transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU). They were observed in the PACU for 1 to 2 hours and then either transferred back to the general ward after meeting the required modified Aldrete score24 or transferred to the ICU.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were expressed as mean±standard deviations for data with normal distribution or as median with interquartile range for data with non-normal distribution. Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages. The investigators performed subgroup analysis for analysis of LV diastolic function according to BMI and for risk factor and predictive score determination. Here, the investigators categorized data of patients with normal diastolic function and diastolic dysfunction grade I as group 1, whereas those with grades II and III diastolic dysfunction were assigned to group 2. These groups were then compared using the chi-square test, Student’s t-test, and the Mann–Whitney U-test. Factors with P≤0.1 were included in multiple logistic regression analysis.

A predictive model was subsequently constructed from the multivariate analysis and factors of interest. The model demonstrated the prognostic ability to differentiate between patients with morbid obesity and those with significant diastolic dysfunction, as determined using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, with the optimal cutoff identified by Youden’s index. Bootstrap validation was conducted to assess overfitting. Calibration of the model predictions was verified against expected outcomes, and clinical efficacy was evaluated through decision curve analysis. The ROC curve was used to identify the best cutoff point for sensitivity, specificity, negative predictive value, positive predictive value, area under the curve (AUC), and the 95% confidence interval (CI). All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 28 (IBM Corp.), and STATA SE-Standard Edition version 14.1 (StataCorp LLC).

RESULTS

During the study period, 130 patients with morbid obesity were scheduled for bariatric surgery; of these, 15 were excluded, and an additional two withdrew as they did not meet the study criteria (Fig. 1). Of the remaining 113 patients, most were women (65.49%), and the most common comorbidities were OSA with continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) use, HT, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. The prevalence of diastolic dysfunction was 45.1% (95% CI, 36.3% to 54.1%) (n=51), with individual rates of 21.2% (95% CI, 14.4% to 29.6%), 19.5% (95% CI, 12.9% to 27.7%), and 4.4% (95% CI, 1.4% to 10.0%) for grades I (n=24), II (n=22), and III (n=5) diastolic dysfunction, respectively (Table 1). General analysis showed that patient age, BMI, underlying CAD, and type 2 diabetes mellitus were significantly different between the groups. Other intraoperative data, including type of surgery, operative time, blood loss, and amount of fluid administered, were comparable between the groups (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis according to patient BMI showed no significant differences in the grade of diastolic dysfunction between the groups categorized according to BMI (Table 2). Comparing normal diastolic function and diastolic dysfunction grade I with the high-grade diastolic dysfunction group (grades II–III) demonstrated that age, CAD, and chronic kidney disease (CKD) were significant factors for high-grade diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity (Table 3). Multivariate analysis confirmed age (odds ratio [OR], 1.04; 95% CI, 1.01 to 1.08; P=0.024) and OSA with CPAP use (OR, 4.22; 95% CI, 1.02 to 17.55) as significant risk factors for high-grade diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery.

To develop a predictive score, each significant factor from multivariate analysis and factors of interest were assigned a score using the coefficients of the individual factors (Supplementary Table 1): 2 points for age, 51 points for CAD, 102 points for CKD, and 63 and 2 points for OSA with and without CPAP machine use, respectively. Subsequently, a binary logistic regression model was fitted: (age×2)+(CAD×51)+(CKD×102)+(OSA without CPAP×2)+(OSA with CPAP×63).

The predictive model demonstrated an excellent fit, as shown by the Hosmer–Lemeshow test (0.166; P>0.05) and a −2 log-likelihood of 51.801. The AUC indicated good discriminative power, with a value of 0.749 (95% CI, 0.644 to 0.854) (Supplementary Fig. 2A). Internal bootstrap validation confirmed the model reliability in the same population, with a c-statistic of 0.749 (overfitting=0.0001). The scores for high-grade diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity (AUC, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.57 to 0.76) indicated that a total score >120 points provided the best diagnostic index. This score demonstrated a sensitivity of 77.8% (95% CI, 57.7% to 91.4%), specificity of 55.8% (95% CI, 44.7% to 66.5%), negative predictive value of 88.9% (95% CI, 77.4% to 88.9%), and positive predictive value of 35.6% (95% CI, 23.6% to 49.1%) (Table 4). The calibration slope was 1, indicating that the model predictions matched the observed outcomes (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Supplementary Figure 2C shows the relationship between predicted and observed risk. The optimal cutoff point for the predictive score was >120 points, indicating a predicted risk probability of 17.4% for high-grade diastolic dysfunction. Decision curve analysis (Supplementary Fig. 2D) demonstrated that targeting interventions based on the predictive score for individuals at high risk of high-grade diastolic dysfunction is more beneficial than applying a universal or no intervention.

In this study, one patient developed minor oral injury. Additionally, 13 patients (11.5%) experienced desaturation in the PACU, but they recovered with overnight stepwise oxygen support (oxygen mask with bag, from an oxygen cannula). Two cases (one with normal diastolic function and one with grade II diastolic dysfunction) with multiple comorbidities were observed in the ICU without reintubation. No difference in postoperative adverse events, including oral injury, pulmonary aspiration, MI, congestive HF, and desaturation, was observed between the groups. The median lengths of ICU and hospital stays were 1 and 5 days, respectively (Table 5).

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate diastolic function in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery using the TDI criteria assessed using TEE. The overall diastolic dysfunction prevalence was as high as 45.13%, with high-grade (grades II–III) diastolic dysfunction affecting 23.89% without a correlation with BMI. Age and OSA with CPAP use were significant risk factors for high-grade diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery. No significant difference in perioperative major adverse events was observed between patients with normal and impaired diastolic function.

Patients with morbid obesity possess high cardiac risks and are difficult to subject to cardiac evaluation, especially preoperative assessment. Previous studies by Rocha et al.5, Tavares et al.7, and Michalopoulou et al.8, who performed TTE for patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery, reported that the exclusion of patients with poor acoustic windows was a limitation of their studies. The overall diastolic dysfunction prevalence of their cohorts ranged from 32.7% to 54.6%, mostly as a result of diagnostic criteria variations. To assess patients’ diastolic function, the study investigators selected TEE to reduce the technical limitations of TTE and categorize diastolic dysfunction severity using the simplified TDI criteria proposed by Swaminathan et al.21 because it is simple to perform, reliable in anesthetized ventilated patients, and proven to be associated with major adverse perioperative cardiac events. Interestingly, 25% of the patients with morbid obesity had high-grade diastolic dysfunction and may require proper preoperative assessment and optimization, along with the patients with systolic disease.13

In this study, no relationship was observed between higher BMI and significant diastolic dysfunction. Previous studies by Rozenbaum et al.25 and Shemirani et al.26 reported the significant relationship between individuals with BMIs of >35 kg/m2 and diastolic dysfunction incidence. However, among patients with morbid obesity, no relationship was found between increased BMI and increased diastolic dysfunction prevalence, even in the case of super morbid obesity (BMI >50 kg/m2), similar to as seen in previous work.8,27

Regarding risk factors for diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity, the results showed that older age is one of the associated factors, consistent with the results of a previous study.7 Moreover, age was a risk factor among the general population, which may be related to LV hypertrophy due to cardiac remodeling in the aging heart, thereby leading to ventricular stiffness and resulting in diastolic dysfunction.28,29

OSA is significantly correlated with obesity as a result of several factors, including fat deposition in the upper airway, respiratory tract narrowing, and decreased muscle tone in the upper airway region, thereby leading to multiple hypoxic and apneic episodes.30 Various studies have reported that OSA is a significant risk factor for cardiac disorders, including diastolic dysfunction.30-32 Wachter et al.31 concluded that the degree of diastolic dysfunction increases with OSA severity. Furthermore, OSA is associated with postoperative complications and poor outcomes.33-35 In the authors’ center, CPAP therapy is specifically indicated for patients with OSA who met certain criteria, including showing an inadequate response to conservative management (such as weight loss and exercise), those with unsuccessful surgical intervention (such as uvulopalatopharyngoplasty or radiofrequency volumetric tissue reduction), and severe OSA cases deemed unsuitable for surgical treatment (such as severe comorbidities). In this cohort, OSA with CPAP machine use, which partially identifies patients with more severe OSA, was significantly associated with high-grade diastolic dysfunction.

This study developed a predictive score using significant risk factors identified through multivariate analysis, comprising age and OSA with CPAP use, along with other factors of interest like CAD, CKD, and OSA without CPAP. The scoring model is straightforward and practical, offering good sensitivity and a strong negative predictive value. A score >120 points indicates a 17.4% risk of high-grade diastolic dysfunction. While this risk level is considered moderate, the score can assist in clinical decision-making for bariatric surgery candidates, including need for additional preoperative cardiac assessments or specific intraoperative monitoring and fluid management.

In this study, the most common postoperative complication was desaturation in the PACU. However, all cases were minor and successfully treated with overnight oxygen support. A multicenter cohort study by Morgan et al.36 reported that type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic respiratory disease, and OSA were significant preoperative predictors for diastolic dysfunction in 4.9% of ICU admissions following bariatric surgery with revision or open surgery. In that cohort, the median length of hospital stay was 5 days. In their study, Proczko et al.37 reported a reduced hospital stay from 3.7 to 2.1 days following successful implementation of the Enhanced Recovery after Bariatric Surgery protocol, which is a promising protocol for improving patient care.

Although advancements in surgical techniques and intraoperative anesthetic management have contributed to a relatively low incidence of postoperative complications following bariatric surgery—even among patients classified as moderate to high risk due to obesity, the prevalence of diastolic dysfunction identified in this study may not, by itself, justify alterations to current preoperative assessment protocols. Nonetheless, the authors suggest that a more accurate estimation of the prevalence of high-grade diastolic dysfunction in morbidly obese individuals, along with the development of a simple predictive scoring system based on these findings, could serve as a foundation for establishing refined preoperative evaluation protocols in the future.

This study had several limitations. First, three cardiac anesthesiologists performed cardiac evaluation, which may affect the accuracy of the measurement. However, the protocol was provided before patient recruitment. Second, the diagnostic criteria for diastolic dysfunction were from a previous study using TDI and may have limited the general implications. Third, this study had a relatively small sample size for detecting the relationship between high-grade diastolic dysfunction and postoperative outcomes in bariatric surgery candidates. Future research with a larger sample size is needed.

In conclusion, the overall prevalence of diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery was as high as 45.1%, with high-grade diastolic dysfunction accounting for 23.89%. OSA with CPAP machine use and older age were significant risk factors for high-grade diastolic dysfunction in patients with morbid obesity undergoing bariatric surgery. The proposed simple predictive scoring model may help to improve the preoperative assessment protocol.

Prevalence of Obesity among School-going Children in India: A Comprehensive Systematic Review, Meta-analysis, and Spatial Analysis
Authors: C Deepa, Kalesh M. Karun, Amitha Puranik, Jyothika Jeevan, Manish Barvaliya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Childhood obesity is a notable public health concern in India and other developing nations. There are many published articles on the prevalence of obesity among school children in various states of India. Estimating obesity prevalence at state and national levels will help understand its distribution and guide effective prevention strategies. To estimate the pooled prevalence of obesity among school-going children in India using a systematic review and spatial analysis. This systematic review and meta-analysis include cross-sectional studies from 1995 to 2023, reporting the prevalence of obesity among school-going children in India. Two authors independently screened and extracted data from PubMed, Scopus, and the Web of Science. Study quality was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, and the random effect model was used as there was high heterogeneity (I2 >50%). Spatial analysis and cumulative meta-analysis were performed using R and STATA software. A total of 125 articles were selected based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The overall pooled prevalence of obesity was found to be 6.97% (95% CI: 5.97, 7.97). Among the different regions of India, the highest pooled prevalence was found in the Northern region, that is, 8.58% (95% CI: 5.47, 11.69), and the lowest in the Central region, that is, 5.63% (95% CI: 3.95, 7.31). The cumulative analysis indicated a rising trend in the prevalence of obesity among school-going children over the years. This meta-analysis offers vital insights into its scope and geographical distribution, helping to develop effective strategies to prevent its persistence into adulthood. Trial registration: The PROSPERO registration number is CRD42023431574.

BACKGROUND

India has witnessed a rise in childhood obesity in recent times. This trend is often linked to the country’s economic growth and transition in nutrition.[1] Childhood obesity can lead to a host of cardiovascular and endocrine issues as children grow older. Across both developed and developing nations, overweight and obesity are now significant public health problems. India is currently experiencing a dual nutritional challenge. On one hand, undernutrition persists due to poverty, while on the other, obesity rates are climbing due to rapid industrialization and urbanization. Global survey data indicate that the prevalence of overweight and obesity not only varies by region and gender but has also significantly increased over the last thirty years.[2] If the current trend persists, global obesity rates are projected to hit 18% in men and exceed 21% in women by 2025. India is now the third most obese nation globally, trailing only behind the United States and China.[3] Data from the National Family Health Survey 2015-16 (NFHS 4) indicates a consistent increase in obesity rates in India over the past ten years.

Overweight and obesity have become widespread issues, reaching epidemic levels in developed countries and showing a rapid increase in developing nations. These conditions are often seen as the initial indicators of a broader set of non-communicable diseases known as the “New World Syndrome,” which places a significant strain on both socioeconomic resources and public health systems, particularly in less affluent countries. Adolescence, a critical period of rapid growth and hormonal changes, heavily relies on proper nutrition. Research indicates that the prevalence of overweight among adolescents varies from 10% to 30%.[45] The rising trend of childhood obesity is particularly alarming, as it sets the stage for obesity in adulthood.[6] For instance, the rate of childhood obesity in India saw an increase from 9.8% to 11.7% between 2006 and 2009.[7] This growing trend underscores the urgent need for effective public health strategies and interventions to address this global health challenge.

Numerous studies have documented the prevalence of obesity among school children in various states of India. A pooled estimate of the obesity prevalence at the state level and for India as a whole will be beneficial to understand the magnitude and spatial distribution of the event, thereby facilitating the implementation of appropriate action strategies to prevent its progression into maturity.

METHODS

Protocol registration

The present protocol has been duly registered with PROSPERO[8] (International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews) and has been formulated in agreement with the guidelines issued by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocol (PRISMA-P). PROSPERO registration number is CRD42023431574.

Exploration approach of primary studies

Two reviewers independently searched the titles and abstracts for potentially appropriate studies and excluded unrelated studies. If discrepancies arose, they were deliberated upon or escalated to a neutral third party for a final decision. Thereafter, articles meeting the standards were retrieved in full text and reviewed independently by the two authors to make a final selection of studies for review. A comprehensive search was conducted using appropriate keywords to retrieve all relevant articles. The electronic bibliographic databases, namely Scopus, Web of Science, and PubMed, were used for the literature search. All search results were imported into Rayyan QCRI software to ensure a systematic, comprehensive search and to document the selection process. This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.

Inclusion criteria and Exclusion criteria

Types of studies

Cross-sectional studies (Jan 1995-June 2023) that provide the prevalence/proportion of obesity among school-age adolescents in India were included. Studies published in English were only considered.

Type of participants

The type of participants eligible for the study includes school-going children aged 5 to 19 years in India. There are no specific gender restrictions.

Type of outcome

The primary outcome of the study is the prevalence of obesity (general obesity).

Data extraction

Using a data extraction form, information was extracted from the research articles in a systematic format. Full-text articles from the aforementioned databases was downloaded, and the data extracted from them were entered into Microsoft Excel under the following headings: Study ID, Year of publication, Year of conduct, Prevalence (p), Sample size (n), Standard error (SE), Region/State, geographical regions namely North (N), South (S), Central (C), West (W), East (E), North East (NE) and gender of the study participants.

The search strategies [Supplementary File 1] encompass both free-text terms and medical subject headings (MeSH) and are tailored to suit the specific requirements of each individual database. The following MeSH terms were included: children, obesity, prevalence, and India.

Quality assessments

The included studies underwent quality assessment utilizing the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), which was revised for cross-sectional studies. This tool evaluates each study based on eight items, grouped into three categories: selection of the study groups, comparability of the groups, and ascertainment of either the exposure or outcome of interest. The maximum score of 9 is given to high-quality studies, and a score less than 5 indicates a weak study.[9]

Statistical analysis

The data was extracted using Microsoft Excel for management and analysis. The standard error of prevalence for each original article was calculated. Meta-analysis was employed to consolidate prevalence data. The heterogeneity between the prevalence of the studies was checked using the Cochran Q test and I2 statistic.[10] A random effects model using the restricted maximum likelihood method was used due to the presence of significant heterogeneity (I2 > 50). Results were visually represented through a forest plot, incorporating individual prevalence, pooled estimates, and 95% confidence intervals.

Meta-analysis, subgroup analysis, spatial analysis and cumulative meta-analysis, etc. were performed in STATA 16 software and R 4.3.0 software.

The proposed study conducted a spatial analysis to assess the distribution of the prevalence of obesity among school-going children in different states and union territories of India and also in different geographical regions[11] namely the North, South, Central, West, East, and Northeast. The analysis was done using choropleth maps, in which the prevalence rates within each region were georeferenced and depicted using a color scale, with higher rates represented by deeper shades of red. The number of studies included in the meta-analysis was indicated by yellow circles of varying sizes.

Subgroup analysis was done based on the state and union territories, geographical areas, and gender of the participants. Sensitivity analysis was performed by excluding studies of poor quality, that is studies with a score less than 5 in the NOS. Cumulative meta-analysis[12] was conducted by sequentially adding studies, ordered by their year of conduct of the study (provided within the square bracket of the study ID in the cumulative forest plot), to examine the temporal trends in the proportion of obesity. Potential publication bias was assessed using Egger’s test and a funnel plot.

All the articles included in this review were cross-sectional studies, with sample sizes ranging from 111 to 67,741, totaling 510,605 participants. Of the studies, 139 reported data separately for males and females, while 76 did not specify gender. The studies were conducted in various settings, including schools and hospitals. The majority of studies were conducted in South India (37.6%), followed by North India (18.4%), West India (12.8%), and Northeast India (10.4%). The East and unmentioned regions each accounted for 7.2%, while Central India had the fewest studies at 6.4%.

During the quality assessment of the included articles, the quality score ranged from 4–10 out of 10 points. Among the 125 included studies, 9 were of poor quality, 49 were of moderate quality, 48 were of high quality, and 19 were of very high quality. The characteristics and quality assessment are provided in Supplementary File 2.

Results of meta-analysis

The overall pooled prevalence of obesity among school-going children in India was found to be 6.97% (95% CI: 5.97%, 7.97%). Details are given in Supplementary File 3.

Subgroup analysis based on gender reveals that the overall pooled estimate of obesity was 6.37% (95% CI: 5.08%, 7.66%) among males; 6.38% (95% CI: 4.86%, 7.89%) among females, and 7.74% (95% CI: 5.78%, 9.69%) among a combination of both male and female participants. It is observed that there is no statistically significant difference in the pooled proportion of obesity among genders (Qb(2) = 1.50, P = 0.473). Details are given in Supplementary File 4.

Subgroup analysis was performed concerning different geographical regions of India such as Central, North, South, West, Northeast, and East, by including the studies that mentioned the geographical region. Among the different areas of India, the highest pooled prevalence was found in the Northern region, that is 8.58% (95% CI: 5.47%, 11.69%), and the lowest pooled prevalence was found in the Central region, that is 5.63% (95% CI: 3.95%, 7.31%). It is observed that there was a significant difference in the pooled estimate of prevalence among different regions of India (Qb(7) = 56.89, P < 0.001). The results of the subgroup analysis of the prevalence of obesity among school-going children based on geographical regions of India are given in Supplementary File 5.

Subgroup analysis was performed concerning different states and union territories of India by including studies that provided prevalence estimates specific to each state or union territory. Among the various states of India, the highest pooled prevalence was found in Arunachal Pradesh, that is, 17.92% (95% CI: 14.39%, 21.46%), and Delhi, that is, 13.57% (95% CI: 6.11%, 21.04%); and the lowest pooled prevalence was found in Manipur, that is, 0.80% (95% CI: 0.49%, 1.10%), which was based on a single study. It was also observed that there was a statistically significant difference in the prevalence of obesity in different states and union territories of India (Qb(26)= 417.28, P < 0.0001). The details are provided in Supplementary File 6.

Sensitivity analysis

In sensitivity analysis, the prevalence of obesity was found to be 6.69% (95% CI: 5.68%, 7.70%), which was not much different from the pooled estimate in the main analysis of 6.97% (95% CI: 5.97%, 7.97%).

The spatial distribution of the prevalence of obesity in different regions of India is done by means of spatial analysis. Cumulative meta-analysis

A cumulative meta-analysis was conducted by sequentially adding studies, ordered by the year of study (provided within the square bracket of Study ID), to investigate the temporal trends in the proportion of obesity. A total of 102 studies published during 1981˗2020 reported the year of the study and the prevalence of obesity. Based on studies from 1981 onwards, there is evidence of a growing prevalence of obesity among school-going children. Details are given in Supplementary File 7.

Publication bias was assessed using a funnel plot, which appeared asymmetrical [Supplementary File 8], suggesting potential bias. This was confirmed by Egger’s test, which yielded a P value of less than 0.001.

DISCUSSION

Childhood obesity, reaching epidemic levels in Asia, poses risks for chronic conditions such as cardiovascular diseases and Type 2 diabetes. It is vital to assess prevalence in children and strategize prevention to mitigate its persistence into adulthood.[138139] This meta-analysis aimed to examine the prevalence of obesity among school-going children in India and explore potential variations based on gender, geographical regions, and at the level of states and union territories. The results of this study explain the epidemiological landscape of childhood obesity in India, offering valuable understanding for policymakers and public health practitioners. The overall pooled estimate of obesity prevalence among school-going children in India was found to be 6.97%. Subgroup analyses based on gender revealed comparable estimates of obesity prevalence among male (6.37%) and female (6.38%) study populations, with no statistically significant difference observed among these groups. These findings suggest that obesity affects both genders similarly among school-going children in India.

Additional subgroup analyses focusing on geographical regions and states/union territories of India offered valuable perspectives on regional variations in obesity prevalence. Distinct discrepancies were noted across various geographical regions and at the level of states/union territories. The highest pooled prevalence of obesity was found in Arunachal Pradesh (17.92%) and Delhi (13.57%), while the lowest prevalence was observed in Manipur (0.80%), albeit based on limited data availability. These results highlight the importance of targeted interventions and region-specific public health policies to address the varying burden of childhood obesity across different states and union territories in India.

A sensitivity analysis was conducted by excluding studies with poor quality. The results of the sensitivity analysis reaffirmed the stability of the main findings, with a minimal difference observed in the prevalence estimates of obesity. This highlights the reliability and consistency of the pooled estimates across different methodological approaches.

In addition, the cumulative meta-analysis revealed a consistent upward trend in the prevalence of obesity among school-going children, spanning from 1981 to 2020. This temporal analysis underscores the importance of continuous monitoring and intervention efforts to address this concerning public health issue.

The strength of this study lies in its ability to offer a comprehensive analysis of obesity prevalence in India across different states/regions and genders. Through cumulative meta-analysis, it tracks the evolving trends in obesity prevalence over time. The spatial analysis reveals the geographical distribution of obesity throughout India. In future endeavors, if one can identify the risk factors associated with the prevalence of obesity in various regions and states of India, it will undoubtedly enable stakeholders to prioritize and allocate resources more effectively. However, using only cross-sectional studies in this review may limit the establishment of causal relationships due to their snapshot nature.

CONCLUSION

This meta-analysis provides valuable insights into the prevalence of obesity among school-going children in India and highlights the need for targeted public health interventions to address this growing health concern. The present study reveals that the prevalence of obesity among school-going children has increased over the years. The spatial analysis revealed that there is a significant difference in the prevalence across various regions and states of India. It highlights the multifaceted nature of obesity prevalence in India, urging targeted interventions, continuous monitoring, and sustained efforts across diverse demographic segments.

Optimizing electricity consumption in direct reduction iron processes using RSM, MLP, and RBF models
Authors: Erfan Gholamzadeh, Ahad Ghaemi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study addresses the critical challenge of optimizing energy consumption in direct reduction iron (DRI) units, a vital component of the steel industry. By utilizing operational data from a DRI unit, this research identifies and analyzes the key factors influencing energy consumption through three advanced modeling approaches: RSM, MLP, and RBF neural networks. The RSM model demonstrated strong predictive capability, achieving a coefficient of determination (R2) of 0.9879. However, the ANN models surpassed the RSM model in terms of accuracy. Among the ANN models, the MLP model exhibited the highest performance, with an R2 of 0.99601 and a MSE of 0.00037, while the RBF model achieved an R2 of 0.99336 and an MSE of 0.00062. Leveraging the optimized MLP model, this study identifies optimal operational conditions that minimize energy consumption. The findings indicate that strategic adjustments to parameters such as cooling gas flow and main burner flow can lead to substantial energy savings. Specifically, the model predicts daily savings of 60,000 kWh and annual savings of approximately 21,900,000 kWh, reflecting a 10.34% improvement in energy efficiency. These results underscore the potential of machine learning to enhance operational efficiency and sustainability in energy intensive industries, providing a robust framework for data-driven energy management strategies.Keywords: Direct reduction iron, RSM, MLP, RBF, Energy savingsSubject terms: Chemical engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Modeling and optimizing electricity consumption in the steel industry, particularly within direct reduction units, represents a critical challenge due to the sector’s substantial energy demands. Addressing this challenge is essential for enhancing energy efficiency, reducing operational costs, and minimizing environmental impact. As one of the most energy intensive industries globally, the steel sector requires effective electricity consumption management strategies to align with global sustainability goals. Accurate prediction and optimization of electricity usage are fundamental to achieving these objectives. Previous research has explored various approaches to improving energy efficiency and predicting electricity consumption in industrial applications. For instance, one study utilized historical electricity consumption data and advanced algorithms, such as time series regression and gradient descent, to optimize energy use. The results demonstrated measurable reductions in electricity consumption, contributing to enhanced sustainability1. Another study investigated energy consumption prediction in the steel industry using deep learning and machine learning techniques. By leveraging neural networks and decision trees, researchers developed predictive models that significantly outperformed traditional forecasting methods, improving prediction accuracy by up to 15%2.

Further research integrated principal component analysis with regression trees to enhance forecasting precision by eliminating redundant variables and refining input data quality. This combined approach improved prediction accuracy by approximately 12%, highlighting its value in industrial energy management3. Additionally, studies on Industry 4.0 technologies suggest that digital transformation and smart manufacturing provide new opportunities for optimizing energy consumption. Implementing Industry 4.0 solutions, such as process automation and real time monitoring, has been shown to reduce electricity demand in steel production by 10%4.

Machine learning has been extensively utilized for electricity consumption forecasting in industrial settings. Techniques such as linear regression, neural networks, and decision trees have improved monthly electricity consumption predictions, enhancing energy management strategies by achieving up to 20% better accuracy5. Even in data limited environments, artificial intelligence techniques like transfer learning and neural networks have maintained prediction accuracy, reducing the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) by 8% compared to traditional methods6. Other research has incorporated geographical and industrial context using graph based machine learning models, improving forecasting precision by 10% and optimizing energy distribution7.

Comprehensive reviews of machine learning applications in energy forecasting emphasize the advantages of these predictive techniques over traditional statistical models. While challenges such as model interpretability and selection persist, deep learning and ensemble learning approaches consistently yield the highest prediction accuracy, often reducing forecasting errors by 15% or more8. Hybrid modeling techniques, such as integrating Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks with Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) models, have also been proposed, reducing forecasting errors by 10% compared to standalone models9.

Energy efficiency improvement strategies in the steel industry have identified advancements such as waste heat recovery, process optimization, and advanced control systems, which together enhance energy efficiency by up to 20%10. Ensemble learning techniques, including Random Forest and Gradient Boosting, have demonstrated superior accuracy in electricity forecasting, with one study reporting a 12% improvement in prediction accuracy over traditional machine learning models11. Despite these advancements, challenges in sustainable energy management remain under Industry 4.0 conditions. While automation and smart energy management systems could reduce industrial electricity consumption by 15%, barriers such as high implementation costs and technological complexity must be addressed12. Considering the superior interpretability and modelling accuracy of Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KAN) compared to traditional multilayer perceptrons (MLP), recent studies have demonstrated their potential in optimizing industrial-scale energy systems, including thermal power plants. For instance, embedding the Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) into the loss function of both KAN and MLP improved predictive robustness and enabled optimization frameworks to achieve up to 42.17 ± 0.88% thermal efficiency while minimizing turbine heat rate to 7487 ± 129 kJ/kWh in a 660 MW plant13. The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into industrial operations holds significant potential for enhancing performance and supporting informed decision-making in large-scale facilities such as coal-fired power plants. Recent studies have developed AI-based modelling and optimization frameworks capable of improving plant efficiency through advanced predictive analytics and operational optimization. For instance, by training artificial neural network (ANN) models on real industrial datasets, high predictive accuracy has been achieved (R2 = 0.99, RMSE = 2.69 MW), with sensitivity analysis revealing that main steam flow rate can contribute up to 75.3% of power generation variability. Optimization using nonlinear programming has demonstrated measurable operational benefits, including fuel savings of 3 t/h, a 1.3% increase in thermal efficiency, and an annual reduction of 50.5 kt in emissions. Moreover, analyses have shown that stricter emission constraints can directly impact maximum power capacity, highlighting the trade-offs between environmental goals and energy output. These findings illustrate the role of AI-driven optimization in advancing the net-zero transition of high-emission industries by enabling both efficiency gains and emission reductions14.

Building on these existing studies, the present research aims to compare three modeling techniques RSM, RBF neural networks, and MLP neural networks for electricity consumption prediction and optimization in direct reduction units. The optimized model will then be used to predict electricity consumption under various operational scenarios, determining optimal operating points that minimize electricity usage while maintaining production efficiency. Through advanced modeling techniques, this research contributes to the development of sustainable energy management strategies in the steel industry.

Electricity consumption in the process

The direct reduction process is highly energy dependent, necessitating a precise evaluation of electricity consumption across its various stages. While natural gas primarily serves as the fuel for generating reduction gas, electricity is essential for powering mechanical equipment, control systems, and material handling systems, all of which play a critical role in maintaining operational efficiency. This section provides a detailed analysis of the primary electricity consuming components in the direct reduction process, specifically using the Midrex method15.

The electricity consumption of major equipment in the direct reduction process is influenced by factors such as gas flow rate, pressure conditions, material handling capacity, and overall operational efficiency. Identifying and understanding these factors is essential for optimizing energy use and improving plant efficiency. In the Midrex direct reduction process, the primary electricity consuming components include recirculating gas blowers, natural gas and reduction gas compressors, fluid transfer and circulation pumps, material handling systems, and control and instrumentation systems.

Recirculating gas blowers are responsible for circulating reduction gas within the reactor. After interacting with iron ore pellets and undergoing reduction reactions, the gas exits the reactor and is recirculated through the blowers for reuse in subsequent cycles. Their electricity consumption depends on the gas flow rate, system pressure drop, and equipment efficiency, typically ranging from 2 to 5 MW16. Similarly, natural gas and reduction gas compressors increase the pressure of both gases during the process. Natural gas must be pressurized before entering the reactor to facilitate effective reduction reactions, while reduction gas is recompressed after passing through various stages. The electricity consumption of these compressors is influenced by operational pressure, gas flow rate, and compressor type, typically ranging from 3 to 7 MW, with larger units potentially exceeding 10 MW17.

Fluid transfer and circulation pumps are utilized at various stages of the process, such as in cooling systems, hydraulic fluid transfer, and liquid handling. Their efficient operation is critical for maintaining temperature control and ensuring effective fluid transport. The electricity consumption of these pumps is influenced by the type of fluid, operating pressure, and pump capacity, generally ranging from 1 to 3 MW18. Material handling systems, including conveyors, mechanical elevators, and transport equipment, are essential for moving iron ore pellets and sponge iron during different process stages. Their electricity consumption depends on the material handling capacity and the physical properties of the transported materials, typically ranging from 500 kW to 2 MW19.

Control and instrumentation systems, which consist of automated monitoring and control units, industrial sensors, and data management systems, regulate plant operations and process parameters. Although their electricity consumption is relatively low (generally below 500 kW), they play a vital role in optimizing overall plant performance and maintaining process stability.

Given that the unit under review consumes between 150 and 200 million kWh of electricity annually, estimating the electricity consumption of key sections in the direct reduction process using the Midrex method helps identify major influencing factors and contributes to energy optimization within the plant.

Modeling

Response surface methodology

Response Surface Methodology (RSM) is a powerful and widely used technique for evaluating, modeling, and analyzing the effects of multiple operational variables and their interrelationships22. This method is employed across a wide range of industries, including chemical engineering, manufacturing, and process optimization, where it aids in identifying optimal parameter settings to achieve desired outcomes. Datasets derived from experimental studies are used to fit models in RSM, enabling the application of both mathematical and statistical approaches to assess complex system behaviors23. In recent years, RSM has gained significant attention due to its ability to optimize complex processes by simultaneously addressing multiple input variables. This approach provides a comprehensive analysis of the interactions between these variables, leading to more efficient process design and enhanced product quality24. One of RSM’s key advantages is its ability to reduce the number of experiments required to establish accurate models. By incorporating design of experiments methodologies, RSM maximizes the information gained from a limited set of data25.

However, RSM has certain limitations. For instance, the accuracy of the model largely depends on the experimental design, and the method may struggle to accurately model highly nonlinear or complex systems involving many variables. Additionally, the quadratic models used in RSM may not fully capture the intricate behaviors of some systems, particularly those where higher order interactions or nonlinearity beyond quadratic terms are significant. In such cases, it is essential to complement RSM with other methodologies, such as higher order quadratic models or nonlinear approaches, to ensure the robustness of the predictions26. Artificial neural networks

The Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), inspired by the structural and functional characteristics of the human brain and nervous system, has seen widespread adoption in recent decades. A neural network is a sophisticated statistical and computational model designed to replicate the patterns and behaviors of biological neural networks, enabling the simulation of various complex phenomena. The use of ANNs for data classification dates back to the 1940s, marking the beginning of their extensive application in diverse fields31.

ANNs offer numerous advantages, including exceptional processing speed for solving complex problems, the capability to model intricate relationships between input and output data, flexibility through adaptive network modifications, error handling, tolerance, and the ability to continuously learn from data. Within an ANN, neurons serve as the fundamental units of data analysis. These neurons process numerical data by accepting input signals, which are then weighted and summed. The weighted inputs are combined with a bias term before being passed through an activation function to determine the network’s output32. The goal of an ANN is to optimize the weight values (ω) associated with each input in order to accurately model the underlying function. After the inputs are weighted and combined with the bias, the resulting value is passed through a transfer function, which generates the predicted output values. This output is used to assess the model’s performance and accuracy. As represented in Eq. (2), the net input to a neuron is the sum of weighted inputs (ωi xi) along with the bias term (b). Radial basis function

Conclusion

This study focuses on modeling and optimizing energy consumption in DRI units using RSM, MLP, and RBF neural networks. Among the models evaluated, the MLP model demonstrated superior performance, achieving an R2 value of 0.99696 and a MSE of 0.0024689. The model predicted daily energy savings of 60,000 kWh and annual savings of 21,900,000 kWh, reflecting a 10.34% improvement in energy efficiency. These savings were primarily achieved through strategic adjustments to cooling gas flow and main burner flow rates. Despite the promising results, this study has some limitations. These include reliance on data from a single DRI unit and the inherent “black box” nature of the MLP model, which constrains interpretability. To overcome these challenges, future research should aim to expand datasets to encompass multiple units and incorporate interpretability techniques such as SHAP or LIME to provide deeper insights into model behavior. Furthermore, integrating real-time data analytics along with Industry 4.0 technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT) and digital twins can significantly enhance energy optimization efforts. Additionally, future investigations should incorporate cost-benefit analyses and environmental impact assessments to comprehensively evaluate the economic and ecological feasibility of the proposed strategies. These advancements would support sustainable energy management practices within the steel industry and pave the way toward more efficient and environmentally responsible operations.

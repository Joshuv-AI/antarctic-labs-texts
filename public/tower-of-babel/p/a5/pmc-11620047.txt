Mapping digital entrepreneurial intention: A comprehensive bibliometric study
Authors: Mohammad Naim Ismail, Wan Mohd Hirwani Wan Hussain
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study provides a bibliometric analysis of scholarly works from 2010 to 2023 that discuss intentions for digital entrepreneurship. The report attempts to shed light on the primary contributors, research trends, and significance of this area in relation to digitalization and entrepreneurship. Using the Scopus database, a total of 134 pertinent scholarly publications were found and examined. Beginning with a summary of the publication trends for the given time period, the bibliometric analysis is conducted. It shows a consistent increase in the quantity of papers on digital entrepreneurial intention, demonstrating the scholars' growing interest and attention in this subject. To pinpoint the main contributors and their geographic distribution, the study goes further and looks at how publications are distributed across years, authors, institutions, and nations. The analysis identifies a number of productive scholars who have made significant contributions to the field of digital entrepreneurial intention in terms of authors. The understanding and growth of digital entrepreneurship have advanced significantly thanks to their knowledge and reliable publication output. The analysis also highlights networks and collaboration between institutions and authors, demonstrating the collaborative nature of this field of research.Keywords: Digital entrepreneurial intention, Digital entrepreneurship, Bibliometric analysis, Systematic review, SCOPUS database

Introduction

The tendency or drive of people to engage in entrepreneurial activities, specifically in the digital space, is referred to as digital entrepreneurial intention (DEI). Digital entrepreneurship has emerged as a thriving and promising sector of company innovation as a result of the quick development of digital technologies and the broad adoption of online platforms [[1], [2], [3]]. Digital initiatives like online start-ups, e-commerce platforms, mobile applications, and digital service providers are established, developed, and managed in this context [4]. For scholars, policymakers, and practitioners, understanding DEI is essential because it sheds light on the factors that motivate individuals to undertake digital entrepreneurship [5]. It is feasible to pinpoint the main influences, obstacles, and factors that affect potential digital entrepreneurs' intentions to start their own businesses by looking at their underlying motivations, aspirations, and decision-making processes.

As mentioned by Ref. [6], the term DEI refers to a broad range of activities, including the exploration of various digital business opportunities, the creation of novel digital business models, the acquisition of digital skills, the exploitation of digital technologies, and the use of online platforms for customer engagement, sales, and marketing. A mindset that embraces risk-taking, innovation, adaptability, and a strong desire for independence and self-determination is also necessary [7]. The significance of DEI extends beyond individual aspirations and affects technological advancement, economic growth, and job creation at large [1]. Digital entrepreneurship has the potential to transform well-established businesses and marketplaces while simultaneously promoting economic growth in both developed and developing countries. According to Ref. [8], it can promote creativity, provide opportunities for employment, and contribute to society's overall digital transformation.

Numerous researchers have done systematic reviews of factors affecting entrepreneurial intention, including personality [9,10], individuality [11], self-efficacy [12,13], fear of failure [14], gender [15], emotional intelligence [16], innovativeness [17], family role [18], entrepreneurship education [[19], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24], [25]], and the university's environment [26,27], discussing the potential studies for future research, but without potential trends for future research. Recent systematic studies have focused on several subtopics of entrepreneurship, like sustainable entrepreneurial intention [28,29], ecological entrepreneurship [29], social entrepreneurship [30], entrepreneurship in the gig economy [3], and entrepreneurial capacity [31], however, no bibliometric analysis has been conducted on the DEI landscape. The identification of trends and changes in the study of entrepreneurial intention is also made possible by bibliometric analysis, which also sheds light on how digital technologies and entrepreneurial intent interact and how their focus is changing over time.

Researchers have tried to investigate the variables that affect the intention of engaging in digital entrepreneurship, including personality traits, environmental factors, social networks, education, and resource availability [32]. This research seeks to shed light on the factors that influence people's decisions to engage in digital entrepreneurship and to provide information for the creation of programmes, legislation, and other supports that can help create an environment that is favourable to such activity.

In this context, performing a bibliometric analysis on the subject of “DEI” can add to the body of knowledge by giving a thorough overview of the academic literature, identifying key works and contributors, and revealing new trends and knowledge gaps. As suggested by Ref. [33], bibliometric analysis can offer important insights into the current state of research, advise future research paths, and guide policy and practise in boosting digital entrepreneurship by mapping the intellectual structure of the subject and looking at patterns of publishing and citation.

Literature review

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric analysis, a quantitative research technique with a long history in the study of scientific literature, has broadened its initial focus on the productivity of individual scholars to include more uses like mapping knowledge structures, discovering research trends, and informing research policy [34,35]. Pioneers like Eugene Garfield established the groundwork for bibliometric methods in the mid-20th century by introducing citation indexing and metrics like the Science Citation Index and the Journal Impact Factor [36]. Researchers are now able to conduct more thorough analyses using co-citation analysis, bibliographic coupling, and social network analysis to better understand information flows and research communities with the help of advancements in computer capability, data accessibility, and analytical tools.

In the context of entrepreneurial intention, bibliometric analysis provides useful insights into the scholarly landscape [3,31,37]. It enables researchers to look at the variables affecting a person's desire to start a digital business, comprehend the intellectual framework of the area, and discover research gaps. Researchers are able to discover influential publications, significant contributions, and developing areas for study in the field of digital entrepreneurship by mapping citation networks and co-citation patterns [38]. In order to show the current condition of the DEI's academic structure and new trends, as described by Ref. [39], extensive bibliometric analysis was performed due to the review's vast scope, huge dataset, and involvement.

The objective of this study is to compile, identify, and assess the scholarly publications that are relevant to the topic at the point of study and listed in Scopus. When the scope of the research was vast and the dataset was too big for manual review, bibliometric analysis was utilised to summarise datasets of intellectual structure and trends in DEI. For analysing the performance of datasets, total publications (TP), total citations (TC), and average citations (AC) were employed. For the purpose of finding metrics connected to citations and publications, the following terms were used: number of cited publications (NCP), citations per cited publication (CCP), h-index (h), and g-index (g). To examine the relationship between publications and cited papers, the science mapping method of citation analysis and co-citation analysis was used. This study uses bibliometric methods to provide insights into the literature on DEI research by discovering all academic research publications that have been published in the Scopus database over the years and the trends within them. By doing this, it is feasible to comprehend what is now understood about academic research on DEI.

Methods

Bibliometrics is a science-based discipline focused on the quantitative study of papers published in a particular field of study [33]. Through bibliometric indicators, bibliometric analysis enables an analysis of the impact or influence of scholarly publications on quality or performance [23]. Similar to how bibliometric studies, which originated in the field of information science and libraries, have spread to include all study areas and are now crucial in many different fields [40]. These studies make it possible to see a thorough examination of a particular subject, providing a road map for scholars to discover newly emerging sub-fields of study and make original contributions [41]. As a result, the major goal is to offer helpful indications that highlight pertinent aspects of themes, authors, research, journals, and so forth. In order to provide a thorough review of this topic, this study has a particular focus on DEI studies.

In the beginning, the bibliometrics research method adhered to the PRISMA guidelines [42]. The Scopus Database was chosen as the data source for this study because it is widely regarded as one of the world's premier scientific citation indexes, containing high-quality research publications such as prestigious journals, books, conference proceedings, and review papers [43]. It has been identified as a primary source for academic publications and allows users to search for and discover new publications.

After selecting Scopus as the search engine, the query string ((“cyber” OR “digital∗" OR ″e-comm∗" OR “ecomm∗" OR “social media” OR “SNS”) AND (“entrepreneur∗ intent∗" OR “entrepreneur∗ determina∗" OR “entrepreneur∗ purpose” OR “entreprising intent∗" OR “buss∗ intent∗")) was employed to retrieve data from the database. Fig. 1 displays the initial results based on the PRISMA steps. The findings and analyses were then provided in the final report once the results had been synthesised and reviewed. The aim of the study is to give scholars useful information about the patterns in DEI publications, which may serve as a basis for future research and discussions aimed at broadening this field of study.

Discussion

This study was motivated by the three research questions, which are.

•How has DEI evolved and spread?

How has DEI evolved and spread?

•What are the patterns and trends in DEI research?

What are the patterns and trends in DEI research?

•Who are the highly cited researchers and institutions in the field of DEI?

Who are the highly cited researchers and institutions in the field of DEI?

In response to the first research question, it can be said that academics from around the world are eager to learn about and understand the role that digitalization plays in entrepreneurial intention [55,56]. This is due to the enormous growth in DEI field publications since 2020, the majority of which are research articles. With a total of 55 and 9 citations, respectively, Sustainability (Switzerland) and Heliyon are the top emerging journals. This brings us to the conclusion that, following the advent of the COVID-19 epidemic, digital entrepreneurship is going to receive a lot of attention, especially in educational institutions [57].

The outcomes then show patterns and trends derived from keyword analysis used in the DEI study, which is based on the second research question. Social sciences and business, management, and accounting have the broadest range of topics covered. Research on DEIs typically refers to “entrepreneurial intention,” “entrepreneurship,” “social media,” “students,” “digital entrepreneurship,” “entrepreneurial education,” and “the theory of planned behaviour.” Asian authors—primarily those from India, China, and Indonesia—are among the most often mentioned in the DEI area. It may be argued that authors from Asia have developed an interest in DEI, with the primary areas of study being business management and social sciences. Due to the assumption that students make up the majority of the population being investigated, the emerging pattern of field research on digital entrepreneurship is increasingly focused on social media usage and often references Ajzen's theory of planned behaviour. Researchers can gain a better understanding of the present state of knowledge and learn more about the trends and significant contributions to DEI research by conducting a bibliometric study. This type of analysis can help reveal areas that need more research and show where the body of knowledge is lacking [39].

The third research question then reveals that Yi-shun Wang is the most active author, followed by Shin-jeng Lin, while the most cited articles are “Digitalization of the Economy and Entrepreneurship Intention” and “Graduate Entrepreneurs: Intentions, Barriers and Solutions”, with 883 total citations for DEI's 134 papers. Following China, the United States, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, the United Kingdom, and Taiwan, India is one of the top countries for DEI research. National Changhua University of Education, Hong Kong Polytechnic University, BRAC University, Le Moyne College, National Chi Nan University, and Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University are the top research institutions in this analysis. This field offers new opportunities for young researchers to investigate digital entrepreneurship by looking at the emerging topics of the most active scholars mentioned, as well as the nations and top institutions that have an interest in DEI, with the possibility of more citations for a published paper in an emerging DEI trend.

This extensive bibliometric analysis sheds light on the changing field of digital entrepreneurship and indicates a notable increase in interest in comprehending the relationship between digitization and entrepreneurial intention. This growing interest—which has been especially noticeable since the COVID-19 pandemic—highlights the growing dependence of entrepreneurial activities on digital platforms [58]. The findings of the study demonstrate a broadening of both geography and theme in DEI research, with notable contributions from Asian academics, a focus on entrepreneurial education, and the critical role of social media. These findings are consistent with the global trend towards innovative digital business models and the need for a comprehensive approach to digital entrepreneurship platforms [59]. Moreover, the identification of prominent scholars and organisations driving the DEI conversation not only enhances the current scholarly landscape but also indicates promising directions for future research. The predominance of concepts like “social media,” “entrepreneurial intention,” and “the theory of planned behaviour” indicates a critical intersection between the discourse on digital entrepreneurship and decision-making processes and DEI. This convergence promotes an integrated research agenda that fills in the gaps in the literature about entrepreneurial decision-making identified by Ref. [60] and connects DEI studies with the broad field of digital entrepreneurship. Based on the ground-breaking research of [61], this study takes a sophisticated look at DEI's critical role in the game-changing dynamics of digital entrepreneurship. The need for a comprehensive understanding of digital entrepreneurial ecosystems and the influence of digital technologies on entrepreneurial intent is echoed by the trends and prominent people in the sector that have been discovered. This convergence with academic demands for an all-encompassing framework for digital entrepreneurship highlights the need for empirical studies into the processes underlying digital entrepreneurial intentions, which in turn promotes a more thorough integration of DEI research into the larger field of digital entrepreneurship studies. By providing this synthesis, the study advances the scholarly conversation and lays the foundation for further investigations that tackle the intricacies of digital entrepreneurship in a globalised society.

The forward-looking agenda emphasises the need to identify how digital ecosystems either support or obstruct entrepreneurial aspirations and calls for a comprehensive investigation into these ecosystems. The focus of these studies needs to be on determining the essential elements and obstacles to success, as well as the diverse effects of innovation and technology adoption on entrepreneurial goals. This calls for an in-depth comprehension of the role emerging technologies play in forming and developing digital businesses, underpinned by empirical research that explores the subtleties of technology's impact on the emergence and development of entrepreneurship. In addition to conducting empirical investigations, our framework, which draws inspiration from influential studies like [62,63], offers a comprehensive analysis of the impact of digital platforms and technology on entrepreneurial intent. This entails examining the function of digital ecosystems and platforms in various economic and cultural contexts, therefore broadening our knowledge of the global environment around DEI.

It is essential to do a thorough analysis of the policy and educational contexts that influence digital entrepreneurship and its goals. This area of the research agenda looks for solutions for effectively fostering and assisting digital entrepreneurial ecosystems. Further research should focus on how digital businesses can overcome societal obstacles while maintaining ethical standards, as the societal effects and ethical aspects of digital entrepreneurship call for a closer look. The integration of entrepreneurial decision-making theories [60] with DEI research can provide novel insights into the ways in which digital entrepreneurs navigate uncertainty and arrive at strategic decisions. The development of a thorough understanding of DEI in the context of digital technology depends on this integration. Comparative research across cultural and institutional contexts will also shed light on various DEI practices, outcomes, and policies, providing the subject with a global perspective.

Conclusion

It is crucial to do a bibliometric study of DEI research since it provides a complete and accurate assessment of the research that has been done. It can be used by researchers to identify the most important papers and research topics, as well as the most important authors, groups, and countries involved in DEI research. By studying publication patterns and trends, bibliometrics can assist in identifying knowledge gaps and prospective study areas [65].

The increasing number of publications on DEI indicates a growing interest in and recognition of the importance of digital entrepreneurship in policy agendas. Policymakers should prioritise supporting and promoting digital entrepreneurship initiatives to foster economic growth and innovation. The focus on specific subject areas, such as business, management, and accounting, suggests the need for targeted policies and programmes to support entrepreneurship education and training in these fields. This can include curriculum development, funding for entrepreneurship programs, and support for entrepreneurial ecosystems. The prominence of keywords related to students and entrepreneurship education highlights the importance of integrating entrepreneurship education into academic curricula at all levels. As opposed by Ref. [66], policymakers should consider incorporating digital entrepreneurship education into school and university programmes to nurture entrepreneurial skills and mindsets among students. The presence of keywords related to social media and digital entrepreneurship indicates the need for policies that support the use of digital platforms and technologies for entrepreneurial activities. Policymakers should create an enabling environment for digital entrepreneurship, including access to digital infrastructure, supportive regulations, and funding opportunities.

In conducting the bibliometric study of DEI, several inherent biases must be acknowledged to enhance the robustness of the findings. Primarily, the exclusive use of the Scopus database introduces a selection bias by omitting significant publications accessible through other databases like Web of Science or Google Scholar, potentially skewing the literature's representativeness. Furthermore, the specificity of the search terms and criteria may exclude relevant studies that do not precisely align with the predefined keywords, potentially overlooking unconventional or emerging contributions to the field. The strict inclusion criteria and data extraction guidelines further limit the diversity of considered studies, excluding important interdisciplinary insights that could enrich our understanding of DEI. Moreover, the reliance on standard bibliometric indicators and citation analysis tends to favour well-established studies, which could marginalise newer, yet-to-be-recognised innovative ideas. Addressing these limitations is crucial for a more comprehensive and representative analysis of the DEI landscape.

The identified patterns and trends in DEI research can guide future research directions. The highly cited articles identified in the study can serve as a starting point for researchers to build upon existing knowledge and contribute to the field. Researchers can further investigate the topics covered in these articles, replicate studies in different contexts, and explore new dimensions and factors influencing digital entrepreneurship. The study highlights the need for more research on the impact of digitalization on entrepreneurial activities, the role of education in fostering entrepreneurial skills, and the influence of psychological and behavioural factors on entrepreneurial intentions and actions. Future research can delve deeper into these areas to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamics of digital entrepreneurship [67].

In conclusion, the implications for policy and research indicate the significance of fostering and supporting digital entrepreneurship initiatives, incorporating entrepreneurship education into academic curricula, establishing a supportive environment for digital entrepreneurship, and concentrating on particular subject areas and research topics to advance knowledge in the field of DEI. These implications might serve as a roadmap for scholars and policymakers as they develop objectives for policies, programmes and research that support digital entrepreneurship.

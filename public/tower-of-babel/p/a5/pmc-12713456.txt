Liposomal and Nanomaterial-Based Strategies for Targeted Alzheimer’s Disease Therapy
Authors: Balajothi Balagurusamy, Vinodha Ganesan, Vidya Gopi, Pradeep Ilayaperumal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) remains a major neurodegenerative disorder with limited therapeutic options. Liposomal drug delivery has emerged as a promising strategy to enhance drug bioavailability and targeted delivery across the blood–brain barrier. This review explores the role of liposomes and nanomaterials in AD therapy, focusing on their versatility for drug delivery, including intranasal formulations, gene therapy, and reactive oxygen species (ROS)-responsive systems. Various liposomal formulations, such as mannose-modified, antibody-targeted, exosome-like, and biomaterial-based carriers, have shown significant potential in improving therapeutic efficacy. Natural compound-loaded liposomes, including polyphenols, tannic acid, and plant extracts, offer neuroprotective benefits. Furthermore, the inhibition of amyloid-β (Aβ) aggregation, a key pathological feature of AD, is addressed through innovative liposomal approaches, including peptide-conjugated, chiral-modified, and transferrin-targeted liposomes. This review highlights the synergistic role of glymphatic clearance and microglial phagocytosis in reducing the amyloid burden. Liposomal-based strategies are promising for advancing AD treatment by improving drug stability, specificity, and brain-targeting efficiency.

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline and memory loss. The development of effective therapeutic strategies for AD is a major challenge in modern medicine represented in Scheme . Nanotechnology, particularly the use of liposomes as drug delivery vehicles, has emerged as one of the most versatile and widely studied nanocarrier systems for drug delivery. These spherical vesicles, composed of one or more phospholipid bilayers, offer several advantages that make them attractive for therapeutic applications. Notably, liposomes exhibit excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability, and they are capable of encapsulating both hydrophilic and hydrophobic therapeutic agents. Furthermore, their surfaces can be readily modified to achieve targeted delivery and prolonged circulation times, enhancing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity. The approval and widespread use of several liposomal drug formulations underscore the clinical relevance of liposomes. For example, Doxil (liposomal doxorubicin) and AmBisome (liposomal amphotericin B) have been successfully integrated into clinical practice, demonstrating improved pharmacokinetics and reduced adverse effects compared to their conventional counterparts. These examples highlight the translational potential of liposomal systems and justify the continued focus on their development and optimization. Given these advantages and their proven clinical success, liposomes have become a central focus in the field of drug delivery research. Liposomes offer versatility in delivering therapeutic agents, including small hydrophobic molecules, natural compounds, and gene therapy agents, directly to the brain, thereby enhancing efficacy and reducing the side effects of treatments. This review explores the advances in liposomal strategies for AD therapy, focusing on the design and development of liposomes as versatile nanocarriers, their role in targeting neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, and their potential in inhibiting Aβ aggregation. Additionally, it examines innovative liposomal formulations, including antibody-targeted and mannose-modified liposomes, and discusses the noninvasive delivery methods and advanced characterization techniques employed in AD therapy. The exploration of natural compounds and novel liposomal systems further highlights the potential of these nanocarriers in treating AD.

Challenges in Targeted Therapy for AD

AD presents multiple challenges to effective targeted therapy due to its complex and progressive nature. One of the main obstacles is the blood–brain barrier (BBB), a protective membrane that blocks most drugs from entering the brain, limiting the effectiveness of many therapeutic agents. In addition, AD involves several interlinked pathological processes such as amyloid-β plaque accumulation, tau protein tangles, oxidative stress, inflammation, and neuronal degeneration. This multifactorial pathology means that targeting a single pathway often yields limited results, highlighting the need for multitargeted or combination therapies. The problem is further compounded by the fact that AD is usually diagnosed at a later stage, when significant and irreversible brain damage has already occurred, leaving a narrow window for effective treatment. Furthermore, many AD-targeting drugs can cause off-target effects and toxicity, especially when used long-term. Patient-specific factors such as genetics (e.g., APOE ε4 status) and variability in drug metabolism also influence treatment outcomes, making personalized approaches more important yet difficult to implement. Conventional drugs often suffer from poor solubility, rapid clearance, and low brain bioavailability, requiring higher doses and increasing side effects. Moreover, many promising therapies that show success in animal studies fail during human clinical trials due to differences in physiology and drug response. These combined challenges underline the urgent need for advanced delivery platforms: such as liposomes and nanomaterials; that can enhance drug targeting, improve brain delivery, and support sustained, multifunctional treatment strategies for AD.

Versatile Nanocarriers for Drug Delivery

The critical role of these nanocarriers in penetrating the BBB is a significant factor in treating neurodegenerative disorders, including AD and Parkinson’s disease (PD). The ability of nanotechnology to enhance drug delivery to targeted central nervous system (CNS) regions while minimizing systemic toxicity represents a major advancement in AD treatment. Innovative AD treatment approaches have emphasized magneto-regulated strategies that use magnetic nanoparticles for precise and remote therapy. This approach, alongside biological and physical therapies, highlights the potential for significant advancements in AD treatment through controlled external magnetic fields.

Liposomal formulations have also been designed for CNS disorders, demonstrating enhanced drug solubility and permeability across the BBB while significantly reducing infarct volumes. Surface modifications of liposomes with peptides, antibodies, and RNA aptamers facilitate targeted delivery to brain receptors, offering a novel approach for disease-modifying treatments in neurodegenerative diseases. Additionally, nanotechnology plays a crucial role in improving AD management by utilizing nanoparticles with enhanced magnetic and optical properties for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes.

Nanoscavenger approaches, such as the use of high-density lipoprotein (HDL) to address microglial dysfunction in AD, have been explored. By combining phosphatidic acid-functionalized HDL with curcumin and siRNA targeting β-site APP cleavage enzyme 1 (siBACE1), these nanocarriers penetrate the BBB to target Aβ plaques, effectively reversing memory deficits and neuroinflammation in AD models. Therapeutic nanosweepers designed to target Aβ deposits leverage neutrophils for enhanced BBB penetration and Aβ removal.

The global burden of neurological disorders necessitates the development of novel therapeutic strategies that can overcome the limitations of current treatments, particularly in crossing the BBB. Nanotechnology offers a promising solution through the use of nanoscale materials like quantum dots, dendrimers, metallic nanoparticles, polymeric nanoparticles, carbon nanotubes, liposomes, and micelles, which enhance drug delivery, reduce toxicity, and improve therapeutic efficacy in neurological disorders. Multifunctional nanoenabled delivery systems (NDS) have been developed to target multiple pathogenic factors of AD simultaneously, offering the potential for enhanced therapeutic efficacy and diagnostic precision. ,

Lipid nanocarriers and brain-targeted liposomal nanoparticles enhance bioavailability and direct brain delivery, bypassing the systemic circulation and first-pass effect. This approach is crucial for improving the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of AD therapies, ultimately reducing side effects and enhancing therapeutic outcomes. Recent developments include multifunctional nanoliposomes designed to enhance BBB and cell membrane penetration. For instance, nanoliposomes encapsulating polyethylenimine/miR-195 have shown improved cognitive function in AD mouse models, surpassing the efficacy of donepezil and demonstrating comparable results to aducanumab. Combining these nanoliposomes with donepezil further improved cognitive outcomes and reduced AD biomarkers.

Lipid-based nanoparticles encapsulating curcumin have been explored to address Aβ accumulation and enhance cognition in animal models. Curcumin’s potential in reducing oxidative stress further supports its therapeutic role. Hybrid cell membrane-coated nanoparticles targeting neuroinflammatory lesions have also demonstrated significant reductions in amyloid plaque deposition and cognitive impairments.

Noninvasive methods, such as liposome-coated nanoparticles, are promising for BBB drug delivery. For instance, liposome-packaged ligustilide (LIG) presents a significant breakthrough in AD research, demonstrating its potential in mitigating oxidative stress, preserving mitochondrial function, and reducing β-amyloid (Aβ) deposition in APP/PS1 mouse models. Oxidative stress, a primary contributor to AD pathology, leads to metabolic imbalance, protein misfolding, and neuroinflammation, with mitochondria serving as both a key source and target of reactive oxygen species (ROS). The study highlights LIG’s ability to restore mitochondrial fission–fusion balance, prevent structural damage, and enhance neuroprotection via the PKA/AKAP1 signaling pathway. By reducing ROS-induced damage and promoting antioxidant responses, LIG not only alleviates cognitive impairments but also influences crucial pathways involved in amyloidogenesis and synaptic integrity. These findings provide strong experimental evidence that LIG-loaded liposomes (LIG-LPs) offer a promising therapeutic strategy for AD, leveraging both their antioxidant properties and mitochondrial-targeting mechanisms to counteract neurodegeneration. Despite these promising outcomes, certain limitations must be acknowledged, including the need for further validation in clinical settings and long-term safety assessments of LIG-LPs in humans. While the study establishes a strong link between mitochondrial dysfunction and AD progression, additional research is required to explore whether LIG’s effects extend to other neurodegenerative pathways, such as tau pathology and neuroinflammation. Moreover, the specificity of PKA/AKAP1 signaling in mediating LIG’s neuroprotective effects warrants further mechanistic studies. Future directions should focus on optimizing LIG-LPs’ bioavailability, evaluating their efficacy in diverse AD models, and investigating potential combinatory therapies with other antioxidant or amyloid-targeting agents. Additionally, exploring the epigenetic influence of LIG on neurodegenerative processes could uncover novel therapeutic targets, further strengthening its role in AD treatment.

In summary, the integration of nanotechnology in AD therapy offers a multifaceted approach to overcoming the challenges associated with drug delivery across the BBB. The advancements in nanocarrier design and functionality hold significant promise for improving the efficacy and safety of AD treatments, paving the way for innovative therapeutic strategies that could revolutionize the management of neurodegenerative diseases. As the field progresses, it is imperative to address the challenges associated with the scalability and reproducibility of these nanotechnologies to ensure their successful translation into clinical applications.

Liposomal Delivery of Small Hydrophobic Molecules

AD presents a complex challenge due to its multifaceted etiology, necessitating novel therapeutic strategies. One of the most promising advances in recent years has been the development of microRNA (miRNA)-based therapies, particularly miR-195, which is known to regulate multiple AD-associated genes. A recent study introduced nanoliposomes encapsulating polyethylenimine (PEI)/miR-195 complexes (DPMT@PEI/miR-195), engineered with p-aminophenyl-alpha-d-mannopyranoside (MAN) and cationic cell-penetrating peptide (TAT). These modifications significantly enhanced the ability of the liposomes to cross the BBB and penetrate neuronal membranes, addressing one of the primary obstacles in RNA-based therapeutics.

In preclinical trials, DPMT@PEI/miR-195 exhibited remarkable efficacy in APP/PS1 transgenic mice, an established AD model. When administered intravenously, this novel formulation demonstrated superior cognitive benefits compared to donepezil and effects comparable to aducanumab, a monoclonal antibody therapy (shown in Figure ). Additionally, the combination of DPMT@PEI/miR-195 with donepezil provided enhanced neuroprotective effects, particularly in older mice, highlighting its potential synergy with existing AD treatments. Importantly, this therapeutic approach effectively reduced pathological markers such as Aβ plaques, phosphorylated tau (AT8), and microglial activation (CD68), without notable adverse effects.

Intranasal Delivery and ROS-Responsive Formulations

Innovative liposomal formulations, such as intranasal codelivery systems, have been introduced to tackle AD. For example, the study demonstrates that KLVFF@LIP-CeO2, a multifunctional liposomal nanocarrier, effectively inhibits Aβ aggregation and mitigates oxidative stress, the two major pathological hallmarks of AD. The system leverages the Aβ-targeting KLVFF peptide to disrupt fibril formation while utilizing ceria enzymes (CeO2) to scavenge ROS, reducing neuronal toxicity. Characterization studies confirmed the stability, biocompatibility, and efficient intracellular uptake of the formulation, while in vitro assays showed significant reductions in ROS levels and Aβ-induced neuronal apoptosis. The intranasal delivery approach facilitated direct brain accumulation, enhancing bioavailability and minimizing systemic toxicity.

In vivo, evaluation using APP/PS1 transgenic AD mice revealed that KLVFF@LIP-CeO2 significantly improved cognitive function reduced Aβ plaque deposition, and alleviated oxidative stress. Behavioral tests confirmed enhanced memory and learning abilities, while histopathological analysis demonstrated reduced neuronal damage. These findings suggest that these liposomal nanoplatforms offer a synergistic and noninvasive therapeutic strategy for AD treatment. The study underscores the clinical potential of multitargeted nanomedicine in addressing neurodegenerative disorders and highlights intranasal drug delivery as a promising alternative for enhancing brain-targeted therapies.

Additionally, lithospermic acid B (LA) has demonstrated significant potential in restoring mitochondrial function in AD by coactivating mitophagy and mitochondrial biogenesis through the PINK1/LC3B/P62 and PGC-1α/Nrf2 pathways. However, its clinical translation has been hindered due to its hydrophilic nature, necessitating an efficient delivery system. To address this, a dual-targeting liposomal formulation (MT-LIP@LA) was developed, utilizing d-mannosamine-cholesterol and Tet1-functionalized DSPE-PEG2000 for targeted brain and neuronal delivery. This formulation exhibited a remarkable 4.3-fold increase in brain accumulation compared to conventional liposomes and successfully localized to neurons at AD lesion sites. Mechanistic studies revealed that the enhanced brain uptake was facilitated by mannose-mediated binding to GLUT1 at the blood–brain barrier and caveolin-Golgi pathway trafficking. By improving mitochondrial health, MT-LIP@LA effectively prevented neurodegeneration and cognitive decline in a 3 × Tg-AD mouse model.

The study further elucidated the synthesis, characterization, and in vivo behavior of MT-LIP@LA, confirming its ability to target and restore neuronal mitochondria. The optimized liposomal formulation was prepared using reverse evaporation technology, ensuring stable encapsulation of LA. In vivo, imaging and intracellular trafficking studies validated its superior uptake and retention within brain cells. Moreover, central neuron targeting studies confirmed MT-LIP@LA’s colocalization with neuronal markers, demonstrating its specificity and efficiency in delivering LA to diseased neurons. These findings provide a promising strategy for enhancing mitochondrial function in AD and offer a potential therapeutic avenue for neurodegenerative disorders requiring precise brain-targeted drug delivery.

Innovative Liposomal Systems for Gene Therapy

Gene therapy has emerged as a transformative approach for treating complex neurological diseases like AD, where multifaceted pathological pathways hinder single-drug treatments. Recent advancements in liposomal nanotechnology offer promising strategies for the targeted, synergistic delivery of nucleic acids across the BBB. A notable innovation is the development of biomimetic exosome–liposome hybrid nanovesicles (TSEL) for dual gene delivery in AD therapy. These vesicles codeliver BACE1 siRNA and TREM2 plasmid DNA to simultaneously downregulate Aβ production and reprogram microglial phenotypes from pro-inflammatory (M1) to anti-inflammatory (M2). The system exploits the natural targeting ability of exosomes and the BBB-penetrating capacity of angiopep-2 peptides. In vivo studies in APP/PS1 mice demonstrated significant cognitive improvements and reduced Aβ deposition, underscoring the potential of TSEL as a synergistic gene therapy platform.

Complementing this, Raman Spectroscopy (RS) has been proposed as a powerful tool for the biochemical characterization of functionalized liposomes. RS enables rapid, label-free analysis of liposomal components, assessing their composition, stability, reproducibility, and functionalization using minimal sample volumes. This technique supports quality control and formulation optimization of liposome-based nanomedicines, crucial for translational success. Together, these innovations in liposomal design and characterization pave the way for robust, targeted gene therapy approaches in neurodegenerative disease treatment and beyond.

Furthermore, treatment with Ang-Sal/Ica-Lip in APP/PS1 transgenic mouse models led to marked improvements in synaptic health, reduced neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress, and restored cognitive function.

These findings highlight the therapeutic promise of integrating natural compounds with brain-targeted liposomal systems, offering a multipronged approach to alleviate AD pathology and improve patient outcomes. Mannose-Modified Liposomes

The recent nanomedicine approaches have focused on mannose (MAN)-modified liposomes designed to actively target the BBB through glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1), a membrane protein overexpressed in brain endothelial cells. In a novel strategy, curcumin (CUR) and ginsenoside Rb1 (Rb1), two neuroprotective natural compounds with poor bioavailability were coencapsulated into MAN-modified liposomes (MAN-CUR/Rb1). These liposomes demonstrated optimal size (∼110 nm), structural stability, and efficient BBB penetration both in in vitro BBB models and in vivo imaging studies. Enhanced uptake by neuronal (N2a) and endothelial (bEnd.3) cells confirmed the targeting efficacy.

Furthermore, in vivo studies in APP/PS-1 transgenic mice, a model of AD, revealed that MAN-CUR/Rb1 liposomes significantly improved cognitive function, reduced oxidative stress, inhibited neuroinflammation, and prevented neuronal apoptosis (shown in Figure ). This indicates their promising therapeutic potential. While the current findings are limited to preclinical models, this work highlights mannose-functionalized liposomes as a promising brain-targeted nanodelivery system for AD and potentially other CNS disorders, warranting further translational research.

Transferrin-Modified Osthole Liposomes

A drug delivery system using transferrin-modified PEGylated liposomes to transport osthole, a natural compound with neuroprotective properties across BBB. The goal was to improve treatment for AD. The liposomes showed an enhanced ability to cross the BBB and target the brain in a mouse model of AD (APP/PS-1 mice). Results indicated improved drug absorption in brain cells, better pharmacokinetics, reduced Aβ plaque accumulation, and improved cognitive function in mice. The findings suggest that this nanocarrier system could be a promising strategy for treating AD.

Transferrin-Pep63 Liposomes (Tf-Pep63-Lip)

Targeting the multifaceted pathology of AD, Transferrin-Pep63 liposomes (Tf-Pep63-Lip) represent a novel multifunctional nanotherapeutic designed for early intervention. These liposomes integrate transferrin for BBB targeting, phosphatidic acid (PA) for Aβ interaction, and the neuroprotective peptide Pep63.

Tf-Pep63-Lip effectively crosses the BBB and delivers Pep63 into the brain. In APP/PS1 AD mouse models, treatment significantly reduced Aβ burden in the hippocampus, rescued impaired synaptic plasticity, and improved cognitive performance in behavioral tests. Mechanistically, Tf-Pep63-Lip achieved this through the inhibition of Aβ1–42 aggregation; disaggregation of existing Aβ fibrils; enhanced microglial chemotaxis and phagocytosis; restoration of NMDA receptor trafficking via Pep63-mediated blockade of Aβ–EphB2 interaction; importantly, no adverse effects were observed in either AD or wild-type mice.

Tf-Pep63-Lip is a promising therapeutic strategy for AD, offering a combination of targeted delivery, Aβ modulation, and synaptic repair. Its safety and multifunctionality make it a strong candidate for further preclinical and clinical development in AD combination therapy.

CXCR4-Modified Liposomes

Osthole (Ost), a natural coumarin compound, has shown potential in treating AD, but its clinical use is limited due to poor solubility, low bioavailability, and difficulty crossing the BBB. To overcome these challenges, researchers developed CXCR4-modified liposomes loaded with Osthole (CXCR4-Ost-Lips) to enhance brain delivery and therapeutic effects. In this study, the surface of the liposomes was modified with a CXCR4-targeting peptide, which helps direct the drug to the brain. When tested in APP-SH-SY5Y cells (a cellular model of AD), the modified liposomes showed improved cellular uptake and protected the cells from damage. In animal studies, CXCR4-Ost-Lips increased Ost accumulation in the brain, prolonged drug circulation time, and improved cognitive function in AD mouse models. The CXCR4-modified liposomes offer a promising delivery system for Osthole, potentially improving its effectiveness as a treatment for AD.

ApoE2-Encoding Plasmid DNA Liposomes

Delivering genes to the brain has long been a challenge in treating neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s. In this work, dual-functionalized liposomes, named RVGMAN and PenMAN, were designed to carry a therapeutic gene called ApoE2, which is known to offer protective effects against Alzheimer’s. These liposomes encapsulate ApoE2 plasmid DNA complexed with chitosan, enhancing both protection from enzymatic breakdown and transport across the BBB. Using a lab-based BBB model, showed that these liposomes significantly improved gene transfer into brain cells and enabled strong ApoE2 expression. Importantly, in vivo experiments in mice confirmed that the liposomes efficiently delivered the gene to the brain without causing toxicity or inflammation, demonstrating excellent biocompatibility. These results suggest that RVGMAN and PenMAN liposomes offer a safe, effective, and brain-targeted gene delivery platform, paving the way for future studies in Alzheimer’s mouse models to assess their ability to reduce amyloid beta buildup, a hallmark of the disease.

Antibody-Targeted Liposomes

The antibody-targeted liposomes utilizing receptor-mediated transcytosis (RMT) have shown considerable promise for transporting therapeutics into the central nervous system (CNS). A recent innovation involves decorating liposomes with the scFv46.1 antibody, which selectively binds to both human and murine BBB endothelial cells and can cross the BBB via transcytosis. These scFv46.1-functionalized liposomes (46.1-Lipo) demonstrated enhanced uptake in vitro BBB models and significantly increased brain-specific accumulation in vivo following intravenous injection. Notably, pharmacokinetic and biodistribution analyses showed that 46.1-Lipo carrying pralidoxime achieved up to a 10-fold increase in brain delivery compared to nontargeted controls. This selective targeting of brain vasculature underscores the potential of antibody–liposome conjugates for site-specific delivery of CNS-active agents. While further studies are required to determine the system’s ability to transport therapeutic doses effectively, these findings position antibody-targeted liposomes as a versatile and promising platform for brain-targeted drug delivery.

Liposomes loaded with polyphenol-rich grape pomace extracts have shown promising neuroprotective effects in a rotenone-induced in vitro model of PD, a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by oxidative stress and limited treatment options. This work highlights the potential of using grape pomace-based antioxidant liposomes not only as a neuroprotective strategy but also as a sustainable, circular solution for utilizing agricultural waste, with future research aimed at exploring their anti-inflammatory effects, particularly in microglial cytokine modulation.

A multifunctional liposome platform incorporating self-assembled cyclic d,l-α-peptide (CP-2) has been developed to target early toxic amyloid β oligomers (AβOs). Lactoferrin-Modified Liposomes

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a severe brain condition with high rates of death and disability, mainly due to brain inflammation. Diosmetin, a natural flavonoid, has shown protective effects in brain injury but is limited by poor brain delivery. To improve its therapeutic potential, researchers developed lactoferrin-modified, long-circulating liposomes loaded with diosmetin (Lf-Dios-Lcl). These specialized liposomes are designed to cross the BBB and enhance diosmetin’s delivery and availability in brain tissue. In lab studies using immune cells (BV2 microglial cells), Lf-Dios-Lcl reduced harmful proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1β and TNF-α), increased beneficial anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10), and promoted microglial cells to shift into a protective, anti-inflammatory M2 state. Overall, Lf-Dios-Lcl showed strong brain-targeting ability and reduced neuroinflammation, suggesting its promise as an effective treatment for ICH by regulating immune responses in the brain. ,

Hydroxy-α-sanshool Liposomes

Hydroxy-α-sanshool (HAS) is a natural compound known to improve cognitive function, but its poor stability limits its use in clinical treatments. To overcome this, researchers developed liposomes loaded with hydroxy-α-sanshool (HAS-LPs) for intranasal delivery, aiming to improve its stability and effectiveness for treating AD. HAS-LPs were prepared using the film dispersion method with a specific ratio of ingredients (HAS:cholesterol:soybean lecithin = 1:4:16). The resulting liposomes had a uniform size, good stability, and a sustained release profile following the Higuchi model, which is commonly used to describe drug release from liposomes. Importantly, these liposomes were found to be safe for nasal administration, showing no damage to the nasal mucosa. To test their effects, the liposomes were administered intranasally to mice with memory impairments caused by d-galactose. Behavioral tests, including passive avoidance and the water maze, showed that HAS-LPs improved learning and memory in these Alzheimer’s-like mice. Pathological analysis also revealed that HAS-LPs helped protect brain neurons from damage. Compared to free HAS, the liposomal formulation led to better accumulation of the compound in both blood and brain tissue, indicating effective brain-targeting ability. Overall, HAS-LPs not only improve the delivery and stability of hydroxy-α-sanshool but also show strong potential as a treatment to enhance memory and protect brain function in AD.

The study concludes that liposomal DHA–PC is more effective than dietary DHA–PC in improving cognitive function and mitigating AD-related pathology in SAMP8 mice. The enhanced efficacy is likely due to improved absorption, stability, and targeted delivery to the brain via the liposomal nanocarrier. This research highlights the potential of lipid-based nanocarriers for delivering omega-3 fatty acids like DHA to the brain, suggesting a promising approach for AD prevention or treatment. Liposomal formulations may overcome dietary limitations and provide enhanced therapeutic benefits in neurodegenerative disorders.

Luteolin-Loaded Bilosomes

The optimized bilosomes exhibited favorable physicochemical characteristics: a nanoscale size (∼153 nm), high entrapment efficiency (∼70%), and sustained drug release (∼80% over 8 h). Rivastigmine Liposomes

The inhibition of acetylcholinesterase (AChE) by phenolic compounds, with a focus on comparing different AChE isoforms, including human AChE (hAChE), electric eel AChE (eeAChE), and a mutant human AChE (dAChE4). A key aspect of the research is the development of a nanodisc-based membrane model, which better mimics the natural, membrane-bound state of AChE. Liposome-Based Strategies for Alzheimer’s Disease Therapy

Functionalized Liposomes for Antiamyloid Activity

Menthol-Modified Quercetin Liposomes (Men-Qu-Lips)

Liposomal formulations represent a promising strategy for AD treatment, demonstrating the ability to cross the blood–brain barrier, reduce oxidative stress and neuroinflammation, and enhance cognitive function in aged models. Liposomal delivery systems have also been explored for brain targeting, gene therapy, and cancer treatment. Modifications incorporating peptides and ligands, such as phosphatidic acid and curcumin, have been employed to improve targeting and inhibit Aβ aggregation. These findings highlight the potential of liposomes as multifunctional carriers for AD therapy.

Astaxanthin-Loaded Liposomal Nanoparticles (PEG-ATX@NPs)

Astaxanthin (ATX), a potent antioxidant, has been encapsulated within liposomal nanoparticles (PEG-ATX@NPs) to enhance its solubility and therapeutic efficacy. ATX exhibits the ability to scavenge formaldehyde (FA), a compound known to promote Aβ aggregation, a key factor in AD progression. In APP/PS1 mouse models, PEG-ATX@NPs have been shown to reduce Aβ deposition, decrease oxidative stress markers, and improve cognitive function. These findings highlight the potential of liposomal ATX formulations as a promising therapeutic strategy for AD.

Caffeic Acid-Loaded Liposomes with Transferrin Modification

Caffeic acid (CA)-loaded liposomes modified with transferrin (Tf) have been developed to enhance brain targeting and improve therapeutic efficacy. CA exhibits antiamyloidogenic, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, but its chemical stability and bioavailability present challenges. The Tf-functionalized liposomes, optimized for size and stability, efficiently encapsulated CA and demonstrated sustained release properties. In vitro studies showed their ability to prevent Aβ aggregation, inhibit fibril formation, and disaggregate mature fibrils, highlighting their potential as a promising strategy for AD therapy.

Chiral Aspartic Acid-Modified Liposomes (l- and d-Asp-DPPE Liposomes)

Chiral aspartic acid-modified liposomes (l- and d-Asp-DPPE) have been designed to target Aβ aggregation in AD. Both l- and d-liposomes have shown efficacy in mitigating Aβ-induced pathology, with d-liposomes exhibiting stronger therapeutic effects. In APPswe/PS1d9 mouse models, d-liposomes demonstrated significant improvements in cognitive function and a reduction in Aβ deposition. These findings highlight the potential of chiral liposomal formulations as a promising therapeutic strategy for AD.

2-Benzothiazolyl-Decorated Liposomes

The development of multifunctional liposomal systems for AD has led to the investigation of 2-benzothiazole-decorated liposomes as potent theragnostic agents. Among these, liposomes decorated with 2-(4′-aminophenyl)­benzothiazole (AP-BTH) have shown exceptional potential.

PEGylated liposomes incorporating AP-BTH-lipid at 5% and 10% concentrations demonstrated superior physicochemical stability, serum protein integrity, and long-term storage stability. Notably, these AP-BTH-liposomes effectively inhibited Aβ1–42 fibril formation, as confirmed by circular dichroism and Thioflavin T assays, unlike their unsubstituted counterpart (BTH-LIP), which had no inhibitory effect. Further, the 5% AP-BTH-LIP exhibited strong interactions with brain endothelial cells and significant transport across an in vitro BBB model, highlighting their brain-targeting capabilities.

The dual functionality of AP-BTH-liposomesantiamyloid activity and BBB permeability position them as promising candidates for targeted Alzheimer’s therapy. In particular, 5% AP-BTH-LIP emerges as a stable, effective, and brain-accessible system, warranting in vivo validation for AD theragnostics.

Synergistic Role of Glymphatic Clearance and Microglial Phagocytosis

The selective detection of cyanide ions (CN–) has gained significant attention due to their high toxicity and environmental impact. In this work, a novel fluorophore, 3-(4-(diphenylamino)­phenyl)-1-(phenyl)­prop-2-en-1-one, was synthesized via a simple and efficient mechanochemical approach. The synthesized fluorophore exhibited remarkable photophysical properties, with a strong fluorescence response specifically toward CN– in aqueous and organic media. Spectroscopic analyses confirmed that the detection mechanism involves a nucleophilic addition reaction at the α,β-unsaturated ketone site, leading to a significant fluorescence enhancement. Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations further supported the binding interaction and electronic transitions involved in the sensing process, making this fluorophore a promising candidate for real-time CN– monitoring.

The fluorophore demonstrated excellent selectivity and sensitivity toward CN– over other competing anions, with a low detection limit suitable for practical applications. Its rapid response, high stability, and minimal interference from environmental factors suggest its potential for real-world sensing applications in environmental and industrial settings. Compared to existing CN– sensors, this mechanochemically synthesized fluorophore offers a cost-effective and environmentally friendly alternative with comparable or superior performance. Future work could explore its application in biological systems and real sample analysis to expand its utility. This work highlights the significance of fluorophore design for developing efficient, selective, and sustainable chemosensors for toxic anion detection.

Other Novel Liposomal Strategies

Exosome-Like Liposomes

Exosomes are tiny vesicles released by cells that can carry proteins and genetic material. They are naturally biocompatible and have shown promise as drug carriers, but they are difficult to produce in large amounts and often vary in size and content. To address this, researchers have developed exosome-like liposomes, which are artificial vesicles designed to behave like natural exosomes but with more consistent properties and easier production.

In this study, scientists created these liposomes using a special mix of phospholipids and tested them for delivering curcumin, a natural compound known for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, which may help protect the brain in AD. These exosome-like liposomes were small (under 200 nm), stable for at least six months, and had high efficiency in loading curcumin (up to 94%). The liposomes were found to be safe for cells, showing no toxicity in both neuronal (SH-SY5Y) and fibroblast (L929) cell lines. They could enter cells and deliver curcumin directly into the cytoplasm. The curcumin-loaded liposomes reduced oxidative stress, improved cell health, and protected neurons better than free curcumin alone. Tests on zebrafish embryos showed that the liposomes were well tolerated and concentrated in brain-related areas, supporting their potential for treating brain diseases. Overall, these exosome-like liposomes offer a promising new way to deliver drugs like curcumin into the brain safely and effectively, making them a valuable strategy for AD therapy.

Clinical Studies and Translational Challenges

Despite promising preclinical results, the clinical translation of liposomal and nanomaterial-based strategies for Alzheimer’s disease (AD) therapy remains limited. Early phase trials with liposomal drugs and nanoparticle formulationssuch as rivastigmine and curcumin-loaded carriershave shown improved CNS drug delivery, safety, and bioavailability. However, large-scale studies demonstrating clear cognitive benefits in AD patients are lacking. Key translational challenges include physiological differences between animal models and humans, particularly regarding the blood–brain barrier and AD pathology. Limited biomarkers, clinical heterogeneity, and stringent regulatory requirements further hinder progress. Additionally, scaling up nanoparticle production under GMP while maintaining quality and consistency remains a significant barrier to widespread clinical adoption.

Future Perspectives

The nanotechnology-based strategies show immense promise for improving AD therapy, several translational challenges must be addressed. These include bridging the gap between animal models and human disease, developing better biomarkers for early stage intervention, and ensuring the safety, scalability, and regulatory compliance of novel formulations. Continued investment in interdisciplinary research and collaborative clinical studies will be essential to bring these innovative therapies from bench to bedside.

Summary

Recent advancements in liposome-based strategies for inhibiting Aβ aggregation highlight their potential in AD therapy. Noninvasive approaches, such as liposome-coated nanoparticles, have emerged as promising drug delivery systems for crossing the BBB. For example, liposome-packaged Ligustilide has demonstrated significant efficacy in reducing oxidative stress and cognitive impairment. The versatility and efficacy of liposomal delivery systems in targeting and mitigating Aβ pathology have been well-documented, with several formulations showing promise in preclinical models. However, challenges remain in translating these findings to clinical applications, including optimizing BBB penetration, enhancing drug loading and controlled release, and ensuring safety and efficacy in human trials. Continued research into liposome modifications and their therapeutic potential offers a promising avenue for developing more effective treatments for AD.

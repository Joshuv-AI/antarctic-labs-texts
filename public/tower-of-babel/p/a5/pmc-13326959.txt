Effects of the Fluid Replacement Method During Online Hemodiafiltration on the Solute Removal Performance and Biocompatibility Using the Asymmetric Cellulose Triacetate Membrane
Authors: Kenji Sakurai, Yoshitaka Kurihara, Fumi Yamauchi, Hiromi Hosoya, Takeshi Saito
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionPre‐dilution online hemodiafiltration (Pre‐HDF) is predominantly used in Japan, whereas post‐dilution online HDF (Post‐HDF) is more common in Europe. An asymmetric cellulose triacetate (ATA) membrane may improve biocompatibility. This study compared biocompatibility and solute removal performance between Pre‐ and Post‐HDF using the ATA membrane.MethodsEight stable maintenance hemodialysis patients underwent both Pre‐ (60 L/session) and Post‐HDF (12 L/session) using an ATA membrane. Blood flow rate was 250 mL/min, total dialysate flow rate was 500 mL/min, and treatment time was 4 h. Biocompatibility and solute removal parameters were evaluated.ResultsWhite blood cell and platelet counts showed mild decreases; inflammatory markers remained unchanged in both modalities. CD62P expression increased more in Post‐HDF. Reduction rates of urea, creatinine, and α1‐microglobulin were higher in Post‐HDF, while β2‐microglobulin was comparable. Albumin leakage was greater in Post‐HDF.ConclusionsUsing the ATA membrane, Pre‐ and Post‐HDF showed comparable biocompatibility, while Post‐HDF provided superior solute removal performance.Keywords: asymmetric cellulose triacetate membrane, biocompatibility, post‐dilution online HDF, pre‐dilution online hemodiafiltration, solute removal efficiency

Introduction

In Japan, the number of patients undergoing hemodialysis has been decreasing in recent years, but it had been increasing until recently, and as of the end of 2023, approximately 343 000 patients were receiving hemodialysis treatment [1]; the number of hemodialysis patients has shown an upward trend globally [2]. Under this trend, the technique of online hemodiafiltration (HDF) is being increasingly selected for hemodialysis, with about 135 000 patients currently receiving hemodialysis by this technique in Japan [1]. According to the Web‐based Analysis of Dialysis Data Archives (Wadda) system [3], which uses statistical data from the Japanese Society for Dialysis Therapy (JSDT), as of 2023, post‐dilution online HDF (Post‐HDF) was used only for about 8500 patients in Japan, and pre‐dilution online HDF (Pre‐HDF) mode is predominantly used in Japan. In Europe, on the other hand, Post‐HDF is the predominantly used mode [4]. This is likely attributable to the fact that the dialysis membranes currently used in Japan (type II or higher according to a previous dialyzer classification used in Japan) have a relatively large pore size with a β2 microglobulin (β2‐MG) clearance of 70 mL/min or greater and albumin sieving coefficient (SC) of 0.01, whereas, in contrast, the high‐flux dialyzers used in European and other countries have β2‐MG clearance rates of 15 mL or greater and albumin SC of less than 0.001. Thus, the dialysis membrane design concept in Japan differs from that in Europe and other countries, resulting in differences in the substitution mode and substitution volume between Japan and Europe.

Several studies were conducted to investigate the potential influence of differences in substitution fluid inflow on biocompatibility [5, 6]. Given that the substitution volume is larger in Pre‐HDF than in Post‐HDF, it was hypothesized that platelet activation and related phenomena might be more likely to occur during Pre‐HDF. During Post‐HDF, on the other hand, the substitution fluid is added after passage of the blood through the hemodiafilter, which can result in non‐physiological blood concentrations, resulting in poor biocompatibility due to excessive contact with blood components. Initial evaluations using the polyester polymer alloy (PEPA) membrane focused on differences in inflammatory markers and lymphocyte stimulation test, indicating a higher degree of biocompatibility in Pre‐HDF than in Post‐HDF [5]. A subsequent study comparing plasma levels of CD62P (a cell surface marker of activated platelets), inflammatory cytokines, and related parameters found no differences in dialyzer biocompatibility between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF [6]. Because the HDF treatment settings (blood flow rate, replacement fluid flow rate, etc.) were not uniform between these two studies, the results suggest the possibility that the biocompatibility may not differ between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF depending on the treatment setting. Furthermore, in the latter study—where no differences in biocompatibility were observed between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF—the serum level of the platelet activation marker CD62P was measured, rather than its expression level on the platelet surface.

In recent years, a new dialyzer membrane has been launched commercially in Japan. This membrane was created by converting the cellulose triacetate (CTA) membrane having a symmetric structure into a membrane with an asymmetric structure and improved surface smoothness. The amount of protein adhering to the surface of this new membrane after clinical use has been shown to be significantly reduced [7]. In an in vitro experiment, no increase in the level of the thrombin‐antithrombin complex (TAT) was observed following long‐time circulation through this asymmetric triacetate (ATA) membrane, whereas the level of TAT rose following long‐time circulation through membranes other than the ATA membrane [8]. Thus, it would appear that the ATA membrane is associated with less fouling and has higher antithrombotic potential, favorably affecting the biocompatibility.

The present study was undertaken to compare the biocompatibility and solute removal efficiency of an ATA membrane hemodiafilter between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF, and also performing flow‐cytometric measurement of the expression level of CD62P on the platelet surface.

Methods

The study was conducted in patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis therapy at our clinic, in whom dialysis had been initiated at least 6 months earlier and the circulatory dynamics had stabilized.

This study was a single‐center, single‐group crossover trial conducted between November 2018 and January 2019. Patients who had been receiving Pre‐HDF were switched to Post‐HDF for weekend dialysis sessions for 1 month (4 weeks), with blood sampling performed midweek during the final week of treatment. Subsequently, the same patients were switched back to Pre‐HDF for weekend dialysis sessions for another month (4 weeks), with blood sampling again performed midweek during the final week of treatment (Figure 1).

Overview of the study schedule. In this study, each treatment mode was maintained for 1 month, and evaluations were conducted on the final treatment day of each month (midweek). For all participants, treatment was first performed using Post‐HDF, followed by Pre‐HDF.

This study protocol was reviewed and approved by our clinic, approval number [2018‐02]. This case report was written in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The patients have signed the informed consent.

Settings for the Treatment

For both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF, the ATA membrane was used as the hemodiafilter (FIX‐210S eco, Nipro Corporation, Osaka, Japan). The blood flow rate was set at 250 mL/min, the total dialysis fluid flow rate (QD_total) at 500 mL/min, and the treatment time at 4 h. The substitution volume was set at 60 L/session (15.3 L/h) for Pre‐HDF and at 12 L/session (3 L/h) for Post‐HDF. Under these settings, dialysis treatment was performed for 1 week, and blood was sampled from each patient in the middle of the week. In this study, solute removal occurred through diffusion and filtration. The experimental conditions were adjusted so that the reduction rate of β2‐MG (11 800 Da), representing a solute with an intermediate molecular weight among the measured solutes, was equivalent between the two modes. The removal efficiencies of solutes with higher and lower molecular weights were then compared.

Our dialysis center uses a central dialysis fluid delivery system (CDDS) to supply dialysate. We employed Kindaly 4E (Fuso Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd., Osaka, Japan), which was prepared by dissolving powder into liquid form with devices DRY‐50A and DRY‐50B. The dialysate solution was then delivered via the CDDS of DAB‐E to patient monitor equipment DCS‐100NX (Nikkiso Co. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) for the dialysis sessions.

Blood sampling before the start of HDF was conducted directly through the needle puncture for dialysis. Sampling at 30 min after the start of HDF and that upon completion of HDF were made via the arterial side sampling port of the blood circuit. The waste dialysis fluid was collected at 2 L/h by means of partial pooling with the use of a roller pump from the branching circuit, thereby branching the dialysis fluid exit circuit in a T‐shaped manner.

The biocompatibility and solute removal efficiency were evaluated and compared.

Evaluation of biocompatibility was conducted by analyzing the percent changes in the white blood cell (WBC) count and platelet (PLT) count before, at 30 min after the start, and at completion of dialysis, the changes over time and percent changes of the platelet positive rate for the surface marker CD62P, and the pre‐dialysis versus post‐dialysis percent changes (CR) in the plasma levels of high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein (CRP), high‐sensitivity IL‐6, high‐sensitivity TNF‐α, pentraxin (PTX)‐3, and soluble intercellular adhesion molecule‐1 (ICAM‐1) using Equation (1).

High‐sensitivity CRP was measured by a latex agglutination assay. IL‐6, TNF‐α, and soluble ICAM‐1 were measured by enzyme immunoassay methods. PTX‐3 was measured by an enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay method. The platelet surface expression of CD62P was measured as follows. Before the start of dialysis, 5 mL of blood was collected through the puncture needle into a syringe and transferred into a sample tube filled with ACD solution. This tube was placed upside down for mixing. The sample prepared thus was subjected to flow‐cytometric analysis with FACSCalibur within 12 h of the blood sampling to measure the expression level of CD62P.

Evaluation of the solute removal efficiency was conducted by calculating the single pool Kt/V for urea (sp Kt/V), the reduction rates (RRs) of urea, creatinine, β2‐MG, prolactin (PRL), and α1‐microglobulin (α1‐MG), albumin (Alb) leakage, and the clear space for urea, creatinine, β2‐MG, and α1‐MG. Statistical Analysis

The results are expressed as means ± SD. The data were subjected to a paired t‐test and p < 0.05 was regarded as being indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Patients Characteristics

A total of eight patients (4 men and 4 women) were included in the study. The mean age of the patients was 58.4 ± 14.9 years, the mean dialysis vintage was 112.8 ± 109.3 months, and the mean dry weight (DW) was 54.8 ± 8.0 kg. The underlying disease was diabetic nephropathy (DMN) in 3 patients, other diseases in 4 patients, and was unknown in 1 patient.

Biocompatibility

The WBC and PLT counts tended to decrease slightly after both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF, but the magnitude of their changes did not differ significantly between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF (Figure 2).

The changes in platelets and white blood cells over time (n = 8, mean ± SD). There were no significant differences in the changes in PLT and WBC over time between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF.

The PTX‐3 increased after both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF, with no significant difference in the degree of increase between the two modes. High‐sensitivity CRP, IL‐6, TNF‐α, and soluble ICAM‐1 showed no elevation after either Pre‐HDF or Post‐HDF, with no significant differences between the two methods (Figure 3).

The changes in CRP, IL‐6, TNF‐α, PTX‐3, and ICAM‐1 before and after dialysis treatment (n = 8, mean ± SD). TNF‐α showed lower values after dialysis compared to before dialysis. On the other hand, PTX‐3 showed higher values after dialysis compared to before dialysis. No changes were observed in other markers before and after dialysis. There were no significant differences in all evaluation markers between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF.

The CD62P positive rate increased at 30 min after the start and at completion of dialysis by both the Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF modes, with no significant differences in the degree of change between the two methods. However, the rate of change in the CD62P positive rate (pre‐dialysis level set as 100%) was significantly higher after Post‐HDF than after Pre‐HDF (Figure 4).

The expression rate of CD62P during treatment and its rate of change (n = 8, mean ± SD). After the start of treatment, the expression rate of CD62P increased, with no significant difference between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF. However, when evaluated by the rate of change in expression, Post‐HDF showed a significantly higher value than Pre‐HDF after 240 min.

Solute Removal Efficiency

The sp Kt/V was significantly higher after Post‐HDF than after Pre‐HDF, although the value after Pre‐HDF was not low either. The reduction rate of β2‐MG was 81.2% after both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF. On the other hand, the reduction rates of urea, creatinine, and α1‐MG were significantly higher after Post‐HDF than after Pre‐HDF (Figure 5).

Sp Kt/V and the reduction rates of urea to α1‐MG (n = 8, mean ± SD). Sp Kt/V, the reduction rates of urea, creatinine, and α1‐MG were significantly higher in the Post‐HDF than in the Pre‐HDF.

Alb leakage was significantly higher Post‐HDF than Pre‐HDF, but Alb leakage ≥ 5 g was not observed in any of the patients. The CS was higher Post‐HDF than Pre‐HDF for each of the substances from urea to α1‐MG. Among others, the CS for α1‐MG was significantly higher Post‐HDF than Pre‐HDF (Figure 6).

The clear space of urea to α1‐MG and albumin leakage (n = 8, mean ± SD). The clear space of α1‐MG and albumin leakage was significantly higher in the Post‐HDF than in the Pre‐HDF.

Discussion

The present study conducted in Japan was designed to evaluate and compare the influence of differences in the substitution fluid inflow method for on‐line HDF (performed using a hemodiafilter free of polyvinylpyrrolidone [PVP] and bisphenol A [BPA]) on the biocompatibility and solute removal efficiency. This study focused primarily on the flow‐cytometric quantification of CD62P expression on the surface of activated platelets, as a key indicator of platelet activation.

To appropriately evaluate biocompatibility, study conditions other than the variables under comparison were standardized as much as possible. The ATA membrane is available in three types, with the pore size decreasing in the order of FIX‐U, S, and E. In the present study, we selected FIX‐S to ensure enhanced reduction rate of α1‐MG without excessive albumin leakage, while using the same type of hemodiafilter for both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF. Accordingly, the same‐lot hemodiafilter was used for both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF treatments, and treatment settings were selected to achieve approximately equivalent mean reduction rate of β2‐MG in both modalities. The substitution volumes were set to achieve comparable removal rates of β2‐MG between the two modes of HDF. By aligning the removal rate of β2‐MG, it became possible to evaluate the removal performances for smaller solutes primarily cleared by diffusion and for larger solutes primarily cleared by filtration. The study design was based on the view that since solute removal efficiency and solute formation can occur simultaneously during dialysis, interpretation of the results for the parameters evaluated as indicators of biocompatibility would be difficult if the reduction rate of medium and/or large molecular weight solutes differed significantly between the two methods. In the present study conducted using such a design, the mean reduction rate of β2‐MG was 81.2% after both Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF, which enabled easier interpretation of the results concerning biocompatibility.

The PTX‐3 level increased after dialysis. This increase after dialysis is attributable to the inability of online HDF to remove PTX‐3 by either diffusion or filtration, because this substance with a molecular weight of 40 000 assumes the form of an octamer under physiological conditions [9]. High‐sensitivity CRP (MW: 105000) is a protein whose blood level is known to increase in the presence of inflammation or partial tissue collapse. This substance is also not removed during dialysis. The mean percent change in the level of this substance was about 87%–94%, suggesting that it was not formed during dialysis. ICAM‐1 (MW: 80000–110 000) is one of the adhesion molecules involved in the intercellular interactions of the immune system and is not removed during dialysis. The mean percent change in the level of this molecule was about 97%–100%, suggesting that it was also not formed during dialysis. IL‐6 (MW: 21000–28 000) is an inflammatory cytokine whose level increases when the dialysis membrane is not biocompatible. During dialysis, this substance is both formed and removed. In the present study, the IL‐6 level after dialysis was lower than its pre‐dialysis level, suggesting that the amount of IL‐6 removed during dialysis was larger than the amount formed during dialysis and that the membrane used was favorable in terms of biocompatibility. TNF‐α is an inflammatory cytokine with a molecular weight of 17 kDa, having a wide range of biological activities, including exerting actions on macrophages, inflammation, genesis, and differentiation. This substance is also formed and removed during dialysis. In the present study, its level after dialysis was lower than its pre‐dialysis level, suggesting that the membrane used showed favorable biocompatibility. Of all the parameters evaluated in the present study, only the pre‐dialysis versus post‐dialysis percent change in the CD62P positive rate differed between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF. However, the pre‐dialysis versus post‐dialysis percent change in the CD62P positive rate was as small as about 1%–2%. So, these results suggested that the difference in biocompatibility between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF is not large under the treatment settings adopted in the present study, although further analysis is needed about future influence of such a difference in platelet activity.

In this study, the removal performance of small molecules was reduced in Pre‐HDF as compared with Post‐HDF due to the decreased diffusive efficiency resulting from the lower dialysate flow through the hemodiafilter. In addition, due to the improved filtration efficiency, Pre‐HDF showed a lower removal rate of α1‐MG and less albumin leakage as compared with Post‐HDF. These findings clearly indicate that under the present treatment conditions, post‐HDF provides superior solute removal performance as compared with Pre‐HDF. Alb leakage was 4.5 g at the maximum, with excessive Alb leakage not observed in any of the patients. This result suggested that the dialysis procedures were carried out under appropriate settings in the present study. It has been reported that active removal of α1‐MG alleviates subjective symptoms in dialysis patients, such as bone/joint pain and restless legs syndrome [10], thus attracting close attention to the importance of α1‐MG removal. More recently, it was shown that newly formed α1‐MG in the liver exhibits antioxidative activity [11]. If α1‐MG in the blood is removed and new α1‐MG is formed in the liver of dialysis patients, newly formed α1‐MG manifests antioxidative activity, followed by deterioration of its activity. Therefore, removal of deteriorated α1‐MG to the maximum extent possible by means of dialysis is now considered important. In the present study, the reduction rate of α1‐MG was also relatively high (Pre: 33.1% ± 5.8%, Post: 37.4% ± 3.9%).

In this study, we compared the solute removal performance and biocompatibility of the ATA membrane between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF under the current treatment conditions. We have also evaluated the solute removal performance and biocompatibility of PEPA and PS membranes between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF in previous studies [5, 6], although the treatment conditions and evaluation criteria were different between our current and previous studies. Moving forward, it will be necessary to align treatment conditions and evaluation criteria to better assess the solute removal performance and biocompatibility of different membranes in Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF.

Vanholder et al. reported the importance of environmental issues, pointing out the necessity of early countermeasures to address the significant issue of environmental pollution arising from dialysis therapy [12]. In regard to Post‐HDF, it has been reported that even when the QD_total is reduced from 500 to 400 mL/min, the solute removal efficiency remains unchanged [13]. Furthermore, no significant difference was observed in the solute removal efficiency of Post‐HDF when comparing QD_total of 400 and 350 mL/min, with the filtration volume controlled by convection volume [14]. So, if biocompatibility does not differ between Post‐HDF and Pre‐HDF, it is desirable that Post‐HDF is applied more extensively in Japan from now on with anticipation of less adverse impacts on environments.

A limitation of this study was that it was carried out at a single center, in a small number of subjects. However, the results of this study suggested that the biocompatibility does not differ greatly between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF if an appropriate hemodiafilter is used without excessively increasing the blood flow rate and substitution flow rate. This finding may encourage us to reconsider the superiority of Post‐HDF.

Conclusions

A comparative evaluation of biocompatibility between Pre‐HDF and Post‐HDF using an ATA hemodiafilter demonstrated that, although the pre‐ to post‐dialysis percent change in the positive rate of CD62P differed between the two modalities, the magnitude of this difference was small. Moreover, no significant differences were observed in any of the other biocompatibility markers between the two modes.

Regarding solute removal efficiency, Post‐HDF performed with the FIX‐210S eco under appropriate settings showed favorable solute removal efficiency ranging from small to large‐middle molecules without causing excessive albumin leakage.

The effect of using laparoscopic imaging screens of different sizes (55-inch vs. 27-inch) on surgical parameters in hysterectomy surgery: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Mustafa Can Sivas, Berna Aslan Cetin, Karolin Ohanoglu Cetinel, Ipek Emine Geyikoglu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMonitors that can provide 2-dimensional (2D) or 3-dimensional (3D) images, as well as high-definition (HD) or 4 K-HD image quality, offer various conveniences to surgeons. While studies comparing 2D and 3D screens exist in the literature, studies are needed to investigate the effects of different monitor sizes on surgical success. This study aimed to examine the impact of screen size on surgical parameters in patients who have undergone a hysterectomy using either a 55-inch or a 27-inch monitor.MethodsPatients who underwent laparoscopic hysterectomy and salpingectomy/salpingo-oophorectomy between May 2022 and July 2023 were retrospectively screened. Two groups were created. GroupA consisted of patients who underwent surgery using a 55-inch (140 cm) monitor with both 2D and 4 K-HD features. GroupB consisted of patients whose surgery was performed using a 27-inch (69 cm) monitor with both 2D and 4 K-HD features. A total of 72 women (n(GroupA) = 36, n(GroupB) = 36) were included in the study.ResultsNo statistically significant difference was observed between groups regarding age, number of cesarean sections, or body mass index (p > 0.05). Additionally, no statistically significant difference was found between groups regarding the total surgery duration (GroupA, GroupB; (126.86 ± 50.42), (128.97 ± 44.29), p = 0.851, respectively). When evaluating the percentage decrease in hemoglobin values before and after surgery, no statistically significant difference was observed between groups (GroupA, GroupB; (-0.11 ± 0.06), (-0.11 ± 0.07), p = 0.746, respectively). There were no statistically significant differences between groups regarding hematocrit changes, frequency of intraoperative complications, postoperative infections, the time to first gas passage, and length of hospital stay (p > 0.05).ConclusionsIn laparoscopic monitor systems with 4 K-HD resolution, the screen size, whether 27-inches or 55-inches, may not significantly affect surgical parameters.Keywords: Hysterectomy, Laparoscopic monitor, Screen size, Surgery duration, 4 K HD

Background

Laparoscopic surgery is a frequently used method in abdominal surgery, offering a less invasive option for patients and more comfort for surgeons. Technological advancements in laparoscopic surgical equipment have been rapidly progressing from the past to the present [1, 2]. One of the laparoscopic equipment pieces is the laparoscopic monitors, which allow the visualization of data sent from the camera. The combination of the laparoscopic camera, monitor, image processor, and light source is called the laparoscopic imaging system [3]. Depending on the features of the laparoscopic imaging system, monitors generally provide two-dimensional (2D) images, although there are also monitors that offer three-dimensional (3D) images [2]. Nowadays, in 2D imaging laparoscopic systems, apart from the classic 27-inch (69 cm) monitors, large 55-inch (140 cm) monitors are also used. The resolution quality of 2D monitors can be high-definition (HD) or even higher quality 4 K HD [4].

Studies in the literature regarding laparoscopic imaging screens have indicated that 3D monitors provide better depth perception than 2D monitors, positively affecting surgical parameters. In operations using 3D monitors, a shorter total surgery duration has been observed. Surgeon orientation and grasping ability have been noted to be better and more successful [5–9]. There are studies comparing 3D and 2D screens in the literature, however there is currently no study investigating the effects of different monitor sizes on surgical success.

This study aims to investigate the effects of different monitor sizes on surgical parameters in patients who underwent laparoscopic hysterectomy using monitors with both 2D and 4 K resolution features, with screen sizes of either 27 or 55 inches. This may help inform the necessity of devices that serve a similar purpose but are more costly in health expenditures. In addition, surgical operations where the use of these devices will make a difference can be revealed.

Methods

This study was conducted as a retrospective observational study. It was adhered to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. Ethical approval for the research was obtained from the Local Ethics Committee of Basaksehir Cam and Sakura City Hospital (date: 12.07.2023, number: 311).

Study design

Between May 2022 and July 2023, patients who underwent laparoscopic hysterectomy and salpingectomy/salpingo-oophorectomy at Basaksehir Cam and Sakura City Hospital were screened retrospectively and respectively. Inclusion/exclusion criteria were applied by reviewing surgical notes and system records, and the types of laparoscopic monitors used during the surgeries were identified. To ensure consistency and minimize the impact on surgical duration, patients whose surgical notes indicated “no adhesions to the intestines or omentum”, “uterus with ≤ 10 weeks size”, “vaginal cuff closing with laparoscopic suturing” were included in the study. Patients who were operated on by surgeons (MCS, BAC, KOC) with at least four years of experience in performing laparoscopic hysterectomy were included in the study. The surgeons in the study had no visual impairments, such as myopia or hyperopia, that required the use of corrective lenses or glasses. Preoperative hemoglobin values of all patients were > 10 g/dL as required by elective surgery. Patients with a body mass index (BMI) ≥ 40 were excluded from the study, as obesity could affect surgical bleeding and duration. Patients whose surgical notes indicated “presence of additional surgical interventions outside routine surgical procedures”, “presence of adhesiolysis”, “diagnosis of myoma” were excluded from study. Patients with three or more previous cesarean sections or those who received pre-, intra-, or postoperative blood transfusions were also excluded from the study. All patients included in the study had undergone routine surgical steps which included sequentially; sealing and cutting of the round ligaments, bladder mobilization, sealing and cutting of bilateral infundibulopelvic or utero-ovarian ligaments, uterine arteries, and cardinal ligaments, creation of the vaginal cuff with using monopolar energy, and laparoscopic cuff suturing. Sealing and cutting procedures for all patients had been performed with using an advanced bipolar energy source. Patients were divided into two study groups;

Group A: Patients who underwent surgery using a 55-inch (140 cm) monitor with both 2D and 4 K HD features (55-inch VISERA 4 K SONY LMD-X550S Surgical Monitor, SONY, Tokyo, Japan) were included in Group A.

Group B: Patients who underwent surgery using a 27-inch (69 cm) monitor with both 2D and 4 K HD features (27-inch 4 K LG Surgical Monitor with Mini-LED, LG Electronics, Seoul, Republic of Korea) were included in Group B.

Differences between the two groups were analyzed in terms of surgery duration, decrease in hemoglobin/hematocrit, day of first postoperative gas passage, length of hospital stay, and complication/infection rates. Surgery duration was defined as the time between the first skin incision and the completion of the surgery. The decrease in hemoglobin/hematocrit was determined by calculating the difference between the hemoglobin/hematocrit values taken upon admission to the hospital for surgery and those taken 24 h after surgery. The percentage decreases in hemoglobin and hematocrit values were taken as a basis for determining the amount of bleeding. The length of hospital stay was determined based on the number of days until discharge.

Operating room and surgical equipment

All operations had been performed in two different operating rooms with the same physical characteristics, 7.5 × 8 m in size. The operating room tables were mobile tables that could perform forward-backward, up-down, trendelenburg and reverse trendelenburg maneuvers. The tower carrying the laparoscopic unit equipment was wheeled and easily movable structure. The 27-inch monitor was movable at the top of the laparoscopic tower via angled joints. The 55-inch monitor was a single piece of equipment on its own, with a wheeled and easily movable structure that allowed it to be placed at the desired distance thanks to the 4-meter connection cables.

Statistical analysis

When determining the sample size, the calculation was performed in accordance for a power value of 0.80, a margin of error of 0.05 and an effect size of 0.6. Whether the variables followed a normal distribution was examined using the Shapiro-Wilk Test. Variables that followed a normal distribution are presented with mean and standard deviation values, and the Independent Samples t-test was used to analyze differences between the two groups. Variables that did not follow a normal distribution are presented with median, minimum, and maximum values, and the Mann-Whitney U Test was used to analyze differences between the two groups. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 22.0 software. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The study continued until a total of 72 patients were reached, with 36 patients in each group meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The ages of the included patients ranged from 37 to 80 years. During the research process, a total of 21 patients were excluded from the study: 5 patients who had dense adhesions of the uterus and adnexa to the intestines or surrounding tissues noted in the surgical notes, two patients who received preoperative or intraoperative blood transfusions, three patients who had three or more previous cesarean sections, six patients who had a history of myomectomy in their surgical history, and five patients who did not meet other criteria.

When evaluated the patient data from Group A, operated with a 55-inch screen, and Group B, operated with a 27-inch screen, no statistically significant differences were observed between the groups in terms of age, number of cesarean sections, or BMI (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

Discussion

In this study, when the groups were evaluated, it was determined that the participants’ parameters, such as the number of previous cesarean sections, age, and BMI, were similar, in a way that contributes to standardization. The average total operation time of Group A (126.86 ± 50.42 min) and the total operation time of Group B (128.97 ± 44.29) were found to be close to each other. In hysterectomy operations, the use of a larger screen size did not contribute to the faster completion of the operation. When considering that larger screens can show tissue layers and vascular structures more clearly, in the evaluation made on whether large screens can minimize the risk of possible bleeding and reduce the total amount of blood loss by shortening the intervention time in case of bleeding; there was no difference in the amount of hemoglobin/hematocrit decrease. It was determined that smaller screens did not result in a disadvantage in terms of the bleeding parameter. No complications were observed in either screen size. All patients had gas passage in the first 24 h after surgery and no postoperative infections. The length of hospital stay was similar for patients in both screen size. Larger screen size did not contribute to these parameters.

Studies on laparoscopic screens in the literature have generally compared screens that provide 2D or 3D images. In a study conducted by Honeck et al. on a pelvic model in an ex-vivo setting, it was reported that the number of missed grasps was significantly lower in the 3D system compared to the 2D system, and orientation and depth perception were better [5]. In the study by Harada et al., in an educational box, participants with no experience showed higher scores in performing tasks such as suturing and knot tying on the 3D monitor compared to 2D [10]. Komatsuda et al. reported shorter suturing times in laparoscopic partial nephrectomy surgeries performed using a 3D screen system, which they stated was effective in reducing ischemic time [8]. Zheng et al. compared 2D and 3D laparoscopic screens in patients with gastric cancer undergoing laparoscopic gastrectomy. Although the surgery duration was not shorter in the 3D system, it was found to have less bleeding [11]. Generally, studies have shown that laparoscopic performances were higher on 3D screens [12]. In a study by Zwart et al., surgical parameters of patients undergoing pancreaticojejunostomy and hepaticojejunostomy with 3D or 2D laparoscopy were compared. It was found that the operation duration was 15.5 (10.2–24.5) minutes shorter with 3D laparoscopy [13]. Similar studies providing comparable results indicating that surgeries performed with 3D screens have shorter durations compared to those with 2D screens are increasingly appearing in the literature [7, 14–18]. A systematic review examining 31 randomized controlled trials reported that when using 3D screens, the error rate was significantly reduced compared to 2D screens, and the operation duration was shorter [19]. In addition to these studies, there was also a study investigating the effect of HD or 4 K HD resolution screens on surgical success. A study by Zhang et al. mentioned that 4 K HD laparoscopic screens provide higher-quality image than HD screens, so improving surgical conditions and reducing adverse outcomes. In this study, the 4 K HD screen was more successful in terms of optical angle coordination, visual acuity, brightness, resolution ratio, and refresh rate [6].

In contrast to all these studies, our study investigated the effect of screen size difference on surgical parameters rather than the dimension (2D − 3D) difference provided by screen specifications or the resolution quality (HD − 4 K HD) difference. All patients were selected from among those who had been operated on with 2D dimension and 4 K HD resolution quality screens and so the dimension and resolution factors were standardized. Our study is the first in the literature to examine the impact of different laparoscopic screen sizes on surgical parameters in hysterectomy surgery. Unlike ex vivo conditions such as educational boxes, data were analyzed of operative conditions in which a specific surgical procedure and participant standardization were achieved. As a result, in hysterectomy surgeries, laparoscopic monitors with a screen size of 27 inches and 4 K HD features did not cause any disadvantage in surgical parameters, especially regarding the bleeding parameter. Similarly, laparoscopic monitors with a screen size of 55 inches and 4 K HD features did not result in a shorter total operation time advantage. The fact that no differences were detected in surgical parameters suggests that 55-inch monitors, which cost approximately 4 to 6 times more, do not create a positive difference for the patient in laparoscopic hysterectomy surgeries. Considering that 55-inch monitors are more expensive than standard-sized monitors, studies on monitor sizes in laparoscopic hysterectomy and other gynecological surgeries will ensure that the right surgical equipment is used in the right surgical operations. In this way, especially environments where budgetary constraints affect equipment procurement decisions, hospital-based efficient use of budgets can be ensured in purchasing laparoscopic monitors.

Limitations of the study

The surgeons performing the operations were experienced specialists performing laparoscopic hysterectomy for at least four years. Even so, a study model where the same surgeon performs all surgeries would be more suitable for standardization. Since the operations could be performed at the most comfortable distance for the surgeon due to the easy movement of the operating room table, laparoscopic unit and monitor, no standardization was applied in the surgeon-monitor distance in this study. In this study, while comparing the superiority of the screens, the ease/difficulty and comfort factors experienced by the surgeon while performing the surgical steps were not evaluated. Examining the parameters that take into account the surgeon’s experience will increase the power of the study. Although statistically justified, the sample size limits the ability to detect smaller but potentially clinically relevant differences. No complications were encountered in the study groups. This may be due to the small size of the patient pool. Significant differences may be obtained in larger study groups.

Conclusions

In laparoscopic monitor systems with 4 K HD resolution, the screen size, whether 27 inches or 55 inches, may not have a significant advantage or disadvantage on surgical parameters. Further studies involving large patient groups are needed.

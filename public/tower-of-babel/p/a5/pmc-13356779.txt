ABSTRACT
AbstractPrecise prediction of agricultural commodity prices is key to assisting farmers, traders, policymakers and supply chain stakeholders to alleviate the uncertainty, and enable them to make sound decisions. The fluctuation in the price of soybean is very unpredictable because of the interplay of market forces, weather unpredictability, trade, output and farming methods. This paper suggests a multi-source machine learning model that is capable of forecasting the accurate price of soy beans using past market prices, arrivals, trade as well as weather forecasting, crop supply and area under cultivation. The dataset of a complete prediction of the prices of soybean between Jan 2015 to March 2026 was built by using the data provided in AGMARKNET, NCDEX, government trade portals, meteorological agencies and state agricultural databases. Exploratory data analysis was done to learn price distributions and regional differences, then systematic data preprocessing, normalization and feature engineering such as rainfall deviation, price range and arrivals per cultivation area was done. Several machine learning and deep learning models were implemented, such as Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, ensemble methods, dense neural network, attention-based models, and a proposed Wide and Deep Network (AgroWDN) was tested. RMSE, MAE, MSE and R2 measures were used to evaluate model performance on training, validation and test datasets. Experimental outcomes depict that AgroWDN model is, by all means, more efficient than the traditional machine learning and deep learning methods, making the least prediction errors and R2 at 98.97; it implies a high predictive performance. These results prove that the method of multi-source data fusion and hybrid deep learning structures are effective in price prediction in agriculture. The suggested framework provides a scalable and useful solution to the profitable prediction of soybean prices and may be applied to other crops and markets.Keywords: Soybean price forecasting, Machine learning, Deep learning, Agricultural commodities, Multi-source data, AgroWDN

Introduction

Price prediction of agricultural commodities is an important factor in the stability of the economy, food security and sustainable income of farmers, traders, and policy makers. Soybean is a strategically important oilseed crop since it has wide application in food products, animal feed and also in industry and it is closely connected with local and global markets. Nonetheless, the prices of soybean are very fluctuating and depend on a compound interaction of elements including past market procession, climatic variability, crop unpredictability, trade policies, and farming procedures. Conventional statistical and econometric forecasting tools fail to embody these non linear and interdependent interactions such that only a little predictive success is achieved in dynamic market environment [1]. More recent developments in machine learning have shown a high potential of modeling complex agricultural systems through the learning patterns that are presented by large and heterogeneous data[2]. It has already been demonstrated in previous research that adding historical price information and the arrival of the market can greatly enhance the accuracy of short-term predictions, but these methods often tend to disregard the long-term impact of weather anomalies, crop supply rules, and trade flows worldwide [3]. The effects of climatic factors on crop yield and supply in the market have a direct impact on the market and have significant pressure on the prices of commodities through deviations in rainfall and extreme weather conditions [4]. Likewise, fluctuations in area cultivated and production in the country are the same as factors that respond to the choices of farmers in terms of planting, and policy incentives, which also drive a price movement [5]. The trade indicators such as the import and export volumes and values are also important in determining the domestic soybean prices because they connect local markets to the international demand and supply chains [6]. In spite of the fact that some machine learning and deep learning models, including Random Forests, Gradient Boosting and neural networks, have been used to predict agricultural prices, the majority of the existing studies use small sets of features or single-source data, which limits the ability to generalize and reduces the strength of the system [7]. Besides, traditional deep learning models tend to compromise between memorization of past trends and generalization to different and varying factors of influence. To deal with such challenges, recent studies have emphasized the usefulness of hybrid and ensemble learning models that combine structured and unstructured aspects of various sources of data [8]. Driven by these constraints, this research paper suggests a multi-source machine learning system to successfully predict soybean prices through the use of historic market prices, arrivals data, weather indicators, crop supply data, trade data and cultivation area data. The proposed solution will use the state-of-the-art feature engineering and hybrid deep learning architectures to encompass linear and nonlinear dependence on the dynamics of soybean price. The framework is tested with a high-volume data over 10 years, which will allow obtaining a solid test under different market and weather conditions. This work has made contributions such as systematic multi-source data integration, widespread comparative investigation of machine learning and deep learning models, and the progress of a high-preciseness predictive framework that is appropriate to real-world agricultural choice support systems [9].

Related Work

Recent studies on agriculture commodity price prediction have also paid more attention to the use of machine learning and deep learning methods in order to address the weaknesses of the classical statistical models. The initial research found that tree-based algorithms like Random Forest and Gradient Boosting can be used to successfully extract nonlinear relations between past prices and market arrivals hence resulting in a higher quality of predictions of the key crops [10]. Afterward, the methods of ensemble learning that include several regressors were presented to increase robustness and minimize the variance of volatile agricultural markets [11]. As the number of large-scale agricultural datasets has increased, deep learning models, such as multi-layer perceptrons and recurrent neural networks, have become more prominent since such models are capable of learn temporal dependencies in price series that are complex [12]. A number of studies emphasized the benefits of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in terms of their ability to model sequential price changes, especially when such factors as historical price trends and seasonal trends are dominant in the market [13]. Nevertheless, such models are usually based on the price history and miss exogenous issues which have a great impact on crop prices. In response to this deficiency, recent research has focused on incorporation of weather factors, including rainfall, temperature and drought indices, in predictive models whereby climatic aberrations have been shown to significantly influence crop output and the resultant market prices [14]. Similar studies have investigated the use of production and supply-side pointers, such as area under cultivation and total output, and found high correlations between the planting decisions of farmers and price volatility [15]. Moreover, trade-related factors like imports, exports, and international price indicators have been included to capture the increased interconnectedness in the local markets in the countries to the international supply chains, which leads to a better forecasting performance in the medium and long-term [16]. All of these developments notwithstanding, the majority of current researches utilize small set of features or concentrate on one-source data which means that they cannot be simultaneously applied to different regions and timeframes. Recently, neural networks based on attention and hybrid deep learning networks have been suggested, which dynamically weighs the factors of influence and increases model interpretability [17]. Even wide-and-deep learning models, which were initially created to address recommender systems, have been applied to structured agricultural data with promising results due to their joint learning of memorization and generalization patterns [18].

Novel contributions of agrowdn framework

The AgroWDN architecture is an extension of the conventional Wide-and-Deep learning architecture, where the learning model is customized to suit the agriculture commodity forecasting problem. AgroWDN is distinct from the conventional Wide-and-Deep networks that mostly fuse sparse categorical features with dense features, by incorporating a multi-source agricultural feature fusion strategy that brings market prices, arrivals, weather indicators, trade statistics, crop production as well as cultivation-area information together and fuses them in a unified forecasting framework. To improve context awareness of agriculture, domain-engineered variables such as rainfall deviation, arrival-to-cultivation ratio, and seasonal supply indicators and price volatility measures are added. The wide component learns the explicit historical relationships between markets and the deep component learns the nonlinear relationships between climatic, supply-chain and market prices. Furthermore, feature normalization, temporal alignment and heterogeneous data integration are agricultural forecasting specific techniques used in the framework. This customization helps AgroWDN to very successfully record both long-term market behaviour and the short-term environmental impact. Thus, the novelty of AgroWDN is the adaptation on the agricultural domain and the fusion of multi-source data in conjunction with the framework of feature engineering for soybeans.

The proposed AgroWDN framework extends the conventional Wide-and-Deep Network architecture through several agricultural-domain-specific modifications, as discuss in Table 1. Unlike the standard Wide-and-Deep model, which primarily combines sparse categorical features and dense embeddings, AgroWDN integrates heterogeneous agricultural information obtained from market, weather, trade, and crop supply sources. A dedicated feature-engineering module was introduced to generate rainfall deviation, price volatility, and arrival-to-cultivation ratio features that capture agricultural market dynamics. The wide component was redesigned to learn explicit historical price interactions and regional market relationships, while the deep component was optimized to model nonlinear dependencies among climatic, production, trade, and market variables. Furthermore, temporal alignment and multi-source data fusion mechanisms were incorporated to synchronize monthly observations from different databases. These modifications enable AgroWDN to capture both long-term market behavior and short-term environmental influences, resulting in improved forecasting performance compared with the conventional Wide-and-Deep architecture.

Method details

Dataset description

The final dataset comprised of 4018 monthly records of soybean from January 2015 to March 2026 from various sources including AGMARKNET, NCDEX, IMD weather database, government trade statistics and state agricultural departments. Information about modal price, arrivals, rainfall, temperature, export volume, import volume, production estimate and cultivation area were in each record.

The soybean dataset used in this study consists of monthly observations collected from January 2015 to March 2026, as discus in Table 2a. Data were aggregated from multiple agricultural and economic sources, including AGMARKNET, NCDEX, IMD weather records, government trade statistics, and state agricultural departments. The final dataset contained 4018 records covering soybean-producing markets across the selected regions. The target variable was the modal soybean price expressed in Indian Rupees per quintal (INR/quintal). The dataset integrates market prices, arrivals, weather indicators, trade statistics, production estimates, and cultivation-area information to support comprehensive soybean price forecasting

All the numerical variables were Min-Max scaled before model training. The time series alignment was done to synch the monthly data of the markets, weather, trade and supply. To capture supply-demand dynamics for soybean prices, feature engineering was done by using rainfall deviation, price volatility and arrival to cultivation ratio.

The missing data for numbers, where the percentage is below the 3.2% level, have been substituted with the median. Non-completeness of records was determined by the number of missing data, with records with >30% percentage missing from the data not analyzed. To avoid the information leakage, the data was split chronologically to 70% for training, 15% for validation and 15% for testing.

The hyperparameters listed in Table 3 were chosen to achieve acceptable forecasting accuracy, convergence stability, and model generalization. To facilitate hierarchical feature extraction from multi-source agricultural data, the architecture of three hidden layers of 128, 64 and 32 neurons was used. ReLU activation functions were used to enhance the nonlinear learning capability while Adam optimizer with learning rate of 0.001 was used to optimize the parameters. A number of 32 for batch size and 100 epochs for training were found to have stable converging models. Additionally, the amount of overfitting was reduced and predictive robustness was improved in the face of different market conditions for soybeans by adding a dropout rate of 0.30.

Random Forest is an ensemble based machine learning algorithm which trains a series of decision trees based on bootstrapped samples of the training data and combines the forecasting of the decision-trees by averaging. Random Forest is an excellent fit in the soybean price prediction in terms of nonlinear interaction between market prices, arrivals, weather conditions, and trade variables. Everything is random, which is its inherent characteristic, which minimizes overfitting and enhances generalization in space and time. The model is not sensitive to noise and can efficiently process large-dimensional agricultural data, which is an appropriate choice as a powerful baseline predictor.

Deep learning methods used

Dense neural network (DNN)

A Dense Neural Network (DNN) is comprised of several fully connected layers of neurons with each neuron linked to the rest of the neurons in the next layer. In the context of forecasting soybean prices, DNN is a nonlinear model that learns the complex association among the multi-source predictors that include market prices, rainfall predictors, trade, and cultivation information. The network processes the input features to obtain high-level representations of the input features through successive hidden layers and nonlinear activation functions. Large data sets can be effectively predicted through DNNs, however, they need fine-tuning to prevent overfitting, and normalization and regularization are the key to stable and precise prediction of prices.

The Fig. 1 shows the Dense Neural Network that was used in predicting the prices of soybean with the layered architecture. The model is built up of several fully connected hidden layers which gradually convert inputs of multi-source such as market, weather, and trade and cultivation information into more abstract formats. Nonlinear activation functions allow the network to estimate complicated dependencies between variables. Continuous price predictions are generated at the output layer. The architecture is useful in dealing with large scale datasets, however it needs to be appropriately normalized as well as tuned to achieve steady learning and avoid overfitting.

Attention model

Attention model improves deep learning with dynamically allocated importance weights on input features or time steps. When applying the model to predict prices of soybean, attention mechanism can enable the model to pay more attention to factors that are relevant including abnormal rainfall, abrupt change of arrival, or fluctuation of trade and less attention to the less relevant inputs. Attention models are very useful in cases where prices are affected by several interacting factors at various time and spatial levels.

Fig. 2 gives the attention-based deep learning architecture which highlights the influential factors on the prices of soybean. The attention mechanism provides adaptive weights to the learned feature representations, enabling the model to place importance on the important inputs of abnormal rainfall, abrupt variations in the arrivals, or trade variations. Such a method is especially applicable to unstable agricultural markets when the significance of influencing factors in different regions and timeframes changes.

In regularized neural network, penalty terms have been introduced in the loss term to regulate the complexity of the model and to avoid overfitting. Regularization methods like L1 and L2 limiting the magnitudes of weights have a generalizing effect on learning that is smooth and more general. In hoping to predict soybean prices, regularization is useful to stabilize the training of models with high-dimensional, multi-source agricultural data. Regularized networks enhance the performance of generalization in various regions and time intervals eliminating the sensitivity to noise and outliers which creates more efficient and stable price predictions.

Method validation

Time-Series validation strategy

Soybean prices have high temporal dependence and were not split using random train-test splitting as a result. Data was organized in chronological order, where the initial observations were taken for training and subsequently the subsequent observations were used for validation and testing. A rolling-window validation was also performed to assess model stability for various forecasting horizons. Information leakage was avoided by using feature normalization parameters that were estimated only from the training data, and then applied to the validation and test sets. This protocol ensured that future information were never used during model training and that the model forecasting performance were assessed in a realistic way under operational conditions.

To preserve the temporal structure of soybean price data and avoid information leakage, a rolling-window validation strategy was employed instead of random train–test splitting. The complete dataset consisted of monthly observations from January 2015 to March 2026. For each validation iteration, an expanding training window containing approximately 70% of the available observations was used, followed by a 15% validation horizon and a 15% testing horizon. The forecast horizon was fixed at six months, while the rolling step size was one month. A total of five rolling validation windows were evaluated. Feature scaling parameters were estimated exclusively from the training partition of each window and subsequently applied to the validation and test sets. This procedure ensured that future information was never used during model development and provided a realistic assessment of forecasting performance under operational conditions.

Limitations and future work

Although performance in forecasting is strong there are still a number of limitations. Firstly, the accuracy of the models can be largely dependent on market, weather, trade and agricultural data and their quality and completeness. Limitations in records, reporting delays and the uncertainty in measurements can impact on the reliability of the prediction. Second, external factors such as geopolitics, policy and macroeconomic factors that impact soybean prices were not specifically considered in the current framework. Third, data was mainly collected from the agricultural markets in India making it difficult to extrapolate the results to other countries with varying market structures and production systems. Fourth, unforeseen events in the market, like export restrictions, extreme weather conditions and/or global supply-chain disruptions may result in forecasting uncertainty that goes beyond what has been seen in the training. Last, the AgroWDN was evaluated only on soybean data, and has shown improvement compared to the other machine learning and deep learning models. Future research is needed to study the transferability across different agricultural commodities, to include the use of real-time satellite and remote sensor data and to study temporal architectures using transformers for long horizon forecasting. Further research with cross-country datasets and external validation will further increase the robustness and ability to deploy in practice.

Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment of locally advanced or metastatic nonsquamous non-small-cell lung cancer (final analysis of RATIONALE-304: a randomized phase III trial)
Authors: S Lu, J Wang, Y Yu, X Yu, Y Hu, Z Ma, X Li, W He, Y Bao, M Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe purpose of this study was to report an updated, final analysis with longer follow-up for the open-label phase III RATIONALE-304 study of first-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for advanced nonsquamous non-small-cell lung cancer (nsq-NSCLC).Materials and methodsPatients with histologically confirmed stage IIIB/IV nsq-NSCLC were randomized (2 : 1) to 4-6 cycles of tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy and pemetrexed every 3 weeks, followed by maintenance tislelizumab and pemetrexed, or platinum-based chemotherapy and pemetrexed alone every 3 weeks followed by maintenance pemetrexed. The primary endpoint was independent review committee (IRC)-assessed progression-free survival (PFSIRC). Overall survival (OS), safety, and tolerability were secondary endpoints.ResultsOverall, 334 patients were randomized (tislelizumab plus chemotherapy: n = 223; chemotherapy: n = 111). At final analysis (median follow-up 16.1 months), safety/tolerability profiles in both arms were consistent with the interim analysis. Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy continued to demonstrate prolongation of PFSIRC versus chemotherapy alone {stratified hazard ratio (HR) 0.63 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.47-0.86]; median PFSIRC 9.8 months (95% CI 8.9-11.7 months) versus 7.6 months (95% CI 5.6-8.0 months), respectively}. OS stratified HR for tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy was 0.90 (95% CI 0.63-1.28), with median OS of 21.4 months (95% CI 17.7 months-not estimable) versus 21.3 months (95% CI 15.6 months-not estimable), respectively. At a subsequent ad hoc analysis (median follow-up 19.3 months), OS HR between arms was 0.85 (95% CI 0.63-1.14); when adjusted for crossover using the two-stage method, the OS HR was 0.68 (95% CI 0.48-0.96).ConclusionsAfter longer follow-up, first-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy continued to demonstrate a manageable safety profile and a favorable PFS benefit over chemotherapy alone in patients with advanced/metastatic nsq-NSCLC.Key words: anti-PD-1 antibody, non-small-cell lung cancer, first line, stage IIIB-IV, tislelizumab

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most common malignancies in adults and remains the leading cause of cancer death worldwide.1,2 Tislelizumab, a monoclonal antibody with high binding affinity to the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor was specifically engineered to minimize Fcγ receptor binding on macrophages.3,4 First-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was evaluated for untreated, locally advanced or metastatic nonsquamous non-small-cell lung cancer (nsq-NSCLC) that was not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy in the RATIONALE-304 study. Results from a prespecified interim analysis (median follow-up: 9.8 months) showed that the study met its primary endpoint, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in independent review committee (IRC)-assessed progression-free survival (PFSIRC) for patients with nsq-NSCLC receiving first-line tislelizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone [stratified hazard ratio (HR) 0.65, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.46-0.90, P = 0.0044].5 The IRC-assessed objective response rate (ORRIRC) also favored tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (57.4%) versus chemotherapy alone (36.9%). First-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was generally well tolerated, with a safety/tolerability profile consistent with that of other PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy in advanced or metastatic NSCLC.6, 7, 8 First-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy also showed a significant PFS benefit in patients with advanced or metastatic squamous (sq)-NSCLC, demonstrated by positive outcomes of the RATIONALE-307 study (NCT03594747), which also met its primary endpoint of PFSIRC at prespecified interim analysis,9 while second-/third-line tislelizumab monotherapy significantly prolonged overall survival (OS) versus docetaxel in the RATIONALE-303 study (NCT03358875) in patients with advanced sq- or nsq-NSCLC.10

Based on these data, tislelizumab received approval from the Chinese National Medical Products Administration for use in combination with chemotherapy as first-line treatment of patients with untreated locally advanced or metastatic sq- or nsq-NSCLC and as monotherapy in the second-/third-line setting in pretreated advanced or metastatic sq- or nsq-NSCLC.

This report details updated safety and efficacy data from the protocol-specified final analysis of RATIONALE-304, which included an additional 6.3 months of median study follow-up versus the interim analysis. Further, the effect of subsequent treatment on OS after disease progression, including open-label crossover to tislelizumab from the chemotherapy arm, was assessed. An ad hoc analysis of OS was conducted beyond the final analysis data cut-off (DCO) to examine the sustained benefits of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy using more mature data.

Materials and methods

Study design and treatment

RATIONALE-304 (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03663205) was an open-label, multicenter, randomized, phase III study conducted at 47 study sites in China, designed to investigate the safety and efficacy of first-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (platinum-pemetrexed) versus chemotherapy alone for patients with locally advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC. The study methodology for RATIONALE-304 has been reported5; Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728 shows the study design. Patients were randomized 2 : 1 to tislelizumab 200 mg intravenously with chemotherapy [cisplatin 75 mg/m2 or carboplatin area under the curve (AUC) 5 with pemetrexed 500 mg/m2] once every 3 weeks for 4-6 cycles, followed by maintenance tislelizumab plus pemetrexed or chemotherapy alone (cisplatin 75 mg/m2 or carboplatin AUC 5 with pemetrexed 500 mg/m2) every 3 weeks for 4-6 cycles followed by maintenance pemetrexed. Randomization was stratified by tumor cell (TC) programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) membrane expression (<1% versus 1%-49% versus ≥50%) and disease stage (IIIB versus IV). Treatments were continued until radiographic progression per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) or clinical progression, unacceptable toxicity, investigator decision, or withdrawal of patient consent. Patients in the chemotherapy arm could cross over to tislelizumab monotherapy upon IRC-confirmed disease progression. RATIONALE-304 was conducted in conformance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and was approved by the relevant Institutional Review Board/Independent Ethics Committee for each study site. Written informed consent was obtained before study participation.

Patients

Eligible patients were adults (aged 18-75 years) who were treatment-naive for histologically confirmed, locally advanced (stage IIIB; not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy), or metastatic (stage IV) nsq-NSCLC, as defined by the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual 7th Edition,11 and without known sensitizing EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements per tissue-based analyses. Other key inclusion criteria included an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≤1 and ≥1 measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1. Patients were ineligible if they had uncontrolled or untreated brain metastases, history of interstitial lung disease or noninfectious pneumonitis, active or relapsed autoimmune disease, or if they had received prior treatment with any systemic immunosuppressive agents within 14 days before study treatment initiation.

Endpoints and assessments

The primary endpoint was PFSIRC per RECIST v1.1 in the intent-to-treat (ITT) population (all randomized patients). Secondary endpoints included: OS in the ITT population, antitumor activity in the ITT population, determined by ORR, duration of response (DoR) and time to response, assessed by IRC and by investigator per RECIST v1.1, and investigator-assessed PFS (PFSINV).

During screening, TC PD-L1 expression was assessed using the Ventana PD-L1 (SP263) assay at a central laboratory. Tumor assessments were scheduled every 6 weeks for the first 6 months, every 9 weeks through the remaining 6 months of year 1, and every 12 weeks thereafter, regardless of dose delays to manage toxicities. Safety and tolerability were assessed in terms of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and treatment-related TEAEs in the safety population, defined as all patients receiving any dose of tislelizumab or chemotherapy. AEs were graded in accordance with the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Immune-mediated AEs (imAEs) were determined by a predefined programmatic algorithmic approach and were based on a list of preferred terms, without manual medical adjudication.

Statistical analysis

The statistical methodology for RATIONALE-304 has been reported.5 For the primary endpoint, it was estimated that 215 PFSIRC events at final analysis could provide 85% power to detect an HR of 0.65 at a one-sided α of 0.025, and the planned enrollment was 320 patients. As the primary endpoint was met at interim analysis,5 however, no formal significance testing was carried out for this final analysis of PFSIRC; therefore, the P value presented is descriptive. RATIONALE-304 was not powered to demonstrate superiority between tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone for OS.

Categorical variables were summarized by the number of patients; continuous variables were reported using descriptive statistics. Time-to-event endpoints were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methodology, with the Brookmeyer and Crowley method used to estimate the 95% CIs for median PFS, OS, and DoR. Stratified HRs and associated 95% CIs were calculated using a stratified Cox proportional hazard model, with the treatment arm as the only covariate, disease stage and TC PD-L1 expression as stratification factors, and with Efron’s method for handling tied events.

A two-stage analysis12 was carried out to estimate the effect of in-study crossover to tislelizumab on post-progression survival (PPS) using data from patients in the chemotherapy alone arm who progressed per IRC assessment before any subsequent anticancer therapy. Further information is included in Supplementary Appendix, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728.

Results

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics

From 23 July 2018, through 31 July 2019, 334 eligible patients were randomized to receive tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (n = 223) or chemotherapy alone (n = 111) (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). At final analysis DCO (26 October 2020), 60 (18.0%) patients in the ITT population remained on treatment [tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, n = 54 (24.2%); chemotherapy alone, n = 6 (5.4%)] (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). With a median study follow-up of 16.1 months (range 0.0-27.2 months), there was a higher rate of voluntary withdrawal/loss to follow-up with chemotherapy alone [n = 15 (13.5%)] versus tislelizumab plus chemotherapy [n = 8 (3.6%)] at final analysis, similar to that observed at interim analysis [n = 12 (10.8%) and n = 9 (4.0%), respectively].

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics in the ITT population have been reported for the interim analysis5 (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

Treatment exposure

Median duration of exposure to tislelizumab was 2.5 weeks longer at final analysis [34.1 weeks (range 3.0-118.0 weeks)] versus interim analysis; the median number of tislelizumab treatment cycles increased from 10 cycles (range 1-25 cycles) to 10.5 cycles (range 1-37 cycles). Median relative dose intensity of tislelizumab was 93.8% (range 57.1%-103.3%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm. Exposure to cisplatin and carboplatin was high and similar between arms, with median relative dose intensity being ∼96%. Most patients received ≥9 cycles of tislelizumab [9-12 cycles, n = 43 (19.4%); >12 cycles, n = 94 (42.3%)]. The median number of carboplatin or cisplatin cycles was 4.0 (range 1-6 cycles) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and the chemotherapy alone arm. The median number of pemetrexed cycles was 9.5 (range 1-36 cycles) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 6.5 (range 1-31 cycles) in the chemotherapy alone arm. Subsequent systemic anticancer therapies were received by 47.5% (106/223) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 68.5% (76/111) of patients in the chemotherapy alone arm; 7.2% (16/223) and 50.5% (56/111) of patients, respectively, received subsequent immunotherapy. Of the 56 patients in the chemotherapy alone arm who received subsequent immunotherapy, 40 (71.4%) crossed over to tislelizumab monotherapy. Median time from randomization to subsequent immunotherapy was 35.1 weeks (range 6.0-76.9 weeks) and median time from end of chemotherapy treatment to subsequent immunotherapy was 2.6 weeks (range 0.1-49.0 weeks).

Safety and tolerability

The safety population included 222 patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 110 in the chemotherapy alone arm. All patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 109 patients (99.1%) in the chemotherapy alone arm experienced one or more TEAE (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728), and safety results were consistent with those at the interim analysis.5 The most frequently reported TEAEs were anemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia; these were mostly of grade 1 or 2 (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

While there was a higher incidence of ≥grade 3 TEAEs and serious TEAEs (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm, event rates appeared to be similar after exposure adjustment [exposure-adjusted event rate (EAER) for ≥grade 3 TEAEs: 25.8 versus 26.5 events/100 person-months with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone, respectively; EAER for serious TEAEs: 6.4 versus 5.5 events/100 person-months, respectively (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728)]. The higher incidence of ≥grade 3 TEAEs was mostly due to neutropenia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). Discontinuation of any treatment component due to TEAEs occurred in 30.6% and 10.0% of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and the chemotherapy alone arm, respectively, with the difference mainly driven through discontinuation of chemotherapy components (26.1% versus 10.0%, respectively). TEAEs leading to permanent discontinuation and dose modifications or treatment delays of tislelizumab occurred in 14.4% and 64.0% of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, respectively (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). Rates of treatment-related TEAEs were generally consistent with the overall TEAE profile (Supplementary Tables S3 and S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). Overall, TEAEs leading to death were reported in nine patients (4.1%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and two patients (1.8%) in the chemotherapy alone arm, and were considered as treatment-related in four patients (1.8%) and one patient (0.9%), respectively. Among these, three patients (1.4%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and one patient (0.9%) in the chemotherapy alone arm experienced pneumonitis events. The remaining patient (0.5%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm experienced myocarditis. Deaths due to AEs or disease progression within 30 days of the last dose date occurred in four patients (1.8%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and three patients (2.7%) in the chemotherapy alone arm.

EAERs per 100 person-months were lower in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm for any TEAE (287.9 versus 329.0, respectively) and for treatment-related TEAEs (234.2 versus 261.0, respectively) (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). EAERs per 100 person-months were similar in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm for TEAEs leading to death (0.4 versus 0.3, respectively) and TEAEs leading to discontinuation (3.9 versus 3.1, respectively) (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

ImAEs of all grades occurred in 45.0% (100/222) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 10.9% (12/110) of patients in the chemotherapy alone arm (Supplementary Table S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728). Pneumonitis, myocarditis/pericarditis, and endocrinopathies (diabetes mellitus) were the most common categories of ≥grade 3 imAEs, occurring in 4.1%, 1.4%, and 1.4% of patients, respectively, in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm; ≥grade 3 pneumonitis occurred in 0.9% of patients in the chemotherapy alone arm.

Progression-free survival

At final analysis DCO, 201 PFSIRC events were reported. The risk of disease progression or death was 37% lower with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone [stratified HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.47-0.86); stratified log-rank test, P = 0.0013]. Median PFSIRC was longer (9.8 months; 95% CI 8.9-11.7 months) with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone (7.6 months; 95% CI 5.6-8.0 months) (Figure 1A). Estimated PFSIRC event-free rates at 12 and 18 months were 39.9% (95% CI 32.8% to 46.8%) and 26.6% (95% CI 19.5% to 34.3%), respectively, in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, and 20.1% (95% CI 11.6% to 30.2%) and 11.3% (95% CI 4.6% to 21.2%), respectively, in the chemotherapy alone arm. The observed PFSIRC benefit with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone was maintained across most predefined subgroups (Figure 1B). When assessed by PD-L1 expression status, the PFSIRC HR for tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone was 0.83 (95% CI 0.53-1.28) in the TC PD-L1 expression <1% subgroup and 0.48 (95% CI 0.32-0.72) in the ≥1% subgroup (Figure 1B). The PFSIRC HR was 0.90 (95% CI 0.49-1.63) in the TC PD-L1 expression 1%-49% subgroup and 0.29 (95% CI 0.16-0.50) in the ≥50% subgroup (Figure 1B).

PFSINV outcomes were consistent with PFSIRC results, with a median PFSINV of 9.7 months (95% CI 7.7-11.7 months) with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and 5.6 months (95% CI 4.8-7.9 months) with chemotherapy alone (stratified HR 0.55; 95% CI 0.42-0.73) (Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

Antitumor activity

Superior antitumor responses and DoR assessed by IRC at final analysis were observed for patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm (Table 1), consistent with interim analysis. ORRIRC was 57.8% (95% CI 51.1% to 64.4%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 36.0% (95% CI 27.1% to 45.7%) in the chemotherapy alone arm. Among 129 responders who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, the median DoRIRC was 10.6 months (95% CI 8.4-15.8 months), whereas in the 40 responders who received chemotherapy alone, the median DoRIRC was 6.9 months (95% CI 5.0-10.6 months) (Table 1). Median time to response per IRC was 11.3 weeks in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 11.6 weeks in the chemotherapy alone arm. Complete response rate per IRC was higher with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (4.9%) versus chemotherapy alone (1.8%). ORRIRC was consistently higher in patients treated with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone across TC PD-L1 expression subgroups (<1%: 41.8% versus 27.1%, respectively; 1%-49%: 62.3% versus 44.4%, respectively; ≥50%: 73.0% versus 41.7%, respectively) (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

Overall survival

At final analysis DCO, 142 (42.5%) deaths occurred in the ITT population, 96 (43.0%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 46 (41.4%) in the chemotherapy alone arm, representing an additional 71 deaths (44 versus 27, respectively) since the interim analysis. Median OS was not reached in either arm at interim analysis, with a median follow-up time of 9.8 months.5 At final analysis, with a median follow-up time of 16.1 months, median OS was 21.4 months [95% CI 17.7 months-not estimable (NE)] in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 21.3 months [95% CI 15.6 months-NE] in the chemotherapy alone arm (stratified HR 0.90; 95% CI 0.63-1.28) (Figure 2A). The estimated OS event-free rates in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm were 76.4% versus 69.4%, respectively, at 12 months and 55.4% versus 55.3%, respectively, at 18 months. The OS results from subgroup analyses were exploratory and varied due to limited sample sizes (Supplementary Figure S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103728).

Median OS using the two-stage method to adjust for the effect of in-study crossover to tislelizumab on PPS was 21.4 months (95% CI 17.7 months-NE) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus 16.5 months (95% CI 14.1 months-NE) in the chemotherapy alone arm (stratified HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.47-1.07) (Figure 2B). The two-stage analysis showed OS improvement with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone and estimated a shrink parameter of 0.373 (indicating a 37.3% reduction in PPS time if patients would not have crossed over to tislelizumab).

In ad hoc updated analyses in the ITT population using a later DCO (15 July 2022) and with a median follow-up of 19.3 months, the stratified OS HR for the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm was 0.85 (95% CI 0.63-1.14) (Figure 3A). In the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms, respectively, 12-month OS rates (95% CI) were 76.7% (70.5% to 81.7%) and 68.2% (58.2% to 76.2%), 24-month OS rates were 47.0% (40.3% to 53.5%) and 44.1% (34.1% to 53.6%), and 36-month OS rates were 38.0% (31.5% to 44.5%) and 31.8% (22.7% to 41.3%). At the later DCO, subsequent immunotherapy had been received by 52.3% (58/111) of patients in the chemotherapy alone arm [of whom 72.4% (42/58) crossed over to tislelizumab] and by 10.8% (24/223) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm. Among those in the chemotherapy alone arm who crossed over to tislelizumab, median time from last dose of chemotherapy to subsequent tislelizumab was 2.6 weeks (range 0.1-34.6 weeks). At this later DCO, the stratified OS HR from the two-stage supportive analysis was also slightly lower versus final analysis [0.68; 95% CI 0.48-0.96 (Figure 3B)]. In this supportive analysis, in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms, respectively, 12-month OS rates (95% CI) were 76.7% (70.5% to 81.7%) and 60.7% (50.4% to 69.4%), 24-month OS rates were 47.0% (40.3% to 53.5%) and 35.1% (24.1% to 46.3%), and 36-month OS rates were 38.0% (31.5% to 44.5%) and 25.4% (15.4% to 36.7%).

Discussion

The final results from RATIONALE-304 are consistent with, and expand upon, the positive results reported at interim analysis.5 With longer follow-up, tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy continued to demonstrate prolonged PFSIRC versus chemotherapy alone as first-line treatment of advanced/metastatic nsq-NSCLC. PFS benefits with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy were consistent across most patient subgroups. Greater PFS benefit with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy over chemotherapy alone was observed in the TC PD-L1 expression ≥1% subgroup (HR 0.48) than in the TC PD-L1 expression <1% subgroup (HR 0.83). This difference is likely largely driven by the PFS benefits in the TC PD-L1 expression ≥50% subgroup (HR 0.29), consistent with previous studies of PD-1 pathway inhibition in advanced NSCLC.6,7

At final analysis, median OS in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm was 21.4 months versus 21.3 months in the chemotherapy alone arm (stratified HR 0.90); the OS HR was maintained at the subsequent later ad hoc DCO (stratified HR 0.85). In RATIONALE-304, crossover to tislelizumab monotherapy was permitted for patients randomized to the chemotherapy alone arm who had IRC-confirmed disease progression, as patients might benefit from tislelizumab versus docetaxel as second-line and above treatment, based on superior efficacy demonstrated in other studies with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies.13, 14, 15, 16 Thus, RATIONALE-304 was designed to demonstrate PFSIRC superiority, as there was no intent to detect OS superiority under a crossover design. In addition, OS assessment can be confounded by voluntary withdrawal, loss to follow-up, and effective subsequent lines of therapy, including in-trial crossover.17 Given the open-label study design of RATIONALE-304, more patients in the chemotherapy alone arm (13.5%) withdrew or were lost to follow-up than in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm (3.6%). At the latest DCO, 52.3% of patients had received subsequent immunotherapy in the chemotherapy alone arm, of whom most (72.4%) had crossed over to tislelizumab. These patients were expected to have an OS improvement, similar to the OS efficacy demonstrated in the RATIONALE-303 study of tislelizumab monotherapy as second-/third-line therapy for patients with advanced/metastatic NSCLC.10 It was notable that, possibly due to the crossover design, median OS in the chemotherapy alone arm of RATIONALE-304 (20.1 months) was longer than in the control arms of KEYNOTE-189 (10.6 months)6,17 and IMpower130 (13.9 months).18 Moreover, within the subgroup that crossed over to tislelizumab, the median time from last dose of chemotherapy to subsequent tislelizumab was 2.6 weeks (minimum time to crossover: 0.1 weeks). Although caution should be exercised when making cross-trial comparisons, crossover to tislelizumab in the chemotherapy arm in RATIONALE-304 was more common and occurred within a shorter timeframe post-chemotherapy than crossover to pembrolizumab in the chemotherapy arm in the global KEYNOTE-189 trial.17 In the final OS analysis of KEYNOTE-189, 40.8% of patients from the chemotherapy arm crossed over to pembrolizumab,17 and a minimum interval of 3 weeks or one cycle following the last dose of chemotherapy was required before initiating pembrolizumab.6,17 To assess the impact of crossover on OS benefit in RATIONALE-304, a supportive two-stage method analysis was carried out, which showed a trend toward improvement in OS in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm. The improved OS HR in the two-stage supportive analysis (stratified HR 0.71) relative to that in the main analysis (stratified HR 0.90) at final analysis DCO, and similarly in the two-stage supportive analysis (stratified HR 0.68) relative to that in the main analysis (stratified HR 0.85) at the ad hoc DCO, suggests that the OS benefit observed in the chemotherapy arm of the RATIONALE-304 study was partially attributed to in-study crossover and the subsequent use of second-line tislelizumab. In the absence of crossover, a clear OS benefit for tislelizumab was demonstrated in the RATIONALE-303 study. In RATIONALE-303, patients whose disease progressed on first-line platinum-based chemotherapy greatly benefited from tislelizumab monotherapy versus docetaxel, with a 29% reduction in the risk of death for patients with nsq-NSCLC (HR 0.71) and median OS of 18.6 versus 13.8 months, respectively.10

Overall, when using the two-stage method, OS results suggest the possibility of a survival benefit for tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in RATIONALE-304. Additionally, median OS in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm of RATIONALE-304 (21.4 months at final analysis DCO) was comparable with immunotherapy arms in global first-line NSCLC studies, including KEYNOTE-189 (22.0 months),6,17 CheckMate 9LA (15.6 months),19 IMpower130 (18.6 months),18 IMpower132 (17.5 months),20 and IMpower150 (19.0 months)21 and with other studies conducted in China, such as ORIENT-11 (24.2 months).22,23 Additionally, median OS in the chemotherapy arm of RATIONALE-304 (21.3 months) was longer than in the comparator chemotherapy arms in KEYNOTE-189 (10.6 months),6,17 CheckMate 9LA (10.9 months),19 IMpower130 (13.9 months),18 IMpower132 (13.6 months),20 IMpower150 (14.7 months),21 ORIENT-11 (16.8 months),22,23 and CHOICE-1 (17.1 months)24 and was comparable with that in CameL (20.9 months).25 Heterogeneity in study populations and/or trial designs (such as differences in subsequent immunotherapy regimens in the chemotherapy alone arms), however, may have influenced OS analyses in these trials.

No new safety signals were detected with the longer follow-up time at final analysis versus interim analysis. The higher cumulative incidence of serious TEAEs in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm (39.2% versus 22.7%, respectively) is to be expected, given that the duration of exposure to treatment was longer in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm. EAER data demonstrated that tislelizumab plus chemotherapy did not exacerbate toxicity from chemotherapy. The comparable incidences of TEAEs, serious AEs, TEAEs leading to death, and TEAEs leading to permanent discontinuation at final analysis versus interim analysis demonstrated no new safety signals associated with additional exposure to treatment with tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy. This indicates that tislelizumab has an acceptable safety profile for continued use.

A potential limitation of the current study is the enrollment of only Chinese patients, which may limit the generalizability of these data. RATIONALE-304 also excluded patients with known sensitizing EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements, and the potential value of checkpoint inhibitors in such patients remains unclear. The two-stage method is a recognized methodology for adjustment of crossover effect on OS,12 which is applicable when treatment switching can only occur after a specific disease-related time point, such as disease progression. To effectively apply the two-stage method, covariates that could impact PPS and were not balanced between switchers and non-switchers were identified and fitted in an accelerated failure time model to estimate the crossover effect. The potential bias of the two-stage method under this assumption was somewhat reduced after justifying all the covariates collected from the study.

Overall, RATIONALE-304 provides clinical evidence for the benefit of tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy versus platinum-based chemotherapy alone. Tislelizumab with platinum and pemetrexed had favorable PFS benefit and was generally well tolerated, with acceptable toxicity, as a first-line treatment in patients with locally advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC.

Strict carbohydrate restriction enables volume-based FDG-PET/CT to outperform SUVmax for risk stratification in cardiac sarcoidosis
Authors: Yoshimitsu Fukushima, Takahiro Ando, Kenji Yodogawa, Tsutomu Igarashi, Hiromitsu Hayashi, Shinichiro Kumita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFDG-PET/CT SUVmax reflects focal metabolic intensity but not the patchy inflammatory burden of cardiac sarcoidosis (CS). We evaluated whether volume-based total cardiac metabolic volume (tCMV) and total cardiac metabolic activity (tCMA), measured after optimized suppression, predict long-term major adverse cardiac events (MACE) better than SUVmax.MethodsForty-eight consecutive patients with CS classified by JCS 2016 criteria underwent FDG-PET/CT after 24-h strict carbohydrate restriction without high-fat supplementation and delayed cardiac imaging at 120 min. tCMV, tCMA, and SUVmax were quantified at a fixed SUV threshold of 1.70. MACE was assessed over a median of 7.3 years. DeLong tests and Firth-penalized Cox regression evaluated prognostic performance.ResultsMACE occurred in 18 of 48 patients (37.5%). tCMA (AUC, 0.766) and tCMV (AUC, 0.764) outperformed SUVmax (AUC, 0.606; DeLong P = 0.008 and 0.013). High tCMA predicted higher MACE rates (log-rank P = 0.0003). In multivariate Firth-penalized Cox regression, tCMA (HR per unit, 1.0010 [95% CI, 1.0005–1.0016]; HR per IQR, 1.4093 [1.1671–1.7019]; P = 0.0002) and NYHA class (HR, 2.7460 [1.6209–4.6521]; P = 0.0002) were independently associated with MACE. Adding tCMA to NYHA class and troponin T provided modest incremental discrimination (ΔC-index, 0.081; 95% CI, 0.001–0.206).ConclusionsUnder optimized suppression, tCMA captured global inflammatory burden rather than focal intensity and improved MACE risk stratification in CS. Volume-based quantification may complement SUVmax and conventional risk markers, pending external validation.

Introduction

Cardiac sarcoidosis (CS) is characterized by non-caseating granulomatous infiltration of the myocardium, which disrupts electrical conduction and impairs contractile function through three successive histological stages: edema with inflammatory cell infiltration, granuloma formation, and subsequent fibrotic scarring [1], [2], [3]. Unlike ischemic cardiomyopathy, in which the extent and territory of injury are largely determined at the time of coronary occlusion, the inflammatory burden in CS fluctuates over time. It follows a characteristically patchy, multifocal distribution that does not conform to coronary vascular territories [4], [5], [6], [7]. This spatiotemporal heterogeneity poses a fundamental challenge to risk stratification. Patients with identical left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) values may harbor vastly different volumes of active granulomatous disease, and sudden cardiac death (SCD) can occur even in the absence of significant systolic dysfunction [8], [9]. Cardiac involvement accounts for a substantial proportion of sarcoidosis-related mortality [6], [10], underscoring the need for imaging-based approaches that directly quantify the burden and activity of myocardial inflammation.

Conventional prognostic markers in CS, namely LVEF, N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and New York Heart Association functional class, predominantly reflect the hemodynamic consequences of cumulative myocardial injury rather than the inflammatory process driving disease progression. Updated diagnostic frameworks accordingly emphasize directly characterizing the myocardial inflammatory substrate [11], [12]. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) with late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) has shown consistent prognostic value in CS, particularly when enhancement involves multiple ventricular segments or exceeds defined volume thresholds [13], [14], [15], [16]. Because LGE reflects both replacement fibrosis and active granulomatous infiltration, it cannot, by itself, distinguish irreversible scar from potentially treatable active disease. In contrast, 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (FDG-PET/CT) visualizes active myocardial inflammation, an earlier and potentially reversible stage of the disease [17], [18], [19]. However, volume-based quantification critically depends on complete suppression of physiological uptake and on the delineation threshold. Residual uptake can produce spurious volume estimates and confound prognostic assessment.

The maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) is the most commonly used metric in FDG-PET/CT imaging. However, it reflects only the single voxel with the highest FDG uptake and does not capture the spatial extent or total volume of inflammatory involvement [20]. This limitation is particularly relevant in CS, where myocardial inflammation characteristically exhibits a patchy, multifocal distribution that is poorly represented by focal intensity alone. Reliance on SUVmax may thus systematically underestimate the true inflammatory burden and inadequately stratify patients by disease severity.

To overcome this limitation, quantitative FDG-PET/CT parameters extending beyond focal SUVmax have been developed and evaluated for cardiac sarcoidosis, including serial uptake monitoring and volume-based measures such as cardiac metabolic volume (CMV) and cardiac metabolic activity (CMA), which integrate both the extent and intensity of FDG uptake [21], [22], [23]. CMV, defined for each inflammatory lesion, reflects the volume of myocardium demonstrating elevated FDG uptake above a given threshold. CMA, calculated as the product of CMV and mean SUV within that lesion, represents the metabolic activity of each focus and is conceptually analogous to total lesion glycolysis (TLG) in oncologic PET imaging [24]. When these per-lesion values are summed across all myocardial foci, the resulting total CMV (tCMV) and total CMA (tCMA) provide an integrated measure of global inflammatory burden. In the context of CS, tCMA may thus capture the cumulative pathophysiological impact of active inflammation across the entire myocardium, a dimension that neither SUVmax nor per-lesion CMV/CMA alone can represent.

Previous studies of volume-based FDG-PET/CT parameters in CS have adopted varying lesion-delineation thresholds, including patient-specific blood-pool-referenced values and empirically selected fixed SUV cutoffs [20], [25], [26], [27], [28]. Such thresholds are inherently sensitive to the completeness of physiological FDG suppression, which depends on the dietary preparation protocol and acquisition timing. Building on our pilot study, in which 24-h strict carbohydrate restriction with a low-carbohydrate, high-fat supplement near-completely suppressed physiological myocardial FDG uptake [28], the present study used strict carbohydrate restriction alone, without a high-fat supplement, in an Atkins-style protocol to avoid high-fat-induced insulin stimulation of myocardial glucose uptake [29]. This degree of suppression enabled a fixed SUV threshold of 1.70 for volumetric delineation, substantially lower than in prior studies, minimizing threshold-dependent variability and improving sensitivity for low-grade inflammatory lesions. Cardiac imaging was additionally acquired 120 min after FDG administration, allowing further physiological washout and enhancing lesion-to-background contrast. Despite these methodological advances, the prognostic significance of volume-based parameters quantified under these optimized conditions has not been evaluated against long-term major adverse cardiac events (MACE).

It was hypothesized that volume-based FDG-PET/CT parameters, quantified under stringent dietary suppression with a low fixed SUV threshold and delayed cardiac imaging, would provide superior discriminative performance and independent prognostic value compared with conventional SUVmax. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to evaluate the prognostic value of total cardiac metabolic volume (tCMV) and total cardiac metabolic activity (tCMA) derived from FDG-PET/CT in patients with CS, and to compare their performance with SUVmax for predicting long-term MACE.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study was a prospective observational study. Seventy-two consecutive patients with suspected CS were screened between January 2015 and December 2019, corresponding to the period during which an Atkins-style strict carbohydrate restriction protocol without a high-fat dietary supplement was employed for physiological myocardial FDG suppression at our institution. After excluding 8 patients with alternative diagnoses (infectious myocarditis, n = 2; dilated cardiomyopathy, n = 2; hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, n = 3; cardiac amyloidosis, n = 1), 64 patients with confirmed CS remained. Of these, 16 patients who underwent multiple FDG-PET/CT examinations during the study period were excluded to avoid informative censoring and correlated within-patient measurements, which would violate the independence assumption of the single-baseline prognostic model used in this study. Restricting the cohort to baseline-only scans ensured methodological clarity for evaluating the prognostic value of baseline volumetric parameters for the first occurrence of MACE. This yielded a final study population of 48 patients.

CS diagnosis was initially made prospectively using the JCS 2006 diagnostic criteria in force at the time of enrollment. After publication of the updated Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Cardiac Sarcoidosis (JCS 2016) [10], all enrolled patients were retrospectively reassessed against the JCS 2016 criteria, and the final diagnostic classification reported herein is based on this harmonized assessment. The reclassification did not change the inclusion or exclusion status of any patient, because the major diagnostic domains (histologic, clinical-major, clinical-minor) overlap substantially between the two versions.

Study protocol

The study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines established by the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Central Ethics Committee of Nippon Medical School and classified as a prospective non-interventional study. Consent was obtained through an IRB-approved opt-out procedure, with study information and the opportunity to decline participation made publicly available on the hospital website.

To suppress physiological FDG uptake in the myocardium, patients were placed on a 24-h strict carbohydrate restriction (glucose intake <10 g over the 24 h preceding FDG administration) [28]. Whole-body and cardiac PET/CT scans were acquired at 60 min and 120 min, respectively, after intravenous administration of FDG (4 MBq per kg of body weight) using a PET/CT scanner with 16-slice CT (GEMINI TF 16, Philips Healthcare, Tokyo, Japan). Whole-body PET scans from head to mid-thigh were obtained at 5 min per bed position, whereas cardiac PET scans were obtained over 10 min in 1 bed position. Images were reconstructed using Time-of-Flight and 3D-OSEM algorithms with a voxel size of 4 × 4 × 4 mm. In addition, low-dose CT scans were obtained (120 kV, 100 mAs, collimation: 16 × 1.5 mm, pitch: 0.69, slice thickness: 3 mm, increment: 1.5 mm). CT attenuation correction was used to create PET images, and fused PET/CT images were created.

Patient blood test data, including angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), troponin T (TnT), N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), C-reactive protein (CRP), and blood glucose (BG), were obtained at the time of FDG administration.

Data analysis

Volumes of interest in the myocardium were automatically drawn using the accumulation quantification software syngo.via (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany), with a threshold SUV of 1.70, as described by Kobayashi et al. [28]. CMV and CMA were calculated for each lesion and for the overall analysis. Patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on SUVmax, total CMV (tCMV), and total CMA (tCMA) to assess the clinical significance of these parameters. The cutoff values of the indices were determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

Evaluation of prognosis

Patients were followed from the initial FDG-PET/CT scan until the last clinical visit or until an event occurred. The median follow-up duration was 7.3 [6.4–8.6] years. The primary endpoint was the occurrence of major adverse cardiac events (MACE), defined as the composite of cardiac death, hospitalization due to deterioration of heart failure, or development of sustained ventricular tachycardia. Sustained ventricular tachycardia was defined per the JCS/JHRS 2020 Guideline on Pharmacotherapy of Cardiac Arrhythmias as a ventricular rhythm at a rate ≥ 100 beats/min lasting ≥30 s documented by electrocardiography, Holter monitoring, or implantable cardioverter-defibrillator interrogation, or requiring termination by device therapy or external cardioversion [30]. Non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (<30 s) was not counted as a MACE component. Secondary endpoints included analysis of each component of MACE. Patients were censored at the date of the last visit if no event had occurred.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD for normally distributed data. Non-normally distributed data, such as SUVmax, tCMV, and tCMA, were presented as medians with 25th and 75th percentiles. Categorical variables were presented as counts (%).

The Mann-Whitney U test and Pearson chi-square test were used to compare the results between the high and low groups. ROC curves using the Youden index [31] were used to determine optimal cutoffs for SUVmax, tCMV, and tCMA to predict the primary endpoint. The proportion of event-free patients was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the high and low SUVmax, tCMV, and tCMA groups were compared using the log-rank test. Firth's penalized likelihood method was applied to Cox proportional hazards regression analysis to reduce small-sample bias in parameter estimation. Univariate Cox regression was performed on 24 candidate variables spanning clinical, laboratory, echocardiographic, FDG-PET/CT, device, and medication categories (Supplementary Table S1). Given the limited number of events, univariate comparisons are reported as exploratory; only effects robust in the multivariate model were interpreted as primary findings. For the multivariate model, tCMA was forced as the primary variable. Additional covariates were selected from univariate-significant variables (P < 0.05) based on AIC, clinical relevance, and multicollinearity avoidance, with constraints imposed by the events-per-variable ratio. For interpretability, HRs for tCMA and tCMV were also computed per 1 SD, 1 IQR, and 100-unit increase. Robustness was evaluated by L2 (ridge)-penalized Cox regression across penalties λ = 0.01–1.0. Pairwise comparisons of the areas under the ROC curves (AUCs) were performed using the DeLong method for paired AUCs. Incremental discrimination from adding tCMA (and tCMV) to a base clinical model (NYHA class and troponin T) was assessed by the change in Harrell's C-index with 2000-iteration bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and by category-free net reclassification improvement and integrated discrimination improvement at a 5-year horizon; given the limited number of events, the reclassification metrics were interpreted as supportive. A sensitivity analysis repeated the primary association using a more stringent composite endpoint of cardiac death and sustained ventricular tachycardia. The dates of disease onset and diagnosis were determined by chart review; the onset-to-diagnosis, onset-to-FDG-PET, and diagnosis-to-FDG-PET intervals were summarized, and a sensitivity analysis stratified the cohort at the median onset-to-FDG-PET interval. In a pre-specified exploratory analysis restricted to the subset who underwent cardiac magnetic resonance before FDG-PET/CT, patients were cross-classified by tCMA status (using the ROC-derived cutoff) and LGE status, and the resulting four groups were compared by the log-rank test. Reported sensitivity and specificity are apparent (in-sample) values, without bootstrap optimism correction or external validation.

No a priori sample size calculation was performed. The cohort comprised consecutive patients enrolled during the unified Atkins-style protocol window (January 2015 to December 2019); patients were followed until the last clinical visit or the occurrence of a MACE event. The achieved events-per-variable ratio of 6.0 is below the conventional 10-EPV heuristic for unpenalized Cox but is supported for penalized methods that mitigate small-sample bias.

P values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Primary descriptive, ROC, and Cox analyses were performed using BellCurve for Excel (Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Revision-specific model-search, incremental-discrimination, hard-endpoint, onset-stratified, and independent-verification analyses were performed using Python scripts archived in the Analysis directory, with lifelines, statsmodels, and custom Firth-penalized Cox routines.

Patient prognoses

The median SUVmax was 3.72 (IQR, 2.74–5.75), the median tCMV was 50.17 (IQR, 19.50–142.84) mL, and the median tCMA was 100.23 (IQR, 39.12–382.42) (Table 2). Of the 27 patients who underwent cardiac MRI before FDG-PET/CT, 21 (77.8%) demonstrated LGE. During the follow-up period, 18 patients (37.5%) experienced MACE (cardiac death [n = 3], hospitalization due to deterioration of heart failure [n = 15], and sustained ventricular tachycardia [n = 6]), whereas 30 patients (62.5%) remained event-free. Individual patients could contribute more than one MACE component.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to determine optimal cutoff values for predicting the primary endpoint of MACE (Table 3). For tCMA, the optimal cutoff was 96.28, yielding a sensitivity of 88.9% and a specificity of 66.7% (area under the curve [AUC], 0.766). For tCMV, the optimal cutoff was 51.87 mL, with a sensitivity of 83.3% and a specificity of 70.0% (AUC, 0.764). For SUVmax, the optimal cutoff was 8.72, achieving a sensitivity of 27.8% and a specificity of 96.7% (AUC, 0.606). For LVEF, the optimal cutoff was 42.0%, with a sensitivity of 66.7% and a specificity of 86.7% (AUC, 0.771). Among these parameters, tCMA and tCMV demonstrated higher AUC values and more balanced sensitivity–specificity profiles than SUVmax (Supplementary Fig. S1). Pairwise DeLong comparisons confirmed that tCMA and tCMV both provided statistically significantly greater discrimination than SUVmax (tCMA vs SUVmax: ΔAUC = 0.160 [95% CI, 0.043–0.278], P = 0.008; tCMV vs SUVmax: ΔAUC = 0.158 [95% CI, 0.033–0.284], P = 0.013), whereas tCMA and tCMV did not differ significantly from each other (ΔAUC = 0.002, P = 0.859). LVEF also showed high discriminative performance (AUC, 0.771), although its ΔAUC vs SUVmax did not reach statistical significance (ΔAUC = 0.166 [95% CI, −0.092–0.424], P = 0.208), likely reflecting limited power in this cohort. NYHA class showed modest discrimination (AUC, 0.667; cutoff 2.00; sensitivity 38.9%, specificity 93.3%). Based on the cutoff values, patients were assigned to high- and low-parameter groups: tCMA (26 and 22 patients, respectively), tCMV (24 and 24, respectively), LVEF (16 and 32, respectively), SUVmax (6 and 42, respectively), and NYHA class (9 and 39, respectively). Among the 26 high-tCMA patients, 16 (61.5%) experienced MACE, compared with 2 of 22 low-tCMA patients (9.1%). Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that the MACE rate was significantly higher in the high-risk groups than in the low-risk groups across all parameters (log-rank P = 0.0003 for tCMA, 0.0006 for tCMV, 0.0003 for LVEF, 0.0004 for SUVmax, and 0.0006 for NYHA class) (Fig. 2).

On Firth-penalized Cox proportional hazards regression analysis (Table 4), univariate analysis identified significant associations for tCMA (HR per unit, 1.0009; 95% CI, 1.0004–1.0014; P = 0.0002), tCMV (HR per unit, 1.0028; 95% CI, 1.0012–1.0043; P = 0.0004), SUVmax (HR, 1.1831; 95% CI, 1.0409–1.3447; P = 0.0101), LVEF (HR, 0.9443; 95% CI, 0.9129–0.9767; P = 0.0009), NYHA class (HR, 2.2491; 95% CI, 1.4246–3.5508; P = 0.0005), troponin T, and NT-proBNP. To improve clinical interpretability, rescaled hazard ratios were computed (Table 4): for tCMA, the HR per 1-IQR increase (343 units) was 1.3724 (95% CI, 1.1610–1.6224), per 1-SD increase (723 units) was 1.9476 (95% CI, 1.3693–2.7701), and per 100-unit increase was 1.0966 (95% CI, 1.0444–1.1514). For multivariate model construction, tCMA was specified as the primary study variable. Among candidate models evaluated by AIC comparison with tCMA forced, the model including tCMA, troponin T, and NYHA class yielded a near-optimal, parsimonious fit with an events-per-variable ratio of 6.0. NYHA class was selected over LVEF because it was uncorrelated with the forced primary variable tCMA (Spearman r = −0.023, P = 0.878), whereas LVEF and NYHA class were themselves substantially correlated, making their joint inclusion redundant. In this multivariate Firth model, tCMA (HR per unit, 1.0010; 95% CI, 1.0005–1.0016; HR per IQR of 343 units, 1.4093 [95% CI, 1.1671–1.7019]; P = 0.0002) and NYHA class (HR, 2.7460; 95% CI, 1.6209–4.6521; P = 0.0002) remained independently associated with MACE, whereas troponin T retained nominal statistical significance (P = 0.042). Its per-unit hazard ratio is uninterpretable because troponin T spans a narrow concentration range, giving a per-unit estimate whose 95% CI spans several orders of magnitude. The hazard ratio is therefore expressed per interquartile-range increase of 0.014 ng/mL, 1.2158 (95% CI, 1.0074–1.4674). A sensitivity analysis using L2-penalized (ridge) Cox regression demonstrated that the direction of the tCMA and NYHA effects was preserved across a broad range of penalty strengths (λ = 0.01, 0.10, and 1.0). The effect magnitude attenuated at the strongest penalty (λ = 1.0), as expected under aggressive shrinkage. Incremental discrimination and sensitivity analyses

Adding tCMA to a base clinical model (NYHA class and troponin T; C-index 0.744) increased Harrell's C-index to 0.825, with a ΔC-index of 0.081 (2000-iteration bootstrap 95% CI, 0.001–0.206); the category-free net reclassification improvement (NRI) was 0.91 (95% CI, 0.08–1.42) and the integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) was 0.192 (95% CI, 0.024–0.386) (Table 5). For tCMV, the ΔC-index was 0.064 (95% CI, −0.008–0.202), with NRI 0.79 (95% CI, 0.05–1.41) and IDI 0.159 (95% CI, 0.013–0.337). These analyses indicate incremental discrimination when tCMA is added to a minimal base clinical model (NYHA class and troponin T), although the lower bound of the ΔC-index confidence interval (0.001) lies close to the null. Because the NRI and IDI confidence intervals were wide, consistent with the limited number of events, these reclassification metrics are interpreted as supportive, alongside the pairwise DeLong comparisons against SUVmax. In a sensitivity analysis using a more stringent composite endpoint of cardiac death and sustained ventricular tachycardia (8 events, heart-failure hospitalization excluded), tCMA remained associated with outcome (Firth-penalized Cox HR per unit, 1.0008; 95% CI, 1.0002–1.0015; P = 0.0162), and patients with high tCMA had worse event-free survival than those with low tCMA (7 versus 1 events, log-rank P = 0.0453).

The median interval from disease onset to diagnosis was 3.4 months (IQR, 1.0–11.8), from disease onset to FDG-PET/CT 21.2 months (IQR, 6.1–40.9), and from diagnosis to FDG-PET/CT 6.2 months (IQR, 1.3–25.1). When the cohort was stratified at the median onset-to-FDG-PET interval (21.2 months), the prognostic separation by tCMA was preserved in both the longer-interval stratum (24 patients, high versus low tCMA, 8 of 12 versus 1 of 12 events, log-rank P = 0.0045) and the shorter-interval stratum (24 patients, high versus low tCMA, 8 of 14 versus 1 of 10 events, log-rank P = 0.0326, Firth-penalized Cox HR per unit, 1.0009, 95% CI, 1.0001–1.0016; P = 0.0201). In the longer-interval stratum, the Cox point estimate was not estimable because high tCMA almost completely separated events; the Kaplan-Meier/log-rank result and event split are therefore reported. Thus, comparable prognostic value was obtained in patients with a considerable time since onset.

Exploratory PET + LGE subgroup analysis

A pre-specified exploratory analysis combined tCMA status (high vs low using the ROC-derived cutoff of 96.28) with LGE status on cardiac magnetic resonance in 27 of 48 patients (56.3%) who underwent CMR before FDG-PET/CT (21 of 27, 77.8% demonstrated LGE). Four-group comparison demonstrated a clear stratification gradient (log-rank P = 0.017): patients with both tCMA-high and LGE-positive findings experienced MACE in 6 of 9 cases (66.7%), whereas patients in the three remaining combinations (tCMA-high/LGE-negative, tCMA-low/LGE-positive, tCMA-low/LGE-negative) accumulated only 2 of 18 events (11.1%). The small number of events in the CMR-imaged subgroup limited statistical power for formal combined multivariable modeling. This analysis is therefore presented as hypothesis-generating only. Numerically, the absolute event rate in the tCMA-high/LGE-positive group (66.7%) was approximately six-fold higher than that in the remaining three groups combined (11.1%), suggesting a disproportionate, rather than merely additive, increase in risk when active inflammation and established fibrosis coexist. The directional consistency of this gradient supports the conceptual framework of complementary information from FDG (active disease) and LGE (fibrotic substrate) in CS risk stratification.

Discussion

This study provides the first long-term prognostic evaluation of volume-based FDG-PET/CT quantified under high-fat-free strict carbohydrate restriction with a fixed SUV threshold of 1.70 and delayed imaging. In this prospective observational study, we demonstrated that volume-based quantitative parameters derived from FDG-PET/CT, specifically tCMV and tCMA, provide significant prognostic information for predicting the primary endpoint, MACE, in patients with cardiac sarcoidosis. Patients in the MACE group exhibited substantially higher tCMV and tCMA values than those in the non-MACE group, and these differences were statistically significant. In the multivariate Cox regression analysis, tCMA remained an independent predictor of MACE, along with NYHA functional class. In contrast, troponin T retained only nominal statistical significance. Its per-unit hazard ratio was uninterpretable given the narrow concentration range, so it is reported per interquartile range (Results). This effect attenuated under ridge penalization and likely reflects shared variance with NYHA class and the concentration of myocardial injury within the more symptomatic (and hence higher-NYHA) subset of the cohort. The discriminative performance of tCMA and tCMV, as assessed by ROC analysis, was superior to that of SUVmax.

The two representative cases illustrate the contrasting patterns of volume-based parameters in patients at divergent risk levels. Despite similar NYHA class I status and positive CMR findings, the low tCMV and tCMA in the first case were consistent with prolonged event-free survival. In contrast, the markedly elevated values in the second case indicated an imminent risk of MACE.

The pathophysiological characteristics of CS may help explain the superior prognostic performance of tCMA compared with SUVmax. tCMA quantifies the global burden of myocardial granulomatous inflammation by integrating the spatial extent and metabolic intensity of active disease. Extensive granulomatous inflammation increases the substrate for both progressive myocardial dysfunction and arrhythmogenesis through several mechanisms. Active granulomatous inflammation leads to subsequent fibrosis, which disrupts standard myocardial architecture and conduction pathways [7]. Progressive fibrotic replacement of functional myocardium contributes to systolic dysfunction and heart failure, while patchy fibrosis creates the heterogeneous substrate necessary for reentrant ventricular arrhythmias [7]. Therefore, tCMA may serve as an integrated marker capturing both arrhythmogenic substrate formation and the propensity for heart failure progression, thereby explaining its association with the composite MACE endpoint.

A methodological strength of this study is the rigorous dietary preparation used to suppress physiological myocardial FDG uptake, a prerequisite for accurate volumetric quantification. In our pilot study, Kobayashi et al. showed that 24-h strict carbohydrate restriction (glucose <10 g) with an LCHF supplement nearly completely suppressed physiological uptake, reducing left ventricular SUVmax from 2.98 to 1.31 compared with conventional fasting [28]. However, high-fat loading stimulates insulin secretion, and insulin promotes myocardial GLUT4 translocation and glucose uptake, paradoxically enhancing myocardial FDG uptake until insulin declines 6–8 h later [29]. The present study, therefore, omitted the high-fat supplement. An Atkins-style protocol (glucose <10 g for 24 h) promotes substrate switching to free fatty acids in the absence of insulin stimulation. Combined with a fixed SUV threshold of 1.70 and delayed imaging at 120 min, this maximizes the reliability of volume-based quantification. A fixed threshold offers procedural simplicity, although the absolute SUV value depends on scanner calibration, reconstruction, and dietary preparation. The 1.70 cutoff, therefore, requires cross-platform harmonization or per-site recalibration before use at other institutions, whereas referenced thresholds based on the blood pool or descending aorta may transfer more robustly across sites.

The weaker prognostic performance of SUVmax and tCMV relative to tCMA is best understood in the context of how volume-based parameters were developed for CS. SUVmax reflects only the highest-uptake voxel and is susceptible to noise and partial-volume effects [32]; more fundamentally, focal measurements cannot distinguish a single highly active lesion from extensive but moderately active disease, despite the latter likely carrying greater prognostic weight. This mismatch with the patchy, multifocal distribution of CS pathology [4], [5], [6] prompted the use of volumetric approaches. Ahmadian et al. adapted metabolic tumor volume and total lesion glycolysis to CS, proposing per-lesion CMV and CMA that integrate spatial extent and metabolic intensity [22], [24]; CMA outperformed visual assessment and SUVmax for predicting LVEF decline, arrhythmias, and heart failure [21], [22]. We extended this concept by summing per-lesion values into tCMV and tCMA to capture the global inflammatory burden. tCMV reflects spatial extent only and cannot separate widespread low-grade from limited high-grade inflammation, whereas tCMA weights inflammation volume by metabolic intensity.

Accurate quantification of CMV/CMA and their summed counterparts requires careful choice of the threshold defining abnormal FDG uptake. Whereas earlier studies used left ventricular blood-pool SUVmax × 1.5 [21], [22], later work favored the descending aorta as a more robust reference. Furuya et al. showed that steroid therapy increases hepatic uptake, whereas the descending aorta pool remains stable [33]. Manabe et al. established a descending-aorta SUVmean × 1.5 threshold with excellent interobserver reproducibility, unaffected by fasting duration [34], [35]. Adequate suppression of physiological uptake is essential. Prolonged fasting (>18 h) achieves complete suppression (CMV = 0 mL), enabling accurate delineation [34]. Our 24-h carbohydrate restriction with a fixed SUV threshold of 1.70 [28] yielded reproducible tCMV and tCMA measurements.

Tuominen et al. found that total cardiac metabolic activity exceeding 900 MBq and right ventricular FDG uptake strongly predicted cardiovascular events, including ventricular tachycardia, LVEF decline, and death [36]; their tCMA was computed on a radioactivity basis (MBq) rather than the SUV-based calculation (SUV × mL) used here and by Ahmadian et al. [22], so absolute values are not directly comparable across studies. The coefficient of variation of myocardial FDG uptake likewise correlates with disease severity and treatment response, supporting the view that global inflammatory characteristics, rather than focal intensity, drive outcome [37], [38]. Specific distribution patterns also carry prognostic weight, as Manabe et al. linked interventricular-septal uptake to atrioventricular block [38]. Our study extends these regional and pattern-based observations by integrating inflammatory activity across the entire myocardium into a single quantitative parameter, tCMA.

The clinical implications of our findings merit consideration. tCMA may facilitate risk-adapted management strategies in CS. Patients with elevated tCMA values may warrant more aggressive immunosuppressive therapy and closer surveillance. Whether tCMA should inform the timing of guideline-directed device evaluation remains to be established in externally validated cohorts. In contrast, those with lower values might be appropriate candidates for less intensive monitoring. The ILLUMINATE-CS registry has identified reduced LVEF, elevated BNP, history of ventricular arrhythmia, and post-diagnosis radiofrequency ablation as independent predictors of adverse outcomes in CS [39], underscoring the importance of early intervention before irreversible functional decline develops. tCMA, by quantifying the active inflammatory burden, may help identify patients who would benefit most from early, aggressive immunosuppressive treatment.

Furthermore, recent evidence has underscored the potential of volume-based parameters as quantitative endpoints for assessing treatment response. Ahmadian et al. demonstrated that patient-specific blood-pool-referenced CMV and CMA were superior to fixed SUV thresholds and SUVmax for identifying complete metabolic response to steroid therapy, as SUVmax reduction alone may fail to capture residual spatially distributed disease [24]. In the prospective PRESTIGE trial, Morimoto et al. quantified CMA using a liver-referenced threshold and demonstrated a dramatic reduction in median CMA from 203.3 to 1.0 after six months of prednisolone therapy; responders, defined by a 70% or greater reduction in CMA, experienced significantly fewer subsequent cardiac events, including sudden cardiac death and heart failure hospitalization [40]. These findings collectively support the transition from qualitative visual interpretation to objective, reproducible risk stratification and suggest that tCMA could serve as both a baseline prognostic marker and a serial quantitative endpoint for monitoring therapeutic efficacy.

An emerging body of work further reinforces the complementary roles of FDG-PET and CMR in cardiac sarcoidosis. Kazui et al. recently demonstrated that combined assessment of late gadolinium enhancement and FDG uptake provides superior prognostic stratification compared with either modality alone, consistent with the pathophysiological dichotomy between irreversible fibrotic substrate (captured by LGE) and potentially reversible active granulomatous inflammation (captured by FDG) [41]. This conceptual framework aligns with our finding that tCMA, as a quantitative index of the active inflammatory burden, is independently associated with outcome after adjustment for conventional markers of cumulative myocardial injury. Additionally, Takeuchi et al. reported that reduced 18F-FDG uptake in the basal interventricular septum, likely reflecting established fibrosis within a regionally critical territory, predicts fatal ventricular arrhythmic events in CS, underscoring the value of integrating regional uptake patterns with global volumetric measures [42]. In our cohort, the exploratory four-group PET + LGE analysis (Results) was directionally consistent with the complementary contributions of active inflammation (FDG) and established fibrosis (LGE) to long-term cardiac risk. However, the small event count within the CMR-imaged subgroup precluded formal combined multivariable modeling. Future prospective studies with a larger subset undergoing both modalities are warranted to formally quantify the incremental prognostic value of combined volumetric PET and LGE assessment.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. The sample size of 48 patients was relatively modest, and the number of events was limited, which could have inflated effect estimates in the multivariate analyses, as reflected in the wide confidence intervals. Most patients were in NYHA class I or II, which may limit generalizability to patients with more advanced disease. Some patients had already received steroids or undergone ICD implantation at the time of PET imaging, and we were unable to stratify for these treatments, introducing potential confounding. Because corticosteroids suppress myocardial FDG uptake and thereby lower measured tCMV and tCMA, the volumetric values in 34 of 48 patients (70.8%) already receiving steroids at imaging may underestimate their peak inflammatory burden, an effect that would tend to attenuate rather than inflate the observed association with MACE. Cardiac MRI was available in only 27 of 48 patients (56.3%); the remaining patients did not undergo CMR, frequently because of device-related contraindications, so the combined PET and LGE analysis is exploratory, and a fully powered head-to-head prognostic comparison with LGE was not possible. The composite endpoint was weighted toward heart-failure hospitalization (15 of the contributing events). However, the association of tCMA with outcome persisted in a sensitivity analysis restricted to a more stringent composite of cardiac death and sustained ventricular tachycardia (log-rank P = 0.0453), arguing against a purely heart-failure-driven effect. The interval from disease onset to FDG-PET/CT varied widely (median 21.2 months; IQR, 6.1–40.9), and the prognostic value of tCMA was preserved in patients imaged longer after onset (log-rank P = 0.0045), supporting generalizability across disease stages. The cutoff values for tCMV and tCMA were derived internally using ROC analysis without bootstrap optimism correction or cross-validation, which may lead to an overestimation of their prognostic performance; external validation in independent cohorts is essential. In addition, the 24 univariate Cox comparisons were performed without formal correction for multiplicity, although tCMA (P = 0.0002), NYHA class (P = 0.0005), and LVEF (P = 0.0009) all showed strong univariate associations that would remain significant under conservative adjustment. Serial imaging was not performed, precluding assessment of whether changes in tCMV and tCMA over time correlate with therapeutic response and long-term prognosis. Finally, the segmentation approach using a fixed SUV threshold may be influenced by site-specific acquisition and reconstruction parameters, dietary preparation, and individual variations in physiological myocardial FDG uptake, and interobserver reproducibility was not formally assessed.

Future studies should address these limitations by enrolling larger multicenter prospective cohorts and externally validating the proposed cutoff values. The role of serial tCMV and tCMA measurements in monitoring treatment response and guiding the intensity of immunosuppressive therapy warrants investigation. Integration with CMR-derived parameters, including LGE extent and T1/T2 mapping, may provide complementary information regarding both active inflammation and established fibrosis. Advances in automated segmentation using artificial intelligence may improve the reproducibility and clinical applicability of volumetric FDG-PET/CT analysis.

Conclusions

In this prospective observational study, volume-based quantitative parameters derived from 18F-FDG-PET/CT provided clinically meaningful prognostic information in patients with cardiac sarcoidosis. tCMA, which captures the global extent and metabolic intensity of active granulomatous inflammation, demonstrated the strongest and most consistent association with long-term major adverse cardiac events and remained independently associated with outcome after adjustment for conventional clinical and imaging variables. tCMA is intended to complement, not replace, established risk-stratification tools. Although adding tCMA improved discrimination over a base clinical model comprising NYHA class and troponin T, its incremental value over a full clinical and imaging model that also incorporates LVEF and CMR-LGE remains to be demonstrated in larger cohorts.

These findings indicate that quantifying the active granulomatous inflammatory burden throughout the myocardium, rather than measuring focal metabolic intensity alone, may improve risk stratification in cardiac sarcoidosis. Because FDG-PET/CT selectively visualizes the active inflammatory stages preceding irreversible fibrosis, tCMA may identify patients at the highest risk who would benefit most from early immunosuppressive intervention before progression to the fibrotic stage. In our cohort, an exploratory four-group analysis combining tCMA status and LGE on cardiac magnetic resonance suggested that quantitative volumetric FDG-PET/CT may complement LGE, which predominantly reflects established fibrosis. However, this signal was generated within a 27-patient CMR subset and requires confirmation in larger prospective cohorts before influencing risk-adapted clinical management, including decisions regarding immunosuppressive therapy and device implantation. Further multicenter studies with larger cohorts and external validation are warranted to confirm these results and to define the role of serial volumetric assessment in guiding therapeutic strategies.

Estimation of diameter, mass and volume of Lanzhou lily bulbs based on YOLO instance segmentation
Authors: Yongjun Ding, Haibo Huang, Wang Jing
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Lanzhou lily is the only sweet lily species cultivated in China, contributing approximately 20% to Lanzhou’s total agricultural output value. In recent years, lily bulbs’ quality issues such as declining single-clove rates and bulb shrinkage have emerged, making rapid and accurate assessment of bulb viability crucial for mitigating degeneration. To address the subjectivity and inefficiency of manual screening, this study proposes a novel method. This method is based on the YOLOv8-seg instance segmentation model and is designed for high-precision prediction of lily bulb mass, diameter, and volume. A dataset was constructed by randomly selecting 500 lily bulbs with different morphologies, capturing their images using a mobile phone, and acquiring ground truth data (mass, diameter, volume) with an electronic balance, a digital vernier caliper, and a measuring cylinder, respectively. The YOLOv8-seg model was employed to achieve instance segmentation of the bulbs, followed by extraction of 22 morphological features via OpenCV. Combining the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and Mutual Information (MI), retaining three key predictors. Subsequently, six machine learning models were constructed to predict bulb mass, diameter, and volume. The experimental results indicate that the artificial neural network (ANN) model has the strongest generalization ability. The coefficient of determination between the predicted values and the measured values for the diameter, volume, and mass of lily bulbs are 0.8478, 0.9844, and 0.9981, respectively. The performance of the ANN model on the test set is as follows: the mean square error (MSE) for diameter prediction is 8.3175 and the mean absolute error (MAE) is 2.3019; the MSE for volume prediction is 1.0912 and the MAE is 0.7951; the MSE for mass prediction is 0.3135 and the MAE is 0.4318. This method enables the direct, rapid prediction of bulb diameter, volume, and mass from RGB images, providing agricultural practitioners with an efficient and reliable tool for bulb viability detection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54341-2.Keywords: Instance segmentation, Morphological parameter prediction, Artificial neural network, PCASubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Lanzhou lily, as a national geographical indication product and a representative of Gansu’s “Gansu Flavor” specialty agricultural products, has become an important symbol of Gansu agriculture due to its unique quality and widespread market recognition. The total planting area of lilies across the city reaches 137,000 mu, accounting for 55% of the province’s total planting area, with an annual total output value of 5.68 billion yuan, forming a full industrial chain development model covering planting, processing, and sales1(Lanzhou Lily Industry High-Quality Development Plan (2024–2030)). In lily production, high-quality bulbs possess strong bulb vitality—defined as the potential of bulbs to germinate into seedlings, sustain growth, and develop into high-quality plants—and this vitality is directly linked to key quality parameters. These observable and quantifiable parameters serve as the core basis for predicting bulb vitality. However, current bulb quality assessment still primarily relies on manual screening, which is highly subjective, time-consuming, and labor-intensive, thus becoming a bottleneck restricting the further development of the Lanzhou lily industry.

In the early stage, many scholars adopted machine learning methods for segmentation and morphological processing to extract 2D features2–8 including projected area, major axis, minor axis, equivalent diameter, etc. However, such methods are often limited by insufficient accuracy and poor generalization. Specifically, the model performance degrades significantly under varying backgrounds or different lighting conditions. Furthermore, most relevant studies are only conducted under controllable laboratory conditions, which makes it difficult to adapt to practical application scenarios.

Consequently, other researchers have delved into deep learning-based methods, employing deep learning networks such as SAM9, Mask R-CNN10 and Unet11,12 for segmentation tasks. However, SAM is a large-scale model. Using its pre-trained version may result in unsatisfactory accuracy on specific datasets, whereas retraining it from scratch incurs excessively high computational costs.The two-stage pipeline of Mask R-CNN is time-consuming, and preliminary studies have demonstrated its poor performance in the edge segmentation of bulbs.U-Net is more suitable for semantic segmentation tasks and performs well when only one object exists in an image. For the group detection scenario of lily bulbs, however, instance segmentation methods are more applicable.YOLOv8-seg13 is an instance segmentation model extended from the YOLOv8 framework released by Ultralytics. Its backbone network constructs a multi-scale feature pyramid using standard convolutions and the C2f module. The neck adopts BiFPN to realize bidirectional feature interaction, and the detection head adopts a decoupled structure combined with dynamic convolution to optimize mask prediction. This improves the multi-scale object detection capability and segmentation boundary accuracy, and can effectively solve the above.

It is evident that the integrated methodology comprising image segmentation, geometric parameter extraction, and phenotypic prediction modeling constitutes a viable technical pathway for crop phenotyping research. However, studies on Lanzhou lily bulbs have mostly focused on classification problems14, while few have paid attention to phenotypic analysis. Therefore, this study focuses on lily bulbs as the research subject, employing YOLOv8-seg for bulb mask extraction, combined with OpenCV based computation of mask shape features, to construct prediction models for bulb diameter, volume, and mass. This approach aims to establish a scientific foundation for breeding Lanzhou lily.

Methodology

Dataset

At the solar greenhouse of Gansu S.K.Y. Ecological Technology Co., Ltd., 500 Lanzhou lily bulbs of various shapes were selected. Prior to data collection, formal permission for bulb sampling and imaging was obtained from Gansu S.K.Y. Ecological Technology Co., Ltd. The 500 bulbs were divided into two groups: the first 400 bulbs and the last 100 bulbs. Through a randomized sampling protocol with replacement, bulbs were selected for imaging using an HONOR 30 PRO smartphone under natural lighting conditions, yielding a total dataset of 794 bulb photographs. All 500 bulbs were pre-assigned unique serial numbers, during imaging, a paper note marked with the corresponding bulb number was placed next to each bulb to directly clarify the association between each photograph and its corresponding bulb.

Due to variations in shooting distance and angle among different photographs, coupled with the irregular shape of lily bulbs and their random placement during multiple samplings, the same bulb exhibits distinct imaging features. As shown in Fig. 1, the No. 4 bulb marked with red bounding boxes displays different morphological appearances in various images. Therefore, the 2100 independent samples extracted from the first 400 bulbs were used as the training set, and the 794 independent samples extracted from the last 100 bulbs were used as the test set; such division is reasonable and no data leakage occurs in model training and testing. Each image included a 600 mL Pepsi bottle cap for image calibration. The mass, diameter, and volume of each bulb were manually measured using an electronic balance (Model: JM-B20002, Accuracy: ±0.01 g), a digital Vernier caliper (Model: DL3944, Accuracy: ±0.1 mm), and a graduated cylinder. To enhance the accuracy of the model training, the image dimensions were standardized to 2240 × 1680. Additionally, Labelme was used to label the lily bulbs and Pepsi bottle caps, and the annotated JSON files were saved. The data collection process is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Method

Pixel-level segmentation was performed using YOLOv8-seg, followed by calculation of 22 geometric features of the segmentation mask using OpenCV. Multiple collinearity effects were eliminated through correlation analysis (with features having an absolute Pearson correlation coefficient |r| < 0.3 with the target attributes being removed) and variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis (retaining only features with VIF < 10), and feature selection was performed. Finally, SVR, XGBoost, gradient boosting, decision trees, random forests, and artificial neural networks were used to construct prediction models for lily bulb diameter, volume, and mass (Fig. 3).

The experimental procedure.

YOLOv8-seg model

The YOLOv8 model released by Ultralytics demonstrates exceptional speed and accuracy in image processing and detection. Compared to previous YOLO versions, YOLOv8 has undergone significant optimizations and improvements in multiple aspects. YOLOv8-seg is an instance segmentation model extended from the YOLOv8 framework, with its core architecture retaining the efficient feature extraction mechanism of YOLOv815. The backbone network of YOLOv8-seg constructs a multi-scale feature pyramid using standard convolutions and lightweight C2f16 modules. The C2f modules maintain gradient propagation efficiency while reducing model complexity through cross-stage local connections, effectively balancing parameter count and feature reuse capabilities. The neck of YOLOv8-seg adopts a bidirectional feature pyramid network17(BiFPN) architecture, which transmits high-level semantic information through a top-down upsampling path and integrates shallow-level detail features through a bottom-up downsampling path, forming bidirectional feature interaction and significantly improving the model’s detection capability for objects of different scales. YOLOv8-seg’s detection head adopts a decoupled structure, separating classification and regression tasks, while integrating dynamic convolutional optimization for mask prediction to improve the boundary accuracy of instance segmentation15 (Fig. 4).

YOLOv8-seg.

Experimental environment

All experiments were conducted on the Windows 11. The hardware configuration included an NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU and an Intel Xeon E5-2680 v4 CPU. For model training, the hyperparameters were set as follows: batch size = 2, LR = 0.01, and epochs = 300. The dataset contained 794 images in total, which were divided into a training set, validation set, and test set at an 8:1:1 ratio.

Segment the bulb images using the YOLOv8-seg network to obtain the mask image for each bulb. Next, use the image processing methods provided by OpenCV to calculate 22 geometric features, with details available in Table S1 of the Supplementary Materials. Regression models

To accurately predict the diameter, volume, and mass of lily bulbs, six representative regression models were implemented: Support Vector Regression¹⁸ (SVR), Extreme Gradient Boosting Regression19 (XGBoost), Gradient Boosting Regression20 (GBR), Decision Tree Regression21 (DTR), Random Forest Regression22 (RFR), and Artificial Neural Network Regression (ANN). All models underwent hyperparameter optimization via grid search, with the optimization process validated using five-fold cross-validation. The evaluation metrics used were MSE, MAE, and R2. The cross-validation results showed that after hyperparameter optimization, all models exhibited stable prediction performance across the 5 folds: for diameter prediction, the average R2 values ranged from 0.74 to 0.85; for volume prediction, they ranged from 0.941 to 0.985; and for mass prediction, they ranged from 0.9823 to 0.9983 (detailed results of the 5-fold cross-validation for each model are provided in Supplementary Table S2). Results

Segmentation performance

This study utilizes the YOLOv8-seg, SOLOv223, Mask-Rcnn24, and Queryinst25 models to perform segmentation on bulb images, generating high-quality bulb masks to support subsequent geometric feature extraction and regression modeling. Within these masks, bulbs are assigned a grayscale value of 100 while bottle caps (serving as calibration markers) are assigned a grayscale value of 200. The segmentation process aims to accurately identify both the bulb regions and calibration objects (e.g., bottle caps), ensuring the generated mask images comprehensively cover the bulb contours to prevent missed detections and false positives. As shown in Fig. 5, SOLOv2 can’t maintain the circular shape of bottle caps, with the segmentation contours of the calibration markers being severely distorted. Mask R-CNN generates bulb masks that deviate from the bulbs’ actual shape, resulting in inaccurate morphological representation. Queryinst misses detections of bulbs in some test images. By contrast, YOLOv8-seg effectively avoids these issues, delivering stable and accurate segmentation performance.

Segmentation results.

The training process of the YOLOv8-seg model in Fig. 6 exhibits typical training convergence characteristics. For segmentation loss (Fig. 6a), Lseg rapidly decreased from an initial value of approximately 11.0, experiencing steep declines within the first 15 epochs, indicating effective optimization of segmentation head parameters. The loss curve subsequently plateaued, stabilizing near 0.25 after 80 epochs, the learning process of mask prediction had basically reached a stable state. Bounding box regression loss Lbox (Fig. 6b) declined from an initial 2.8 to around 0.5 over 100 epochs, with minor fluctuations observed near epoch 80, potentially attributable to complex sample interference. The segmentation accuracy metric mAP50 (Fig. 6c) rose sharply from 0, reaching a critical inflection point at 0.85 by epoch 25 before converging to 0.98, where early metric saturation signifies rapid recognition capability for standard samples. The mAP50-95 (Fig. 6d) demonstrated accelerated growth to 0.65 at epoch 50, ultimately achieving 0.95 after extended training, reflecting the extended optimization required for precise localization at high IoU thresholds. Smoothed trajectories confirm all metrics stabilized beyond epoch 100 without overfitting oscillations.

Feature-phenotype correlation

A correlation analysis was conducted between the geometric features extracted by OpenCV and the diameter, volume, and mass of lily bulbs. The results showed that the bulb projection area (), perimeter (), and major axis length of circumscribed ellipse (\:MA) exhibited significant positive correlations with the phenotypic parameters. The correlation coefficients with area were 0.95, 0.97, and 0.98, respectively, with perimeter, 0.96, 0.95, and 0.95, respectively, and with major axis, 0.93, 0.94, and 0.95, respectively. Additionally, the correlations between shape complexity (\:SC), ellipticity of circumscribed ellipse (\:E), and convex hull circularity () and phenotypic parameters were also low, indicating that these features have limited explanatory power for phenotypic parameters. Furthermore, there were significant correlations among the features, necessitating the elimination of multicollinearity among them (Fig. 7).

Feature selection

The Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)26 is used to detect multicollinearity among features. When VIF < 10, it indicates that the multicollinearity between features is within an acceptable range. Mutual Information (MI) measures the dependence between features and the target variable; a higher MI score means the feature contributes more predictive information to the target, reflecting its importance for prediction. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) reduces the dimensionality of features by mapping original high-dimensional features to a low-dimensional space while retaining the maximum amount of variance information.

Tables 4 and 5 present the results of the three feature selection methods.From the perspective of PCA importance, features such as major_axis (1.4461), box_volume (1.3959), rect_width_mm (1.3937), and volume_integral (1.3538) rank high. This indicates that these features contribute significantly to the construction of principal components and can effectively preserve the variance information of the data. In terms of MI scores, all scores fall within the range of 1.2570 to 1.5106, with little overall difference—suggesting that all features contribute substantially to the prediction of the target attribute.

Only 3 features were retained through VIF screening, which implies that severe multicollinearity existed among the original features. These three features, namely bounding rectangle width, major axis length of the circumscribed ellipse, and circumscribed ellipsoid volume, are closely related to the biological traits of Lanzhou lily bulbs, which are approximately ellipsoidal in shape. The circumscribed ellipsoid volume can directly fit the actual volume of the bulb, indirectly support mass prediction via the strong positive correlation between volume and mass, and has an inherent scale correlation with the bulb diameter. The bounding rectangle width corresponds to the horizontal span of the minimum bounding rectangle of the segmented bulb region, which is strongly correlated with the bulb diameter in the projection direction; it can assist in diameter prediction and optimize the prediction performance of volume and mass when combined with other features. The major axis length of the circumscribed ellipse corresponds to the maximum scale of the bulb’s two-dimensional projection, serving as the core feature for diameter prediction and helping to characterize the overall scale of the bulb to support the prediction of volume and mass. These three features work synergistically to comprehensively describe the bulb morphology and fully meet the regression prediction requirements of diameter, volume, and mass. Overall, the three features not only eliminated multicollinearity via VIF but also played an important role in capturing the core variation in the data through PCA. Although the MI scores of some of these features are not the highest globally, their comprehensive performance is superior: they ensure model stability while capturing the critical information linking the data to the target, making them more suitable for subsequent analyses.

Model development and evaluation

Since the same bulb in different images was treated as an independent sample, we built the attribute prediction datasets accordingly. Specifically, the 2100 independent samples extracted from the first 400 bulbs were used to form the attribute prediction training set, while the 794 independent samples extracted from the last 100 bulbs were used to build the attribute prediction test set. Six regression methods were implemented: Support Vector Regression (SVR), XGBoost, Gradient Boosting Regression (GBR), Decision Tree Regression (DTR), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Artificial Neural Network Regression (ANN). Using circumscribed ellipsoid volume (), bounding rectangle width (), and major axis length of circumscribed ellipse (\:MA) as inputs, the models predicted lily bulb diameter, volume, and mass respectively.

The prediction results on test set are shown in Table 6. The coefficient of determination (R2) for all models exceeds 0.8. However, the prediction accuracy for diameter is lower than that for volume and mass. This is because lily bulbs are irregular spherical objects, and 2D images lack depth information, leading to inevitable discrepancies between the extracted diameter and the actual diameter. Additionally, GBR and RFR based on tree structures demonstrate excellent predictive potential and have relatively simple computational processes, making them suitable for developing portable monitoring instruments. However, such models carry the risk of overfitting, which can impair their generalization capabilities. Future improvements can be achieved through parameter adjustments and increasing data diversity.

Discussion

Discussion on segmentation performance and light sensitivity

The YOLOv8-seg segmentation model achieves outstanding performance in the object segmentation task, with all evaluation metrics attaining high levels. Specifically, the Precision reaches 0.998, while mAP and mAP50-95 are 0.995 and 0.984, respectively, which fully validates the effectiveness of the model in the current task. The main reason for the model’s favorable segmentation results is that the experimental scenario used in this study is relatively simple, with little background interference and clear target features. This allows the model to accurately capture the target regions and demonstrate high segmentation performance. The experimental results of phenotypic prediction demonstrate a high correlation between model predictions and actual values, validating the strong phenotypic representation capability of shape features. Changes in light conditions have limited impact on shape feature extraction, indicating low light sensitivity of the model. However, the experimental results were obtained under normal natural lighting conditions. Further validation is required to determine whether high accuracy can be maintained under complex lighting conditions such as high light or shadow. Future field trials involving different varieties and imaging conditions will be conducted to further validate the model’s generalizability.

Limitations and improvement exploration of bulb diameter measurement

Although the models for bulb volume and mass prediction achieve satisfactory predictive performance, with R² values of 0.9844 and 0.9981 respectively, obvious limitations still exist in the bulb diameter measurement task. The R² is only 0.8478 and the MAE reaches 2.3. This is mainly attributed to the large shape variations among lily bulbs, while the 2D imaging technique adopted in this study relies on a single projection of the bulb contour for diameter measurement.This measurement method is prone to edge detection errors, particularly when bulbs exhibit irregular contours or localized surface depressions. A single projection struggles to fully capture their true form—this limitation underscores the urgent need to explore more precise diameter measurement methods. Measurement techniques based on 3D reconstruction represent a crucial direction for addressing this challenge.

Measurement technologies based on 3D reconstruction can be broadly categorized into active and passive measurement techniques. Active measurement technologies acquire depth information on seed surfaces by actively emitting signals such as laser beams or specific wavelength light, including laser triangulation27, terrestrial laser scanning28, and time-of-flight cameras29. These methods offer high measurement speed suitable for batch inspection of seeds, but their application costs are high due to constraints from specialized equipment and radiation safety requirements. Passive measurement techniques, conversely, perform 3D reconstruction solely by receiving image data under natural or auxiliary lighting. Representative technologies include stereo vision and multi-view reconstruction30. These methods rely on camera parameter calibration or estimation, entail substantial computational demands, and feature matching can lead to cumulative errors.

Deep learning possesses highly abstract and complex feature representations. 3D reconstruction techniques based on deep learning can better capture the 3D coordinate information of seed surface through multi-view image fusion. This approach not only avoids the dimensionality loss inherent in 2D projections but also enables the extraction of key parameters such as maximum diameter and average diameter from point cloud data across arbitrary cross-sections, fundamentally addressing the deficiency of missing depth information. However, training deep learning models relies on large amounts of labeled samples, requiring high-precision 3D scanning equipment to obtain true 3D values, which is operationally complex. Furthermore, the number of input views is closely related to reconstruction accuracy and processing time. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct research on semi-supervised learning and optimal view construction for lily bulbs.

Analysis of dataset diversity and model generalization

In this study, the range of lily bulb diameter was 54.90 mm, with a mean of 32.17 mm. The range of volume was 54.85 mL, with a mean of 15.99 mL. The range of mass was 55.89 g, with a mean of 14.82 g. All phenotypic parameters showed a wide variation range, indicating that the dataset used in this study has sufficient diversity. This provides solid data support for the reliability of the research conclusions and validates the high reliability of the prediction model established in this study. However, limited by experimental conditions, this study only conducted experiments on Lanzhou lily bulbs. The effectiveness and generalization of the prediction model for other lily varieties remain to be further verified.

Conclusion

This study proposes a computer vision and machine learning-based phenotyping method for lily bulbs. The results show:

High-precision segmentation of bulbs was achieved using the YOLOv8-seg model (with an mAP of 0.995). After extracting 22 geometric features via the OpenCV algorithm, three optimal prediction indicators were selected by combining Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and mutual information analysis to address the multicollinearity issue. Based on this, an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) regression model was constructed, which achieved coefficients of determination (R2) of 0.8478, 0.9844, and 0.9981 for the prediction of bulb diameter, volume, and mass, respectively. Compared with traditional models such as Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Random Forest (RF), this model exhibited significant advantages in processing nonlinear phenotypic data, providing a reliable intelligent technical approach for the rapid and non-destructive detection of phenotypic parameters of lily bulbs.

This study has the limitation that the prediction accuracy of lily bulb diameter is relatively insufficient. Compared with the high-precision prediction results of volume and mass, the coefficient of determination (R²) for diameter prediction is only 0.8478, and the mean absolute error (MAE) reaches 2.3 mm. This issue is mainly attributed to the use of two-dimensional (2D) imaging for diameter measurement in this study. Lily bulbs exhibit large shape variations, and some bulbs have irregular contours or local surface depressions.Since 2D imaging can only capture a single projection of the bulb outline, it cannot fully and truly represent the three-dimensional (3D) morphological characteristics of the bulbs. This further causes deviations in contour edge detection and ultimately reduces the accuracy of diameter prediction.

Future research will focus on bulb diameter prediction based on three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction and illumination sensitivity experiments. 3D reconstruction technology will be introduced to replace the traditional two-dimensional (2D) imaging method. By acquiring complete 3D point cloud data of lily bulbs and accurately extracting multi-section diameter parameters, the diameter prediction error caused by 2D projection can be solved, and the accuracy of phenotypic detection can be further improved.Meanwhile, systematic illumination sensitivity experiments will be carried out. Complex illumination scenarios such as high light and shadow will be constructed, and corresponding datasets will be collected to test the model performance under different illumination conditions.The influence law of illumination will be quantitatively analyzed, and the feature extraction module of the model will be optimized to enhance its anti-interference ability against illumination variations, so as to ensure the model’s adaptability to actual field application scenarios.

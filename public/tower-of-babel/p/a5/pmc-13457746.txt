Ultrasonic markers for screening intra-abdominal hypertension in patients with pancreatic diseases
Authors: Bao-Quan Chen, Xiao-Yi Yan, Wan-Ying Jia, Yan-Fen Yang, Xian-Shui Fu, Ke Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurately assessing intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) is of significant clinical importance in laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy (LPD) and severe acute pancreatitis (SAP), which is closely linked to complications and mortality. This study aimed to identify potential ultrasonic markers for screening IAH in patients who underwent LPD for pancreatic cancer and those with SAP.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 27 patients who were diagnosed with pancreatic cancer and underwent LPD, and 19 patients with SAP admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) from February 2022 to August 2022. Baseline demographic, ultrasonic markers, and clinical records were obtained and analyzed to identify potential ultrasonic markers for screening IAH. The diagnostic performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUROC).ResultsComparative analysis between the two groups showed that in both the LPD and SAP groups, patients with IAH exhibited significantly lower right diaphragm thickening rate (RDTR) and significantly higher right renal interlobar arterial resistive index (RIRI) than those without IAH (all P<0.05). Univariate analysis and multivariate regression analysis showed that the RIRI and RDTR were independent predictors of IAH in the LPD group (P=0.004 and P=0.025, respectively); and the combination of RIRI and RDTR showed promising diagnostic performance for IAH, with an AUROC of 0.894, sensitivity of 93.33%, and specificity of 91.67%. Meanwhile, for the SAP group, only the RDTR served as the marker for screening IAH with an AUROC of 0.872, a sensitivity of 86.67%, and a specificity of 83.33%.ConclusionsThis preliminary study suggests that clinicians should remain alert to the occurrence of IAH when abnormal ultrasonic markers are screened. Regular intra-abdominal pressure monitoring is recommended in such cases. However, these findings require validation in larger prospective cohorts.Keywords: Ultrasonic markers, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy (LPD), severe acute pancreatitis (SAP), intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH)

Introduction

Intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) is a steady-state pressure within the abdominal cavity, primarily generated by the hydrostatic pressure of intra-abdominal organs, typically maintained at 5–7 mmHg. Intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) refers to a pathologic process characterized by the progressive or recurrent elevation of IAP, which subsequently affects visceral blood flow and organ function (1,2). In 2017, the World Society of the Abdominal Compartment Syndrome released the latest consensus and clinical practice guidelines on IAH (3). According to the guidelines, IAH is defined as a sustained or recurrent elevation of IAP to ≥12 mmHg. Laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy (LPD) is a standard surgical procedure for treating resectable benign and malignant tumors of the ampulla and the pancreatic head-neck region (4,5). However, due to the complex anatomical structures and the high technical demands for digestive tract reconstruction, this procedure is often associated with challenging postoperative complications that affect patient outcomes. Among these complications, the most common is pancreatic fistula, followed by IAH. Severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) is a critical inflammatory condition of the pancreas, characterized by rapid progression and potential multi-organ dysfunction, which remains one of the most common acute abdominal emergencies in emergency departments and the intensive care unit (ICU). It is important to note that IAH occurs in up to 60% of patients with SAP (6,7), which is consistent with this study’s findings. The World Society of the Abdominal Compartment Syndrome also highlights LPD and SAP as significant risk factors for IAH. IAH can reduce abdominal perfusion, increasing the risk of devastating surgical complications such as anastomotic leak (8-10). It is closely associated with mortality in patients who undergo LPD. Meanwhile, the development of IAH has been shown to be a prognostic marker of disease severity in SAP cases (6). Therefore, accurately assessing the presence of IAH in patients who have undergone LPD and in those with SAP is of significant clinical importance.

Currently, some clinicians estimate IAP or diagnose IAH based on changes in clinical examinations such as abdominal circumference and abdominal distension, demonstrating poor sensitivity and accuracy (11). The diagnosis of IAH primarily relies on bladder pressure measurement (12,13). This invasive method can cause discomfort and pressure transducers are costly. Ultrasound, as a non-invasive, convenient, and repeatable imaging technique, has been widely used in clinical practice. To date, some researchers have attempted to assess IAP by ultrasound. Previous studies have independently established the utility of ultrasound for assessing IAP (14,15). However, these studies have included heterogeneous patients without etiological grouping. We hypothesize that the optimal ultrasound marker for IAH is etiology-dependent, not one-size-fits-all. Therefore, this study aimed to identify potential ultrasonic markers for screening IAH in patients who underwent LPD (surgical group) for pancreatic cancer and those with SAP (non-surgical group) separately, which could potentially provide clinicians with non-invasive and early warning markers to screen IAH. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0330/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

Data were prospectively collected from patients who underwent LPD for pancreatic cancer or were diagnosed with SAP in the ICU of Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University between February 2022 and August 2022 and retrospectively analyzed. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) patients admitted to the ICU after undergoing LPD or with SAP {defined according to The Revised Atlanta Classification [2012]}; (II) availability of IAP measurements; and (III) completion of relevant ultrasound examinations within 24 hours of ICU admission. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) age <18 years; (II) history of hypertension >10 years (14) [may lead to renal arteriolar sclerosis and decreased vascular compliance, thereby causing an elevation of the renal resistive index (RI)]; (III) conditions affecting bladder pressure measurements, such as pregnancy or cystitis; (IV) other conditions potentially affect the acquisition of ultrasonic markers, including chronic kidney disease, mechanical ventilation, or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD); and (V) incomplete ultrasound or clinical data. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee at Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (No. 2022LWB252). Since the data are fully anonymized, the Ethics Committee granted a waiver of informed consent.

Clinical information

Clinical data were collected, including patient demographics and key physiological and laboratory parameters: age, gender, body mass index (BMI), breathing, heart rate, mean arterial pressure (MAP), oxygenation index, platelet count, serum total bilirubin, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), serum creatinine, and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score (16,17). IAP measurement was performed by a specialized nurse before the ultrasonic examination (within 30 minutes), and the operational procedure is shown in Figure 1. The severity of IAH is classified into four grades: I, 12–15 mmHg; II, 16–20 mmHg; III, 21–25 mmHg; and IV, >25 mmHg. When IAP exceeds 20 mmHg persistently, accompanied by new organ dysfunction or failure, the condition is diagnosed as abdominal compartment syndrome (ACS).

Ultrasonic examination

All ultrasonic examinations were performed within 24 hours of ICU admission by the same ultrasonographer using the Mindray Nuewa I9 system (Mindray, Shenzhen, China), equipped with both high-frequency (frequency: 3–14 MHz) and low-frequency probes (frequency: 1.2–5 MHz). In addition, the ultrasonographer was unaware of the concurrent IAP measurement results at the time of examination, as IAP values were not made available to them until after the ultrasonic report was completed.

The patient was positioned in the supine position, and a low-frequency probe was used to scan the kidney from the right flank along the posterior axillary line. Color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI) was activated to visualize renal vasculature; spectral Doppler was then applied to measure the RI at three sites: the right renal arterial RI (RARI, Figure 2A), the right renal segmental arterial RI (RSRI, Figure 2B), and the right renal interlobar arterial RI (RIRI, Figure 2C). For each site, three similar and complete waveforms were selected, and the mean value was calculated for consistency. Then, a high-frequency probe was positioned between the 6th and 12th intercostal spaces along the right anterior to posterior axillary line to obtain an image of the diaphragm. Measurements were taken for diaphragmatic thickness at the end of expiration (DTee) and at the end of inspiration (DTei) in spontaneously breathing patients (Figure 2D). Right diaphragm thickening rate (RDTR) was calculated with the equation: RDTR = (DTei − Dtee)/Dtee × 100%.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to test for normality. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as means ± standard deviations (SDs) and analyzed using the t-test; non-normally distributed continuous variables are presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test; and categorical variables are expressed as absolute figures with percentages and compared between groups using Fisher’s exact or Chi-squared test. Univariate and multivariate analysis were conducted to identify potential ultrasonic markers for screening IAH. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to calculate the area under the ROC curve (AUROC), sensitivity, and specificity to assess the cut-offs and diagnostic performance of ultrasonic markers for screening IAH. A P value <0.05 was considered indicative of a statistically significant difference.

Results

Comparison of patient characteristics and ultrasonic markers

We ultimately identified 46 patients meeting the inclusion criteria from a cohort of 66 patients admitted to the ICU who underwent LPD for pancreatic cancer or those with SAP. The flowchart of patient recruitment is shown in Figure 3. Among them, 27 patients had undergone LPD, and 19 patients had SAP [the main etiologies were as follows: gallstone (n=10, 52.6%), hyperlipidemia (n=5, 26.3%), alcoholic (n=2, 10.5%), and post-endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (n=2, 10.5%)]. In the LPD group (n=27), 15 patients (55.6%) developed IAH; 13 patients (68.4%) developed IAH in the SAP group (n=19). The RDTR of the IAH group was significantly lower than that of the non-IAH group in the LPD and SAP groups. The RIRI of the IAH group was significantly higher than that of the non-IAH group (all P<0.05). The differences in other ultrasonic markers and patient characteristics, including RARI, RSRI, age, gender, BMI, SOFA score, heart rate, or breathing, were not significantly different (Table 1).

Diagnostic efficacy of ultrasonic markers for screening IAH

The diagnostic efficacy of ultrasonic markers for screening IAH is shown in Table 3 and Figure 4. In the LPD group, the combined index (defined as the predicted probability from univariate and multivariate analysis using RIRI and RDTR as independent variables) demonstrated better diagnostic performance for IAH, with an AUROC of 0.894 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.716–0.979], a sensitivity of 93.33%, and specificity of 91.67%. For patients with SAP, only RDTR was found to be optimal, with an AUROC of 0.872 (95% CI: 0.640–0.979), sensitivity of 86.67%, and specificity of 83.33% at a cut-off value of 15.00%. Given the limited sample size, these models should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate both consolidation of existing evidence and conceptual advancement beyond prior research. Previous studies have conflated distinct pathophysiological mechanisms between surgical and medical subgroups, which limited their clinical utility (14,15). In contrast, we addressed a critical limitation of the “one-size-fits-all” approach by implementing subgroup-specific analysis. Our research focused exclusively on patients who underwent LPD in the surgical group and SAP patients in the non-surgical group. This approach reduced the interference of confounding factors, making our conclusions more specific. We found that an RIRI >0.690 in the LPD group was associated with higher rates of IAH. The AUROC, sensitivity, and specificity of RIRI were 0.850 (95% CI: 0.660–0.957), 80.00%, and 91.67%, respectively. We also found that the RI value of different renal artery segments has different predictive value for IAH; compared to RARI and RSRI, only RIRI was an ultrasonic marker for screening IAH in the LPD group. This may be attributed to the progressive branching of the renal artery, from the aorta to segmental arteries and then to interlobar arteries, with a gradual narrowing of the vessel diameter. This progressive reduction in diameter likely makes these vessels more susceptible to external pressure from IAH (18,19). As a result, the impact of IAH becomes more pronounced at the interlobar artery level, leading to a more significant increase in the RIRI value, thereby improving its diagnostic utility for detecting IAH. We also identified RDTR as a potential ultrasonic marker of IAH with a cut-off value of 16.00%, an AUROC of 0.817 (95% CI: 0.621–0.938), a sensitivity of 92.31%, and a specificity of 83.33%. To our knowledge, this marker has been widely used to assess diaphragmatic function in patients with pulmonary diseases (20-22). In the evaluation of IAH, RDTR serves as a complementary tool to RIRI; it is worth mentioning that combining the RIRI and RDTR was identified as a better diagnostic tool with an AUROC of 0.894 (95% CI: 0.716–0.979), a sensitivity of 93.33%, and a specificity of 91.67% in the LPD group. In the SAP group, we found that only RDTR below 15.00% was associated with higher rates of IAH, with an AUROC of 0.872 (95% CI: 0.640–0.979). Similar to the LPD group, the elevated IAP in patients with SAP can also compress the diaphragm and restrict its downward movement, thus leading to a decrease in diaphragmatic thickening fraction (23,24).

There is a key difference in the pathophysiological processes responsible for increased IAP and IAH in the surgical and nonsurgical diseases examined. IAH is primarily caused by the spread of pancreatic inflammation in the SAP group, accumulation of abdominal fluid, and edema of peripancreatic tissues. The IAH resulting from systemic inflammatory responses tends to be diffuse and is mainly localized around the pancreas. In SAP, abdominal organs generally do not directly compress the renal arteries, making the elevation of RIRI a possible explanation for the elevation of RIRI being less present. This also explains why RIRI is significant in the Mann-Whitney U test but not an independent predictor in the univariate and multivariate regression analysis. In contrast, IAP regulation and hemodynamic changes were more pronounced in the LPD group. These changes can lead to local vascular compression and altered renal blood flow, increasing resistance in the right renal artery. Additionally, the accumulation of gastrointestinal luminal contents (e.g., gas and fluid) and organ edema caused by postoperative inflammatory responses may exacerbate IAH. This imposes additional pressure on the right renal artery, leading to an elevation in RIRI, which remains an ultrasonic marker of IAH (25,26). Nevertheless, due to the limited sample size, these findings should be interpreted with caution.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the limited number of cases enrolled and the diagnostic thresholds were optimized on the same dataset used for performance evaluation, which limits the statistical power and precision of our estimates. Consequently, the reported diagnostic performance metrics should be viewed as preliminary. Our implementation in clinical practice would benefit from confirmation through larger, prospective, and ideally multicenter studies. Second, although we excluded patients with known chronic kidney disease, hypertension, and COPD, we did not screen for undiagnosed conditions (e.g., early-stage COPD) that may affect ultrasound parameters. Additionally, vasopressor use and fluid balance were not controlled for, which may independently influence RIRI and RDTR. These limitations should be addressed in future studies. Last, a single examination was performed for each patient without dynamic monitoring. We believe the measurement of RDTR or RIRI can be easily mastered by critical care physicians and holds potential for advancing real-time dynamic ultrasound monitoring of IAH in the future.

Conclusions

Of the four ultrasonic markers (RARI, RSRI, RIRI, and RDTR), RIRI and RDTR may be useful for non-invasive screening of IAH in patients who have undergone LPD. It is also important to note that in the SAP group, only RDTR can be used for evaluation of IAH in this study population. Therefore, when abnormal ultrasonic markers are noted, there should be a high suspicion of IAH. Clinicians can be promptly alerted to monitor IAP and implement interventions such as adjustment of fluid replacement, body position change, and so on (27,28), thereby reducing the incidence and mortality associated with IAH/ACS.

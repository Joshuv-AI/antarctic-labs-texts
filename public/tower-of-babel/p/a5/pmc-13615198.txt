AQP4-IgG Dynamics and Exploratory Assessment of SERA-3 in Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder Treated with Satralizumab
Authors: Miaoxin Yu, Xiang Li, Lei Cui, Jinsong Jiao, Qing Sun, Yeqiong Zhang, Renbin Wang, Dantao Peng, Yang He, Yujuan Jiao, Weihe Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNeuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is an autoimmune condition driven by aquaporin-4 immunoglobulin G (AQP4-IgG). The current treatment paradigm focuses on mitigating clinical relapses and disability accumulation, leaving the underlying serological activity unaddressed. This study evaluated the capacity of satralizumab to achieve a comprehensive remission, as defined by the Serological, Relapse, and Accumulated-disability Remission (SERA-3) target.MethodsIn this multicenter, real-world cohort study, patients with NMOSD from three tertiary centers in China initiating satralizumab were enrolled. AQP4-IgG levels were measured at baseline, 6, and 12 months. Clinical efficacy [annualized relapse rate (ARR), Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS)] and safety were evaluated. Heterogeneity in antibody response was analyzed, and baseline characteristics potentially associated with titer reduction were explored.ResultsOf the 19 patients who were AQP4-IgG seropositive at baseline, 7 (36.84%) met the strict definition of SERA-3 Complete. Of these, 4 patients achieved seroconversion at month 6 and remained seronegative through month 12, whereas 3 first achieved seroconversion at month 12. An additional 5 (26.32%) showed a reduction in AQP4-IgG titers. Satralizumab treatment was associated with marked clinical benefit. Among 33 patients included in the clinical efficacy analysis, 27 (81.82%) remained relapse-free during follow-up, median ARR decreased from 0.92 (IQR 0.56–1.59) to 0 (IQR 0–0), and EDSS scores remained stable in most patients. Exploratory analyses suggested that patients achieving serological response were older at disease onset (57.42 ± 17.82 versus 42 ± 14.24 years, p = 0.039) and had experienced fewer pre-treatment relapses (median 2 versus 5, p = 0.049). Satralizumab was generally well tolerated, including in older patients.ConclusionsOur findings support SERA-3 as a potential treatment framework for NMOSD. Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Current neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) treatment focuses on preventing relapses and disability, leaving the underlying serological disease activity unaddressed.This study investigated whether satralizumab could achieve a novel, comprehensive remission target (SERA-3) encompassing serological, relapse, and disability outcomes. What was learned from the study? Seroconversion was achieved in 7 of 19 evaluable patients (36.84%), meeting the strict definition of SERA-3.Older age at onset and fewer pre-treatment relapses showed exploratory associations with serological response, supporting a future hypothesis‑generating framework that could inform treat-to-target strategies after prospective validation.

Introduction

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is an autoimmune disorder of the central nervous system driven by aquaporin-4 immunoglobulin G (AQP4-IgG) and characterized by frequent relapses leading to cumulative disability [1, 2]. AQP4-IgG not only serves as a diagnostic biomarker but also as a key pathogenic effector driving disease activity. While the association between short-term AQP4-IgG fluctuations and imminent relapse risk remains debated [3, 4], substantial evidence supports that increases in AQP4-IgG levels precede clinical attacks and are associated with more severe relapses [5]. Conversely, sustained seronegativity is theoretically linked to reduced autoimmune activity and relapse risk. Achieving durable suppression or clearance of AQP4-IgG remains a critical goal of NMOSD therapy.

The persistence of AQP4-IgG seropositivity despite conventional immunosuppressants or next-generation B-cell-depleting biologics underscores a major therapeutic limitation. While these agents effectively reduce relapse rates, they rarely achieve sustained suppression or seroconversion of AQP4-IgG [6]. This observation highlights a fundamental pathophysiological gap: eliminating circulating B cells alone is insufficient to eradicate the true sources of pathogenic antibody production, which are tissue-resident B cells [7] and long-lived plasma cells that persist in protective niches such as the bone marrow [8]. Moreover, emerging strategies targeting antibody-secreting cells (e.g., anti-CD38/anti-CD138 antibodies or chimeric antigen receptor T-cell immunotherapy therapies) remain experimental and unvalidated in NMOSD [9], thus raising the question: how can we comprehensively silence the autoimmune humoral response?

A more effective strategy may involve targeting the central cytokine signals that regulate the entire lifecycle of AQP4-IgG-producing cells. The interleukin-6 (IL-6) signaling pathway, which promotes B-cell activation, plasmablast differentiation, and plasma cell survival, is a key therapeutic target [10, 11]. Consequently, inhibition of the IL-6 receptor (IL-6R) offers a potential solution to disrupt the continuous generation of pathogenic auto-antibodies. Satralizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting IL-6R, is the first Food and Drug Administration-approved IL-6 pathway inhibitor for NMOSD. Clinical trials have demonstrated its efficacy in reducing relapse rates [12], and real-world reports suggest that it may induce AQP4-IgG seroconversion in some patients [5]. While reductions in antibody titers have also been reported with other biologics, such as inebilizumab [13], AQP4-IgG seroconversion appears to be less commonly described. This positions satralizumab as a promising therapy that not only controls disease activity but may also offer the potential for serological remission, advancing NMOSD treatment beyond clinical quiescence.

However, the landscape of serological response to satralizumab in real-world practice remains unclear. The rate of AQP4-IgG seroconversion and the clinical predictors of successful response are poorly understood. This knowledge gap limits our ability to individualize therapy and constrains the evolution of treatment goals beyond relapse control alone. To address this, our multicenter cohort study systematically maps longitudinal AQP4-IgG dynamics, quantifies seroconversion rates, and examines baseline predictors of antibody reduction under IL-6 receptor blockade. By elucidating how satralizumab modulates the pathogenic humoral response in real-world practice, these findings aim to inform the development of more comprehensive, biology-informed treatment goals that integrate serological remission with clinical outcomes.

Methods

Study Population

This real-world cohort study enrolled patients with NMOSD from three tertiary medical centers in Beijing, China: China-Japan Friendship Hospital, First Hospital of Tsinghua University, and Peking University People's Hospital. Recruitment occurred between January and December 2024 at these three centers, and follow-up ended on October 31, 2025. All patients were diagnosed according to the 2015 International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria for NMOSD [1].

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) confirmed NMOSD diagnosis with documented history of AQP4-IgG seropositivity at any time before satralizumab initiation; (2) a minimum follow-up duration of 3 months; (3) availability of complete medical records in the institutional NMOSD registry; and (4) for the subgroup assessing serological dynamics, patients must have at least two AQP4-IgG measurements taken ≥ 6 months apart after starting satralizumab.

For the overall cohort assessing clinical outcomes, all patients who received at least one dose of satralizumab were included to evaluate efficacy, safety, and retention rates. The subgroup evaluating AQP4-IgG dynamics was limited to those with sufficient serial antibody testing data.

This study was conducted in accordance with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the China-Japan Friendship Hospital (Approval No. 2025-KY-396–1). Informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians.

Satralizumab Administration and Concomitant Treatments

After baseline clinical and laboratory assessments, eligible patients received at least one dose of satralizumab. The standard dosing regimen consisted of subcutaneous injections of 120 mg at Weeks 0, 2, and 4, followed by maintenance dosing of 120 mg every 4 weeks thereafter.

For patients who were receiving conventional immunosuppressive therapies (ISTs) prior to satralizumab initiation, background ISTs were continued in combination with satralizumab. The dosage of concomitant ISTs was adjusted based on each patient’s clinical stability and the treating physician’s judgment. In this cohort, most patients received mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) at maintenance doses of 1000–2000 mg/day or oral glucocorticoids (GC) with gradual tapering (prednisone equivalent 5–40 mg/day at baseline). Two patient received azathioprine (AZA) at doses of 100–150 mg/day in combination with low-dose glucocorticoids, consistent with standard clinical practice.

Clinical Data and Efficacy Evaluation

The baseline clinical data were retrospectively retrieved from the electronic medical record systems of the participating institutions. Key parameters included: (1) demographics: age at NMOSD onset, age at satralizumab initiation, sex and body mass index (BMI); (2) disease history: coexistent with other autoimmune diseases, clinical course prior to satralizumab, number of attacks before satralizumab, annualized relapse rate (ARR), symptoms at onset [e.g., optic neuritis (ON), transverse myelitis (TM), area postrema syndrome (APS), brainstem syndrome (BS)], baseline Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score [14] before satralizumab, and time from the last attack to satralizumab initiation; (3) previous treatment: type and duration of prior ISTs, and concomitant immunosuppressive regimens at the time of satralizumab initiation (e.g., monotherapy with GC, MMF, or AZA).

Clinical response to satralizumab was evaluated based on changes in ARR and EDSS scores before and after treatment. Patients who completed at least 6 months of follow-up after satralizumab initiation were included in the clinical efficacy. To ensure reliable annualized rate calculations, only patients with a pre-treatment disease duration of at least 6 months were included. The pre-treatment ARR was defined as the total number of relapses from diagnosis to treatment initiation, divided by the corresponding disease duration (in years). The on-treatment ARR was calculated by dividing the total number of post-satralizumab relapses by the aggregate patient-years of observation (from the first dose to the data cutoff, October 31, 2025). A clinical relapse was defined as the occurrence of new or worsening neurologic symptoms lasting ≥ 24 h, accompanied by new or enhancing lesions on MRI, which could not be attributed to fever, infection, or other causes. For patients followed up for ≥ 12 months, changes in ARR and EDSS from baseline to the last follow-up visit were compared.

AQP4-IgG Dynamics Study

AQP4-IgG levels were measured using a validated fixed cell-based assay performed by an independent certified laboratory. Serum samples were collected at baseline (pre-treatment) and at 6 and 12 months following the initiation of satralizumab therapy. Patients who received plasma exchange or immunoadsorption during follow-up and lacked complete longitudinal antibody data were excluded from the AQP4-IgG dynamics analysis.

Patients with available AQP4-IgG measurements at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months were included in the longitudinal AQP4-IgG dynamics analysis. In cases where additional tests were required due to clinical events, extra serum samples were collected as deemed necessary by the treating physician. The primary serological outcome was treatment-associated antibody improvement, defined as AQP4-IgG seroconversion and/or reduction in antibody titer (serological response), assessed among patients who were AQP4-IgG seropositive at baseline. A secondary exploratory analysis was performed among patients with available longitudinal antibody measurements to characterize patterns of AQP4-IgG dynamics, including seroconversion, titer reduction, stable serostatus, and seronegative-to-seropositive conversion.

Predictor analyses of serological response and evaluation of SERA-3 attainment were restricted to patients who were baseline-seropositive. Serological findings were further interpreted in the context of SERA-3, defined as AQP4-IgG seronegativity together with relapse-free status and stable disability. Disability stability was defined as the absence of sustained EDSS worsening during follow-up, reflecting functional outcomes following relapse-associated events. Serological outcomes were correlated with clinical endpoints, including relapse rates and changes in the EDSS.

Laboratory Tests and Safety Assessment

Laboratory evaluations were performed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months after initiating satralizumab therapy. These assessments included complete blood counts, liver and renal function panels, coagulation profiles, serum immunoglobulin levels (IgG), lymphocyte subset analysis (CD19⁺ B cells), and other relevant biochemical parameters.

Safety monitoring was conducted throughout the treatment period. Adverse events occurring during hospitalization or follow-up, such as infections, hypersensitivity or infusion-related reactions, thromboembolic events, and other clinically significant complications, were documented. Laboratory results were reviewed for potential treatment-related causality, and any abnormalities were evaluated.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were described as mean and standard deviation with a normal distribution and median (interquartile range, IQR) with non-normal distribution, and categorical variables were expressed as count and percentage. Group comparisons (e.g., based on AQP4-IgG dynamic changes) were performed using independent t tests (normal distribution), Mann–Whitney U tests (non-normal distribution), chi-square tests, or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Two-tailed p values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

One patient was excluded due to a change in treatment preference, leaving 33 patients for the primary efficacy and safety analysis. For the AQP4-IgG dynamics study, 11 patients were excluded for the following reasons: pending follow-up (n = 5), incomplete AQP4-IgG monitoring (n = 4), and loss to follow-up (n = 2). Therefore, the analysis cohort consisted of 22 patients with complete AQP4-IgG data at both baseline and follow-up visits, who were included in the evaluation of AQP4-IgG dynamics. Clinical Efficacy of Satralizumab

A total of 33 patients were included in the efficacy, drug-retention, and safety analyses. The median follow-up duration was 51.22 months (IQR 23.07–98.32) from disease onset. The median follow-up duration after satralizumab initiation was 5.89 months (IQR 4.86–10.63; range 3–18 months), reflecting the inclusion of recently enrolled patients who had not yet completed long-term follow-up. During follow-up, 4 patients experienced disease relapse while on satralizumab therapy. Detailed clinical characteristics of these patients are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. Overall, of the 33 patients who completed at least six months of follow-up, 27 remained relapse-free and continued satralizumab treatment without experiencing intolerable adverse events.

To further evaluate longitudinal clinical efficacy, changes in ARR and disability were assessed in the 22 patients who completed 12 months of follow-up. In this subgroup, the total observation time was 5.514 patient-years. The median ARR declined from 0.92 (IQR: 0.56–1.59) at baseline to 0 (IQR: 0–0) after 12 months of treatment. EDSS scores at baseline, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months were 2 (0–6), 2 (0–6), 2 (0–4.5), and 2 (0–4.5), respectively, indicating overall stability or modest improvement in disability over time. Among patients receiving concomitant oral GC at baseline (n = 13), the dose was either tapered or maintained at a low level (5–10 mg/day prednisone equivalent) during satralizumab treatment, consistent with standard clinical practice.

AQP4-IgG titers at baseline and follow-up are shown in Fig. 3. In the 22 patients with 12-month follow-up and available longitudinal serological data, heterogeneous patterns of AQP4-IgG dynamics were observed. Specifically, 7 patients (31.82%) seroconverted to negative (4 at month 6 with sustained negativity through month 12; 3 at month 12 only), 5 (22.73%) showed a decrease in titers, 4 (18.18%) remained stable, and 6 (27.28%) experienced an increase in AQP4-IgG levels.

Clinical outcomes based on AQP4-IgG dynamics showed that patients in the serological response group tended to show lower ARR and modest improvements in EDSS scores compared with those in the no-response group, although these differences did not reach statistical significance.

Among 22 patients, 19 patients who were AQP4-IgG seropositive at baseline were included in the primary serological response analysis. In this baseline-seropositive subgroup, 7 of 19 patients achieved seroconversion, representing 36.84% of evaluable patients. This is consistent with the strict definition of SERA-3. In addition, 5 patients demonstrated significant titer reduction accompanied by favorable clinical trends, which were considered as exploratory indicators of treatment-associated serological improvement.

Among the 7 patients who attained SERA-3 during follow-up, 4 achieved this status at month 6 and remained in SERA‑3 at month 12, representing an observed duration of 6 months. The remaining 3 patients achieved SERA‑3 at month 12; therefore, their duration beyond the study endpoint could not be assessed with the current 12-month follow-up. Importantly, all 7 patients were SERA‑3‑positive at the final visit.

Exploratory Analysis of Baseline Characteristics Associated with Serological Response

To explore potential baseline associations with serological response, we first performed an exploratory analysis including all 22 patients with longitudinal AQP4-IgG data. As shown in Table 2, patients achieving serological response (n = 12) were older at NMOSD onset (57.42 ± 17.82 versus 42.0 ± 14.24 years, p = 0.039) and experienced fewer pre-treatment relapses (median 2 versus 5, p = 0.049) than non-responders. They also tended to be older at satralizumab initiation, have shorter disease duration, lower baseline EDSS, and shorter interval from last attack to treatment, although these differences did not reach statistical significance.

Safety and Tolerability

Satralizumab, whether used as monotherapy or in combination with conventional immunosuppressive therapies, was generally well tolerated in this cohort (Table 3), including older patients. Among the 33 patients evaluated for safety, 10 were aged over 65 years at satralizumab initiation, and 3 were older than 80 years. Overall, treatment-related adverse events were mild to moderate in severity and manageable. Mild adverse events occurred in 11 patients, and serious adverse events occurred in 1 patient.

Two patients developed leukopenia and neutropenia, one of which improved with intermittent administration of granulocyte-stimulating agents. Two patients developed hypogammaglobulinemia during follow-up, both remaining clinically stable without severe infections. Mild elevation of serum creatinine was observed in one patient; however, this abnormality predated satralizumab initiation and did not progress during treatment, suggesting a limited association with the drug. One patient experienced COVID-19 infection and herpes zoster infection during the satralizumab use period.

One patient who had previously developed Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia following inebilizumab therapy was switched to satralizumab, with no recurrence of infection after 6 months of treatment, but with elevated tumor markers, which was not considered related to satralizumab. Another patient with rituximab-induced low serum IgM levels was transitioned to satralizumab, and IgM remained stable without further decline during 18 months of follow-up.

A single case of cerebral infarction occurred in an 84-year-old female after the first dose of satralizumab, leading to treatment discontinuation; she has since been maintained on low-dose corticosteroid therapy. Apart from these cases, no serious treatment-related adverse events or opportunistic infections were reported among the older participants.

Discussion

Our study provides three key advancements in the understanding of satralizumab in NMOSD management. First, we move beyond the binary question of clinical efficacy and delineate, for the first time in a real-world setting, the heterogeneous landscape of serological responses, confirming that a substantial proportion of patients achieve meaningful AQP4-IgG reduction. Second, our data generate the compelling hypothesis that earlier intervention with IL-6 blockade, prior to the establishment of a protracted autoimmune state, may be critical for optimizing serological outcomes. Finally, our findings support the concept of “serological remission” as a potentially achievable and desirable treatment goal, which may inform a more precise therapeutic strategy in NMOSD.

The capacity of satralizumab to induce serological remission was reflected by a 36.84% rate of sustained AQP4-IgG seroconversion, a rate that appears comparable to, or numerically higher than, those reported in prior studies [5, 15], although cross-study comparisons should be interpreted with caution. For instance, Valentino et al. reported that rituximab reduces AQP4-IgG titers in responders at 3 months post-infusion and during long-term follow-up, although exact seroconversion rates were not provided [16]. For inebilizumab, a recent prospective study reported a 29.8% rate of titer reduction and a 16.4% seroconversion rate at 1 year [13]. Compared with these B-cell-directed therapies, IL-6 receptor blockade targets a key cytokine involved in B-cell activation and plasma cell survival, potentially modulating the pathogenic antibody reservoir through mechanisms complementary to, rather than overlapping with, direct B-cell depletion [17, 18]. These observations highlight that different therapeutic strategies may converge on antibody modulation through distinct biological mechanisms and suggest that satralizumab may achieve clinically meaningful serological outcomes in a real-world setting.

We further explored factors associated with serological response and observed that AQP4-IgG reduction was more frequently seen in patients with older age at onset and lower pre-treatment relapse burden. Although these findings did not reach statistical significance in multivariate analysis, they suggest that disease stage and immunological context may influence responsiveness to IL-6 blockade. Notably, patients with fewer prior relapses and shorter disease duration tended to show more favorable serological responses, supporting the hypothesis that earlier intervention before the establishment of sustained autoreactive immune memory, may enhance the likelihood of achieving serological remission. These observations are consistent with the concept that timely immunomodulation may influence antibody dynamics in NMOSD, as supported by evidence linking early treatment to improved clinical outcomes and by the established role of IL-6 in promoting plasmablast survival and AQP4-IgG production [18, 19].

The association between AQP4-IgG dynamics and clinical outcomes appears to be multifactorial [20–22], and should be interpreted with caution. In this study, serological remission was defined strictly as AQP4-IgG seronegativity, while reductions in antibody titers were analyzed as exploratory findings to avoid ambiguity related to physiological fluctuation. In this context, serological remission, particularly sustained AQP4-IgG seronegativity, may represent a complementary therapeutic dimension alongside relapse prevention. In our cohort, relapse events occurred mainly in patients with serological instability or suboptimal treatment exposure, suggesting that AQP4-IgG dynamics can serve as an integrative biomarker of immune control. These observations support the potential clinical utility of incorporating longitudinal serological assessment into disease monitoring. From a practical perspective, treatment strategies may benefit from integrating clinical and serological information, including careful evaluation of treatment adherence and appropriate adjustment of concomitant immunosuppressive therapy. However, the extent to which serological changes directly translate into improved clinical outcomes remains to be further validated in prospective studies.

Regarding safety, aligning with long-term data from Sakura studies [23], satralizumab was generally well-tolerated in our cohort, including among older patients, with 10 patients over 65 years of age. The most common adverse events were mild to moderate and manageable, including leukopenia, neutropenia, and hypogammaglobulinemia. Importantly, one patient experienced a cerebral infarction following the first dose, leading to treatment discontinuation. Although this event was considered potentially unrelated to the drug, it underscores the importance of careful benefit–risk assessments, especially in older patients, a group often underrepresented in clinical trials. Furthermore, an elevation in serum creatinine was observed in one patient, but it was deemed unrelated to satralizumab, as levels were already elevated prior to treatment.

Collectively, our findings confirm the efficacy of satralizumab and highlight the limitations of a treatment framework focused solely on relapse prevention and disability stabilization. Persistent AQP4-IgG may reflect ongoing subclinical immune activity, indicating that antibody dynamics can represent a modifiable therapeutic dimension. Accordingly, we propose a multidimensional framework, SERA-3, comprising serological remission (AQP4-IgG seronegativity), relapse remission (ARR = 0), and disability stability (absence of sustained EDSS worsening). This framework may capture a deeper level of disease control; however, its serological component is most applicable to therapies that modulate B-cell function or antibody production, and may not extend to complement inhibitors.

A key next step is to determine whether attainment of SERA-3 can inform therapeutic decision-making, including treatment de-escalation. In our cohort, sustained SERA-3 attainment was associated with stable disease control, supporting its potential as an exploratory composite endpoint for immunological response, although its role in guiding therapy withdrawal requires further validation. Emerging evidence also suggests that asymptomatic radiological activity may represent an additional dimension of disease burden [24, 25]. Therefore, SERA-3 should be viewed as an evolving framework that may be expanded to incorporate imaging-based measures and emerging biomarkers (e.g., glial fibrillary acidic protein) as the field advances.

This study has limitations, including its small cohort size, which precluded the identification of statistically significant predictors, and the relatively short, 12-month follow-up period. In addition, only a subset of patients completed longitudinal serological follow-up and were included in the AQP4-IgG dynamics analysis, introducing the possibility of selection bias. Moreover, detailed data on concomitant oral glucocorticoid dosage adjustments were not systematically recorded, historical anti-MOG-IgG status was unavailable for patients who were AQP4-IgG seronegative, and the AQP4-IgG assay was based on a fixed-cell cell-based assay, which may not be directly comparable across assay platforms. The absence of a control group necessitates cautious interpretation. However, these limitations do not diminish the central finding of heterogeneous yet achievable seroconversion; rather, they clearly define the agenda for future research. Our work specifically calls for large-scale, prospective studies designed with serological end points as a primary outcome to validate the exploratory associations identified in this study and to establish the long-term clinical value of achieving and maintaining AQP4-IgG negativity.

Conclusions

Our findings support SERA-3 as a hypothesis-generating framework for assessing multidimensional disease control in NMOSD, pending validation in future studies. In this real-world cohort, IL-6 pathway inhibition enabled attainment of SERA-3 in a substantial proportion of patients, supporting further investigation of this framework as a candidate endpoint for future biomarker-guided therapeutic strategies.

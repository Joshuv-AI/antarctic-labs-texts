Trends of asthma mortality in china: a comprehensive analysis by population, region, and sex with future projections
Authors: Hang Su, Wen Hua, Wuping Bao, Zichong Xu, Xue Zhang, Lei Zhao, Lei Han, Yishu Xue, Huahao Shen, Min Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAsthma prevalence in China has risen due to rapid industrialization, environmental changes, and improved diagnostics. While efforts in asthma control have expanded nationwide, research on asthma-related mortality remains limited. Understanding death patterns from asthma is important to identify key regions and populations at risk, and to support more effective prevention and treatment strategies suitable for China’s population and healthcare system.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study using China CDC’s DSPs data, stratifying asthma mortality by sex, region, urban-rural residence, and age. This is a nationwide population and mortality survey study, including data on the total surveyed population and deaths from 2008 to 2021, covering approximately 25% of the total population. Trend analysis was conducted on the total surveyed population stratified by age, sex, and region to explore patterns of mortality changes. Based on mortality trend changes, decomposition analysis of asthma mortality and future trend projections considering population changes were performed to further identify key regions and populations.ResultsAsthma mortality in China showed significant regional variation, with higher rates in the southwestern region and lower rates in northern and coastal regions. The crude mortality rate of asthma in China (2008–2021) generally decreased, especially in the older population (33.8% decline), but increased in individuals aged 15–19 in recent years. Mortality rates peaked around 2015 and then declined annually. Geographically, the most substantial decline rate was observed in the eastern region (10.5% decline), followed by the central region (8.1% decline). However, the decline rate in the western region was less pronounced and even displayed an upward trend, particularly in urban areas. Both decomposition analysis and BAPC analysis revealed an unclear mortality trend in urban areas of the western region, while mortality declined in the eastern and central regions.ConclusionThe asthma mortality rate in China has experienced a comprehensive decline over the past decade. However, rates in certain groups, like the older population and those in western China, remained higher than the global average, while in the youth population a slight upward trend was observed. Addressing these disparities is crucial for future efforts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-025-03930-4.

Introduction

Asthma, a chronic respiratory disease. In China, the prevalence of asthma with wheezing symptoms among individuals aged 20 and above is 4.2%, which is estimated to correspond to 45.7 million asthma patients [1]. Additionally, approximately 2.9% of children are also affected [2]. Since 2010, the prevalence of asthma among adolescents (aged 14–18) and adults in China has shown an upward trend [3]. The increasing prevalence of asthma in China is likely attributed to the rapid industrialization process and profound environmental and lifestyle shifts over the past few decades along with the increase in the diagnosis technique. The above studies provide data on asthma prevalence, but there is relatively little research on asthma mortality in China.

Asthma-related mortality in China is influenced by several unfavorable factors, including the large and growing older population, significant regional disparities in access to medical services, and persistent traditional beliefs—such as resistance to corticosteroid use. Furthermore, China exhibits a distinctive trend diverging from other countries like the UK [4] and the US [5], showcasing the highest prevalence of asthma among the older population [1]. Aging has been recognized as an important factor in asthma pathophysiology, symptoms, and therapeutic responses [6]. Elderly asthma patients tend to present with increased severity of symptoms and pose more challenges in management, often requiring higher doses of inhaled corticosteroids and long-acting β-agonists when compared to patients under the age of 45 [7]. The above factors may result in a mortality trend among the Chinese population that differs from that of Western countries.

In recent years, China has made significant progress in asthma prevention, control and treatment strategies. These initiatives have been systematically strengthened, including the implementation of standardized asthma management programs and a sustained increase in national healthcare investment, with a particular emphasis on improving access in remote areas. Moreover, there has been a concerted effort to provide comprehensive education for primary care physicians across various levels of medical institutions. A nationwide multicenter survey launched in 2015 showed that the asthma control rate in ten cities, including Beijing, increased from 28.7% in 2008 to 39.2% in 2015 [8]. Despite these advancements, the current status of asthma mortality in the country remains uncertain, thus prompting our investigation into the evolving trends of asthma mortality in our nation. Understanding these changes is crucial for potentially identifying certain regions and populations for targeted asthma prevention and control. Additionally, such insights could provide guidance on the future direction of efforts.

Our study utilized data from 2008 to 2021, sourced from disease surveillance points (DSPs) on asthma mortality. Through a hierarchical analysis, we revealed significant variations based on age, geography, urban-rural disparities, and sex. This comprehensive examination of asthma mortality trends over an extended period provides valuable insights into the real-world state of asthma management in China, especially following shifts in our approach to drug therapy and symptom control.

Methods

Data source

We conducted a retrospective population-based descriptive study using data derived from the DSPs system administered by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) from 2008 to 2021. The DSPs system, instituted by the Chinese central government in 1978, employs a sampling survey methodology to delineate the general characteristics of the national population.

To address challenges posed by the vast expanse and uneven development across the country, coupled with the indistinct demarcation between pediatric and adult healthcare departments, the CDC stratified asthma mortality into subgroups based on geographic regions, urban and rural residence, and age (categorized every five years, except for 0–1 years, 2–4 years and over 85 years group, as shown in Fig. 5). Provinces in China were categorized into eastern, central, and western regions based on geography. Specifically, the eastern region includes Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Liaoning, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Shandong, Guangdong, and Hainan; the central region includes Shanxi, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, and Hunan; and the western region includes Inner Mongolia, Guangxi, Chongqing, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Tibet, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Xinjiang.

Each surveillance point represented a distinct district and compiles data from death certificates issued by medical institutions, households, or other pertinent locations within its jurisdiction. Causes of death in DSPs were uniformly determined according to a standardized protocol by personnel stationed in hospitals or local CDC offices. In 2013, the CDC streamlined and expanded the original death cause registration system and additional death cause reporting systems. This integration led to a Nationwide expansion of monitoring points from 161 to 605, covering over 200 million individuals, thus constituting approximately 24% of the total population. Consequently, a greater number of cases from western and rural areas were captured in the dataset.

The provincial-level data in China were obtained from the CDC. The dataset includes the crude asthma mortality rates in 2013. Patients whose underlying cause of death was attributed to asthma (coded as J45 and J46 in the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision) were selected and included in our study.

Statistical analysis

First, we calculated the mortality rates of asthma in all age groups in China from 2008 to 2021 and plotted curves to describe the trend. Mortality was calculated per 100,000 persons. The population was grouped into 20 age groups every 5 years from 0 to 1 years to over 85 years by CDC, then we merged these groups into four major groups through standardization: 0–14 years, 15–19 years, 20–44 years, 45–64 years, and over 65 years based on the formula below:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{SMR}\;=\;\frac{\sum{\mathrm{nP}}_{\mathrm z}\;\cdot{\mathrm{nM}}_{\mathrm x}}{\sum{\mathrm{nP}}_{\mathrm x}}$$\end{document}

SMR is the abbreviation for standardized mortality rate, and “nPx” & “nMx” stands for age-specific population and age-specific mortality, respectively. “n” represents the class interval of the age groups, and “x” represents the starting age of each age-specific group.

Trend analysis

Given the significantly larger scope of the population census since 2013 compared to all previous years, we focused more on the data from 2013 to 2021 and calculated the trend of mortality for each subgroup. The Spearman correlation test was employed to analyze trends. In our criteria, a correlation coefficient of 0 implies no observable trend in mortality rate changes. All the above steps were executed in R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and all figures were generated using the ggplot2 package. Subsequently, heat maps of the obtained results were generated to visualize the diversity of trends in different subgroups using GraphPad Prism (version 9.5.1). The heatmap based on asthma mortality rates in various countries around the world was created using ArcGIS 10.8.

Decomposition analysis

We utilized the decomposition method to break down the changes in the death number of asthma into contributions from aging, population growth, and epidemiological changes. Unlike descriptive trend analyses that summarize net changes, decomposition isolates the independent quantitative contributions of each driver to observed burden variations [9]. Through this approach, we precisely delineated how population structures, growth trajectories, and evolving disease risks collectively influenced national asthma mortality patterns.

Projecting future mortality trends using BAPC model

The Bayesian Age-Period-Cohort (BAPC) model, used in epidemiology and biostatistics, was used to analyze the relationship between incidence rates and time [10]. This model decomposes time into three distinct dimensions: age effects, period effects, and cohort effects, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of changes in disease burden. By integrating these dimensions, the BAPC model provides a nuanced approach to studying temporal trends and their underlying factors, making it a valuable tool for researchers and policymakers in addressing public health challenges. All the above steps were executed in R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The BAPC package was used to predict the 2022–2030 mortality trend. The BAPC model was implemented using the BAPC, INLA (version INLA_24.12.11), remotes (R 4.4.3), and foreach (R 4.4.3) packages.

Results

Asthma mortality in 2021

The mortality rates of asthma in both males and females in China exhibited significant regional disparities. Southern and southwestern regions (e.g., Hubei (5.10/100,000), Yunnan (5.20/100,000), Guizhou (4.47/100,000), Guangxi (4.41/100,000), Hainan (5.02/100,000)). In contrast, northern and northwestern regions (e.g., Heilongjiang (0.27/100,000), Inner Mongolia (0.55/100,000), Tibet (0.51/100,000), Qinghai (1.70/100,000), Xinjiang (2.71/100,000)) had lower mortality rates. The eastern coastal regions displayed uneven mortality rates, with some areas showing moderate rates (e.g., Guangdong (1.91/100,000), Fujian (2.44/100,000), Shanghai (1.85/100,000), Tianjin (2.64/100,000)) and others exhibiting lower rates (e.g., Zhejiang (0.74/100,000), Jiangsu (1.19/100,000), Shandong (0.98/100,000)). Special administrative regions (e.g., Hong Kong (1.37/100,000), Macau (1.75/100,000)) had lower mortality rates. Overall, climate, air quality, geographical environment, and healthcare conditions were the primary factors influencing asthma mortality rates in both males and females. Trends of asthma mortality, 2013–2021

Given the larger scope of the population census since 2013, we focused on data from 2013 to 2021 and computed mortality trends for each subgroup. The trends (r, Spearman) for each subgroup are shown in Fig. 6. Overall, the mortality rate showed a decreasing trend, particularly in the older population, especially in the group aged 65–84. Significant decreases were observed in individuals aged 25–29 (36.4% decline, P < 0.001), 55–59 (17.0% decline, P = 0.008), 65–69 (39.5% decline, P < 0.001), 70–74 (33.3% decline, P < 0.001), 75–79 (38.0% decline, P = 0.008), and 80–84 (34.9% decline, P < 0.001). The overall mortality rate decreased from 1.52/100,000 to 1.45/100,000 (4.6% decline, P = 0.081).

The eastern region saw the greatest decline (10.5%, P = 0.081), followed by the central region (8.1%, P = 0.552). The western region showed less of a decline, with a slight increase in western cities from 1.39 to 1.78 (28.1% increase, P = 0.205). This regional imbalance was evident, especially in individuals aged 65–84. Notably, asthma mortality increased among men aged 40–44 in eastern rural areas (45.5%, P = 0.005) and women aged 45–49 in central (93.8%, P = 0.003) and western rural areas (47.4%, P = 0.026).

After standardization, data were stratified by age (Fig. 6). Mortality declined most in the 65 + group (33.8%, P < 0.001), reaching 8.12/100,000 in 2021. Decomposition analysis of mortality changes from 2013 to 2021

The decomposition analysis (Fig. 7, table S1) attributed asthma mortality changes (2013–2021) to population growth, aging, and epidemiological change. In urban males, deaths increased by 110, with a decline of 27 in the east but rises of 32 and 105 in the central and western regions. Population aging reduced mortality, while population growth and epidemiological change increased it. For urban females, deaths fell by 16 in the central region but rose by 24 and 106 in the east and west, with the western region driving over 90% of the increase. It is evident that the rise in asthma-related deaths in urban areas was predominantly driven by increases in the western region.

BAPC-projected trend of age-standardized mortality rate for 2022–2030

To adjust for age structure, we calculated age-standardized asthma mortality rates (ASMR) for 2013–2021 and used the BAPC model to project trends for 2022–2030 (Fig. 8, table S2). In eastern China, mortality declined significantly except for rural males, where ASMR may reach 0.65 (0.50)/100,000 in 2030. The central region showed a steady decline with narrow confidence intervals. Although fluctuations in mortality were observed in rural areas of central China, they did not alter the overall downward trend, nor did they lead to an increase in variability.

Discussion

Asthma mortality and prevalence in China

Numerous studies worldwide have investigated trends in asthma mortality [11–15]. Our research contributes evidence for ongoing surveillance of asthma-related health outcomes in China. Previously, Tong conducted research that identified the variations in asthma mortality rates across Chinese provinces between 2014 and 2020 [16]. Building on this, our analysis explores the influences of age, sex, urban-rural residence, and regional differences in asthma mortality in China from 2008 to 2021. Notably, significant geographic and age-related disparities persist, with the highest mortality rates observed in rural areas of the western region and among individuals aged 65 and over.

Previous studies have shown that the prevalence of asthma in China has been increasing year by year. Before 2000, the prevalence was 1.25%, and by 2016, it had more than doubled, reaching 3.33% [17]. However, the overall prevalence of asthma in China remains significantly lower than that in the five major European countries (France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the United Kingdom) [18], and is comparable to that of South Korea [19]. Although the prevalence of asthma in China is showing a significant upward trend (projected to affect approximately 9.76% of the total population by 2050) [17], the asthma mortality rate continues to decline considerably—and at an even greater rate. This suggests that healthcare professionals in China have made asthma a key focus in the pursuit of public health, striving to improve early diagnosis and treatment, enhance patients’ quality of life, and reduce the overall disease burden.

Asthma mortality differences between China and other countries

Our study revealed that the characteristics and trends of asthma mortality among children and adolescents in China differ significantly from those observed in other countries. In the global pediatric population, asthma mortality rates were reported as 0.26/100,000 in Mexico (ages 0–14) in 2016 [20] and 0.19/100,000 in Finland during 1999–2015 [21]. In contrast, the rate in China was substantially lower, at 0.02/100,000 in 2021. Among adolescents, asthma mortality was reported as 0.36/100,000 in the United States (ages 15–24) [22], and 0.23–0.28/100,000 in Costa Rica (ages 10–20) [23], whereas in China (ages 15–19), the mortality was 0.04/100,000 in 2021.

Compared to the findings of Liu et al. (2008–2018) [24], our analysis indicates a 100.0% increase in the mortality rates among adolescents aged 15–19 in the more recent period (2019–2021), suggesting a notable upward trend. This increase may be partly attributed to a rising prevalence of allergic diseases among the younger population [25]. Another potential contributing factor is the transitional status of this age group, which may not be fully integrated into either pediatric or adult healthcare systems, potentially leading to gaps in diagnosis, clinical management, and treatment.

Our study reveals pronounced disparities in asthma mortality across age groups and regions in China, with elevated rates among older adults and residents of western rural areas. Asthma mortality rates among middle-aged individuals were reported as 0.56 to 0.86/100,000 in the United States (aged 25–44) [22], 0.62/100,000 in Puerto Rico (aged 15–34) [26], and 0.14/100,000 in China (aged 20–44) in 2021. For individuals in their fifties, reported asthma mortality rates were 1.33–1.63/100,000 in the United States (aged 45–64) [22], 0.34/100,000 in Spain (aged 35–64) [15], and 0.61/100,000 in China (45–64 years) in 2021.

Among adults aged 65 and above, asthma mortality reached 3.20/100,000 in the United States [12], 8.03/100,000 in Puerto Rico (65 + years) [26], and 7.50/100,000 in France (65 + years) [27]. In comparison, the rate in China was 8.12/100,000 in 2021. Within China, regional disparities were observed: lower mortalities were recorded in the eastern (7.43/100,000) and central (7.37/100,000) regions, while the west region exhibited a higher rate of 10.33/100,000, with male mortality reaching 11.52/100,000. These findings underscore the need for targeted public health interventions tailored to vulnerable populations.

Regional trends of asthma mortality in China

Our analysis showed that asthma mortality in China remained low among younger age groups; however, with increasing age, rates progressively rose to levels comparable to those in developing countries and, in some cases, exceeded those reported in most developed Nations. From 2013 to 2021, asthma-related mortality declined across sexes and regions, with the most pronounced reductions observed among individuals aged 65 and older.

From a regional perspective, the central region had the lowest mortality rate, followed by the eastern region, while the western region had the highest. The western region emerged as a critical area of concern, characterized by elevated mortality rates and an absence of substantial downward trend. This issue is especially prominent in urban areas of the western region, where mortality remain substantially significantly higher than in other parts of the country and exhibit irregular, unpredictable fluctuations. In contrast, rural areas within the western region, although initially presenting with higher mortality, have experienced a notable and rapid decline, with rates approaching those observed in central China.

Previous studies have shown that the eastern region of China has the highest prevalence of asthma [17], but its mortality rate is much lower than that of the western region, which may be attributed to better medical conditions in the east. In contrast, although the western region had a relatively low prevalence [17], its mortality rate was the highest, which warrants attention. These findings highlight the necessity of region-specific public health strategies and improved asthma management, particularly in urban western settings.

Age trends of asthma mortality in China

Our findings reveal a distinct epidemiological profile of asthma in China, characterized by a disproportionately high burden among older adults. This contrasts with patterns observed in many high-income countries, where asthma is more commonly considered a childhood disease [4, 5, 28]. Late-onset asthma has been associated with higher mortality compared to early-onset forms of this disease [29]. Notably, 26.2% of Chinese adults with asthma exhibit airflow limitation, a proportion considerably higher than the 16.2–18.8% reported in high-income countries [30, 31]. This elevated prevalence of airflow limitation may reflect inadequate asthma management and could partly explain the increased mortality among elderly individuals in China. Additionally, comorbidities such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), malignant neoplasms of the respiratory tract, and cardiovascular diseases are known to increase the risk of asthma-related death [32]. This risk is particularly pronounced in older adults, emphasizing the complex interaction between asthma and age-related comorbid conditions.

Directions for alleviating the burden of asthma

Despite overall low asthma mortality in China, a substantial and growing burden is concentrated among the older population. Asthma-related mortality among individuals aged 65 and above in China exceeds that reported in several other countries [11, 12, 15, 33]. This disparity may be attributed to insufficient recognition and prioritization of asthma as a public health concern in China, compounded by regional inequalities in healthcare infrastructure that limit access to comprehensive care, particularly in remote and underserved areas. Given the rapidly aging population, the number of elderly individuals with asthma is expected to rise, highlighting the need for strengthened healthcare preparedness to manage more complex and severe disease presentations.

Behavioral and environmental risk factors, particularly smoking, play a critical role in shaping asthma mortality patterns in China. Smoking is possibly a dominant influence factor for asthma mortality, as revealed previously [32]. In China, tobacco use was the leading cause of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and the second leading cause of premature death in 2017 [34]. In our analysis, asthma-related mortality was consistently higher in males than females across nearly all subgroups, with the most pronounced sex differences observed in the older population—potentially reflecting the higher prevalence of smoking among men. Additionally, evidence suggests that smoking impairs the effectiveness of asthma treatments [35, 36]. Given the widespread prevalence of smoking in China, intensified efforts toward smoking prevention and cessation should be prioritized as part of a broader strategy to reduce asthma mortality.

To address the elevated burden observed among specific populations—such as older males and residents of western regions—several targeted interventions should be considered. First, asthma screening programs should be strengthened in western and rural areas, where early detection is often limited by inadequate healthcare infrastructure. Second, public health initiatives should be implemented to promote smoking cessation among men, particularly in older age groups, in order to reduce preventable asthma-related deaths. Third, access to essential asthma medications and primary care services in underserved regions should be improved through expanded public insurance coverage, the development of telemedicine, and community outreach programs. Finally, the training of healthcare providers in rural and under-resourced areas should be enhanced to improve their capacity for accurate diagnosis, effective treatment, and long-term management of asthma in high-risk populations. The implementation of these region- and population-specific strategies may contribute significantly to reducing asthma mortality and alleviating the broader burden of the disease in China.

Limitations and strengths

Our research findings should be interpreted in light of certain limitations. First, the analysis focused exclusively on crude asthma-related deaths, potentially omitting indirect or associated causes of death. Although the DSPs system applies rigorous quality control and validation procedures, potential misclassification or miscoding of causes of death cannot be entirely ruled out, which may affect the accuracy of asthma mortality estimates. Second, the death certificates lacked information on important variables such as asthma severity, occupational exposures, smoking status before death, family history, and comorbidities (e.g., COPD, lung cancer, and cardiovascular disease), limiting the ability to assess risk factors comprehensively. Third, our study may be subject to potential biases in data collection. For instance, data from rural or remote areas may be less complete or less accurately reported compared to urban regions, possibly leading to underestimation or misrepresentation of mortality in these areas. Finally, the use of truncated data, especially in more recent years where reporting may be incomplete or delayed, could result in missing values or underreporting, affecting the generalizability and interpretation of trends—particularly for non-urban populations. These limitations highlight the need for improved data coverage and more detailed epidemiological information in future studies.

Despite these limitations, our study offers several important strengths. First, it represents the investigation of asthma mortality in China with a considerable sample size, covering approximately 24% of the National population. Second, cases were categorized into 720 subgroups based on geographical region, urban-rural residence, sex, and age, enabling a detailed analysis of temporal trends across these dimensions, which were visualized using heat maps. Finally, we explored the correlation between reductions in asthma mortality among individuals aged 65 and older and variables such as average asthma-related healthcare expenditures, government health investment, and concentrations of specific air pollutants.

Conclusion

Overall, asthma mortality in China has shown a notable downward trend. Mortality rates have remained low among children, adolescents, and middle-aged individuals, but were comparatively higher among the older population. Between 2008 and 2021, mortality declined across most age groups; however, an increase was observed among adolescents aged 15–19. Geographically, the western region consistently exhibited higher mortality than the central and eastern regions and experienced a slower rate of decline. These findings underscore the need for further research to identify modifiable risk factors and emphasize the importance of enhancing asthma self-management, particularly among the older adults and populations in the western region.

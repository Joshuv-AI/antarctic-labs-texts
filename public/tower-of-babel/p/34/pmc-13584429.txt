IMPACT—Introduction of Mycotic Prophylaxis At Cystectomy Trial: perioperative mycotic prophylaxis with fluconazole to reduce postoperative complications following cystectomy—study protocol for a national multi-center, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial
Authors: Mie Skjøttgaard Ynddal, Hein Vincent Stroomberg, Maja Vejlgaard, Jørgen Bjerggaard Jensen, Claus Moser, Ulla Nordström Joensen, Andreas Røder
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and objectivesCystectomy with urinary diversion is one of the most complex surgeries in the urological specialty involving surgery on multiple organ systems. The postoperative risk of mortality and complications is considerable, and the first months after surgery are often the most critical. When creating the urinary diversion, the abdominal cavity is exposed to bacteria and fungus from the small intestine, and after the surgery, micro-leakage from the intestinal anastomosis may also contribute to a local infectious response. This contamination is suspected to increase the risk of bowel paralysis that, even with modern ERAS protocols, significantly impacts the postoperative recovery. Emerging data show that approximately 50% of the patients undergoing cystectomy have Candida albicans in samples from the terminal ileum. Current guidelines support the use of perioperative antibiotics, but there are currently no guidelines on antimycotic prophylaxis. We present a randomized clinical trial that tests whether incorporating antimycotics into the perioperative antimicrobial prophylactic regimen can reduce major complications after cystectomy.Materials and methodsThis is a multi-center, double-blinded, randomized clinical trial including participants from all five centers in Denmark performing cystectomy. The study includes adult patients planned for cystectomy with an ileal conduit with no contraindications to fluconazole and no active treatment for mycotic infections. The 420 participants are randomized 1:1 to receive a single dose of perioperative fluconazole or placebo. Primary outcome is major postoperative complications, defined as Clavien-Dindo classification III–V, within 90 days following cystectomy. Secondary outcomes are days alive and out of hospital, gastrointestinal function, nasogastric tube placement, length of stay, readmissions, quality of life, and microbiological test results. We aim to reduce the absolute rate of Clavien-Dindo III–V complications from 30 to 18%. The study is a superiority study and analysis will be performed as intention-to-treat.DiscussionPatients undergoing cystectomy face a high risk of surgical complications, many of which can be attributed to infections. There is a growing concern about the consequences of fungal infections after abdominal surgery. Introduction

Background and rationale

Cystectomy with construction of a urinary diversion remains one of the most high-risk elective abdominal surgeries, with a 4.7% risk of postoperative mortality and 59% risk of intra- and postoperative complications within 90 days [1]. The procedure involves surgery on both the urinary and gastrointestinal system. The ileal conduit is the most common type of urinary diversion, where a section of the terminal ileum is isolated to form a stoma. The proximal end of the isolated segment is connected to the ureters, and the distal end is let through the abdominal wall and everted. This part of the procedure exposes the abdominal cavity to bacteria and fungus from the intestinal content during surgery. The intestinal anastomosis is left vulnerable to postoperative micro-leakage, contributing to a high risk of a local inflammatory response in the days following cystectomy. This may lead to postoperative complications. Although enhanced recovery programs have improved outcomes, there is still a lack of high-quality evidence to support the use of perioperative prophylaxis of infections [2, 3].

To decrease the risk of infection, current guidelines recommend using perioperative antibiotics against different bacteria, and empiric antibiotics are therefore routinely administered during the procedure in all five Danish centers performing cystectomy [4]. The antibiotic is chosen locally using a pragmatic approach, resulting in different regimens across the five centers [5]. When it comes to fungal infections, however, no guidelines address the use of antimycotic prophylaxis for cystectomy patients. Emerging data show that 48% of patients have Candida albicansin samples from the terminal ileum [5].

Fungal infections have previously been associated with a higher risk of mortality in patients undergoing gastrointestinal surgery. A Danish observational study discovered a reduction in candidemia rates when adding fluconazole to the prophylactic antibiotic treatment for patients with gastrointestinal tract perforations or who were reoperated after colorectal surgery [6, 7]. Therefore, international guidelines have started supporting the use of prophylactic fluconazole during gastrointestinal surgery and for patients with recurrent gastrointestinal perforations or anastomotic leakage [8, 9].

A retrospective study suggests that a broader antimicrobial regimen including fluconazole in addition to perioperative antibiotics can reduce the risk of infectious complications after radical cystectomy [10]. Although recurrent gastrointestinal perforations and anastomotic leakages after cystectomy are rare, the contamination from the primary surgery itself could be enough to initiate the local response, and we lack information on the true incidence of postoperative invasive candidiasis [10]. Known risk factors for invasive candidiasis are, besides gastrointestinal surgery, indwelling catheters, broad-spectrum antibiotics, mechanical ventilation, stay in the intensive care unit, parenteral nutrition, diabetes, and immunocompromisation, many of whom are seen in patients undergoing cystectomy [11]. The compelling theoretical and clinical rationale for addition of antifungal prophylaxis to standard-of-care antibiotic prophylaxis for patients undergoing cystectomy provides the basis for this study. This protocol describes a national ongoing multi-center, double-blinded, randomized clinical trial investigating the effect of perioperative mycotic prophylaxis on postoperative complications.

Objectives

Primary objective

The primary objective is to explore whether treatment with prophylactic antimycotic medicine can reduce major postoperative complications within 90 days after cystectomy compared to placebo.

Secondary objective

The secondary objective is to explore the effect of antimycotic prophylaxis compared to placebo on exploratory outcomes as days alive and out of hospital (DAOH), gastrointestinal function, nasogastric tube placement, length of stay (LOS), readmissions, and quality of life (QOL) and descriptive microbiological test results for patients undergoing cystectomy with ileal conduit.

Methods

Study design

IMPACT is a multi-center, double-blinded, randomized, clinical trial. The study is designed as a superiority study. Participants planned for cystectomy with ileal conduit are randomized in a 1:1 allocation to receive a perioperative infusion of study medicine containing either a single dose of 400 mg fluconazole or placebo containing isotonic saline solution. The allocation is blinded to patients, personnel, and data analysts. A collection of urine samples, anal swabs, and samples from the terminal ileum will be analyzed for cultivation and susceptibility. The analysis will provide information on fungal species identification and antifungal susceptibility. The microbiological test results will not influence the participation in the study. The participants are followed for 90 days, and the primary outcome is collected through prospective manual review of the electronic medical records.

This protocol is written according to the SPIRIT reporting guidelines [12]. For the complete SPIRIT checklist, please see the Appendix.

Study setting

The study is initiated and sponsored by the Urological Research Unit at Rigshospitalet. Inclusion and data collection will take place at all five urological departments in Denmark performing cystectomy: Rigshospitalet Copenhagen University Hospital, Herlev-Gentofte Hospital, Odense University Hospital, Aarhus University Hospital, and Aalborg University Hospital.

Interventions

Arm 1: fluconazole

The intervention is a single dose of intravenous fluconazole administered during the operation. The infusion will be administered before the bowel segment is isolated. The intervention consists of 400 mg fluconazole in 200 ml isotonic solution of sodium chloride. Due to the package size, the infusion will be administered consecutively from two packages of 100 ml. The duration of the infusion will be approximately 30 min.

Arm 2: placebo

The control is a single dose of 200 ml isotonic solution of sodium chloride. Due to the package size, the infusion will be administered consecutively from two packages of 100 ml. The duration of the infusion will be approximately 30 min.

Operative procedure

The participants undergo cystectomy as a standard of care procedure. The indication for performing a cystectomy with ileal conduit can be both benign and malignant conditions. The operation will be performed using either open or robot-assisted technique.

The operation includes removal of the urinary bladder, including the prostate for male patients. If the operation is performed as a radical cystectomy, a lymph node dissection is performed, and in some cases, a hysterosalpingo-oophorectomy and vaginectomy are performed in female patients. A segment of the terminal ileum is isolated, the intestinal continuity is reestablished, and the ureters are anastomosed to the proximal end of the isolated segment. The distal end of the segment is led through the skin to form a urostomy, allowing urine to pass from the kidneys, through the ureters and the conduit, to a stoma bag. All five departments adhere to ERAS protocols and no bowel preparation is used at any site.

Besides the intervention, the treatment of the participants will be standard of care according to the guidelines of the local department.

Due to different complication rates, the trial does not include other urinary diversions than the ileal conduit.

Samples

Three samples will be collected for fungal cultivation and susceptibility: a urine sample, an anal swab, and a sample from the terminal ileum. The urine and anal swab will be collected either at the outpatient clinic up to 14 days before the operation or at the operating room before draping the patient. The sample from the terminal ileum will be collected during the operation when the terminal ileum is resected to form the conduit.

Investigational medicinal product

The investigational medicinal product (IMP) is a clear, odorless fluid with a viscosity matching the viscosity of the placebo drug.

The IMP and the placebo drug are delivered from the study pharmacy, the Capital Regional Pharmacy, already blinded. The IMP is packed in cardboard boxes for each participant with the medicine ID number labeled on each box. Inside the boxes, the two infusions are wrapped in dark plastic to ensure the blinding. The anesthesia staff will unbox and administer the study medicine at the same time as administering the standard-of-care infusions. The IMP will be administered before bowel perforation. The prescription of the IMP and the completed infusion of the IMP will be registered electronically. The dose of fluconazole was chosen during the trial design phase based on pragmatic recommendations on mycotic prophylaxis in abdominal surgery and in consultation with the trial microbiologist.

Treatment discontinuation and study withdrawal

In case a participant experiences intolerable side effects, the IMP will immediately be discontinued and a physician will assess whether unblinding is necessary. In case a participant withdraws consent or an investigator deems participation unsuitable, study participation will be terminated, and the participant will proceed with the standard of care hospital procedures. If this occurs before administration of the IMP, the subject will leave the trial, and to maintain the 1:1 allocation, the randomization number will be reused for the next subject.

If the participant withdraws consent, the withdrawal will be registered in the trial database, and no further data will be collected. The data already collected until the point of withdrawal will be stored and be included in the statistical analysis, according to the statistical analysis plan. All data will be stored for 10 years after the trial ends. The results of the study-specific cultivation and susceptibility analyses will be visible in the patient record.

Adherence

The study is expected to have a high degree of adherence. The IMP is administered as a single-dose infusion, and the samples will be collected by the staff present at the department. There are no extra visits planned. The participants will be invited to fill in a QOL questionnaire before and after the operation. To support adherence, the questionnaires will be sent by e-Boks, a system for electronic mail from the public authorities in Denmark, and can be filled out electronically. Two reminders will be sent for the follow-up questionnaire. Participants who do not use e-Boks will be offered paper alternatives.

Allocation sequence generation

Randomization in varying block sizes will be prepared by the Capital Regional Pharmacy using the website SealedEnvelope (https://www.sealedenvelope.com/). The allocation sequence will be implemented upon start of inclusion. When a participant is included, they will be allocated the next randomization number for the specific site. The randomization follows the central allocation sequence and is not stratified by site. The pharmacy will distribute envelopes together with study medication following the order form. The envelopes will be marked with randomization numbers and are exclusively to be used in case of the need for unblinding. The envelopes will be delivered to the study site together with the corresponding medicine to be stored in a locked cabinet. In the case of an emergency, the participant’s randomization arm can be unblinded. During the entirety of the inclusion period, the Capital Regional Pharmacy will store the blinded sequence allocation, and after final data collection of the last subjects’ follow-up and analysis of the data, the blinding will be lifted.

Concealment of allocation

The allocation sequence for the study medicine is generated and conducted by the Capital Regional Pharmacy. The study medicine is delivered from the Capital Regional Pharmacy to each study site in blinded containers labeled with a unique study identification number from 1 to 420.

All individuals involved in the trial, including physicians responsible for participant inclusion, are blinded to the content of each study medicine container. The blinding remains throughout the study period. The blinding is lifted after completion of the follow-up of the last participant.

The sealed allocation sequence ensures that physicians responsible for participant inclusion are blinded to both current and previous allocations.

An independent statistician will be provided with an anonymized allocation list for impartial assessment of the interim and final data analyses.

Assessment tools

Clavien-Dindo classification

Clavien-Dindo classification is a standardized grading system for postoperative complications in surgical research [13]. The scale is a rating from I to V, with major complications being graded as III–V. Grade I is defined as any deviation from the normal postoperative course but without the need for pharmacological, surgical, endoscopic, and radiological interventions and grade V is defined as the death of a patient [14].

Days alive and out of hospital

DAOH is used as a patient-centered outcome measure. It accounts for postoperative length of stay, readmissions, and deaths within 30 and 90 days. The day of discharge will count as 1 day of admission, regardless of the number of hours spent in the hospital. Same-day readmission and discharge will count as readmission of 1 day.

Quality of life questionnaires

Health-related QOL is collected using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) questionnaires. Participants will fill out the two questionnaires QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BLM30 before the operation and 90 days postoperatively.

Data and management

The signing of the informed consent gives the study researchers direct access to the subjects’ health records in order to retrieve the necessary data. The collected data is listed in the Appendix.

Data will be collected from the patient records until postoperative day 90.

The patient-reported outcomes will be collected from the QOL questionnaire at baseline and 90 days postoperatively.

All data will be entered into an online database created on Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap). The database is a secure online platform designed to manage research data. Only data collectors with an assigned password can enter the database. The local data collectors can only access the participants that are included at the local site. The study coordinator can oversee all collected data but will be blinded to the Danish Personal Identification Numbers of participants included at other sites to comply with the rules of the Danish General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). All consents are stored in a locked facility only accessible by personnel involved in the study.

To ensure high-quality data across all study centers, several measures have been taken. This includes site initiation visits and training of staff. When transferring the complications from the patient record to REDCap, categories of complications are available from a drop-down menu. A description of the interpretation of the Clavien-Dindo grading is also available. This structure is made to ensure consistency in the grading of complications. Any questions or need for clarification is addressed to the coordinating investigator and solved in collaboration with the sponsor. The sponsor will have the final word on any disagreements on outcome assessment. At the semiannual meetings in the Danish Bladder Cancer Group, study progress and challenges will be discussed. Data monitoring will be performed by the Good Clinical Practice units. The available validation systems of the REDCap database will be applied.

The study medicine will be administered by healthcare professionals during the operative procedure. Administration of the medicine is therefore not dependent on patient compliance. After administration, the healthcare professional will register it in the patient record. The primary investigator will transfer the registered administration to REDCap. Any deviations from the study protocol will be visible in the patient record. The primary outcome is measured using a recognized classification system, and the electronic patient record in Denmark supports thorough information on the postoperative period, leading to a high quality of data.

All data will be made available to third parties for control purposes (external monitoring, quality control, etc.) in accordance with Danish law.

Personal data will be stored electronically in REDCap in accordance with the Danish Health Law (Sundhedsloven) and GDPR, hereby including Databeskyttelseforordningen and Databeskyttelseloven.

Privacy will be ensured by the duty of confidentiality for healthcare professionals and by safe storage of data.

During data analysis, patient data will be pseudo-anonymized with a serial code ID number. The code list will be kept electronically by the trial physician on a secured logged drive at a server at Rigshospitalet.

Data will be processed using the newest available version of the statistical analysis software R.

Blinding

The study medicine is delivered in individual boxes, marked with a unique study ID, one for each participant. The hospital pharmacy has blinded the study medicine inside the box. The study medicine is packed in randomized order following the allocation sequence.

Blinding will include participants, care providers, data collectors, outcome assessors, and data analysts.

The allocation sequence will be revealed after the last participant has been followed for at least 90 days and data has been analyzed. The database will be locked before analysis. Data analysis will be performed by the study coordinator and trial statistician and presented to the study team and collaborators. The manuscript(s) will be written by the study coordinator and will be presented to co-authors for amendments.

Statistics

The primary endpoint is an absolute reduction in the rate of major postoperative complications defined as Clavien-Dindo III–V within 90 days postoperative. The risk of major postoperative complications following RC has recently been reported in several Danish studies. Two randomized trials have found a major complication rate of 40% [15] and 31.2% [16]. A retrospective observational study from the initiating study site has reported a major complication rate within 90 days of 37% [17]. In an international systematic review, the rate was reported to be 16.9% [1]. When taking these different results into account, the sample size calculation for this study is based on a pragmatic major complication rate of 30%. When introducing a prophylactic intervention, the demonstrated effect must be clinically meaningful, given potential side effects and costs.

We expect a reduction in major postoperative complications from 30 to 18%, corresponding to an absolute reduction of 12% and a number needed to treat of 8.3 patients to avoid 1 major complication. With an 80% power, alpha spending-adjusted significance level of 2.4%, and a 5% dropout rate, 420 participants need to be included in the study.

The enrollment is expected to be complete within 2–3 years.

An interim analysis will be performed after 210 (50%) participants have been followed for 90 days. The primary analysis will be performed, and results will be presented to an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC). The DMC will have the final decision on whether to terminate the trial based on efficacy, futility, or safety recommendations. The non-binding threshold for early termination of the trial due to efficacy will be a Z-value greater than 2.797. The non-binding threshold for futility will be recommended if the 95% confidence interval of the primary analysis does not reach a major complication rate reduction of 5%. If the DMC judges the trial to be unsafe, the trial can also be terminated early.

The enrollment of participants will continue during the assessment of the interim analysis unless a critical safety concern has been raised.

The analysis will include a summary of age, gender, vital signs, perioperative information, and prior surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. All demographic and baseline characteristics will be stratified by study center. The categorical variables will be presented by frequency and percentages. The continuous variables will be reported by medians and interquartile range.

When 420 patients have been enrolled and completed the intervention, the study will be closed for enrollment. The sponsor will notify the study sites when this occurs. All safety information will be listed by study center and subject number.

The primary outcome will be assessed by the following analyses. The primary analysis will consist of reporting counts with frequencies accompanied by exact 95% confidence intervals. We will analyze differences between arms by Fisher’s exact test, accompanied by odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval. A secondary predefined analysis will include a multivariable logistic regression adjusting for demographic and clinical variables, and a Bayesian beta-binomial model with weakly informative priors (normal (0,1) on log OR). The result of the Bayesian beta-binomial will be summarized as median OR and 95% credible interval.

A descriptive table clarifying both the number of patients with a complication and the number of complications will be provided.

DAOH will be analyzed as a categorical outcome with a cutoff value of >80 days, to be able to compare it to previous research [17].

The time to gastrointestinal function will be analyzed by t-test or, in case it is normally distributed, a non-parametric alternative.

The health-related QOL EORTC QLQ-C30 and the QOL EORTC QLQ-BLM30 questionnaires will be summarized at inclusion and at 90 days postoperative to see changes from the baseline assessment.

Changes from baseline will be categorized as no change, a little change, moderate change, and very much change. A specification of whether the change was better or worse will be added. Differences in the distribution of the categories will be assessed by chi-square. For the change analyses, we will follow the framework laid out by Osoba et al. [18]

Missing data is expected to be limited as the only active participant required from the patients is when they fill out the online questionnaires. All other data is collected from the patient record. When necessary, missing data will be dealt with by imputation. We plan to do a complete-case analysis as missing data is expected to be limited. We will do sensitivity analysis using multiple imputation, using 20 imputations.

Recruitment

To ensure sufficient participant recruitment, the study is conducted in collaboration with the five sites in Denmark performing cystectomy. Together, the five sites perform around 400 cystectomies annually. The participant enrollment is expected to be high due to the simple setup of the study. The recruitment is expected to be within 2–3 years.

The principal investigator at each study site is overall responsible for the local recruitment and eligibility assessment of participants. Daily recruitment can be delegated to a local doctor after conducting relevant training. Screening of potential participants will be monitored by the GCP units.

Monitoring

The trial has been approved by the Danish Medicines Agency (DMA) and the Danish Medical Research Ethics Committees (MREC). Approvals were obtained from the Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS) with the EU trial number: 2023–506226-36-00. The regional Good Clinical Practice Unit (GCP Unit) will monitor the trial. A DMC that is not involved in the trial will evaluate the trial at 50% inclusion.

Safety reporting

All serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported in the electronic trial database using a separate form where a detailed description of the adverse event/reaction is given according to the CTCAE criteria, version 5.0.

The patient group is known to have a high risk of complications and readmissions due to the morbidity of the surgical procedure. Thus, it is expected that they will often experience SAEs, such as hospitalization due to infections or bleeding, as a reflection of the operation and not the IMP.

Ethics and dissemination plan

The trial will be conducted according to the protocol, the International Council for Harmonization (ICH)-GCP guidelines, the DMA guidelines, the Danish MREC, and the Helsinki Declaration.

In case of changes to the protocol, this will be reported to the relevant authorities, and changes concerning safety will be reported to the participants.

Discussion

Postoperative complications remain one of the biggest challenges in patients undergoing cystectomy. Compared to other elective abdominal surgeries, this procedure is still associated with a very high risk of mortality and morbidity. A systematic review has identified a mortality of 4.7% and a complication rate of 59% within 90 days after surgery, where the most common types are of gastrointestinal (29%) and infectious (26%) origin [17]. There is also a high risk of reoperation of 12%, and a retrospective study has identified fascial dehiscence as the most frequent reason for reoperation [1, 17]. By conducting this trial, we address these events by proposing an association between perioperative infection and contamination and postoperative major complications. With the introduction of antimycotic prophylaxis, we aim to interrupt the cascade of events caused by the contamination from the intestinal content that we hypothesize is causing many of the postoperative complications.

Many precautions are already taken to reduce postoperative complications, and only patients declared fit for the procedure undergo this comprehensive surgery. Since the introduction of the ERAS principles and minimally invasive surgery, the focus on perioperative care has gained significant landmarks in terms of perioperative optimization [19]. Remaining fit for adjuvant oncological treatment has also put improvement of surgical recovery high on the agenda. Although evidence-based optimization of cystectomy is increasing, the recommendations on antibiotic prophylaxis are pragmatic. Within the small geographical area of Denmark, the approaches to prophylactic perioperative antibiotics vary with the use of either piperacillin/tazobactam, cefuroxime, or ciprofloxacin, sometimes in combination with metronidazole. There are currently no centers using antimycotic medicine as a part of the routine perioperative prophylaxis.

Nonetheless, the international microbiological guidelines on mycotic prophylaxis in abdominal surgery indicate that this patient group might benefit from an extended mycotic approach, and a study has shown that the mycotic growth from the terminal ileum in general consists of species susceptible to fluconazole [5]. The mapping of the mycotic composition of the urine and the samples of intestinal content is expected to show the same in our study, but even with a negative result from the trial, the results will still contribute with valuable information for making guidelines and recommendations, in terms of avoiding overtreatment of patients. Analysis for yeast in the study-specific samples and the presence of Candida spp. will be used to evaluate the effect of the IMP.

In terms of participant safety, this study is categorized as a low-intervention, phase IV trial. We introduce a new prophylactic use of an already well-known and widely used drug. Based on decades of data on efficacy and safety, fluconazole is considered a well-tolerated medication. The potential side effects are generally limited and last no longer than the systemic clearance of the drug. The plasma half-life of fluconazole is 25–30 h for adults, with 80% being excreted through the kidneys [20, 21]. Since the marketing of the drug more than several decades ago, fluconazole is still one of the most preferred drugs to treat a wide range of mycotic infections in humans, and even with fluconazole being a relatively old antimicrobial, a study has shown that Candidaspp. found in the urine after cystectomy are most often susceptible to fluconazole, and a single-dose treatment duration is not expected to add further to the risk of resistance [22]. Thereby, the study is considered safe on a patient and societal level.

For fluconazole to be considered for introduction into the standard-of-care program, the treatment must show a noticeable and meaningful effect. Only by looking for a great effect can we justify investing economy and risking potential side effects. Therefore, an ambitious goal with a clinically meaningful outcome has been set as the primary analysis.

One of the limitations of this study is that, contrary to the systemic mycotic infections and candidemia that can be diagnosed with a blood sample, it is more difficult to quantify the consequences of intraabdominal mycotic contamination since the surgical site is not accessible for sampling in the postoperative period, and we must therefore rely on the study design. In our belief, the intraoperative samples together with postoperative registration of recovery are the closest we can get to understanding the mechanism of the postoperative complications. Although the intervention may prevent some cases of measurable systemic mycotic infections and candidemia, we expect an accompanied reduction of other types of gastrointestinal and infectious complications [23].

The strength of this study lies greatly in the multi-center setting and the blinding of the allocated treatment for all involved parties. We aim to make the data from this study as generalizable and as patient-centric as possible. In terms of participant inclusion, the multiple sites will make participant inclusion feasible within the timeframe. Registration of the primary outcome is standardized by using the Clavien-Dindo classification system to assess information from the participants’ records. Adherence to the patient-reported outcome, QOL questionnaires, might be limited, despite the actions taken to prevent missing data. The data will be registered in the study REDCap database, which is set up to help classify the complications, but the classification of complications still relies on the local assessor to some extent.

In conclusion, we believe that this multi-center, double-blinded, randomized clinical trial will provide valuable information on the efficacy of the use of antifungal prophylaxis to reduce early complications after cystectomy. These results will be vital for evidence-based guidelines on perioperative prophylaxis in cystectomy.

Trial status

The protocol is version 1.5 as of August 18, 2025. Recruitment began on the 24th of June 2024. The recruitment is expected to be complete in the 2nd quartile of 2026.

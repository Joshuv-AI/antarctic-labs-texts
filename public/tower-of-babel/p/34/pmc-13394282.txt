Chalcogen-driven supramolecular organization and emission modulation of ladder-type heteroacenes for high-performance organic semiconductors
Authors: Yusei Tanaka, Tatsuya Mori, Yu Seok Yang, Yuka Kojiguchi, Subham Ranjan, Takuma Yasuda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heteroacene-based organic semiconductors hold considerable promise for high-performance organic electronics; however, precise control of their solid-state organization remains challenging. Here, we employed a five-ring-fused bis(benzo[4,5]chalcogeno)[3,2-c:2′3′-e]chalcogenophene scaffold featuring triple chalcogen integration as a versatile platform for systematic chalcogen engineering in ladder-type heteroacenes. A series of octyl-terminated derivatives incorporating sulfur, selenium, and tellurium (BBTT-8, BBSeSe-8, and BBTeTe-8, respectively) were investigated to determine how chalcogen identity governs the supramolecular assembly and electronic functionality of these semiconductors. Combined experimental and theoretical analyses revealed that chalcogen variation reorganizes the intermolecular interaction motifs and molecular packing, thereby directing supramolecular organization and modulating the physicochemical properties and charge transport. Concomitantly, the emission characteristics evolved from fluorescence to phosphorescence with heavier chalcogens, which is consistent with enhanced spin–orbit coupling. Notably, single-crystal organic field-effect transistors based on BBTT-8 demonstrated high hole mobilities exceeding 15 cm2 V−1 s−1. These results highlight chalcogen engineering as a powerful strategy for linking molecular design, supramolecular organization, and electronic functionality in organic semiconductors.

Introduction

The incorporation of heteroatoms into π-conjugated frameworks is a powerful strategy for modulating the electronic structure and intermolecular interactions, thereby enabling diverse functionalities spanning charge transport and photophysical responses.1–5 Heteroacenes, fused polycyclic π-conjugated systems with heteroatoms embedded in their backbones, have emerged as a versatile class of organic semiconductors with charge-carrier mobilities surpassing those of amorphous silicon.6–8 In particular, sulfur-containing thienoacenes, especially the alkylated derivatives of [1]benzothieno[3,2-b][1]benzothiophene (BTBT) and dinaphtho[2,3-b:2′,3′-f]thieno[3,2-b]thiophene (DNTT) have been established as benchmark organic semiconductors, building on the foundational contributions of Takimiya et al.,9–12 and enabling hole mobilities exceeding 10 cm2 V−1 s−1 in organic field-effect transistors (OFETs). This success has driven the development of a variety of thienoacene-based p-type semiconductors with enhanced and diversified performance.13–23 Such high mobilities are generally associated with favorable solid-state packing motifs, most notably herringbone arrangements, which enable two-dimensional intermolecular electronic coupling.6–8,24,25 However, these motifs represent only a subset of the accessible structural landscape, and alternative self-organized configurations attainable through molecular design and heteroatom engineering may provide equally viable platforms for efficient charge transport. Despite this recognition, the ability to deliberately harness heteroatom-derived interactions to direct supramolecular organization across this broader structural landscape remains limited, leaving the fundamental structure–property–function relationships insufficiently understood.

Extending beyond sulfur, heavier group 16 elements, such as selenium and tellurium, offer a distinctive avenue for modulating intra- and intermolecular electronic perturbations.7,26 Their larger atomic radii and enhanced polarizability lead to distinct interaction motifs, including directional chalcogen bonding associated with σ-hole characteristics, which can compete with or complement conventional van der Waals, π⋯π, and CH⋯π interactions. Concomitantly, the increased spin–orbit coupling (SOC) inherent to heavier chalcogens enables unique photophysical features, such as efficient intersystem crossing (ISC) and phosphorescence.27–30 Despite these attractive features, systematic investigations across the full chalcogen series (S → Se → Te) within a unified heteroacene framework remain scarce, primarily because of synthetic challenges and the complex reactivity of heavier chalcogen congeners.31–41 In particular, tellurium-containing heteroacenes with semiconducting characteristics remain largely unexplored.31,37,39,41

On the basis of these considerations, heteroacene frameworks that densely incorporate multiple chalcogen atoms provide a compelling platform for promoting chalcogen-derived interactions. In our previous study, solution-processable dithieno[3,2-b:2′,3′-d]thiophene (DTT)-based semiconductors were shown to form dense S⋯S interaction networks in the solid state, thereby enabling hole mobilities exceeding 10 cm2 V−1 s−1 in single-crystal OFETs without relying on π–π stacking.16 This finding highlights an alternative paradigm for charge transport beyond conventional π–π-mediated mechanisms. The related fused bis(benzo[4,5]chalcogeno)[3,2-c:2′,3′-e]chalcogenophene (BBXX) framework has also been explored, with reported mobilities ranging from 0.5 to 18.9 cm2 V−1 s−1 depending on the active-layer morphology.19,42–44 However, the link between the incorporation of multiple chalcogen atoms and the supramolecular organization and electronic structure remains unclear.

Here, we focus on BBXX as a versatile platform for chalcogen engineering of ladder-type heteroacenes. By constructing a series of octyl-terminated derivatives incorporating sulfur, selenium, and tellurium (BBTT-8, BBSeSe-8, and BBTeTe-8; Fig. 1), we established a unified material system to directly elucidate the role of chalcogen identity in shaping the supramolecular organization and governing the electronic and photophysical properties of these materials. This design, which incorporates three chalcogens, enables extended chalcogen-mediated interaction networks, leading to efficient intermolecular electronic coupling and charge transport.

Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization

BBTT-8, BBSeSe-8, and BBTeTe-8 were synthesized from the common precursor 1,4-bis(2-bromo-4-octylphenyl)buta-1,3-diyne using a unified synthetic protocol based on the intramolecular triple-cyclization strategy developed by Yamaguchi et al.45 This method enables straightforward access to π-cores comprising three consecutively fused chalcogenophene units by simply varying the elemental chalcogen sources (S8, Se, or Te powder), offering a concise and modular entry to heteroacenes embedding three chalcogens. Notably, despite containing three Te atoms within the fused π-framework, BBTeTe-8 was readily obtained in 77% yield without the need for isolation of any intermediates. Full synthetic details are provided in the SI. All the target heteroacenes were characterized by 1H and 13C NMR spectroscopy, matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry, and elemental analysis. Their molecular structures were further confirmed by single-crystal X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis (vide infra).

Thermal properties were evaluated using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) (Fig. S1). The 5% weight-loss decomposition temperatures (Td) increased systematically in the order BBTT-8 (346 °C) < BBSeSe-8 (359 °C) < BBTeTe-8 (371 °C), indicating that incorporation of heavier chalcogen atoms did not compromise, but rather slightly enhanced, the thermal robustness of the fused π-cores. Consistently, DSC measurements revealed that the isotropization temperatures (Tiso), corresponding to the transition to the isotropic liquid phase, also increased in the order BBTT-8 (223 °C) < BBSeSe-8 (261 °C) < BBTeTe-8 (274 °C). This trend suggests differences in intermolecular interactions and packing motifs among the three compounds.

Photophysical properties and chalcogen-driven luminescence modulation

The photophysical properties of the BBXX series were examined in dilute toluene solutions (Fig. 2 and Table S1) and thin films (Fig. S2). The lowest-energy absorption maxima (λabs) exhibited a systematic red-shift, from 352 nm (BBTT-8) to 366 nm (BBSeSe-8) and 386 nm (BBTeTe-8), reflecting progressive narrowing of the optical gap (Eg = 3.40, 3.25, and 3.00 eV, respectively) upon the incorporation of heavier chalcogens. A similar bathochromic trend with chalcogen substitution has been observed in related heteroacenes,37,41 and is primarily attributed to progressive destabilization of the HOMO and HOMO−1 energy levels from sulfur to tellurium, resulting in a narrowed HOMO–LUMO gap (Fig. S3).

In contrast to the relatively modest changes in absorption, the photoluminescence (PL) properties demonstrated a more pronounced chalcogen dependence (Fig. 2b and c). Although BBTT-8 displayed only fluorescence (λF = 365, 380 nm) in the near-UV-to-blue region, BBSeSe-8 exhibited distinct dual emission comprising fluorescence (λF = 380, 398 nm) and phosphorescence (λP = 576, 617 nm) in deoxygenated solution at room temperature. In BBTeTe-8, red-to-near-IR phosphorescence (λP = 616 nm) became dominant with a negligible fluorescence component. The absolute PL quantum yields (ΦPL) decreased from 13% for BBTT-8 to 4% for BBSeSe-8 and 3% for BBTeTe-8, reflecting enhanced nonradiative decay and ISC arising from stronger SOC in the heavier congeners, as discussed below. Notably, the longer-wavelength phosphorescence was completely quenched by triplet oxygen in air.

Time-resolved PL measurements (Fig. 2d and e) confirmed the assignment of the lower-energy emission to phosphorescence, with lifetimes of τP = 180 µs for BBSeSe-8 and 35 µs for BBTeTe-8. These phosphorescence lifetimes are approximately 103–104 times longer than the fluorescence lifetime of BBTT-8 (τF = 16 ns). This difference originates from the intrinsically low radiative rates of spin-forbidden phosphorescence in BBSeSe-8 and BBTeTe-8 (kP = 2 × 102 and 9 × 102 s−1, respectively), which are approximately four orders of magnitude lower than the fluorescence radiative rate in BBTT-8 (kF = 8 × 106 s−1). Notably, distinct room-temperature phosphorescence in solution, particularly for BBTeTe-8, is uncommon for heteroacene systems.

To elucidate the origin of this chalcogen-driven emission modulation, the excited-state characteristics were examined using natural transition orbitals (NTOs) and SOC calculations (Fig. 3). The results indicate that the incorporation of heavier chalcogens enhances SOC, thereby promoting ISC and an efficient population of triplet (T1) states, which leads to a qualitative alteration in the emission pathway. In BBTT-8, the excited states are dominated by singlet character owing to negligible SOC (≤0.05 cm−1) for both S1–T2 and S1–T1 couplings, resulting in purely fluorescent emission. In contrast, BBSeSe-8 exhibits significantly enhanced SOC, particularly between S1 and a closely lying T3 state (35.8 cm−1), which accelerates ISC and populates the triplet manifold, giving rise to the observed fluorescence–phosphorescence dual emission behavior. Notably, in BBTeTe-8, the S1 state possesses a distinct excitation character with an n–π* contribution, resulting in a significantly large S1–T1 SOC value (57.9 cm−1) that enables faster ISC and dominant phosphorescence even at room temperature. These observations underscore the pivotal role of heavy-atom effects in governing emission pathways in the BBXX series.

Chalcogen-driven supramolecular organization

To elucidate how chalcogen substitution influences the self-assembly behavior, single-crystal XRD analyses were performed for the BBXX series (Fig. 4a–c and Table S2). All three heteroacenes adopt lamellar packing motifs along the molecular long axis, wherein the π-conjugated cores form periodically layered structures separated by insulating alkyl chains. Within each layer, the π-cores are arranged in a two-dimensional herringbone motif. Unlike conventional herringbone packings governed primarily by weak van der Waals interactions, the present structures are reinforced by an extended network of directional chalcogen-centered intermolecular interactions, as demonstrated by short chalcogen⋯chalcogen (S⋯S, Se⋯Se, and Te⋯Te) and chalcogen⋯sp2-carbon contacts (Fig. S4).

Right: intralayer molecular arrangements and magnified views of three neighboring molecules connected through multiple chalcogen⋯chalcogen (pink) and chalcogen⋯sp2-carbon (light blue) contacts; octyl chains and hydrogen atoms are omitted for clarity. (Bottom panels d–f) Selected natural bond orbitals (NBOs) for two closely interacting molecules of (d) BBTT-8 (β-phase), (e) BBSeSe-8, and (f) BBTeTe-8 extracted from the crystal structures.

BBTT-8 exhibited polymorphism depending on the recrystallization conditions, a behavior also observed for a structurally related analog.19 The α-phase (space group Pbcm, centrosymmetric; Fig. S5) was preferentially obtained from concentrated solutions and formed plate-like crystals, whereas the β-phase (space group Cmc21, non-centrosymmetric; Fig. 4a) was obtained from more dilute solutions, yielding needle-like crystals. Both phases adopt herringbone motifs; however, the α-phase features a more cofacial π-core arrangement. In the β-phase, two sulfur atoms on one side of a BBTT core cooperatively interact with a single sulfur atom of a neighboring molecule, forming a continuous head-to-tail arrangement along the c-axis (i.e., the crystal growth direction). This packing involves an extended network of multiple short S⋯S and S⋯C contacts (3.54 and 3.46 Å, respectively), both shorter than the sums of the corresponding van der Waals radii (3.60 and 3.50 Å),46,47 which is indicative of stabilizing intermolecular interactions. Additional S⋯C contacts (3.39–3.49 Å) along the a-axis further reinforce this arrangement. In contrast, BBSeSe-8 crystallizes exclusively in a single thermodynamically stable phase (space group Pbcm, centrosymmetric; Fig. 4b) characterized by a cofacial herringbone arrangement closely resembling the α-phase of BBTT-8. In this structure, analogous Se⋯Se (3.52 Å) and Se⋯C (3.41 Å) contacts extend along the b-axis.

Unlike the unidirectional head-to-tail arrangements observed for BBTT-8 and BBSeSe-8 along the crystal growth direction, BBTeTe-8 preferentially formed head-to-head dimers (Fig. 4c). These dimers adopt an N-shaped tellurium configuration defined by three Te⋯Te contacts (3.62–3.83 Å), which are significantly shorter than the sum of the van der Waals radii (4.12 Å).46,47 The tail-to-tail arrangement is much less developed, involving only a single, longer Te⋯Te contact (3.94 Å). These distinct packing modes can be explained in terms of open versus closed chalcogen⋯chalcogen interaction motifs: the head-to-tail geometry of BBTT-8 and BBSeSe-8 enables propagation into an extended, open network, whereas the head-to-head geometry of BBTeTe-8 facilitates the formation of a closed dimer stabilized by convergent Te⋯Te interactions. The preference for such dimerization reflects the strong, directional nature of Te⋯Te interactions, together with geometric factors associated with the larger atomic radius of tellurium.

To gain molecular-level insight into the electronic origin of chalcogen-centered interactions, natural bond orbital (NBO) analyses were performed on representative molecular pairs extracted from the crystal structures (Fig. 4d–f). In BBTT-8 (β-phase), the S⋯S interactions are characterized by charge transfer (CT) from the sulfur lone pair into antibonding S–C and C–C orbitals of adjacent molecules (i.e., and ), with relatively small stabilization energies (0.21 and 0.44 kcal mol−1, respectively), indicative of weak but directional chalcogen bonding.48,49 In BBSeSe-8, analogous CT interactions (i.e., and ) exhibit larger stabilization energies (0.40 and 0.58 kcal mol−1, respectively), consistent with stronger intermolecular cohesion. In BBTeTe-8, multiple interactions operate within the head-to-head dimer, each exceeding 1 kcal mol−1, thereby stabilizing the closed dimeric arrangement. Collectively, these results demonstrate that systematic chalcogen variation modulates both the strength and topology of the intermolecular interactions, thereby governing the supramolecular organization. Intriguingly, substitution of only three atoms within the 75-atom molecular framework is sufficient to reshape the interaction landscape, giving rise to distinct supramolecular structures and highlighting the sensitivity of structure–property–function relationships in the solid state.

Single-crystal OFETs: structure–transport correlation

Building on the foregoing structural insights, we next evaluated the charge-transport properties using single-crystal OFETs. Such devices eliminate the extrinsic limitations arising from the grain boundaries and structural disorder inherent to polycrystalline thin films, thereby enabling direct access to the intrinsic transport behavior. Single crystals of the heteroacenes, with uniaxially elongated needle- or ribbon-like morphologies, were grown on octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS)-treated SiO2/Si substrates by drop-casting, followed by deposition of gold electrodes to afford a top-contact, bottom-gate OFET configuration (Fig. 5a; see the SI for details). In these devices, optimal charge-transport properties are generally achieved along the crystal growth direction, where effective intermolecular interactions operate (Fig. S6).

Among the series, BBTT-8-based devices delivered outstanding transistor performance, affording a maximum hole mobility of 15.3 cm2 V−1 s−1 and an on/off current ratio of over 105. Statistical analysis over eight devices afforded an average mobility as high as 8.7 cm2 V−1 s−1, underscoring the reproducibility of the transistor characteristics (Fig. S7 and Table S3). The resulting well-ordered supramolecular organization is conducive to band-like transport with minimal energetic disorder.

In contrast, BBSeSe-8- and BBTeTe-8-based devices exhibited more moderate hole mobilities of up to 0.57 and 1.3 cm2 V−1 s−1, respectively (Fig. 5c and d), corresponding to several ten-fold reductions relative to that of BBTT-8. This charge transport attenuation upon heavier chalcogen substitution suggests a fundamental change in the underlying transport characteristics. Further analysis of intermolecular electronic couplings and their structural origin is presented below. Nevertheless, BBTeTe-8 surpasses the 1 cm2 V−1 s−1 criterion—a benchmark that remains rarely achieved for tellurium-containing small-molecule31,37,39,41 and polymer semiconductors50–54—demonstrating that efficient charge transport can still be retained even with heavy-chalcogen incorporation.

Electronic coupling and charge-transport mechanism

To rationalize the experimentally observed charge-transport properties in single-crystal OFETs, we analyzed the intermolecular electronic couplings between frontier orbitals and evaluated carrier transport within the semiclassical Marcus electron-transfer (hopping) framework.55–57 In the BBXX series, the HOMO and HOMO−1 lie in close energetic proximity (Fig. S3), suggesting a possible multi-orbital contribution to hole transport.58 Accordingly, the transfer integrals (t) for both orbitals, which correspond to the electronic couplings (V) in Marcus formalism, were calculated from the single-crystal structures (Fig. 6). Within this context, charge transport proceeds via thermally activated hopping between neighboring molecules mediated by the spatial overlap of the HOMO and/or HOMO−1.

(Middle) Intermolecular transfer integrals (t) for the HOMO and HOMO−1 between the corresponding dimer pairs, together with the reorganization energies (λ), calculated at the PW91/TZP level. (Bottom) Calculated anisotropic hole mobilities based on the hopping transport model using these transfer integrals.

For BBTT-8, the HOMO-derived transfer integrals are moderately distributed across all relevant dimer pairs (t = 15.8–42.7 meV, Fig. 6a). Notably, the HOMO−1 couplings are significantly larger (≈100 meV) and similarly isotropic, providing additional strongly coupled transport channels. This dual-orbital, multidirectional coupling network enables efficient hole transport. In contrast, BBSeSe-8 exhibits pronounced anisotropy in its HOMO couplings, with a large t value (88.6 meV) confined to the π-stacking direction, indicative of a quasi-one-dimensional electronic structure (Fig. 6b). Meanwhile, the HOMO−1 couplings remain relatively large (>50 meV) across multiple directions, partially mitigating this anisotropy. BBTeTe-8 shows a distinct electronic coupling pattern (Fig. 6c), with moderate HOMO couplings along the π-stack (t = 24.9 meV) and markedly large interactions (t = −93.9 meV) in a close-contact dimer. These variations can be rationalized by the differing spatial distributions of the frontier orbitals: the HOMO is primarily distributed over the π-conjugated core, whereas the HOMO−1 is dominated by the lone-pair (n) orbitals of the three chalcogen atoms.

On the basis of this framework, anisotropic mobilities were estimated from the calculated transfer integrals60 (Fig. 6, bottom). The estimated mobilities were µ = 1.9, 1.7, and 1.8 cm2 V−1 s−1 for BBTT-8, BBSeSe-8, and BBTeTe-8, respectively, along the dominant transport direction. For BBSeSe-8 and BBTeTe-8, the calculated mobilities are in reasonable agreement with the experimental values, consistent with transport predominantly governed by the classical hopping mechanism. In contrast, BBTT-8 exhibited experimental mobilities (>15 cm2 V−1 s−1) that were more than one order of magnitude higher than the calculated values. This pronounced discrepancy suggests that the charge-transport behavior of BBTT-8 cannot be fully described within the classical hopping framework and may involve band-like or transiently delocalized transport contributions.61–64

Solution-processed thin-film OFETs

Although single-crystal devices provide useful insights into intrinsic charge-transport properties, evaluation in thin-film form is crucial for assessing their relevance to solution-processed electronics. Therefore, thin films of BBTT-8 were prepared by dip-coating.22,23 Bare SiO2/Si substrates were immersed in a toluene solution of BBTT-8 (1.5 g L−1) and withdrawn at a constant rate (1 µm s−1), followed by thermal annealing at 100 °C. The resulting films were structurally characterized using out-of-plane and in-plane XRD (Fig. 7a). The XRD patterns revealed a lamellar structure with pronounced out-of-plane (00l) reflections observable up to the tenth order, indicating a well-ordered edge-on orientation of the BBTT-8 molecules relative to the substrate. The in-plane diffraction profile consistently exhibited three distinct peaks assignable to the (110), (200), and (020) reflections, indicative of a herringbone arrangement within the layers. Although the diffraction pattern does not exactly correspond to either the α- or β-phase single-crystal structure, the dip-coated films retain the characteristic out-of-plane lamellar organization and in-plane herringbone packing (Fig. S8), likely reflecting kinetically controlled molecular assembly during solution processing.

To evaluate the in-plane charge-transport properties, four-crossed Au electrodes were deposited onto the films, allowing measurements along directions parallel (//) and perpendicular (⊥) to the dip-coating direction (Fig. 7b–d). The BBTT-8-based thin-film OFETs exhibited high hole mobilities of up to 5.9 cm2 V−1 s−1 with on/off current ratios of 107 in the // direction, while comparable performance was retained in the ⊥ direction (µ = 4.2 cm2 V−1 s−1), indicating weak anisotropy in charge transport within the film plane. This behavior can be attributed to the two-dimensional electronic coupling inherent to the BBTT-8 packing motif, mediated by extended S⋯S interactions.

Conclusions

In this study, we demonstrated that precise chalcogen substitution provides a powerful means to control the intermolecular interactions and supramolecular organization in ladder-type heteroacenes, thereby governing their charge transport properties. Systematic variation of the chalcogen (S → Se → Te) revealed how chalcogen-centered interactions can be harnessed to tune the functional properties. The sulfur-based analog (BBTT-8) formed a cooperatively reinforced, extended S⋯S interaction network in the single crystal, enabling exceptionally efficient charge transport. Single-crystal OFETs based on BBTT-8 exhibited a hole mobility exceeding 15 cm2 V−1 s−1, demonstrating that such cooperative S⋯S interactions are key to achieving highly efficient charge transport. Beyond single crystals, BBTT-8 also afforded high-performance solution-processed thin-film OFETs with hole mobilities up to 5.9 cm2 V−1 s−1. In parallel, the incorporation of heavier chalcogens resulted in distinct photophysical behaviors. The selenium analog (BBSeSe-8) exhibited dual fluorescence–phosphorescence emission in solution at room temperature, whereas the tellurium analog (BBTeTe-8) showed predominantly phosphorescence, reflecting the enhanced SOC associated with the heavy-atom effect. This study established a general design principle linking heteroatomic substitution, supramolecular assembly, and functional properties, thereby providing a versatile platform for high-performance heteroacene-based organic semiconductors.

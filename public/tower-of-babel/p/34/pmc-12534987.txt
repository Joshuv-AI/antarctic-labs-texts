Role of vitamin D supplementation in prevention of new onset atrial fibrillation in post-coronary artery bypass graft patients in vitamin D deficient and insufficient populations: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Nidhi Lanka, Oluwasolabomi Onadipe, Servet Yuce, Bishoy Fahim, Mahek Fatima, Hriday Shah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) is a common complication following coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), contributing to increased morbidity, prolonged hospitalization, and elevated healthcare costs. Emerging evidence suggests that vitamin D (Vit. D), via its anti-inflammatory effects and modulation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), may have a cardioprotective role.ObjectiveThis systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the efficacy of preoperative Vit. D supplementation in reducing the incidence of POAF among CABG patients, with a focus on populations with Vit. D deficiency and insufficiency.MethodsOur study was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 Guidelines. The electronic databases PubMed, Cochrane Library and Google Scholar were searched from 2014 to 2024. Randomized controlled trials and retrospective observational studies involving adult subjects, evaluating Vit. D deficient and/or insufficient patients who have undergone CABG and received Vit. D supplementation in addition to standard care, compared to patients receiving standard care alone or with placebo, and reporting incidence of new-onset POAF were included. Statistical analysis was performed using RevMan v5.4 software, utilizing a random effects model with heterogeneity assessed using the I2 statistic.ResultsA total of four randomized controlled trials were included in our study, evaluating a sample size of 694 (Vitamin D group- 342, Control group- 352; mean age- 61.8 years, 42.6% females). Our meta-analysis revealed that preoperative Vit. D supplementation significantly reduced the incidence of POAF in post-CABG patients with sub-optimal Vit. D levels (RR = 0.55, 95% CI: 0.40–0.76, p = 0.0003, I2 = 1%). However, no significant effect was noted in the Vit. D insufficient population. Supplementation did not significantly affect length of hospital stay (MD = -0.62, 95% CI: -0.74–0.50, p = 0.28) or duration of intubation (MD = 0.00, 95% CI: -0.26–0.26, p = 0.99).ConclusionPreoperative Vit. D supplementation appears beneficial in reducing POAF risk in deficient CABG patients, but not in insufficient populations. These findings support targeted supplementation as a potential adjunct in perioperative care, though further large-scale studies are warranted to define optimal dosing strategies and broader applicability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05223-4.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common type of cardiac arrhythmia [1], and postoperative AF (POAF) is one of the most frequent complications following cardio-pulmonary bypass graft surgery (CABG), affecting 20% to 40% of patients [2]. This condition is associated with increased morbidity, longer hospital stays and higher healthcare costs [3]. Despite significant advancements in perioperative care, effective strategies for preventing new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) have been sparse, and therefore, modifiable risk factors require further research [4].

Vitamin D (Vit. D), traditionally known for its vital role in maintaining bone mineral metabolism, has recently gained attention as a pleiotropic hormone with regulatory effects on cardiovascular health [5]. Both experimental and clinical studies have suggested that, Vit. D deficiency may contribute to myocardial remodeling [6], systemic inflammation [7] and autonomic imbalance, all key mechanisms involved in the development of POAF [8]. These effects appear to be aggravated in the context of Vit. D deficiency. On the other hand, supplementation of Vit. D in patients with low levels has been reported to alleviate these mechanisms, potentially lowering the risk of developing POAF and/or NOAF [8–12].

Beyond these specific effects, the existing research regarding Vit. D supplementation to mitigate arrhythmic complications is inapposite. These variations can largely be attributed to the designs of studies, characteristics of the populations studied, and protocols used in supplementation [8, 10, 11]. For instance, some studies focus on specific subgroups of patients with differing baseline Vit. D levels, while others include heterogeneous populations, leading to inconsistent outcomes. Similarly, the dosing regimens, duration of supplementation and methods used to assess Vit. D status vary widely across studies, further complicating the ability to draw definitive conclusions. Moreover, the lack of substantial and robust comprehensive analyses synthesizing evidence into more meaningful perspectives has also represented an important gap for understanding the potential cardioprotective value of Vit. D in this clinical context. Addressing these limitations could pave the way for more targeted and evidence-based recommendations regarding Vit. D supplementation in cardiovascular care.

This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to evaluate the impact of Vit. D supplementation on the incidence of NOAF in CABG patients with consideration to both Vit. D deficient and insufficient populations. We will also evaluate its effect on the length of hospital stay and overall postoperative morbidity and mortality. By addressing key gaps in the literature, this study seeks to provide clinically relevant insights into whether targeted Vit. D interventions can mitigate the burden of POAF and improve postoperative outcomes.

Methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 Guidelines [13]. Our review protocol is registered with PROSPERO (Registration ID: CRD42025631262).

Search strategy

The databases used for the literature search were PubMed, Google Scholar and Cochrane Library, with the last search being performed on 12/20/2024 at 12:17:24 IST. Relevant keywords combined with Boolean operators were used, with advanced search options employed where appropriate. The keywords used were- (“atrial fibrillation” OR “AFib” OR “new onset atrial fibrillation” OR “NOAF” OR “postoperative atrial fibrillation” OR “POAF” OR “postoperative complications”) AND (“coronary artery bypass grafting” OR “coronary artery bypass graft surgery” OR “CABG” OR “cardiac surgery”) AND (“vitamin D” OR “vitamin D supplementation” OR “cholecalciferol” OR “ergocalciferol”). Studies identified from each database were exported to Rayyan [14]. The detailed search strategies with the filters used and the total number of results for each database can be viewed in Table below. (Table S1)

Data screening

The studies retrieved were screened independently by two reviewers (Yuce S, Fatima M) with conflicts being resolved by a third reviewer (Lanka N), as per pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Randomized controlled clinical trials (RCTs) and retrospective observational studies evaluating adult Vit. D deficient and/or insufficient patients who have undergone CABG surgery and received Vit. D supplementation in addition to standard care compared to patients receiving standard care only +/- placebo and reporting about incidence of NOAF; English language studies published in the last 10 years (to ensure relevance, validity and wider applicability) were included. On the other hand, studies evaluating patients < 18 years of age, patients undergoing combined CABG and cardiac surgeries other than CABG, patients with pre-existing AFib, studies reporting arrhythmias other than AFib; case reports, case series, review articles; studies in languages other than English and animal studies were excluded.

The studies identified after the initial search were first screened to delete duplicates. Next, a manual screening of the titles/abstracts was done and studies found to be ineligible were excluded. Subsequently, manual screening of the full texts was undertaken and the final studies selected were considered for quality assessment. Full texts were retrieved for all selected studies undergoing risk of bias assessment.

Risk of bias assessment

Two authors (Lanka N, Yuce S) independently performed the quality appraisal with conflicts being resolved by a third author (Onadipe O). Appropriate quality assessment tools were used for each study type to evaluate the risk of bias and quality of individual studies. The Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool [15] was used for Randomized Clinical Trials, which evaluates each study across five domains - randomization process, deviation from intended intervention, missing outcome data, measurement of outcome and selection of the reported data. Studies assessed to have ‘low risk’ or ‘some concerns’ were considered for final review. Those having high risk or unclear risk of bias were removed.

Two reviewers (Yuce S, Fahim B) extracted relevant data based on the following parameters from the eligible studies in Microsoft Excel Sheets [16]:

Baseline characteristics: Study name, author, year of publication, country, study design, sample size, age (median age, interquartile range), gender (% female), baseline Vit. D levels (deficient/insufficient), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left atrial diameter (LAD), platelet count, C-reactive protein (CRP), cross clamp time, cardiopulmonary bypass time, Vit. D supplementation (dose, route, timing), control- standard of care only/placebo and follow-up time.

Primary outcomes: Incidence of new onset AFib (NOAF)/postoperative AFib (POAF) including definition of NOAF/POAF used in each study.Secondary outcomes: Incidence of major adverse cardiovascular outcomes (MACE), incidence of cardiac arrest, incidence of cardiac tamponade, incidence of blood transfusion, length of intubation, length of ICU-stay, length of hospitalization and 30-day mortality.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using RevMan (Review Manager (RevMan) [Computer program Version 5.4., The Cochrane Collaboration, 2020]). A random-effects model was applied to account for potential heterogeneity across studies. Forest plots were generated using the same software to visually represent the pooled effect sizes and confidence intervals. Risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used to analyze dichotomous outcomes, while the weighted mean difference (MD) was calculated for continuous variables. Statistical significance was determined at a p-value of less than 0.05 (p < 0.05). Heterogeneity among the included studies was assessed using the Higgins I² statistic, with values interpreted as follows: low heterogeneity (I² = 25–49%), moderate heterogeneity (I² = 50–74%) and high heterogeneity (I² ≥ 75%). For studies with I²>50%, sensitivity analysis was performed by systematically excluding one study at a time (leave-one-out method) to identify the source of heterogeneity. Additionally, subgroup analyses were conducted based on predefined factors such as dose of Vit. D supplemented. All results are reported with corresponding statistical values and visually summarized using forest plots.

Results

Study selection

The initial literature search yielded a total of 89 studies relevant to our review (71 from PubMed, 6 from Google Scholar and 12 from Cochrane Library). Of these, published results were not available for 2 studies and these were excluded, with 87 articles considered for screening. 13 studies were found to be duplicates and were removed and the remaining 74 studies underwent a thorough manual screening as per our pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. A total of 4 studies that met our eligibility criteria were included in our review. Full texts for all 4 studies were sought and retrieved. (Figure 1)Fig. 1PRISMA flow chart

Risk of bias

All 4 studies finalized after data screening were evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool [15]. Two of these studies, namely the ones by Kara H, et al. [17] and Alirezaei T et al. Study characteristics

Baseline characteristics

A total of four studies were included, all of which were RCTs. These studies were conducted between 2018 and 2023 at multiple locations, with two studies conducted in Iran, one in Turkey and one in Cyprus. A total population of 694 was studied with a mean age of 61.8 years and a mean gender ratio of 42.65% females. All patients included in the studies underwent on pump CABG surgery in a non-emergent setting. The selected participants were identified as either Vit. D deficient or Vit. D insufficient, with diagnostic cutoff values varying across the studies, specified in the table below. The mean Vit. D level in patients classified as Vit. D deficient was 12.11 ng/ml, while those classified as Vit. D insufficient had a mean level of 25.54 ng/ml. The average LVEF across all studies was 53.28%, the mean LAD was 40.13%, and the mean CRP level was 0.72 mg/l (excluding one study where the measurement was taken postoperatively). Oral Vit. D was administered in a range of doses 2–5 days preceding the surgery, with outcomes compared against those observed in individuals receiving either standard care alone or placebo. All patients received preoperative medications including beta-blockers, statins, ACE-inhibitors or ARBs with or without anti-platelets and diuretics. Intraoperative parameters were recorded, with the average cross-clamp time being 58.8 min (with mean cross clamp time unspecified in one study) and the mean cardiopulmonary bypass duration as 96 min (with this duration unspecified in two studies). The follow-up period varied across studies. Medical history showing the percentage of patients with hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, smoking history and COPD are mentioned where applicable. Study outcomes

*for deficiency plus insufficiency groups

Cerit L et al. [11] reported that the incidence of POAF was significantly different between the treatment and control groups in patients with Vit. D deficiency (18% vs. 29%, p = 0.02). However, both treatment and control groups had similar incidence of POAF in patients with Vit. D insufficiency (31% vs. 33%, p = 0.538), indicating no significant difference in this group. The average length of hospital stay was not significantly different between the two groups. (8.1 ± 2.3 days vs. 7.9 ± 2.1 days, p = 0.891).

Talasaz AH et al. [18] demonstrated that the total incidence of POAF was 15.29%, with significantly lower rates in the treatment group (9 patients [9.68%]) compared to the control group (21 patients [20.39%], p = 0.038). The study also reported that, Vit. D supplementation resulted in significantly shorter ICU stays (2.21 days vs. 3.86 days, p = 0.001) and hospital stays (7.40 days vs. 9.58 days, p = 0.022). Additionally, the rate of postoperative complications including MACE, cardiac arrest, cardiac tamponade and the need for blood transfusions were reported in this study, however these were not found to be significantly different between the two groups.

In the study by Kara H et al. [17], the total incidence of POAF was 19.82%. The treatment group had a significantly lower incidence of POAF than the control group, with 12.07% of patients in the treatment group experiencing POAF, compared to 27.59% of patients in the control group (p = 0.034). There was no significant difference between the treatment and control groups in terms of the length of intubation (9.41 ± 4.35 vs. 9.36 ± 3.99 h, p = 0.947) or the length of hospitalization (7.14 ± 1.97 vs. 7.81 ± 2.25 days, p = 0.091).

In the study by Alirezaei T et al. [8] the total incidence of POAF was 46 (18.7%), with a significantly lower rate seen in the intervention group compared to the control group (14 patients [11.4%] vs. 32 patients [26%], p = 0.003). There was no significant difference between the treatment and control groups in terms of the length of intubation (5.09 +/- 1.11 vs. 5.1 +/- 1.01 h, p = 0.886) or the length of hospitalization (19.70 ± 7.43 vs. 19.27 ± 6.64 days, p = 0.976).

There were no studies that reported data on 30-day mortality. (Tables 2 and Table S2)

Results of meta-analysis

Incidence of POAF

Our pooled analysis of all four studies revealed that Vit. D supplementation led to a significantly lower incidence of POAF compared to the control [RR = 0.55, 95% CI: 0.40–0.76, p = 0.0003] with a low level of heterogeneity [I2 = 1%] across studies. This result is demonstrated in the forest plot below (Figure 2). It is important to note that this analysis only considered Vit. D deficient populations as incidence of POAF in Vit. D insufficient groups was only reported by one study, Cerit et al. 2018. [11] While Kara et al. 2019 [17] reported baseline Vit. D levels in both Vit. Discussion

Our meta-analysis included four RCTs, evaluating a total of 694 patients, which is the most extensive study to date on the relationship between Vit. D supplementation and the incidence of POAF, to the best of our knowledge. Ours is also the only meta-analysis reporting on the effect of Vit. D supplementation on the duration of intubation. We found that Vit. D supplementation before CABG surgery significantly reduced the incidence of POAF in patients who were Vit. D deficient. However, it was noted that the studies assessed reported inconsistent findings in Vit. D insufficient populations. We also assessed secondary outcomes, including duration of intubation and length of hospital stay, which were not found to be significantly impacted by Vit. D supplementation. This is in line with most studies, with the exception of Talasaz et al. [18], who reported a statistically significant reduction in length of hospital stay in those who received Vit. D supplementation.

POAF is the predominant arrhythmia after CABG surgery, most commonly occurring between the second and fourth postoperative days. It is associated with significant complications, such as stroke, length of hospital stay, cost of health care as well as hemodynamic instability [19]. Quality of life is undermined by these adverse outcomes, making the prevention of POAF a key objective in improving recovery and reducing complications after cardiac surgery.

The pathophysiology of POAF is not yet fully understood but is largely driven by the inflammatory response. One of the major causes of this inflammation is surgical trauma, particularly evident after CABG surgery. This process is known to cause Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS), which increases the levels of inflammatory markers. CABG also leads to increased oxidative stress and production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), all of which render the atrium pro-arrhythmic 19. Furthermore, coronary artery disease (CAD) is a well-established contributor to atrial fibrillation (AF), as chronic myocardial ischemia promotes atrial fibrosis, inflammation, and remodeling of the left atrium, all of which provide a substrate for arrhythmogenesis. Additionally, systemic inflammation and neurohormonal activation seen in CAD may amplify atrial electrical instability, increasing the risk of POAF [19, 20]. Additional causes include elevated levels of hs-CRP and serum CRP, which have been linked to atrial remodeling and oxidative stress, low levels of serum magnesium and low serum Vit. D levels [21, 22]. Furthermore, the size of the left atrium also plays a significant role in the pathophysiology of POAF, with each millimeter increase in left atrial diameter correlating to a proportional rise in the likelihood of developing POAF [23]. These mechanisms collectively contribute to the risk of POAF in affected patients.

Low Vit. D levels have been implicated in the mechanisms of POAF in multiple ways. Chen et al.. reported that serum 25-hydroxy vitamin D levels were correlated with LAD, hs-CRP and pulmonary systolic pressure and significant associations were found between Vit. D deficiency and AF in Chinese patients with nonvalvular persistent AF [24]. Hanafy et al. demonstrated that deficiency of Vit. D is closely associated with cardiovascular disease including heart failure, hypertension and myocardial infarction [25]. Additionally, a meta-analysis concluded that both hs-CRP and conventional serum CRP levels before open-heart surgery were associated with POAF [26], with low Vit. D levels linked with higher CRP levels, which promote inflammation, atrial remodeling and POAF [27].

It is also important to note that, Vit. D plays an important role in the cardiovascular system, mainly through the expression of nuclear steroid Vit. D receptors (VDR) in cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells. These receptors play a role in calcium metabolism and provide protection against remodeling and hypertrophy of the myocytes [26, 28]. Thus, Vit. D supplementation has a role in increasing electrical stability and offsetting the chance of AF to develop [29]. It is also considered a negative regulator of the renin angiotensin aldosterone system (RAAS) and it inhibits renin expression, thereby preventing the hyperactivation of RAAS. Hyperactivation of RAAS has been linked to hypertension, fibrosis and other cardiovascular complications, all of which also predispose to AF [30, 31]. Vit. D also aids in calcium homeostasis, and its deficiency can lead to intracellular calcium dysregulation and arrhythmogenic activity [24].

Subsequently, we see that the prevention of POAF by Vit. D supplementation is largely attributed to its anti-inflammatory effect. It has been found to suppress several pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL- 6, IL-12, IL-1β, IFN-γ, TGF- β, protein kinase C (PKC) and mitogen activated protein kinases 1 (MAPK1) [32–35]. This effect may help minimize the cosmic inflammation associated with CABG. However, there are still major knowledge deficits concerning the role of Vit. D in prevention of POAF.

Existing literature on the connection between low 25(OH) vitamin D levels and AF has been conflicting. Our findings align with some previous studies emphasizing the link between Vit. D deficiency and cardiovascular risks. Zitterman et al.. highlighted the prevalence of deficient 25(OH)D levels in cardiac surgery patients and their association with MACE risk [32]. Wang et al.. found a two-fold higher risk of cardiovascular disease at 25(OH)D levels of 20 nmol/L compared to 60–75 nmol/L [36]. Hanafy et al.. demonstrated that 1,25(OH)2D has direct electromechanical effects on the atrium, potentially preventing or terminating AF [25]. Chen et al. revealed lower 25(OH)D levels in persistent AF patients and linked elevated PTH levels, caused by Vit. D deficiency to atrial enlargement and remodeling [24]. Liu et al.. identified Vit. D deficiency as a moderate predictor of POAF after CABG and AF in the general population [37].

In contrast, several prior studies failed to establish a conclusive correlation between low Vit. D levels and the occurrence of AF or POAF, often due to methodological limitations. Rienstra et al. found no significant increase in AF incidence with Vit. D deficiency [38]. The Rotterdam study, a 12-year prospective cohort study, reported no link between Vit. D deficiency and AF risk in the elderly [39]. However, it did not account for surgical procedures or POAF specifically and its findings were based on univariate analysis, leaving room for confounding factors. Albert et al.. demonstrated no preventive effect of Vit. D supplementation on POAF over five years, but this study was conducted in the general population with a small subgroup of Vit. D deficient patients and did not account for surgical procedures and underlying vascular disease [40].

The findings of our study were in line with those of previous meta-analyses done on this topic [12, 41].

Our study has some limitations which must be acknowledged. First, it consisted of just four RCTs involving 694 patients, which makes it the largest meta- analysis on this topic, but the sample remains relatively small for making broad conclusions. There is also a limit on the generalizability of our findings as these trials were focused predominantly on Middle Eastern and Mediterranean populations. Furthermore, there was wide heterogeneity between studies in terms of the definition and duration of POAF, dose and timing of Vit. D supplementation (with Talasaz et al. [18] studying the highest dose administered) and Vit. D cut-offs for defining deficiency and insufficiency. Most studies also did not distinguish between Vit. D deficiency and insufficiency and all incidences of POAF were in the deficient group unless otherwise specified. Only two categorized patients into deficient and insufficient groups on the basis of baseline Vit. D levels, with just one reporting POAF incidence in insufficient patients. Cerit et al. [11] reported no preventive effect of Vit. D on POAF in insufficient populations but comparisons were limited, highlighting the importance of additional studies specifically among Vit. D insufficient patients. Finally, publication bias and diversity of methodologies used in trials are additional factors that may have affected the results.

Thus, we envision the need for further studies on Vit. D deficiency in relation with POAF, especially in the Vit. D insufficient and even the sufficient/general populations, to draw definitive conclusions. Although supplementation may be considered as an adjunct in carefully selected vitamin D deficient patients, definitive clinical recommendations should await evidence from larger, multi-center trials that establish both its efficacy and optimal dosing regimen. Until then, clinicians should interpret these findings as hypothesis-generating and apply them cautiously in individual patient contexts. More RCTs are needed following these studies to evaluate how Vit. D supplementation can be harnessed to develop effective perioperative strategies for the prevention of POAF and reduction of associated morbidity and mortality.

Conclusion

CABG is a frequently performed surgical procedure for patients with severe coronary artery disease. This procedure carries the risk of significant postoperative complications, including AF, which increases the likelihood of both mortality and morbidity. Furthermore, a low level of Vit. D has been shown to increase that risk. Our study explored whether the preventative administration of Vit. D preoperatively would be beneficial for patients at risk of developing POAF following cardiac surgery. This meta-analysis demonstrated the potential benefits of Vit. D supplementation in significantly reducing POAF incidence as compared to control, especially in the Vit. D deficient patients (RR = 0.55, 95% CI: 0.40–0.76, p = 0.0003, I² = 1%). However, our analysis was not able to establish conclusive results in the Vit. D insufficient populations and did not show a statistically significant impact on the length of hospital stay and duration of intubation or significant differences in incidence based on dosage of Vit. D administered. It is important to note that small sample size, heterogeneity of studies and lack of generalizability are limitations of this study. We emphasize on the need for large, well-defined studies, especially in Vit. D insufficient and even sufficient patients to comprehensively explore the role of Vit. D in the prevention of POAF.

A blockchain-enabled IoT framework for smart electro-medical waste management
Authors: K. Suresh Kumar, T. Ananth Kumar, Christo Ananth, Mathiyazhagan Narayanan, Hari Mohan Rai, Saurabh Agarwal, Wooguil Pak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Electro-medical waste (EMW) management presents critical challenges related to traceability, regulatory compliance, and operational safety in healthcare environments, where improper handling can pose serious risks to public health and the environment. To address these challenges, this work proposes a blockchain-integrated Internet of Things framework, termed BIOT-EMW, which combines IoT sensing, blockchain-based auditability, and edge-level intelligence to enable secure and transparent EMW lifecycle management. A convolutional neural network-based computer vision module is deployed at the edge to automate EMW classification and reduce manual intervention. The performance evaluation of the proposed BIOT-EMW system emphasizes system-level operational metrics, including latency, delay, bandwidth, power consumption, resource utilization, and scalability, rather than exhaustive model-level benchmarking. Experimental results show that edge-level processing latency ranges from 3.45 to 9.33 ms, end-to-edge communication delay from 6.43 to 12.11 ms, bandwidth usage from 24.54 Mbps to 34.87 Mbps, and device-level power consumption from 6.5 to 13.31 mW, with resource utilization between 78 and 92% and scalability reaching up to 97%. In a prototype-scale experimental testbed, the BIOT-EMW framework shows feasible and efficient operation with promising scalability for automated electro-medical waste management in smart healthcare facilities.

Introduction

The rapid growth of electro-medical waste (EMW) poses important risks to public health and the environment, mainly in healthcare facilities where improper handling can lead to contamination, regulatory violations, and safety hazards. Traditional waste management systems often lack end-to-end traceability, real-time monitoring, and automated compliance mechanisms, making them inadequate for managing the complex lifecycle of EMW. With the deployment of connected medical devices and digital healthcare infrastructure, there is an upward need for intelligent, secure, and scalable waste management solutions personalized specifically to electro-medical waste streams.

Recent advances in the Internet of Things (IoT) have enabled real-time sensing and monitoring across healthcare environments. IoT devices equipped with sensors and cameras can capture critical information related to waste generation, location, and environmental conditions, enabling data-driven waste handling and operational optimization1. However, IoT-based systems deployed in healthcare settings face persistent challenges related to security, privacy, and data integrity, particularly when sensitive operational data must be shared among multiple stakeholders2,3. Existing detection and response frameworks often rely on centralized architectures that are vulnerable to cyberattacks and data tampering, limiting their suitability for regulated medical environments4.

Blockchain technology has emerged as a promising solution for enhancing transparency, trust, and auditability in distributed systems4. By maintaining an immutable, decentralized ledger, blockchain enables the secure recording of transactions without relying on a centralized authority4. Each transaction is cryptographically linked to previous records, ensuring data integrity and traceability across the system lifecycle5. In healthcare-related applications, blockchain has been increasingly explored to support secure data sharing, compliance verification, and tamper-resistant logging of critical events3,6. Nevertheless, directly integrating blockchain with IoT systems introduces challenges, particularly due to the computational and energy constraints of IoT devices and the overhead associated with consensus mechanisms7,8.

To address these limitations, recent studies have investigated blockchain architectures tailored to resource-constrained IoT environments and secure integration mechanisms5,7. In parallel, smart contracts have been proposed to automate rule enforcement and time-sensitive operations, enabling event-driven execution without manual intervention9. When combined with IoT sensing, such mechanisms can automate compliance tracking and operational workflows in healthcare waste management. However, many existing approaches focus on security or traceability in isolation and do not adequately address the end-to-end requirements of electro-medical waste handling.

In addition to secure data management, accurate identification and classification of EMW items remain critical challenges. Manual sorting is error-prone and exposes personnel to health risks. Computer vision and deep learning techniques, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have demonstrated strong performance in visual recognition tasks and are increasingly applied in industrial and healthcare automation10,11. Deploying CNN-based classification at the edge can reduce latency and communication overhead, making it suitable for real-time waste segregation in healthcare facilities12. Nonetheless, most prior works emphasize model accuracy rather than system-level performance, and few consider the combined constraints of latency, power consumption, scalability, and regulatory compliance inherent to EMW management.

Motivated by these gaps, this work proposes a Blockchain-Integrated IoT framework for Electro-Medical Waste management (BIOT-EMW) specifically designed for healthcare environments. The proposed framework integrates IoT sensing, edge-based 3D CNN classification, and blockchain-based auditability to enable secure, automated, and energy-efficient EMW lifecycle management. Rather than recording raw sensor or image data on the blockchain, the system selectively logs lightweight, compliance-critical metadata, reducing communication overhead and power consumption while preserving traceability.

Recent developments in IoT technologies have enabled significant automation and real-time monitoring in the smart city and healthcare fields. Power consumption and battery limits remain major challenges, particularly for large-scale and long-term IoT deployments. Most IoT devices rely on limited-capacity batteries that are difficult to replace or recharge. Communication, continuous sensing, and data transmission consume a large share of energy, reducing device lifetime and increasing maintenance costs. To address this, low-power communication protocols such as LoRaWAN, Zigbee, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and NB-IoT are widely used, as they employ duty cycling and adaptive transmission to balance energy efficiency and latency8. However, in applications such as medical waste monitoring, where timely response and reliability are critical, aggressive duty cycling can reduce system performance despite saving energy.

To address battery limitations, energy-harvesting techniques offer a promising solution for powering IoT devices. These methods convert ambient energy, such as solar, thermal, RF, or mechanical sources, into electrical power. Solar energy is commonly used in outdoor smart city settings, while RF and thermal harvesting are more suitable for indoor environments such as hospitals3,8. Recent studies show that hybrid energy-harvesting models that integrate multiple ambient energy sources can significantly improve energy availability and robustness in energy-harvesting systems. The time dimensions of the energy harvesting are another important factor in the literature. Harvested energy tends to be intermittent and dependent on environmental conditions, and therefore, it is time-variant. Consequently, time-aware energy management schemes, such as adaptive duty cycling, energy prediction, and scheduling, which match sensing, computing, and communication tasks with harvested energy, have been considered by researchers5. These approaches help extend device lifespans and maintain QoS. In the case of IoT systems with blockchain, energy consumption is even more important due to the additional computational and communication overhead introduced by cryptographic functions and consensus algorithms. Several works propose solutions to the energy overhead challenge of IoT environments in terms of lightweight blockchain models, off-chain storage, and energy-efficient consensus algorithms (e.g., PoS or delegated consensus models)6. These insights directly motivate the design of the proposed BIOT-EMW system, which focuses on low power consumption, efficient resource utilization, and scalability for the clinical monitoring system.

In addition to energy harvesting, energy-efficient data transmission mechanisms are a critical part to increase the operational lifetime of IoT devices. Although it is not possible to achieve fully power-free communication, new research has shown near-passive and ultra-low-power transmission methods that can drastically reduce energy use. One such approach is backscatter communication, in which IoT nodes modulate and reflect existing RF signals rather than generating new ones, thereby consuming only microwatts of power20. Backscatter-based systems are suitable for short-range sensing applications in healthcare and smart environments. Low-Power Wide-Area Network (LPWAN) technologies, such as LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, and Sigfox, are also used extensively in power-constrained IoT applications. These protocols are used for long-range communication with very low transmission power, employing narrowband modulation, adaptive data rates, and long sleep cycles.

Although recent articles have covered how blockchain can be used for data security, IoT for real-time sensing, and deep learning models for image-based classification, the gap in their combined use for managing electro-medical waste in smart cities is significant. The existing literature focuses either on secure data processing or automated classification, but rarely on both in a scalable, real-time, and unified system. Moreover, not many methods offer end-to-end tracing, automatic scheduling, and AI-enhanced waste classification for EMW, a problem gaining momentum due to its criticality in the urban healthcare setting. The limitations are overcome in this study, which proposes an all-encompassing BIOT-EMW approach integrating blockchain, IoT, and 3D CNNs to ensure secure, intelligent, and sustainable waste management.

Integration of blockchain technology

Blockchain technology is integrated into the BIOT-EMW system to ensure data security, transparency, and traceability throughout the waste management lifecycle. The waste generation, collection, and disposal stages are recorded as transaction proof for the implementation of the blockchain. This enables real-time monitoring, auditing, and accountability, reducing the risk of improper disposal and environmental harm. IoT device-based networks are strategically deployed within the healthcare facilities, electronic manufacturing units, and waste collection points. These devices might collect real-time data on waste generation, composition, and disposal. The IoT network might ensure accurate, up-to-date information for decision-making by enabling efficient resource allocation and waste management. The BIOT-EMW system employs predictive analytics to forecast the volume and types of EMW generated within specific timeframes.

This proactive approach optimizes waste collection plans by allocating resources efficiently, thereby mitigating the environmental impact of waste. Data-driven insights enable improved decision-making in the waste management process. The heart of the system lies in its advanced computer vision capabilities. Using 3D computer vision and CNNs, the system accurately identifies, classifies, and segregates various types of electronic and medical waste. This automation speeds up waste sorting by reducing human error and ensuring proper disposal or recycling. The BIOT-EMW system includes a module to track the sustainability and operation of IoT devices, as many healthcare facilities and urban healthcare ecosystems feature them. This ensures the proper functioning of the waste management equipment, thereby improving the system’s overall efficiency. The BIOT-EMW system provides a user-friendly interface for waste management professionals, urban planners, and other stakeholders. The system is designed to meet the growing waste management needs of healthcare facilities and urban healthcare ecosystems. It prioritizes interoperability by allowing seamless interaction with existing waste management infrastructure and systems.

The proposed EMW system operates as a lightweight, event-driven workflow tailored to energy-constrained edge environments. Upon detection of an electromagnetic waveform anomaly, the edge node performs local preprocessing and inference using the proposed 3D-CNN model under strict power and latency constraints. Only compact metadata describing the detected event (e.g., timestamp, classification label, confidence score, and device identifier) is generated and prepared for transmission. This metadata is then forwarded to the blockchain layer, where smart contracts immutably record the event and enforce access control policies, while raw EMW signal data remains off-chain to minimize communication and storage overhead. The overall design emphasizes energy efficiency, low latency, security, and scalability, which are critical requirements for distributed EMW monitoring and management systems.

Novelty and key contributions of the BIOT-EMW framework

IoT, blockchain, and deep learning were discussed individually in the context of smart healthcare and waste-management applications; their integration for managing EMW has received limited attention in integrated system-level studies. EMW is distinguished by its unique characteristics, such as a mixed electronic-biological structure, strict adherence to regulations, real-time tracing requirements, and energy-limited sensing conditions, unlike traditional municipal or medical waste. The suggested BIOT-EMW framework presents several unique and practice-oriented innovations, the key ones being as follows. To begin with, the offered system introduces a hybrid edge-blockchain architecture that is optimally suited to EMW cases, where intensive, continuous, high-volume sensing data are processed in situ, while event-driven, lightweight metadata are recorded on the blockchain. Contrary to the standard blockchain-IoT design, where all sensor data is logged, BIOT-EMW reduces communication overhead, energy consumption, and latency by selectively recording only important classification and compliance data. Second, a 3D CNN-based model for waste classification is adapted to the specific characteristics of electro-medical waste, leveraging spatial and depth information to differentiate hazardous medical parts and electronic components, as well as mixed materials. In contrast to generic object-detection CNNs, the given model is trained to operate with minimal energy and computational requirements, enabling real-time inference when deployed in the tight energy and computational constraints often found in hospital waste bins and collection points. Third, the suggested framework offers an event-based smart contract approach, with transactions on blockchains and automated processes (e.g., scheduling pickups, verifying compliance) directly triggered by CNN classification results and thresholds. This is in contrast to current systems, which rely on manual input and periodic logging and do not allow real-time automation of EMW handling workflows, with no opportunity to alter results. At last, the performance of the suggested design is confirmed through a comprehensive assessment using EMW-specific datasets and operational conditions. The findings indicate lower latency, lower power consumption, and better scalability than the representative IoT-blockchain frameworks, demonstrating that the contributions are not just abstract ideas but practical system-level benefits.

Experimental setup and evaluation protocol

The performance of the proposed BIOT-EMW framework was evaluated using a controlled experimental testbed designed to simulate real-life electro-medical waste (EMW) management scenarios in smart healthcare environments. The testbed integrates IoT sensing, edge-based vision processing, and blockchain-based logging under realistic network conditions. IoT nodes were implemented on low-power embedded computing boards that act as smart bins, equipped with vision and environmental sensors, including fill-level and temperature sensors. Edge gateway–class devices were deployed to perform local CNN inference, minimizing communication overhead. The communication environment consisted of a mixed wired–wireless network representative of hospital or smart-facility settings. Images of EMW were captured under controlled conditions at waste collection points, covering representative categories including infectious, pharmaceutical, chemical, and electronic medical waste. Data size and composition were selected to reflect operational diversity rather than large-scale benchmark training.

Latency, delay, bandwidth, and power consumption were measured at the edge and network interface level, reflecting prototype-scale deployment conditions. Latency is defined as the elapsed time from the moment an event is detected at the edge node to the completion of edge-side processing, including signal acquisition, preprocessing, inference, and payload construction for blockchain submission. The time to blockchain confirmation or consensus finality is intentionally excluded, as it depends on network conditions and blockchain configuration rather than the proposed edge intelligence framework. This definition isolates the computational and communication overhead attributable to the EMW system itself. Delay represents the end-to-edge communication time required to transmit the generated metadata from the edge gateway to the blockchain interface or backend service. Bandwidth usage is calculated based on the size of metadata transmitted per waste event over the active network link. Power consumption is measured at the device level. It includes sensing, local computation, and energy for wireless transmission. All reported values are obtained from repeated experimental runs under identical conditions and averaged across multiple trials to reduce transient variation.

The experimental platform employs a private blockchain framework for logging compliance-critical metadata and operates over a local wired–wireless network. Communication between IoT nodes and the edge gateway uses energy-efficient wireless links, while edge-to-blockchain communication relies on a stable local network connection. Scalability is evaluated by gradually increasing the number of concurrent IoT nodes and monitoring system response stability. Overall, the reported results represent prototype-scale experimental validation, rather than synthetic simulations or full-scale field deployment.

Practical considerations

Consider a large urban hospital that generates various types of EMW, such as used syringes, expired pharmaceuticals, and malfunctioning electronic medical equipment. In this setup, IoT-enabled smart bins are installed in different departments (e.g., ICU, pathology lab, radiology unit). When a medical device is discarded, the sensor in the smart bin identifies the item, captures environmental parameters (e.g., temperature and location), and sends the data to the central platform.

Simultaneously, a 3D CNN-based vision module classifies the item as hazardous electronic waste. This classification, along with time, location, and waste type, is logged on the blockchain ledger. A smart contract is triggered when the bin reaches a predefined threshold, automatically generating a pickup request and notifying the authorized waste-handling company. The waste handler accepts the task, and the transaction is recorded on the blockchain for auditability. The entire lifecycle—from waste generation to classification and final pickup—is transparent, tamper-proof, and automated, thereby ensuring compliance with healthcare waste management protocols.

Deployment and cost considerations

The proposed BIOT-EMW framework is designed with practical deployment and cost sensitivity in mind, recognizing the budgetary, operational, and regulatory constraints commonly encountered in healthcare environments. The assumed deployment scale follows a progressive, modular approach, beginning at the ward or department level (e.g., intensive care units, laboratories, radiology units), extending to hospital-wide deployment, and ultimately supporting multi-site healthcare networks such as hospital clusters or urban healthcare systems. This staged deployment strategy allows institutions to incrementally adopt the system while minimizing upfront investment and operational disruption.

To support cost-effective and manageable operation, the framework adopts a private or consortium blockchain architecture rather than a public blockchain. In regulated healthcare contexts, the participating entities (e.g., hospitals, licensed waste handlers, and regulatory authorities) are known and trusted stakeholders. A private/consortium blockchain reduces transaction fees, lowers consensus overhead, improves latency, and simplifies governance while still preserving immutability, auditability, and controlled data access. This choice aligns with regulatory compliance requirements and avoids the unpredictable costs and performance variability associated with public blockchain networks.

The primary cost drivers of the BIOT-EMW system include IoT sensing devices (cameras and environmental sensors), edge computing units, network connectivity, and routine maintenance. To mitigate these costs, the framework emphasizes the use of low-power, low-cost IoT hardware, edge-level processing to reduce cloud dependency, and energy-efficient communication protocols. Maintenance costs are further reduced through automated, smart-contract-driven workflows that minimize manual intervention in waste tracking, scheduling, and compliance verification.

From a data management perspective, a hybrid on-chain/off-chain storage strategy is employed to balance cost, scalability, and regulatory compliance. Only lightweight, compliance-critical metadata—such as waste category, timestamp, location, and cryptographic hash references—are recorded on the blockchain. High-volume data, including raw images or sensor streams, is stored off-chain at the edge or in secure cloud repositories. This design significantly reduces blockchain storage and transaction costs while ensuring traceability, auditability, and adherence to healthcare data protection regulations.

Discussion

The outcomes of the suggested BIOT-EMW system indicate that it can be successfully used to manage electro-medical waste by leveraging IoT devices, blockchain, and 3D CNN-based classification. The system demonstrates low latency and delay, which are paramount in medical settings for real-time waste monitoring and scheduled pickup. BIOT-EMW performs better in terms of bandwidth usage and consumes much lower power and resources than current solutions like ML-BCT-SG and AI-IoT-Sec, and therefore is an appropriate solution for large-scale, resource-intensive smart city applications. Data integrity, traceability, and regulatory compliance are ensured through the implementation of smart contracts to automate waste pickup, verify compliance, and maintain audit trails. Additionally, the system’s high scalability demonstrates its readiness for implementation in environments where the volume and density of IoT devices increase. Nonetheless, there are some shortcomings. The system can experience performance issues when dealing with a highly unstable network, and the smart contracts can automatically run important activities. Still, in some unusual instances, they can be overridden. Future studies have the potential to address federated and distributed model training on local devices and implement dynamic consensus mechanisms to achieve more energy efficiency. The suggested model fills a significant gap by providing a secure, intelligent, and automated system for EMW management, with practical implications for intelligent healthcare and smart city waste management.

Power and charging challenges in IoT devices

A limitation to implementing the suggested BIOT-EMW system is that IoT devices have power and charging constraints. They are devices that can be placed in medical bins, collection points, and/or other healthcare settings and operate under remote or semi-autonomous conditions, usually with limited battery power. Frequent recharging or battery changes may prove expensive and logistically challenging, especially in large-scale or distributed deployments. To solve this, different approaches may be embraced. Energy-harvesting devices, such as solar panels or thermal electrical generators, can extend battery life by converting environmental energy into electricity. Additionally, low-power communication systems such as LoRaWAN, ZigBee, or BLE can significantly reduce energy consumption for data transmission. Devices may also be set to operate in duty-cycled or sleep mode, activating only when required to transmit data, which reduces power consumption. Another practical solution is edge computing, where data can be processed locally, eliminating the need for continuous contact with cloud servers. Wireless charging pads can be installed in fixed locations, providing constant power without manual handling. All these methods improve the sustainability, reliability, and scalability of IoT-based EMW systems, particularly in smart city implementations, where constant online and high-availability, and high maintenance are important.

Conclusion

Blockchain integration provides a robust platform for improving transparency, security, and traceability in electro-medical waste (EMW) management systems. By combining IoT devices, blockchain technology, and computer vision techniques, the proposed BIOT-EMW framework enables automated identification, secure tracking, and reliable lifecycle management of electro-medical waste in healthcare environments. The integration of edge-level CNN-based classification supports timely waste segregation, while blockchain-based data logging ensures immutability and accountability of critical waste-handling events. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves low latency, reduced power consumption, efficient resource utilization, and good scalability under prototype-scale EMW scenarios. Overall, the BIOT-EMW framework offers a practical and efficient solution to key challenges in electro-medical waste management in modern healthcare facilities.

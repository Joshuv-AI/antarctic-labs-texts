Bleeding Risk Is Not Increased When Initiating Therapeutic Plasma Exchange in Adults Using Exclusively Albumin Replacement Fluid 2 Days After Percutaneous Kidney Biopsy
Authors: Jay S Raval, Marian A Rollins‐Raval, Yara A Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe practice of selecting replacement fluid for therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) after percutaneous kidney biopsy is variable and often depends on the number of days after biopsy that the apheresis treatment plan is initiated. It has been previously reported that most centers utilize plasma for at least part of replacement fluid for TPE after kidney biopsy, and quite often for many days. In this retrospective study, 100 consecutive patients who underwent percutaneous kidney biopsy and had TPE initiated 2 days after biopsy were analyzed. Half of these patients had exclusively albumin replacement fluid, and the other half had replacement fluid with both albumin and plasma. Rates for bleeding at the kidney biopsy site were identical and occurred in 1 patient (2%) in each cohort. Introduction

The practice of selecting replacement fluid for therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) after percutaneous kidney biopsy is variable and often depends on the number of days after biopsy that the apheresis treatment plan is initiated. The Coagulation Subcommittee of the American Society for Apheresis (ASFA) Clinical Applications Committee conducted a survey revealing that most centers utilize plasma for at least part of replacement fluid in TPE after kidney biopsy, and quite often for many days [1]. We assessed biopsy‐associated bleeding in patients who initiated TPE 2 days after their percutaneous kidney biopsy and had replacement fluid using exclusively albumin versus both albumin and plasma.

Methods

In this retrospective, single center study, 100 consecutive patients who underwent percutaneous kidney biopsy followed by having their initial TPE procedure 2 days after biopsy were analyzed. Laboratory tests were performed prior to kidney biopsy per institutional protocol. A post‐biopsy ultrasound was routinely performed shortly after kidney biopsy and prior to initiating TPE, but after this such imaging was not performed unless there was a clinical concern for bleeding. All procedures were performed with the Spectra Optia Apheresis System (Terumo BCT, Lakewood, CO) using an apheresis‐compatible central venous catheter or arteriovenous fistula, and each TPE exchanged 1.0 plasma volumes. Treatment protocol for replacement fluid was not standardized, and half of patients received exclusively albumin replacement fluid (i.e., no plasma cohort), whereas the other half received replacement fluid with both albumin and plasma (i.e., some plasma cohort) in ≤ 1:1.

All patients were 18 years or older, had neither active bleeding nor history of bleeding disorders, were not receiving systemic therapeutic anticoagulation or antiplatelet drugs (prophylactic heparin, enoxaparin, or low‐dose aspirin were acceptable), had no other invasive procedures planned besides TPE, had no disease indication that necessitated use of plasma as at least part of replacement fluid [2], and had laboratory values of platelets ≥ 50 × 109/L, INR < 2.0, and hemoglobin ≥ 7 g/dL. The outcome of interest was biopsy‐associated bleeding and documentation of any abnormal bleeding was examined for the complete TPE treatment course for all patients. Patient demographic, clinical, and laboratory data were collected, analyzed, and compared between the two cohorts. Continuous data were presented as means and categorical data were expressed as percentages; Student's t‐test and Fisher's exact test were used for analysis with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05.

Results

All 100 patients included for analysis initiated TPE 2 days after percutaneous kidney biopsy. There were no significant differences between any of the assessed variables (see Table 1). Biopsy‐associated bleeding occurred in 1 of the 50 patients in each cohort (bleeding rate of 2% vs. 2%, p ≥ 0.05). A small hematoma for the sole patient in each cohort was the only biopsy‐associated bleeding complication, and neither patient required transfusion support nor suffered kidney loss due to bleeding. There was no significant association regarding who performed percutaneous kidney biopsy and bleeding; in the no plasma cohort, the patient who had biopsy‐associated bleeding had their biopsy performed by an attending faculty physician, whereas in the some plasma cohort it was performed by a fellow‐in‐training (p ≥ 0.05).

Discussion

In this retrospective analysis, we demonstrated that initiating a TPE treatment plan using exclusively albumin replacement fluid 2 days after percutaneous kidney biopsy does not increase bleeding risk compared to TPE performed with both albumin and plasma replacement fluid. Biopsy‐associated bleeding rates in both cohorts were low at 2% and our institution's overall major percutaneous kidney biopsy bleeding rate was < 2%, in keeping with other reported rates [3, 4]. Though there were not enough bleeding events to meaningfully comment on the association of training status and risk of bleeding in the current study, other analyses identified no risk attributed to training status [5, 6], consistent with our experience.

The 100 patients in the current analysis had low bleeding risk [7, 8], and so it is unknown whether individuals with greater bleeding risks can be safely treated with TPE using exclusively albumin replacement fluid 2 days after percutaneous kidney biopsy. While there is increased concern for TPE‐associated bleeding complications, it is noteworthy that TPE treatment courses utilizing some plasma as replacement fluid were counterintuitively associated with higher rates of bleeding events [9], though this was most likely the result of residual confounding or indication bias for the use of plasma. In conclusion, bleeding rates are not increased when initiating a TPE treatment plan using exclusively albumin replacement fluid 2 days after percutaneous kidney biopsy. Future studies assessing bleeding risk in this patient population receiving TPE should concentrate on the duration within 1 day after percutaneous kidney biopsy as well as those with greater bleeding risks.

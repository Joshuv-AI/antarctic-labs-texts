Optimization of genome editing strategies for targeted integration at chicken sex chromosome loci
Authors: Zhi Cao, Chen Chen, Yufan He, Wanhong Wei, Jiuzhou Song, Wei Han, Qian Xue, Guohong Chen, Bichun Li, Kai Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe precise targeted integration of large exogenous genes into chicken sex chromosomes is of particular interest for sex-linked trait manipulation, sex-control breeding, and the development of avian bioreactor models. However, efficient targeted integration at sex chromosome loci remains technically challenging, and optimized editing strategies for these loci are still lacking. To improve targeted genome editing at two previously identified chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci, EE0.6 and NC_006127.4, this study systematically evaluated and optimized key parameter affecting editing efficiency. First, we evaluated the effects of different sgRNA combinations on targeted knockout efficiency, establishing the advantage of a dual-sgRNA/Cas9 architecture, which achieved knockout efficiencies of 86.67% and 75.00% at the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci, respectively. We next introduced the Cas9 nickase (Cas9n) system, which has previously been reported to exhibit improved editing specificity, and evaluated its performance at both target loci. Quantitative analysis showed that the dual-sgRNA/Cas9 targeting system successfully mediated the precise targeted integration of a 1.1-kb SV40-mCherry reporter cassette, reaching 100% (28/28) at the EE0.6 locus and 80.00% (20/25) at the NC_006127.4 locus. Based on this result, this study further investigated the effects of donor homology arm (HA) lengths (200 bp, 600 bp, and, 1000 bp) and vector topologies (circular and linearized) on targeted knock-in efficiency. The results revealed that in the circular donor system, the optimal HA lengths for the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci were 200 bp (50.4% ± 4.4%) and 600 bp (30.1% ± 1.2%), respectively. However, upon the introduction of linearized donors with free ends, the knock-in efficiency of the exogenous target fragment was significantly enhanced, and its HA length preference underwent a significant reversal. The optimal HA length for EE0.6 was extended to 600 bp (78.9% ± 1.0%), whereas that for NC_006127.4 was shortened to 200 bp (49.9% ± 0.4%). In summary, this study established an efficient targeted integration strategy for chicken sex chromosome loci. The optimized system provides a foundation for future applications in sex-linked breeding and avian bioreactor development.Keywords: Chicken, CRISPR/Cas9, Gene editing, Sex chromosome, Targeted integration

Introduction

Precise modification of trait-associated loci has become an important strategy in modern livestock and poultry molecular breeding, and efficient site-specific gene knockout or knock-in is a prerequisite for elucidating gene function and regulatory mechanisms. As a highly efficient genome-engineering tool, the CRISPR/Cas9 system utilizes specific sgRNA-mediated recognition and Cas9 nuclease cleavage to achieve precise targeted genomic modifications. Compared to traditional transgenic techniques, CRISPR/Cas9 significantly enhances the efficiency of site-specific targeted modifications and reduces the cost and difficulty of establishing transgenic animal models. Currently, the CRISPR/Cas9 system has become an indispensable tool for functional genomics research and animal genetic engineering. (Niu et al., 2014; Frangoul et al., 2021). In fundamental biological research, CRISPR/Cas9 has enabled the generation of a variety of genetically modified animal models, including targeted knock-in rodents, transgenic pigs, and reporter cell lines (Bäck et al., 2019; Sper et al., 2017; He et al., 2020). In agricultural breeding, the CRISPR/Cas9 system has been effectively applied to generate virus-resistant pigs (Salgado et al., 2024), double-muscled livestock with enhanced meat yield (Lv et al., 2016), high-cashmere-yield goats (Hu et al., 2021), and to establish a fluorescence marker-based sex identification system in chicks (Lee et al., 2019; Pfann et al., 2025). Collectively, these studies highlight the tremendous potential of CRISPR/Cas9 technology for both animal model generation and molecular breeding.

Despite the remarkable advances achieved with CRISPR/Cas9 technology in mammalian models (Niu et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2015), its application in avian species remains challenging because of the unique biological characteristics of birds. At present, avian genome editing mainly relies on primordial germ cell transplantation or in vivo electroporation approaches. However, unlike mammals, genetically modified avian embryonic stem cells cannot be readily incorporated into fertilized embryos because of the oviparous reproductive characteristics of birds. As a result, the efficiency of genome editing and the production of transgenic poultry remain relatively low. Among the available genome engineering targets, sex chromosome loci are of particular interest because they enable the stable inheritance of sex-linked transgenes. Consequently, these loci have attracted increasing attention for applications in sex-control breeding, chick sex identification, and avian bioreactor development (Lee et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2024). Our previous work identified EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 as two candidate safe-harbor loci on chicken sex chromosomes with potential for stable exogenous gene integration (Wu et al., 2024). The establishment of efficient targeted integration strategies for these loci is of considerable importance for both basic research and poultry breeding applications.

Although candidate safe-harbor loci have been identified on chicken sex chromosomes, the editing characteristics of these loci remain largely unexplored, and efficient strategies for targeted knockout and knock-in have not yet been systematically optimized. In gene knockout applications, editing outcomes are strongly influenced by sgRNA design and the mode of Cas9-mediated DNA cleavage. Previous studies have shown that dual-sgRNA strategies can enhance target-site disruption, while Cas9 nickase (Cas9n) systems may improve editing specificity through coordinated targeting of adjacent genomic sites (Ran et al., 2013; Hryhorowicz et al., 2016; Anzalone et al., 2020). In contrast, targeted knock-in relies primarily on homology-directed repair (HDR)-mediated integration of exogenous DNA into the host genome. Because HDR competes with the dominant non-homologous end joining pathway, precise transgene insertion remains considerably less efficient than targeted gene disruption (Auer et al., 2014; Byrne et al., 2015; Gwon et al., 2025). Recent studies have developed several approaches to enhance HDR efficiency, including donor template engineering, 3′-overhang double-stranded DNA donors, and other optimized repair templates that facilitate precise integration (Gutierrez-Triana et al., 2018; Wierson et al., 2020). Moreover, accumulating evidence indicates that donor template design, including donor topology and homology arm length, plays a critical role in determining HDR efficiency and integration fidelity (Zhang et al., 2017; Simora et al., 2020; Wierson et al., 2020). However, the effectiveness of these strategies has not been systematically evaluated in chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci.

Therefore, the present study focused on two candidate chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci, EE0.6 and NC_006127.4. Using the PX458 vector as the backbone, we systematically evaluated different sgRNA expression strategies and Cas9 cleavage modes, including Cas9n. In addition, donor topology and homology arm length were optimized to improve targeted integration efficiency. In summary, this study systematically characterized the editing behaviors of two candidate chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci and identified locus-dependent differences in targeted integration efficiency and homology arm preference. These findings provide practical guidance for genome engineering at chicken sex chromosome loci and support future applications in poultry breeding and bioreactor development.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The chicken embryo fibroblast cell line DF-1 was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, CRL-12203) and routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination by PCR. Cells were cultured in DMEM (ATCC, #30-2002) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, #16000-044) and 1 × penicillin–streptomycin (Procell, #PB180121) at 37°C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO₂. Cells were passaged when confluence reached approximately 80% to 90%. After removal of the culture medium, cells were washed once with sterile PBS and digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, #25200056) at 37°C for 1 to 3 min. Digestion was terminated by adding complete medium when cells became rounded and detached. Cell suspensions were collected, centrifuged at 250 × g for 5 min, and resuspended in fresh medium for subsequent culture. For transfection experiments, DF-1 cells were seeded into 24-well plates 24 h before transfection to achieve 70% to 80% confluence at the time of transfection.

sgRNA design and construction of knockout vectors

Based on the chicken reference genome (Gallus_gallus-6.0), sgRNAs targeting the sex chromosome safe-harbor loci EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 were designed using the CRISPOR online platform. All sgRNA oligonucleotides (Table S1) were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology (Nanjing, China). Annealed double-stranded oligonucleotides were ligated into the BbsI-digested PX458 vector (Addgene, #48138) using T4 DNA ligase (NEB, #M0202S). Positive clones were verified by Sanger sequencing, and endotoxin-free plasmids were prepared using a plasmid maxi kit (Tiangen, #DP117) for subsequent transfection experiments.

Optimization of CRISPR/Cas9 transfection conditions

To determine the optimal transfection conditions, four commercial transfection reagents were evaluated using logarithmically growing DF-1 cells (70%–80% confluence). Cells were seeded into 24-well plates at a density of 1.5 × 10⁵ cells/well 24 h before transfection. High-purity plasmid DNA was used for all transient transfection experiments, and the total plasmid amount per well was maintained at 1 μg. For Lipofectamine, 2000 (Invitrogen, #11668030), 1 μg plasmid DNA and 2 μL reagents were separately diluted in 50 μL Opti-MEM, incubated for 5 min at room temperature, combined, and incubated for an additional 15 min before addition to cells. The medium was replaced with fresh complete medium after 24 h. For Lipofectamine, 8000 (Beyotime, #C0533), 1 μg plasmid DNA and 1.5 μL reagent were mixed in 50 μL Opti-MEM and directly added to cells. For Fugene HD (Promega, #E2311), plasmid DNA and reagent were mixed at a ratio of 1 μg: 3 μL in 50 μL Opti-MEM and incubated at 37°C for 15 min before transfection. For ViaFect (Promega, #E4893), plasmid DNA and reagent were mixed at a ratio of 1 μg: 3 μL in 50 μL Opti-MEM and incubated at room temperature for 15 min before transfection.

At 48 h post-transfection, cell morphology and fluorescence were observed under a fluorescence microscope, and Cas9 expression was further evaluated by qRT-PCR and western blot analysis.

Effect of different sgRNA expression strategies on knockout efficiency

To facilitate efficient dual-sgRNA–mediated genome editing, a dual-sgRNA/Cas9 system was developed. A commercially available pCMV-sgRNA-hU6 Twinstrep plasmid (Cat. No. HH—CAS-072; Haijihaoge Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) was used as an intermediate vector. DNA fragments containing dual sgRNA expression cassettes were amplified using Phusion High-Fidelity DNA Polymerase and specific primers (sgRNA1-F/sgRNA2-R, Table S3). Purified PCR products were inserted into the PX458 vector using a seamless cloning kit (Vazyme, #C112-01/02). The resulting constructs, PX458-EE0.6-2sgRNA and PX458-NC_006127.4-2sgRNA, were confirmed by Sanger sequencing.

Construction and evaluation of the Cas9 nickase system

To reduce nonspecific effects caused by double-strand DNA breaks, a Cas9 nickase system carrying the D10A mutation was introduced. PX461 (Addgene, #48140), which expresses Cas9n, was used as the backbone vector. Single-sgRNA and dual-sgRNA PX461 constructs targeting each locus were generated following the procedures described in Sections “sgRNA Design and Construction of Knockout Vectors” and “Effect of Different sgRNA Expression Strategies on Knockout Efficiency”. DF-1 cells were harvested 48 h after transfection, and Cas9n expression was evaluated by qRT-PCR.

Construction of HR-Donor knock-in vectors

DF-1 genomic DNA was used as the template to amplify upstream and downstream homologous arms of 200 bp, 600 bp, and 1,000 bp flanking the target loci using high-fidelity DNA polymerase (Table S1). The fragments were assembled into pUC57 vectors in the "HA-L–SV40-mCherry–HA-R" configuration via seamless cloning between the EcoRI and HindIII restriction sites, generating circular donor vectors with varying homology arm lengths (EE0.6/NC_006127.4-200/600/1000HA-donor). The detailed sequence information for the SV40-mCherry cassette is provided in Supplementary Table S2. For linear donor experiments, circular donor plasmids were linearized by HindIII digestion and purified by gel extraction. The optimized Cas9/sgRNA knockout vectors and HR donor vectors with different homology arm lengths or topological structures were co-transfected into DF-1 cells at a 1:1 mass ratio, with a total plasmid amount of 1 μg per well. At 48 h post-transfection, targeted integration efficiency was evaluated. Before nucleic acid extraction, cells were washed three times with PBS to remove residual extracellular plasmids. Total RNA was extracted and treated with DNase I prior to reverse transcription for qRT-PCR analysis of exogenous mCherry expression. Genomic DNA was subsequently isolated, and targeted integration was evaluated by PCR using junction-specific primer pairs, with one primer located outside the homology arm and the other within the inserted cassette, followed by sequencing analysis to confirm precise integration events. HDR efficiency is estimated based on semi-quantitative PCR band intensity analysis and represents a relative population level.

Protein extraction and western blot analysis

Transfected DF-1 cells were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors on ice for 30 min. Lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4°C for 15 min, and supernatants were collected for protein quantification using a BCA protein assay kit. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes at a constant current of 300 mA for 90 min. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at room temperature and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. After incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature, protein signals were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence reagents. The following antibodies were used: mouse anti-Cas9 (Abcam, #7A9-3A3; 1:1,000), rabbit anti-β-actin (ABclonal, #AC033; 1:2,000), HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (ABclonal, #AS003; 1:5,000), and HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (ABclonal, #AS014; 1:5,000).

T7E1 cleavage assay for sgRNA activity evaluation

Genomic DNA was extracted from transfected cells, and 400- to 600-bp fragments containing the target sites were amplified using specific primers (Table S4). Approximately 200 ng purified PCR product was denatured and reannealed in NEBuffer 2 to generate heteroduplex DNA using the following program: 95°C for 5 min, cooling to 85°C at −2°C/s, and then cooling to 25°C at −0.1°C/s. Subsequently, 1 μL T7E1 enzyme (NEB, #M0302L) was added and incubated at 37°C for 30 min. Digested products were separated on 2% agarose gels, and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

TA cloning analysis of editing efficiency and mutation types

PCR products containing the target sites were purified by gel extraction and ligated into the pMD19-T vector (Tsingke, #007VS). Recombinant plasmids were transformed into DH5α competent cells (Tsingke, #TSC—C14) and plated on LB agar containing ampicillin. After overnight incubation, 20 to 30 individual colonies from each group were randomly selected for Sanger sequencing. Editing efficiency was calculated as follows:

To ensure representativeness, colonies were randomly picked without pre-selection of colony morphology or size. This TA cloning-based assay reflects the mutation frequency at the individual clone level and is suitable for evaluating the proportion of edited alleles among sampled bacterial colonies.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRNzol reagent and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the HiScript III RT SuperMix kit (Vazyme, #R323-01). Quantitative PCR was performed using Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, #Q711-02/03) on a Bio-Rad CFX96 Real-Time PCR System with gene-specific primers (Table S1). The amplification conditions were as follows: 95°C for 30 s, followed by 35 cycles of 95°C for 10 s and 60°C for 30 s. β-actin was used as the internal reference gene, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2^-ΔΔCt method.

Data statistics

All experiments were performed in at least three biological replicates. Data are expressed as mean ± SEM. One-way ANOVA or t-tests were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 software. P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant (*), and P < 0.01 was defined as highly statistically significant (**).

Results

Construction and activity evaluation of genome editing vectors targeting chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci

To establish an efficient genome editing system targeting the chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci EE0.6 and NC_006127.4, specific CRISPR/Cas9 cleavage systems and corresponding HDR donor templates were first constructed and evaluated. Three sgRNAs (sgRNA1–3) were designed for each locus (Figs. 1A and 2A) and individually cloned into the PX458 vector (Fig. 1B and 2B). Validation in DF-1 cells demonstrated that all recombinant vectors efficiently expressed Cas9 protein (Fig. S1). T7E1 mismatch cleavage assays further confirmed that all 6 sgRNAs exhibited effective DNA cleavage activity in vitro at their corresponding target sites (Figs. 1C and 2C).

To quantitatively evaluate editing efficiency, TA cloning followed by Sanger sequencing was performed. At the EE0.6 locus, the mutation frequencies of sgRNA1, sgRNA2, and sgRNA3 were 79.17% (19/24), 70.83% (17/24), and 73.91% (17/23), respectively. At the NC_006127.4 locus, the corresponding mutation frequencies were 75.00% (18/24), 62.50% (15/24), and 61.54% (15/26), respectively (Figs. 1C and S1D). Among the tested sgRNAs, sgRNA1 consistently exhibited the highest editing efficiency at both loci and was therefore selected for subsequent knockout and knock-in experiments.

Effect of sgRNA combination strategies on genome editing efficiency

To determine whether multiplex sgRNA combinations could improve genome editing efficiency, different sgRNA pairs were evaluated at the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci. T7E1 cleavage assays demonstrated that the sgRNA combinations sg1+2, sg1+3, and sg2+3 effectively induced DNA double-strand breaks at both target sites (Figs. 3A–B and S2). TA cloning and Sanger sequencing further showed that the sg1+2 combination produced the highest synergistic editing efficiency, with mutation frequencies reaching 86.70% (13/15) at EE0.6 and 75.00% (9/12) at NC_006127.4 (Figs. 3B and S2B), which were higher than those obtained using single sgRNA transfection (Figs. 1C and 2C).

To improve delivery consistency and reduce variability associated with cotransfection, the optimal sgRNA pair (sg1+2) was assembled into a single PX458 vector using a tandem expression strategy, generating the dual-sgRNA constructs PX458-EE0.6-2sgRNA and PX458-NC_006127.4-2sgRNA (Fig. S3A). qRT-PCR and western blot analysis confirmed efficient transcription and translation of the Cas9 system in DF-1 cells (Fig. S3B–C). The editing efficiency of the single-vector dual-sgRNA system was subsequently evaluated. T7E1 assays combined with grayscale quantification showed cleavage efficiencies of 69.17% and 66.24% at the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci, respectively. Consistently, TA cloning and Sanger sequencing revealed indel frequencies of 86.67% (13/15) and 75.00% (9/12), respectively (Fig. 3C). Compared with the dual-plasmid cotransfection strategy, the single-vector system achieved comparable or higher editing efficiency and was therefore selected for subsequent knock-in experiments.

Effect of different Cas9 cleavage modes on genome editing efficiency

Although wild-type Cas9 efficiently induces DNA double-strand breaks, a Cas9n system carrying the D10A mutation was introduced based on previous reports of improved editing specificity. We subsequently evaluated its genome-editing performance at the target loci. To evaluate the editing activity of Cas9n at chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci, sgRNAs targeting EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 were first cloned into the PX461 vector containing the Cas9n expression cassette (Fig. S4A). qRT-PCR and western blot analysis confirmed efficient expression of Cas9n in DF-1 cells (Fig. 4A–B). T7E1 cleavage assays and TA cloning demonstrated that paired sgRNA cotransfection efficiently induced mutations at both target loci. Among the tested combinations, sg1+2 again exhibited the highest editing efficiency, with mutation frequencies reaching 93.33% (28/30) at EE0.6 and 80.77% (21/26) at NC_006127.4 (Figs. 4C and S4B).

To further reduce variability associated with multiple-plasmid delivery, a dual-sgRNA/Cas9 co-expression architecture (PX461-Cas9n-2sgRNA) was subsequently constructed (Fig. S5A). Quantitative analysis showed that the single-vector Cas9n system further improved editing efficiency. At the EE0.6 locus, TA cloning revealed a mutation frequency of 100% (28/28), whereas T7E1 grayscale quantification indicated a cleavage efficiency of 65.07%. Collectively, these results demonstrated that the single-vector Cas9n dual-sgRNA system achieved highly efficient genome editing.

Effect of homology arm length on knock-in efficiency

After establishing the optimal cleavage system, the effect of homology arm length on HDR-mediated knock-in efficiency was investigated. Donor vectors containing 200-bp, 600-bp, and 1,000-bp homologous arms were constructed for both the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci (Fig. S6A). Cotransfection experiments confirmed efficient co-expression of the editing system and donor constructs in DF-1 cells (Figs. 5A–B, 6A–B, and S6B–C). To verify precise integration of exogenous sequences, genomic DNA was extracted from cotransfected cells and analyzed using PCR amplification with primers spanning the homologous recombination junctions, followed by semiquantitative grayscale analysis. The results demonstrated a clear locus-specific effect of homology arm length on HDR efficiency. At the EE0.6 locus, all 3 donor constructs successfully mediated targeted integration (Fig. 5C). Grayscale quantification revealed a negative correlation between homology arm length and integration efficiency, with the 200-bp donor achieving the highest HDR efficiency (50.4% ± 4.4%; Table 1).

Effect of linearized donor vectors on knock-in efficiency

The topological structure of donor DNA is an important factor influencing intracellular DNA repair. Compared with circular plasmids, linearized double-stranded DNA with exposed ends is generally considered more favorable for homologous recombination-mediated integration. Therefore, linearized donor vectors were generated by single-enzyme digestion in this study. Cotransfection experiments demonstrated efficient co-expression of the editing system and donor templates in DF-1 cells (Figs. 7A and S7). To evaluate the effect of donor topology on HDR efficiency, donor vectors containing the previously validated 200-bp and 600-bp homology arms were selected, and the knock-in efficiencies of circular and linearized donors were compared at both target loci. Genomic PCR combined with semiquantitative grayscale analysis demonstrated that linearized donors consistently exhibited higher knock-in efficiency than circular donor vectors with the same homology arm length (Fig. 7B–C and Table 1, Table 2, Table 3).

More importantly, donor linearization altered the homology arm preference at both loci. At the EE0.6 locus, the optimal homology arm length shifted from 200 bp in the circular donor system to 600 bp in the linear donor system, achieving an HDR efficiency of 78.9% ± 1.0% (Table 3). In contrast, at the NC_006127.4 locus, the linear donor system favored the shorter 200-bp homology arm, which exhibited higher integration efficiency (49.9% ± 0.4%) than the 600-bp donor (Fig. 7C and Table 3). These findings suggest that recombination activity associated with free DNA ends may be influenced by local chromatin environments at different genomic loci. Finally, Sanger sequencing of PCR products confirmed precise integration of the exogenous mCherry sequence into both the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 target sites (Fig. 7D–E). Taken together, these results identified the combination of the Cas9n dual-sgRNA editing system and linearized donor templates as the optimal strategy for targeted genome editing at chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci.

Discussion

CRISPR/Cas9-mediated targeted knock-in is a key approach for poultry genome engineering and molecular breeding, but its efficiency is constrained by delivery methods, cleavage activity, and homologous recombination rates. In this study, the chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 were selected to systematically optimize genome editing performance across three parameters: transfection conditions, sgRNA/Cas9 expression strategies, and donor template configurations. For plasmid delivery, four commercial transfection reagents were preliminary screened in DF-1 cells. ViaFect demonstrated superior transfection efficiency and minimal cytotoxicity compared with FuGENE HD and Lipofectamine-based reagents, and was therefore selected as the standardized delivery vehicle for all downstream genome-editing evaluations. Beyond delivery methods, the efficiency and accuracy of the CRISPR/Cas9 system are governed by sgRNA design, coordinated component expression, and Cas9 cleavage modality. Single-vector expression systems improve multiplex editing performance and reduce experimental variability by synchronizing the delivery of Cas9 and multiple sgRNAs (Du et al., 2023; Villiger et al., 2024). Consequently, we utilized the PX458 co-expression plasmid to construct single-vector dual-sgRNA systems (PX458-2sgRNA) targeting the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci. Quantitative analysis showed that this tandem configuration achieved editing efficiencies of 75.00% to 86.67% in DF-1 cells. All evaluated dual-sgRNA combinations exhibited higher cleavage activity than single-sgRNA controls. Furthermore, this single-vector framework substantially outperformed the multiplex editing efficiencies previously reported in avian cells, which were limited to approximately 0.32% (Goshayeshi et al., 2021).

The higher performance of the dual-sgRNA strategy is attributed to its capacity to simultaneously induce two independent double-strand breaks. This coordinated cleavage facilitates structural deletion of the intervening genomic segment, bypassing single-site nonhomologous end-joining pathways that frequently result in unproductive repairs. Furthermore, this synergistic cleavage is modulated by the sequence characteristics of individual sgRNAs. Optimizing parameters such as GC content stabilizes RNA-DNA hybridization, while refined sgRNA secondary structures minimize self-complementarity, thereby enhancing Cas9 loading and targeted cleavage within the chromatin environments of the sex chromosomes (Mao et al., 2020; Villiger et al., 2024). In addition to optimizing sgRNA targeting strategies, improving editing specificity is another important consideration in CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing. In the present study, wild-type Cas9 was replaced with the D10A Cas9n to evaluate the performance of a paired nickase system at the two chicken sex chromosome loci. The single-vector Cas9n-2sgRNA system produced high mutation frequencies among sequenced clones, reaching 100% (28/28) at the EE0.6 locus and 80.00% (20/25) at the NC_006127.4 locus. Previous studies have generally reported that paired Cas9 nickase systems improve editing specificity at the expense of editing efficiency (Ran et al., 2013). In contrast, a relatively high mutation frequency was observed in our study. One possible explanation is that Cas9n and the paired sgRNAs were co-expressed from a single vector, which may have promoted synchronized expression and coordinated targeting of adjacent genomic sites. By driving Cas9n and both paired sgRNAs from a single plasmid backbone, the system ensures spatial-temporal synchronization and stoichiometric balance, leading to coordinated nicking of adjacent genomic strands before transient DNA repair occurs.

HDR is the primary pathway for precise targeted integration of exogenous DNA, but its efficiency is constrained by competition with the dominant non-homologous end joining pathway. While various HDR-enhancement strategies—including donor template engineering, short-homology designs, and 3′-overhang modifications—have been developed to promote repair machinery recruitment (Gutierrez-Triana et al., 2018; Wierson et al., 2020; Gwon et al., 2025), their efficacy at chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci remains largely unexplored. Homology arm length and donor topology are two critical parameters governing this process. Previous studies demonstrate that the optimal length of homology arms varies significantly across species, ranging from tens of base pairs in fish to several hundred or thousand base pairs in mammals (Zhang et al., 2017; Simora et al., 2020). In our experiments, the EE0.6 locus favored shorter 200-bp homology arms, whereas NC_006127.4 exhibited optimal integration with 600-bp arms. In addition to homology arm length, donor DNA topology is another important determinant of knock-in efficiency. Compared with conventional circular plasmids, linearized double-stranded DNA has been reported to facilitate more efficient targeted integration (Zhang et al., 2017; Shin and Lee, 2020). For example, engineered 3′-overhang double-stranded DNA donors were recently reported to substantially improve precise integration of large DNA fragments, whereas HDR-boosting donor architectures that enhance recruitment of DNA repair factors have also been shown to increase targeted knock-in efficiency across multiple genomic loci and cell types (Han et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2024). Our results in DF-1 cells were consistent with these findings, as linearized donor templates increased knock-in efficiency by 1.2- to 2.1-fold compared with circular donors. Notably, donor linearization also altered the preferred homology arm length at both loci, indicating that HDR outcomes are jointly influenced by donor configuration and locus-specific genomic features.

One possible explanation for this advantage is that linear donor DNA containing free double-strand ends can be directly recognized by intracellular recombination complexes without requiring plasmid relaxation and supercoil resolution (Yang et al., 2024). This may facilitate rapid end resection and more efficient entry into the HDR pathway (Lau et al., 2020). Interestingly, donor linearization also altered the preferred homology arm length at both loci. At the EE0.6 locus, the optimal homology arm length shifted from 200 bp in circular donors to 600 bp in linear donors, whereas the preferred length at NC_006127.4 shifted from 600 bp to 200 bp. This reversal strongly suggests that targeted integration is not regulated by a single factor, but instead results from interactions between donor topology, free DNA end dynamics, and the local chromatin environment at specific genomic loci.

The local chromatin status of avian sex chromosomes likely dictates these distinct structural preferences. Chromatin accessibility and nucleosome packaging regulate both nuclease cleavage and the recruitment of repair factors like RAD51 required for strand invasion (Schep et al., 2024). The NC_006127.4 locus resides on the Z chromosome, which maintains a more open, euchromatic state in transcriptionally active regions to accommodate dosage compensation mechanisms in birds (Mank, 2013). Under circular donor conditions, this permissive architecture allows longer (600-bp) homology arms to facilitate homology searching. However, when the donor is linearized, its highly reactive free ends lower the kinetic barrier for strand pairing (Yang et al., 2024), making a shorter 200-bp arm sufficient to drive recombination before exonuclease degradation occurs. Conversely, the EE0.6 locus is located within the highly condensed heterochromatin matrix characteristic of the avian W chromosome or sex-linked repetitive regions (Bellott et al., 2017). In this restrictive environment, circular plasmids face severe spatial diffusion barriers and topological constraints, restricting alignment and leaving short 200-bp arms as the only kinetically viable option. When the donor is linearized, however, overcoming the structural constraints and dense nucleosome array of heterochromatin requires longer 600-bp homology arms to efficiently recruit chromatin remodeling factors and stabilize the synaptic complex. Despite the efficiency gains of linearized templates, their preparation remains labor-intensive, and exposed linear DNA is highly susceptible to intracellular exonucleases, which can compromise overall donor stability (Gutierrez-Triana et al., 2018). Therefore, future application of this system in chicken primordial germ cells will require further optimization to balance knock-in efficiency with donor stability.

In summary, this study established an efficient targeted knock-in platform based on a dual-sgRNA/Cas9 co-expression architecture combined with locus-specific linearized donor templates. This strategy substantially improved large-fragment integration efficiency at chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci. However, it should be noted that genomic integration was evaluated using PCR- and fluorescence-based assays at an early time point, and transient donor expression or residual episomal DNA cannot be completely excluded. In addition, HDR efficiency was estimated using semi-quantitative PCR band intensity analysis, which provides a population-level comparison but does not fully reflect allele-specific editing outcomes in diploid cells. Therefore, future studies should further validate stable genomic integration through clonal cell isolation, long-term expression analysis, and junction or long-read sequencing, together with more precise sequencing-based quantification of HDR events. Comprehensive off-target analysis will also be required to evaluate the specificity and safety of the optimized system.

Conclusion

In the present study, a CRISPR/Cas9-based targeted genome editing system was established and optimized for the chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci EE0.6 and NC_006127.4. The results demonstrated that the single-vector dual-sgRNA strategy markedly improved genome editing efficiency, achieving knockout efficiencies of 86.67% and 75.00% at the EE0.6 and NC_006127.4 loci, respectively. Introduction of the Cas9 nickase (Cas9n, D10A) system further enhanced editing specificity and achieved highly efficient cleavage activity at the EE0.6 locus, reaching 100% mutation frequency in vitro. During targeted knock-in experiments, linearized donor templates exhibited substantially higher homologous recombination efficiency than conventional circular plasmids, with the highest integration efficiency reaching 78.9% at the EE0.6 locus. In addition, donor topology was found to influence the preferred homology arm length in a locus-dependent manner, indicating that HDR efficiency is jointly affected by donor structure and local genomic context. Collectively, these findings provide a practical genome-editing strategy for chicken sex chromosome safe-harbor loci and establish a technical foundation for future applications in sex-linked poultry breeding and avian bioreactor development.

Prediction of Post-stenting Lumen Dimensions Using High-Definition Intravascular Ultrasound
Authors: Wei Wu, Shijia Zhao, Yash Vardhan Trivedi, Sree Sindhu Vijayarao, Priyansh Patel, Parth Vikram Singh, Varsha Miriyala, Muhammad Fiyaz Alam, Rahul Chikatimalla, Abdulkader Shaar, Yaman Issa, Changkye Lee, Emmanouil S Brilakis, George Dangas, Yiannis S Chatzizisis

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurate prediction of stent-plaque interaction is crucial for optimizing lesion preparation strategies and improving percutaneous coronary intervention outcomes. High-definition intravascular ultrasound (HD IVUS) provides detailed imaging of plaque composition and vessel dimensions, enabling computational modeling to predict post-stenting lumen dimensions.ObjectivesThe purpose of this study was to develop and validate a patient-specific HD IVUS-based computational framework for the characterization of coronary plaque components and prediction of post-stenting lumen dimensions.MethodsNine patients underwent HD IVUS and coronary angiography before and after stenting. Using pre-stenting HD IVUS images, plaque components (fibrolipid, fibrous, and calcified tissue) were segmented and reconstructed in 3 dimensions. All procedural steps were simulated computationally, and post-simulation lumen dimensions were compared with the actual post-stenting HD IVUS lumen dimensions.ResultsPredicted and actual lumen dimensions post-stenting demonstrated high agreement. The mean lumen diameter difference was 0.17 mm (+5.5% error), with 95% limits of agreement ranging from −0.26 to 0.60 mm. Agreement was also strong for minimal lumen diameter and minimal in-stent cross-sectional area, with differences of −0.02 mm (95% limits of agreement −0.23 to 0.19 mm) and −0.11 mm2 (95% limits of agreement −0.93 to 0.72 mm2), respectively. Predicted stent geometry assessed by eccentricity showed high agreement with HD IVUS-derived stent shape (bias 0.06; 95% limits of agreement −0.10 to 0.21). Stent apposition was accurately predicted by the computational simulations.ConclusionsWe presented and validated a patient-specific computational simulation framework that uses high-resolution intracoronary imaging of plaque components to precisely predict the lumen dimensions post-stenting.Key words: computational simulation, coronary artery stenting, high-definition intravascular ultrasound, patient-specific 3D reconstruction, plaque composition

Atherosclerotic plaque composition significantly influences coronary stent-wall interaction and clinical outcomes, including in-stent restenosis and stent thrombosis.1 Accurate visualization of plaque morphology, encompassing lipid-rich, fibrous, and calcified components, is essential for optimizing coronary stenting.2 High-definition intravascular ultrasound (HD IVUS) provides high-resolution cross-sectional imaging of coronary arteries, enabling precise characterization of plaque burden and vessel dimensions.3

Although HD IVUS is routinely used to guide lesion preparation and stent sizing, its role in clinical practice remains largely descriptive. Patient-specific computational modeling offers the potential to extend intravascular imaging from description to prediction by simulating how a lesion may respond to stent deployment based on pre-coronary intervention imaging.4 While patient-specific stent simulations have previously been developed using optical coherence tomography (OCT),5 similar approaches based on HD IVUS, which provides deeper tissue imaging and broader clinical adoption, have not been systematically studied.6

The purpose of this study was to: 1) develop a computational framework derived from HD IVUS to characterize coronary plaque and predict post-stenting lumen dimensions; and 2) evaluate the accuracy of the computational predictions against post-stenting HD IVUS lumen dimensions.

Materials and methods

Study population

The research protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board at the University of Miami (IRB approval number: 20251139). The study included 9 patients with coronary artery disease who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Each patient underwent HD IVUS and angiography before and after PCI. Supplemental Table 1 provides a detailed description of the steps of each PCI procedure. Of note, 2 different drug-eluting stent platforms were used: the Synergy everolimus-eluting stent and the Resolute Onyx zotarolimus-eluting stent.

HD IVUS segmentation and 3-dimensional reconstruction of coronary arteries

HD IVUS pullbacks were acquired at a constant speed of 1.0 mm/s and coregistered with angiography using corresponding landmarks (eg, side branches, stenoses). The lumen and vessel wall on HD IVUS images were segmented using EchoPlaque 4.0 (INDEC Medical Systems) and imported into Rhinoceros 7.0 (Robert McNeel & Associates) for 3-dimensional (3D) reconstruction of the lumen and wall (Figure 1). Segmentation and 3D reconstruction of plaque components, including fibrolipid, fibrosis, and calcium, were performed in Rhinoceros. All HD IVUS images were segmented and analyzed by 2 experienced operators, each with prior experience of segmenting more than 20,000 HD IVUS images. To ensure accuracy, the 3D reconstructed wall and material geometries were projected onto the 2-dimensional IVUS frames (Figure 1).7

Example of 3-Dimensional Reconstructed Coronary Artery and Plaque

Left panel: Angiographic view of the stenosed coronary artery and the corresponding lumen segmentation; middle panel: 3-dimensional reconstructed lumen, wall, and plaque constituents; right panel: Back-projection of the 3-dimensional reconstructed artery on the high-definition intravascular ultrasound images. 3D = 3-dimensional.

Interoperator and intraoperator reproducibility were calculated using 180 HD IVUS images randomly selected from outside the study cohort. These images were independently segmented by 2 experienced operators. Each operator performed segmentation on the same data set, and for intraoperator assessment, repeated segmentations were performed after a time interval of 4 weeks, blinded to the initial results. Reproducibility was quantified using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) (single measurement, 95% CI). Interoperator agreement was 0.99 (95% CI: 0.98-0.99) for lumen area, 0.94 (95% CI: 0.92-0.95) for vessel area, 0.75 (95% CI: 0.68-0.81) for fibrolipid area, 0.87 (95% CI: 0.83-0.90) for fibrosis area, and 0.91 (95% CI: 0.88-0.93) for calcium area. Intraoperator reproducibility was also high overall, including lumen segmentation (ICC 0.99 and 0.96 for the 2 operators). For the remaining tissue components, intraoperator ICCs ranged from 0.90 to 0.98. Detailed intraoperator and interoperator reproducibility results are provided in Supplemental Table 2.

Plaque composition

The 3D reconstructed wall and plaque were meshed with tetrahedral elements. Distinct mechanical properties were assigned to fibrolipid, fibrotic, and calcified tissues based on prior experimental studies.4,8, 9, 10, 11 The normal arterial wall was assigned fibrolipid tissue properties. The perivascular tissue was modeled as a hyperelastic material, whereas plaque components and normal wall were modeled as a nonlinear hyperelastic-plastic material (Supplemental Figure 1).8

Patient-specific computational simulations

Computational simulations replicated each step of the actual PCI procedures (Supplemental Table 1), as previously described.12 Simulations were executed using the Abaqus/Explicit dynamic solver (Dassault Systèmes). Stent and balloon components were aligned with the luminal centerline, and clinically realistic pressures were applied to the balloon’s inner surface for expansion.

Post-stenting HD IVUS lumen dimensions and shape

The lumen was segmented in post-stenting HD IVUS images as described above. The stented segments were divided into 50 axially equidistant cross-sections, and in each cross-section, the mean lumen diameter (MeanLD) and stent eccentricity were quantified. The same measurements were performed in the simulated stent segments and compared with the corresponding values derived from HD IVUS. In addition, minimal lumen diameter and minimal in-stent cross-sectional area were measured for each case as clinically relevant focal indices.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5 (GraphPad Inc) and R 4.5.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Agreement between post-stenting computational predictions and HD IVUS-derived lumen dimensions was evaluated using Bland-Altman and/or linear mixed-effects analysis. The relationship between calcium burden or plaque length and the differences between predicted and actual lumen dimensions was assessed using Spearman’s correlation coefficient.

Results

Accuracy of predicted lumen dimensions and shape post-stenting

3D plaque reconstruction and multistep computational simulations of coronary stenting were successfully completed in all cases (38 total simulations; average 4 per case) (Supplemental Table 1). Figure 2 depicts the sequential angiographic steps of a coronary intervention, requiring multiple balloon inflations for lesion preparation and stent optimization, and the corresponding computational simulations.

Note the multiple pre-dilatations, stenting, and post-dilatations, which were computationally simulated. (Vessel wall boundaries: black, lumen boundaries: pink, calcified plaque: green).

Qualitative assessment demonstrated close visual agreement between computationally predicted and HD IVUS-derived MeanLD (Figure 3). Per-case Bland-Altman analysis showed minimal differences in MeanLD (−0.1 to 0.36 mm) with narrow 95% limits of agreement (Supplemental Figure 2). Overall, the mean difference between predicted and measured post-stenting lumen dimensions was 0.17 mm (+5.5% error), with 95% limits of agreement ranging from −0.26 to 0.60 mm (Figure 4A). Using a linear mixed-effects model with case treated as a random effect, we found a mean difference of 0.17 mm between simulation-predicted and HD IVUS-derived lumen diameter (95% limits of agreement: 0.08-0.26 mm; P = 0.005).

Bland-Altman analysis demonstrated a bias of −0.02 mm (95% limits of agreement: −0.23 to 0.19 mm) for minimal lumen diameter and a bias of −0.11 mm2 (95% limits of agreement: −0.93 to 0.72 mm2) for minimal in-stent cross-sectional area, suggesting good agreement between prediction and post-stenting HD IVUS (Supplemental Figures 3A and 3B).

The difference between predicted and actual lumen diameter was not significantly associated with calcium burden (Spearman’s ρ = 0.23, P = 0.56) or plaque length (Spearman’s ρ = −0.38, P = 0.32), suggesting that prediction error was not influenced by these lesion morphology measures in this data set (Supplemental Figures 4A and 4B). Consistent with this, when plaque length and calcium burden were included as a fixed-effect covariate in the mixed-effects model, both showed a nonsignificant association with prediction error (plaque length: β = −0.0044 mm per mm, 95% limits of agreement: −0.0098 to 0.0011, P= 0.160; calcium burden: β = 0.0069 mm per 1% calcium burden, 95% limits of agreement: −0.0098 to 0.0237, P = 0.444).

Predicted stent geometry demonstrated good agreement with HD IVUS-derived stent shape (lumen eccentricity bias = 0.06; 95% limits of agreement −0.10 to 0.21) (Figure 4B).

Discussion

In this work, we combined established knowledge, equipment, and techniques, including: 1) experimental data from the literature on plaque tissue mechanical properties;4,8, 9, 10, 11 2) high-resolution intracoronary imaging of plaque components with HD IVUS; and 3) validated patient-specific computational stent simulations12 to build and validate a new patient-specific computational framework that predicts post-stenting lumen dimensions. Both quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrated very high agreement between predicted and actual post-stenting lumen dimensions, advancing pre-stenting HD IVUS-based simulations to a prospective predictive tool that can estimate post-stenting lumen dimensions and provide clinical insight into expected procedural outcomes prior to stent deployment (Central Illustration). Of note, the predictive accuracy of the simulations was independent of lesion complexity parameters (Supplemental Figures 3A and 3B, Figures 4A and 4B).

High-Definition Intravascular Ultrasound–Based Computational Prediction of Lumen Dimensions Post-Stenting

The workflow integrates clinical percutaneous coronary intervention data, including angiography and high-definition intravascular ultrasound–based plaque segmentation, to generate a patient-specific 3-dimensional digital twin. This digital twin serves as the foundation for multistep computational simulations that replicate procedural steps and predict post-stenting lumen dimensions, which are subsequently validated against post-stenting high-definition intravascular ultrasound lumen measurements. Abbreviation as in Figure 4.

Our study is innovative in presenting an end-to-end pathway that translates high-resolution intracoronary imaging into a tool for mapping plaque components and stiffness and predicting lumen expansion after stenting (Central Illustration). The predictive computational framework reproduced dynamic changes in vessel morphology, capturing lumen expansion and strut behavior, which may be particularly valuable in complex or calcified lesions, where the optimal lesion preparation and final stent expansion can be difficult to assess from imaging alone.2,9

From an engineering standpoint, our computational framework can: 1) evaluate new stent platforms under realistic plaque conditions, informing device design and optimization; and 2) serve as a benchmark for simplified or machine learning–based surrogates that enable faster simulations but require validation on larger cohorts.13 The integration of HD IVUS with finite-element modeling also lays the groundwork for inverse modeling. Accurate post-stenting reconstructions could enable estimation of patient-specific arterial and plaque mechanical properties through inverse finite-element techniques, supporting “vascular profiling” that may improve risk stratification and detection of vulnerable plaques beyond conventional imaging.14

Limitations

The study has several limitations. First, the sample size of 9 patients supports a proof-of-concept, and larger, more diverse data sets are needed to generalize the findings. Second, small regions of normal vessel wall, mostly on the contralateral side of eccentric plaques, were classified as fibrolipid. Given the relatively small volume of normal wall (average 14% of total plaque volume), we believe this simplification had minimal impact on simulation accuracy. Third, the workflow relied on semiautomatic HD IVUS segmentation and 3D reconstruction. Future work will focus on full automation of these steps to improve scalability and facilitate clinical adoption.15 Additional investigations may extend this framework to higher-resolution intravascular modalities, such as DeepOCT, IVUS/Near-Infrared Spectroscopy, or combined OCT/IVUS,5,16 to determine whether enhanced plaque characterization further improves predictive accuracy.17 Moreover, integration of plaque morphology with 3D modeling may enable prospective evaluation of lesion-specific treatment strategies, including stent implantation vs drug-coated balloon angioplasty, with subsequent assessment of imaging and clinical outcomes.18 Finally, larger cohorts will be required to determine whether IVUS-based simulations can predict lesion-specific procedural risks, such as edge dissection or vessel injury, relative to achieved stent expansion.

Conclusions

In this work, we presented and validated a patient-specific computational simulation framework that uses high-resolution intracoronary imaging of plaque components to accurately predict post-stenting lumen dimensions. This framework has the potential to evolve into a predictive tool for lesion preparation and personalized PCI planning, with the goal of improving patient-specific clinical outcomes.

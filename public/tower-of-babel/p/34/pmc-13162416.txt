Nomogram for predicting 30-day mortality in patients with infective endocarditis: a retrospective study
Authors: Zhenzhen Cai, TingTing Song, Shengfeng Qiu, Ning Dong, Ting Wang, Jun Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prognosis for patients with infective endocarditis (IE) remains suboptimal. This study aimed to identify risk factors for 30-day mortality after hospital admission in IE patients and to construct a prognostic prediction model.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study of 391 patients diagnosed with IE between January 2017 and September 2024. Enrolled patients were randomly allocated to a training cohort and a validation cohort in a 7:3 ratio. Within the training cohort, patients were stratified into survival and non-survival groups based on 30-day outcomes. Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and logistic regression were employed for feature selection and prediction model construction. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsSurgical, lower lymphocyte count (LY), prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), higher lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), higher creatine kinase (CK), and higher UREA were identified as independent risk factors. The nomogram model demonstrated an AUC of 0.889 in the training cohort and 0.783 in the validation cohort. Calibration curves showed excellent alignment between predicted and actual outcomes, and DCA confirmed the model’s positive net clinical benefit across reasonable thresholds.ConclusionsSurgical, LY, APTT, LDH, CK, and UREA are significant predictors of prognosis in IE patients. The risk prediction model constructed from these factors shows high clinical utility for assessing the 30-day mortality risk in hospitalized IE patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-026-03445-w.Keywords: Infective endocarditis, LASSO regression, Nomogram, 30-day mortality

Background

Infective endocarditis (IE) is an inflammatory condition of the endocardial surface of the heart, typically involving the valves, ventricular walls, or adjacent great arteries, caused by bloodstream infection with bacteria, fungi, or other pathogens such as viruses or rickettsiae. According to the 2023 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines on IE management, the post-treatment survival rate is 85%–90% at one year and 70%–80% at five years. Primary causes of mortality include heart failure from valvular dysfunction, uncontrolled infection, and embolism to vital organs [1]. IE may present acutely with sudden onset and rapid clinical deterioration, or as a subacute or chronic disease accompanied by low-grade or even absent fever, fever is the most common symptom and finding in IE patients [2]. Its diverse and non-specific clinical manifestations often render diagnosis challenging. The 2023 Duke-International Society for Cardiovascular Infectious Diseases (ISCVID) diagnostic criteria incorporate susceptibility factors, clinical features, blood culture results, pathological findings, laboratory markers, imaging, and surgical observations. These updated criteria also acknowledge novel pathogen detection methods—such as metagenomic sequencing—and advanced imaging techniques like cardiac CT. Despite these diagnostic advances, the implementation of new testing modalities in primary care settings remains limited, and overall clinical outcomes for IE patients have not substantially improved in recent decades. Consequently, the early identification of high-risk patients is a critical component of IE management. Such identification facilitates rapid clinical risk stratification, optimizes diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, enables rational allocation of medical resources, and supports individualized treatment to enhance patient quality of life.

As a systemic disease, IE affects not only the heart but also multiple organ systems. Therefore, accurately assessing a patient’s clinical status may require the integration of diverse indicators. Predictive models can analyze extensive patient data—including clinical characteristics, laboratory results, and treatment responses—and can flexibly account for nonlinear relationships and complex interactions to forecast prognosis and provide personalized risk assessments. This study employed LASSO-logistic regression analysis to identify risk factors for IE and developed a nomogram prediction model for 30-day mortality risk in IE patients. The model aims to support prognosis evaluation and risk stratification in clinical practice.

Methods

Patient selection

In this retrospective study, we collected data from IE patients who were hospitalized at the First Affiliated Hospital With Nanjing Medical University between January 2017 and September 2024. Inclusion criteria: (1) All patients met the modified Duke diagnostic criteria [3]; (2) No antibiotic treatment or surgical treatment was received before diagnosis; (3) Age was 18 years old or above; (4) The patient’s clinical data is complete. Exclusion criteria: (1) Combined with cerebral infarction or cerebral hemorrhage; (2) Combined with local infections of other tissues or organs or systemic infectious diseases; (3) There was cognitive dysfunction or coma at admission; (4) Female patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding; (5) Lack of complete clinical data or unclear prognosis. The retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (Approval number: 2023-SR-982). No informed consent was obtained as this was a retrospective study and the participants are anonymised in the study.

Data collection

Patients also underwent routine blood testing in a fasted state in the first 24 h after admission, these data were retrospectively collected through the electronic medical record system, including (1) Demographic characteristics of the patients: gender, age, and past medical history including hypertension, diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease. (2) Clinical features: surgery, cerebral hemorrhage, atrial fibrillation, cerebral infarction, NYHA class III-IV, moderate or severe valvular lesions, rupture of chordae tendinca. (3) Laboratory indicators: procalcitonin (PCT), c-reactive protein (CRP), leukocyte (WBC), lymphocytes (LY), monocytes (Mo), neutrophils (NE), hemoglobin (HGB), hematocrit (HCT), red cell distribution width (RDW), platelets (PLT), prothrombin time (PT), international normalized ratio (PT-INR), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), fibrinogen (FIB), thrombin time (TT), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), γ-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase (CK), hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (HBDH), total bilirubin (TBIL), direct bilirubin (DBIL), indirect bilirubin (IBIL), cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high density lipoprotein (HDL), low density lipoprotein (LDL), lipoprotein a (LPa), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), globulin (GLB), glucose (GLU), urea nitrogen (UREA), creatinine (CREA), uric acid (UA), calcium (Ca), phosphoru (Phos).

Follow up

Patients were followed up for 30 days and divided into the survival and non-survival groups according to their clinical outcomes.

Statistical analysis

The statistics were conducted utilizing SPSS version 26.0 statistical software. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used for assessing normality. Quantitative variables are expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) / mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were assessed by the Mann-Whitney U test or the Student’s t-test. The Chi-square test was employed to analyze categorical variables. Utilizing the parameters identified through LASSO regression, a predictive nomogram was constructed through the use of multivariate logistic regression. The model’s discriminative performance was estimated through the area under the Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), sensitivity and specificity. Calibration curve and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to evaluate the calibration degree and clinical benefits of the nomogram model. The criterion for statistical significance was established at a threshold of 0.05.

Results

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics

A total of 391 IE patients were enrolled in this retrospective study. The study design is illustrated in Fig. 1. Participants were randomly allocated to a training cohort and a validation cohort in a 7:3 ratio (n = 274 vs. n = 117). No statistically significant differences in baseline clinical data were observed between the two cohorts (P > 0.05). Within the training cohort, patients were subsequently divided into two groups based on 30-day survival status. Univariate analysis of these groups identified several factors significantly associated (P < 0.05) with clinical outcomes in IE patients. These included surgical intervention, comorbidities (kidney disease, liver disease), and a range of laboratory parameters: PCT, CRP, LY, NE, LY%, MO%, PLT, PT, PT-INR, APTT, FIB, TT, D-dimer, AST, LDH, CK, HBDH, TBIL, DBIL, IBIL, TG, HDL, LPa, GLU, UREA, Crea. Risk factor screening for IE patients based on Lasso regression

The optimal cut-off values for laboratory variables with p < 0.1 (Table 1) were calculated using the maximum Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1). These values were then used to convert the continuous variables into binary categorical variables, as shown in Supplementary Table 1. These laboratory variables were incorporated into the LASSO regression analysis for risk factor screening. LASSO regression analysis screened out a total of 6 laboratory indicators (LDH, UREA, neutrophil percentage (NE%), CK, APTT, and lymphocyte count (LY)) (Fig. 2). Since surgery has been confirmed as a factor affecting patient prognosis, we have included it in the multivariate analysis. Multivariate analysis further demonstrated that surgery: hazard ratio (HR) = 0.377, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.154–0.922, p = 0.033; LY: HR = 0.189, 95% CI 0.076–0.474, p < 0.001; APTT: HR = 3.910, 95% CI 1.535–9.963, p = 0.004; LDH: HR = 6.830, 95% CI 2.558–18.235, p < 0.001; CK: HR = 3.238, 95% CI 1.323–7.929, p = 0.010; UREA: HR = 4.731, 95% CI 1.903–11.765, p = 0.001 were independently associated with the 30-day mortality of IE (Table 2).

Construction and validation of the nomogram model

A nomogram model of the clinical outcome of IE patients 30 days after admission was constructed based on the independent risk factors screened out by multivariate LASSO-Logistic regression analysis. The risks of each factor to the clinical outcomes of inpatients with IE are reflected in the form of scores, and the total score can be obtained by adding up the scores of each factor. The predicted probability value of clinical outcome risk for IE patients can be obtained based on the total score. The higher the total score, the higher the risk of adverse events in the clinical outcome of IE patients. The model demonstrated excellent performance with an AUC of 0.889 in the training cohort and an AUC of 0.783 in the validation cohort. Both in the training cohort and validation cohort, calibration curves revealed that the predictive probabilities of the model were in excellent alignment with the actual results. The decision curve analysis (DCA) suggested that the model had a good threshold probability and clinical net benefit, see in Fig. 3. When evaluated on the training set, the model exhibited the highest performance among all predictors, with a good sensitivity of 78.1% and specificity of 90.6%. The DeLong test indicated that the model’s AUC was significantly different from those of the single variables (Table 3).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, LASSO regression and multivariate logistic regression were employed to systematically identify risk factors associated with 30-day mortality in IE patients. The analysis identified surgery, LY, APTT, LDH, CK and UREA as independent risk factors. Furthermore, the developed nomogram demonstrated promising predictive capability, excellent calibration, and significant clinical utility in both the training and validation cohorts, the nomogram may assist in clinical risk stratification.

Current evidence suggests that relying solely on prolonged antibiotic therapy may delay definitive treatment in IE patients [4, 5]. Early surgical intervention can significantly reduce complication rates and early mortality [6]. While research indicates that early surgery offers greater benefit than conservative management, the optimal timing remains controversia [7–9]. This benefit is likely attributable to surgery’s role in completely eradicating the infectious focus, repairing damaged valves to preserve cardiac function, and correcting structural abnormalities. Our findings corroborate this, indicating a better short-term prognosis for IE patients who underwent surgery.

Lymphocytes are key indicators of immune function. A decreased lymphocyte count is closely linked to the body’s stress response, potentially due to inflammation-induced mechanisms such as impaired lymphocyte division, redistribution, and accelerated apoptosis, which are modulated by inflammatory cytokines [10]. Previous studies have shown that intense inflammatory responses elevate cortisol and catecholamine levels, potentially causing lymphocyte marginalization and sequestration within lymphoid organs [11]. Consequently, lymphopenia is significantly correlated with disease severity and is associated with adverse prognoses across various infectious diseases.

A prolonged APTT may indicate an underlying coagulopathy, which is itself associated with reduced survival. In patients with prosthetic valves or atrial fibrillation, anticoagulant therapy directly influences APTT levels [12]. Both prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) and arrhythmias are linked to more severe IE outcomes and an increased risk of embolism. Compared to more complex coagulation assays, APTT provides clinically relevant information for IE management and is readily accessible in most settings.

LDH is a ubiquitous cytoplasmic enzyme. Elevated levels are observed in conditions including myocardial infarction, rhabdomyolysis, myocarditis, malignancies, and renal disease. Infection can increase LDH, establishing it as a common inflammatory biomarker [13]. CK is an enzyme abundant in skeletal muscle, brain tissue, and cardiac myocytes. Both CK and LDH are released following myocardial injury and are standard markers for assessing the extent of cardiac damage [14]. These biochemical indicators are routinely available in clinical practice.

UREA is a metabolic end-product of the hepatic urea cycle. Elevated UREA in IE patients may result from reduced renal clearance due to cardiorenal syndrome, combined with increased production from microbial metabolism [15]. Studies have also identified UREA as an independent predictor of short-term mortality in IE [16].

This study comprehensively evaluated factors related to 30-day mortality in hospitalized IE patients, including baseline characteristics (age, gender), comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, renal failure), IE-related complications (e.g., heart failure, stroke), and laboratory markers (e.g., white blood cell count, C-reactive protein). It identified surgery, LY, APTT, LDH, CK, and UREA as independent risk factors and constructed a prognostic prediction model based on these variables. This model may assist clinicians in rapid risk stratification, optimizing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, allocating medical resources efficiently, and ultimately improving patient outcomes.

This study also has some limitations. First, the sample size is relatively small, which may limit statistical power. Second, while our model showed superiority over individual predictors, its performance relative to the STS-IE score could not be assessed. This limitation stemmed from substantial missing retrospective data, particularly for STS-IE components like causative pathogens, paravalvular abscess, and multi-valve involvement. A prospective comparison is therefore needed to clarify the model’s clinical utility. Finally, as a single-center retrospective study, the strict inclusion/exclusion criteria, lack of clarity regarding timing and standardization of laboratory measurements, and potential confounding in surgical treatment may affect the generalizability and interpretation of the findings.

Conclusions

This study developed and validated a clinical nomogram for predicting 30-day mortality in hospitalized IE patients. The nomogram incorporates six readily available variables: surgery, LY, APTT, LDH, CK and UREA. It demonstrated good predictive performance, offering a practical tool to assist clinicians in enhancing patient management and formulating targeted interventions, thereby helping to mitigate the risk of short-term adverse outcomes in IE patients.

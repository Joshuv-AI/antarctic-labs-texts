Implementation of a Tiered Cardiac Telemetry System: An Operational Blueprint at Mayo Clinic
Authors: Levi W Disrud, Tara A Gosse, Zach D Linn, Anthony H Kashou, Peter A Noseworthy, Angela Fink, Dawn Griffin, Blade Faust
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the operational outcomes and implementation effects of tiered cardiac telemetry monitoring in a hospital environment using an innovative technology.Patients and MethodsThe research focuses on assessing the precision, speed, and reliability of alerts generated by a wireless device in adult patients aged 18 and above, concurrently monitored by a hardwired, continuous cardiac monitor. Using an agile methodology, we tested and validated a nonhardwired, cellular-connected continuous cardiac monitor (InfoBionic MoMe) in 162 patients. A comparison was made between the wireless device and the standard hardwired system, conducted at Mayo Clinic Hospital with Institutional Review Board approval from June 6, 2022, to December 15, 2022.ResultsThe study revealed a high correlation of events captured compared with the standard care model. Differences in algorithms, alarm parameters, and operational considerations impacting clinical implementation were observed. Connectivity improvements during the study reduced latency from 3-5 minutes to 30 seconds. Delayed alarms were attributed to device damage (4.5% of cases) and poor cellular connections (29% within 31-60 seconds).ConclusionThe implementation of tiered cardiac telemetry in hospital environments, coupled with advancements in remote cardiac monitoring, supports expanded bedside telemetry capabilities and near real-time remote monitoring postdischarge. Although the study successfully validated the wireless device concept, improvements are needed before implementation for inpatient cardiac monitoring. Further research and technological enhancements can build on these findings to enhance health care practices in this domain.

The coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic has driven an increase in hospital census and patient acuity, requiring a different approach to bed management and utilization. Within the hospital practice, patients with any type of monitoring needs have historically been placed in an intensive care unit or progressive care unit bed. As we work to increase efficiency and take steps toward acuity flexibility, there is a need to have “plug-and-play” monitoring capabilities available to every general care bed. Traditional telemetry systems, although offering real-time insights within hospitals, struggle with scalability owing to resource limitations, technology costs, and data processing costs.

To address these challenges, the goal is to enhance cardiac monitoring by introducing a tiered telemetry system, making inpatient monitoring more adaptable. This system classifies patients by risk, optimizing resource allocation and reducing unnecessary transfers between hospital areas. Our aim is to prioritize bringing the monitor to the patient rather than vice versa, using cutting-edge technologies for seamless data collection.

In tier 1, patients receive continuous real-time monitoring supervised by a technician, ensuring immediate detection of cardiac activity changes. Tier 2 introduces ambulatory multilead continuous monitoring, enabling patient mobility within the hospital. Tier 3 extends mobility further with ambulatory single lead continuous monitoring, allowing patients to leave the hospital if needed. This tiered approach emphasizes personalized care, minimizes hospital transfers, and improves patient comfort while efficiently managing monitoring resources.

The study focused on establishing a tier 2 monitoring strategy using a wireless cardiac monitoring device, providing continuous recordings, full disclosure review, and event-based alerts. The objective was to create a blueprint for implementing a tiered cardiac telemetry system that improves efficiency and consistency in monitoring cardiac health.

Patients and Methods

Our first step in operationalizing tiered telemetry was to validate and authenticate novel technology. An agile methodology was adopted, commencing with the development of a minimum viable product followed by a validation phase. Agile represents an iterative approach and incremental delivery of solutions, emphasizing swift value delivery to patients. It also encourages continuous reflection and adaptation for improvement and ensuring a prompt response to changes. The validation process involved simultaneous monitoring of patients using both a standard-of-care system and wireless technology (InfoBionic MoMe [MoMe]) to evaluate service quality, technology performance, and overall system functionality. This approach successfully confirmed the effectiveness of the wireless device for inpatient cardiac monitoring, providing substantial support for the implementation of tiered cardiac telemetry monitoring using innovative technologies. Other cardiac monitoring devices were considered for comparing performance. However, these devices typically use only a single electrocardiogram lead for identifying arrhythmias. In contrast, the MoMe device employed in our study supports up to 6 electrocardiogram leads, providing multiple axial reference points. This capability enhanced the accuracy of rhythm interpretation, offering a more comprehensive assessment compared with single lead devices.

In 2022, an ongoing prospective pilot study was initiated at Mayo Clinic Hospital in Rochester, Minnesota, Saint Mary’s Campus, which has approximately 1265 licensed beds and 64 operating rooms. Adult participants were recruited and enrolled to assess the feasibility and capabilities of the wireless telemetry monitoring device. All participants gave informed consent adhering to both Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines, and documents were approved and reviewed by Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board.

Workflow Architecture

Consenting cardiac care unit patients were connected to the 3-lead wireless device alongside standard monitoring. The target group comprised those with acute cardiac conditions needing continuous monitoring for potential arrhythmias. Oversight was by Cardiology Certified Rhythm Analysis Technicians. Alarm Parameterization

In health care, alarm parameterization involves automated alarm event generation, categorizing abnormalities like abnormal heart rates, rhythm irregularities, and life-threatening conditions. Inconsistent clinical alarms, as noted by Assis et al,1 can compromise response time. Synchronizing alarm parameters across devices addressed this issue. A retrospective review assessed the efficiency of alarm management by evaluating device-generated alarm notifications. The accuracy and appropriateness of triggering alarm parameters were scrutinized, and if inaccuracies emerged, adjustments were made. This fine-tuning ensured alarms were triggered only during clinically relevant events, enhancing the system’s reliability and reducing false alarms.

Results

The study assessed wireless device alerts’ precision, speed, and reliability in adult patients monitored alongside the standard inpatient telemetry system. Of 162 patients, 41% (67 individuals) experienced alarms from the standard system, averaging 1.8 alarms per patient. The wireless device had an average delay of 27 seconds, ranging from 2 seconds to 3 minutes and 40 seconds, with 22% (27 alarms) not captured. Despite challenges, it effectively detected various arrhythmias, achieving a 100% capture rate for high-rate and low-rate rhythms, pauses, non sustained ventricular tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, and atrial fibrillation high heart rate. Notably, supraventricular tachycardia had an 80% capture rate, with 1 instance misclassified as non sustained ventricular tachycardia. Algorithmic limitations were found in capturing QTc rhythms, bigeminy/trigeminy, missed beats, pacemaker issues, and accelerated idioventricular rhythm. Although high capture rates indicate real-time monitoring effectiveness, future algorithmic improvements could enhance clinical utility.

Notification Timing/Connectivity

To address concerns about notification timing and connectivity, collaborative efforts with the device vendor were made to improve the wireless device’s inpatient utility. Algorithmic and device setting adjustments reduced latency from 3 to 5 minutes to a more responsive 30 seconds. However, 4.5% of alarms exceeding 60 seconds were due to device damage, highlighting the need for robust maintenance protocols. Additionally, 29% of alarms within 31-60 seconds resulted from inadequate cellular connection, impacted by the hospital’s infrastructure. Despite a cell booster installation, persistent connectivity issues underscore the need for more comprehensive solutions. Discussion

As the demand for cardiac monitoring rises, there is a need to establish tailored monitoring approaches capable of effectively managing patients with a range of cardiac monitoring intensity requirements. This need will expand as the trend toward home-based care for high-acuity conditions gains traction. This study investigated the outcomes and implementation effects of a tiered cardiac telemetry monitoring system using innovative technologies within a hospital environment. The insights from this study contribute to understanding the effectiveness of these methods in improving patient care and optimizing resource utilization in the health care setting.

Effectiveness of Tiered Cardiac Telemetry Monitoring

The introduction of a tiered cardiac telemetry monitoring system transforms hospital health care, focusing on patient needs. Categorizing patients into risk tiers customizes monitoring, minimizing unnecessary movements, and delivering resources efficiently. Aligned with Warren et al,2 prioritizing patient safety during intrahospital transport is crucial. Patient movements impact costs and satisfaction, as noted by Mortensen et al.3 Thus, a strategic approach to intrahospital movements is vital for improving health care efficiency and satisfaction.

Advantages of Tiered Monitoring

The tiered telemetry monitoring system provides several advantages:

•It enables health care providers to assign monitoring resources more efficiently, making certain that higher-risk patients receive the necessary monitoring while still attending to the needs of lower-risk patients.

Integration of Remote Patient Monitoring

The dynamic landscape of health care, shaped by innovations like remote patient monitoring (RPM), underscores the importance of continuous patient care beyond hospital confines. Telemetry monitoring, integral to RPM, offers ongoing surveillance and assistance as patients transition to home-based care. This seamless integration highlights the adaptability of tiered telemetry monitoring throughout various stages of patient treatment, especially during the transition to home settings.

Effective RPM programs require a proficient clinical care team capable of monitoring data and responding to alerts.4 Governance and staffing models for RPM programs encompass centralized and hybrid approaches. In the centralized model, a dedicated team of RPM registered nurses handles initial data trend triage and forwards validated care escalation to primary or specialty care providers. Conversely, the hybrid model involves a centralized team of RPM registered nurses performing similar functions but transferring validated care escalation for clinical oversight.4

Considering the future framework and scalability of tiered telemetry, the choice between centralized and decentralized alarm notifications is important. A centralized model, with a specialized remote monitoring team overseeing rhythm interpretation, supports scalability.4 In contrast, a decentralized model distributes responsibility for rhythm interpretation among a multidisciplinary team, emphasizing the need for skilled clinical care teams to enhance the effectiveness of RPM and improve patient outcomes in home-based care.

Limitations

Although the study yielded promising results, it acknowledges certain limitations, notably the reliance on cellular connectivity for the wireless device, which posed challenges within the hospital’s infrastructure. Overcoming these limitations necessitates ongoing collaboration with technology vendors and infrastructure enhancements. Technological Enhancements

This study underscores the critical role of alarm parameterization in cardiac monitoring for prompt intervention and patient safety. Aligning the wireless device’s alarm criteria with the standard system was vital for accurate rhythm abnormality detection. Technological enhancements improved alarm notification speed and reliability. Analysis identified inadequate alarm settings, resulting in missed alarms, notably for low-rate events. Adjustments were made to detect bradycardia within 45 to 50 beats per minute. Although modifications improved issues, algorithm detection limitations necessitate further research for integrated effectiveness.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this research underscores the impact of tiered cardiac telemetry monitoring could have in the accessibility of cardiac monitoring. We also highlight some limitations, including challenges in cellular connectivity, which will require further technological refinement and infrastructure improvements. Future research should prioritize refining device parameters, evaluating long-term impacts, and addressing key considerations such as device connectivity and infrastructure enhancements to optimize acuity-based cardiac monitoring in hospital settings.

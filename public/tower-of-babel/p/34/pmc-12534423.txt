Computational evaluation using machine learning for analysis of membrane desalination process powered by solar energy
Authors: Muteb Alanazi, Tareq Nafea Alharby
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNumerical simulation of mass transfer in membrane contactors for water purification was carried out in this study. The models were developed to estimate permeate volume, a critical performance variable in membrane desalination. We analyzed and compared the accuracy of models: Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and a Stacking ensemble method combining MLP and XGBoost. Data preprocessing techniques were applied to improve model performance, including outlier detection using Z-scores and log transformation for normalization. A distinctive contribution of this work is the innovative application of the Jellyfish Optimizer (JO) for hyperparameter tuning, which enhanced model robustness and set it apart from conventional optimization approaches. Unlike conventional optimization algorithms, JO’s biologically inspired dynamics offers a novel balance between exploration and exploitation, leading to superior tuning of model hyperparameters. The models’ predictive accuracy was evaluated using R², RMSE, and MAE with an 85%–15% train–test split. The Stacking model outperformed single models, achieving R² values of 0.99060 (training), 0.97627 (cross-validation), and 0.99043 (testing), along with the lowest test errors (RMSE = 6.17921), confirming its efficiency in predicting permeate volume. The results show that stacking significantly improves prediction accuracy in complex tasks like membrane desalination.Keywords: Model, Machine learning, Separation, Removal, MembraneSubject terms: Biomedical engineering, Chemical engineering

Introduction

Integration of membrane water treatment into solar energy has been recognized as a new technology in water purification which can be applied for treatment of water in rural areas1. Porous membranes are preferred for application with solar energy due to lower energy consumption compared to nonporous membranes. Electrodialysis (ED)2 and membrane distillation (MD)3 are two important membrane processes which can be used in combination with solar energy. ED is an electrical driven membrane system, whereas MD is a temperature driven system for separation of water impurities. In MD for water desalination, thermal energy constitutes the primary energy requirement.

Recently, various types of MD have been developed for water treatment such as direct-contact membrane distillation (DCMD), vacuum membrane distillation (VMD), and air-gap membrane distillation (AGMD)4–7 with their inherent characteristics. For analysis of MD processes, computational methods have been developed such as computational fluid dynamics (CFD) for finding temperature and concentration distribution in the process. CFD is a powerful tool which can be used for process troubleshooting, design, and optimization8. Using this method, governing equations of the process are solved via an appropriate numerical scheme, and the desired variables are obtained in the process. Due to the complexity of CFD, models based on machine learning (ML) have been recently applied for simulation of MD process to analyze mass transfer. These models need some data for training which has been provided by a CFD simulation, so-called hybrid modeling strategy9,10. This strategy is more efficient compared to CFD modeling of membranes in terms of solution time and fitting accuracy which make them suitable for process control as well.

Therefore, there is a need for further development of ML algorithms in prediction of MD processes powered by solar energy applicable in water purification to assess its mass transfer performance. This research gap is addressed in this study by utilization of some machine learning models to predict permeate volume in solar-based MD process. Pressure and time were selected as input variables, while permeate volume was predicted based on these parameters.

Machine learning offers advantages over conventional computational tools by enabling systems to learn from data through algorithms that iteratively improve model accuracy. Some models have been recently developed for energy and environmental applications based on machine learning11–13. This field aims to develop algorithms capable of autonomously analyzing experimental data and improving iteratively through model training. Regression analysis plays a crucial role in predicting continuous numerical outcomes from input data, making it a cornerstone of predictive modeling14. Unlike CFD-based models, which are grounded in mechanistic principles for simulating processes, ML techniques are data-driven and lack an explicit mechanistic foundation. However, they offer substantial advantages for process optimization, requiring significantly lower computational cost.

In this study, we employed three ML techniques, i.e., Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and a Stacking ensemble method combining MLP and XGBoost to predict permeate volume in membrane desalination processes integrated into solar energy. With its deep learning architecture allowing flexibility in capturing intricate patterns within the data, MLP was chosen since it can replicate complicated, non-linear relationships between input features. XGBoost, on the other hand, was chosen for its high scalability and performance in supervised learning tasks, where its sequential tree-building approach enables the model to minimize residual errors iteratively, making it highly effective in regression tasks. The decision to employ Stacking was driven by its potential to improve predictive accuracy by combining the strengths of both MLP and XGBoost. Combining these models and a rigorous hyperparameter tuning process using the Jellyfish Optimizer (JO) was selected to ensure optimal model performance in predicting permeate volume, a highly dynamic and complex process. The novelty of this work lies in employing the JO for hyperparameter tuning during model training. Unlike real-time adjustment approaches, our use of JO focuses on systematically navigating the search space to identify optimal parameters before deployment, ensuring enhanced stability and predictive robustness of the trained models. In addition, we emphasize the advantage of the stacking ensemble strategy: while the MLP effectively captures complex nonlinear relationships, XGBoost excels at identifying dominant structured trends. Their combination through stacking integrates these complementary strengths, enabling more accurate and generalizable predictions than either model alone.

This paper’s contributions include exploring the integration of ML models—specifically MLP, XGBoost, and a Stacking ensemble approach—to predict permeate volume, demonstrating the effectiveness of combining deep learning with gradient boosting techniques for enhanced prediction accuracy in membrane process. A key novel aspect of this work is the application of the JO for hyperparameter tuning, which is inspired by the unique movement patterns of jellyfish in ocean currents. This innovative optimization technique improves model performance by efficiently navigating the search space for optimal hyperparameters, further enhancing the predictive capabilities of the models. Additionally, the research investigates the impact of data preprocessing, including outlier detection and log transformation, in refining model inputs to boost the overall accuracy of predictions. The study not only establishes the superiority of the Stacking ensemble model in comparison to individual models but also demonstrates the potential of ML for complex, practical applications in desalination processes. This approach sets a new benchmark for integrating multiple ML methods and optimization techniques in environmental and industrial prediction tasks.

Materials and methods

Membrane data

The process under investigation in this work is a membrane desalination unit which operates using solar energy to produce potable water. Mass transfer across the membrane is a critical parameter, quantified by measuring the permeate volume collected on the permeate side of the membrane unit. This study employs a publicly accessible dataset consisting of 45 observations. This dataset was extracted from15 which reported modeling and experimental works on measuring permeate volume in solar-based membrane desalination process. Each observation contains two predictors—Time (min) and Pressure (kgf/cm²)—as well as a response variable, Permeate Volume (mL). The inputs have been selected from the analysis of process and evaluation of their effects and importance on the permeate volume of purified water.

Outlier detection

Z-score approach was used for detecting outliers to enhance the models’ accuracy and robustness. It aids in discovering hidden patterns by pinpointing observations that deviate considerably from the rest16. The Z-score method is one of the possible techniques for detecting outliers and applied in this study for the dataset prior to ML modeling.

The Z-score measures the divergence of a data point from the mean in standard deviation units. Data points with Z-scores exceeding 2.5 or 3 are typically regarded as outliers18. This method is widely used for identifying outliers in a dataset. First, we determine the Z-score for all of the data points, which, in terms of standard deviations, indicates how far that point is from the mean. Then, we set a threshold, which is 3 standard deviations in this study. Any data point with a Z-score beyond this threshold is classified as an outlier. This approach offers a definitive and quantitative means to identify anomalous data points, facilitating their removal and enhancing the accuracy and generalizability of models.

Extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost)

XGBoost is a modern algorithm, extensively employed for supervised learning tasks. Its key advantages include high scalability, rapid processing, and outstanding performance, making it a preferred option in both ML competitions and real-world applications. The algorithm builds an ensemble of decision trees in sequence, where each tree corrects the errors of the one before it, leading to a more powerful predictive model27,28. The core idea behind XGBoost is to optimize a differentiable loss function by iteratively adding trees that minimize the residual errors. Stacking ensemble method

Stacking is a technique in ensemble learning that merges several base models to form a superior meta-model. Using the unique strengths of various models enhances overall prediction accuracy. In this research, we applied a stacking approach with XGBoost and MLP as the foundational models29,30.

The stacking method involves training multiple unique base models utilizing the identical dataset. A meta-model is subsequently developed to produce the final output by employing the predictions from the base models. This strategy enables the meta-model to determine the optimal way to integrate the base models’ outputs, which can uncover intricate patterns and enhance overall performance31. The stacking ensemble method can be summarized in the following steps:

1. Base Model Development: Train the base models (XGBoost and MLP in this case) on the training dataset. Each base model independently learns to generate predictions.

2. Prediction Generation: The trained base models produce predictions on the validation set, which subsequently serve as input features for the meta-model.

3. Meta-Model Development: The meta-model is trained utilizing the outputs of the base models as input features, while the actual target values serve as the output.

4. Final Prediction: Initially, the trained base models produce predictions on the test dataset, which are subsequently integrated by the meta-model to yield the final output.

Results and discussion

The models’ predictive performance was assessed using an 85%−15% train-test split. The training set, consisting of 85% of the dataset, was utilized to train the MLP, XGBoost, and Stacking models. Performance metrics, including MAE, RMSE, and R² score, were determined for both the training and test sets to compare model accuracy. Additionally, cross-validation was performed to ensure robustness and mitigate overfitting. The results highlight the predictive capabilities of each model and demonstrate the efficacy of ensemble learning in improving prediction accuracy. Although the dataset comprises only 45 observations, its adequacy is justified by the low-dimensional nature of the input space (two predictors: Time and Pressure) and the high consistency of model performance across cross-validation, training, and test sets. The close alignment of R² scores and error metrics across these splits (e.g., stacking model R²: 0.99060 training, 0.97627 cross-validation, 0.99043 test) indicates that the models generalize well without overfitting. Nevertheless, the small dataset size remains a limitation, and future work should validate the models on larger and more diverse datasets to confirm their robustness in broader contexts.

Table 1 presents the assessment metrics of the final models—MLP, XGBoost, and the Stacking model—based on training, cross-validation, and test data. The results demonstrate that the stacking model consistently outperforms both MLP and XGBoost across all evaluation criteria. Specifically, the stacking model achieves the highest R2 (0.99060 on training, 0.97627 in cross-validation, and 0.99043 on test data), demonstrating superior predictive accuracy. Additionally, the stacking model reports the lowest MAE and RMSE values, indicating improved error minimization compared to the individual models. Given these findings, the stacking approach effectively integrates the strengths of MLP and XGBoost, making it the most appropriate model for accurate prediction.

Although the stacking model exhibits strong generalization with no signs of overfitting—as evidenced by the close alignment of R² scores across training (0.99060), cross-validation (0.97627), and testing (0.99043)—some prediction variability may arise from factors not captured in the model, such as membrane fouling dynamics, temperature variations, or feed water quality. While the residual plots show a well-balanced error distribution, the relatively small dataset (45 observations) may limit the model’s exposure to a wider range of operating conditions.

The superior performance of the Stacking model is related to the complementary strengths of its base learners—MLP and XGBoost. MLP, with its deep learning architecture, excels at capturing non-linear relationships and subtle patterns within the data, particularly when interactions between input features are complex. XGBoost, on the other hand, is highly effective at handling structured data and identifying dominant trends through its gradient-boosted decision trees. By combining these two models, the stacking ensemble leverages MLP’s capacity for function approximation and XGBoost’s robustness and interpretability. The meta-model in the stacking architecture learns to weigh their predictions in a way that mitigates individual weaknesses, leading to reduced bias and variance and ultimately improved generalization performance.

The findings of this study confirm that combining ensemble learning with bio-inspired optimization can deliver highly accurate predictions for complex desalination processes. The Stacking ensemble consistently outperformed individual models by integrating the complementary strengths of MLP and XGBoost, while the JO provided a distinctive contribution through its ability to efficiently tune hyperparameters during training. These results establish the novelty of this work in both methodological design and application. However, certain Limitations must be acknowledged. The dataset comprised only 45 observations, which restricts the diversity of operating conditions available for training. Although cross-validation, preprocessing, and ensemble modeling helped mitigate overfitting and demonstrated robust generalization, the small sample size remains a critical limitation in applying machine learning to real-world scenarios. Additionally, the study focused exclusively on pressure and time as input variables, while other important factors such as temperature fluctuations, fouling effects, and feedwater quality were not explicitly modeled. Addressing these constraints with larger datasets and additional features will be essential for further validating and extending the approach.

Conclusion

This study presented a machine learning framework for predicting permeate volume in solar-powered membrane desalination processes. Among the evaluated models, the Stacking ensemble combining MLP and XGBoost consistently outperformed individual models, achieving R² values above 0.99 and the lowest error metrics. A key novelty of this work is the integration of the Jellyfish Optimizer (JO) for hyperparameter tuning during model training, which significantly improved predictive robustness compared to conventional optimization methods. The ensemble approach further enhanced performance by utilizing the complementary strengths of MLP (capturing nonlinearities) and XGBoost (capturing structured trends), demonstrating the value of combining diverse models for complex process prediction.

Despite these advances, the study is constrained by the Limited dataset of only 45 observations. While the issue of overfitting was mitigated through preprocessing, cross-validation, and ensemble learning, the small sample size remains a critical limitation for machine learning applications. Future work should validate the approach with larger and more diverse datasets, explore additional ensemble strategies, and extend the application of JO-based tuning to other environmental and industrial prediction tasks.

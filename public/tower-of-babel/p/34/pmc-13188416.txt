The factors influencing parents and clinicians in medical decision making for fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia at a tertiary referral centre in Beijing: a five-year retrospective study
Authors: Chenxiao Hou, Shijing Song, Jingjing Wang, Qingqing Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe emerging demands for the most up to guide parents and clinicians in medical decision making for fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia has become an urgent issue. We aimed to analysis the proposal for prenatal assessment of the fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia at a tertiary referral centre in Beijing. The type and frequency of malformations associated with fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia and the factors associated with pregnancy outcome were evaluated.MethodsAll prenatal congenital diaphragmatic hernia cases detected by ultrasound between July 2017 and December 2022 at the Beijing Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital were included. The data were compared between the born group and unborn group. The characteristics of the anomalies associated with congenital diaphragmatic hernia cases and the observed-to-expected lung area to head circumference ratio in born group were analysed. The maternal age, gestational age at diagnosis, genetic testing, ultrasound information, and pregnancy outcome were statistically analysed using two independent-sample t-test and χ2 square test. Univariate analysis was performed on the characteristics of these two groups, followed by multivariate analysis .ResultsA total of 105 fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia were retrospectively reviewed. Overall mortality rates of isolated and non-isolated congenital diaphragmatic hernia cases in the born group were 34.4% and 30.0%, respectively. Antenatal diagnosis rates of congenital heart disease and increased nuchal translucency thicknesses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia were significantly different between the two groups (7% vs. 29%, p < 0.05; 2% vs. 23%, p < 0.05). In the multivariate analysis, the risk factors independently associated with pregnancy termination were gestational age at diagnosis (odds ratio [OR] 0.887, 95% CI 0.802–0.981, p < 0.05) and congenital heart disease (OR 0.158, 95% CI 0.030–0.841, p < 0.05).ConclusionsWe call for a more positive advice for parents of fetuses with isolated, non-critical and non-isolated congenital diaphragmatic hernia based on an increased survival rate. The likelihood of termination of pregnancy in congenital diaphragmatic hernia is increased by early detection and the combination of congenital heart disease.In the born group, critical congenital heart disease and increased nuchal translucency thicknesses seem to be associated with a higher risk of neonatal mortality.

Background

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) is a birth defect associated with high mortality. The incidence rate is approximately 1/2500–1/5000 live births [1]. Postnatal CDH is associated with varying degrees of developmental pulmonary hypoplasia and arterial hypertension which are the main causes of death, and CDH is also frequently associated with other congenital malformations [2]. The parents may be not fully understand the view that the survival of patients with only CDH is much better than that of fetuses with congenital malformations, leading to decision to terminate pregnancy [3]. However, after the 1980s, with further understanding of the pathology of CDH and progress in neonatal respiratory support technology, the death rate of patients with CDH decreased from 50% to approximately 20–30% [4]. Therefore, accurate prenatal assessment of fetuses with CDH and individualised prognostic assessment are particularly important for guiding decision-making as the postnatal condition has improved. There are many clear risk stratifications for high-risk isolated CDH, such as the observed-to-expected lung area to head circumference ratio (O/E LHR, typically < 25%) [5], liver herniation [6] and stomach herniation [7]. However, in prenatal assessments, associated anomalies are also important because the severity of CDH drives patient outcomes and alters the overall survival risk of patients with non-isolated CDH [8]. Cardiac abnormalities are reportedly the most prevalent major congenital anomalies associated with fetuses with CDH [9]. Recently, it has been reported that survival was excellent in patients who underwent repair for both congenital heart disease (CHD) and CDH [10]. In addition, the mortality of patients with non-isolated CDH was associated with maternal age. The risk ratio was highest for maternal age ≥ 40 years [11]. Moreover, a multicentre ultrasound screening study of 78,639 pregnancies reported that nearly 40% of fetuses with CDH had increased nuchal translucency (NT) at 10–14 weeks of gestation. Increased NT thickness may be a marker of intrathoracic compression-related pulmonary hypoplasia [12]. Spaggiari et al. reported that an increased NT thickness in fetuses with CDH was associated with poor outcomes [13]. Mortality rates increased when the first diagnosis was made in the first, second, or third trimester [14]. Advanced genetic testing is also needed at the same time as a standardized ultrasound assessment is performed [15]. As increasing amounts of data are generated in this field, periodic updates are required beyond isolated CDH cases. Considering that fetal disease severity and the presence of additional fetal abnormalities are influencing factors in parental decision-making, we assessed the frequency and types of associated malformations in a tertiary obstetric consultation centre as well as the relevant factors (maternal age, gestational age at diagnosis, genetic testing, increased nuchal translucency thickness). This study investigated the outcomes of fetuses with CDH diagnosed by ultrasound at the Beijing Obstetrics and Gynaecology Hospital Affiliated with Capital Medical University from 2017 to 2022. The results can enhance the confidence of parents and clinicians in the medical decision-making for fetuses with CDH and can be used to update proposal policies.

Methods

This was a single-centre retrospective cohort study performed at the Beijing Obstetrics and Gynaecology Hospital Affiliated with Capital Medical University between July 2017 and December 2022, a national and level 3 referral centre. Pregnant women in this study received routine first-trimester and mid-trimester fetal ultrasound scan at our hospital according to ISUOG Practice Guidelines [16, 17]. All CDH ultrasound examinations were performed by two experienced sonographers. Ultrasound imaging was performed with GE Voluson E10 ultrasound machine using a SC1-5D transducer with frequency range of 1.5 to 6 MHz (trans-abdominal image). When a prenatal diagnosis of CDH was detected, the pregnant women was immediately referred to prenatal diagnosis centre. First, they would be recommended to be offered aneuploidy screening or diagnostic testing. Meanwhile, antenatal counselling in CDH pregnancies about the prediction of pulmonary hypoplasia was provided. In left-sided CDH, an O/E LHR < 25% (between 22 and 32 weeks of gestational age)、liver herniation and right CDH predicted poor outcome. Besides, further antenatal counselling was offered to CDH pregnancies regarding surgery (including “readiness criteria,” patch repair, type of repair and surgery on extracorporeal life support) and long term surveillance and the management of gastroesophageal reflux at Children’s Hospital of Capital Institute of Pediatrics which was our cooperative hospital for postnatal CDH management.

Overall, the characteristics of each fetuses diagnosed with CDH by ultrasound were estimated according to the pregnancy outcome. The data were compared between the born and unborn groups (including stillborn, therapeutic abortion, and spontaneous abortion cases). Amniocentesis was performed for fetal karyotyping in all patients in the born group but not all patients in the unborn group, as the parents of CDH fetuses may refuse further examination while considering pregnancy termination. Maternal age, gestational age at diagnosis, genetic testing results, ultrasound data, and pregnancy outcomes were statistically analysed.

Statistical analysis

The data were analysed using SPSS 23.0. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (%) and compared using two independent-sample t-test and χ2 square test. Significant risk factors in the univariate analysis were selected for inclusion in the multivariate analysis using an inclusion threshold of p < 0.20. Multivariate analysis was performed to determine the independent associations between associated malformations and pregnancy outcomes in fetuses with CDH. Multiple logistic regression was performed, with p < 0.05 considered significant. The results are presented as ORs with 95% CIs.

Results

Outcomes related to termination of pregnancy

A total of 105 fetuses with CDH diagnosed by ultrasound between July 2017 and December 2022 were retrospectively reviewed in a tertiary obstetric consultation centre. Seven fetuses with CDH were excluded because of inadequate documentation. Of the 98 fetuses that were included, 42 (43%) were born and 56 (57%) were terminated during pregnancy. The baseline characteristics of these two groups with all analysed CDHs were shown in Table 1. The results of the univariate and multivariate analyses were shown in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. An earlier diagnosis of gestational age, increased NT thickness, and a higher percentage of CHD and musculoskeletal malformations were used in multivariate analysis, which demonstrated that gestational age at diagnosis (OR 0.887, 95% CI, 0.802–0.981; p < 0.05) and combined CHD associated with CDH (OR 0.158, 95% CI, 0.030–0.841; p < 0.05) remained significant risk factors.

Outcomes related to neonatal survival

There were 32 isolated CDH patients and 10 non-isolated CDH patients in the born group. The overall mortality rates of isolated and non-isolated CDH cases in the born group were 34.4% (11/32) and 30.0% (3/10), respectively (Fig. 1). In the born group, 9 infants died before surgical treatment, 33 were referred to the same tertiary children’s hospital for surgical treatment, and 28 survived until discharge (28/42). The characteristics of the anomalies associated with the 10 non-isolated CDH cases in the born group are shown in Table 3. In the unborn group, 15 isolated CDH cases terminated pregnancy. There were no significant differences in gestational age at diagnosis, maternal age, or the O/E LHR between isolated CDH cases in these two groups.(Table 4). The neonatal outcomes of the born group could be served as a source of hope for CDH parents.

Discussion

From 147,346 pregnant women who underwent prenatal ultrasound examination in our institution between July 2017 and December 2022, 105 fetuses with CDH diagnosed by ultrasound were found, yielding morbidity incidence 7/10,000. The morbidity incidence was higher than a reported prevalence rates 1.82 per 10,000 births related to epidemiology of CDH among 24 million Chinese births during the period from 2007 to 2019 (live births, stillbirths and terminations of greater than or equal to 28 weeks of gestational age) [18]. We analyzed that our institution had consulted with more abnormal pregnant women was one reason for this incidence discrepancy. The fetal prognosis of CDH patients is a delicate process that requires reliable information on disease development. Some centers utilize MRI for diagnosis and severity assessment, but this can also be determined by ultrasound with fairly good accuracy [19]. When CDH is identified prenatally, parental counselling should be conducted in a maternal and child referral centres with multidisciplinary expertise in these prenatal risk factors [20]. With advancements in perinatal diagnosis and treatment technology and the use of ECMO, the mortality rate of CDH patients has gradually decreased [21]. Increased levels of clinical management associated with CDH have led to an overall improvement in the reported outcomes of CDH [22]. However, studies based on the born populations have not considered the “hidden mortality rates”. The hidden mortality rate was defined as the number of non-live born CDH cases, including those who died early or newborns who did not undergo surgery at a tertiary children’s hospital [23]. In our study, the birth rate was only 42.9%. There were 15 isolated cases in the unborn group. Moreover, the survival rate of patients with isolated CDH was 65.6% in the born group. Considering the good outcome of isolated CDH, we emphasize the need for critical thinking when providing parental counselling about isolated CDH fetuses. Comprehensive prenatal counselling about CDH pregnancies should be done by prenatal diagnosis physician and postnatal management surgeon. At the same time, we should pay attention to the gap of survival rates for cases treated postnatally between our study and studies from high income countries [4, 5].

In this study, there was a significant difference in associated cardiac structural abnormalities between CDH fetuses in the unborn and born groups(28.6% vs. 7.1%). The overall incidence of cardiac structural abnormalities reported in our study was similar to that reported in the literature [24]. Crude mortality after repair was markedly lower but differed between isolated cases and non-isolated cases [11]. It has been reported that the survival rate is the lowest for CDH patients with a single ventricle and total mixed lesions, and poor for those with duct-dependent circulation and non mixed lesions. Survival to discharge was higher in non-critical infants with shunts [25]. In our study, the most common incidence rates were ventricular septal defects, followed by left ventricular hypoplasia syndrome, aortic stenosis or aortic hypoplasia, Tetralogy of Fallot, and double outlets of the right ventricle. A ventricular septal defect in a born patient who underwent surgical repair has survived until now. The other two, associated with Tetralogy of Fallot and left ventricular hypoplasia, died in the neonatal stage without surgical treatment. The good outcome of born CDH infant associated with ventricular septal defects led us to think critically about prenatal consultation for the four fetuses with CDH associated with ventricular septal defects in the unborn group. The presence of additional anatomical anomalies was the most important predictor of mortality among live-born infants. We emphasize the need for accurate prenatal assessment for fetuses with non-critical and non-isolated CDH.

Another finding of this study was that the overall percentage increase in NT thickness was 14.2%. The only liveborn patient with CDH associated with increased NT thickness died during the neonatal stage. An increased NT is a marker of intrathoracic compression-related pulmonary hypoplasia. Increased NT may be a marker of abnormal lung development, which cannot be reversed even by intrauterine surgery as early as 10–14 weeks [12]. This study emphasised the poor outcomes of fetuses with CDH associated with increased NT. One limitation of this study was the use of non-invasive prenatal testing for non-live births, which might have led to the lack of additional genetic information in the pregnancy termination group. Although chromosomal abnormalities are very important prognostic parameter, the genetic basis of CDH remains incompletely defined [26, 27].

The gestational ages at diagnosis in this study were 26.79 ± 5.497 gestational weeks and 21.84 ± 5.742 gestational weeks in the born group and unborn group, respectively. Fetuses diagnosed with CDH before 25 gestational weeks had a worse postnatal outcomes than those diagnosed after 25 gestational weeks [28]. In terms of medical decision making for fetuses diagnosed with CDH before 25 gestational weeks tend to terminate pregnancy. In our study CDH infants were routinely intubated for invasive mechanical ventilation immediately after birth (received either conventional mechanical ventilation or high-frequency oscillation). Concomitant gastric decompression was achieved by insertion of a naso- or orogastric tube. Following immediate postnatal stabilization, surgery was conducted through the chest with an open approach (thoracotomy) or with minimally invasive surgery (thoracoscopy). In literatures related to pulmonary hypertension, those CDH patients with severe hypoxaemia, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) was the next step in most managementalgorithms. However, ECMO was not involved in our study as well as the use of inhaled nitric oxide (iNO) and sildenafil (a phosphodi esterase 5 inhibitor) which might enhance survival. Another limitation of this study was lack of prenatal treatment in the form of fetoscopic endoluminal tracheal occlusion (FETO). Although in fetuses with isolated severe CDH on the left side, FETO performed at 27 to 29 weeks of gestation reportedly resulted in a significant benefit over expectant care with respect to survival to discharge, there was no such prenatal treatment in Beijng area as far as we know [5]. This probably was a direction of what we need to do in the future to provide hope to parents of early-diagnosed CDH fetuses.

Conclusions

We emphasize the need of a more positive advice for fetuses with isolated, non-critical and non-isolated CDH based on the good outcomes of isolated, non-critical and non-isolated CDH infants in this study. Critical CHD and increased nuchal translucency thicknesses seem to be associated with a higher risk of neonatal mortality for fetuses with CDH. The likelihood of pregnancy termination for fetuses with congenital diaphragmatic hernia is increased by early detection and the combination of CHD.

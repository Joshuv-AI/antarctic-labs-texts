The relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and its competitiveness in China: The moderating effect of employee stock ownership plan
Authors: Yushan Cao, Mengshu Wan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Using data from Chinese publicly listed companies spanning from 2010 to 2022, this study examines the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship, employee stock ownership plans (ESOPs), and corporate competitiveness. The findings show that the influence of corporate entrepreneurship on enterprise competitiveness is significantly “U-shaped”. As the degree of corporate entrepreneurship increases, it initially demonstrates a significant negative impact on enterprise competitiveness, followed by a significant positive impact. Mechanism testing reveals that the implementation of ESOPs can expedite the enhancement of corporate competitiveness. Further analysis suggests that compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship can contribute to sustained enhancements in enterprise competitiveness. At the same level of corporate entrepreneurship, the increase in competitive capabilities of large companies surpasses that of Small and Medium-sized (S&M) companies. This paper provides empirical evidence and theoretical guidance for studying the economic implications of different types of corporate entrepreneurship.

Introduction

It is an essential issue how established firms achieve high performance and gain sustained competitiveness in a in a rapidly changing global business environment. Some studies suggest that corporate entrepreneurship is a strategic planning approach to promote sustainable development [[1], [2], [3]]. In China, entrepreneurship is one of the key driving forces of sustained economic development [4]. In 2022, the State Administration of Taxation of China introduced 120 tax incentives aimed at various stages and key areas of enterprise innovation and entrepreneurship, covering the entire lifecycle of enterprises. Specifically, during the initial stage, tax incentives were provided to technology entrepreneurship incubators, university science parks, venture capital firms, and other entrepreneurial platforms. During the growth stage, tax benefits were extended to technology transfer, scientific research innovation talents, and similar initiatives. In the mature stage, tax incentives were offered to high-tech enterprises, manufacturing firms, software companies, and others. These measures are intended to maintain a vibrant environment for innovation and entrepreneurship among Chinese companies. Therefore, exploring the theoretical paradigm of motives-behaviors-performance in Chinese enterprise innovation and entrepreneurship holds significant practical value.

From a global perspective, the competitiveness of enterprises holds decisive significance for their survival and development. It serves not only as a vital indicator reflecting the high-quality development of enterprises but also as a key element in achieving the country's economic growth rate, impacting the development of enterprises, the prosperity of industries, and the rise and fall of nations. Given existing economic consequence studies, corporate entrepreneurship is the panacea for a firm's sustainable economic development [5], and there exists a linear relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and firm performance [6], even has a positive effect [2]. However, in contrast, enterprise competitiveness generally changes with the variation of the company's lifecycle [7]. It believes that entrepreneurship is the primary driving force of the economic process, causing "prosperity" through multiple revolutionary occurrences in the economic organism and frequently leading to "recession" due to disruptions caused by new products and methods [8]. In the short term, the inherent high risk of corporate entrepreneurship may temporarily impact or even suppress the performance level of enterprises. However, in the long term, entrepreneurial activities can promote sustainable development of enterprises and economic prosperity. It is reasonable to infer that there may exist a nonlinear relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. Therefore, examining the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and firm competitiveness in China from a micro-mechanism perspective holds theoretical significance. This perspective also provides an important angle for effectively enhancing enterprise competitiveness.

Furthermore, effective corporate governance mechanisms contribute to mitigating principal-agent problems [9], incentivizing agents to leverage their human capital advantages, align with corporate objectives, and thereby enhance firm competitive advantage. As an important institutional design to improve corporate governance, ESOPs have a supervisory incentive effect which is beneficial for mitigating proxy conflicts [10]. However, other scholars argue that ESOPs lead to several issues such as insider control and earnings management [11,12]. It is worth exploring whether ESOPs can alleviate the temporary inhibitory effects brought about by corporate entrepreneurship? Additionally, it is noteworthy that the academic community has shown increasing favor towards exploring the relationship between different types of corporate entrepreneurship and economic growth. Especially as innovation-driven development has become a national strategy, China's advantages in resources such as innovative talents and emerging technologies continue to accumulate, placing innovation-based entrepreneurship in a golden period of development [13]. Theoretically speaking, innovative corporate entrepreneurship2 tends to enhance the enthusiasm for innovation and entrepreneurship among employees, thereby promoting their high-quality development. This, in turn, can stimulate broader societal innovation and entrepreneurship, driving economic transformation and upgrading. Consequently, innovative corporate entrepreneurship has the potential to sustainably enhance enterprise competitiveness.

This study intends to conduct an in-depth analysis of the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship, ESOPs, and enterprise competitiveness using a sample of Chinese publicly listed companies from 2010 to 2022. The research findings reveal a significant positive U-shaped relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. Specifically, as the level of corporate entrepreneurship increases, it initially exhibits a notable negative impact on enterprise competitiveness, followed by a significant positive impact. These conclusions hold true even after robustness tests such as sensitivity analysis and instrumental variable approaches are conducted. Mechanism testing indicates that the introduction of ESOPs can significantly expedite the process of enhancing enterprise competitiveness. Moreover, companies implementing ESOPs demonstrate stronger competitiveness at the same level of entrepreneurship compared to those that do not. Further investigation suggests that innovative entrepreneurship, in contrast to arbitrage entrepreneurship, is more effective in fostering sustained enhancements in enterprise competitiveness.

The main contributions of this study are as follows. Firstly, existing research predominantly focuses on the theoretical paradigm of opportunity identification, entrepreneurial behavior, and entrepreneurial performance within the context of new ventures and small to medium-sized enterprises [[16], [17], [18]]. However, compared to new ventures or small to medium-sized enterprises, larger existing firms with appropriate scale are potentially better positioned to lead in entrepreneurship. Thus, we incorporate corporate entrepreneurship into the study of entrepreneurship to enrich traditional entrepreneurial theory.

Secondly, this study extends the research scope of enterprise competitiveness. There is a limited discussion from the perspective of corporate governance (supervisory or incentive mechanisms) regarding enterprise competitiveness. Few studies explore the impact of scattered elements within corporate governance (largely individual characteristics within the organization) on enterprise competitiveness. For instance, some literature suggests a positive correlation between long-term CEO incentives and enterprise competitiveness [19], as well as how the implementation of AI-based partner relationship management contributes to sustainable enterprise competitiveness [20], and how high levels of governance constraints enhance firm performance [21].

Thirdly, this paper offers deeper insights into the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and competitiveness, thereby providing new theoretical foundations for studying the economic consequences of corporate entrepreneurship. Our research proposes the use of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) as an alternative to measure corporate entrepreneurship. Compared to traditional methods, this approach offers a more accurate and objective evaluation of corporate entrepreneurship, thereby beneficially complementing the literature in the field of corporate entrepreneurship. Additionally, it provides empirical evidence and policy recommendations for studying the economic consequences of different types of corporate entrepreneurship.

The structure of the manuscript is organized as follows: section 2 comprises literature review and hypothesis development; section 3 delineates the research design; section 4 presents empirical analysis; section 5 entails further analysis; and section 6 concludes with research findings and policy recommendations.

Literature review and hypothesis development

An overview of corporate entrepreneurship

The conceptual definition of corporate entrepreneurship can be traced back to Miller's proposal in 1983, wherein corporate entrepreneurship refers to entrepreneurial activities within existing organizations aimed at achieving sustainable development goals through product, market, and technological innovations [22]. Subsequently, the academic community defines corporate entrepreneurship as the process wherein an individual or a group associated with an existing organization establishes a new organization or encourages renewal or innovation within that organization [23], through strategies such as technological innovation, creativity, and enterprise risk-taking to enhance competitive advantage [[24], [25], [26]]. In other words, corporate entrepreneurship can either create new ventures within existing companies to rejuvenate them [6] or establish new enterprises or organizations to expand the company's competitive scope [27].

As of now, research on corporate entrepreneurship primarily focuses on its relationship with corporate performance or organizational performance. A study utilizing data from 108 enterprises found a positive correlation between corporate entrepreneurship and future financial performance [6]. Additionally, it was observed that corporate entrepreneurship exhibits a strong positive correlation with high performance in dynamic and hostile environments [28]. Moreover, there exists a positive relationship between corporate entrepreneurship strategies and innovation, supported by a survey of 171 production companies [29]. Furthermore, the higher the degree of corporate entrepreneurship, the faster the company's return on assets (ROA) and revenue growth [30], among other findings. However, these empirical studies are primarily based on questionnaire surveys, with limited empirical research based on large samples.

Impact of corporate entrepreneurship on enterprise competitiveness

From the perspective of factors influencing enterprise competitiveness, it is well established that corporate resources have a significant direct impact [31,32], and strategic actions undertaken in response to external environmental changes also play a crucial role in shaping competitiveness [33]. It can be inferred that utilizing corporate entrepreneurship to integrate various underutilized resources within a company, is closely linked to sustained competitive advantage. However, studies that specifically explore enterprise competitiveness from the perspective of corporate entrepreneurship are relatively scarce.

Corporate entrepreneurship involves not only iterative replacements of products or technologies within an organization but also represents a form of creative destruction [8]. On one hand, enterprises often need to adopt riskier alternatives to ensure the success of their entrepreneurial endeavors, even if it means abandoning previously effective methods or products. On the other hand, to achieve higher financial returns, enterprises may inevitably incur significant debt, allocate substantial corporate resources to rapidly launch new products into new markets, and invest in undeveloped technologies. Both innovative internal development and proactive external investments entail inherent risks. To some extent, companies in the early stages of entrepreneurship may take action without knowing the outcome, bearing considerable risks that can impact enterprise competitiveness. Consequently, in the early stages of corporate entrepreneurship, there is a possibility that enterprise competitiveness may be suppressed or even diminished.

However, as previously discussed, “creative destruction” also plays a significant role in driving economic growth [8]. Past research has found a significant U-shaped relationship between entrepreneurial spirit and the quality of economic growth [34]. The Resource-Based View (RBV) is widely regarded as one of the most influential theoretical frameworks for understanding how firms attain and sustain competitive advantage [35]. Future competitive advantage of firms will inevitably rely on their resources and capabilities that foster sustainable environmental practices [36]. According to RBV, firms are aggregates of various resources, and the heterogeneity of these resources determines differences in enterprise competitiveness. These resources encompass human, capital, information technology, equipment, and knowledge resources, categorized into tangible and intangible forms. A critical pathway to enhancing competitiveness involves dynamically reconfiguring and innovatively integrating existing resources to develop a range of new products that create and deliver value to customers. Ultimately, these resources become valuable, scarce, and difficult to replicate [37], constituting the valuable, rare, and inimitable resources that competing firms cannot easily acquire in the short term. Sustainable competitive advantage is thus achieved by reconfiguring firm resources into valuable, rare, and inimitable forms. Over the long term, varying levels of entrepreneurial orientation within companies allow for the eventual arrangement of resources into various valuable and difficult-to-replicate heterogeneous resources, thereby determining differences in their competitiveness. As entrepreneurial investment by companies is a strategic choice aimed at acquiring and maintaining sustained competitive advantage [28], it can be argued that the enduring goal of a company's entrepreneurial endeavor is to earn the market value of its products (or services) and generate above-average profits [38], thereby enhancing its competitiveness. During the process of corporate entrepreneurship, especially in the recombination and reconfiguration of irreplaceable resources such as human and knowledge resources, competing firms are unable to acquire identical resources within a short period. This aspect provides firms with opportunities to generate profits above normal levels, thereby achieving sustained competitive advantage.

In summary, although corporate entrepreneurship may temporarily disrupt existing resource combinations, in the long run, it serves as a means of creating value and innovation through continuous product or process innovation and technological improvement to enhance the future competitive position and sustained performance growth of the company. During the process of corporate entrepreneurship, especially in the recombination and allocation of irreplaceable resources such as human resources and knowledge resources, competitive enterprises cannot obtain the same resources in the short term. This provides enterprises with the opportunity to create profits higher than normal, thereby gaining sustained competitive advantages.

Based on the above arguments, this study proposes the following hypothesis.Hypothesis 1Corporate entrepreneurship has a U-shaped relationship with enterprise competitiveness. Initially, as corporate entrepreneurship increases, it negatively impacts competitiveness. However, as it continues to increase, it begins to have a positive impact.

Corporate entrepreneurship has a U-shaped relationship with enterprise competitiveness. Initially, as corporate entrepreneurship increases, it negatively impacts competitiveness. However, as it continues to increase, it begins to have a positive impact.

The moderating effect of ESOPs

In addition to the factors influencing enterprise competitiveness mentioned above, effective corporate governance is also a necessary condition for enhancing competitiveness and improving operational performance within enterprises [39]. However, there is relatively limited research that discusses the impact of corporate governance on enterprise competitiveness from a governance perspective. Few studies have explored the influence of internal corporate governance characteristics on competitiveness. For instance, research suggests that there has a positive correlation between long-term CEO incentives and enterprise competitiveness [19]. Furthermore, factors such as larger management team size, older average age of managers, higher proportion of male managers, and higher educational qualifications among management correlate with stronger management competitiveness and enhanced enterprise competitiveness [40]. Nevertheless, these studies have not taken a stance on any specific corporate governance mechanism when examining factors influencing enterprise competitiveness.

ESOPs are a unique equity arrangement that gradually developed in China's state-owned enterprises during the transition to the corporate system and the introduction of the shareholding system. ESOPs are a form of equity incentive whereby internal employees, including management and core staff, subscribe to a portion or entirety of the company's equity through capital expansion or new capital investment. ESOPs are designed to enhance agent rights by incentivizing employees, although they may inadvertently empower agents excessively, leading to issues such as insider control and earnings management [11,12,41]. However, fundamentally, ESOPs aim to collectively incentivize the management, core employees, and even extend to regular employees, aligning their interests with those of shareholders and mitigating conflicts between employees and shareholders [42]. This alignment stimulates management to increase research and development investments, fostering technological innovations [43,44], thereby enhancing the company's innovation capabilities [45]. In essence, core employees, as direct participants in technical research and innovation, leverage their human capital and intellectual assets to directly influence corporate innovation outcomes [46], effectively transforming underutilized resources into valuable, scarce, and difficult-to-replicate resources. According to RBV, after confirming the financial capital shares contributed by management and their proficiency in intellectual capital, as well as the financial capital shares contributed by core employees and their proficiency in research and manufacturing human capital, ESOPs reorganized the original resources of the enterprise. The reallocated resources possess valuable, scarce, and hard-to-replicate, provide the basis for the continued acquisition of competitiveness.

Therefore, ESOPs may have a certain promoting effect on enterprise competitiveness, thereby accelerating the achievement of the turning point of the U-shaped curve of corporate entrepreneurship, mitigating the negative effect of corporate entrepreneurship on enterprise competitiveness in the early stage, and accelerating the improvement of enterprise competitiveness in the later stage.

Based on the above arguments, this study proposes the following hypothesis:Hypothesis 2ESOPs positively influence the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. They make the U-shaped curve steeper and shift it to the left, indicating that ESOPs help improve competitiveness more quickly.

ESOPs positively influence the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. They make the U-shaped curve steeper and shift it to the left, indicating that ESOPs help improve competitiveness more quickly.

Analysis of the impact of the type of corporate entrepreneurship on the competitiveness of firms

Furthermore, some scholars have conducted in-depth research on the types of corporate entrepreneurship, particularly exploring the impact of different types of corporate entrepreneurship on economic growth. The classification method introduced by the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor in 2001 is widely adopted in academia, which categorizes entrepreneurial activities into survival entrepreneurship and opportunity entrepreneurship based on entrepreneurial motivations. In comparison to survival entrepreneurship, opportunity entrepreneurship, especially those based on innovative opportunities, exhibits more prominent potential for value creation [[47], [48], [49]]. However, this simple dichotomy between survival entrepreneurship and opportunity entrepreneurship may not provide a comprehensive interpretation for the diverse entrepreneurial activities in economies such as China, which are undergoing institutional transformation, transitioning growth models, and experiencing regional development imbalances [15]. Therefore, some scholars have categorized entrepreneurship into arbitrage entrepreneurship and innovative entrepreneurship from the perspective of entrepreneurial opportunity discovery [14,15]. They suggest that as the level of marketization increases, the promoting effect of innovative entrepreneurship on economic growth continuously strengthens, while the promoting effect of arbitrage entrepreneurship on economic growth weakens [15]. This paper adopts this dichotomy, distinguishing between arbitrage entrepreneurship and innovative entrepreneurship in corporate entrepreneurship. This categorization aligns well with China's innovation and entrepreneurship development strategy and provides an effective approach for empirically studying the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship types and enterprise competitiveness.

Undoubtedly, corporate entrepreneurial strategies exert a significant influence on firm competitiveness, underscoring a close and intricate relationship between the two. Arbitrage entrepreneurship, for instance, involves entrepreneurs continuously reallocating resources through entrepreneurial activities, thereby improving the efficiency of organizational resource allocation and moving the market from an unbalanced state towards equilibrium. On the other hand, innovative entrepreneurship involves introducing new products, adopting new production methods, and discovering new markets to disrupt the existing equilibrium and expand the organization's production possibility frontier. In simple terms, arbitrage entrepreneurship focuses on “dividing the cake,” while innovative entrepreneurship emphasizes “baking the cake.” The former aims to seize a larger share within a fixed market size, while the latter aims to expand the market size beyond the existing boundaries.

Both innovative and arbitrage companies aim to provide valuable products or services to customers, catering to the heterogeneous demands of products to create new sources of value and establish competitive advantages unique to the enterprise. However, the essence of entrepreneurship lies in innovation, which involves integrating and recombining existing resources such as idle production factors and production conditions to form "new combinations" and introduce new uses and production systems [8]. Moreover, the theory of innovation-driven economic growth posits that the introduction and imitation of exogenous technologies cannot serve as a stable force driving economic growth. Instead, endogenous innovation is the key factor driving technological change and economic growth. Thus, compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship is less susceptible to imitation by competitors and is more likely to enable enterprises to form close and stable relationships with supply chain partners. These long-term trust relationships can help enterprises maintain normal business operations in uncertain environments. Enterprises that produce innovative products earlier are more likely to trigger imitation by others. Therefore, companies engaged in innovative entrepreneurship are more likely to capture market share, leading to stronger competitive advantages. Hence, it is hypothesized that, compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship has a more significant positive impact on firm competitiveness.

Based on the above arguments, this study proposes the following hypothesis:Hypothesis 3Compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship has a more significant positive impact on enterprise competitiveness.

Compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship has a more significant positive impact on enterprise competitiveness.

Research design

Data source and sample selection

The sample in this study includes enterprises listed on the Chinese Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchange during the period 2010–2022 as the research sample. The sample data are screened according to the following steps: (1) excluding financial listed companies; (2) excluding the listed enterprises that are listed on the ST/ST∗3stock exchange; (3) excluding missing information values. To mitigate the influence of extreme values, a 1 % level two-tailed truncation is applied to continuous variables. After screening, we identify a total of 4391 listed companies, resulting in 27,862 firm-year observations. Corporate entrepreneurship

Corporate entrepreneurship implies that firms tend to utilize entrepreneurial opportunities consistently and intentionally to achieve growth. Such entrepreneurial opportunities tend to seek advantage through technological renewal, innovation, and shifting corporate risk [[24], [25], [26]]. Since firms' entrepreneurial strategies include investing in research and development (R&D), brand equity, organizational capital, and human capital [5,53] and may have a significant impact on firms' resource allocation. In this study, corporate entrepreneurship can be measured in terms of the intensity of R&D investment, brand equity investment intensity, organizational capital investment intensity and human capital investment intensity of a firm's product strategy [2,3].

Specifically, R&D investment intensity (RD) is the ratio of R&D expenditures to sales revenues; brand equity investment intensity (brand) is the ratio of brand expenditures4 to sales revenues; organizational capital investment intensity (Org) is the ratio of organizational capital5 to sales revenues; and human capital investment intensity (Hm) is the ratio of employees' compensation to sales revenues. This study uses the sum of the four indicators, namely R&D investment intensity, brand equity investment intensity, organizational capital investment intensity and human capital investment intensity [3], as an indicator of the degree of entrepreneurship of the firm (Enrtepit).

Other variables are consistent with equation (1). ESOPit∗Enrtepit2 is the secondary interaction term between the moderator variable and the explanatory variable, and the sign of the coefficient of the interaction term can decide whether the U-curve is flat or steep, whether the U-curve is shifted to the left or to the right to change the location of the inflection point. If corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness present a U-shaped relationship, when β5 is significantly less than 0, the regulation of the ESOPs will make the U-shaped curve between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness flatten, or it may become an inverted U-shaped curve; when β5 is significantly greater than 0, the regulation of the ESOPs will make corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness present a steep U-shaped curve; when β5 is insignificant, ESOP does not play a moderating role.

In most empirical analyses identifying a U-shaped relationship, if the coefficient of the quadratic term of the explanatory variable is significant and the estimated extremum falls within the data range, it can be inferred that a U-shaped relationship exists between the explanatory variable and the dependent variable. However, this criterion is considered somewhat weak [58]. Furthermore, Lind and Mehlum drew upon the framework developed by Sasabuschi devised the Utest command to test the presence of U-shaped and inverted U-shaped relationships between two variables [58,59]. The Utest provides a precise test of whether a U-shaped (or inverted U-shaped) relationship exists within a specified interval. Therefore, this study further rigorously examines whether a U-shaped relationship exists between company entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness using the Utest, ensuring that the extremum falls within the corresponding critical value range. Only through the combination of these factors can the U-shaped relationship between company entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness be conclusively verified.

Table 8 presents the results of the Utest, indicating a significant U-shaped relationship between company entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness at a 95 % confidence interval level (p-value close to 0), with the reported extremum being 0.341. Based on this, we construct a more intuitive U-shaped relationship graph (Fig. 1). It is evident that when the degree of company entrepreneurship is less than 0.341, company entrepreneurship negatively impacts firm competitiveness, albeit to a lesser extent. Alternative enterprise competitiveness measures

To ensure the robustness of the research findings, this study replaced the measurement method of the dependent variable. In academia, the asset contribution rate is commonly used as an indicator reflecting the profitability of enterprise assets, serving as a measure of enterprise competitiveness. This is because it not only measures the core indicators of business operations and profit levels but also to a large extent describes the operational performance and level of competitiveness of the enterprise [60,61]. Therefore, this study uses the asset contribution rate6 of the enterprise (Comp_alt) as an alternative variable for the dependent variable "Competition." The results, as shown in Table 7, column (3), demonstrate that the sign and significance of the coefficients of the explanatory variables remain unchanged. The signs and significance of other variables' coefficients also remain consistent with the results of the baseline regression model, providing further support for Hypothesis H1.

Alternative corporate entrepreneurship measures

Drawing on research in the corporate entrepreneurship domain, the main explanatory variables in the regression are measured as aggregates from four aspects of the enterprise: RD, brand, Org, and Hm [2,3]. Instrumental variable method: 2SLS regression

This study may inevitably face the issue of reverse causality, where companies with stronger competitiveness have an advantage in strategic expansion, leading to more proactive entrepreneurial activities, and consequently, potentially higher levels of corporate entrepreneurship. To ensure the robustness of the research findings, this study employs two-stage least squares (2SLS) to address potential endogeneity issues. This study utilizes the median level of competitiveness of other companies in the same industry and year (Med_Competition) and the natural logarithm of the percentage of R&D personnel in the company plus one (lnRDpr_N) as instrumental variables [62]. Considering the presence of quadratic terms of the explanatory variables in the original model, both these instrumental variables and their quadratic terms are included in the model and estimated using the generalized method of moments (GMM). The GMM method is more suitable for handling nonlinear data, avoiding issues of heteroscedasticity or autocorrelation in disturbance terms [63].

Panel threshold model

Based on the aforementioned tests, it is evident that the impact of corporate entrepreneurship on enterprise competitiveness is not linear, suggesting the presence of a threshold effect. To further investigate and validate the regression results, this study employs a panel threshold model to re-examine the U-shaped relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. This approach aims to precisely explore the relationship between these factors under the condition of mitigating endogeneity concerns. Hansen introduced a non-dynamic panel threshold regression model, which not only estimates the threshold value but also conducts significance tests to validate its correctness [65]. The model incorporates the threshold value as an unknown variable into the analysis, constructing a segmented function of explanatory variables' regression coefficients, and subsequently estimates and tests the significance of the obtained threshold values and threshold effects. This study constructs the following panel threshold model for panel data [64]:(3)Competitionit=μi+β1′EnrtepitI(qit≤γ)+β2′EnrtepitI(qit>γ)+βn′Controlsit+eitIn this study, i denotes the company, t denotes the year, qit represents the threshold variable, and γ denotes the unknown threshold. The observations are divided into two ‘regimes’ depending on whether the threshold variable qit is smaller or larger than the threshold γ. The error eit is assumed to be independent and identically distributed (iid) with mean zero and finite variance σ2. where I (.) is the indicator function. An alternative intuitive way of writing (3) isCompetitionit={μi+β1′Enrtepit+eit,qit≤γμi+β2′Enrtepit+eit,qit>γ

The model effectively operates as a segmented function model, where qit≤γ, the coefficient of Enrtepit is β1′ ; whereas when qit>γ, the coefficient becomes β2′ . Given the potential for lagged effects in corporate entrepreneurship, particularly concerning a comprehensive indicator such as enterprise competitiveness, this study selects the lagged one-period corporate entrepreneurship (Enrtepi,t−1) as the threshold variable for panel threshold model regression. Robust cluster standard errors are employed to address autocorrelation issues in panel data. As the panel threshold model regression requires balanced panel data, company samples lacking continuous annual data from 2010 to 2022 are excluded from the full sample, leaving 4104 firm-year observations. In conclusion, the moderation by employee stock ownership plans enhances enterprise competitiveness at similar levels of entrepreneurial activities, thereby accelerating the process of enhancing enterprise competitiveness, confirming Hypothesis 2.

Subsequently, we plotted the U-shaped relationship between firm entrepreneurial activities and competitiveness, with the reported inflection point at 0.3. The solid line depicts the U-shaped relationship from the baseline regression, while the dashed line represents the U-shaped relationship incorporating the moderating variable. Compared to the model without the moderating variable, the inflection point shifts leftward from its original position of 0.341 to 0.3.

Fig. 4 illustrates that the inflection point of the U-shaped curve has shifted to the left, and the dashed line is slightly steeper than the solid line. This indicates that the introduction of ESOPs has significantly shortened the process of enhancing enterprise competitiveness. Observing the vertical axis in the figure, it is evident that companies that have implemented ESOPs exhibit stronger competitiveness at the same entrepreneurial level compared to those that have not implemented ESOPs. Notably, after incorporating the moderating variable of ESOPs, the slope of the dashed line does not increase dramatically. This suggests that although ESOPs have a significant positive impact on comprehensive indicators of enterprise competitiveness, there may be negative effects, such as insider control, free-riding, and earnings management, as discussed in the previous literature [11,12,41].Fig. 4U-shaped relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and firm competitiveness after joining ESOPs.Fig. 4

Conclusions and implications

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study examines the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship, ESOPs and enterprise competitiveness using data from Chinese listed companies from 2010 to 2022. The main findings are summarized as follows. Firstly, corporate entrepreneurship exhibits a significant positive U-shaped relationship with enterprise competitiveness. As the degree of corporate entrepreneurship increases, it initially demonstrates a significant negative impact on enterprise competitiveness, followed by a significant positive impact. After undergoing robustness tests such as alternative variable measurement approaches, PCA, endogeneity checks, and model replacements, the results remain consistent. Secondly, mechanism tests indicate that ESOPs play a positive moderating role in the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship and enterprise competitiveness. The implementation of ESOPs can shorten the time process for enhancing enterprise competitiveness. Thirdly, further analysis reveals that compared to arbitrage entrepreneurship, innovative entrepreneurship can facilitate the sustained enhancement of enterprise competitiveness. At the same level of corporate entrepreneurship, the increase in competitive capabilities of large companies surpasses that of S&M companies.

Practical implications

This study provides valuable management insights and guidance for startups, growth-stage firms, and mature enterprises. These companies often face challenges such as limited resources and high risks during corporate entrepreneurship endeavors. The research offers a systematic and effective approach to measuring corporate entrepreneurship, demonstrating empirical evidence on how capital, talent, technology, and other critical resources contribute to gaining competitiveness.

Corporate entrepreneurship involves innovative opportunities for resource orchestration and reconfiguration, often requiring adjustments in governance structures and optimization of internal management mechanisms to support innovation and competitive advantage. The study's findings can assist companies in developing tailored ESOPs based on a comprehensive understanding of their functionalities and impacts, effectively balancing risks and rewards in incentive design.

Our research underscores the pivotal role of innovation in enhancing enterprise competitiveness. Entrepreneurship goes beyond mere replication or imitation; it necessitates forward-looking investments in technology research and development. Companies must select appropriate entrepreneurial strategies based on their specific conditions and environmental characteristics, particularly amidst rapid economic transformations. Identifying arbitrage and innovation entrepreneurial opportunities is crucial for fostering high-quality development and strengthening competitive positioning. Thus, companies need to intensify efforts in research and development to drive technological advancement, delivering innovative products and services to society and accelerating the promotion of enterprise competitiveness. These insights provide valuable guidance for decision-makers, enabling practical applications on efficient resource utilization amid uncertainties and challenges to achieve strategic objectives and enhance market competitiveness.

Listed companies engaged in corporate entrepreneurship often engage in cross-industry collaborations and ecosystem building. Collaborations with other enterprises, startups, or technology partners accelerate innovation processes, resource sharing, and exploration of new market opportunities. Emulating such cross-boundary collaboration models can foster collaborative innovation along the industrial chain, thereby boosting overall competitiveness.

Limitations and prospects

This study has certain limitations that should be noted. Firstly, in constructing a comprehensive competitiveness evaluation system, this study predominantly employs financial indicators to measure enterprise competitiveness across dimensions of size, growth, and efficiency. The approach does not incorporate objective evaluation methods such as Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process or the entropy method, potentially leading to selection bias. Secondly, the study examines the relationship between corporate entrepreneurship levels and competitiveness across the entire sample, without considering how competitiveness paths vary across different stages of the corporate lifecycle.

Future research could explore the relationship between companies' investments in technological innovation and their competitiveness. It could investigate which types of technological innovations are most effective and how firms can achieve leadership positions in technological innovation in the market. Additionally, research could explore the role of ESOPs in promoting technological innovation and digital transformation within companies, particularly in today's digital environment. Understanding how talent management strategies influence firms' innovation capabilities and competitiveness, including strategies for attracting and retaining technical talent to drive technological innovation and enhance market competitiveness, is also a promising avenue for further inquiry. Lastly, further discussions could focus on how companies can enhance their competitiveness through organizational structure, process innovation, and flexible management practices, especially as resilience becomes increasingly vital in rapidly changing market environments.

An integrated neutrosophic Z-numbers based CRITIC-EDAS decision model for smart solar panel evaluation in sustainable energy planning
Authors: Muhammad Zafar Abbas, Rukhshanda Anjum, Amir Hussain, Mohaira Ahmad, Izatmand Haleemzai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rise of environmental concerns, the transition to sustainable energy has become an international priority. Solar energy has emerged as the most eco-friendly source of renewable energy. Solar panels are crucial to this transition for the successful deployment of solar energy technologies. Given the long-term investment, selecting optimal solar panels remains challenging due to imprecision and subjectivity in real-world decision-making. To solve this problem, this article proposes a novel multi-criteria decision-making approach for the optimal selection of smart solar panels, that is, neutrosophic Z-numbers criteria importance through intercriteria correlation evaluation based on distance from average solution (CRITIC-EDAS). The neutrosophic Z-numbers (NZN) provide a potent mathematical structure for managing uncertainty by integrating truth, indeterminacy, and falsity with reliability factors. The main contribution of this paper is to apply the criteria importance through intercriteria correlation (CRITIC) in a NZN environment and an alternative evaluation method called evaluation based on distance from average solution (EDAS). The CRITIC determines the objective weighting of the criteria, and the EDAS ranks the alternatives. A real-world case study is conducted for Turpan, one of the hottest and most solar energy-intensive cities in China, to demonstrate the applicability of the proposed approach with NZN. The perceptions of three expert decision makers are integrated to evaluate and rank the alternatives to solar panels concerning several pertinent design criteria. In addition, the accuracy and reliability of the proposed model are checked by conducting sensitivity and comparative analyses.Keywords: Sustainable energy, Green technology assessment, Multi-criteria decision-making, Neutrosophic Z-numbers, Renewable energy planning, Solar energy, Smart solar panel selectionSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

The increasing global demand for energy, along with rising environmental concerns, has placed renewable energy and sustainable resources at the forefront of modern energy strategies. In contrast to fossil fuels, solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal energy sources are renewable, offering cleaner and more eco-friendly alternatives to power generation. The utilization of renewable energy reduces greenhouse gas emissions, mitigates the impacts of climate change, and enhances energy security by reducing dependency on imported fuels. In recent times, the adoption of renewable energy has become more cost-effective, which is advantageous for both developed and developing countries. Among the renewable sources, solar energy is regarded as one of the most promising sources of renewable energy due to its abundance, sustainability, and minimal environmental consequences. Its adoption has increased significantly as countries aim to decrease carbon footprints and fulfill global sustainability objectives. Solar energy generates 3.6% of global electricity production, with a notable increase of 22% in installed capacity from 2021 to 2022. By the end of 2022, solar energy contributed nearly 31% of total installed renewable energy capacity, making it the second most utilized renewable resource after hydropower1. However, despite the growing adoption of solar technology, selecting the most suitable solar photovoltaic (PV) panels remains a complex and challenging task for decision-makers due to multiple conflicting criteria.

With the rapid advancement in PV technologies, a wide range of solar panels (e. g., monocrystalline, polycrystalline, thin film, and new generation hybrid models) are now available in the market. Decision-makers often struggle to evaluate these alternatives effectively, especially when multiple conflicting criteria must be considered simultaneously. The efficiency, life span, cost, environmental considerations, and other relevant factors make the selection of a solar panel difficult. Therefore, there is a strong need for a robust and intelligent decision-making framework that can handle uncertainty and support the selection of the most appropriate solar panel for long-term sustainability and optimal performance. However, evaluation of smart solar panel still faces several critical challenges. Decision-makers must deal with conflicting criteria such as cost, efficiency, durability, and environmental impact, while simultaneously accounting for incomplete, uncertain, and sometimes unreliable expert information. For instance, the effectiveness of PV panels can vary under fluctuating weather conditions, the durability of newer technologies often lacks long-term performance data, and expert assessments are often inconsistent regarding cost-performance trade-offs. These factors introduce significant uncertainty into the decision-making process. Traditional fuzzy and Z-number-based approaches only partially address these issues, leaving a gap in capturing both uncertainty and reliability in a unified way. This motivates the development of a more detailed framework.

To address all these challenges, recent studies have extensively applied fuzzy set theory and multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods for the evaluation and selection of solar panels. Fuzzy set theory has become increasingly crucial for dealing with uncertainty in solar energy applications. Classical fuzzy sets effectively cope with the vagueness in linguistic judgments. In contrast, more advanced extensions such as intuitionistic fuzzy sets, Pythagorean fuzzy sets, and picture fuzzy sets provide richer representations of hesitancy and indeterminacy in solar energy evaluations. For example, Pythagorean fuzzy SWARA-VIKOR and picture fuzzy Z-AHP with TOPSIS models have been used for the selection of solar panels, capturing uncertainty as well as conflicting expert biases effectively2,3. Models based on artificial intelligence integrated with fuzzy systems have also shown potential in solar applications. For instance, an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system was developed for predicting solar radiation to enhance the accuracy of making PV installation decisions4. Also, the evaluation of solar cells using circular Pythagorean fuzzy sets has been done, deepening the understanding of expert judgment modeling5. Although these approaches have advanced solar panel evaluation, several shortcomings remain unresolved. These existing models primarily capture vagueness and hesitation in expert judgments, but fail to incorporate the reliability or confidence level of the provided information. As a result, the outcomes may be biased or unstable when expert opinions are inconsistent or incomplete. During the evaluation of smart solar panels, several layers of imprecision were observed that could significantly affect decision accuracy. Expert opinions and linguistic terms were commonly used, making the data inherently imprecise. Environmental factors such as solar irradiance, temperature fluctuations, and site-specific conditions also varied significantly, making the assessment even more complex. These combined elements created a high level of imprecision that traditional fuzzy or crisp decision-making models could not fully capture. Since, intuitionistic fuzzy sets, Pythagorean fuzzy sets, and picture fuzzy sets, have been used to address these challenges in solar panel evaluation. Although these models have advanced the capability to represent vague and imprecise information, they are still not enough to address the multifaceted nature of uncertainty. All of these often neglect to model the reliability of the data and the hesitation levels of decision-makers explicitly. This limitation becomes critical in real-world energy planning scenarios, where expert opinions and performance evaluations are not only imprecise, but also influenced by uncertain and confidence-based judgments.

On the other hand, widely adopted MCDM methods AHP, TOPSIS, VIKOR, SWARA, and DEMATEL have been commonly used in renewable energy assessments, including solar panel and site selection. Most of these methods are either based on crisp data or traditional fuzzy logic, which limits their ability to address the complexity and subjectivity in actual decision-making environments. These methods may lead to oversimplified outcomes when expert assessments are inconsistent or unreliable. Despite the growing application of fuzzy and Z-number-based methods in renewable energy planning, a thorough review reveals that there is an apparent lack of research that focuses on the application of neutrosophic logic alongside Z-numbers. Some research has utilized Z-numbers independently or in combination with basic fuzzy environments. Still, the powerful combination of neutrosophic logic and Z-number modeling remains largely unexplored in this domain. All these shortcomings represent a significant research gap. To address these gaps, the use of Z-numbers, combined with neutrosophic logic, provides a more robust framework to represent and process such uncertain data. NZN extend the classical Z-numbers framework by incorporating the neutrosophic components of truth, indeterminacy, and falsity, together with the reliability of the information. This enriched structure enables NZNs to capture not only vague and imprecise expert judgments, but also the degree of hesitation and confidence associated with them. Therefore, NZN offer a much more effective and realistic approach to the complex decision-making problems. Yet, their potential remains largely unexplored in solar panel evaluation. The lack of such an integrated approach in the context of solar panel selection highlights the need for a more advanced and comprehensive methodology, which this study aims to address. This study develops a new hybrid approach that combines the CRITIC and EDAS methods within the NZN framework. The CRITIC method is effective for objectively determining the weights of criteria by considering both the contrast intensity and the conflict among them, eliminating the need for subjective judgments. Whereas, the EDAS method offers a simple yet powerful way to rank alternatives by evaluating their relative distance from the average solution, making it well-suited for handling MCDM problems. When these two techniques are integrated in the NZN environment, their effectiveness is significantly enhanced. NZNs provide a comprehensive representation of uncertain data by incorporating both the truth-based evaluations and the confidence levels of decision-makers, which traditional fuzzy models fail to capture. This powerful integration ensures a more balanced, transparent, and reliable decision-making process. The practicality and robustness of the proposed CRITIC-EDAS approach is demonstrated through a real-world case study focused on the selection of optimal smart solar panels, reflecting both technical performance and sustainability aspects. This application shows the methodology’s capability to support strategic planning and investment in renewable energy planning. Literature review

The NZN10 framework plays a central role in addressing uncertainty in complex decision-making scenarios, offering a realistic reflection of uncertain information in comparison to traditional fuzzy models. A NZN is a hybrid structure that merges the concept of neutrosophic sets and reliability introduced by Zadeh’s Z-numbers. This dual-layer approach enables NZN to capture the nature of an uncertain assessment and quantify the associated credibility or confidence level. Such a formulation is helpful in real-world problems, where information is not only vague or incomplete but often accompanied by imprecision about its trustworthiness. Also, NZN can model human hesitation, handle contradictory judgments, and represent expert opinion that is imprecise and doubtful in terms of credibility within a rigorous mathematical structure.

Where classical fuzzy sets limit uncertainty to a single membership degree, intuitionistic fuzzy sets add non-membership and a degree of hesitation. Yet, both frameworks still fail to provide the answer to how much uncertainty there is in the given information. Type-2 fuzzy sets handle more complexity but introduce computational challenges and often lose interpretability. On the other hand, NZN suspends type-2 interpretability challenges while further enriching the model without introducing considerable computational strain by elegantly incorporating both information content and its trustworthiness through paired structures of truth, indeterminacy, and falsity. Such capability makes NZN particularly suitable for high-stakes, long-term decision-making problems, such as energy planning, where both the quality and trust in expert judgments are critical. Overall, NZN serves as a powerful and flexible tool for uncertainty modeling, providing a solid foundation for effective decision-making in uncertain environments. An R-norm picture fuzzy discriminant measure14 for supplier selection was integrated into a modified TOPSIS approach to effectively handle uncertainty. In the context of complex picture fuzzy sets, a novel similarity measure15 was introduced to demonstrate its superiority in pattern recognition and medical diagnosis. Furthermore, a parametrized discriminant measure for picture fuzzy environments16 was developed and applied to banking site selection using a delta-parametrized minimum discriminant measure. MCDM methods have been extensively used to assess and rank the alternatives of solar panels in uncertain environments. Similarly, an integrated fuzzy DEMATEL and fuzzy MOORA have been used to evaluate the locations of solar power plants18. Research indicates that there is a growing willingness to invest in solar technologies among consumers due to increased electricity prices and environmental awareness. Even though technological advancements in solar energy offer great opportunities for sustainable energy development, challenges such as public awareness and integration into existing energy systems must be addressed to realize its potential fully. The ongoing advancement of solar technologies, as well as community-centric approaches, will be essential in overcoming these barriers. Public awareness and acceptance remain significant challenges, as fossil fuels still dominate global electricity production, contributing to 73.5% in 201729. Although fuzzy, intuitionistic and Z-number based MCDM methods have been widely used in renewable energy evaluation, several limitations exist. First, the extension of Z-numbers to NZN has rarely been applied in energy-related decisions. Second, most studies continue to use traditional ranking methods like TOPSIS, AHP, or VIKOR that frequently fail to resolve contradictory assessments and sensitivity problems. In contrast, advanced methods such as EDAS and CRADIS, known for stronger discrimination and stability, remain underutilized. Third, the literature is dominated by subjective weighting approaches, while objective methods such as CRITIC have received limited attention in uncertain environments, particularly when used together with NZN. These gaps highlight the need for a unified framework that integrates NZN to model complex uncertainty to achieve robust and reliable rankings of renewable energy alternatives. Next, we provide a brief overview of the EDAS and CRITIC method.

The CRITIC method overview

The CRITIC method32 is an objective and systematic method for estimating the weighting of criteria in MCDM problems. Unlike approaches based on expert judgment, CRITIC determines weights based on the internal structure of the decision matrix, which minimizes bias and enhances consistency. This is done by simultaneously considering two important factors of each criterion: the degree of contrast or variation, which can be captured by standard deviation, and the degree of interaction or relationship with other criteria, which correlation coefficients can capture. For our study, this objectivity was essential as solar panel selection involves multiple technical, environmental, and economic criteria. By applying CRITIC, we ensured that the weight assignment reflected the actual data structure and relationships, thereby enhancing the accuracy of the evaluation process under complex conditions. Several recent studies have focused on enhancing decision-making frameworks by improving criteria weighting and aggregation techniques. An objective method for determining decision-maker weights33 was proposed by modifying the CRITIC method within spherical fuzzy sets, introducing improved normalization and aggregation techniques validated through multiple case studies. To better handle nonlinear interactions between criteria, the CRITID34 method was proposed, which enhances the traditional CRITIC method by way of advanced dependence measures like distance correlation, making the evaluation process more robust and accurate. In the context of probabilistic uncertain linguistic environments, a GRP-based PUL-MAGDM35 method was developed, where the CRITIC method was employed to calculate attribute weights, and optimal alternatives were identified using PULPIS and PULNIS. While tackling industrial problems in real-time, a CRITIC36 method was used to evaluate coal suppliers for a coal-electricity-integrated firm using short-term transaction data. Further integrating fuzzy set theory with rough set techniques, a novel Pythagorean fuzzy rough CRITIC-REGIME37 methodology was presented for evaluating sustainable supply chains. CRITIC is particularly useful in complex situations like evaluating renewable energy technologies, where diverse and often interrelated criteria must be assessed objectively. In one of the most well-known studies, a CRITIC-TOPSIS38 framework was applied for the optimal allocation of solar PV farms in the top five cities of Saudi Arabia. Martínez et al.39 also evaluated the placement of PV systems in the Caribbean region of Colombia using CRITIC combined with PCA and Entropy weighting methods in a TOPSIS framework. In Vietnam, researchers implemented a CRITIC-CoCoSo MCDM model to aid in the site selection of hybrid solar-wind plants40. Ilham et al.41 created the CW-TOPSIS framework by combining CRITIC for weight determination and TOPSIS for evaluating optimal solar PV compensation under different tariff structures in Malaysia.

The EDAS method overview

The EDAS method42 is a modern MCDM method that is developed to evaluate and rank alternatives based on their relative performance with respect to an average reference point. Unlike traditional MCDM methods, which rely on ideal or anti-ideal solutions, EDAS uses a more realistic evaluation approach by taking the average as the center of value for each criterion. The core principle of EDAS revolves around calculating two main distances for each alternative: positive distance from average (PDA) and negative distance from average (NDA). The PDA measures how much an alternative surpasses the average (a desirable outcome), while the NDA measures how much it lags below the average (an undesirable outcome). Both distances are normalized and combined with a given set of weights for the criteria, providing an aggregate value for each alternative. One of the main advantages of EDAS is its computational simplicity, which makes it easily understandable and applicable in real-world decision scenarios. Moreover, by avoiding the dependency on ideal or worst-case values, the EDAS method provides a more balanced and practical evaluation of alternatives, which is suitable for situations involving incomplete or linguistically expressed data, which is common in real-world renewable energy evaluations. We selected EDAS because its distance-based evaluation aligns well with our aim of ranking solar panel options in a balanced and robust manner, particularly when integrated with CRITIC for objective weighting in the NZN environment. Recent advancements in fuzzy-based decision-making models have further strengthened the applicability of MCDM techniques to complex and uncertain environments. The authors43 formulated circular improved Pythagorean fuzzy Dombi operational laws and aggregation operators and fixed the issues posed by circular Dombi operational aggregation operators in connection with EDAS by resolving policy-related problems. PFEDAS44 is a fuzzy extension of EDAS, which overcomes drawbacks in traditional EDAS-based aggregation. PFEDAS maintains uniqueness in individual evaluations, which is critical for group decision-making scenarios. The hybrid MCDM framework, which combining DEMATEL and EDAS45 enhances the applicability of integrated methods by permitting objective evaluations of social aid recipients. Here, DEMATEL determines the criteria weights while EDAS produces the ranking. A robust overdue decision-making method was proposed, where EDAS was modified with Pythagorean probabilistic hesitant fuzzy information for urgent decision-making scenarios to select effective COVID-19 treatments46. Research problem and contribution

In the context of solar panel selection and renewable energy planning, researchers have explored decision-making frameworks to manage the inherent complexity and uncertainty of the task. Studies focusing on solar panel selection have applied traditional methods such as TOPSIS, AHP, and VIKOR51,52. These models offer utility in more controlled settings, often assume deterministic or simply fuzzy inputs, thereby falling short in capturing the multidimensional nature of uncertain data that arises in real-world expert judgments. Even extensions based on fuzzy logic, like intuitionistic fuzzy TOPSIS53 and Pythagorean fuzzy SWARA-VIKOR54, primarily focus on imprecision but lack a mechanism to incorporate the credibility or reliability of the information, which is an essential feature in long-term investments like solar infrastructure. To address the identified gaps, this study proposes a new hybrid approach to decision-making using NZN, referred to as the NZN-based CRITIC-EDAS approach. This methodology is the first known attempt in the literature to integrate CRITIC and EDAS in a NZN environment for smart solar panel selection. NZN enriches the decision-making process by not only modeling truth, indeterminacy, and falsity independently but also incorporating the reliability of each component, making them superior to other fuzzy models. Furthermore, while CRITIC has been applied in energy-related studies to derive some objective weights and EDAS exhibited merits for evaluating options relative to their average solution distance, there has been no research to utilize these two approaches under the NZN framework. This gap is significant because traditional fuzzy CRITIC or EDAS methods do not handle uncertainty and reliability simultaneously. Results and discussion

Case study

Turpan is located in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region of China. It is one of the hottest and sunniest places in the country, with average annual sunshine exceeding 3000 hours and summers frequently exceeding 40 degrees Celsius. Turpan’s environment is unique in China when it comes to Solar Energy. As of the latest census, Turpan’s population is estimated to be over 650000, with steadily increasing demand for electricity due to urban development, expansion in agriculture, and industrial activities. The current energy supply for Turpan still heavily relies on fossil fuels. This sole reliance brings several environmental and sustainability challenges. The local government, aligned with China’s broader “West-East Power Transmission” and “Green Energy Corridor”, has launched an initiative to shift the city towards renewable energy sources, with a particular emphasis on solar PV technology. With the climatic advantages of the city, the deployment of smart solar panel systems has been identified as a strategic move to maximize energy capture and efficiency. To ensure a comprehensive and context-sensitive decision framework, the research team created an expert panel made up of three highly qualified decision-makers. The profile of these decision-makers is provided in the Table 5. The expert panel in this case study is composed of three members. Three major considerations were used to select decision-makers. First, the problem of renewable energy planning in Turpan needs interdisciplinary visions, especially techno-economic, technical-performance, and policy-regulatory experience. Thus, specialists had been selected to address these complementary points. Second, the choice was based on not only professional status but also work experience in the field of renewable energy systems, so the decisions were made on the basis of applied knowledge, and not theoretical abstraction. Third, the group of the decision-makers was kept on the minimal level of three, which is a compromise between consideration of different opinions and unjustified complexity and redundancy, in line with best practices in MCDM studies. Therefore, the final panel of an energy economist, a renewable energy technologist, and an environmental policy advisor provides a comprehensive and context-sensitive foundation for evaluating solar panel alternatives under uncertain conditions.

The results of the numerical example indicate that the alternative Trina Solar Vertex S+ achieves the highest ranking score, as it consistently performs well across all considered criteria, particularly in efficiency, dust & weather resistance and tracking capability, which are the most important factors under the hot and dusty environment of Turpan. LG NeON R Prime takes second place, mainly due to its strong efficiency and temperature coefficient, which make it a reliable choice under high-irradiance conditions. Sun Power Maxeon 6 gets a moderate score displaying average strengths of temperature performance and tracking and low resilience to environmental challenges. In contrast, JA Solar DeepBlue 3.0 ranks lowest because of its deficits in both efficiency and thermal behavior, which limit its applicability in this setting. These results are the direct consequence of the combined weighting and ranking system: the CRITIC method objectively identified efficiency, dust resistance, and temperature coefficient as the most influential criteria by capturing both their contrast intensity and conflict among factors, while the EDAS method translated these weighted evaluations into final performance scores by assessing the deviation of each alternative from ideal and anti-ideal solutions. This depth analysis not only explains why the final ranking was so, but it goes further to clarify the soundness of the proposed NZN-based framework.

Although effective in structured environments, these methods fall short when dealing with complex, ambiguous, or imprecise real-world scenarios, especially in long-term strategic decisions such as renewable energy investments.

In contrast, the proposed CRITIC-EDAS technique operates within the NZN framework, which integrates both fuzzy logic and reliability measures to capture a more comprehensive spectrum of uncertainty. NZNs extend classical fuzzy and Z-numbers theories by representing truth, indeterminacy, and falsity components, each with associated reliability, offering a significantly richer and more realistic modeling of uncertain human judgments. This is crucial for high-stakes domains like energy planning, where subjective evaluations, incomplete knowledge, and future unpredictability must be carefully managed. Furthermore, while VIKOR, TOPSIS, and WASPAS rely solely on crisp preferences, our hybrid approach combines objective weight determination through CRITIC and alternative evaluation through EDAS in a NZN environment. This dual-layered structure not only enhances the robustness of the data but also ensures that uncertainty is explicitly modeled and interpreted, leading to more trustworthy and informed decisions. Thus, even though the final rankings may align numerically, the theoretical depth, uncertainty handling capability, and practical relevance of the proposed methodology distinguish it as a more advanced and superior decision-making tool. Managerial implications

The newly proposed CRITIC-EDAS methodology based on NZN offers an unparalleled contribution to energy planners, sustainability strategists, and government policy makers by providing a robust and transparent decision-making tool under uncertainty. In terms of the development of renewable energy, particularly with solar infrastructures in areas like Turpan in China, which presents advantageous climatic and geographical conditions, this model provides a decision-making model equipped for dealing with uncertainty, expert trustworthiness, and long-term strategic implications. The application of NZN theory, considering its components of truth, indeterminacy, and falsity, and their respective reliabilities, is crucial in regions where data is imprecise or incomplete. With the help of this model, policymakers can systematically investigate smart solar panel technologies, ensuring that selected investments align with national sustainability targets, economic constraints, and technological innovation goals. The model integrates the CRITIC method to eliminate all forms of human subjectivity by deriving criterion weights. At the same time, EDAS ranks the alternatives based on their distance to the average, ensuring a practical and balanced decision that reflects real-world conditions.

The proposed NZN-based CRITIC–EDAS framework achieves practical adaptability across multiple managerial contexts and provides reliable rankings of alternatives. In the healthcare decision-making process, particularly in the evaluation of medical equipment or the management of pandemic responses, expert evaluations and performance criteria are often vague and imprecise. Here, the NZN model transforms the experts’ evaluations into a formal decision matrix. CRITIC is thereafter used to focus on the assessment of each criterion by measuring the variability in expert scores and the inter-criteria correlation so that highly discriminating and less redundant factors are appropriately weighted. Medical equipment or strategies are then ranked using EDAS, which calculates distances from the average solution, enabling hospital managers to prioritize those strategies that best optimize the efficiency, cost, and safety tradeoff under uncertain conditions. Similarly, the framework can be used for the selection of suppliers or the design of distribution networks in supply chain management. The performance metrics, such as delivery reliability, cost stability, and sustainability, are modeled within NZN bounds, objective weights assigned by CRITIC, and alternatives ranked using EDAS to arrive at the most resilient and cost-efficient supply chains.

Regarding smart city planning, where technologies such as IoT platforms or urban mobility systems are to be chosen while meeting environmental, economic, and social goals under ambiguous policy and infrastructure limitations, the NZN model capturing the vagueness of planners’ assessments, CRITIC fairly weighting the criteria as per the preset requirements, and EDAS ranking competing technology options based on their distance to an ideal holistic achievement benchmark. This exact procedure is carried out for environmental risk assessments and disaster preparedness planning to devise alternative policies for resource allocation, accounting for uncertain environmental variables or resource-allocated values and multiple expert opinions, thus arriving at a rational and prioritized action plan. In every case, NZN-CRITIC-EDAS, as an integrated approach, not only handles incomplete, unreliable, or linguistic data and provides a holistic outcome, but also ensures objectivity, transparency, and reproducibility of the entire process. The NZN-CRITIC-EDAS allows planners dealing with intricate and turbulent environments to use this approach with confidence. To summarize, the methodology not only aims to improve the precision and trustworthiness of critical decisions but also fosters a holistic, multidimensional systems approach to planning, which is beneficial for stakeholders and managers dealing with complex and volatile situations.

Limitations of the proposed methodology

Despite the effectiveness of the proposed CRITIC-EDAS methodology in the NZN environment, some limitations remain that need to be addressed. One key limitation lies in the computational complexity of the NZN framework, which involves multilayered processing of uncertainty, reliability, and indeterminacy that increases the computational burden, especially in large-scale decision problems. In addition, the NZN accuracy relies heavily on the subjective biases of the decision-makers, which can lead to inconsistency in the results if expert evaluation is not trustworthy. Moreover, while CRITIC objectively determines criteria weights, it relies on the availability of complete and quantitative data, which may not always be feasible in all real-world applications. The EDAS approach is practical in handling deviations from the average solution, but it assumes linearity in preference structures, which cannot support complex decision-making frameworks. The direct generalization of the proposed methodology to other domains may be constrained without domain-specific adaptation. The performance of the proposed method depends on the accurate identification of relevant criteria, expert availability, and the nature of incomplete data. Although the framework is theoretically flexible, applying it to other sectors would require careful modification of the criteria set, validation with domain experts, and empirical testing to ensure methodological robustness and contextual relevance. Finally, while the proposed methodology has demonstrated utility for structured decision problems, more work needs to be done to extend it to unstructured and dynamic environments.

Conclusion

In this article, we have developed a novel decision-making methodology using NZN for choosing smart solar panels to minimize the complexity and uncertainty challenges within the context of sustainable energy planning. The NZN provides a robust mathematical structure that merges the neutrosophic logic by incorporating truth, indeterminacy, and falsity with a secondary component that conveys the reliability of the corresponding information. This dual-layered representation makes NZN well-suited for modeling real-world situations where expert judgments and data are often imprecise or incomplete. Based on this foundation, we designed a hybrid CRITIC-EDAS methodology that offers a comprehensive and objective solution for MCDM problems. For this purpose, the CRITIC method is applied first to capture unbiased, data-driven weights of the evaluation criteria by considering their variability and interdependencies as well as incorporating cross-criterion dependency. Subsequently, the EDAS method is applied to evaluate and rank the smart solar panels based on their distances from the average solution, offering a practical and interpretable assessment approach. The integration of CRITIC and EDAS within the NZN environment serves as a significant contribution of this study. When embedded in the NZN framework, this hybrid approach effectively captures imprecision and reliability in expert judgments. This integration enhances the decision-making process, making it more comprehensive, reliable, and suitable for addressing real-world problems. The proposed methodology has been used in a real-world case study located in Turpan, China, which is an area with intensive solar radiation and a growing energy demand. Alongside three experts from the relevant industry, we evaluated four smart solar panel alternatives: Sun Power Maxeon 6 AC, LG NeON R Prime, JA Solar DeepBlue 3.0, Trina Solar Vertex S+, based on four relevant technical and environmental criteria. The alternative Trina Solar Vertex S+ emerges as the best smart solar panel option. This contribution addresses the gaps in existing MCDM approaches that often assume complete certainty or fail to incorporate reliability explicitly. The CRITIC-EDAS decision-making framework based on NZN showed its real-life usefulness by capturing the uncertain judgments of experts and providing a rational and consistent ranking of the alternatives. The proposed methodology holds strong scientific value in promoting green energy solutions. The integration of CRITIC and EDAS within the NZN framework yields an advanced method for the evaluation and selection of smart solar panels. This is important because solar energy plays a key role in reducing carbon emissions and fighting climate change. Furthermore, it contributes scientifically by extending MCDM theory to incorporate dual-layer uncertainty modeling, improving decision-making in renewable energy systems and other high-stakes domains.

Despite the strength of the proposed NZN-based CRITIC-EDAS approach, certain limitations remain. The method relies heavily on expert judgments. Although the model uses NZN, it may still introduce bias if the expert panel lacks domain expertise. Additionally, the computational complexity increases as the number of criteria and alternatives increases, which may pose challenges in large-scale decision problems. The proposed methodology assumes a fixed weight distribution and does not adapt dynamically to changing environmental or technological conditions.

This research offers various avenues for future work. First, the proposed NZN-based CRITIC-EDAS methodology can be extended by incorporating dynamic weight adjustment mechanisms to better reflect changing environmental conditions, market trends, or evolving technological advancements. Second, other forms of fuzzy environments, such as interval-valued, bipolar, or picture fuzzy neutrosophic Z-numbers, can extend the domains of this methodology. Also, future research can develop rigorous distance measures for NZN, establishing their mathematical properties and validity, and then integrate these measures into the CRITIC-EDAS framework so that the entire decision-making process can operate directly within the NZN environment without intermediate defuzzification. Furthermore, NZN-based decision-making systems could be integrated with machine learning, enabling responsive, real-time adaptive weight and evaluation in contexts such as smart grid management, renewable energy forecasting, and even disaster response planning. Future research could also apply network-based methods such as DEMATEL or ANP to capture the interdependencies and causal relationships among the criteria that the current framework does not fully address. Further application of the methodology to other areas of renewable energy, such as wind or biomass technologies, could strengthen the robustness and generalizability of the methodology. Overall, this study provides a practical and expandable framework for reliable decision-making, providing a valuable contribution to the theory and practice of sustainable energy systems and beyond.

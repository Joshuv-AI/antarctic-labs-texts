Marked clinical and haemodynamic improvement with sotatercept in severe refractory pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with corrected complex congenital heart disease
Authors: Daniel Ramos, Laura Dos, Arianna Andrade, Juan José Chiriboga, Berta Saez-Gimenez, Álvaro Cantero, Eva Revilla-Lopez, Cristina Berastegui, Manuel López-Meseguer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive disease associated with high morbidity despite combination therapy. Sotatercept, a recombinant fusion protein that acts as an activin signaling inhibitor, has shown significant clinical and haemodynamic benefit in phase 3 trials. However, evidence in PAH associated with congenital heart disease (PAH-CHD) is very limited.Case presentation37-year-old woman with repaired pulmonary atresia with ventricular septal defect and major aortopulmonary collateral arteries, who developed severe PAH years later. Despite treatment with tadalafil, ambrisentan, and high dose subcutaneous treprostinil, she remained at intermediate-high risk, with WHO-FC III, NT-proBNP 3773 pg/mL and a 6-min walk distance (6MWD) of 480 m. Sotatercept was initiated through an expanded access program. Over the following months, she markedly improved to WHO-FC II, NT-proBNP 230 pg/mL and 6MWD 540 m. Right heart catheterization showed a reduction in mean pulmonary artery pressure from 48 to 26 mmHg, PVR from 14 to 5.4 Wood units, and an increase in cardiac index from 1.17 to 2.91 L/min/m2.ConclusionA significant and rapid improvement in haemodynamics and functional capacity was observed after starting sotatercept, in a patient with severe PAH-CHD refractory to maximal therapy. Sotatercept may represent an effective rescue therapy and a potential therapeutic option for complex and severe PAH patients.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive disease characterized by pulmonary arterial pathologic remodeling and vasoconstriction that lead to increased pulmonary vascular resistances. It carries high morbidity and mortality risks despite major therapeutic advances over the last decades. Even under maximal combined vasodilator therapy, outcomes remain poor [1].

For decades, available PAH specific drugs only targeted pathological pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction, but not pulmonary vascular remodeling [2]. Research on PAH pathobiology highlights dysregulated signaling within the transforming growth factor-β superfamily as a key driver of endothelial dysfunction and progressive vascular obstruction. It affects bone morphogenetic proteins and activin signaling pathways. Sotatercept is a first-in-class activin receptor type IIA ligand trap that partially restores this imbalance and induces reverse remodeling in preclinical murine models [3].

Pivotal trials of sotatercept, including the phase 3 STELLAR (Sotatercept Trial in Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension) trial, have demonstrated substantial clinical benefit, with gains in exercise capacity, improvements in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) and NT-proBNP, and an 84% reduction in the risk of death or clinical worsening versus placebo [4]. Pooled analyses further emphasized improvements in right ventricular (RV) function and pulmonary arterial compliance [5].

However, data on sotatercept in PAH associated with congenital heart disease (PAH-CHD), particularly in adults with complex, repaired defects, is very limited, as this population is poorly represented in the pivotal trials. Indeed, sotatercept did not demonstrate clinical benefit for this population in the phase 3 STELLAR trial, probably due to the small sample size of PAH-CHD patients recruited, and the consequent reduced statistical power [4].

We report a case of severe PAH-CHD, refractory to maximal vasodilator therapy, that expressed substantial and rapid clinical and haemodynamic improvement after initiating sotatercept.

Case presentation

37-year-old woman with a history of congenital heart defect, consisting of pulmonary atresia with ventricular septal defect (PA-VSD), confluent hypoplastic pulmonary arteries, and major aortopulmonary collateral arteries (MAPCAs). She underwent three palliative procedures during childhood and a successful complete repair at age 21.

In PA-VSD with MAPCAs, pulmonary blood flow is supplied directly from the systemic circulation at systemic pressures either by MAPCAs, Blalock-Taussig shunts or VSD (after right ventricle to pulmonary arteries conduit placement) (Fig. 1). Chronic exposure of the pulmonary vascular bed to high-pressure flow promotes endothelial dysfunction, medial hypertrophy, and progressive segmental vascular remodeling. At the age of 30, severe PAH was diagnosed by right heart catheterization (RHC), showing suprasystemic pulmonary pressures, mild right-to-left shunt, cardiac output (CO) 2.76 L/min and cardiac index (CI) 1.17 L/min/m2. Dual oral therapy with tadalafil and ambrisentan was initiated as first line treatment, and early in the following months selexipag was added, obtaining transient benefit. However, she later deteriorated with rising NT-proBNP (3000 pg/mL), worsening RV function, and recurrent exertional syncope. RHC showed mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) 48 mmHg and right atrial (RA) pressure 16 mmHg. She underwent percutaneous pulmonary valve implant due to severe pulmonary regurgitation assuming to contribute to her clinical deterioration, but no clear benefit was observed.

Due to progressive clinical decline, selexipag was substituted by subcutaneous treprostinil, titrated to a maximal dose of 55 ng/kg/min. Despite maximal triple therapy, the patient remained at intermediate-high risk: WHO functional class III, NT-proBNP 3773 pg/ml, and 6-min walk distance (6MWD) 480 m (desaturation 82%). Other parameters included VO2 15.7 ml/kg/min (48%), TAPSE/sPAP ratio 0.19, and severely impaired RV ejection fraction (30%) in cardiac MRI. As she remained in an unsatisfactory risk status (COMPERA 2.0: intermediate-high, REVEAL Lite 2.0: intermediate) and was considered ineligible for lung or heart–lung transplantation (mainly due to prior thoracotomies), sotatercept was initiated in June 2024 through an expanded access program and titrated according to the recommendations of the drug label.

Following sotatercept initiation, multidimensional risk stratification showed substantial improvement. The REVEAL Lite 2.0 score decreased from 7 (intermediate risk) to 2 (low risk), while the COMPERA 4-strata assessment improved from intermediate-high to low risk, reflecting a two-strata risk reduction. She experienced marked symptomatic improvement, achieving WHO functional class II, and able to climb four flights of stairs and walk 4 km daily. Her NT-proBNP dropped to 230 pg/mL, 6MWD increased to 540 m (SpO2 nadir 85%), and VO2 peak 69% predicted, and TAPSE/sPAP ratio rose to 0.28. Sotatercept was well tolerated, and no related adverse effects were reported. Repeat RHC confirmed a striking haemodynamic improvement: mPAP 26 mmHg, (systolic/diastolic 49/14 mmHg), RAP 1 mmHg, PAWP 1 mmHg, CO 4.6 L/min, CI 2.91 L/min/m2, and PVR 5.4 WU. Regarding safety, 18 months after starting sotatercept the patient has presented occasional mild epistaxis as the single drug related side effect. No changes in treatment regime have been considered.

Discussion

This case illustrates a notable response to sotatercept in a patient with severe PAH associated with corrected complex CHD, at intermediate-high risk despite prostanoid-based triple therapy. This improvement is remarkable, particularly given the long-standing, severe PAH with insufficient response to prior therapies. Noteworthy, our patient experienced a significant improvement in haemodynamics, contrary to the results of the pivotal trial that could not prove CO improvement. These findings are consistent with the overall multidimensional clinical improvement observed in our patient [4]. More specifically, the marked improvement in VO2, TAPSE/sPAP and NT-proBNP underscores a potent RV unloading effect attributable to sotatercept.

In a post-hoc analysis from the STELLAR trial, sotatercept significantly reduced mPAP (−13.9 mmHg), PVR (−254.8 dyn s cm−5) and RAP (−2.7 mmHg), and improved TAPSE/sPAP ratio (+0.12 mm·mmHg−1) and NT-proBNP (−996.74 ng/L) without increasing CO, indicating enhanced RV efficiency and reverse remodeling [7].

Importantly, we acknowledge that the change in haemodynamics from baseline to post-sotatercept reflects both sotatercept as well as 3 years of transition to treprostinil therapy to a dose of 55 ng/kg/min. Therefore, the observed haemodynamic improvement cannot be exclusively attributed to sotatercept. However, the rapid and marked clinical, biomarker, and exercise capacity improvements observed shortly after sotatercept initiation strongly support an additive therapeutic effect.

While PAH-CHD patients were included in the STELLAR trial [4], this case provides important real-world evidence supporting sotatercept in this specific, challenging population. PAH-CHD often involves unique mechanisms such as chronic RV pressure overload and fixed pulmonary vascular disease, making the degree of "reversibility" observed here highly significant. Furthermore, these patients are often deemed ineligible for transplantation due to surgical complexity, making sotatercept a relevant therapeutic alternative that may contribute to long-term disease stabilization.

Crucially, our patient tolerated sotatercept well, with mild epistaxis as a single side effect attributable to the drug, which did not require any treatment modification. This supports a favorable safety profile in this sort of complex patient.

Conclusion

Treatment with sotatercept resulted in substantial clinical and haemodynamic improvement as add-on therapy in a case of severe, refractory PAH-CHD, illustrating its potential role as an effective rescue therapy in advanced, prostanoid-based triple-therapy failure. Thus, sotatercept might be considered as a treatment option for severe PAH-CHD cases.

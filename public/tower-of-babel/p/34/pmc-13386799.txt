SNP-based association analysis of key genes involved in triglyceride biosynthesis reveals genetic regulation of oil content and fatty acid composition in tree peony seeds
Authors: Meiying Zhang, Ningning Tong, Shun He, Zhengyuan Ren, Xiushuang Qi, Xinhui Pei, Zheng’an Liu, Liping Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOilseed tree peony is a high-value woody oil crop with significant economic and nutritional value, playing a crucial role in addressing global demand for healthy edible oils, yet its genetic improvement for oil-related traits is hindered by the lack of efficient molecular markers.ResultsIn this study, 141 tree peony germplasms were analyzed for oil content (OC) and fatty acid (FA) composition, revealing significant variations in total fatty acids (TFAs, 186.74-301.82 mg/g), unsaturated fatty acids (UFAs, 181.27-293.47 mg/g), and dominant α-linolenic acid (ALA, 41.77–64.73%). Correlation analysis and principal component analysis indicated that ‘Sai Huang Hou’ was an excellent germplasm with superior oil traits. Genome-wide identification uncovered 18 triglyceride biosynthesis genes, including 3 PoGPATs, 5 PoLPATs, 10 PoDGATs, with conserved domains and stage-specific expression patterns during seed development. Among these, PoGPAT9 and PoLPAT2 exhibited dynamic expression, while PoDGAT2 reached its peak during late maturation. Association analysis identified a total of 16 significant association combinations involving 6 phenotypic traits and 9 SNP loci, explaining 2.52–8.22% of phenotypic variation. Multiple comparisons of genotypic effects at significantly associated loci on oil-related traits showed that the 485 bp mutation in PoLPAT2 increased oil content by 1.40%. For PoDGAT2, the 759 locus mutation elevated linoleic acid content by 3.81 mg/g and reduced α-linolenic acid content by 10.30 mg/g relative to the wild genotype.ConclusionThis study provides new insights into the genetic regulation of lipid metabolism in tree peony seeds and offers molecular markers for breeding high-quality oilseed varieties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-09057-w.

Introduction

Plant oils are essential for of global food security and human nutritional health [1, 2], which primarily composed of monoacylglycerol (MAG), diacylglycerol (DAG), and triacylglycerol (TAG), with TAG as the dominant and functionally critical component [3, 4]. The lipid biosynthesis pathway is highly conserved in plants [5], and follows the classic acyl-CoA-dependent Kennedy pathway. Fatty acids (FAs) are de novo synthesized in plastids assembled into TAG in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and finally encapsulated by oil body proteins into stable oil bodies for cytoplasmic storage [6]. Within this pathway, the primary precursors for TAG synthesis are glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P) and cytoplasmic acyl-CoA [7]. At the ER, Glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (GPAT), lysop-hosphatidyl acyltransferase (LPAT), and diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT) act sequentially to catalyze glycerol backbone acylation: GPAT generates LPA at the sn-1 position, LPAT produces PA at the sn-2 position, and DGAT finally synthesizes TAG for storage in oil bodies [8, 9], and oil body proteins form a monolayer membrane to encapsulate TAG and maintain oil body stability [10].

In the Kennedy pathway, GPAT, LPAT and DGAT are key rate-limiting enzymes determining TAG biosynthesis efficiency, and their coding genes are promising targets for oilseed genetic improvement [11, 12]. Previous studies have demonstrated that overexpression of these genes effectively promotes TAG accumulation and optimizes fatty acid composition [13]. Heterologous expression of sunflower HaGPAT9 isoforms in yeast was found to increase the levels of C16:0 and C16:1, while overexpression of PfGPAT9 significantly elevated the contents of total fatty acids (TFA) and α-linolenic acid (ALA) in seeds [14]. Similar genetic associations between GPAT and OC have been documented in Arabidopsis and Euphorbia lathyris [15, 16]. Overexpression of AhLPAT2 increases OC by 12.2% and TFA content by 11.8% to 24.6% in A. thaliana T3 seeds without altering other agronomic traits [17], while EgMADS3 modulates EgLPAT expression to mediate medium-chain fatty acid and total lipid synthesis [18]. In Camelina sativa, CsDGAT1 and CsDGAT2 exert a lesser effect on seed OC than CsDGAT3, yet their influence on TAG biosynthesis is sufficient to alter FA composition [19]. Overall, GPAT, LPAT and DGAT in the Kennedy pathway are universally vital for improving oil yield and quality in model plants and oil crops.

Oilseed tree peony (represented by Paeonia ostii) is a newly authorized woody oil crop in China, boasting substantial economic and nutritional value [20]. Its seeds have an oil content exceeding 20%, with unsaturated fatty acids accounting for 90%-among which α-linolenic acid (ALA), an essential fatty acid for humans that cannot be endogenously synthesized, reaches up to 40% [21]. Such a high content of ALA endows oilseed tree peony with an irreplaceable advantage in meeting the global demand for healthy edible oils, as ALA plays crucial roles in regulating blood lipids, protecting cardiovascular health, and promoting brain development. This unique nutritional superiority has made oilseed tree peony a focus of research in the field of oil crop nutrition. With genome sequencing has progressed [22], a small number of studies have focused on the cloning and functional characterization of Kennedy pathway genes in oilseed tree peony. For example, PoDGAT1 was first isolated from P. ostii seeds, and its protein sequence shares high homology with DGAT1 from other oil crops such as Glycine max and Brassica napus, and PoLPAT1 was found to be highly expressed in tree peony seeds, while its heterologous expression in yeast significantly increased total lipid content, suggesting its potential role in promoting TAG synthesis [23–25]. However, compared with well-studied oil crops, research on oilseed tree peony’s lipid synthesis pathway poorly understood. The sequence polymorphisms of core Kennedy pathway genes across germplasms lack systematic analysis, and the genetic variations responsible for variations in oil content and fatty acid composition among accessions still remain unclear. Moreover, as a perennial woody plant, oilseed tree peony suffers from inefficient conventional cross-breeding with a long cycle of over 10 years. Its large genome and abundant repetitive sequences further constrain traditional breeding, thereby urgently requiring efficient marker-assisted breeding (MAB) tools based on key lipid synthesis genes.

Marker-assisted breeding is a modern approach that uses trait-related molecular markers to screen superior genotypes at early stages, shortening breeding cycles and improving selection efficiency [26]. Association analysis, as the core of MAB, exploits population linkage disequilibrium to combine phenotypic and genetic polymorphisms and pinpoint loci associated with target traits [27]. Association analysis is classified into genome association study (GWAS) and candidate gene association study (CGAS) [28]. GWAS utilize genome-wide makers to identify functional mutation sites, whereas CGAS performs sequence-level analysis on candidate genes to reveal the genetic basis of phenotypic variation [29, 30]. In plant genetic improvement, CGAS has become an efficient and targeted strategy for mining key genes and elite variations. Previous researchers performed LD and candidate gene association analysis on 216 Camellia oleifera accessions, and detected 2 indels and 362 SNPs in four candidate genes associated with agronomic traits [31]. Lu et al. conducted CGAS on 200 soybean accessions for GmDGK7 and GmTPR in relation to oil content and fatty acid composition. SNP1 of GmDGK7 was significantly associated with oil content, while SNP2 of GmTPR correlated with oil content, stearic acid, oleic acid and linoleic acid levels [32]. Overall, these studies confirm that CGAS is a feasible and reliable approach for dissecting complex quantitative traits. They also provide a solid methodological reference for the present study to characterize sequence polymorphisms of core Kennedy pathway genes and unravel the genetic basis of variations in oil content and fatty acid composition across oilseed tree peony germplasms.

Despite the conserved roles of Kennedy pathway genes, the genetic variations of GPAT, LPAT, and DGAT and their associations with lipid traits in oilseed tree peony remain unclear. Leveraging an association panel of 141 diverse accessions, this study integrated phenotypic variation analysis with CGAS. Based on prior transcriptome screening, PoGPAT9, PoLPAT2, and PoDGAT2 were identified as core candidates for functional characterization and sequence polymorphism analysis. By evaluating SNPs and population structures, we performed association mapping to identify superior alleles contributing to enhanced oil and fatty acid accumulation. These findings are expected to lay a theoretical foundation and provide technical support for molecular marker-assisted breeding, facilitating the development of high-oil tree peony varieties and the industrial growth of this unique crop.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Association Panel: Mature seeds from 141 tree peony accessions (4 foreign, 45 Northwest China, and 92 Central Plains varieties) were collected in 2020 from the Peony Resource Garden of Luoyang Academy of Agriculture and Forestry Sciences (112°28’E, 34°28’N). For each accession, seeds were harvested from 3 to 5 representative plants of uniform age and blended into a unified mixed sample. Three subsamples were taken from the mixture, and each subsample was analyzed in triplicate for oil content and fatty acid components. Detailed variety information is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Gene Cloning and Expression Analysis: Samples were derived from 16-year-old P. ×suffruticosa ‘Zi Guang Ge’ plants at the China National Botanical Garden (116°12’E, 39°59’N). Seeds were sampled at 10, 30, 60, 75, and 105 days after pollination (DAP) starting in April 2021. Harvested seeds were immediately wrapped in foil, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C for RNA extraction.

Determination of FAs and OC

The OC was determined by low-field nuclear magnetic resonance (LF-NMR) using a Niumag PQ001 analyzer (Niumag, Suzhou, China) equipped with a 0.5 T permanent magnet and a 25 mm probe coil [33]. Dried seeds were analyzed in triplicate at a constant probe temperature of 32 °C. The CPMG pulse sequence was applied with the following parameters: TW = 4000, NS = 16, TE = 0.35, NECH = 6000. Calibration was performed using a standard curve generated from a series of known masses of pure tree peony seed oil (purity > 99%). The OC was quantified based on the linear relationship between the total T2 relaxation signal intensity and oil mass (R2 > 0.99).

Total lipids were extracted from 0.1 g of ground kernels using chloroform–methanol (1:2, v/v) at 4 ℃ for 1 h [33]. For transmethylation, the extracted lipids were incubated with sulfuric acid/methanol (5:95, v/v) at 90℃ for 60 min. The resulting fatty acid methyl esters (FAMEs) were recovered with n-pentane, evaporated under nitrogen, and re-dissolved in n-hexane for GC-MS analysis.

FAMEs were analyzed using a Shimadzu GC-MS system with an HP-88 capillary column (30 m×0.25 mm, 0.20 μm). Helium was the carrier gas (1.0 mL/min). The oven temperature started at 100 °C (2 min), rose to 230℃ at 15℃/min, and held for 5 min. The injector was set at 250℃ (10:1 split). FAs were identified via the NIST05 Library and quantified using external standard curves. Standard curves of five FAs were constructed using standard solutions prepared at a series of concentrations: 1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.375, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625, 0.03125, 0.015625, and 0.007813 mg/mL. An aliquot of 1.0 mL of each standard solution was transferred into a brown sample vial for further analysis. Standards were analyzed together with experimental samples, and the concentration of each fatty acid in the samples was calculated based on the standard curves and the peak area ratio of each component.

Cloning of candidate gene

Candidate genes PoGPAT9, PoLPAT2, and PoDGAT2 were identified from our laboratory’s transcriptome database, which was generated using the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform and assembled via Trinity. Total RNA was extracted from frozen seeds using the HiPure Plant RNA Mini Kit (Magen, China). Full-length cDNAs were amplified using Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme, China). The PCR conditions were: 95℃ for 3 min; 35 cycles of 95℃ for 15 s, 58℃ for 15 s, and 72℃ for 1 min; followed by 72℃ for 5 min. Amplified fragments were ligated into the pEASY-Blunt Simple Vector (TransGen, China) and transformed into DH5α cells. Positive clones were validated through Sanger sequencing (Sangon, China) after BLAST analysis against the NCBI database to confirm CDS regions.

Transcriptome data and quantitative RT-qPCR analysis

Transcriptome data were generated from seeds at five developmental stages (10, 30, 60, 75, and 105 days after pollination, DAP) via Illumina sequencing platform. Raw sequencing reads were processed using the standard bioinformatics pipeline: low-quality reads (Phred score < 20), adapter sequences, and contaminating reads were removed to obtain clean reads. Clean reads were then subjected to de novo transcriptome assembly using Trinity software to generate unigenes. Gene expression levels from the transcriptome data were quantified as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) and log₂-transformed for heatmap visualization.

For validation, RT-qPCR was performed using seeds at the same stages with three biological and technical replicates. Total RNA (1 µg) was reverse-transcribed using HiScript III RT SuperMix (+ gDNA wiper) (Vazyme, China). RT-qPCR was conducted on a StepOne system (Applied Biosystems, USA) using 2×M5 HiPer SYBR Green mixture (Mei5bio, China) with the program: 95 °C for 3 min, then 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s. The Tubulin gene served as the internal control, and relative expression was calculated using the 2-△△CT method. Primers are listed in Table S2.

Analysis of protein physicochemical properties and chromosome localization

The physicochemical properties (molecular weight, isoelectric point, instability index, aliphatic index, and grand average of hydropathicity [GRAVY]) of the proteins encoded by PoGPAT9, PoLPAT2, and PoDGAT2 were predicted using ProtParam software (http://www.expasy.org/tools/protparam.html). Online tools were used for functional characterization: SignalP 5.0 (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/SignalP-5.0/) for signal peptide prediction, NetPhos 3.1 (https://servibces.healthtech.dtu.dk/service.php?NetPhos-3.1) for phosphorylation site prediction, TMHMM 2.0 (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/service. php? TMHMM-2.0) for transmembrane structure prediction, ProtScale (https://web.expasy.org/cgi-bin/protscale/protscale.pl) for hydrophobicity analysis, and SOPMA (http://npsa-pbilibcp.fr/cg-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=npsa_sopma.html.) for secondary structure prediction.

Chromosome localization and phylogenetic tree analysis

Based on the identified Peony GPATs, DGATs, and LPATs family transcriptome main sentences and gene annotation files, the Gene Location function of TBtools was utilized to visualize the chromosomal localization of 18 genes. These genes were then named sequentially from left to right across chromosomes 1 through 5.

Perform protein alignment on NCBI using the obtained amino acid sequences of PoGPAT9, PoLPAT2, and PoDGAT2, genome sequence of A. thaliana was downloaded from the TAIR database (https://www.arabidopsis.org/). Amino acid sequences of GPATs, LPATs, and DGATs gene families in tree peony were retrieved using TBtools software. The gene family amino acid sequences of GPAT related species referenced Zhu et al. [34], Safder et al. [35], and Xu et al. [36]; the amino acid sequences of the gene families of LPAT related species are referenced to Arroyo-Carlo et al. [37]; the amino acid sequences of DGAT related species refer to Yin et al. [24]. The phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using the Maximum Likelihood (ML) method in MEGA X (v10.0). The best-fit amino acid substitution model (e.g., JTT + G+I) was identified based on the lowest Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) scores. Branch support was evaluated with 1000 bootstrap replicates. The final tree was visualized and annotated using iTOL (v6.0).

Analysis of gene structure, conserved motifs, and conserved domains

The structures of introns, exons, and non-coding regions of the 18 gene family members were predicted using the NCBI GenBank database, and visualized using the “Visualize Gene Structure (Basic)” function in TBtools. Conserved motifs (MOTIFs) of the protein sequences were predicted using the MEME Suite website (https://meme-suite.org/meme/) with the following parameters: number of motifs = 10, motif width = 6–50 amino acids. The prediction results were visualized using TBtools software. Conserved domains of the protein sequences were identified using the Batch CDD Search tool on NCBI, and the results were visualized using “Visualize NCBI CDD Domain Pattern” function in TBtools.

Prediction of promoter cis-acting elements and collinearity analysis

The 2000 bp upstream sequences of the 18 candidate genes were obtained through TBtools. Cis-acting elements in the promoter regions were predicted using the online tool PlantCARE (https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/). Duplicate elements, unannotated elements, and TATA/CAAT boxes were excluded, and the remaining functional elements were visualized using TBtools software.

Genome sequences and annotation information files of A. thaliana, G. max, A. hypogaea, and Z. mays were downloaded from the Ensembl Plants database (https://plants.ensembl.org/). Collinearity analysis between tree peony and these four species was performed using the “Dual Systeny Plot for MCscanX” function in TBtools, and collinearity maps were generated.

Sequence polymorphism, population structure, candidate gene association analysis

Sequencing results of candidate gene from 141 tree peony accessions were obtained by PCR and proofread via ClustalX 2.1. Subsequently, all fragments were assembled and corrected to intact full-length sequences using DNAMAN software. Multiple sequence alignment was then performed in MEGA 11 software, Automatic SNP calling and nucleotide variation detection were carried out based on the aligned sequences. Low-quality sites and sequencing errors were manually filtered to obtain validated and reliable SNP loci for subsequent analyses.

The aligned sequences were imported into DnaSP 5.1 software to calculate nucleotide diversity (π) and theta (θ) values. Then, the aligned *.fasta files were converted to *.aln format using ClustalX 2.1 software, then to *.xmfa format using xmfaConvertor software, and finally to *.str format using the xmfa2struct.bat batch processing program. The *.str files of candidate gene were merged and edited using Microsoft Excel. Population structure analysis was performed using Structure 2.3.4 software with the following parameters: Burnin Period = 100,000, Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) iterations = 100,000, K value = 1–10, and 10 replicates per K value. The optimal K value was determined using the Structure Harvester online tool (https://taylor0.biology.ucla.edu/structureHarvester/).

Phenotypic data of 8 oil-related traits (OC, PA, SA, LA, OA, ALA, TFA and UFA), sequence alignment files of candidate gene, and population structure data were imported into Tassel 5.0 software. Genotype data were filtered to exclude SNPs with a minor allele frequency (MAF) < 0.05. Population structure was used as a covariate, and association analysis between SNPs and phenotypic traits was performed using the mixed linear model (MLM). False discovery rate (FDR) correction based on the Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) algorithm was performed for all SNP association tests to control false positive results. SNPs with FDR < 0.05 were identified as significantly associated loci, and the coefficient of determination (R2) was calculated to evaluate the contribution of each SNP to phenotypic variation.

Data analysis

Phenotypic data of oil traits were processed using Excel 2016 and presented as mean ± standard deviation. Due to the different units of oil content and fatty acid composition, the data were normalized prior to further analysis. Correlation analysis, principal component analysis (PCA), cluster analysis, and significance of difference analysis were performed using SPSS 27.0. All figures were generated using OriginPro 2024.

Results

Phenotypic variation analysis of oil-related traits in tree peony germplasm resources

FA chromatographic peaks of 141 tree peony varieties seeds form different cultivation groups were well separated, and all FAs traits were normally distributed (Supplementary Figure S1). Total fatty acids (TFA) of seed lipids mainly included five major FAs. Among these, saturated fatty acids (SFA) includes PA and SA, unsaturated fatty acids (UFA) includes OA, LA, and ALA. The content of TFA ranged from 186.74 mg/g (‘Long Yuan Hong’) to 301.82 mg/g (‘Sai Huang Hou’), and the content of UFA ranged from 181.27 mg/g (‘Ceng Zhong Xiao’) to 293.47 mg/g (‘Sai Huang Hou’). The content ranges of five major FAs were as follows: PA ranged from 2.31 mg/g (‘Qing Long Yao Jin Mo’) to 6.47 mg/g (‘Mo Zi Ying Jin’), SA ranged from 0.81 mg/g (‘Qing Long Yao Jin Mo’) to 3.96 mg/g (‘Xiang Yu’), OA ranged from 26.73 mg/g (‘Ye Guang Bei’) to 87.79 mg/g (‘Sai Huang Hou’), LA ranged from 42.24 mg/g (‘Zi Yao Tai’) to 81.07 mg/g (‘Qi Zhuang Shan He’), and ALA ranged from 80.73 mg/g (‘Long Yuan Hong’) to 157.55 mg/g (‘Zi Qi Dong Lai’). The oil content (OC) of 141 germplasms ranged from 15.46% (‘Choujuraku’) to 26.30% (‘Hu Die Ying Ri’), with an average of 21.56% and coefficient of variation 10.69%, with detailed FA composition of each cultivar provided in Supplementary Table S3. The proportion of each fatty acid of 141 varieties is shown in Supplementary Table S4.

After normalizing the OC and FA data from 141 tree peony germplasm accessions, correlation analysis was first performed (Fig. 1A). The results indicated that OC was positively correlated with SA and LA, but weakly correlated with other FAs. OA, and TFA and UFA was positively correlated with all FAs except OC (Supplementary Table S5). Further, principal component analysis (PCA) extracted three principal components (PCs) with eigenvalues > 1, whose cumulative variance contribution rate reached 79.45% (PC1: 48.18%, PC2: 18.63% and PC3: 12.64% (Fig. 1B, Supplementary Table S6). Specifically, PC1 was mainly determined by PA, SA, OA and LA, PC2 was dominated by ALA, UFA and TFA, and PC3 was primarily associated with OC. Then, systematic cluster analysis of oil content and FA composition in 141 tree peony germplasm accessions was performed (Fig. 1C). Among all germplasms, ‘Sai Huang Hou’ showed the best overall performance in lipid traits, ranking as the optimal germplasm resource (Fig. 1D, Supplementary Table S7). The population clustered into 4 groups, the number of varieties in each cluster was 7, 36, 45, and 53. Moreover, analysis of significant differences revealed substantial variations in UFA components and TFA content among clusters, reflecting obvious genetic differentiation in lipid metabolism traits across germplasm groups (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Table S8).

Genome-wide identification and phylogenetic analysis of oils synthesis-related genes (PoGPAT, PoLPAT, and PoDGAT)

Based on P. ostii genomic data, 18 oils synthesis-related genes in Kennedy pathway were identified, including 3 PoDGATs, 5 PoLPATs, and 10 PoGPATs. Chromosomal localization analysis showed that 17 genes were randomly distributed across 4 chromosomes, while Pos.gene71803 was located on an unassigned scaffold (Fig. 2A). Physicochemical property prediction revealed that the proteins encoded by these genes contained 310 to 571 amino acids, with molecular weights of 32.8-63.61 kDa, isoelectric points of 6.05–9.44, instability index of 32.95–51.65, and aliphatic indices of 37.39-113.31. Most proteins had positive hydropathicity (GRAVY) values (-0.281 to 0.411), indicating a predominance of hydrophobic proteins, and subcellular localization predictions showed that most proteins were localized to the plasma membrane, with a few distributed in and ER (Supplementary Table S9). The phylogenetic tree analysis showed that PoDGAT, PoLPAT, and PoGPAT proteins shared high homology with their homologs in other species, suggesting conserved biological functions across plants (Fig. 2B, Supplementary Table S10).

Further, the gene structure, protein domains, conserved motifs, and phylogenetic tree of PoDGAT, PoLPAT, and PoGPAT families were analyzed. 10 relatively conserved motifs were obtained from these 18 proteins, and the length of these motifs ranged from 29 to 50 (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Table S11). After the NCBI-CDD database annotation, it was found that Pos.gene8005, Pos.gene77683, Pos.gene42408, and Pos.gene24967 shared the PLN02177 superfamily domain, this domain was highly conserved in the GPAT family. These results indicated that different gene family members had relatively stable structures in sequence, but there were also partial differences, with deletion or increase. To explore the evolutionary mechanism of these 18 genes, collinearity analysis was performed between tree peony and four representative plants (A. thaliana, Arachis hypogaea, B. napus, G. max) (Fig. 3A). The results showed 5 collinear gene pairs between tree peony and A. thaliana, 22 pairs between tree peony and A.hypogaea, 7 pairs between tree peony and B. napus, and 23 pairs between tree peony and G. max. The presence of collinear genes among different species suggested evolutionary conservation from a common ancestor, while significant differences in the number of collinear pairs reflected evolutionary divergence due to species differentiation.

Expression profiling of 18 oils synthesis-related genes

Transcriptome data analysis of tree peony seeds at different developmental stages was performed to explore the expression patterns of the 18 identified genes (Fig. 3B). The result revealed that Pos.gene65741, Pos.gene42408, and Pos.gene50976 were gradually upregulated during seed development, and Pos.gene77683 and Pos.gene71803 showed stable expression across four developmental stages. Pos.gene8005, Pos.gene7995, Pos.gene79002, Pos.gene51075, Pos.gene5802, Pos.gene1953, and Pos.gene57740 were significantly downregulated with seed maturation. Most genes exhibited the highest expression in the early stage of seed development, followed by a gradual downregulation trend, indicating their critical roles in the seed filling stage.

Further, 3 genes were named PoGPAT9 (Pos.gene51075), PoLPAT2 (Pos.gene5802) and PoDGAT2 (Pos.gene1953) with lengths of 1299 bp, 1356 bp and 1245 bp were cloned. Protparam software was used to predict the physicochemical properties of proteins encoded by the genes PoGPAT9、PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2 in tree peony (Supplementary Table S12). RT-qPCR analysis was performed on seed samples at 10 d, 30 d, 60 d, 75 d and 105 d after pollination (Fig. 3C), showing that PoGPAT9 and PoLPAT2 first decreased and then gradually increased as seeds matured, while PoDGAT2 first increased, then decreased, and finally gradually increased again, with the highest expression at 105 d, followed by 75 d.

Polymorphism, LD, and population structure analysis of key candidate genes

Polymorphism analysis was performed for PoLPAT2, PoDGAT2, and PoGPAT9 across 141 tree peony germplasm accessions. Multiple primer pairs were designed targeting the CDS sequences of the three genes. However, the amplification efficiency of primers designed for PoGPAT9 was consistently unsatisfactory, thus, only PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2 were included in further analyses. The results showed that PoLPAT2 contained 31 polymorphic sites (27 SNPs and 4 Indels) with nucleotide diversity π = 0.00413, and θ = 0.00409; PoDGAT2 had 23 polymorphic sites with π = 0.0042, and θ = 0.00425. Then, linkage disequilibrium analysis of SNPs in PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2 were performed for 141 tree peony genes (Fig. 4A). The results indicated that low in both genes, and the degree of linkage disequilibrium gradually decreased with increasing nucleotide sequence distance. Population structure analysis showed that the K value peaked at K = 2, dividing the association population into two subgroups (Fig. 4B).

Association analysis between candidate genes and oil-related traits

Association analyses was conducted between the candidate genes and 8 oil-related phenotypic traits (Table 1). A total of 16 16 significant marker-trait associations were identified, involving 6 phenotypic traits and 9 SNP loci. For PoLPAT2, 6 significant associations were detected, with the phenotypic variation explained (R²) ranging from 2.52% (OC) to 5.24% (LA), averaging 3.48%. For PoDGAT2, 11 significant associations were found, with R² ranging from 2.78% (LA) to 8.22% (oleic acid, OA), averaging 4.46%.

The effects of different genotypes at the significantly associated loci on oil-related traits were further analyzed using multiple comparisons. The results revealed that, among the associated loci, three showed significant phenotypic differences between their alternative alleles. For PoDGAT2, the homozygous genotype at the 759 locus was significantly associated with both LA and ALA contents. Compared with the TT genotype, the AA genotype had an average LA content 5.181 mg/g higher, while the average ALA content was 10.304 mg/g lower. In addition, the AA homozygous genotype at the 867 bp locus was associated with OA content, and the presence of the C allele (CC/CT genotype) was linked to higher OA levels than TT genotype. For PoLPAT2, the homozygous genotype at the 485 bp mutation locus associated with OC had an average OC 1.396% higher than the heterozygous genotype (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Tree peony is an important ornamental and economic plant native to China, valued not only for its aesthetics but also for its rich seed oil, which contains abundant UFA such as ALA and other UFAs [22]. ALA is an essential ω-3 polyunsaturated FA that acts as precursor for docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), making it crucial for human brain and retinal development [38]. Therefore, the selection of germplasm resources with high-quality tree peony seed oil composition is a primary objective in tree peony breeding. However, FAs composition is a complex quantitative trait controlled by polygenic inheritance and subject to the interaction between genotype and environmental factors [39]. To explore the genetic regulation mechanisms of oil content and fatty acid composition in tree peony seeds, this study systematically characterized the phenotypic variation in OC and FA composition across a diverse panel of tree peony germplasms. It then identified and cloned key genes within the Kennedy pathway, analyzing their expression patterns during seed development. Finally, candidate gene association analysis successfully detected significant SNP loci associated with oil-related traits, thereby elucidating the genetic basis of lipid biosynthesis in tree peony. These findings provide crucial molecular markers and theoretical support for the marker-assisted breeding of high-oil and high-quality tree peony varieties.

Phenotypic variation analysis and cultivar characteristic comparison of oil content and fatty acid traits in germplasm resources serve as the fundamental premise for exploring high-quality genetic resources of oil crops, as they systematically reveal the genetic diversity of target traits and directly provide precise targets for directional breeding of oil-rich or functionally superior varieties [40–42]. In this study, an association population consisting of 141 tree peony cultivars was employed to determine OC and FAs, followed by an analysis of their variation characteristics. The results revealed that the oil content of different tree peony cultivars ranged from 15.46% to 26.30%, TFA ranged from 186.75 to 301.82 mg/g, with UFA accounting for the majority of the lipid profile, and ALA constituting 41.77% to 64.73% of TFA. The coefficient of variation of OC and FAs ranged from 10.49% (OC) to 34.15% (SA). This broad variation exceeded the ranges reported in previous studies on tree peony germplasm, such as those observed in nine wild tree peony species, 30 tree peony cultivars, and 448 P. ostii accessions [22, 43, 44], indicating the rich genetic diversity of the association panel used in this study. Furthermore, the extensive phenotypic variation observed in this study established a solid foundation and clear direction for the genetic improvement of oilseed tree peony. Notably, ‘Sai Huang Hou’ emerged as a superior germplasm, exhibiting the highest levels of TFA (301.82 mg/g), UFA (293.48 mg/g), and LA (87.79 mg/g). Its balanced and high-performance profile makes it an excellent candidate for breeding high-yield varieties with comprehensive oil quality. Additionally, the identification of cultivars with exceptionally high OC and ALA proportions, such as ‘Hu Die Ying Ri’ (OC, 26.30%) and ‘Zi Yao Tai’ (ALA, 64.73%), confirmed significant potential for further enhancing oil yield and nutritional value through selection and hybridization. In conclusion, this study not only validates the breeding potential of the current germplasm but also pinpoints specific cultivars that can be directly utilized as core parents to accelerate the development of next-generation oilseed tree peony varieties.

Glyceride molecules are the basic structural elements of cell membrane system and the main form of seed energy storage [45]. Kennedy first comprehensively described the individual enzymatic steps leading to the biosynthesis of major phospholipid molecules and TAG, describing the homonymous pathway of glycerol synthesis has always been one of the hallmarks of lipid biochemistry. Previous studies have shown that Kennedy pathway has been proved to be crucial for seed oil accumulation [46]. As core acyltransferase families in this pathway, GPAT, LPAT, and DGAT mediate acylation reactions at different stages, respectively, and their synergistic effects directly determine the synthesis efficiency and accumulation of TAG. In this study, a systematic analysis was performed on the PoGPAT, PoLPAT, and PoDGAT gene families in tree peony. Based on the genomic data of P. ostii, 18 oils synthesis genes related to the Kennedy pathway were identified, including 10 PoGPATs, 3 PoDGATs, and 5 PoLPATs. This member distribution was consistent with that in A. thaliana, indicating the conserved expansion of these gene families during plant evolution. Most of these proteins were hydrophobic (GRAVY: -0.287 to 0.411) and localized to the plasma membrane or endoplasmic reticulum, which was consistent with the functions of acyltransferases. This was because the ER was the primary site for TAG synthesis, while members localized to the plasma membrane were involved in membrane lipid synthesis or signaling pathways [8, 47]. Collinearity analysis revealed 5, 22, 7, and 23 gene pairs between P. ostii and A. thaliana, A. hypogaea, B. napus, and G. max, respectively. These shared collinear genes confirmed the common ancestral origin of these species and the evolutionary stability of the lipid synthesis pathway. In summary, this study systematically characterizes the P. ostii PoGPAT, PoLPAT and PoDGAT gene families. Their conserved distribution, structure, localization, and collinearity, support the Kennedy pathway’s core role in seed oils synthesis.

Further, transcriptome data analysis revealed the differential expression patterns of 18 candidate genes at different developmental stages of tree peony seeds, which provided an important basis for screening key genes related to oils synthesis. Based on the transcriptome expression characteristics, we successfully cloned three key candidate genes related to oils synthesis, namely PoGPAT9 (Pos.gene51075), PoLPAT2 (Pos.gene5802), and PoDGAT2 (Pos.gene1953). Analysis of the expression levels of these three genes in seeds at different developmental stages revealed that the expression levels of PoGPAT9 and PoLPAT2 showed a trend of decreasing first and then gradually increasing with seed maturation. This pattern was consistent with the expression profile of VfGPAT9 in Vernicia fordii reported by Li et al. [48] and BnLPAT2 in B. napus described by Zhang et al. [49], which was also aligned with the dynamic changes in the process of oils synthesis and accumulation in V. fordii and Perilla frutescens respectively. As for PoDGAT2, its expression level increased from 10 d to 30 d after seed development, decreased slightly at 60 d, and then rose progressively, reaching the highest level at 105 d. This expression pattern was consistent with that of PrDGAT2 documented by Yang et al. [50], suggesting that PoDGAT2 might be associated with the massive synthesis of plant oils and the accumulation intensity of α-linolenic acid. On this basis, association analysis of the three genes with 8 oils synthesis-related phenotypic traits was conducted.

As the development of molecular markers and the rapid advancement of sequencing technologies, association analysis has been widely applied in multiple plants. Previous studies identified 62 genome-wide significant SNPs associated with FA composition in 520 B. napus germplasms [39]. Sung et al. [51] detected 43 genomic regions significantly associated with FAs in 621 G. max germplasms. Similarly, 20 pathways significantly related to fat concentration were also found in 487 Pisum Sativum [52]. Lou et al. [53] identified 41 SNPs from 34 transcripts in Torreya grandis without a reference genome based on transcriptome-referenced association study (TRAS), several of which were significantly associated with fruit traits. In tree peony research, such analyses have mostly focused on ornamental and yield-related traits using SSR markers [54–57], few studies have conducted association analysis based on SNP markers. In this study, we amplified and sequenced PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2 in an association population of 141 tree peony cultivars for the first time, using seed oil content and fatty acid profiles as phenotypic traits. Sequence analysis revealed 27/23 SNPs and 4/0 Indels in the CDS region of PoLPAT2/PoDGAT2, with an average of 1 SNP per 44/52 bp and a nucleotide diversity (π) value of 0.00413/0.00422, respectively. These results indicate that both PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2 exhibit a moderate level of nucleotide diversity. Previous studies have shown that woody plants generally have low levels of linkage disequilibrium (LD), and outcrossing plants exhibit lower LD than self-pollinating plants [58]. As a facultative outcrossing woody species, tree peony displays low LD levels in the present association population, which is consistent with the findings of Wu et al. [59] and Cui et al. [60]. In association analysis, for PoLPAT2, a total of 6 marker-trait associations were identified, involving 5 SNP loci and 4 phenotypic traits; for PoDGAT2, 11 marker-trait associations were detected, consisting of 4 SNP loci and 7 phenotypic traits. Notably, some SNP loci were associated with multiple traits, while certain traits were linked to multiple loci, which may be attributed to the inherent nature of the traits or the presence of pleiotropy [61, 62]. Association analyses of complex quantitative traits in plants typically explain only a small proportion of phenotypic variation [63]. The 5 SNP loci in PoLPAT2 accounted for 2.52%–5.24% of the phenotypic variation, whereas the 4 loci in PoDGAT2 explained 2.78%–8.22% of the variation. These findings confirm that quantitative traits are controlled by multiple minor-effect genes [64]. Furthermore, statistical analysis was performed on oil-related traits corresponding to different genotypes of the associated SNP loci within PoLPAT2 and PoDGAT2, leading to the identification of 5 elite allelic variants. For PoLPAT2, the mutant genotype at locus 485 was associated with a 1.396% higher oil content compared to the wild-type genotype. Similarly, the mutant genotypes at loci 24 and 521 correlated with increased linoleic acid content. For PoDGAT2, the mutant genotype at locus 484 was linked to a 3.81 mg/g higher linoleic acid content relative to the wild type, and the mutant genotype at locus 759 was associated with a 10.30 mg/g higher α-linolenic acid content. In summary, the identification of SNPs related to the OC and FAs of tree peony seeds provides new insights and perspectives on the genetic mechanism of oil accumulation in tree peony seeds, and these loci hold significant potential for application in molecular marker-assisted breeding of oil-used tree peony.

Conclusion

To explore the genetic regulation mechanisms of oil content and fatty acid composition in tree peony seeds, this study used 141 tree peony germplasms with diverse genetic backgrounds as materials to systematically analyze the phenotypic characteristics of their OC and FA composition. Significant variations were found in traits such as TFA and UFA, with ALA accounting for 41.77%-64.73% of TFA. From the tree peony genome, 18 Kennedy pathway oils synthesis-related genes were identified, and three key genes (PoGPAT9, PoLPAT2, PoDGAT2) were cloned and their expression patterns clarified. Association analysis identified 16 SNP loci significantly associated with 6 oil-related traits, and 6 from PoLPAT2 explaining 2.52%-5.24% of phenotypic variation, 11 from PoDGAT2 explaining 2.78%-8.22%. These findings enhance our understanding of the genetic regulation of lipid metabolism in oilseed tree peony and provide crucial molecular markers and theoretical support for the marker-assisted breeding of high-oil and high-quality tree peony varieties.

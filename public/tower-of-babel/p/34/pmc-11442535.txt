Cohomogeneity one solitons for the isometric flow of G2-structures
Authors: Thomas A Ivey, Spiro Karigiannis
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe consider the existence of cohomogeneity one solitons for the isometric flow of G2-structures on the following classes of torsion-free G2-manifolds: the Euclidean R7 with its standard G2-structure, metric cylinders over Calabi–Yau 3-folds, metric cones over nearly Kähler 6-manifolds, and the Bryant–Salamon G2-manifolds. In all cases we establish existence of global solutions to the isometric soliton equations, and determine the asymptotic behaviour of the torsion. In particular, existence of shrinking isometric solitons on R7 is proved, giving support to the likely existence of type I singularities for the isometric flow. In each case, the study of the soliton equation reduces to a particular nonlinear ODE with a regular singular point, for which we provide a careful analysis. Finally, to simplify the derivation of the relevant equations in each case, we first establish several useful Riemannian geometric formulas for a general class of cohomogeneity one metrics on total spaces of vector bundles which should have much wider application, as such metrics arise often as explicit examples of special holonomy metrics. Keywords: G2-structures, Isometric flow, Solitons, Cohomogeneity one

Introduction

Let (M7,φ) be a 7-manifold equipped with a G2-structure φ, inducing a Riemannian metric g, an orientation, and a Hodge dual 4-form ψ=⋆φφ. The torsion tensor T of φ is a 2-tensor which measures the failure of φ to be parallel, and hence the failure of the induced metric g to have holonomy contained in G2. As there are many G2-structures on M which induce the same metric g, it is natural to consider a “best” representative of the isometry class [φ]g of G2-structures on M inducing the metric g. This problem was first considered by Grigorian [10]. He showed that for M compact, if we restrict to the isometry class [φ]g, then the negative gradient flow of the functional ∫M|T|2volg yields the geometric flow

called the isometric flow of G2-structures. In particular, the Euler–Lagrange equation for critical points of this functional, restricted to the isometry class [φ]g, is the equation divT=0, corresponding to divergence-free torsion. It makes sense to consider the isometric flow (1.1) even in the noncompact case, as a natural geometric flow of G2-structures all inducing the same Riemannian metric g.

Analytic aspects of the isometric flow have been studied by various authors. Bagaglini [1] established short-time existence and uniqueness. Both Grigorian [11] and Dwivedi–Gianniotis–Karigiannis [5] derived Shi-type estimates giving bounds on higher derivatives of the torsion in terms of bounds on the torsion, proved almost monotonicity formulas and ϵ-regularity theorems, and established stability (long time existence and convergence to a G2-structure with divergence-free torsion given initial conditions with sufficiently small torsion). Grigorian [11] also obtained more refined stability results when the isometry class [φ]g of the initial G2-structure admits a torsion-free representative. Dwivedi–Gianniotis–Karigiannis [5] also considered the structure of the singular set and established the relation between type-I singularities and shrinking solitons. (See [12] for a survey of results on the isometric flow.)

Recent work of Fadel–Loubeau–Moreno–Sá Earp [8] generalizes the isometric flow to any H-structure on a Riemannian n-manifold, where H is a Lie subgroup of SO(n). Dwivedi–Loubeau–Sá Earp [7] formulate a theory of isometric solitons and self-similarity for general H-structures, and [8] extends this formulation to a larger class of flows, while Fowdar–Sá Earp [9] construct isometric solitons for quaternionic-Kähler structures on R8. Finally, in another recent preprint, Dwivedi–Gianniotis–Karigiannis [6] show that a natural coupling of the isometric flow with the Ricci flow of metrics has good short-time existence properties.

The present paper is concerned with the analysis of particular examples of solitons for the isometric flow when the initial G2-structure φ0 is torsion-free, and such that the soliton solutions to the flow are of cohomogeneity one. More precisely, we consider the following torsion-free G2-structures:

Remark 1.3

When (M,φ¯) is R7 equipped with the standard torsion-free G2-structure inducing the Euclidean metric, then it was shown in [5, Remark 5.21] that the non-existence of shrinking isometric solitons on (R7,φ¯) would imply that type I singularities of the isometric flow do not occur. This is because appropriately rescaling about a type I singularity of the isometric flow yields a shrinking isometric soliton on the Euclidean R7 with the standard G2-structure.

As we prove in Theorem 3.17 that such shrinking isometric solitons do indeed exist, the occurrence of type I singularities cannot be excluded. Indeed, in [8, Example 2.17], the first known example of a finite-time singularity for the isometric flow is presented. It is not clear if this singularity is of type I, but if it is, then it would be interesting to see which shrinking isometric soliton it induces on R7. □

Remark 2.16

In (2.13) we have changed some notation from [5, Section 2.5]. Namely, we have changed their X to Y because we use X in the description (f, X) of a member of the isometry class [φ¯]g, and we have also replaced their c by 2c to avoid many factors of 12 in our subsequent computations. □

The isometric soliton (φ,Y,c) is called shrinking, steady, or expanding if c is positive, zero, or negative, respectively. This nomenclature is chosen in analogy with solitons for the Ricci flow. Note that despite this, the associated self-similar solution φ(t) of the isometric flow induces the same Riemannian metric g for all time, so the metric is not changing. The action of the scaling of φ and the action by diffeomorphisms combine in such a way to ensure that the metric g of φ(t) is independent of t, although the G2-structure φ(t) definitely does evolve in time. The precise correspondence between the triple (φ,Y,c) and the self-similar solution of the isometric flow is explained in [5, Lemma 2.17], but we do not have need for it here.

Note also that since the metric g is fixed, we do not have the freedom to scale φ. It then follows from (2.13) that we cannot scale Y or c either. This is in contrast to solitons for the Ricci flow, where one can always scale the metric to ensure c∈{-1,0,1}.

Because we fix the class [φ¯]g of G2-structures inducing the same Riemannian metric g, we can describe φ by (2.2) in terms of the pair (f, X). Thus, the data of an isometric soliton consists of the quadruple (f, X, Y, c) where (f, X) is determined up to an overall sign and satisfies f2+|X|2=1.

Formulas for a particular class of Riemannian metrics

In this section we consider the total space of a real vector bundle as a smooth manifold, equipped with a special class of Riemannian metrics. This situation includes as particular cases all the torsion-free G2-manifolds we consider in this paper, namely cylinders over Calabi-Yau manifolds, cones over nearly Kähler manifolds (including the Euclidean space R7 over the round sphere S6), and the Bryant–Salamon G2-manifolds. The induced Riemannian metrics in these examples can all be considered as particular cases of this special class of metrics. Treating them all in this way allows us to derive general formulas for certain Laplacians and other geometric quantities which we can then apply to all of these examples.

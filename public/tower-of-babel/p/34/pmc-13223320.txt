Efficiency optimization of enterprise resource planning based on deep reinforcement learning: achieving more efficient business process automation
Authors: Dong Xi, Vicky Nie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems play a key role in modern enterprise management. Business Process Management (BPM), as a core subsystem of ERP, is responsible for coordinating and automating enterprise workflows. However, traditional BPM systems struggle with efficient task scheduling under dynamic load changes. This often leads to reduced execution efficiency, especially under heavy loads. When the load exceeds the processing capacity of the engine cluster, it can cause severe Service Level Agreement (SLA) violations. To address the above issues, this study optimizes the task scheduling architecture for Business Process Management (BPM) in cloud environments from the perspectives of system modeling and scheduling mechanism optimization. The service-level-aware task scheduling process is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). On this basis, a Service-Tiered Request Scheduling (SRS) framework is constructed. The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is adopted as the policy learner to realize adaptive scheduling decisions for tasks of different service levels. In service-tiered load tests, results show that under normal load, the Service Level Agreement (SLA) violation rate of SRS-PPO is 6.6%, slightly higher than that of the traditional Earliest Deadline First (EDF) algorithm. Nevertheless, the Quality of Service (QoS) over-provisioning rate of SRS-PPO is reduced by 74.57% compared with EDF. Under heavy-load conditions, the SLA violation rate of SRS-PPO is 21.33%, which outperforms the comparison methods and is 7.04% lower than that of EDF. Its QoS over-provisioning rate is 10.12%, a decrease of 60.81% compared with EDF (p < 0.05). Experimental results demonstrate that SRS-PPO has application potential in handling SLA violations in high-load environments, improving task scheduling flexibility and resource utilization efficiency.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47766-2.Keywords: Business process management, Task scheduling, Deep reinforcement learning, Proximal policy optimization, Service classificationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In today’s wave of digital transformation, Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems have become widely adopted across various organizations. They serve as core tools for integrating enterprise resources and improving management efficiency1,2. ERP systems provide a unified data platform and a collaborative business framework by integrating key functions such as finance, supply chain, production, and human resources through modular design3. Within the ERP system architecture, the Business Process Management (BPM) subsystem plays a central role. It coordinates task execution and data flow among different business modules, enabling end-to-end process automation and optimization4,5. However, as business complexity grows and operational environments become more uncertain, traditional BPM systems often face challenges. These include low scheduling efficiency and uneven resource allocation when dealing with dynamic load fluctuations and sudden business requests.

Most mainstream BPM task scheduling strategies currently rely on static rules or heuristic algorithms, such as Earliest Deadline First (EDF)6. These strategies perform reasonably well under low or moderate load conditions. However, under high load or sudden load spikes, they often fail to respond effectively due to a lack of real-time system awareness and adaptive capabilities. This can lead to task delays and backlogs, and even result in Service Level Agreement (SLA) violations, which severely affect operational efficiency and customer satisfaction7. Moreover, traditional strategies struggle to distinguish between requests with different service levels. They lack effective prioritization, limiting the precision and intelligence of resource utilization. In recent years, with the rapid development of deep learning and reinforcement learning (RL), scheduling methods based on Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) have drawn increasing attention. RL learns optimal strategies through continuous interaction with the environment and trial-and-error, making it especially suitable for handling scheduling problems characterized by uncertainty and dynamic changes8,9.

Based on the above background, this study starts from the actual demands of BPM task scheduling in enterprise-level ERP systems. It studies the scheduling optimization problem that balances quality of service and resource efficiency under dynamic load conditions. Different from existing studies that mainly focus on algorithm-level improvements, this study emphasizes the system modeling and decision-making mechanism design for scheduling problems in ERP business process scenarios. Specifically, the BPM task scheduling process with different service levels and SLA constraints is modeled as a service tier-aware Markov Decision Process (MDP). The trade-off between SLA violation risks and excessive resource supply is depicted under a unified framework. On this basis, the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is adopted as a policy optimization tool. A Service-Tiered Request Scheduling (SRS) method suitable for cloud-based BPM execution environments is constructed. By introducing business service level information, this method guides the scheduling strategy to make adaptive decisions in different load scenarios. Thus, it effectively reduces the SLA violation rate in high-load environments and improves the overall resource utilization efficiency of the system.

Recent related work

Review of business process scheduling

BPM, as a core component of ERP systems, is mainly responsible for modeling, executing, and monitoring business tasks. Its scheduling efficiency directly affects the overall operational performance of an enterprise. Existing literatures can be mainly classified by scheduling model types and ERP functional modules. Heuristic and rule-based scheduling methods are widely applied in traditional ERP systems. Such methods usually rely on indicators such as task priority, deadline or resource utilization for task sorting and allocation. For example, Pereira et al. (2021)10 applied heuristic algorithms to optimize incident management processes in IT service management, showing potential in improving response efficiency. However, these methods lack the scalability analysis for different enterprise and industry scenarios. Filatov and Yerokhin (2023)11 improved the multi-objective optimization algorithm R-NSGA-II for BPM applications, but did not fully consider the impact of computational complexity on real systems. They yield high computational complexity in large-scale ERP systems, which restricts the feasibility of practical deployment. Saif et al. (2023)12 explored resource scheduling using multi-agent systems in multi-cloud environments. Although they proposed an automated framework, compatibility issues with different cloud platforms remained. Olyai et al. (2024)13 combined process mining with smart factories and used heuristic algorithms to enhance process intelligence. However, their study struggled to handle enterprise-level BPM tasks with multiple priorities, high loads and complex business semantics.

In recent years, with the widespread adoption of cloud computing and microservices architecture, business process execution environments have become more distributed, elastic, and complex. Scheduling methods in distributed and cloud environments have attracted increasing attention, especially for the production, supply chain and financial modules of ERP systems. Wang et al. (2022)14 studied cloud-edge collaboration in smart grids. They optimized distributed energy resource scheduling to improve power distribution efficiency. However, the scalability and real-time scheduling performance of their method in real deployments still require further validation. Ahmed et al. (2024)15 compared energy-efficient management algorithms for task scheduling on cloud platforms. They analyzed the differences in workflow execution time and proposed optimization strategies, yet failed to provide explicit solutions for the SLA constraint guarantee of high-priority tasks. Sreelatha et al. (2025)16 proposed a joint optimization framework for resource allocation and task scheduling for multi-cloud environments. In the first stage, this method adopted a hybrid electric search and beetle swarm algorithm to optimize task scheduling. In the second stage, it improved the Seagull algorithm based on game theory to achieve load balancing, thereby improving the overall resource utilization efficiency in multi-cloud environments. However, this method was mainly based on swarm intelligence and game optimization strategies, and lacked an adaptive learning mechanism for dynamic business priorities and SLA differentiated constraints, which limited its generalization ability in complex ERP/BPM scenarios. Jiang et al. (2025)17 proposed a multi-objective task scheduling and resource allocation method based on directed acyclic graph modeling and the local search enhanced nondominated sorting genetic algorithm-II for edge heterogeneous computing environments, integrating an adaptive dynamic voltage and frequency scaling strategy. This method achieved a good balance between energy consumption and execution efficiency. Nevertheless, its optimization framework relied on static multi-objective evolutionary strategies, had limited responsiveness to real-time dynamic load changes, and did not fully consider semantic constraints and service level differences in enterprise-level business processes. Although these methods can improve efficiency in specific modules, they lack business semantic modeling for the overall ERP system and a multi-module coordination mechanism, resulting in limited usability in enterprise-level high-load environments.

Application status of RL in task scheduling

In recent years, RL, especially DRL, has been introduced into the field of task scheduling, focusing primarily on algorithm model optimization and load adaptability. In cloud computing and workflow management modules, DRL algorithms can learn optimal scheduling strategies through interaction with the environment. Lu et al. (2024)18 introduced an adaptive sharding method based on DRL. The approach optimized blockchain applications in UAV networks, improving processing efficiency and response speed. However, this method was mainly designed for blockchain tasks in unmanned aerial vehicle networks. Its scheduling optimization objectives differ greatly from the task types and business constraints of enterprise BPM systems, leading to obvious limitations in direct usability and performance guarantee in enterprise ERP/BPM scenarios. Mangalampalli et al. (2024)19 designed a DRL-based task scheduling algorithm, DRLBTSA, for cloud computing. The algorithm significantly improved scheduling efficiency. However, this algorithm did not integrate the business semantics and service level constraints of ERP/BPM systems, resulting in applicability limitations in enterprise-level applications. Lilhore et al. (2025)20 proposed a hybrid cloud resource scheduling model combining deep reinforcement learning with improved ant colony optimization and water wave algorithm. It improved resource allocation efficiency through two-stage optimization guided by real-time DRL feedback and an energy-saving mechanism. This method achieved improved performance in energy consumption control and task completion time. However, its scheduling strategy was mainly designed for general hybrid cloud scenarios and lacked systematic modeling for high-priority task guarantee and SLA differentiated management in ERP/BPM systems. Pan et al. (2025)21 proposed a deep reinforcement learning-based dual-scheduling framework for SaaS providers. It achieved collaborative optimization of QoS-aware task scheduling and load-aware elastic scaling, which reduced cloud resource leasing costs while ensuring service quality. Although this method emphasized QoS constraints, its model focused on cloud leasing cost optimization and did not thoroughly consider the coupling between multi-level task priorities and complex business semantics in enterprise-level business processes.

In terms of the management of high-priority and low-priority tasks, some studies have started to focus on the load scheduling problem with multiple service levels. Saikai et al. (2023)22 optimized agricultural resource scheduling through high-dimensional sensor feedback, demonstrating the potential of DRL in complex task environments, yet their scheduling model has limited relevance to the business logic of enterprise process tasks. Ramezani Shahidani et al. (2023)23 proposed a multi-objective load balancing method for edge-fog-cloud architectures, but failed to explicitly incorporate task priorities and SLA constraints into the model. Fan et al. (2025)24 proposed a two-agent deep reinforcement learning algorithm for edge computing scenarios with task-data decoupling. It jointly optimized service deployment, task scheduling, and resource allocation, improving the adaptive capability of the system in dynamic environments. However, this method was mainly applied to edge computing architectures and lacked targeted analysis of cross-module process collaboration and SLA constraint management in ERP systems, which limited its direct applicability in enterprise-level BPM scheduling. Therefore, existing DRL scheduling studies still lack comprehensive considerations of high-priority task guarantee, service level differences and SLA constraints in the BPM module of ERP systems.

Based on the above analysis, existing literatures can be divided into three categories by model type: heuristic/rule-based, distributed optimization-based and RL-based methods. In terms of ERP module applications, relevant studies mainly focus on production, supply chain and workflow management. In terms of optimization objectives, traditional methods prioritize execution time and energy consumption, while RL methods focus on load adaptability and efficiency improvement. However, these studies often neglect the business semantics, multi-priority requests, SLA constraints of ERP/BPM tasks and resource optimization under high load conditions, thus leaving an explicit research gap for the proposed service-tiered SRS-PPO scheduling framework.

Table 1 shows that existing studies have made some progress in scheduling algorithm optimization, load balancing, and energy consumption control. However, most methods do not systematically model business semantics, multi-priority tasks, and SLA constraints in ERP/BPM systems. In particular, the balance between resource optimization and quality-of-service guarantee under high-load scenarios has not been fully addressed. Therefore, it is necessary to construct a service hierarchical scheduling framework for enterprise-level business processes to achieve multi-service-level awareness and dynamic load adaptive optimization.

Although DRL has been successfully applied to cloud computing and workflow scheduling problems in existing studies and achieved good performance in dynamic environments, most of these studies focus on system-level load balancing or the optimization of scheduling algorithms themselves, with insufficient attention paid to the business semantic characteristics of BPM tasks in enterprise-level ERP systems. On the one hand, existing methods usually treat tasks as homogeneous requests and lack systematic modeling of different service levels, business priorities and SLA constraints, making it difficult to effectively cope with actual business processes with multi-priority and mixed service requirements. On the other hand, although some studies have introduced RL frameworks, their scheduling models are often divorced from the architecture of specific business systems and lack targeted design for BPM execution engines and ERP application scenarios, thus limiting the practicality and deploy ability of the methods in enterprise environments.

A service-tiered request scheduling framework is proposed in this study. By explicitly introducing service level information and SLA constraints into the scheduling decision-making process, a service tier-aware Markov decision model is constructed. Under this framework, the PPO algorithm is adopted as the policy optimizer to realize the adaptive learning of BPM task scheduling strategies under different load conditions. It can prioritize the execution quality of high-service-level requests in high-load environments and effectively reduce the phenomenon of excessive resource supply, thus providing an intelligent optimization scheme with strong engineering feasibility and application value for BPM task scheduling in ERP systems.

The optimization method of enterprise resource planning efficiency

The architecture design of enterprise business process scheduling system

To achieve efficient task scheduling and resource allocation, this study designs a business process scheduling system architecture based on DRL (as shown in Fig. 1), aimed at handling multi-tiered service requests and complex high-load environments. The architecture follows a cloud-native design principle, offering high flexibility and scalability. It consists of four main functional modules: the Task Reception and Classification Module, the Service Level Identification Module, the SRS-PPO Scheduling Engine, and the Task Execution and Feedback Module. Each module plays a critical role in the overall system, ensuring the efficient operation and intelligent decision-making of the system.

Schematic diagram of business process scheduling system architecture based on DRL.

In Fig. 1, in this architecture, the Task Reception and Classification Module is responsible for receiving external business process requests from the ERP system and performing initial classification of tasks. By analyzing information such as task type, priority, and deadline, the system can assign an initial service level to each task. The Service Level Identification Module primarily defines task priority based on the SLA. Depending on the SLA requirements of different tasks, the tasks are divided into three service tiers: high, medium, and low. This module uses a rule-based algorithm to automatically identify task priority and passes this information to the scheduling engine. The SRS-PPO Scheduling Engine Module uses the PPO algorithm25 from DRL to dynamically adjust the task scheduling strategy. This module calculates the optimal scheduling solution in real time based on service level, current load, task deadline, and other factors. The Task Execution and Feedback Module is tightly integrated with execution nodes and is responsible for carrying out the task assignments output by the scheduling engine, then returning the execution results to the system for feedback. The feedback information includes task completion status, system resource usage, and more. This information is used to train the RL model, further optimizing the scheduling strategy, creating a feedback loop for continuous improvement.

Therefore, the design of the architecture fully considers the complexity and dynamics of task scheduling in the ERP system, so that the system can still operate efficiently in the scene of high load and concurrent tasks. Next, the modeling process of specific problems of the system will be introduced in detail, and the structure of service hierarchical request scheduling model based on SRS-PPO will be deeply analyzed.

Analysis of service tiered request scheduling model based on PPO

To resolve scheduling conflicts between high-priority and low-priority tasks, and maintain good resource utilization under heavy load, the request scheduling problem is modeled as an MDP problem. This study proposes a service-tiered request scheduling method based on DRL (SRS-PPO). It takes business request data as input and realizes the online learning and decision update of scheduling strategies through the Actor-Critic structure in the RL framework, thus completing the service-tier-aware task allocation process in a dynamic load environment. The overall architecture is shown in Fig. 2.

Schematic diagram of SRS-PPO architecture.

In Fig. 2, the SRS-PPO scheduling engine is mainly composed of a user request data input module, an Actor network, a Critic network, and a state value evaluation module, with its workflow as follows: (1) State perception phase. The system first collects the state information at the current moment from the business process scheduling environment, including the characteristics of the request queue, resource occupancy status and system load level, and encodes such information into a RL state vector that is input to the Actor-Critic network. (2) Strategy decision-making phase. The Actor network outputs the probability distribution of each candidate scheduling action according to the current policy function, and generates specific task allocation decisions based on sampling results, thus realizing priority scheduling control for requests of different service levels. (3) Value evaluation phase. The Critic network estimates the state value of the current state-action pair, and calculates the advantage function by combining the immediate reward obtained after actual execution, which is used to measure the contribution of the scheduling decision to long-term returns. (4) Strategy update phase. Based on the clipped policy gradient mechanism of PPO, the parameters of the Actor and Critic networks are updated stably. This ensures the effectiveness of strategy improvement while avoiding the training instability caused by excessive updates, thus gradually learning the optimal scheduling strategy adapted to dynamic load environments. Through the above closed-loop interaction process, SRS-PPO can realize adaptive scheduling of service-tiered requests on the premise of satisfying SLA constraints, and effectively reduce the violation rate and the phenomenon of excessive resource supply in high-load scenarios.

Experimental evaluation

To validate the proposed model, the ClarkNet-HTTP dataset (https://ita.ee.lbl.gov/html/contrib/ClarkNet-HTTP.html) is chosen as the data source for experimental evaluation. The ClarkNet-HTTP dataset provides rich task scheduling scenarios and network traffic information, suitable for simulating multi-task scheduling and resource allocation problems. To map it to the ERP/BPM task scheduling problem, business semantic abstraction is performed on the original network requests. Each request is regarded as a task in the BPM system, and the request size, response time and arrival time are mapped to the task execution time, resource demand and deadline. Meanwhile, requests are classified into high, medium and low priorities according to their importance or service levels to simulate the scheduling requirements of requests with different service levels in enterprise businesses. All data is then randomly split into a training set (80%) and a test set (20%). In the experiments, a scheduling scale closer to that of an enterprise-level BPM environment is adopted, with the number of resource nodes set to 20 and the total number of tasks set to 100 to simulate the high-concurrency scenario of a medium-sized ERP system. The resource demand and execution time of each task are randomly generated and standardized in accordance with the distribution of enterprise businesses to ensure the diversity and rationality of the training data. Task queues are divided by service level, and high, medium and low-priority tasks are stored in separate queues, which guarantees that the model could learn the service-tiered scheduling strategy.

The experimental environment is configured with TensorFlow and OpenAI Gym. The training process runs on a server with high computing power, equipped with an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU to accelerate deep learning model training and optimization. The open-source tool Gatling is used to simulate scenarios where tenants send concurrent requests. To ensure the comparability of the experimental results, several hyperparameters are set, including learning rate, batch size, discount factor, and PPO algorithm clipping range. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the SRS-PPO model in task scheduling, it is compared with EDF27, Random Scheduling (RS)28, Traditional Priority Scheduling (TPS)29, and the model proposed by Mangalampalli et al. (2024). EDF is a classic scheduling algorithm that prioritizes tasks with the earliest deadlines. Random Scheduling (RS) schedules tasks based on random selection and serves as the baseline for comparison. TPS schedules tasks according to priority but does not consider load changes or SLA guarantees. The evaluation includes three metrics under both normal and high load conditions. First, the QoS over-provisioning rate indicates the proportion of resources allocated beyond what is required during task execution, reflecting how much the system exceeds the task’s actual needs. The SLA violation rate shows the proportion of SLA breaches, reflecting tasks that are not completed on time. Resource utilization efficiency measures the efficiency of system resource usage, reflecting the rationality of resource allocation and system resource utilization.

In Figs. 4, 5 and 6, under normal load conditions, the SRS-PPO model demonstrates significant advantages over traditional algorithms in three key indicators. First, in terms of QoS over-provisioning rate, SRS-PPO consistently performs better than the other algorithms. For example, with 500 training steps, SRS-PPO has an over-provisioning rate of 26.5%, much lower than EDF (99.07%) and the other comparison algorithms. As the training progresses, SRS-PPO continues to optimize its scheduling strategy, reducing the over-provisioning rate to 17.6%, significantly outperforming traditional methods. Second, in terms of SLA violation rate, SRS-PPO also shows superior performance. Its violation rate is 15.5% after 500 steps, compared to 16.7% for EDF. As training progresses, the violation rate for SRS-PPO gradually decreases, eventually dropping to 6.6% after 10,000 steps, providing better SLA guarantees than the other algorithms.

In terms of resource utilization efficiency, SRS-PPO also performs well. After 500 steps, SRS-PPO achieves a resource utilization efficiency of 80.5%, higher than the 70.5% to 77.5% of the other algorithms. As training progresses, SRS-PPO’s resource utilization efficiency continues to improve, eventually reaching 90.3%, significantly higher than EDF’s 85.5%. To verify the statistical reliability of the performance improvements, independent-sample t-tests are conducted on the experimental results of each algorithm in the stable phase. The results show that compared with RS and TPS, SRS-PPO achieves p < 0.05 in three indicators: reducing QoS over-provisioning rate, lowering SLA violation rate, and improving resource utilization, indicating that the performance improvements are statistically significant. These results show that SRS-PPO not only effectively reduces QoS over-provisioning and SLA violations but also enhances resource utilization efficiency, highlighting its advantages in efficient scheduling and resource optimization. The superior performance of the SRS-PPO model can be mainly attributed to the following factors. First, SRS-PPO explicitly introduces service level information and SLA constraints, enabling the policy to prioritize the execution quality of high-priority tasks. Second, the PPO algorithm can adaptively adjust the task scheduling strategy and continuously optimize resource allocation through interaction with the environment. Third, the model balances task completion time and resource utilization efficiency during policy updates to achieve multi-objective optimization. The computational overhead of SRS-PPO is within an acceptable range, and its policy update process supports distributed deployment, which provides engineering feasibility for practical applications in real ERP/BPM systems.

The SRS-PPO model exhibits favorable scalability in practical ERP/BPM system deployment. Since both policy update and task scheduling decisions rely on the Actor-Critic framework of the PPO algorithm, the policy network and value network can be processed in parallel on multi-node servers via distributed training, thereby adapting to the multi-task and multi-resource scheduling requirements of medium and large-scale ERP systems. Meanwhile, the computational overhead of the PPO algorithm during the inference phase is relatively low, as each task scheduling only requires forward propagation to generate action decisions, thus meeting the response time requirements in real-time scheduling scenarios. In addition, model training can be accelerated by GPU, supporting batch training and experience replay, which improves training efficiency while ensuring convergence and stability. For dynamic load variations in practical deployment, SRS-PPO can adapt to new task distributions through incremental training or online fine-tuning, thereby maintaining policy stability and scheduling performance. Overall, the proposed method achieves high-performance scheduling within the experimental scale and possesses engineering feasibility for migration to real-world ERP/BPM systems, providing reliable technical support for enterprise-level applications.

The SRS-PPO model exhibits satisfactory performance in simulated high-load scenarios; nevertheless, non-negligible constraints remain for its deployment in real industrial environments. Current policy performance relies heavily on the distribution characteristics of training data and parameter configurations (e.g., learning rate, PPO clip range, etc.), implying that the generalization ability of the model has yet to be verified when facing completely unknown business load patterns. Short-term performance fluctuations may occur during the initial training phase of the model or under extreme resource scarcity with a surge in high-priority tasks, resulting in a temporary rebound in the SLA violation rate.

Existing experimental evaluations are mainly based on the abstract mapping of the ClarkNet-HTTP dataset, which still differs from real-world complex enterprise-level ERP business logic. Different industries impose distinct semantic constraints on business processes, and the cross-industry generality of the proposed algorithm has not been verified through large-scale multi-node field tests. Future improvements will focus on introducing an abnormal load prediction mechanism and enhancing the model’s robustness to dynamic environments via online fine-tuning or incremental learning techniques to provide more stable technical support in variable enterprise-level application scenarios.

Conclusion

This study proposes a service-tiered request scheduling method (SRS-PPO) based on PPO algorithm, aiming to optimize the scheduling efficiency of BPM tasks in ERP systems. Experiments are conducted in simulated high-load and dynamic task environments. The results show that SRS-PPO has certain advantages over traditional heuristic scheduling algorithms and some existing DRL models in reducing the SLA violation rate, decreasing QoS over-provisioning, and improving resource utilization efficiency. By introducing a hierarchical scheduling mechanism with service levels and SLA constraints, the proposed method achieves differentiated processing of tasks with different priorities and demonstrates favorable stability and scalability within the experimental scale. Overall, SRS-PPO provides an engineering-feasible intelligent optimization scheme for task scheduling in enterprise-level ERP/BPM systems, and offers an effective reference for scheduling problems involving multi-task, multi-service-level, and dynamic load scenarios.

Nevertheless, this study still has several limitations. The generalization ability of the model needs further verification in larger-scale, multi-task, multi-resource configurations and complex real business scenarios. SRS-PPO relies heavily on policy training and system state information, and its performance is sensitive to the quality of training data, load patterns, and parameter settings. The dataset used in the experiments differs from actual ERP/BPM systems, and practical deployment may require online fine-tuning or incremental training to adapt to business fluctuations. Future research can further enhance the robustness of the algorithm, expand cross-industry applicability, combine other deep learning methods, and explore abnormal load prediction, online adaptive strategies, and dynamic business priority adjustment to improve the stability, reliability, and practicability of the model in complex enterprise environments.

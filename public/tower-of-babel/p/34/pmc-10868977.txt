Frequency of Systemic Diseases and Oral Lesions Among the Institutionalized Elderly Subjects in the Northeast of Iran
Authors: Zohreh Dalirsani, Ala Ghazi, Mohammad Ghanbari Firouzabadi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:With ageing, the number of systemic diseases and the consumption of drugs increase. Moreover, some oral lesions, especially denture-related lesions, are commonly observed.Aim and Objective:The purpose of this study was to determine the frequency of systemic diseases and oral lesions in the elderly in the Mashhad Geriatric Institutes.Methods:Institutionalized elderly people, who could cooperate for oral examination, enrolled in this descriptive cross-sectional study. The frequency of oral lesions and their systemic diseases were recorded in the self-made checklist. For statistical analysis, Student’s t-test and Chi-square test were utilized. The significance level was considered to be 0.05.Results:During the study, 224 elderly subjects with a mean age of 75.63 ± 11.22 years were evaluated. The mean duration of residency in the elderly nursing centres was 1.87 ± 1.37 years. Hypertension, Alzheimer’s disease and diabetes were the most common systemic diseases, and about 47% of the patients had two or multiple concurrent diseases. Among participants, 97.1% had at least one oral lesion. The most common oral lesions were fissured tongue (75%), sublingual varicosity (68.3%) and hairy or coated tongue (38.4%), respectively, which are classified as normal variations of oral mucosa. The most common pathological lesions were denture stomatitis, frictional keratosis and lichenoid reactions.Conclusion:Regarding to the high frequency of oral lesions among ageing people, regular examination of the oral mucosa for early detection of oral lesions and appropriate treatments is recommended.

Introduction

In recent decades, with progress in medical sciences and improvements in general hygiene, life expectancy has increased in most societies. Therefore, the number of people belonging to the elderly group has increased. The older population imposes an expanded range of problems on the hygiene system. Although, in some societies such as Iran, elderly people are considered to be over 60 years, in most high-income countries, old age is defined as more than 65 years old.[12]

Population ageing is a global phenomenon. World Health Organization has estimated that the percentage of elderly people (older than 60 years) will increase from 12% to 22% of the entire population between 2015 and 2050.[34] During previous decades, population ageing was an important concern for developed countries. In recent years, fertility rates have decreased, and life expectancy has increased because of better healthcare and improvements in quality of life. Therefore, the growing trend of ageing has also been observed in developing countries in Asia and Africa.[5] In Asia, the population of elderly subjects will rise to 923 million until 2050.[46]

Since older people have more systemic and oral diseases, early diagnosis and proper treatment of their diseases are a main challenge for clinicians.

In recent years, ways to improve the quality of life of the elderly have been taken into more consideration. Therefore, clinicians are responsible for maintaining public oral health and should be aware of the needs of different age groups. Determining these needs requires conducting regional and global epidemiological studies in order to delineate the oral health status of populations and design a comprehensive programme for the oral health of the elderly population.[78]

Furthermore, elderly people should be able to consume a variety of food groups to regiment their health. The loss of natural teeth and teeth decays, the decrease in oral health and the appearance of periodontal and oral lesions lead to problems in the eating process and malnutrition, which consequently affect general health. Therefore, oral and teeth health play an important role at this age, and personal hygiene should be taken into consideration to improve their quality of life.

In one study conducted in 2012 at Mashhad Geriatric Institutes, 98% of the subjects had at least one oral lesion, of which the most common were a fissured tongue, atrophic glossitis and sublingual varicosity.[9] Also, a comparative study between public and private nursing homes in Tehran revealed that the most common oral lesions were fissured tongue, candidiasis, tongue atrophy, denture-induced stomatitis and sublingual varicosity.[10]

The high percentage of oral lesions observed in the elderly can be related to a wide range of systemic conditions and medications, nutritional disorders, chronic trauma, especially due to wearing ill-fitting dentures. Besides, impaired immunologic reactivity and carcinogen metabolism, as well as atrophy of the oral epithelium, lead to the development of more oral lesions in the elderly population.[11]

In one study performed in India on elderly people referred to an eye clinic, about 33% of the aged patients reported systemic disease. Hypertension and diabetes were the most common illnesses reported in the subjects who had visual problems.[12] There is a rational link between some systemic diseases, such as hypertension, and cerebrovascular and Alzheimer’s disease; the exact mechanism has not been known.[13] A systematic review published in 2021 concluded that cerebrovascular diseases, hypertension, dementia, and coronary artery disease were the most common systemic illnesses among elderly subjects evaluated in previous studies performed in Asian countries, including Taiwan, China, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, India, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.[4]

Since awareness of the elderly’s oral status in each region plays an important role in the training of dental students and dentists as well as the planning of elderly public health programmes, in this study we evaluated the systemic diseases and oral lesions of the elderly living in Mashhad Nursing Homes.

Materials and Method

Selection and description of participants

This was a descriptive cross-sectional study that was performed on institutionalised elderly people in Mashhad city, between December 2019 and March 2020. Nine elderly institutions were selected among different regions of Mashhad.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

In these centres, all people who were aged 60 years and over and were able to cooperate for oral examination and fill out the prepared checklists were included in this study. Among them, the subjects who had severe Alzheimer’s or other mental or physical disorders and could not cooperate with the researchers were excluded from the research. All subjects signed informed consent forms before beginning the study [Figure 1].

Flowchart for the selection of subjects

Data collection

A self-made checklist was prepared, and demographic data, including sex, age, educational level and marriage status, were collected. In addition, the frequency of oral lesions, the number of remaining teeth, and the use of any partial or full denture were recorded in the checklists. The information about the systemic diseases, consumption of the drugs, and duration of residency in the nursing homes was collected through the subjects’ medical files. An oral examination was performed through two oral and maxillofacial medicine specialist, and the oral lesions were recorded after confirmation by both of them. The oral lesions and systemic diseases were categorised based on oral medicine book references.

The protocol of this study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences with approval number IR.MUMS.DENTISTRY.REC.1399.004.

Statistical analysis

After entering the data into SPSS software (version 20), parametric statistical methods, including the Student’s t-test and Chi-square test, were employed for the assessment of the variables. The significance level was considered to be 0.05 for statistical tests.

Results

Demographic information

In this study, 224 elderly subjects with a mean age of 75.63 ± 11.22 years were evaluated.

Among them, 135 of the participants were women, whose mean age was 76.24 ± 10.92 years, and 89 of them were men with a mean age of 74.63 ± 11.68 years.

The average duration of residency in the geriatric institutes was 1.87 ± 1.37 years.

Also, the educational level in most of the subjects was before diploma (65.6%). A smaller percentage of them had a diploma, a bachelor’s degree or higher than a bachelor’s degree, respectively.

In addition, 1.3% of them were smokers, and only about 7% had more than 20 teeth in their mouths.

Teeth status

Among the elderly subjects, 45% were edentulous and wore a complete denture. Despite being completely edentulous, 24% of the participants did not use the denture. Also, 15% had a partial denture, and 3% of them were partially edentulous without wearing the partial denture. About 50.4% of women and 42.5% of men were completely edentulous; the Chi-square test showed that there was no significant difference between them (P = 0.48).

Systemic diseases

Hypertension, mild Alzheimer’s disease, diabetes and other cardiovascular diseases were the most common systemic diseases. The frequency of other diseases in these subjects was below 10%. Moreover, 106 patients (about 47%) had more than one disease concurrently. Among them, 59% of men and 40% of women suffer from more than one illness [Table 1].

Oral lesions

Among participants, 97.1% had at least one oral lesion. The oral lesions were divided into pathological lesions and normal variations. The most common lesions were fissured tongue (75%), sublingual varicosity (68.3%) and coated or hairy tongue (38.4%), which are classified as normal variations of oral mucosa.

Among the pathological lesions, denture stomatitis (14.3%), frictional keratosis (12.5%) and lichenoid reactions (9.4%) were the most common lesions that were clinically diagnosed in the subjects [Table 3]. After confirmation of the diagnosis by two oral medicine specialists, these lesions were placed under appropriate treatments.

Discussion

In this study, only 2.9% of elderlies did not have any oral lesion; however, fissured tongue, sublingual varicosity and coated or hairy tongue were the main oral lesions of the elderly participants in the present study, which are considered normal variations.

These lesions were also reported in other studies as common lesions in the elderly persons.[91114] According to the previous studies, between 77.6 and 100% of the studied people had at least one oral lesion.[9101415]

One study in Turkey revealed that the most elderly subjects (87.6%) had one or more mouth lesions, and the frequency of these lesions increased with aging. Also, there was a positive relationship between oral lesions and the presence of systemic diseases, denture use and smoking.[11] Although the above study was performed on outpatients attending dental school, the present study was conducted among the elderly residents of nursing homes.

Denture stomatitis, papillary hyperplasia, candidiasis and varix were other common lesions reported in some previous studies.[1415]

One study in Brazil reported different results: squamous cell carcinoma, leucoplakia, haemangioma, fibroma and prosthetic stomatitis were the most common clinical diagnoses. However, that study was performed on elderly patients who were referred to medical referral centres for their oral lesions.[16] This could explain the difference in reported lesions between that study and other studies.

The percentage of oral lesions in the elderly in one study conducted in India[17] was lower than the studies evaluating the elderly oral lesions in Iran; however, the frequency of these lesions in another study performed in Turkey was similar to studies conducted in Iran.[910111417]

It seems that cultural and socioeconomic status, oral habits, hygiene status, educational level and genetics could justify these differences.

The high prevalence of oral lesions reported in this study and other previous studies could be related to the wide and diverse range of lesions, including normal variations of the oral mucosa, that were evaluated.

Moreover, the high frequency of oral lesions in elderly people could be related to their systemic diseases, medicines and low level of oral hygiene, as well as inappropriate individual and nursing care in this population. Since older people may suffer from some systemic diseases and use several drugs and dental prostheses, as well as they have lower ability for performing oral hygiene, they are usually more commonly involved with oral lesions as compared to other age groups. The elderly are not able to correctly perform oral health activities; therefore, some oral lesions related to poor oral hygiene appear in their oral cavity.

On the other hand, some premalignant or malignant lesions are more common in older people. Also, they have lower access to primary care services, which could be effective in the presence of untreated oral lesions.

During ageing, many physical and mental changes gradually occur, which make the elderly susceptible to chronic diseases, physical and mental disabilities, loneliness and isolation, a lack of social support and individual dependence, which threaten performing daily life activities.

In the present study, most subjects had one or more diseases, and 51% of the participants regularly used at least one drug.

In another study conducted in the north of Iran, about 67% of the non-institutionalized elderly people had at least one systemic illness; hypertension and diabetes were the most common diseases,[18] which was in line with the present study findings. In the Asian studies assessed in one systematic review, cerebrovascular illnesses, hypertension and dementia were the most common systemic diseases among the old participants.[4]

One study in Turkey revealed that there was a significant correlation between the presence of systemic diseases and the prevalence of oral lesions.[11]

In another study in India, the patients’ lifestyle influenced the percentage of chronic illnesses, as the subjects belonged to lower socioeconomic status and were involved with fewer unhealthy lifestyle-related diseases, especially cardiovascular conditions and diabetes. On the other hand, they may suffer from some infectious disease, such as tuberculosis, more than people with a higher socioeconomic level. However, that study was only performed on subjects who attended in an outpatient clinic for eye care services. It is rational to claim that a lower socioeconomic level leads to lower access to health facilities, which could influence the finding.[12] While institutionalised subjects face various challenges in accessing healthcare, in addition to economic level, therefore, their health status may differ from that of other elderly subjects.

In the present study, most of the participants were partially or completely edentulous, while other studies reported various percentages of subjects being edentulous.[111617] The prevalence of partial and complete edentulous participants was reported in more than 45% of the senior population in another study in Poland.[19] Among the elderly examined in the present study, 60% of participants used a complete or partial denture. In the study performed in Brazil, 50.8% of referred patients had some type of denture, and most of them wore a complete denture.[16] In another study conducted in Turkey, 76% of subjects used dentures, and the percentage of men who wore one type of denture was higher than that of women.[11] In our study, the number of completely edentulous females was insignificantly more than that of males.

In another cross-sectional study in India, only 20% of outpatient participants visited a dental clinic used dentures,[17] while about 14% and 31% of complete and partial edentulous subjects in one survey in Poland did not wear any dentures.[19]

In the field of oral health, ageing is associated with an increase in oral lesions, including dental caries, tooth luxation and some malignant and pre-malignant lesions. On the other side, many oral lesions are caused by some systemic conditions, drug consumption and the use of various dental prostheses. With respect to a growing trend of the elderly population, it is necessary to adapt the healthcare system. It needs to formulate suitable strategies to improve health services for better assessment and treatment of older people.[520]

Briefly, the high prevalence of oral and dental problems in the studies conducted in Iran reinforces the need for more oral care and regular and periodic oral examinations by dentists in the geriatric institutes. These facilities improve their quality of life and increase the elderly’s life expectancy.

Conclusion

The most common oral lesions were normal variations of the oral mucosa. Among pathological lesions, denture stomatitis, frictional keratosis and lichenoid reactions were commonly observed. Concerning high frequency of oral lesions in the elderly, regular oral examination for early detection of oral lesions is recommended.

Real-world cost avoidance associated with bevacizumab biosimilar adoption in a tertiary public hospital in Ecuador: a cost-minimization and budget impact analysis
Authors: Sara Rodríguez, Carla Arias, María del Carmen Aynaguano, Carol Moreno, David Armas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe increasing use of high-cost oncology therapies poses significant challenges for healthcare systems, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. The introduction of biosimilars offers a potential strategy to optimize pharmaceutical expenditure while maintaining clinical outcomes. However, real-world economic evidence from Latin American public healthcare settings remains limited.ObjectiveTo evaluate the economic impact of bevacizumab biosimilar adoption in a tertiary public hospital in Ecuador through a cost-minimization analysis and a budget impact analysis based on real-world institutional data.MethodsA retrospective observational economic evaluation was conducted using institutional pharmacy data available from January 2019 to June 2, 2026. The database was extracted on June 2, 2026; therefore, data for 2026 represent year-to-date observations. A cost-minimization analysis (CMA) was performed under the assumption of clinical equivalence between originator bevacizumab and its biosimilar (MB02), focusing on direct drug acquisition costs. A budget impact assessment was conducted using an originator-only counterfactual scenario, comparing observed biosimilar expenditure with the hypothetical expenditure that would have occurred if the same number and presentation mix of biosimilar vials had been acquired at originator prices. Cost estimates included real expenditure, hypothetical originator costs, and mean acquisition cost per vial. Uncertainty was explored using bootstrap resampling techniques.ResultsA total of 6,431 bevacizumab vials were dispensed during the study period, 91.9% of which corresponded to the biosimilar. Biosimilar acquisition costs were substantially lower than those of the originator product across both vial presentations. Under the originator-only counterfactual scenario, hypothetical expenditure was estimated at USD 5,462,097.60 compared with observed biosimilar expenditure of USD 1,963,376.00, resulting in estimated cost avoidance of USD 3,498,721.60 (64.1%). These findings demonstrate substantial cost avoidance and pharmaceutical expenditure containment associated with biosimilar adoption.ConclusionThe incorporation of a bevacizumab biosimilar in a tertiary public hospital in Ecuador was associated with substantial cost avoidance under an originator-only counterfactual scenario. These findings support the role of biosimilars as a strategy to improve efficiency and contribute to expenditure containment in in oncology pharmaceutical spending within resource-constrained healthcare settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12962-026-00799-w.

Introduction

The use of biologic therapies has significantly transformed the management of multiple malignancies, including colorectal cancer, metastatic colorectal cancer, and non-small cell lung cancer. These agents have improved clinical outcomes and expanded therapeutic options; however, their high acquisition costs represent a substantial and growing burden on healthcare systems, particularly in resource-constrained settings.

Biosimilars have emerged as highly comparable alternatives to reference biologic medicines in terms of quality, safety, and efficacy, while offering lower costs [1–3]. The introduction of biosimilars has also been associated with increased market competition and changes in utilization patterns [4]. Their introduction has been associated with meaningful reductions in pharmaceutical expenditure, with international evidence reporting price decreases ranging from 30% to 47% following market entry [5, 6]. In the case of bevacizumab, real-world studies have demonstrated significant economic benefits, including reductions in treatment-related expenditure of up to USD 6,379 per patient without compromising clinical outcomes [7], as well as substantial reductions in monthly treatment costs in high-income healthcare systems [8].

Despite this growing body of international evidence, the adoption and evaluation of biosimilars in low- and middle-income countries remain heterogeneous. In Ecuador, the regulatory framework for biologic medicines was updated through Ministerial Agreement No. 00385–2019 [9], establishing pathways for biosimilar approval and use. However, locally generated real-world economic evidence remains scarce, limiting context-specific decision-making for resource allocation and pharmaceutical procurement within the public healthcare system.

In this context, there is a critical need to assess the economic implications of biosimilar implementation using real-world data from local healthcare settings. This study aims to evaluate the economic impact of bevacizumab biosimilar (MB02) adoption in a tertiary-level public hospital in Ecuador through a cost-minimization analysis and a budget impact assessment based on an originator-only counterfactual scenario. By quantifying acquisition costs and potential cost avoidance associated with biosimilar adoption, this study provides locally generated evidence to inform pharmaceutical procurement and resource allocation decisions within the Ecuadorian public healthcare system.

Methods

Study design and analytical framework

A retrospective observational economic evaluation was conducted, combining a cost-minimization analysis (CMA) and a budget impact analysis (BIA) from the perspective of a tertiary public hospital. The study was based on real-world utilization and using institutional pharmacy data available from January 2019 to June 2, 2026. The database was extracted on June 2, 2026; therefore, data for 2026 represent year-to-date observations [10].

The analysis assumed clinical equivalence between originator bevacizumab and its biosimilar (MB02), supported by phase III clinical trials and regulatory comparability exercises [11]. Under the assumption of equivalent health outcomes (ΔE ≈ 0), a cost-minimization approach was applied, focusing exclusively on differences in direct medical costs.

All costs are reported in U.S. dollars (USD) using the nominal acquisition prices paid by the institution at the time of procurement during the study period.

Only direct acquisition costs recorded in the institutional procurement system were included. Taxes, distribution costs, rebates, inventory management costs, and drug wastage were not available and were therefore not included in the analysis.

Data sources

Data were obtained from the institutional pharmacy information system (Hosvital) of Eugenio Espejo Specialty Hospital. The dataset included aggregated records of drug procurement and dispensing.

Utilization was quantified using the total number of dispensed vials recorded in the QUANTITY variable of the institutional database. Each dispensing record could contain one or more vials. The weighted average cost per vial was calculated as total expenditure divided by the total number of vials used for each category. This approach reflects real-world variations in procurement prices and utilization volumes.

Cost-minimization analysis

Observed real-world costs following the introduction of the biosimilar were compared with a counterfactual scenario assuming exclusive use of the originator product.

The hypothetical cost was estimated by applying originator unit costs to the observed number of biosimilar vials used for each presentation (100 mg and 400 mg), using aggregated real-world data. The hypothetical expenditure was calculated separately for the 100 mg and 400 mg presentations using originator acquisition costs and the observed quantity of biosimilar vials dispensed.

Budget impact analysis

Budget impact was assessed using an originator-only counterfactual scenario. The hypothetical expenditure was estimated by applying originator unit costs to the same number and presentation mix of biosimilar bevacizumab vials observed during the study period [12].

Statistical analysis

The analysis was primarily descriptive, including frequencies, totals, weighted averages, and temporal trends in utilization and expenditure.

Inferential statistical methods were not applied, as the objective was to estimate real-world economic impact rather than compare clinical outcomes.

Uncertainty was assessed using non-parametric bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations and one-way deterministic sensitivity analyses. In each iteration, dispensing records were resampled with replacement, and cost avoidance was recalculated under the originator-only counterfactual scenario. The 95% confidence interval was estimated using the percentile method [13].

Analyses were performed in R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) using the boot package in RStudio.

Reporting considerations

The study was conducted in accordance with the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS 2022) guidelines [14].

Results

A total of 6,431 bevacizumab vials were dispensed between 2019 and 2026 (Table 1). Of these, 5,912 vials (91.9%) corresponded to biosimilar bevacizumab and 519 vials (8.1%) to the originator product.

The utilization pattern varied across years and reflected changes in institutional procurement rather than a continuous substitution process. Biosimilar bevacizumab was the only product procured until August 2019, following its incorporation into the Ecuadorian National Electronic Catalogue. After the expiration of the procurement agreement, the biosimilar was temporarily removed from the Electronic Catalogue, and the originator product became the only available procurement option from 2020 until February 2021. Biosimilar bevacizumab was reintroduced into the Electronic Catalogue in August 2021 and has remained the only product procured by the institution since then. Periods without recorded dispensing corresponded to temporary stock shortages rather than changes in clinical practice.

In 2019, all dispensed vials (n = 478) corresponded to the biosimilar. In 2020, both products were used, with 395 originator vials (54.1%) and 335 biosimilar vials (45.9%). By 2021, biosimilars accounted for 79.9% of utilization (485 of 607 vials), increasing to 99.6% in 2022 (559 of 561 vials).

From 2023 onward, all dispensed bevacizumab corresponded to the biosimilar, reflecting complete institutional adoption. Across all years, the 400 mg presentation predominated, representing 54.5% of total utilization (3,504 of 6,431 vials). The highest annual utilization was observed in 2025 (1,396 vials), followed by 2023 (1,299 vials) and 2024 (1,166 vials).

Under the originator-only counterfactual scenario, acquisition of the same number and presentation mix of biosimilar bevacizumab vials would have resulted in estimated expenditure of USD 5,462,097.60 (Table 4).

Observed expenditure for biosimilar bevacizumab was USD 1,963,376.00, resulting in estimated cost avoidance of USD 3,498,721.60. This corresponds to 64.1% of the hypothetical originator-only expenditure.

These findings indicate that biosimilar adoption was associated with substantial cost avoidance and expenditure containment despite increasing utilization over time.

Deterministic sensitivity analysis

A one-way deterministic sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate the impact of uncertainty in originator acquisition prices on the estimated cost avoidance. Originator unit costs were varied by ± 20% relative to the base-case values while maintaining the observed utilization volumes and presentation mix.

Cost avoidance remained substantial across all scenarios. Under the lower-bound scenario (-20% originator prices), estimated cost avoidance was USD 2,406,302.08. Under the upper-bound scenario (+ 20% originator prices), estimated cost avoidance increased to USD 4,591,141.12. The base-case estimate remained USD 3,498,721.60 (Table 5).

These findings indicate that the economic advantage associated with biosimilar adoption remained robust across a plausible range of originator acquisition prices.

Uncertainty analysis (bootstrap)

Bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations was performed to assess the robustness of the estimated cost avoidance. The mean estimated cost avoidance was USD 3,497,843, with a 95% confidence interval ranging from USD 3,443,896 to USD 3,548,402. The bootstrap estimates were highly consistent with the deterministic base-case estimate of USD 3,498,721.60, supporting the robustness of the estimated cost avoidance associated with biosimilar adoption (Fig. 1).

Discussion

This study provides real-world pharmacoeconomic evidence on the institutional impact of bevacizumab biosimilar adoption in a tertiary public hospital in Ecuador. The findings demonstrate substantial cost avoidance and expenditure containment, consistent with international evidence reporting significant economic advantage following the introduction of biosimilars in oncology [5, 6].

Importantly, the economic benefit observed in this study occurred alongside a marked increase in treatment utilization. This suggests that biosimilar adoption contributed to expenditure containment despite increasing utilization for therapy. This effect is particularly relevant in resource-constrained healthcare systems, where financial limitations directly affect the sustainability of high-cost oncology treatments [7, 8].

These findings are consistent with studies conducted in high-income settings, where biosimilar introduction has been associated with both price reductions and increased treatment uptake [5, 6, 15]. However, this study contributes novel evidence from a middle-income country context, where budget constraints are more pronounced and the opportunity cost of high-cost oncology therapies is substantially higher. The inclusion of real-world institutional data strengthens the external validity for comparable tertiary public hospitals in middle-income Latin American settings [16].

The use of a cost-minimization approach is methodologically justified by the assumption of therapeutic equivalence between originator bevacizumab and its biosimilar, as demonstrated in phase III clinical trials and regulatory comparability assessments [11]. Under this framework, cost differences become the primary determinant for decision-making, particularly in institutional procurement processes.

Beyond economic considerations, biosimilar adoption is influenced by structural and behavioral factors, including clinician confidence, regulatory clarity, and the availability of local evidence [17]. In this context, the generation of real-world pharmacoeconomic data may play a critical role in reducing barriers to adoption and supporting evidence-based decision-making within healthcare institutions.

The uncertainty analysis demonstrated that the estimated cost avoidance remained highly stable across 1,000 bootstrap iterations. The close agreement between the deterministic estimate (USD 3.50 million) and the bootstrap mean (USD 3.50 million), together with the relatively narrow 95% confidence interval, supports the robustness of the findings.

In addition, deterministic sensitivity analyses varying originator acquisition prices by ± 20% demonstrated that cost avoidance remained substantial across all tested scenarios, supporting the robustness of the economic findings.

From a policy perspective, the findings suggest that biosimilars can serve as a strategic tool to improve the efficiency of pharmaceutical resource allocation and support the sustainability of oncology services. In middle-income countries, where healthcare resources are limited, such strategies may help optimize resource allocation while maintaining the availability of high-cost therapies.

Limitations and recommendations

This study has several limitations. First, the economic evaluation was based on the assumption of therapeutic equivalence between the biosimilar and the originator medicine, supported by phase III clinical trials and regulatory comparability assessments [11]. However, clinical outcomes were not evaluated in the local cohort; therefore, findings should be interpreted within the context of economic impact and resource utilization. In addition, patient-level information was not available; therefore, direct conclusions regarding treatment access, number of patients treated, or treatment capacity cannot be drawn from the present analysis.

The economic evaluation considered only acquisition costs and did not include additional procurement-related costs such as taxes, distribution, rebates, or wastage.

Second, the analysis was conducted from an institutional perspective using administrative dispensing and expenditure data. Consequently, indirect costs and broader societal impacts—such as out-of-pocket expenses, productivity losses, and access barriers—were not captured, potentially underestimating the total economic benefit.

Third, although uncertainty was explored using non-parametric bootstrap resampling, more advanced approaches—such as probabilistic sensitivity analysis or full cost-effectiveness modeling—were not performed.

Finally, the use of aggregated dispensing data limited the ability to assess patient-level variability, treatment patterns, clinical outcomes, and healthcare resource utilization beyond pharmaceutical expenditure, which may influence the generalizability of the findings to other settings.

Recommendations for future research include incorporating patient-level data, adopting a societal perspective, and evaluating long-term clinical and economic outcomes associated with biosimilar adoption across multiple institutions.

Policy implications

The findings of this study have relevant implications for healthcare planning in Ecuador and similar middle-income settings. The incorporation of biosimilars into routine clinical practice could contribute to optimizing pharmaceutical expenditure and improving the sustainability of public healthcare systems through substantial cost avoidance associated with biologic therapies [5, 6].

International evidence indicates that biosimilar introduction promotes market competition and sustained price reductions [5, 6, 15]. However, effective adoption depends on clear regulatory frameworks, transparent procurement processes, and strengthened clinician confidence supported by accessible scientific evidence and active pharmacovigilance systems [17].

The generation of local, real-world economic evidence can support evidence-based decision-making in the public sector, including:

Conclusion

The adoption of bevacizumab biosimilar in a tertiary public hospital in Ecuador was associated with substantial cost avoidance under an originator-only counterfactual scenario. Despite increasing utilization over time, biosimilar implementation contributed to expenditure containment, generating an estimated cost avoidance of USD 3.50 million during the study period.

The close agreement between the deterministic analysis and the bootstrap uncertainty assessment supports the robustness of these findings. These results suggest that biosimilars may represent an effective strategy to improve the efficiency and sustainability of oncology pharmaceutical spending in resource-constrained healthcare systems.

The generation of local real-world economic evidence can support institutional procurement decisions and contribute to evidence-based pharmaceutical policy in middle-income countries.

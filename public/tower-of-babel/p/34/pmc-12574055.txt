Upper eyelid predominance of meibomian gland dysfunction in postmenopausal women with primary acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction
Authors: Haili Jin, Xianjie Chen, Yin Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to investigate the differential impairment of meibomian glands (MGs) in the upper and lower eyelids among postmenopausal women with primary acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction (PANDO). Additionally, we evaluated the correlations between MG morphological and functional parameters and clinical indicators of tear film stability.MethodsA total of 115 eyes from 115 postmenopausal women with PANDO and 115 eyes from 115 matched control subjects were enrolled in the study. Upper and lower MG loss, and non-invasive tear meniscus height (NITMH) were evaluated using the Keratograph 5 M. Tear break-up time (TBUT), corneal fluorescein staining (CFS), meibum grade, and Marx’ lines were all examined using a slit-lamp biomicroscope.ResultsCompared to controls, the PANDO group exhibited significantly higher MG loss scores in both the upper (p = 0.045) and lower (p = 0.036) eyelids, and worse meibum quality in the upper eyelids (p = 0.049). Marx’s lines scores were significantly more severe in the PANDO group for both eyelids (upper: p < 0.001; lower: p = 0.02). Within the PANDO group, the MG loss score was significantly higher in the upper eyelid compared to the lower eyelid (p < 0.001). The meibum grade score also showed a significantly higher value in the upper eyelid relative to the lower eyelid (p = 0.045). In contrast, the Marx’s lines score was significantly lower in the upper eyelid than in the lower eyelid (p = 0.032). Furthermore, the meibum grades of both the upper (p = 0.035) and lower (p = 0.001) eyelids demonstrated a negative correlation with TBUT, but no significant association was observed with CFS. However, no correlation was found between MG loss in either eyelid and TBUT or CFS.ConclusionsPostmenopausal women with PANDO show greater impairment in the upper MGs than in the lower MGs.This pattern may be a distinctive feature of ocular surface damage in this population. These findings provide new insights into PANDO-related ocular changes and may inform clinical assessment and management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-025-04439-4.Keywords: Primary acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction, Postmenopausal women, Meibomian gland dysfunction, Upper eyelid

Background

Primary acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction (PANDO) is a prevalent cause of epiphora in adults, characterized by idiopathic inflammation and fibrosis of the nasolacrimal duct (NLD) [1–3]. This condition predominantly affects postmenopausal women, with epidemiological data indicating a female-to-male incidence ratio of up to 3:1, suggesting a potential role for hormonal factors in its pathogenesis [4, 5]. Concurrently, meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) is widely acknowledged as the principal etiological factor in evaporative dry eye disease (DED), particularly among postmenopausal individuals, where age-related glandular atrophy and hormonal fluctuations contribute to its increased prevalence [6–8].

Some studies have reported that 20% to 27% of patients with lacrimal duct obstruction experience dry eye symptoms following surgical intervention, despite the successful restoration of lacrimal duct function [9–11]. Therefore, patients with lacrimal duct obstruction may already have ocular surface damage before surgery. It is essential to evaluate the ocular surface status in these patients during episodes of epiphora. Emerging evidence supports a potential association between PANDO and ocular surface damage [12, 13]. The chronic inflammation associated with PANDO, as evidenced by elevated tear cytokine levels in other studies [14–17], is likely to be a key contributor to the exacerbation of MGD observed in our cohort. Recent findings further indicate structural and functional alterations in the meibomian glands (MGs) among postmenopausal women diagnosed with PANDO [12]. However, current investigations into MGD in PANDO patients have predominantly employed generalized assessments without considering topographical differences between upper and lower eyelids. This methodological limitation is particularly significant given the anatomical and functional distinctions: the upper eyelid contains 30–40 MGs—approximately 25% more than the lower eyelid—and plays a disproportionately greater role in lipid secretion [18, 19]. Several studies have reported higher MGD severity in the lower eyelids within general populations, suggesting that disease progression may initiate earlier in the inferior palpebral region [20–23]. Whether this pattern extends to individuals with PANDO—particularly hormonally vulnerable postmenopausal women—remains an unresolved issue warranting dedicated research. Furthermore, the interactions between specific MG morphological and functional parameters and tear film stability in PANDO remain poorly understood. Clarifying these relationships could elucidate underlying mechanisms linking PANDO to dry eye and inform the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

This study aimed to investigate the differential involvement of MGs in the upper and lower eyelids among postmenopausal women with PANDO, as well as to assess the correlations between MG parameters and clinical measures of tear film function. The findings may help identify distinct patterns of ocular surface damage in this patient cohort, thereby facilitating the development of etiology-directed therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This prospective observational study enrolled postmenopausal women who visited Wuhu Eye Hospital between March 2021 and February 2023. All participants presented with epiphora and were diagnosed with PANDO based on lacrimal irrigation and dacryocystography findings. Diagnostic criteria comprised: presence of epiphora (with or without purulent discharge), confirmed nasolacrimal duct obstruction(NLDO), and absence of traumatic or secondary causes. Age- and menopause duration-matched volunteers without epiphora, NLDO (confirmed by lacrimal irrigation), or active ocular surface disease were recruited from the general ophthalmology clinic during the same period. Exclusion criteria included: premenopausal status; systemic disorders affecting tear film stability (e.g., Sjögren’s syndrome, diabetes mellitus); active ocular inflammation; chronic use of topical ocular medications (> 3 months); prolonged contact lens wear (> 6 months); and history of ocular trauma or surgical intervention. In cases of unilateral PANDO, the affected eye was selected as the study eye. For participants with bilateral PANDO, the eye with more pronounced symptoms was selected as the study eye to include the most clinically relevant presentation and to avoid the statistical non-independence of data from both eyes of the same individual. For control subjects, the right eye was selected for analysis.The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Wuhu Eye Hospital (Approval No. 20210107) and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants following comprehensive verbal and written explanation of the study procedures.

Ocular parameters examinations

All experimental procedures were conducted consistently between 9:00 and 11:00 AM under controlled environmental conditions, with ambient temperature maintained at 22–28 °C and relative humidity levels kept within a range of 40–50% RH. MG loss and non-invasive tear meniscus height (NITMH) were evaluated using the Keratograph 5 M (Oculus GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). NITMH was measured using built-in tools to determine the distance between the upper and lower tear meniscus boundaries at a point perpendicular to the eyelid margin, centered on the pupil. Each eye is measured twice, and the average is calculated. The scoring system for MG loss per eyelid ranged from 0 to 3, based on the percentage of gland dropout: Grade 0 (no loss observed, 0%), Grade 1 (loss affecting less than one-third of total MGs, < 33.3%), Grade 2 (loss involving one-third to two-thirds of total MGs, 33.3% to 66.6%), and Grade 3 (loss exceeding two-thirds of total MGs, >66.6%) [24, 25]. Tear breakup time (TBUT) was assessed using sodium fluorescein strips (Tianjin Jingming New Technology Development Co., Ltd., China), and the average of three consecutive measurements was calculated for statistical analysis. Corneal staining was evaluated according to the SICCA Ocular Staining Score (OSS) protocol [26], adapted from the NEI Workshop guidelines, across four zones-superior nasal, inferior nasal, superior temporal, and inferior temporal—with each zone assigned a score based on the extent of fluorescein uptake: 0 (no staining), 1 (1–30 discrete staining dots), 2 (staining covering one-third to three-fourths of the zone), and 3 (presence of filaments, confluent staining, or ulceration). The corneal fluorescein staining (CFS) score was calculated by summing the individual scores from all four zones. After TBUT and CFS assessments, use a 10× cobalt blue filter and the widest slit-lamp beam to examine the position of the Marx’s line relative to the MG orifices. Divide the eyelid margin into three equal parts: medial, central, and lateral. Score each part as follows: 0 – Marx’s line entirely on the conjunctival side; 1 – partial overlap, less than half of the orifice; 2 – overlap greater than half of the orifice; 3 – Marx’s line entirely on the skin side. Add the scores from all three segments to determine the Marx’s line score for the upper and lower eyelids [27]. Meibum quality grade was assessed in both upper and lower eyelids, focusing on the eight central MGs, and categorized into four distinct grades: clear (0), cloudy (1), cloudy with particulate debris (2), and inspissated (3) [28]. All subjective clinical assessments were performed by a single ophthalmologist who underwent prior standardized training on the specified grading scales to ensure consistent application of criteria throughout the study.

Statistical analyses

All data were subjected to statistical analysis using SPSS 22.0 software. In this study, variables such as age, menopause duration, and ocular parameters were found to deviate from a normal distribution according to the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Consequently, these variables were expressed using median and interquartile range [M(P25, P75)]. Differences in baseline characteristics between the PANDO and control groups were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables. Differences in scores related to MG loss, meibum grade, and Marx’s line score in the upper and lower eyelids within each group were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Differences in these parameters between the PANDO and control groups for each eyelid were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Spearman’s correlation analysis was conducted to assess the associations among MG loss, meibum grade, TBUT, NITMH, and CFS within the PANDO group. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This prospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Wuhu Eye Hospital (Approval No. 20210107) and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. After a comprehensive explanation of the study’s purpose and potential consequences, written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Discussion

This study identifies a distinct pattern of MGD characterized by predominant upper eyelid involvement in postmenopausal women with PANDO. We found that the degree of MG loss and the severity of meibum quality impairment were significantly greater in the upper eyelids compared to the lower eyelids. Crucially, this pattern of upper eyelid predominance gains profound significance when viewed against our findings from the age and menopause duration matched control group. The comparative analysis reveals that MG loss in both eyelids and meibum quality in the upper eyelid were significantly worse in the PANDO group than in controls. This provides compelling evidence that the observed MGD is not merely an accentuation of age-related or postmenopausal change, but rather a distinct pathological signature directly associated with PANDO itself.

Previous investigations into MGD have consistently reported more severe involvement of the lower eyelids in the general population, suggesting that disease progression may initiate earlier in the inferior palpebral region [20–23]. However, emerging evidence indicates that the topographic pattern of MGD may vary depending on the underlying etiology. For instance, Wu et al. [29] observed that patients with dermatochalasis-associated MGD exhibited more severe MG loss in the upper eyelids. Our findings align with this concept, establishing PANDO as another condition linked to a unique pattern of upper-eyelid-predominant MGD.

The mechanisms underlying this differential eyelid vulnerability warrant further investigation but can be hypothesized within the context of PANDO pathophysiology. The chronic inflammation associated with PANDO, evidenced by elevated tear levels of cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-8, and MIP-1α [14, 16, 17], is likely a key contributor. We postulate that the upper eyelid MGs, being more numerous and responsible for the majority of lipid secretion [18, 19, 30], may be disproportionately susceptible to the pro-inflammatory microenvironment created by chronic tear stasis. Furthermore, the impaired drainage and elevated tear meniscus (as indicated by the significantly higher NITMH) may allow inflammatory mediators to bathe the eyelid margin more persistently, potentially affecting the orifices of the more prolific upper MGs to a greater extent.

An intriguing finding was the significantly higher Marx’s line score in the lower eyelid compared to the upper eyelid within the PANDO group, despite worse MG parameters in the upper lid. Marx’s line displacement is a recognized indicator of eyelid margin inflammation and hyperosmolarity [27, 31]. This dissociation suggests that the changes in Marx’s line in PANDO patients are not solely driven by the same inflammatory processes causing MG dropout and meibum alteration. The lower lid findings may be more influenced by mechanical factors related to epiphora and tear overflow, whereas the upper lid MG pathology appears to be a more direct consequence of the specific inflammatory milieu of PANDO.

The data from our age and menopause duration matched control group are pivotal for interpreting the findings. The control group exhibited a pattern of predominant structural MG loss in the upper eyelids, aligning with the known paradigm of age-related physiological attrition. However, the critical distinction was that no significant difference in meibum quality was found between the upper and lower eyelids in controls (P = 0.107), indicating preserved secretory function despite structural decline. In stark contrast, the PANDO group showed not only significantly worse MG loss but also a significant functional degradation of meibum quality specifically in the upper eyelids. This establishes a clear dichotomy: physiological aging in our cohort manifested as predominant structural loss, while PANDO superimposed a unique functional deficit. This upper-lid-specific vulnerability strongly supports the hypothesis that PANDO creates a distinct perilimbal microenvironment that disproportionately compromises upper MG function.

Our correlation analyses indicate that the quality of meibum, rather than the extent of MG loss, is the primary factor correlated with TBUT in this cohort, which is consistent with observations reported by Eom et al. in patients with obstructive MGD [23]. It is noteworthy that despite significantly worse meibum quality and MG loss, the PANDO group had similar TBUT and CFS scores to the control group. This apparent paradox may be explained by the opposing effects of PANDO: the obstruction leads to an increased tear volume (higher NITMH), which may provide a relative thickening of the lipid layer and a dilutional effect that transiently stabilizes the tear film and rinses away debris and inflammatory mediators, thereby mitigating corneal epithelial damage [31]. Concurrently, the documented hypotonicity of tears in PANDO [32] may counteract the hypertonicity typically observed in postmenopausal dry eye [33], further explaining the lack of significant difference in CFS. Meanwhile, this study found that NITMH shows no correlation between MG loss, meibum grade, TBUT, and CFS. The primary underlying factor is that obstruction of the NLD, which leads to impaired tear drainage, represents the main determinant of elevated NITMH. This phenomenon “masks” the effects of structural and functional damage to the MGs on tear evaporation and ocular surface health in postmenopausal women with PANDO. NITMH reflects the static volume of the aqueous layer, whereas MG function determines the quality of the lipid layer that prevents excessive tear evaporation. In cases of MGD, this typically manifests as accelerated tear evaporation (shortened TBUT) and corneal epithelial damage (positive CFS). However, when concurrent NLDO is present, NITMH may remain relatively high, thereby eliminating any direct correlation with meibum grade, CFS, and TBUT. Therefore, clinicians should recognize that in the presence of lacrimal duct obstruction, NITMH cannot serve as a reliable indicator for assessing dry eye severity or tear film stability. Clinical evaluation and management should prioritize comprehensive assessment of MG function (including meibum quality and extent of gland loss), tear film stability (TBUT), and ocular surface health status (CFS), supplemented by tear secretion tests such as the Schirmer I test.

The findings of this study have direct implications for the clinical assessment and management of postmenopausal women with PANDO. Firstly, our results underscore the necessity of a comprehensive ocular surface evaluation that specifically includes assessment of the upper eyelid MGs in these patients. Relying solely on lower eyelid assessment may lead to an underestimation of the severity of MGD. Secondly, the identification of upper eyelid-predominant MGD could serve as a valuable preoperative prognostic marker. Patients with significant MG loss prior to dacryocystorhinostomy may be at higher risk for persistent dry eye symptoms postoperatively, despite successful resolution of epiphora. Therefore, we propose that MG evaluation be integrated into the preoperative workup for PANDO. This would allow for improved patient counseling and the potential initiation of preemptive MGD management prior to or concurrently with lacrimal surgery. Ultimately, a more holistic treatment approach that addresses both the obstructive lacrimal pathology and the concomitant MGD may lead to better overall patient outcomes and satisfaction.

Several study limitations warrant consideration. First, the cross-sectional design precludes definitive conclusions regarding causality between PANDO and the observed MG changes; the upper eyelid predominance could be either a consequence or a potential predisposing factor. Future longitudinal studies are essential to clarify the temporal sequence and causative nature of this association. Second, to ensure statistical independence, only the more symptomatic eye was included in cases of bilateral PANDO. While this is a common methodological approach, it may introduce selection bias and precludes analysis of inter-eye variability. Future studies designed to examine laterality would require a different statistical framework. Third, although mechanistic hypotheses involving inflammatory pathways and tear osmolarity are proposed based on the literature, the study did not include direct measurements of tear inflammatory biomarkers (e.g., cytokines) or osmolarity. The lack of this objective biochemical data limits our ability to definitively validate the proposed mechanisms. Future studies specifically designed to incorporate these measurements are essential to confirm or refute the implicated pathophysiological pathways and to better understand the distinct perilimbal microenvironment in PANDO. Fourth, the assessment of parameters like meibum quality and Marx’s line relies on examiner judgment. Although performed by a single experienced clinician to ensure internal consistency, the lack of formal inter-observer reliability testing is a limitation. Future studies would benefit from incorporating multiple masked graders. Finally, given the focus on postmenopausal women, the lack of hormonal level assessment limits our capacity to correlate MG alterations with individual endocrine status and define its precise role in the observed pathophysiology. Future studies incorporating hormonal profiling are warranted.

Conclusions

This study identifies a characteristic pattern of meibomian gland (MG) impairment that predominantly affects the upper eyelids in postmenopausal women with primary acquired nasolacrimal duct obstruction (PANDO). By employing a carefully matched control group, we demonstrate that this upper-eyelid-predominant MGD is a specific pathological feature of PANDO, distinct from age-related changes. This pattern, characterized by more severe gland loss and worse meibum quality in the upper eyelids, appears to be a distinctive hallmark of ocular surface damage in this population. The structural and functional disruption correlated with tear film instability but not directly with corneal staining, highlighting a complex pathophysiology involving inflammatory processes and glandular dysfunction. Our findings underscore the critical importance of evaluating the upper eyelids in the clinical assessment and management of postmenopausal women with PANDO. Future longitudinal studies are warranted to elucidate the mechanistic links between chronic lacrimal drainage obstruction, hormonal changes, and regional MG vulnerability.

Targeting syndecan-2 inhibits papillary thyroid cancer invasiveness and de-differentiation
Authors: Rui Liu, Xin Lv, Huiling Wang, Yuqing Zhang, Zhen Cao, Lianhong Zou, Ziwen Liu, Chaojie Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe high incidence of recurrence and metastatic disease remain the major issues for papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) patients. Previous studies have demonstrated that Syndecan-2 (SDC2) plays a key role in multiple cancers progression. However, the potential role of SDC2 in PTC progression and recurrence remains unclear.MethodsFirst, we performed bioinformatics analysis and western-blotting analysis to explore the potential prognostic value of SDC2 in PTC. Then we applied transient siRNA knockdown and plasmid overexpression to alter SDC2 expression level in PTC cell line B-CPAP and KTC-1. After that, we carried out scratch wound healing assay, transwell assay and cell counting kits-8 (CCK8) assay to explore the cell migration, invasiveness and viability. We also explored expressions of mesenchymal and epithelial markers, multiple thyroids differentiating markers and hedgehog signaling members to address the potential underlying mechanisms.ResultsFirstly, we found a significant negative correlation of SDC2 expression with advanced disease characters in PTC. Then bioinformatic analysis indicated the SDC2 expression was closely related to multiple key pathways and thyroid differentiation markers. Targeting SDC2 expression significantly influenced the growth and invasion of PTC cells according to series of assays. Western-blotting results of α-SMA and ZO-1 also indicated the altered EMT process. Furthermore, attenuated SDC2 expression also leads to the PTC de-differentiation, which could be due to hedgehog signaling alteration.ConclusionsOur findings suggest that SDC2 would be a promising therapy target for advanced radioiodine refractory thyroid cancer, but the role in PTC progression is complicated and requires further exploration.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12902-025-02055-3.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer (TC), which accounts for the majority of the endocrinal tumor, has been reported as dramatically increasing incidence in many countries [1]. The trend was largely due to increased detection of papillary thyroid cancer (PTC), which as a result of the widespread application of neck ultrasound combined fine needle aspiration cytology examination [2]. Most PTC patients would obtain a favorable prognosis after an appropriate surgical approach, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) suppressive therapy with or without radioactive iodine ablation [3, 4]. However, with the diverse biological background, PTC prognosis ranges from very well to local/distant metastatic recurrence [5], about 2/3 of which would exhibit refractory to iodine-131 therapy (RAIR) or other features of de-differentiating. The RAIR would in turn leads to poorly differentiated TC (PDTC) or anaplastic thyroid cancer (ATC), accounting for the majority of thyroid cancer-related deaths [6, 7]. However, the fundamental mechanism of postulating RAIR or de-differentiation remains unclear, and effective management of advanced thyroid cancer are still challenging [7, 8].

Syndecan-2 (SDC2), also named as fibroglycan, have been reported to participate in numerous cell functions related to carcinogenesis [9, 10]. Consisting of an ecto-, single transmembrane and cytoplasmic domain, which interact with extracellular matrix molecules, intracellular kinases and actin cytoskeleton, indicates the key role of SDC2 during cancer initiation and progression [11–14]. Recent studies also revealed that SDC2 serves as a oncogene in multiple cancers including colon cancer, breast cancer, and pancreatic cancer [15–17]. For PTC, several reports on individual predictive models for post-surgical management had simultaneously SDC2 included as key factor simultaneously, indicating the importance of PTC prognosis [18, 19]. However, the potential role of SDC2 in PTC progression and recurrence remains unclear. Here, we explored the clinical significance of SDC2 in TCGA-THCA cohort, GEO datasets, and Human Protein Atlas. Integrated bioinformatic analyses of SDC2 related genes and pathways were also performed. After that, we carried out knockdown or overexpression of SDC2 in differentiated or poorly differentiated PTC cell lines. Multiple assays were performed to identify the impact of SDC2 on PTC invasion and growth. Finally, we also explored the potential mechanism of SDC2 in PTC progression and de-differentiation.

Methods

Obtaining transcriptional data from public database

We downloaded level-3 RNA sequencing data of PTC in the form of counts from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), updated in July 2019 [20]. TCGA, a landmark cancer genomics program, molecularly characterized over 20,000 primary cancer and matched normal samples spanning 33 cancer types. We applied a transcript per million (TPM) transformation and normalized the sequencing data with a base-2 logarithm. Cases with incomplete prognosis data were excluded. We obtained clinical data from the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena database, which provides interactive online visualization of seminal cancer genomics datasets. We also obtained datasets GSE29265 and GSE33630 [21], including PTC with paired normal thyroid samples from the GEO database for further analysis. Pan-cancer analysis across 32 types of TCGA cancer cohorts on SDC2 expression was based on TIMER 2.0 [22]. Representative immunobiological staining pictures were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas [23].

K-M analysis and identification of prognosis-associated factors

We applied Kaplan-Meier analysis to justify the prognosis value of SDC2 expression level on the progression-free interval (PFI) and overall survival (OS) period in TCGA-THCA cohort. Next, we used univariate and multivariate Cox regression models to identify meaningful prognostic parameters. Clinical information included age, gender, T stage, N stage, histological type, the status of extrathyroidal extension, residual tumor, pathological stage, and SDC2 expression level. Parameters with P < 0.1 in univariate Cox regression analysis were enrolled for further multivariate analysis. The K-M analysis and Cox regression model were applied with R’s “survival” package.

Identification of the SDC2 related genes and pathways

We explored the potential positive and negative related genes of SDC2 expression in the TGCA-THCA cohort by the “LinkFinder” module of the Linkedomics database, which allows flexible exploration of associations between a molecular attribute of interest and all other attributes for a selected cancer cohort [24]. After the identification of related genes, we then explored the biological understanding with the “LinkInterpreter” module by performing gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) through accessing the comprehensive functional category database in WebGestalt [25].

Cell culture and target gene interference

Human PTC differentiated cell line B-CPAP and poorly differentiated cell line KTC-1 [26] was kindly provided by the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Cell lines were cultivated under the circumstance of 5% CO2, 37 °C, in RPMI Medium 1640 (Invitrogen, 11875-093) with FBS (Gibco), with Non-essential Amino Acids (Invitrogen, 11140050), Glutamax (Invitrogen 35050061), and Sodium Pyruvate (Invitrogen, 11360070) added. Knockdown or overexpression of SDC2 was applied through pre-designed siRNA (Ribobio, stB0007808) versus negative control siRNA or plasmid (Sino Biological, HG10355-CH) versus C-terminal His-tagged vector by transfection reagent (Beyotime, Lipo8000) following manufacturers’ instructions. Western blotting analysis

Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer. 20 µg of protein was loaded on each well of 10% prefabricated gels and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore, U.S.). First antibodies included SDC2 (1:5000 dilution, Proteintech, China), ZO-1 (1:2000 dilution, Sigma-Aldrich, U.S.), a-SMA (1:2000 dilution, Invitrogen, U.S.), sodium iodide symporter (NIS) (1:1000 dilution, Proteintech, China), thyroid stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) (1:1000 dilution, Proteintech, China), beta-actin (1:5000 dilution, Lablead, China) and beta-tubulin (1:5000 dilution, Proteintech, China), followed by secondary goat anti-mouse or anti-rabbit IgG-HRP antibody incubation (1:10000 dilution, Lablead, China). Membranes were then incubated with enhanced chemiluminescence reagent to visualize the bands. Reagents, buffers, gels were purchased from Lablead.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis

We used TRIzol (TIANGEN, China) to lysate and isolate RNA from cells following the manufacturer’s protocol as previously described [27]. We then conducted qPCR after isolation of RNA, removal of DNA contamination, and reverse transcription for cDNA synthesis, as described previously with housekeeper (GAPDH) mRNA for normalization via the 2−ΔΔCt method [28]. The relative gene expression level of thyroid differentiating markers including thyroglobulin (TG), NIS, TSHR, thyroid peroxidase (TPO), paired box gene-8 (PAX-8) and thyroid transcription factor-1 (TTF-1) were explored. Also, The relative gene expression level of hedgehog signaling members including hedgehog acyltransferase (HHAT), HH-interacting protein (HHIP), Indian hedgehog (IHH), sonic hedgehog (SHH), desert hedgehog (DHH), Patched receptor (PTCH1) and glioma-associated oncogene (GLI1) were explored [29]. Each experiment was repeated three times. Primers were designed and manufactured by Sangon, China. Sequences of primers used for RT-qPCR experiment were listed in Table 2.

Scratch wound-healing assay

Cells were lysed with 5% trypsin (Invitrogen, U.S.) and counted, then 10*105 cells were inoculated in each well of 6-plate. When the cells reached 90–100% confluence, then we carried out a scratch wound-healing assay to explore the influence of SDC2 knockdown in PTC cells migration. We used a 200µL sterile plastic tip to scratch a wound line across the surface of the plate and then removed suspended cells with PBS. After that, the normal medium was replaced with a reduced serum medium (2% FBS) for suppressing cell proliferation. Cells were continuously cultured for 48 h, images of different time points were taken with a microscope. Each assay was repeated three times. The area between borderline of different time points were measured and analyzed.

Cell counting Kits-8 (CCK8) assay

Cells were lysed and counted, then 5*103 cells were inoculated in each well of 96-well plate and cultivated. Then we carried out a CCK8 assay following the manufacturer’s protocol (Meilunbio, China). 450 nm OD value of wells without cells as blanks in each time point was obtained after incubation with 10ul of CCK8 reagent for 2 h. Each experiment well was repeated six times. The proliferation of cells was presented as absorbance.

Cell invasiveness (transwell) assay

Cells were lysed and counted in serum-free medium, then 8–10*104 cells were inoculated in each transwell chamber (Corning, USA) of 24-well plate and cultivated, then add 800ul of full-medium in lower chamber. Then we carried out a transwell assay following the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells that infiltrated through polycarbonate membrane after incubation for 36 h were fixed and stained, then counted under microscope. Each experiment well was repeated three times.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.1.1 (Kick Things) and GraphPad Prism 8.3.4 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). Continuous data were analyzed using unpaired or paired t-tests. Categorical variables were analyzed using Chi-squared tests. Multiple testing corrections were applied with Benjamini and Hochberg method for difference and enrichment analyses. P-values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

SDC2 expression was closely related to pivotal clinical characters and prognosis of PTC

Next, we wanted to confirm the clinical importance of SDC2 expression level and pivotal clinical characters of PTC. In patients divided by age (≤ 45 or > 45), T stage (T1&T2 or T3&T4), N stage (N0 or N1), AJCC stage (I&II or III&IV), progression status (progression-free or recurrence), histological type (classical & follicular or tall cell), residual tumor (R0 or R1&R2) and extrathyroidal invasion (None or yes), the SDC2 expression level were lower in patients with > 45, T3&T4, N1, stage III&IV, recurrent disease, tall cell type, residual tumor R1&R2 and with extrathyroidal invasion, the differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), as shown in Fig. 2A-H. We next investigated the prognostic value of SDC2 expression in the PTC patients from TCGA-THCA cohort. For the relatively well survival outcome of PTC, we also compared progression free interval to evaluate the prognostic value of SDC2. For the recurrence status, patients with lower SDC2 expression level had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.001), and for the survival status, the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), as shown in Fig. 2I, J. To explore the prognostic value of SDC2 expressions during relevant clinical parameters of PTC, we carried on a univariate and followed multivariate Cox proportional to identify the prognosis-related parameters. Univariate analysis revealed that the parameters including age, T stage, AJCC stage, histological type and extrathyroidal invasion status were with P < 0.1 and were then enrolled into multivariate analysis. After the exclusion of samples with incomplete prognosis-related parameters and follow-up data, we included a total of 480 PTC cases for further multivariate analysis. SDC2 expression level (P < 0.05) was identified as independent prognosis-related factors in both the univariate and multivariate analysis. SDC2 were closely related to thyrocyte differentiation markers’ expression and Hedgehog signaling expression

As PTC acquires mutational burden, they undergo de-differentiation from well-differentiated PTC to PDTC or ATC [7], which accompanies cancer aggressiveness, refractory to radioactive iodine, and presented as loss of thyrocyte differentiation markers [31]. Spearman correlation tests have revealed that SDC2 expression is correlated closely to thyrocyte differentiation markers expression before. Furthermore, since the pivotal role of hedgehog signaling in thyroid hormone synthesis [35, 36], we also explored the relative alterations. Discussion

As previously described, the incidence of PTC dramatically increased in decades and has been a heavy burden for patients’ health and medical insurance systems [37]. Most PTC patients will have a good prognosis; however, 10–20% of them would suffer from metastatic recurrence, even subsequently RAIR, due to complex tumor heterogeneity [5]. In present, diagnoses of PTC before surgery relied primarily on neck ultrasound sonography and pathological exam following fine-needle inspiration [38, 39]. However, reliable molecular marker for predicting PTC prognosis is still lacking [40]. As a heparan sulfate proteoglycan family member, SDC2 emerges as a pivotal role in embryonic angiogenesis [41], multiple strains of cancer cell growth, and metastasis [42, 43]. Also, SDC2 Aberrant methylation also has been applied in stool sample for early colorectal cancer diagnoses [16, 44]. Reported as a key component in several predictive models for predicting PTC prognosis [45, 46], we were interested in the predictive value of SDC2 in PTC. In our study, subgroup analysis, immunohistological analysis, and Cox proportional regression analysis also revealed the pivotal clinical significance, which managed us to explore the underlying unclarified mechanism of SDC2 in PTC progression. To achieve this, we performed gene interference of SDC2 in well or poorly differentiated PTC cell lines. Following the fact that lower SDC2 expression level accompanies advanced disease characters in PTC, the knockdown of SDC2 also leads to enhanced invasiveness according to our study. While the results were also significant in PDTC cell with overexpressed SDC2, which suggested that SDC2 plays as a tumor suppressor in PTC. Our finding were in concordance with former reports by Wang et, al, who firstly discovered the phenomenon that the significant attenuated SDC2 expression in ATC samples compared to PTC samples [30]. EMT is a morphological alteration in which cells develop a fibroblast-like phenotype with a reduction in the expression of adhesion molecules, resulting in loss of the contact inhibition mediated by the tissue structure. Eventually, loss of connection to the basement membrane and acquisition of mesenchymal phenotypes promoted cancer cells migration and metastasis [47]. In our study, western blotting results indicated the altered EMT process with SDC2 gene interference. Thus, we supposed that the phenomenon that a lower SDC2 expression level accompanies advanced clinical characters in PTC could be due to the altered EMT process.

Recent reports indicated the pivotal roles of abnormally silenced thyroid-specific genes and transcription factors such as TG, NIS, TSHR, TPO, PAX-8, and TTF-1 [48, 49]. Targeting those genes or transcriptional factors have been reported partially prevented and reversed the trends of de-differentiation [49, 50]. After Pearson correlation analysis in TCGA-THCA cohort, we were also interested in the influences of SDC2 expression on thyroid differentiating markers expression. What surprised us was that interference of SDC2 significantly affected the expression of TSHR, PAX8 and NIS, indicating that SDC2 of the pivotal molecular switch for PTC cell differentiation and thyroid hormone synthesis process. During our findings, SDC2 knockdown in differentiated BCPAP cells leads to decreased expression of NIS and TSHR, indicating the pivotal role of SDC2 in maintaining the iodine uptake ability of thyroid cancer cells and requires further study. Also, overexpression of SDC2 in metastatic de-differentiated KTC-1 cells leads to increased expression of NIS and TSHR, which also provided better evidence for the research findings. Considering the negative correlation of differentiation level with the invasiveness of TC [51, 52], and the significance of enhanced EMT in thyroid cancer progression and de-differentiation [53, 54], our findings suggests a strong correlation of SDC2 level and TC invasiveness and differentiation. For the hedgehog signaling pathway, studies have confirmed its’ crucial role in thyroid growth, development, and tumor progression, including the synthesis of thyroid hormones [35, 36, 55]. Heparan sulfate proteoglycan molecules and HHIP work together to sequester GLI, maintaining the normal activation level of the hedgehog pathway, and SDC2 is precisely an important member of the heparan sulfate proteoglycan family [56]. Based on the findings, we speculate that SDC2 can affect the activation of the hedgehog signaling pathway, leading to the progression and dedifferentiation of thyroid cancer. Our results also clarified that attenuated SDC2 expression would promote PTC progression and de-differentiation through altered EMT process and hedgehog signaling.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study firstly comprehensive analyzed the pivotal prognostic value of SDC2. Further, interference of SDC2 affected PTC cell invasiveness, accompanied by changed EMT process and multiple thyroids differentiating markers, which could be due to generally altered hedgehog signaling. These findings indicated the complicated and pivotal role for SDC2 in PTC progression and suggested that SDC2 would be a promising target for the intervention of advanced thyroid cancer with RAIR. At the same time, there were some limitations in our study. The first was that the samples we explored of TCGA-THCA, and GEO datasets was collected from North American and Caucasian people. Possible biases would occur when applying the conclusion in different regions. Another, involving both knockdown and overexpression in other kinds of cell lines or in-vivo study would lead to a more robust conclusion. Last, understanding the up and downstream effectors related to SDC2 requires further exploration.

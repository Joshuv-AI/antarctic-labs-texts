Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential as a risk factor for atrial fibrillation: Evidence of a causal relationship by Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Qing Zhang, Guohao Wang, Zhangpu Yan, Weiling Xu, Shaofeng Zhang, Jun Li, Ling Liang, Changqing Sun, Rong Tang, Joachim Pircher, Qiang Xie, Wuyang Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) has emerged as an independent cardiovascular risk factor, with recent studies suggesting a link to atrial fibrillation (AF). However, the causal nature of this relationship, particularly the role of DNMT3a and TET2 mutations, remains unclear.ObjectiveThis study aimed to assess the causal relationship between CHIP and AF and identify potential mediating pathways.MethodsWe conducted a bidirectional Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis using genome-wide association study data for CHIP from the UK Biobank (n = 368,526) and AF data from 2 large, independent cohorts (287,805 individuals from FinnGen and 1,030,836 from 6 additional AF studies). A 2-step MR mediation analysis was used to explore potential intermediate risk factors.ResultsInverse variance weighted MR analysis demonstrated a causal effect of CHIP on increased AF risk (meta-inverse variance weighted, odds ratio [OR] 1.057; P = .001), including both DNMT3a- and TET2-related CHIP (DNMT3a, OR 1.048, P = 4.56 × 10-4; TET2, OR 1.038, P = .025). Causal associations between overall CHIP, including DNMT3a-CHIP, and AF were validated by a 2-sample MR study in 2 independent cohorts. Reverse MR showed no evidence of AF causing CHIP. The mediation analysis identified elevated systolic blood pressure as a key mediator in the DNMT3a-CHIP–AF pathway, accounting for 7.8% of the effect (P = .034).ConclusionOur findings provide genetic evidence that CHIP, particularly DNMT3a-related mutations, causally but modestly increases AF risk. There is no support for reverse causation. Elevated systolic blood pressure was identified as a significant mediator linking DNMT3a-CHIP to AF.Keywords: Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), DNMT3a-CHIP, TET2-CHIP, Atrial fibrillation, Hypertension, Systolic blood pressure

Key Findings

▪Mendelian randomization analyses using 2 large, independent atrial fibrillation (AF) genome-wide association studies demonstrated a significant but relatively modest causal association between clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) and increased AF risk—both for overall CHIP (odds ratio [OR] 1.06; P = .001) and for specific driver mutations, including DNMT3a-CHIP (OR 1.05; P = 4.56 × 10-4) and TET2-CHIP (OR 1.04; P = .025).

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) imposes an escalating global health burden,1 and inflammation-driven profibrotic remodeling of the left atrium plays a central role in its pathogenesis.2, 3, 4 AF often coexists with hypertension—particularly elevated systolic blood pressure (SBP)—as well as coronary artery disease (CAD), cardiomyopathy, and heart failure, and is further influenced by metabolic and hormonal conditions such as diabetes, obesity, hyperthyroidism, and sleep apnea.2,5, 6, 7 However, these established risk factors do not fully explain AF onset or progression, indicating additional mechanisms remain to be defined.8

Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) is characterized by somatic mutations in hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) that result in the proliferation of mutant clones without accompanying hematological disorders.9 This age-related condition affects at least 10%–20% of adults older than 70 years.10 The most often altered genes in CHIP are the epigenetic regulators DNMT3a and TET2, which collectively represent more than 70% of CHIP cases.10 Mutations in DNMT3a generally disrupt DNA methylation patterns, whereas mutations in TET2 hinder DNA demethylation processes, both leading to dysregulated gene expression and modifications in HSC self-renewal and differentiation. Notably, CHIP has been identified as an independent risk factor for cardiovascular illnesses, with numerous extensive studies indicating a 1.5–2.5-fold heightened risk of atherosclerotic events among CHIP carriers.11,12 The mechanistic connection between CHIP and cardiovascular pathology seems to be facilitated by heightened NLRP3 inflammasome activation and augmented synthesis of proinflammatory cytokines (notably interleukin 1β and interleukin 6 [IL-6]) by mutated macrophages, thereby accelerating atherosclerotic plaque development and fostering myocardial inflammation and fibrosis.10,13 These findings establish CHIP as a novel, age-related cardiovascular risk factor operating through distinct inflammatory pathways.14, 15, 16, 17, 18

CHIP is recognized as being linked to cardiovascular illnesses, including CAD and heart failure. Recent observational studies have extended this association to arrhythmias, particularly AF, given that both CHIP and AF share common pathophysiological features, including chronic inflammation and aging.14 In an East Asian cohort, CHIP mutations were more common among patients with AF, with TET2 (odds ratio [OR] 1.65) and DNMT3a mutations (OR 1.45) most strongly associated.19 Similarly, data from the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) and the UK Biobank (UKB) showed large CHIP clones (variant allele frequency [VAF] ≥10%) had a meta-hazard ratio of 1.12 for AF.20 Although these findings support an association between CHIP and AF, the causality of this link remains unresolved, and reverse causation cannot be excluded.

Mendelian randomization (MR) is a robust genetic epidemiology technique that uses naturally occurring genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to clarify causal links between risk factors and health outcomes. Using the random distribution of genes during meiosis, analogous to randomization in clinical trials, MR mitigates confounding variables and reverse causation present in observational studies.21 This method effectively connects association and causation, rendering it a helpful instrument for finding medicinal targets and directing public health actions.21,22 A 2-sample MR study is a classic approach to estimate the causal effect of an exposure on an outcome by analyzing associations in 2 independent samples.23 In this study, we conducted bidirectional, 2-sample MR analyses using large genome-wide association study (GWAS) datasets—powerful approaches for identifying genetic contributions to complex traits and diseases—for CHIP (stratified by DNMT3a and TET2 mutations) and AF. A 2-step mediation MR framework was further used to examine potential intermediate pathways linking CHIP to AF risk.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a 2-sample MR study using publicly available GWAS summary datasets (Table 1). The study adhered to core MR assumptions (Figure 1): (1) genetic variants are strongly associated with the exposure, (2) independent of confounders, and (3) affect the outcome only through the exposure.

Bidirectional MR was performed to evaluate the causal relationship between overall CHIP—including DNMT3a- and TET2-CHIP—and AF, as well as the possibility of reverse causality (Figure 1). We also performed a 2-step MR mediation analysis to assess whether established AF risk factors mediate the CHIP–AF association. Study reporting followed Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology using Mendelian Randomization24 guidelines (Supplemental Table 1).

GWAS summary data

Summary association estimates linking single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) to each phenotype were obtained from the relevant GWAS (detailed in Table 1). Overall CHIP including gene-specific CHIP variants (DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP) was from a large exome-sequencing study of 368,526 UKB participants, which identified 25,657 CHIP carriers across 23 CHIP-related genes.25 Given that GWAS data were derived from a meta-analysis of 6 AF cohorts (60,620 cases and 970,216 controls).26 For replication, we used GWAS from the FinnGen cohort (55,853 cases and 231,952 controls) for AF. Summary data from GWASs for established AF risk factors, including heart failure, CAD, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, obesity, sleep apnea, hyperthyroidism, SBP, diastolic blood pressure (DBP), cardiomyopathy, and diabetes, were also used. All datasets (Table 1) were deidentified and publicly accessible with appropriate ethics approvals.

IVs selection

In MR studies, IVs refer to genetic variants, typically SNPs, used as instruments. All used SNPs for exposures, mediators, and outcomes in this study were derived from different large GWAS research presented in Table 1. We adhered to the rigorous criteria for IV picks.27 SNPs linked to exposures at genome-wide significance (P < 5 × 10-8) were identified as candidate instruments. Linkage disequilibrium r2 quantifies the correlation between 2 genetic variants, and a strict threshold of r2 < 0.001 and physical distance of >10,000 kb ensures that selected SNPs are minimally correlated and independent as instruments.28 SNPs associated with confounders or outcomes (P < 5 × 10-6) were omitted.

We assessed instrument strength using the F-statistic, calculated as F = β2 / SE2, where β represents the SNP–exposure effect and SE denotes its standard error. Given that F > 10 is typically deemed sufficient, we implemented a rigorous criterion and preserved solely variations with F > 10.29 We used Steiger filtering to confirm that the causal direction proceeds from exposure to outcome, rather than the inverse (Supplemental Table 2). Comprehensive results are presented in Supplemental Excel Files 1 and 2.

MR statistical and sensitivity analyses

Causal estimates were obtained using 4 statistical methods: inverse variance weighted (IVW),30 weighted median,31 weighted mode,32 and MR-Egger regression.33 IVW is considered the principal approach.30 Causality was determined if the subsequent conditions were satisfied: all 4 approaches produced effect estimates in the same direction (ie, OR consistently >1 or <1), the IVW result was statistically significant (P < .05), and there was no evidence of directional horizontal pleiotropy, as shown by a nonsignificant MR-Egger intercept (P > .05).28 The heterogeneity among instrumental factors was assessed with Cochran’s Q statistic. Outlier variations were identified and rectified using MR-PRESSO34 (version 1.0). Outlier SNPs were eliminated, and the MR analyses were reiterated to evaluate the robustness of the causal estimates. We evaluated pleiotropy and the impact of individual variants by funnel plots and MR-Egger intercepts and conducted leave-one-out studies to determine robustness against the removal of single SNPs26 (Supplemental Figures 1–4). All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.3).

Replication and meta-analysis

To corroborate the MR results associating CHIP with AF, we conducted a replication of the studies using an independent, extensive AF GWAS from FinnGen. We assessed total CHIP (comprising DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP) as the exposure and AF as the outcome. The specifics of the FinnGen AF GWAS are presented in Table 1. Subsequently, we conducted a meta-analysis integrating IVW values from the 2 MR cycles. The I2 statistic is a commonly used metric for heterogeneity in meta-analysis, derived from Cochran’s Q statistic, indicating the amount of variability in effect estimates owing to between-study variance. A fixed-effects model was used when heterogeneity was minimal (I2 < 50%); conversely, a random-effects model was used when heterogeneity was substantial (I2 > 50%).35

Mediation analysis

We used the IVW method to estimate the causal effect of CHIP on candidate mediators (Table 1) and of each mediator on AF. The candidate mediators selected for this study were established risk factors for AF, including heart failure, heart failure and CAD, heart failure and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, heart failure and overweight, CAD, sleep apnea, hyperthyroidism, SBP, DBP, cardiomyopathy, type 2 diabetes, and type 1 diabetes. A 2-step MR mediation analysis was conducted only when both links—CHIP → mediator and mediator → AF—were statistically significant by IVW (P < .05). In the MR study, the causal effect (β) can be calculated by ln(OR). In particular, first, we estimated the effect of CHIP on each mediator, β1. Second, we estimated the effect of each mediator on AF, β2. The indirect (mediated) effect was computed as βindirect = β1 × β2, and the mediated proportion as (β1 × β2) / βtotal, where βtotal is the overall CHIP → AF effect or DNMT3a-CHIP → AF effect, estimated by IVW. Confidence intervals (CIs) and P values for the product term and the mediated proportion were derived using the delta method.36 IVW served as the primary estimator, and total effects were decomposed into direct and indirect components.37

Patient consent statement

This research adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. This study used publicly available, deidentified summary-level genetic association data to conduct MR analyses. The contributing studies obtained ethics approval and informed consent from participants. No individual-level identifiable data were used, and no new data collection or interventions were conducted; therefore, additional patient consent was not required.

Results

CHIP causally increases AF risk

We conducted 2-sample MR analyses using CHIP GWAS data from 368,526 UKB participants25 to assess the causal relationship between CHIP and AF. The AF GWAS originated from the amalgamation of 6 cohorts,26 yielding summary SNP-trait data for 1,030,836 individuals (Table 1). These cohorts include the Nord-Trøndelag Health Study, deCODE, the Michigan Genomics Initiative, DiscovEHR, UKB, and the AFGen Consortium. A total of 24 IVs were discovered for overall CHIP and DNMT3a-CHIP, and 6 for TET2-CHIP, according to the IVs selection criteria (described in Methods). Cochran’s Q test revealed heterogeneity (Ph = .005) (Table 2), whereas MR-Egger regression suggested potential horizontal pleiotropy (Pintercept = .044, as shown in Table 2 and Supplemental Excel File 3), affecting the set of IVs related to overall CHIP and AF. We subsequently used MR-PRESSO to identify the outlier, which is the SNP (rs11746711). Upon the exclusion of the outlier SNP, the overall CHIP exhibited a statistically causative relationship with heightened AF risk (IVW OR 1.043; 95% CI 1.003–1.084; P = .036), corroborated by consistent results from alternative MR methodologies (including MR Egger, weighted median, and weighted mode; Figure 2 and Supplemental Excel File 4). Furthermore, it did not demonstrate horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity (Pintercept = .106; Ph = .144) (Table 2), indicating the robustness of the MR findings. Genetically predicted DNMT3a-CHIP had a substantial association with AF (IVW OR 1.035; 95% CI 1.003–1.069; P = .032), corroborated by the results of 3 other MR techniques in the same direction (Figure 2). Nonetheless, the research did not demonstrate a significantly causal connection between TET2-CHIP and AF (Figure 2). MR-PRESSO analysis and Cochran’s Q test demonstrated the robustness of the MR results for DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP in relation to AF, respectively (Table 2). To confirm the acceptance of overlap between cohorts of the exposure and the outcome, we meticulously examined the cohort descriptions of the corresponding GWAS datasets and computed the overlap percentage. The projected sample overlap between overall CHIP (including DNMT3a- and TET2-related CHIP) and the aggregated 6 cohorts of AF was roughly 0.33–0.35. Burgess et al38 indicate that this degree of overlap is anticipated to result in negligible bias (∼0.05), as assessed with the web tool (https://sb452.shinyapps.io/overlap/). Consequently, the detected overlap is unlikely to significantly affect the validity of our causal inferences.

To validate our findings, we conducted a replication of the MR study with AF GWAS data from the FinnGen cohort (n = 287,805), which is distinct from the GWAS of 6 AF cohorts. The findings (Figure 2) demonstrated significant correlations for overall CHIP (IVW OR 1.094; 95% CI 1.027–1.164; P = .005) and DNMT3a-CHIP (IVW OR 1.076; 95% CI 1.027–1.128; P = .002). Cochran’s Q test revealed heterogeneity among all 3 sets of independent variables (overall CHIP to AF, DNMT3a-CHIP to AF, and TET2-CHIP to AF) (Table 2). Nevertheless, MR-Egger regression indicated an absence of horizontal pleiotropy in the 3 sets of IVs (all Pintercept > .05) (Table 2 and Supplemental Excel File 3). Furthermore, all MR methodologies produced directionally consistent outcomes (Figure 2), hence affirming the reliability of the MR results.

Meta-analysis of both outcome datasets supported causal relationships (Figure 2 and Supplemental Figure 5): overall CHIP to AF (IVW OR 1.057; 95% CI 1.022–1.092; P = .001), DNMT3a-CHIP to AF (OR 1.048; 95% CI 1.021–1.076; P = 4.56 × 10-4), and TET2-CHIP to AF (OR 1.038; 95% CI 1.005–1.072; P = .025). Although TET2-CHIP did not reach significance in individual cohorts, the combined meta-analysis indicated a modest but significant effect.

No evidence that AF causally influences CHIP

Reverse MR analyses assessed whether AF directly affects CHIP. In all statistical analyses, genetic prediction of AF showed no association with overall CHIP (IVW OR 1.024; 95% CI 0.992–1.056; P = .142), DNMT3a-CHIP (OR 1.035; 95% CI 0.999–1.072; P = .054), or TET2-CHIP (OR 1.014; 95% CI 0.943–1.090; P = .711) (Figure 3). Replication using AF FinnGen data yielded the same null outcomes (Figure 3). The combined meta-analysis of both cohorts revealed no significant reverse causal effect (Figure 3 and Supplemental Figure 6). Although Cochran’s Q test indicated the presence of heterogeneity in the MR analysis of AF on DNMT3a-CHIP (Supplemental Excel File 5), no evidence of pleiotropy or outlier bias was discovered (MR-Egger regression, all Pintercept > .05) (Supplemental Excel File 6). This segment of the results indicates that genetically predicted AF is unlikely to be connected with the overall risk of CHIP, including DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP.

SBP as a mediator in DNMT3a-CHIP–AF association

To identify potential mediators, we evaluated established AF-related risk factors using a 2-step MR analysis (Table 1). First, we assessed the association of CHIP and its subtypes with each risk factor. Overall, CHIP was linked to cardiomyopathy (IVW OR 1.090; P = .014) (Table 3). DNMT3a-CHIP was causally associated with increased SBP (β1 = 0.013; P = .030) and cardiomyopathy (OR 1.077; P = .016). Owing to unresolved pleiotropy in the CHIP–CAD analysis (MR-Egger intercept P = .007; after removing outliers, MR-Egger intercept P = .022) (Supplemental Excel File 7), CAD results from overall CHIP were excluded (Table 3).

Next, we tested whether these candidate mediators causally influenced AF risk. Using GWAS data from 6 AF cohorts, we observed significant causal associations of SBP (OR 1.235; P = 2.28 × 10-22), DBP (OR 1.236; P = 7.09 × 10-24), heart failure (OR 1.317; P = .008), CAD (OR 1.130; P = 2.40 × 10-22), and sleep apnea (OR 1.093; P = .025) with AF (Table 4). Genetic prediction of cardiomyopathy was not shown to be significantly associated with AF risk (Table 4). As calculated by the method of the 2-step MR mediation analysis, SBP was identified as the sole significant mediator linking DNMT3a-CHIP to AF (proportion mediated 7.8%; P = .034), suggesting that increasing SBP may partly explain the increased AF risk conferred by DNMT3a-CHIP (Figure 4).

Discussion

This study provides the first genetic evidence for a causal relationship between CHIP and AF, using large, independent European GWAS datasets. Our key findings are as follows: (1) overall CHIP, including DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP, is causally associated with increased AF risk; (2) no causal effect of AF on CHIP was observed; and (3) SBP mediates the DNMT3a-CHIP–AF association.

Over the past decade, CHIP has gained recognition as a novel risk factor for several cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), supported by substantial clinical and experimental evidence. CHIP has been linked to CVDs, including atherosclerosis and ischemic heart disease, primarily through inflammation.12,39 TET2-CHIP promotes interleukin 1β and IL-6 expression via NLRP3 inflammasome activation,40 whereas DNMT3a mutations—most common in aging—have been shown to drive macrophage infiltration and T-cell activation in animal models.41,42 Inflammatory cytokines (eg, IL-6, CXCL1, CCL5) are elevated in DNMT3a-deficient macrophages.40 Given that inflammation plays a recognized role in AF pathophysiology, these findings support a plausible biological link between CHIP and AF.4,43,44 Although several observational studies have reported associations between CHIP and AF, the causal association and direction should be further determined.14,19,20,25,45,46 A cohort study19 in East Asia showed increased CHIP prevalence in patients with AF, with TET2 and DNMT3a mutations most strongly associated (TET2-CHIP to AF, OR 1.65, P = .03; DNMT3a-CHIP to AF, OR 1.45, P = .01). A UKB analysis45 linked CHIP to incident AF (fully adjusted model, hazard ratio [HR] 1.11; P = .001) but found no significant associations for DNMT3a and TET2 mutations after full adjustment. A prospective analysis20 in ARIC and UKB cohorts further supported the role of large CHIP clones (VAF ≥10%) in AF risk, with significant associations seen only for TET2-CHIP. These inconsistencies may be caused by differences in study design, ancestry, and sequencing sensitivity. To further clarify the causal relationship, we used large datasets (CHIP from UKB and AF from both a 6-study meta-analysis and FinnGen) and applied rigorous MR methods. Compared with a previous MR study47 that included individuals with hematologic malignancies, our study excluded such patients, used a larger sample, and confirmed the findings in 2 independent AF datasets—thus improving causal inference.

Our findings confirmed a consistent causal relationship between CHIP and AF after excluding the outlier SNP rs11746711 (from the MR study of overall CHIP to AF GWAS across 6 combined studies). This result was supported by 4 MR methods and validated using 2 independent AF GWAS datasets. The causal effects of overall CHIP, encompassing DNMT3a-CHIP and TET2-CHIP, on AF are relatively small (OR 1.04–1.06), considering that chronic inflammation is a principal cause of AF. A previous study47 investigating the causal relationship between clonal hematopoiesis (CH), comprising individuals with malignant diseases, and AF through MR revealed that the causal effects of CH on AF are relatively modest (overall CH to AF, OR 1.09, P = 4.9 × 10-4; DNMT3a-CH to AF, OR 1.06, P = 8.1 × 10-3; TET2-CH to AF, OR 1.00, P = .71). In summary of pertinent prospective studies, CHIP is a significant predictor of elevated AF risk, with a modest predictive impact (HR 1.11–1.12).20,45,48 Collectively, we assert that CHIP causally elevates the risk of AF with a moderate impact revealed by the MR study. The potential reasons we considered are as follows: first, AF is a multifactorial disease with numerous established risk factors, and CHIP may represent merely 1 of many contributors to its pathogenesis; second, the specific inflammatory pathways activated by CHIP mutations may exert variable effects on atrial tissue in contrast to their well-documented influence on vascular inflammation and atherosclerosis.14,49,50 Moreover, the impact of CHIP on AF may be diminished by competing hazards, especially in older populations where both illnesses are more common. Finally, the moderate effect magnitude might reflect heterogeneity in CHIP's influence across different patient categories. An additional mechanistic study is necessary to elucidate the causal link between CHIP and AF.

Interestingly, although TET2-CHIP did not achieve statistical significance in individual cohorts, the aggregated meta-analysis revealed a modest yet significant effect (OR 1.038; P = .025). The reduced sample size of TET2-CHIP compared with DNMT3a-CHIP (n = 3918 vs n = 16,219) likely accounts for the nonsignificant MR results in AF GWAS analyses, illustrating how limited statistical power can obscure modest causal effects and implying that TET2-CHIP contributes to AF risk. In relation to previous research, Schuermans et al45 examined CHIP’s predictive capability for AF among the UKB population and found that TET2-CHIP does not substantially predict an elevated incidence of AF (HR 1.15; P = .09). A prospective cohort analysis using the ARIC dataset and UKB indicated that TET2-CHIP is associated with an increased risk of AF (HR 1.18; P = .04).20 Moreover, Lin et al48 demonstrated that Tet2 enhances the tendency for AF by elevating Nlrp3 expression and activating CaMKII (Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II) in murine models. Consequently, we conclude that TET2-CHIP exerts a modest yet significant influence on elevating the risk of AF. The fundamental mechanism behind the causal relationship necessitates additional inquiry.

CHIP pathogenesis involves both intrinsic and extrinsic factors. Mutations in TET2 and DNMT3a enhance HSC self-renewal, whereas aging and cardiovascular risk factors alter the bone marrow niche.51, 52, 53, 54 Inflammatory states may promote clonal expansion and dominance.55 Although AF has also been linked to systemic inflammation, the potential causal influence of AF on CHIP has been hypothesized but not previously investigated. Our reverse MR analysis found no evidence that AF causally influences CHIP, reinforcing CHIP as an upstream factor.

We next explored potential mediators of the DNMT3a-CHIP–AF relationship and identified elevated SBP as the only significant mediator, accounting for 7.8% of the association. SBP is a well-established risk factor for AF, yet few studies have examined its link with CHIP.56,57 A 2019 study46 observed increased SBP in individuals with DNMT3a-CHIP and therapy-resistant hypertension. The East Asian cohort further supported this relationship, reporting higher hypertension prevalence in patients with AF with CHIP.19 Mechanistically, DNMT3a-mutant macrophages were found to enhance fibrosis via heparin-binding EGF-like growth factor, potentially contributing to vascular stiffness and AF risk.58 However, the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood and require further investigation. Our study suggests that DNMT3a-CHIP may contribute to increased SBP, providing additional evidence to support further investigation into the impact of CHIP on hypertension.

Our findings yield several key clinical implications: CHIP testing may enhance AF risk stratification, particularly in borderline or unexplained cases; aggressive blood pressure management may be especially beneficial for DNMT3a-CHIP carriers to mitigate AF risk; emerging CHIP-targeted therapies for CVD prevention may confer modest benefits for AF prevention; and our results reinforce the role of inflammation in AF pathogenesis, supporting the potential utility of anti-inflammatory approaches in select patients.

This study has several strengths. We used large, nonoverlapping datasets with robust IV selection, conducted bidirectional MR with replication, and applied a mediation analysis to explore biological pathways. However, limitations include potential lack of generalizability to non-European populations, absence of mutation-specific VAF analysis, and limited detection of rare CHIP variants by exome sequencing.

Conclusion

Our findings provide genetic evidence that CHIP, particularly DNMT3a-CHIP, is causally associated with increased AF risk. We also identify elevated SBP as a potential mediator in this association. These results advance our understanding of CHIP-related arrhythmogenesis and highlight novel targets for risk stratification and preventive strategies.

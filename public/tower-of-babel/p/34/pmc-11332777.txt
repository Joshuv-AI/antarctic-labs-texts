Path analysis for affecting the physical fitness of marine majors: A cross-sectional observational study from the perspective of health
Authors: Yuepeng Sun, Yu Su, Yufeng Yuan, Quanjun Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The physical fitness of marine major students is critically linked to their academic performance and future career prospects. To investigate the influence of various environmental factors on the physical fitness of maritime majors and to identify potential strategies for improving physical fitness among this population. A cross-sectional observational study was conducted among maritime students. The study surveyed and field-tested 517 male marine majors across 1st to 3rd-grade students using a combination of questionnaire surveys and on-site testing methods. There were no significant differences in body mass index (BMI) and social support among these 1st to 3rd-grade students; however, there were differences in sports lifestyle and physical fitness index (PFI). Social support positively correlated with a sports lifestyle. There is no correlation between BMI and PFI. Social support influences the sports lifestyle of marine majors, which in turn affects BMI and PFI. Pleasure-type and fitness-type sports lifestyles serve as mediators between social support and PFI, while BMI is also a mediator between sports lifestyle and PFI. Social support affects the sports lifestyle of marine majors, which affects BMI and directly influences PFI.

1. Introduction

In November 2015, China submitted the “Maritime Labour Convention, 2006” ratification to the International Labour Organization, and it was officially implemented in the following years.[1] The “Maritime Labour Convention, 2006” mandates each member state to maintain the health and well-being of seafarers and emphasizes the necessity for preventative measures such as promoting and educating health.[2] Studies have shown that seamens’ physical exercise and health promotion behavior could improve their well-being and work efficiency.[3,4] However, students’ participation in physical activities is generally insufficient, and the number of college students participating in exercise has significantly reduced.[5] According to studies, the number of children and adolescents in China aged 9 to 22 who exercise less than an hour a day is notably high, particularly among male college students aged 18 (82.5%) and female college students aged 21 and over 89.8%.[6] This reduction of exercise time will inevitably reduce their level of physical fitness.

College students majoring in navigation require strong physical fitness to support their career development, which is important in developing the social economy. The low physical activity participation rate of college students is a universal concern, and increasingly, researchers are focusing on improving physical activity levels. Numerous studies have discovered that subjective support and the degree to which assistance is offered and executed are advantageous in promoting college students’ healthy lifestyles.[7] Social support is the exogenous motivation of college students’ social development, and it can promote their physical activity behaviors at all stages.[8] However, for Marine students, the relationship between environmental factors and physical health is still less concerned. Furthermore, current studies have overlooked the role of student’s weight status, and environmental factors may play a different role in promoting physical activity levels in overweight and obese students than in normal-weight students. Overweight and obesity have been shown to reduce exercise participation, which may be related to weight gain, decreased rate of muscle contraction, and increased oxygen consumption.[9] As the body mass index (BMI) increases, individuals who exercise must overcome greater resistance, which is a great challenge for overweight and obese individuals.[10]

The ecological approach differs from traditional individual-centric approaches by considering the broader context in which individuals operate. It acknowledges that individuals are not isolated entities but are shaped by and shape their environment. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between environmental factors and physical fitness level, proposing that BMI may moderate the relationship between physical fitness and social support (Fig. 1).

All participants in the study were thoroughly informed about the purpose, content, potential benefits, and risks of the study, and they provided written informed consent. The study utilizes data from a physical fitness test organized by Dalian Maritime University, which is not expected to have any adverse effects on the participants. The research protocol was reviewed by the Ethics Committee of Dalian Maritime University, which determined that the study qualified for an ethical exemption due to the nature of the test’s content and process.

Our test subjects mainly come from students in 3 majors: maritime, marine engineering, and maritime electronics. These 3 majors encompass the professional maritime practitioners trained by Chinese maritime universities, thus our research sample size is representative. The independent variables in this study are BMI and physical lifestyle, while the dependent variable is physical fitness index (PFI).

2.2. Questionnaire design

Child and Adolescent Social Support Scale: The Child and Adolescent Social Support Scale questionnaire comprises 10 items, and the specific implementation is referred to previous studies.[11] The internal consistency coefficients α of the 3 subscales of subjective support, objective support, and support utilization of college students are 0.845, 0.814, and 0.874 respectively. The total scale is 0.906. The test–retest reliability was 0.630, 0.799, and 0.720 respectively, whereas the total scale was 0.821. Structural validity: root mean square error of approximation is 0.03, normed fit index, non-normed fit index, and comparative fit index are all above 0.9.

Sports Lifestyle Questionnaire[12]: The scale comprises 30 items and is divided into 2 subscales: self-demand and social expansion. The self-demand subscale has 4 factors, such as healthy type, challenging type, pleasure type, and bodybuilding type. The social expansion includes learning type and social type. The internal consistency coefficient α of the whole scale is 0.867, and the retest reliability is 0.716. The internal consistency coefficient α of the factor is between 0.549 to 0.868, and the retest reliability coefficient is between 0.567 to 0.825, all of which reach the significance level of 0.01. Structural validity: root mean square error of approximation is 0.053, the goodness of fit index is 0.917, the adjusted goodness of fit index is 0.895, comparative fit index is 0.926 and non-normed fit index is 0.919.

2.3. Field test

According to the national physical fitness test requirements, the height and weight of college students are measured by the height and weight meter. Each student takes an average of 3 measurements; the height is accurate to 0.1 cm, and the weight is accurate to 0.1 kg. BMI = weight/height2 (kg/m2).

After having their height and weight measured, participants proceeded to a series of standardized tests. The maximal oxygen consumption (VO2max) test was administered individually, distinct from the group of tests that included pushups, cantilever-overhang, wall-squatting, rolling-belly, trunk-extension, sit-and-reach, and balance tests, all of which were conducted in a standardized testing environment. Upon arriving at the testing site, participants completed these tests in a predetermined sequence. To ensure uniformity and minimize measurement bias, the examiners received standardized training before the test.

This field test adopts the American FITNESSGRAM Fitness Test, in which an average of 2 measurements are taken, and all data readings are accurate to 0.1. The VO2max of the 20-meter turn-back run equals 31.025 + 3.238 × (8 + 0.5 × level)‐3.248 × age + 0.1536 × (8 + 0.5 × level) × age.

The calculation of the PFI is the Z score of 8 physical fitness indexes. The Z score equals the measured value of each physical fitness index reduces the average of each physical fitness index in each group, and is divided by the standard deviation of each PFI. Add the Z scores of all physical abilities to get PFI. PFI = Zvo2max + Zpush-ups + Zpull-ups + Zcantilever-overhang + Zsquatting-against-the-wall + Zrolling-belly + Ztrunk-extension + Zsit-and-reach(left) + Zsit-and-reach(right) + Zbalance. The higher the PFI score, the greater the participant’s physical fitness.

2.4. Mathematical statistics method

All the data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0, and descriptive statistics, including the mean and standard deviation (X¯±SD) were employed for feature analysis. Correlation analysis and path analysis were used to explore the correlation between the PFI of maritime college students, and variance analysis was used to compare grades. P < .05 is the significant difference level.

3.3.5. Path analysis of the influence of social support, sports lifestyle, and BMI affecting PFI

Figure 2 displays 6 significant paths that influence the PFI of maritime college students; firstly, subjective support → pleasant sports lifestyle → PFI; secondly, subjective support → pleasant sports lifestyle → BMI → PFI; thirdly, subjective support → fitness-type sports lifestyle → PFI; fourthly, subjective support → fitness-type sports lifestyle → BMI → PFI; fifthly, objective support → pleasant sports lifestyle → PFI; and sixthly, objective support →pleasant sports lifestyle → BMI → PFI. In the influence of maritime college students’ PFI, pleasant and fitness-type sports lifestyles may be a “mediation variable.’’ At the same time, BMI is not only a direct variable but also a “mediation variable” of sports lifestyle affecting PFI.

In addition, social support has no direct impact on PFI, whereas subjective support and objective support factors indirectly affect PFI by 3 variables: pleasant and fitness-type sports lifestyle and BMI. The social support utilization element, however, has no direct or indirect effect on PFI. The 6 social support paths have “indirect effects” on PFI due to the influence of objective support variables is smaller than the impact of subjective support factors.

Otherwise, pleasant and fitness-type sports lifestyles have both direct and indirect effects on PFI, whereas BMI has a direct effect on PFI. In addition, the impact of a pleasant sports lifestyle on BMI is negative, while that of a fitness-type sports lifestyle is positive.

4. Discussion

PFI is a comprehensive index reflecting individual physical fitness level,[13] our study findings suggest that maritime students’ PFI and sports lifestyle significantly decline from the first to the third year, independent of BMI changes. Social support, particularly subjective support, indirectly affects PFI through sports lifestyle and BMI. Both pleasant and fitness-type sports lifestyles directly and indirectly influence PFI, highlighting the need to foster healthy sports habits. These insights are crucial for developing targeted interventions to improve maritime students’ physical fitness and well-being.

In our study, we found that all 6 factors associated with a sports lifestyle were positively correlated with the PFI, confirming that an active engagement in physical activity is beneficial for enhancing the physical fitness of maritime students. This aligns with previous study, such as numerous studies that have shown sports to be instrumental in strengthening physical fitness and improving overall health.[14] Chen Huawei study has demonstrated that college students who adopt positive sports lifestyle habits tend to have better physical fitness compared to their peers.[15] These findings underscore the importance of physical activity in maintaining and improving the physical fitness of maritime students, suggesting that active sports lifestyle is a key factor in preventing a decline in physical fitness.

In this study, we included students from the navigation major with normal weight, overweight, and obesity, which were differentiated using BMI. Studies have shown a negative impact between BMI and physical fitness, especially concerning physical fitness that requires participants to move fast.[16–18] Therefore, our study further analyzed the correlation between the 6 factors of sports lifestyle and BMI. We found that a pleasant sports lifestyle is negatively correlated with BMI, whereas a fitness-type sports lifestyle is positively correlated with BMI. On this basis, we conducted regression analysis and path analysis of the pleasant sports lifestyle, fitness-type sports lifestyle, BMI, and PFI. Our findings revealed a significant statistical relationship among these 3 factors. Both the pleasant sports lifestyle and the fitness-type sports lifestyle have a direct impact on the PFI, and they can also indirectly influence the PFI through BMI.

The relationship between BMI and physical fitness has been well-documented in numerous studies. It is established that an elevated BMI can lead to slower muscle contractions and increased oxygen consumption, which in turn diminishes the body’s exercise capacity. Nevertheless, the literature presents a mixed view on the nature of this relationship. Some research has demonstrated a linear association between BMI and physical fitness, suggesting that as BMI increases, physical fitness decreases in a consistent manner.[19,20] Conversely, other studies have uncovered a nonlinear relationship, indicating that the decline in physical fitness may not be proportional to the increase in BMI, or that there may be thresholds beyond which the relationship takes on a different character.[18,19] In our study, we extend the current understanding by examining the role of BMI as a mediator in the relationship between lifestyle factors, such as pleasant sports lifestyle and fitness-type sports lifestyle, and the PFI. Our findings suggest that BMI plays a significant mediating role in this complex interplay, highlighting the importance of considering BMI as a critical factor in the context of lifestyle and fitness outcomes. Our study investigates the impact of perceived and objective support on the fitness lifestyles and PFI of maritime students. We find that subjective support positively affects PFI directly and indirectly through both pleasant sports lifestyle and fitness-type sports lifestyle. In contrast, objective support influences PFI only indirectly via pleasant sports lifestyle. These findings highlight the importance of enhancing subjective support to improve the physical fitness of maritime students, suggesting a targeted approach to maximize the effectiveness of support mechanisms in this context. Huang Meirong and Zhang Yanping research on college students using the ecological theory proved that the social factors influencing college students’ sports lifestyle include social support factors and media support.[21]

The study’s strengths include the comprehensive PFI and the demonstration of social support’s direct and indirect effects on fitness. It also shows a positive link between fitness-oriented sports lifestyles and PFI. However, it has limitations such as a limited sample of maritime students, which may affect generalizability, and the lack of consideration for other influential factors like age and gender. The ecological theory is not fully utilized to analyze the social environmental factors affecting fitness.

In conclusion, this study reveals that there is a strong correlation between the BMI and PFI of maritime major college students; the physical lifestyle may alter the PFI by influencing BMI; moreover, social support can change the PFI by affecting the physical lifestyle of maritime major college students, as well as by influencing individuals’ BMI.
